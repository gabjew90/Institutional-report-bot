## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI infrastructure is the trade and the market

*The 2026 capex cycle has gone from upside surprise to the single load-bearing earnings story in the index, and the desks calling for a fade are losing the argument.*

- Dell's F2027 revenue guidance jumped to $165-$169 billion from $138-$142 billion prior, with AI server revenue raised to $60 billion from $50 billion and EPS lifted to $17.90 from $12.90 (Goldman). Dell ripped 40% overnight on the print, Lenovo +50% in two weeks. The capex story is now also a hardware-margins story
- Anthropic raised $65 billion at a $900 billion valuation (Goldman); Microsoft's commercial RPO sits at $627 billion, up roughly 99% year over year; AWS backlog at $364 billion excluding Anthropic. The ten-figure forward bookings are the proof that hyperscaler capex is meeting actual demand, not racing ahead of it
- Citadel pegs 2026 AI capex at $750 billion as the backdrop for elevated US growth pricing. Goldman raised its year-end S&P target to 8,000 with $340 of 2026 EPS, 24% year-over-year, on this trade
- The semiconductor TAM for AI data center systems is heading from $264 billion in 2025 to over $1.7 trillion by 2030, HBM memory alone going to $168 billion at a 37% CAGR (BofA). Wafer fab equipment is +24% year over year in 2026 to $144 billion. Picks-and-shovels names sit on a multi-year order book
- Nomura measures the 41% weight of hyperscaler-semis-memory names in the S&P and 52% in the Nasdaq. The trade is the index now, not a sleeve of it

The mechanism is simple. When a quarter of S&P earnings growth is tied to one capex cycle and the same names are 41% of the index by weight, every dollar of incremental backlog announcement is mechanically a positive index move regardless of breadth. The catch-up trade in software (IGV up 33% since mid-April, 7 straight up weeks, $1.5 billion of May inflows per LSEG) is the leveraged-beta version of the same thesis. The bear case is real: MIT research pegs 95% of enterprises at zero return on GenAI investment so far, the Entelligence index puts useful output per dollar of token spend at 18 cents, and $182 billion of data-center debt issuance in 2025 (roughly double 2024) means the capex is increasingly debt-funded. Those numbers describe a unit-economics gap that closes either when revenue lands or when the funding cycle breaks. Until META or GOOGL guides capex DOWN, the binding constraint is supply, not demand. The bias for US equity exposure stays long the hardware-and-memory complex via $SOXX, with $MU as the cleanest single-name expression of the memory-pricing leg.

### The Hormuz risk premium is being unwound while the tankers stay home

*Oil traded on two timeframes at once last month. Spot inventories say the supply hole is worse than the futures curve is pricing.*

- Tanker transits through Hormuz are down roughly 90% from normal levels, with Goldman flagging 10 million barrels per day of lost Gulf-area crude as the initial shock volume. WTI is down 17% on the month, the worst since April 2025
- RBC counts 300 million barrels of lost production since the original imminent-MoU story broke three weeks ago, and projects 30-to-40-day inventory cover by October, the lowest reading in the dataset since 2016
- The current global inventory drain rate while the Strait stays restricted is 10 to 14 million barrels a day (Market Ear). That is not a soft-landing setup
- Citadel models the immediate market reaction if the Strait reopens cleanly: 10-year Treasuries rally 12.5bp, S&P pops 1.71%, broad dollar falls 0.53%. The asymmetry says the pain is shorts-of-oil in a reopening, not longs
- Goldman warns the second-order risk is demand destruction. 2% of global GDP runs through Middle Eastern oil under normal input-output accounting, and 27% of global GDP is theoretically exposed under a Leontief-style bottleneck where Middle East crude is genuinely non-substitutable

Twelve banks reference Hormuz as a live catalyst, and the price action has gone the other way because the futures curve trades the headlines while the physical market trades the missing barrels. The opposite read here is that ceasefire chatter is increasingly serious, refined-product inventories will get one or two reload cycles before the autumn squeeze, and the Trump-Iran deal headlines that lifted equities last week could resolve by July. That risk is the entire reason WTI is at $54 instead of $90. But you do not refill a 300-million-barrel inventory hole in two weeks of headline diplomacy, and the physical inventory math says the price has to converge UP to the spot reality, not down to the futures fantasy. Long $BNO at $50 is the cleanest expression. The $USO leg is a roll-cost trap and not the trade.

### Positioning is at the limit on both sides of the book

*Hedge funds have rebuilt all the gross they took off in March, and the size of the levered single-stock ETF complex makes any unwind faster than the last one.*

- Hedge fund gross exposure is at the 100th percentile across all measured lookbacks (Market Ear). Long-versus-short ratio in semis, hardware, and power names is 3x, versus 1.6x for the rest of the US equity book (Morgan Stanley). The crowd is sized at the limit
- Levered single-stock ETF AUM is $187 billion, an all-time high (Nomura). The complex amplifies daily moves in the very names — NVDA, TSLA, MSTR — that already drive the index
- Software short interest is at the 100th percentile vs the past six years (Market Ear). Software gross book exposure is at the 87th percentile on a 12-month basis (Morgan Stanley). The squeeze fuel is still there
- Retail trading volume is 10% above the January 2021 meme-bubble record. Daily gross options premium is tracking 1.9x its historical average, about 13% above the 2021 peak (Citadel). Call option volume is 70% of total (Market Ear)
- 28% of S&P 500 constituents are outperforming the index over 30 days, a 1st percentile observation across 30 years (Citadel). Ten names did 67% of the run since end of March

Pain trades cut both directions from here. The positioning math says the next 5% in the index goes the way the largest cohort is wrong. With gross at the 100th percentile and 67% of returns concentrated in ten names, the asymmetric vulnerability is a single MAG7 guide-down — which would force concentrated unwinds across an enormous levered-ETF wrapper at the same time. Nomura calls out the levered-ETF AUM specifically as the catalyst that makes the next crowding reversal disorderly. The defense is that retail flow and the buyback machine ($865 billion of 2026 YTD authorizations, annualized to $1.5 trillion per Citadel) keep absorbing supply, and a soft landing plus an Iran deal puts the squeeze fuel in software shorts to work. The cleanest hedge for an index-long book here is downside through $QQQ puts sized to a 5% drawdown over four weeks. The bias on the long side stays $MU and the memory complex, where positioning is heavy but the demand backlog is verifiable.

### Software has eight straight weeks of up. The bear thesis just got revoked

*The setup that the AI bubble was crowding out enterprise software spend was wrong, and short positioning is still at the highs.*

- IGV is up 36% in 7 weeks (Market Ear). $1.5 billion of May inflows into IGV, the third-largest monthly haul since November 2025 (LSEG). The flow is institutional, not retail
- Software short interest is in the 100th percentile vs the past six years (Market Ear); software gross book exposure is at the 87th percentile on a 12-month basis (Morgan Stanley)
- The JPM-flagged IGV June 82/92 call spread has returned roughly 4x. The structure that worked the bounce is still cheap because skew is in puts
- The 2-month gain in the S&P Tech sector (April-May) is +36.1%, the third best two-month tech print since 1990 (Market Ear). Momentum factor exposure is at 50% above 2021 highs in the Prime book
- The contrast: SOX is up 165% over one year while IGV was essentially flat into mid-April. The catch-up is what the May squeeze priced

The mechanism is that software companies that survived the "AI is going to eat their lunch" narrative now have the same AI-tied revenue tailwind without the capex burden semis carry. Margins are levered to that. The bear case is that an enterprise IT budget hit from a 4.6% unemployment rate by year-end (Goldman) and slowing real income growth (1.3% Q4/Q4 in 2026 per Goldman) is what bites software guides in the second half. That risk is real for verticals exposed to small business, but the IGV book is dominated by names with multi-year backlogs that are paid through next year regardless. The trade is staying long $IGV through summer, with the JPM call spread structure still the cheapest expression for someone with US options exposure.

### Breadth is widening, and it changes what the index needs to keep working

*Equal-weight and small-cap participation is finally joining the rally, and the implication is that the next 5% in the index does not need the same five names to do all the work.*

- The Market Ear, Goldman, and RBC all flag broadening into equal-weight and small-cap names off the late-April lows. Industrial cyclicals positioning at the 39th percentile is conservative (Market Ear) — there is room for re-engagement
- Industrials short exposure is at the 90th/96th percentile on a 1- and 5-year lookback (Market Ear). The same setup that fueled the software squeeze applies to the industrial book
- The Q1 S&P earnings growth print landed at roughly 25.5% year over year (Citadel), versus the 13% expected at the start of the season. The earnings power is no longer concentrated narrative
- Forward S&P EPS estimates are up to roughly $350 from $235 at the start of the year (Citadel). Goldman's 8,000 year-end target is based on 24% EPS growth to $340 for 2026
- Citadel flags 28% of S&P constituents outperforming the index over 30 days as a 1st percentile observation. The complement is also true: when breadth bottoms here, it usually mean-reverts UP, not down

The mechanism is earnings convergence. With Q1 prints coming in roughly double the expected rate and forward estimates already lifted across the board, the second-derivative story shifts from "ten names doing all the work" to "everyone else catching up." The bear case is that breadth widens on softer data, which would actually be a regime change (slowing growth + small-cap participation = nothing good historically). But the data we have is a 25.5% earnings beat and a record buyback authorization run, not a slowdown. Long $RSP (the equal-weight S&P) versus a partial $SPY hedge captures the catch-up without taking the concentration risk. For options exposure, $IWM call-spreads through July are the highest-asymmetry expression of the same setup.
