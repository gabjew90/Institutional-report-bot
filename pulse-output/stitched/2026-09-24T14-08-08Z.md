# Cuts out, hikes in

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

September business surveys came in far hotter than anyone modeled, and the bond market responded by deleting the rate-cut path and pricing hikes instead. Stocks fell alongside bonds, small caps took the worst of it, and energy was the only sector group that gained. The positioning data underneath tells a less obvious story. Hedge funds are re-grossing, led by quantitative funds, while JPMorgan's measure of how crowded US stock positioning has become sits near five-year lows, a reading that has historically marked a trough rather than a top. Trend-following funds are carrying their most extreme bet against ten-year Treasuries and German government bonds in a year. Deutsche Bank describes discretionary investors as very cautious. A hawkish repricing landing on top of already-light positioning is why the selloff has stayed orderly so far.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The bond market deleted the cuts and started pricing hikes

*The repricing has further to run, and equities have not finished discounting it.*

- The US composite business survey hit a five-year high of 58.4 in September against 55.3 expected (Deutsche Bank). A beat that wide is what repriced the whole curve in one session instead of over weeks.
- The ten-year Treasury yield jumped 15.2 basis points (one basis point is one hundredth of a percent) on Wednesday to 5.11%, the highest since 2007 and the biggest single-day move since April 2025 (Deutsche Bank). Equity valuations built on a falling discount rate stop working at that level.
- Traders now price 37 basis points of Fed hikes by year-end, close to even odds on two more, with October alone at 71% (Deutsche Bank). A month ago that same curve carried cuts, so this is a reversal rather than a trim.
- Wednesday's five-year Treasury auction tailed 3.20 basis points on weak end-user demand (JPMorgan). Buyers are not stepping up at these yields yet, which is the mechanical reason the move extended instead of stalling.

The chain runs from activity to costs to policy. Business surveys this strong, arriving with the fastest monthly rise in input costs since 2020 (JPMorgan), remove the disinflation assumption that justified a cut, and a central bank forced to resume tightening after a pause loses any ability to talk long-term yields back down.

JPMorgan reads the same selloff as an entry point rather than a warning. Its fair-value estimate for the ten-year is 5.5%, and its strategists argue that past episodes of yields overshooting toward fair value have been buying opportunities in equities. That case is stronger than the last two sessions make it look, but it carries its own timing problem, because 5.5% sits nearly 40 basis points above where the ten-year closed Wednesday. Even the constructive house's model requires more yield pain before its buy level arrives, which is why its own near-term equity stance is tactically cautious into the Fed. Wednesday's auction is the tell on which side gets tested first: if 5.11% were genuinely cheap against a 5.5% anchor, end users would have taken down the five-year paper, and instead the auction tailed. The quarterly growth reading and the inflation report due at 8:30 AM ET on September 30 are the first hard test of whether September's growth surprise is real or a one-month survey artifact.

### JPMorgan is long tech and buying protection on it at the same time

*The insurance it just bought is the more informative half of that pair.*

The core position is unchanged, an overweight in technology split across mega-cap names, memory and semiconductors, funded by a short in the Russell 2000. What is new is the hedge. JPMorgan has started buying put options on its own earnings-vulnerability basket rather than on semiconductors directly, arguing the basket carries similar directional exposure at lower volatility and is therefore a cheaper way to carry the position through month-end rebalancing. The risk it is paying to cover is already showing up in single names. $SHOP fell 7% on concern that Meta builds payments into its Muse agent, and $EXPE and $CART sold off on the same fear of being cut out of their own transactions even as they announced partnerships (JPMorgan). Insurers that blocked Muse from their marketplace outperformed on the day. JPMorgan holds long technology, weighted to mega-caps, memory and semiconductors, against short $IWM on a three-to-twelve-month view, and names the Fed meeting outcome as the risk to that short leg.

### China's AI profit pool gets a number, and a summit to price it

*The forecast is big enough to underwrite the trade, and today's meeting decides its timing.*

JPMorgan initiated broader coverage of Chinese artificial intelligence with a constructive view, putting US$544 billion of cumulative operating profit on the value chain between 2026 and 2030. The shape of that number matters more than its size. Models and applications are expected to deliver about a third of industry profits but close to two-thirds of the growth in annual earnings across the period, which argues for owning the platforms rather than the hardware that trains them. $BABA and $TCEHY are the named beneficiaries. The $FXI summit trade flagged September 22 has its first answer on tariffs, with the US and China extending their trade truce by two months and holding tariffs at current levels until January 10, and the Trump-Xi meeting itself running today. JPMorgan is long Chinese internet through $KWEB and prefers Hong Kong-listed shares over mainland ones into China tech earnings.
