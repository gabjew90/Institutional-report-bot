# Jobs hot, AI cracks

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

Friday's jobs print scrambled the easy-cuts story and Monday's tape paid for it. The AI bid that has been carrying the index since spring took its biggest single-day reset of the cycle, with leveraged ETF unwinds and momentum-factor pain visible across the chip names. The geopolitical backdrop did not help, with Israel-Iran exchanges of fire over the weekend and the EU sanctioning Iranian officials over Hormuz transit restrictions. Underneath, banks read the corpus as still constructive on US economic resilience and on the AI capex cycle even after the reset, with a vocal minority (Goldman desk color, Academy Securities, BNP Paribas, The Market Ear) warning that crowded positioning and leveraged-ETF flows have changed the way the unwind transmits through the tape.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex stays structural but the leveraged-flow unwind is the new transmission

*The AI infrastructure cycle is not the part that cracked on Friday, the way the market trades it is.*

- Goldman expects AI to add 3.3 percentage points to US capex growth in 2026, and the bank flags hyperscaler advance orders worth roughly $2 trillion in compute resources as the demand floor under the chip and power names
- Morgan Stanley forecasts hyperscaler capex of $1 trillion by 2027 and pencils aggregate memory market revenue of $890B in 2026, with PC memory short by 15% and smartphone memory short by 12% in 2027 because DRAM wafer capacity is only expanding 30% by then
- UBS keeps TSMC 2026 capex at $56B and reads $NVDA's $124B purchase commitment as visibility that the order book is not just one buyer; Raymond James adds that earnings revisions are still moving with AI capex spend
- The Market Ear and Goldman desk color flag levered-equity ETF AUM near $300B (with cross-asset exposure controlling roughly $750B in equities) and over $100B in levered tech-ETF AUM specifically, with $SPY futures' 7,400-7,375 zone now the must-hold for the systematic complex; below that the next gravity is 7,200 (the 50-day)
- Bank of America and Bloomberg add that earnings growth is still being lifted by the capex boom, while warning that risk swung from "despair to exuberance" inside two months

The mechanism: when leveraged and 0DTE flows control short-term price formation, the structural earnings story (memory cycles, hyperscaler orders, payrolls revisions tied to AI capex) keeps validating the bull case, while day-to-day price action is set by short gamma in levered ETFs that buys strength and sells weakness. Friday's payrolls forced a vol re-rate, and the levered complex was a one-way seller into it. The bear case from Academy Securities and BNP Paribas is that this is the first crack in a positioning bubble where retail 0DTE gambling and inverse-ETF rebalancing wrote the rally and will write the unwind; The Market Ear's must-hold $SPY 7,375 and $QQQ 29,000 Fibonacci level are the explicit triggers worth watching. The picks-and-shovels side has not flinched: $TSM 2026 capex unchanged, memory shortfalls intact, $NVDA orders not cancelled, $MU memory cycle still structurally tight. Long $SMH and long $MU into the next print, with $SPY put spreads as the tail hedge against another levered-ETF flush.

### Hormuz is still open, energy is the global inflation transmission

*Banks treat Hormuz transit as functioning but expensive, with Iran-conflict energy inflation already showing up in the May data.*

- The EU sanctioned Iranian officials over Hormuz naval restrictions overnight, with Iran's envoy to Moscow saying the strait will stay open with new transit fees added; Israeli strikes on western and central Iran continued through Sunday
- Crédit Agricole CIB pegs Energy CPI YoY at 24.3% peaking in May 2026, Bank of America has energy goods inflation contributing 0.9 percentage points to headline CPI on a 29.2% YoY print, and Wells Fargo flags May energy goods at +8% month-over-month as the primary driver of headline CPI
- ANZ Research reads headline CPI as +0.5% month-over-month in May driven by high energy prices, while Lloyds, SEB, UniCredit all carry the Iran-war-as-inflation-shock through their May macro work
- BlackRock, Deutsche Bank, JPMorgan, UBS, Goldman, and The Market Ear treat the Middle East theme as live but largely refuse to take a directional oil call from here ("oil market calm masks a host of unknowns," per the Reuters wire this morning)
- Only Goldman Sachs has staked a one-direction view on actual Hormuz normalization lifting risk markets

The mechanism: transit fees are a steady-state tax on every barrel that moves through Hormuz, which keeps refined-product margins fat and feeds into headline inflation on a 3-month lag exactly as the May numbers are showing. The market is pricing the Strait staying functional, but the trade is in the floor under energy prices and the inflation pass-through, not in oil-spike scenarios that depend on a closure that nobody is forecasting. The pushback is the Goldman desk's normalization-lifts-risk read, which would clip energy and bid duration; the corpus's response is that even normalization at fees keeps energy CPI elevated for months, and the May print is the receipts. Long $XLE into the next Iran headline, with the secondary expression long $BNO over $USO if Brent's geopolitical premium reasserts.

### The Fed is off the cuts script and at least one desk now sees a hike

*Friday's payrolls killed the last cut for 2026 and the curve is starting to price something the Fed has not endorsed.*

- Bank of America explicitly removed the final 2026 cut after the jobs print, with the desk's terminal range at 3.00-3.25% by end-cycle and an indefinite hold near neutral as the new baseline
- UBS, Raymond James, ANZ Research, and Deutsche Bank converge on a wait-and-see Fed with the easing bias coming out of the June statement, and Deutsche Bank notes markets are now fully pricing a Fed rate hike by December
- Crédit Agricole CIB pushes back: the bar for a hike is higher than the curve thinks, and the base case is an extended hold with Fed Funds upper bound at 3.75% through year-end
- Natixis flags the same easing-bias removal but lines up with Crédit Agricole that hiking is a tail outcome, not a base case
- The Fed blackout period started June 6 ahead of the FOMC, and the discovered-theme cluster around Kevin Warsh as a potential Fed chair pick (ANZ Research, Berenberg, Deutsche Bank, Nordea) is a slow-burn risk to the 2027 path that is not in the current curve

The mechanism: a 172k payroll print against an ~85k consensus, energy-driven CPI feeding through, and a Fed that has just left the easing bias on the table is the textbook setup for a curve that flattens at the front and sells the long end. The bear case to "no cuts in 2026" is the Crédit Agricole / Natixis read that the bar for a hike is much higher than the Deutsche Bank pricing implies, and that the next data point will determine whether the front-end pricing is sustainable. The corpus's response is that the labor data plus the energy pass-through is exactly what stops the Fed from cutting, and the Wednesday CPI is the next test. Long $TLT puts into CPI Wednesday, with the trade closed if the print comes in cool on core month-over-month.

### ECB hikes Thursday and the consensus is for one and done

*Banks are lined up on a 25 basis point ECB deposit-rate hike to address energy-driven inflation pressure.*

- Goldman Sachs expects the Governing Council to raise the deposit rate by 25 basis points (0.25 percentage points) at the June 11 meeting, with a final raise in September bringing the deposit rate to 2.50%
- UniCredit, SEB, Scotiabank, Natixis, Mizuho International, and Morgan Stanley all carry the same 25bp June 11 hike call, with UniCredit calling 2.50% by September as the terminal
- Deutsche Bank pegs the post-hike deposit rate at 2.25% and signals that the ECB framing will lean on Iran-war energy pass-through to justify the move
- Lloyds, JPMorgan, and Mizuho keep the consensus, no skeptical bank in the corpus is pushing back on the size or timing of the call
- The discovered Bank of England read (Taylor sees rates on hold barring worst-case scenario, per the wires this morning) is the policy contrast — the Fed and ECB are tightening while the BOE pauses

The mechanism: the ECB hike is the cleanest policy expression of the Iran-war energy story playing out in the May macro data. The bull case from the rates side is that one quarter-point clip plus a September follow-on is already priced and the euro should fade into the cut, which is consistent with $UUP's bid yesterday. The pushback is that "priced for lift-off" is a high bar to actually print, and a dovish surprise from Lagarde at the presser would unwind the cross-asset positioning very fast. The corpus has no bear case worth surfacing here, which itself is a sign of crowded positioning. Long $UUP into the meeting, take profits on a dovish-side surprise.

### The contrarian voices: rotate out of the lead and into what nobody owns

*Three desks are explicitly calling the AI/momentum unwind and naming where to rotate into, not just out of.*

- Bank of America's Global Economic Weekly (the "AI, Iran war and K-shaped economy" note) ties the AI top to the same factors that argue for rotation into defensive cash-flow and dividend names
- UBS House View - Daily Asia frames the consensus as crowded long-tech and short-defensives, with the explicit recommendation to take down the active overweight in MAG7
- World Gold Council's May ETF commentary cuts the other way: gold ETF outflows say even the inflation-hedge consensus is leaning the same direction, and the rotation into hard-asset reflation has yet to start
- The corpus also carries Goldman desk color flagging that "leverage" and "momentum" both moved in reverse on Friday, which is the technical confirmation of the rotation thesis from the day-after data
- Underweighted candidates leveraged-speculation (8 banks) and equity-rotation (5 banks) cluster around the same pivot point: the trade after the AI top is not "short AI"  it is "long what AI has crowded out"

The mechanism: when leveraged ETF flows and 0DTE positioning have been the marginal buyer of the lead theme, the rotation does not look like a bear market in the index, it looks like a sharp underperformance of the names that the systematic and retail complex were forced to own. The pushback is that the corpus is still mostly long the AI capex theme (Goldman, UBS, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America all in the supportive camp), which means the rotation is a tactical play, not a regime change. The corpus's own response is that yesterday's $SPY -2.58% with $QQQ -4.80% is the first day this cycle that the rotation actually expressed through relative performance rather than just relative positioning. Long $XLV paired against a half-sized short $XLK position, with $VYM dividend-heavy ETF as the income complement if the unwind extends.
