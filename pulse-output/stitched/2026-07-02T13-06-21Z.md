# Buildout intact, trade broken

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The research reads like two markets at once. The AI infrastructure buildout keeps accelerating, with banks raising capex forecasts and the memory and equipment chain sold out into next year, yet the crowded trade sitting on top of it just suffered its most violent unwind of the year, and the money coming out is showing up in health care, small caps and software. Underneath that rotation, the Fed conversation has flipped from cuts to live hikes on hot labor data, Europe's inflation is cooling faster than forecast, and oil's war premium keeps draining as the Strait of Hormuz reopens. Crypto is the conspicuous outlier, with bank desks cutting Bitcoin forecasts on dead ETF demand. Everything funnels into this morning's June payrolls report, which decides whether a July Fed hike becomes a coin flip.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI buildout is fine, the AI trade is not

*The trillion-dollar data-center spend is intact and spreading into power and equipment, so own the suppliers being pulled into the buildout instead of the crowded chip names everyone already owns.*

- Annual AI capex is headed toward $1 trillion next year (UBS), and hyperscaler cash capex is estimated to pass $1 trillion in 2027 (Morgan Stanley). The spend is growing into the slowdown fears, not out of them.
- Cloud revenue at the major platforms grew 40% in Q1 and is expected to accelerate through 2026 (UBS). The revenue side of the buildout is showing up, which is what keeps boards approving the checks.
- $MU capex jumps from $27bn this fiscal year to a modeled $50bn next (Goldman), and Samsung committed a 15-year W2,450tn investment plan with 76% earmarked for semiconductors. Memory demand still outruns supply, so the equipment chain stays sold out.
- AI names now make up a record 51% of the S&P 500 including the Mag-7 (Goldman). Concentration that extreme means sharper drawdowns whenever the story wobbles, which is exactly what June delivered.
- Hyperscaler stocks fell roughly 17-20% from June 1 (Goldman, UBS) even as their spending plans grew. The market is repricing who wins from the spend, not whether the spend happens.

The capex is diffusing outward, from chips into the power plants, grid equipment and industrial gear needed to actually run the data centers, which is why the winner list is broadening faster than index leadership (JPMorgan and BofA both call it the broadening trade with real legs).

The crack to take seriously is Meta reportedly building a cloud business to sell its spare AI compute. If the biggest buyer of compute has excess capacity to rent out, the scarcity justifying all this spending is softer than assumed, and enterprise customers from Amazon to Walmart are already capping their AI token budgets (Deutsche Bank). But Meta's move preserves optionality rather than signaling a 2027 capex cut (JPMorgan), and the latest $MU results showed no relief at all in AI hardware supply constraints (UBS). The bull case is that capex keeps accelerating and broadening into Q2 earnings, where consensus sees 22% S&P 500 profit growth. The risk is a single hyperscaler capex guide-down, which would crack the entire picks-and-shovels chain at once. Resolution arrives with the cloud revenue and capex guides at earnings season starting July 14. The lean is long the power and equipment side of the diffusion, $NXT, $GEV and $PWR, and Goldman just added $NXT to its Conviction List with a $168 target on its evolution into a broader power technology platform, the strongest single-name backing this trade has.

### Warsh means the hawkish talk

The Fed under new Chair Kevin Warsh is tilted toward raising rates, and bonds are still priced as if he is bluffing. Markets now price 37bp of hikes by year-end, a full swing from the 50bp of cuts expected earlier this year (Goldman), and July hike odds jumped to roughly 36% from near zero after Warsh told the Sintra forum he will "disappoint" anyone expecting loose policy. The dissent is real and named: Morgan Stanley stands alone calling for no change through 2026, UBS also argues the market is overpricing tightening, while Goldman and JPMorgan frame a hawkish pivot and TS Lombard goes furthest, one hike this year, four next, and a 4.65-4.90% terminal rate against the 3.9% the market assumes. Rate risk lands unevenly, since 40% of the Russell 2000 carries floating-rate debt (JPMorgan). Cost of being wrong: short-maturity Treasuries pay you roughly 4% while you wait and $TLT drifts rather than rips. Cost of missing it if the hikes land: long-dated bonds sell off again and every rate-sensitive position repricing with them. Carry: positive on the short end either way. The lean is short $TLT, keep bond money in short maturities.

### The job market will not cool

Hot labor data, not Fed speeches, is what actually forces hikes, and this morning's payrolls print is the trigger. May job openings came in at 7.594 million against 7.3 million expected (Morgan Stanley, ING), the 3-month payroll average hit 188k in May, the highest in two years (Deutsche Bank), and the rates market chose to trade the labor resilience while ignoring the soft data (Mizuho). For today's June print the street sits near 110k, JPMorgan is at 125k, Goldman at 130k with the whisper near 137k, and unemployment is expected steady at 4.3%. The soft side does exist, consumer confidence printed a weak 91.2 and BofA cut its Q2 GDP tracking to 2.1%, but the market has decided jobs outrank it. Watch in this order: (1) the headline number at 8:30 AM ET, where a 100k-160k print carries 65% odds and historically lifts stocks (JPMorgan), so the lean is long $SPY only inside that band and flat outside it, (2) whether the print tops 150k, which pushes a July hike toward a coin flip and breaks the dollar higher (Goldman), (3) average hourly earnings at +0.2% monthly for the wage-heat check, (4) the unemployment rate, where anything below 4.3% feeds the economy-heating-back-up read.

### Europe's inflation blinked first

Euro-area inflation is cooling faster than forecast, which lets the ECB wait until September while the Fed argues about hiking this month, and currencies trade that gap. June headline inflation printed 2.8% against 3.0% expected and core 2.4% against 2.5% (Goldman), with France slowing to 2.0% from 2.8% and Germany to 2.4% (UBS, Morgan Stanley), so the cooling is broad rather than one country's quirk. Markets cut ECB year-end hike pricing to 0.95 hikes from 1.25 (Crédit Agricole), Goldman and Natixis both put the next move in September, Lagarde called inflation risks "more broadly balanced," and council members Wunsch and Kazaks floated holding in July. The pushback is that ECB officials still see energy costs bleeding into services and food prices, which would shorten the pause. If euro core inflation re-accelerates back above 2.5% before the September meeting, this thesis is dead. Until then the trade is short EURUSD, with last week's 1.1325 low (Goldman) the level in play into payrolls.

### Rotate before July does it for you

The most crowded version of the AI trade is unwinding on schedule, and the exits point at health care and small caps. Goldman's high-beta momentum basket dropped roughly 9-10% in a single session after a record +57% first half, and the same basket has finished lower every July since 2021. Momentum exposure in hedge fund books sits at the 92nd percentile of the past five years (Goldman Prime) and gross leverage at 338% is a 98th percentile reading (JPMorgan), the kind of crowding that turns small unwinds violent. The money is already moving: BofA clients sold $9.9bn of single stocks, the fourth-largest outflow on record, while small and micro-caps took in their largest inflows ever, and health care just posted its best week in four years while still trading at a 3% discount to global equities versus its usual 5% premium (UBS), with a mean +7.5% edge over the S&P in Q3 of midterm years and +11% for biotech (JPMorgan). The counter is that fundamentals in the momentum longs remain strong and July 14 earnings could bail the book out. Does the momentum unwind finish before those earnings arrive to rescue it? Long $XLV plus $IWM says you do not need the answer, because that is where the outflows are already landing.

### Oil's fear premium keeps leaking

With the Strait of Hormuz reopening, oil rallies are for fading, not chasing. Brent just logged its worst quarter since early 2020, down over 38% (ING), and sits near $72, essentially back at pre-war levels (UBS, TS Lombard). US production hit a record 13.93m barrels a day with record exports of 13.61m (ING), so the marginal barrel no longer depends on the Gulf, and tanker traffic through the Strait keeps normalizing, with outbound trips averaging 15.6 vessels a day and inbound near 11 (Morgan Stanley's daily tracker). The floor is real though: US crude inventories drew for a tenth straight week and the strategic reserve for a fourteenth (JPMorgan), and prices look close to a bottom on inventory depletion and prospective Chinese buying (TS Lombard). If a Doha breakdown puts the supply scare back on before the August 18 US-Iran deal deadline (Rabobank expects extension, not collapse), the short is dead. Until then the trade is short USO on rallies, sized small because the physical draws limit the downside.
