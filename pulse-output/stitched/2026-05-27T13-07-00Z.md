# Semis melt, Hormuz holds

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The dominant cross-bank story heading into today's open is a semiconductor rally that has gone vertical alongside a Hormuz situation that refuses to resolve cleanly. Multiple desks frame today's tape as a feedback loop, parabolic AI-linked equity moves running against thinning hedges and a macro short book at decade highs. Goldman, JPMorgan, and Morgan Stanley are all pushing hyperscaler capex forecasts sharply higher into 2026 and 2027, which is how the semis melt-up is being justified despite stretched technicals. At the same time, Rabobank flags roughly 1,500 ships still trapped in the Strait of Hormuz and JPMorgan sees a potential US-Iran MOU within days, so the oil tape is sitting on a binary outcome with desks split on whether to fade the war premium or stay long Brent.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hormuz holds the binary while desks split

*Rabobank says the disruption lasts months while JPMorgan positions for an imminent MOU, and that gap is the trade.*

- Rabobank counts about 1,500 ships trapped in the Strait of Hormuz and projects Q3 2026 Brent averaging $120/bbl, meaning their base case is at least three more months of physical disruption regardless of headlines
- JPMorgan sees a potential US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding in coming days as the catalyst to broaden the equity rally beyond AI, with reopening trades framed as still working
- Deutsche Bank's morning note flags oil up on the latest US strikes but says talks are proceeding in parallel, capturing the two-track dynamic the rest of the corpus echoes
- Goldman Sachs Sales Trading has Brent around $105/bbl in their note this morning, well off Rabobank's $120 forecast level
- Rabobank also cites the US 5Y-30Y curve widening to 84bp from a one-year low of 81bp, which it reads as the bond market starting to price the oil-driven inflation persisting

The split is not a soft disagreement, it is two banks looking at the same shipping data and the same diplomatic backchannel and reaching opposite conclusions about which side breaks first. Rabobank sees physical supply that takes months to refill (refined-fuel inventories are tight per several desks) and reads the curve steepening as the bond market agreeing. JPMorgan reads the negotiation cadence as suggesting a deal is close enough that the war premium fades into a relief rally, with $USO and $BNO compressing back toward pre-strike levels. The defense Rabobank flags is mechanical, even a six-week ceasefire does not refill the physical supply hole because shipping reroutes and storage rebuilds take time. The bull case for fade-the-premium needs a clean MOU headline, not a Marco Rubio quote about negotiations ending the war in a few days. Long $BNO captures the supply-stays-tight side, and the trade is invalidated by a full ceasefire announcement that lasts more than a week.

### AI infrastructure capex floor keeps ratcheting higher

*Three Tier-1 desks raised their hyperscaler capex numbers in two weeks, and the AI capex floor is now the entire bull case for US equities.*

- Goldman Sachs raised its 2026 hyperscaler capex estimate to $754B, up 83% year-over-year, and now expects AI-infrastructure beneficiaries to account for roughly half of S&P 500 EPS growth this year
- Morgan Stanley's revised 2026 US hyperscaler capex sits at $805B (up from a $433B estimate a year ago) and projects the long-term path reaching $1.3T by 2028
- JPMorgan revised 2026 data center capex growth to +80% (up from +63%) and 2027 to +50% (up from +40%), with memory now ~30% of hyperscaler capex versus 8% in 2023-24
- Goldman lifted its S&P 500 year-end 2026 target to 8,000 from 7,600, with 2026 EPS pegged at $340 for +24% earnings growth, supporting a 21x forward multiple
- Micron Technology joined the trillion-dollar market cap club, and SK Hynix passed $1T as well, with MU returning 859% over the last year per Deutsche Bank's Early Morning Reid

The mechanism is straightforward and dangerous. Capex of this magnitude going into AI infrastructure means that what was a buyback budget two years ago is now a GPU and memory budget, so the same set of names funding shareholder returns is funding the supply chain that NVDA, MU, MRVL, and TSM service. With memory rising from 8% to 30% of hyperscaler capex, MU's parabolic price action is being underwritten by a demand curve revision that did not exist twelve months ago. The bear case Goldman itself flagged is also concrete. Hyperscaler depreciation expense is rising fast, enterprise productivity gains from AI are still early, and a single MAG7 capex guide-down would crack the picks-and-shovels names hardest. Morgan Stanley's invalidation level here is explicit, a significant guide-down in hyperscaler capex or a collapse in median Russell 3000 earnings growth ends the trade. Long $SOXX captures the basket exposure, and the cleanest single-name is $MU on the memory share-shift.

### Hawkish Fed shift while real yields do the tightening

*ING calls it explicitly hawkish, Goldman cautions that the real-yield path is doing the same job the Fed used to do, and rate-sensitive equities sit in the crossfire.*

- ING reads the Fed as moving into a hawkish phase, focused on removing its implicit easing bias, with fed funds futures pricing about 75% probability of a hike by year-end per JPMorgan and 70% per Rabobank
- Goldman Sachs measures the US 10-year real yield (the yield after subtracting expected inflation) at 2.1-2.2%, a level reached only a handful of times in history, and flags it as growth-negative
- 30-year Treasury yields touched 5%, the highest since 2007 per The Market Ear, while the 10Y sits at 4.49% after a 7.3bp drop yesterday per Deutsche Bank
- Goldman points out that breakevens (the inflation rate the bond market is pricing in) are anchored near 2.4%, so the entire 25bp yield move was real yields rising, not inflation re-accelerating
- The S&P 500 has nonetheless put together eight consecutive winning weeks per Goldman's Squeeze Risk note, and Goldman raised its sentiment indicator to only +0.3 (modest), suggesting the rally is not yet sentiment-extended

The mechanism Goldman flags is that real yields rising without inflation rising tightens financial conditions through the discount-rate channel without giving multiples any reflationary offset. That is the textbook environment for long-duration assets (long-dated bonds, long-dated growth equities) to underperform. Goldman's own caveat is that the market is currently reading the move as a growth upgrade, supported by AI investment and a resilient labor market, which is why $SPY is at all-time highs while the 30-year is at a 17-year yield high. The pushback ING flags from the cross-bank corpus is that breakevens above 2.35% (Goldman's upside-break level) would reframe the move as reflation and re-anchor risk assets. Until that happens, the bias is short $TLT and long $UUP into the late-May Core PCE print, and the trade breaks on a clean PCE undershoot that lets the curve bull-steepen.

### Melt-up positioning runs into a squeeze-risk setup

*Eight straight weeks of S&P gains, hedge funds at record semis weight, and macro shorts at a ten-year high, the setup that goes both ways depending on which dam breaks first.*

- The Market Ear measures S&P call skew at the 96th percentile (very strong demand for upside calls) with put skew at the 4th percentile (almost no demand for downside protection), inverted call skew on 25% of S&P 100 names per Goldman, in line with 2021 meme-squeeze levels
- Goldman flags macro short positioning at a ten-year high while CTA long exposure to global equities is $90bn and S&P 500 corporate buyback windows are open on 99% of names, a stack of upward-bias mechanics
- Hedge fund portfolio weight in semiconductors is at a record 10% per Goldman's mid-day color, while SOX bounced 18% from washout lows in a few sessions and SOXL bounced 60% in five sessions per The Market Ear
- SOX monthly RSI sits at 87, the highest since the dot-com bubble, with KOSPI returning 12% over the past four sessions and +98% year-to-date per Deutsche Bank
- The Market Ear's June 17 FOMC forward implied vol sits at 21% versus 14% baseline, the highest premium in the curve, meaning the option market is pricing the FOMC as the binary event that could break the melt-up

The mechanism is two-sided and that is exactly the problem. If macro shorts cover into a benign Core PCE print tomorrow plus a Hormuz MOU, the squeeze is mechanical because positioning is asymmetric and call skew is already inverted. If hedges remain thin into an inflation print that runs hot, the lack of downside protection turns a 2% pullback into a 6% one because there is no buyer of last resort. The Market Ear's bear case names the trigger directly, a meaningful macro surprise or a momentum shift that forces volatility sellers to cover would unwind the leverage stack. Goldman's bull case rests on NVDA's $20B standalone CPU revenue and persistent hedge fund tech buying being enough to absorb any short cover. The cleanest single-asset expression of the two-sided risk is long $VIXY call spreads into the June FOMC, paid for by selling rich $SPY upside calls.

### Consumer signals are not yet in the tape

*Walmart's biggest earnings-day drop in three years and gas prices up 42.7%, against a tape that has not registered the shift.*

- Walmart's earnings-day move was -7%, the biggest in three years per Goldman's Squeeze Risk note, the kind of bellwether single-name reaction that historically front-runs broader consumer weakness
- US gas prices were up 42.7% in early May versus the year-ago period per JPMorgan, with total spending growth at 5.1% year-over-year (barely above April's 5.0%), implying real spending growth is now barely positive
- US airfares are up 20.7% year-over-year through April per Morgan Stanley, sitting alongside the gas number as the inflation pieces the Fed cannot easily dismiss
- $GLD has seen $9.5bn of three-month net outflows per JPMorgan, the largest since summer 2013, while crypto ETFs took $1.4bn of weekly outflows, both signaling defensive positioning is being unwound
- Morgan Stanley frames the disconnect as US economic inelasticity, demand staying resilient because tech and household balance sheets are strong, but the bear-case invalidation it names is a significant guide-down in hyperscaler capex or a collapse in median Russell 3000 earnings

The mechanism the consumer-cracks read implies is rotation, not a tape break. Walmart's print and the gas-price wedge mean the bottom-half-by-income consumer is feeding less of the spend growth that gets aggregated into the headline number, so $XLY-style discretionary leadership becomes harder to sustain even as $XLP defensive names remain underowned. Morgan Stanley's pushback is that AI capex is so large that it shows up in headline GDP and earnings before the consumer drag does, which is why the surface tape looks fine while the bellwether single-names crack. The defense for taking the consumer signal seriously is that gas at +42.7% and airfares at +20.7% are two months stale already and the data should worsen, not improve, in the Core PCE release tomorrow. Long $XLP relative to $XLY captures the rotation, and a clean Core PCE print at 0.3% (consensus) would invalidate by giving the consumer one more month of nominal spending tailwind.
