## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The market is pricing hikes into a hiring freeze

*With no Fed guidance to anchor them, rates markets are trading oil instead of jobs, and long-dated Treasuries are the wrong place to hide this week.*

- July payrolls printed -23k against an 80k consensus and Goldman's own 75k estimate, with prior months revised down. The three-month average collapsed to 20k from 111k, and Goldman puts the underlying hiring pace near 5k. Hiring has effectively stopped.
- The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, but participation dropped to 61.4% (Goldman). The jobless rate improved because people left the workforce, not because they found jobs.
- Wage growth printed 0.05% for the month against a 0.3% consensus (Goldman). No wage-driven inflation pressure in sight.
- The market's one-year inflation bet (the 1-year inflation swap) jumped 11 basis points in a day, the biggest single-day rise in four weeks, and futures still price 34 basis points of hikes by year-end (Deutsche Bank). Weak jobs would normally mean cuts. Oil is overriding the labor data.
- $125 billion of fresh Treasury supply lands this week (Deutsche Bank): $58bn of 3-year notes Tuesday, $42bn of 10-years Wednesday and $25bn of 30-years Thursday, into 30-year yields already at their highest since 2007.

The mechanism is the missing map. Kevin Warsh's Fed has stopped telegraphing its next move, so every inflation print and every auction reprices the curve in real time instead of being absorbed by guidance, and with Treasury bills at 22% of outstanding US debt, the government's own refinancing cost moves with every swing (Bloomberg).

TS Lombard takes the other side of the hike pricing with high conviction, in their words "in the end, there is no rate hike in September," because the labor market is deteriorating under the surface. They can be right about the meeting and still not save bondholders this week, because the near-term pain is not the Fed decision, it is CPI Wednesday and a 30-year auction Thursday hitting a market with no guidance anchor. Bloomberg's rates read frames the one true off-switch: volatility stays elevated until Warsh clearly explains how he reacts to data. Does Wednesday's CPI break the September-hike pricing before Thursday's 30-year auction breaks the long end? Short $TLT says you don't need to answer, both paths keep long-dated bonds under pressure this week.

### The AI revenue showed up, and so did the debt

*The buildout is starting to pay, so own the hyperscaler funding it from cash flow rather than the ones borrowing to keep up.*

This earnings season gave the AI trade its first hard proof of return: hyperscaler AI revenue contribution inflected (Goldman, their highest-conviction call of the window), with S&P 500 earnings up about 25% year over year in the first half excluding private-asset gains (Goldman). The financing is the catch. Headline EPS growth of +46% falls to +28% once private markups are stripped out, SoftBank borrowed $10 billion against its OpenAI stake, Nvidia is backstopping a $250 billion data center project against $215 billion of total annual revenue, and hyperscaler lease commitments have hit $1.5 trillion (Goldman). Credit markets are charging for it: hyperscaler bond spreads (the extra yield investors demand to hold their debt) are 32bps wider this year while the rest of investment grade is flat, and hyperscalers took 40% of this year's 30-year bond deals (Deutsche Bank). Microsoft is the only major hyperscaler generating positive free cash flow, the cash left over after capex. The lean is long $MSFT, the AI return without the borrowing. What flips it is Deutsche Bank's slow-ramp scenario, where AI revenue lags, leverage rises and the credit pressure spreads to the equity.

### The bounce is rented, not owned

*This is a short-covering rally with defensive positioning underneath, so keep the upside and own the hedge.*

Goldman's most-shorted basket rose 13% in four days and the Mag7 posted their largest four-day gain since spring 2025, while hedge funds cut Korean semiconductor allocations to 4.1% from 7.2% at end-June. Retail is hedging, not chasing: Citadel Securities' July data shows $5.4 billion a day of retail options premium, up 65% in a year, with a record 62% of contracts in index and ETF products versus a 43% historical average, record premium allocated to puts, and speculative themes like crypto and quantum down 60-80% year over year. BTIG caught the bounce and is stepping off, with the high-beta momentum rebound at roughly 25% against a +35% median recovery after a first leg down. Their trigger is 10-year real rates (yields after inflation) above 2.50%, versus 2.35% now. If real rates stay below 2.50% through this week's CPI and auctions, the squeeze can run, and if they break above, the next leg lower starts. Owning $QQQ puts while staying invested covers both answers.

### Silver is dragging gold into a forced chase

*Systematic shorts are being squeezed out of precious metals and positioning says the chase has room to run.*

Silver rose 8.3% from July 28 to August 5 with open interest up $2.4 billion while managed-money length sat near two-year lows, and that flip in momentum is forcing CTA short-covering, per Goldman. The gold side has a China bid: 225 tonnes imported year-to-date versus 28 tonnes in the first half of 2025, with Shanghai open interest up 19k lots in a top-5 move on a three-year lookback (Goldman). Yet overall gold positioning sits at just +3 on Goldman's -10 to +10 scale versus +10 in January, so the chase is far from crowded. The counters are real: central bank buying is down 28% year over year, and silver's physical market has not tightened, with lease rates actually softening. The trade is long $SLV through call spreads rolled to higher strikes, and buying $GLD dips toward $4,000-4,100 where central bank purchases have built a floor (Goldman). If gold breaks and holds below $4,000, the structural-bull thesis is dead and the chase unwinds with it.

### The Hormuz toll booth is still open for business

*Oil's risk premium stays underpriced while reopening is a negotiation, not a schedule.*

Iran tied any Strait of Hormuz reopening to US concessions over the weekend and elevated a hardliner to its top security post. The Iran-Oman transit framework bars US and Israeli vessels and adds cargo fees, terms Washington has not accepted (Deutsche Bank). Four desks flag Hormuz as the window's unresolved catalyst with no directional consensus, which is itself information: nobody is positioned for either resolution. Refined-product shortages plus European winter demand argue diesel and fuel prices higher (Rabobank), energy was the only S&P 500 sector to clearly advance on the deal news (Deutsche Bank), and Goldman sketches $100 oil if hostilities re-escalate and energy shipments are curtailed. Cost of being wrong on a real reopening: oil gives back its recent bounce. Cost of holding no energy exposure if the strait stays shut: the $100 scenario. Long $BNO carries that asymmetry.
