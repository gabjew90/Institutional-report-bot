## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

*The AI infrastructure spending boom is the highest-conviction cross-bank story in the corpus, with four banks supportive and zero pushback on the spend cycle itself.*

- $725B 2026 US tech firm capex estimate (The Market Ear) — the investment pool for AI infrastructure
- $200bn Anthropic backlog now accounting for $GOOGL revenue (Goldman, reported via The Information)
- SK Hynix +10% overnight (Goldman) — memory supply chain is bid alongside the hyperscalers
- 35-55% AI themes performance since the start of April (Goldman) — five weeks of compounding
- Goldman raised 2026 regional earnings growth forecast to 60% from 45%
- 17% Q1 S&P 500 earnings growth (UBS) — fastest pace in four years
- Datadog +31% afterhours Wednesday (live news) confirmed the software-AI infrastructure read

Three forces are reinforcing the trade. Hyperscaler capital plans are ratcheting higher with every earnings cycle, the supply chain feeding the build (memory, optical, custom silicon, networking) is bid because compute demand is outrunning supply, and the read-across into software is now landing in print form rather than guidance form. UBS's House View frames it as "the AI theme remains a key driver, with strong results not only among large tech platforms" but throughout the broader stack. Goldman's TMT desk says "every part of the supply chain gets bought," and the Anthropic backlog data point shows enterprise commitments are being booked rather than just promised.

The pushback we would anticipate is the dot-com parallel. BTIG's data shows the top 10 NDX stocks averaged 784% over the past year, exceeding the 1999 average of 559% and the 622% pre-March-2000 peak. The Market Ear floats a 25-30% potential SOX correction back to the 50-day moving average. Goldman's chart of the day flagged an 8% one-day drawdown in High Beta Momentum, a top-ten move in five years and the fifth such instance in 2026, with Prime Book momentum exposure at the 99th percentile over one year and 100th over five. Goldman's own partner asked "what could go wrong" with one-third of MAG7 profits coming from private-equity investment gains rather than AI revenue. That risk is real but the binding constraint is that the earnings keep landing. AMD reported a clean beat-and-raise, SanDisk shares were up roughly 15% post-earnings, and Datadog's +31% afterhours print on Wednesday extended the validation into software. Goldman's own historical work on aggressive momentum unwinds notes they have "served as opportunities to buy the dip" with a typical one-week recovery window. The bias here is long the AI infrastructure stack through $SMH, scaled in on the next momentum air-pocket rather than chased at the highs.

### The bond market is fighting the Fed on cut timing and the bond market is winning

*Three banks sized this theme up and they disagree on what the Fed actually does next, with TS Lombard and The Market Ear skeptical the Fed can ease in any meaningful way.*

- 3.25% Federal funds rate Dec 2026 forecast (UBS) — implies two cuts in second half of 2026
- 4.00% 10Y Treasury yield Dec 2026 forecast (UBS)
- 4.35% current US 10Y yield (UBS), down 8 bps on the day Wednesday
- 5% 30-year Treasury yield level (The Market Ear), described as the critical pain threshold for stocks
- $200B Fed balance sheet addition Powell already made to keep short rates frozen (TS Lombard)
- $660B coupon-to-bill swap requirement to reach the Fed's 20-25% target ratio (TS Lombard)
- 8.5% reserves as a percentage of bank assets (TS Lombard) — approaching scarcity levels
- -3 standard deviation impulse in $TLT (Goldman) — an extreme bond market move

Three things are happening at once. UBS thinks markets have overpriced the risk that central banks tighten further, and that two cuts in 2H26 plus a 10Y yield drift to 4.00% is the right base case. TS Lombard's structural read says the Fed will cut the funds rate as a political gesture but let repo spreads widen, which is policy tightening through plumbing rather than rate paths. The Market Ear's tape read is the bond market is so spooked it is pricing the Fed to stay on hold for a full year. The reserves number from TS Lombard at 8.5% of bank assets is the binding constraint, because below that level the Fed loses control of overnight rates and has to add liquidity regardless of what the funds rate is doing.

The pushback we would anticipate is precisely UBS's framing: energy-driven inflation is a three-month-lag story that fades as Hormuz resolves, the labor market is softening with private payrolls expected at 75k, and the curve is overdue to price in cuts as growth slows. Norges Bank's surprise hike to 4.25% from 4.00% is a foreign-bank counterexample, not a Fed signal. Even granting that pushback, the 30Y at 5% is the level the market has flagged as the trigger. A sustained break above forces the Fed to choose between cutting the funds rate to satisfy the political pressure and holding the long end down, and a balance sheet that has already added $200B says they cannot do both. UBS's own Dec 2026 forecast for the 10Y at 4.00% would be a 35-basis-point rally from current levels, the kind of move long-dated Treasuries deliver when growth slows faster than the cuts are priced. The bias is long $TLT scaled into NFP at 8:30, with conviction reduced if the 30Y breaks 5% sustained.

### The Hormuz peace deal cracked overnight and the supply scar is back in the price

*Two banks divided on whether the diplomatic round was real, and the overnight US strikes on Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas just broke the bull case.*

- $108 to $98 Brent move on the May 6 MoU optimism (SEB) — the rally that just unwound
- -8.1 mb API crude draw versus -2.8 mb consensus (SEB) — biggest in roughly three months
- -11% distillate stocks versus the five-year average (SEB) — physical tightness, not a paper market
- $270bn Iranian reparations demand as part of Iran's 14-point response list (SEB) — the gap to a deal
- 50% Polymarket probability of Strait of Hormuz normalization by end of June (SEB)
- $120 WTI Goldman desk estimate for the price level that would shift the equity narrative
- 5-10 year breakevens at the highest since 2023 (Bloomberg) — inflation expectations agree with the pessimist
- 2.9% 1-year forward headline CPI swap, up from 2.4% expected pre-war (Bloomberg)
- High-$80s December 2026 WTI, up from roughly $70 in early April (Bloomberg)

SEB's argument was that each round of US-Iran diplomacy since March has rallied risk and pushed crude prices on the announcement, then faded as substance failed to materialize. Bloomberg's read is structural even with a peace deal: market-based inflation gauges are pricing a longer oil shock regardless of the diplomatic outcome, with five-to-ten-year breakevens at multi-year highs. The Reuters reports overnight on US strikes against Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas, plus Iranian state media claims of missile fire on US navy vessels, are the substance failing to materialize in real time. UBS's optimistic read on the Strait of Hormuz reopening (which drove the 11% Brent decline into Wednesday) is the read that just got broken.

The pushback we would anticipate is that diplomacy resumes within days, the strikes are tactical messaging rather than escalation, and oil retraces back toward $98 as it did on the original peace headlines. Trump's own message that the war "will be over quickly" is the bull case. Even granting that, the supply scar is the binding constraint. Distillate stocks at minus 11% versus the five-year average and the largest API crude draw in three months mean the physical market is tight regardless of which way the diplomatic headlines run. Goldman's desk has been clear that $120 WTI is where the narrative shifts from "energy is a known problem" to "energy is the problem," and a sustained move toward that level under renewed strike risk reprices the entire equity rally. The bias here is long oil exposure through $BNO, paired with energy sector longs through individual names like $XOM rather than buying $SPY into the open.

The memory and CPU complex was the laggard inside the broader AI trade through April, and earnings are now closing the gap. AMD's beat was clean rather than driven by accounting tailwinds, the post-earnings price action says the buy side believes the data center accelerator story, and SanDisk became the top single-name retail purchase last week with a 4.5-sigma imbalance. SK Hynix gapping plus 10% overnight extends the read into the Korean memory complex, which has been the leading indicator on global memory pricing for the last two cycles. JPMorgan's framing in Retail Radar is that this is a catch-up trade because the relative performance versus broader AI was lagging into earnings.

The pushback we would anticipate is the supply-constraint narrative cutting the other direction. Arista Networks fell 14% after a cautious supply outlook despite beating on the headline numbers, which signals that supply chain bottlenecks could become an earnings drag rather than a tailwind. Goldman's chart of the day shows momentum unwinds at extreme levels, with Prime Book exposure at the 99th percentile, which means a single negative print can trigger sharp re-pricing across the complex. Even granting that, the 145% SOX rolling performance is well shy of the 264% dot-com peak, the AMD market cap milestone is fundamental rather than narrative-driven, and the SOX correction risk Goldman flags is a 25-30% pullback to the 50-day, not a regime change. The cleanest expression for someone with single-name memory exposure is long $MU paired with $SNDK on dips, sized to absorb a near-term momentum unwind.
