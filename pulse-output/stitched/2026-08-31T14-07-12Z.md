# Hawks land, gold cracks

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The research that landed over the weekend all points one direction. A new Fed chair used his first Jackson Hole to tell the market that financial conditions are not tight enough, and 2-year Treasury yields repriced a September hike from a long shot to a coin flip. Gold and bitcoin, which spent August running on a story about the dollar losing value, both gave ground as inflation-adjusted yields rose and the dollar firmed. Nvidia's quarter was strong enough to lift chips and software, but the credit market did not join in, and the widest disagreement in this weekend's research is between equity desks buying the AI buildout and credit desks pricing the debt behind it.

Ulta reported Thursday after the close and beat the consensus this pulse published. Same-store sales grew 3.8% against the 2.4% the Street wanted and the 2.8% Deutsche Bank modelled, with earnings of $6.55 a share against a $6.20 Street estimate and a 12.5% operating margin against 12.1%. Deutsche Bank kept its Buy and cut the target to $671 from $695.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The September hike the market spent August pricing out

*Warsh told the market policy is too loose, and short-dated Treasuries believed him.*

- September hike odds moved from roughly 35% to between 58% and 60% in the two sessions after the speech (JPMorgan, Bloomberg), so the meeting is now a coin flip with a hawkish tilt and every data release before September 16 carries a policy consequence
- The 2-year Treasury yield jumped 11 basis points to 4.34%, a basis point being one hundredth of a percent, while 30-year yields barely moved, which is what a market pricing a hiking cycle rather than an inflation problem looks like
- Second-quarter core PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred price measure, was revised up to a 3.6% annual pace, and July came in at 0.25% for the month and 3.34% over the year (Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan), so the disinflation trend is thinner than it looked a month ago
- Warsh published his own yardstick, the share of consumer spending categories running above 3% inflation, and it sits at 54% against a 77% pandemic peak (Morgan Stanley), which tells every desk exactly which number to track and how far it still has to fall
- Corporate profits rose 41% quarter over quarter at an annual rate in the second quarter, the best in five years (JPMorgan), and an economy printing that gives a hawkish chair no reason to wait

Warsh has removed forward guidance and replaced it with two published thresholds, so policy now gets priced off each data release instead of off a speech. That is why this week's payrolls number and manufacturing survey will move rates more than either has in a year.

Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs both still have the Fed on hold through September. Morgan Stanley reads the speech as positioning rather than guidance and forecasts six-month annualized core PCE at 3.02% in August against 3.46% in July, which would hand the committee its cover, and JPMorgan splits the difference by keeping December as its base case. The trouble with the on-hold case is that it needs August's data to cooperate, and Deutsche Bank has payrolls rebounding to plus 65,000 from minus 23,000 with hourly earnings up 0.4%, which would confirm the full-employment reading Warsh built the speech on. The long $TLT read this pulse carried Thursday, built on stretched trend-follower shorts, went the wrong way inside a session as short-dated yields jumped and 30-year yields did not follow. Watch in this order: (1) Friday's payrolls, where Deutsche Bank's plus 65,000 against a minus 23,000 prior either confirms the hike or kills it, (2) Tuesday's manufacturing survey, where Deutsche Bank's 55.8 against a 55.6 prior would remove the cooling-economy argument for a pause, and (3) the August inflation print on September 11, which JPMorgan says decides the path.

### Credit is charging more for the AI buildout than equity is

*The chips are selling and the debt behind them is getting more expensive, and only one of those two facts is in the share price.* Nvidia's five-year credit default swaps, the cost of insuring its debt against default, closed at 83 basis points, near an all-time high and more than double where they have historically traded, in a week the stock added about $450 billion of market value (Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs). The company also paused the compute partnership program that guaranteed to rent out unsold chip capacity, which is the mechanism holding rental prices up, and disclosed $530.5 billion of commitments and guarantees sitting outside its balance sheet and running past 2032. Morgan Stanley initiated coverage of the credit at Neutral and models $200 billion of all-in exposure by the end of 2028, calling the risk too opaque to step into. Goldman Sachs is on the other side with a Buy and a $300 target, arguing Chinese production usage is real and that the $500 billion financing consortium removes a bottleneck rather than creating one. Bloomberg counted $19 million of quantified realized AI returns across 919 earnings calls at the 60 largest US financial firms, and Goldman's own broad AI basket is 46% off its highs. Does a GPU rental price hold once Nvidia stops standing behind it, or does $SMH reprice before anyone finds out?

### The buyback cannot outrun the deficit

*Washington is buying back long-dated bonds to hold its borrowing costs down and the money is still leaving for gold.* Treasury's buyback operations run at a maximum of $4 billion a month against $5.5 trillion of outstanding bonds (TS Lombard), and Goldman Sachs estimates the enlarged program absorbs about 30% of new long-dated supply while arguing that inflation, not supply, sets where yields settle. Federal debt has grown $32 trillion since 2006 against $19 trillion of nominal output, with deficits near 6% of GDP in a full-employment economy (Apollo), and US debt to GDP is 122% against France's 113% with a gross interest bill above $1 trillion a year (Bloomberg). Gold ETFs took in $4.4 billion last week, the best week since October, and spot bitcoin funds took nearly $2 billion (Goldman Sachs). The long gold read this pulse has carried since July went against it Friday, a 2.9% drop as the speech pushed 10-year inflation-adjusted yields 6 basis points higher and a record $32.6 billion of speculative buying started unwinding. Cost of being wrong: a crowded position with trend-follower trigger levels that sat 5% to 6% above spot before Friday's flush, so the unwind has room. Cost of missing it if right: Goldman's $4,900 target, which its desk says carries upside risk. Carry: none, which is why size matters more than entry here.

### The shortage moved from crude to diesel

*Crude found a way around the war. Refining capacity did not.* Persian Gulf crude exports have recovered to 70% to 80% of pre-war levels while refined product exports are stuck near 40%, and diesel margins are up 225% over the year and jet fuel 234% against just 34% for crude itself. Goldman Sachs estimates global refinery runs are down almost 7 million barrels a day with outages 60% above seasonal norms, and raised its 2027 forecast for US diesel margins over Brent to $63 a barrel from the $27 it published in February, the largest forecast revision anywhere in this weekend's research. US diesel and gasoline inventories are down 9% and 7% over the year, so the shortage now shows up in storage rather than only in price, and Morgan Stanley's commodity desk lifted Brent targets to $90 for this quarter and $100 for the fourth. Academy keeps energy a portfolio overweight with $XLE up 42% this year. Goldman does not expect refinery runs back at seasonal norms until the second half of 2027 and puts the bear case at $40 diesel margins on a 1 million barrel a day demand drop plus a faster Middle East restart, so if runs have not visibly normalized by the end of 2026 the $63 forecast is the base case rather than the bull case.

### The rotation out of the AI trade has already started

*Four desks are now writing the market top into their notes, and the positioning data says the crowd has not moved yet.* JPMorgan went tactically cautious and is telling clients to cut net long exposure and run market-neutral until the September 16 Fed meeting, citing the worst-performing month of the year for the S&P 500 with a live policy decision inside it. Momentum is already rolling over, with JPMorgan's two momentum indices down 21% and 34% from June highs and Goldman's broad AI basket 46% off its highs and one bad session from late-July lows. Breadth is the tell: the S&P excluding AI names fell 0.7% on the session Nvidia added most of its market value (Goldman Sachs), which is what a narrow market topping out looks like from the inside. Health care has outperformed the S&P for three consecutive months and JPMorgan flags it as the defensive that historically works into midterm elections, which makes $XLV the rotation the corpus is actually arguing for. Hedging is the other half, with the VIX below 16 and a third of S&P names carrying three-month implied volatility below the fifth percentile of the past six months, so $VIXY and September index calls are the cheapest protection since 2021. Cost of being wrong: you lag a melt-up by a few points in a seasonally weak month. Cost of missing it if right: you rotate late out of the most crowded factor in the market, into a live Fed meeting, with Goldman's tax-loss-selling basket already trailing by nearly 30% and October ahead.
