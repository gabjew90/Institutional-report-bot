# Crude snaps the rally

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

Energy is running the whole board. Middle East escalation pushed crude through triple digits, and the research reads that less as a commodity story than as the reason long-dated Treasury yields are at multi-year highs and equity multiples are compressing with them. The European Central Bank decision and the US wholesale inflation numbers both landed this morning and the first desk reads are still coming in. Stocks are working on a third straight down session, with the AI complex holding up better than the rest of the market because this week's technology conference commentary stayed strong on orders even as the financing cost of the buildout got worse.

Positioning is the wrinkle. Hedge fund net exposure sits in the bottom few percent of its one-year range, mutual funds are underweight the AI names, and the options market is pricing almost no movement into a week that carries an inflation print and two large software results. That is a market braced for a shock it has not paid to hedge.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The energy shock is now the constraint on everything else

*The Hormuz disruption has stopped being an oil story and become the reason long-dated Treasury yields and equity valuations are both breaking at the same time.*

- Brent closed at $101.69 on Wednesday, breaking $100 for the first time this cycle (JPMorgan), which is the level where energy stops trading as a sector story and starts landing in the core inflation number
- Goldman raised its December Brent target to $85 and flagged a path above $120 if 2027 Gulf output stays 4 million barrels a day below pre-war levels, while the options market's odds of Brent above $100 by March 2027 went from 6% to 25% in a month, so the tail repriced faster than the forecast did
- Société Générale puts 30% odds on a stalemate that carries Brent to $130 by December and 20% on escalation to $150, against a 50% base case of a narrow US-Iran deal near the November midterms, which means the range of outcomes is skewed upward rather than centered
- Refined fuel is the tighter market. Global refinery utilization is at record levels and the profit refiners earn turning crude into diesel and gasoline has hit unprecedented levels relative to crude itself (Société Générale), and that is the channel that reaches gasoline and diesel prices rather than just the futures screen
- The 10-year Treasury yield hit 4.84% and the 30-year 5.31%, the highest since 2007 (Deutsche Bank, Bank of America), while the extra return investors demand for owning stocks instead of bonds has shrunk to 2.1%, the lowest reading since 2002 (JPMorgan), so every further move in yields comes straight out of what equities are worth

The transmission runs from crude to refined fuel to headline inflation to central banks that thought they were finished, and the bond market prices that sequence before the data confirms it. Odds of a September Federal Reserve hike moved to 63% from 61% (Deutsche Bank), China's August producer prices came in at 3.8% year over year against 3.6% expected, and the US Treasury's $6 billion buyback plan landed below what desks wanted, so the one buyer who could have absorbed 30-year supply showed up small.

JPMorgan takes the other side of the oil leg and argues against material further upside from here, pointing to excess supply outside the Middle East, electrification-driven demand weakness, and Iranian exports still running at roughly two-thirds of pre-war levels. Goldman's own base case is closer to that than to the headlines: of the 543 million barrel decline in global visible oil stocks since the war started, only 26 million barrels came out of OECD commercial storage, which is the category that actually sets the Brent price. That inventory point is why Goldman's December target is $85 and not $120, and it does nothing for the refined-product problem, because refinery utilization above 97% is historically where unplanned outages spike and the scarcity signal is sitting in refining margins, not in crude. Société Générale names energy the most attractive hedge in equities for exactly this risk and is long $XLE against it, and Goldman's own hedge is long global natural gas and refined fuel rather than crude, specifically the deferred March to December 2027 European diesel spreads. The read carried since August 31, that a disappointing buyback would break long-dated Treasuries, is the one that landed: the operation came in below expectations and the 30-year hit its highest level since 2007. Friday's 8:30 AM inflation print is what decides whether the fuel pass-through is already inside the core number.

### AI demand is fine, the infrastructure bill is what changed

*AI demand is still running ahead of what suppliers can deliver, and what decides this trade from here is the cost of financing the infrastructure, not the order book.*

Broadcom reaffirmed AI revenue targets of $115 billion for FY27 and $230 billion for FY28 at Goldman's Communacopia conference and said demand is running above that outlook. Cisco booked $9.3 billion of AI orders in FY26 from almost nothing and put agentic workloads at five times the network bandwidth of human ones. Alphabet's cloud deals above $100 million are growing more than twice year over year. Société Générale argues the other half: this buildout is debt-financed and sensitive to long-term rates, with aggregate hyperscaler capital spending approaching 30% of revenue (Goldman), free cash flow negative through 2027, and $266 billion of AI-related bond issuance already done this year against $139 billion in all of 2025 (JPMorgan). Enterprise adoption is the softer leg, with 22.4% of firms reporting AI use, up 0.9 points from July (Goldman), and spending among the top 1% of enterprise customers down 9.7% in a month. The newer leg is physical: Goldman has robot shipment forecasts quadrupling with unit costs falling about 7% a year, which is where the next round of this capital spending goes. Goldman is telling clients to buy the dip in AI hardware, with its AI power basket sitting in the 4th percentile of its one-year positioning range. Oracle and Adobe both report after the close today, and Oracle's backlog is the first hard test of whether the software side of this has earnings behind it.

### The yen is the carry trade's problem, not Japan's

*The Bank of Japan raises rates on September 18, and the yen move that follows is the most likely source of forced selling in US mega-cap stocks.*

Goldman forecasts a move to 1.25% at that meeting and a slow path to 1.75% by July 2027. Bank of America puts the odds of the September hike at 98%. A Bank of Japan official signaled this week that rate increases continue in order to keep underlying inflation from running well above 2% (Deutsche Bank). The yen has already appreciated sharply on strong wage data, and Goldman's delta-one desk describes the resulting unwind as diffuse selling across US mega-caps rather than one visible blow-up. TS Lombard adds the flow leg: Japan's government pension fund has lifted its allocation to Japanese government bonds to 27% from an official 25% weight, which makes the money coming home structural rather than purely rate-driven. The gap between the VIX and dollar-yen volatility has widened sharply, which says the US equity market is not paying to hedge this. Watch in this order: (1) the September 18 decision and whether 1.25% arrives with guidance toward more, (2) dollar-yen volatility against the VIX, which is where the stress shows up first, (3) Treasury Secretary Bessent's warning against betting against the Treasury on Japanese policy, which is the closest thing to an official floor under the yen.

### The rotation out of the lead trade, and what it rotates into

*The market is priced for no landing, no Fed hike, no capital-spending cut and no Democratic sweep all at once, and every one of those is now a live risk.*

Michael Hartnett at Bank of America calls that combination a perfect consensus and argues a Democratic sweep triggers a 10%-plus equity selloff and pops the AI bubble, an outcome Polymarket prices at 50%. His mechanism is the bond market: with the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.81% and the 30-year Treasury yield at 5.31%, he argues AI spenders underperform until the 30-year gets back below 5%. BTIG reads the same signal off the price action and calls this a topping process rather than an event, with the equal-weight S&P 500 excluding AI names posting its worst day in two months at -1.39% and losing its 50-day average, private equity and credit managers rolling over after the July rally, gasoline futures up about 30% since early August and retail diesel near $6 a gallon. TS Lombard argues the 10-year belongs at 5% or higher on fundamentals alone. Cost of being wrong: you carry commodities and gold through a rally that leaves them behind. Cost of missing it: a 10% drawdown in which bonds sell off alongside stocks, because the cushion equities used to carry over bonds is already down to 2.1%. Hartnett's own stated rotation is long commodities and gold, $GLD and $DBC, with short financials and a short dollar as the sweep hedge.

### Index options are priced for a week that isn't coming

*S&P 500 stocks are moving apart from each other at a record rate while every macro input is moving at once, and that combination is what makes index options cheap.*

How closely S&P 500 stocks move together has fallen to 11 on the six-month measure, a level last seen in 2007 and 2018. The options market is pricing a 1% move in the S&P 500 for the week and a 0.91% move in the Nasdaq 100, into an inflation print and a night of large software earnings. One-month expected swings on the average Nasdaq 100 stock have compressed from about 57 to about 40, and the premium traders pay for downside protection over upside is near a 20-year low. Expected swings in semiconductors have halved since July, from 65 to 36 on Goldman's VXSMH measure. Société Générale's base case is that this regime holds, with large single-stock moves and low correlation persisting the way they did in the late 1990s, and average pairwise correlation across S&P 500 sectors at 20% against 66% at the start of 2020. Goldman's derivatives desk is the other side of that trade and carries long volatility as an explicit one-to-three-month call, pricing the VIX October 20/30 call spread at $0.91. Watch in this order: (1) Friday's inflation print against that 1% weekly pricing, (2) dollar-yen volatility against the VIX for the carry-unwind channel, (3) whether correlation lifts off 11 at all, because none of this resolves while stocks keep moving independently.
