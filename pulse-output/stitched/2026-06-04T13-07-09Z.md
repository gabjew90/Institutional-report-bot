Heading into the open, the tape is wrestling with two things at once. ## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hormuz is reopening on the futures curve, not the physical curve

*The headline risk is draining out of oil futures while the physical supply hole still hasn't filled — the gap is the trade.*

- Brent futures down 22% from the late-March peak even though Hormuz remains contested (Goldman)
- Front-month oil futures down 12% since US-Iran progress headlines (Citadel Securities)
- Citadel pegs the probability of normal Hormuz traffic at 22% by July 1, 50% by August 1 — the curve has priced something closer to 80%
- Goldman flags global jet fuel demand 6% below trend; Asian ethylene plant utilization down 14 percentage points since February; India naphtha demand down 0.15mb/d YoY
- BofA puts the 2Q26 oil deficit at 3.4m b/d, returning to balance only in 2027 — that math doesn't match a $66 Brent print

Two stories are pulling oil in opposite directions. The futures market is trading the ceasefire optimism and a softer China demand pulse (Reuters: Iranian oil slips to discount on poor Chinese demand, China cuts retail gasoline prices June 5). The physical market is still short — Saudi-Kuwait-UAE-Qatar-Bahrain-Iraq collectively shut in 10.5M b/d in April per SEB, and global airline chiefs are meeting today to talk about the fuel shock. The bear case is straightforward: a real ceasefire announcement and the physical price catches down to futures, refined-fuel stocks rebuild over summer, and the trade is dead. That case requires a hard de-escalation that has been promised three times this year and has not held — Israel hit Lebanon overnight despite the ceasefire deal, and the Reuters wire is "keeping traders guessing." The bias here is long $BNO outright into the supply-deficit window rather than chasing $USO; the WTI futures curve is where the optimism is concentrated and the Brent-WTI spread should widen if the physical scarcity wins out.

### AI capex is now funding itself, and that's the problem

*The $700B hyperscaler buildout has reached the point where 85% of AI's revenue is the spending itself — that's a recycled-money loop, not a self-sustaining business.*

- TS Lombard: 85% of AI-ecosystem revenues come from recycled capex; the buildout is 2% of US GDP in 2026
- Bloomberg: total AI buildout spend hits $700B this year; Microsoft's 2026 capex includes $25B attributed to higher component pricing, with $80B in unfilled Azure orders
- Broadcom guided FY AI chip revenue to $56B vs the street's $57.6B; 3Q26 specifically to $16B vs $17.2B (Goldman TMT desk) — first real airpocket in the build-out narrative
- Alphabet raised $35B in equity, the largest US equity capital raise in history, plus an $80B Bloomberg-tracked secondary that flips Q4 net equity supply from -$44B to +$40B
- Micron DRAM prices up ~60%, NAND up ~70% in latest quarters (Bloomberg) — the memory squeeze is what's keeping the capex math from collapsing right now

The mechanism is uncomfortable when laid out. Hyperscalers are buying their own demand by paying GPU vendors who pay foundries who pay equipment makers. The picks-and-shovels names (Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron, ASML) keep getting fed; the end-customer revenue line is the part that has to show up to make the loop self-funding. Citi and Goldman's bull case: agentic AI raises token consumption 24x by 2030, which closes the loop in time. The skeptic case from TS Lombard, The Market Ear, and Bloomberg: capex growth at 80% YoY against backlogs of $1.5T means somebody renegotiates a contract first, and Alphabet's record equity raise is the tell that even the most cash-rich names are out of organic firepower. The Goldman 1-Delta desk's own warning that we are "operating in a structurally" overextended setup, with $60bn in single-stock levered ETFs (doubled since April), says the unwind path is binary. The setup leans toward long the picks-and-shovels (Applied Materials, $ASML) over the hyperscalers ($MSFT, $GOOGL) into the next earnings cycle, with hedges via $SPY puts paid for by selling the structurally low vol.

### The bubble call from BofA and the contrarian rotate-out

*When Hartnett puts a "bubble" label on the tape and four other notes hit the wire arguing what to buy if not AI, that is the corpus telling you the lead has gotten too crowded.*

- BofA Bull & Bear Indicator at 8.5, up from 8.0, approaching record (Hartnett — "What To Trade When This Bubble Bursts")
- 4% of S&P 500 stocks at new highs vs 20 stocks at the March 2000 internet bubble top (Hartnett)
- 67% of the S&P 500 rally since end-March came from just 10 companies (Citadel/Scott Rubner cited by Bloomberg)
- 25% of stocks outperformed the MSCI ACWI in May; only 39% of active funds outperformed (BofA Global Research)
- $60bn in levered single-stock ETF AUM, doubled since early April (Goldman 1-Delta)
- BTIG: 9 consecutive up days, 10 up weeks (longest since 1985) on negative breadth — never happened before
- 31 global YTD rate cuts vs 12 hikes (Hartnett) — liquidity backdrop is the prop holding it together

The contrarian read is not "short the index tomorrow." It is that the rotate-out trade has a date. The corpus has four separate notes ("Nobody Wants NVDA," "Sell in May," "Iran Deal would risk AI rotation," "The year the Magnificent Seven stopped being magnificent") all arguing the same thing: the dispersion between the top ten and the rest of the index has gotten too large for the index to keep working as a single instrument. The bull pushback from Goldman's vol desk is real — "skew is broken" because puts are too cheap and the dealer book is in a position that mechanically buys dips, which is exactly what 9 straight up days on bad breadth produces. The bear case wins when an actual catalyst lands (Iran deal, MAG7 guide-down, Powell at Jackson Hole), because the same mechanical positioning that's holding the index up flips on a single forced unwind. The cleanest rotation expression here is long $RSP (equal-weighted) over $SPY into the next NFP print, with $VIXY as the tail hedge while the Goldman Vol Panic Index sits near record lows.

### Payrolls walks in tomorrow with two banks above consensus

*BofA at 95k and Scotiabank at 125k versus an 85k consensus says the labor data is firmer than the cuts crowd is positioned for.*

- BofA Global Research May NFP forecast 95k vs 85k consensus; unemployment held at 4.3%
- Scotiabank May NFP forecast 125k vs 85k consensus
- JOLTS job openings 7.618m, up from a revised 6.877m, with 91% of the rise in professional/business services (Scotiabank)
- TS Lombard: profit upturn historically front-runs the hiring cycle by 1-2 quarters, and the profit cycle just turned
- ISM Services PMI 54.5 (vs 53.8 estimate), prices paid at 71.3, employment 47.9 — services growing with the labor sub-component still soft

The mechanism that matters is the rate-cut path. The market is positioned for two cuts in 2H — if NFP prints 100k+ with unemployment unchanged and prices paid still in the 70s on ISM Services, the September cut becomes a coin flip and the 10Y has more room to back up. Scotiabank itself flags the labor data reliability issue (single-month volatility, professional/business hiring concentration) as a real reason to fade conviction. That's fair, and the 30Y briefly breached 5% yesterday before settling, so some of this is already in the curve. The defense for the firmer-than-consensus call is that JOLTS doesn't lie about openings even when payrolls do, and the openings line has reaccelerated. The bias is short $TLT into the print with $UUP as the secondary expression if the print is hot.

### ECB on June 11 is the hike nobody is hedging

*The market has priced a 25bp ECB cut at 99% for next week; UBS is calling for a 25bp hike instead — the carry trade is set up to break on a single phrase from Lagarde.*

- UBS expects ECB to hike 25bp at the June 11 meeting; sees terminal at 2.5%
- Eurozone CPI accelerated to 3.2% YoY from 3.0% in April (Natixis)
- 10Y Bund 3.00% — move above suggests the market is pricing persistent inflation/energy shock (Mizuho)
- Danske Bank: 2026 Euro-area real GDP growth cut to 0.7% from 1.2% on the Hormuz shock — the ECB has the inflation reason without the growth cover
- ECB rate hike cycle being flagged across Deutsche Bank, Danske, Mizuho, UBS, UniCredit

The setup is asymmetric. Consensus is a cut and UBS is the lone hawk; if Lagarde matches UBS, EURUSD breaks higher and the front end of European rates repositions in a single session. The pushback is that the growth picture is too weak for any central bank to surprise hawkishly — Finland trend unemployment is at the highest level since 1999, Eurozone PMIs are soft, and the carry trade arithmetic works because the macro doesn't justify a hike. The defense is that UBS specifically argues this is about the supply-shock inflation impulse, not demand — the ECB hiked into stagflation before (2008), and the inflation print is too sticky for the cut crowd to be right. The bias is long EURUSD calls into the meeting with $FXE as the cleaner US-tradable expression.
