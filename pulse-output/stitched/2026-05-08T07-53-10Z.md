## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex super-cycle is being confirmed by earnings

*The AI infrastructure build is no longer a forecast. Q1 earnings are confirming the cash going in is sticking, and the pullbacks are still buying opportunities.*

- S&P 500 Q1 earnings on track for 17% growth, the fastest pace in four years (UBS), with 24.6% year-over-year for the SP500 (Deutsche Bank's "stunning quarter")
- Anthropic hit $30B annualized revenue in April, an 80x jump, with a SpaceX/Colossus 1 data center partnership (Goldman); $200B Anthropic backlog reported as accruing to GOOGL via The Information
- Goldman raised regional 2026 earnings growth to 60% (from 45%); Korea EPS revisions are running at 216% year-to-date
- The top 10 Nasdaq 100 stocks are up an average 784% over the past year, exceeding the 622% average that led into the March 2000 peak (BTIG via The Market Ear), with $SNDK up 3960% on its own
- AI themes are up 35-55% since the start of April; momentum exposure on the Goldman Prime Book is at the 99th percentile on a 1-year basis and 100th percentile on a 5-year
- Datadog reported Wednesday after the bell with a 31% afterhours pop, dragging $SNOW and $MDB sympathy bids

The cash flowing through hyperscalers is showing up as actual revenue, not just guides. Datadog's print and the Anthropic ARR figure are second-wave software confirming the first-wave chip spend, which is the part bears keep saying won't materialize. The Goldman desk's framing of compute scarcity (every part of the supply chain gets bought) plus UBS naming AI as the primary driver of the four-year-best earnings pace gives this theme cross-bank backing across the most important sell-side voices.

The bear case here is the dot-com mirror Goldman's own One-Delta partner is waving. The risk is not that AI fails commercially. The risk is that AI succeeds so completely that compute commoditizes, frontier model gains compress, and capacity outstrips demand. The Market Ear's data layer reinforces it: the SOX 14-day RSI above 80 is rare in 30 years of data, and the SOX index is 56% above its 200-day moving average. A 25-30% correction would just bring the index back to its 50-day. Goldman's own 8% one-day drawdown in High Beta Momentum on Wednesday, a top-10 single-day move in five years, is what unwind capitulation looks like in real time.

The defense rests on two specific things. Goldman's own historical work shows aggressive momentum unwinds inside structural bull themes have served as buy-the-dip opportunities, and JPMorgan is still actively financing new AI-related long ideas in its model portfolios. The earnings confirmation from Datadog, the SpaceX/Anthropic compute partnership, and Korea's 216% YTD EPS revision are not the data prints that mark a top. The bias here is long memory ($MU) over CPU-exposed names, with pair-trade exposure via long $SOXX into a defined hedge.

### The bond market is pricing the Fed wrong, and 30-year yields are the line

*Bond traders are pricing the Fed to stay on hold or even hike again, and at least one of the three banks covering this disagrees. The 30-year Treasury at 5% is the level that decides who is right.*

- The 30-year Treasury yield sat at 5% as the threshold both Bank of America's Hartnett and The Market Ear flagged as the "Maginot Line" for stocks, a durable break above which would trigger material equity volatility
- UBS expects two Fed cuts in the second half of 2026 with the federal funds rate at 3.25% by December, against market pricing that has been hovering around no cuts at all
- Citi's rates desk calls the 20-25% probability of a 2026 Fed hike currently embedded in the curve "excessive," with a base case of three 25-basis-point cuts in September, October, and December
- TS Lombard's "Warsh Fed" thesis: the incoming Fed chair will cut the policy rate but tighten financial conditions through the repo market, swapping roughly $660bn of coupons into bills, with inflation projected above 3% in 2027
- 10-year Treasury yield closed Wednesday at 4.35%, down 8 basis points on the peace-deal tape (UBS); the dollar held steady this morning despite renewed US-Iran fighting (Reuters)
- Citi's NFP scenario analysis points to negative 15k jobs against a 65k consensus; JPMorgan's desk has 50k

Three banks make this a real cross-bank theme rather than one-desk noise. The split is whether the bond market's "no cut and maybe hike" pricing is a genuine read of inflation broadening or an overreaction to the energy shock that fades. The 30-year level is the trigger that resolves the question. Goldman's "spot up vol up" framing of equity behavior, where stocks are realizing more volatility on the way up than on the way down, is what happens when the market is pricing two contradictory regimes at once.

The opposite read is structural. TS Lombard's prediction that inflation broadens above 3% in 2027 is corroborated by Bloomberg's commodity-versus-Treasury ratio screaming re-acceleration regardless of the peace deal. Even if Brent settles 10% lower from here, the bond market may not be wrong, just early. UBS's own May headline CPI projection was raised to 0.72% month-over-month with a peak of 4.44% headline, driven by gasoline. Higher CPI plus a Fed seen as politicized via Warsh raises the term premium, not lowers it.

The defense from UBS's side is that the energy shock is already in the market and the curve is over-extrapolating. The Citi rates desk's framing, that a weak NFP print at 8:30 today catalyzes the bull rates scenario, is the cleaner short-term setup. With CPI on May 12 and the FOMC on June 17, the calendar is dense with cut-versus-no-cut catalysts, and 30-year yields are sitting on the level that separates the two regimes. Long $TLT into the NFP print, with the trade thesis cracking on a sustained break above 5% on the 30-year as Hartnett flagged.

### Hormuz reignites overnight, and the supply scar lasts past any deal

*Wednesday's peace memo was the rally fuel. Overnight US strikes and Iranian retaliation are the reminder that the supply hole takes months to close even when the headlines say "deal."*

- US military strikes hit Iran's Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas after Wednesday's close (Reuters/Fox); Iran accused the US of breaching the ceasefire and fired on US navy assets after the US targeted an Iranian oil tanker
- Brent dropped 7.8% intraday Wednesday to $96 before stabilizing around $100 (JPM/ING); Goldman flags $120 WTI as the level that would shift the market narrative
- OPEC April production fell to 20.55m barrels per day, the lowest level since 1990 (ING), with Kuwait and Iran disruptions driving the drawdown
- OECD product stocks are well below their 5-year average heading into Q3 (SEB), and even with a signed deal physical Hormuz transit takes weeks to months to resume due to mine-sweeping and stranded crew rotations
- US commercial crude inventories fell 2.3m barrels last week with exports down 1.7m barrels per day week-over-week (ING); refined product tightness is the structural anchor
- Polymarket betting odds put roughly above-50% probability of normalized Hormuz transit by end-June (SEB), implying the optimism is partial rather than full

Headlines move flat oil price hard, but the physical supply situation is one layer deeper and slower-moving. Goldman remains "skeptical of a quick resolution," Mizuho is fading the optimism on the basis that Iranian concessions are structurally difficult, and SEB explicitly recommends staying long volatility because diplomatic-leak rallies have consistently faded. The Bloomberg framing is the binding constraint: longer-dated inflation breakevens are rising because the oil shock is being priced as a structural rather than a transitory event, and that is true regardless of what the peace memo says.

The bull case for a fast resolution is real and worth naming. Iran is reportedly reviewing a 14-point US memo with a 30-day Hormuz reopening framework, the UAE's exit from OPEC and US dollar swap lines hint at a coordinated supply realignment, and Wednesday's tape said the smart money believes it. If the deal sticks, Brent could revisit the mid-$80s and the inflation impulse fades.

That bull case took its first hit overnight. The strikes on Qeshm and Bandar Abbas plus Iranian missile retaliation are exactly the kind of fade pattern Mizuho and Goldman were warning about. SEB's point that even a signed deal does not reopen the Strait for weeks, plus product stocks already below 5-year averages, is what makes long oil exposure work as a hedge through the negotiating cycle rather than a directional bet on the deal failing. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline, with refined-product tightness the floor that holds the trade together.

### Retail is leading institutions into the AI/memory catch-up trade

*The retail flow signal is one a US options trader can act on directly: the chase is on, and the names being chased are the same memory and AI infrastructure exposures the institutions are starting to crowd into.*

- Retail buying levels are at the 77th percentile of their 1-year history, with single-stock flows at the 81st percentile (JPMorgan Retail Radar)
- Tech hardware saw its second-largest weekly retail inflow on record, with the bid concentrated in $SOXX, $SOXL, $QQQM, $EWY for ETF expressions and $SNDK, $MU, $INTC, $LITE, $ANET for single names
- Speculative call activity is extreme, with $MU, $INTC, and $SNDK each trading more call volume than $SPY itself (The Market Ear)
- Goldman Long-Only flows are rotating into AI infrastructure while taking profits in memory, the flip side of the retail trade going the opposite direction
- Retail options trading share is at fresh highs, particularly in $TSLA, $MU, $NVDA, $GOOGL, $AMD, $META, $AMZN, $SNDK, $INTC, $AAPL
- Dealer gamma is "peak long" across the street (Goldman), which compresses realized volatility in indices but amplifies single-stock moves on idiosyncratic news

Retail is not a contrary indicator anymore in this regime. The single-stock flows are matching exactly where Long-Only money is rotating, which means the chase is the trade rather than the sign of the top. The Goldman desk's recommendation to buy front-end volatility on names where the term structure is too steep is the institutional reflex to the same retail signal.

The pushback Goldman is making is on positioning concentration. With Prime Book momentum exposure at the 100th percentile on a 5-year basis and AI themes up 35-55% since April, this trade is the kind that gets violently unwound on a single capex guide-down from META or GOOGL. Wednesday's 8% drawdown in High Beta Momentum was a preview of how fast the unwind comes when one names cracks.

The defense is sequencing. The picks-and-shovels names on the memory side, $MU and $SNDK in particular, are operating with three-year-plus order books for high-bandwidth memory (Crédit Agricole's Korea note tied to AI chip exports up 148% year-over-year January through April). The order-book visibility is what keeps these names paid even through a momentum unwind. Long $MU calls into the print remains the cleaner expression than chasing the broad index higher.
