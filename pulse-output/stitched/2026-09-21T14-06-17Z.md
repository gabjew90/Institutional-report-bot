# Crude cracks, hikes stay

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP] Oil is giving back its war premium this morning on reports of US-Iran diplomacy and recovering Saudi exports, and Treasury yields are coming down with it. That is the same chain that has run in the other direction for two months, with crude setting yields and yields setting stocks. Last week the world's central banks hiked into the shock rather than looking through it: the Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan all raised rates in the same month, the first time that has happened on record.

Positioning going into this is light, not heavy, which is why a de-escalation headline is worth this much. Investor sentiment surveys sit at their most bearish since May 2025, hedge fund net exposure is in the bottom third of its one-year range, and systematic trend-followers carry a small long. The research window closed before any of this morning's move, so what follows is the argument underneath it, not a reaction to it.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The war premium is leaving crude, the fuel shortage is not

*The part of the oil price that headlines can erase is leaving, and the part that sets inflation is staying, so the hiking cycle survives the ceasefire chatter.*

- Brent at $106 carries a $16 premium to JPMorgan's $90 fair value, which prices in another 4 million barrels a day of supply losses on top of the 10 million already gone. None of those barrels have actually been lost yet, so a diplomacy headline reprices all $16 without changing a single cargo.
- Global inventories have drawn only 555 million barrels since the conflict started, one-third of the 1.6 billion JPMorgan projected back in April. The market cleared the shock by destroying demand, which is running 4.4 million barrels a day below last year, rather than by emptying tanks.
- Diesel is at an all-time high of $6.31 a gallon heading into winter with inventories at all-time lows (JPMorgan), and US refineries are running at 97% of capacity (BofA). There is no spare plant to fix that, so the fuel that actually feeds consumer prices stays tight whatever crude does.
- Record diesel prices are what keeps consumer price inflation printing above 5% (BofA), and Nordea has both the Fed and the ECB hiking twice more with the Iran conflict feeding through diesel, jet fuel and fertilizer.
- Hedge funds sold global energy stocks at the fastest pace in four months last week, yet their energy holdings are still at the 77th percentile of the past year (Goldman prime brokerage). The de-risking has started and is nowhere near finished.

Crude is where the headline risk sits and refined fuel is where the physical shortage sits, and those two things have come apart. A ceasefire rumour instantly reprices barrels the market has only feared losing, but it does not rebuild a drained diesel tank or build a refinery. That is how you get crude at an eleven-day low and central bankers still raising rates in the same week.

JPMorgan is on the other side of this and says so plainly: the market is over-pricing the risk of further supply losses given where inventories actually are, and fair value is $90, not $106. For the first time since the conflict began the bank has no baseline view at all, because $100 oil and 4% inflation were supposed to be the red lines and both were crossed without an endgame. If JPMorgan is right, the premium keeps bleeding, and the central banks that hiked last week have tightened into a shock that was already fading. What that argument does not reach is the refined side. JPMorgan's own note has the system turning fragile below 7.65 billion barrels of global inventory, with the US strategic reserve holding only 30 million barrels of usable cushion and the rest having to come from Japan, South Korea and China. Europe is short jet fuel into the fourth quarter and diesel stocks are at record lows, so the crude premium can bleed out while the fuel that sets the inflation print stays scarce. BofA is short 2-year Treasuries ($SHY) on the view that policy is still not restrictive, and Thursday's Trump-Xi meeting in Washington is the date JPMorgan names as the one thing that would take the $16 out of crude.

The long oil call this pulse carried from September 14 flagged early de-escalation as its own risk, and that is exactly what arrived, so that read is closed and it cost money.

### The AI trade has moved from cash flow to the bond market

*Hyperscalers have stopped paying for the buildout out of pocket and started borrowing for it, which makes the AI trade a credit story now.*

- Goldman lifted its 2027 forecast for hyperscaler gross bond issuance to $420 billion, more than 60% above the $250 billion it expects for 2026.
- AI-related borrowers have already issued $578 billion globally this year, with hyperscalers at 40% of that, and AI names now account for 12% of the US investment-grade bond market by size (Goldman).
- Capital spending growth at AA-rated borrowers ran 65% from a year earlier in the second quarter, the tenth consecutive quarter that capital spending growth has exceeded 35% (Goldman).
- Investors are positioned for it: net allocation to the biggest seven tech names has climbed to 20%, the 95th percentile of the past three years, up from a 14% low in June (Goldman).

Free cash flow no longer covers the spending, so the marginal dollar of AI capacity now comes from bondholders, and that puts the trade at the mercy of the same yields the oil shock has been setting. The counter comes from inside the same houses. BofA's fund manager survey has a record net 33% of investors saying companies are overinvesting in AI, and Goldman's own equity strategists point out that semiconductor gross margins at 70% against a 15-year average of 55% would knock 10% off S&P 500 earnings if they simply reverted. Watch in this order: (1) Goldman's desk has implied volatility on $NVDA at multi-year lows and is replacing stock with outright calls, naming the December 240 strike, which is the cheapest way to hold the exposure if the credit story holds; (2) whether hyperscaler capital spending guidance peaks, which BofA calls the single best hope for broad market upside; (3) semiconductor gross margins on the next reporting round.

### Warsh hiked and told the bond market why

*The Fed's first hike since 2023 came with a higher neutral-rate message attached, and that message is worth more to bond investors than the 25 basis points.*

The Federal Reserve raised rates unanimously by 25 basis points, one-hundredth of a percent each, to 3.75%-4.00%, and the median estimate of the neutral rate rose to 3.25% from 3.06%. Chair Warsh named three reasons long-term rates belong higher: a strong economy, competition for capital from AI borrowing, and commodity prices (Deutsche Bank). The projections have inflation back at 2% only in 2029. Goldman responded by adding an October hike and raising its year-end 10-year Treasury forecast to 4.75% from 4.40%, and BofA wants 75 basis points of tightening between September and December with a 5.00% 10-year by year-end. The split is real: Bank J. Safra Sarasin calls this the start of a cycle with another hike in December and risks tilted to more in 2027, while Natixis reads the identical meeting as a one-off to buy time, one more move in December and then a hold through all of 2027. Money markets have the October meeting at a coin flip, so it settles cleanly: if October passes with no hike, Goldman's second-hike call is dead and the 2-year at 4.744% has to come down. Until then the corpus argument is for a flatter curve, with Treasury buybacks in the 20- to 30-year sector holding longer-dated yields down while the 2-year stays pinned, which is BofA's stated curve trade in $TLT.

### Narrowing breadth: rotation, or the start of a market top

*The index is holding up because four sectors are holding it up, and the average stock has already rolled over underneath it.*

Only 2% of global equity market indexes are trading above both their 50-day and 200-day average prices, down from 66% a week earlier, and BofA's Bull and Bear indicator is stuck at 9.5, a sell reading (BofA). Pictet counts 32% of S&P 500 members sitting at least 20% below their own peaks with the advance-decline line down to 1,462 from its July high, and puts the decisive level at 7,492 on the index. Investors have been rotating out of the semiconductor names that enable AI and into financials since the Nasdaq market top on June 1. None of this is capitulation, and that is the problem: the damage is spread across the average stock while the index is carried by a handful, which is the signature of a market rolling over from the inside rather than one selling off. Watch in this order: (1) Goldman's desk is buying European power and renewables as the value-growth expression of cheaper energy plus data-center demand, and European stocks ($EFA) and US utilities ($XLU) are where that read lands for a US account; (2) the 7,492 level on the S&P 500, which Pictet says separates rotation from an extended correction; (3) the share of index members in their own bear market moving above 40%, which is Pictet's stated line for advanced deterioration.

### Japan hiked and the yen fell anyway

*A central bank raising rates to a 31-year high should support its currency, and the fact that it did not is the trade.*

The Bank of Japan raised rates 25 basis points to 1.25%, the highest since 1995, but did it on a 7-2 vote with both dissents coming from board members appointed by Prime Minister Takaichi. The yen weakened past 157 to the dollar and the Japanese government bond curve steepened, because the market read the split as political ground for stopping rather than continuing. August core inflation came in at 1.7% against 1.8% expected, with the narrower core measure the bank watches steady at 1.9%, which does not force the next move. UBS and Goldman both put that next hike in January 2027. The other half of the story is Treasuries: Japan's holdings of US government debt have fallen more than $80 billion this year, but $60 billion of that is short-dated bills sold to fund a record $90 billion of currency intervention in July, against only $11 billion of actual long-term Treasury sales out of $1.1 trillion held (UniCredit). Does the Bank of Japan hike again before the yen takes out 160, or does the yen force the hike?
