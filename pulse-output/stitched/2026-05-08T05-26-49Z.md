### The bond market is calling the Fed's bluff and three banks see it differently

*Yields stuck high are flashing the warning sign for stocks, and the corpus is split on whether this is a buyable repricing or the start of a real policy mistake.*

- 30Y Treasury yield held the 5% line as the critical pain threshold for stocks (The Market Ear); -3 standard deviation impulse in $TLT
- UBS targets a 3.25% policy rate by December 2026 — the supportive view is markets have exaggerated tightening risk
- TS Lombard models the Fed needing a $660B portfolio swap (coupons into bills) to hit a 20-25% bills target, with reserves now at 8.5% of bank assets and approaching scarcity
- Powell already added $200B to the Fed balance sheet to keep short rates anchored (TS Lombard); repo rate becoming the de facto policy lever is the working hypothesis if Warsh tacks
- Citi's NFP forecast: -15k vs +65k Street consensus, unemployment up to 4.4% from 4.3% — a soft print catalyzes a rates rally
- Bloomberg flags 5y/10y breakevens at the highest levels since 2023; 1y forward headline CPI swap at 2.9% (up from 2.4% pre-war)

The split is real. UBS sees the dollar/rate market as having overstated central bank tightening risk and projects 3.25% by year-end. TS Lombard and The Market Ear take the other side: TS Lombard's working thesis is that Warsh, if confirmed, makes repo the policy rate and tightens that way even while cutting headline Fed funds — the cuts are real but not stimulative. The Market Ear notes traditional rate-vs-equity models are failing, and rates not oil are the real speed limit. Today's NFP at 8:30 AM ET is the immediate fork. Citi sees -15k against +65k consensus, which would be the soft print that cracks the "no cuts" trade and sends $TLT sharply higher.

The case against the dovish read is real: UBS's own April core CPI projection at 0.37% MoM and May headline at 0.72% with peak headline of 4.44% in May is not a soft inflation profile. Bloomberg is flagging 5-year and 10-year breakevens at 2023 highs and a 1-year forward CPI swap at 2.9% — the bond market is saying inflation is not done, even if the Fed wants to cut. Even granting that, today's setup is binary on the labor print. If NFP comes in materially below the 75k estimate or unemployment rises to 4.4%, the rate-cut path gets validated and $TLT moves sharply higher; if it surprises to the upside, the front end gets repriced harder and the 30Y back near 5% becomes the catalyst that unwinds positioning. The trade lean here is a barbell: own short-dated $TLT calls into the 8:30 print rather than holding directional rate exposure overnight.

### The Hormuz situation is back on the wires and the corpus is skeptical it sticks

*A Memorandum of Understanding moved Brent from $108 to $98 on hope alone, and overnight strikes are saying the substance behind the headline is thin.*

- Brent opened May 6 at $108/bbl before the MoU collapse; $98 was the pre-strike base (SEB)
- Reuters/CNBC overnight: US military struck Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas; Iran missile fire on US Navy; UAE countering Iranian air attack
- API crude draw of -8.1mb (vs -2.8mb consensus), the biggest in three months — physical market is tight regardless of the diplomatic theater (SEB)
- Distillate stocks are -11% versus the 5-year average; implied product demand at 20.3 mbd is +2.6% YoY (SEB)
- Bloomberg: oil decline of 10% on peace headlines paired with a 10bps drop in 10Y yields, but 5y/10y breakevens added 40-50bps since the start of the war
- Iran's response list demands $270bn in reparations — SEB rates the path to a signed deal as low

Each round of Hormuz diplomacy has produced a brief risk rally and a crude price pop that subsequently faded (SEB). The pattern is now embedded: an MoU appears, Brent dumps 10%, breakevens stay elevated, and within 48 hours the substance fails to materialize. Overnight is the latest example — within hours of Trump saying the ceasefire still held, US strikes hit Qeshm and Bandar Abbas, Iran fired on the US Navy, and UAE countered an Iranian air attack. The physical market doesn't care about the headline: API draws are still huge, distillate stocks still light, and product demand is still up 2.6% YoY.

The pushback we would anticipate is that the energy minister talking points and the IEA "troubled waters" framing are fading-headline noise — peace eventually arrives, supply normalizes, and Brent grinds to $80-90. SEB itself rates the bull case as a clean MoU pushing Brent to $80-90 and the bear as a Trump pivot to strikes pushing Brent over $120. Even granting the bull case, the physical setup is not consistent with the price decline. The asymmetric setup is long the Brent ETF $BNO into the next Hormuz incident: a fresh strike sends front-month Brent vertical while the back-month barely moves, and SEB itself flagged the binary nature with options skew favoring long vol. A signed and verified agreement with clear terms on the Iranian uranium stockpile would be the only invalidator; until then the supply scar takes months to refill.

The pushback we would anticipate is that gold's failure to break $4800/oz on previous attempts is the binding signal — the squeeze setup needs a catalyst it doesn't yet have, and the relative-value crowd argues equities at 17% earnings growth offer better expected returns. The bias for someone with US risk exposure is to own the call spread rather than direct $GLD; sustained failure to break $4800/oz invalidates the squeeze thesis and the position decays out, which is the right asymmetry given a $5900 institutional target.
