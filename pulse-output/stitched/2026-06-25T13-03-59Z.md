# Oil cracks, rates cave

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The research tape this morning is one story feeding another. Strait of Hormuz shipping reopened, the oil risk premium drained out, crude fell back to pre-conflict levels, and that single move pulled the rug from under the hawkish-Fed trade that dominated earlier in the week. Treasuries ripped higher, the bond market's inflation pricing collapsed, and bets on Fed rate hikes that stood at two full moves on Monday have been more than halved. On top of that, Micron's blowout quarter reignited the AI-infrastructure call just as Goldman's prime desk flags the heaviest tech selling in months and hedge funds quietly rotate out of the crowd. The dollar is breaking out anyway, pressing gold below a level it had held since November and pinning bitcoin near its lows for the year. The whole set-up funnels into this morning's core PCE inflation print, the one number that tells you which of these moves was right.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Oil cracked the Fed, and the front-end has more to give

*Crude's collapse just did the Fed's tightening work in reverse, and the market is still priced for hikes the data no longer needs.*

- Brent fell below $75 for the first time since the Iran war began, back near the $72 it traded before the conflict, as Hormuz shipping reopened (Deutsche Bank, Rabobank). The oil-driven inflation scare that justified emergency hikes is gone, so the rate-hike premium has nothing left to stand on.
- The 10-year Treasury yield dropped about 10bps to 4.39% and the 2-year fell roughly 6bps to 4.21%, the biggest one-day 10-year move since October (Rabobank, Deutsche Bank). Cash is piling into bonds for the price gain as yields fall, what the desks call a duration grab.
- Fed-hike pricing collapsed from two full hikes earlier in the week to about 40bps at the peak and roughly 27bps by October (Rabobank). The hawkish trade unwound in 48 hours.
- The 1-year inflation swap (the inflation rate the bond market prices for the next year) fell to 2.16%, the lowest since October 2024 (Deutsche Bank). The market is betting the energy scare is over for good.

One shock did all of it at once. Pulling the supply premium out of oil takes the pressure off headline inflation directly, and a Fed that no longer faces a $100-oil tail has no reason to keep hike risk in the curve. ING argues the front end (2-year Treasuries) has the most room left to fall because growth is softening underneath the inflation noise, and a benign core PCE reading near 0.2% would be the trigger.

The other side is loud and named. Bank of America points out the Fed just marked its own year-end inflation forecast up to 3.3% from 2.7%, and that core inflation will not finish the year below 3% unless monthly prints average 0.20%, which makes today's hike-pricing fair rather than excessive. Crédit Agricole goes further, looking for May headline PCE to accelerate to 4.1% from 3.8% and core to tick up to 3.4%, with new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh standing up an inflation task force after five years of overshoot. Both camps agree the answer lands this morning. If core PCE prints at or below 0.2% month-over-month, the front-end repricing has another leg and long-dated Treasuries via $TLT keep their bid. If it comes in at 0.3% or hotter and confirms the 4.1% headline, the hike trade snaps back and the rally is done. The bias into the number is long $TLT, sized small because the print is binary.

### Micron blew the doors off and made the AI buildout call louder

*Micron just turned memory from a boom-bust commodity into contracted AI infrastructure, and three banks raised year-end targets behind it.*

Micron ($MU) reported $41.46bn in quarterly revenue against $36bn expected, up 346% from a year ago, with gross margin of 84.9% versus 39% a year earlier, and guided next quarter to $50bn against $43bn (Goldman Sachs). The number that matters is $100bn in minimum contracted revenue across 16 strategic customer agreements, backed by $22bn in customer deposits, which locks demand in rather than leaving it to the spot cycle. Shares were indicated up about 16%. JPMorgan lifted its S&P 500 year-end target to 7,800 and pegs AI power-infrastructure growth at an 82% three-year rate through 2028, Barclays moved to 7,800 as well, and Citi sees the optical-interconnect market reaching $92bn by 2028. The pushback is Goldman's own desk: chasing the beneficiaries at peak earnings is dangerous, and the real tell is that the spenders (Google, Microsoft, Amazon) traded heavy while OpenAI and Broadcom showed off a custom chip to cut out 75%-plus supplier margins. The lean is long $MU on the contracted-revenue visibility, with the spenders' price action as the early-warning signal that the cycle is turning.

### The AI trade is the most crowded long in the market

*The buildout is real and the positioning is washed out at the same time, which is exactly why the tape feels so violent.*

Goldman's prime desk saw the largest net selling in global tech in about five months (-2.6 standard deviations) and calls AI the most crowded long in the market, with hedge funds rotating to record-high exposure in Asia and record-low exposure in the US. The Mag 7 is already down 11.6% from its May 28 peak, in correction territory. UBS sees conviction eroding and funds trimming AI to rotate into US cyclicals like industrials and financials. The counter comes from the same building: Goldman's flow desk notes gross and net Mag 7 exposure has fallen to one-year lows, which it reads as interesting entry points on the long side, and JPMorgan says shorts are getting pressed in the AI-vulnerable names. Does the crowd capitulate before the spenders guide down, or does washed-out positioning set the next bounce? Light Mag 7 exposure argues most of the pain is behind, so the trade is rotating from leveraged semis toward financials ($XLF) rather than shorting the AI complex outright.

### The dollar is breaking out into the rate-cut bets

*The textbook says a dovish repricing should sink the dollar, yet it is the strongest major currency in the world right now.*

The dollar index hit its highest level since May 2025 even as Fed-hike bets collapsed, with EUR/USD breaking support at 1.14 and USD/JPY pushing toward 162, overbought enough (14-day RSI near 72) that US and Japanese officials are signaling possible intervention (Rabobank). The de-dollarization story is overstated, with the dollar's share of global lending, debt, and payments stable, and gold's jump to 29% of central-bank reserves from 11% mostly the higher gold price rather than dollar selling (JPMorgan). A strong dollar is the headwind under everything priced against it. Bitcoin ($BTC) was pushed to 2024 lows on the move, and gold broke below $4,000 for the first time since November, with Goldman cutting its year-end gold target to $4,900 and Deutsche Bank flagging $3,800 downside. Does a weekly close above 102 trigger the bigger squeeze, or does intervention near 162 cap it? Until 102 breaks cleanly, the strong dollar keeps a lid on any crypto and gold bounce, so the bias stays long the dollar via $UUP.
