# Scare fades, Fed looms

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The dominant move heading into the week is the energy-supply scare leaking back out of the market on the US-Iran framework to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and the cross-bank positioning picture says the fast money was already leaning that way. Hedge fund net leverage has dropped back to the 8th percentile of its 12-month range, the low end of the five-year average, with one-week positioning indicators flipping from better than +2 standard deviations to worse than -2, a violent swing from recent euphoria to neutral. At the same time money is still pouring into the crowded names, with a record weekly tech-fund inflow and over $31bn into global equity funds. The setup into a heavy central-bank week, Warsh's first FOMC and a likely Bank of Japan hike, is a market that has de-risked at the desk level while retail keeps buying the same trade.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil scare is coming out of the price

*The energy-supply premium is unwinding, and the physical data says it should keep bleeding lower.*

- June flows through the Strait of Hormuz recovered to 5.1 million barrels a day, up from 2.2-3.3mbd across March-May (JPMorgan) — the disruption is already reversing, so the supply premium has lost its anchor
- Goldman cut its 2027 average Brent call to $80 from $85 and sees $90 in the fourth quarter of 2026 — the house view is structurally lower oil even before the deal
- Oil's price reaction to each new escalation headline is shrinking — the market has stopped paying up for the same threat
- Goldman expects Hormuz flows to normalize to about 70% capacity by late August
- Morgan Stanley flipped to a tactically bullish risk stance and sees front-month Brent below $85

The supply hole the market spent weeks pricing turned out smaller than feared, and with barrels physically moving again the premium has nothing to hold it up. The opposite read comes from Rabobank, which warns the framework is a fragile arrangement that could disappoint, and refined-fuel inventories stay tight enough that a single breakdown headline snaps the thread. Physical flows recovering ahead of any paper resolution is the tell that the trade has momentum regardless of the next wobble. The bias is short $USO and an energy-sector underweight into the de-escalation.

### The AI build is still being underestimated, but the bill is showing

*Hyperscaler spending keeps getting revised higher, and the bottlenecks are where the real demand lives.*

- 2026 hyperscaler capex is tracking about $750B, up 84% from 2025 and $200B above where estimates started the year (Goldman) — the second upward revision in a month
- Consensus 2027 capex sits near $920bn and Goldman thinks even that is conservative, sketching $1.1-1.4T
- Morgan Stanley models NVIDIA compute-sold compounding around 210% a year through 2028 and DRAM wafer capacity expanding 30% by 2027 — memory is the structural bottleneck
- A record $12.3B flowed into tech funds in a single week, with $18.8bn into the sector — the money is still chasing
- TS Lombard's pushback: 85% of AI revenue is capex recycling, the same handful of companies paying each other

The spend is real and the constraints, power, memory, labor, are where it bottlenecks, which is why the picks-and-shovels names keep getting paid. The sharpest counter comes from Goldman itself, which flags that the capex lifting semiconductor earnings today will weigh on hyperscaler return on equity later, and notes the corpus is heavily one house's view. The bottlenecks resolve that tension because the demand for power and memory is real money regardless of whose income statement it lands on. The lean is long memory ($MU, Morgan Stanley Overweight) and the power read-through, the independent power producers ($VST, $CEG) and utilities ($XLU) that the build cannot happen without.

### The Fed is done cutting, and Warsh is the wildcard

*The rate-cut path is gone, and the open question is whether Warsh's first meeting opens the door the other way.*

- Goldman no longer expects any 2026 cuts and pushed its final two cuts all the way to June and December 2027
- ING expects a hold this week with no hike, while Bank of America argues that by not hiking into this data the Fed is effectively easing
- May core PCE is tracking 0.31% against a prior 0.28% expectation — still sticky
- The consumer side is cooling fast: University of Michigan one-year inflation expectations fell to 4.6% (down 0.2pp) and the 5-10 year measure dropped to 3.4%, well below forecasts
- MUFG flags the real swing factor as whether Warsh signals the Fed is actively considering raising rates

The hawkish repricing across desks is running straight into softening household inflation expectations, which is the tension that makes this meeting live rather than a formality. A hold that reads hawkish bids the dollar and pressures long bonds, while any dovish surprise from a new chair the market does not know yet snaps the other way. Does Warsh open the door to a hike before core PCE rolls over? Long $TLT is the bet that he doesn't and the cuts come back, long $UUP is the bet the hawkish flip has further to run.

### Smart money already left the trade retail is buying

*Desk positioning has flipped to neutral while flows stay max-long the same names, the late-cycle setup that rewards rotation.*

- Hedge fund net leverage is back at the 8th percentile on a 12-month basis, down 4% across all strategies (JPMorgan, The Market Ear) — the fast money already de-grossed
- One-week positioning swung from better than +2 standard deviations to worse than -2 — euphoria to neutral in days
- The momentum factor took a roughly 16% intraday drawdown and touched its 200-day average (JPMorgan) — the factor that carried the tape is cracking
- Yet $31bn flowed into global equity funds and a record amount into tech — passive and retail are still pouring in
- Multiple desks are flagging late-cycle positioning and "nobody wants" contrarian titles

Desks de-grossing while retail buys the same crowded names is the textbook late-cycle handoff, and the momentum break is the first crack. The honest counter is that breadth is genuinely improving, with 56% of MSCI ACWI members above their 200-day line and room for a breadth thrust (JPMorgan). That breadth thrust only happens if the laggards take leadership, which is the rotation itself, not a reason to stay in the leaders. If the momentum factor doesn't reclaim its 200-day average by month-end, the unwind is real, and until then the trade is rotating into equal-weight $RSP over $SPY, paired with $XLU as the defensive complement.

### A BoJ hike decides whether Japan broadens

*The Bank of Japan meeting this week is the catalyst that says whether Japan's rally spreads past the chip names.*

- A 25bp hike to 1.00% is widely expected this week (ING, Mizuho), with Goldman seeing a 1.5% terminal rate
- JPMorgan pegs the Ministry of Finance intervention trigger near 161.95 to the dollar — the yen is the pressure valve
- JPMorgan is the lone directional voice, arguing the equity gains are broadening beyond the AI-chip leaders
- The communique's QT taper plans are the second signal desks are watching

A hike plus a firmer yen reshuffles who leads, and the corpus is split between treating Japan as a pure rates-and-currency story and JPMorgan's call that the rally is widening into domestics and financials. The risk is that a hawkish BoJ and a stronger yen hit the exporters that led the move in the first place. The offset is that broadening into domestic names is exactly what a normalization cycle should produce. Does the yen break 162 and force the Ministry's hand before the hike even lands? For US exposure, long $DXJ leans yes that the hedge matters, while $EWJ leans no.
