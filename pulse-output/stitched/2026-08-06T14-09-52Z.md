# Record highs, credit whispers

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The S&P 500 pushed to a record close on two engines, earnings beats that keep landing well above the historical norm and reports of a provisional Iran-Oman arrangement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which knocked roughly 5% off Brent on Tuesday before prices stabilized. Under the record print, the tape is less tidy. Chip stocks wobbled after AMD's guidance disappointed elevated expectations, memory names SanDisk and Western Digital guided below the most bullish hopes, and a cautious tech mood spread from Asia overnight. Yesterday's data leaned the same two ways at once: ISM services held at 54.1 with strong new orders, but its employment gauge sank to 47.4 while prices paid jumped to 70.3, and ADP private payrolls printed 44k, the weakest since January. Friday's jobs report now carries the week.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI melt-up is a squeeze, and its own confirmation signals have not signed off

*This rally's fuel is trapped positioning rather than new AI news, so the move is to buy the parts of the trade that have not squeezed instead of chasing the parts that have.*

- Goldman's prime brokerage desk recorded the largest liquidation of global technology stocks on record between July 24 and July 29, and TMT-focused hedge funds lost 10.2% in July, the worst month in their history (JPMorgan). The natural institutional buyers were forced out at the lows, so this bounce is running on thin ownership.
- Monday's session delivered a 1.79% S&P 500 move against an options-implied 0.4% (Goldman), on a record day for total S&P call volume (JPMorgan). Dealers who sold those calls are short gamma, meaning they must buy as the market rises, which mechanically amplifies every up-move.
- Credit default swaps on the hyperscalers (the cost of insuring the big cloud companies' debt, which rises when investors get nervous) are widening again, the same signal that showed up before July's tech selloff.
- The Korea ETF $EWY sits 22% below its all-time high and Taiwan's $EWT 9% below (JPMorgan) while US semis rally. The stocks that led the AI boom are not confirming this rebound.
- The demand side is not the problem: the Mag7's AI backlog is near $1.7 trillion and growing more than 150% year over year against roughly 80% capex growth (JPMorgan), and Goldman just raised its 2027 AI circuit-board market forecast by 38% to $38 billion.

The buyers changed, not the story. Forced sellers exited in July, retail flowed back through memory names and semiconductor ETFs, and short-gamma dealers buy strength, so price is currently set by flows while the deepest-pocketed holders have structurally less capacity to return.

The bull rebuttal is straightforward: JPMorgan is tactically long here, arguing the Mag7 trades more than 2 standard deviations below its average forward price-to-earnings multiple while second-quarter profit margins hit 16.7% versus 12.9% a year ago, and cheap plus accelerating usually wins. Cheap and accelerating still needs holders with capacity, though, and the same JPMorgan flow work concludes hedge fund capacity for tech is structurally reduced, leaving the tape leaning on retail, the least stable ownership base in a drawdown. The positioning read is long $QQQ on dips only, not on chases, until either credit spreads or Korea confirm.

**The Korea sub-thread.** JPMorgan reads $EWY and $EWT as catch-up trades with room to run. The skeptical read is that SK Hynix and Samsung keep failing at their 21-day moving averages while Korean implied volatility near 80 prices roughly 5% daily swings, so the laggards may be a warning rather than a discount. Does the KOSPI break its downtrend before hyperscaler credit spreads break wider? Long $EWY is the bet that it does, sized for the 5%-a-day tape.

### The Hormuz deal is a toll booth, not a peace treaty

*Oil's floor holds because this arrangement defers the hard questions instead of answering them.*

Iran and Oman reached a provisional deal on Strait of Hormuz shipping: a 60-day arrangement under which Iran suspends its toll ambitions for 2 to 4 months and vessels transit under a notification regime (TS Lombard), which in practice leaves Iran controlling the strait while looking cooperative. The sticking points that killed prior attempts are all still live. Iran reportedly wants a 7% transit fee with exemptions for Russia and China against a US proposal of 5% (Rabobank), a Houthi attack on a Saudi tanker has already cut Gulf shipping traffic, and Tehran spent the morning threatening Gulf states over any new US strikes. Brent found a floor around $80 and the bond market's oil-driven rally stalled there (Mizuho). The trade is long $BNO at the floor. A signed deal that genuinely resolves the toll dispute is what ends it.

### September's rate cut is the rally's weakest leg

*The market is holding a coin-flip cut in one hand and hot services inflation in the other, and one of them drops.*

Pricing for a September Fed cut slipped to 54% from 58% (Deutsche Bank) while ISM services prices paid jumped to 70.3 against 65.0 expected and Kansas City Fed's Schmid explicitly backed a hike to finish the inflation job. ADP's 44k was soft, but JPMorgan and Goldman both kept their official payroll forecasts at 75k because ADP misses the government's first print by 64k on average (JPMorgan). The sharper claim comes from TS Lombard: Fed Chair Warsh holds a 7-5 committee majority, US fundamentals will eventually force tightening rather than easing, and the 10-year yield reaches 6% in 2027. With the 10-year term premium (the extra yield investors demand for holding long-term bonds) at 0.8% versus a 1.2% long-run average (JPMorgan), long bonds are priced for the friendly outcome. Cost of being wrong: a September cut that is already half priced, worth a modest bounce in $TLT. Cost of missing it if the hawks are right: a multi-year repricing toward that 6% call. The lean is short $TLT.

### Earnings breadth is the real bull case

*The quarter is beating by enough, in enough places, to fund a rally that does not depend on seven stocks.*

About 80% of S&P 500 companies have beaten estimates versus a 73% historical norm, the median beat is 5.8% against a 3.5% average, and second-quarter earnings growth is tracking toward 30% on an underlying basis versus the 28% expected going in (UBS). July's equal-weighted S&P 500 gained 0.9% while the cap-weighted index slipped 0.1% (UBS), which is what broadening looks like in price. The breadth runs from Eli Lilly ($23.0 billion in revenue versus $20.6 billion expected, full-year guidance raised by $2.5 billion at the midpoint) to Palantir (Deutsche Bank upgraded to Buy, $200 target). The cleanest single-name expression is Shopify: 32% GMV growth against the Street's 28%, third-quarter revenue guided to low-30s growth versus the Street's 27%, and Deutsche Bank reiterating Buy with a $175 target on a reacceleration narrative it expects to drive estimate revisions and multiple expansion. If the third-quarter print lands below that low-30s guide, the reacceleration thesis is dead. Until then the trade is long $SHOP.

### Europe's upgrade has a doubter in the room

*The earnings recovery is real enough that the region's biggest skeptic just raised its own European growth forecast.*

UBS upgraded European and Eurozone equities to Attractive, favoring banks, consumer discretionary, health care, and industrials, with European earnings growth forecast at 25% across 2026 and 2027. JPMorgan raised its Europe third-quarter GDP forecast to 1.25% annualized, well above the ECB's 0.5%, and lifted Spain to 3%, yet still prefers US and Asian equities over the region, so the upgrade is contested at the top. Watch in this order: (1) European bank earnings follow-through, where the lean is long $HSBC, Goldman calls the selloff on China's reported offshore-insurance tax an overreaction since insurance is roughly 4 to 5% of earnings, and UBS flags a 19.2% return on tangible equity, (2) the ECB's September decision, with a hike 84% priced (Deutsche Bank), delivered without cracking the activity data would validate the upgrade, (3) the periphery-core gap, Spain's services PMI at 58.3 and Italy's at 52.5 against sub-50 prints in France and Germany, which tells you how broad the recovery actually is.
