# Hormuz unravels

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The peace-deal optimism that's been sitting underneath this 8-week S&P rally is fraying. Overnight saw the US and Iran trade fresh airstrikes, the Strait of Hormuz draft framework Trump publicly walked back, and oil bid back up after rolling lower into Tuesday. Underneath it all, AI capex revisions keep marching higher across the desks even as Goldman's flow guru warns that hedge-fund leverage is at a 3-year extreme and the upside-only positioning has no room to disappoint. Cooler core PCE in this morning's print took the inflation-scare edge off briefly, but the bond market's reflex from here is being driven more by oil than by the data.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI capex super-cycle keeps getting bigger

*Hyperscaler capital plans are still being revised up, not down, and the desks treating this as already-priced are the ones getting it wrong.*

- Morgan Stanley raised 2026 US hyperscaler capex to $805bn from $433bn a year ago, and pencils $1.3 trillion by 2028 — the chunkiest revision of any forecast line on the buy side this cycle
- Morgan Stanley also lifted 2026 S&P 500 earnings growth to +23% from +17%, and 2026 business fixed investment to +7% from +3%, citing AI demand that is "insensitive to higher financing costs and energy prices"
- Goldman calls the AI capex theme "monumental" and "consistently underestimated in magnitude," with the GS AI basket +38% year-to-date versus +4% for the S&P 500 ex-AI cohort
- Barclays flags ByteDance 2026 AI capex of $70bn — the China leg of the story — and Vertiv liquid cooling as mission-critical infrastructure plumbing the build-out depends on
- JPMorgan's EM Strategy team stays constructive on AI hardware momentum and raised Korea-led MXEF targets to 2000/2200/1500 base/bull/bear, with 2026 EM EPS revisions up 55% year-over-year on Korean IT growth of 203%

The mechanism is the same story Morgan Stanley calls "inelastic demand." Companies are treating AI infrastructure spend as a strategic necessity rather than a discretionary capex line, so the usual transmission channel where higher financing costs and energy prices crimp investment isn't operating. The picks-and-shovels read goes through power, cooling, and memory — Vertiv on the cooling side, and the cloud security guides ($CRWD, NET) flagged by Barclays as raising capex on memory cost pressure.

The bear case is real and Goldman's own desk surfaces it: token cost compression. DeepSeek cut token pricing 75%, Xiaomi MiMo by 99%, and an 8B-parameter Qwen model now runs locally on a four-year-old MacBook on a 5GB download. If inference economics collapse fast enough, the demand-expansion logic for the hyperscaler build-out cracks. Bloomberg's separate read picks up the same risk from a positioning angle, calling the AI-momentum trade "the best run on record" with the standard warning sign that record crowding precedes regime change. The Goldman counter is that strategic necessity dwarfs unit economics on a 3-year horizon, and that the breadth of the build-out (Snowflake's $6bn Amazon deal driving the stock +37% post-earnings, Marvell flagging a $10bn custom silicon opportunity by 2028) tells you this isn't one hyperscaler's story to break. The bias here is long the picks-and-shovels names over the application-layer hyperscalers — exposure through $VRT, $AMAT and memory ($MU) reads cleaner than chasing META's cloud entry or the mega-cap rotation.

### The Hormuz peace deal is fraying and oil isn't priced for it

*Three months of "the deal is close" has the oil curve priced for resolution, and a single weekend of strikes can repricethat back hard.*

- Goldman flat-out says "oil markets are underpricing the risk of a prolonged disruption in the Strait of Hormuz" — the markets baked in an imminent MOU that hasn't materialized
- Goldman points to prediction markets pricing 61% odds of Hormuz traffic normalization by end of July — the consensus view the spot market is following, and the level at which a re-escalation forces a violent re-pricing
- Rabobank notes the leaked draft MOU has a 60-day ceasefire, Hormuz reopening, and an oil-for-oil arrangement — but pointedly excludes Iran's missile program and regional proxies, which is exactly the gap that just blew up overnight as US strikes hit a military site in Iran and IRGC retaliated against a US airbase
- JPMorgan's morning desk reads sentiment as "stagflationary following fresh US strikes" — the cross-asset signal is dollar bid, gold lower into PCE (despite the strikes), and equities faltering as Gulf hostilities heat up
- Deutsche Bank surfaces the structural mechanism: near-term US bond yields are being driven primarily by oil prices, with the correlation tighter than usual since the war started

Three months of "MOU coming" has compressed implied vol in oil markets and let prediction markets drift to 61% normalization odds. The reality overnight is messier — Trump publicly dismissed the leaked Iranian-drafted framework, US forces hit a site near Bandar Abbas, and Kuwait reported intercepting hostile missile and drone attacks. Refined-fuel inventories were already tight before the strikes; Rabobank's note explicitly flags that oil below $100 amid this escalation looks like markets "clutching at straws."

The bull case for the existing oil-lower trade is that prior cycles of escalation have de-escalated within days, and Iran has a structural incentive to keep the leverage of Hormuz access rather than blow it up entirely. The opposite read — and the one that catches the desk flat-footed — is that the leaked deal explicitly excluded Iran's missile program, the one item this regime can't trade away, so the framework was never going to land. The cleanest expression for an options trader sitting in front of a short-vol oil position is long $BNO (or $USO) calls into the next Hormuz headline — Goldman's flagged 61% normalization probability is the level that breaks if a sustained closure materializes, which would re-rate $XLE and energy names in a single session. Long Brent over short $UUP is the second-derivative pair if the dollar's risk-off bid fades as the trade leaks into US-fronted oil supply.

### Hedge-fund leverage is at a 3-year extreme and dealers are losing the cushion

*The same desks calling the rally are now warning that positioning has eaten through the room for any downside surprise.*

- Goldman's flows guru flags hedge fund gross leverage +5.5 points week-on-week, the largest one-week increase in over three years, with Info Tech gross/net exposures at 5-year highs and semis at a record 10% of hedge fund portfolio weight
- The Market Ear shows S&P call skew at the 96th percentile (extreme upside demand) and put skew at the 4th percentile (minimal hedging), with hedge funds and mutual funds both rotating aggressively out of Software and into Semis
- Bloomberg notes speculators are net short across $SPY, $QQQ, $IWM, $DJI and only marginally long the S&P 400 mid-cap — exactly the asymmetric "futures shorts vs cash longs" setup that fuels squeeze-mechanics on dips
- Goldman's NDX showed one of the highest short-cycle risk-adjusted returns in the past 40 years; eight consecutive weeks of S&P 500 gains has compressed the US Vol Panic Index to 2.6 from a 9.5-10 reading in March and April
- The Market Ear separately flags the technical breakdown in $NVDA testing the lower end of its trend channel and below the 21-day, with SOX index reversing from the upper end of its trend channel — the AI-momentum unwind canary, if there is one

The mechanism is the dealer-gamma cushion losing thickness. Eight weeks of grind-higher with extreme call demand has dealers long upside gamma, which means they sell strength and buy weakness — that stabilizes the tape on the way up. But when dealer positioning flips (which the call-skew compression and put-skew collapse are setting up), the same dealers turn into a destabilizer who buys strength and sells weakness. Goldman's flow guru calls this a "vulnerable bullish weight buckle" where profit-taking forces call buyers to unwind and short-vol sellers to cover into a one-way tape.

The optimistic read is that month-end mechanical flows and retail relentless buying keep the bid intact in the near term, with $303bn of MSCI rebalance trade ($11bn NVDA sells against $3.1bn AAPL and $2.9bn MSFT buys) clearing today and tomorrow. The pushback comes from the same Goldman desk's risk warning: positioning risk hasn't dropped even as macro fear has collapsed, and JPMorgan's US Tactical Positioning Monitor 1-week change has already turned -1.5z. The combination of overnight Iran strikes plus a 3-year-high in hedge-fund gross leverage plus collapsed downside hedging is the exact setup where a single bad print or geopolitical re-pricing creates an air pocket. The bias for someone with US equity exposure is long $VIXY calls or long-dated $SPY puts as a cheap hedge — the Market Ear's note explicitly flags that "volatility terms for hedging are currently at very cheap levels, providing an attractive entry for tail-risk protection."
