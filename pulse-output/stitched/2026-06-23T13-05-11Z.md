# Gravity finds the casino

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The story the research keeps circling is a single trade carrying the whole market: AI, expressed through memory and semiconductors, sitting on the most leverage and the most crowded positioning desks have flagged in years. Overnight that fragility showed its teeth. Korea's leveraged memory complex unwound hard, with Samsung and SK Hynix doing most of the damage, and the desks are unanimous that this was a flow-and-leverage event rather than a break in AI demand. Underneath, the structural capex story is still ramping. Above it, a new Fed under Kevin Warsh has turned hawkish, pulled its easing bias, and stopped telling the market what comes next, which has bid the dollar, lifted short yields, and knocked gold. Oil keeps drifting lower as the US-Iran truce holds the Strait of Hormuz open, though every bank that touches it warns the deal is a 60-day armistice, not a peace. Positioning, not fundamentals, is the swing factor into Micron tomorrow and core PCE on Thursday.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI buildout is real, the leveraged way the market plays it is the fragile part

*The spending behind AI is still accelerating, but the overnight crack in Korea is a warning about the leverage stacked on top of the trade, not a signal that demand is rolling over.*

- UBS sizes US hyperscaler spending at $1.8T this year and next, with Asia's AI hardware supply chain set for 72% earnings growth off the back of it, so the capex pipe is still filling
- Goldman models the global server market compounding 38% a year to $1.4T by 2030, accelerated servers growing 45%, which is the demand the buildout is chasing
- The crowding is the catch: hedge fund net leverage is at a 4-year high after the sharpest 4-week jump in five years (Goldman), and semiconductors now carry a market-cap-to-revenue-share ratio above 6x in global indices, double the Magnificent 7 (JPMorgan)
- The single-stock leverage is extreme, with a 2x daily SK Hynix product alone holding $16.7B, the largest single-stock leveraged ETF in the world, and that name fell 13% overnight as Korea's index dropped 10% (Goldman)
- The buildout also feeds inflation, with AI-driven memory prices adding 0.4pp to year-on-year core PCE by December and peaking near 0.6pp in the second half (Goldman)

The power side is where the spend lands physically. Chevron just signed a 20-year contract for a gigawatt-scale Permian data center project Barclays pegs at roughly 30%-plus levered returns and $1.4bn of annual pre-tax cash flow, JPMorgan models global power demand compounding 4.2% a year for 15 years, and UBS sees data centers going from 4% to 9% of US electricity by 2035. The arms-race logic is what keeps the capex coming: Goldman's read is that rising Chinese competition forces hyperscalers to keep spending rather than be outspent.

The bear case is real and JPMorgan names it directly. Semiconductor volatility creeping higher into 6x-plus concentration is the classic setup for a VaR shock, where one volatility spike forces leveraged funds to dump in a self-reinforcing slide, exactly what Korea just printed. Goldman's own desk adds that the largest capital allocators are the ones most exposed to over-investment risk, and the first hyperscaler to signal a capex slowdown is likely to be rewarded, especially as cheaper Chinese open models like GLM-5.2 and a 3B-parameter model scoring 94.3 on a 2026 math benchmark let real work migrate to smaller, cheaper compute. The defense is that the Korean break was mechanical, with Goldman attributing it to flow exhaustion and a pension fund turning into a $1.5B mechanical seller, not a demand miss, and the second-order beneficiaries are sold out regardless of which chip wins. The cleaner way to own the buildout right now is its physical layer, the power names and infrastructure ($CVX, independent power producers like $VST and $CEG, $XLU) rather than chasing the leveraged memory single-names into Micron's print, with $SMH and $MU the high-beta expression for anyone willing to size for another 10% air pocket.

### The Warsh Fed stopped telling you what comes next

*The new Fed has gone hawkish and quiet at the same time, and the market is pricing hikes the data has not yet earned.*

Warsh's first meeting was a unanimous hold at 3.50-3.75%, but he stripped the easing bias and skipped the dot plot entirely, and UBS describes the reaction as a bear-flattening, where the 2-year yield jumped 16bps to a 16-month high of 4.2% while the 30-year barely moved. The market now prices better than a 50% chance of a September hike and roughly 48bps of hikes by mid-2027 (Mizuho), with Citadel Securities going furthest at three 25bp hikes to 4.25-4.50% and calling the proactive Fed "easier to deal with." The skeptics push back hard: the TSL note argues killing forward guidance just buys more volatility and false starts, while Goldman and UBS both say the hike pricing is too aggressive and Goldman holds a no-hike baseline. Bloomberg adds the uncomfortable backdrop that excess liquidity has gone negative for the first time since 2021. The honest question is whether Thursday's core PCE prints hot enough to validate the hawkish pricing, or whether the market climbs down. The bias here is long $TLT, with the front-end repricing the part that looks overdone.

### The Hormuz truce is open for business and nobody trusts it

*Oil keeps fading on the reopening, but the deal is a 60-day armistice with a built-in fight over tolls, and the US has no inventory buffer left if it breaks.*

The 14-point US-Iran memorandum signed June 17 reopened the Strait of Hormuz, which carries 21 million barrels a day and over a quarter of seaborne oil trade (UBS), and Brent has drifted toward the high $70s with Goldman holding an $80 year-end target. The catch is the back half of the deal: Iran wants to charge roughly $2M per ship in transit tolls after day 60, Rabobank puts even odds the armistice fails after the US midterms, and ANZ thinks 1-2 million barrels a day of supply may be permanently lost regardless. The asymmetry is what makes this tradable. The cost of being wrong is oil drifting to $75 as flows normalize, an annoyance. The cost of missing it is the toll fight reigniting with the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve at 331.2 million barrels, its lowest since July 1983, leaving no cushion (Rabobank), while managed money already sits at record crude shorts (Goldman). That payoff skew favors owning $BNO outright rather than fading it with the crowd.

### The rotation nobody is positioned for, out of the crowded ten

*The most crowded trade on earth is quietly being sold by the people who built it, the megacap leaders are rolling over, and the rotation underneath is the real signal.*

Hedge funds have cut Magnificent 7 exposure to a 1-year low and are actively shorting the cohort, Microsoft has fallen more than 20% from its June 1 high into a bear market, and communication services names like Alphabet and Meta are at their most oversold in over two decades, the picture of leadership topping out even as the chip index ran 90% quarter-to-date. UBS frames the structural problem: nearly 40% of self-managed portfolios hold more than half their value in 10 or fewer stocks, and the firm is steering clients to broaden into Asia, Europe, and cyclicals. Goldman's flow desk sees the same reflationary, pro-growth tone showing up as small caps and equal-weight quietly outperforming, while Bloomberg warns net equity supply has turned positive for the first time since 2021, a structural headwind for the cap-weighted index. Watch in this order: (1) the cleanest rotation is $RSP equal-weight over $SPY paired with $IWM small-caps as the breadth play, sized to the concentration unwind rather than a single day; (2) whether Micron's print breaks the memory bid that has masked Mag7 weakness; (3) whether liquidity keeps tightening, which is what turns a rotation into a drawdown.

### Gold got cut but not abandoned

*Both desks that cover it lowered their targets on the hawkish Fed, and both stayed structurally long for the same reason: central banks keep buying.*

Goldman cut its December gold target to $4,900 from $5,400 and UBS trimmed to $4,600 for year-end while keeping a $5,200 June-2027 call, both blaming delayed Fed cuts and a rising real-yield drag that has pushed the 2-year-to-gold correlation to -0.6 (UBS). The metal is already down 22% from its January high and just logged its seventh weekly drop in nine weeks. What neither will abandon is the structural bid: 45% of 76 surveyed central banks plan to add reserves (World Gold Council), and official buying ran near 50 tonnes a month. Goldman's own scenarios bracket it cleanly, $4,440 if the hawkish read wins, $6,000 if hedge demand rebounds. The cost of being wrong is real yields grinding gold lower into the low $4,000s. The cost of missing it is the central-bank diversification bid reasserting the moment the Fed blinks. UBS calls dips below $4,000 a buying level, which is the cleanest way to accumulate $GLD without chasing.
