# Hormuz reopens, oil cracks

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The dominant thread across the desk today is the reported US-Iran peace agreement and the deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Research reads it as a fast unwind of the war premium: oil forecasts are coming down, energy equities are giving back the conflict bid, and the broad tape is in relief-rally mode with risk appetite firmer and hedges getting sold. Underneath the geopolitics, two policy events frame the week. Japan's central bank has already moved, and the US Fed meets tomorrow with a brand-new chair in the seat. The crypto complex is quiet and trading on its own clock rather than chasing the equity move.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The war premium in oil is draining out

*The Hormuz reopening deal is a sell-the-rally regime for crude, and the banks are cutting forecasts to match.*

- Goldman cut its Q4 2026 Brent forecast to $80 from $90 and its 2027 average to $75 from $80, assuming Gulf exports normalize to pre-war levels by end-July, so the house view is now structurally lower oil
- Goldman models a 3.2 million barrels-a-day oil surplus in 2027, which is the supply glut that caps any rally from here
- ING pegs ICE Brent already down about 30% from its conflict peak, so a large chunk of the war premium has already left the price
- JPMorgan clocked WTI down 487 basis points in a single session on June 15, the fourth straight down day, confirming the move is underway not anticipated
- Hormuz flows still need to climb roughly 12 million barrels a day to fully hit pre-war export levels (Goldman), which is why physical premiums have a floor even as paper sells off

Crude is repricing for a return of barrels, not a shortage. The forecasts moving lower in lockstep across Goldman, ING and Mizuho mean the desk is treating elevated prices as an exit, not an entry. Where this breaks: the largest tanker operator told the FT that actual Strait transit will take weeks to resume, and spot premiums slipped to pre-war levels but did not collapse, so the physical market is pricing a slower normalization than the futures curve. That gap is real but it is a timing argument, not a direction argument, because a 3 million barrel surplus into 2027 swamps a few weeks of shipping friction. The bias here is to fade oil strength, with energy-sector and $BNO longs the cleaner thing to be selling into any ceasefire-doubt spike.

### AI capex is still the spending supercycle, with one crack worth watching

*The infrastructure build-out remains the dominant growth engine in the corpus, even as Goldman flags the bill coming due on aging gear.*

- Goldman lifted 2026 consensus capex for the top 5 hyperscalers to $755B, about $80B above prior consensus and 80% higher than a year ago, so the spend is still accelerating not plateauing
- S&P 500 capex grew 38% year-on-year in Q1 2026 versus just 1% for buybacks (Goldman), meaning companies are plowing cash into building rather than returning it
- Total hyperscaler capex plus R&D is set to hit $1 trillion in 2026 across the eight global names (Goldman), the scale number that makes this a macro force not a sector story
- Global data-center power demand is modeled to grow 220% by 2030 versus 2023, 60% of it in the US (Goldman), which is the multi-year demand tail under the picks-and-shovels names
- The capex-beneficiary basket is up about 25% year-to-date versus MSCI All-Country World (Goldman), so the market is already paying for durability

JPMorgan calls the AI upstream theme a dominant, durable driver and frames it as a capex supercycle reshaping equity returns. The contested point is profitability: Goldman warns that rising hyperscaler depreciation expenses, as all this new equipment ages, will reverse some of the return-on-equity benefit the S&P has enjoyed. That is the genuine bear thread, and it is a Goldman-versus-JPMorgan split worth respecting rather than smoothing over. The defense is that the depreciation drag is a 2027-plus problem while the order books for the equipment makers are full now, with Goldman reiterating Buy on Applied Materials ($AMAT, PT $520) for its deposition and etch exposure. Watch in this order: (1) hyperscaler capex guides at the next earnings round, the first place a pullback shows, (2) $AMAT and semicap order trends as the real-time demand read, (3) the depreciation line in hyperscaler filings as the Goldman risk crystallizes. Net positioning view: long the equipment makers like $AMAT, with the rare-earth supplier $MP (Goldman Buy, PT $80) as the government-backed supply-chain leg.

### Japan's rate-hike cycle is intact and the equity market is leading on it

*The Bank of Japan just hiked again, and the next leg is a grind toward a genuinely higher long-run rate.*

- The BoJ raised its policy rate to 1.00% from 0.75% at this week's June meeting, a widely expected move that came on a 7-1 vote (Goldman), confirming the hiking cycle is still live
- Mitsubishi UFJ Morgan Stanley sees the rate climbing to 1.5% over the next year as the terminal level, so the market should price more tightening, not a pause
- The BoJ's own neutral-rate estimate is 1.1% to 2.5% (Goldman), meaning even at 1.00% policy is still stimulative and has room to run
- Monthly JGB purchases hold around ¥2 trillion (Goldman), so the balance-sheet runoff is steady and not a shock variable
- Japanese AI-semiconductor stocks drove about 60% of the equity market's gains since April, with ¥16 trillion of buybacks announced year-to-date (JPMorgan), so the domestic bid has a real engine

The mechanism is that a central bank still well below its own neutral estimate can keep tightening without choking an equity market that is being pulled up by the global AI trade and a record buyback wave. The pushback is that a faster BoJ or a stronger yen would pressure the carry flows that have funded global risk, and a yen squeeze is the classic way Japan tightening bleeds into everything else. The falsifiable line: if the BoJ does not deliver the next hike toward 1.5% by mid-2027 as MUMSS expects, the yen-strength thesis is dead and the carry trade survives, and until then the trade is long Japanese equity exposure through $DXJ where the buyback and AI-semi bid is doing the work.

### The Fed meets tomorrow with a new chair and a dovish lean priced too lightly

*Goldman thinks the Fed cuts more than the market expects, and Kevin Warsh's first meeting is the wildcard.*

- Goldman's probability-weighted Fed path is explicitly more dovish than market pricing, so the house sees lower rates than the curve does
- Goldman pegs the 2026 Fed funds median dot at 3.625%, up from 3.375% in the March projections, with the target range expected to hold at 3.50-3.75% tomorrow
- The 10-year Treasury yield slipped to about 4.44% into the meeting, down a couple of basis points, so the bond market is leaning the same dovish way
- The Fed balance sheet is down to $6.7 trillion from a $9.0 trillion pandemic peak (Crédit Agricole), the backdrop for how much runoff room is left
- Seven separate desks flag Warsh's debut as chair as the event to watch, with none willing to call which way he leans, so the uncertainty itself is the trade

This is the first FOMC of the Warsh era, and the dot plot plus the press conference will set the tone for whether the dovish Goldman case or the steadier market path wins. The counter is that a new chair often plays it safe to establish credibility, which argues for a status-quo hold and no dovish surprise. The defense is that the projections, not the rate decision, carry the signal, and Goldman's read is that the committee's own dots already drifted higher even as the probability-weighted path stays soft. The falsifiable line: if the SEP dots tomorrow do not move dovish and Warsh sounds neutral, the long-duration trade has no fresh catalyst, and until that print the bias is a modest long $TLT into the meeting as the cleanest expression of the dovish lean.

### Tesla's delivery number is tracking ahead, but the US is the soft spot

*Both desks covering Tesla think Q2 deliveries beat the Street, with Europe and China carrying a weak US.*

- Goldman raised its 2Q26 delivery forecast to 420K from 405K, above the 400K Visible Alpha consensus, so the setup is a beat-the-number quarter
- The independent commentary read lands at the same 420K versus a 400K consensus, an unusual convergence that strengthens the signal
- US deliveries are running down mid-teens percent year-on-year through May (Goldman), which is the soft underbelly the headline number hides
- The beat is therefore an international story, with Europe and China demand offsetting the domestic decline
- No desk in the corpus is arguing the bear side on the delivery number itself, so the risk is in the mix, not the total

The number that prints will likely top consensus, but the composition matters more than the headline for anyone trading the follow-through. The bear case is straightforward: a beat built on overseas strength while the home market falls mid-teens is a lower-quality beat, and any guide that leans on a fragile US recovery gets faded fast. The defense is that consensus is already sitting below both serious estimates at 400K, so the bar to clear is low and the surprise skews positive on the print itself. Does the delivery beat hold the stock, or does the mid-teens US decline become the story the day after? Long $TSLA into the delivery release is the cleaner expression if you trust the number over the mix.
