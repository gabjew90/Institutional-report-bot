# Fed drops the cut

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The research that piled up over the last 24 hours is dominated by one event: the June Fed meeting landed hawkish, and nineteen different desks are still arguing about what it means. The easing bias is gone from the statement, roughly nine of eighteen policymakers now pencil in a hike this year, and new chair Kevin Warsh stripped the communication down to the bone. Underneath that, the AI buildout keeps swallowing every incremental research dollar, with capex forecasts marching higher into 2027 even as a few voices warn the trade has gone late and crowded. The third thread is oil and geopolitics: banks spent the window treating the US-Iran deal and the Strait of Hormuz reopening as the swing factor for energy, and the tape has already started pricing a truce that the headlines now say is slipping. Positioning reads cautious-but-engaged: nobody is calling a top out loud, but the contrarian notes flagging concentration risk got louder.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The Fed pulled the cut and the market still doesn't believe it

*The June meeting was a real hawkish turn, but the bond market is betting the Fed blinks before it ever hikes, and we think that bet is too confident.*

- The easing bias is gone from the statement and forward guidance was removed entirely, with around nine of eighteen policymakers now projecting at least one hike this year (BofA, Goldman) — the Fed took the cut off the table that markets were leaning on a month ago
- Natixis amended its call to an "extended hold," reading the dots as credible — the desk that moved is moving toward the Fed, not away from it
- UBS still sees the policy rate "ultimately move lower" and pencils the 10-year Treasury yield back to 4.00% — the rate-cut camp is betting hawkish talk fades into actual easing
- ANZ's forecast doesn't get the funds rate down to 3.0-3.25% until Q3 2027, and Berenberg sees a 3.75-4.00% year-end rate — even the doves are pushing the cuts far out
- The split is real: BofA, Goldman and Natixis read the hawkish signal as something that sticks, while UBS, Berenberg, Scotiabank, UniCredit, SEB, ANZ and MUFG all expect rates lower eventually

This is a genuine bank-versus-bank disagreement, not a consensus with a footnote. The hawkish camp says a Fed that just deleted its easing bias and watched nine members draw in hikes is not a Fed that cuts in the next two meetings. The dovish camp says the talk is cheap and the economy forces easing regardless. The bear case for staying long duration (long-dated Treasuries, where small yield moves swing the price hard) is that growth rolls over fast enough that the Fed cuts anyway and the doves are simply early. Where that breaks down is the follow-through: the desks calling for lower rates also put those cuts in 2027, not 2026, so the near-term path is a Fed on hold staring at sticky inflation, which is exactly the regime that punishes bonds priced for a soft landing. The bias here is to fade the rate-cut optimism, leaning short $TLT and long $UUP into next week's PCE print, with the recognition that this is a Friday-pre-holiday-thin-liquidity setup.

### Warsh runs a quieter Fed and that alone adds volatility

*Cutting the Fed's words to the bone removes the cushion markets leaned on, so expect bigger reactions to every data point.*

Eighteen desks flagged the change in how the Fed communicates under Warsh, and the read is near-unanimous: the post-meeting statement was cut to roughly 130 words and forward guidance was effectively terminated, with questions about future policy deflected (Rabobank). The mechanism is simple. When the Fed stops telling traders where rates are headed, the market has to price uncertainty on its own, and that means sharper moves around each inflation and jobs release. BofA's read is that a data-dependent, centrist Warsh actually lands hawkish to anyone who expected dovishness, while Goldman frames it as an "impossible tightrope walk of neutrality." Either way, the hand-holding is over. For someone with US options exposure, this argues for owning volatility into the data calendar rather than fading it, with $VIXY the cleanest expression of a market that has lost its guidance cushion.

### AI capex keeps eating every spare dollar

*The data-center buildout is still the only place incremental money is going, and the spend numbers are getting bigger, not smaller.*

The capex forecasts moved up again: UBS now models $820 billion of AI-related spend in 2026, up 68% year over year, rising to $990 billion in 2027, and pegs US hyperscaler spending at $1.8 trillion across 2026-2027. Citadel Securities frames the same cycle as $0.75 trillion this year scaling to $1.25 trillion next. Goldman expects company budgets for generative AI to double over the next three years. On the single-name side, Goldman calls ASML's guide of 90 EUV tools a floor with upside to 110-120, and BE Semiconductor's CMD raised targets on hybrid bonding adoption (BofA puts a €401 target on it). The caveat is the loud one: the contrarian voice warns the final phase of a mania is often the most profitable and the most dangerous, and the UBS-heavy sourcing on this theme means it is closer to one very loud house view than fifteen independent reads. So the question a trader has to answer for themselves: does a hyperscaler capex guide-down show up before the AI supply chain reprices higher, or does the spend just keep compounding? The lean is long the picks-and-shovels names through Micron's ($MU) June 24 print, which is the next hard read on memory demand.

### Oil is pricing a truce the headlines keep delaying

*The market already faded the war premium, but a peace deal that keeps slipping leaves crude exposed to a snap-back the tape isn't braced for.*

Brent is heading for a roughly 9% weekly loss as traders price the US-Iran truce and the Strait of Hormuz reopening, with the strait carrying about 12.5 million barrels a day of transit (JPMorgan) and SEB marking Brent near $77.9. The problem is the deal is fragile: Scotiabank calls it "a pause, not peace, with more drama likely still to come," and JPMorgan's framing is that the US "waved the white flag," with a US$300 billion reconstruction figure stipulated in the MOU. Meanwhile inventory buffers are depleted (Global X, ANZ), so the physical market is recovering from an already-thin starting point even as paper prices fall on the truce headlines. That is the asymmetry. Cost of being wrong on a long-oil tilt here: another leg lower if the truce firms and Hormuz flows normalize fully. Cost of missing it if the talks collapse again, as this morning's scrapped Geneva meeting hints: a fast repricing of the supply premium back into a market with no inventory cushion. Watch in this order: (1) whether the rescheduled US-Iran talks actually convene, (2) refined-product inventories, (3) Hormuz transit volumes returning to the 12.5M-bbl baseline. The tilt favors owning cheap upside in $BNO rather than chasing the downside.

### The rotation trade for when AI looks like a market top

*The same window that has banks raising AI capex also has five desks warning the leadership is topping, and that rotate-out signal deserves its own seat.*

The skeptical bloc is small but explicit: BofA flags market-bubble risk, one desk warns of a semiconductor value-at-risk shock (a forced-selling event if the crowded trade unwinds), and others point to compute scarcity and a possible dollar breakout as signs the leadership is rolling over toward a market top. None of this is a call to short the index outright. It is a call to stop paying up for the most concentrated exposure. If the worry is that the top handful of names carry the whole market, the cleanest answer is to spread the bet: long the equal-weight S&P ($RSP) over the cap-weighted $SPY, paired with long utilities ($XLU) as the defensive complement that pays you while you wait. The honest counter is that every bubble call for the last year has been early, and the capex numbers in the section above say the earnings are real. The reason to hold the rotation anyway is that you are not betting against AI, you are betting against paying a concentration premium for it.
