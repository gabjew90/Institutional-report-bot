# Payrolls triple the forecast

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The Bureau of Labor Statistics put August payrolls at 162,000, against a Street bar of 55,000 and a prior month that lost 23,000 jobs. That is the print, not an estimate. Unemployment held at 4.1%. Every desk in this window had published a forecast under 70,000, and the print cleared all of them before the opening bell. It landed on a market that had spent Wednesday and Thursday pricing the opposite. Fed Governor Waller said he could support holding rates steady if the recent disinflation held, and swap pricing for a September increase fell hard behind him.

Underneath, the research splits three ways. Energy is the inflation problem nobody has solved, with European gas above its 2022 wartime peak and Gulf oil flows running at two-thirds of pre-war levels. The AI trade is being repriced on economics rather than demand, because the price charged per unit of AI usage is falling faster than usage is growing. And equity protection has become historically cheap heading into the two weeks that hold a CPI print and a live Fed meeting.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The dovish week died at 8:30 this morning

*September is a live Fed meeting again, and the labor data just took away the excuse for sitting still.*

- August payrolls printed 162,000 against the Street's 55,000 and a prior month of minus 23,000. Three times the bar, and the widest forecast-to-print gap of any release in this window.
- Every desk in the corpus was under it. JPMorgan's Feroli had 50,000, Deutsche Bank 65,000, Goldman 40,000. JPMorgan called 30,000 to 70,000 the range that lets the Fed sit still, and the print cleared the top of it by more than 90,000.
- Waller's comments had cut September hike pricing to 51% from 63% at Wednesday's close (Deutsche Bank), with JPMorgan marking it near 50%. Goldman had it at 66% earlier in the week. The whole move happened on labor-market softness that the official count just contradicted.
- The ISM services prices-paid index hit a four-year high of 72.6 in August against 70.0 expected (Deutsche Bank), and JPMorgan puts the twelve-month average at 68.5, the highest since April 2023. That series leads inflation by about three months, so it describes the Fed's problem in December, not August.
- Unemployment held at 4.1%, exactly where JPMorgan forecast it. The jobs came from hiring rather than from people leaving the workforce, which removes the one reading that would have let the Fed treat the headline as noise.

Waller's case for holding rested on the labor market cooling enough to let disinflation finish the job, and Wednesday's ADP count of 38,000 against 47,000 expected (Goldman) supported him. The official number says the opposite, and it arrives on top of a services price index at a four-year high. The short $TLT read carried here since August 31 rested on a September increase priced at 60% to 70%, Waller broke that on Wednesday, and this morning put it back.

JPMorgan's desk argues the decisive number is next Friday's CPI rather than today's payrolls, and points out the labor detail is softer than the headline, with Challenger job cuts up 58% month over month in August. That cuts less than it looks. The three-month Challenger average is down 33% year over year and August cuts were the lowest for that month since 2022, both JPMorgan's own figures, so the layoff data does not carry the dovish case either. And JPMorgan's stated condition for a hold was core CPI at +0.1% to +0.2% month over month with payrolls near 50,000, so half of that condition is already gone. Next Friday's CPI on September 11 decides the September 16 meeting on JPMorgan's own arithmetic, and a core print above +0.2% leaves the hold case without a leg.

### AI's problem is the price, not the demand

*The AI trade is being repriced on what a unit of usage sells for, and the argument runs through one bank's own building.*

Goldman's Privorotsky desk says more demand at lower prices is not automatically bullish when the price falls faster than volume grows, and the numbers carry him: the Silicon Data token index fell 29% in August to $0.97 per million tokens, more than half off the May peak near $2.05, while OpenRouter token volume rose 47% month over month against a 7% rise in dollar spend (JPMorgan). TS Lombard and Deutsche Bank take the other side, arguing compute scarcity is what has driven hyperscaler outperformance and that AI usage generates revenue by itself. Credit is the tell rather than equity, because the five rated hyperscalers are on track for roughly $737 billion of 2026 capital spending, about 38% of revenue, and 78 of the 91 hyperscaler bonds issued this year traded below their issue price by late August (Goldman). Two-thirds of the research here is Goldman's, so read it as one house arguing with itself. Goldman has $AVGO a Buy to $540 after AI semiconductor revenue grew 221% year over year to $16.7 billion and management guided that line to $115 billion in FY27, which prices the stock at 12 times management's own FY28 earnings.

### Refined product is the rates trade now

*The 10-year Treasury is taking its direction from diesel, not from the Fed.*

Goldman's one-delta desk puts it plainly: relief on rates requires relief on energy, and the desk trade is still inverse heating oil against the 10-year. European gas broke through €70 per megawatt hour this week, above its 2022 wartime peak, and US diesel cracks have been moving in lockstep with the 10-year yield. The supply picture is worse than the visible data shows and calmer than the panic implies at the same time. Goldman estimates total Persian Gulf oil exports at 15 to 16 million barrels a day once dark tanker crossings near Oman are counted, against only 10 million visible, which is two-thirds of pre-war flow and a net hit of 7.9 million barrels a day. Five desks track Hormuz as a live supply risk and not one will say whether it reopens, which is the honest state of it. Goldman's commodity research is long the March 2027 to December 2027 European diesel time-spread and sees more than 100% upside if the spread holds here, while the same house marks Brent fair value at $80 against a $95 spot, so $15 of the price is risk premium rather than barrels.

### The rotation BofA is actually calling

*Index protection into November's midterms is priced about right. The mispricing is one layer down, in small caps and in where the froth has moved.*

S&P 500 options are pricing a 0.82% move the day after the midterms, against a 1.3% average one-day move after every midterm since 1975 (BofA). That gap is not the trade, because BofA's own argument is that midterms have rarely produced large broad-market moves at all. They produce differences between individual stocks. What is mispriced is where the risk sits: regional banks and small caps carry less election premium than their 2024 presidential-election moves justify, so BofA's targeted hedge is $IWM puts rather than index protection, taken while tail put skew sits near record steepness. The same screen says the froth has relocated. BofA's bubble-risk gauge now puts US healthcare and cybersecurity at the most bubble-like price action it tracks, while the Kospi has left bubble territory outright at a reading of 0.52 with the KOSPI 200 at 6.2 times forward earnings, the 1st percentile of its own ten-year history, and BofA rotates long dividend exposure out of European banks into the Euro Stoxx 50. Two-thirds of the research here is one house, so read it as BofA's book and not a consensus. Cost of being wrong: the 0.82% priced move is roughly right and the small-cap puts expire worthless for a few tenths of a percent of notional. Cost of missing it if right: you own index protection that pays nothing while the dispersion you were actually exposed to happens underneath it.

### Someone is blowing up in volatility

*Equity protection has gone historically cheap in the two weeks before a live Fed meeting.*

Nomura's McElligott has $IWM three-month at-the-money implied volatility at the 0.2 percentile, S&P skew and put skew back to the 2nd percentile, and dispersion profitability down to the 5th percentile, which is the shape of dealers and market makers taking real losses rather than a market at peace. BTIG's Krinsky has the five-day CBOE composite put-call ratio at 0.82, among the lowest readings in years, with the share of Russell 3000 stocks above their 50-day average at its weakest since early April, and expects the retest of the 7600 breakout level to fail toward 7200 to 7300, roughly 7% to 8% off the highs. Goldman's volatility desk has Nasdaq 100 average-stock implied volatility down about 20 points since mid-July to year-to-date lows and tells clients to hedge when they can rather than when they have to. Watch in this order: (1) next Friday's CPI, into which Goldman's VIX October 20/30 call spread costs about $1.00 for roughly ten times leverage, (2) the September 16 Fed meeting, which is the date JPMorgan's desk says to stay market-neutral until, and (3) whether the S&P 500 holds 7600, because BTIG's downside case only opens below it.
