# AI's bond bill lands

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

Crude is climbing again because both of the sea routes Gulf oil has to travel are contested at the same time, and the research written over the weekend treats that as a supply problem with months to run rather than a headline that fades by Friday. Equity desks are defensive going into Wednesday, with several flagging that systematic funds have already flipped from buying to selling and that the number of individual stocks making new lows keeps climbing while the index holds near its high. Bond yields are at cycle highs and the Federal Reserve is expected to raise rates on Wednesday rather than hold.

The question this week is not whether companies keep spending on artificial intelligence. It is who lends them the money, at what price, and what happens to the equities when that price keeps rising.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI buildout is now a bond trade

*Demand for AI computing has stopped being the variable that decides these stocks, and the cost of the debt paying for it has taken over.*

- Five hyperscalers plus Nvidia have sold $320 billion of bonds this year including financing vehicles, which JPMorgan measures at 68% of all new long-dated Treasury borrowing over the same stretch. The buildout is bidding for the same long-term lenders as the US government.
- AI-linked bond sales reached $400 billion year to date (BofA Global Research), and Goldman expects another $230 billion of ordinary investment-grade supply in September alone. The pressure is coming from supply, not from anything demand is doing.
- The 10-year Treasury yield is at 4.96%, the highest since October 2023 (Deutsche Bank), already past the 4.75% year-end level Goldman raised its forecast to. Every quarter the buildout waits to borrow, it borrows more expensively.
- Oracle's quarter last Thursday showed cloud infrastructure revenue up 121%, chips running at 97.9% utilization, and $664 billion of contracted work not yet delivered. Nothing in the demand data argues for slowing down.
- The largest spenders added $403 billion of annual capital spending between early 2024 and mid-2026, against $184 billion for the other 495 companies in the index (Deutsche Bank). This is one concentrated borrower wearing five names.

The buildout used to be paid for out of operating cash flow and is now paid for with long-dated debt, so the same yield that sets the government's borrowing cost also sets the hurdle every data center still on a drawing board has to clear. That is why the complex sold off last week on a bond move rather than on anything a company said, and why Morgan Stanley's estimate that AI investment has added an average of 0.6 percentage points a year to US growth since 2025 makes a funding squeeze here a macro event rather than a sector one.

BofA Securities looks at the same supply and calls it absorbable, particularly if the issuance is paced, and the credit quality backs them up: JPMorgan puts hyperscaler borrowing at 1.6 times earnings, half the level of the broader investment-grade market. Goldman's credit desk takes the other side and is blunt about it, calling AI paper a long-term underweight and saying strength after this next stretch should be sold. The positioning data is with Goldman rather than with the balance sheets, because investment-grade long-short ratios sit at two-year lows and short positions in corporate bond ETFs rose 14% in a single week, which is what it looks like when the people who own this paper start hedging it instead of holding it. Goldman's analysts still carry $NVDA at Buy to $300 and Deutsche Bank has $ORCL at Buy to $300, and Wednesday's 2:00pm rate decision and projections are what tell you whether those targets get financed near 5% or somewhere worse.

### Both ends of the Gulf are contested now

*This oil bid is a supply story with two separate chokepoints, which is why it has not faded the way the last three escalations did.*

Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline after a drone attack, the route that exists specifically so crude can bypass the Strait of Hormuz. Iran-aligned Houthi forces took Mocha and reached Perim Island, putting them on the Bab el-Mandeb at the other end of the Red Sea. Saudi crude supply fell 2.3 million barrels a day in August to 6 million (IEA), and US retail diesel topped $6 a gallon for the first time (JPMorgan). The crowding is now the bigger risk than the geopolitics, with trend-following funds 91% long Brent against 45% at the end of August, and Goldman still classifies Iran as a left-tail risk where de-escalation rather than escalation is the move that reprices the complex hardest. Gulf ministers were due to meet Iran in Oman about shipping access, a meeting that has already slipped once, and Goldman flags those discussions as the main input into oil sentiment today.

### The ECB hiked and the fight is about December

*Two and a half percent is a floor rather than a ceiling, but the market has already run further than the central bank is likely to go.*

The ECB raised its deposit rate a quarter point to 2.50% last week. Deutsche Bank reads the statement and projections as consistent with another move to 2.75% by December. Rabobank calls 2.50% the end of it and forecasts nothing further through 2027. Bank of America holds the third position, that the ECB is genuinely hawkish but the market has priced roughly five more hikes against an adverse scenario the bank itself thinks needs far worse energy prices than these. Energy is the swing factor and it is moving the wrong way, with European gas at €80 per megawatt hour, the highest since late 2022 (Citi), while German 10-year yields have already broken 3.50%, a post-2009 high and past the 3.25% Goldman raised its year-end forecast to. Watch in this order: (1) the next euro area inflation reading, which decides December and is where the long euro position lives, with Société Générale putting August at 3.3%, (2) European gas holding above €80, and (3) whether the December projections push the inflation path up again.

### Six desks say hike, Goldman says hold

*The Fed raises rates on Wednesday, and the argument that matters is over what it signals about October and December.*

August core inflation printed 0.29% month over month against the 0.2% expected, up from 0.22% in July (Deutsche Bank). Société Générale, Northern Trust, TS Lombard and Deutsche Bank all look for a quarter-point move Wednesday, and Bank of America carries the out-of-consensus call for three quarter-point hikes across the rest of this year. Goldman is the dissent that matters, reading the same inflation print as benign enough to allow a hold and expecting the committee to avoid signalling anything beyond this week. Academy Securities makes the harder argument, that raising rates does nothing about supply-driven energy inflation and adds to the government's own interest bill. The bond market has already voted, because last week's Treasury buyback was tripled to $6 billion and still came up short at $5.19 billion, with the 10-year sitting at 4.96%. Does the 10-year take out 5% and hold it before Wednesday's projections arrive, or does the Fed's own forecast put the ceiling in first?

### The quality rotation is already running under the index

*The crowded winners are rolling over underneath a market top that the headline index still hides, and the defensive side of that rotation is where the research is actually positioned.*

Nearly 5% of the Russell 3000 hit 52-week lows last week while the index itself sat near its high. Goldman's systematic desk has the short-term trigger levels for trend-following funds broken on the S&P 500, the Nasdaq 100 and the Russell 2000 at the same time, with a cluster of mechanical sellers already activated, and BTIG has small caps through their 50-day average and headed for the 200-day. The money is not leaving equities, it is moving down the quality ladder: Bank of America has high free-cash-flow stocks, tracked by $VFLO, up 37% this year, and Morgan Stanley has that quality rotation broadening out of technology into health care ($XLV), financials and higher-end consumer. Goldman's flow desk calls the move into software ($IGV) and cybersecurity a pain trade, which is what a rotation looks like when the people who need to own it are positioned against it. Cost of being wrong: you underperform another melt-up in the same ten names that already carry the index. Cost of missing it if right: you hold that concentration through the unwind the breadth data says has already started.
