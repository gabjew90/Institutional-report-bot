# The AI bill lands

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The research that landed over the weekend argues one thing from five directions: money got more expensive, and the bill is landing on the AI trade. The Fed raised rates for the first time this cycle and told everyone it does not yet consider policy restrictive. Damage to refineries and shipping routes keeps feeding the kind of inflation a central bank cannot look through. Long-term government borrowing costs pushed to levels last seen before the financial crisis.

Underneath a resilient index, the internals are worse than the headline. Fewer stocks are participating, the riskiest corporate debt is widening, and hedge funds are carrying unusually light exposure while retail buys. No desk is calling a top. Several are buying protection because option prices have not caught up to the macro.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI build is now a bond-market problem

*The cash big tech needs for data centers is competing with the US Treasury for the same lenders, and a 10-year yield that just closed above 5% for the first time since 2007 is what that competition costs.*

- The 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 5.00%, up 6 basis points on the day, its first close above 5% since 2007 (Deutsche Bank). Basis points are hundredths of a percent. That is the level where stock valuations start paying real rent for the money behind them.
- Warsh gave three reasons long-term yields are rising, and one of them was competition for capital from the big cloud companies (One River Asset Management). The Fed is now naming the data-center build as a rate driver in its own right.
- BofA raised 2026 capital spending estimates to $195 billion for Alphabet and $145 billion for Meta, and Morgan Stanley has the five largest cloud operators' combined free cash flow bottoming at minus $265 billion in the third quarter of 2027. A hole that size gets filled with borrowed money.
- Term premium on Treasuries, the extra yield investors demand for lending to the government for a long stretch, has moved above investment-grade corporate spreads (HSBC Asset Management). Lenders now demand more to hold government debt than blue-chip company debt, which reverses the whole post-2008 pattern.
- BofA has $META at Buy to $835 because the market assigns roughly $4 billion per gigawatt to its AI capacity, against the $40 billion per gigawatt implied by the capacity deals Anthropic and Google signed with SpaceX. The stock market is not pricing the capacity the bond market is funding.

Both borrowers are bidding for the same pool of savings. The big cloud companies are funding a spending wave out of debt markets at the same moment the Treasury refinances an unusually short pile of its own, with 60% of US debt maturing inside four years (Société Générale), and RBC counts 66 gigawatts of data centers actively under construction worldwide, up 21 gigawatts since March, with another 304 gigawatts planned. The price of money is what clears that queue.

Allianz Research reaches the opposite conclusion over a longer window. If AI delivers the productivity it promises, the same forces pull long-term rates roughly 50 basis points lower across a decade, because faster growth repairs the government's debt path, and Goldman's Pasquariello adds that the bond market has already done the tightening work, which leaves the setup for stocks better than it was a month ago. Allianz's own path to that lower number runs the US 10-year through a 135 basis point round trip, and the up-leg comes first. Northern Trust and HSBC read the same combination of AI spending and government debt as a permanent step up in the rate the economy settles at, not a detour, and Morgan Stanley has already moved its terminal Fed rate to 4.25-4.50%. Goldman's desk is buying December-2026 $SPY puts that only pay if 10-year rates stay above 4.8%, which prices the yield move and the stock move as one trade.

### Refineries, not barrels, are the squeeze

*The oil story stopped being about crude supply and became about the machines that turn crude into diesel and jet fuel.*

Bernstein counts Ukrainian drone strikes on roughly 90% of Russia's refining capacity, 80% of that in 2026 alone, with more than 2 million barrels a day currently offline, about 2% of world refining capacity, and Russia has extended its diesel and gasoline export ban to the end of January 2027. Allianz Research puts the net hit to flows through the Strait of Hormuz at 4.3 million barrels a day, the US refining margin at 62 euros a barrel in August, 68% above its five-year average, and global refining investment down 15% from a year earlier. That combination is why Morgan Stanley threw out its disinflation baseline and replaced it with an oil risk premium, cutting 2027 growth to 2.3% and lifting 2026 core inflation to 3.2%. The cushions are gone too, with US strategic reserves at 285 million barrels on September 15, the lowest since April 1984.

Most of the supply work here comes from one house, and Allianz Research's own base case has Brent consolidating to 76 dollars next year if the Saudi pipeline is repaired quickly. The outage running past six weeks is what kills that case, and Bernstein rates $XOM Outperform to $182 on refining margins staying near records.

### Xi lands in Washington and options are priced for a quiet week

*The one hard catalyst on this week's calendar is an event the options market has not marked at all.*

Xi is expected in the US from September 23 to 25, with AI, compute, trade, rare earths and the Iran war on the agenda (Goldman Sachs). The PBOC has walked its dollar-yuan fixing below 6.70 for the first time since 2022, which is how Beijing signals it wants a calm backdrop going in. Academy reads the agenda as cyber, AI and compute, with Washington needing more from Beijing than the other way round. Options on $FXI are near multi-year lows and carry no extra premium for the summit dates (Goldman Sachs). Deutsche Bank has Asian equities already advancing on summit optimism with technology shares leading, so the good outcome is partly in the price and the bad one is not priced at all. The Bear Traps Report is buying $KWEB near $25 into it, and the tell is whether export controls on high-end chips make the agenda: a constructive readout on compute lifts semis and China internet together, a breakdown re-prices both.

### The Fed's next two hikes are a forecast, not a fact

*The market is priced for more tightening than this Fed actually delivers.*

The FOMC raised rates 25 basis points to 3.75-4.00% unanimously, and Warsh said policy is still not clearly restrictive (Société Générale, HSBC Asset Management). Morgan Stanley answered by moving its terminal rate to 4.25-4.50% on two more hikes in December and March, and Goldman's economists now pencil in another 25 basis points in October. The other side is just as named. Generali calls the four hikes priced through 2027 excessive because sticky inflation is already eating into real incomes, HSBC puts the ceiling at three more before tightening starts damaging growth, and Goldman's house view is that the Fed underdelivers against market pricing, with its terminal rate unchanged at 3.25-3.50%. August core PCE, the inflation measure the Fed actually targets, ran at 3.2%. If core inflation has not turned lower by the December meeting, Generali's over-priced call is dead and Morgan Stanley's 4.25-4.50% stops being the high end and becomes the floor.
