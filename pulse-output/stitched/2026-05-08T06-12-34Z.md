# Iran ceasefire cracks overnight, AI capex still the only story working

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The peace-deal trade that drove yesterday's session unwound after the close. US strikes on Bandar Abbas and Qeshm port flipped the tape from a Wednesday risk-on melt-up (driven by the alleged 14-point US-Iran memorandum of understanding and AMD's beat-and-raise) into a defensive overnight session. Equities had been partying like 1999 going into the strikes, with semiconductor momentum running 4-sigma and the top 10 NDX names up an average of 784% over twelve months. ## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex is still the only earnings story that's actually working

*Earnings season has confirmed the AI infrastructure build, and institutional flow is still chasing it.*

- Anthropic ARR hit $30bn as of April, an 80x increase, with the new SpaceX Colossus 1 partnership expanding compute supply (Goldman Sachs)
- Goldman desk has institutional Tech flows running 2-3x the YTD average, with long-onlys rotating into AI infrastructure and taking profits in memory
- Datadog +31% on Wednesday print, dragging Snowflake and MongoDB with it on AI-workload monetization read-throughs
- Korea is rerating on the AI chip cycle, with semiconductor exports up 148% YoY in Jan-April and HBM capacity already sold out (Crédit Agricole CIB)
- Goldman raised regional 2026 earnings growth to 60% from 45% on AI leadership and reform tailwinds
- AMD beat-and-raise Wednesday afterhours added the chip leg of the same trade

The mechanism is straight-line. Hyperscalers are spending the cash at a rate that 12 months ago felt aggressive and now looks conservative, and the whole supply chain (memory, networking, foundry, software-monitoring) is getting paid as the orders land. JPMorgan is shifting model portfolios specifically to finance new AI-related long ideas, and Goldman's Sales & Trading desk has $3.8bn of MOC buy imbalance and Prime Book exposure at the 99th percentile of one-year history. The flow is not slowing.

The pushback we would anticipate is the dot-com echo. SOX weekly RSI is at the highest level since March 2000, the 14-day RSI has cleared 80 (a level seen perhaps three times in 30 years), and SNDK has put up a 3960% twelve-month return that beats Qualcomm's 2600% peak from the actual 1999 mania. The 25-30% SOX correction back to its 50-day moving average is the bear's exit ramp. Even granting that the RSI math is screaming overbought, the fundamental gap from 1999 is real (revenue and earnings are landing, not just price targets), and 1Q26 S&P earnings growth is tracking 17%, the fastest pace in four years (UBS). For US risk-asset exposure, the cleanest expression is the AI infrastructure ETFs ($SOXX, $SMH) over single-name memory chasing, with picks-and-shovels names ($AMAT, Applied Materials) preferred for those who want to express the build without paying the SNDK multiple.

### The bond market is calling the Fed's bluff into NFP

*Yields refuse to come down even as oil round-trips, and that is the tell.*

- US 10Y at 4.35% (down 8bps Wednesday on peace headlines) with the 30Y still pinned at the 5% pain threshold (Bank of America, Hartnett)
- Citi NFP forecast at -15k against +65k consensus, with unemployment expected to tick from 4.3% to 4.4%
- UBS revised May headline CPI projection to +0.72% MoM (from +0.52%) on retail gasoline pass-through, with Bloomberg's gauge now pricing 2.9% one-year forward CPI versus 2.4% pre-war
- Citi base case is three 25bps Fed cuts (September, October, December) and they call the market's 20-25% hike probability excessive
- TS Lombard argues the Fed needs to swap roughly $660bn of coupons into bills to reach its target portfolio, structurally lifting longer-dated yields regardless of policy rate moves
- Crédit Agricole's positioning data shows leveraged accounts still net short the front of the curve as of the latest CFTC update

Three forces are pulling yields higher at once. Oil-driven inflation pass-through into core PCE is a 3-month-lag story that has just begun, the Treasury supply mix is shifting longer-dated, and the incoming Warsh-Fed framing (cut the policy rate, tighten financial conditions via the repo rate) means the curve cannot rally on a cut headline the way it used to. UBS still expects two cuts in 2H26 and a 4.00% 10Y by year-end. The market disagrees in size.

The pushback we would anticipate is that a soft NFP (Citi's -15k forecast or anything sub-zero) does the Fed's job for it, drives the 30Y back below 5%, and reopens the duration-rally trade (long-dated Treasuries appreciating as yields fall). That risk is real, but the lesson of every weak print this cycle has been that yields rally for one session and then fade as the next CPI confirms the supply-side pressure is structural. Even granting a soft payrolls surprise today, the May 12 CPI print and the May 13 PPI print are the gates that decide whether the bond market is right. The trade lean here is short-dated Treasuries (the 2-year and shorter end of the curve) over the 30Y, with $UUP positioned as the asymmetric expression if the dollar bid from this week's strikes holds.

### The Iran ceasefire trade just cracked, and oil is the cleanest read

*The US strikes on Bandar Abbas reopen the supply-disruption thesis the equity market had already priced out.*

- Brent dropped 7.8% Wednesday on the alleged 14-point peace memo, then reversed sharply on the strikes overnight (JPMorgan)
- OPEC April production fell to 20.55m b/d, the lowest level since 1990, on Kuwait and Iran disruptions (ING)
- US commercial crude inventories drew 2.3m barrels last week with exports collapsing 1.7m b/d week-over-week
- IEA Director said energy markets are heading into "troubled waters" with the Iran war, and global oil inventories are trending toward record lows with demand destruction not yet here (UBS Asia Compass)
- UBS marks Strait of Hormuz reopening odds at 40%, down from 80% two weeks ago, and a $150 Brent scenario would compress Indian FY27 GDP from 7% to 6.3%
- Goldman desk flagged $120 WTI as the level at which the macro narrative breaks for equities

The mechanism is that physical oil markets have been pricing scarcity for weeks (refined fuel inventories at 8-year lows, Europe's jet fuel running dry by June) while paper futures kept hoping for a diplomatic resolution. Wednesday's memo got equities to the highest of the year and crushed crude in a single session. The strikes overnight have reopened the same gap. Shell beat by 9% in Q1 and lifted its dividend 5% on this cycle, Aker BP and Harbour Energy pre-released strong cash flow, and the JPM 2026 Brent average is now $82.10, up 20% year-on-year.

The pushback we would anticipate is that this is another headline-driven false start, the negotiations resume by Monday, and Brent settles back into the high $90s as Saudi spare capacity reassures the market. That risk is real but the supply scar takes months to heal regardless of the diplomatic timeline (refinery maintenance, IMO sanctions enforcement, OPEC compliance), and the IEA warning suggests the Q3 demand-destruction story is not where the energy story ends. The setup leans long Brent over WTI, with $BNO the cleanest expression for US options traders and energy infrastructure names ($EPD, $WMB, $OKE) the lower-vol way to participate without timing a single headline.

### Inflation expectations are quietly resetting higher, and it's the trade nobody owns

*Five and ten-year market-priced inflation rates have re-broken to 2023 highs while everyone is watching the spot oil tape.*

- 5- and 10-year inflation breakevens (the inflation rate priced into the bond market) at the highest levels since 2023 with December 2026 WTI futures at high-$80s, up from $70 in early April (Bloomberg)
- 1-year forward headline CPI swap is now 2.9%, up from 2.4% pre-war, and 5-10 year breakevens have widened 40-50bps since the conflict began
- Q1 Central Bank gold purchases hit 244 tonnes, up 17% quarter-over-quarter, with UBS reiterating a $5,900/oz year-end target
- 30Y UST yield pinned at 5% with TLT showing a -3 standard deviation impulse (Goldman) is the canary that the inflation reset is real
- US fertilizer proxy prices (urea, ammonia) are rising and will push food CPI higher with a lag (Bloomberg)
- TS Lombard projects inflation will broaden in 2027 rather than disinflate, and Fed Staff already estimate 0.8pp of core inflation pass-through directly from tariffs

The mechanism is layered. Energy is the trigger, fertilizer and food are the second-derivative pass-through, and tariff pricing is the structural floor. The market is treating each piece in isolation and missing the regime. Gold is the asset that does not need a narrative because central banks are buying it in bulk regardless of US policy.

The pushback we would anticipate is that the Iran situation does ultimately resolve, oil settles back in the $80s, and breakevens compress back to the 2.5% area. That risk is real but the central bank gold bid is tracking real-asset diversification away from the dollar and US Treasuries, not just oil. Even granting a peace deal scenario, the structural reset in long-run inflation pricing already happened over the last six weeks, and a $5,900 gold target needs only a continuation of the central-bank flow that is already underway. For someone with US options exposure, the bias is long $GLD via call spreads (the volatility has collapsed, making upside structures cheap), with $TLT short the cleanest pair against it if the conviction is that long-dated yields stay pinned near 5%.
