The "picks and shovels" trade is no longer a theme, it is showing up in the income statements at $AMD, $GLW, $VRT, $SNDK, and the names that supply them.

The pushback we would anticipate is the parallel Goldman Partner note asking "what could go wrong" with AI fever. For US options exposure, this leans toward owning the supply-chain breadth via $SOXX or layering single-name exposure to $AMD and $GLW into pullbacks rather than chasing $NVDA into print.

### The repo-rate trick is how the Fed delivers a "cut" without easing

*The cleanest read of TS Lombard, The Market Ear, and UBS together is that the Fed cuts the funds rate while letting repo spreads widen, so policy stays effectively tight even as headlines say "easing."*

- TS Lombard: Fed needs to swap roughly $660bn of coupons into bills to hit its 20-25% target ratio
- Bank reserves at 8.5% of bank assets, approaching the scarcity threshold (TS Lombard)
- $200bn already added to Powell's balance sheet to keep short rates frozen (TS Lombard)
- UBS sees two cuts in the second half of 2026, calling the market "exaggerated" on tightening risk
- 30Y Treasury yield stabilized around 5% with the Market Ear flagging it as a "critical pain threshold" for stocks (Goldman desk: -3 sigma impulse already showing in $TLT)
- Crédit Agricole rate-positioning chart pack: futures positions still leaning short the long end

Two views collide. UBS reads the curve as overreacting, expecting cuts to come and risk assets to benefit. TS Lombard and The Market Ear read the same setup and conclude the Fed will trim the policy rate but engineer the effective rate tighter via repo spreads (Warsh's potential framework as new chair). The trader-relevant point is that "two cuts" priced from UBS and "no real easing" from Lombard can both be true at once, depending on which rate the market starts pricing off of.

The pushback we would anticipate is that with $725bn capex commitments and 17% S&P Q1 earnings growth (UBS, fastest in four years), equity multiples don't actually need rate cuts to hold up. That counter has weight. Even granting it, what breaks the rally is not the funds rate, it is 30Y above 5% sustained. That is the level The Market Ear and Goldman both name as the equity pressure point, and it is where the Fed's repo trick lands hardest. The setup favors owning long-duration Treasury exposure via $TLT against a clean break of 30Y back below 4.85%, with a stop at a sustained close above 5.10% on the long bond.

### Hormuz is back in the active column, and the breakevens already knew

*Late-afternoon US strikes on Iranian ports flipped the morning's peace-deal trade, but the inflation read in the bond market never bought the ceasefire in the first place.*

- Reuters wires after 5 PM ET: US military strikes on Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas confirmed. Iran accuses US of violating ceasefire
- Brent fell from $108 to $98 intraday on initial peace headlines (SEB), recovered late on strikes
- 5-10 year breakevens are at the highest level since 2023 (Bloomberg)
- Breakevens have widened 40-50 bps since the start of the war (Bloomberg)
- Distillate stocks at -11% vs five-year average. API crude draw of -8.1mb (biggest in 3 months) reported this week (SEB)
- December 2026 WTI futures pricing the high-$80s (Bloomberg), up from roughly $70 in early April
- Reuters: pre-strike oil-price options bets totaled $7bn

The setup is asymmetric. Headlines can drag spot Brent $10 in either direction in one session, but the December 2026 curve has barely moved off the high-$80s. That is the inflation market saying the supply scar lasts months even if the diplomatic story resolves. Refined-fuel inventory levels are the binding constraint, not Hormuz transit on any given day. Europe's jet fuel runs near empty by June, and you cannot refill that inventory with one MoU.

The pushback we would anticipate is that a real, durable peace deal would crack physical pricing along with futures and breakevens together, sending the energy complex 20-30% lower in a week. That is the bull case for $TLT and the bear case for energy. Even granting that scenario, the late-day strikes today are the asymmetric hint: peace headlines are reversible, supply destruction takes quarters to repair. For US energy exposure, the trade lean is long Brent through $BNO ahead of any fresh Hormuz incident, with loss of $85 sustained on Brent as the line that says the supply scare is over.
