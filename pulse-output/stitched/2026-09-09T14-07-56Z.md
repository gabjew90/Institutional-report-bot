# Oil breaks, options asleep

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

Two forces landed in this research window at once. Gulf tanker attacks pushed crude into triple digits while the sell side was still revising oil forecasts upward from well below spot, and a US labour market that came in far hotter than anyone expected moved the argument from rate cuts to whether the Fed hikes this month. Underneath both, every measure of expected market movement sits near multi-decade lows, so desks keep describing the same setup in different words: the risk is real, the protection is cheap, and almost nobody owns it. Positioning is split in an unusual way. Trend-following funds are close to fully invested while discretionary hedge funds carry among the least money at risk in a year, which is why the same market reads as complacent to one desk and as dry powder to another.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Oil at $100 walks into Friday's inflation print

*The energy shock that just pushed crude to triple digits will land in Friday's US inflation report as a hard number, and the options market has priced almost none of it.*

- Crude reached USD 100 a barrel after the latest wave of tanker attacks (UBS), against Goldman Sachs's Brent forecast of $85 for December 2026, raised by $5 only this week. Spot is running about $15 above the sell side's year-end number, which is what a genuine shortage looks like before the revisions catch up.
- JPMorgan's economists have August energy prices rising 2.5% for the month, led by a 4.2% jump in motor fuel. That is the channel through which a tanker attack in the Gulf becomes a US government statistic.
- The S&P 500 options bet that pays off on a move in either direction on Friday costs about 100 basis points, one percent of the index (Goldman Sachs). Traders are paying almost nothing to be positioned for the print.
- Options assign a 25% probability to Brent above $100 at the March 2027 expiry, up from 6% a month ago (Goldman Sachs). The oil market has repriced this tail. Equity volatility has not.
- Bond markets price roughly a 63% chance of a quarter-point hike on September 16 (JPMorgan). An upside surprise from fuel does not just hit stocks, it decides the Fed.

Motor fuel is a direct line item in the consumer price index, so crude that moved on Gulf headlines three weeks ago prints as inflation on Friday, and it arrives at a Fed whose chair has said publicly that prices are not falling fast enough.

The case against trading it is that fuel mostly hits the headline number, while core inflation, the measure that strips out food and energy, is where the Fed looks. JPMorgan has core at +0.21% for August, Bank of America at 0.22%, Goldman Sachs at 0.23%, all clustered at or barely above the 0.2% survey, and none of those forecasts carries the oil move. Two things cut against that. Airfares are running 26-27% above last year on JPMorgan's tracking, which is jet fuel entering core inflation through a different door, and the policy split is far wider than the forecast cluster suggests. UBS now expects two hikes this year, September and December, taking the target range to 4.00-4.25%. TS Lombard argues Kevin Warsh finds a way through this data to avoid moving at all, because an inverted yield curve no longer slows an economy where federal borrowing is the marginal debt. Goldman's house view is still no hike even as its own desk marks the probability at 60%. The September 2 read here, long $USO and $XLE against a futures market priced for the strait reopening, settles at triple-digit crude and it cut the right way. Friday at 8:30 AM ET decides the rest, and JPMorgan's desk has drawn its line at 0.30% on core, above which it expects the S&P to fall 1.5% to 2.5%.

### The AI buildout is now a bond-market story

*The hyperscalers have become one of the biggest borrowers at the long end of the Treasury market, which makes the AI trade and the 30-year yield the same trade.*

Five hyperscalers plus Nvidia have issued about $320 billion of debt in 2026, equal to 68% of new long-dated Treasury borrowing on a ten-year-equivalent basis (J.P. Morgan), and Goldman Sachs expects AI-related investment-grade credit supply to grow another 40% to roughly $340 billion in 2027 on top of the $300 billion already sold this year. The reason it matters is that the savings pool being asked to absorb record government issuance is the same one funding the data centres, and hyperscaler capital spending and research now runs $870 billion a year, up from $460 billion in 2024. Index-level earnings growth is now substantially AI-driven, which is what makes a financing squeeze an equity problem rather than a credit one. JPMorgan treats that debt reliance as the risk itself, while Goldman Sachs argues the binding constraint on deployment is physical build capacity rather than financing or chips, and rates $JPM a Buy to $452 precisely because the capital intensity wins a large bank underwriting share. $ORCL reports Thursday after the close, down 25% since its last set of results, and it is the first hard read on whether the backlog behind all that borrowing is converting into revenue.

### The Treasury's first big buyback lands today

*Long-dated government bonds are the most fragile part of this market and today's buyback is the first real test of whether the Treasury can hold them.*

Treasury runs its first long-dated buyback under Secretary Bessent's higher caps this morning, with desks looking for more than $4 billion, and Bank of America's desk says a purchase well short of the cap pushes long-dated yields higher rather than lower. It matters because the 30-year yield's one-month average has held above 5.2% for the first time since 2003, August's Twist operation already failed to cap the 10-year, which still trades above where it sat when that operation was announced (J.P. Morgan), and the pressure is global: 10-year German government bonds hit a 15-year high and the Japanese equivalent touched 3.00%, the highest since 1996. Deutsche Bank's counter is that this is fair rather than broken, putting 10-year value at about 4.8% against a 5.5% ten-year average of nominal growth, and calculating a positive twelve-month return from here as long as yields stay under 5.5%. Watch in this order: (1) today's purchase size against the cap, (2) Thursday's producer price report at 8:30 AM ET, (3) whether 30-year yields hold above 5.2% into Friday. Goldman Sachs would rather own the 5-year against the 10-year, a bet that 10-year yields rise faster.

### The rotation the desks are quietly recommending

*Three houses spent this week publishing on where to shelter rather than what to chase, and that shift is the signal.*

Bank of America's strategists recommend large-cap value as ballast against both hike risk and credit stress, naming $PWV, $PVAL and $AVLV, and flag Media and IT Services as the value traps to avoid. Their evidence is specific: in every credit stress cycle since the 1990s, the second half of the spread widening came with negative S&P 500 returns. UBS moved its income advice off short-dated bonds because their advantage over cash narrows as the Fed keeps hiking, steering clients toward emerging market credit, high yield and subordinated debt for a 5-7% annual yield, and raised its Treasury forecasts to 4.25% on the 2-year and 4.5% on the 10-year by June 2027, 100 and 40 basis points above its prior numbers. Goldman Sachs, UBS and Citi argue the opposite over twelve months, that profit growth carries stocks through both the geopolitical and the rate shock, while JPMorgan has stepped back to neutral for the next few weeks. Does the value ballast pay before the credit widening Bank of America describes starts, or only once everybody already owns it?

### Nobody is paying for protection

*Every measure of expected market movement is sitting at a level that has never held for long.*

Six-month realized correlation across the S&P 500, a measure of how much its stocks move together, sits at 11, a reading hit twice in 25 years, in February 2007 and January 2018, and both times volatility spiked afterwards (Goldman Sachs). It comes alongside an intraday VIX of 13.8 on September 4, the lowest of the year, a MOVE index at 73, which is the bond market's equivalent of the VIX and sits in the 14th percentile of five years, and S&P options pricing just a 0.82% move for the midterm election outcome two months out (Bank of America). When stocks move independently the index absorbs single-name damage without going anywhere, which is the exact condition that reverses hard once one macro number forces everything to move together, and funds that size equity exposure mechanically to volatility are at the 100th percentile of allocation (Deutsche Bank), leaving selling as their only available move. The counterweight is Goldman Sachs's prime brokerage data, which has fundamental long/short funds with net money at risk in the 1st percentile and gross at the 19th, so much of what would be forced out in a shock is already out. Watch in this order: (1) Friday's core inflation print, (2) the September 16 FOMC, (3) put positioning into the midterms, where Bank of America is selling the October 16 VIX put to fund a November 22-30 call spread.

### Europe hikes tomorrow and then the real argument starts

*The ECB's quarter-point move on Thursday is fully priced, so the whole trade is in what the statement says about December.*

Goldman Sachs calls Thursday's 25 basis point increase the final hike of the cycle, resting that on below-trend second-half growth and an assumption of lower energy prices ahead. Deutsche Bank looks for one more in December, taking the deposit rate to 2.75%, and the market is priced beyond both. The energy assumption underneath Goldman's call is the weak point. European gas storage entered the heating season at 65% against an 82% five-year average, Qatari liquefied gas exports through the strait have collapsed 96%, and Dutch TTF gas traded at EUR 74 per megawatt-hour on September 7 (UBS) against Citi's probability-weighted winter estimate of EUR 61. A cold snap that drives storage toward the 30% critical level reprices gas to EUR 100-150 and takes the lower-energy assumption with it. Thursday at 8:15 AM ET, the hike is not the event. The guidance is, and Goldman's last-hike call and Deutsche Bank's December call cannot both survive it.
