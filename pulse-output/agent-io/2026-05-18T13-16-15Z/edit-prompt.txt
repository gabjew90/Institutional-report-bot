4. **Asset-class outperformance / breakouts.** Crypto running while equities chop, gold breaking new highs, oil rolling 5%+ on the day, sector rotation visible in ETF flow extremes — multi-asset divergences when they're extreme enough to be the story.
5. **Central bank actions and surprises.** FOMC outcomes, surprise rate decisions, Powell/Warsh testimony that *actually moved markets* (not routine speeches), surprise QE/QT announcements. ECB/BOJ/BOE actions only if they spill into US assets.

8. **Currency / FX shocks with US linkage.** Sudden DXY moves ≥ 0.5%, BOJ yen intervention, EM currency stress (peso, lira, won) when it visibly affects US assets, dollar-funding stress signals.
9. **Volatility regime shifts.** VIX spike ≥ 5 points in a session, term-structure inversion (front-month VIX > back-month), major options expiry imbalances that visibly moved spot.

### Rate-cut hopes are dying, and equities haven't priced it

*Hot inflation, a 5% 30-year auction, and a Mizuho data-acceleration call are pushing the Fed wait-and-see camp.*

- The 30-year Treasury auction cleared at a 5.00% coupon for the first time since November 2007 (Crédit Agricole CIB) — institutional demand is being tested at the long end, and the level is what Goldman Sachs flags as the threshold where bonds start to reflect fiscal stress and weigh on equities
- The 10-year UST yield sits at 5.12% (Academy Securities, Friday's close), with Mizuho International tracking the 10-year toward 4.60% and the UK 30-year gilt at 5.86%, the highest since 1998 — the global long end is broken, and the US is not insulated
- Fed rate-hike expectations are now in the curve (The Market Ear); the SFRZ7 contract is where Mizuho expresses the higher-for-longer view

Three forces are pulling yields higher at the same time. Oil-driven inflation is feeding core CPI on the standard 3-month lag, fresh long-dated Treasury supply is hitting at a 5% clear, and a Fed that cannot credibly cut into a 3.8% headline print has lost the option of jawboning the curve lower. Goldman Sachs makes the equity link explicit: higher for longer is a problem for equities, especially non-profitable tech.

The contrarian read comes from JPMorgan: markets are pricing a more hawkish Fed than JPM forecasts, leaving room for a rate-cut surprise into the next dovish data point. The defense is that even on JPM's own view, the front end of the cuts has been pushed out, not erased, and the long end's supply problem is independent of the Fed's reaction function. The bias here is long $TLT against trimmed index-equity exposure, and a META capex guide-up at the next print would be the cleanest signal that the AI-capex trade can absorb a higher long-end.

### Breadth is narrow, momentum is at the dangerous part

*The same melt-up that is lifting the index is now showing the warning patterns that historically preceded reversals.*

- Bloomberg flagged 30 S&P 500 stocks making new 52-week lows yesterday at the same time the index posted a 10% monthly return — the historical average forward 1-month return when more than 30 stocks hit new lows is 1.5%, a long way from the recent run rate
- The Market Ear notes the momentum factor has rallied more than 20% over the past 3 months, the historical threshold for identifying sharp momentum episodes since 1980 — average subsequent month gain is 6%, but the variance widens
- Goldman Sachs flags $3 trillion in call notional traded last week and a Risk Appetite Indicator at 1.1, the highest level in over five years — the bid is mechanical, dealers are short gamma, and any spot pullback forces selling into weakness
- JPMorgan adds extreme hedge-fund positioning and Z-scores suggest a tactical reversal is imminent, with Deutsche Bank putting bank-sector positioning at the 8th percentile across investors — pension and SWF flows are running thin in the rotation laggards
- $QQQ has technical support at $680 (Fibonacci) and $660 secondary (The Market Ear) — the levels every short-gamma desk is watching, and the levels below which the dealer hedge flow flips from suppressing to amplifying volatility

The mechanism is straightforward. The index keeps grinding higher on a narrow set of AI-linked names, the call-options demand on those same names is pulling dealers short gamma at higher and higher prices, and the unwind risk is concentrated in the same trade hedge funds are crowded into. Goldman Sachs' counter-read is that narrow breadth alone is not a bearish directional indicator, but the bull case rests on AI revenue continuing to deliver, not on breadth healing.

The skeptical desks are not arguing against the bull thesis; they are arguing that the entry point is bad. The defense from the AI camp is that fundamentals remain quite strong, which is fair on the revenue side. But if the SMH 3-month ATM volatility is already at the 100th percentile and the index is still grinding, the asymmetry has shifted: the upside is priced and the downside is cheap. The bias is long low-delta $SPY puts as a tail hedge, paired with long $SMH on any retracement below the next technical level.
