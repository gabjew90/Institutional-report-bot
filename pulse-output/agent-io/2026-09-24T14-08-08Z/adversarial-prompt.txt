Two Federal Reserve officials told markets before noon to expect another rate increase, and the bond market took them at their word. The ten-year Treasury yield pushed to 5.16%, above Wednesday's close that was already the highest since 2007, and stocks slipped with it. $SPY is 0.53% lower and $QQQ weaker at -0.80% early in the session, with long-dated Treasuries no safer, $TLT down 0.87%. - **US-Iran talks produced no progress and crude jumped.** $USO is up 4.11% and $BNO up 3.69%, the biggest moves on the board, with shipping through the Strait of Hormuz still running below its ten-day average and Saudi Arabia intercepting Houthi missiles Thursday morning. ## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The bond market deleted the cuts and started pricing hikes

*The repricing has further to run, and equities have not finished discounting it.*

- The US composite business survey hit a five-year high of 58.4 in September against 55.3 expected (Deutsche Bank). A beat that wide repriced the entire rate path in one session instead of over weeks.
- The ten-year Treasury yield closed Wednesday at 5.11%, its highest since 2007 (Deutsche Bank), and pushed to 5.16% Thursday morning. Stock valuations built on the assumption that rates would fall stop working at that level.
- Traders now price 37 basis points of Fed increases by year-end, close to even odds on two more, with October alone at 71% (Deutsche Bank).
- Wednesday's five-year Treasury auction cleared 3.20 basis points above where the market was trading beforehand, with weak demand from real money buyers (JPMorgan). That is the mechanical reason the selloff extended instead of stalling.

The chain runs from activity to costs to policy. Surveys this strong, arriving with the fastest monthly rise in input costs since 2020 (JPMorgan), remove the disinflation assumption that justified a cut, and a central bank that has to resume tightening after a pause loses any ability to talk long-term yields back down. Williams and Paulson saying it out loud Thursday morning is confirmation rather than new information.

JPMorgan reads the same selloff as an entry point. Its fair value estimate for the ten-year is 5.5%, and its strategists argue that past episodes of yields overshooting toward fair value have been buying opportunities in equities. That case is stronger than the last two sessions make it look, and it carries a timing problem, because 5.5% sits more than thirty basis points above where the ten-year trades now. Even the constructive house needs more yield pain before its buy level arrives, which is why its own near-term equity stance is cautious into the Fed meeting. Wednesday's auction is the tell on which side gets tested first: if Wednesday's 5.11% were genuinely cheap against a 5.5% anchor, real money buyers would have taken down the five-year paper, and instead the auction cleared wide. The growth and inflation reports due at 8:30 AM ET on September 30 are the first hard test of whether September's surge is real or a one-month survey artifact, and JPMorgan's 5.5% is the level that settles whether this is an overshoot to buy or a repricing to respect.

### JPMorgan is long tech and buying protection on it at the same time

*The insurance it just bought is the more informative half of that pair.*

The core position has not changed, more technology than benchmark across mega-caps, memory and semiconductors, paid for with a bet against small caps through short $IWM on a three to twelve month view. What is new is the hedge: JPMorgan is buying put options on its own basket of companies whose earnings are most exposed to AI agents taking their business, rather than on semiconductors, because that basket moves the same direction for less premium into month-end when funds reset position sizes. The risk it is paying to cover is already live in single names. $SHOP fell 7% on concern that Meta builds payments into its Muse agent, and $EXPE and $CART sold off on the same fear of being cut out of their own transactions even as they announced partnerships with it (JPMorgan), while insurers that kept Muse out of their marketplaces outperformed. Meta widened its hardware lineup again Thursday, so the channel JPMorgan is hedging against keeps getting bigger. JPMorgan names the Fed meeting outcome as the risk to the short small-cap leg.

### China's AI profit pool gets a number, and a summit to price it

*The forecast is big enough to underwrite the trade, and today's meeting decides its timing.*

JPMorgan initiated broader coverage of Chinese artificial intelligence with a constructive view and put US$544 billion of cumulative operating profit on the companies in that chain between 2026 and 2030. The shape of the number matters more than the size: models and applications should deliver about a third of industry profits but close to two thirds of the growth in annual earnings over the period, which argues for owning the platforms rather than the hardware that trains them, with $BABA and $TCEHY the named beneficiaries. The $FXI summit trade flagged Tuesday has its first answer on tariffs, with the US and China extending their trade truce by two months and holding tariffs where they are until January 10, and the Trump-Xi meeting itself running today. JPMorgan is long Chinese internet through $KWEB and prefers Hong Kong listed shares over mainland ones into China technology earnings, which is the position today's readout prices.
