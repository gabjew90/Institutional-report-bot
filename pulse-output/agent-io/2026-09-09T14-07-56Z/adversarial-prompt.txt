# Oil breaks, options asleep

## 1. RECAP

Brent went through $100 a barrel this morning after the biggest wave of attacks on shipping since the war with Iran began, and US stocks are treating it as someone else's problem. Half an hour into the session $SPY sits at $763.89, down 0.27%, with $QQQ a shade higher. The buying went into commodities instead, and $BTC is holding the $79k area. The positioning underneath explains the calm: trend-following funds are close to fully invested while discretionary hedge funds carry among the least money at risk in a year, so the same market reads as complacency to one desk and as unspent buying power to another.

- **Tanker attacks widened and Brent cleared $100.** Iran and the US hit tankers overnight and into this morning, one of them in Iraqi waters, and shipping costs through Hormuz have run higher since the war started. Crude carried the move: $BNO +1.94% and $USO +1.68% so far in the session.
- **The hedge went into gold, not bonds.** $GLD is up 1.50% to $405.71 while $TLT has not moved and the 30-year yield has eased to 5.24% from 5.26%. Two days before the August consumer price report, buyers are paying for the inflation hedge rather than the growth scare.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Oil at $100 walks into Friday's inflation print

*The energy shock that just pushed crude to triple digits lands in Friday's US inflation report as a hard number, and the options market has priced almost none of it.*

- Brent cleared $100 a barrel this morning on the latest wave of tanker attacks, against Goldman Sachs's Brent forecast of $85 for December, raised only this week. Spot is running about $15 above where the banks see the year ending, which is what a real shortage looks like before the forecasts catch up.
- JPMorgan's economists have August energy prices rising 2.5% for the month, led by a 4.2% jump in motor fuel. That is the channel through which a tanker attack in the Gulf becomes a US government statistic.
- The S&P 500 options position that pays off on a move in either direction on Friday costs about one percent of the index (Goldman Sachs). Traders are paying almost nothing to be positioned for the print.
- Options now put a 25% probability on Brent above $100 at the March 2027 expiry, up from 6% a month ago (Goldman Sachs). The oil market has already priced that outcome. Equity volatility has not.
- Bond markets price roughly a 63% chance of a quarter-point hike on September 16 (JPMorgan). An upside surprise from fuel does not just hit stocks, it decides the Fed.

Motor fuel is a direct line item in the consumer price index, so crude that moved on Gulf headlines three weeks ago prints as inflation on Friday, and it arrives at a Fed whose chair has said publicly that prices are not falling fast enough.

The case against trading it is that fuel mostly hits the headline number, while core inflation, the measure that strips out food and energy, is where the Fed looks. JPMorgan has August core at 0.21% and Goldman Sachs at 0.23%, both at or barely above the 0.2% survey, and neither forecast carries this week's oil move. Two things cut against that. Airfares are running 26% to 27% above last year on JPMorgan's tracking, which is jet fuel entering core inflation through a different door, and the policy split is far wider than the tight forecast cluster suggests: UBS now expects hikes in both September and December, while Goldman's house view is still no hike even as its own desk marks the probability at 60%. The long $USO and $XLE call this product published last week, against a futures market priced for the strait reopening, has settled at triple-digit crude. Friday at 8:30 AM ET decides the rest, and JPMorgan's desk has drawn its line at 0.30% on core, above which it expects the S&P 500 to fall 1.5% to 2.5%.

### The AI buildout is now a bond-market story

*Big tech has become one of the largest borrowers of long-term money in the market, which makes the AI trade and the 30-year Treasury yield the same trade.*

Five of the largest cloud companies plus Nvidia have issued about $320 billion of debt this year, equal to 68% of the government's new long-term borrowing over the same stretch (J.P. Morgan). The pool of savings being asked to absorb record government borrowing is the same pool funding the data centers, and spending on AI capacity and research now runs $870 billion a year against $460 billion in 2024. Most of the S&P 500's earnings growth is now driven by that spending, which is what turns a financing squeeze into an equity problem rather than a credit one. That debt reliance is where JPMorgan locates the risk. The counter from Goldman Sachs is that the real constraint on deployment is physical build capacity rather than money or chips, and it rates $JPM a Buy to $452 on the underwriting share all this borrowing hands a large bank. $ORCL reports Thursday after the close, down 25% since its last set of results, and it is the first hard read on whether the backlog behind the borrowing converts into revenue.

### The Treasury's first big buyback lands today

*The longest-dated government bonds are the most fragile part of this market, and this morning's buyback is the first real test of whether the Treasury can hold them.*

Treasury runs its first buyback of long-dated bonds under Secretary Bessent's higher caps around 11 a.m., with desks looking for more than $4 billion. A purchase well short of the cap pushes long-term yields higher rather than lower (Bank of America), which is the opposite of the point. The 30-year sits at 5.24% this morning, and its one-month average has held above 5.2% for the first time since 2003. The Treasury already tried a version of this in August, buying longer bonds and selling shorter ones, and the 10-year still trades above where it sat when that plan was announced (J.P. Morgan). Deutsche Bank's counter is that this is fair rather than broken: its estimate of 10-year value is about 4.8%, which is where the 10-year trades right now, and it calculates a positive twelve-month return from here as long as yields stay under 5.5%. The size of the purchase against the cap at 11 a.m. is the whole event.

### The rotation the desks are quietly recommending

*The week's client notes are about where to shelter rather than what to chase, and that shift is the signal.*

Bank of America's strategists want large-cap value as ballast against both hike risk and credit stress, naming $PWV, $PVAL and $AVLV, and flag Media and IT Services as the value traps to avoid. Their evidence is specific: in every credit stress cycle since the 1990s, the S&P 500 fell through the second half of the period when corporate borrowing costs were widening against Treasuries. UBS moved its income advice out of short-dated bonds, whose advantage over cash narrows as the Fed keeps hiking, and into higher-yielding corporate and emerging market debt for a 5% to 7% annual yield, while raising its Treasury forecasts to 4.25% on the 2-year and 4.5% on the 10-year by June 2027, a full point higher on the 2-year than its prior call. The opposite case over twelve months comes from Goldman Sachs: profit growth carries stocks through both the geopolitical shock and the rate shock. Bank of America's three named funds are the version of this with instruments attached, and what decides it is whether corporate borrowing costs start widening before the trade gets crowded.

### Nobody is paying for protection

*Every measure of expected market movement is sitting at a level that has never held for long.*

Across the S&P 500, the six-month measure of how much its stocks move together sits at 11, a level hit twice in 25 years, and volatility spiked after both (Goldman Sachs). The VIX printed its low of the year last Friday at 13.8 intraday, and S&P options price a 0.82% move for the midterm election outcome two months out (Bank of America). Brent went through $100 this morning and $VIXY has moved 0.64%. When stocks move independently the index absorbs damage in single names without going anywhere, which is the exact condition that reverses once one macro number forces everything to move together. Funds that size their equity exposure mechanically to volatility are at the 100th percentile of allocation (Deutsche Bank), leaving selling as their only available move. The counterweight is Goldman Sachs's hedge fund client data, which has long/short funds carrying net money at risk in the 1st percentile, so much of what a shock would force out is already out. Bank of America is selling the October 16 VIX put to fund a November 22-30 call spread, the cheap version of owning that risk into Friday.
