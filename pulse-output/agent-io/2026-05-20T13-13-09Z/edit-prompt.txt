4. **Asset-class outperformance / breakouts.** Crypto running while equities chop, gold breaking new highs, oil rolling 5%+ on the day, sector rotation visible in ETF flow extremes — multi-asset divergences when they're extreme enough to be the story.
5. **Central bank actions and surprises.** FOMC outcomes, surprise rate decisions, Powell/Warsh testimony that *actually moved markets* (not routine speeches), surprise QE/QT announcements. ECB/BOJ/BOE actions only if they spill into US assets.

8. **Currency / FX shocks with US linkage.** Sudden DXY moves ≥ 0.5%, BOJ yen intervention, EM currency stress (peso, lira, won) when it visibly affects US assets, dollar-funding stress signals.
9. **Volatility regime shifts.** VIX spike ≥ 5 points in a session, term-structure inversion (front-month VIX > back-month), major options expiry imbalances that visibly moved spot.

### The long end is breaking, and this is a fiscal story, not a Fed story

*Thirty-year yields at multi-decade highs are being driven by government borrowing and rebuilt risk premium, which is why the move has legs the Fed cannot talk down.*

- The 10-year Treasury yield has pushed to about 4.67%, its highest since early 2025 and roughly 45 basis points, just under half a percent, above its April lows (Rabobank)
- The 30-year yield has settled above 5.12%, a level not seen since 2007 (UBS)
- Bloomberg attributes about 60% of the bond sell-off since the Iran war began to real yields, meaning it is growth and supply fear, not just inflation, doing the damage
- Options on long-dated Treasuries saw their one-month implied volatility jump 3 points last week, a clear sign of stress building in the bond market (Goldman Sachs)
- Bank of America's survey shows investors at their most underweight bonds since June 2022, and One River notes US federal debt at $39 trillion against a $1.9 trillion annual deficit, about 6% of GDP

The driver is term premium, the extra yield investors demand for the risk of holding long bonds, being rebuilt after years of being suppressed. War-driven inflation lit the fuse, but a flood of new government debt at the long end is what keeps it burning. The pushback is that yields this high eventually pull buyers back in, and Asia and the Middle East still hold trillions in Treasuries. The problem is that being early is not the same as being wrong: with the deficit still widening and Citi flagging a fresh multi-decade-high target on the 30-year, the supply keeps coming. The bias here is short long-dated Treasuries through $TLT.

A new chair means an uncertain reaction function, and an uncertain reaction function is itself a reason for volatility even before any decision is made. The counter is that no desk has staked a directional view yet, so this is genuinely unpriced rather than mispriced.
