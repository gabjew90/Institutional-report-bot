# Buyback bid fades fast

## 1. RECAP

Half an hour into Friday's session the tape has barely moved after Thursday's rate-driven selloff, with $SPY +0.26% and $QQQ +0.05%. The 30-year Treasury yield is back at 5.26%, up 2.5 basis points (one basis point is one-hundredth of a percent) and higher than where it sat before Treasury announced its expanded buyback of long-dated bonds. $GLD is doing the work instead at +1.18%, and $BTC has run 5.69% since 8pm ET. Iran headlines kept coming overnight and oil ignored them, with $BNO up 0.45%. Underneath the quiet, desks are rotating out of crowded technology winners into healthcare and defensives after the worst day for computer-driven long/short funds in more than two years.

- **$WMT fell 9.15% Thursday on the first hard crack in the US consumer.** Revenue landed at $187.94 billion and earnings at $0.81 a share, but US comparable sales grew only 2.6%, the slowest in six years, and customer traffic growth halved to 1.5% from 3% in the prior quarter. Management named fuel above $4 a gallon as the level that changed shopping behavior.
- **Gold is rebounding on debt worries, not on a weaker dollar.** $GLD is +1.18% with bond-market jitters and US debt concerns doing the buying, while the dollar itself has not moved this morning ($UUP unchanged). That makes this a bond-market trade wearing a currency costume.
- **$BTC ran 5.69% to $77,229 while equities sat still.** $ETH +2.49% and $SOL +3.80% came with it, and no single headline sits behind the move, which points at flows rather than news.
- **The technology unwind left no cushion under it.** Active mutual funds are underweight the largest technology names by 7.2 percentage points and hold 1.2% cash, near a record low, so there is no sidelined money queued up to buy this dip. Hedges are priced for calm anyway, with $VIXY -1.19%.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Treasury bought one day of calm and the bond market took it back

*The buyback expansion changed who blinks first, not where 30-year yields end up.*

- Treasury doubled its buyback operations in 10 to 30-year bonds to at least $4 billion each, roughly $144 billion a year (Goldman). That retires about 16% of new 20-year and 30-year supply.
- It bought exactly one session of relief. The 30-year yield fell on the announcement, has already given the move back, and sits at 5.26% this morning, above where it was before Treasury spoke.
- Corporate borrowers have sold $1.77 trillion of investment-grade debt this year, up 59% from last year (Nomura). Every dollar of that competes with the government for the same buyers of long-dated paper.

The buyback is a swap, not a subtraction. Treasury retires long-dated bonds and pays for it by issuing shorter ones, so the government's own borrowing shifts toward shorter maturities while the deficit that creates the bonds keeps running and the corporate borrowing wave keeps hunting the same long-term buyers. The $TLT read flagged in yesterday's pulse, that the buyback expansion would pin long-dated yields, did not survive a full session, and the direction here has flipped.

Two readings sit inside the same firm. Goldman's rates and equity desks call it a deliberate signal that caps swings in long-dated bonds and is therefore good for stocks and gold. The macro team published the rebuttal within 24 hours: a program like this can change the path of yields, rarely the destination. Against that corporate supply Nomura calls it too small to matter, and Deutsche Bank's clients are treating it as a stopgap rather than a structural fix. Where Goldman's own desk put its money answers the argument, long stocks and gold while avoiding long-dated bonds. If the 30-year is still above 5.25% when Fed Chair Warsh gives the Jackson Hole keynote next Friday morning, August 28, the buyback was a two-day trade and the next tool has to come from the Fed rather than the Treasury.

### Gold cleared the line that matters

*The dollar is the pressure valve for a Treasury that wants lower yields, and gold is where that pressure goes.*

Gold cleared its 200-day average price at $4,500, and the desk view at Goldman went from a tactical long to a regime change, with a $4,900 year-end target anchored on central bank buying and $5,000 in view beyond it. Positioning is already a top-five reading of the last three years, gold funds are tracking their biggest week of inflows since April, and buyers traded more than four $GLD calls for every put. The logic is blunt: propping up bond prices means letting the dollar absorb the strain instead. Gold is a long at JPMorgan on that same argument that the dollar loses purchasing power. Crowding this heavy cuts both ways, and the $4,500 line just cleared is what decides which way. Back below it and this is a failed breakout rather than a new trend. The equity way in, per Goldman, is $EGO, rated Buy with five catalysts flagged through year-end.

### The unwind was positioning, not fundamentals

*Nothing broke in the numbers this week, which is what makes the systematic damage a positioning event rather than a repricing.*

Computer-driven long/short funds lost 1.4% in a single session, their worst day in more than two years, and the trade of owning this year's winners has now taken 24 single-day drops of more than 5% in 2026, more than the prior five years combined (Goldman). No earnings miss and no data print caused it, which is the whole point: this was crowded money leaving, not the market repricing anything. Where it went is visible in healthcare, where $MRNA closed up 177% on Phase 3 melanoma vaccine data. Protection into the next two weeks is unusually cheap, with three-month options on small-cap stocks at their lowest price in two years, which is an odd thing to see going into a Fed chair's first Jackson Hole. The hedges the desks are actually buying are $IWM November $270 puts (Nomura) and September $IWM puts and put spreads into Jackson Hole and the September Fed meeting (Goldman).

### The consumer slowed in the same week inflation repriced

*Walmart's traffic number is the first hard evidence that $4 fuel is changing behavior.*

The same session that showed the consumer cracking showed inflation expectations going the other way. The inflation rate priced into the market for the next twelve months jumped 16 basis points, its biggest daily move since March (Deutsche Bank), and a Goldman basket of stocks built for slow growth with rising prices is up 6.7% on the week, which is the position the desks have already taken. The counter is that this is not a weak economy at all. A survey of factory activity in the Philadelphia Fed's region printed 47.4, its highest since April 2021, with capital spending plans back at levels not seen in decades. Expensive is not the same as recessionary. That leaves the bind Warsh walks into, as Goldman puts it: dovish guidance rescues short-term rates but feeds the inflation expectations already moving, and hawkish guidance tightens into a consumer that just slowed. Wednesday's core PCE print at 8:30am is the checkpoint, with core PCE reaching 2.9% by December on Goldman's forecast.

### The capex math finally works, the pricing math does not

*The AI buildout now has an auditable return, and the thing it sells is getting cheaper by the week.*

The return finally has a hard number attached to it: roughly 22% after-tax on Alibaba's incremental AI spending against the 10% it costs to fund, with outside cloud revenue accelerating (JPMorgan). That settles whether the spending earns anything. It does not settle what the output sells for. The cost of running a given AI workload has fallen 38% since the end of June (Goldman), and open-weight models are now handling customer demos at a fraction of a frontier model's cost and several times its speed (Deutsche Bank). OpenAI is the live version of that squeeze, growing revenue to $6.7 billion last quarter while its operating loss widened to $12.3 billion. Politics is the second constraint, with Pennsylvania's executive order limiting new data-center permits already pressuring $TLN. Whether the price of AI output falls faster than the cost of producing it decides the sector, and JPMorgan's answer is to own the operator that can show the receipts, Overweight $BABA to $210, with Nvidia's Wednesday print the next read on whether the spending side still has buyers.

### Sanctions detail lands Monday

*The oil market is pricing a settlement that the shipping data says is not coming.*

Treasury Secretary Bessent promised the toughest sanctions ever imposed on Iran with the detail due Monday and asked China to cooperate, Iran answered by promising a devastating response, and Iranian oil offers to Chinese buyers are already falling as the blockade bites. Ship transits through the Strait of Hormuz are running in single digits. None of it is in the price. $BNO is up 0.45% this morning and Brent at $93.78 sits near the top of a range Goldman reads as the market betting against real escalation, while JPMorgan puts the odds of Hormuz normalizing before year-end at 30%. The trade the desks are in is refined fuel rather than crude, because crude can be re-routed and diesel cannot. The highest-conviction energy call on Goldman's book is diesel refining margins several months out, on the assumption that refinery outages persist, and Nomura flags the same margins as the channel through which this reaches global manufacturing. Refining margins grinding toward triple digits per barrel are why $VLO, $MPC and $PBF stopped trading on historical valuation multiples. Chinese product exports and demand destruction at these prices are the bear case, and Monday's detail decides whether this stays a headline or becomes a supply event.
