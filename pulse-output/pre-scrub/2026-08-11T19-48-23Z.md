# Hormuz shut, hikes back

## 1. RECAP

The reopening trade that carried the first week of August is coming apart. Iran said Tuesday that the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until the US meets its conditions, crude took the bid again, and US equities ground lower all session with tech the heaviest part of it. $SPY and $QQQ are each about a quarter percent lower into the close while $BNO is up 1.64%, and $BTC slipped under $63,500 with no divergence from $ETH or $SOL. The friction underneath is positioning. Sentiment gauges are at their most bullish since early 2021, and hedge funds went back to adding short bets a week after the biggest squeeze since 2020. Tomorrow morning's July inflation report is where it gets settled.

- **Iran tied the strait to its own terms.** Tehran's security council said Hormuz stays closed unless the US meets its conditions, and Trump put his choices as letting Iran's economy fail or hitting it hard. Oil was the only major asset bid today, $BNO +1.64% and $USO +1.23%.
- **Attacks on shipping widened.** Four crew were killed in a Red Sea attack and a missile strike was reported in the Gulf. Gulf equity markets fell as deal odds faded, and crude held its gain into the US close.
- **Funds were cutting index exposure ahead of the print.** A record $21.6 billion of Nasdaq futures was sold in the week to August 4, the first net short position since May 2025 (Goldman). $QQQ led the market lower today at -0.26%, and nobody paid up for protection into tomorrow, with $VIXY down 0.92%.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The reopening trade is dead and the hike is back on the table

*Last week the market paid up for a Middle East resolution and a Fed on hold, and both bets are unwinding one day before the inflation print that decides them.*

- Brent rose 4.99% to $87.72 a barrel Monday with the strait still shut, and $BNO added another 1.64% today, so the energy cost that feeds inflation is climbing again right as desks were writing it off
- September Fed hike odds are 52%, up from 44% a week ago, which means the market is repricing tightening rather than easing
- futures carry about 47 basis points of increases at the peak and 31 basis points by the December meeting, a basis point being one-hundredth of a percentage point, while a 3.5% inflation rate has historically come with more than 100 basis points of tightening
- forecasts run from no change at all this year to 75 basis points of increases (Bank of America), so the biggest desks sit three hikes apart working from the same data
- options price roughly a 0.9% move for Wednesday against a 1.1% recent average, so the print is being underwritten as a non-event

The chain runs from the strait to the pump to the print. A blocked chokepoint lifts crude, crude lifts fuel and airfares a month or two later, and a central bank defending a 3.5% inflation rate cannot cut while that is happening, which is why the curve has drifted back toward tightening with stocks near highs.

The case against is the calendar. Wednesday's report covers July, collected before August's crude move, with energy prices falling 1.2% inside it and core inflation at 0.19% against 0.2% consensus (Goldman). JPMorgan has core at 0.22% and 2.5% against a year ago, and its scenario work puts real damage only above 0.30% core, worth 1.5% to 2.5% off the S&P 500.

A soft July number does not undo an August oil move, and the bond market has stopped waiting for one. Long-dated yields hit new highs while stocks recovered and the inflation rate priced into bonds fell, which is a market pricing lasting supply damage rather than a one-week headline. Crude still trades below its recent peaks against a strait that never reopened. Long $BNO. A deal that reopens the strait kills the trade, and the talks in Oman are where that would show up first.

### The AI buildout is now a credit story

*The spending is real, and from here it runs on borrowed money that the bond market is having trouble swallowing.*

Planned hyperscaler spending of $1.1 trillion runs about $150 billion past the cash those companies generate, and roughly $400 billion of new debt is lined up to cover the gap, landing on an investment-grade market Goldman's traders already describe as showing indigestion. The counter comes from inside the same bank, where strategists put 2026 buybacks at twice total corporate issuance and call the bubble comparisons overdone. Both readings point the same way on power, which gets paid whether or not the borrowers ever earn a return on the compute, and Goldman just lifted its 2030 data-center electricity demand forecast to 170% growth from 117%. Long $SRE and the utilities feeding the buildout rather than the names doing the borrowing. The cost of being wrong is a utility that lags a tech rally.

### Chips got dumped and the catalyst is Thursday

*The semiconductor selloff went far enough that chip prices no longer match chip earnings, and Applied Materials is the first real test of that gap.*

July took about 20% out of the value of the semiconductor and memory complex (Goldman), driven by forced selling rather than downgrades, and bearish positioning in chips and chip equipment now sits at the 99th percentile, the most crowded short in US equities. Roughly $3 billion left semiconductor ETFs in a week while $9.1 billion went into $QQQ, so the money left chips without leaving tech. Funds have rebuilt AI exposure everywhere except memory, the one corner Morgan Stanley shows with no ownership recovery since the unwind. The honest counter to buying the most-shorted part of the market is that bubble-like behavior in the group is still there on Bank of America's read, and the same desk reiterates Buy on $AMAT with a $720 target into Thursday's print. Long $AMAT into that print. A soft guide with memory still unowned ends it.

### The other 493 are carrying the quarter

*Profit growth has broadened past the AI complex, and the second half belongs to the parts of the market that have not run yet.*

76% of S&P 500 companies beat, matching the best rate since 2021, with earnings tracking 30% growth once Alphabet and Amazon investment mark-ups are stripped out (Bank of America). The median AI-linked stock grew earnings 28% against 12% for everyone else, a real gap that has stopped widening, and Mag7 profit growth excluding one-offs has slipped below the rest of the index for the first time since 2022 (JPMorgan). Buying has already spread into biotech, housing and financials. The tell is that beats are not being paid, with companies that beat on both earnings and sales outperforming by 0.8 percentage points, about half the historical average. Does the rest of the index keep delivering as growth decelerates, or does the bid run straight back to the seven names it came from? Long $XLI says the first.

### The labor market is cooling, not cracking

*July's payroll drop is a hiring slowdown rather than the front edge of a recession, which keeps the Fed's attention on prices instead of jobs.*

Payrolls fell 23,000 in July while private payrolls still grew 30,000, which is a stalled hiring rate rather than a wave of layoffs. Unemployment at 4.1% is a 13-month low, but the labor force shrank 264,000 in the same month, so the improvement is arithmetic and not hiring. Temporary help employment, which turns before the broader payroll number does, is close to growing again for the first time since the pandemic. That mix gives the Fed no cover to cut and no reason to panic, which leaves tomorrow's inflation number doing all the work. If core comes in at or above the 0.30% JPMorgan flags as its damage threshold, a 30-year Treasury yield already at 5.24% keeps climbing instead of mean-reverting. Short $TLT, with Thursday's 30-year auction the near-term risk to it.

### Sentiment is at 2021 levels and the fast money is already hedging

*Positioning has finally caught up to the rally, and the funds that chased it are the ones now buying protection.*

Bank of America's Bull and Bear indicator hit 9.7, the highest since the early-2021 meme-stock peak, with large-cap positioning in the 87th percentile and mega-cap tech in the 95th. Buying the winners and selling the losers has stopped working, with that trade's six-month return now negative and its 200-day trend rolling over, the same shape as the late-2021 top that failed. The other side of the argument is JPMorgan's raised 2026 S&P 500 target of 8,000, built on positioning being near historical norms. The cost of being wrong here is gold and staples lagging a September melt-up. The cost of missing it is full exposure to the most crowded market since 2021 going into an inflation print. Long $GLD with $XLP alongside, sized small, because gold is running into resistance at $4,400 to $4,500 an ounce. Equal-weight $RSP is not the vehicle, since it is already overbought and prone to air pockets after a momentum unwind.
