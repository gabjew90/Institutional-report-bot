## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex stays structural, but the leveraged-flow unwind is the new transmission

*The AI infrastructure cycle is not what cracked Monday. The way the market trades it is.*

- Goldman expects AI to add 3.3 percentage points to US capex growth in 2026 and flags hyperscaler advance orders worth roughly $2 trillion in compute resources as the demand floor under the chip and power names.
- Morgan Stanley forecasts hyperscaler capex of $1 trillion by 2027 and pencils aggregate memory market revenue of $890B in 2026, with PC memory short by 15% and smartphone memory short by 12% in 2027 because DRAM wafer capacity (the production line for the memory chips inside every device) is only expanding 30% by then.
- UBS keeps $TSM 2026 capex at $56B and reads $NVDA's $124B purchase commitment as visibility that the order book is not just one buyer. Raymond James adds that earnings revisions are still moving with AI capex spend.
- Levered-equity ETF AUM is near $300B (with cross-asset exposure controlling roughly $750B in equities) and over $100B sits in levered tech-ETF AUM specifically. The Market Ear's $SPY futures 7,375 zone is the must-hold for the systematic complex; below that the next gravity is 7,200 (the 50-day).
- Bank of America warns that risk swung from "despair to exuberance" inside two months, while the underlying earnings growth is still being lifted by the capex boom.

When leveraged and 0DTE flows (zero-day-to-expiry options that force dealers to hedge every tick) control short-term price formation, the structural earnings story keeps validating the bull case while day-to-day price action gets set by short gamma in levered ETFs that buy strength and sell weakness. Friday's payrolls forced a vol re-rate, and the levered complex was a one-way seller into it. Academy Securities and BNP Paribas push back that this is the first crack in a positioning bubble where retail 0DTE gambling and inverse-ETF rebalancing wrote the rally and will write the unwind, with The Market Ear's 7,375 $SPY and 29,000 $QQQ Fibonacci levels as the explicit triggers. The picks-and-shovels side has not flinched: TSMC 2026 capex unchanged, memory shortfalls intact, $NVDA orders not cancelled, $MU memory cycle still structurally tight. Long $SMH and long $MU into the next print, with $SPY put spreads as the tail hedge against another levered-ETF flush.

### Hormuz is open, energy is the global inflation transmission

*Banks treat Hormuz transit as functioning but expensive, with Iran-conflict energy inflation already showing up in May macro data.*

- The EU sanctioned Iranian officials over Hormuz naval restrictions overnight, and Iran's envoy to Moscow said the strait will stay open with new transit fees added. Israeli strikes on western and central Iran continued through Sunday.
- Crédit Agricole CIB pegs Energy CPI YoY at 24.3% peaking in May 2026. Bank of America has energy goods inflation contributing 0.9 percentage points to headline CPI on a 29.2% YoY print, and Wells Fargo flags May energy goods at +8% month-over-month as the primary driver of headline CPI.
- ANZ Research reads headline CPI at +0.5% month-over-month in May driven by high energy prices. Lloyds, SEB, and UniCredit all carry the Iran-war-as-inflation-shock through their May macro work.
- BlackRock, Deutsche Bank, and JPMorgan treat the Middle East theme as live but refuse to take a directional oil call from here, with the wires noting "oil market calm masks a host of unknowns" this morning.
- Goldman Sachs is the one bank with a stand-alone view that actual Hormuz normalization would lift risk markets.

Transit fees are a steady-state tax on every barrel moving through Hormuz, which keeps refined-product margins (the spread between crude cost and gasoline/diesel selling price) fat and feeds into headline inflation on a roughly three-month lag, exactly as the May numbers are showing. Spot is pricing the strait staying functional, but the trade is in the floor under energy prices and the inflation pass-through, not in oil-spike scenarios that depend on a closure nobody is forecasting. Goldman's normalization-lifts-risk read would clip energy and bid duration if Hormuz fully unwinds, but the corpus answers that even normalization-at-fees keeps energy CPI elevated for months. The May print is the receipts. Long $XLE into the next Iran headline, paired with long $BNO over $USO if the Brent geopolitical premium reasserts.

### The Fed is off the cuts script, and one desk now sees a hike

*Friday's payrolls killed the last 2026 cut, and the curve is starting to price something the Fed has not endorsed.*

- Bank of America explicitly removed the final 2026 cut after the jobs print, with the desk's terminal range at 3.00-3.25% by end-cycle and an indefinite hold near neutral (the rate that neither stimulates nor restrains the economy) as the new baseline.
- UBS, Raymond James, ANZ Research, and Deutsche Bank converge on a wait-and-see Fed with the easing bias coming out of the June statement. Deutsche Bank notes markets are now fully pricing a Fed rate hike by December.
- Crédit Agricole CIB pushes back that the bar for a hike is higher than the curve thinks, with the base case an extended hold and Fed Funds upper bound at 3.75% through year-end. Natixis lines up: hike is a tail outcome, not a base case.
- The Fed blackout period started June 6 ahead of the FOMC, so no Fed-speaker volatility this week. Wednesday's CPI prints into a vacuum of policymaker color.
- The discovered Kevin Warsh-as-next-Fed-chair cluster (ANZ Research, Berenberg, Deutsche Bank, Nordea) is a slow-burn risk to the 2027 path that is not in the current curve.

A 172k payroll print against an ~85k consensus, energy-driven CPI feeding through, and a Fed that just left the easing bias on the table is the setup for a curve that flattens at the front and sells the long end (short rates anchored, long rates rise on inflation). Crédit Agricole and Natixis push back that the bar for an actual hike is much higher than the Deutsche Bank pricing implies, and the next data point determines whether the front-end pricing is sustainable. The corpus answers that the labor data plus the energy pass-through is exactly what stops the Fed from cutting, and Wednesday's CPI is the next test. Long $TLT puts into CPI Wednesday, taking the position off if the print comes in cool on core month-over-month.

### ECB delivers Thursday with the consensus lined up at 25bp

*Banks are aligned on a 25 basis point ECB deposit-rate hike to address energy-driven inflation pressure.*

- Goldman Sachs expects the Governing Council to raise the deposit rate by 25 basis points (0.25 percentage points) at the June 11 meeting, with a final raise in September bringing the deposit rate to 2.50%.
- UniCredit, SEB, Scotiabank, Natixis, Mizuho International, and Morgan Stanley all carry the same 25bp June 11 hike call. UniCredit names 2.50% by September as the terminal rate.
- Deutsche Bank pegs the post-hike deposit rate at 2.25% and signals the ECB will lean on Iran-war energy pass-through to justify the move.
- Lloyds, JPMorgan, and Mizuho keep the consensus. No skeptical bank in the corpus is pushing back on the size or timing.
- The Bank of England read on the wires this morning, that Taylor sees rates on hold barring worst-case scenario, is the policy contrast: Fed and ECB tighten or hold tight while the BOE pauses, which is why $UUP caught a bid Monday.

The ECB hike is the cleanest policy expression of the Iran-war energy story playing out in the May macro data. One quarter-point clip plus a September follow-on is already priced, and the euro should fade into the second hike, consistent with $UUP's Monday move. The risk is that "priced for lift-off" is a high bar to actually print, and a dovish surprise from Lagarde at the presser would unwind cross-asset positioning very fast. The corpus has no bear case worth surfacing here, which itself is a sign of crowded positioning. Long $UUP into the meeting, with profit-taking on a dovish-side surprise.

### The rotation trade: out of the lead, into what nobody owns

*Three desks are explicitly calling the AI/momentum unwind and naming where to rotate into, not just out of.*

- Bank of America's Global Economic Weekly ("AI, Iran war and K-shaped economy") ties the AI top to the same factors that argue for rotation into defensive cash-flow and dividend names.
- UBS House View frames the consensus as crowded long-tech and short-defensives, with the explicit recommendation to take down the active overweight in MAG7 (Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Nvidia, Tesla).
- World Gold Council May ETF commentary cuts the other way: gold ETF outflows say the inflation-hedge consensus is leaning the same direction as everyone else, and the rotation into hard-asset reflation has not started.
- Goldman desk color flagged Monday as the day "leverage" and "momentum" both moved in reverse, which is the day-after technical confirmation of the rotation thesis.
- The leveraged-speculation theme (10 banks) and equity-rotation theme (22 banks supporting US economic resilience) cluster around the same pivot: the trade after the AI top is not "short AI," it is "long what AI crowded out."

When leveraged ETF flows and 0DTE positioning have been the marginal buyer of the lead theme, the rotation does not look like a bear market in the index. It looks like a sharp underperformance of the names the systematic and retail complex were forced to own. The pushback is that the corpus is still mostly long the AI capex theme, which means the rotation is a tactical play and not a regime change. The corpus answers that Monday's $SPY -2.58% with $QQQ -4.80% is the first session this cycle where the rotation expressed through relative performance rather than just relative positioning. Long $XLV paired against a half-sized short $XLK, with $VYM as the income complement if the unwind extends.
