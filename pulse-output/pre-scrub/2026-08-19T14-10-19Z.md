# Chips crack, gold rips

## 1. RECAP

Gold is doing the talking half an hour into Wednesday's session. $GLD is up 2.80%, the largest move on the board, with the dollar softer and 30-year Treasury yields down 8 basis points (one basis point is a hundredth of a percent) to 5.20% after Tuesday's tech selloff sent money looking for somewhere else to sit. Equities are split rather than broken, with $SPY at +0.31% while $QQQ is -0.37% and the semiconductor damage from Tuesday still working through. The US-Iran memorandum lapsed overnight, tanker traffic through Hormuz is still slowing, and oil is sitting on a three-week high without adding much this morning. $BTC is near $65,300. Fund manager cash is at a multi-decade low, which describes a market that is fully invested and not remotely convinced.

- **Gold ran and the dollar gave way.** $GLD +2.80% against $UUP -0.71%, the widest pair in the snapshot. Central banks buying into a bond market short of natural buyers is the argument behind it, and it is BofA's highest-conviction call this week.
- **Long-dated Treasuries rallied on soft US data.** The 30-year yield fell 8.3 basis points to 5.20% and the 10-year fell 5.3 to 4.65%, with $TLT +1.56%. That is the first real pullback from the multi-decade highs long bonds set this month, and it came with Iran risk still live rather than in spite of it.
- **Target reported this morning at $2.46 a share on $26.54 billion of revenue, after Home Depot's $4.92 on $47.86 billion Tuesday morning.** No consensus figures were posted for either. Retail is the direct test of whether household spending is slowing the way the no-hike case needs it to, and Walmart Thursday before the open is the number that settles it.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI trade is changing hands

*Money is rotating out of semiconductors into the memory, storage and power the buildout consumes, and Tuesday's selling is that rotation rather than the end of the theme.*

- Goldman's basket that owns recent winners against recent losers fell 8% Tuesday, one of its ten largest daily losses since ChatGPT launched. The S&P 500 is barely 1% off its high, so this is one crowded trade unwinding rather than a market-wide break.
- Semiconductors ($SMH) fell 6.5% Tuesday while software rose 1% (Goldman). Money switched legs inside AI instead of leaving it.
- Second-quarter fund filings show large holders trimming $NVDA and $AVGO while putting fresh money into memory, storage and the power complex. The institutions selling the chips are buying what the chips plug into.
- Long global semiconductors was the most crowded position for 53% of fund managers in August, down from a record 82% in July (BofA). Most of the crowd left before this drawdown, which is why it has not turned into a general flush. Goldman's hedge fund clients now run total exposure at 203.5% of their capital, less borrowed up than 96% of the past year.

The buildout is being financed rather than earned, and that changes who collects. Nine large technology companies have signed $3 trillion of AI infrastructure commitments that do not show up as debt on their books, about five times their annual capital spending (Goldman), which pushes returns toward whoever owns the compute and the electricity and away from whoever wins the model race.

JPMorgan reads the same selling as noise, with client positioning neutral at the 40th percentile and its desk buying dips. The trigger it names, Anthropic reporting $65B of annualized revenue against implied expectations of $75-80B, is a sentiment miss rather than a spending cut, and SK Hynix answering with a 40 trillion won buyback is not what a company watching demand roll over does. What that reading misses sits beneath the index. Individual stocks have stopped moving together at one of the fastest rates since 2020 and 2022 (Bloomberg), and that is what let one sector break while the index held. The semiconductor index has already turned back at its 50-day average and lost short-term support, with 10,400 the next shelf below. Nvidia reports after the close on Wednesday, August 26, and that print is what separates a rotation inside AI from the start of an exit.

### Forty trillion dollars of debt and no natural buyer

US national debt passes $40 trillion within days, and servicing it cost $1.4 trillion over the past year, on pace to overtake Social Security as the government's largest single outlay (BofA). AI, hyperscaler and data-centre bond sales pile on at $269 billion so far this year, twelve times the 2015-2024 annual average. The buyer side is thinning at the same time, with JPMorgan cutting its 2026 foreign demand forecast for Treasuries to $450 billion from $500 billion and pulling its Bank of Japan rate-hike call forward to September, which keeps Japanese savings at home. BofA is short bonds outright and does not see 5-year yields breaking 3.25% without a recession. UBS takes the other side, wanting another 100 to 230 basis points of yield on 2- and 5-year Treasuries before the income they pay covers the price risk. This morning is what being short costs: the 30-year fell to 5.20%, back under the 5.30% JPMorgan already had reached, and 5.50% is the zone it names next.

### Gold is the one thing both sides of the bond argument want

Gold is BofA's top call this week, framed as the hedge against a falling dollar and a bond market short of buyers, and it is working today with $GLD +2.80% and the dollar down 0.71%. Goldman carries a $4,900 an ounce forecast for year-end 2026 anchored on central bank buying, and its trading desk holds gold alongside the S&P 500. UBS steps its targets up to $4,600 by December and calls pullbacks toward $4,000 a place to add. The buying is physical and measurable, with the PBoC adding 20 tons in July, its largest month since October 2023. UBS also supplies the way this breaks, putting gold at $3,850 if the Fed hikes this year, which makes the September 16 FOMC the direct threat to the position and this afternoon's minutes the first read on it.

### Goldman is arguing with itself about September

Jan Hatzius has written the September hike down to near-zero, pointing at underlying job growth of 5,000 in July against the roughly 50,000 needed to hold unemployment steady, an unemployment rate that fell to 4.1% only because people left the workforce, and core PCE, the inflation measure the Fed watches with food and energy stripped out, up 0.13% in June. He counts 4 to 5 of the 12 voters supporting a hike against 9 of 18 participants who pencilled one into the June projections. Goldman's own trading desk says the opposite, that 6-7% deficits and relentless new bond supply make this a supply problem rather than a policy-discipline problem, and the Fed may be forced to hike into weaker data to steady the 30-year. The market splits the difference near 30% to 35% for September 16, while 72% of fund managers do not expect a hike before the November midterms (BofA). Today's 2:00 PM minutes settle which argument has institutional backing: if the committee is not treating long-dated borrowing costs as a constraint on policy, the forced-hike case has nothing behind it and September goes back to being a labour-market question.

### Oil is priced for a strait that has not reopened

The US-Iran memorandum expired with no move to extend it, Hormuz traffic is still slowing, and Brent held $91.71 overnight against WTI at $85.89. JPMorgan has cut the probability that the strait normalises by year-end to 36% from 50% at the start of August. The physical market is already routing around the problem, with Saudi loadings at Egypt's Sidi Kerir terminal hitting a record 2.17 million barrels a day while flows from Yanbu through Bab el-Mandeb fell 90% in early August (MUFG). Goldman's commodity strategists name the stake, warning that global oil inventories reach record lows if the strait stays shut. Inventories are what turn a shipping problem into a price problem, so weekly stock draws decide this, not the next headline. Tanker traffic resuming through Hormuz is what breaks it.
