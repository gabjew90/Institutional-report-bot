# Hormuz unravels

## 1. RECAP

Heading into Thursday's open, the peace-deal optimism propping up an 8-week S&P rally is fraying. US and Iran traded fresh airstrikes overnight, Trump publicly dismissed the leaked Hormuz framework, and oil futures are bid this morning after rolling lower into Tuesday. Yesterday's session left $SPY essentially flat (-0.02%) and $QQQ down -0.11%, with $USO closing -4.36% and $BNO -3.69% on the peace-deal hopes that have since cracked. $BTC is trading $73.5k this morning (-1.28% overnight), $ETH -1.69%, $SOL -1.89%, all giving back risk in sympathy with the geopolitical re-pricing. The Goldman flow desk had already flagged hedge-fund gross leverage at a 3-year extreme heading into this, with dealer hedging cushions running thin (dealers are on the hook to buy more the higher the market goes) and downside hedging at the 4th percentile. That's exactly the positioning setup that breaks badly when overnight geopolitical risk shows up.

- **US-Iran ceasefire unraveling overnight.** US carried out fresh strikes near Bandar Abbas, IRGC retaliated against a US airbase, Kuwait intercepted hostile missiles and drones, and Trump publicly dismissed the leaked Iranian-drafted Hormuz framework. Oil rose on the headlines and yesterday's -4.36% in $USO and -3.69% in $BNO is now being unwound in this morning's futures action. Gold sits at a two-month low ($GLD -1.33% yesterday) as the dollar bid took precedence over the safe-haven trade.
- **Core PCE 0.2% vs 0.3% est (8:30 AM ET).** A cooler-than-expected inflation print on the Fed's preferred gauge, with headline PCE MoM 0.4% vs 0.5% est. Core PCE YoY held at 3.3% in line. On its own this is a no-cut path easing message that should bid $TLT, but the overnight move in oil prices and inflation-fear flow into gold is dominating the rates response.
- **Q2 GDP 2nd estimate 1.6% vs 2.0% est (8:30 AM ET).** Growth revised below consensus and below the prior 0.5% advance only modestly, the GDP price index came in cooler at 3.5% vs 4.5% est. The mix is soft-growth, soft-prices, which the bond market would normally read as bid for long-dated Treasuries (the longer the maturity, the bigger the price move when yields shift). The offset is the oil-driven inflation risk now driving near-term yields more than anything else.
- **Hedge-fund positioning at 3-year extreme.** Gross leverage rose +5.5 points week-on-week (largest one-week increase in over three years), Info Tech gross/net at 5-year highs, semis at a record 10% of HF portfolio weight. S&P call pricing sits at the 96th percentile versus put pricing at the 4th, meaning traders are paying up for upside and almost nothing for downside protection. With $VIXY -2.07% yesterday and the US Vol Panic Index compressed to 2.6 from 9.5-10 in March-April, this is the most one-way book the desks have run into a geopolitical shock in this cycle.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI capex super-cycle keeps getting bigger

*Hyperscaler capital plans are still being revised up, not down, and the desks treating this as already-priced are getting it wrong.*

- Morgan Stanley raised 2026 US hyperscaler capex to $805bn from $433bn a year ago, with $1.3 trillion pencilled by 2028. That's the chunkiest revision of any forecast line on the buy side this cycle.
- Morgan Stanley also lifted 2026 S&P 500 earnings growth to +23% from +17% and 2026 business fixed investment to +7% from +3%, citing AI demand described as insensitive to higher financing costs and energy prices.
- Goldman calls the AI capex theme consistently underestimated in magnitude, with the GS AI basket +38% year-to-date versus +4% for the S&P 500 ex-AI cohort.
- ByteDance 2026 AI capex is pencilled at $70bn on the China leg (Barclays), and Vertiv liquid cooling shows up as critical infrastructure plumbing the build-out depends on.
- JPMorgan's EM team raised Korea-led emerging-market targets to a 2000 base case, with 2026 EM earnings revisions up 55% year-over-year on Korean IT growth of 203%.

The unifying logic is the inelastic-demand read Morgan Stanley keeps coming back to. Companies are treating AI infrastructure as strategic necessity rather than discretionary capex, so the channel where high financing costs and high energy prices crimp investment isn't operating. The picks-and-shovels read runs through power, cooling, and memory: Vertiv on the cooling side, memory broadly, and the cloud-security guides ($CRWD, NET) that are raising capex on memory cost pressure. Bulls point to the breadth of the build-out as evidence this isn't one hyperscaler's story to break: Snowflake's $6bn Amazon deal drove the stock +37% post-earnings, Marvell flagged a $10bn custom-silicon opportunity by 2028. The risk Goldman's own desk surfaces is token-cost compression. DeepSeek cut token pricing 75%, Xiaomi's MiMo 99%, and an 8-billion-parameter Qwen model now runs locally on a four-year-old laptop off a 5GB download. If inference economics collapse fast enough, the demand-expansion logic for the hyperscaler build cracks, and Bloomberg's separate read flags the AI-momentum trade as the best run on record with the usual warning that record crowding precedes regime change. The play here is to lean into infrastructure ahead of application-layer mega-caps. Long $VRT and $MU into the next set of hyperscaler capex prints.

### Hormuz peace deal is fraying and oil isn't priced for it

*Three months of "the deal is close" has the oil curve priced for resolution, and a weekend of strikes can reprice that back hard.*

- Goldman flat-out says oil markets are underpricing the risk of a prolonged Strait of Hormuz disruption. Spot prices baked in an MOU that hasn't materialized.
- Prediction markets are pricing 61% odds of Hormuz traffic normalization by end of July (Goldman), the level that breaks if re-escalation forces a violent re-pricing.
- The leaked draft framework had a 60-day ceasefire and Hormuz reopening, but pointedly excluded Iran's missile program and regional proxies (Rabobank). That's exactly the gap that blew up overnight as US strikes hit a site near Bandar Abbas and IRGC retaliated.
- JPMorgan's morning desk reads cross-asset sentiment as stagflationary following the strikes, with dollar bid and gold initially lower into PCE before the inflation-fear bid kicked in.
- Deutsche Bank surfaces the structural mechanism: near-term US bond yields are driven primarily by oil prices right now, with the correlation tighter than usual since the war started.

Three months of "MOU coming" compressed implied vol in oil markets and let prediction markets drift to 61% normalization odds. The reality overnight is messier. Trump publicly dismissed the leaked framework, US forces hit a military site near Bandar Abbas, IRGC said it targeted a US airbase, and Kuwait reported intercepting hostile missiles and drone attacks. Refined-fuel inventories were already tight before the strikes. Rabobank's note explicitly flags that oil below $100 amid this escalation looks like markets clutching at straws. The bull case for staying short oil is that prior cycles of escalation have de-escalated within days, and Iran has a structural incentive to preserve the leverage of Hormuz access rather than blow it up entirely. The opposite read, the one that catches a short-vol desk flat-footed, is that the leaked deal explicitly excluded Iran's missile program, the one item this regime can't trade away, so the framework was never going to land. With $USO -4.36% and $BNO -3.69% yesterday into the optimism, the asymmetry now sits with longs on any re-escalation. Long $BNO calls into the next Hormuz headline.

### Hedge-fund leverage at a 3-year extreme and dealers are losing the cushion

*The same desks calling the rally are now warning positioning has eaten through the room for any downside surprise.*

- Goldman's flow desk flags hedge-fund gross leverage +5.5 points week-on-week, the largest one-week increase in over three years. Info Tech gross/net exposures sit at 5-year highs, semis at a record 10% of hedge-fund portfolio weight.
- S&P call pricing sits at the 96th percentile (extreme upside demand) versus put pricing at the 4th percentile (minimal downside hedging), with hedge funds and mutual funds both rotating aggressively out of Software and into Semis.
- Bloomberg notes speculators are net short across $SPY, $QQQ, $IWM and $DJI futures and only marginally long S&P 400 mid-caps, the asymmetric futures-shorts vs cash-longs setup that fuels squeeze mechanics on dips.
- Eight consecutive weeks of S&P gains has compressed the US Vol Panic Index to 2.6 from 9.5-10 in March-April. $NVDA is testing the lower end of its trend channel and below the 21-day, with the SOX index reversing from the upper end of its trend channel.
- JPMorgan's US Tactical Positioning Monitor 1-week change has already turned -1.5z, the first negative inflection in two months.

The mechanism is the dealer hedging cushion losing thickness. Eight weeks of grind-higher with extreme call demand has left dealers in a position where they sell strength and buy weakness, which stabilizes today's price action on the way up. But when dealer positioning flips (which the compression in call demand and collapse in put demand are setting up), the same dealers turn into a destabilizer that buys strength and sells weakness. Bulls argue the month-end mechanical flows keep the bid intact in the near term, with the $303bn MSCI rebalance trade ($11bn $NVDA sells against $3.1bn $AAPL and $2.9bn $MSFT buys) clearing today and tomorrow. The pushback from the same Goldman desk is that positioning risk hasn't dropped even as macro fear has collapsed, and now there's an overnight Iran shock plus a 3-year-high in hedge-fund gross leverage plus collapsed downside hedging. That's the exact setup where a single bad print or geopolitical re-pricing creates an air pocket. Hedging is also cheap by historical standards, with the volatility terms structure compressed to multi-month lows. Long $VIXY calls and long-dated $SPY puts as a low-cost tail hedge into the next 1-2 weeks of headline risk.
