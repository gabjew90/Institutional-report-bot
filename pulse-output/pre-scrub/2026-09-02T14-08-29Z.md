# Hike odds go vertical

## 1. RECAP

Markets are trading without conviction into a Fed meeting that has turned into a live risk. Half an hour into the session $SPY is up 0.10% and $QQQ is down 0.27%, with the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.79% after Tuesday took it to its highest level since January 2025 on renewed Middle East tension. $BTC is holding the $77k area. The split that matters is not in prices, it is in who owns them. Hedge funds are carrying less stock exposure than at almost any point in the past year while systematic trend-following funds are carrying the most, so the same market reads as under-owned or over-owned depending on which group you measure.

- **Dell and Palo Alto Networks both reported Tuesday after the close.** Dell earned $7.04 a share against the $5.01 consensus this pulse published, on $46.97bn of revenue against a $45.85bn bar, and the stock traded 8.4% higher after the print. Palo Alto beat on earnings at $1.02 against $0.99 and came up a shade short on revenue, $3.41bn against $3.42bn.
- **August ISM manufacturing missed on Tuesday.** The factory activity survey came in at 54.6 against 55.2 expected and 55.6 prior, and job openings landed at 7.271m against 7.313m. The prices-paid side of the same report, expected at 70.5 against 71.1 prior, has not shown up in desk commentary yet. Softer data did not dent the hike pricing, and the 10-year still closed at a multi-year high.
- **Gold is the one clean move this morning.** $GLD is up 1.17%, extending an August that gained about 10% and ranked as its best month in decades. Gold rising while the market prices a rate hike is the tension worth watching, because higher rates are normally what breaks it.
- **Crude is giving back part of Tuesday's escalation premium.** $USO is down 1.23% after Middle East headlines pushed Brent to $95.39 earlier in the week. The refined-fuel side is not following crude lower, which is the subject of the diesel theme below.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The September hike stopped being a tail risk

*The bond market has decided the Fed hikes on September 16, and long-dated government bonds are breaking in three countries at once.*

- Odds of a September hike have gone from 27% in mid-August to 69%, with Goldman Sachs at 66% and JPMorgan at 58%. A month ago this meeting was not a market event.
- The 10-year Treasury yield printed 4.80% on Tuesday, its highest since January 2025, and sits at 4.79% now. Every stock valuation being written this week is measured against a borrowing cost nobody modelled in June.
- Japan's 10-year yield went above 3% for the first time since 1996 and the UK 10-year hit 5.22%, a post-2008 high. Buyers for long-dated government debt are thinning everywhere at once, not just here.
- Funds that size their stock exposure off how calm the market is are at maximum allocation, the highest reading in their own history, and a falling market would force an estimated $163bn of mechanical selling out of that group (Bank of America). The buyers who pushed this market up have nothing left to add and a rule that makes them sell into weakness.

The pricing is not coming from the data, it is coming from what traders think this Fed does with the data. The data going into the meeting is softening rather than firming, with Tuesday's factory survey missing and job openings coming in light. Kevin Warsh's hawkish message, plus Governor Barr's stated support for further hikes if inflation does not moderate, has convinced the market that this Fed tightens into a slowdown rather than waiting, and oil holding higher keeps that stance locked in.

The hold case is being made by Goldman while Goldman's own screen shows 66% odds of a hike. Its economists expect the August core inflation reading to land soft enough to keep the Fed on hold, and the Dallas Fed's trimmed mean inflation measure, which throws out the biggest price moves in both directions, has run 2.35% over six months, close to where the Fed wants it. The problem is that those are arguments about what the Fed should do, and the bond market clears on supply either way. The stock of Treasury notes and bonds outstanding grew 6.8% over the past year while the Fed's own holdings of them grew 0.7%, so that debt gets absorbed at whatever yield buyers demand no matter which way Warsh votes, and core PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred measure of underlying price growth, is still running at 3.5% over six months. JPMorgan names the September 11 inflation print as the thing that settles the meeting, and Friday's payrolls report at 8:30 comes first, with the Street at 55,000 jobs and JPMorgan at 50,000. A second consecutive negative payroll number is the one outcome that takes the hike off the table.

### Dell made the enterprise AI case better than a chip maker could

*The strongest AI evidence this week came from a server vendor's profit margin, not from anything with a chip in it.*

Dell earned $7.04 a share against the $5.01 the Street wanted, more than tripling last year's number, and server and storage revenue grew 89%. The figure that decides the argument is the profit margin on that business, 15.0% against Goldman's 11.7% estimate, because the standing bear case on AI servers is that they are revenue without profit. Goldman reiterated Buy with a $425 target and lifted its FY27 AI server revenue outlook to $74bn from $60bn, and the stock traded 8.4% higher after the print. Demand is also broadening past the big cloud providers into ordinary companies and governments, with 2027 spending by cloud builders forecast at $1.2trn to $1.5trn against roughly $800bn this year. The risk is memory prices, where Goldman sees the increase slowing from here and the fear is that a slowdown marks the cycle peak. Broadcom reports after tonight's close against $3.30 a share on $29.95bn, and JPMorgan's desk warns the numbers can be strong and still not move the stock because investors are already leaning long into it.

### The earnings are real and the cash is gone

*AI is producing record profits and negative cash flow at the same time, and only one of those is in the price.*

The four largest cloud builders spent $165bn in the second quarter and their free cash flow, the money left over after the building is paid for, fell to $7bn from $60bn the quarter before. Bank of America counts $3.1trn of AI-linked debt sitting off company balance sheets, up $1.3trn in three months, and reads widening AI bond spreads as the early signal of the bubble it called in late 2025. Goldman's answer is that the money shows up one layer higher, in software, where the sector rallied 6.7% in a day on results from Salesforce, CrowdStrike and Okta, and Goldman's desk owns September upside calls on $IGV because almost nobody is positioned there. The hole in that answer is rates. Software gets marked down alongside the chips every time long-term yields rise, and they rose again on Tuesday, so the software leg needs the September meeting to go the hold camp's way to work cleanly.

### Goldman is arguing with itself about gold

*The flight out of paper money that made August gold's best month in decades is still intact, and the chart broke anyway.*

Gold gained about 10% in August and gold funds took in $7.3bn last week, the biggest week since October 2025 (Bank of America). Goldman's derivatives desk is long, buying upside and selling the downside protection that got expensive, on the view that the debasement story is not going anywhere. Goldman's materials desk is on the other side of its own house, pointing at a broken 100-day average price, a stretched move, Chinese selling and rising hike odds. The correction has run $400 from the peak and pulled the momentum reading from 73 down to 46, which clears the overheated condition without saying which way it resolves, and $GLD is up 1.17% this morning. The 200-day average at $4,221 is the line: lose it and the August run was positioning rather than a real move out of paper money, and the thesis is dead until the next policy shock.

### The diesel hole is a capacity problem, not a headline trade

*Refining capacity does not come back because a ceasefire gets announced, and that is what makes this the most durable energy call this week.*

Global refinery runs are down 7 million barrels a day from a year ago and US diesel inventories are 9% lower (Goldman Sachs). Goldman raised its fourth-quarter forecast for the US diesel margin, the profit a refiner earns turning crude into diesel, to $75 a barrel over Brent and its 2027 average to $63, up from the $27 it was forecasting in February. A revision that size is not a sentiment call, it is a capacity call, and it is why the trade survives a de-escalation headline that would take crude down. Crude is already going the other way this morning, with $USO down 1.23% as some of Tuesday's Middle East premium comes out after Brent traded at $95.39 on the escalation. Goldman's own stated condition is that a full recovery in refinery runs requires geopolitical de-escalation, and continued drone strikes on Russian refining capacity keep pushing that recovery further out.
