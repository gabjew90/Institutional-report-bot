## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hormuz holds, the supply scar lasts months

*The street is fading Hormuz headlines into the open, but the bigger problem is what's already locked in.*

- 10.5 million barrels per day of crude shut-in during April per the EIA (SEB Iran chartpack), up from 7.5 million in March. Peak shut-in could hit 10.8 million in May. That's already in the inflation pipeline.
- The EIA models the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed through May, with 2026 inventory draws of 2.6 million barrels per day (LSEG). The bull case for oil isn't fading.
- Brent sits near $99.7. SEB models a $150-200 Brent if the closure runs through June or July, against an EIA $106 average for May-June. The asymmetry skews hard one way.
- UBS pegs April headline CPI at 3.7% YoY (up from 3.3% in March) on the oil pass-through, and global oil demand revised down to +200k bpd from +600k. Demand destruction is starting.

Iran has shifted from full blockade to controlled-corridor mode, letting Chinese tankers through under IRGC supervision (this morning's Reuters headline), which the equity desk is fading as de-escalation. The EIA already locked in May at peak shut-in, refined-product inventories in Asia are thin enough that Japan and South Korea are pulling 39.7% more coal-fired generation, and US wheat is set for its lowest production since 1972 partly because fertilizer costs ran on the energy crisis. The peace-deal framing the market wants from this week's Beijing summit doesn't fix any of that on the timeline.

Bears will argue the Chinese-transit allowance is a meaningful pressure-release valve, and a Trump-Xi verbal commitment could compress the geopolitical premium another five to ten dollars per barrel. Grant that fully. The supply hole stays through summer driving season regardless of headlines, refined-fuel inventories do not refill in weeks, and a third consecutive monthly jump in US energy PPI says the pass-through is already in the system. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz flare, sized small for the headline whip.

### AI capex isn't slowing, Cisco just confirmed it

*The build-out got stronger this week, not weaker.*

- Cisco raised FY26 AI orders from north of $5B to $9B in one quarter, with product orders accelerating to +35% YoY (Goldman TMT). That's contracted revenue, not aspirational guidance.
- 2026 hyperscaler capex consensus sits at $755B, up 83% versus 2025 (Goldman). The largest single-year capital build in tech history.
- UBS sees 1Q26 cloud-provider revenue growth accelerating to roughly 45% from 34% in 4Q25, with aggregate order backlog supporting 45-50% growth into 2Q26.
- DDR4 8Gb spot prices ran +20% week-over-week into Samsung union-strike risk hitting May 21 (Goldman TMT). Memory tightness is back in the data.
- AI-linked software pricing is up 5% MoM and 14% YoY. UBS calls it out as a contributor to sticky core inflation, which means the build-out is now showing up in consumer price data.

The bear case has been that hyperscaler spending is speculative and earnings-fragile. Cisco lifting the order book by roughly $4B in one quarter is the rebuttal: this is contracted, multi-year revenue showing up at the network and silicon vendors. Nebius reported +684% revenue growth and upgraded contracted power to north of 4 gigawatts from 3. Even this week's CPI math has AI-linked software pricing contributing measurably to core inflation. That's monetization in the inflation data, not just spend in capex slides.

The fair counter Goldman desk has flagged is real: about one-third of MAG7 profits came from private-equity investment gains rather than AI revenue, leaving earnings more cyclically vulnerable than the headline number suggests. And the broad index now shows record 0DTE call volumes, dispersion (cross-stock vol gap) near 64%, with implied index correlation near 3% (Bloomberg), all top-of-cycle markers. Grant both. The picks-and-shovels names like $AMAT, $ASML, and memory ($MU) on the supply side are sold out into 2027 and don't depend on near-term momentum staying intact. Long $SMH, paired against keeping $SPY broadly hedged.

### Hidden leverage under the semis rally

*The Goldman desk is screaming tail hedge on semis, and the options market isn't pricing the gap.*

- Levered semiconductor ETFs now force roughly $2B of buying or selling per 1% move in the sector, having doubled in six to nine months (Goldman desk). That mechanical flow is the underappreciated risk.
- Aggregate long semiconductor exposure in those levered products is around $100B (Goldman). A flow time-bomb sitting on top of fundamentals.
- $SOXX has rallied 70% since the end of March (Goldman). The rubber band is fully stretched.
- 10-delta puts in $SMH and the AI-leaders basket (deep out-of-the-money puts, i.e. cheap insurance with a roughly 10% chance of paying out) look cheap relative to the embedded forced-selling profile of the levered ETF complex (Goldman desk).
- BTIG's base case is an "equal and opposite" $SOXX move back to the 50-day moving average, roughly 20% lower, on a momentum reversal trigger.

Levered ETFs rebalance daily. If $SOXX gaps lower in a session, the rebalancing forces accelerating selling at the close, which feeds back into spot. That's how a -3% session can become a -7% close. Underwriters of cheap 10-delta puts haven't priced the asymmetry because the spot-up-vol-up regime has trained the market that semis only go up.

Bulls would say the rally is fundamentally underwritten by Cisco, Nvidia, and Broadcom order books, not just flow. True. But mechanics and fundamentals operate on different timescales, and a multi-week gap-down on a single bad print can happen even if the multi-year story stays intact. BTIG's whiplash signature (NDX RSI, the relative-strength index, at 82, on the very-overbought end of the 0-100 scale, with the momentum index swinging +5.4% one day and -5% the next) is already visible in the same data. Long-dated tail puts on $SMH funded by selling near-dated upside calls, sized small because the unwind trigger is still unknown.

### The Fed isn't cutting into this PPI print

*The two-cut-by-year-end view is dead. The next argument is whether the next move is a hike.*

- April PPI surged +1.4% MoM versus +0.5% consensus, the sharpest topline rise since December 2022 (PNC).
- PPI YoY at 6.0%. Energy PPI +7.8% in April and +18.6% since February (PNC). The energy pass-through is broadening, not narrowing.
- Services PPI +1.2% MoM, the strongest monthly jump since March 2022 (PNC). Inflation is escaping the goods-only narrative.
- UBS revised the Fed cut forecast to December 2026 and March 2027, from September and December 2026. BofA's RIC: no Fed cuts in 2026, pushed to 2H 2027. PNC: zero further easing in 2026.
- Retail Sales Control Group printed +0.5% versus +0.4% estimate this morning, with headline Retail Sales in line at +0.5%. The consumer hasn't rolled over.

Three forces argue this print. Energy is still feeding through goods and services PPI, AI-linked software is contributing 14% YoY price acceleration, and the consumer is still spending. Three FOMC dissenters at the April meeting (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) already signaled the hawkish camp wasn't on board with easing. That camp now has a 1.4% PPI print to point at. The dominant market read is now attaching higher probability to a hike than a cut from here (ING).

The fair counter UBS would lead with: yields can still fall even with hotter inflation if growth softens. Unemployment ticked up to 4.34%, and a deeper labor signal could force the Fed's hand by Q4 regardless of inflation. True, but the labor data hasn't broken yet, and supply-side inflation isn't responding to Fed policy anyway. Short $TLT into the May 20 FOMC minutes, paired against long $UUP. The rate-differential support for the dollar widens as the Fed sits.
