## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hedge fund leverage at three-year fastest pace into AI

*Hedge funds doubled down on the AI trade right when the smart-money positioning data says the room is full.*

- Tech gross and net exposures hit the 100th percentile as a share of US prime book, a 5-year high (Goldman). Anyone who needed to be long AI already is.
- US long-short gross leverage jumped +5.5 points in a single week, the fastest re-leveraging pace in more than three years (Goldman). That's the unwind risk, not a fresh bull signal.
- Hedge fund momentum-factor tilt sits at the 90th percentile (Goldman). The same crowd is on the same side of the same trade.
- BofA flagged a +853bp hedge fund net tilt toward IT entering Q2 2026, the largest quarterly increase on record. Institutional buying stopped being marginal and turned into a stampede.
- 25% of S&P 100 stocks have inverted call skew (CBOE), meaning calls now cost more than puts in a quarter of large-caps, the same single-stock-gamma demand that defined 2021.

Three forces compound for the longs. Earnings revisions are accelerating, with Jefferies' revisions ratio at the 94th percentile and 34% of companies guiding higher. The AI capex print is real, Goldman's TMT desk has hyperscaler capex still rising into 2026, ASML is hiring 1,000 more engineers in Taiwan, and NAND revenue surged 83.7% QoQ to $38.9B (TrendForce), so the spend is visible in the supply chain. Buybacks are running at 1.6x last year's pace with $95.2B of new authorizations last week alone (Birinyi). The opposite read is positioning itself, the next marginal dollar coming into AI has to come from somebody not yet long, and the bucket is empty. Goldman flags AAII bull-bear negative on the same day prime-book tech is at a 5-year high, a not-loved rally where the sponsorship is professional, not retail. The 7th-percentile social-media sentiment reading (JPM) confirms consensus money is doing the buying, and consensus money runs for the door together. Long $NVDA and $MSFT outright, paired with long $QQQ puts where inverted index skew makes the downside leg the cheap part of the structure.

### Bond market still pricing hikes, JPM is calling the misframe

*The OIS curve is pricing a Fed hike, not a cut, and JPM is calling that misframe.*

- Market-implied OIS rate-hike probability rose to 75%, up from 62% the prior week (JPMorgan). The curve is moving the wrong way for risk assets, fast.
- US 12-month forward P/E sits at 21x, which JPM calls stretched against real yields. When long-term yields rise to compensate for inflation, the equity multiple has to compress.
- 5% on the US 10-year is JPM's pain threshold, historically the level that triggers economic and equity-valuation damage. We're below that line, but not by much.
- US Low-Vol basket is down 6% from February highs (JPMorgan). The bond-proxy equity bid has been unwinding alongside yields.
- Franklin Templeton has the 2-year Treasury yield at 4.10%, above fed funds, meaning the 2-year is leading the Fed, and it's leading higher, not lower.

Two stories run side by side. Western Asset has shifted its end-2026 Fed rate expectation to 3%, away from a deeper cutting cycle, so the cut path is being repriced in real time. At the same time the inflation backdrop is grinding up, Crédit Agricole has US core PCE at 3.3% YoY (up from 3.2%) and Eurozone core HICP at 2.6% (up from 2.2%). A Fed that can't cut into re-accelerating inflation has to let the front end drift, which is what the OIS curve is now showing. The honest pushback is that part of the OIS move is mechanical, repo and Treasury supply squeezing the front of the curve rather than pure rate-expectation repricing. Even granting that, the trade-relevant point is that equities at 21x forward earnings are priced for cuts the OIS market is no longer pricing. Thursday's Core PCE print is the next live test, a hot number extends the hike-pricing thesis, an in-line print gives the Low-Vol bid a window to reload. Long Low-Vol names ($SPLV) with a tactical short on high-beta cyclicals via $XLY.

### Hormuz overhang stays live, but the supply hole takes months to refill

*The Iran and Hormuz situation is a live two-way headline risk, and the physical oil market is operating with a 12.8m bpd hole regardless.*

- 12.8m bpd of global oil supply lost since February, 12% of pre-war supply (UBS). That number doesn't move on a US-Iran headline, it moves on physical resolution.
- 50-60% rise in WTI and Brent since hostilities erupted (Rothschild & Co). Price has already done the work, the question is whether the supply hole closes or widens from here.
- Brent currently around $97.9, down from $103.5 last Friday (SEB), a 50% retracement from the end-April peak (Crédit Agricole has Brent at $98 1st contract) suggesting headline risk is partly priced out.
- 67% of global crude moves by sea, 62% of that through key chokepoints (JPMorgan). Structural vulnerability is the spine of the JPM Port Authority piece.
- Goldman's scenario projection: SPX dips 3% if war ends AND AI enthusiasm dissipates simultaneously, linking the two tails in the same trade.

Six banks reference US-Iran diplomatic negotiations as a live catalyst (Barclays, Crédit Agricole, Goldman, JPM, SEB, UBS). None make a directional call. That's the signal, the corpus is unwilling to underwrite either side of the binary. JPM's framing has the most actionable specifics, a Hormuz Resolution trade in airlines, autos, homebuilders, lodging, retailers if a memo of understanding lands, and a continued bid in energy and defense if it doesn't. Rothschild & Co frames the 50-60% Brent rally as already in the price, which means the asymmetric setup runs the other way now, a ceasefire-driven Brent drop is more violent than a continued-conflict Brent grind. The case for staying long oil exposure is physical, refined fuel inventory tightness doesn't reset on a diplomatic headline. UBS estimates the Iranian tariff per tanker shipment at USD 2 million, an economic rounding error globally, meaning a partial resumption doesn't reset the price either, the supply hole has to close before the trade reverses. Rubio's overnight "has to be open one way or the other" line keeps both tails open. Long $XLE outright, paired with a small long $XLY tail as the ceasefire-scenario hedge.

### Software is the most-shorted, most-hated trade, setup for a squeeze

*Software shorts sit at a 6-year high while the group trades at a 39% valuation discount, the asymmetry is mathematical.*

- Software short interest at the 100th percentile relative to the past 6 years (Goldman). The room to cover is enormous.
- Software group valuation discount: 39% below the trailing 5-year average (Morgan Stanley), 29% cheap to history even adjusting for growth.
- Buyback execution flow +40% vs the prior week, with 1.6x year-to-date pace vs 2025 ADTV (Goldman). The corporate bid is back, and software names disproportionately use buybacks over dividends.
- Discretionary order share +40% (Goldman). Active money is the marginal buyer, not passive flow, so single-name selection matters more than index participation.
- The fundamentals haven't collapsed, Barclays raised $TENB to $28 (from $27), Elastic ($ESTC) is on a 15% revenue-growth path Q4, $BRZE has its 1Q27 print as the next data point.

The setup is classic mean-reversion-into-squeeze. The crowd has been on the wrong side of software for long enough that any catalyst, a guide-up at earnings, a software-specific M&A print like the Delivery Hero bid (€38/share from Uber, up from a rejected €33), forces covering. The smart counter is that software is cheap because the AI capex flowing through hyperscalers isn't landing in vertical SaaS, semis are taking the dollars, software keeps getting the funding cuts. Goldman's GSXULOWQ basket projects 20% downside in a higher-for-longer rates environment, and software low-quality sits in the heart of that basket. Stick to the profitable end, skip the unprofitable software bucket. Long $IGV as the clean short-cover expression, with single-name conviction in $CRM and $ADBE over speculative names.

### Gold trimmed at UBS, central-bank demand is the floor

*UBS just cut its gold forecast to USD 5,500 per ounce, and even with the cut, the bull case is intact because central banks are still buying.*

- USD 5,500/oz: UBS year-end 2026 and 1H27 gold price forecast, down USD 200-400/oz from previous. The trim is a fair-value reset, not a regime change.
- 536 metric tons: Q1 2026 investment demand (UBS), held up even with prices near record highs.
- 244 metric tons: Q1 2026 central bank purchases (UBS), surprised positively. Central banks are still net buyers.
- 15% India gold import duty (raised from 6%). Physical demand creates its own supply elasticity.
- Rothschild & Co flags 2026 AI capex at $0.5-1 trillion, hitting $1T in 2027. Gold's relevance as a non-USD reserve asset rises as the US runs a structural deficit to fund the buildout.

The marginal buyer of gold has changed. Investment demand softened recently, UBS notes that explicitly, but central-bank flows did not. Central-bank demand isn't interest-rate sensitive the way ETF demand is, it's reserve-policy driven. As the AI capex cycle forces persistent US fiscal expansion, and as US-Iran tensions push reserve diversification into the conversation, the central-bank bid is structural. The bear case is that gold has done a lot of work and the rate-cut narrative is pricing out, Western Asset's 3% end-2026 Fed rate vs prior deeper cuts means real yields rise and gold's negative correlation kicks in. That risk is priced in the UBS forecast cut itself. The structural floor is the central-bank flow. Long $GLD as a portfolio hedge, sized up if a Hormuz escalation accelerates de-dollarization conversations, sized down on a ceasefire memo.

### US gasoline pump prices, the consumer pressure point

*The 50-60% Brent rally hasn't fully passed through to the pump yet, and the consumer-credit backdrop makes the political pressure asymmetric.*

- Three desks flag US retail gasoline as both a consumer and a political pressure point heading into the summer driving season.
- UMich consumer sentiment sits at 44.8, the lowest reading in 74 years (ZeroHedge BNPL coverage), so any further pump-price pass-through hits an already-fragile consumer.
- BNPL usage and credit-card delinquency trends are deteriorating into the same window, compounding the political response function.
- The Hormuz Resolution path (JPM) sees the pass-through reverse if a ceasefire memo lands, with airlines, lodging, and retailers as the relief beneficiaries.
- A ceasefire breakdown sends pump prices higher into a stretched consumer, which raises the odds of fiscal response and an SPR draw.

The gasoline channel is the consumer-facing transmission belt for the Hormuz story. The macro link is direct, every 10c at the pump removes roughly $11B in annualized consumer spending, and at the current consumer-sentiment level that bites disposable income for $XLY tape. The pressure on the administration to act, either via SPR releases or by pushing harder for a diplomatic resolution, scales with where pump prices sit when the news cycle hits the summer driving season. The trade is the consumer-discretionary path, long $XLY into a ceasefire memo, with the energy hedge in $XLE running concurrently as the supply-side floor. Pair long $XLE with long $XLY into the next Hormuz headline cycle.
