## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The Fed pulled the cut and the market still doesn't believe it

*The June meeting was a real hawkish turn, but the bond market is betting the Fed blinks before it ever hikes, and that bet looks too confident.*

The June meeting deleted the easing bias and removed forward guidance entirely, with around nine of eighteen policymakers now projecting at least one hike this year (BofA, Goldman). That is the cut markets were leaning on a month ago, taken off the table. The split that follows is a genuine bank-versus-bank fight, not a consensus with a footnote: the hawkish camp (BofA, Goldman) reads a Fed that just watched nine members pencil in hikes as one that does not cut in the next two meetings, while a dovish bloc led by UBS still sees the policy rate "ultimately move lower" and pencils the 10-year Treasury yield (the benchmark US government borrowing rate) back to 4.00%. The tell is in the timing. The desks calling for lower rates put those cuts in 2027, not 2026 (ANZ doesn't get the funds rate to 3.0-3.25% until Q3 2027), so the near-term path everyone is actually describing is a Fed on hold staring at sticky inflation. That is the regime that punishes long-dated Treasuries priced for a soft landing. The dovish defense is that growth rolls over fast enough to force easing regardless, which makes the doves early, not wrong. Next Thursday's PCE print (the Fed's preferred inflation gauge) is the first test. Short $TLT, paired with long $UUP, into PCE, sized for thin pre-holiday liquidity.

### Warsh runs a quieter Fed and that alone adds volatility

*Cutting the Fed's words to the bone removes the cushion markets leaned on, so expect bigger reactions to every data point.*

The post-meeting statement was cut to roughly 130 words and forward guidance was effectively terminated, with questions about future policy deflected (Rabobank). When the Fed stops telling traders where rates are headed, the market has to price the uncertainty itself, and that means sharper moves around each inflation and jobs release. BofA reads a data-dependent, centrist Warsh as landing hawkish to anyone who expected dovishness, and Goldman frames it as an "impossible tightrope walk of neutrality." Either way the hand-holding is over, which mechanically widens the reaction function to the GDP and PCE prints stacked up next Thursday. For someone with US options exposure, this argues for owning volatility into that data rather than fading it. Long $VIXY into next week's data.

### AI capex keeps eating every spare dollar

*The data-center buildout is still the only place incremental money is going, and the spend numbers are getting bigger, not smaller.*

The capex forecasts moved up again: UBS now models $820B of AI-related spend in 2026 (up 68% year over year), rising to $990B in 2027, and pegs US hyperscaler spending at $1.8T across 2026-2027. Citadel Securities frames the same cycle as $0.75T this year scaling to $1.25T next. That breadth matters more than any single number, because two independent shops sizing the same multi-year ramp tells you this is the infrastructure stack getting bid, not one hyperscaler's pet project. On the single-name side, Goldman calls ASML's guide of 90 EUV machines (the lithography tools that print the most advanced chips) a floor with upside to 110-120. The loud caveat: the contrarian voice warns the final phase of a mania is often the most profitable and the most dangerous, and yesterday's chip-led rally shows how much of the tape now rides on this one trade staying intact. The question a trader has to answer is whether a hyperscaler capex guide-down shows up before the supply chain reprices higher, or whether the spend just keeps compounding. Micron's June 24 print is the next hard read on memory demand. Long $SOXX into the Micron print.

### Oil is pricing a truce the headlines keep delaying

*The market already faded the war premium, but a peace deal that keeps slipping leaves crude exposed to a snap-back the tape isn't braced for.*

Brent is heading for a roughly 9% weekly loss as traders price the US-Iran truce and the Strait of Hormuz reopening, with the strait carrying about 12.5 million barrels a day of oil shipments (JPMorgan) and SEB marking Brent near $77.90. The problem is the deal is fragile, and this morning made that concrete: the Friday Geneva talks were scrapped overnight. Scotiabank calls the truce "a pause, not peace, with more drama likely still to come," and JPMorgan's framing is that the US "waved the white flag," with a $300B reconstruction figure stipulated in the agreement. The asymmetry is in the physical market: inventory buffers are depleted (ANZ), so paper prices are falling on truce headlines while the actual barrel market is recovering from an already-thin starting point. A long-oil tilt risks another leg lower if the truce firms and Hormuz flows normalize fully. Missing it costs more if talks collapse again, because the supply premium reprices back into a market with no cushion to absorb it. Long $BNO calls for cheap upside into the next Hormuz headline.

### The rotation trade for when AI looks like a market top

*The same window that has banks raising AI capex also has desks warning the leadership is topping, and that rotate-out signal deserves its own seat.*

The skeptical bloc is small but explicit: BofA flags market-bubble risk, one desk warns of a forced-selling event if the crowded semiconductor trade unwinds, and others point to compute scarcity and a possible dollar breakout as signs the leadership is rolling over. None of this is a call to short the index outright, it's a call to stop paying up for the most concentrated exposure. If the worry is that a handful of names carry the whole market, the cleanest answer is to spread the bet into the equal-weight S&P over the cap-weighted index, with utilities as the defensive complement that pays a dividend while you wait. The honest counter is that every bubble call for the last year has been early, and the capex numbers above say the earnings are real. The reason to hold the rotation anyway is that you are not betting against AI, you are betting against paying a concentration premium for it. Long $RSP over $SPY, paired with long $XLU.
