## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI infrastructure capex floor keeps ratcheting higher

*Three Tier-1 desks raised their hyperscaler capex numbers in two weeks, and the AI capex floor is now the entire bull case for US equities.*

- Goldman raised 2026 hyperscaler capex to $754B, up 83% year-over-year, and now expects AI-infrastructure beneficiaries to account for roughly half of S&P 500 EPS growth this year
- Morgan Stanley's revised 2026 US hyperscaler capex sits at $805B (up from $433B a year ago) and projects $1.3T by 2028
- JPMorgan revised 2026 data center capex growth to +80% (up from +63%) and 2027 to +50% (up from +40%), with memory now ~30% of hyperscaler capex versus 8% in 2023-24
- Goldman lifted its S&P 500 year-end 2026 target to 8,000 from 7,600, with 2026 EPS pegged at $340 for +24% earnings growth
- Micron and SK Hynix both crossed $1T market cap, with $MU returning 859% over the last year

Capex of this magnitude going into AI infrastructure means what was a buyback budget two years ago is now a GPU and memory budget. The same set of names funding shareholder returns is funding the supply chain that $NVDA, $MU, $MRVL, and $TSM service, and Goldman's S&P 500 target reset to 8,000 is being underwritten by that revision rather than any consumer or rate story. Memory moving from 8% to 30% of hyperscaler spend is the line item that has rerated $MU specifically, and the demand curve behind that revision did not exist twelve months ago. Bulls argue the capex cycle is multi-year and the picks-and-shovels names get the most direct pull-through. The risk Goldman flags inside its own note is that hyperscaler depreciation is rising fast, enterprise productivity gains from AI are still early, and a single MAG7 capex guide-down would crack the basket hardest. Morgan Stanley names the invalidation directly, a significant guide-down in hyperscaler capex or a collapse in median Russell 3000 earnings growth ends the trade. Long $SOXX for basket exposure, with long $MU as the single-name memory tilt.

### Hormuz holds the binary while desks split

*Rabobank says the disruption lasts months while JPMorgan positions for an imminent MOU, and that gap is the trade.*

- Rabobank counts about 1,500 ships trapped in the Strait of Hormuz and projects Q3 2026 Brent averaging $120/bbl, meaning the base case is at least three more months of physical disruption regardless of headlines
- JPMorgan sees a potential US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding (a framework deal short of a full ceasefire) in coming days as the catalyst to broaden the equity rally beyond AI, with reopening trades framed as still working
- Goldman's Sales and Trading desk has Brent around $105/bbl in this morning's note, well below Rabobank's $120 base case
- Rabobank also cites the US 5Y-30Y curve widening to 84bp from a one-year low of 81bp, reading the bond market as starting to price oil-driven inflation persisting
- $USO -2.78% and $BNO -3.45% yesterday on MOU optimism, with refined-fuel inventories still tight per several desks

This is two banks looking at the same shipping data and the same diplomatic backchannel and reaching opposite conclusions about which side breaks first. Rabobank sees physical supply that takes months to refill and reads the curve steepening as the bond market agreeing with that view. JPMorgan reads negotiation cadence as suggesting a deal is close enough that the war premium fades into a relief rally, with $USO and $BNO compressing back toward pre-strike levels. The defense for Rabobank is mechanical, even a six-week ceasefire does not refill the physical supply hole because shipping reroutes and storage rebuilds take time. The bull case for fading the premium needs a clean MOU headline, not a Marco Rubio quote about negotiations ending the war in a few days. Long $BNO captures the supply-stays-tight side, invalidated by a full ceasefire announcement that holds more than a week.

### Hawkish Fed shift while real yields do the tightening

*ING calls it explicitly hawkish, Goldman cautions that the real-yield path is doing the same job the Fed used to do, and rate-sensitive equities sit in the crossfire.*

- ING reads the Fed as moving into a hawkish phase focused on removing its implicit easing bias, with fed funds futures pricing about 75% probability of a hike by year-end per JPMorgan and 70% per Rabobank
- Goldman measures the US 10-year real yield (the yield after subtracting expected inflation) at 2.1-2.2%, a level reached only a handful of times in history, and flags it as growth-negative
- 30-year Treasury yields touched 5%, the highest since 2007, while the 10Y sits at 4.49% after a 7.3bp drop yesterday per Deutsche Bank
- Goldman points out breakevens (the inflation rate the bond market is pricing in) are anchored near 2.4%, so the 25bp yield move has been real yields rising, not inflation re-accelerating
- The S&P 500 has nonetheless put together eight consecutive winning weeks per Goldman's Squeeze Risk note

Real yields rising without inflation rising tightens financial conditions through the discount-rate channel (higher real yields make future cash flows worth less today) without giving multiples any reflationary offset. Long-duration assets, long-dated bonds and long-dated growth equities, structurally underperform in that mix. Goldman's own caveat is that the market is currently reading the move as a growth upgrade backed by AI investment and a resilient labor market, which is why $SPY is at all-time highs while the 30-year sits at a 17-year yield high. The pushback ING flags is that breakevens above 2.35% (Goldman's upside-break level) would reframe the move as reflation and re-anchor risk assets. Until that happens, short $TLT into tomorrow's Core PCE, paired with long $UUP, with the trade breaking on a clean PCE undershoot that lets the curve bull-steepen.

### Melt-up positioning runs into a squeeze-risk setup

*Eight straight weeks of S&P gains, hedge funds at record semis weight, and macro shorts at a ten-year high, the setup goes both ways depending on which dam breaks first.*

- S&P call skew sits at the 96th percentile (very heavy demand for upside calls) with put skew at the 4th percentile (almost no demand for downside protection), and Goldman flags inverted call skew on 25% of S&P 100 names in line with 2021 meme-squeeze levels
- Macro short positioning at a ten-year high per Goldman, CTA long exposure to global equities at $90B, and S&P 500 buyback windows open on 99% of names, a full stack of upward-bias mechanics
- Hedge fund portfolio weight in semiconductors at a record 10% per Goldman, while semis monthly RSI (a momentum gauge, anything above 70 is overbought) sits at 87, the highest since the dot-com bubble
- KOSPI returning 12% over the past four sessions and +98% year-to-date per Deutsche Bank, a benchmark for how far the global tech bid has gone
- June FOMC forward implied volatility (option-market pricing of how much it expects rates to move) at 21% versus a 14% baseline, the highest premium on the curve, naming June FOMC as the binary event the market is pricing as the break

The two-sided setup is the trade's whole tension. If macro shorts cover into a benign Core PCE tomorrow plus a Hormuz MOU, the squeeze is mechanical because positioning is asymmetric and call skew is already inverted. If hedges stay thin into an inflation print that runs hot, the lack of downside protection turns a 2% pullback into a 6% one because there is no buyer of last resort. Bulls point to $NVDA's $20B standalone CPU revenue and persistent hedge fund tech buying as enough to absorb any short cover. The bear trigger named is a meaningful macro surprise or a momentum shift that forces volatility sellers to cover, unwinding the leverage stack. Long $VIXY call spreads into the June FOMC, paid for by selling rich $SPY upside calls.

### Consumer signals are not yet in the tape

*Walmart's biggest earnings-day drop in three years and gas prices up 42.7%, against a tape that has not registered the shift.*

- $WMT's earnings-day move was -7% per Goldman's Squeeze Risk note, the biggest in three years and the kind of bellwether single-name reaction that historically front-runs broader consumer weakness
- US gas prices up 42.7% in early May versus the year-ago period per JPMorgan, with total spending growth at 5.1% year-over-year (barely above April's 5.0%), so real spending growth is now barely positive
- US airfares up 20.7% year-over-year through April per Morgan Stanley, the inflation pieces the Fed cannot easily dismiss
- $GLD has seen $9.5B of three-month net outflows per JPMorgan, the largest since summer 2013, while crypto ETFs took $1.4B of weekly outflows, defensive positioning being unwound on both sides
- Morgan Stanley frames the disconnect as US economic inelasticity, demand staying resilient because tech and household balance sheets are strong

The signal implies rotation, not a tape break. $WMT's print and the gas-price wedge mean the bottom-half-by-income consumer is contributing less to the spend growth that gets aggregated into the headline number, so $XLY-style discretionary leadership becomes harder to sustain even as $XLP defensive names remain underowned. Morgan Stanley's pushback is that AI capex is so large it shows up in headline GDP and earnings before any consumer drag does, which is why the surface tape looks fine while the bellwether single-names crack. The defense for taking the consumer signal seriously is that gas at +42.7% and airfares at +20.7% are two months stale already and the data should worsen, not improve, in tomorrow's Core PCE release. Long $XLP, short $XLY captures the rotation, and a clean Core PCE print at 0.3% (matching consensus) would invalidate by giving the consumer one more month of nominal spending tailwind.
