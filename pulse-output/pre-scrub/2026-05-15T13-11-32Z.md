## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex revisions still ratcheting higher

*The AI infrastructure build has stopped being a forecast. The order books are confirming it in real time, and each cycle brings bigger numbers.*

- Goldman expects hyperscaler capex plus R&D to exceed $1 trillion in 2026E, with the run-rate compounding through the decade
- Cisco's F3Q26 AI hyperscale orders hit $1.9B, prompting Goldman to lift its F2026 AI hyperscale order guide to $9B from $5B and the 12-month price target to $116 from $75
- BofA raised its 2030 AI data-center systems market estimate to $1.7 trillion from $1.4 trillion
- Corporate capex grew 38% year-on-year in Q1 2026, with 80% of S&P returns this year tied to AI-driven names (Goldman)
- Applied Materials guided semi-equipment growth above 30% in calendar 2026, raised from a prior 20%-handle, the second cycle of revisions higher in a month

Cisco's order book is the kind of single-customer data point that closes the gap between "AI is a story" and "AI is a P&L." Goldman's lift to a $9B order guide (up from $5B in one cycle) and BofA's $300B move on the 2030 TAM are not noise revisions, they are the third consecutive round of upgrades, and the picks-and-shovels stack is now sold out into 2027. That sold-out condition is what gives this cycle a structural floor regardless of any single hyperscaler's next print. The risk Bloomberg and Nomura flag is that 40% of the index's leg higher since March has come from semis alone, and a reflexive flow reversal could take $SOXX down 15% in a session, with SOX at 62% above its 200-day moving average. The order data is fresh and trending the right way. The breadth case is a positioning observation that needs a catalyst to actually break the cycle, and NVDA on May 20 is that catalyst. Long $AMAT into next-cycle earnings, paired with a shorter-dated $SOXX put for the breadth-break tail.

### Hormuz disruption keeps building, oil floor stays high

*The Strait is still 95% blocked, gasoline inventories are draining at record pace, and the supply hole gets harder to refill each week the conflict drags.*

- Dated Brent is at $117/barrel (BCA), with the physical market pricing a scarcity premium that futures haven't fully absorbed
- Strait of Hormuz flows are 95% below normal on a 4-day moving average of vessel counts (Goldman Oil Tracker), with the total hit to Persian Gulf oil flows estimated at 15 mb/d
- Global oil inventories are drawing at 11-12 mb/d, a record pace offsetting 14.5 mb/d of production losses
- US gasoline inventories are 5% below the historical seasonal median and have been drawing at 0.7 mb/d since April, with US wholesale prices $21/bbl above Asia and Europe
- BCA pegs the daily cost of the Iran war to the US federal government at $400 million in munitions, maintenance, and equipment replacement

Overnight Trump and Araqchi did the opposite of de-escalating, and oil futures are up 2% on the headlines. ING and Mizuho are the desks making this an explicit directional call ("every day adds to the pressure"), with 19 other banks treating it as live context. The mechanism is refined-product tightness compounding even when crude futures soften on a peace headline, and the US gasoline market is where consumer inflation actually shows up. The opposite read is the Iran-for-Taiwan bargain Rabobank flagged, where Beijing helps de-escalate Hormuz in exchange for US restraint on Taiwan and the supply scare unwinds. Trump's overnight "aligned on Iran" line with Xi keeps that path open. Even if crude flushes $10 on a peace memo, the gasoline cracks stay wide for weeks because the inventory hole takes months to refill. Long $BNO outright, with Goldman's $85 Brent break as the explicit unwind trigger.

### The 30-year tells the Fed it can't cut

*Warsh inherits a long end that is repricing for sticky inflation, hot demand data, and a Treasury supply pipeline that has nothing to do with the front end of the curve.*

- The 30-year Treasury yield topped 5.1% Friday morning, the highest in nearly a year, with the move attributed to inflation signals muddying rate expectations under the new chair (CNBC)
- April headline CPI ran 3.8% year-on-year, the highest since May 2023, and April core CPI came in at 0.4% month-on-month, above consensus (BofA)
- Markets are now pricing roughly 50% odds of a 25-bp rate hike by year-end, a full repricing from cuts that were in the curve a month ago
- Yesterday's retail sales print landed +0.5% headline (in line) with Control Group +0.5% (vs +0.4% est) and Ex Autos +0.7% (vs +0.6% est), and YoY retail sales accelerated to +4.9%
- Goldman's Q2 GDP tracking estimate was nudged up 0.3pp to +1.9%, against initial jobless claims of 211k (+12k week-on-week, above expectations)

Three forces are pulling long-end yields higher at once. Oil-driven inflation feeds core CPI on a multi-month lag, the hot Retail Sales print strips the soft-landing optionality out of the Fed's hand, and the Treasury supply calendar lands on a new chair without a reservoir of credibility to absorb a tail. The repricing is not about the next FOMC meeting. It is about whether long-dated bonds can hold any premium when the curve has no anchor. The bull case for duration is that the move is overshooting, with terminal Fed funds still pinned at 3.0-3.25% per BofA, and a hawkish blow-off that fades as Hormuz resolves. The pushback is that even at terminal 3%, the 30-year does not clear at 5.1% unless the market is also pricing a fiscal premium, and a Warsh-led Fed cannot credibly defend long-end yields without re-opening the policy debate it was just confirmed to settle. May 20 FOMC Minutes are the first read on whether this Fed tolerates 5%+ long-end or signals a balance-sheet response. Short $TLT into the next 30-year auction window, paired with long $UUP if the rate-differential lift starts to push the dollar higher off the inflation re-acceleration.

### Trump-Xi summit puts Iran and Taiwan on one ledger

*The Beijing meeting is the cleanest cross-asset catalyst on the calendar because the Iran de-escalation path and the Taiwan posture are being bargained simultaneously.*

- Seven banks (Citi, Goldman, ING, JPMorgan, Rabobank, UBS, and others) flag the Trump-Xi meeting as a near-term macro catalyst with no consensus direction
- The administration announced an $11B US weapons package for Taiwan in December, the largest ever, and Trump has separately threatened 50% tariffs on countries supplying military weapons to Iran
- Overnight, Trump said the US and China are "aligned on Iran" and that "Iran must make a deal soon," followed by Iran's foreign ministry stating it has "no trust" in US intentions and Trump's "will not be much more patient" line (Reuters)
- China publicly told USTR Greer it wants the Strait of Hormuz open and free of curbs (Reuters)
- BRICS talks ended without a joint statement, exposing divisions on Iran across the bloc

For the first time in this cycle, two of the biggest macro overhangs (Hormuz supply and China policy stance on chips and Taiwan) are being decided in one room, and the cross-asset response hinges on which trade Beijing actually agrees to. The bull case from Rabobank is that Beijing helps de-escalate Hormuz in exchange for US restraint on Taiwan, which would crack oil meaningfully and reignite China-exposed semis and ADRs. The bear case is that Beijing extracts concessions on Taiwan while the Iran war keeps dragging, leaving oil bid and adding a chip export-control overhang to US semis. Rabobank's flagged invalidation is a formal public US statement reaffirming current Taiwan policy without ambiguity, which would price the bull path out. Long $BABA calls as the asymmetric beneficiary if a constructive readout lands, sized as a defined-risk catalyst trade rather than a directional bet.

### Warsh as chair and the curve he inherited

*The new chair walks in to a 30-year at 5.1%, a hot Retail Sales print on the desk, and roughly 50% market-implied odds of a hike by year-end. The transition itself is the catalyst.*

- Six desks flag the Warsh appointment as a policy-transition event worth tracking, with no directional consensus yet
- CNBC's overnight piece pinned the 30Y move to "inflation signals continuing to muddy interest rate expectations under the new Federal Reserve chair Kevin Warsh"
- April headline CPI 3.8% YoY (highest since May 2023), April core 0.4% MoM, both above consensus (BofA)
- Yesterday's Retail Sales hit on three of four prints (headline in line, Control Group, Ex Autos, and YoY all above estimate)
- May 20 FOMC Minutes are the first formal communication under the new chair

The Warsh prior is hawkish on inflation credibility and skeptical of balance-sheet expansion, and the data set he inherits is exactly the regime where that prior bites. The bull case for duration is that Warsh telegraphs a tolerance threshold for long-end yields (a soft cap signal would crush the term premium), and the bear case is that he leans into the hawk read to anchor his credibility, which keeps $TLT bleeding. May 20 Minutes will not have Warsh's vote yet, but they will be parsed for any hint of balance-sheet posture. Long $TLT calls dated past May 20 only as an asymmetric "Warsh blinks" lottery ticket, otherwise stay short duration via the $TLT trade in the prior theme.

### Semis are carrying the market, and 9% of the index is breaking down

*The narrowest leadership in years means the bear hedge to the AI capex theme is already pricing. You just have to read where it is.*

- 40% of the index's gains since March have come from semiconductor names alone (Bloomberg)
- Nomura estimates $SOXX could slump 15% in a single session in a reflexive mechanical flow reversal
- 9% of S&P 500 components hit 52-week lows on Thursday, the highest daily reading in the recent sequence, even as the index pushed higher (BTIG)
- The Korea ETF $EWY dropped 7% in a single session, the second-worst down day since March 2020, on chip-related "AI tax" headlines
- The TDEX index measuring demand for deep out-of-the-money tail-risk protection has hit its lowest levels in a long time, meaning hedges have been abandoned even as breadth divergence widens

ANZ, Bloomberg/Nomura, and Jefferies flag semiconductor concentration as a primary index risk, but none stakes a directional call on chip stocks themselves. The mechanism is reflexive. When 40% of returns come from one cluster of names and tail hedges have been bid away, even a small flow reversal becomes self-reinforcing on the way down. The bull pushback is that the underlying earnings still support the multiples, with Cisco's order book and the broader picks-and-shovels guide-ups confirming the demand wave. The counter is that the $EWY single-session drop and 344 S&P names red on an up day are the exact pattern that precedes a positioning unwind, regardless of fundamentals. Long $MU over CPU-exposed names, with a slightly longer-dated $SOXX put as the hedge against the breadth-break tail.
