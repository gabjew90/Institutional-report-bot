# Cooler PCE, hotter war

## 1. RECAP

Heading into Friday's open, traders are looking at a tape that wants to rally on inflation cover but keeps getting yanked back by Iran headlines. Yesterday's session closed with $SPY +0.55% and $QQQ leading at +0.84% on the back of a softer-than-expected core PCE print and a weaker GDP second estimate, but the bid stayed contained as overnight US strikes on Iran muddled what had been a clean ceasefire trade. $GLD ran +1.05% as the inflation-hedge angle held even with PCE cooler. $BTC slipped to $73,294 (-0.53% on the UTC day) and $ETH to $1,999, signaling that crypto isn't yet leading the risk-on read. Underneath, the cross-desk positioning color is that household saving is contracting fast while consumer sentiment sits near record lows, but Tier-1 banks are split on whether that's wealth-effect drawdown or the first crack in real spending.

- **Core PCE Price Index MoM came in at 0.2% vs 0.3% est (April data, released Thursday 8:30 AM ET).** The headline inflation read the Fed actually targets is cooling, which is dovish on the margin and gives the cut camp fresh cover. $TLT closed +0.52% on the print and the dollar drifted (-0.18% on $UUP), the textbook dovish-PCE reaction.
- **GDP Growth Rate QoQ 2nd Est printed 1.6% vs 2.0% est (Thursday 8:30 AM ET).** Q1 growth got revised down meaningfully, with the GDP price index also softer at 3.5% vs 4.5% est. Slower growth plus cooler prices is the soft-landing combination, and the bond bid plus equity bid both lined up with that read.
- **US struck Iran again overnight (8:05 PM ET Thursday) after Trump publicly denied a Hormuz deal.** Oil tumbled into the wire on US-Iran framework-deal hopes earlier in the session and then the strike headline hit, with $USO down 0.19% on the day after running lower most of the session and dollar weaker on ceasefire expectations. The tape is whipping on every Iran headline and the trade is now headline-by-headline.
- **Japan's April oil imports printed the lowest since 1962 as the Iran war disrupted supply (Reuters, overnight).** This is the first hard-data confirmation of the supply-chain bleed and feeds into the inflation-stickiness pushback even as core PCE cooled. Gold's $GLD +1.05% session and its third straight monthly fall on the broader print show the cross-current.
- **Costco delivered a lukewarm quarter Thursday after the close.** The miss-but-not-bad print is the first marquee tape on consumer wallet share since PCE landed, and it landed in the middle of the consumer-stress vs wealth-effect debate without breaking either side cleanly.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex is the earnings engine, and a real minority calls it a bubble

*The AI capex cycle is paying out in earnings growth right now and that is the entire reason the index is bid, but four desks are putting capital-destruction risk on the same page.*

- Goldman, UBS, and Citi attribute roughly half of this year's S&P earnings growth to companies tied to AI infrastructure spending, which is what is actually driving the market higher rather than valuation multiples expanding
- Marvell guided up on data center visibility through 2027, the kind of customer-confirmation signal that resets the duration of the bid for the entire supply chain
- Dell is set up for an earnings beat and an FY27 guide higher on AI server demand, with Lenovo in the same position per Goldman, so AI server monetization is showing up at the box-builder level, not just at the chip layer
- Jefferies' GREED & fear note frames this as a capex cycle where "a lot of capital will be destroyed," and BofA Securities points out that AI-stock concentration mirrors prior bubble peaks
- The direct GDP contribution from AI investment is significantly smaller than the headline capex numbers because the spend is import-leaky (Zürcher Kantonalbank flags this), so the headline buildout overstates the domestic growth feed

What is being repriced is the persistence of the bid, not the existence of it. Bulls and bears agree on the spending, they disagree on the payoff, and the bull case keeps winning on duration: Marvell's 2027 visibility and the Dell / Lenovo box-builder monetization both say the bid has at least four more quarters of customer confirmation behind it before any guide-down lands. The risk one corner of the Street flags is that when a single MAG7 hyperscaler trims a capex number, the entire picks-and-shovels trade gets re-rated on the same multiple compression that broke prior cycles, and Jefferies is explicitly framing this as a capital-destruction setup rather than a soft landing. Long $SOXX as the cleanest single-instrument expression of the picks-and-shovels stack.

### The Fed split is real and BofA is the outlier

*Two desks see the Fed staying tight because inflation hasn't cooled enough, but BofA's argument is that this FOMC is structurally dovish and will cut the second the labor data gives it cover.*

- Yesterday's core PCE at 0.2% MoM vs 0.3% estimate (the inflation read the Fed targets, not the headline CPI) landed cooler than every desk's published forecast and gives BofA's view fresh evidence
- SEB and ING both argue the Fed is unlikely to cut again any time soon because services inflation hasn't broken and the energy bleed-through from the Iran war is still in the pipeline
- BofA Global Research's counter is that the dot plot and the Powell language history both point to a fundamentally dovish FOMC, with cuts arriving as soon as a single softer payrolls print clears the political ramp
- The June 5 unemployment print (consensus 4.3%, with private payrolls consensus 85K vs 123K prior) is the next forcing function for the split, and a sub-85K read pulls BofA's view forward
- Dispersion across the front of the Treasury curve has flattened on disagreement about which side of the print the cut lands, not directional agreement, so the front end is paying you for taking a side

The setup is a directional dispersion trade rather than a timing trade. The bull case for BofA's view is that the Fed cuts on the first soft NFP and the front end of the Treasury curve gets re-bid, with 2-year yields falling 25-40 basis points (a quarter to nearly half a percentage point) across the move. The risk Goldman and Deutsche Bank both flag is that the SEB / ING side wins, services PCE stays sticky, and the Fed holds through the summer, in which case the long end of the curve gets the entire bear-steepening because Treasury supply is the binding constraint. Where this resolves is the June 5 jobs print, and the cooler PCE has already done some of the work for the dovish side. Long $TLT into the next labor print.

### Iran and Hormuz are the dominant overnight variable and nobody has a direction call

*A dozen desks flag the Hormuz / US-Iran story as the biggest cross-asset risk on the page, and they all agree it's binary without picking a side.*

- The Strait of Hormuz risk and US-Iran conflict cluster shows up across 12+ desks this window, the broadest single-topic coverage in the corpus, and not one desk has staked a directional call
- Overnight headlines whipsawed twice: US-Iran framework deal hopes pulled oil down and softened the dollar, then a confirmed US strike on Iran (8:05 PM ET Thursday) and Trump's denial of a Hormuz deal reversed both moves
- Japan's April oil imports hit the lowest level since 1962 on the war disruption (Reuters, overnight), which is the first hard-data confirmation that the supply-side bleed is real even with futures rolling on deal hopes
- Six desks flag the Mideast supply-chain disruption thread as a real second-order signal, with the read that physical oil markets are pricing scarcity even as paper futures trade on the diplomatic headlines
- Bank of America and Goldman both note that the dollar's weekly drift lower has been driven by ceasefire expectations rather than fundamentals, so a deal failure could snap that back fast

The trade is volatility, not direction. The optimistic read is that the framework deal closes in the next week, $USO and $BNO both flush, $SPY catches a relief bid, and the dollar stays soft. The risk the desk consensus sits with is that another strike round or a Hormuz closure bids physical oil sharply, $BNO outperforms $USO on the scarcity premium, and the equity tape sells off as the inflation pickup re-rates the Fed cut path back. The Japan-imports data point is the asymmetry signal: physical supply is already being disrupted, so the upside scenario is more contained than the downside. Long $BNO calls into the next Hormuz headline.

### Consumer pressure is real, the read on it is contested

*Six banks have consumer confidence at record lows and the household saving rate contracting fast, but BofA's pushback is that this is wealthier households spending down a portfolio runup, not evidence of real distress.*

- Goldman, ING, JPMorgan, SEB, UBS, and Bank of America all flag US consumer sentiment at or near record lows, which is broader consensus than any other softness signal in the corpus
- The household saving rate is contracting and disposable income is deteriorating across the same desks, which is the cleanest cross-bank agreement on the underlying data
- SEB notes Q1 consumer spending was weak, the hard-data anchor under the softer sentiment readings, and ING's read is that "consumer spending power is coming under increasing pressure"
- BofA's pushback is that the saving rate tends to be lower when household net worth is well above trend, so a contracting saving rate during an equity runup reads as wealth-effect drawdown rather than stress
- Costco's lukewarm Thursday afterhours print lands in the middle of the debate, soft enough to validate ING's read at the bulk-buyer level without breaking the wealth-effect framing

The optimistic read for the BofA framing is that the K-shape economy is doing its usual thing, the top quintile is spending down portfolio gains while the bottom three quintiles are buckling under sticky services inflation, and aggregate spending holds up because the spenders at the top are doing the work. The risk three Tier-1 desks flag is that the K-shape eventually meets a labor weakness print, the top quintile stops spending because the wealth signal flips, and the saving-rate compression turns into a hard demand impulse on the way down. Where this resolves in the next month is the consumer-discretionary tape, with a wallet-share miss across the bulk-buyer cohort validating ING's read and a Lululemon-style beat reinforcing the wealth-effect call. Long $XLP against short $XLY captures the directional bias without naming a single ticker.

### ETF and crypto flows are softening and bond ETFs are getting bid

*Flow tape across US equity ETFs and spot bitcoin ETFs is deteriorating while bond ETFs still attract money, and the cross-asset rotation is risk-off without being panic-off.*

- BofA, BlackRock, and Bloomberg all flag deteriorating flows into US equity ETFs and spot bitcoin ETFs as a live concern this window
- Bloomberg has Treasury and corporate bond ETF inflows running steady through the same window, which is the early defensive signal rather than a 2022-style flush
- China fund and South Korea equity outflows are the EM-Asia leg of the same rotation, with the Iran war jitters layered on top per Indian benchmark data overnight
- Goldman flags Value-factor outflows running, signaling the rotation is also happening inside the US equity tape, out of cyclicals into Quality and Growth rather than just out of equities entirely
- $BTC's overnight slip to $73,294 (-0.53% UTC day) fits the same flow pattern, with retail being the marginal buyer that's pulling back

The optimistic read for the rotation reading correctly is that bond ETF inflows are the early-cycle defensive signal, Treasuries get bid before equities flush, and the divergence between rising bond flows and falling equity / crypto ETF flows is exactly what you see ahead of a volatility expansion. The risk is that the divergence is shallow, the rotation reverses in two weeks as the Iran story resolves one way or another, and the bond-ETF bid is just positioning ahead of the Fed split breaking dovish anyway. Where this resolves is the spot bitcoin ETF flow tape, with a second week of outflows confirming the risk-off rotation and a single week of strong inflows reversing the read entirely. Long $TLT paired against short $XBI as the speculative-flow proxy.

### The AI infrastructure buildout is broader than the chip layer

*The capex story is showing up at the hyperscaler-adjacent layer (silicon, packaging, power, miners pivoting to AI cloud) and the breadth is the bull case rather than the headline.*

- 2027 hyperscaler capex revisions are the dominant spending theme across Goldman, JPMorgan, and Zürcher Kantonalbank, the cross-desk floor for the duration call
- Goldman flags IREN's $1.6 billion purchase agreement under an NVIDIA AI cloud contract as a live example of bitcoin miners pivoting compute to AI services, with existing capacity getting rerated as AI infrastructure
- Adjacent supply chains (custom silicon, packaging, servers, solar, silver) are all getting rerated as AI infrastructure plays per the same desks plus UBS, with the breadth itself being the signal
- Hyperscaler CFOs are starting ROI scrutiny on AI capex per several desk notes, the first leading signal that a guide-down could land in the next two quarters
- Chinese internet companies are taking a conservative AI capex approach (UBS), which is a sourcing-risk signal more than a demand signal because it tightens global supply-chain dependency on US hyperscalers

The optimistic read is that breadth is durable, and when a power utility, a silver miner, a memory name, and a packaging substrate name all get bid on AI capex, the cycle has at least 18 months of duration left because every adjacent name is racing for share of an already-funded buildout. The risk is that the breadth is itself the bubble signal, every cycle peaks when the second-order names start trading on the primary catalyst, and the Chinese conservatism plus hyperscaler ROI scrutiny is the early-warning that the primary catalyst is rolling over. Where this resolves is in MAG7 capex guides through the next two quarters, with a single guide-down validating the bear setup and another round of upward revisions extending the bid into 2027. Long $URA and $URNM captures the power-buildout side without paying for the consensus chip-equipment names.
