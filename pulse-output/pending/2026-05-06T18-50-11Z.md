JPMorgan's desk view is that a signed deal triggers bull-steepening (long-dated Treasury yields falling faster than short-dated yields, which is what happens when inflation fear unwinds without a Fed signal needed), a weaker dollar, and lower oil. Goldman's commodities desk has Brent at $90/bbl by 4Q26 — roughly $20 below the recent high. Citi already had Brent drifting toward $110 with WTI at $102 and a 27% one-year backwardation (the market still thinks today's barrel is worth far more than next year's). The asymmetric expression here is long the Brent ETF $BNO into any fresh Hormuz incident — you capture the spike on a kinetic surprise while a deal-driven drift lower hurts you slowly.
