# Momentum breaks, hike bets fold

## 1. RECAP

Stocks are opening the week with a relief bid after the crowded AI and momentum trades took their worst multi-day beating since 2020. $QQQ is leading at +1.40% in the first half hour, $SPY is up 0.56%, and $VIXY, the volatility ETF, is bleeding 1.9% lower as panic hedges come off. Trump opened the session by ringing a first-of-its-kind bell from the White House, flanked by CEOs. Crypto skipped the party: $BTC is off 2.7% overnight to just under $62,000, near cycle lows after a month of record outflows from the spot Bitcoin ETFs. Gold keeps grinding higher while bonds sit flat early.

- **Thursday's June payrolls printed 57k against roughly 113k expected,** with prior months revised down another 74k. Year-end rate-hike pricing collapsed from about 0.40 to 0.28 percentage points on the miss, taking a July hike off the table and kicking off the buying in bonds and gold that is still running this morning.
- **Fed Chair Warsh leaned dovish, toward easier policy,** saying inflation risks have declined and floating a phase-out of the dot plot, the Fed's quarterly chart of officials' rate forecasts, within a year. Rate markets took it as cover for the on-hold case, and hike bets that had stretched to near-record extremes in futures are unwinding.
- **The oil market's war premium, the extra dollars crude carried for supply risk, is finishing its round trip.** Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd began returning to the Suez Canal this morning, ten Japan-related ships cleared the Strait of Hormuz overnight, and OPEC+ is moving to raise output, leaving Brent near $70 after touching $112 in mid-May. Tehran is burying Khamenei this morning with crowds calling for vengeance on Trump, so the embers still glow even as the tankers sail.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The wipeout was leverage, not thesis

*The two-day momentum crash was a leverage accident inside a still-intact AI spending cycle, and the bounce favors the platforms over the chips.*

- Goldman's basket of the market's hottest momentum names fell 18% in two days, the worst move since Covid, and sits 24% below its peak against a 12% average for past washouts, so the forced selling has already overshot the norm
- The fuel was crowding, not news: hedge fund gross leverage, the total size of funds' borrowed bets, sits at the 98th percentile of the past five years (JPMorgan), and retail traded a record $6.8B a day in options premium in June, six times the historical average
- The damage concentrated where the leverage lived: SK Hynix dropped 25% from its high in eight sessions, and $MU is testing 1049, its 20-day trend line, flagged in research as the trigger for another 20% of downside
- The spending that anchors the trade did not move: May global chip sales ran 119% above a year ago, on pace for $1.5-1.6 trillion in 2026 (JPMorgan), and AI capital spending tracks toward $820B this year and $990B next
- $META added a genuinely new angle: its reported plan to sell spare AI compute is sized at $9-30B of incremental 2027 revenue, up to 21% above analyst estimates, converting the scariest capital-spending line in tech into a revenue story

Extreme crowding met thin summer trading and quarter-end resets, so the exit door shrank exactly when everyone reached for it. The bear case is bigger than positioning: the index tracking what companies actually spend on AI model usage is down about 20% from its May peak, and the gap between AI investment and the sales backing it has widened to 46%, wider than the 32% gap at the 2001 telecom bust. If customers really are losing willingness to pay, the whole trade reprices. The falling-price data reads differently on the supply side, though: token usage, the metered unit AI models bill by, is up eightfold this year, and memory prices are tracking toward 30-40% gains next quarter against a 10-15% consensus, which is what demand outrunning supply looks like, not a demand collapse. Samsung's earnings, teed up as the taster for the season, are the first live read on that memory claim. The stronger side of the trade is the platforms over the chips, Morgan Stanley's call, since the hyperscalers, the giant cloud platforms doing the buying, already served their underperformance sentence and the compute-resale plan is exactly the capital-discipline signal the market has been paying for. Long $META rather than buying the chip dip. The thesis dies if the token-spend index fails to reclaim its May peak, if a second hyperscaler starts selling spare capacity, or if $MU closes below 1049.

### The hike scare lost its fuel

*The 57k June jobs print took a July hike off the table, and rate markets are still pricing more tightening than the data supports.*

The drop in unemployment to 4.2% was manufactured by 720k people leaving the workforce, with participation at 61.5%, its lowest since 1976. Without that exodus, unemployment would have printed near 4.6% (Morgan Stanley's math), and Warsh floating a dot-plot phase-out tilts the Fed's messaging easier on top of it. The remaining hike pricing still looks too aggressive to Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan, both of whom have the Fed on hold all year, with Morgan Stanley seeing core inflation at 3.0% against the Fed's own 3.3% projection. The other side lives at Citadel Securities, which calls a July hike underpriced and keeps a base case of hikes in September and December. With hike bets stretched to near-record extremes in the futures market, there is more room for hike pricing to fall than to rise. Long $IEF, the 7-10 year Treasury ETF. This morning's ISM services print, landing right at press time, is the first test, and the July 14 CPI is the big one: a hot number puts the hike trade right back on.

### Oil rallies are for selling now

*With Hormuz flows back near normal and OPEC+ adding barrels, crude strength is supply-driven relief in reverse.*

Brent has round-tripped from $112 in mid-May to about $70, a level that prices in almost none of the war risk that dominated June. Tanker flows through the Strait of Hormuz are back around 14 million barrels a day, ten Japan-related ships cleared the strait overnight, Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd are steaming back into the Suez Canal, and OPEC+ just raised production quotas. The repair reads as done at JPMorgan, which explicitly recommends buying equity dips caused by Iran headlines because the conflict premium keeps decaying. The pushback (Morgan Stanley, ANZ): Persian Gulf export recovery still lags, the war drained global inventories to dangerous lows, so the cushion against the next disruption is thin, and it is an open question whether OPEC+ can actually deliver the barrels it just promised. ANZ also expects global oil demand to contract 1.5 million barrels a day in 2026 before recovering 2 million in 2027, which caps the upside even if supply wobbles. Short $USO on rallies. A renewed disruption to Hormuz flows kills the thesis on the spot.

### The other 493 stocks woke up

*The earnings recovery is spreading beyond the ten biggest names, and the cheapest way in is the financials nobody owns.*

The median S&P 1500 stock is delivering double-digit earnings growth for the first time in years (Morgan Stanley), while the top ten names still make up 39% of the index (JPMorgan), a gap that historically closes from both directions. Financials grow earnings 14% and trade at 12 times earnings, cheap against the market, with positioning Morgan Stanley calls extremely light. Biotech compounds the case, historically outperforming when rates fall, and healthcare hedge-fund positioning still sits in the 32nd percentile. The Russell 2000 also just had its AI-infrastructure weight cut from 15% to 7% in the index reshuffle, so small caps are no longer a leveraged AI bet in disguise. Does the S&P 493's earnings acceleration survive the reporting season that starts in two weeks, or does the market sprint back to the same ten names the moment chip stocks bounce? Long $KRE, the regional-bank ETF, says it broadens.

### The rotation trade: sell signals on the crowd's favorites

*Positioning indicators are flashing sell on the consensus trades, and the rotation lifeboats are gold and healthcare.*

BofA's Bull & Bear indicator, its zero-to-ten gauge of investor positioning and flows, hit 9.5, near an all-time high, and its sell signal has been live for six weeks, a setup that has historically preceded global equity losses of 2-3% over the following two to three months. The flows say the smart money is already moving: $55B went to cash in a week alongside the biggest US equity outflow since March, yet tech funds still track toward a record $152B annual inflow, so the crowd has not actually left its favorite trade. The rotation candidates are the war-and-inflation laggards. Gold is holding the $4,000 battleground, with $GLD adding another 0.7% this morning. Silver sits just under its $64 breakout with almost no fast-money bets riding it, and hedge funds have been net buyers of healthcare for two straight weeks. Long $GLD, with $XLV, the healthcare ETF, as the defensive complement. If gold loses $4,000 or silver keeps failing at $64, the rotation thesis is dead and the consensus trades keep the crown.
