# Brent knocks on 100

Stocks opened soft and oil opened bid. Half an hour into the session $SPY is -0.41% and $QQQ -0.33%, small moves for a morning that started with fresh attacks in the Gulf, while $BTC is the weakest piece of the risk complex at -1.75% to $77,725. Gold slipped and the dollar eased, so this is not a broad rush into safety, it is a specific bid for energy and a general reluctance to carry risk into Friday's inflation print. Money came into this week crowded in US mega-cap tech and thin almost everywhere else, and inside the AI trade it has rotated from the companies building data centers toward the companies charging for what runs on them.

- **$INTC up almost 5% before the open.** It is the loudest single-name move of the morning, on a session where OpenAI's new Astra model is the dominant AI story. Nothing in the index moved with it, so this is a single-stock event rather than a semiconductor bid.

Nasdaq positioning outside dealers came into September at the top of its two-year range (Goldman), which is why a single decimal on Friday's inflation print can move a lot of stock.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil curve is betting the strait reopens

*The forward market is priced for a Strait of Hormuz reopening that has not arrived in six months of waiting, and the physical market is going to charge for that mistake.*

- Brent hit a six-week high near $97 overnight while a barrel for delivery six months out costs $83.20, a $14 gap that only closes downward if the strait actually reopens, so the shape of the curve is the trade rather than the spot price
- Persian Gulf exports actually run 15 to 16 million barrels a day once you count tankers that switch off their tracking transponders, against 10 million visible to shipping trackers (Goldman Sachs), which is why $100 has not broken yet
- Net disruption to Gulf flows is still 7.9 million barrels a day (Goldman Sachs) and Qatari gas loadings have collapsed 96%, so the crude problem is manageable and the fuel problem is not
- US diesel hit $5.783 a gallon, the highest since mid-2022, and a European refinery now clears more than $40 turning each barrel of crude into fuel (JPMorgan), which makes refiners the profit engine of this shock rather than its victim
- A face-saving deal to reopen the strait runs at 55% against 45% that Washington holds the line through the midterms (JPMorgan), a coin flip the forward market is pricing as if it were already settled

Crude has an escape valve and refined fuel does not. Tankers can go dark and reroute, but a barrel that never reaches a working refinery never becomes diesel, and diesel is what moves the freight, the harvest and the shelf price.

The bear case is that rerouted shipping and China's switch to electric transport cap this below $100, and Goldman's own tanker work supports it, because a 5 million barrel a day blind spot in the visible data means the physical shortage is genuinely smaller than the screens imply. What that read leaves out is the refining layer. European refining profits above $40 a barrel and four-year-high diesel are refining numbers, not crude-supply numbers, and the inflation runs through the pump and the freight bill no matter how many crude barrels sail. Morgan Stanley took the other side of Goldman's read, raised its fourth-quarter Brent forecast to $100 and tells clients to hold oil and gas equities as the hedge, with $XLE the broad expression and $SHEL its top pick among the European majors. The condition that kills the trade is a sustained reopening of the strait. If that has not happened by the time the $83.20 six-month barrel rolls into March, the gap closes upward and $USO goes with it.

### Software has the AI moat, the energy demand has the bill

*The AI trade has moved from the companies building capacity to the companies charging for it, and who pays for the electricity underneath decides whether that holds.*

Morgan Stanley now treats AI as a protective moat around software rather than a threat to it, naming $NOW, $TEAM and $CRM, and has $NVDA leading AI semiconductors for the first time since late 2024. AI software rose 5.4% on the day after Broadcom's print, about twice its normal daily swing, while AI semis lagged (Goldman), a split wide enough to be a rotation rather than noise, and Goldman followed it with a Buy on $SNOW after the company lifted next-year product revenue growth to 36% from 31%. Electricity rather than chips is what caps how far this can run, and the local fights over who pays for new power lines decide whether the cost lands on the buildout or on households. The funding is the other pressure point: capital spending and research across the biggest cloud companies hit $870 billion over the last twelve months, and their borrowing plus Nvidia's amounts to 68% of all new long-term government borrowing this year (JPMorgan). $ADBE and $ORCL report Thursday after the close, the first earnings test of whether AI adds to software budgets or eats them.

### Warsh's credibility hike hangs on one decimal

*The September Fed decision stopped being about the economy and became about whether a new chair needs to prove he is not taking orders from the White House.*

August payrolls printed 162,000 against the 55,000 expected, with another 55,000 added to prior months (Deutsche Bank), and futures now put a September hike near 60%. The hike is required precisely because of the accusations of political subservience, with the trigger set at a 0.3% core inflation reading (TS Lombard), while Goldman's desk says 0.2% is low enough for the Fed to sit still. Consensus for Friday's August report is 0.2% core and 0.4% headline, and Deutsche Bank's own forecast of 0.21% core with 0.38% headline, lifted by gasoline, sits on the hold side of that line. One decimal point on one release decides the September 16 meeting. If core prints 0.2% or below, the hike priced between now and then unwinds and 30-year Treasury yields fall back with it.

### Gridlock is the midterm trade

*November is not about who wins, it is about which spending fights get easier afterward, and the record says a split Congress is the best outcome US stocks can get.*

Betting markets put Democrats taking the House at 85% while Republicans hold a 53% edge in the Senate (JPMorgan), and since 1950 a divided Congress has delivered 20% S&P 500 returns over the two-year term against 18% under single-party control. Redrawn district maps are worth roughly 15 seats to Republicans, which caps how big a Democratic House majority can get and keeps the split outcome the base case. The limit on the whole trade is that the White House veto and the Senate filibuster survive even a sweep (JPMorgan), so election baskets are pricing policy that may never pass. Goldman has the S&P 500 flat from early August to Election Day in midterm years, a 0% median return, then up 6% in the three months after, and names health insurers that depend on Obamacare subsidies as the clearest beneficiary of a House flip, with $XLV the liquid way to hold it.

### What to buy when everyone already owns the Nasdaq

*The US mega-cap trade is fully owned and the earnings growth has moved offshore, which makes international the cheaper version of the same bet.*

Nasdaq positioning outside dealers came into September higher than 92% of readings over the past two years, after a record $51.3 billion of buying in the four weeks to September 1, and $37.5 billion of that was hedge funds closing out bets against the market rather than adding fresh conviction (Goldman). Emerging market shares trade at 10 times next year's expected earnings against 18 for developed markets, a 45% discount that has pulled in $75 billion this year, and non-US equities are already 4% ahead of the US (JPMorgan). Morgan Stanley takes the other side and stays with US equities, arguing positioning there is lighter than the percentile suggests. The cost of being early is that another leg of geopolitical escalation or more pressure from rising bond yields pulls money back into US equities, against a second consecutive year of non-US outperformance off a 45% discount if it lands. $EEM and $EFA are the liquid expressions of the JPMorgan call.
