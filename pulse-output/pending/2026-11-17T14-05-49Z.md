# Nvidia Loses Its Banker

## 1. RECAP

Heading into today's open, stocks are coming off a technology-led selloff. $SPY closed Monday down 1.91% and $QQQ lost 2.67%, one session before Nvidia reports tonight for the first time under interim chief executive Colette Kress, and stock futures are slipping again this morning. Interest rates are the other pressure. Core inflation has surprised to the upside three months running, core PCE (the Fed's preferred inflation measure) stood at 3.1% in September (Nordea), a December rate increase is priced, and Goldman now expects a third increase in March 2027. Fed Chair Warsh spoke at noon Monday and desk reads of his remarks are still coming in.

- **October retail sales (8:30 AM ET) beat.** Sales rose 0.21% on the month against forecasts of 0.1% to 0.2%, and 0.63% excluding autos against 0.4% expected, after September gains of 0.8% and 0.9%. JPMorgan had forecast no growth in total sales, so spending is holding up while the Fed is raising rates, and the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.14%, up 1.6 basis points (one basis point is a hundredth of a percent).
- **Home Depot missed before the open.** It earned $3.86 a share against the $3.96 expected, on revenue of $43.40B against $44.09B. The shortfall on both lines arrives the same morning the national retail report came in above forecast.
- **Funds are positioned against stocks into Nvidia.** Trend-following funds have flipped to betting against stocks and funds that size positions by market volatility cut leverage from the 65th to the 30th percentile of their range (JPMorgan), while retail investors keep buying Nvidia. Bets against the Nasdaq 100 are near the maximum those models allow (Goldman), which sets up a sharp rally if tonight's report is taken well after Monday's $VIXY jump of 9.03%.
- **Oil fell through new attacks in the Gulf.** Iran attacked a US military radar system in Bahrain overnight and a tanker's crew is unaccounted for after a second attack north of Oman's Khasab. $BNO still closed Monday down 3.93%, with rising Mideast exports outweighing the attacks.
- **Crypto is flat.** $BTC is holding near $65,000.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The lender at the center of the AI trade just changed hands

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive, president and director with immediate effect. Chief financial officer Colette Kress took the job on an interim basis while keeping the finance role, and Harvey C. Jones became the company's first chair. The stock fell as much as 13% during the session and closed about 4% lower, leaving the market value back under $4 trillion and level with Microsoft. Fourteen sell-side desks cut ratings or price targets within hours.

The chips for the next two years are already sold, so the argument is about money rather than demand. Capacity is sold out for 2027 and largely for 2028, and the product roadmap is set about three years out, which is why Wolfe Research sees no effect on fundamentals, earnings or orders through at least 2028. What Huang ran personally was the lending. UBS puts Nvidia's spare cash at roughly $50 billion to $60 billion a quarter, a large share of which has been recycled into funding the companies that buy its products, and UBS's Tim Arcuri estimates that even a temporary pause could pull $50 billion or more out of the system at a time when borrowing costs are rising. The last quarterly filing carries $530.5 billion of commitments, buybacks and guarantees, including a $105 billion rent backstop for OpenAI's Ohio site.

That is where the banks split. Goldman's James Schneider expects every signed commitment to be honored and the next round to be harder to arrange, and cut his target to $220 from $300 while keeping a Buy. J.P. Morgan is Underweight the credit after default insurance on Nvidia hit record highs, and expects new deals to take longer or require more compensation. Piper Sandler takes the other side, arguing a change of chief executive does not alter the investment case, and keeps a $300 target on 14 times 2028 earnings of about $23. On China the gap is wider: J.P. Morgan stripped the roughly 8% of first-half revenue billed to China and other regions out of its fiscal 2028 estimates, cutting earnings about 10% and its target to $215, while Wolfe calls those shipments immaterial and further export controls irrelevant to the business.

Kress reports third-quarter results on Tuesday November 17, the first test of whether the financing platform stays open to new deals. Three months after Huang launched it, the $500 billion platform has produced none, and the earliest a deal could plausibly be announced is January 2027.

### Who pays for the data centers now

Non-investment-grade borrowing for AI data centers has stopped, with no new deals in the month since late September against roughly 30 over the prior year. AI high yield spreads, the extra yield investors demand over Treasuries, have widened to roughly 425bp, and one basis point is a hundredth of a percent. Comparable non-AI paper sits near 265bp. AI investment grade has widened to about 140bp, roughly 40bp more than non-AI investment grade. The blockage is completion risk, since a tenant's investment-grade guarantee only takes effect once the building is finished, and only 468MW of the roughly 4GW financed by the bonds Goldman tracks has been delivered. Goldman's Miriam Wheeler reads that as a split market rather than contagion, and the top of it never closed, with Morgan Stanley counting about $67bn of unsecured investment-grade bonds priced by the largest cloud operators inside five business days.

### Funds sold the best chip week since April

Hedge funds net sold US stocks for a second straight week and in five of the last six, and this time the selling came from fresh single-stock shorts rather than dumping longs. The long/short ratio in US information technology fell to 1.5, the lowest in Goldman's prime brokerage records going back to 2016. The share of fund capital actually exposed to the market dropped to 43.3% among US fundamental long/short managers, a five-year low, after gross exposure fell 8.7 points in midterm week, the largest drop since March 2020. Readings this light have historically preceded an average 6.3% S&P gain over the following three months, though Goldman's own strategist calls the setup a blank slate with little consensus either way. Hedgers are not convinced: the week before the midterms saw 55.8 million puts trade, the most ever.

### Three hot inflation prints, three hikes

October core consumer prices rose 0.30% on the month, a third consecutive upside surprise, and the three-month annualized pace reached 3.7%, the fastest since early 2025. In Goldman's view, a third 25bp hike in March 2027, taking the peak policy rate to 4.25-4.50% and pushing the first cut back to December 2027. Governor Waller said a 50bp December move should be considered at a 0.3% print, while RBC calls 50bp highly unlikely and now expects back-to-back 25bp hikes in December and January. JPMorgan still expects 25bp in December, with the median committee member projecting multiple further hikes in 2027. The bond market moved further than either: cumulative hike pricing through September 2027 went to about 90bp from about 47bp in a week, and the 2-year Treasury yield had its worst week since 2024. Friday's producer prices were softer where it counts for the Fed's preferred gauge, so Goldman cut its October core PCE estimate to 0.30% from 0.35%.

### Two of every three announced gigawatts are not real

Bernstein rescored every North American data center project in its database and credits only 199 of 586 announced, under-construction or land-held gigawatts, 34%. Stage of development removes 198GW and tenant quality another 113GW, while state politics takes out just 28GW, the smallest of its four filters and the one Wall Street spent the week modeling. Goldman's Alec Phillips puts about 45% of planned US capacity in states likely to tighten regulation, with Texas alone roughly 25%. Morgan Stanley moves about 5GW of US capacity from 2028 into 2029 and leaves combined 2027 capex unchanged at roughly $1.66tr, up 75% on this year, which deepens its projected power shortfall through 2028 to 44GW from 38GW. Restricting new supply raises the value of compute already running, which is the week's cleanest explanation for cloud operators beating chipmakers.

### Tokyo is where the stress actually is

BofA's cross-asset stress index rose from +0.15 to +0.20 over the week. The average hid the shape: it jumped to +0.17 on Thursday, the largest one-day rise since October 10, 2025, the same session the Nasdaq had its worst day since the Covid crash. Japan's regional score jumped 0.50 to roughly 1.7 while the US score rose 0.06 to below 0.2, leaving Japan about nine times as stressed as the US. The single most stressed of the 41 gauges is Tibor-OIS, Japan's interbank funding spread, whose five-day move ranked above 99% of all moves since 2000. The Nikkei fell 6% on Friday into a technical bear market, 20% below its June peak. The yen rose only 50 basis points on the Nasdaq's worst day, and Goldman still argues its yen hedge works but needs a bigger and broader shift in expectations than has happened so far.
