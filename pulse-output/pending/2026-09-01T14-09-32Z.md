# Bonds break, vol sleeps

## 1. RECAP

Markets opened Tuesday with the bond market setting the terms. The 10-year Treasury yield is at 4.78%, its highest since January 2025, and the damage is landing where long-dated cash flows live: $QQQ is down 1.30% in the first half hour of trading and $GLD has given back 2.33%. Middle East escalation is the accelerant, with $USO up 2.69% as new attacks and US attention on Iran's main oil export island put a war premium back into crude. $SPY is off 0.62%, a young tape rather than a verdict. $BTC is soft near $77.6k, down about 1% since the overnight, tracking risk rather than leading it.

- **Oil is repricing the war premium.** Renewed Middle East attacks and a US focus on Kharg Island, the terminal Iran ships most of its crude through, put $USO up 2.69% at the open. The grinding war has also drawn down the US emergency crude reserve, so the cushion that capped earlier spikes is thinner this time.
- **The 10-year is at its highest yield since January 2025.** At 4.78% it has barely moved today, but the level is doing the work: $QQQ is down 1.30% and $GLD down 2.33% as higher discount rates hit long-duration equities and a non-yielding asset hardest. Mortgage rates went to their highest since June 2025 on the same move, which is how this reaches the real economy.
- **Protection is getting bid off the cheapest levels of 2026.** $VIXY is up 1.79% from a VIX that closed Friday at 14.4, its lowest since December 2025, and Citadel is telling clients the risk-reward of owning equity protection into a historically weak month looks compelling. Hedge fund net exposure sits at a one-year low while the systematic trend-followers sit near the top of their range, so the two crowds are positioned in opposite directions going into it.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The Fed just became the whole trade

*A September rate increase is now the base case, short-term yields have further to reprice, and equity investors are the last ones to move.*

- September rate-increase pricing went from 36% last Thursday to 58% on Friday to 67% this morning (Deutsche Bank), a repricing of policy direction rather than policy pace, and it happened on Warsh's words rather than on new data
- Core PCE inflation, the measure the Fed actually targets, rose 0.25% on the month against a 0.1% forecast (JPMorgan), the print that gave the speech its teeth
- The 10-year Treasury yield closed Monday at 4.75%, its highest since January 2025, and Japan's 10-year touched 3% for the first time since 1996 (Deutsche Bank), so this is a global repricing of long-term borrowing costs and not a US-only story
- Rate markets now price 60 basis points, one basis point being a hundredth of a percent, of increases by June 2027 (JPMorgan), which is a series of moves rather than one insurance hike
- The government's benchmark revision cut 79,000 jobs from the year through March, released Friday, so the labor evidence that would normally argue against tightening is being set aside

What changed is not the inflation data but what the policy rate is now responsible for. Warsh called 2% inflation on the personal consumption measure a firm, fixed target and put the short-term rate back as the main tool, which strips out the discretion the market had been pricing, so every inflation print now maps straight onto the expected policy rate. That is why higher yields are landing hardest on gold and on long-dated growth stocks, and why this morning has $GLD down 2.33% with $QQQ down 1.30% while $SPY is only off 0.62%.

Goldman's economists expect core inflation near 0.2% for August, a pace they read as consistent with holding, and their rates desk has been scaling into long Treasury positions through $TLT and $IEF after short-term yields jumped on Friday. The problem with waiting for that print is where the repricing is happening. Friday's move was concentrated in short maturities, with 2-year yields jumping while 30-year yields actually fell, which is the market pricing policy rather than long-run inflation, and owning 30-year bonds does not protect against it. JPMorgan abandoned its bet on a widening 2-year to 10-year gap after the speech and stays short 10-year US Treasuries against German government bonds, with a December increase as its base case and the risk skewed earlier. The August CPI report on September 11 settles this. A core print near Goldman's 0.2% hands short-term yields back this move, and anything firmer stops the September meeting being a debate at all.

### Memory pricing is the number that keeps getting revised up

*The component layer of the AI build is still being marked higher, and the concentration of who is buying is the risk in it.*

Goldman doubled its forecast for high-bandwidth memory pricing to 100% growth year over year from 50%, on suppliers steering capacity toward the server memory that AI accelerators consume, and sees the chipmaking-equipment upturn running through at least 2028. The pipeline funding that is $1.4 trillion of data-center capital spending in 2027 (JPMorgan). What makes the forecast fragile is who is doing the buying. OpenAI and Anthropic alone could absorb 40% to 50% of all incremental computing power next year (Goldman), because Anthropic has at times generated $50 million of revenue per megawatt against an underlying cost several times smaller, which lets the frontier labs outbid everyone else for capacity. Two customers setting the clearing price is a powerful bid right up until one of them slows its funding, and there is no third buyer sized to replace either. Goldman has Applied Materials at Buy with a $165.27 target on this exact call.

### Broadcom Wednesday decides whether the chip drawdown is over

*The unwind in AI chip momentum has already run deep, and options are not priced for the bounce.*

Broadcom reports Wednesday after the close against consensus of $3.30 a share on $29.95 billion, and JPMorgan's desk scores positioning in the name at +3 on a scale running from -5 to +5, so the crowd is already leaning long into it. The drawdown behind it has mostly run its course, with JPMorgan's classic momentum factor down roughly 34% from its June high and its quantitative desk reading the AI semiconductor correction as past its worst phase. Options have not caught up. Chip options price only 1.08 times the move that software options price, below their average since 2021, so betting on a chip rally costs less than the same bet on software. Goldman's desk is buying that gap, selling one-month upside calls on its software basket to fund the same calls on its AI leaders basket. The counter comes from JPMorgan, which warns the numbers can be spectacular and the stock still fail to move, which is what a crowd already at +3 usually delivers. Wednesday night's guidance is what settles it.

### Two desks, cheap options, opposite bets

*Protection is priced for a calm September that the bond market is not delivering.*

The 10-year Treasury yield moved from 4.65% to 4.78% in three sessions and the bond market's own volatility gauge barely registered it. Equity protection is more relaxed still. The VIX closed Friday at 14.4, its lowest since December 2025, and one-month implied volatility on the S&P, the size of the move options are pricing in, sits at 11.39 and in the 3rd percentile of the past year. Goldman and JPMorgan agree the price is wrong and split on which side to buy. Goldman is buying one-month S&P calls struck just above spot at 37 basis points, near the cheapest that trade has screened, while JPMorgan is buying VIX products and shorting $LQD and $HYG into the September wave of new corporate bond sales. Citadel lands on JPMorgan's side, telling clients the risk-reward of owning protection here is compelling. A clean close above 4.8% on the 10-year is what wakes bond volatility and pushes it into stocks, and the 10-year is at 4.78% as this posts. Without that close by Friday's payrolls report, the rates move stays orderly and everyone who bought protection pays for it.

### The people who could sell already did

*Exposure sits at a one-year low everywhere except the systematic funds, and that is exactly where the next forced trade comes from.*

Goldman's hedge fund client book shows US long/short net exposure, what is left after subtracting short positions from long ones, at 47.6% and in the 1st percentile of the past year. Retail is out alongside them, with $4.39 billion pulled from US equity funds last week. The exception is the trend-following funds, holding roughly $147 billion of global equity index exposure at the 85.5th percentile and still adding to it. Their S&P selling trigger sits at 7620, and $SPY at $762 this morning puts the index right on that line. Around September 15 roughly 40% of the S&P 500 enters the window where companies stop buying back their own shares, so the corporate bid steps back at the same time. That is the asymmetry: the discretionary crowd has nothing left to sell and would have to chase any rally, while a break of 7620 is mechanical selling into a stretch with no buyback support underneath it. Goldman's own expressions are two standalone trades, September $SPY calls and a November S&P put whose strike is set by where the index runs to rather than by where it trades today.
