# Crude spikes, momentum snaps

## 1. RECAP

Markets opened risk-off Wednesday after Trump declared the interim Iran accord "over," pulling the ceasefire trade off the table and putting the Strait of Hormuz back in play. $SPY slipped half a percent in the first half hour and $QQQ held roughly flat, early price action rather than a full-day verdict. The move has two engines: an energy supply scare as oil jumps and gold slides, and a positioning story underneath, where hedge funds have now sold US tech for a fourth straight week and the high-beta momentum unwind from last week is still bleeding. Bonds and crypto came along for the risk-off ride, with the 30-year Treasury yield holding above 5%.

- **Iran deal collapse reignites the oil scare.** Trump said the interim accord to end the war is "over," and a damaged Qatari LNG tanker awaits salvage after a strike as Strait of Hormuz transit risk climbs. Brent-tracking $BNO jumped 3.3% and $USO 2.7% on the supply threat, while gold fell more than 1% ($GLD -1.1%) as 30-year Treasury yields firmed.
- **The tech unwind still hasn't washed out.** Info Tech is the most net-sold US sector for a fourth straight week, and the high-beta basket of stocks that swing harder than the market is down about 20% in five days. $VIXY +1.5% at the open confirms hedges are getting bid, not sold.
- **Crypto sold with risk.** $BTC -2.2% and $SOL -4.1% since yesterday evening, tracking the equity risk-off rather than trading on anything crypto-specific.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil supply scare is real for now, but the surplus is already scheduled

*Trade the physical squeeze from the Iran strikes as a spike, not a regime, because the same desks flagging the risk premium are already cutting their 2027 forecasts.*

- 80-plus targets struck by US forces and Iranian oil waivers revoked, stranding roughly 60 million barrels of Iranian crude (Goldman). Real barrels off the water, the physical hole under the bounce, not just a headline.
- Brent back near $76 and WTI up about 4% at the time of writing (MUFG, ANZ). The move is in spot prices, not a curve-wide re-rating that would signal a lasting shift.
- Strait of Hormuz transit running 52% below pre-conflict levels (HSBC). The chokepoint is already throttling flow, so the risk premium is grounded in real supply, not just fear.
- Refined-fuel inventories drawing fast: API crude down 400k barrels, gasoline down 2.9 million, distillate down 1.8 million (ING). That keeps prices bid even when crude balances look comfortable.

US strikes plus revoked waivers pulled real barrels out while refined-fuel inventories were already tight, and a Hormuz running at half its normal traffic means the physical market cannot refill quickly. That is why the bounce showed up in spot prices and refining margins, the profit refiners make turning crude into fuel, rather than in the back of the futures curve, where a genuine multi-year supply shock would price.

The other side is that HSBC is cutting oil forecasts, not raising them: a mini-glut now, then a 3.7 million barrel-a-day surplus by 2027 as Gulf flows normalize and China's oil demand structurally erodes on electrification. Washington has every incentive to keep prices capped ahead of the midterms, and Iran only needs leverage over Hormuz, not $150 oil, is the political read (Goldman). Both points are right about 2027, and neither changes the next few weeks, where refined-fuel inventories are still drawing and ANZ warns the renewed tanker attacks and waiver revocation could tip the interim peace deal into full collapse before any surplus shows up. Long $XLE on the physical squeeze, and treat HSBC's 2027 surplus call as the reason to sell strength rather than chase the rally.

### AI capex is real, the trade sitting on top of it wasn't built to hold

*The buildout keeps compounding, but the crowded momentum trade riding it just had its most violent unwind since the COVID reopening.*

The split is simple: the capex is real and the leveraged bet on top of it was overcrowded. The high-beta basket of stocks that swing harder than the market fell about 20% in five days, faster than the 2020 reopening unwind, and Info Tech has been the most net-sold US sector for a fourth straight week. This is a positioning event, not an earnings event. Underneath it, Goldman raised 2026 AI infrastructure capex to $755B, up more than 80% year-on-year, so the spending is still accelerating even as the trade expressing it breaks. The problem for dip buyers is that positioning has not washed out. Hedge funds' total borrowed exposure sits at the 85th percentile, which is high, but their net directional bet is only mid-range, so there is still fuel to sell more, and Goldman warns the drawdown could run up to twice as deep. Long the chip-equipment suppliers $AMAT and $LRCX over the crowded $SMH momentum trade.

### The 30-year Treasury is breaking, and the Fed just got repriced toward hikes

*The oil shock did more to the bond market than to stocks, and the 30-year Treasury yield is where it is showing.*

Long-dated Treasuries took the oil shock harder than equities did. The 10-year yield sits at 4.57% and the 30-year at 5.07%, holding above 5% for the first time in nearly a month. Inflation expectations did the damage, with the New York Fed's one-year measure at 3.7%, the highest since September 2023. Traders now price about 0.34 percentage points of rate hikes by December, meaning the market leans toward the Fed raising rather than cutting, up on the day. The structural risk one desk lays out is that new Fed chair Warsh stays too easy for too long, then gets forced into a catch-up hiking cycle (TS Lombard), while Goldman pins the selloff on heavy government borrowing and Treasury supply rather than Fed guidance. The counter is next week's data, where UBS's June CPI preview looks for the largest headline drop since April 2020 on falling gasoline. Short $TLT, which tracks 20-year-plus government bonds, while the oil scare runs, with that CPI print the catalyst that could snap the trade shut.

### What to buy if the AI trade is done leading

*With momentum cracking, the real "not-AI" rotation is defensive quality, because the popular emerging-market alternative is just tech in disguise.*

Pharma is the better "what if not AI" trade than the obvious one. GLP-1 weight-loss drugs top UBS's theme rankings, Novo Nordisk trades at 12.5 times expected earnings, below where the GLP-1 story first accelerated, and Medicare coverage for those drugs began July 1 at a $50 monthly patient cost, a real volume catalyst. The obvious alternative is a trap: buying an emerging-market ETF today is effectively a large tech bet, because the EM index's top-10 concentration is 39.4%, higher than the S&P 500's 36.7%, with 30% of the index in TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix (Deutsche Bank). The cyclical-rotation alternative is shaky too, with global factory activity rolling over to its weakest reading since March (HSBC). Long $XLV, and skip $EEM as a rotation because it is the same AI trade wearing a costume.

### SpaceX draws two Overweights on the orbital-compute story

*Two banks initiated the same high-conviction call this week, and the whole thesis rides on one engineering milestone.*

Two desks put fresh buy ratings on the orbital-compute story this week. Morgan Stanley started $SPCX at Overweight, their term for a buy expected to beat the market, with a $300 base-case target, pointing to Colossus and Colossus II already at 1.0 gigawatt of compute. JPMorgan matched the Overweight with a $225 target and a $28 trillion-plus total addressable market, the full revenue opportunity if they capture everything. Both bear cases are the same single point: the entire thesis depends on Starship reaching full, rapid reusability on schedule, and radiation, heat and maintenance could keep space-based compute from reaching cost parity with the ground by 2031. Long $SPCX into the next Starship reusability milestone, which is the whole call, and size it for the binary it is.
