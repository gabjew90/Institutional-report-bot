### Hormuz peace memo collides with a still-broken supply chain

The Iran story flipped overnight, but the supply damage is locked in. ING's commodities desk: roughly 13 million barrels per day of oil supply remains disrupted, US refined-fuel inventories are at multi-year lows — gasoline stocks at the lowest seasonal level since 2014, distillates the lowest since 2005. Bank of America put a name on it: "the summer of $4 gas," with futures keeping pump prices sticky above $4/gal through summer regardless of what happens at the negotiating table. ANZ flags aluminum specifically — 9% of global primary output sits in the conflict zone, the futures curve is in its deepest backwardation since 2007 (meaning physical metal right now costs more than metal three months out, which is what a real shortage looks like), and 1.8% of 2026 global production is at risk. Goldman's basics note adds that Brent December '26 futures held firmly above $90 even as the front month sold off — the curve is telling you the scarcity premium is real for months to come, not days.

The bull case Mizuho and Rabobank are leaning on: a credible US-Iran framework reopens the Strait, oil rolls toward Goldman's $90 4Q26 target, and the energy-driven inflation pulse fades fast enough for the Fed to cut twice this year. The bear case ING and The Market Ear desks keep coming back to is structural: the inventory hole takes months to refill even with a deal, and a single fresh headline (a tanker hit, a rejected memo, a stalled signing) sends Brent vertical because, in Mizuho International's words, the market is "one oil shock away from retesting yield highs." The CMA CGM container ship being hit in Hormuz this morning, the same morning the peace-memo headlines stacked up, is exactly that asymmetric risk in real time.

**Trade Implication.** Long $BNO (the Brent oil ETF) into next week, paired with May puts as protection. Why: even if a peace deal lands tomorrow, US gasoline and distillate inventories are at multi-year lows — refined fuel doesn't refill in days, so Brent stays bid for months while the supply hole closes. Risk: shipping traffic actually resuming through Hormuz with no follow-on incidents AND product inventories visibly rebuilding — that's the market saying the scarcity premium is gone, and the puts cap your downside on the long.
