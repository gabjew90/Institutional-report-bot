# Oil shock, soft print

## 1. RECAP

Two forces are pulling prices in opposite directions roughly half an hour into today's session. Overnight the US-Iran fight reignited and the shooting moved to the Strait of Hormuz itself, with Iran hitting UAE tankers and the US completing its latest wave of strikes, driving crude to a four-week high and bidding energy and gold. Cutting the other way, June inflation printed soft enough to pull a near-term Fed hike off the table, and stocks and bonds caught the relief. $SPY sits +0.27% early with $QQQ leading at +1.04% as chips do the heavy lifting, and $BTC firmed above $63k with $ETH outrunning it at +5% on the risk-on turn. Underneath, hedge funds are re-risking the AI trade after a brutal June even as the broad crowd sits at stretched leverage heading into a historically weak stretch.

- **June CPI came in cold (8:30 AM ET).** Headline prices fell 0.42% on the month against an expected 0.1% dip, dragging the annual rate to 3.88% from 4.2%. Core, which strips out food and energy, dipped 0.02% versus the +0.2% the desks penciled in, easing the yearly core to 2.86% from 2.9%. That is the disinflation the market was braced against, and S&P 500 futures flipped positive while the 10-year Treasury yield eased about 0.03 of a percentage point to 4.58%.
- **Oil shock overnight on US-Iran and Hormuz.** The conflict resumed, Iran attacked UAE tankers, the US finished its latest wave of strikes, and Trump formally notified Congress the fight is back on. Crude hit a four-week high, with $BNO running +3.41%, $USO +2.56%, and $GLD +2.03% as the safe-haven and supply-disruption bids stacked. China's June crude imports hit a near-decade low as the war choked flows, pulling even more spare supply out of the seaborne market.
- **Big-bank earnings opened strong (before the open).** JPMorgan beat at $6.14 EPS on $57.35B revenue, with Citi ($3.15), Wells Fargo ($1.96), and Bank of America ($1.21) all clearing the bar and Goldman also reporting. Season kicked off on a beat, giving the risk-on tone a second reason to hold this morning.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil bid is a supply hole, not a headline

*The overnight jump in crude is a physical disruption in the Strait of Hormuz, not a knee-jerk to a war headline, and that is what gives it staying power.*

- Brent jumped 9.6% in a single session, the sharpest rise since March 2020 (Deutsche Bank), and is up 41% since January, which reads as a repricing of supply rather than a one-day spike.
- The Strait of Hormuz normally moves 21 million barrels a day (UBS), and JPMorgan now puts the odds of it reopening to normal by year-end at 56%, down from 90% in late June, so the market is pricing a lasting chokepoint.
- The crack spread, the profit a refiner makes turning crude into fuel, blew out to $64.38 from a June low of $43.51 (JPMorgan), meaning refined-fuel supply is tighter than crude itself.
- European natural gas is caught in the same net, with TTF prices up 78% year-to-date and storage sitting 22% below the 10-year average, so the shock is bleeding into gas and power.
- A proposed 20% toll on Hormuz cargo would skim an estimated $400-500m a day off roughly $2.5bn of daily flows (Deutsche Bank), a tax on every barrel that moves and a floor under prices.

Physical barrels are the story. Tankers under attack and a contested strait mean the oil that still moves costs more to insure and reroute, and refined fuel is where the squeeze shows up first because those inventories were already thin. China's near-decade-low June imports pull still more spare supply out of the seaborne market.

The counterweight is Deutsche Bank's own pain-threshold work, which says Brent around $80 to $90 is not high enough to seriously dent stocks, and that it takes a sustained move above $110 to trigger the wealth and financial-stress effects that hurt risk assets. A credible ceasefire and a reopened strait would restore roughly a fifth of global LNG supply almost overnight. That threshold cuts the other way this week, because refined products are already at fresh highs while crude sits in the $80s, so the tightness is landing in the part of the barrel that feeds inflation fastest, and refilling that hole takes months no matter what a truce headline says. UBS, which stays constructive on equities, still has its base case as a bumpy resolution that does not normalize shipping until the second half of 2026. Long $BNO and $USO for the US expression, or the energy sector through $XLE, rather than fading a move that outlasts any single ceasefire headline.

### The AI bottleneck moved to memory, and credit is the tell

*The AI trade is narrowing to the memory and equipment names that are genuinely supply-constrained, while the credit market quietly prices the financing risk the equity crowd is ignoring.*

- AI-related capex is running up 68% this year to $820B and toward $1tn in 2027 (UBS), with Goldman's build reaching $1.2tn and 120 gigawatts of compute by 2028, up from 30 today.
- JPMorgan's flow desk calls memory and semis the core equity buy, pointing to steady inflows into $SOXX and $2.6bn into DRAM funds last week.
- SK Hynix's $26.5bn US listing came seven times oversubscribed even after the stock fell 15% on Monday, so appetite for the bottleneck names is holding despite the volatility.

The wrinkle is credit. Widening default-insurance spreads on the hyperscalers, the cost to insure their bonds against non-payment, say debt investors are being paid to worry, and Oracle is down 46% since June 2nd. That financing strain lands in 2027 when free cash flow turns, not now, and the equipment makers moving toward $250bn of annual spend by 2028 keep getting paid through this cycle. Long memory and semis via $SOXX, with equipment names like $AMAT as the lower-beta version of the same trade.

### The hot-CPI scare just got defused

*June inflation came in soft enough to take a near-term rate hike off the table, and the bond market has not fully repriced the relief.*

- Core CPI dipped 0.02% in June and the yearly core eased to 2.86%, under the 0.3% monthly pace Fed Governor Waller warned would force tightening, so the hawkish trigger never fired.
- The desks were braced for the opposite, with Goldman modeling 0.17% core and JPMorgan 0.22%, and July hike odds sitting near 43% going in, which makes the print a genuine upside surprise on relief.
- The 10-year yield eased to 4.58% on the number, and UBS is telling clients to lock in still-elevated yields on short and medium high-quality bonds before they drift lower.

The one thing that reopens the debate is oil. A sustained energy spike keeps inflation above target and hands the hawks their reason back, which is live given this morning's crude move. Long $TLT into the back half of the week. If core PPI on Wednesday prints hot or Warsh sounds openly hawkish in testimony, that trade is dead and long-term yields snap higher, but until one of those lands the soft inflation number does the talking.

### Rotate out of the crowded top, into breadth

*Mechanical and leveraged funds are topping out near the highs of their equity range just as July seasonality turns against them, so the bigger risk is a positioning unwind.*

- Deutsche Bank has systematic strategies, the rules-based funds that buy as volatility falls, at the 71st percentile of equity exposure and vol-control funds at the 91st, with little room left to add.
- JPMorgan puts hedge-fund gross leverage at the 99th percentile over five years, the most stretched in the post-2020 data, so a lot of borrowed money is already in the trade.
- Morgan Stanley's data shows July is historically the biggest month for hedge funds cutting exposure, and June was the largest month of AI net selling on record even as prices held.

The offset is that hedge-fund net leverage sits at just the 10th percentile, which leaves dry powder if a dip gets bought. Long equal-weight $RSP and small caps $IWM over crowded mega-cap $QQQ, where breadth is actually improving, and watch whether $VIXY stays bid as a cheap tail hedge into the de-grossing window. The tell is the first real down day, whether forced systematic selling overwhelms the record buyback bid.

### Gold is doing two jobs at once

*Gold hedges the geopolitical shock and the debt-driven debasement trade at the same time, which is why the structural bid keeps reappearing on dips.*

- UBS targets gold at $4,600 by year-end and $5,200 over 12 months, against roughly $4,120 now (RBC), so the house view is another leg up rather than a top.
- Central banks are on track to buy 750 to 1,000 metric tons this year (UBS), a price-insensitive buyer that sets a floor under the metal.
- Gold is still down 26% from its January peak (UBS), so this is a rebuild off a correction, not a chase at the highs.

The risk is real yields, the return on inflation-protected Treasuries, because stronger data and a Fed on hold raise the cost of holding an asset that pays nothing, and gold slipped 1.1% on Monday when the US-Iran exchanges briefly calmed. Being early costs a little, since gold pays nothing while stocks grind higher. Being absent means no hedge at all if Hormuz escalates or the soft-inflation story flips back into debasement. Accumulate $GLD on dips toward the correction lows.
