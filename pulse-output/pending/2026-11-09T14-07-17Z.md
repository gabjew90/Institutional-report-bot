# Rebound runs on hedges

## 1. RECAP

Stocks are rallying hard an hour into the session, with $QQQ up 2.75% and $SPY up 1.88%. The session is still young, so these moves can change by the close. Desks spent the weekend working out why the market bounced so hard after a midterm result that looked bearish on paper. Goldman and Nomura put hedge cash-ins and leveraged-fund flows at the center of it. $BTC sits near $75,000 and has not joined the rally. Bitcoin already reflects a Democratic Senate, and the bid from bitcoin ETFs has stalled (Goldman crypto desk).

- **Insurance is being sold.** $VIXY, the volatility fund, is down 9.51%, which fits traders selling the hedges they bought before the vote. $SPY is up 1.88% alongside it.
- **Oil fell while the Iran standoff dragged on.** $BNO is down 3.73% and $USO is down 2.96%. Iran says no unauthorised vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz overnight after its navigation aids were destroyed. The UAE's Gargash warns that a US-Iran settlement looks difficult.
- **Gold rose as oil fell.** $GLD is up 1.29%, and cheaper oil easing inflation fears is the stated reason for the gain.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The post-vote rebound is traders cashing in their insurance, and the leveraged dip-buyers are still underwater

*The bounce after the vote is mostly a mechanical unwind of hedges, and mechanical flows run out.*

- Nasdaq 100 futures fell as much as 2.4% overnight as Democratic wins piled up, then recovered about 929 points by the New York morning. A round trip that fast points to positioning, because nothing about earnings changed in those hours.
- The VXN, the price of Nasdaq 100 options insurance, sat at 30.5 going into the vote (Goldman's Rich Privorotsky). Protection was still expensive, so cashing it in is a large source of buying.
- Levered ETFs took in a record $13B in October, including $3B on Friday Oct 30, their biggest day of the year (Goldman). Those buyers bought into a Nasdaq 100 drop that same day, so many are holding losses.
- Nomura's Charlie McElligott estimated $35B of forced selling by those funds into that Friday's close, as the banks on the other side of their contracts sold stocks to reset exposure. Every down day repeats the same selling.
- October brought the biggest drop in professional risk appetite in five years (Goldman's Lee Coppersmith). ETFs that lagged the Nasdaq 100 took in 2.4% of their assets, three times the 0.8% that went into the ones that beat it. Professionals cut exposure while retail added.

A triple-leveraged fund has to hold three times its assets in stocks, so it must sell after losses, and the selling lands in the last hour of the session. The rebound had a second source. Traders who bought put options before the vote sold them back once the scare faded. One desk-note author points out that after those puts are cashed, no mechanical flow is left to push the market higher.

The buyer's case comes from Goldman's Privorotsky. AI infrastructure valuations are already cut hard, hedges placed in recent days still need cashing, and volatility should fall fast, which lifts stocks in the short term. McElligott sees two layers behind the rebound, a hedge-monetization layer and a narrative layer, while the desk-note author reads it as mostly hedge cash-ins. BofA's Michael Hartnett warned that the blue wave marks a shift to populist socialism, with stocks, the dollar and yields falling into year-end. $QQQ is up 2.75% this morning, so the unwind is still running, and the bounce can last a few more sessions. Tuesday's 8:30 AM ET CPI decides the next leg, with the calendar showing 0.3% on the headline. Another Nasdaq 100 drop into the close hands the 3x funds a second forced sale.

### Texas rules, not party control, set the data center timeline

Texas holds 26.7% of planned US data center capacity (Goldman's Privorotsky). Gina Hinojosa, who beat Greg Abbott with roughly 53%, says she will keep the state's permit freeze in place until broader regulation passes. That could push gigawatts due online in 2027 into 2028, and Goldman expects short-term moratoria in states holding about 45% of planned US capacity. Pause proposals appeared in 20 of the 36 governor races, and the average US build now costs $49 billion per gigawatt, up $20 billion in three years. Nomura's McElligott takes the other side. He treats forced moderation as relief for hyperscaler spending and for the worry that AI financing competes with Treasury debt for buyers. $EQIX is Morgan Stanley's Top Pick, on the argument that supply constraints strengthen pricing for owners of already-powered capacity, over a 3 to 12 month horizon. The next hard date is ERCOT's December 10 verification audit, with final allocations nominally in the first half of 2027.

### Gold's forced-selling washout left it under fair value

Gold fell 3.7% on Friday Oct 30 with yields and the dollar both lower. Bloomberg's Simon White reads that as cash-raising to cover losses elsewhere, plus selling by trend-following funds as their long-term signals turned negative. The semiconductor index had its 41st-worst decline in over 30 years the same day. The drop put gold below White's fair-value model (yields after inflation, the dollar, the S&P 500 and copper) for the first time since the rally began in 2023. He expects that to give prices a durable base for a gradual recovery. Gold bounced 2.9% to $4,053.60 on Nov 4, which McElligott reads as markets pricing no tolerance for DOGE-style spending cuts under a Democratic Congress, plus possible easing of Gulf and Iranian selling. $GLD is up another 1.29% this morning as oil falls. US retail has not given up yet by White's read of gold ETF flows, and gold was lower again the day he wrote. If US holders start selling, the washout is not finished.
