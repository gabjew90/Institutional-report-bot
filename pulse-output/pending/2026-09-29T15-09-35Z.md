# Rates Take The Wheel

## 1. RECAP

Stocks are close to flat about 90 minutes into Tuesday's session after Monday's rate-driven selloff, with $SPY down 0.02% and $QQQ up 0.47%. The 10-year Treasury yield is holding at 5.25%, the 19-year high it touched Monday, and the 30-year sits at 5.58%, so interest rates are still setting the mood. Trend-following funds are about 99% of their maximum short in 10-year Treasuries, so any drop in yields would force them to buy bonds back. - **Iran talks headline (overnight).** Trump denied offering Iran sanctions relief for nuclear concessions, while Tehran said it is ready for talks. Oil funds fell on it, with $USO down 2.07% and $BNO down 1.67%, so the market is pricing talks that continue rather than a supply shock that worsens.
- **Gold bounce.** $GLD is up 1.23% after touching a seven-week low overnight. It is rising even with the 10-year yield at a 19-year high, which is unusual because higher yields normally make gold less attractive.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Five And A Quarter Breaks The Session

The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.25% intraday on Monday, a 19-year high. The S&P 500 closed down 77 basis points (one basis point is a hundredth of a percent) at 7,683, with 334 of its members finishing red. A midday report that Washington would release frozen Iranian assets in exchange for nuclear concessions was rebutted by Tehran within hours, and oil and yields rose on the round trip, which is what pushed equities lower.

The damage concentrated in the high-beta themes. Goldman's agentic AI basket fell 250 basis points intraday. Its basket of the seven largest technology companies fell 130, and its basket of unprofitable technology names fell 240. The one-month move in real yields, what is left of a yield after expected inflation, is among the worst since 2013, and the 10-year has now risen seven months running, tied for the longest streak in 50 years.

Two desks read the same session in opposite directions. Goldman's equities desk argues rates, energy and AI risk are unchanged but better priced now, so the distribution of outcomes has turned more constructive, with light positioning cushioning the downside and the index at 19 times forward earnings, in line with its 10-year average. JPMorgan's market intelligence desk went further, moving back to tactically bullish from the cautious stance it took on August 31, on the view that yields settle here and oil drifts lower, with Jay Barry forecasting a 5.05% 10-year at year-end. BTIG takes the other side and expects a high-correlation selloff soon, pointing at credit, where CCC-rated high-yield spreads have widened in 11 of the last 14 sessions toward 2022 levels and investment-grade technology default-insurance costs sit at their widest since 2023.

A level settles it. Goldman's short-term pivot for the S&P is 7,665, and the index closed just above it, having already passed the technical levels that desk ties to negative forward returns. The question clients are asking is what breaks if the 10-year heads toward 6%.

### Credit Is Where The Strain Shows

Investment-grade spreads widened about 4.6 basis points this week on Goldman's IG100 index, and high yield widened about 18. Deutsche Bank measured last week's US high-yield move at 27 basis points, the biggest weekly widening in almost a year. Supply is why the move stuck. $42 billion of investment-grade bonds priced this week, taking the year to $1.82 trillion, up 26%, with roughly another $50 billion expected next week including as much as $32 billion from Paramount. The offset is the coupon. All-in investment-grade yields near 5.95% are close to multi-year highs, which makes the income argument stronger than the spread argument. AI has become a credit story in its own right, with single-name default-insurance volumes on AI borrowers up about 400% from a year ago.

### What The Buildout Has To Earn Back

Goldman put a number on the AI question. Six hyperscalers spending $1.73 trillion on 2026-27 capacity need $1.42 trillion of cumulative revenue across 2028-30, which works out to $11.6 billion per gigawatt and clears a 15% return on invested capital. The case that this is reachable rests on cloud backlog of $1.69 trillion, up 152% from a year ago, covering 59% of the requirement before any new bookings. Assuming zero return, the same six companies still need $920 billion just to cover depreciation and running costs. Morgan Stanley's math is friendlier, at 25% to 50% incremental returns. BCA Research's Peter Berezin sits at the other end, arguing $10 trillion of annual AI revenue may be needed to monetize data-center spending at this pace.

### Diesel Is The Part That Is Actually Tight

Trump rejected Iran's proposed seven-day truce and its plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, with talks continuing. Brent was up 2.58% at $107.01 on Monday morning and WTI closed up 71 basis points at $93.09. Refined fuel is tighter than crude. US diesel and gasoline inventories sit at 15-year lows, retail diesel averaged $6.52 a gallon on Wednesday, 91 cents above a month ago, and the White House is preparing a 90-day ban on diesel exports. Morgan Stanley's quantitative desk measures the correlation between WTI and the 10-year Treasury yield as the tightest in 35 years, which is why an oil headline now prices as a rates headline. The global supply shortfall has narrowed from roughly 7 million barrels a day in March to about 1 million in the third quarter, so the argument is that oil does not need the conflict to end, only for the disruption to stop getting worse.

### The Hike Path Nobody Agrees On

Fed funds pricing puts October hike odds above 70% and still climbing, after moving from 53% to 64% across last week, with the forward curve carrying just over 90 basis points of further tightening. Deutsche Bank's base case is two more increases, December and March, with room for an earlier October move if the data keeps running hot. UBS takes the other side and expects only one further increase, in December, on the view that underlying inflation stays moderate. Officials are not helping the doves. Cook flagged that the AI buildout itself can be inflationary, and Barkin declined to say AI is immune to rate pressure. Core PCE, the inflation measure the Fed watches with food and energy stripped out, prints Wednesday, with Deutsche Bank forecasting +0.27% for August.

### Two Crowded Shorts In The Same Room

Systematic trend-followers sit at roughly 99% of maximum short in 10-year Treasuries, about $22.7 billion notional, and Goldman's funds positioning measure shows an extreme underweight to long-dated bonds, a setup that has historically been followed by lower 10-year yields over the next three months. Those funds also hold extreme upside convexity in bonds, so a rally would force more buying on the way up. The second crowded short is utilities, where Goldman recorded the largest short selling in almost six years and JPMorgan's prime brokerage data puts hedge-fund exposure at its lowest since 2018, about 2.5 standard deviations below normal. The two are wired together. Between September 4 and 25 the 10-year rose 39 basis points while $XLU fell about 8.4% against $SPY. BofA estimates its Treasury trend position holds unless the 10-year falls roughly 18 basis points in a week, which is the trigger to watch.
