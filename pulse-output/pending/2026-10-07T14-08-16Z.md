# Bonds break, stocks slip

## 1. RECAP

Stocks are lower half an hour into the session, with $SPY -0.59% and $QQQ -0.80%, as Treasury yields climb to a new high and the dollar firms. Hedge funds have cut their stock exposure to the lowest since April 2025 (Goldman), so the funds most likely to sell have largely sold, and this morning's drop is coming from the bond market instead. $BTC fell 2.88% to $83,088 since 8 PM ET, and $ETH is down 4.91%, falling harder than $BTC.

- **Treasury yields keep rising.** The 10-year yield rose to 5.34% from about 5.27% and the 30-year to 5.72%, with $TLT -0.74%. Yields rose even though a soft September jobs report took an October Fed rate hike off the table for most desks, while ISM services input costs sit at the highest since July 2022 (JPMorgan).
- **Dollar up, gold down.** $GLD -1.68% as $UUP rises 0.55% ahead of the 2:00 PM ET Fed minutes. Higher yields and a firmer dollar both raise the cost of holding gold.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Stocks are holding while bonds break

*The 10-year Treasury yield at its highest since 2002 is a price the stock index has not paid yet, and one of the two has to move.*

- The 10-year yield is 5.34% this morning, above the 5.33% level that marked the highest since 2002 (BofA), and the Treasury-market volatility gauge (MOVE) jumped from 77 to 113, back to March-panic levels while the stock-market VIX stays calm, so bond traders are paying for protection that stock traders are not buying.
- The median S&P 500 stock sits 17% below its all-time high while Nvidia nears a $6 trillion market value (Goldman), so a handful of AI names are carrying an index that most stocks are not.
- September payrolls rose 29,000 against 90,000 expected, which is why futures odds of an October Fed hike fell to 15% from nearly 75% a week earlier.
- US bond futures saw their largest weekly net buying in five years as the selloff ran, so buyers showed up in size and yields still rose.

Three things are holding the index up against that rate: earnings gains concentrated in AI names, buybacks (Nvidia added $150 billion to its plan), and the habit of buying every tech dip. None of them lowers the borrowing cost every other stock and every other borrower now pays, which is why Goldman's US homebuilder basket is down 17.5% in three months. $QQQ is down 0.80% half an hour in, a young move, but it points the way the argument says.

If bond volatility stays high, the index falls to meet breadth, and if it calms, breadth rises to meet the index (Morgan Stanley's Wilson). Hartnett at BofA wants clients to start adding long-term Treasuries after record-low returns, and expects Washington to use open-ended Treasury buybacks and caps on long-term yields to stop 30-year yields from breaking the AI trade before the midterms. The next Fed hike is pushed to December, but the 10-year yield is up about 133 basis points (hundredths of a percent) in eight months, close to the 137 of the 2013 taper tantrum, and this week brings $58 billion of 3-year, $39 billion of 10-year and $22 billion of 30-year Treasury supply (Goldman). The Fed minutes at 2:00 PM ET and today's 10-year auction are the next test of Hartnett's bond bid via $TLT, and the selloff is confirmed if $MDY falls below 666 and $IJR below 135 (BofA).

### France is the weak link in European debt

*French borrowing costs, not Italian or Greek ones, set the pace in Europe, and the argument is whether it stays there.*

The gap between French and German 10-year yields widened 32 basis points last week to 141, the largest weekly move in Bloomberg data back to 1990 (Deutsche Bank). Deutsche says French bonds yield 40 to 50 basis points more than the political risk justifies, but its strategists are not ready to buy them outright. Italy and Greece are the next place to look (ING), where spreads also widened (Italy by 23 basis points last week), and European banks hold large positions in these bonds, so a selloff would reach them first (Goldman). European stocks have held up on earnings, with Goldman expecting a 22% rise in the second half, and the STOXX 600 still fell 1.1% last week. The 2011 crisis peak near 190 basis points is the marker, and at 141 the French gap is about three-quarters of the way there.

### AI's bill is moving to the bond market

*The AI buildout is paying for itself in earnings today and borrowing tomorrow.*

Consensus has hyperscaler capex growing 116% year over year in the third quarter and S&P 500 earnings up 27%, with technology and energy supplying nearly 80% of that increase (Goldman). AI-related market financing is expected to reach $500 billion in 2026, more than double last year, and over 80% of capex through 2030 needs debt or equity markets (Carlyle). Investor worry has moved from valuations to leverage, even though payback on the infrastructure is put at under three years. Meta carries $628 billion of commitments off its balance sheet against $112 billion of debt, which lifts its enterprise value 35% (Needham). Google was ordered to halt work on two Finnish data centers this morning, a reminder that sites and power are constraints too. Earnings season opens the week of October 12 with 70% of S&P 500 market value reporting by month-end, and the capex guides there decide whether the financing worry gets an answer.

### Oil's supply gap outlasts the ceasefire headlines

*Oil is cheap to own through options even though the physical shortage will take years to refill.*

Saudi Aramco's CEO says it could take up to two years to refill global inventories, with 3 billion barrels lost since the conflict began and 1 billion drawn from stocks, and Kuwait Petroleum puts the refined-product shortfall at 6 million barrels a day. Shell said overnight it expects record refining margins. Prices are quieter than that implies because physical shortages eased as Hormuz transit and the East-West pipeline restarted (Goldman). Options that pay if Brent falls are getting more expensive while options that pay if it rises are getting cheaper, because dealers sold puts and funds are buying futures outright to hedge their rate losses. Morgan Stanley measured a record +60% three-month correlation between Brent and the 10-year yield, so an oil spike now lands on bonds and stocks together. Goldman's route is cheap upside bets on Brent, either far-out calls or call spreads, which $BNO options replicate, and it calls the setup finished once call prices normalize or demand for upside protection returns.

### Hedge funds have already sold

*The selling that a stock-market drop needs has largely happened, which leaves a squeeze as the bigger risk.*

US stock-picking hedge funds' net exposure to stocks (long positions minus short) is 46.4% of capital, the lowest since April 2025 and the 2nd percentile of five years, after three straight weeks of cuts (Goldman). Non-dealers sold $63.7 billion of S&P 500 futures over six weeks, and over 70% of that was existing longs being sold rather than new bets against the market (Goldman). Trend-following funds are slated to sell another $18.5 billion of global stocks this week. Small caps are where funds have pulled back most, with rule-based funds' exposure to the Russell 2000 at the 7th percentile of its range and record short interest from leveraged funds, so a drop in the 10-year yield forces those shorts to buy back first, which would lift $IWM. Deutsche Bank measures aggregate positioning at the 60th percentile and discretionary investors at the 27th, so the washout sits in hedge funds and trend followers, not across the whole market. The same 10-year auction that tests the stock index tests the squeeze.
