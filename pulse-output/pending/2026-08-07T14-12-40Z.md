# Payrolls turn negative

## 1. RECAP

Two hours into Friday's session the jobs report is driving stocks, bonds, and gold alike, and the market is treating a genuinely ugly print as good news. Stocks are rallying on the rate-cut math: $SPY is +0.68% with $QQQ out front, exactly the reaction JPMorgan's desk positioned for when it went tactically long equities into the print. $GLD is the standout mover, +2.42% and on track for its best week since January. $BTC is holding the $65k area. Under the surface, retail money is rotating out of software into memory and chip names at a record weekly pace, and credit markets are still pricing more stress in the biggest AI borrowers even as the index recovers.

- **July payrolls contracted by 23,000 against an 85,000-gain consensus (prior +57,000).** Unemployment still fell to 4.1% versus a 4.2% estimate, a mixed signal, but the market is trading the payroll miss: the 5-year yield eased 3 basis points to 4.36% and $QQQ is up +1.16% as rate-sensitive growth leads the rally.
- **Gold's best week since January.** $GLD is +2.42% at $399 as inflation fears ebb, and the payroll contraction adds fuel because falling yields cut the cost of holding metal that pays no interest.
- **Software earnings are holding up even as retail sells the group.** Datadog posted $0.65 EPS on $1.12B revenue Thursday morning, and Atlassian is rising today after a strong quarter. The prints cut against the record retail exodus from software covered below.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### A negative jobs print is extending the rebound

*The soft print arrived even softer than anyone forecast. What breaks the rebound from here is the leadership gap, not the Fed.*

- JPMorgan's economists went into the morning below the Street at 75k July payrolls versus an 85k consensus, with a miss as the friendly outcome in their scenario work. The actual print undershot everyone: payrolls contracted by 23k.
- Unemployment fell to 4.1% when JPMorgan's pre-print call had it rising to 4.3%. The mixed signal softens the recession read even as the payroll count shrank, which keeps the benign rate-cut story intact for now.
- Short-term yields were already sliding into the print, with the 2-year down to 4.24% (JPMorgan), and they eased again on the number this morning. Falling short-term yields are the market pricing Fed cuts closer, which is the fuel this rally has been running on.
- Second-quarter earnings delivery is the cushion under the trade: blended earnings growth above 47% year over year with profit margins at 16.7%, an expansion JPMorgan attributes to Mag7 earnings. The rally is not running on hope alone.

This is a bad news is good news tape. A shrinking payroll count pulls rate-cut bets forward, yields fall, and the biggest growth names benefit first because their valuations are the most sensitive to rates. The early tape is following that script.

The counter is the leadership gap. The Nasdaq rebounded, but high-beta tech (the names that swing hardest with the market) and the former AI leaders have not confirmed the move, the Nasdaq 100 is still stuck below the top of its recent range near 30,000, and the cost of insuring the biggest cloud builders' debt against default kept rising through the recovery. A rally the leaders refuse to join is a rally the bulls still have to prove. Against that, JPMorgan's desk argues the momentum unwind has already run its course and money is rotating rather than retreating, trimming financials for industrials while staying long the Mag7 and Korean chipmakers. Watch yields first, then breadth, then AI credit: continued yield relief keeps the trade working, the former leaders joining the move or the Nasdaq 100 clearing 30,000 confirms it, and insurance costs on AI-linked debt need to stop widening before the all-clear. Long $NVDA while the yield relief holds.

### Metals momentum is feeding on itself

*The gold and silver breakouts are now self-reinforcing, with systematic buyers forced in and strategic buyers refusing to sell.*

Goldman's trend-following model flipped positive on silver after the August 5 close, forcing rules-based funds that were short to buy back in, and options flow swung to call buying as demand for downside protection faded. Gold has the structural bid: Chinese gold ETFs have logged 14 consecutive days of inflows, the longest streak since March, and Goldman calls continued central-bank diversification the main driver of its constructive outlook. The move is confirming live, with $GLD tracking its best week since January, positioning that came into August washed out, and the August-to-September window seasonally one of gold's strongest. The tension is a single level: silver has to clear and hold $64, and a failure there or a break back below its 50-day average (the rough two-month trend line) puts the squeeze on hold. Long $GLD, with $SLV as the higher-octane expression while $64 is in play.

### Retail is rotating, not leaving

*Retail flows are rotating out of software and into memory and semiconductors, which reads as renewed AI appetite rather than exit.*

Retail investors dumped software at the fastest weekly pace on record, led by heavy selling in $MSFT and $PLTR after earnings, while buying memory and chip names like $MU and $AMD (JPMorgan flow data). Retail call-option volumes pulled back from all-time highs even as institutional call buying in the S&P 500 stayed hot, and SpaceX saw its first retail outflows since June 23. The risk to the bullish read: this is profit-taking dressed up as rotation, and it lands exactly where leadership is already failing to confirm the rebound. Until that resolves, the flow says own what retail is accumulating. Long $MU and $AMD over software.
