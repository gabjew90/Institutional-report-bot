# Nobody priced this cycle

## 1. RECAP

The Fed raised rates for the first time since 2023 and then said it is not finished, and it is the bond market, not the stock market, taking that literally. Half an hour into the session $SPY is down 0.46% and $QQQ is flat, which is not how an index reacts when it believes two or three more increases are coming. Crude is firm, $USO up 0.96%, with traffic through the Strait of Hormuz still running under its ten-day average. Crypto is the one place with a real move, $BTC up 5.30% since last night. Positioning going into today's quarterly options expiry is as empty as it has been all year.

- **Yields took the message first.** The 10-year Treasury yield is at 5.00%, up 4.9 basis points (one basis point is one hundredth of a percent), the 5-year is up 5.3, and $TLT is off 0.53%. Standard Chartered lifted its three-month target for the 10-year to 5.0-5.25%, and the market got there on the first morning after the meeting.
- **Crypto ran on its own.** $BTC is up 5.30% since 00:00 UTC, $ETH 4.84% and $SOL 7.14%, while stocks are flat to lower and yields are higher. A move that size against that backdrop is crypto trading on its own flows, not on a change in risk appetite.
- **$2 trillion of options exposure settles at today's close.** The dealer hedging that has been absorbing moves in both directions, worth $3.8 billion at current prices, rolls off with it (Goldman). $VIXY is already up 1.53% ahead of it, which is the only part of the market pricing anything for what happens once that cushion is gone.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The Fed started a cycle the market still refuses to price

*The market prices 95 basis points of increases over the next year, and the record of past cycles says that is far too little.*

- Deutsche Bank fit all 15 Fed hiking cycles since the mid-1950s against the inflation rate each one began from. Their 3.55% September inflation forecast maps to more than 200 basis points of increases in the first year, against the 95 priced. Traders are underwriting the shallowest cycle on record at the moment the Fed raised its own destination.
- 16 of 18 policymakers now project at least one more increase this year, against half of them in June (UBS), and the median estimate of where rates settle in the long run went to 3.2% from 3.1%. The committee moved the finish line in the same meeting it moved rates.
- Goldman shifted to a second increase in October while Deutsche Bank has December and March. That is a difference of months in when the next increase lands, not a disagreement about whether it lands.
- August retail sales rose 1.2%, beating the 0.8% economists expected (Goldman). Consumer demand is not what stops this.

Warsh called the move removing a dose of accommodation rather than the start of a fight, but the committee also deleted its language about supply shocks driving prices. That is the Fed saying out loud that it will no longer look past an energy-driven rise in inflation, and that one edit turns every oil headline into a rates headline.

The counter runs through UBS, which calls the four increases now priced into the curve excessive. The cycle record answers it: the 10-year yield has risen in the first year of 11 of the last 12 tightening cycles, by an average of 114 basis points, and it has already covered part of that ground before the second hike is even on the table. The short $TLT read this pulse has carried since late August was built on hawkish projections and roughly even odds of an October move, and yesterday delivered both, so that one paid.

October 28 is the next checkpoint, and the inflation reports that land before it are what decide the odds. Goldman has year-end inflation at 3.2%, 20 basis points under the Fed's own 3.4%, and reads that gap as room to pause after the October increase rather than skip it. A meaningfully cooler print is what takes the second increase back out of the curve, and it is the only thing on the calendar that can.

### The barrel is setting the bond market

*Crude, not AI, is moving yields right now, and one Saudi pipeline decides the next leg.*

Goldman sizes a one-to-two month outage of the 3.5 million barrel a day East-West pipeline at roughly $5 on fair value, which is why headlines that the line was being restored knocked crude down 3% in a session. Positioning is the vulnerable part. Speculative funds added $9.3 billion of Brent exposure through the first week of September, seventy percent of that traders closing bearish bets rather than new buyers coming in, and net long positions now sit at the 88th percentile of their two-year range with oil options still priced calmly through the whole surge. That is a crowded long with no protection under it. The long $USO read this pulse opened September 14 has lost ground, and the pipeline restarting did that, not demand fading. Does the East-West line reopen before the Houthis take another run at Saudi infrastructure? Nomura is long energy equities and long commodities as its best risk-adjusted trade and is buying October options rather than chasing the price.

### The AI build-out moved onto the bond market

*Spending is still accelerating. What changed is who is funding it.*

Capital spending at investment-grade borrowers grew 65% from a year earlier in the second quarter, and Goldman raised its 2026 forecast for total high-grade corporate bond sales by $200 billion to $2.3 trillion, roughly a quarter of it AI-related. The build is now a borrowing story rather than a cash-flow story, and that is what makes the growth forecast matter. UBS still has AI capital spending going from $900 billion this year to $1.2 trillion next, while BofA has the big cloud companies slowing from above 90% growth to roughly 35%, and the gap between those two is whether the debt gets serviced out of revenue or rolled over. Inside the trade the market has already picked a side, with data centers and the chips in them bought while software and AI-productivity names are sold (Goldman), which cuts against the software-over-semiconductors read this pulse ran yesterday. BofA's concentration math is what to hold onto, because semiconductors alone have to supply 9.6 of the 15.4 percentage points of 2027 earnings growth. UBS has $NVDA as a Most Preferred name on demand it says outruns what can be built, and the next round of cloud spending guidance is what settles which half of this is right.

### Where Goldman and UBS part company on the index

*The split is not about earnings. It is about how fast yields get where they are going.*

UBS has US equities Attractive with an 8,100 target for December and 8,400 by next June, built on 25% earnings growth this year and 14% next. Goldman is neutral over three months and overweight over twelve, and the reason is entirely the bond market. Its work puts the extra return investors demand for owning stocks instead of bonds back at late-1990s levels, and finds that a 10-year yield move of roughly 50 basis points in a month, or 30 in two weeks, is historically what turns equity returns negative. The 10-year has already covered 4.9 of those 30 this morning. BofA frames the same tension through inflation, with current equity prices consistent with 1.7% against its own economists' 3.2% forecast for this year. Speed is the falsifiable part. If the 10-year adds 30 basis points inside two weeks, Goldman's threshold is hit and the three-month neutral call was right. Short of that, UBS's 8,100 stays the base case into December.

### Positioning is empty going into the biggest expiry of the quarter

*Nobody is long, and $2 trillion of options exposure settles today.*

Goldman has systematic trend-following funds holding $37.5 billion of US equities, the 22nd percentile of the past year, with the index sitting within 1.1% of the level that flips those funds from buyers into sellers. Hedge fund net market exposure is at the 5th percentile of a year, so the discretionary crowd has no length to defend either. Both of those sit on top of a settlement that removes the dealer hedging that has been damping every move in both directions, which is what makes the next move violent in whichever direction it goes. Being wrong on the upside costs a squeeze off the most washed-out sentiment reading of the year. Being wrong on the downside costs more, because Goldman models $199 billion of systematic selling over the following month if prices fall, with no cushion left to absorb it. The level that decides it is Goldman's flip point 1.1% below spot, where the selling starts mechanically rather than on any headline.
