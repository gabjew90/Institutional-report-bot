## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Desks are buying the chip flush

*The chip selloff since mid-June was a positioning washout, not the top, and the desks are stepping in to buy it.*

- Goldman's hedge-fund book shows global semiconductor net allocation, which is just how much of the book is tilted toward the sector, ran from a tenth of the book at the start of the year to a record 24% in June and is back to 19% now. That is 80% of this year's net buying already reversed, so the crowded trade has been cleared out.
- The US-semis slice walked the same path in Goldman's book, 7% to a record 14% to 11% now, still elevated versus its own one-year history but well off the peak. This is real money trimming, not a retail panic.
- The semiconductor sector took a peak-to-trough fall of more than 15% and is posting its fastest recovery from that depth in over 30 years. That snapback speed says dip-buyers are already active.
- Texas Instruments posted a strong quarter and guidance, which JPMorgan reads as the analog-chip recovery (the older, non-AI chips in cars and factory gear) still inflecting up. The corner of chips furthest from the AI-GPU story is turning higher too.
- J. Safra Sarasin keeps $NVDA at Strong Buy as its best-of-breed AI name, so conviction on the leader never wavered through the drop.

The setup is a cleared-out trade meeting demand that never actually broke. Hedge funds spent June piling into chips, got shaken out in the back half, and the names fell faster than the story changed. With the book already flushed, there is less forced selling left to feed a deeper leg down, and the soft tech action this morning is Wednesday's Tesla miss doing the work, not anything in the chip demand picture. The bear read is that valuations did the work, not fundamentals, and a single big cloud builder cutting its capital-spending guidance at next week's Meta or Microsoft prints would pull the floor out. Set against that, the spending curve is still being marked up, not down. JPMorgan models 2027 capital spending near $300B each for Google and Amazon and $200B for Meta, with big investors already penciling $325-350B against a $250B analyst consensus. A guide-down would have to crack a curve that keeps getting revised higher, and June's washout means the last forced sellers have mostly already sold. Long $SMH into the late-July mega-cap prints, with $NVDA the one name the desks never cut.

### The real AI shortage is power and cooling

*The buildout money is landing hardest on the companies that feed and cool the racks, and that is a cleaner risk-reward than the chips themselves.*

- GE Vernova lifted its year-end backlog target to 125 GW from 110, with power backlog already at 53 GW from 44, and Goldman keeps a Buy at a $1,268 target. That is contracted order growth, not a hope trade.
- Bernstein pegs liquid cooling at 30-40% of new data-center capacity and rising as AI server racks run hotter than air cooling can handle, worth $1.3-1.8M of cooling gear per megawatt of capacity.
- Data-center landlords $DLR and $EQIX both hold Bernstein Outperform calls (targets $232 and $1,222) on their ability to deliver capacity on schedule.

This is the same demand wave behind the chips, one layer down where the pricing is less stretched, and the news flow keeps feeding it, with a White House official flagging that China's Moonshot AI got hold of Nvidia's newest chips despite the export ban. The counter Bernstein raises on itself is that cooling economics get commoditized as Google's in-house designs spread across the industry. The backlog is already signed, though, and the content per megawatt keeps climbing even as those in-house designs spread. Long the power-and-cooling names, $GEV, $VRT, and $ETN, rather than chasing the chips higher.

### Oil is pricing a supply scare the curve has not confirmed

*Thinning tanker traffic through Hormuz is a real supply risk, and the cheapest way to own it is outright, not as a spread.*

- Tanker crossings through the Strait of Hormuz are averaging 10 a day in July (UBS), down from the mid-to-high teens in late June and 50 in February. Physical flow is quietly tightening.
- A full Hormuz closure would pull 20.5 million barrels a day of supply (UBS), and Raymond James models up to 6 million barrels a day at risk from a Houthi blockade of Red Sea ports.
- Brent, which UBS pegged near $95 at a six-week high, is climbing again this morning on fresh buying with $USO +5.88% and $BNO +4.55% on the overnight escalation, though a momentum gauge (RSI) reading of 68 is the most overbought since April, so the move is stretched short-term.

Oil is doing exactly what the physical picture argues it should, and prices are moving live this morning after Houthi forces said they struck Saudi tankers and US strikes on Iran ran a 12th straight night. No desk has staked a firm price call on Brent, which is the gap a trader can lean into. The cost of being wrong on a ceasefire headline is a fast $5 to $8 give-back in Brent. The cost of missing a real closure is a supply hole that refills over months, with China already drawing down its own stockpiles by about 1 million barrels a day. Long $BNO outright, not the $BNO-versus-$USO spread where the daily churn of rolling futures contracts drowns the signal.

### Tariffs are climbing the ladder again

*The effective US tariff rate is stepping higher through legal side-doors, and the margin hit lands before the headlines fade.*

- Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act, a rarely-used provision that skips the normal review process, now carries a 50% rate on Canadian imports, announced Monday (UBS, Raymond James).
- Section 301 rates of 10-12.5% are expected on roughly 60 countries by Friday, which nudges the effective US tariff rate (the average duty actually paid across imports) to 10.6-10.8% (UBS).
- A 200% tariff on generic drug imports was announced Tuesday and takes effect August 1 (Goldman), with UBS seeing a 100% rate stick on generics if production does not move onshore by 2028.

Nobody in the corpus stakes a direction, but the read-through is plain: a higher effective rate is a margin drag and a small push to the price level, and it shows up before the negotiation noise settles. The open question is whether the rate holds above 10% into the July 30 GDP and inflation prints or gets bargained down first. Gold pulled back this morning with yields firmer ($GLD -1.99%), which is a cleaner entry than a chase. Long $GLD as the hedge on the policy-uncertainty and inflation read.

### Own the index without the top-heavy bet

*Froth is showing up across desks, and the play is the index without the concentrated top, not cash.*

- The froth calls are coming from a Tier-1 desk, not the fringe, with BofA circulating notes like "Time billionaires want more risk. Is AI to blame?"
- Retail risk-taking is picking back up again (Deutsche Bank), while Bernstein questions whether the data-center build pipeline is even viable, so the caution signals are stacking under a market that leans on a narrow cohort.

That flag is cashing this session, with $VIXY +5.36% and the crowded top rolling over. The cost of being wrong is giving up a little upside if the mega-caps keep leading. The cost of being right is sidestepping the air pocket when the top-ten unwind. Long $RSP over $SPY, the equal-weight S&P 500 that owns all 500 names in equal size rather than leaning on the biggest ten, paired with a small $VIXY position as crash insurance rather than going to cash.
