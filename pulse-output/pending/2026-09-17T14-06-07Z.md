# Bought the hike

## 1. RECAP

Stocks are bouncing back from Wednesday's Fed selloff. $SPY is up 0.97% and $QQQ 1.42% in the first twenty minutes of trading, and $VIXY is down 4.02%. The stranger move is in bonds, where the 10-year Treasury yield has slipped from just above 5.00% to 4.95% the morning after the most hawkish Fed meeting of this cycle. That says more about how heavily traders were already positioned for higher yields than about anything the committee said. $GLD is the biggest gainer on the board at +2.19%, which is the market saying that tightening into an oil supply shock does not fix the inflation problem it was aimed at. $BTC sat the bounce out entirely, flat at $76,267 while equities rallied.

- **The Fed hiked a quarter point Wednesday afternoon to a range of 3.75% to 4.00%, matching what economists expected.** The hawkish part was the projections published alongside it, where 16 of 18 officials put at least one more increase on the board before year-end and the committee's long-run rate estimate rose from 3.06% to 3.25%, the highest since 2016 (JPMorgan). Stocks sold off into Wednesday's close on it.
- **August retail sales ran far hotter than expected, out Wednesday at 8:30 AM ET.** Sales excluding autos rose 1.39% against the 0.6% economists wanted and the headline rose 1.24% against the 0.8% expected, both reversing July declines, and the 10-year finished Wednesday just above 5.00% with the hike fully locked.
- **Oil is lower on peace headlines.** Trump said Wednesday evening the Iran war is hopefully towards its end and US officials met Houthi representatives in Oman over the weekend, taking $BNO down 2.07% and $USO down 1.22%, though only three ships transited the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday.
- **The Bank of England decision landed at 7:00 AM ET and the first desk reads are still coming in.** Consensus had it holding at 3.75% even with UK inflation at 3.1%, and what matters for a US trader is whether a central bank can stand still while the Fed hikes, with the UK 10-year already at a post-2007 high of 5.37% (Deutsche Bank).

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The hawkish hike was the bond market's relief

*The Fed delivered the most hawkish outcome available and Treasuries rallied anyway, because everyone who wanted to be short already was.*

- The Fed raised its policy rate a quarter point to 3.75-4.00%, with 16 of 18 officials projecting at least one more increase this year (Goldman Sachs). The increase itself was never the question. The projections were.
- The committee's long-run rate estimate moved from 3.06% to 3.25%, the highest since 2016 (JPMorgan). That is a permanent markup on what money costs rather than a cyclical adjustment, and it lands on every valuation model that discounts future cash flows.
- Odds of an October hike now sit at 51% (Goldman Sachs). With two more moves in the market's pricing, a pause becomes the surprise that rallies bonds instead of the base case.
- Deutsche Bank had bond positioning at the 5th percentile going in, as one-sided a bearish bet as 2018 and 2022. There was almost nobody left to do the selling by Wednesday afternoon.
- Goldman raised its year-end 10-year yield forecast from 4.40% to 4.75% when it added the hike. The house that just turned more hawkish on policy still sees the 10-year finishing the year below where it traded Tuesday.

Hiking into a hot inflation number is what a central bank does when it still has credibility to spend, and the bond market took it that way, even though the energy supply shock driving that inflation is the one thing a rate rise cannot fix. The selloff that carried the 10-year above 5% Tuesday, its first time there since October 2023, ran on the fear the Fed had fallen behind. A quarter point plus a higher long-run estimate removed exactly that fear, and the yield is back at 4.95% this morning.

The short $TLT read this pulse has carried since August 31 was built on the 10-year sitting just under 5%. It broke above 5% Tuesday and the read paid.

Where the two largest houses part company now is timing. Goldman has the second hike in October, arguing the projections and the 51% pricing make it the path of least resistance. JPMorgan reads the same statement and keeps its second hike in December, pointing to growth forecasts revised up and a lower 2026 jobless-rate path. The fight matters less than it looks, because both agree on where rates finish, with Goldman holding its end-of-cycle range at 3.25% to 3.50% and adding a third cut in 2028. What decides the trade is whether the inflation data between now and then keeps forcing the issue. The October meeting settles Goldman against JPMorgan, and the 51% priced into it is the number that says whether this week's bond rally is bears closing out or a real turn.

### Saudi Arabia's shut pipeline is doing the Fed's job in reverse

*The supply hole in Middle East crude is why this hiking cycle exists, and one de-escalation headline does not close it.*

Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline is shut after attacks, putting roughly 4% of global supply at risk (Goldman Sachs), and the export math has already broken rather than being forecast to break. Crude leaving Yanbu ran 1.1 million barrels a day in August against 4 million from April through June, and Saudi production fell to 6.2 million barrels a day from 10.9 million in February. The shortage has moved past crude into the fuels people actually buy, with diesel margins at a record and European gas storage at 68% against an 84% five-year average (ING). That is why oil and the 10-year yield now move almost in lockstep, at a 0.96 correlation, and why an energy story is a rates story this cycle. The bear case showed up Wednesday evening with the President saying the war is hopefully towards its end, $BNO is lower on it, and Goldman's desk is already buying downside puts in oil against exactly that. If Yanbu is not back toward 4 million barrels a day by the end of October, the price pressure the Fed just hiked into is still sitting in the next round of inflation prints.

### The AI trade split in two and the money went to software

*The AI selloff is a rotation inside the theme, not an exit from it.*

JPMorgan's software-versus-semiconductor basket moved 12.1% on Monday, the largest single-day move in its history, which is the market repricing which half of the AI trade gets paid rather than whether it gets paid at all. Goldman's AI basket sits 45% below its high, the steepest fall since ChatGPT launched, and the Philadelphia semiconductor index trades at 21 times expected earnings against 33 times in June (UBS). The two biggest houses read that opposite ways. JPMorgan is buying the decline across Mag7, semis and memory on the view that the pullback does not turn into falling revenue or thinner margins, while Goldman's desk recommends puts on its own AI Beneficiaries basket and tells clients to buy AI hardware anyway. Spending settles it, and UBS has global AI capital spending at $900 billion this year rising 33% to $1.2 trillion in 2027, against an objection from the lending side that the buildout is financed too loosely. If the next round of guidance from the big cloud companies in late October does not come down, the fall was a rotation and the discount on semis closes.

### The rotation a hiking cycle is supposed to pay, and has not yet

*Tightening cycles have historically rewarded value stocks and punished the crowded growth trade, and that has not started.*

Bank of America launched a 12-month S&P 500 target of 7,800, roughly 2% above where the index trades, and paired it with two rotation calls rather than an index view. The value basket rests on the record that cheap stocks beat expensive ones while the Fed is tightening. The call on property landlords carries more conviction, with $AHR at a $71 target and $WELL at $292, built on the pricing power those owners gain as new construction slows. The backdrop is an index that has gone nearly six months without a 5% pullback while earnings concentration in a handful of tech names sits at a record and investor cash on the sidelines is near an all-time high of $8 trillion. The honest objection is that a tightening cycle is what usually kills a rate-sensitive landlord, and Bank of America names higher-for-longer policy as the risk on both calls itself. If the index gets through the October earnings run without a 5% pullback, the rotation stops being a correction trade and has to win on relative earnings alone.

### Friday's expiry is where the cushion disappears

*What is holding this market steady is mechanical, and it rolls off Friday.*

Friday is September monthly options expiry. Dealers who hedge S&P options are sitting net long $9.9 billion right now, which means they have been selling into strength and buying weakness and muting every move (Bank of America). Next week they are net short $0.7 billion across expiries, with the September 18 expiration alone contributing negative $1.9 billion of that swing, and below 7,600 they get pushed the other way, adding to whichever direction the market picks rather than damping it. Trend-following funds hold $123 billion of equity exposure with their first selling trigger at 7,642, and the corporate buyback window shuts this week for roughly 40% of the S&P 500, so the standing bid thins out in the same week the hedging flips. Institutions are already paying for that, buying October and November VIX calls at strikes 28 to 34 (Cboe). The position bleeds premium if nothing happens, against three mechanical sellers stacked below 7,642 with the buyback bid gone.
