# War premium bleeds out

## 1. RECAP

Markets are half an hour into a risk-on session and the whole thing traces back to one weekend headline: the White House pulled back a planned strike on Iran and put talks back on the table. The extra price every asset carried for the risk of a closed Strait of Hormuz is coming out of all of them at once. $SPY is +0.83% with $QQQ lagging at +0.63%, so the bounce is not being led by the crowded tech names that broke last month. Yields fell across the curve and crude is down hard. Crypto sat this one out, with $BTC near $63.2k and $ETH -1.44% on the day.

- **Trump cancelled the strike on Iran to chase a deal instead.** Crude handed back a chunk of July's spike inside the first half hour, with $USO -6.67% and $BNO -5.68% session-to-date. Tehran said this morning it has no current talks running with Washington, and markets ignored the denial.
- **Long-term Treasury yields got their first real relief in a month.** The 10-year yield fell 5.5 hundredths of a percentage point to 4.69%, the 30-year fell 4.6 to 5.23%, and the 5-year fell 5.7 to 4.40%. Energy was the belt running geopolitics into inflation and then into long bonds, and it is running backwards. The 30-year still yields 54 hundredths of a percentage point more than the 10-year, so the steepening problem is loosened, not solved.
- **July's positioning wreckage is the backdrop to this bounce.** Semiconductors had their worst month since 2008 and the momentum trade, meaning the crowded winners that funds pile into together, took its worst four-day drawdown on record. Retail flows into tech hit the 0.8th percentile of the past year (JPMorgan), close to the thinnest reading in twelve months. $QQQ trailing $SPY this morning says the money coming back in is not going back to the same names.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The war premium is coming out of crude

*The single trade that defined July, long oil against a closing strait, is unwinding in real time, and the inflation math built on top of it unwinds with it.*

- Brent is down more than 5% this morning after the White House cancelled a planned strike following appeals from Gulf allies (Deutsche Bank), and $BNO is -5.68% session-to-date. The single biggest driver of how markets traded in July is now running in reverse.
- Brent rose 23.6% in July, the best-performing major asset of the month (Deutsche Bank). The move now unwinding is the one that did the damage in the first place.
- Headline PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred price gauge, ran 3.67% year over year against 2.80% in February. That acceleration was an energy story, which makes cheaper crude the fastest available route to softer inflation prints, faster than anything the Fed can do.
- Odds of the Strait of Hormuz normalizing rose 8 percentage points in a single session (JPMorgan), and Iran is negotiating transit service fees with Oman rather than a closure. A toll booth is a cost. A blockade is a supply shock. Markets spent July pricing the second one.

The strait was never actually closed. What the market priced through July was the tail where it might be, and the moment Iran started haggling over the price of passage rather than the fact of it, that tail got cheap in a hurry. Energy was the transmission belt from geopolitics into inflation into 30-year Treasury yields, so pulling the premium out of crude loosens all three at once, which is exactly what is happening this morning.

The risk in taking the off-ramp at face value: Iran is broadening its target set and taking delivery of Chinese weapons, which JPMorgan reads as a risk profile wider than current pricing reflects, and Tehran keeps publicly denying that any talks exist. A regulated strait still moves oil, and that holds even in the ugly version of this story, which is why Standard Chartered's $70 to $90 WTI band treats noisy de-escalation as the base case and a move above $100 as the tail. Standard Chartered's $100 line is the level that would put the premium back on. Do not chase crude down here, and fade $USO strength on the next flare-up rather than buying it.

### The 30-year bond has no anchor

*Today's yield relief is an energy story. The reason the 30-year lost its anchor in the first place is still sitting there untouched.*

Warsh's FOMC press conference pulled forward guidance without explaining what replaces it, and 30-year yields repriced straight to new highs on a steepening curve, meaning long yields rising faster than short ones. JPMorgan pulled its next hike forward to December 2026, lifted its year-end 10-year forecast to 4.85% from 4.70% and its 30-year to 5.40% from 5.20%, and is positioned for more of that steepening. Deutsche Bank goes further with a baseline of half a percentage point of hikes this year. Bond volatility has jumped while equity volatility has barely moved, so equity hedges are the cheap way to own rates risk right now. Goldman's line is that the market forces a hike unless data turns unambiguously soft before September, which makes Friday's payrolls the test, not this morning's rally of 5.5 hundredths of a percentage point. Short $TLT into the fall, and a genuinely weak payroll print is what breaks it.

### The rotation out of momentum is not finished

*This bounce in the crowded names is positioning, not fundamentals, and the rotation into everything else is the trade that keeps working.*

The momentum index that strips out sector bets fell 17.4% over four days, the worst on record, and its tech version fell 36% against a prior record of 20% set in 1999 to 2001 (BTIG). Goldman's high-momentum basket sits 23% below its 200-day average, and semiconductor crowding, meaning how many funds hold the same chip names at once, collapsed from the 99th to the 91.8th percentile. The rotation shows up in the returns too, with tech down 3.0% through earnings season while the rest of the market rose 1.0%. BTIG calls the crash finished but wants to fade the resulting 20% rally into the 50-day average, flagging rising 30-year yields as the specific headwind for the names that led. The counter is that the money is already coming back to hyperscalers off the bottom of their long-run range, and $QQQ underperforming $SPY this morning argues it is not there yet. Long $XLV, the defensive JPMorgan keeps overweight, against short $SMH into 50-day strength.

### The cloud bill finally showed up as revenue

*The AI spending argument stopped being about how much and became about who converts it, and two names answered the question this week.*

AWS grew 37% with an operating income beat $3.5B above the high end of guidance (JPMorgan), and Microsoft's cloud sales rose 43% while quarterly capital spending jumped 70%. Aggregate 2026 hyperscaler spend is tracking near $750bn, roughly 80% higher than last year (Goldman), so the bill is real and it is being paid. Meta narrowed 2026 capital spending to $130 to $145B and fell about 10% post-market, punished for showing the spending without the return. The doubt worth taking seriously is funding rather than demand, since a buildout this size eventually has to be borrowed against, and that is a 2027 problem rather than a this-quarter one. Long $AMZN and $MSFT, the two that showed the money. A capex guide-up without a matching revenue line is what would flip this.

### The world's biggest funding trade blinked

*Washington joined Tokyo in buying yen for the first time since 1998, which changes the cost of every position funded in cheap borrowed yen.*

Coordinated intervention landed last week and the yen firmed again overnight, with CME yen futures printing a volume spike above 231,000 contracts on August 1 and 2 as traders who borrow in yen to fund positions elsewhere cut exposure. USD/JPY traded below its 200-day average for the first time since September. JPMorgan reads 160 as the line Japanese authorities will defend and still forecasts 160 in the third quarter and 164 in the fourth, so this caps dollar upside rather than starting a yen bull market, and 157.25 to 157.50 is the first zone to sell dollars into (Goldman). $UUP is only -0.18% this morning, which means the dollar has not priced much of it yet. Short $UUP. JPMorgan's 160 level breaking to the upside and holding there kills the trade outright.
