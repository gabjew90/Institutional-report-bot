# Oil premium drains out

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil scare is unwinding faster than the supply hole closes

*The war premium has drained out of crude, but the physical Gulf is still half-clogged and prices sit below where the two biggest desks see them at year-end.*

- Brent has fallen roughly $13 a barrel over the past ten days to about $73.6, a weekly drop of more than 10% as the Middle East war-risk premium came out (UBS, Goldman). The panic bid is gone.
- Persian Gulf exports are already back to about 66% of normal levels after the Iran deal (Goldman). Flows are reopening, which is what dragged the price down.
- 40 million barrels of Iranian oil have shipped since the deal and 30 million barrels of non-Iranian Gulf floating storage have been released, but an estimated 50 to 100 million barrels are still trapped in the Gulf (UBS). The backlog clears over weeks, not in one headline.
- Q4 Brent is targeted at $80 (Goldman) and $85 by year-end (UBS), both above the current $73.6, so the research says the selloff has overshot.

The gap is between paper and physical. Futures repriced the resolution the moment the deal hit the wires, but the physical market still has tens of millions of barrels stuck behind a Strait that only just reopened, so the supply hole takes weeks to refill regardless of the peace headline. That is why both Goldman and UBS keep year-end targets well above spot even while they acknowledge the premium is gone. The bear case is real: HSBC pegs the post-deal oil decline at around 25%, and if the 50 to 100 million trapped barrels flush out fast into a market that no longer fears disruption, $73 looks generous, not cheap. What argues against that is the physical timeline. Refilling channels and rebuilding the export cadence is a multi-week process, and the options desk shows traders still paying up for upside, with one-month oil calls struck well above spot richening 8 points week-over-week. Adding to the read, the US-Iran talks in Doha this week are running on mixed messages, so even the deal that broke the premium is not fully locked. Long $USO or $BNO into the gap between $73.6 spot and the $80 to $85 the desks are targeting, with $XLE the equity proxy if you want the oil majors instead of the futures roll.

### AI is still the earnings engine even as the leaders cooled

*The AI build is paying the market's profit growth, so the June pullback in the biggest names is a rotation inside the trade, not the end of it.*

- AI infrastructure is on track to drive about 60% of S&P 500 second-quarter profit growth, against an overall earnings-growth bar of 22% year-over-year, the highest pre-season bar since 2021 (Goldman). This is not a side story, it is most of the growth.
- The five biggest hyperscaler shares fell about 20% on average in June even as that profit contribution rose (UBS). Leadership cooled while the fundamentals did not.
- UBS cut its combined chip and hardware weight from 76% to 61% and lifted defensive AI-ecosystem exposure from under 1% to 20%, a move down the supply stack, not out of it.
- Per-unit memory cost for an iPhone is projected to hit $200 by year-end from $50 a year ago (UBS), and the US memory basket is climbing month-to-date (Goldman), so the content growth is concentrating in memory chips.

The counter is capex fatigue, since a 20% drop in the biggest names says the market is starting to ask harder questions about the return on AI spend. The defense is that the spending is broadening rather than stalling, with Korea committing KRW 881 trillion to chip hubs plus KRW 550 trillion to AI data centers and Japan unveiling a ¥101.6 trillion plan. The Applied Materials price-target hike on this morning's watch lists is the same signal at the single-name level, with the bid moving toward the equipment makers that supply the build. Long memory through $MU, leaning into the picks-and-shovels and away from the most expensive leadership names.

### The rotation trade as the AI leaders roll over

*The skeptical desks are flagging the cap-weighted index topping out on the same stretched names, and the play is to rotate into what the AI bid left behind rather than chase the leaders.*

- Goldman's funding-spread indicator (a gauge of how crowded leveraged long positions are) sits at its 99th percentile over five years, the most stretched positioning reading in years, so the crowd is leaning the same way.
- The five biggest hyperscalers slid in June and the largest names trade at their lowest forward earnings multiple since ChatGPT launched, so the leadership is rolling over while the index still leans on it.
- Two desks flag rotate-out angles explicitly, from a software catch-up call to defensive consumer and staples exposure (Goldman, Rabobank), so the dissent is multi-desk rather than a lone bear.

The question is whether the top-heavy index keeps working, or whether the next leg comes from everything the AI trade left behind. If breadth wins, the expression is long $RSP, the equal-weight S&P, paired with long $XLU utilities as the defensive complement, so you rotate out of the concentration without being short the theme.

### Gold is the rebound trade after the first slip in months

*The pullback below a long-held level is a buying level, not a top, because the central-bank buying behind it never stopped.*

- Gold fell below $4,000 for the first time since November and sits about 26% below its January high (UBS), the kind of flush that resets positioning, and $GLD is firming +0.90% this session.
- UBS carries a 12-month target of $5,200 and $4,900 is the end-2026 call (Goldman) on emerging-market central-bank diversification, so both desks see new highs from here.
- Poland bought about 18 metric tons and China about 10 metric tons in May (UBS), so the official-sector bid is still showing up in the data.

The cost of being wrong is a few percent more downside if the dollar firms on oil-driven disinflation. The cost of missing it is the move back to the desks' targets if the central-bank bid reasserts. Long $GLD on dips below $4,000.

### Softer inflation lets the Fed look less hawkish

*The May inflation print took the worst-case Fed off the table, even as the new leadership talks tough on the 2% goal.*

- Headline PCE (the Fed's preferred inflation gauge) came in at 0.4% month-over-month, below the 0.5% consensus (UBS), which UBS reads as support for a less hawkish Fed.
- The market now prices only about a third of a percent (0.34%) of hikes by December (UBS), and the funds rate has sat in a 3.5% to 4.5% range for over 18 months (Goldman).
- Goldman trimmed its year-end headline inflation forecast by about 0.2 point after the Hormuz deal eased energy pressure, and new Fed leadership still reaffirmed the 2% target as non-negotiable (One River), the offsetting hawkish note.

This is about to get tested. Thursday's payrolls print is the next read, with consensus at 110K new jobs against 172K prior, and ISM Manufacturing lands Wednesday. If core PCE keeps printing at or below consensus, the less-hawkish read holds and long-dated Treasuries get relief. If energy-driven inflation reasserts because the oil overshoot snaps back, the thesis is dead. Until then the bias is long $TLT.

### Comcast's NBCUniversal split is the catalyst on the radar

*The corporate separation is a value-unlock event multiple desks are watching, even before anyone has staked a directional call.*

- Barclays, Goldman, and JPMorgan all flag the planned Comcast and NBCUniversal separation as an event worth tracking, multi-desk attention without a consensus view yet.
- Comcast also landed an upgrade on this morning's watch lists, so the name is getting fresh attention into the split.
- The setup is the standard sum-of-the-parts unlock, where splitting a slower cable business from media and streaming assets can close a conglomerate discount.

No desk has a directional bet yet, so this is a watch-and-size situation rather than a high-conviction trade. For those who want exposure, long $CMCSA into clarity on the separation terms.
