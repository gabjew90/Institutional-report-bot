# Nvidia loses its founder

## 1. RECAP

Heading into Monday's open, stocks are coming off a losing Friday led by technology. $SPY closed Friday down 0.36% and $QQQ lost 1.20% as investors stepped back ahead of two AI tests this week, Nvidia's earnings and the Anthropic IPO. $VIXY, which rises when traders expect bigger swings, gained 2.46%. Crypto is weaker this morning too, with $BTC down 1.51% today near $64,400 and $SOL off 2.35%. Fed Chair Warsh speaks at noon, with investors already braced for a rate increase next month, so the week starts with both the largest AI stock and the path of interest rates in question.

- **Nvidia reports Tuesday after the close without its founder.** Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive and left the board, finance chief Colette Kress is interim CEO, and four downgrades plus a wave of price-target cuts are already published. $NVDA was already down about 30% in six weeks after the midterm elections put data center construction limits on the ballot in several states, and $QQQ fell 1.20% Friday ahead of the report.
- **Retail and hedge funds are on opposite sides.** Retail investors have been buying Nvidia at a record pace while hedge funds have cut their net stock exposure to a five-year low. $VIXY rose 2.46% Friday with that gap unresolved going into the report.
- **A December Fed rate increase is fully priced.** October core CPI, which leaves out food and energy, rose 0.3% for a third straight month, and headline CPI rose 0.4% against the 0.3% expected. The 10-year Treasury yield is back above 5%, at 5.14% this morning, the dollar is at its highest since spring 2025, and gold hit a 52-week low last week.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Nvidia without Huang is a valuation problem before it is an earnings problem

*The business is sold out for two years, so the damage from losing the founder shows up in what investors will pay for each dollar of profit, not in the profit itself.*

- Goldman kept its Buy but cut its target to $220 from $300, valuing the stock at 22 times expected earnings instead of 30. The whole cut comes from a lower valuation and none from lower profit forecasts.
- JPMorgan cut its target to $215 from $320 and removed China-related shipments, about 8% of first-half revenue, from its fiscal 2028 numbers. That reflects export-control risk and not the CEO change.
- Four desks downgraded the stock in a week, and two others put a floor near $150, about 9 to 10 times next year's earnings, which would be a 20-year low for that valuation.
- Retail bought $111 million of the stock in the first 30 minutes after the resignation, 40 times a normal open, and Nvidia was 61% of all retail net buying that week (Vanda Research).

Huang personally negotiated the supply contracts and the financing deals that fund Nvidia's own customers. A pause in that financing could remove more than $50 billion from the AI industry by one desk's estimate, and execution risk stays higher for the next 12 to 18 months even with an order backlog that runs 12 to 24 months out (Goldman).

The desks that still say buy point to that backlog. Their case is that the company is sold out for 2027 and most of 2028, and that at about 10 times fiscal 2028 earnings the risk is already in the price. Seaport, with a Sell and a $180 target, answers that customers will use the distraction to shift chip orders to AMD and Broadcom. That rotation showed up the day Huang resigned, when AMD and Broadcom each rose 6% while $NVDA fell 4.5%. Both sides can be right for a while, because locked-in orders protect 2027 revenue and do nothing for the valuation until a permanent CEO is named. Tuesday's report after the close is the first test under Kress. The bar is a modest beat against company guidance of $108.0 billion in revenue and a repeat of the roughly 70% revenue target for next fiscal year, and Goldman holds its Buy with the $220 target into the print.

### Data center spending keeps growing and the bond market charges more for it

AI-related spending reaches a $1.4 trillion annual rate by the end of 2027 in Goldman's forecast, up from $900 billion at the end of this year, so demand is not the question. How it gets paid for is. Big tech companies sold $182 billion of bonds this year against $17 billion in 2024, and the extra yield investors demand on that debt is 140 basis points (1.4 percentage points), the widest in five years (Bank of America). Lenders now charge chipmakers more than the cloud giants for the first time, while the price of options on the same companies' stocks has barely moved. CoreWeave shows the strain at the smaller end, burning $7.5 billion of cash in the third quarter. Goldman's equity outlook has the spending boom peaking in 2027, and Morgan Stanley would cut hardware suppliers and add software companies that use AI. Bank of America's hedge for the gap is a December 2027 put spread on a basket that includes $AMZN, $META and $GOOGL, a position that pays if stocks fall to match what bonds already price.

### The midterms pushed construction later and made existing power scarcer

Democrats swept the midterms, and Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has pledged a moratorium on new data center construction. About 20% of US data center projects slip from 2028 to 2029 in Morgan Stanley's estimate, and the bank kept its forecast for big tech capital spending at $1.6 trillion for 2027, so the money moves later and is not cancelled. The risk to 2027 construction is modest in Goldman's view because most projects in Texas, Georgia and Michigan already hold permits. The trading conclusion from Citadel Securities is that companies already running data centers with secured power gain pricing power, and hardware suppliers face deferred orders. Morgan Stanley raised its estimate of the US power shortfall through 2028 by 15% to 44 gigawatts. Watch in this order: (1) power that is already connected to the grid, where Morgan Stanley recommends owning $VST and $CEG, (2) GPU rental prices, already up 50% in a year for Nvidia's H100 chip, and (3) whether other states copy the Texas moratorium.

### A December rate increase is priced and the argument is over the size

October core CPI rose 0.3% on the month for the third time running, and a quarter-point increase in December is now the base case. SEB puts the market-implied chance of a half-point move at 15% after Fed Governor Waller raised the idea. A third increase in March 2027, which would take the policy rate to a peak of 4.25% to 4.50%, is now in the forecast, with the first cut pushed back to December 2027 (Goldman). The other side comes from Citi's consumer team: record credit card balances of $1.29 trillion and weaker confidence among stock-owning households may stop the Fed short of the three quarter-point increases markets price. The bond market is leaning one way. Trend-following funds are betting against Treasuries as heavily as their models allow (Citi), and last week's $25 billion sale of 30-year bonds drew weaker demand than usual. Does Chair Warsh, who speaks at noon today, endorse a half-point move or talk it down?

### Retail is buying the dip while the Wall Street rotation runs the other way

Retail investors are buying Nvidia at a record pace, and the institutional money is moving away from it. The companies that sell AI hardware saw their median forward P/E (share price divided by expected earnings over the next year) fall to 18.6 from 27.4, and the companies that use AI have beaten them by 26 percentage points in about seven weeks. Chip stocks have room for another 10% drop and the cloud giants are the preferred alternative (JPMorgan), and Goldman's 2027 outlook adds selected defensive sectors, healthcare first. The other side of that trade is mechanical. Trend-following funds flip from selling to buying the S&P 500 above 7,676 (Citi), bets against $QQQ are the largest since 2023 (JPMorgan), and Goldman still estimates those funds sell about $31 billion of S&P futures this week whichever way prices go. Cost of rotating early: missing a rally that forces short sellers to buy back, with the trigger a short distance above Friday's close. Cost of chasing: owning chip stocks into the 10% drop JPMorgan describes.
