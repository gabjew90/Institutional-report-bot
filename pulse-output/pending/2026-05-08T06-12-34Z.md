### The bond market is calling the Fed's bluff into NFP

*Yields refused to come down even as the oil panic faded Thursday, and that is the tell.*

The 10-year Treasury yield is at 4.35% after dropping 8 hundredths of a percent Thursday on the peace headlines, but the 30-year is still pinned at the 5% pain threshold that Bank of America's Hartnett has been pointing to as the line in the sand. Citi's payrolls forecast is -15k against the +65k street consensus, with the unemployment rate expected to tick from 4.3% to 4.4%. UBS revised the May headline CPI projection up to +0.72% month-over-month from +0.52% on retail gasoline pass-through, and one-year forward CPI swaps are now pricing 2.9% versus 2.4% before the Iran flare-up. Citi's base case is three 25 hundredths-of-a-percent Fed cuts (September, October, December) and they call the 20-25% hike probability the market is pricing excessive. TS Lombard estimates the Fed needs to swap roughly $660 billion of longer-dated bonds into short-dated bills to hit its target portfolio, which structurally lifts 30-year yields no matter what the policy rate does. Leveraged accounts are still net short the 2-year per the latest CFTC data.

Three forces are pulling yields higher at once. Oil-driven inflation pass-through into core PCE is a three-month-lag story that just started, the Treasury supply mix is shifting to longer-dated bonds, and the incoming Warsh-Fed framing (cut the policy rate while tightening financial conditions through the repo rate) means the curve cannot rally on a cut headline the way it used to. UBS still sees two cuts in the back half of 2026 and a 4.00% 10-year by year-end. The bear case is a soft payroll print this morning sub-zero a la Citi's call, the 30-year falling back below 5%, and the long-dated bond rally trade reopening as those bonds appreciate (the longer the maturity, the bigger the price move when yields shift). Every weak print this cycle has rallied yields for one session and then faded as the next CPI confirmed the supply-side story is structural. The May 12 CPI and May 13 PPI prints are the gates, and the lean is short-dated Treasuries (2-year and shorter) over the 30-year, with $UUP the asymmetric expression if the dollar bid from this week's strikes holds.

### Iran ceasefire trade just cracked, and oil is the cleanest read

*US strikes on Bandar Abbas reopen the supply-disruption thesis the equity market had already priced out.*

Brent dropped 7.8% Wednesday on the alleged 14-point peace memo and then reversed sharply on the strikes overnight, per JPMorgan. OPEC April production fell to 20.55 million barrels per day, the lowest level since 1990, on Kuwait and Iran disruptions, per ING. US commercial crude inventories drew 2.3 million barrels last week with exports collapsing 1.7 million barrels per day week-over-week. The IEA director called energy markets "troubled waters" and global oil inventories are trending toward record lows with the demand-destruction leg not even here yet. UBS marks Strait of Hormuz reopening odds at 40%, down from 80% two weeks ago, and a $150 Brent scenario would compress Indian fiscal year 2027 GDP from 7% to 6.3%. Goldman's desk has flagged $120 WTI as the level where the macro narrative breaks for equities.

Physical oil markets have been pricing scarcity for weeks (refined fuel inventories at 8-year lows, Europe's jet fuel supply running out by June) while paper futures kept hoping for a diplomatic resolution. Wednesday's memo got equities to the highs of the year and crushed crude in a single session. The strikes overnight reopened the same gap. Shell beat Q1 by 9% and lifted its dividend 5%, Aker BP and Harbour Energy pre-released strong cash flow, and JPMorgan's 2026 Brent average forecast is now $82.10, up 20% year-on-year. The bear case is another headline-driven false start, negotiations resume by Monday, and Brent settles back into the high $90s as Saudi spare capacity reassures the market. The supply scar takes months to heal regardless of the diplomatic timeline (refinery maintenance, sanctions enforcement, OPEC compliance), and the IEA warning suggests Q3 demand destruction is not the end of this story. Long Brent over WTI through $BNO is the cleanest options-friendly expression, and US energy infrastructure names ($EPD, $WMB, $OKE) are the lower-vol way to participate without trying to time a single headline.

### Inflation expectations are quietly resetting higher, and it's the trade nobody owns

*5- and 10-year market-priced inflation rates have re-broken to 2023 highs while everyone is watching spot oil.*

The inflation rate priced into 5- and 10-year bonds is at the highest level since 2023, with December 2026 WTI futures at the high $80s versus $70 in early April, per Bloomberg. The 1-year forward CPI swap is at 2.9%, up from 2.4% before the Iran flare-up, and 5-10 year inflation pricing has widened 40-50 hundredths of a percent since the conflict began. Q1 central bank gold purchases hit 244 tonnes, up 17% quarter-over-quarter, with UBS reiterating a $5,900 per ounce year-end target. The 30-year yield pinned at 5% with $TLT showing a -3 standard deviation move, per Goldman, is the canary that the inflation reset is real. US fertilizer proxy prices (urea, ammonia) are rising and will push food CPI higher with a lag, per Bloomberg. TS Lombard projects inflation broadening in 2027 rather than disinflating, and Fed staff already estimate 0.8 percentage points of core inflation pass-through directly from tariffs.

The mechanism is layered. Energy is the trigger, fertilizer and food are the second-derivative pass-through, and tariff pricing is the structural floor. The market is treating each piece in isolation and missing the regime. Gold is the asset that does not need a narrative because central banks are buying it in bulk regardless of US policy. The bear case is that Iran ultimately resolves, oil settles in the $80s, and the inflation rate the bond market is pricing compresses back toward 2.5%. The central-bank gold bid is tracking real-asset diversification away from the dollar and Treasuries, not just oil, and the structural reset in long-run inflation pricing already happened over the last six weeks. A $5,900 gold target only needs the central-bank flow that is already underway to keep going. For US options exposure, the bias is long $GLD via call spreads (volatility has collapsed, which makes upside structures cheap), with short 30-year Treasury exposure as the pair if conviction is that 30-year yields stay pinned near 5%.
