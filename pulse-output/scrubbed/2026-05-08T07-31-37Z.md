## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex moved from forecast to arithmetic

*The hyperscaler infrastructure story is showing up in revenue lines now, and the latest prints are dragging the second-wave software names with them.*

Datadog's +31% Thursday afterhours print is the cleanest tell that the AI build is now showing up in revenue lines, not guidance. Snowflake and MongoDB ripped in sympathy because the read-through is direct. When cloud-infrastructure software prints like that, it means the hyperscaler capex from the last two quarters is converting into customer compute spend on the platforms above it. Anthropic at $30B annualized revenue (up 80x year-over-year, with a SpaceX partnership announced to use the Colossus 1 cluster) is the same picture from the model-layer side. Arm could capture 40-45% of server share by 2030 (Goldman), the structural shift underneath the cycle.

Bulls argue this is the second cycle of capex revisions higher in a month, with breadth across the infrastructure stack and S&P 500 Q1 EPS growth running at 24.6% year-over-year, the fastest in four years. The risk Goldman's One-Delta desk flags is bubble-shaped. SOX 14-day RSI is above 80 (a level rarely seen in 30 years), the SOX sits 56% above its 200-day moving average, and the top 10 NDX names average 784% gains over the last year, which is above the dot-com peak comparable. The fundamentals defense is concrete though, because Applied Materials and Tokyo Electron are sold out into 2027, so the cycle has runway left before any guide-down lands. Long $AMAT into the next capex revision cycle.

### NFP at 8:30 is the next test of the rate-cut path

*Sell-side desks reach three different answers on Fed direction, and this morning's print decides which one is closest.*

The dispersion is the signal. Citi forecasts -15k against a Street consensus of +65k, JPMorgan and Natixis sit at +50k, and UBS is at +100k. When the range runs from negative prints to triple-digit positive in the same week, the data has stopped being noise and started being decisive. The market is currently pricing roughly 20-25% probability of a Fed HIKE in 2026, which Citi's rates strategists call excessive. UBS's two-cuts-in-2H26 view treats current "no cut" pricing as too hawkish, while a Warsh-led Fed could deliver a cosmetic funds-rate cut while tightening through the repo channel.

A weak print this morning lands directly into the long-dated bond argument and you get a fast move in $TLT before the curve recalibrates. The pushback is the inflation side. UBS's own May headline CPI projection is 4.44% on gasoline pass-through, with April core CPI at 0.37% versus the 0.33% Bloomberg consensus. Bloomberg's commodity-versus-Treasury ratio is signaling re-accelerating inflation regardless of the Iran outcome, and the inflation rate priced into 5-10 year bonds is up 0.40-0.50 percentage points over the past month. Hartnett at Bank of America is watching the 30-year at 5% as the line that, if broken durably, opens the door to real equity damage. The defense is that rates are too high for current data, and a soft NFP this morning is the cleanest path to that revaluation. Long $TLT calls into the print, sized for the dispersion in consensus rather than the consensus itself.

### Hormuz peace deal cracked overnight

*The market priced 11% downside in Brent on a memo Iran was still reviewing. Strikes on Bandar Abbas in the last twelve hours invalidate the framework before it was signed.*

Brent had fallen as low as $96 earlier in the week on the 14-point MoU framework before stabilizing around $100. Then the US struck Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas Thursday evening, Iran accused Washington of breaking the ceasefire, and oil futures gapped higher overnight. The physical setup never matched the financial setup. OPEC production fell to 20.55 million barrels per day in April, the lowest level since 1990, on Iran and Kuwait disruptions. US commercial crude inventories drew 2.3 million barrels last week, and OECD product stocks remain well below five-year averages. Even a signed deal does not resume Strait of Hormuz transit immediately because mine-sweeping and crew rotations take weeks to months, and Iran has demanded $270B in reparations as part of the framework.

Bulls of the supply-shock trade argue the inventory hole takes months to refill regardless of the headline cycle. Mizuho's strategist explicitly called for fading peace-deal optimism, citing structural concessions (Hormuz access plus nuclear facilities) too steep for Iran to actually accept. The risk that breaks the trade is a hard, durable ceasefire announced in the next 48 hours combined with Iran fast-tracking the stockpile transfer, which would compress oil through the headline channel even if physical scarcity persists. Given the overnight strikes, that path looks narrower this morning than it did at Wednesday's close. Long $BNO into the next escalation headline.

### Momentum unwind is the rotation tell, not a buy-the-dip

*Goldman's High Beta Momentum factor fell 8% Wednesday, a top-ten one-day move over the last five years, driven by both legs unwinding at once.*

The factor's 8% drop on May 7 hit both legs simultaneously. Software outperformed earnings expectations on the long side while secular AI themes sold off after an extreme run, and the high-momentum shorts ripped because positioning was crowded. Momentum positioning sits at the 99th percentile over one year and the 100th percentile over five years in Goldman's hedge-fund positioning data. Long-only investors showed "capitulatory stop-ins" in semiconductor and AI names following a 50% rally since early April. That symmetry is the reason this reads as a rotation rather than a top, because when both legs of a momentum book unwind together at extreme positioning, what typically follows is redistribution into higher-conviction names rather than a clean pullback.

Bulls point to Goldman's historical pattern where aggressive momentum unwinds precede "buy the dip" opportunities for top themes, and the underlying earnings story remains intact at 24.6% EPS growth with all 11 sectors positive. The bear pushback is that 99th-to-100th percentile positioning is exactly the setup that produces a real correction when the macro catalyst lands. A weak NFP plus the Hormuz reflare plus SOX RSI above 80 is the combination that takes positioning down hard, not the kind of micro-shake that produces a buy. The durable signal is what the long-only money is doing under the surface, moving out of speculative memory like SanDisk and into platform AI exposure with cash flow attached. Long $MSFT, paired with avoiding the most-extended speculative single-names.

### Gold squeeze setup is rebuilding into a tight market

*Light positioning, a technical breakout, and a $5,900/oz UBS year-end target give gold the cleanest right-tail in this multi-asset stack.*

Non-commercial gold positioning has been in hibernation since February, which means there is a real pool of capital that has not yet expressed a view at current prices. Gold cleared a short-term downtrend line and a wedge formation this week, with central bank buying still the pillar of the bid. UBS's $5,900/oz year-end target is the directional confirmation from a Tier-1 desk and it is already on the books, not a stretch revision. The Bloomberg market-based inflation gauges signaling re-acceleration are the same gauges that argue against $TLT working cleanly post-NFP, which makes gold a better expression of the same inflation read.

Bulls argue gold has recently traded inversely to the VIX, which is unusual, and a reconnection to the historical positive-correlation relationship would force a catch-up rally. The bear case is that a successful Hormuz resolution removes the geopolitical inflation impulse, which would compress gold through the same channel that took oil from $110 to $96 earlier this week. That risk is partial because, even before the Iran shock, central bank demand and dollar-debasement narratives carried the bid, so the geopolitical premium is incremental rather than load-bearing. Long $GLD as a hedge against rate volatility around NFP.
