## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil supply hole is real and ceasefire headlines will not fill it

*The Strait of Hormuz closure has knocked a structural hole in global oil supply, and the inventory math says it lasts months regardless of the diplomacy.*

- SEB pegs the global oil inventory drawdown at 9 to 10 million barrels a day, meaning the world is burning through stored oil far faster than it can replace it
- Roughly 6 million barrels a day of refined fuel previously moved through the Strait, a flow that is hard to reroute and represents a lasting loss, not a blip
- The IEA measured a 117 million barrel global stock draw in April alone, confirming the squeeze is showing up in the hard data
- Brent crude is still near $110 and WTI near $108 even with de-escalation headlines crossing the wires, so the physical market is not buying the peace talk
- SEB frames the building blocks of a global stagflationary shock, slow growth and rising inflation together, as falling into place

Oil that came out of storage and strategic reserves bought time, but those barrels are a one-time cushion, and once they are gone the shortfall hits the spot market directly. Bulls on a price collapse argue a single ceasefire announcement or a breakthrough in the US-Iran nuclear talks could snap oil lower fast, since a chunk of today's price is fear rather than physics. That case got fresh air overnight as oil dipped on Trump ceasefire comments. The problem is that Iran answered by threatening war "beyond the region" if the US attacks, the war is now treated as a live backdrop rather than a passing scare, and refilled inventories take months of normal flows to rebuild even after a deal. Long $BNO into the next supply headline.

### AI capex is real, but the trade is dangerously crowded into tonight's print

*The spending boom on AI computing power is structurally intact, yet positioning has gone so one-sided that Nvidia's earnings tonight are a single-point risk for the whole index.*

- Consensus points to 87% earnings growth for the AI infrastructure complex in 2026, and Nvidia carries April-quarter estimates of 80% revenue growth and 120% profit growth (UBS)
- The semiconductor index has rallied roughly 70% since its late-March lows, adding more than $5 trillion in market value, and sits about 60% above its 200-day average price, the furthest stretched since 1999 (Goldman Sachs)
- Bank of America's fund manager survey names "long global semiconductors" the single most crowded trade in the market at 73% conviction
- Hedge fund exposure to momentum stocks sits in the 96th percentile of the past five years, a level of crowding that historically precedes sharp unwinds (Goldman Sachs)
- Options pricing implies a 6% move in Nvidia on tonight's print, so one report can swing the whole market

Compute demand is genuinely supply-constrained and the earnings keep confirming the build, so the long-term call is not the problem. The crowding is. The counter from desks watching the current move is that the melt-up is finally colliding with the one thing the rally ignored, rising interest rates, and that chip names are likely to catch down to a weaker broad market. The Goldman pushback is that recent chip weakness is profit-taking, not a regime change, citing a strong first quarter and improving productivity. Both can be true, and with Nvidia reporting after today's close, the cleaner expression is staying long the theme through $SMH while owning downside protection through the print rather than chasing it naked. Long $SMH paired with $SMH puts into the Nvidia print.

### The market has stopped betting on rate cuts and started betting on hikes

*Fewer or no Fed cuts is now the base case, the swing has further to run, and the dollar is the cleanest way to ride it.*

- Research from JPMorgan, ING and Berenberg describes a market that has fully unwound its dovish stance and now prices in Fed rate hikes outright
- UBS expects roughly a quarter-point hike over the next year
- The dollar climbed to a six-week high overnight on those rate-hike bets, a direct live read-through to positioning
- Bank of America's survey shows the share of investors naming a second wave of inflation as the top tail risk jumped to 40% from 26% a month ago
- Goldman now expects fewer or no cuts globally, with the Iran war named as the force that re-lit inflation that never fully went away

The transmission is the oil shock feeding straight into inflation expectations, which forces the Fed to hold or tighten rather than ease. The honest counter from JPMorgan is that the bond market has gone more hawkish than even its own Fed forecast, so the repricing may be overdone. The defense is that Goldman leans the other way entirely, and this afternoon's Fed minutes at 2:00 PM ET land into a confirmed leadership transition that gives the hawkish case fresh fuel rather than closure. Long $UUP.

### Thirty-year Treasuries are breaking, and this is a fiscal story, not a Fed story

*Thirty-year yields at multi-decade highs are being driven by government borrowing and rebuilt risk premium, which is why the move has legs the Fed cannot talk down.*

- The 10-year Treasury yield has pushed to about 4.67%, its highest since early 2025 and just under half a percent above its April lows (Rabobank)
- The 30-year yield has settled above 5.12%, a level not seen since 2007 (UBS)
- About 60% of the bond sell-off since the Iran war began comes from real yields rising, meaning growth and supply fear, not just inflation, is doing the damage
- One-month implied volatility on long-dated Treasury options jumped 3 points last week, a clear sign of stress building in the bond market (Goldman Sachs)
- Bank of America's survey shows investors at their most underweight bonds since June 2022, with US federal debt at $39 trillion against a $1.9 trillion annual deficit, about 6% of GDP

The driver is term premium, the extra yield investors demand for the risk of holding long bonds, being rebuilt after years of being suppressed. War-driven inflation lit the fuse, but a flood of new government debt in 30-year Treasuries is what keeps it burning. The pushback is that yields this high eventually pull buyers back in, and Asia and the Middle East still hold trillions in Treasuries. The problem is that being early is not the same as being wrong: the deficit is still widening, there is a fresh multi-decade-high target on the 30-year (Citi), and the supply keeps coming. Short long-dated Treasuries through $TLT.

### A Warsh Fed is the policy wildcard the bond market has not priced

*Kevin Warsh taking the Fed chair is a known unknown, and the minutes this afternoon are the first place that uncertainty gets a price.*

- Bank of America, JPMorgan, Nuveen and UBS all flag Warsh's confirmation as Fed chair as a live policy catalyst, a rare level of cross-desk attention for a personnel story
- The Fed releases the minutes of its last meeting at 2:00 PM ET today, the first formal policy signal into the transition
- One River's market-cap-to-GDP gauge sits at 232%, an all-time extreme, meaning equities have a long way to fall if the policy backdrop turns
- About $3.8 trillion of Treasuries are held by Asia and the Middle East, a foreign bid that a credibility wobble at the Fed could test (Bank of America)
- Markets are already carrying a hike in the next year, so a hawkish-leaning chair would confirm rather than shock

A new chair means an uncertain reaction function, and an uncertain reaction function is itself a reason for volatility even before any decision is made. The counter is that no desk has staked a directional view yet, so this is genuinely unpriced rather than mispriced. That is the point: an unpriced catalyst landing into a market this stretched is a volatility event, not a directional one, and this afternoon's minutes are the first read on it. Long $VIXY into the 2:00 PM release rather than a directional stock bet.
