# Software squeezes crowded semis

## 1. RECAP

Half an hour into Friday's session the index is flat and everything underneath it is not. $SPY sits +0.05% while $QQQ is -0.24%, with chips giving ground after Marvell's report and software holding what it took this week. $VIXY is +1.79% into Fed Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote. The real moves are in crypto, where $BTC is -1.79% and $SOL -4.79% since midnight UTC while stocks have not budged, and in oil, where $USO is -1.37% on the day the US tightened a naval blockade on Iranian exports. Hedge funds came into this week with net exposure at a one-year low after the fastest cut to technology in more than two years, which leaves almost nobody positioned for a rotation.

- **Fed Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote started at 10:00 ET,** minutes before this went out, and no text or desk read has landed yet, so the reaction is what to watch rather than the headline. Rates have not moved on it, with the 10-year at 4.68% and the 30-year at 5.20%, both essentially unchanged from yesterday, and $VIXY at +1.79% is the only thing twitching.
- **Marvell reported Thursday after the close at $0.94 a share, exactly the consensus this pulse published, on $2.74bn of revenue against a $2.76bn bar.** Options had priced an 8.8% move on the Google custom-chip question (JPMorgan) and an in-line quarter does not settle it, so the stock is being sold as a casualty of its own expectations while $QQQ carries the drag at -0.24%.
- **The US has replaced airstrikes with a naval blockade that is cutting Iranian oil exports,** and Iran has published conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz while mediators work the same file. Crude fell anyway, $USO -1.37% and $BNO -1.30%, because the reopening talk is worth more to the price right now than the lost barrels.
- **Crypto broke away from stocks overnight,** $BTC -1.79% and $SOL -4.79% since midnight UTC, with $ETH down less at -0.79%. The selling ran straight through news that should have helped, BitGo buying NYDIG's institutional trading business into a reported pickup in trading activity, which makes this a positioning move rather than a news move.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Everyone is long the chips and short the software that just beat

*Technology's crowded side is no longer Nvidia. It is the software everyone sold to fund it.*

- US software long/short positioning sits at the 4th percentile since 2018 (JPMorgan). Almost everyone who could be short software already is, which turns an earnings beat into forced buying rather than a slow re-rating.
- Hedge funds cut US net exposure by 3 points to 48.3%, a one-year low, and technology took its largest cut in over two years (Goldman Sachs). The natural sellers left before the news arrived.
- CrowdStrike added $333mn of new yearly subscription revenue on Wednesday's print, up 51% and 17% above the $284mn modelled, and raised its full-year target for that line (Goldman Sachs). Goldman moved the stock to Buy at $230.
- Salesforce missed on revenue at $11.35bn against the $11.43bn this pulse published, but the revenue already under contract for the next twelve months grew 14% stripping out currency, and its AI product line passed $1.5bn of yearly subscription revenue from $1.2bn three months earlier (Deutsche Bank). The case that AI destroys software needed that second number to stall and it accelerated instead.
- Retail bought semiconductors and hardware and sold software every week of August (JPMorgan). The flow that has to reverse has not started reversing.

This is about ownership, not fundamentals. Incumbent software vendors are wiring AI into products their customers already run and charging more for the same seat, and the earnings finally show it, but the money that would normally buy that news is committed to the other side of the trade. That is why software outran chips hard enough this week to break a trend that has held for years, in the same week Nvidia beat.

JPMorgan's technology desk is not buying it, and the objection is specific. Salesforce's subscription revenue grew 11% after currency, a point short of what investors wanted, the guidance raise leaned on acquisitions rather than the core business, and the desk sees real interest in fading the post-earnings jump. Roughly two-thirds of S&P 500 companies talked about AI productivity on second-quarter calls and only 2% put a number on the earnings impact, so the fundamental argument is nowhere near settled. It does not have to be. This trade needs the positioning to unwind, and the positioning is measurably one-sided. Goldman kept a Sell on Veeva after a 2.5% revenue beat and still raised the target to $180, which is what it looks like when the bearish side keeps marking its own numbers higher. The question is whether $IGV clears $110 before the crowd that is short software finishes buying it back, or the ratio snaps back to the chip side. Goldman has CrowdStrike a Buy to $230 and Deutsche Bank has Salesforce a Buy to $275, and both calls need that level to give way.

### Nvidia settled the demand question and moved the argument to who pays for it

The read this pulse published Wednesday was a big-investor bar of $94bn to $95bn and guidance of $107bn to $108bn against a $104.5bn consensus. Revenue came in at $96.22bn against the $93.63bn consensus, earnings at $2.22 a share against $2.13, and the October quarter guided to $108bn, so both bars cleared. The forward frame is what moved: management put next fiscal year's revenue growth near 70% against analysts at 44%, and pulled the top five cloud buyers' capital spending to $800bn in 2026 and $1.3trn in 2027, a full year earlier than analysts had it. The objection has shifted to financing rather than demand. The $500bn outside-capital platform and $366bn of future commitments only work if rental rates on older chips hold up, and a ceiling on compute demand would crater those rates and void the long asset lives the accounting assumes (Bloomberg). Gross margin guidance of 74.0% against 74.8% expected is the first crack that way. Goldman has it a Buy to $300 and JPMorgan an Overweight to $320 into December 2027, and Broadcom on Wednesday September 2 is the first real test of whether custom silicon gets financed the same way.

### One market maker just stepped in front of the whole bond market

Citadel Securities flipped bullish on 30-year Treasuries this week, and the argument is positioning rather than economics. Trend-following funds are shorter long-dated Treasuries than at almost any point in their recent history, 2.28 standard deviations past their own norm, and in the sixteen comparable episodes since 2003 that began with yields above 4.5%, yields fell an average of 48 basis points, one basis point being a hundredth of a percentage point, over the following sixty days. Goldman takes the other side, and the case is that the Treasury cannot fix this alone. The buyback program's monthly ceiling only doubled to $4bn against $5.5trn of bonds outstanding, and long-dated yields are being set by fiscal policy running loose into repeated energy shocks rather than by who is buying. The scoreboard favors Goldman so far, with 42 basis points of Fed hikes now priced by June 2027 and Japan's two-year yield at its highest since 1995. Warsh is the resolution and he is speaking right now. JPMorgan's mapped downside is a 3% to 4% S&P drop if he sounds hawkish on rates and the balance sheet at once, which is also the outcome that breaks Citadel's trade.

### The ceasefire headline came and went and the strait is still shut

Wednesday brought reports that a US and Iran framework including free navigation through the strait was close (Goldman Sachs), and oil fell more than 3% on it. Since then the US has swapped airstrikes for a naval blockade on Iranian exports, Iran has published conditions for reopening rather than reopening, and a projectile hit a tanker this week while the diplomacy was being reported (Mizuho International). Commodity vessel transit is running at five against a ten-day average of fifteen (Goldman Sachs). The cross-asset stake is bigger than crude. European gas at 65 euros per megawatt hour is where Asian industrial demand starts shutting down, and 100 euros is in play if the strait stays closed through winter (Goldman Sachs). Energy positioning is crowded after net buying in twelve of the last thirteen weeks (Goldman Sachs), so the unwind runs both ways, which is how crude falls 1.3% on a blockade headline. What resolves this is a signed navigation and transit-revenue agreement, and until one exists the daily transit count is the number to watch instead of the headlines.

### The next food shock is being priced in the grain pits while everyone watches chips

Cereal prices are up 22% on the year through July across wheat, corn, barley and rice, and HSBC expects global grain production to fall below consumption in the 2026/27 season, the first shortfall since 2020/21 and the largest since 2006/07. Wheat jumped 6% this week alone as Russia moved to escalate against Ukraine and Black Sea shipments dropped, putting wheat and corn at three-year highs (Deutsche Bank). Cocoa is up 50% in a few months on weather. The second channel is slower and worse. The Hormuz closure is constraining fertilizer and sulphur supply, which sets next season's yields rather than this one, and JPMorgan has warned the next food crisis will not be a short one. This is a supply story with no demand offset, which is why export restrictions are already appearing and making it worse, and it lands in headline inflation regardless of what growth does. If the 2026/27 balance sheet stops showing production below consumption, the thesis dies with it.
