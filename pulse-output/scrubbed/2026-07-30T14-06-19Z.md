# Forced sellers run dry

## 1. RECAP

Tech is ripping half an hour into Thursday's session and the sellers who caused the damage are out of inventory. $QQQ is up 2.82% and $SPY up 1.17%, with $VIXY off 4.66%, as hedge funds finish the largest three-day cut to position size since November 2022 and the trend-following funds that ride price momentum sit flat across Nasdaq, Korea, Taiwan and Japan. None of that selling was a verdict on earnings. The 30-year Treasury bond disagrees with the mood. Its yield is at 5.21%, its highest since 2007, one day after a Fed hold that three regional presidents voted against. $GLD is up 1.39% and $BTC is holding the $65k area.

- **The Fed held Wednesday afternoon and three regional presidents voted against it.** Rates stayed at 3.50-3.75% with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all pushing for a quarter-point hike, and buyers of 30-year Treasuries read the hold as tolerance for inflation rather than patience. The 30-year yield is up another six hundredths of a percentage point this morning to 5.21% while the 5-year has barely moved at 4.37%, and the sell-off has now run two straight sessions.
- **8:30 AM ET: Q2 advance GDP and core PCE inflation.** Both numbers hit this morning and the first desk reads are still coming in, so watch the reaction rather than the headline. Consensus was 2.1% on GDP and 0.2% on monthly core PCE against JPMorgan's 2.0% and 3.4% core, with Goldman down at 1.8% on trade and inventory effects, and the dollar is softer into it ($UUP -0.63%) with $GLD up 1.39%. The Bank of England also decided at 7 AM ET, consensus a hold at 3.75%.
- **Another round of US strikes on Iran overnight, and oil sold off anyway.** The military says the latest strikes ran two hours across dozens of targets, and Egypt says a drone hit two ships at a Mediterranean port near the Suez Canal. $BNO is down 1.71% and $USO down 1.42% on Oman-Iran talks, even as QatarEnergy bought 33 US LNG cargoes to cover what it cannot ship through Hormuz and Shell posted its second-highest profit on record at $9.8bn on war-driven prices.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The semiconductor selling stopped before the spending did

*The people who had to sell chips are done selling, and the spending cycle they were selling keeps getting bigger.*

- Hedge funds recorded the largest three-day reduction in position size since November 2022, and momentum names fell another 7% in a session (Goldman). That selling was mechanical, driven by risk limits rather than earnings, which means it stops when the positions are gone rather than when the news improves.
- Funds that borrow to magnify the daily move have unwound about 85% of their spring build-up, and trend-following signals in Nasdaq, Korea, Taiwan and Japan have collapsed to flat (JPMorgan). The automatic sellers are out of inventory.
- 2026 hyperscaler capex forecasts, meaning the cash the big cloud companies commit to data centres and chips, moved to $770bn from $758bn in under a month, and 2027 went to $1tn from $925bn (JPMorgan). The cycle being sold is still being revised up, not down.
- Microsoft printed $90bn of revenue, up 17%, with earnings 11% above consensus on a two-point beat in Azure, and Goldman lifted its target to $640. The largest AI spender just showed the spending arriving as cloud revenue.
- Meta went the other way. Free cash flow, the cash left over once the buildout is paid for, collapsed 91% to $784m on $30bn of quarterly capital spending, and Goldman kept its Buy while cutting the target from $815 to $725. Same buildout, opposite cash profile, and that split is the entire argument in two prints.

Two different things happened at once and the market did not tell them apart. Position-driven selling hit every AI-linked name regardless of what its own numbers said, while the spending plans underneath those names were being marked higher, so the exit left prices below where the fundamentals sat rather than at them. $QQQ up 2.82% in the first half hour of Thursday is what that gap closing looks like.

Where this gets dangerous is the credit market. The cost of insuring hyperscaler debt against default has climbed all year, with Oracle's up 0.7 of a percentage point in 2026, and $412bn of AI-linked high-grade corporate bonds have already been sold this year against roughly $250bn more expected from the cloud companies alone (Goldman). That is a 2027 funding problem rather than a this-week problem, and the same desk counts $1.3tn to $1.4tn of unused borrowing room sitting behind it. Lam Research then guided next-quarter revenue to $8.10bn against a $7.13bn consensus, which is not what the front edge of a spending cut looks like. The crack in the story is that the unwind is not finished everywhere, with Leopold Aschenbrenner's AI-focused fund reported this morning to be unwinding trades and facing possible forced liquidation. Long $SMH, and take it through defined-risk call spreads (buy a call, sell a higher-strike one, so the most you can lose is the premium) the way Bank of America is structuring it rather than outright, because chip volatility is running at ten times the S&P 500 and the outright position gets stopped out of a thesis that is probably right.

### Three dissents and a 2007 yield

*The Fed held, and the long bond called it a mistake.*

The FOMC left rates at 3.50-3.75% with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all dissenting for a quarter-point hike (Goldman), the loudest internal split in years, and the 30-year has since pushed through 5.20% to a level last seen in 2007 while short-term yields sat still. Warsh made it worse by saying rising market interest rates could substitute for further Fed hikes, an open invitation for 30-year bonds to keep selling off. The market prices a September hike near 60%, Goldman expects a hold for the rest of 2026, and a quarter-point hike every quarter from September is also on the table (TS Lombard). If a September hike lands, long bonds get bid and the position bleeds a quarter. If the Fed sits still into JPMorgan's 3.4% core inflation forecast, 30-year yields have no obvious ceiling and every rate-sensitive stock reprices underneath them. What you earn while you wait is 30-year yields rising faster than short-dated ones, which is exactly what happened this morning, with the 30-year up six hundredths of a percentage point against one for the 5-year. Short $TLT.

### Memory got sold to the floor

*The memory washout is retail capitulation, not a demand signal.*

Retail traders dumped $213m net across Micron, SanDisk, Seagate and Western Digital in a single session, 88% of all net retail selling that day, and SanDisk has fallen from a June peak above $2,300 to $1,023. Every major memory name now sits on long-term chart support with the 100-day average in the same area, the point where pressing fresh shorts gets expensive rather than cheap. Samsung's second-quarter operating profit beat estimates Wednesday night on soaring AI chip demand, which is not what a demand problem looks like. Qualcomm cuts the other way, guiding next-quarter earnings to $2.15 against a $2.35 consensus and blaming elevated memory prices for a sequential drop in handset revenue, so the prices hurting the phone makers are the same prices meant to rescue the memory makers. This sector's problem is positioning, not pricing. Long $MU on the support test, sized small, and add $STX and $WDC only once $MU holds.

### Hormuz is still jammed and the oil price keeps forgetting

*Oil keeps pricing a de-escalation that the shipping data says has not happened.*

QatarEnergy has told buyers it still cannot deliver contracted LNG through September, 21 cargoes have been cancelled since the disruption began in April, and it has now bought 33 US cargoes to cover the shortfall, with the first QatarEnergy-controlled tanker in nearly three weeks clearing Hormuz Wednesday night. Brent was above $87 on the last round of strikes (MUFG), yet $BNO is down 1.71% this morning with US and Iranian forces trading fire overnight and a drone hitting ships near the Suez Canal. Goldman's counter is the feedback loop: every spike in energy prices or drop in asset prices pulls a de-escalation response out of the administration, which is why the market looks through the conflict, and why its inflation team already cut the 2026 US headline inflation forecast by 0.4 points on cheaper energy. That call keeps being right, and it is still a bet on politics rather than on barrels. Through the whole position-cutting wave, energy was the only sector where hedge funds did not cut exposure (Goldman). Long $BNO outright rather than against $USO, because the gap between those two is mostly the monthly cost of rolling futures contracts, not the supply story.

### The rotation trade for people who think this is a bubble

*The right answer to a crowd this concentrated is not to short it, it is to stop being only in it.*

TS Lombard calls AI investment the first clear candidate for overinvestment since the subprime boom, with the big cloud companies out of their own free cash flow and the endgame pencilled in around mid-2027. Chipmakers and their equipment suppliers now sit at 14% of the entire US stock market's value. JPMorgan Asset Management calls this the fourth-strongest momentum run since 1995, second in speed only to 1999, with the leaders already showing signs the run has peaked. Investors keep buying anyway because shorting a market that is melting up is painful, which is what a late-stage bubble looks like from the inside. Timing is the problem. Equal-weight $RSP against $QQQ, meaning the version of the S&P 500 that holds every company in the same size against the Nasdaq 100, already sits at a 20-year extreme over 40 days (BTIG), and this morning $QQQ beat $SPY by another point and a half. If hyperscaler debt keeps clearing at current spreads through the 2027 funding wave, the overinvestment call is dead and this was only ever a rotation. Long $RSP paired with $VEA for the developed-market leg outside the US, sized as a partial reallocation rather than a short.
