## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex super-cycle is being confirmed by the prints

*The infrastructure build is no longer a forecast. Q1 earnings and Datadog's Wednesday afterhours blowout are confirming that the cash going in is sticking, and that's what makes the dips buyable, not toppy.*

Datadog's +31% afterhours print Wednesday is the kind of data point that reframes the bear thesis. Bears have been arguing the second-wave software side of AI never shows up in revenue. The print is what showing up looks like, and $SNOW and $MDB caught sympathy bids in extended trade. UBS has S&P 500 Q1 earnings on track for 17% growth, the fastest pace in four years, with AI named as the primary driver. Goldman raised regional 2026 earnings growth to 60% (from 45%) on the same logic. Every part of the supply chain is getting bid because compute is genuinely scarce. Anthropic's $30B annualized revenue figure (an 80x jump) and the SpaceX/Colossus 1 data center partnership are the demand-side mirror of the chip spend.

The bear case Goldman's own desk keeps waving is the dot-com mirror. SOX 14-day RSI sat above 80, rare in 30 years of data, and the index was 56% above its 200-day moving average heading into Wednesday. Wednesday's 8% one-day drawdown in High Beta Momentum (a top-10 single-day move in five years) is what the unwind looks like in real time, and Goldman's read of what hedge funds are doing shows momentum exposure at the 99th percentile over the past year, which means there is real fuel for it. The risk isn't AI failing commercially. It's AI succeeding so completely that compute commoditizes and capacity outstrips demand sometime in 2027.

The defense is order-book visibility. Memory ($MU, $SNDK) is operating with three-year-plus high-bandwidth memory order books, and Goldman's own historical work shows aggressive momentum unwinds inside structural bull themes have served as buy-the-dip opportunities. Korea AI chip exports running +148% year-over-year January through April is the export-data print that says the demand is real, not a positioning artifact. Long $SOXX into Datadog's read-through, with $MU as the higher-conviction single-name expression because the longer those memory order books run, the bigger the upside as orders convert to revenue.

### The 30-year is the line, long bonds are the NFP trade

*Bond traders are pricing the Fed to stay on hold or even hike again. UBS and Citi disagree, and the 30-year Treasury at 5% is the level that decides who wins.*

The 30-year Treasury at 5% is what BofA's Hartnett calls the "Maginot Line" for stocks. A durable break above triggers material equity volatility because at 5%, real money allocators have a yield they don't need to take equity risk to earn. UBS expects two Fed cuts in the second half of 2026 with the funds rate at 3.25% by December, against market pricing that has been hovering near no cuts at all. Citi's rates desk calls the 20-25% probability of a 2026 hike currently embedded in the curve "excessive" and bases case three 25-bp cuts in September, October, and December. The split is whether the bond market's hawkish pricing reads inflation broadening correctly or is overreacting to the energy shock.

The structural pushback is real. Inflation broadening above 3% in 2027 is corroborated by the commodity-versus-Treasury ratio, which is screaming re-acceleration regardless of any peace deal. UBS itself raised May headline CPI to +0.72% month-over-month with a peak of 4.44%, driven by gasoline. A higher CPI regime plus a Fed seen as politicized through the incoming chair raises the term premium, not lowers it, and that's the bond-bear's strongest argument and the reason the 30-year keeps probing 5%.

The defense from UBS's side is that the energy shock is already in the curve and a weak NFP at 8:30 catalyzes the bull-rates scenario. With CPI on May 12 and PPI on May 13, the calendar is dense enough that one in-line print on either side resolves the regime question. Citi's desk has -15k for today's print and JPM's has 50k against the 65k consensus, so even an in-line miss does work here. Long $TLT into NFP. The trade thesis cracks on a sustained break above 5% on the 30-year.

### Hormuz reignites, the supply scar outlasts any deal

*Wednesday's peace memo was the rally fuel. Overnight US strikes and Iranian retaliation are the reminder that the supply hole takes months to close even when the headlines say "deal."*

US military strikes on Iran's Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas at 17:11 ET Wednesday, followed by Iranian missile fire on US navy assets, are exactly the fade pattern Goldman and Mizuho were warning about. Brent dropped 7.8% intraday Wednesday to $96 before stabilizing around $100 (JPM/ING) on the peace memo. Oil futures are bid pre-market on the strikes. OPEC April production fell to 20.55m barrels per day, the lowest since 1990. Kuwait and Iran disruptions are doing the drawdown work, and even a signed deal does not reopen the Strait for weeks because of mine-sweeping and stranded crew rotations. OECD product stocks are well below their 5-year average heading into Q3, which is the structural anchor under the trade.

The bull case for fast resolution is real. Iran is reportedly reviewing a 14-point US memo with a 30-day Hormuz reopening framework, and Polymarket has roughly above-50% odds of normalized Hormuz transit by end-June. If the deal sticks, Brent could revisit the mid-$80s and the inflation impulse fades. Wednesday's price action was pricing exactly that.

But the bull case took its first hit overnight. Goldman remains "skeptical of a quick resolution," and even with diplomatic progress the physical reopening of the Strait runs on a different clock than the headlines. US commercial crude inventories fell 2.3m barrels last week with exports down 1.7m barrels per day week-over-week. Refined fuel inventories falling fast is the floor that keeps long oil exposure paying through the negotiating cycle, not just on a deal break. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline.

### Retail flow is the institutional flow now

*The retail flow signal is something a US options trader can act on directly. The chase is on, and the names being chased are the same exposures Long-Only money is rotating into.*

Retail buying levels sat at the 77th percentile of their 1-year history with single-stock flows at the 81st percentile (JPMorgan Retail Radar). Tech hardware just printed its second-largest weekly retail inflow on record, concentrated in $SOXX, $SOXL, $SNDK, $MU, $INTC. The signal that matters is that speculative call activity is so extreme that $MU, $INTC, and $SNDK each trade more call volume than $SPY itself. Goldman Long-Only flows are rotating into the same AI infrastructure exposures, which means retail is leading the trade rather than fading it, a regime change from the 2021 retail-as-contrarian setup.

The pushback Goldman is making is on concentration. With hedge fund momentum exposure at the 100th percentile over the past five years and AI themes up 35-55% since April, this is the kind of trade that gets violently unwound on a single capex guide-down from META or GOOGL. Wednesday's 8% drawdown in High Beta Momentum was a preview of how fast it comes when one name cracks. Dealers across the street are positioned so they have to buy strength and sell weakness, which compresses realized vol in indices but amplifies single-stock moves on idiosyncratic news, exactly the regime where positioning unwinds happen in single names before they hit the index.

The defense is the multi-year visibility on the memory side. $MU and $SNDK are operating with three-year-plus HBM order books, and Korea AI chip exports running +148% year-over-year January through April is the export-data confirmation that the demand is structural, not a positioning artifact. Long $SNDK calls. The order-book visibility is what keeps the name paid even through a positioning purge.
