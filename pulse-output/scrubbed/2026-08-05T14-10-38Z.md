# Peace trade meets FOMO

## 1. RECAP

Markets are trading risk-on again Wednesday, with a drafted Hormuz peace deal and a full-blown momentum chase pushing prices higher at the same time. Half an hour in, $SPY is +0.60% and $QQQ +0.36%, an early move extending a squeeze that has already pushed short covering to levels not seen since 2020 and demand for upside options to records. The strange one is $GLD, ripping +3.46% this morning after scaling a one-month peak Tuesday, helped by peace hopes easing inflation worries. Oil keeps bleeding, $USO -0.96% early, while $BTC sits quiet near $64,400. The yen is drifting after last week's coordinated Tokyo-Washington intervention, the first since 1998.

- **Hormuz draft deal on the table.** Washington and Tehran held what the White House called an all-day negotiation Tuesday, and the proposed terms reportedly give Iran control of inbound Strait traffic with outbound flows routed through Omani waters. Crude has shed roughly 10% since Friday on the prospect.
- **ISM services printed at 10:00 ET** (consensus 54.5, prior 54.0). The number hit this morning and the first desk reads are still coming in, so watch the reaction rather than the headline.
- **Stock bears are capitulating.** The Nasdaq 100 just logged one of the ten most bullish stats of the past decade, with one index watcher saying "pessimists threw in the towel and optimism was running amok." That squeeze, not fresh conviction buying, is what this morning's rally is extending.
- **Houthis attacked a Saudi tanker in the Red Sea overnight.** Oil bounced briefly on the headline Tuesday night before the slide resumed, which tells you the market is trading the peace deal, not the shooting.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI trade did a U-turn and the chase is still early

*The market spent July pricing AI capex as a liability, one earnings week repriced it as the asset, and positioning has not caught back up to that flip.*

- Cloud backlogs sit near $1.7 trillion and are growing more than 150% year over year against capex growth of about 80% (JPMorgan). Demand for the computing is compounding roughly twice as fast as the spending, which inverts the return-on-investment fear that drove the July selloff.
- The buildout has a second year: 2027 hyperscaler capex is forecast to grow another 65% on top of 2026's 100%-plus pace (JPMorgan), and memory chips for AI servers are seen doubling in price next year (Goldman). A spending cliff is not in any bank's numbers.
- The two prints that flipped sentiment: $AMZN posted 37% AWS growth and raised capex to $220B, and $MSFT grew Azure 43% once currency swings are stripped out, drawing a reiterated $600 target from Morgan Stanley. The spend converts to revenue, not just promises.
- The chase is measurable: SPX index call volume hit a record 4 million contracts and the gap between put and call pricing saw its largest two-day collapse in nearly a decade (Goldman). Investors are paying up for upside, not protection.
- The re-rating starts from a discount, not froth: the S&P technology sector trades near 20x forward earnings, the 1st percentile of its one-year range and below its 23x ten-year average (Morgan Stanley).

Earnings removed the reason people sold, but July's forced selling removed the positions, so the recovery is running on a market structurally under-invested in its own biggest theme. The buildout is also widening beyond chips. Schneider grew organic sales 17% against consensus near 11% and Vertiv raised its 2026 organic growth guide to 31%, the power-and-cooling side of the same capex, and Washington's reported move to ban Chinese optical transceivers pushes datacenter networking orders toward US names like $MRVL and $COHR. Palantir makes the monetization case in miniature: revenue accelerated to 93% growth and drew a fresh Buy upgrade on the back of it.

The strongest objection is Goldman's own warning that more than $100B of chip exposure sits in total return swaps, borrowed-money structures that can force selling if momentum turns, and that last week's rally was mostly short covering rather than fresh long buying. Hedge funds have layered shorts on the broad AI theme at the 99th percentile of history even while keeping their longs (Goldman), and the worry got a live illustration this morning when the CEO of a bank that financed the fund called the Situational Awareness hedge fund meltdown a warning shot for leveraged markets. The rebuttal is in the flow data itself: the three-day burst of funds cutting longs and shorts at once was the largest on record (Morgan Stanley), total borrowed exposure sits at only the 18th percentile of the past year (Goldman), and the desks tracking it say the forced selling ran faster and further than they expected. The fuel for another washout mostly burned in July. Long $MSFT and $AMZN, the two names whose results carry the thesis and whose analyst support is freshest.

### The forced selling in chips looks finished

*Semiconductors just took a washout severe enough that the sellers are now the shortage.*

The July damage was historic: the SOX chip index fell 21% in a month, one of its worst in two decades, erasing $1.5 trillion of market value with retail selling in tech running 80% above the prior record. Funds cut their borrowed chip exposure faster than expected (JPMorgan) and the hedge-fund accounts Goldman services flipped net short on global semiconductors for the year, yet buyers never left: semiconductor ETFs took in a record $13B in a single month even as the index fell. The tell is whether the SOX clears its 21-day average near 12,000, the level flagged as first resistance. Long $SMH into that test. Losing Nasdaq 100 support at 26,182 (Pictet) is the line that says the bounce failed.

### The momentum crash is following a well-worn script

*The wreckage in the market's hottest stocks fits a historical template, and the template says bounce before it says bottom.*

The momentum factor, a basket of the market's hottest high-flyers, fell 35% in 26 trading days, almost exactly matching the average initial decline of past boom-bust episodes (BTIG). In those episodes the median recovery was a 35% bounce over 14 days, which maps to a retest of the 50-day moving average by mid-August. The caution is seasonal and macro: August and September are the two worst months for the average S&P stock since 2010, and a fast move higher in 30-year Treasury yields would cut the bounce off regardless of positioning. Four straight Nasdaq sessions of 1%-plus gains, a setup seen only 17 times before, say the market is following that script so far. Long $QQQ into the 50-day retest.

### The rotation trade: out of crowded US consensus, into unloved Europe

*Bearish expectations for Europe look peaked, and nobody is positioned for the region to surprise.*

Euro-area data is beating forecasts while positioning in the euro is heavily short, and even the house making the bullish case calls its own 0.6% growth forecast for 2026 gloomy (TS Lombard). Earnings back the data: European second-quarter profits are tracking 14.4% growth against 11.5% expected, and the US valuation premium over the rest of the world has shrunk to 22%, the lowest in over six years. A Hormuz deal helps Europe most, since the region wears the energy shock. The pushback is technical: the Euro Stoxx 50 is testing resistance at 6,430 with momentum fading under the surface (Pictet), so the entry is late-summer sloppy. Cost of being wrong: a few percent lost to a range. Cost of missing it: record-short euro positioning unwinding with nobody aboard. Long $FEZ.

### The Hormuz peace trade is live

*Oil's scarcity premium is unwinding on a deal that exists mostly on paper, which makes the follow-through the whole trade.*

Crude has dropped roughly 10% since Friday as negotiators drafted an interim arrangement, possibly a 60-day deal that could be announced as soon as today (JPMorgan, citing Treasury Secretary Bessent), with Iran keeping control of inbound shipping and outbound flows routed through Omani waters (Mizuho). Overnight's Houthi attack on a Saudi tanker in the Red Sea bought only a brief bounce, and $USO is lower again this morning, so the market is trading the deal rather than the shooting. Desks split on durability, not direction: skeptics doubt a lasting US-Iran equilibrium but advise against fighting the momentum (Mizuho), while Deutsche Bank warns that markets have seen plenty of false dawns and a failure to announce the interim deal imminently is what invalidates the move. Short $USO on a signed deal with confirmed tanker follow-through, and treat the $70-75 support zone (JPMorgan) as where the easy money in the short is gone.
