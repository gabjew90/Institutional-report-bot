# The snapback nobody trusts

## 1. RECAP

US stocks are treading water half an hour into the session while the real action sits in commodities. $SPY is flat at +0.08% and $QQQ is a touch red at -0.12%, digesting this week's violent snapback in beaten-up chip names rather than extending it. The mover is oil. $USO is up 2.66% and pressing a six-week high as an 11th straight night of US strikes on Iran, thinning Hormuz shipping traffic, and Rubio's line that Tehran is not serious about talks drain the ceasefire hopes that had been capping crude. Gold is riding the same safe-haven bid, with $GLD up 1.73% to a two-week peak. $BTC is soft near $66k, down about 1% overnight. Stocks are quiet on the surface because the fight underneath them is not: desks are split on whether Tuesday's chip bounce is a bottom or a bull trap, and the loudest positioning voices are leaning defensive.

- **Oil is pressing a six-week high on Middle East escalation.** $USO +2.66% and $BNO +1.72% as US strikes on Iran ran an 11th straight night, Hormuz vessel crossings fell further, and Rubio said Iran is not serious about talks. The ceasefire premium that had been leaking out of crude is being rebuilt.
- **Gold hit a two-week peak.** $GLD +1.73% on the same haven bid plus a softer dollar, even as the 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to 4.64%. Money is paying up for protection rather than chasing yield.
- **Positioning sits at a record extreme.** Sentiment gauges are as stretched as they have ever been, and after Friday's options expiration the market lost the positive dealer hedging flow that normally absorbs selling. That removes a cushion, so any renewed chip weakness has less to soak it up, which is exactly why the defensive camp is loud this morning.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The record momentum bounce is a fade, not the floor

*Tuesday's record snapback in beaten-up winners is a relief bounce inside a bigger unwind, not the all-clear to chase chips.*

- **A violent bounce, not a new trend.** The momentum basket, the recent market winners traded as a group, jumped more than 9% Tuesday, its best day in five years, right off a 33% drawdown (Goldman). Snapbacks that size are the signature of forced selling exhausting itself, not fresh buying showing up.
- **The buyers look like chasers.** Dip-buying came through leveraged ETFs, with the 3x semiconductor fund $SOXL trading a record share of $SPY's dollar volume. That is retail-style chasing, the opposite of the patient real-money accumulation that marks an actual bottom.
- **Positioning is off the highs but not washed out.** Hedge-fund borrowed exposure on the big prime brokerage desks is still elevated at 312.5%, the 82nd percentile, and the beaten-up momentum pair sits 32% below its highs (Goldman). There is more to unwind if this rolls over.
- **The dampener is gone.** $SMH sits 20% below its record, and with dealer hedging flipped after Friday's expiration, chip weakness now feeds on itself instead of getting cushioned.
- **The bull case is real but early.** Tech investors have already cut info-tech exposure hard, and technology ETFs pulled a 3.1 standard-deviation inflow last week, far above normal (JPMorgan). Forced de-risking like that often front-runs a recovery.

The selloff started as a forced deleveraging in Korea's memory chipmakers and spread into US tech through the tight link between the momentum basket and the AI trade. A group that just fell a third popping 9% in a session is what oversold bounces look like, and the flows driving this one are levered ETF dip-buying rather than real-money accumulation.

The other side, held by Goldman's top tech trader, is that the drawdown is the opportunity, since bottom-up fundamentals are strong and forced de-risking usually front-runs a recovery. That read needs volatility to calm down, and it has not. The momentum group is swinging about four times as hard as the S&P 500, a record gap, which is why Goldman's own derivatives desk says the complex is still a couple of weeks from a real bottom. Until the bounce clears its old highs on heavy volume, it is a place to sell. Long $SMH puts, or long the Mag 7 against short semiconductors (BofA).

### AI capex became a debt story

*The buildout is now funded by borrowing, and the first crack shows up in credit spreads, not the stock charts.*

AI capex is on track for about $870bn by the end of 2026, and the new wrinkle is how it gets paid for. The five biggest hyperscalers are set to issue roughly $190bn of debt this year, up from $40-50bn in 2022 (JPMorgan), while their free cash flow is already turning negative. The first crack shows up in credit, not the charts: the cost to insure investment-grade tech debt has climbed to 87bps from 59bps last September (BofA). What masks the risk is that these stocks are barely moving together right now, a near-record low that hides how much shared borrowing sits underneath them. If they start trading in lockstep again, index volatility snaps higher and the Nasdaq's fear gauge with it. Long an August $VIX call spread.

### China is collapsing the price of intelligence

*Chinese open-weight models are gutting the cost of AI, and value is migrating from the US model owners to whoever runs and applies them.*

Moonshot's Kimi K3 landed July 17 at 95% of US-model capability for roughly 10% of the cost (Goldman), the same shock that wiped $600bn off Nvidia in a single day when DeepSeek launched in January 2025. Markets felt it right away, with the Mag 7 down 1.8% and chips lower on release day (Deutsche Bank). The bull counter is that US frontier models keep a scarcity premium through closed, paid access. The July 29 hyperscaler prints settle it: a capex guide higher that defends the return on AI spending kills the commoditization scare for now, and any guide-down confirms it. Until then, the value is shifting to whoever runs and applies the cheap models, not the US owners. Long $BABA (JPMorgan Overweight, $200 target) into the July 29 slate.

### Oil's physical hole versus the paper price

*The oil futures curve is pricing a clean resolution the physical market is not delivering.*

Oil is climbing again today, with $USO up 2.66% and pressing a six-week high as Hormuz shipping traffic collapses, down to roughly 12 crossings a day from about 110 before the conflict. The futures curve still is not buying it: the base case for Brent sits near $80 while the tail case models $100 to $120 on a prolonged disruption (Goldman). The physical picture is tighter than the paper price admits, with US crude inventories at 43 days of supply, the lowest in 45 years, even as investors have cut their working oil-price assumption from $86 to $71 (BofA). Positioning and the physical data point in opposite directions. The bear case is that one ceasefire headline snaps the premium overnight, so keep this small. Long $BNO, sized light because the headline risk cuts both ways.

### The record-extreme positioning trade

*With sentiment pinned at a record extreme, the summer trade is to get defensive while everyone else buys the dip.*

BofA's Bull and Bear gauge sits at 9.6, a record, and last week $119.6bn fled cash, the biggest outflow since April, while $25bn poured into emerging-market stocks, the most since April 2025 (BofA). Readings this stretched have historically paid to fade, and the playbook is defensive: own defensives, dividends, and the dollar over chasing the chip bounce. The catch is the long-bond leg. With inflation still firm and the 10-year yield at 4.64%, right at the 4.65% breakout trigger (BofA), owning long-dated Treasuries is the wrong side of the trade here. So skip the bond leg and play defense through the dollar, which also hedges the oil-and-rates shock if the Middle East premium sticks. Long $UUP.
