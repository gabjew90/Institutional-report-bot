## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex is driving the rally, and Cisco just confirmed it

*Every time the market doubts the AI build, a print like Cisco's lands and the doubt evaporates.*

- Big-four hyperscaler 2026 capex (Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta) at roughly $700B (UBS), now roughly 100% of those firms' operating cash flow. Capital that used to fund buybacks is buying GPUs.
- Anthropic's annualized revenue run-rate hit $45B, up from $9B six months ago (UBS). The application-layer monetization bears keep saying is missing is showing up at the largest model vendor.
- Cloud growth at the leaders accelerated to 40% with a $2T backlog (UBS), so the spending shows up in revenue with a lag, not just on the capex line.
- Cisco's +14% session on a "networking supercycle" guide-up is the latest single-name confirmation that the build-out is widening beyond GPUs into networking, memory, and power.
- AI-related stocks up roughly 50% from March lows (UBS), with the 2030 AI data-center systems market estimate raised to $1.7T from $1.4T.

The mechanism behind the rally is simple. Four buyers with effectively unlimited balance sheets are pulling forward years of compute spend, which lands in semis, memory, power, real estate, and storage in roughly that order. UBS's $700B 2026 capex print equates to a full year of operating cash flow getting redirected from buybacks to GPUs, and the cloud-revenue acceleration to 40% with a $2T backlog (UBS) is the monetization showing up on a lag. Cisco's blowout this morning extends the breadth, the networking layer is now guiding higher too, and that confirms the trade is bigger than $NVDA alone.

The bear case has two specific shapes. One is that the rally is sitting on two concentrated bets where an unwind in either takes down the index (Bloomberg/Nomura). The other is UBS's own pushback, capex without visible application-layer monetization stops being enough to drive stocks higher, and a deceleration in cloud growth or an IPO supply wave would be the trigger. The defense is that cloud acceleration to 40% and Anthropic's $9B-to-$45B run-rate ramp are exactly the monetization prints bears keep asking for, and Cisco's print this morning says the build is broadening into the second-derivative names. Long the picks-and-shovels stack via $SOXX, paired with $AMAT for memory and process-tool exposure that hasn't yet gapped the way $NVDA has.

### Oil is a supply story banks are bracing for but not chasing

*The physical market is hand-to-mouth, the forward curve is pricing resolution, and equity desks are barely engaged today.*

- Dated Brent (the physical-delivery price, what refiners actually pay) at $117/barrel with spot Brent around $104, roughly 30% above pre-war levels (UBS).
- Global oil inventories have drained to 7.8 billion barrels from 8.2 billion in February (UBS). BCA flags depletion running at 11-12 mb/d against 14.5 mb/d of production losses, meaning the physical market is hand-to-mouth.
- LSEG measured peak Middle East shut-in output at 10.8 million b/d in May, up from 10.5 million in April.
- JPMorgan's Q2 Brent forecast is $103/bbl on a June 1 Hormuz reopening, with a year-end 2027 target at $64/bbl, roughly 20% below the forward curve. The Tier-1 read is that the supply scare fully unwinds.
- Another suspected tanker attack off Oman overnight (India condemned), the war is in week ten with no resolution in sight, and the White House is now publicly scrambling for gas-price relief per Reuters.

Two forces are pulling oil in opposite directions. Inventories are draining fast enough that even a ceasefire takes months to refill product stocks, which is what's keeping Brent bid in the physical market. Forward curves are pricing a clean resolution, which is why JPMorgan's $64 2027 target is consistent with the steep backwardation collapsing the moment Hormuz reopens. The skeptic's view is that ten weeks in with Iranian headline inflation at 73.5% (SEB), Tehran has political pressure to escalate, not de-escalate, and the tanker attack overnight is exactly the kind of event that keeps the supply premium sticky.

The defense is that the cumulative cost of war (SEB estimates $300B to $1T against Iran's 2025 GDP of $371B) puts a hard ceiling on how long this can drag on. $USO +0.33% and $BNO +0.34% with another tanker incident on the wires tells you positioning is already long the scare and traders are waiting for a clean break either way. Long $XLE outright into the next Hormuz headline. The energy ETF gives you the supply-premium exposure without the daily noise of the oil futures, and it's where the next OPEC surprise lands first.

### Consumer resilience is a contested call with a three-month clock

*Retail Sales internals beat this morning, but the underlying labor and inflation data say the cushion is thinner than the headline suggests.*

- Retail Sales Control Group +0.5% (est +0.4%), Ex Autos +0.7% (est +0.6%) this morning, the consumer is still spending into tax-refund season.
- PNC tracks 3.4% YoY growth in average checking/savings balances. Households are not yet drawing down savings to fund consumption.
- The same PNC dataset flags 26% YoY gas-spending growth into May, almost all of which is the Iran-driven pump-price pass-through, not real demand growth.
- UBS has US unemployment at 4.34% and edging up in April, with headline April CPI at 3.8% driven by rents and fuel. Real spending power is leaking, not building.
- Retail trading volumes are up 28% since mid-April with $12T in self-directed equity assets, roughly 10% of total US corporate equity market value, meaning the retail bid into stocks is real but is cannibalizing other discretionary spend.

The mechanism is the lag from labor softening to consumption. Unemployment ticks up first, savings balances hold, then real income compression catches up six to nine months later. PNC explicitly puts the tax-refund tailwind at roughly three months before it fades, which puts the inflection point in mid-summer. The bull case is that the retail-trading volume surge plus the 29% rally in Goldman's "retail favorites" basket is the consumer voting with their brokerage account, not their grocery cart.

The pushback is that 20% of total US trading being retail-driven is a wealth-effect story bounded by the equity rally itself. Break the rally and the floor breaks with it. Tuesday's PPI shock (+1.4% MoM, +1.0% Core, both five-to-six times consensus) makes that breakage more likely, because it puts CPI pass-through back in the conversation and squeezes the Fed-cut path Warsh inherits. Long $WMT into next week's print as the trade-down beneficiary, the same setup PNC's resilience read points to and the kind of name that holds up when discretionary cracks.
