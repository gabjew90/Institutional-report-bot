## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI wobble was a positioning flush, not a fundamental break

*Friday's selloff hit the crowded AI trade, but the spending cycle behind it is broadening and still has room to run.*

- TS Lombard pegs global AI capex at $800B for 2026, and Franklin Templeton sees US hyperscaler spend going from roughly $700B in 2026 to $1 trillion in 2027, so the budget driving the trade is still climbing, not rolling over
- Alphabet's $85B equity raise reinforces upside to 2027-2028 capex (UBS), and the next leg of spend broadens beyond GPUs into CPUs, memory and optics, putting fewer eggs in the $NVDA basket
- Citi's new Vera CPU runs 3x to 6x faster than standard chips on data work, and agentic AI needs 4x more CPU cores per gigawatt of capacity (UBS), so the compute bottleneck is moving down the stack and widening the set of winners
- Goldman's momentum factor (the basket of stocks that have been going up, which funds pile into) sits in the 90th percentile on a 5-year lookback, and the SOX chip index was 76% above its 200-day average on Wednesday, the most stretched since March 2000

The boom is real but the positioning got ahead of it. When everyone owns the same momentum names, a small shift forces a violent unwind as funds dump the same exposures at once. The bear case is the sharpest in the corpus: Goldman's flows desk warns the real risk is not "AI is dead" but dangerously synchronized crowding, and 85% of AI ecosystem revenue is just capex being recycled between the same handful of companies (TS Lombard), so durability needs real outside demand to show up. The tell that yesterday was a flush and not a top is that the S&P stripped of its AI names finished higher even as $QQQ fell 1.15%, so this was funds dumping one crowded factor, not the market repricing the story. Citi reiterated $NVDA at Buy with a $300 target on the Vera Rubin product cycle, treating the dip as a level to add. Stay long the AI build-out through the broader picks-and-shovels names rather than the single most-crowded ticker. Long $SOXX on the broadening thesis, sized to ride the unwind out.

### Hormuz keeps a floor under oil while futures fade the headlines

*The supply risk in the Strait of Hormuz is real enough to keep oil bid, even as paper markets price a quick resolution.*

- Brent bottomed near $93 on June 5 before the latest Israel-Iran strikes, then bounced to $97.80 by June 8 (Natixis), so the geopolitical premium is being added and stripped almost daily
- ANZ Research models a 9.1 million barrel-a-day net supply deficit and assumes 10.5 million barrels a day of Gulf losses if the chokepoint is hit, a hole that takes months, not days, to refill
- Only about 5 ships a day moved through Hormuz as of June 6 (Natixis), a sign the disruption is already live, not hypothetical
- Chinese crude imports fell to 7.8 million barrels a day in May, the weakest since October 2017 (ING), and US energy ETFs closed down yesterday ($USO -2.85%) even with fresh strikes hitting the wires, so soft demand is why futures keep fading the spikes

Two markets are pricing two different worlds. The physical market sees a real supply hole while futures keep betting on resolution because Chinese demand is weak and inventories are building. That tension was live overnight: fresh US-Iran strikes crossed the wires, world shares dipped and oil rose on the headline, yet $USO still sits down on the day. The counter-case is genuine. US-Iran nuclear talks are live across four desks (JPMorgan, Franklin Templeton, Nuveen, Raymond James), and a single de-escalation headline could snap the premium out in a day. The defense is the inventory math: ANZ's deficit and the trickle of traffic through Hormuz say the supply gap is physical and slow to close regardless of the diplomatic noise. For US exposure, long energy through $XLE or oil via $BNO outright rather than a paper-versus-physical spread, because the daily roll noise in those spreads drowns the thesis on any given day.

### The Bank of Japan hikes and the world is underpricing it

*Japan is about to raise rates into a global market that has stopped paying attention, and the spillover runs straight through US bonds.*

- Goldman and HSBC both call a quarter-point hike at the June 16 meeting, with Goldman having pulled its forecast forward from July
- Natixis sees the policy rate going to 1.0% from 0.75%, the highest in decades
- Japan's wholesale inflation ran 6.3% in the latest print versus a 5.6% survey (JPMorgan), the data forcing the central bank's hand
- HSBC notes a JPY3 trillion supplementary budget for energy subsidies, and Goldman expects monthly government bond purchases to settle near ¥2 trillion from 2027, so the era of free Japanese money is ending in slow motion

Higher Japanese rates matter to a US trader because they shrink the gap that has made it cheap to borrow yen and buy everything else. As that gap narrows, the trade that funds risk globally by borrowing cheap yen gets more expensive, which can pull money out of the same crowded US tech names that just wobbled. The cost of being wrong here is small: if the Bank of Japan holds, nothing changes and the trade barely costs you anything to carry. The cost of missing it if right is large, because a hike that revives the yen squeeze can hit US long-dated bonds and momentum stocks at the same time, and that is exactly the pairing that just sold off. Own some protection in $TLT and trim the most crowded equity exposure into the meeting. Long $TLT calls into the June 16 decision.

### What to own when the AI crowd unwinds

*The smart money is de-risking the consensus trade, the froth is coming off the top, and the rotation has somewhere to go.*

- Citi clocked the recent selloff at -1.6% on the S&P and -3.9% on the Nasdaq, with the SOX chip index down 10% in a single Friday session (Bloomberg), so the pain was concentrated in the crowd, not the broad market
- Goldman's prime desk shows hedge fund net exposure (how much funds are betting on stocks rising after offsetting their shorts) at 53.9%, the 66th percentile, with puts cheaper than calls right before the drop, meaning traders were paying up for upside rather than downside protection
- JPMorgan's desk shifted to a cautious near-term view, expecting choppy markets and more weakness in AI and tech, while still saying buy the dip over the next two weeks
- Silver broke its 200-day average and key $68 support, and one desk flags it as a leading indicator for a semiconductor correction, a price-action warning the momentum unwind has further to run

The signal is positioning, not fundamentals. When the crowd is this one-sided and the hedges are this thin, the path of least resistance is down until the leverage clears, and $QQQ leading yesterday's drop while $SPY held to -0.29% is that drain in real time. The bull pushback is that the broad index uptrend is intact, with defensive sectors catching a bid and the May jobs report solid, so this is rotation rather than collapse. That is the point: money is leaving the top-heavy names and looking for a home. The question to answer for yourself is whether the chip index reclaims its 200-day before tomorrow's PPI, or whether the silver-style breakdown spreads to chips. Long the equal-weight S&P ($RSP) over the cap-weighted $SPY to capture the breadth rotation, paired with health care ($XLV), where Citi's $LLY Buy target sits at $1,500 on the Foundayo obesity-drug data for a defensive complement that does not depend on the AI crowd holding.
