## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hyperscaler capex is no longer a forecast, it's the print

*The AI capex story moved from forward guide to accomplished fact this earnings cycle, and the size of the spend is reshaping how the S&P 500 deploys cash.*

Goldman now models hyperscaler 2026 capex at $755B (+83% year-over-year), and that number is not a peak guess. It's a floor when Alphabet's CEO is on record calling himself "compute constrained" and AMAT plus Tokyo Electron are sold out into 2027. UniCredit's $710-725B range tracks the same direction with Meta guiding 2026 capex to $125-145B and Microsoft's June quarter capex now above $40B. The mechanical implication is the rest of the S&P's cash composition gets rebuilt around this spend: hyperscaler buybacks fell 64% year-over-year in 1Q with minimal growth expected through 2027, and IG bond supply hit $205B in April (nearly double street estimates) largely to finance datacenter buildouts. UBS has 1Q S&P earnings tracking +17% growth, the fastest in four years, and the AI infrastructure trade now accounts for roughly 40% of S&P earnings growth per the Goldman desk.

Bulls argue the supply-side is the binding constraint and the spend is contractually locked, so picks-and-shovels names get paid for years. The risk Goldman's desk flags is that one-third of MAG7 profits came from PE investment gains rather than AI revenue, leaving headline earnings cyclically vulnerable if credit tightens or a single MAG7 quarter delivers a capex guide-down. Wall Street already projects hyperscaler free cash flow falls 70% from peak by end-2026 even as earnings rise, because capex eats the cash. The defense: Datadog +31% afterhours and Fortinet +20% on Thursday's prints already confirmed the second-wave software side, and Infineon's FY26 guide-up triggered a 42% Goldman price-target lift on AI power semis. Long $SOXX.

### The leadership baton inside AI is moving from Nvidia to CPUs and memory

*The same dollars that drove $NVDA for two years are now bidding $INTC, $AMD, and $MU. That's what a maturing capex cycle looks like.*

Friday's price action made it explicit. Intel, AMD, and Micron up double digits on the week while Nvidia lagged, and Apple-Intel foundry headlines added a multi-year revenue lever the Street has not modeled into $INTC. The mechanism is straightforward. When hyperscalers sign $1.8B seven-year compute commitments (Akamai/CIS per Goldman) and Meta guides $125-145B of 2026 capex, the order books spread across the stack, not just GPUs. Memory content per AI server is rising fast (the Roundhill memory ETF DRAM has pulled in $5B since its April launch, $1B in one day this week, which tells you where retail is chasing), and CPU-paired-with-accelerator is the architecture cloud providers are buying. Goldman's TMT desk says Microchip beat-and-raised on industrial, mil/aerospace, and datacenter demand, and the Street is under-modeling the recovery.

The bear case is JPM's positioning read. The global semiconductor long/short ratio is now ~4x, well above the prior 2.5x peak, and Goldman's "capitulatory stop-ins" from long-only buyers chasing AI after a 50% rally since early April. If a single MAG7 quarter delivers a capex-guide-down, the rotation that lifted $INTC and $AMD becomes the rotation that crushes them when the whole complex de-risks. The defense is the contractual lock-in (multi-year cloud deals, sold-out fab capacity), so a single-quarter wobble doesn't break the thesis. Long $MU paired with a trimmed $NVDA position.

### Momentum positioning is at five-year extremes and the unwind has started

*High-beta momentum just had one of its ten worst single-day pullbacks in five years, and hedge-fund exposure at prime brokers sits at the 100th percentile. Late-cycle, not early.*

Goldman's high-beta momentum factor (GSPRHIMO) printed -8% in a single session this week, a top-ten one-day move in five years, with hedge-fund momentum exposure at prime brokers at the 99th percentile one-year and 100th percentile five-year. The flow tension is sharp: Syz has hedge-fund offloading of MAG7 at unprecedented pace with net exposure cut to ~17% of North American net, while trend-following computer funds that buy strength and sell weakness are still long and adding into strength and retail is aggressively buying. That setup means any catalyst that breaks the momentum factor mechanically forces systematic selling on top of hedge fund selling, and retail can't absorb both. Goldman's read of "capitulatory stop-ins" from long-only buyers is the late-cycle pattern signature.

Bulls argue aggressive momentum unwinds in AI have historically been buy-the-dip opportunities because the underlying capex story is structural, not flow-driven. That works when the unwind is positioning-only and macro stays risk-on. The risk is the inverse. Tuesday's CPI surprise or a credible MAG7 capex guide-down would convert a positioning unwind into a thesis unwind, and 99th-percentile positioning has nowhere to go but down. Long $SPY May puts as a hedge against the AI book.

### The bond market is pricing rates wrong, and the cuts may come faster

*Markets are leaning toward "no rate cut in 2026" while two cuts in 2H26 are on the table (UBS). Today's NFP did not break the dovish lean.*

UBS calls the current "no rate cut" pricing too hawkish and expects two Fed cuts in 2H26, resting on the argument that core inflation is decelerating even as headline accelerates. Today's private NFP at 123k vs 75k consensus looked hot at the headline, but Goldman's breakeven jobs analysis says the pace needed to keep unemployment stable is falling from 65k toward 50k by year-end as immigration slows, which means even a "beat" overshoots the new lower breakeven, and the unemployment rate held at 4.3%. The bond market read it that way: $TLT closed +0.50% even on the headline beat with $UUP -0.26%, the opposite reaction you'd expect if traders were repricing hawkish.

The opposing read is ING's: if Brent stays elevated on Hormuz friction, headline CPI doesn't fall fast enough and the Fed has to keep rates restrictive into 2027 to avoid a credibility break. The defense is the disinflation already in the pipeline. Credit card balances are rising at the fastest pace since 2022 (Goldman), restaurant earnings from SHAK, BROS, and CAVA all weak, and the consumer wobbling. None of that argues for a Fed that needs to stay tight. Long $TLT into Tuesday's CPI.

### Hormuz is still closed and the inventory math outlasts the headlines

*The market is pricing a US-Iran deal that has not moved a tanker, and the physical supply hole takes months to fill regardless of diplomatic outcomes.*

Goldman's Oil Tracker has Hormuz exports at 4% of normal and Iranian loadings under 30% of 2025 averages, with 45% of the Persian Gulf crude hit being offset by higher US, Americas, and Russia exports, likely from inventory draws, not new production. There's no net offset on refined products: Americas product exports are up 1.4mb/d but the global decline is 1.8mb/d, leaving a structural shortfall. SEB has Brent at $99/bbl post-MoU headlines and explicitly cautions that physical transit takes weeks to months to resume even after a signed deal, citing minesweeping, stranded seafarers, and $270B in disputed claims. China reportedly reselling crude cargoes (Goldman) signals weak domestic demand, not a loose global product market.

Today's news flow makes the contradiction sharper. US forces struck two empty Iranian tankers, Iran seized the Ocean Koi, and a suspected oil spill was spotted near Kharg Island, but $USO and $BNO both closed -1% on peace-deal optimism. The market is trading the headline. The inventory data isn't. Bears argue if Iran signs, paper longs unwind and Brent breaks below $95 fast, and previous rallies on diplomatic leaks have consistently faded. The defense is OECD product stocks well below 5-year averages going into Q3, which makes structural support for Brent above $95 independent of the deal flow. Long $BNO over $USO to capture the physical-vs-paper dislocation.
