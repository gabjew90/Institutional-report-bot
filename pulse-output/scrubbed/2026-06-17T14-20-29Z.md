Markets are grinding sideways this morning with the real action stacked into the afternoon. The spine of the session is geopolitical relief, a US-Iran framework deal that is draining the war premium out of crude and pulling Middle East barrels back toward the market. Reuters data shows Iranian oil tankers already moving past the US blockade after the deal, and crude has slipped near three-month lows. Equities are barely budging ahead of the 2:00 PM Fed decision, with $SPY +0.05% and $QQQ +0.31% session-to-date, while crypto is the soft spot, $ETH down close to 2% and $SOL off 1.7% so far today. The cross-bank color worth holding onto: managers are fully invested but raising cash to 4.1%, crowded into the same global-semiconductor trade at a record 80%, the kind of "long but nervous" setup that unwinds fast when it breaks.

The reaction in US energy ETFs is muted so far, $BNO +0.77% and $USO +0.55%, both below the level that signals real follow-through, so the relief is being priced in the front of the curve, not panic-bought or panic-sold.
- **Trump put the deal on notice.** "If I don't like it, we'll go back to shooting," which is the headline keeping a floor under crude even as barrels return. The market is treating the deal as probable but reversible, which is why energy is drifting rather than collapsing.
- **BoJ hiked to 1.0% Monday night** from 0.75%, the second leg of Japan's exit from near-zero rates. It bears watching for any dollar-funding ripple, though US assets have not reacted.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil relief trade is a paper-market call the physical barrels can't confirm yet

*The US-Iran thaw is a real supply story, but the futures market is pricing the all-clear faster than the physical market can actually deliver it.*

- Brent has fallen to about $83 a barrel, down 4-5% on the deal headlines and off the $126 four-year high hit April 30 (UBS), and the front contract is down roughly $12 on the week per Crédit Agricole
- Goldman cut its Q4 2026 Brent forecast to $80 from $90 and 2027 to $75 from $80, the house call for lower-for-longer once shipping normalizes
- HSBC warns anything below 70% of normal Hormuz flow leaves oil in structural deficit, with global inventories already drawn to roughly 7.4-7.5 billion barrels by June-July from 8 billion in April
- The split scenario is stark: roughly $60 Brent if exports normalize fast, near $130 if disruptions drag on

The Strait of Hormuz normally moves about 21 million barrels a day (UBS), so a reopening floods the futures market with relief while the actual barrels take weeks to physically move. That gap is the whole trade. The week-on-week $12 drop is the market betting the deal gets signed, not hedging that it won't, which is exactly the optimism HSBC flags as fragile: if the signing slips or implementation stalls, oil snaps back toward pre-deal panic with refined-fuel inventories already tight. UBS names the binding test for the bears, the deal dies if full shipping through the Strait fails to restart within 30 days of mine clearing, a high bar that keeps a near-term floor under prices. With the paper market already paid out the all-clear and the barrels not yet moved, long $XLE is the cheap way to own the risk the reopening stalls.

### Warsh's debut is set up as a hawkish hold, and the risk is he out-hawks his reputation

*Warsh's first meeting today reads as a hold with the cut-signal pulled, and the live risk is that the new chair sounds tougher than the dove markets expected.*

- BofA's Fund Manager Survey has 55% expecting a hawkish hold today and 40% seeing at least one hike in the next 12 months, so the room is braced for tough talk, not cuts
- MUFG expects the statement to drop the "easing bias," the language that hinted cuts were coming, and shift to wait-and-see, with 60% odds on a neutral hold
- May headline inflation ran 4.2% year-on-year (UBS), up from 3.8% in April and the highest in three years
- Goldman and MUFG both peg the 2026 median dot (the rate the typical Fed official expects by year-end) near 3.625%, about where rates sit now

This morning's blowout Retail Sales print, 0.88% vs 0.5% expected, is the dagger in the cut case: a consumer spending this freely with inflation at 4.2% gives Warsh no honest reason to signal easing into his first meeting. A chair drops the easing bias when it wants room to wait, and that is the only stance the data supports right now. The counter is BofA's own read that Warsh leans dovish relative to the rest of the committee, so the hawk markets fear may turn out to be the soft voice in the room, but that is a 2027 story, not today's. The near-term risk is Warsh or the FOMC signaling a stance clearly at odds with market pricing, which is what whipsaws the bond market at 2:30. Short $TLT into the press conference, with a long $UUP overlay if he validates the hawkish hold.

### The AI build keeps getting bigger, and the only real question is whether demand shows up

*The data-center build-out is still accelerating in dollars committed, and the live debate is whether the spending has outrun the revenue meant to justify it.*

- JPMorgan raised total AI capex through 2030 to $5.5 trillion from $5.1 trillion, with $4.1 trillion of it debt-financed, so the build is increasingly funded by borrowing rather than cash flow
- Companies have already sold more than $300 billion of AI and data-center bonds since November (JPMorgan), near a record amount of new debt
- AI capex is set to run $821 billion in 2026 and rise to $986 billion in 2027 (UBS), against a projected $3.1 trillion AI revenue opportunity by 2030
- Only 21% of US companies actually use AI today, even as big-AI property and equipment jumped 50-200% in two years, the gap ANZ keeps pointing to
- The tension is straightforward to a trader: the spend is real and contracted, while the end demand is still a forecast. JPMorgan's case that the gap closes rests on Broadcom, reiterated Overweight with a $580 target on more than an 18-month lead in custom AI chips and a locked roadmap with Google, the kind of multi-year visibility that does not show up in a company spending into thin air. The bear case is ANZ's adoption gap, the worry that a $4.1 trillion debt pile starts asking questions before end-user revenue doubles the way JPMorgan models for 2027. Optics and networking, the shift from copper wiring to light, is becoming the real bottleneck (UBS, Goldman), which is where the next leg of spend lands and where the equipment makers get paid first. Long $AVGO bets the build keeps getting funded before that bill comes due.

### The crowded AI top is a coiled spring, so own the rotation off it

*Managers are fully invested and crammed into the same AI trade, which is historically when leaning off the leaders pays.*

- BofA's Fund Manager Survey shows cash levels rising to 4.1% from 3.9%, so managers are bullish but holding dry powder rather than adding
- "Long global semiconductors" is the most crowded trade in the survey at an all-time-high 80%, meaning the marginal buyer is largely gone
- Micron is trading at 21 times price-to-sales (market value against annual revenue), double its dot-com-peak valuation, on the most-loved corner of the market
- Leveraged-ETF daily rebalancing creates a hidden buy-high, sell-low drag that turns momentum buyers into forced sellers in a real drawdown

Rising cash on top of record crowding is the coiled spring, the setup where the unwind feeds itself once it starts. The bull case is that crowded can stay crowded for months while the build keeps printing earnings, and fighting a trend with this much capex behind it has burned shorts all year. So make it falsifiable: if semiconductor crowding holds near the 80% record and cash keeps building without a single big-cap capex guide-down through the late-July earnings cycle, the rotation case is dead and you stay long the leaders. Until then, own equal-weight $RSP over the cap-weighted $SPY, paired with defensive $XLV, keeping equity exposure while leaning off the crowded top.
