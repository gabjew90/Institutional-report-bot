Levels here are as of about 5:30 AM ET, before the open. Bonds set the tone. ## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Bond market prices a Fed that keeps hiking

*The 10-year Treasury yield at 5.25% prices more Fed hikes than the Fed's own likely path, and the gap between the two is where the market is fragile.*

- The 10-year yield hit 5.25% Monday, a 19-year high (Goldman), so the risk-free return stocks compete against is the highest since before the financial crisis. It was still 5.25% as of about 5:30 AM ET.
- October hike odds are above 70% (Goldman), and the 5-year Treasury auction cleared at 5.033%, the highest since 2006, a sign buyers need more yield to absorb the supply.
- High-yield bonds had their largest one-day drop in 17 months (Goldman), so the rate move is reaching company borrowing costs.

Activity data keeps handing the Fed reasons to hike. The September composite PMI of 58.4 is a 62-month high, jobless claims came in at 197,000, and oil near $107 feeds inflation. Federal debt at 122% of GDP means a steady flow of new bonds, and buyers are demanding more yield to take them.

Standard Chartered expects 50 basis points (hundredths of a percent) of further hikes by June 2027 against roughly 95 priced, so it calls the market too aggressive. WisdomTree's Jeremy Siegel argues the other way, that policy may not be tight enough and could need an October hike plus another by year-end, and Goldman's desk points to seven straight months of rising 10-year yields.

Positioning will settle that argument as much as data will. Goldman's model has trend-following funds 99% of the way to their maximum short in 10-year Treasuries, and BofA puts the trigger for those bets to unwind at an 18 basis point fall in the 10-year yield, about 5.07%. A fall that size forces those funds to buy bonds back, which pushes yields down further. Standard Chartered has already initiated a long 10-year Treasury position on that view, and Friday's September payrolls (PNC expects 85,000 and Scotiabank 75,000, after August's 162,000) are the next print that could start the squeeze. A beat keeps October odds above 70%.

## 3. BRIEFS

### AI spending holds, the bill is what strains

UBS lifts AI capital spending to $1.2 trillion in 2027, up 33% from $900 billion this year, but its Evidence Lab counts nine North American projects delayed or cancelled, 7 gigawatts or 5% of the pipeline. Oracle's Project Jupiter in New Mexico, backed by $18 billion in loans, is the named case. The strain shows up in bonds, where the largest cloud companies' credit curves have steepened 8 basis points against the market since August on ultra-long debt sales (Goldman). Micron reports after today's close. JPMorgan's preview calls for DRAM memory prices up more than 20% quarter over quarter and sees the high-bandwidth memory market at $100 billion in 2027, so the guide decides whether memory demand is outrunning what the builders can power and finance.

### Breadth reads like a market top while the index does not

The median S&P 500 stock sits 16% below its 52-week high, the weakest breadth since the dot-com bubble (Goldman), and only about 25% of index members trade above their 50-day average, the lowest ever with the index within 1% of a record (Raymond James). Goldman estimates trend-following funds would buy $11.5 billion of global equities over the next week in a flat market, but 87% of S&P 500 companies are in a buyback blackout, which removes a steady buyer. Morgan Stanley expects breadth and price to meet in the middle over the next month if bond volatility stays high, with a strong year-end after that. The bond market's volatility gauge (MOVE) is at 96, near a one-year high, while the VIX sits near a one-year low at 14.9, so the next move in bond volatility decides which of those two readings holds.

### Hormuz stays mostly shut and oil stays above $100

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is under 15% of pre-conflict levels (UBS, citing Kpler), in a passage that normally carries about a fifth of global oil and gas. Brent is about $106 (MUFG) after talks stalled when Trump refused a seven-day ceasefire, and oil gained again overnight when he denied he would ease sanctions. Treasury Secretary Bessent estimates only about 15 million barrels of Iranian crude remain in transit, which means China-bound deliveries could stop within two weeks (MUFG). Oil matters beyond energy stocks because it feeds the inflation that keeps the rate-hike case alive, and Tuesday's 4.44% drop in $USO shows how much rides on each headline. UBS still forecasts Brent at $95 by year-end. The deciding items are whether the seven-day plan advances and whether Bessent's two-week window closes with Iranian supply actually cut.

### Gold breaks down and the call is to buy it

Gold fell to $4,160, its lowest since August 5, as higher real yields raise the cost of holding an asset that pays nothing (UBS). Gold sits below its 200-day and 50-day averages and is testing a multi-year trend line, and speculative Chinese buyers are selling ahead of the October 1 to 7 Golden Week holiday. UBS calls the weakness a buying opportunity with a $5,400 target over 12 months, pointing to the People's Bank of China buying 20 tonnes in August, its largest purchase since October 2023. Golden Week is normally the start of peak jewellery buying, which could support demand despite weak Chinese consumer confidence. A break below $4,000 would put UBS's dip-buying call under real strain.
