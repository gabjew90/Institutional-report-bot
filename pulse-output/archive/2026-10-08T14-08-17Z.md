## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### AI is carrying an index while borrowing costs squeeze everything else

*The S&P 500 sits near a record only because AI stocks are holding it up, and the yield investors earn after inflation is rising faster than anything else in the market.*

- The S&P 500 is up 1.28% since late August while the index excluding AI names is down 5.19% (Goldman), so nearly all of the gain sits in one group of stocks.
- AI infrastructure spending accounts for over half of S&P 500 earnings growth this quarter (Goldman), so the profits that justify the index come from the same group.
- 49 of the 54 basis points (hundredths of a percent) the 10-year Treasury yield rose in September came from the yield after inflation rather than from inflation expectations (Citadel Securities), which means investors want more pay for growth risk, not protection against rising prices.
- Hedge funds' stock exposure sits in the 2nd percentile of its five-year range, yet the seven mega-cap tech stocks are about 22% of their US holdings, the highest since 2022 (Goldman), so funds have little to sell but all of it is in the same names.

A higher yield after inflation lowers what every future dollar of profit is worth today, and it hits the parts of the market with distant or debt-funded earnings first. The non-AI half of the index has absorbed that hit, while AI leaders have so far been immune to the bond selloff and the number of stocks driving the index is the smallest since 2000 (Goldman).

A higher cost of capital favors the biggest cloud companies over standalone model builders, because they have distribution and cash flow (Citadel Securities). Goldman's strategists are constructive on AI and point to fundamentals as support for the leadership, but its trading desk warns that large tech stocks, untouched so far by the bond repricing, are now exposed to a bad macro surprise. Part of the squeeze is already in the price, since AI infrastructure stocks have gone from 32 times the earnings analysts expect over the next year in April to 22 times, and with funds this lightly positioned there is little to dump if bonds wobble. The date that decides it is the consumer price report on Wednesday, October 14 at 8:30 AM ET. Goldman's desk names a high print that forces the Fed to hike as the event that breaks this setup, and it has the market pricing about 20% odds of an October hike against about 85% for December.

## 3. BRIEFS

### The 10-year is stuck at 5.3% and oil is the reason

The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.29% this morning, inside the 5.25% to 5.35% range where it is expected to stay pinned (Mizuho). A break lower needs either easier energy markets or clear signs of slowing growth, and with Brent crude back near $101 and $BNO up 3.63% on the Hormuz tanker attacks, neither is on offer today. The Fed side is calmer, yet 30-year Treasury bonds will not rally because buyers are wary of how much new government debt is coming. Options show the strain: implied volatility (what options are pricing for future swings) on $TLT, the long-bond ETF, sits near the top of its one-year range, and trend-following funds are heavily short bonds (Deutsche Bank) while JPMorgan's clients are net long. A clean reception at the coming 10-year and 30-year auctions would say yields have not yet topped. A weak one would say the opposite and would hit rate-sensitive stocks first.

### Central banks keep a floor under gold

Goldman holds its $5,400 end-2027 gold forecast but cut its year-end 2026 fair value to $4,650, with spot near $4,350, because Fed hikes weigh on the funds that hold gold. The support is official buying. Central banks are taking about 91 tonnes a month on Goldman's three-month estimate against 17 a month before 2022, and a World Gold Council survey has 45% of central banks planning to add, the highest share since 2018. Metals Focus sees 2026 buying slowing 15% to 720 tonnes, which it frames as cooling from a record rather than a reversal. Call-option positioning on $GLD is about three times normal, and the dealers who sold those calls must buy more as the price rises, which could amplify a rally (Goldman). The risk is the Fed: three more hikes by year-end would take gold toward about $4,070 on Goldman's math.

### Marvell's investor day pulls money out of chip equipment

Marvell targets 55% to 60% annual revenue growth through fiscal 2031 and about $30 in earnings per share that year, on data center spending it sees rising from roughly $700 billion in 2025 to over $3 trillion by 2030. Goldman raised its earnings estimates 13% on average and its price target on $MRVL to $270 from $220, yet stays Neutral because the stock trades near 9.5 times fiscal 2031 earnings and the timing of new customer chip orders is hard to see. Money rotated out of crowded chip equipment names into Marvell and optical networking companies such as $CIEN, which rose 13%, while $STX and $WDC fell sharply on hard-drive competition worries that spilled onto $AMAT, $KLAC and $LRCX. The rating would turn more constructive on stronger evidence of execution and a clearer path for the biggest customer ramps (Goldman).

### Google locks up Constellation's nuclear output

Constellation signed a 20-year deal with Google for 890 megawatts of new nuclear capacity from upgrades at 11 units, plus a 15-year contract for 2,700 megawatts from its existing fleet in PJM, the grid operator for the Mid-Atlantic and parts of the Midwest. Goldman estimates the roughly 3.6 gigawatts add $449 million to $1.17 billion of free cash flow, 5% to 14% of its 2030 estimate, and the contracted share of Constellation's nuclear fleet is now 28%, with 19 points of that signed recently. $CEG jumped 12% on the news, yet Goldman stays Neutral with a $305 target because other power producers look cheaper. A PJM regulatory ruling that Goldman expects by October 12 is the next test of whether this contracting momentum survives. Goldman's trading desk, which calls its basket of AI-power stocks cleanly positioned, favors buying a call spread on it (a capped bullish options bet) funded by selling a put.
