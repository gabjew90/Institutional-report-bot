# Jobs crack, hikes stay

## 1. RECAP

Forty-five minutes into Monday's session, the market is quiet everywhere except energy. $SPY is flat at +0.1%, $QQQ is barely better, and $VIXY is drifting lower as the market digests a sharp four-day bounce. Oil is the story: $BNO is up nearly 5% on fresh Hormuz brinkmanship out of Tehran, and Treasury yields are creeping higher into a week that stacks CPI Wednesday against $125 billion of government bond auctions. $BTC sits just under $65k, doing nothing while the fight plays out in rates and crude.

- **Iran tied any Strait of Hormuz reopening to US concessions and put hardliner Mohsen Rezaei atop its top security council.** Tehran spent the weekend tempering the reopening hopes it had raised, and crude is repricing the standoff. $BNO +4.96% and $USO +4.89% in the first hour.
- **Friday's July payrolls printed -23k against an 80k consensus, the weakest of the cycle, with prior months revised lower.** Rate markets added to tightening bets anyway, with the market's one-year inflation bet jumping 11 basis points (0.11 percentage point) in a single day, the biggest rise in a month (Deutsche Bank). Rising energy costs are overriding the jobs data.
- **The four-day bounce into this week was short-covering, not conviction.** Goldman's basket of the most-shorted stocks rose 13% in four days and the Mag7 posted their largest four-day gain since spring 2025, while retail options flow shows record premium going into index puts, hedging rather than chasing.

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The market is pricing hikes into a hiring freeze

*With no Fed guidance to anchor them, rates markets are trading oil instead of jobs, and long-dated Treasuries are the wrong place to hide this week.*

- July payrolls printed -23k against an 80k consensus, with prior months revised down. The three-month average collapsed to 20k from 111k, and Goldman puts the underlying hiring pace near 5k. Hiring has effectively stopped.
- The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, but participation, the share of adults working or looking for work, dropped to 61.4% (Goldman). The jobless rate improved because people left the workforce, not because they found jobs.
- Wage growth printed 0.05% for the month against a 0.3% consensus (Goldman). No wage-driven inflation pressure in sight.
- Futures still price 34 basis points of Fed hikes by year-end even after the print (Deutsche Bank). Weak jobs would normally mean cuts. Energy is doing the pricing instead.
- $125 billion of fresh Treasury supply lands this week (Deutsche Bank), 3-year notes Tuesday, 10-years Wednesday, 30-years Thursday, into a long end (the longest-maturity, most rate-sensitive bonds) already at post-2007 highs. The 30-year trades at 5.23% this morning.

Kevin Warsh's Fed has stopped telegraphing its next move, so every inflation print and every auction reprices Treasury yields in real time instead of being absorbed by guidance. With Treasury bills at 22% of outstanding US debt, the government's own refinancing cost moves with every swing.

The other side of the hike pricing comes with high conviction: "in the end, there is no rate hike in September" (TS Lombard), because the labor market is deteriorating under the surface. They can be right about the meeting and still not save bondholders this week. The near-term pain is not the Fed decision, it is CPI Wednesday and a 30-year auction Thursday hitting a market with no guidance anchor, and the one real off-switch, Warsh clearly explaining how he reacts to data, is penciled in for Jackson Hole at month-end, not this week. Does Wednesday's CPI break the September-hike pricing before Thursday's auction breaks the long end? You do not need to answer that. Both paths keep long-dated bonds under pressure this week. Short $TLT.

## 3. BRIEFS

### The AI revenue showed up, and so did the debt

*The buildout is starting to pay, so own the hyperscaler funding it from cash flow rather than the ones borrowing to keep up.*

This earnings season gave the AI trade its first hard proof of return. AI revenue at the hyperscalers (the giant cloud builders: Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta) finally turned meaningfully upward, Goldman's highest-conviction call of the window, and S&P 500 earnings grew about 25% year over year in the first half even after stripping out private-asset gains (Goldman). The financing is the catch. Nvidia is backstopping a $250 billion data center project against $215 billion of total annual revenue, and credit markets are charging for the borrowing: hyperscaler bond spreads, the extra yield investors demand to hold their debt, are 32 basis points wider this year while the rest of high-grade corporate debt is flat (Deutsche Bank). Microsoft is the only major hyperscaler generating positive free cash flow, the cash left over after capital spending. Long $MSFT, the AI return without the borrowing. What flips it is Deutsche Bank's slow-ramp scenario, where AI revenue lags, debt keeps building and the credit pressure spreads to the equity.

### The bounce is rented, not owned

*This is a short-covering rally with defensive positioning underneath, so keep the upside and own the hedge.*

The squeeze that carried stocks into this week was shorts buying back, not fresh conviction, and the retail flow underneath it is defensive. Citadel Securities' July data shows $5.4 billion a day of retail options premium with a record share in index and ETF products rather than single stocks, and record premium going to puts. That is hedging, not chasing. BTIG caught the bounce and is stepping off, with the rebound in high-beta momentum names (the fastest-moving speculative stocks) at roughly 25% versus a +35% median recovery after a first leg down. Their trigger is 10-year real rates, Treasury yields after subtracting inflation, above 2.50% versus 2.35% now. If real rates stay below 2.50% through this week's CPI and auctions, the squeeze can run, and if they break above, the next leg lower starts. Stay invested and own $QQQ puts as the hedge that covers both answers.

### Silver is dragging gold into a forced chase

*Systematic shorts are being squeezed out of precious metals and positioning says the chase has room to run.*

Silver rose 8.3% over the week and a half through last Wednesday while hedge funds' long positions in silver futures sat near two-year lows, and that momentum flip is forcing trend-following funds (CTAs, funds that mechanically chase price moves) to buy back their short bets, per Goldman. The gold side has a China bid: 225 tonnes imported year-to-date versus 28 tonnes in the same stretch of 2025 (Goldman). Yet overall gold positioning sits at just +3 on Goldman's -10 to +10 scale versus +10 in January, so the chase is far from crowded. The counters are real: central bank buying is down 28% year over year, and silver's physical market has not tightened, with lease rates, the cost to borrow physical metal, actually softening. Long $SLV through call spreads rolled up to higher strikes, and buy the $GLD dip when spot gold trades toward $4,000-4,100, where central bank purchases have built a floor (Goldman). If gold breaks and holds below $4,000, the structural-bull thesis is dead and the chase unwinds with it.

### The Hormuz toll booth is still open for business

*Oil's risk premium stays underpriced while reopening is a negotiation, not a schedule.*

The weekend's message from Tehran was that reopening is a negotiation, not a schedule, and crude is repricing that Monday morning. The Iran-Oman transit framework on the table bars US and Israeli vessels and adds cargo fees, terms Washington has not accepted (Deutsche Bank). Nobody has a directional call on the outcome, and that is the tell: the market is not positioned for either resolution. Refined-product shortages plus European winter demand argue diesel and fuel prices higher (Rabobank), energy was the only S&P 500 sector to clearly advance on the last round of deal news (Deutsche Bank), and Goldman sketches $100 oil if hostilities re-escalate and energy shipments are curtailed. The cost of being wrong on a real reopening is oil giving back its recent bounce. The cost of holding no energy exposure if the strait stays shut is the $100 scenario. Long $BNO carries that asymmetry.
