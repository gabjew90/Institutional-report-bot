## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Peace memo on the wires, supply scar lasts months

*Hormuz ceasefire chatter is being priced as an oil top. The corpus says that read is too clean.*

- US crude exports are running at 5.6 million barrels a day, roughly 40% above pre-war levels, which is what happens when buyers don't trust the supply restoration
- Goldman pegs global crude and product inventory draw at 8.7 million barrels a day in May, almost double the pace since the conflict began
- HSBC raised the 2026 Brent average forecast to $95 per barrel, $15 above the prior call, anchoring on a structural shortage rather than a peace dividend
- 15-20% of key global commodities transit the Middle East per HSBC, so even with a deal, insurance, freight, and routing normalization take months
- Goldman's macro short book on US Index and ETF products hit a 10-year high, exceeding levels seen before the prior ceasefire, meaning desk positioning is fading the de-escalation rally, not chasing it

Three things are happening at once that the front-month price action isn't pricing. Diplomatic optics are improving, but the physical market is still drawing crude at a record pace, refined fuel inventories are sitting near multi-year lows, and the largest crude exporter to the world is running 40% above peacetime volumes. The peace headline can compress the war premium. It cannot refill 8-year-low product inventories overnight.

The opposite read is the clean version a market is already pricing. A US-Iran agreement plus Hormuz reopening collapses the war premium, refiner margins normalize within weeks, and long-dated oil futures fade fast. Refined-fuel inventories at 8-year lows are the hard constraint, and the supply hole takes months to refill regardless of which way the headlines run. The bias on dips is long $BNO outright, with $USO sized down given the rolling-cost drag that comes with the front-month futures structure. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline.

The 99th-percentile crowding and 72% top-10 concentration leave the trade vulnerable to a single hyperscaler capex guide-down. Granting that, the picks-and-shovels names are the cleaner expression than the hyperscaler equity itself, since equipment suppliers ($AMAT, $LRCX) are sold through the cycle into 2027 and memory beneficiaries ($MU) get paid regardless of which hyperscaler wins the application layer. ### Bond market is fading the Fed, and the corpus says it is wrong

- 30-year Treasury yield holding 5.00%, sticky despite a 0.05 percentage-point weekly drop, meaning 30-year Treasuries are refusing to participate in any rate-cut narrative
- BCA Research has US wholesale inflation (PPI excluding food and energy) running at a 6.6% annualized rate over the last six months as of April, so inflation pressure is rebuilding underneath the headline
- Bank of America's preview flags 4-5% CPI as the next several months' base case, the kind of print that historically triggers policy tightening rather than easing
- Waller publicly framed the next rate move as just as likely a hike as a cut, and 2-year yields jumped to multi-month highs on the wire
- Goldman pegs 0.45 percentage points as the one-month backup in 10-year yields that starts to bite equity valuations, and current levels are inside that band with the Treasury supply calendar running hot through Q3

Three forces are pulling against the dovish read at the same time. Oil-driven inflation is rebuilding the wholesale pipeline on the standard 3-month lag, new 30-year Treasury supply keeps landing into a buyer base that is already heavily loaded up on long-dated bonds (so dealers absorbing more bonds than they want to hold), and a Fed that pivoted hawkish on Waller's wires has effectively given up the option of talking the curve lower. The Bank of America 4-5% CPI base case and Goldman's 0.45-point valuation threshold point in the same direction.

The bull case for cuts: the labor market is finally in rough balance per Powell's recent framing, and a sustained core CPI print below 2% would force the dovish path back into the curve. Even granting the soft-landing case, the binding constraint is the supply-demand mismatch in long-dated Treasuries. The structural seller from EM oil importers (covered below) adds a flow that does not depend on Fed policy. The setup pressures $TLT and biases the dollar lower on rate-differential erosion into Thursday's PCE print. Short $TLT into Thursday's PCE.

### Warsh as Fed chair, and the conflict-of-mandates problem

*The incoming Fed-chair transition is reframing the 2026 policy track. Three desks flag it as a live policy risk, not background noise.*

- Goldman's macro desk expects monthly balance sheet growth to average $5bn until early 2027, the technical floor that holds regardless of who chairs the Fed
- BCA Research's framing: the central tension is Warsh persuading the FOMC not to hike rates versus the Fed falling behind the inflation curve and being forced into aggressive action later
- HSBC's preview flags 2026 hike risk under Chair Warsh as increasing because of conflicting mandates, with inflation pressure from supply shocks running into political pressure for accommodation
- Goldman's balance-sheet note: Warsh has argued publicly that a smaller balance sheet would allow the Fed to lower the funds rate, framing that is now landing inside the institution
- Ample-reserves framework remains FOMC consensus per Goldman, so the Warsh-flavored case for shrinking the balance sheet faces a higher institutional bar than headlines suggest

The mechanism is institutional, not just personnel. BCA, Goldman, and HSBC frame Warsh's incoming chairmanship as a policy regime change in waiting, even with the ample-reserves framework anchoring against immediate balance-sheet changes. The conflict is between the supply-shock inflation Powell is staring down and the political pressure Warsh has historically been more responsive to.

Where this thesis breaks: Warsh signals continuity at confirmation hearings, the FOMC consensus holds, and the policy path stays effectively unchanged from the Powell-Williams center. That is plausible since Warsh has been a Fed governor before and the institutional bar is high. The defense is that even a continuity signal does not resolve the binding constraint of 6.6% annualized wholesale inflation running into a labor market that needs accommodation. HSBC puts it directly: under Warsh, 2026 hike risk goes up, not down, because the chair has to satisfy two mandates moving in opposite directions. Long $VIXY into Warsh's first FOMC window.

### EM oil importers are selling Treasuries, and the 30-year bid is breaking

*A structural seller of US Treasuries is emerging from oil-importing emerging markets that the bond market has not fully priced.*

- Bloomberg's March data shows record selling by oil-importing emerging markets as they drain dollar reserves to fund crude purchases, which is a mechanical seller, not a sentiment one
- AAII bull-bear spread at -11.9 with mutual fund cash at 1.4% of assets per Goldman prime data, meaning domestic investor cash is too low to absorb a structural foreign-seller wave
- $6.4 trillion sits in US insurer and pension corporate bond holdings as of 4Q25 per Goldman, the depth of the buyer base, but price-sensitivity at current yields is the question
- USD/JPY pushed back through 159, erasing the prior intervention impact, which is the dollar's strength against import-dependent EMs forcing the Treasury sales in the first place
- BCA and Goldman both flag EM currency softness and local-bond vulnerability tied directly to the US 10-year yield level, meaning the loop is reinforcing, not breaking

The reinforcing loop is the trade. Higher oil prices force EM oil importers to draw down dollar reserves, which means selling US Treasuries, which lifts US yields, which strengthens the dollar against EM currencies, which makes oil imports more expensive in local currency, which forces more reserve drawdowns. The mechanism is not exhausted because the oil shock is not exhausted.

The bull case for Treasuries is a clean Hormuz resolution that breaks the oil price floor, short-circuits the loop, slows EM reserve drawdowns, and fades the structural seller. That is the same peace scenario the first theme already flagged as too clean given the inventory data. The defense is that even a partial peace deal does not reverse the EM reserves that have already been drawn, since the March selling is a recorded fact, not a forecast. Pair short $TLT with long $BNO to capture both legs of the loop.
