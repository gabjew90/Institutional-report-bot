## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI is still the only thing this market rewards, and that is now the bear case

*The Mag-7 plus AI-adjacent capex names are the entire equity rally. The desks calling for $1T hyperscaler capex are the same desks telling clients to rebalance.*

- UBS forecasts 2026 AI capex at $820B and 2027 at $990B, with cloud provider revenue running 40% year-over-year in 1Q26. The demand curve has not bent, so AI-tied earnings stay the marginal contributor to S&P EPS.
- BlackRock Investment Institute projects annual hyperscaler capex hits $1 trillion by 2028, roughly equal to total US corporate capex in 2019, concentrated across five names.
- Broadcom's FY27 AI backlog sits above $150B with FY26-28 memory capex at $450B (JPMorgan). The picks-and-shovels names downstream of the hyperscaler order book have multi-year visible revenue.
- $150B of data-center projects are facing delays or cancellations, plus political-backlash risk where policy shifts from regulation to taxing or owning the rents (Goldman). That is a multiple bear case, distinct from a demand miss.
- The crypto leg of the AI complex has paused with $BTC -3.32%, $ETH -1.47% and $SOL -2.66% over the past 24 hours, even as cloud-equity AI earnings keep climbing. The long-crypto vs long-AI-equity rotation that worked in April has stalled.

Three facts are simultaneously true and the desks are not pretending otherwise. AI demand is real and the spending plans are real. The market's reliance on a handful of names to deliver that demand is unprecedented in the post-war record. The funding mechanism for the capex, record corporate debt sales plus AI-tied equity raises, is being flagged as fragile by Deutsche Bank and being labeled "the AI debt bubble" in print. If hyperscaler capex keeps compounding 40% plus, the bulls win. If a single META or GOOGL guide-down lands, the picks-and-shovels names crack first because they are priced for the trajectory rather than the level. UBS itself is the loudest bull on the 2026 capex print and is also telling clients to use the recent strength to rebalance, and when the loudest bull is also flagging concentration risk the asymmetry has shifted. What survives cleanest in either outcome are the picks-and-shovels names with multi-year backlogs over the GPU-leadership names where positioning is already extreme. Long $AVGO and $MU, paired with trims in the most-crowded GPU expression.

### Hormuz is still closed, Iran is reviewing a US proposal, and oil prices are whip-sawing

*Nine desks treat the Strait of Hormuz as an unresolved supply disruption with no scheduled reopening. A US-Iran ceasefire MoU just hit the wires overnight, dropping front-month oil more than 1%.*

- Natixis: daily ship passages through Hormuz are still under 10 versus a pre-conflict baseline of 125-140 ships per day. The chokepoint is not normalizing on the ground regardless of headlines.
- UBS: 21 million barrels per day of oil normally moves through the Strait, roughly 20% of global seaborne crude, with no alternative routing for most of it.
- Rabobank is the lone desk modeling a 7-month total closure, which would knock 2.5-2.8 billion barrels off cumulative production and persist into September.
- Deutsche Bank: Polymarket probability of Hormuz returning to normal by end-June has fallen to 22% from 26% on Sunday. Even the prediction-market crowd has marked the reopening timeline back.
- Deutsche Bank pegs $150/bbl as the Brent price in a prolonged-closure scenario and UBS holds a $105/bbl September Brent target. That is the corpus's implied range to trade against.

The split is the trade. Front-month Brent is pricing the US-Iran MoU headlines, with $USO closing Monday +4.97% before easing more than 1% overnight on the news. Physical ship-traffic data and prediction-market probabilities are pricing closure persistence. When the futures market and the physical market disagree, the futures market eventually re-converges to the physical, historically over weeks rather than days. The opposite read is that an Iran deal genuinely lands in the next two weeks, with Deutsche Bank's analysts flagging June as the negotiation window, and a single Trump-Iran announcement compresses the entire supply premium overnight. That is real risk and the asymmetry argues against a leveraged outright long-oil structure. UBS's $5,500/oz year-end gold target ties the same geopolitical premium to a different instrument that is not subject to the same headline whip. Long $BNO sized for headline risk, paired with long $GLD as the hedge into the next Hormuz print.
