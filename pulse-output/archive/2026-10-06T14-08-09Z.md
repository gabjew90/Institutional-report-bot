# Bonds break, AI holds

As of about 4:34 AM ET Tuesday, before the open, stocks are coming off a firm Monday. $SPY closed up 0.67% and $QQQ led at 0.88%, carried by AI earnings and a positioning base that was already washed out, so there was little left to sell. ## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Treasury yields at 5.3% are a supply problem, and only AI is holding stocks up

*Long-term yields are rising because the world is issuing too much debt, not because of the Fed, and stocks are leaning on the AI trade alone to ignore it.*

- The 10-year Treasury yield sits near 5.3%, its highest in roughly two decades, so borrowing costs across the economy reset higher whatever the Fed does next
- Last week's 5-year auction priced at 5.033%, the first 5% handle since 2007 (BofA), which means buyers now demand a much bigger payment to absorb new government debt
- Hyperscaler bond sales are on track for $250B this year and $420B in 2027 (Goldman), so AI spending adds its own wave of supply on top of Treasury issuance
- The MOVE index, which tracks how much bond prices are expected to swing, reads above 100 (BofA) against a one-year average of 74, while the VIX sits near 15, so bonds are flagging stress that stocks are not pricing
- Trend-following funds are about $390B short global bonds, with US 10-year positions at 99% of their maximum short (Goldman), so any sharp rally forces them to buy back

Friday's payrolls came in at 29,000 against 90,000 expected and October hike odds fell to roughly 15%, yet the 10-year dipped only to about 5.15% on the print and closed the day at 5.27%. Fed expectations are not what is moving long yields. The pressure is a global glut of government and corporate debt plus momentum selling that feeds on itself in thin trading, and the Treasury has tripled its long-dated buyback size to $6B to steady it. Stocks stand on a different support. $NVDA hit a record high after the payrolls print, and BofA calls the belief that AI growth outweighs higher borrowing costs the "AI put."

Goldman has hedge fund net positioning at the bottom of its historical range and leveraged funds at a record short in $IWM futures, so the bond stress is already in positions. Deutsche Bank disagrees on who still has to sell: funds that trade by rules hold equity exposure higher than 92% of past readings, and funds that cut stock exposure when volatility rises are above 98%, so a jump in volatility forces selling rather than buying. BofA's desk sits between them, holding $QQQ calls for a market where rates and equities both rise, and $TLT put spreads as protection against yields climbing further, both over one to three months. What breaks it, per BofA, is a slip in the AI story, and Wednesday's 2:00 PM ET FOMC minutes are the next scheduled test of the rates side.

## 3. BRIEFS

### France's bond spread is dragging European credit lower

*Europe's sovereign stress is spreading past France into Italy and bank bonds, and the ECB has a high bar before it steps in.*

French 10-year bonds now yield 158 basis points (1.58 percentage points) more than German ones, the highest since 2012 and up 72 in a month, while Italy's gap widened 22 last week and the riskiest euro bank bonds are 55 wider (Goldman). Tighter financial conditions since July equal about one ECB hike, so Goldman keeps December as the third and final hike and raised its odds of that path to 60% from 50%. The ECB's crisis tool is meant for bystanders like Spain, not for a country like France whose budget does not stabilize its debt, so the bar for stepping in is high (Goldman). A light October issuance calendar is the one thing that could snap spreads back (Goldman).

### Brent near $100 carries a $20-25 risk premium

*Oil is priced for a supply shock the physical market has already stopped delivering.*

Goldman's commodities desk puts $20-25 a barrel of risk premium in Brent even though Persian Gulf exports are back at the 2025 average of 23.3 million barrels a day. Macro funds are buying outright oil as a hedge against tail risk, so crude now trades with interest rates more than with headlines. Options show the oddity: puts cost more than calls, the reverse of a normal crisis, because dealers are short puts. Deutsche Bank has Brent up 42% in the third quarter. Goldman's high-conviction call over one to three months is calls or call spreads on Brent, which pay off if prices run toward the $130 level where macro books start losing money. The desk's own trigger is an attack that takes Gulf flows back below 50% within a few days, and de-escalation headlines are the risk to that trade.

### AI's debt funding is moving off the balance sheet

*The next stress test for AI spending is who holds the debt that pays for the chips.*

Morgan Stanley counts $3.1 trillion of off-balance-sheet commitments across the hyperscalers, $NVDA and $AVGO, up $1.3 trillion in one quarter. Amazon is reportedly structuring an $8B vehicle for Nvidia chips and Broadcom's banks are raising $60B, including a $42B senior-secured tranche the company backstops. Volumes of default insurance on single AI names are up 400% from a year ago (Goldman), and Oracle's five-year cover hit a record 2.27% of the insured amount a year. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky agrees the circular financing is "undeniably increasing" but calls it not today's problem, while the risk is that bondholders end up owning depreciating hardware if compute demand stalls. AWS raising prices on machine-learning capacity about 15% on October 7, its fourth straight quarterly increase (Goldman), is the demand check that decides whether the "not today's problem" read holds.

### Midterm odds tilt Democratic and desks split on the market read

*A Democratic sweep is priced as gridlock, but a rough patch comes first (Morgan Stanley).*

Prediction markets give Democrats 63% to take the Senate, up 14 points since mid-September, and better than 90% to take the House. Goldman and JPMorgan both argue a sweep means gridlock, and the S&P 500 has gained about 20% over a two-year divided Congress since 1950 (Goldman), though midterm polls have overstated Democrats by about 1.3 points. Democratic outperformance could trigger a near-term move lower on policy uncertainty, and data-center sentiment is weakest under a Democratic sweep (Morgan Stanley). November 3 is the vote and December 11 is the government funding deadline, the kind of standoff that Goldman and JPMorgan's history shows has been a buy-the-dip event for the S&P 500.

### Citi lifts Bitcoin's target to $113,000

*ETF buying is now the main driver of Bitcoin, and Citi is paying up for it.*

Citi raised its 12-month Bitcoin target to $113,000 from $82,000 and Ether to $3,028 from $2,240, with a $5B base-case ETF inflow forecast against flat in June. Its rule of thumb is that $1B of ETF inflows moves Bitcoin about 3.5%. Spot Bitcoin ETFs just logged nine straight days of inflows totaling over $3B, the biggest nine-day haul this year, and both coins trade above their 200-day averages. $BTC pushed through $85,000 to $87,000 before sliding back as inflation-adjusted yields spiked, and sat near $85,900 as of about 4:34 AM ET. Citi's bear case is $43,000 if risk appetite fades, and the failed Clarity Act in the Senate narrows the path to a market-structure law. Continued ETF inflows through the rate spike decide whether the $113,000 target gets traction.
