## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The winner trade cracked, and it is positioning not fundamentals

*The crowded bet on this year's chip and AI leaders is unwinding on forced selling, which makes it a washout to fade into rather than a reason to doubt the businesses.*

- Momentum, the simple strategy of buying whatever has been going up, just had its biggest washout since the early 2000s, and the worst since the December 2022 through February 2023 episode (Goldman). A drop this size is historically a positioning flush, not the opening act of a fundamental bear market.
- The Philadelphia semiconductor index ($SOX) has lagged the broad market by about 20 points since the late-June highs. The exact cohort that carried the market all year is now the epicenter of the selling.
- No earnings estimates have been cut anywhere in the group, so the businesses are intact while the crowd exits the position. This is a rotation out of chips, not a downgrade of them.
- Goldman's desk has an explicit buy on the momentum drawdown across AI, semiconductors and tech hardware for the next one to three months. The same house that flagged the washout now thinks it is close to a level worth stepping into, and JPMorgan's Schlegel sees momentum bouncing on the same one-to-three-month horizon.

When a trade gets this crowded, the holders are increasingly the same leveraged funds owning the same names. A wobble forces the most levered to sell, that selling knocks the price, the next tier gets forced out, and the move feeds on itself with nothing fundamental behind it. That is how the chips can shed 20 points against the market while not a single earnings number gets marked down.

The other side of the debate is loud. Deutsche Bank and Goldman's own strategists argue the drawdown is just running its typical historical script after this group rallied more than 20% in three months, which means there is further to fall before it is done, and JPMorgan's flow work sees more forced selling still queued up in leveraged funds and margin accounts. Both readings can be true without changing the trade. A washout that is two-thirds finished still ends, and the tell that separates a positioning flush from a real top is whether estimates get cut. They have not, which is why the desks that called the drop are now sizing entries instead of adding shorts. Treat this as a flush to buy, not a top to short, but stay hedged into the tail. Long the quality AI leaders on the washout, hedged with $SMH puts until the forced selling clears.

## 3. BRIEFS

### AI's buildout keeps compounding, but the chips get repriced before the story breaks

*The cloud giants keep raising AI spending, so the buildout is real, but the hardware stocks likely take a valuation hit well before any of that spending actually slows.*

US capital spending rose 33% in 2025, and JPMorgan forecasts another 23% in 2026, with its internet analysts still biased to the upside. The fight is over who absorbs the cost. The flood of new corporate debt funding data centers is widening the extra yield investors demand to hold top-rated corporate and data-center-backed bonds (Goldman, Morgan Stanley), and Goldman's read is that hardware valuations compress well before anyone cuts an earnings estimate. JPMorgan takes the other side, still long the hardware names into the upside. The way to own the buildout without owning the valuation risk is the power-and-electricity layer the data centers cannot run without. Long the power stack, $GEV and $VST, over the chipmakers.

### What to buy when the leaders get sold

*The money leaving the crowded AI leaders has to land somewhere, and two corners of the market never got the original bid.*

Fresh research this window keeps warning on the narrow, top-heavy S&P, a market carried by a handful of stocks and asking what to buy if not the megacap AI names. That is the mirror image of the momentum unwind above. Equal-weight S&P ($RSP), which owns the roughly 493 stocks that never stretched, wins if the money simply spreads out across the index. Energy ($XLE) carries a live catalyst the rest of the market lacks, the oil bid from the Strait of Hormuz standoff, and Morgan Stanley's energy-security work argues that premium is structural rather than a one-week spike. Long $RSP over $QQQ, paired with $XLE for the energy leg.

### The leveraged-ETF unwind is the tail nobody agrees on

*Forced selling from leveraged and inverse ETFs is either a nothing-burger or the accelerant, and Bank of America and JPMorgan flatly disagree on which.*

These funds have to rebalance every day to keep their leverage constant, which means they mechanically sell into weakness and buy into strength, and they have been cutting exposure since June. Bank of America ran the numbers and concluded the flows do not pose a serious tail risk to the market right now. The opposite read sees more room for forced selling ahead across leveraged ETFs, options and margin accounts (JPMorgan). Most of the reassuring read traces back to one house, so it is one desk's model rather than a cross-street consensus. Long a little $VIXY as cheap insurance while that disagreement stays unresolved.
