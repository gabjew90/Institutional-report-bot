## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The AI build is now a bond-market problem

*The money big tech needs for data centers is competing with the US Treasury for the same lenders, and a 10-year yield that just printed its first close above 5% since 2007 is what that competition costs.*

- The 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 5.00%, its first finish above 5% since 2007, and was back at 4.96% by roughly 3:30 AM ET Tuesday. That is the zone where stock valuations start paying real rent for the money behind them.
- BofA raised 2026 capital spending estimates to $195 billion for Alphabet and $145 billion for Meta, and Morgan Stanley has the five largest cloud operators' combined free cash flow bottoming at minus $265 billion in the third quarter of 2027. A hole that size gets filled with borrowed money.
- The extra yield investors demand for lending to the government over long stretches has moved above what blue-chip companies pay to borrow. Lenders now want more to hold Treasuries than to hold high-grade corporate debt, which reverses the entire post-2008 pattern.
- Roughly 66 gigawatts of data centers are under construction worldwide, up 21 gigawatts since March, with another 304 gigawatts planned. That queue clears at whatever the price of money turns out to be.
- BofA has $META at Buy to $835 because the market assigns about $4 billion per gigawatt to its AI capacity, against the $40 billion per gigawatt implied by the capacity deals Anthropic and Google signed with SpaceX. The stock market is not paying for the capacity the bond market is funding.

Allianz Research reaches the opposite conclusion over a longer window. If AI delivers the productivity it promises, the same forces pull long-term rates roughly 50 basis points lower across a decade, a basis point being one-hundredth of a percent, because faster growth repairs the government's debt path. Goldman's Pasquariello adds that the bond market has already done the tightening work for the Fed, which leaves stocks better set up now than they were a month ago.

The problem with both arguments is timing. Allianz's own path to that lower rate runs the US 10-year through a 135 basis point round trip, and the up-leg comes first. A decade-long productivity payoff does nothing for a portfolio that has to survive the next two quarters of government and corporate issuance. Goldman's desk is buying a December 2026 lookback put on the S&P 500 index that only pays out if the 10-year swap rate, what it costs to lock in a fixed rate for ten years, stays above 4.8%, which prices the rate move and the stock move as one position. That 10-year rate breaking below 4.8% kills the hedge and the rates-drag case with it.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Refineries, not barrels, are the squeeze

*The oil story stopped being about crude supply and became about the machines that turn crude into diesel and jet fuel.*

More than 2 million barrels a day of Russian refining capacity is offline after Ukrainian drone strikes, about 2% of the world's total, and Moscow has extended its diesel and gasoline export ban to the end of January 2027 (Bernstein). Allianz Research puts the net disruption to flows through the Strait of Hormuz at 4.3 million barrels a day. That combination is why Morgan Stanley dropped its disinflation baseline in favor of an oil risk premium and lifted its 2026 core inflation forecast to 3.2%. The cushion is thin, with US strategic reserves at 285 million barrels on September 15, the lowest since April 1984. Prices went the other way on Monday, $USO down 3.68% on negotiation headlines, before crude turned back up overnight. Allianz's own base case has Brent consolidating to $76 next year if the Saudi pipeline is repaired quickly, and an outage running past six weeks is what kills that case. Bernstein rates $XOM Outperform to $182 on refining margins holding near records.

### Xi lands in Washington and options are priced for a quiet week

*The one hard catalyst on this week's calendar is an event the options market has not marked at all.*

Xi is due in the US from September 23 to 25, with AI, trade, rare earths and the Iran war on the agenda (Goldman Sachs) and compute on that agenda too (Academy), and he arrives while Trump works a run of UN meetings that carry Iran and Ukraine on their own agenda. The PBOC has walked its dollar-yuan fixing below 6.70 for the first time since 2022, which is how Beijing signals it wants a calm backdrop going in. Deutsche Bank has Asian equities already advancing on summit optimism with technology shares leading, so the good outcome is partly in the price. The bad one is not priced at all, because options on $FXI sit near multi-year lows and carry no extra premium for the summit dates (Goldman Sachs). Whether export controls on high-end chips reach the agenda is what decides this. A constructive readout on compute lifts semiconductors and China internet together, a breakdown re-prices both and puts the tariff path back in play.

### The Fed's next two hikes are a forecast, not a fact

*The market is priced for more tightening than this Fed is likely to deliver.*

The FOMC raised rates 25 basis points to 3.75-4.00% unanimously, and Warsh said policy is still not clearly restrictive. Morgan Stanley answered by moving its terminal rate to 4.25-4.50% on two more hikes, December and March, and Goldman's economists pencil in another 25 in October. The other side is named just as specifically. Generali calls the four hikes priced through 2027 excessive, because inflation this sticky is already eating into real incomes, and Goldman's house view is that the Fed underdelivers against what the market has priced. August core PCE, the inflation measure the Fed actually targets, ran at 3.2%. The December meeting settles the argument. If core inflation has not turned lower by then, Generali's over-priced call is dead and Morgan Stanley's 4.25-4.50% stops being the high end and becomes the floor.
