## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The AI melt-up is a squeeze, and its own confirmation signals have not signed off

*This rally's fuel is trapped positioning rather than new AI news, so the move is to buy the parts of the trade that have not squeezed instead of chasing the parts that have.*

- Goldman's prime brokerage desk (the arm that finances hedge funds and sees their flows) recorded the largest liquidation of global technology stocks on record between July 24 and July 29, and hedge funds focused on tech, media, and telecom lost 10.2% in July, the worst month in their history (JPMorgan). The natural institutional buyers were forced out at the lows, so this bounce is running on thin ownership.
- Monday's session delivered a 1.79% S&P 500 move against the 0.4% the options market had priced (Goldman), on a record day for total S&P call volume (JPMorgan). Dealers who sold those calls are short gamma, meaning they must buy as the market rises, which mechanically amplifies every up-move.
- Credit default swaps on the hyperscalers (the cost of insuring the big cloud companies' debt, which rises when investors get nervous) are widening again, the same signal that showed up before July's tech selloff.
- The Korea ETF $EWY sits 22% below its all-time high and Taiwan's $EWT 9% below (JPMorgan) while US semis rally. The stocks that led the AI boom are not confirming this rebound.
- The demand side is not the problem: the Mag7's AI backlog is near $1.7 trillion and growing more than 150% year over year against roughly 80% capex growth (JPMorgan), and Goldman just raised its 2027 AI circuit-board market forecast by 38% to $38 billion.

The buyers changed, not the story. Forced sellers exited in July, retail flowed back through memory names and semiconductor ETFs, and short-gamma dealers buy strength, so price is currently set by flows while the deepest-pocketed holders have structurally less capacity to return.

The bull case has real money behind it. JPMorgan is tactically long, arguing the Mag7 trades more than 2 standard deviations below its average forward price-to-earnings ratio (its price against next year's expected profits, a rare discount versus its own history) while S&P 500 second-quarter profit margins hit 16.7% versus 12.9% a year ago, and cheap plus accelerating usually wins. Cheap and accelerating still needs holders with capacity, though, and the same JPMorgan flow work concludes hedge fund capacity for tech is structurally reduced, leaving the market leaning on retail, the least stable ownership base in a drawdown. Buy $QQQ on dips only, not on chases, until either credit spreads or Korea confirm.

**The Korea sub-thread.** JPMorgan reads $EWY and $EWT as catch-up trades with room to run. The skeptical read is that SK Hynix and Samsung keep failing at their 21-day moving averages (the short-term trend line) while Korean implied volatility near 80 prices roughly 5% daily swings, so the laggards may be a warning rather than a discount. Does the KOSPI, Korea's main stock index, break its downtrend before hyperscaler credit spreads break wider? Long $EWY is the bet that it does, sized for the 5%-a-day tape.

## 3. BRIEFS

### The Hormuz deal is a toll booth, not a peace treaty

*Oil's floor holds because this arrangement defers the hard questions instead of answering them.*

Iran and Oman reached a provisional deal on Strait of Hormuz shipping: a 60-day arrangement under which Iran suspends its toll ambitions for 2 to 4 months and vessels transit under a notification regime (TS Lombard), which in practice leaves Iran controlling the strait while looking cooperative. The sticking points that killed prior attempts are still live. Iran reportedly wants a 7% transit fee with exemptions for Russia and China against a US proposal of 5% (Rabobank), and the risk premium is not gone: a Houthi tanker attack cut Gulf shipping traffic overnight, Tehran threatened Gulf states over any new US strikes, and oil opened higher this morning. Brent found a floor around $80 and the bond market's oil-driven rally stalled there (Mizuho). Long $BNO at the floor. A signed deal that genuinely resolves the toll dispute is what ends the trade.

### September's rate cut is the rally's weakest leg

*The market is holding a coin-flip cut in one hand and hot services inflation in the other, and one of them drops.*

Market pricing for a September Fed cut slipped to 54% from 58% after the services survey's prices paid gauge jumped to 70.3 against 65.0 expected and Kansas City Fed's Schmid explicitly backed a hike to finish the inflation job. ADP's 44k was soft, but JPMorgan and Goldman both kept their official payroll forecasts at 75k because the private count is a poor predictor of the government's first print. The sharper claim comes from TS Lombard: Fed Chair Warsh holds a 7-5 committee majority, US fundamentals will eventually force tightening rather than easing, and the 10-year yield reaches 6% in 2027. With the 10-year term premium (the extra yield investors demand for holding long-term bonds) at 0.8% versus a 1.2% long-run average, long bonds are priced for the friendly outcome. Cost of being wrong: a September cut that is already half priced, worth a modest bounce in $TLT. Cost of missing it if the hawks are right: a multi-year repricing toward that 6% call. Friday's jobs report is the next test. Short $TLT.

### Earnings breadth is the real bull case

*The quarter is beating by enough, in enough places, to fund a rally that does not depend on seven stocks.*

About 80% of S&P 500 companies have beaten estimates versus a 73% historical norm, and the median beat is 5.8% against a 3.5% average (UBS), enough surprise to fund upside beyond the Mag7. The price confirmation: July's equal-weighted S&P 500 (every stock counts the same) gained 0.9% while the cap-weighted index slipped 0.1% (UBS), which is what broadening looks like. The cleanest single-name expression is Shopify: 32% growth in gross merchandise volume (the total value of goods sold on its platform) against the Street's 28%, third-quarter revenue guided to low-30s growth versus the Street's 27%, and Deutsche Bank reiterating Buy with a $175 target on a reacceleration it expects to drive estimate revisions. If the third-quarter print lands below that low-30s guide, the thesis is dead. Until then, long $SHOP.

### Europe's upgrade has a doubter in the room

*The earnings recovery is real enough that the region's biggest skeptic just raised its own European growth forecast.*

UBS upgraded European and Eurozone equities to Attractive with regional earnings growth forecast at 25% across 2026 and 2027, and European shares hit a record high this morning on earnings and US-Iran optimism. The doubt sits at the top: JPMorgan raised its Europe third-quarter GDP forecast to 1.25% annualized, well above the ECB's 0.5%, yet still prefers US and Asian equities over the region. For a US account the actionable leg is the banks. Goldman calls the selloff in HSBC on China's reported offshore-insurance tax an overreaction, since insurance is roughly 4 to 5% of earnings, and the bank earns a 19.2% return on tangible equity (the profit it generates on its core capital base). The ECB's September hike is 84% priced. Delivered without cracking the activity data, it validates the upgrade. Long $HSBC.
