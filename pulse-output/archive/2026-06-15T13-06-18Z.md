## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil scare is coming out of the price

*The energy-supply premium is unwinding, and the physical data says it should keep bleeding lower.*

- June flows through the Strait of Hormuz recovered to 5.1 million barrels a day, up from 2.2-3.3mbd across March-May (JPMorgan), so the disruption the market priced is already reversing in the physical barrels
- Goldman cut its 2027 average Brent call to $80 from $85 and sees $90 in Q4 2026, a structurally-lower-oil house view that predates the deal
- Goldman expects Hormuz flows to normalize to roughly 70% capacity by late August
- Each new escalation headline has moved oil less than the last, the market has stopped paying up for the same threat

The supply hole the market spent weeks pricing turned out smaller than feared, and with barrels physically moving again the premium has nothing to hold it up. That barrels are recovering ahead of any paper resolution is the tell that the trade has momentum regardless of the next wobble. The honest counter from Rabobank is that the framework is fragile and refined-fuel inventories stay tight enough that a single breakdown headline could snap the thread back. Shippers are still cautious on actually transiting the strait until mine-clearance details land, so the reopening is announced but not yet flowing. Short $USO, with an energy-sector underweight into the de-escalation.

### The AI build is still being underestimated, but the bill is showing

*Hyperscaler spending keeps getting revised higher, and the bottlenecks are where the real demand lives.*

- 2026 hyperscaler capex is tracking about $750B, up 84% from 2025 and $200B above where estimates started the year (Goldman), the second upward revision in a month
- Consensus 2027 capex sits near $920B, and even that looks light against a $1.1-1.4T sketch (Goldman)
- Morgan Stanley models NVIDIA compute-sold compounding around 210% a year through 2028, with memory-chip (DRAM) wafer capacity expanding only 30% by 2027, so memory is the structural bottleneck
- A record $12.3B flowed into tech funds in a single week, the money is still chasing the trade
- TS Lombard's pushback: 85% of AI revenue is capex recycling, the same handful of companies paying each other

The spend is real, and the constraints (power, memory, labor) are where it bottlenecks, which is why the picks-and-shovels names keep getting paid rather than the model-builders. The sharpest counter comes from Goldman itself, which flags that the capex lifting chip earnings today will weigh on hyperscaler return on invested capital later. The bottlenecks resolve that tension, because demand for power and memory is real money no matter whose income statement it eventually dents. The lean is long memory ($MU, Morgan Stanley Overweight) plus the power read-through that the build cannot happen without, the independent power producers ($VST, $CEG) and utilities ($XLU).

### The Fed is done cutting, and Warsh is the wildcard

*The rate-cut path is gone, and the open question is whether Warsh's first meeting cracks the door the other way.*

- Goldman no longer expects any 2026 cuts and pushed its final two cuts all the way out to June and December 2027
- ING expects a hold Wednesday with no hike, while Bank of America argues that by not hiking into this data the Fed is effectively easing
- May core PCE (the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, stripping out food and energy) is tracking 0.31% against a prior 0.28% expectation, still sticky
- The household side is cooling fast: University of Michigan one-year inflation expectations fell to 4.6% (down 0.2pp) and the 5-to-10-year measure dropped to 3.4%, well below forecasts
- The real swing factor is whether Warsh signals the Fed is actively considering hiking (MUFG)

The hawkish repricing across desks runs straight into softening household inflation expectations, and that gap is what makes Wednesday live rather than a formality. A hold that reads hawkish bids the dollar and pressures long-dated Treasuries, while any dovish surprise from a chair the market doesn't know yet snaps it the other way. The question is whether Warsh opens the door to a hike before that sticky core inflation rolls over. Long $TLT is the bet he doesn't and the cuts eventually come back. The opposite expression, for traders who think the hawkish flip has further to run, is long $UUP.

### Smart money already left the trade retail is buying

*Desk positioning has flipped to neutral while flows stay max-long the same names, the late-cycle setup that rewards rotation.*

- Hedge fund net leverage is back at the 8th percentile of its 12-month range, down 4% across all strategies (JPMorgan), the fast money has already cut exposure
- One-week positioning swung from better than +2 standard deviations to worse than -2, euphoria to neutral in days
- The momentum factor took a roughly 16% intraday drawdown and touched its 200-day average (JPMorgan), the factor that carried the rally is cracking
- Yet $31B flowed into global equity funds and a record amount into tech, passive and retail are still pouring in
- Breadth is genuinely improving on the other side: 56% of MSCI ACWI members sit above their 200-day line (JPMorgan)

Desks cutting exposure while retail buys the same crowded names is the classic late-cycle handoff, and the momentum break is the first crack. The honest counter is that improving breadth leaves room for a broad-based rally, but that only happens if the laggards take leadership, which is the rotation itself, not a reason to stay in the leaders. If the momentum factor doesn't reclaim its 200-day average by month-end, the unwind is real. Until then the trade is rotating into equal-weight $RSP over $SPY, paired with $XLU as the defensive complement.

### A BoJ hike decides whether Japan broadens

*Tonight's Bank of Japan meeting is the catalyst that says whether Japan's rally spreads past the chip names.*

- A 25bp hike to 1.00% is widely expected at tonight's 10:30 PM ET decision (ING, Mizuho), with Goldman seeing a 1.5% peak rate
- JPMorgan pegs the Ministry of Finance intervention trigger near 161.95 yen to the dollar, the yen is the pressure valve
- JPMorgan is the lone directional voice, arguing the equity gains are broadening beyond the AI-chip leaders into domestics and financials
- Overnight wires flag that the Iran peace deal doesn't change the BoJ's hike plans, so the catalyst is still live tonight

A hike plus a firmer yen reshuffles who leads, and the desks are split between treating Japan as a pure rates-and-currency story and JPMorgan's call that the rally is widening. The risk is that a hawkish BoJ and a stronger yen hit the exporters that led the move in the first place. The offset is that broadening into domestic names is exactly what a normalization cycle should produce, not a reason to fade Japan wholesale. The question is whether the yen breaks 162 and forces the Ministry's hand before the hike even lands. For US-listed exposure, long the currency-hedged $DXJ is the bet the yen firms and the hedge pays, against unhedged $EWJ.
