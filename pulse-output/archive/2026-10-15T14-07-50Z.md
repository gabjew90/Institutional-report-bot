## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The AI selloff is a funding scare, and strong orders cannot fix it

*Chip stocks are falling because lenders now want much more to finance the AI buildout, and a company beating its own sales targets does not change that.*

- Chip stocks fell for a sixth straight session on Wednesday, the worst streak since July (Goldman), and Korea's Kospi is down more than 12% this month (UBS), so this is a global unwind and not a US blip.
- Oracle's price to insure its debt against default sits near a record 261 basis points, and stocks of the biggest cloud spenders are running well ahead of what their own bonds imply (Goldman), so lenders are charging more than shareholders are pricing in.
- The lead banks on a $5.0B loan for GPU cloud startup Volta committed to only $1.3B of it, with the package yielding 11.21% to 11.75%, so they are keeping the risk off their own books.
- Goldman's basket of GPU cloud stocks dropped about 11% on Wednesday, a top-five daily move in five years, because those companies live on borrowed money.
- Developed-market bond yields are up 100 to 120 basis points this year to levels last seen in 2007 (Goldman), which raises the price of every new AI financing.

AI accounts for 49% of this year's US bond sales by highly rated companies (Apollo) and 2027 spending estimates for the big cloud companies have reached $1.13 trillion, so when borrowing costs rise the weakest borrowers reprice first and their stock follows. China's CXMT claiming a memory chip design breakthrough added a pricing threat to memory makers, and Samsung fell 8%, SK Hynix 10% and Kioxia 13% (Goldman).

The selling is a rotation and not a liquidation in the read of Goldman strategist Rich Privorotsky, because hedge funds got "incredibly long one corner and effectively short everything else." The drop is a reset rather than a break, since cloud revenue growth is still expected to reach 58% in the third quarter (UBS). Chips are tracking the 1999-2000 path, with a deeper drop possible in late October (BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky). ASML raised its 2026 sales guide and said customer demand exceeds what it can build, then fell 3.9%, so orders are not setting the price and the credit signals above are. The bear case is that one big cloud spender cutting its capital spending ends the boom, and Meta's report on October 21 after the close is the first read on that (Goldman). Sell any bounce in $SOXX after seven straight losses (BTIG), while the hardware dip ultimately gets bought (Goldman's Privorotsky).

## 3. BRIEFS

### Hot core inflation kept the Fed in play

*A second straight upside surprise in core prices came from shelter and goods, which is harder for the Fed to wave off than gasoline.*

September core CPI rose 0.3% against 0.2% expected, and the annual rate went to 2.6% from 2.4%. Housing was the driver, with the housing cost for homeowners up 0.35% on the month and the Midwest up 0.68% (Goldman), while higher diesel costs have replaced tariffs as the main push on goods prices (PNC). Core prices are running at a 3.4% annualized pace over three months (PNC). Goldman raised its estimate for September core PCE, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, on the data. Bond buyers are looking past all of it today, with the 10-year yield down 14 basis points on the tech selloff. Producer prices printed at 8:30 this morning, so the reaction matters more than the number, and Fed Chair Warsh speaks tonight at 11:30 PM ET, which is the next test of whether the Fed treats this as a trend.

### Banks split on October, agree on December

*The Fed's October 28 decision is a coin flip on paper, and the December hike is the part nobody argues with.*

Market odds of an October 28 hike were near 50% right after the data, and two desks say the print keeps it alive. Several Fed officials want to see the effects of September's hike first, which raises the bar for October (SEB). The odds faded by the close to 35% for October and 87% for December (Goldman). Goldman and TS Lombard both expect at least one more hike in December but split on size, with TS Lombard's Steve Blitz saying to bet on 50 basis points and Goldman expecting 25. NY Fed President Williams said one more hike this year is appropriate and none should be needed in 2027, far less than the three hikes by June 2027 the market prices. The FOMC meets October 27-28, and Warsh's remarks tonight are the first read on which way October breaks.

### Oil sits well above its year-end forecast

*The market is paying a war premium that Goldman's forecast says fades by December.*

Goldman raised its December Brent forecast to $90 a barrel from $85 because of ongoing risks to oil supply, with Brent last at $106, so its year-end view is $16 below the price. Natixis has Brent futures at $107 on October 12 after tanker attacks and a hurricane that shut 25% of US Gulf of Mexico output, and Riyadh's main airport has been closed since October 10 as Houthi attacks reach Saudi soil. Gulf stocks fell this morning after the Houthis threatened more Saudi attacks, though Brent-tracking $BNO is down 1.32% as the growth scare competes with the war premium. SEB's scenario-weighted view is $97 in three months against $100.60 in its note. Iran has stopped answering US conditions in the stalled peace talks (Natixis), so a deal is not the base case. Brent holding above $100 into the October 28 Fed meeting would add to the energy-driven pressure already in headline CPI.

### Goldman and Morgan Stanley disagree on whether the market is healthy

*The index is at a record but the typical stock is not, and the two biggest strategy desks read that gap in opposite ways.*

The five largest S&P 500 companies carry about $22 trillion of value, 30% of the index and the same weight as 434 smaller stocks (Goldman's Bobby Molavi), and the median stock is 15% to 16% below its high. Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson says 85% of S&P names sit more than 10% below their highs, tighter financial conditions are already doing the Fed's work, and stocks now pay less than bonds. Goldman's Tony Pasquariello counters that 123 names are up 25% or more this year against 42 down that much, and that investor positioning is in its 10th percentile. Goldman's basket of high-quality companies rose 16% in eight weeks, the fastest since the 2020 shock. Wilson's two tells are financial stocks and the MOVE index (a gauge of bond-market swings), and Goldman's derivatives desk favors owning $RSP, the equal-weight fund, over the regular index through the October 30 options expiry.
