## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hyperscalers double down, capex now 100% of cash flow

The AI capex super-cycle has stopped being a forecast. Guide-ups, run-rate revenue, and a buyback drawdown are all confirming the build at the same time.

Goldman lifted 2026 hyperscaler capex to $755bn, up 83% year-over-year, a number that now equals 100% of cash flow from operations. UniCredit triangulates to $710-725bn on the same basket, which is roughly 75% above 2025 and five times 2023 levels. Meta lifted its 2026 capex guide to $125-145bn from $115-135bn, and Alphabet raised to $180-190bn. Microsoft's AI annualized revenue run-rate is $37bn, up 123% year-over-year. The spend is converting into revenue, not running into a wall. The clearest tell that boards have moved capital allocation: hyperscaler buybacks were down 64% year-over-year in Q1 2026. Cash that used to buy back stock is now buying GPUs and grid capacity.

The bear case is positioning. BofA's Flow Show puts the AI Big 10 at 40% of S&P 500 market cap, the same concentration seen at historical bubble peaks. Hedge fund books at Goldman's prime brokerage have momentum exposure at the 99th percentile (1-yr) and 100th percentile (5-yr), and high-beta momentum already took an 8% one-day drawdown this week, the fifth such air pocket in 2026. Q1 EPS growth came in at 17% (the fastest in four years) and the 2026 capex figure has been revised up by $80bn in two weeks. That isn't the shape of a rollover. Net debt/EBITDA at hyperscalers is 0.3x, so the build is debt-fundable if cash flow ever lags. Memory and semicaps are where the bid is showing up in price, with $DRAM pulling in $1bn in a single day.

### Hormuz: paper market priced peace, physical market hasn't refilled

The MoU framework that pushed Brent from $108 to $98 isn't signed, the export bottleneck hasn't reversed, and Friday's overnight headlines are reminding the futures curve this isn't a one-headline story.

Strait of Hormuz oil exports are running at 4% of normal levels on a 4-day moving average. The constraint is still binding. US distillate inventories sit 11% below the 5-year average and refinery utilization at 90.1% is slipping when it should be ramping into summer. Brent fell roughly 11% from $108 to $98 on the MoU headline. The framework still includes a $270bn Iran reparations demand and isn't a signed deal. Tanker tracking shows 45% of the Persian Gulf export hit is being offset by US/Americas/Russia barrels, but that re-route adds days to delivery. Friday's headlines (US strikes on two empty Iranian tankers, Iran's seizure of the Ocean Koi, the suspected Kharg Island spill) are the market reminding itself the diplomatic framework is fragile.

The bear case is real: rerouted barrels are partially closing the gap, Brazil's output just hit an all-time high of 4.25mb/d (+17% YoY), and China's oil demand nowcast is down 1.5mb/d (8%) from mid-March. But rerouted barrels arrive on a multi-week lag, and distillate-only product tightness sets the marginal price for refined fuels right now. April CPI energy is forecast +17.3% YoY driven by an 11-12% MoM motor-fuels rise, and the inflation print will drive the oil story whether crude reverses or not. The binary setup (signed deal vs. fresh strike) favors long vol from these levels.

Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline.

### Rate-cut repricing split: easing call vs premature pushback

The bond market is split on whether central-bank tightening fear has run its course, and Friday's NFP beat just gave the skeptics a fresh data point two trading days before CPI.

A 3.25% Fed funds rate by December 2026 is the call from UBS, implying two cuts in 2H26 and a divergence from market pricing of no cuts this year. ING expects a more gradual path, citing oil-driven structural inflation as the binding constraint. April private payrolls landed at +123k vs +75k consensus, with the pre-print forecast dispersion (-15k from Citi, +45k from MUFG) the widest in months, and the actual print tilts the data toward the ING side. 10-year yields finished the day at 4.35%, down 8bps on the geopolitical de-escalation flow. UBS frames the recent yield rise as an opportunity to lock in 4.3-4.4% real income just by holding the bonds, even if the Fed never cuts. Crédit Agricole expects April CPI at 3.77% YoY headline on Tuesday, peaking at 4.2% in May, above the level the Fed can comfortably cut into.

The bull case for cuts is softening growth: the breakeven job-growth model has the labor market needing only 50k/month by year-end to stabilize unemployment, down from 65k now, and the consumer is borrowing through the squeeze with savings at cycle lows. The binding constraint on the Fed isn't payrolls. It's a 4.2% CPI peak in May with energy CPI up 17.3% YoY. The 8.2% U-6 print today is a soft-data tell, but the headline beat is what the front-end will trade on Tuesday morning.

Short $UUP into Tuesday's CPI, paired with long $TLT for the separate UBS leg in long-dated Treasuries, where the longer the maturity, the bigger the price move when yields shift.
