# Crowded trade cracks

[LIVE PRICE RECAP] The research this window points at one thing above all else. The crowded bet on this year's winners, the chip stocks and the AI-leadership complex, is going through one of its sharpest unwinds in years. Desks broadly agree the selling is leveraged positioning being forced out rather than anything broken in the underlying businesses, and they split hard on whether the washout is nearly done and worth buying or has further to fall. Underneath that fight, the AI infrastructure spending story keeps compounding even as the debt paying for it pushes up corporate borrowing costs, oil sits on a geopolitical bid from the Strait of Hormuz standoff, and long-term Treasury yields keep grinding higher.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The winner trade cracked, and it is positioning not fundamentals

*The crowded bet on this year's chip and AI leaders is unwinding on forced selling, which makes this a washout to fade into rather than a reason to doubt the businesses.*

- Momentum, the simple strategy of buying whatever has been going up, just had its biggest washout in years, the sharpest since the early 2000s and the worst since the December 2022 to February 2023 episode (Goldman). A drop this size is historically a positioning flush, not the opening act of a fundamental bear market.
- The Philadelphia semiconductor index ($SOX) has underperformed the broad market by about 20 points since the late-June highs. The exact cohort that carried the tape all year is the epicenter of the selling.
- The move is being described as a massive early-quarter rotation out of chips and not a fundamental one. No earnings estimates have been cut, so the companies are intact while the crowd exits the position.
- Goldman's desk put an explicit buy on the momentum drawdown across AI, semiconductors and tech hardware for the next one to three months. The same house that flagged the washout now thinks it is close to a level worth stepping into.
- JPMorgan's Schlegel also sees momentum bouncing over the next one to three months, a second desk arguing the flush is late-stage rather than early.
