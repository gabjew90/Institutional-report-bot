## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI hyperscaler capex eats buybacks

*The 2026 hyperscaler capex number is no longer a forecast, it is the current run-rate, and earnings keep validating it.*

- Goldman raised 2026 hyperscaler capex to $755B, +83% year-over-year, equal to 100% of operating cash flows
- Hyperscaler buybacks fell 64% YoY in 1Q26 (Goldman) as cash redirected to AI infrastructure
- $200B Anthropic backlog now tied to GOOGL alone (Goldman, citing The Information)
- Hyperscaler net debt to EBITDA of 0.3x leaves ample room for debt issuance to fund capex
- Datadog ($DDOG) +30% on the print breathed life back into software, with RPO growth above 50% in 1Q26 (Citi)
- BofA flags AI Big 10 at 40% of S&P 500, the same concentration profile as Nifty Fifty, Japan in the 80s, and the late-90s Internet bubble

The structural shift Goldman is calling out is not a story about one hyperscaler beating estimates. It is the entire S&P 500 cash-allocation framework changing direction at once. Buybacks were the post-2010 default use of corporate cash, and that default has now been displaced by AI capex for the largest cohort in the index. JPMorgan is reading the same data as a productivity story, arguing AI investment is a key driver of recent economic strength. UBS is leaning the same way across its CEO macro briefing book.

The bear case is the part the corpus is split on. Goldman's TMT desk flagged an 8% one-day drawdown in high-beta momentum, the fifth such instance this year and one of the top ten one-day moves in five years, with Prime Brokerage book momentum exposure now sitting at the 99th percentile on a one-year lookback and the 100th percentile on five years. BofA's read is more pointed: 40% S&P concentration in 10 names is the historical signature of a top, and previous instances ended in bond-yield spikes that broke the equity narrative. The bull defense is that historically, aggressive momentum unwinds have been opportunities to buy the dip in dominant themes like AI rather than tops. The corpus does not support fading the structural capex story on positioning data alone, but it does support being long the picks-and-shovels names where compute scarcity is the binding constraint rather than the most-crowded mega-cap longs. Long $SOXX into the next hyperscaler capex update.

### Bond market is pricing the Fed wrong, and the corpus says so

*Markets have repriced the Fed to a near-no-cut path for 2026, and three out of four banks covering the theme think it is wrong in the other direction.*

- TME flags the bond market is "pricing the Federal Reserve to stay on hold for a year" after a -3 standard deviation TLT impulse
- Goldman has the 30-year Treasury yield support level at 5%, which TME calls the critical pain threshold for stocks
- TS Lombard expects the Fed to cut the funds rate while letting repo rate spreads widen, tightening financial conditions even as the headline cut lands
- TS Lombard also flags $660B of coupons the Fed needs to swap into bills to reach a 20-25% target ratio, with reserves at 8.5% of bank assets approaching scarcity levels
- UBS sees two cuts in 2H26 driven by cooling inflation and moderating growth, the only Tier-1 voice in the corpus genuinely defending the dovish path
- Crédit Agricole has April CPI at 3.77% YoY (3 basis points above 3.74% consensus) and projects the headline peak at 4.2% YoY in May, driven by an 11-12% month-over-month rise in motor fuels

The mechanism three of these banks are converging on is that an oil-driven inflation impulse plus deteriorating Treasury supply absorption is the combination that prevents the Fed from cutting cleanly. Headline CPI at 4.2% in May is not a backdrop into which the FOMC can comfortably deliver a rate cut, even if private payroll and services data soften. TS Lombard's specific call is that you should not look at the funds-rate decision to know whether policy is being eased. You should watch the repo market: the Fed can deliver a political cut and simultaneously tighten financial conditions through the plumbing, and that is a worse outcome for risk than the bond market currently prices.

The pushback comes from UBS, who argues markets have exaggerated the risk that central banks will tighten policy in response to energy prices. UBS reads the energy shock as transitory enough that a Fed cutting cycle still arrives in the second half. Where this thesis breaks is on Crédit Agricole's data: if April CPI prints at 3.77% and May peaks at 4.2%, the mechanical headline number itself becomes the constraint, regardless of how the FOMC reads the underlying. The 30-year Treasury yield holding 5% is the level Goldman desk says rates have to break for stocks to come under real pressure, and that is the level a hot CPI print could clear. Long $TLT into next week's CPI is the cleanest expression of the cuts-eventually view, paired with awareness that the corpus is leaning the other way.

### Hormuz, peace headlines and physical scarcity

*Each round of Iran-US diplomacy has rallied risk and faded as substance failed to materialize, while the physical oil market keeps tightening underneath.*

- SEB tracks Brent moving from $108 on May 6 to roughly $98 on the MoU headlines, a typical fade pattern across diplomatic cycles since March
- API crude draw of 8.1mb is the largest in three months versus 2.8mb consensus (SEB), a real physical-tightness signal
- Bloomberg's December 2026 WTI futures price is in the high-$80s, up from roughly $70 in early April
- 5-year and 10-year breakeven inflation expectations are at the highest levels since 2023 (Bloomberg)
- Goldman has Strait of Hormuz flows running at 4% of normal levels (4-day moving average), with 45% of the Persian Gulf hit being offset by US, Americas, and Russian production
- Polymarket has 50% probability of Hormuz normalization by end of June (SEB)
- TME flags equity markets are ignoring $100 Brent

The bifurcation is clean. Headline-driven futures markets keep trading the diplomatic optics — a memorandum of understanding gets signed, Brent drops $10, equity beta lifts, and the move fades as the next negotiating round shows the same gap between negotiating positions. Bloomberg's read is that breakeven inflation pricing has caught up to the persistence of the disruption, while spot crude has not. UBS sees an 11% Brent decline if the Strait reopens cleanly, with the ratio of bull-to-bear depending on whether shipowners and insurers ratify any diplomatic framework with actual willingness to transit.

The skeptical case is straight from SEB and is the one the corpus has the most evidence for: every diplomatic cycle since March has rallied risk on the announcement and faded as the substance failed to land. Goldman's bear scenario is the durable Iran resolution case, where OPEC fragmentation and structural oversupply pull crude back toward the $80-90 range SEB also flags. The bull case Goldman is defending is that conflict drags on, balances stay tight, and prices stay supported. The single Goldman desk number worth holding onto is the $120 WTI level desk has flagged as the disruption threshold for a regime change in the narrative. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline captures the "physical scarcity does not care about the MoU" view, with downside framed by the SEB-flagged $80-90 fade range.

### Gold's squeeze setup is rebuilding

*The 50-day moving average and $4800 resistance are the breakout level, and central bank buying plus a Tether overlay is the demand stack supporting it.*

- TME has $4800 as the gold 50-day MA and resistance, the level for a sustained squeeze
- Q1 central bank gold purchases of 244 tonnes are up 17% quarter-over-quarter (TME)
- UBS year-end gold target is $5900/oz on the constructive case
- Tether's gold holdings are nearing $20B and growing (Goldman digital assets desk)
- A GLD June 440/480 call spread offers up to 5x max payout (TME) on a sustained move

The setup that TME is flagging is a coiling pattern with a clear technical level and a fundamental demand stack underneath. Central banks bought 244 tonnes in the first quarter and the pace stepped up 17% quarter-over-quarter. Tether is adding what looks like a permanent crypto-overlay bid on top, and UBS's $5900/oz target gives the upside case explicit institutional support. The breakout level is the 50-day moving average around $4800, and the trade has the convexity profile of a long volatility position with a defined-risk option expression.

The bear case TME itself flags is that gold has not been behaving like a fear hedge recently — it is trading inversely to the VIX rather than reacting to volatility, which is a sign the haven-bid mechanism is broken. The invalidation level is straightforward: sustained failure to break $4800 means the squeeze setup never triggers and the option premium decays. The defense is that the central bank flow is structural rather than tactical and the Tether overlay is recurring, so the breakout setup rebuilds even after a failed attempt. Long the GLD June 440/480 call spread is the cleanest expression of the squeeze view, with risk capped at debit paid and convexity preserved if $4800 clears.
