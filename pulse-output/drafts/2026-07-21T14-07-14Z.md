# Chips wobble, money rotates

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The research paints a market pulling in two directions at once. Underneath a calm headline tape, the crowded semiconductor and momentum trade that led all year is unwinding hard, with the cap-weighted indexes dragged lower by chips while the equal-weight S&P 500 and financials, industrials and transport quietly print record highs. Most desks read the selling as mechanical rather than fundamental, hedge funds have cut technology exposure at a record pace and leverage has been flushed out, and several think the reset is close to exhausted. The fresher story is capital rotating into cheap Chinese tech as free open-source AI models raise doubts about the US lead, while oil jumped on renewed Iran and Kuwait tensions. Earnings season is providing a floor, with beat rates running near record highs.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The crowded chip trade is cracking, and it is nearly out of sellers

*The July selloff in chips and momentum is a crowded-positioning reset that is running out of sellers, not the start of an AI downturn.*

- High-beta momentum is down 33% from its late-June high, now up 12% for the year against 60% in June, so the froth is gone but so is the easy trend that funds were riding.
- Hedge funds have sold technology in 6 of the past 8 weeks, the largest stretch of selling in more than 10 years, and pulled $63bn out of leveraged chip ETFs (a 39% cut), which means the forced sellers are mostly done rather than just starting.
- US long/short net leverage has fallen to the 11th percentile of the past year, a level of de-risking that historically precedes a bounce, not more selling.
- JPMorgan estimates the leveraged-ETF unwind in Korea is 75% complete and hedge-fund de-leveraging is more than half done, so the mechanical selling that opened the air-pocket is fading.
- Semiconductors now make up over 20% of daily global trading volume and are set to drive close to half of S&P 500 earnings growth in 2026, which makes this not a sector story but the whole market.

The unwind was about positioning, not the business. When 82% of surveyed fund managers call long semiconductors the most crowded trade on the planet, everyone is trying to leave through the same narrow door, and a small shove turns into a stampede as leveraged ETFs and dealer hedging amplify every down-tick. The buildout underneath the trade never actually broke, TSMC still guided to more than 40% revenue growth for 2026 and ASML's results point to double-digit earnings upgrades. Where this gets dangerous is the concentration itself, a genuine rollover in chips would drag the entire high-beta complex down with it, and momentum is only following its usual script after a 20%-plus run, where roughly a third of past chip drawdowns this size kept falling past 30% before they found a floor. The historical record cuts the other way once the leverage is gone, the average return 20 trading days after a 20% drop in the chip index is a positive 8%, and realized moves in the Nasdaq 100 have been running 1.4 times richer than what options are pricing, one of the widest gaps outside the COVID crash. For someone with US options exposure the trade is owning $QQQ calls into earnings rather than chasing single chip names, with the Nasdaq 100's 28,500 level flagged in the research as the line that says the reset has failed.

### China bids while open-source eats the AI moat

*The freshest trade in the tape is money leaving crowded US and Korean AI names for cheap Chinese tech as free open-source models chip away at America's AI advantage.*

Chinese internet stocks trade at 14 times forward earnings against 25 times for US peers, US firms' use of Chinese open-source model tokens is trending toward a 60% share, and Alibaba has already rebounded more than 30% from its late-June oversold low. Capital is rotating into $KWEB, $CQQQ and $MCHI and out of Korea ($EWY) and Taiwan ($EWT), with Goldman recommending the China AI value chain and JPMorgan flagging Hong Kong tech as a squeeze candidate. The threat to the US names is real, DeepSeek's open model wiped $600bn off Nvidia in a single day back in January, a reminder of how violently this can hit pricing power. The other side is that the White House is weighing a block on US companies using Chinese open-source models, and export controls plus heavy domestic investment still back the US lead. Cost of being wrong on the China bounce is small with positioning this light, cost of missing a Hong Kong tech squeeze if Alibaba clears resistance is a fast 15% to 20%, which argues for $BABA call spreads or $KWEB rather than outright longs.

### The AI buildout is now running on borrowed money

*The AI buildout is increasingly paid for with debt, and the bond market is starting to charge for it.*

Hyperscaler capital spending is set to reach about $750bn in 2026 out of $870bn of total AI spend, and the five biggest hyperscalers' debt issuance has jumped to $190bn this year from $40bn to $50bn in 2022. Goldman puts the sector's 2025 to 2030 funding need at $5.5 trillion to $6 trillion as free cash flow turns negative, and Alphabet alone raised $85bn of equity in June. The desks split on what it means, JPMorgan says the high-grade bond market can easily absorb the supply and stays positive on hyperscalers into 2026, while Goldman warns the shift to debt is already widening credit spreads and concentrating risk. If a Meta or Alphabet capex guide-down fails to show up this earnings season, the credit worry stays a slow burn, and the trade is owning the hyperscalers ($GOOGL, $MSFT, $META) over the AI-hardware names that live off their spending, with high-grade technology spreads as the early-warning light that says the funding math has turned.

### The real regime change is Corporate America spending again

*The regime change that matters is not AI, it is Corporate America swinging from hoarding cash to aggressive spending, and that argues for owning volatility.*

US capital spending rose 33% in 2025 and Morgan Stanley sees a further 23% in 2026 and 26% in 2027, against a 7% average over 2010 to 2025, while global announced mergers and acquisitions are up 64% year over year. This is the end of the capital-light corporate model, and a more aggressive, less predictable corporate and policy backdrop makes large market swings more likely, which is why the call is to own volatility (the trades that pay off when markets move a lot) rather than sell it. The risk to the view is that the market reverts to a calm, grind-higher regime where selling volatility quietly pays better month after month. Watch in this order: (1) the cleanest expression is owning volatility in rates and currencies as central-bank visibility drops, (2) rising M&A activity as confirmation the corporate animal spirits are real, (3) any capex guide-down as the first signal the spending cycle is stalling.

### The quiet rotation out of the crowded lead

*The trade that is actually working is not the AI bounce, it is money leaving the crowded top of the market for the parts that have been left behind.*

While the cap-weighted indexes fell on the chip unwind, the equal-weight S&P 500 hit record highs on rotation into financials, industrials and transport. US banks are posting record 17% year-over-year corporate loan growth and a 19% return on tangible equity, a post-crisis high, with no sign of credit stress. TS Lombard goes further and expects the Fed to start a hiking cycle of 25bps a quarter from this autumn, against a market still pricing cuts, which would keep pressure on the most expensive growth names. Positioning in large-cap technology has already rebounded to the 95th percentile with $71bn of inflows in six weeks, so the crowded side of the boat is still the AI names, not the value and cyclical parts of the market. The counter is that these momentum unwinds have snapped back quickly before, and the banks' own flow desks think the reset is nearly done. The question that decides it: does the equal-weight market keep making highs while chips chop, or does a chip bounce pull leadership straight back to the top ten names? Owning $RSP (the equal-weight S&P, which spreads the bet evenly instead of leaning on the giants) paired with $XLF financials is the way to own the broadening if the answer is the former.
