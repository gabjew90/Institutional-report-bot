# AI capex carries the tape

## 1. RECAP

Hyperscaler spending and AI hardware orders dominate twenty-five different desks. ## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex is the tape and the corpus knows it

*Hyperscaler spending has reached a level where, every time the market doubts the AI build, a single bank prints a higher number and the doubt evaporates.*

- UBS pegs the big-four hyperscaler 2026 capex (Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta) at roughly $700bn — that figure alone is now roughly 100% of those firms' operating cash flow, so capital that used to fund buybacks is buying GPUs
- DWS puts 2026 capex for the six largest US hyperscalers at $700B and calls it a "significant contribution to economic growth" (Wells Fargo agrees, attributing part of its 2.0% Q1 2026 GDP estimate to AI-related investment)
- Anthropic's annualized revenue run-rate hit $45bn, up from $9bn six months ago (UBS) — the application-layer monetization that bears keep saying is missing is in fact showing up in the largest model vendor
- AI-related stocks are up roughly 50% from March lows (UBS) and the 2030 AI data-center systems market estimate was raised to $1.7 trillion from $1.4 trillion (The Market Ear)
- Alibaba's AI-related cloud revenue is now 30% of its external cloud revenue and growing for an eleventh straight quarter (Citi) — the AI cycle is global, not just US-listed

The mechanism behind the tape is simple. Four buyers with effectively unlimited balance sheets are pulling forward years of compute spend, which lands in semis, memory, power, real estate, and storage in roughly that order. Cloud growth at the leaders accelerated to 40% with a $2tn backlog (UBS), so the spending shows up in revenue with a lag, not just on the capex line.

The bear case has two specific shapes. The Market Ear and Bloomberg/Nomura argue the narrative is now priced for perfection and the broader equity rally is sitting on two concentrated bets that an unwind in either takes down the index. UBS itself flags that incremental capex without visible application-layer monetization stops being enough to push stocks higher from here, and a deceleration in cloud growth or an IPO supply wave would be the trigger. The defense is that cloud revenue acceleration to 40% and Anthropic's $9bn-to-$45bn run-rate are the exact monetization prints the bears keep asking for. The bias here is long the picks-and-shovels names — AMAT, $TSM, $MU — paired with selective hyperscaler exposure rather than a one-name $NVDA bet.

### Oil is a supply story banks are bracing for but not chasing

*Twenty banks call out the Strait of Hormuz and Iran conflict as the live oil risk, but only SEB stakes a directional super-spike, and JPMorgan's forecasts hinge on a June reopening.*

- Dated Brent (the physical market price) is $117/barrel and UBS marked spot Brent at $104, up almost 3% on the day, with the oil price already 30% above pre-war levels (UBS)
- Global oil inventories have drained to 7.8 billion barrels from 8.2 billion in February (UBS), and BCA flags depletion running at 11-12 mb/d against 14.5 mb/d of production losses — meaning the physical market is hand-to-mouth
- LSEG measured peak Middle East oil output shut-in at 10.8 million b/d in May, up from 10.5 million in April; SEB has the April shut-in at 10.5 mb/d, up from 7.5 mb/d in March
- JPMorgan's Q2 2026 Brent forecast is $103/bbl on a June 1 Hormuz reopening, but its year-end 2027 Brent target is $64/bbl, roughly 20% below the forward curve — the bank is calling for the supply scare to fully unwind
- ING flags 420k b/d of downward revision to 2026 global oil demand and notes the Iran conflict has now run ten weeks with energy markets still in limbo

Two forces are pulling oil in opposite directions. Physical inventories are draining fast enough that even a ceasefire takes months to refill product stocks, which keeps spot oil bid. But forward curves are pricing a clean resolution, so JPMorgan's $64/bbl 2027 view is consistent with backwardation collapsing the moment Hormuz reopens. The skeptic's view is that ten weeks in, no resolution is in sight and 73.5% Iranian headline inflation (SEB) keeps domestic political pressure on Tehran to escalate, not de-escalate. The defense is that the $300B-$1T cost of war (SEB, versus Iran's 2025 GDP of $371bn) sets a hard ceiling on duration. The setup leans long oil-exposure via $XLE outright into next OPEC headlines, with the $BNO/$USO pair too noisy to trade as a relative-value spread on a daily-pulse signal.

### Consumer resilience is a contested call with a three-month clock

*PNC sees the US consumer still spending on tax refunds and a firm labor market, while Citi, Goldman and UBS keep flagging softening underneath — the support window is roughly three months.*

- PNC tracks 3.4% YoY growth in average checking/savings balances, the household-level evidence that consumers are NOT yet drawing down savings to spend
- The same PNC dataset flags 26% YoY gas spending growth into May, almost all of which is the Iran-driven oil pass-through to the pump (not real demand growth)
- UBS has US unemployment at 4.34%, edging up in April, and headline April CPI at 3.8% driven by rents and fuel — real spending power is leaking, not building
- The Market Ear surfaces 28% growth in retail trading volumes since mid-April and $12tn in self-directed retail equity assets — roughly 10% of total US corporate equity market value — meaning the retail bid into stocks is real but is cannibalizing other discretionary spend
- Citi has UK Q1 GDP at 0.6% QoQ but UK food inflation pushing near 7% (The Market Ear) — the trans-Atlantic read-across is that the consumer-resilience-then-crack pattern is already further along in the UK

The mechanism is the lag from labor softening to consumption. Unemployment ticks up first, savings balances hold, then real income compression catches up six to nine months later. PNC explicitly puts the tax-refund tailwind at roughly three months before it fades, which puts the inflection point in mid-summer. The bull case is that retail's 28% volume surge plus a 29% rally in Goldman's "retail favorites" basket is the consumer voting with their brokerage account, not their grocery cart. The defense for the skeptics is that retail trading activity at 20% of total US trading is a wealth-effect story bounded by the equity rally itself — break the rally and the floor breaks with it. The bias here is paired: long $WMT and consumer staples for trade-down exposure, short discretionary single-names with the lowest earnings revision breadth.
