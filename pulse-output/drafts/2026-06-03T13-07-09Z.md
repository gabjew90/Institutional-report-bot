# Hormuz heats again

## 1. RECAP

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hormuz stays closed longer than equities are pricing

*The split inside the corpus is about timing, not direction. Banks expect a reopening eventually, but Rabobank and MUFG are pushing the date later than the equity tape implies.*

- Rabobank assumes the Strait stays out of normal action through September, with 122 vessels already redirected by US blockade enforcement
- Rabobank pegs cumulative lost production at 2.5 to 2.8B barrels under a 7-month closure, with the current global oil deficit at 11Mb/d
- Brent is trading $94 per SEB and $97.5 per Mizuho's morning quote, with a $10 risk premium embedded in the 6-month future
- MUFG's base case for two Fed cuts in 2026 is explicitly conditional on the Strait reopening and oil rolling back toward $75
- SEB's reopening scenario has WTI converging to $75 and the ECB and Fed pricing in 35bps and 25bps of cuts respectively

Physical oil markets are pricing tightness because vessel reroutes, inventory drawdowns, and a 7-month closure scenario all imply refined-fuel shortfalls within months. Equity markets are pricing resolution because every overnight ceasefire headline gets bought. Today's news is both at once. Trump claims an Iran nuclear-weapon concession, and the IRGC simultaneously hit Kuwait and a US base.

The opposite read SEB sets up is that backwardation in the futures curve already shows the supply scare is in price, and a verified Strait reopening would flush $10 of premium fast. Rabobank's counter is sharper. A formal verified reopening is the trigger, not a tweet, and ceasefire headlines have broken inside weeks twice already this year. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz escalation headline is the trade, and short paper-oil ($USO) is not the hedge because contango roll noise dominates the spread on any given day.

### AI infrastructure consensus keeps moving higher, and the bottleneck is the trade

*The lead theme of the corpus is also the broadest. Capex forecasts keep getting revised up, and the cleanest expression now is the supply chain rather than the hyperscalers themselves.*

- UBS raised the 2026 AI capex forecast to $820B and 2027 to $990B, with 85% concentrated in the big four hyperscalers
- Goldman Sachs sees combined capex of $5.3 trillion across the top four hyperscalers FY25 through FY30, framing the spend as a multi-year structural floor
- Cloud growth at the major platforms accelerated to 40% YoY in Q1 2026 from 34% in Q4 2025, with aggregate backlogs at $2 trillion (UBS)
- Goldman lifted the TWSE target to 51,000 and KOSPI to 12,000 on AI hardware exposure and a memory upcycle the market is still underpricing
- MUFG flags 150% YoY rise in printed circuit assembly manufacturing prices, a direct AI-driven inflation signal in the supply chain
- Goldman TMT desk: ASML bulls are debating capacity expansion beyond 90 units to 120 to 130 units, potentially triggered by July TSMC capex upgrades

The mechanism is unchanged. Hyperscaler capex is being financed by mid-cycle cash flow rather than debt, and the supply chain in memory, MLCC, substrates, and optical components is still capacity constrained. Goldman is now naming the bottleneck explicitly. The supply side is where the next leg of the trade lives.

The opposite read Deutsche Bank lays out: this is either 1999 (bubble extends), 1987 (correction now), or 1996 (warning shot before a longer leg). A +16% S&P move in two months has only happened four times since World War II, and three of those were post-recession bouncebacks. The defense is that the $2 trillion cloud backlog is signed contracts hyperscalers will spend against, not speculation. Long memory via $MU is the cleanest single-name expression of the bottleneck thesis, and $SOXX captures the broader semicap leg without doubling the mega-cap hyperscaler bet.

### ECB hikes next week, dollar should soften, but Hormuz is the wildcard

*Banks are now near-unanimous on a June ECB hike. The unresolved question is whether oil-driven services inflation forces a second one.*

- Deutsche Bank: 99% probability of an ECB hike priced into futures for next week
- KBC: Eurozone inflation accelerated to 3.2% in May (highest since Sept 2023), with core CPI at 2.5%, above consensus
- Natixis revised ECB hike expectations up by 65bps. SEB sees 35bps of further tightening priced for end-2026
- UBS targets EURUSD at 1.20 year-end, with SEB calling 1.19 as a rebound level
- OCBC frames the move as a 25bp insurance hike, with Rabobank flagging eurozone April PPI at 0.6% MoM, decelerating from 3.4% in March

Two things are driving the European inflation re-acceleration. Hormuz-driven energy costs are feeding services and headline simultaneously, which is exactly what the ECB cannot ignore even with a loose labor market. EURUSD lift comes from rate differentials, and a hiking ECB against a Fed that probably holds widens the gap in favor of the euro.

The skeptical read SEB lays out is that a Strait reopening flushes ECB cut pricing back in by 35bps within weeks, and the whole hawkish euro bid reverses inside days. The defense is that next week's hike is locked in regardless of what oil does after the meeting, so the September meeting is the real decision point. The single question every trader has to answer for themselves: does Hormuz reopen before the September ECB meeting, or after? Long $FXE (or short $UUP for the dollar leg) only works if the answer is "after."

### Labor cooling is the NFP Friday stress test

*The strongest cross-desk convergence in the corpus is on May payrolls printing below consensus. Until then, short-dated Treasuries are the trade.*

- Natixis: May NFP forecast of 75k, versus 115k in April and 85k consensus
- JPMorgan: 75k forecast, against the same 85k street estimate
- DBS Group Research has the consensus at 85k, with JOLTS coming in at 7.6mn (highest since May 2024)
- Goldman Sachs has JOLTS at 7.77m, highest since late 2024 (openings still resilient)
- LSEG flags ADP private payrolls forecast of 117k for May, versus 109k in April
- Bank of America: vacancy-to-unemployment ratio at 1.03, highest since January 2025

The setup is a labor market where the openings side has rebounded sharply while the hires side is decelerating. Natixis expects May unemployment to print 4.4% on the unrounded number (versus 4.3% prior). That is a Fed-cut-friendly print without a recession signal.

The skeptical read JPMorgan and DBS push is that the headline-cooling story has been wrong for three months running and one weak print does not make a trend. The defense is the convergence. When Natixis, JPMorgan, DBS, and LSEG all set up similar sub-trend numbers and the JOLTS data has hard-stopped expanding, the soft-landing print is overdue. The falsifiable bet: if Friday NFP prints above 110k or unemployment holds at 4.3%, the soft-landing thesis cracks and the next two weeks belong to long-end Treasuries getting sold. Until Friday morning, long $SHY (short-dated Treasuries) is the trade.

### Consensus is short protection, and the rotate-out is the trade

*Multiple desks across Goldman, Deutsche Bank, MUFG, and SEB are flagging the same setup. The rally has gone too far, and the trade is to rotate out of the AI lead rather than short it.*

- Deutsche Bank chart of the day: S&P 500 rose +16% over April and May 2026, a historic pace seen only four times since World War II (1987, post-oil shock, GFC, Covid)
- MUFG: equity markets are near a local or major top due to extreme positioning and valuations, with a summer sell-off expected to trigger a seasonal rates rally
- 5-delta S&P 500 put/call skew is at the lowest level in over a year, dealers are long gamma, and a 2 to 4% move in either direction flips them
- SEB on a Hormuz reopening: equities would rotate out of AI-heavy S&P 500 exposure into European names that have been subdued by the Hormuz crisis
- Goldman GSPRHIMO basket had its biggest 1-day move ever (+7%), the signature of late-cycle squeeze action

The corpus picture is consistent. Positioning is extended (hedge fund tech adds, retail call chasing, and dealer long gamma all stacked), valuations are tight, and the same set of names that drove the +16% rally are now the most crowded. Skew at multi-year lows means downside hedging is essentially free.

The bull pushback is that long gamma dampens volatility right up until something breaks the regime, and nothing has cracked yet. The defense is that the ingredients are stacked. Complacency, valuation, and one catalyst (NFP Friday, Hormuz news flow, or a single MAG7 capex guide-down) is enough to flip dealers and start the unwind. Watch in this order: (1) NFP Friday, (2) any single-bank capex guide-down inside the next two weeks, (3) the next Hormuz escalation, (4) the next IPO-related sentiment tell. The rotate-out is long equal-weight $RSP versus cap-weight $SPY, with a small $SPY put added because protection has rarely been this cheap.
