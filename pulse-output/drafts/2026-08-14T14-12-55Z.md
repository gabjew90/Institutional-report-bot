# Consumer cracks, yields climb

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The spending number that was supposed to settle the rate argument went the wrong way this morning, and the bond market did not react the way the research said it would. July retail sales came in well below what desks had penciled in, which on the standard playbook should pull yields down and lift stocks. Long-dated Treasury yields went up instead. That single disconnect is the most useful thing in today's research, because it says the bond market has stopped trading the economy and started trading the government's borrowing schedule and the wave of debt now funding AI data centers.

Underneath it, the equity picture is the opposite of stressed. Stocks closed at a record on Thursday, earnings beats are running well above the historical rate, and the money that hedges a market is priced as cheaply as it has been in over a year. Positioning is light rather than crowded, which is the setup that lets a rally keep going. Two desks reading the same set of facts have landed in opposite places, one buying protection into strength and the other calling for more upside.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The consumer buckled and 30-year Treasuries sold off anyway

*The bond market has stopped trading the data and started trading the borrowing, which is why a weak spending report cannot rescue long-dated Treasuries.*

- Retail sales fell 0.58% in July and the measure that strips out cars and gas fell 0.31%, both far below the small gains desks were looking for. The spending engine that carried this expansion stalled outright.
- JPMorgan's preview had called for a 0.4% drop and argued a soft print would push yields lower and support stocks. Yields rose instead, which is that mechanism failing in real time.
- Thursday's 30-year auction cleared at 5.216%, the highest yield any US Treasury auction has fetched since 2001 (Deutsche Bank). Buyers are charging the government more no matter what the consumer does.
- More than all of the rise in the 30-year yield since the July Fed meeting is extra risk premium, the compensation investors demand for holding a bond that long, because expectations for where policy rates settle have drifted lower (Bloomberg).
- Investment-grade bond sales by the big cloud companies are set to rise four-fold between 2025 and 2027 (Goldman Sachs). The AI build has become a bond-supply story landing on the maturities already struggling.

Short-term yields can fall on a weak consumer because they track the next Fed move, while 30-year yields answer to whoever is willing to buy a very long bond in size, and that buyer now faces the government's deficit and a trillion dollars of corporate AI financing at once. Kevin Warsh's decision to give markets less guidance removed the anchor that held that compensation down.

The case against worrying is real. Thursday's soft inflation data cut September Fed hike odds to 35% from more than 50% earlier in the week (Deutsche Bank), jobless claims rose to 209k against 202k expected, and TS Lombard added risk back on that combination, lifting portfolio beta to 1.13 from 0.97 on improving earnings revisions and 3.2% wage growth. All of it is a statement about short-dated bonds, where the Fed actually operates, and none of it explains a 5.216% auction. Goldman Sachs named further increases in long-dated yields the single biggest near-term risk to markets, with 30-year real yields already above 3% at cycle highs. Their cross-asset team has one instruction for this setup and a one-to-three-month clock on it: own options to protect long equity exposure rather than trusting the rally to absorb a rate shock.

### AI chips keep beating and the argument moved to who pays for it

*Chip demand is still accelerating, and the real fight now is over the debt financing the buildout, not the orders.*

Semiconductor revenue grew 134% year over year in June, a tenth straight month of acceleration, with memory up 369% and logic up 38% (JPMorgan). The $AMAT stake flagged August 11 into Thursday's print settled in its favor: revenue of $9.12B against $9.04B expected, fourth-quarter guidance of $10.25B versus $9.66B, and a $645 Goldman target. The split is between two of the largest desks. JPMorgan expects the upcycle to run on order visibility extending into 2029 and 2030, while Goldman's derivatives desk says the easy, fast phase is over, with implied volatility on AI names down more than 10 points since July 29 as clients stopped treating the theme as one block. The doubt is loudest in credit, where Alphabet posted negative free cash flow for the first time in two decades and Meta's second-quarter free cash flow fell 91% (Bloomberg). Nvidia reports August 26, the next hard read on whether orders still outrun the financing bill.

### The rotation out of the winners has already started

*Beating expectations has stopped paying, and the money is moving into gold rather than waiting around for that to change.*

Companies are clearing the bar and getting nothing for it. The beat rate is running at 64% against a 49% historical average, yet the median stock that beats gains only 20 basis points, one-hundredth of a percent each, versus 95 basis points historically, while a miss costs 118 (Goldman Sachs). Tech beats are the worst of it, trailing the index by 192 basis points on the day of the print while non-tech beats gained 75. Retail money read that early. Weekly flows rebounded to the 64th percentile from the 4th and went into gold and inflation-protected bonds rather than tech, with the gold ETF at 2.5 standard deviations above normal demand, miners at 2.9 and inflation-linked Treasuries at 4.7 (JPMorgan). Goldman carries a $4,900 year-end gold target and JPMorgan has turned more constructive on healthcare. Does the market start paying for good results again before the crowd finishes moving into hard assets?

### Downside protection is the cheapest it has been all year

*The hedge is on sale precisely because nobody thinks they need it.*

The premium traders pay for downside protection over upside is the flattest since January 2025 on both one-month and three-month options, and a record 35% of index members now trade with more demand for calls than puts (Citadel Securities). Goldman's index volatility desk moved to recommending outright ownership of $SPY and $QQQ puts against a record high. What sits underneath is mechanical rather than fundamental: a 3% to 4% decline is the zone where trend-following funds, options dealers and leveraged ETFs all turn into sellers at once, and a break above 7,900 on the index forces dealers who sold calls to buy stock (Nomura). Those two prices decide which side runs first, and the hedge is cheap only until one of them trades.

### The Bank of Japan is the September meeting nobody is watching

*Japan raising rates again in September is close to a done deal, and the market has only priced half the story.*

Mizuho moved its forecast to a hike to 1.25% in September, then 1.50% in January 2027 and 1.75% in June 2027, on hawkish messaging from Governor Ueda and board minutes focused on inflation running hot. Market-implied odds of a September move sat near 60% on August 12 (Mizuho) and Goldman had them at 75% by August 14, while Mizuho argues the central bank wants 80% to 90% priced before it moves so the bond market does not lurch. July's intervention to buy yen cut yen-funded positions by roughly 70% in a week (Goldman Sachs), and that borrowing is the plumbing carrying Japanese rates into US assets. Does the pricing reach 80% before the meeting, or does the hike land on a market that has to absorb it in a day?

### Software found a floor and private equity put it there

*Software has quietly repaired itself, and a buyout bid is what turned the bounce into a valuation floor.*

The software index is green on the year after a 40% rally off April lows and sits within 10% of its record (Goldman Sachs), with buying concentrated in data infrastructure and security names rather than spread evenly. Workday jumped 18% on reports of a potential Silver Lake bid, which desks read as private equity setting a price under depressed software multiples. The counter is in the loan market, where distress on software loans has jumped above 25% and nearly 10% of private credit loans now pay interest in more paper instead of cash (Andromeda Capital Management). The floor is being set by buyers whose own funding is getting more expensive. If Silver Lake's interest does not convert into a signed price, that floor is a rumor rather than a bid.

- **August 26: Nvidia reports.** The stock is up 12% in two weeks, so the bar is high, and this is the cleanest test of whether AI orders are still outrunning the debt funding them.
