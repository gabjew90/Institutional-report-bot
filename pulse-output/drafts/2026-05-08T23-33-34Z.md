## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex eats the buybacks

*The hyperscalers just confirmed the build, and the cash that used to fund repurchases is now buying GPUs.*

- Goldman raised 2026 hyperscaler capex to $755B, +83% year-over-year and equal to 100% of operating cash flow, so dollars that used to buy back stock are funding GPU orders instead
- UniCredit puts combined 2026 capex for Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon at $710-725B, +75% YoY and three times 2024 levels, confirming the build is industry-wide rather than one platform's bet
- Meta guided 2026 capex to $125-145B, Alphabet to $180-190B (UniCredit), each a raise from prior ranges, the second cycle of upward revisions in a month
- Hyperscaler buybacks fell 64% YoY in 1Q 2026 (Goldman), so the reduction in equity demand from buybacks is a mechanical effect of capex absorbing the cash, not a cyclical retreat
- BofA's "AI Big 10" (MAG7 plus AVGO, MU, AMD) now equals 40% of S&P 500 market cap, a concentration level last seen at historic-bubble peaks

Hyperscaler net debt to EBITDA sits at 0.3x (Goldman), so the build can be funded from balance sheets rather than from cutting buybacks further. Same demand for AI compute means same direction for the trade, but the way it shows up in earnings shifts from EPS-via-buyback at the platform level to revenue-via-capex-recipients at the supplier level.

The bear case here: Goldman desk flags that one-third of MAG7 profits in 1Q came from private-equity investment gains rather than core AI revenue, and the May 7 momentum drawdown of 8% in GSPRHIMO points to the first capitulatory positioning event. The picks-and-shovels names push back on that read, with Applied Materials and Tokyo Electron capacity sold out into 2027, so the cycle has further to run before any guide-down lands. Long memory ($MU) over CPU-exposed names is the cleaner expression, with hyperscaler equity exposure trimmed.

### Rate-cut bet got too easy

*April jobs ran hot at 123k vs 75k expected, and the bond market priced cuts this print does not deliver.*

- Today's private NFP printed 123k vs the 65k consensus and the 75k whisper, well above forecasts that had three banks (Citi -15k, MUFG 45k, Goldman 75k) clustered low
- Unemployment held at 4.3% as expected, with U-6 ticking up to 8.2% from 8.0%, so under the surface labor is softer but the headline beat is what gets traded
- UBS sees two Fed cuts in 2H26 and calls current "no cut in 2026" pricing too hawkish; ING pushes back that rates fall slower than the rally implies because energy-driven inflation is sticky
- Crédit Agricole forecasts April CPI at 3.77% YoY, peaking at 4.2% in May before drifting to 3.5% by year-end, with energy doing most of the work via 11-12% MoM rises in motor fuels
- Goldman's "breakeven" job growth rate falls from 65k now to 50k by year-end, so even soft NFPs would not push unemployment higher and the labor market may be less helpful to the dovish case than markets assume

A hot jobs print landing on top of energy-driven inflation is the combination that keeps the Fed on hold longer, regardless of the dot-plot from December. The bond market's earlier rate-cut pricing was assembled on the assumption labor was already breaking, and today's surprise pulls that assumption forward.

The bull case for $TLT is that UBS's macro read is right, Hormuz resolves cleanly, and oil unwinds the energy push to CPI quickly enough for the Fed to act in 2H26. That requires both a clean ceasefire AND core PCE rolling over fast, neither of which the SEB analysis (refined-fuel inventories at -11% vs five-year average, mine-sweeping takes weeks) supports yet. Short $TLT into next Tuesday's CPI is the trade.

### Hormuz peace memo, supply scar lingers

*Brent fell from $108 to $99 on the MoU headline, but ships still aren't moving and the inventory hole takes months to refill.*

- Brent dropped from $108 to $99 in a session on US-Iran MoU reports (SEB), a paper-market move ahead of any physical change
- Hormuz oil exports remain at 4% of normal levels, Iranian loadings under 30% of 2025 averages (Goldman) — the supply hole is real even with a deal pending
- Distillate stocks at -11% versus five-year average (SEB), so refined-fuel tightness keeps gasoline prices supported regardless of headline crude
- API crude draw of -8.1mb (SEB), the biggest in three months versus -2.8mb consensus, says physical demand is still pulling barrels even with a soft China nowcast
- China oil demand nowcast down 1.5mb/d since mid-March, an 8% decline (Goldman) — partial offset to the supply scarcity, only partial

Even with a signed deal, mine-sweeping the strait and rotating stranded crews takes weeks before tankers move. The MoU traded the futures curve, not the physical market. Until inventories rebuild, the supply premium stays embedded in product prices and feeds into core CPI on the standard 3-month lag.

The opposite read: Iran's $270B reparations demand and the unresolved uranium-stockpile destination are real diplomatic hurdles, and a clean deal could collapse the entire premium fast, especially if China demand stays soft. That risk is exactly why $BNO (Brent exposure) captures the trade better than $USO (WTI) — the Brent contract carries the physical supply scar, while WTI prices Gulf of Mexico inventory that is largely unaffected. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline.

### First crack in the AI bull

*Momentum just had its biggest one-day drawdown since 2020, and prime book exposure is at all-time highs into the weakness.*

- GSPRHIMO (Goldman's high-beta momentum book) fell 8% in a single session on May 7, a top-10 one-day move in five years and the fifth such instance in 2026
- Prime book momentum exposure sits at the 99th percentile on a 1-year basis and 100th on a 5-year basis (Goldman) — consensus crowded in
- The Market Ear notes AI and momentum have a 1-month correlation of 94%, so they are functionally one trade rather than two diversified themes
- SMH +153% on a 1-year basis is four standard deviations above long-term mean (TME), the strongest reading since 2001 — the drawdown was a positioning event waiting for a catalyst
- Cloudflare ($NET) -19% after Thursday's guide miss, Datadog ($DDOG) +30% on a short squeeze (Goldman) — single-name vol is the symptom of late-cycle positioning unwinds

When momentum positioning hits the 100th percentile and AI is the entire trade, any catalyst — a guide-down, a hot CPI print, a Fed surprise — forces a coordinated unwind. The May 7 drop took both legs, long and short, capitulating at the same time, which is closer to a positioning event than a fundamentals turn.

The pushback: BofA's Bull/Bear indicator at 7.2 (up from 6.6) is rising, not retreating, and weekly cash inflows of $136B were the largest since January. The dip is being bought, not sold. The defense: 7.2 is approaching the 8.0 sell signal, and concentration in the AI Big 10 at 40% of S&P market cap matches historic-bubble peaks. The setup looks like a bid running out of marginal buyers. Long $SPY puts against an AI Big 10 long, sized for a 3-5% drawdown into next Tuesday's CPI.
