## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hyperscaler capex is no longer a forecast — it's the print

*The AI capex story moved from forward guide to accomplished fact this earnings cycle, and the size of the spend is forcing a structural shift in how the S&P 500 uses cash.*

- Goldman Sachs forecasts hyperscaler 2026 capex at $755B, +83% year-over-year, with capex now the single largest use of cash for the S&P 500
- UniCredit puts combined 2026 capex for Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon at $710-725B, a 75% increase over 2025; Meta raised 2026 capex guidance to $125-145B; Microsoft expects June quarter capex above $40B
- Hyperscaler buybacks fell 64% year-over-year in 1Q 2026 with minimal growth expected through 2027 (Goldman) — the buyback-to-capex rotation is now mechanical, not optional
- S&P 500 1Q earnings on track for 17% growth (UBS), the fastest in four years, with AI named as the primary driver across tech platforms, utilities, and infrastructure
- Datadog +31% afterhours and Fortinet +20% (Thursday's prints) confirmed the second-wave software side of the build-out, while Microchip beat-and-raised on industrial and datacenter demand
- Goldman's GS desk: 40% of S&P 500 earnings growth is now tied to the AI infrastructure trade

These aren't separate stories. The hyperscalers are spending three quarters of a trillion dollars, that money lands in the supply chain, and the supply chain prints earnings beats that confirm the spend is real. UniCredit's read — that demand for AI capacity exceeds supply and Alphabet's CEO is on record calling himself "compute constrained" — is what makes the $755B not a peak guess but a floor. The mechanical implication is the rest of the S&P 500's cash composition gets rebuilt: buybacks shrink, IG bond supply grows (April hit $205B, nearly double street estimates per Syz, mostly hyperscaler datacenter financing), and the picks-and-shovels names — semis, networking, power, cooling — get paid for years.

The bear case Syz puts on the table is concrete and worth steelmanning: Wall Street projects hyperscaler free cash flow falls over 70% from peak by end-2026 even as earnings rise, because capex eats the cash. If credit markets tighten or a single MAG7 quarter delivers a capex guide-down, the entire picks-and-shovels thesis gets a fast repricing. Goldman's desk also flagged that one-third of MAG7 profits came from PE investment gains rather than AI revenue, so the headline earnings number is more cyclically vulnerable than it looks. Even granting all of that, the binding constraint right now is supply, not demand. Applied Materials and Tokyo Electron are sold out into 2027 (research consensus), and Infineon just upgraded FY26 guidance with Goldman lifting their price target 42% on AI power semiconductor demand. Long $SOXX.

### Inside the AI trade, leadership is rotating from Nvidia to CPUs and memory

*The same dollar that drove $NVDA for two years is now bidding Intel, AMD, and Micron — and that is what a maturing capex cycle looks like.*

- News tape today: "Wall Street sees changing of the guard in AI as Intel, AMD shares soar while Nvidia lags" — Intel, AMD and Micron surged double digits this week
- Apple-Intel chip deal headlines circulating today; CNBC framed it as a potentially major foundry shift for the iPhone maker
- Goldman TMT desk: Microchip beat-and-raised with strength in industrial, mil/aerospace, and datacenter demand; the desk thinks the Street is under-modeling the fundamental recovery
- JPMorgan: global semiconductor long/short ratio at ~4x, well above the prior peak of 2.5x — positioning is stretched but the trend is intact
- Goldman: AI themes are consolidating after a 50% rally since early April with "capitulatory stop-ins" from long-only buyers — code for forced chasing into the upside

The mechanism is straightforward. When hyperscalers sign $1.8B seven-year compute commitments (Goldman: Akamai/CIS) and Meta guides $125-145B of 2026 capex, the order books get spread across the stack, not just GPUs. Memory content per AI server is rising fast, CPU-host-paired-with-accelerator is the architecture cloud providers are buying, and a credible Apple-foundry pivot would land a multi-year revenue stream onto $INTC that the Street is not modeling. The "Nvidia lagging" framing in the news tape isn't a top — it's the leadership baton moving down the stack as the cycle matures.

The bear case is the cleanest one in the corpus: positioning is at extremes. Goldman Prime Book momentum exposure sits at the 100th percentile over five years, and yesterday's -8% high-beta-momentum unwind was a top-ten move in five years. If a single MAG7 quarter delivers a capex-guide-down, the rotation that just helped Intel and AMD becomes the rotation that crushes them when the whole complex de-risks together. The defense: the spend is contractually locked in (multi-year cloud deals, sold-out fab capacity at AMAT/Tokyo Electron), so a single-quarter wobble doesn't break the thesis. Long $MU and $SOXX, with $NVDA scaled back from full-size — the next leg pays the picks-and-shovels names more than the GPU leader.

### Momentum positioning is at five-year extremes and the unwind has started

*High-beta momentum just had one of its ten worst single-day pullbacks in five years, while Prime Book exposure sits at the 100th percentile — this is the late-cycle setup, not the early one.*

- Goldman GSPRHIMO (high-beta momentum factor) -8% in a single session on May 7, top-ten one-day move in five years
- Goldman Prime Book momentum exposure: 99th percentile one-year, 100th percentile five-year
- AI themes up 35-55% since early April; Goldman desk says client high-touch activity is "exploding" — historically coincides with local tops or bottoms
- JPMorgan: hedge fund positioning rebounded sharply in April, CTA positioning in EU equities hit +2z month-over-month; Japan became the most bought region
- Citi: US equity tactical positioning is "extended long" and vulnerable to profit-taking despite futures holding gains
- Syz: Magnificent 7 hedge fund offloading is at unprecedented pace, net exposure cut to ~17% of North American net while retail aggressively buys in

Two flows are pulling against each other. Hedge funds are quietly trimming MAG7 (Syz's net-exposure data) while CTA models — which are price-following — are still long and adding into strength. The retail bid is filling the gap. That's a setup where any catalyst that breaks the momentum factor mechanically forces CTA selling on top of hedge fund selling, and retail can't absorb both. Goldman's desk read of "capitulatory stop-ins" is exactly the late-cycle pattern: long-only buyers chasing in just as systematic positioning peaks.

The bull case here, defensibly: aggressive momentum unwinds historically have been buy-the-dip opportunities for AI specifically, because the underlying capex story is structural and not flow-driven. That works when the unwind is positioning-only and the macro stays risk-on. The risk is exactly the inverse — a CPI surprise next Tuesday or a credible MAG7 capex guide-down would convert a positioning unwind into a thesis unwind, and 99th-percentile positioning has nowhere to go but down. The cleanest risk-management read is buy short-dated $SPY puts as a hedge against the rest of your AI longs.

### The bond market is pricing rates wrong, and UBS thinks the cuts come faster

*Markets are leaning toward "no rate cut in 2026" while UBS sees two cuts in 2H26 and ING flags oil-driven inflation as the reason cuts come slower.*

- UBS expects two Fed cuts in 2H26; calls current "no rate cut" pricing "too hawkish"
- ING: rates could fall slower than growing optimism suggests; oil-driven inflation could keep central banks vigilant for longer
- Friday NFP: private payrolls 123k vs 75k consensus (beat); unemployment 4.3% in line; the labor market is not breaking, which lets the Fed wait
- Goldman: breakeven job growth (the pace needed to keep unemployment stable) is falling from 65k toward 50k by year-end 2026 due to slowing immigration; that means even today's 123k overshoots the new breakeven
- UBS: views the recent rise in government bond yields as an opportunity to lock in elevated yields, arguing market fears of central bank tightening from energy prices are overdone
- $TLT +0.50% on the day with $UUP -0.26% — bond market read of NFP was net dovish despite the headline beat

This is a real cross-bank tension and the trade is in the disagreement. UBS's view rests on the argument that core inflation is decelerating even as headline inflation accelerates, which gives the Fed cover to cut by year-end. ING's pushback is the oil leg — if Brent stays elevated due to Hormuz friction, headline doesn't fall fast enough and the Fed has to wait. Today's NFP did not resolve it; it landed firm enough that the labor market isn't forcing a cut, but the unemployment rate held at 4.3% and Goldman's breakeven analysis says today's "beat" actually overshoots the new lower breakeven by a wide margin. Pair that with $TLT closing higher even on the headline beat, and the bond market is not buying the "no cut" pricing.

The bear case for $TLT is the ING read: oil stays high, headline CPI doesn't print 2%, and the Fed has to keep rates restrictive into 2027 to avoid a credibility break. The defense is the disinflation already in pipeline — Goldman's labor breakeven is falling, the consumer is wobbling (restaurant earnings from SHAK, BROS, CAVA all weak), and credit card balances are rising at the fastest pace since 2022 per Goldman. None of those signal a Fed that needs to stay tight. Long $TLT into next Tuesday's CPI print.

### Hormuz is still closed — the supply scar lasts longer than the headlines

*The market is pricing a US-Iran MoU that hasn't actually moved a tanker through the Strait of Hormuz, and the physical inventory math says the supply hole takes months to fill regardless.*

- SEB: Brent at $99/bl after MoU reports, but Hormuz physical transit will take weeks to months to resume even after a deal — mine-sweeping, stranded seafarers, demands for $270B
- Goldman Oil Tracker: Hormuz exports are at 4% of normal levels; Iranian loadings under 30% of 2025 averages
- Goldman: 45% of Persian Gulf crude/condensate hit is being offset by higher US, Americas, Russia exports — but this likely reflects inventory draws not new production
- No net offset for Persian Gulf refined products exports; Americas exports up 1.4mb/d, global decline is 1.8mb/d — net product shortfall is structural
- News today: US forces struck two empty Iranian oil tankers per Central Command; Iran response to US peace proposal expected Friday; markets remain in holding pattern
- Goldman: dated Brent has compressed to the paper market — physical premium has collapsed but the supply gap is still real

The market is trading the headline (MoU, ceasefire optimism) and ignoring the inventory math. OECD product stocks are well below 5-year averages going into Q3, refined-fuel inventories are tight, and 4% of normal Hormuz throughput is what the supply side actually looks like. Even if Iran signs Friday, the SEB read is that physical transit takes weeks to months — minesweeping, repositioning crews, settling on destination terms. China is reportedly reselling crude cargoes (Goldman) which suggests their domestic demand is weak enough to monetize the supply, not that the global product market has loosened.

The opposing read is the one the market is currently pricing: a deal lands Friday, sentiment flips, paper longs unwind, and Brent breaks below $95 fast. SEB's note explicitly cautions that previous rallies on diplomatic leaks have consistently faded — so chasing the long-vol or long-oil trade on every headline is a losing pattern. The defense is the inventory math. Refined product tightness is months from resolution regardless of diplomatic outcomes, so the structural support for Brent above $95 is independent of the deal flow. Long $BNO (Brent ETF) over $USO (WTI) — the spread captures the physical-vs-paper dislocation that SEB is flagging.
