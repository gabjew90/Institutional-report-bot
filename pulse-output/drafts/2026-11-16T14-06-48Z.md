# Nvidia loses its founder

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP] Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive and left the board, CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, and the company reports earnings Tuesday after the close with a wave of price-target cuts and four downgrades already published. The stock was already down about 30% from early October after the midterm elections put data center construction limits on the ballot in several states. Retail investors have been buying Nvidia at a record pace while hedge funds sit at their lowest net exposure in five years.

The second pressure is rates. October core CPI rose 0.3% for a third straight month, markets now fully price a Federal Reserve rate increase in December, and the 10-year Treasury yield is back above 5%. The dollar is at its highest since spring 2025 and gold hit a 52-week low last week.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Nvidia without Huang is a valuation problem before it is an earnings problem

*The business is sold out for two years, so the damage from losing the founder shows up in what investors will pay for each dollar of profit, not in the profit itself.*

- Goldman kept its Buy but cut its target to $220 from $300 and now values the stock at 22 times earnings instead of 30 (the price-to-earnings ratio, or P/E), which means the whole cut comes from a lower multiple and none from lower profit forecasts.
- JPMorgan cut its target to $215 from $320 and removed China-related shipments, about 8% of first-half revenue, from its fiscal 2028 numbers, a roughly 10% hit to that year's earnings estimate that reflects export-control risk and not the CEO change.
- Bernstein downgraded to Market-Perform and halved its target to $200, and Oppenheimer, KeyBanc and William Blair also downgraded, so four desks have stopped recommending the stock in a week.
- Piper Sandler and Cantor Fitzgerald both put a floor near $150, about 9 to 10 times next year's earnings, which Piper calls a 20-year low for the multiple.
- Retail bought $111 million of the stock in the first 30 minutes after the resignation, 40 times a normal open, and Nvidia was 61% of all retail net buying that week (Vanda Research).

Huang personally negotiated the supply contracts and the financing deals that fund Nvidia's own customers. UBS estimates a pause in that financing could remove more than $50 billion from the AI industry, and Goldman sees higher execution risk for the next 12 to 18 months even with an order backlog that runs 12 to 24 months out.

The desks that still say buy point to that backlog. Piper Sandler has the company sold out for 2027 and most of 2028, Wedbush sees the stock at about 10 times fiscal 2028 earnings with the risk already in the price, and Wolfe expects zero business impact through 2028. Seaport, with a Sell and a $180 target, answers that customers will use the distraction to buy more chips from $AMD and Broadcom, and Citi recorded that rotation the day Huang resigned, with $AMD and $AVGO each up 6% while $NVDA fell 4.5%. Both sides can be right for a while, because locked-in orders protect 2027 revenue and do nothing for the multiple until a permanent CEO is named. Tuesday's report after the close is the first test under Kress: Stifel expects a modest beat against company guidance of $108.0 billion in revenue and wants to hear the roughly 70% revenue target for next fiscal year repeated, and Goldman holds its Buy to $220 into the print.

### AI infrastructure capex: data center demand growth holds and the bond market charges more for it

Goldman's economists expect AI-related spending to run at a $1.4 trillion annual rate by the end of 2027, about 4% of US GDP, up from $900 billion at the end of this year, and they forecast 8% growth in business investment next year against a 4.2% consensus. Bank of America's derivatives team looks at how that spending is paid for and reaches a colder conclusion. Big tech companies issued $182 billion of bonds in 2026 against $17 billion in 2024, the extra yield investors demand on that debt is 140 basis points (1.4 percentage points), the widest in five years, and lenders now charge semiconductor makers more than the cloud giants for the first time. Stock options on the same companies have barely repriced. CoreWeave shows the strain at the smaller end, with a $1.1 billion quarterly interest bill and $7.5 billion of cash burned in the third quarter. Goldman's equity outlook has the spending boom peaking in 2027, and Morgan Stanley would cut exposure to upstream hardware names and add software companies that use AI. Bank of America's hedge for the gap is a December 2027 put spread on a basket that includes $AMZN, $META and $GOOGL, which pays if stocks catch down to what bonds already price.

### The midterms pushed construction later and made existing power scarcer

Democrats swept the midterms, and Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has pledged a moratorium on new data center construction. Morgan Stanley expects about 20% of US data center projects to slip from 2028 to 2029 and kept its forecasts for big tech capital spending at $1.6 trillion for 2027 and $1.8 trillion for 2028, so the money moves later and is not cancelled. Goldman calls the risk to 2027 construction modest because most projects in Texas, Georgia and Michigan already hold permits. Citadel Securities draws the trading conclusion: companies that already run data centers with secured power gain pricing power, and hardware suppliers face deferred orders. Morgan Stanley raised its estimate of the US power shortfall through 2028 by 15% to 44 gigawatts. Watch in this order: (1) power that is already connected to the grid, where Morgan Stanley is Overweight $VST and $CEG, (2) GPU rental prices, already up 50% in a year for Nvidia's H100 chip, and (3) whether other states copy the Texas moratorium.

### A December rate increase is priced and the argument is over the size

October core CPI, which leaves out food and energy, rose 0.3% on the month for the third time running, and headline CPI rose 0.4% against the 0.3% desks expected. Crédit Agricole and SEB both expect a quarter-point increase in December, and SEB puts the market-implied chance of a half-point move at 15% after Fed Governor Waller raised the idea. Goldman added a third increase in March 2027, which would take the policy rate to a peak of 4.25% to 4.50%, and pushed its first cut back to December 2027. Citi's consumer team takes the other side: record credit card balances of $1.29 trillion and weaker confidence among stock-owning households may stop the Fed short of the three quarter-point increases markets price. The bond market is leaning one way. Trend-following funds hold the largest short position in Treasuries their models allow (Citi), and last week's $25 billion sale of 30-year bonds drew weaker demand than usual. Does Chair Warsh, who speaks at noon today, endorse a half-point move or talk it down?

### Retail is buying the dip while the Wall Street rotation runs the other way

Morgan Stanley advises against buying the AI selloff across the board. The companies that sell AI hardware saw their median forward P/E fall to 18.6 from 27.4, and the companies that use AI have beaten them by 26 percentage points since late September, which is where the bank wants new money to go. JPMorgan sees room for another 10% drop in chip stocks and prefers the cloud giants, and Goldman's 2027 outlook adds selected defensive sectors, healthcare first. BTIG expects the S&P 500 to turn lower from here and would change its mind only above roughly 7,700. The other side of that trade is mechanical. Trend-following funds flip from selling to buying the S&P 500 above 7,676 (Citi), bets against the Nasdaq 100 fund $QQQ are the largest since 2023 (JPMorgan), and Goldman still estimates those funds sell about $31 billion of S&P futures this week whichever way prices go. Cost of rotating early: missing a short squeeze that starts less than 1% above current levels. Cost of chasing: owning chip stocks into the 10% drop JPMorgan describes.
