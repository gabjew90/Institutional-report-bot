# Oil premium drains out

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The dominant story across the desks is the unwind of the Middle East war-risk premium after the US-Iran peace deal, which has reopened Gulf shipping and pulled oil sharply lower. Underneath that, the AI infrastructure trade stays the earnings engine even as the biggest hyperscaler names cooled off, gold is being pitched as the rebound buy after its first slip below a key level since November, and softer inflation data is letting some desks read the Fed as less hawkish than feared. Positioning color leans cautious: funding spreads are stretched to the most extreme reading in five years and the largest AI names sold off through June even as their share of profit growth kept climbing.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The oil scare is unwinding faster than the supply hole closes

*The war premium has drained out of crude, but the physical Gulf is still half-clogged and prices sit below where the two biggest desks see them at year-end.*

- Brent has fallen roughly $13 a barrel over the past ten days to about $73.6, a weekly drop of more than 10% as the Middle East war-risk premium came out (UBS, Goldman) — the panic bid is gone
- Persian Gulf exports are already back to about 66% of normal levels after the Iran deal (Goldman) — flows are reopening, which is what dragged the price down
- 40 million barrels of Iranian oil have shipped since the deal and 30 million barrels of non-Iranian Gulf floating storage have been released, but an estimated 50 to 100 million barrels are still trapped in the Gulf (UBS) — the backlog clears over weeks, not in one headline
- Goldman sees Q4 Brent at $80 and UBS targets $85 by year-end — both sit ABOVE the current $73.6, so the research says the selloff has overshot
- US energy price inflation is running at 112% on a three-month annualized basis from the earlier conflict (Goldman) — that pipeline inflation is already baked in and doesn't reverse just because spot fell

The mechanism is a gap between paper and physical. Futures repriced the resolution instantly the moment the deal hit the wires, but the physical market still has tens of millions of barrels stuck behind a Strait that only just reopened, so the supply hole takes weeks to refill regardless of the peace headline. That is why both Goldman and UBS keep year-end targets well above spot even while they acknowledge the premium is gone. The bear case is real: HSBC pegs the post-deal oil decline at around 25%, and if the 50 to 100 million trapped barrels flush out fast into a market that no longer fears disruption, $73 looks generous, not cheap. The pushback that holds is the physical timeline. Refilling channels and rebuilding the export cadence is a multi-week process, and the Cboe desk shows traders still paying up for upside, with one-month deep out-of-the-money oil calls richening 8 points week-over-week. The cleanest expression of the overshoot is long $USO or $BNO into the gap between $73.6 spot and the $80 to $85 the research desks carry, with $XLE the equity proxy if you want the carry of the majors rather than the futures roll.

### AI is still the earnings engine even as the leaders cooled

*The AI build is paying the market's profit growth, so the June pullback in the biggest names is a rotation inside the trade, not the end of it.*

- AI infrastructure is on track to drive about 60% of S&P 500 second-quarter EPS growth, against an overall growth bar of 22% year-over-year, the highest pre-season bar since 2021 (Goldman) — this isn't a side story, it is most of the growth
- The Big 5 hyperscaler shares fell about 20% on average in June even as that profit contribution rose (UBS) — the leadership cooled while the fundamentals didn't
- UBS cut its combined chip and hardware weight from 76% to 61% and lifted defensive AI ecosystem exposure from under 1% to 20% — the smart-money move is down the stack, not out of it
- Per-unit memory cost for an iPhone is projected to hit $200 by year-end, up from $50 a year ago (UBS), and the US memory basket is up about 26% month-to-date (Goldman) — memory is where the content growth is concentrated

The counter is capex fatigue: a 20% drop in the biggest names says the market is starting to ask harder questions about return on the AI spend. The defense is that the spending is broadening, not stalling, with Korea committing KRW 881 trillion to chip hubs plus KRW 550 trillion to AI data centers and Japan unveiling a ¥101.6 trillion plan. Long memory through $MU or $SMH, leaning toward the picks-and-shovels and away from the most expensive leadership names.

### The rotation trade as the AI leaders roll over

*The corpus's skeptical voices are flagging the cap-weighted index topping out on the same stretched names, and the trade is to rotate out into everything the AI bid left behind rather than chase the leaders.*

- Goldman's funding-spread indicator is at its 99th percentile over a five-year lookback, the most stretched positioning reading in years — the crowd is leaning the same way
- The Big 5 hyperscalers fell about 20% in June and the largest names trade at their lowest forward earnings multiple since ChatGPT launched — the leadership is rolling over while the index still leans on it
- Three desks are flagging rotate-out angles explicitly, from a software catch-up call to defensive consumer and staples exposure (Goldman, Rabobank) — the dissent is multi-desk, not a lone bear

So the question a trader has to answer is simple: does the top-heavy index keep working, or does the next leg come from everything the AI trade left behind? If the answer is breadth, the expression is long $RSP, the equal-weight S&P, paired with long $XLU utilities as the defensive complement, so you rotate out of the concentration without being short the theme.

### Gold is the rebound trade after the first slip in months

*The pullback below a long-held level is a buying level, not a top, because the central-bank buying behind it never stopped.*

- Gold fell below $4,000 for the first time since November and now sits about 26% below its January high (UBS) — the kind of flush that resets positioning
- UBS carries a 12-month target of $5,200 and Goldman sees $4,900 by end-2026 on emerging-market central-bank diversification — both desks see new highs from here
- Poland bought about 18 metric tons and China about 10 metric tons in May (UBS) — the official-sector bid is still showing up in the data

The cost of being wrong is a few percent more downside if the dollar firms on the oil-driven disinflation. The cost of missing it is the 20%-plus move back to the desks' targets if the central-bank bid reasserts. Long $GLD on the dips below $4,000.

### Softer inflation lets the Fed look less hawkish

*The May inflation print took the worst-case Fed off the table, even as the new leadership talks tough on the 2% goal.*

- Headline PCE came in at 0.4% month-over-month, below the 0.5% consensus (UBS), which UBS reads as support for a less hawkish Fed pivot
- The market now prices only about 34 basis points of hikes by December (UBS), and the funds rate has sat in a 3.5% to 4.5% range for over 18 months (Goldman)
- Goldman trimmed its year-end headline inflation forecast by about 0.2 point after the Hormuz deal eased the energy pressure
- The offset is the tough talk: new leadership reaffirmed the 2% target as non-negotiable, framing price stability as the Committee's hard commitment (One River)

If core PCE keeps printing at or below consensus into the next release, the less-hawkish read holds and the long end gets relief. If energy-driven inflation reasserts because the oil overshoot snaps back, that thesis is dead and the trade is over. Until then the bias is long $TLT.

### Comcast's NBCUniversal split is the catalyst on the radar

*The corporate separation is a value-unlock event three desks are watching, even before anyone has staked a directional call.*

- Barclays, Goldman, and JPMorgan all flag the planned Comcast and NBCUniversal separation as an event worth tracking — multi-desk attention without a consensus view yet
- The setup is the standard sum-of-the-parts unlock, where splitting a slower cable business from media and streaming assets can close a conglomerate discount

No desk has a directional bet yet, so this is a watch-and-size situation rather than a high-conviction trade. The modest lean for those who want exposure is long $CMCSA into clarity on the separation terms.
