# Jobs print rules tape

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

Research desks came into Friday positioned for a jobs-day verdict. JPMorgan's market intelligence desk was tactically long equities into the print, betting that a soft number eases yields and extends the rebound, while flow data showed retail money rotating out of software and hyperscalers into memory and semiconductor names. Precious metals carried the strongest momentum on the tape, with systematic funds covering shorts in silver and gold drawing steady Chinese buying. The unresolved question in the research: the rebound's leadership is narrow, and credit markets are quietly pricing more stress in the biggest AI borrowers even as the index recovers.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### A soft jobs print extends this rebound

*A soft jobs print extends this rebound, and the only thing that breaks it from here is the leadership gap.*

- JPMorgan's economics team forecast 75k July payrolls against the Street's 85k, with unemployment ticking up to 4.3% from 4.2%. A miss was the friendly outcome in their scenario work, easing yields and supporting stocks.
- The 2-year Treasury yield had already slipped to 4.24% from a 4.35% high into the print (JPMorgan). Falling short-term yields are the market repricing Fed cuts closer, which is the fuel this rally has been running on.
- Second-quarter earnings delivery is the cushion under the trade: blended earnings growth above 47% year over year with profit margins at 16.7%, up from 12.9% a year ago (JPMorgan). The rally is not running on hope alone.
- Friday's expiring options priced only a 0.7% move in the S&P 500 (JPMorgan), so a surprise in either direction had room to overshoot what the market prepared for.

The mechanism is a "good news is bad news" tape: a weak print pulls rate-cut bets forward, yields fall, and the biggest growth names benefit first because their valuations are the most sensitive to rates.

The counter is the tape itself. The Nasdaq rebounded, but high-beta tech and the former AI leaders did not confirm the move, the Nasdaq 100 has been stuck between 28,800 and 30,000, and the cost of insuring hyperscaler debt against default kept rising through the recovery. A rally the leaders refuse to join is a rally the bulls still have to prove. Against that, JPMorgan's desk argues the momentum unwind has already run its course, and it is rotating rather than retreating, trimming financials for industrials while staying long the Mag7 and Korean semiconductor names.

Watch in this order: (1) yields, where continued easing after the print keeps the tactical long Mag7 trade via $NVDA working, (2) breadth, where the former AI leaders joining the move or the Nasdaq 100 clearing the top of its 28,800 to 30,000 range would confirm the rebound, and (3) hyperscaler credit, where insurance costs on AI-linked debt need to stop widening before the all-clear.

### Metals momentum is feeding on itself

*The gold and silver breakouts are now self-reinforcing, with systematic buyers forced in and strategic buyers refusing to sell.*

Goldman's trend-following model flipped positive on silver after the August 5 close, kicking off systematic short covering, and options flow turned to call buying with 3-month implied volatility rising as demand for downside protection faded. Gold has the structural bid: Chinese gold ETFs logged 14 consecutive days of inflows, the longest streak since March, and Goldman calls continued central-bank diversification the main driver of its constructive outlook. Positioning came into August washed out, and the August-to-September window is seasonally one of gold's strongest. The tension is a single level. Silver has to clear and hold $64, and a failure there or a break back below its 50-day average puts the squeeze on hold. The lean is long $GLD, with $SLV the higher-beta expression while $64 is in play.

### Retail is rotating, not leaving

*Retail flows are rotating out of software and into memory and semiconductors, which reads as renewed AI appetite rather than exit.*

Retail investors dumped software at the fastest weekly pace on record, led by heavy selling in $MSFT and $PLTR after earnings, while buying memory and semiconductor names like $MU and $AMD (JPMorgan flow data). Retail call volumes pulled back from all-time highs even as institutional call buying in the S&P 500 stayed hot, and SpaceX saw its first retail outflows since June 23. The risk to the bullish read: this is profit-taking dressed up as rotation, and it lands exactly where leadership is already failing to confirm the rebound. Until that resolves, the flow says own what retail is accumulating. The lean is long $MU and $AMD over software.
