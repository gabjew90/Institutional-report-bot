# The Unwind Turns Macro

## 1. RECAP
[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

Friday ended the worst week for US stocks since April 2025 with selling in every sector, and the weekend research reads it as a change in kind. The losses started in AI chip and data-center names and spread into the stocks most tied to economic growth, while Treasury bonds, the yen and the Swiss franc rose as money moved toward safety. Late-Friday headlines added to the pressure: reports that the SEC opened a probe into Nvidia's accounting, hawkish comments from officials in Washington, and a Financial Times report that Israel is urging President Trump to restart strikes on Iran (Goldman). Hedge funds bought utilities, telecom, pharma and real estate during the week, a move toward defensive sectors (JPMorgan).

- Nvidia fell 14% on Friday and led the AI group lower, three days after it beat with earnings of $2.68 a share against the $2.48 expected. Goldman's basket of AI chip stocks lost 21% on the week and its data-center basket lost 18%.
- Treasury yields fell across maturities on Friday. The 2-year yield dropped 12 basis points (one basis point is one-hundredth of a percent), the 10-year 10 and the 30-year 7.5 (Goldman).
- October retail sales rose 0.2% on the month, and the control group that feeds GDP rose 0.6% (Natixis, Raymond James). Pending home sales fell 4.7% in October to a record low for the second month in a row (Raymond James).
- Gold, silver, copper, zinc and cobalt hit multi-month lows as the dollar strengthened (Raymond James). The dollar index is at its highest since March 2025.
- Goldman kept its Buy rating on $AMZN with a $370 price target, calling it the most compelling risk and reward among the largest companies it covers.
- The APEC summit in Shenzhen ended with a joint declaration from Trump, Xi and Putin and no major diplomatic breakthrough (Danske Bank, UBS).
- Iranian attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz continued, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard said there will be no nuclear talks until Hormuz is settled (Citi).

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI selloff became a growth selloff

The selling that started in AI stopped being only about AI this week. The S&P 500 fell 8.6% and closed below 7,000, its worst week since April 2025, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 10.4%, its worst week since March 2020. Friday was the S&P's seventh straight down session, a streak last seen in February 2020 and one of only five in 25 years. What changed is where the damage landed. The drawdown that began in October sat inside the AI complex, and over the past week it widened into a broader growth shock. The S&P 500 excluding AI is now 6% below its high, and cyclical stocks, which had tracked defensives for most of the past month, lagged them by 5 percentage points over the past ten days.

Goldman's read of the mechanism is money getting more expensive everywhere at once. A 100 basis point (one-hundredth of a percent) tightening in financial conditions at this speed takes roughly 50bp off growth over the following 12 months, and comparable 100-150bp tightenings produced 10-20% S&P 500 drawdowns in 2018, 2023 and 2025, with the current move at the low end of that band.

The two largest houses split on what that implies for the index. Goldman's downside case is roughly 5,800, the 30-year average multiple of 17 times applied to trend earnings of $340, and the reason it is a real case rather than an arithmetic exercise is that the AI investment boom has lifted S&P 500 earnings about 25% above their 30-year trend. JPMorgan reads the same selloff as an AI de-leveraging event alongside a more hawkish Fed being priced in, not a change in the underlying macro story, stays constructive on risk assets into 2027, and expects the market to rebound as catalysts clear while holding quality growth and the big cloud companies building AI capacity.

One level settles it. JPMorgan's base case holds the S&P above 6800-7000, and a sustained break would open 6150-6315, about a 20% correction from the peak. Goldman is waiting on one of three bottom signals, renewed confidence in AI earnings, a dovish Fed signal, or a large drop in investor positioning, and the third has not happened: nearly $170 billion has flowed into US equity funds and ETFs since the start of the fourth quarter, and asset manager futures length sits in the 82nd percentile of the past decade. Mechanical selling is still scheduled, with Goldman's trend-following models forecasting selling in every scenario next week and triggers at 7596, 7540 and 7119 after those funds sold $57 billion in a month to a $19 billion net short. Goldman's Wilson has already switched his hedge, preferring long bonds paired with long equities over this year's short-bond version.

### Three hikes where cuts used to be

JPMorgan now forecasts quarter-point hikes at the December, January and March meetings, which would leave the target range at 4.50%-4.75% and more than fully reverse the 75bp of insurance cuts delivered between September and December 2025. The October minutes guided to at least a 25bp December hike, with some members open to a larger one. Santander's Stephen Stanley added a March 2027 hike, making three quarter-point increases in roughly six months, and still expects a January skip rather than back-to-back moves. The size argument is live. Waller is advocating 50bp in December and wants last year's three insurance cuts reversed, while Natixis does not expect 50bp but does expect a hawkish meeting that keeps January well priced. The market ended the week with 22bps priced into December and a 60bp peak by the middle of next year. Goldman's rates team thinks that path is too hawkish if current inflation pricing is borne out.

### Funds have sold. Nobody agrees that it is enough.

Goldman's prime brokerage book shows beta-adjusted fundamental long/short net leverage down 8.8 points to 48.2%, a five-year low, with the aggregate long/short ratio across the whole book at new 15-year lows. US single stocks were shorted every day of the week, the heaviest weekly shorting since June 2022 and third heaviest in a decade. Deutsche Bank's aggregate positioning measure sits 0.9 standard deviations below average, the 11th percentile and the lowest since May 2025, with systematic strategies at their biggest underweight in 18 months, which it reads as close to levels positioning has typically bounced from. JPMorgan is not there: funds have cut net risk by a magnitude historically tied to a bounce, but its US positioning metric's four-week change is only about -1 standard deviation against a -1.5 capitulation trigger, and the rebound that usually follows has not arrived. Goldman's strategists are closer to JPMorgan, calling broad investor positioning still elevated.

### The volatility shock and the relief signal

Deutsche Bank classes this as a volatility shock, a jump of more than 1.5 standard deviations, with implied volatility, realized volatility, intraday ranges, the inverted VIX futures curve and stock correlations all spiking into the top decile. In past shocks volatility took 6 to 8 weeks to subside, and outside recessions the S&P 500 fell a further 14% to 16%. Goldman's equity volatility desk reads the same extremity the other way, with its panic index at 9.8 out of 10 and readings above 9.5 having historically signaled near-term relief. The raw numbers are stretched enough for either claim: VIX above 35 with an aggressively inverted curve, the one-week move in VIX the fifth largest on record, the Nasdaq VIX at its highest since Covid, and the index closing more than 4 standard deviations below its 50-day average price, the most extreme reading since August 2015. Options price a 3.27% move through November 27.

### Bonds started hedging stocks again

Treasuries rallied across the curve on Friday as money moved to safety, two-year yields down 12bps, ten-year down 10bps and thirty-year down 7.5bps. That is a change in behaviour rather than one good day. Since the ten-year yield passed 5%, all eight daily S&P 500 declines of more than 1% have come with yields falling, by an average of five bps, where from March through the summer the same declines pushed yields about five bps higher. Raymond James also has the inflation rate priced into ten-year bonds at its lowest since September 2024. Goldman's rates desk is positioned for five-year yields to fall further than two-year yields, entered at 0bp with a 15bp target and a stop at -8bp. Natixis keeps ten-year yields in a 4.90%-5.25% range into the December meeting and will buy dips only above 5.20%-5.25%. Auctions this week total $183bn of two-, five- and seven-year notes.

### Gold and copper broke, and the reason is contested

Gold fell 1.3% on Friday to about $3,575 an ounce, its lowest since September 2025, and is down nearly 8% on the week, which Morgan Stanley puts inside its biggest drawdown since the 1980s. High grade copper futures fell more than 9%, and three-month copper on the London exchange broke trend support at 13800-14050. JPMorgan's cross-asset team reads both as a rates story, with the dollar and Fed repricing the common driver rather than the AI unwind, and has gold trading below what its regression against one-year-forward one-year SOFR implies, so the fall may have overshot. It recommends long gold, copper, silver and platinum. Its own technical team is on the other side, with gold well below critical support at 3800-4000 and a sustained break there implying a measured move to the mid-$2,000s. UBS calls the metal oversold, warns against extrapolating the downtrend, and keeps a USD 5,000 target for late 2027.
