# AI carries, contrarian growls

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The corpus heading into today's open is split right down the middle and it's the same fault line as the past two weeks: AI is doing all of the work in US equities, oil is doing all of the work in macro, and an unusually large group of desks is now openly questioning whether the AI half can keep doing the work. Goldman, JPMorgan, UBS, Deutsche Bank and BlackRock all describe AI capex and AI-adjacent earnings as the dominant driver of the rally; UBS, State Street, Syz, Deutsche Bank and Goldman in their political-risk note simultaneously warn that the concentration is itself the risk. Sentiment into the open: long AI, long oil, long gold, increasingly nervous about all three.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI is still the only thing the tape rewards, and that is now the bear case

*Mag-7 plus AI-adjacent capex names are the entire equity rally, the breadth question is whether anything else joins in 2H, and the same desks that hit a $1T capex forecast are quietly telling clients to rebalance.*

- UBS forecasts 2026 AI capex at $820B and 2027 at $990B, with cloud provider growth running 40% year-over-year in 1Q26 — the demand curve hasn't bent, so AI-tied earnings stay the marginal contributor to S&P EPS
- BlackRock Investment Institute projects annual hyperscaler capex hits $1 trillion by 2028 — for context, that is roughly equal to total US corporate capex in 2019, concentrated in five names
- JPMorgan flags Broadcom's FY27 AI backlog at over $150B and FY26-28 memory capex at $450B — the picks-and-shovels names downstream of the hyperscaler order book have years of visible revenue
- Syz Private Banking and State Street both note the rally is "significantly less robust when excluding AI-related stocks" — the breadth problem (will the rally widen out beyond Mag-7 to small caps and value, or stay narrow) is now in print at multiple desks, not just on Twitter
- Goldman's own note flags $150B of data-center projects facing delays or cancellations and an AI political-backlash risk where policy shifts from regulation to taxing or owning the rents — a different kind of hit to multiples than a demand miss
- The crypto leg of the AI complex has paused: $BTC, $ETH and $SOL are all down on the UTC day even as cloud-equity AI earnings keep climbing — the long-crypto vs long-AI-equity rotation that was working in April has stalled, which is its own signal that the speculative bid is broadening within AI infrastructure rather than expanding across asset classes

Three things are simultaneously true and the desks aren't pretending otherwise. AI demand is real and the spending plans are real (UBS, JPMorgan, BlackRock). The market's reliance on a handful of names to deliver that demand is unprecedented in the post-war record (State Street, Syz Private Banking, Deutsche Bank). Franklin Templeton is alone arguing for the rally to broaden into small caps, value, and EMs; Deutsche Bank, Goldman and Syz Private Banking explicitly push back that US megacap tech concentration stays the regime. And the funding mechanism for the capex — record corporate debt issuance plus AI-tied equity raises — is starting to be flagged as fragile by Deutsche Bank and Syz Group as "the AI debt bubble." If hyperscaler capex keeps compounding 40%+, the bulls win; if a single META or GOOGL guide-down lands, the picks-and-shovels names crack first because they are priced for the trajectory, not the level. The bear case here isn't that AI is a fad. It's that UBS itself is the bull and is also telling clients to use the recent strength to rebalance — when the loudest bull is also flagging concentration risk, the asymmetry has shifted. What survives the cleanest in either outcome are the picks-and-shovels names with multi-year backlogs ($AVGO, $MU) over the GPU-leadership names where positioning is already extreme. The bias for someone with US equity exposure is to keep the AI infrastructure trade on but trim the most-crowded GPU expression in favor of the memory and custom-silicon legs.

### Software just had its squeeze and the desks split on whether to chase or fade

*Software went from the most-hated growth bucket in the market to a record-setting two-day rip, and Goldman, JPMorgan and Raymond James are saying buy it while The Market Ear is saying take profits.*

- Goldman: the Software vs Semis basket moved +7.45% in a single session, the largest one-day move higher in over a year, and IGV daily call option volume hit 280k Friday, the biggest volume day ever
- JPMorgan: software has outperformed the S&P by over 10 percentage points across two sessions, IGV is up 15% over the last three sessions, and long-term software positioning sits in the 1st percentile against semis in the 97th percentile — the mean-reversion gap is the trade
- Vanda Research flags $46M of retail intraday inflows into $IGV, 40% above the previous record set in early February — retail is now chasing the move alongside long-onlys
- Raymond James pegs 2026 software industry earnings growth at 18% — the AI-complement thesis (software captures the productivity layer of the AI build-out) is finally showing up in earnings revisions
- The Market Ear notes IGV is +43% and SOX +45% from the April 14 lows — both legs have moved equally hard, so the relative-value gap that JPM is pointing to has already largely closed at the index level

The pure mechanics: a 1st-percentile long-term positioning print is the cleanest setup for a squeeze in any growth sector, and a single AI capex revision (cloud-providers growing 40% year-over-year) was enough to flip the narrative from "AI is taking software's lunch money" to "AI is software's growth engine." Goldman's desk is seeing genuine long-only demand, not just hedge-fund cover. JPMorgan is staying long the dispersion trade because the positioning percentile gap (1st vs 97th) hasn't closed yet even if the price gap has narrowed. What The Market Ear is pushing back on: after a historic squeeze and a test of major range highs, with retail now setting record inflow days, the marginal buyer has changed and the marginal buyer is the wrong one. Their read is to fade strength here, not chase. The honest read of the corpus is that the squeeze leg is over but the multi-quarter long-software-over-semis thesis is intact for anyone willing to size for daily noise — JPMorgan's 1st-vs-97th positioning gap is the structural argument that holds even after a 15% three-day move. Bias for daily-pulse readers: long $IGV outright into next earnings rather than $IGV over $SOXX as a relative-value trade, since the pair after this week's move is too noisy for a daily signal.

### Hormuz is still closed, Iran is reviewing a US proposal, and the oil tape is now whip-saw

*Nine desks treat the Strait of Hormuz as an unresolved supply disruption with no scheduled reopening, and a US-Iran ceasefire MoU just hit the wires last night sending front-month oil down more than 1%.*

- Natixis: daily ship passages through Hormuz are still under 10 versus a pre-conflict baseline of 125-140 ships per day — the chokepoint is not normalizing on the ground regardless of headlines
- UBS: 21 million barrels per day of oil normally moves through the Strait — that is roughly 20% of global seaborne crude, with no alternative routing for most of it
- Rabobank: a 7-month total closure would knock 2.5-2.8 billion barrels off cumulative production — Rabobank is the one desk calling for the closure to persist into September
- Deutsche Bank: Polymarket probability of Hormuz returning to normal by end-June has fallen to 22%, down from 26% on Sunday — even the prediction-market crowd has marked the reopening timeline back
- Deutsche Bank also pegs $150/bbl as the Brent price in a prolonged-closure scenario, and UBS holds a $105/bbl September Brent target — that is the implied range the corpus is trading off

The split is the trade. Front-month Brent is pricing the US-Iran MoU headlines (oil down more than 1% overnight); physical ship-traffic data and prediction-market probabilities are pricing closure persistence (Hormuz reopen odds falling, refined fuel inventories tight). When the futures market and the physical market disagree, the futures market eventually re-converges to the physical — historically over weeks, not days. The opposite read is that an Iran deal really does land in the next two weeks (Deutsche Bank's own analysts have flagged June as the negotiation window), and a single Trump-Iran announcement compresses the entire supply scare premium overnight. That is real risk and the asymmetry argues against a leveraged long-oil structure. The thesis that survives both scenarios is exposure to the refined-product complex through $BNO and an outright energy-sector position via $XLE — the Brent contango/USO daily noise dominates a pair trade like long $BNO over $USO for daily-pulse purposes. The bias is long $BNO outright with sizing trimmed for headline risk, plus long $GLD as the hedge given UBS's $5,500/oz year-end gold target ties the geopolitical risk premium to a different instrument.

### Nobody wants NVDA: the contrarian no-bid is now eight desks deep

*Eight desks are now writing notes openly arguing the consensus AI long is the wrong trade — Speculation Nation, Nobody Wants NVDA, 10 charts that make us go hmmm — and the absence of a 9th desk rebutting them is itself the positioning signal.*

- The Market Ear's "Speculation Nation" piece notes "positioning is extreme, leverage is exploding, investors are chasing low-quality tech" — the framing isn't a price call, it's a regime call
- Deutsche Bank's "Early Morning Reid" flags that the only non-recession example since WWII of the S&P 500 rising this fast was a few months before a historic market crash — the analog is explicit and unhedged
- Goldman Sachs' political-AI note flags backlash risk shifting from regulation to "capturing rents through taxes and ownership schemes" — different from a demand bear case, this is a multiple bear case
- State Street and Syz Private Banking both quantify the AI-excluded rally as "significantly less robust" — concentration risk now appears in the body of two distinct asset-allocator notes, not just trader commentary
- Rabobank and RBC BlueBay both have skeptical-stance notes on the post-rally credit setup — high-grade credit spreads are sitting at tight enough levels that an AI capex stumble pushes the whole risk complex wider

The mechanism is unusual: eight different desks across different specializations (macro, equities, credit, derivatives) have arrived at the same conclusion via different paths. State Street is worried about the asset-allocator's portfolio. The Market Ear is worried about retail behavior. Deutsche Bank is worried about the historical analog. Goldman is worried about political reflexivity. When skeptics converge on the same conclusion from independent starting points, that is qualitatively different from one bearish desk getting loud. The case for ignoring them is straightforward — the same desks were skeptical at lower prices and the tape kept going. UBS's $7,900 year-end SPX target and Goldman and Deutsche Bank's $8,000 targets are not consistent with the bears being right yet. What the contrarian view actually says is that the marginal positioning is now wrong (retail records, hedge fund leverage climbing, AI-debt issuance at record pace), and a marginal-positioning bear case needs a catalyst more than it needs a thesis. For someone with US equity exposure, the cleanest expression is to add equal-weight ($RSP over $SPY) and small-cap ($IWM) sleeves as a partial rebalance away from the lead, while holding the core AI infrastructure book via $AVGO and $MU rather than the most-extended GPU leadership. Cost of being wrong: 100-200 bps of relative underperformance in a continued melt-up. Cost of missing this if right: the whole shift back to value and quality on the way down.
