# The big trade cracks

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The story is a leverage unwind in the AI trade, not a headline shock. Hedge funds turned net sellers of US stocks for the first time in five weeks, and they did it by adding shorts rather than trimming longs, dropping gross leverage to the 4th percentile of its one-year range. The selling concentrated in technology and momentum, while defensive corners like staples and health care held up. Underneath it, new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish first meeting kept the dollar bid and front-end yields firm, and falling oil from the Iran de-escalation took the inflation-scare edge off without sparking the relief rally everyone expected.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI trade is unwinding from the inside

*This is a positioning unwind, not a growth scare, and the most crowded corner is where it bites first.*

- AI is now 39% of the S&P 500, the most concentrated the index has been since the dot-com peak (Bank of America) — when one trade is this large, a wobble in it is a wobble in the whole index
- Hedge fund gross leverage dropped to 207.3%, the 4th percentile of the past year, as funds shorted into the move instead of buying the dip (Goldman) — the smart-money bid that caught every prior dip is gone
- The semiconductor-to-Nasdaq ratio has surged 46% in 12 weeks to levels last seen in 2000-2001, with chips posting 9 separate 5% up-days in 60 sessions (BTIG) — that frequency of violent up-days clusters around major tops
- Momentum stocks are running more than five standard deviations above their one-year average versus the broad market, and the buyers are retail, not institutions (Bloomberg) — a rally with no professional sponsorship is a rally that can air-pocket
- Korea is the live wire: the KOSPI fell 10%, its volatility gauge hit 95 (implying 6% daily swings), and a single 5% index move forces $4.7B of dealer rebalancing in names like SK Hynix that sit inside the world's largest leveraged ETF (The Market Ear)

The mechanism is leverage meeting thin liquidity. Hyperscaler gross debt has doubled from 0.9x to 1.8x in two quarters to fund the buildout (Société Générale), leveraged ETFs have stacked synthetic short-gamma that forces selling into weakness, and Korea shows what a margin-driven cohort does when it has to de-risk into a market too thin to absorb it. None of that needs an earnings miss to unwind. The bull case is real and worth stating plainly: growth is fine, Goldman just cut its recession odds to 10% from 25%, and AI server rental prices are slipping, which lowers the cost of using all this compute even as it dents the scarcity story. The trouble is that a healthy long-term buildout and a stretched short-term trade can both be true, and the thing cracking right now is the trade, not the buildout. Goldman's own desk concedes that with the hardware names this extended, it is getting harder to justify further large gains. For someone with US options exposure, this favors owning downside on the most crowded vehicle, $SMH puts, rather than fading the broad index here.

### Warsh hands the dollar a tailwind

*Warsh's hawkish debut repriced the front end higher, and the cleanest trade off it is the dollar, not stocks.*

The new Fed chair opened with minimal forward guidance and a price-stability-only message, and nine FOMC members now pencil hikes this year (Goldman). The market has moved to one 25bp hike priced by autumn and 45bp of total tightening through the first half of 2027 (Crédit Agricole). That pushed the 2s30s curve to flatten 19bp as front-end yields jumped 16bp (JPMorgan), and it has the dollar breaking out to its most overbought reading since the 2022 peak (The Market Ear). The pushback comes from ING, which argues two more hikes is overdone and that bonds become the better hedge again now that lower oil has restored the negative link between bonds and stocks, so duration works if the AI drag spreads to the economy. That tension is the whole trade: the front end is pricing a Fed that will not blink, the long end is starting to price the growth risk that unwind implies. Long the dollar via $UUP is the expression that does not depend on guessing which side of the curve wins.

### Where to rotate when the consensus is short tech

*The rotation out of crowded tech is already paying defensives and breadth, and that is where the next leg of leadership sets up.*

In the de-risking session, money rotated into consumer staples, health care, financials, materials and energy while it sold technology, momentum and even utilities (Goldman, Morgan Stanley). Breadth is widening underneath the index, with materials, financials, industrials and consumer names all set for double-digit earnings growth rather than the index leaning on a handful of chip names (Bloomberg). Hartnett is telling clients to take chips off the table with the BofA Bull and Bear Indicator at an extreme-bullish 9.2. JPMorgan is pairing its semiconductor and Korea longs against shorts in Mega-cap tech and unprofitable software, which is the same rotation expressed as a hedge. The skeptical read is that retail keeps buying every dip and a defensive rotation has been a losing bet for two years, so this could fizzle like the others. Watch in this order: (1) equal-weight versus cap-weight, long $RSP over $SPY as the cleanest concentration-unwind expression, paired with (2) long $XLU utilities as the defensive complement that gets bid in every risk-off tape, then (3) $XLV health care, which also carries the biotech catalyst below.

### Oil is pricing a recovery that has not arrived

*The crowd is record-short oil into a market that is physically still tight, which is a squeeze waiting for a headline.*

Managed-money Brent net length has hit a record short after seven straight weeks of selling worth $24.8bn, with new shorts making up about 80% of it and total notional shorts at a 10-year high (Goldman). Prices are grinding lower as the market prices a fast recovery of flows through the Strait of Hormuz, but traffic is still far below pre-war levels and would need roughly 13 to 14 million barrels a day of additional flow to normalize (Goldman, ING). ING calls the sell-off overdone and flags a possible Russian diesel export ban, which at 900k barrels a day would matter more than its earlier fuel restrictions. The counter is that the Iran interim deal could unlock 50mb of Iranian supply and Chinese demand is softer than the official data shows, so the path of least resistance stays lower until positioning flushes. Does Hormuz traffic recover before the shorts have to cover? Long $BNO outright is the way to own the asymmetry, with the daily roll noise too large to make it a pairs trade.

### Biotech breaks out while everyone watches chips

*With pharma writing big checks again, biotech is the one breakout that has nothing to do with the AI trade.*

The biotech ETF $IBB pushed to fresh 52-week highs and cleared its 2021 record, and $XBI is outperforming the S&P year-to-date (The Market Ear). The catalyst is a new deal wave, led by AbbVie's $10.9B purchase of Apogee Therapeutics at a 49.5% premium, with large-cap pharma committing to acquisitions that are dilutive through 2031, a sign management is looking past the patent cliff. Barclays is flagging argenx as a high-conviction name into its Myositis trial readouts. The risk is that a sector breakout led by M&A can stall the moment the deals pause, and biotech has faked out at these levels before. Does $XBI hold its breakout through the next leg of tech de-risking, or does a broad risk-off drag it back in? Long $XBI is the cleanest way to own the rotation into a group with its own catalyst rather than the market's.
