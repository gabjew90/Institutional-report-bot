# The AI trade gets tested

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

Research this morning is built around one tension: the AI infrastructure buildout is still the biggest, most-covered story in the corpus, but the trade sitting on top of it is being repriced right as big-tech earnings land. Ten banks are engaged on AI capex, Alphabet just raised its 2026 spending plan again, and yet the loudest new voices are about free cash flow rolling over and hidden leverage underneath the buildout. Underneath that, oil is the other live wire. The Strait of Hormuz is running at a fraction of its normal flow, distillate cracks are near records, and equity volatility has barely reacted. The read across desks is a market that has priced the AI growth story to perfection and the oil supply shock to almost nothing.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The AI trade gets tested, not broken, into big-tech earnings

*The buildout is real and still compounding, but the market is repricing how it gets paid for, and the megacap capex spenders are where that repricing bites first.*

- Alphabet raised 2026 capex guidance to $195-205B, up from $180-190B, with a 2027 figure now modeled as high as $300-350B (JPMorgan, UBS) and Google Cloud growing 82% year-on-year on a $514B backlog. The spend is accelerating faster than the revenue that justifies it.
- Amazon posted positive Q1 free cash flow of roughly $1B against a $13B decline that consensus had penciled in (Bloomberg Markets Live). Hyperscalers keep defying the cash-crunch calls, which is exactly why the bears think the reckoning is only delayed.
- Hidden off-balance-sheet obligations across the group run to about $1.65 trillion, with $662B of that in data-center leases that never show up as debt (Tyler Durden). This is the leverage the equity market is not looking at yet.
- Hyperscaler capex is set to grow 83% year-on-year in 2026 (Raymond James), and about 75% of Goldman's client conversations last week were about AI. Positioning and attention are both crowded into the same trade.
- Intel is the counter-example the bulls want: capex raised to $20B from $17B, and its data-center and AI segment grew 59% year-on-year (Goldman). The picks-and-shovels layer is still being paid while the hyperscalers spend.

Three things are happening at once. Spending is being pulled forward faster than the cloud revenue that pays for it, free cash flow at the biggest spenders is turning negative for the first time, and the money to fund it is increasingly showing up as leases and purchase commitments rather than cash on hand. That combination does not break the growth story, but it changes who wins inside it. The bear case, and it is a real one, is that enterprises are routing work to cheaper open-source models to cut their own bills (UBS calls it "token optimization"), that Chinese open-weight models are closing the frontier gap and making premium capability harder to monetize (Citadel), and that Wall Street expects Meta, Amazon and Microsoft to print negative cash flow by year-end and lean on debt or dilution to keep spending. The defense is that this same group has beaten negative-cash-flow forecasts every quarter so far, and the hardware layer underneath them, the companies selling the shovels, is sold into 2027 regardless of which model provider wins. Alphabet already reported, so the live binary is Tesla after the close today and Meta, Microsoft and Amazon next week. The bias here is to own the infrastructure layer, long $INTC and the semicap names like $AMAT, and let the hyperscaler capex spenders prove the free-cash-flow math before chasing them. $NVDA stays the cleanest single-name long on that logic, with UBS reiterating Overweight on the view that compute demand gets reallocated toward cheaper models rather than destroyed.

### Oil's supply hole is real and equities are barely hedged

*Crude is up because the barrels physically are not showing up, and the stock market is treating a 40% oil move like it never happened.*

Hormuz flows have collapsed to 5.1 million barrels a day, down from 12.5 million just a week earlier (JPMorgan), and Citadel pegs the March-to-May average at 2.7 million against 20 million before the war. Iranian loadings that were 1.5-2 million barrels a day at the start of the month have fallen to zero (UBS), Brent rose about $28 over three weeks to trade near $92 (Goldman, 7/22), and July was the strongest month for crude since March at roughly +40%. The 2-year Treasury yield is up 92 basis points since the Iran conflict began (Raymond James), and Goldman estimates the war is adding 40 basis points to core inflation. The skeptical read is that the market is pricing only a modest risk premium (The Market Ear) and that demand destruction of over a million barrels a day (UBS) plus a Hormuz reopening compresses the refining margins that have driven this. The problem with waiting for that is inventories: distillate cracks are near record highs in both the US and Europe, refined-fuel stocks have been tight for months, and a supply hole that size takes months to refill even if a ceasefire holds. The bias is long energy outright, with integrated oil names like $REP (Goldman Buy, sector-leading 11% shareholder yield) the cleaner expression than a crude ETF where daily roll costs eat the thesis.

### What to buy when AI froth breaks: the rotation out of concentration

*The thing that unwinds the leaders is not weak AI demand, it is the leverage and crowding stacked underneath the leaders.*

The buildout carries about $1.8 trillion in off-balance-sheet operating commitments and has driven $411B of AI-related high-grade bond issuance so far this year against a $5.8 trillion capex forecast through 2030 (Goldman). Retail sentiment has ripped to the 90th percentile from the 5th in under two weeks (JPMorgan), while hedge funds sit at just the 33rd percentile of net exposure, so the crowd chasing this froth is retail speculation, not the pros. Goldman's own desk is split on whether the record IPO wave is healthy normalization or a classic top signal. When the concentration unwinds, the corpus points at hard assets as the place the money goes, oil and copper and shareholder-yield energy names that are levered to the same inflation the AI power demand is creating rather than to the multiple. The rotation lean is long $XLE energy paired with copper as the hard-asset complement, funded out of the most crowded megacap exposure. Does the buildout inflect revenue before the free-cash-flow math forces the funding, or does the leverage get recognized first?

### The ECB's next move is more likely up than the cut markets expect

*The energy war changes the European rate story, and the base case of gentle cuts into 2027 looks like the wrong side of it.*

TS Lombard's read is that consensus cuts next year off a 2.5% median forecast are misplaced, with a scenario where a persistent energy war pushes the ECB into "demand suppression" and a hike to 3% by early 2027 (this is one house's view, worth weighing as such). The other side is UBS, which argues futures are overestimating how much more the ECB tightens, sees the bank near its peak after the June hike to 2.25%, and expects EURUSD to drift to 1.18 by year-end. Eurozone headline inflation has already cooled to 2.8% in June from 3.2%, which supports the peak-rates camp, but Numera puts a 76% probability on the euro weakening over the next year. Watch in this order: whether Brent holds above $90 into September, whether eurozone core inflation reaccelerates on energy pass-through, and whether the ECB's September meeting language tilts hawkish. A hawkish ECB into an oil shock is the combination that catches euro-rate shorts offside.

### Copper is bid because there is no metal, not because the macro is clean

*Copper can hold up through a growth scare because the near-term shortage is physical, and physical beats sentiment until the metal actually shows up.*

The argument is that copper's physical tightness, shrinking exchange and Chinese inventories, and rising Chinese grid investment outweigh the broader macro uncertainty (Bloomberg). The risk is equally concrete: a softer US tariff outcome closes the import arbitrage and kicks off a long destocking cycle, and a stronger dollar or an AI-led selloff exposes a crowded speculative long. Cost of being wrong here is a drawdown on a positioning washout that the thin inventories will cushion. Cost of missing it if right is a supply-driven grind higher that the macro bears never get filled on. Copper is the hard-asset expression that pairs cleanly with the energy rotation above.
