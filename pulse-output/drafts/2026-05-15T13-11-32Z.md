# Bond shock greets Warsh

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The tape heads into Friday's open caught between two stories that don't want to coexist. Hyperscaler capex revisions keep getting marked higher, AI-adjacent hardware orders are accelerating, and Q1 corporate capex grew 38% year-on-year. At the same time, oil is bid on a Hormuz disruption that nobody can confidently call resolved, the 30-year Treasury yield is sitting at a level last seen during the GFC, and yesterday's retail sales print landed hot on top of an already firm CPI run. Positioning still leans risk-on, with retail trading at 20% of US equity volume and dealer gamma keeping the upside sticky, but the bond market is the dissenting vote.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex revisions still ratcheting higher

*The AI infrastructure build is no longer a forecast, the order books are confirming it in real time, and the bull case is getting bigger numbers each cycle.*

- Goldman expects hyperscaler capex plus R&D to exceed $1 trillion in 2026E, with the run-rate compounding through the decade
- Cisco's F3Q26 AI hyperscale orders hit $1.9B, prompting Goldman to raise the F2026 AI hyperscale order guide to $9B from $5B prior, with the 12-month price target lifted to $116 from $75
- BofA raised its 2030 AI data-center systems market estimate to $1.7 trillion, up from $1.4 trillion
- Corporate capex grew 38% year-on-year in Q1 2026, the steepest reading in the post-pandemic cycle, with 80% of S&P returns this year tied to AI-driven names (Goldman)
- Applied Materials guided semi equipment growth above 30% in calendar 2026, raised from a prior 20%-handle, a second cycle of revisions higher in a month

The build is moving from forecast to ledger entry. Cisco's order book is the kind of single-customer data point that closes the gap between "AI is a story" and "AI is a P&L." Applied Materials and the rest of the picks-and-shovels stack are sold out into 2027, which means the cycle has a structural floor regardless of what any single hyperscaler does at the next earnings print.

The bear case Bloomberg and Nomura flag is that 40% of the index's leg higher since March has come from semis alone, and a reflexive flow reversal could take SMH down 15% in a single session. The Market Ear points at the SOX trading 62% above its 200-day moving average, a stretch comparable only to French equities at the peak of the Mississippi bubble. Even granting that concentration risk, the order data is fresh and trending the right way, while the bear case is a positioning observation that needs a catalyst to actually break the cycle. For US options exposure, the bias is long Applied Materials ($AMAT) through next-cycle earnings, paired with a shorter-dated $SOXX put for the breadth-break tail.

### Hormuz disruption keeps building, oil floor stays high

*The Strait is still 95% blocked, gasoline inventories are draining at record pace, and the supply hole gets harder to refill each week the conflict drags.*

- Dated Brent is at $117/barrel (BCA), with the physical market pricing a scarcity premium that futures haven't fully absorbed
- Strait of Hormuz flows are 95% below normal on a 4-day moving average of vessel counts (Goldman Oil Tracker), with the total hit to Persian Gulf oil flows estimated at 15 mb/d
- Global oil inventories are drawing at 11-12 mb/d, a record pace offsetting 14.5 mb/d of production losses
- US gasoline inventories are 5% below the historical seasonal median and have been drawing at 0.7 mb/d since April; US wholesale prices are $21/bbl above Asia and Europe
- BCA pegs the daily cost of the Iran war to the US federal government at $400 million in munitions, maintenance, and equipment replacement

ING and Mizuho are the only desks making this an explicit directional call ("every day adds to the pressure"), but 19 other banks reference it as live context. The mechanism is that refined-product tightness compounds even if crude futures soften on headlines, and the US gasoline market is the place where consumer inflation actually shows up.

The opposite read is the Iran-for-Taiwan bargain Rabobank flagged into the Trump-Xi meeting: Beijing helps de-escalate Hormuz in exchange for US restraint on Taiwan, and the supply scare unwinds. That risk is real and worth tracking, but refined-fuel tightness doesn't resolve on a single headline. Even if crude flushes $10 on a peace memo, the gasoline cracks stay wide for weeks because the inventory hole takes months to refill. The bias here is long $BNO outright, with the trade closing only on a sustained crude reset that takes Brent below the $85 level Goldman has called out as the supply-scare unwind threshold.

### The 30-year tells the Fed it can't cut

*Warsh inherits a long end that is repricing for sticky inflation, hot demand data, and a Treasury supply pipeline that has nothing to do with the front end of the curve.*

- The 30-year Treasury yield topped 5.1% Friday morning, the highest in nearly a year, with the move attributed to inflation signals muddying rate expectations under the new chair (CNBC)
- April headline CPI ran 3.8% year-on-year, the highest since May 2023; April core CPI came in at 0.4% month-on-month, above consensus (BofA)
- Markets are now pricing roughly 50% odds of a 25-bp rate hike by year-end, a full repricing from cuts that were in the curve a month ago (The Market Ear)
- Yesterday's retail sales print landed +0.5% headline and +0.7% ex-autos, both above consensus, with March revised up; year-on-year retail sales accelerated to 4.9%
- Goldman's Q2 GDP tracking estimate was nudged up 0.3pp to +1.9%, against initial jobless claims of 211k (+12k week-on-week, above expectations)

Three forces are pulling long-end yields higher at once: oil-driven inflation feeding core CPI on a multi-month lag, hot demand data that strips the soft-landing optionality out of the Fed's hand, and a Treasury supply calendar that lands on a new chair without a reservoir of credibility to absorb a tail. The repricing isn't about the next FOMC meeting, it's about whether long-dated bonds can hold any premium when the curve has no anchor.

The bull case for duration here is that the move is overshooting, with terminal Fed funds still pinned at 3.0-3.25% per BofA, and a hawkish blow-off that fades as Hormuz resolves. The pushback is that even at terminal 3%, the 30-year doesn't clear at 5.1% unless the market is also pricing a fiscal premium, and a Warsh-led Fed cannot credibly defend long-end yields without re-opening the policy debate it was just confirmed to settle. For US fixed-income exposure, the trade is short $TLT into the next 30-year auction window, or long $UUP if the rate-differential lift starts to actually push the dollar higher off the inflation re-acceleration.

### Trump-Xi summit puts Iran and Taiwan on one ledger

*The Beijing meeting is the cleanest cross-asset catalyst on the calendar because the Iran de-escalation path and the Taiwan posture are being bargained simultaneously.*

- Seven banks (Citi, Goldman, ING, JPMorgan, Rabobank, UBS, The Market Ear) flag the Trump-Xi meeting as a near-term macro catalyst with no consensus direction
- The administration announced an $11B US weapons package for Taiwan in December, the largest ever, and Trump has separately threatened 50% tariffs on countries supplying military weapons to Iran (Newsquawk schedule)
- Overnight wire traffic captured Trump saying the US and China are "aligned on Iran" and that "Iran must make a deal soon," followed by Iran's foreign ministry stating it has "no trust" in US intentions and Trump's "will not be much more patient" line (Reuters)
- China has publicly told USTR Greer that it wants the Strait of Hormuz open and free of curbs (Reuters)
- BRICS talks ended without a joint statement, exposing divisions on Iran across the bloc

The structural read is that this is the first time in the cycle where two of the biggest macro overhangs (Hormuz supply, China policy stance on chips and Taiwan) are being decided in one room, and the cross-asset response will hinge on which trade Beijing actually agrees to.

The bull case from Rabobank is that Beijing helps de-escalate Hormuz in exchange for US restraint on Taiwan, which would crack oil meaningfully and reignite China-exposed semis and ADRs. The bear case is that Beijing extracts concessions on Taiwan while the Iran war keeps dragging, leaving oil bid and adding a chip export-control overhang to US semis. Rabobank's invalidation level is a formal public US statement reaffirming current Taiwan policy without ambiguity, which would price the bull path out. For US trader exposure, the trade is a short straddle into the summit headlines lifted with long $BABA calls as the asymmetric beneficiary if a constructive readout lands.

### Semis are carrying the market, and 9% of the index is breaking down

*The narrowest leadership in years means the bear hedge to the AI capex theme is already pricing, you just have to read where it is.*

- 40% of the index's gains since March have come from semiconductor names alone (Bloomberg)
- Nomura estimates SMH could slump 15% in a single session in a reflexive mechanical flow reversal
- 9% of S&P 500 components hit 52-week lows on Thursday, the highest daily reading in the recent sequence, even as the index pushed higher (BTIG via The Market Ear)
- The Korea ETF $EWY dropped 7% in a single session, the second-worst down day since March 2020, on chip-related "AI tax" headlines
- The TDEX index measuring demand for deep out-of-the-money tail-risk protection has hit its lowest levels in a long time, suggesting hedges have been abandoned even as breadth divergence widens

ANZ, Bloomberg/Nomura, JPMorgan, Jefferies, and Rabobank all flag semiconductor concentration as a primary index risk, but none stakes a directional call on chip stocks themselves. The mechanism here is reflexive: when 40% of returns come from one cluster of names and tail hedges have been bid away, even a small flow reversal becomes self-reinforcing on the way down.

The bull pushback is that the underlying earnings still support the multiples, with Cisco's order book and the broader picks-and-shovels guide-ups confirming the demand wave. The counter-argument is that the EWY single-session drop and the breadth gap of 344 S&P names down on an up day are the exact pattern that precedes a positioning unwind, regardless of fundamentals. For US options exposure, the trade is long memory ($MU) over AI-CPU-exposed names paired with a slightly longer-dated $SOXX put as the hedge against the breadth-break tail.
