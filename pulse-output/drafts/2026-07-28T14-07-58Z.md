# Chips crack, capex holds

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The research this morning is built around one tension. Wall Street has been de-risking the AI and semiconductor trade hard, positioning has reset off the highs, and the Asian side of the chip supply chain cracked overnight, yet the underlying demand for AI infrastructure keeps showing up healthy in the numbers. Discretionary investors have pulled equity exposure back to the lowest level since early April, the semiconductor index is sitting on the line that held its mid-July sell-off, and the extra yield investors want to hold big cloud companies' bonds pushed to new highs. Underneath, oil kept unwinding its war premium after the US and Iran stepped back, which takes one inflation worry off the table just as the Fed meets this week.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The chip trade is correcting and the crowding says it is not done

*The semiconductor sell-off is not a dip to buy yet, because the most one-sided positioning in the market still has to clear.*

- Hedge-fund positioning in chips sits at the 98th percentile and tech hardware at the 86th (JPMorgan, as of July 27), meaning the bets are about as one-sided long as they have been in a year, so any unwind has a long way to run before it is clean
- The SOX semiconductor index is parked on 11,200, the level that held the July 17 sell-off, with its momentum gauge (RSI, where under 30 is washed out) at 39, the most oversold since the AI melt-up began, so a decisive close below 11,200 opens air underneath
- BTIG puts 22% of downside in $SOXX to its 200-day average if that support breaks, and 23% in high-beta momentum names to their March lows, so the technical floor sits far below where the group trades now
- Korea's memory complex cracked overnight, with Samsung down 14%, Kioxia off 60% from its peak, and the KOSPI down 11% in a session and 35% from its June high, so the AI supply chain is already unwinding where it is most levered
- The extra yield investors demand to hold hyperscaler bonds hit new highs of 145 basis points, about 1.45 percentage points over Treasuries, right after Alphabet's earnings, so the credit market started repricing AI-financing risk while stocks were still buying the dip

Three forces are pulling the group lower at once. The crowding gives a big unwind its fuel, the widening in tech credit is the bond market questioning how all this capital spending gets funded, and China's push into its own chip supply chain caps the long-term revenue of the Western names. The bull case is real and worth stating plainly. Discretionary money has already reset to the 17th percentile, its lowest since early April (Deutsche Bank), the momentum gauge is washed out, and long-horizon valuations look reasonable, which reads as a buying opportunity to some desks. What keeps this from being the bottom is that the reset is only in the discretionary crowd. The systematic and hedge-fund long-short books are still at that 98th percentile, and technical damage on this scale usually takes months to repair, not days. The bias here is to stay cautious on $SOXX until the index reclaims its 50-day average and 11,200 holds on a closing basis.

### China is building its own chip supply chain faster than the market priced

*China's home-grown chip effort is now big enough to threaten the Western equipment names, not just serve its own market.*

CXMT, China's memory-chip maker, debuted up roughly 466% and briefly passed Intel's entire market value (Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs), a signal of how much confidence there is in Beijing's self-sufficiency drive. China is ramping domestic production of DUV lithography tools, the older-generation machines that etch chip patterns, which is the direct reason $ASML broke below its 50-day average. Apple is pushing to source memory from Chinese suppliers like CXMT and YMTC to ease a global shortage, while Micron warns that Chinese entry could gut the US industry and points to $250B of planned US capacity (JPMorgan). Memory contract prices are already decelerating, with DRAM up 16% quarter-on-quarter after a 56% jump the quarter before (JPMorgan). The lean is to fade $ASML rallies rather than chase them while the China tool story builds, since the risk is a permanent haircut to its largest growth region.

### Strip out the positioning noise and AI demand is still real

*The actual demand for AI infrastructure keeps showing up in the numbers even as the stocks correct.*

Oracle's finance chief says its AI-infrastructure projects run 30% to 40% gross margins and high-20s returns on invested capital across 5-to-6-year contracts, with capacity tight and vendors building to serve booked demand rather than speculatively (Deutsche Bank, $300 target). Amazon heads into its 2Q report with AWS momentum and possible upside to operating income (Deutsche Bank, $315 target), sitting on $86.8B of cash against its $200B capex plan. Rental prices for the newest B200 chips rose 7.3% month-on-month while the prior-generation H100 fell 1.1% (JPMorgan), so the money is concentrating in the latest silicon, not fading. Broadcom kept its Overweight rating at JPMorgan on its Samsung memory agreement and top share in AI custom chips. Watch in this order: first, Amazon's capex guide this week, where a raise confirms the buildout and long $ORCL is the cleanest way to own execution without the crowding, second, whether the B200-versus-H100 rental gap keeps widening, third, whether hyperscaler credit spreads stall or keep pushing wider.

### The Fed holds this week, but a hawkish hold still stings

*The Fed almost certainly leaves rates alone, yet the market carries enough hike risk that the tone of the hold matters.*

JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs both expect no change at this week's meeting. Markets are pricing roughly 38% to 40% odds of a hike anyway, and Goldman raised its odds of eventual hikes to 35% from 25%. The cover for a patient hold is soft June inflation, with core CPI at -0.02% month-on-month, the lowest since May 2020 (JPMorgan). The risk sits in the messaging, with expected hawkish dissents from Hammack and Logan, and a Chair in Warsh who could tip a close call toward a surprise. If the statement reads as a clean hold with soft-inflation cover and no more than the two expected dissents, the front end of the bond market gets relief and long $TLT works into it. If Warsh forces a hike or three or more members dissent hawkish, that trade is dead and the dollar catches a bid.
