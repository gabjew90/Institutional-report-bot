# Nobody priced this cycle

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The Fed raised rates for the first time since 2023 and then told everyone it is not finished. Sixteen of eighteen policymakers now pencil in at least one more increase this year, the committee's own estimate of where rates settle in the long run moved up, and the line about looking through supply-driven inflation came out of the statement. Research published overnight is close to unanimous on direction and split only on size. The bond market took the message first.

Underneath the index, two things are doing the work. Crude is setting the swings in interest rates that set everything else, and headlines about one Saudi pipeline flip it either way. The AI trade has stopped moving as one block, with data centers and the chips inside them bought while software and productivity names are sold, and the spending behind all of it increasingly funded with borrowed money rather than cash flow. Leverage and sentiment sit near the bottom of their one-year ranges going into the largest options expiry of the quarter.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The Fed started a cycle the market still refuses to price

*Futures carry 95 basis points of tightening over the next year, and the last fourteen cycles say that number is far too small.*

- Deutsche Bank fit all 15 Fed hiking cycles since the mid-1950s against the inflation rate each began from and got a 0.82 fit. Their 3.55% September inflation forecast maps to more than 200 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percent) of hikes in year one, against the 95 futures carry. Traders are underwriting the shallowest cycle on record at the moment the Fed raised its own destination.
- 16 of 18 policymakers now project at least one more increase this year, against half in June (UBS), and the median long-run rate went to 3.2% from 3.1%. The committee moved the finish line in the same meeting it moved rates.
- Goldman shifted to a second hike in October, Deutsche Bank has December and March, Standard Chartered has December plus another in the first half of 2027, and Rabobank still calls it one and done. That spread is a full percentage point of borrowing cost on every leveraged position.
- Standard Chartered lifted its three-month target for the 10-year Treasury yield to 5.0-5.25% and its dollar index target to 100. This hike gets paid for in bonds and the dollar long before it reaches earnings.
- August retail sales rose 1.2% against the 0.8% economists wanted, with the core measure up 6.7% from a year earlier (Goldman). Consumer demand is not what stops this.

Warsh called the move removing a dose of accommodation rather than the start of a fight, but the committee also deleted its language about supply shocks driving prices, which is the Fed saying out loud that it will no longer look past an energy-driven rise in inflation. That one edit turns every oil headline into a rates headline.

Rabobank reads the shock as stagflationary, expects a cut as early as the fourth quarter of 2027 and treats yesterday as a one-off, and UBS calls the four hikes now priced into the curve excessive. Deutsche Bank's own history answers both, because the market priced 200 basis points at the start of the 2022 cycle and the Fed delivered more than 400, and the 10-year yield has risen in the first year of 11 of the last 12 cycles by an average of 114 basis points. The short $TLT read this pulse has carried since August 31 was built on hawkish projections and roughly even odds of an October move, and yesterday delivered both, so that one paid. October 28 is the next checkpoint, with PNC expecting no move there and a hike in mid-December, Goldman expecting it in October, and two inflation reports landing in between.

### The AI build-out moved onto the bond market

*Spending is still accelerating. What changed is who is funding it.*

Capital spending at the safest corporate borrowers grew 65% from a year earlier in the second quarter, the tenth straight quarter above 35% (Goldman). JPMorgan puts US technology net bond sales at roughly $335 billion this year, and Goldman raised its 2026 forecast for total high-grade corporate bond sales by $200 billion to $2.3 trillion, about a quarter of it AI-related. UBS still has AI capital spending going from $900 billion this year to $1.2 trillion next, while BofA has the big cloud companies slowing from above 90% growth to roughly 35% over the same stretch, and the gap between those two is the question of whether the debt gets serviced out of revenue or rolled over. The trade has already split on it. Data centers and AI chips were bought while software and AI-productivity names were sold (Goldman), and the share of Nasdaq 100 members above their 200-day average price fell from 77% in mid-August to 56% with the index going nowhere (BTIG), which cuts against the software-over-semiconductors read this pulse ran yesterday. BofA's caveat is the one to keep, because the five largest companies are 27% of next-twelve-month index earnings and semiconductors alone must supply 9.6 of the 15.4 percentage points of 2027 earnings growth. UBS has $NVDA as a Most Preferred name on demand it calls supply-constrained, and the next round of cloud capital-spending guidance settles whether that concentration is the engine or the whole risk.

### The barrel is setting the bond market

*Crude, not AI, is what is moving yields right now, and one Saudi pipeline decides the next leg.*

Goldman's and Nomura's desks landed on the same read this week, that energy is the primary driver of market risk and interest-rate swings. Goldman sizes a one-to-two month outage of the 3.5 million barrel a day East-West pipeline at roughly $5 on fair value, with Persian Gulf exports at 17 million barrels a day and 6.7 million of that flow exposed. Headlines that the line was being restored knocked crude down 3% in a session. Positioning is the vulnerable part, because speculative funds added $9.3 billion of Brent exposure between August 4 and September 8, seventy percent of it traders closing bearish bets rather than fresh buyers, net long positions now sit at the 88th percentile of their two-year range, and options on oil stayed calm through the entire surge. The long $USO read this pulse opened on September 14 is the one that got hit, and the pipeline coming back did it rather than demand going away. Does the East-West line reopen before the Houthis take another run at Saudi infrastructure? Nomura is long energy equities and long commodities as its best risk-adjusted trade and is buying October options rather than chasing the price.

### Where Goldman and UBS part company on the index

*The disagreement is not about earnings. It is about how fast yields get where they are going.*

UBS has US equities Attractive with an 8,100 target for December and 8,400 by next June, built on 25% earnings growth this year and 14% next. Goldman is neutral on equities over three months and overweight over twelve, and the reason is entirely the bond market. Its work has the extra return investors demand for owning stocks instead of bonds back at late-1990s levels, and finds that a move in the 10-year yield of roughly 50 basis points in a month, or 30 in two weeks, is what historically turns equity returns negative. BofA frames the same tension differently, noting current equity prices are consistent with 1.7% inflation while its own economists forecast 3.2% this year and 2.4% next. Speed is the falsifiable part. If the 10-year adds 30 basis points inside two weeks, Goldman's threshold is hit and the three-month neutral call was right. If it does not, UBS's 8,100 stays the base case into December.

### Positioning is empty going into the biggest expiry of the quarter

*Nobody is long, and $2 trillion of options exposure settles today.*

Goldman has systematic trend-following funds $37.5 billion long US equities, the 22nd percentile of the past year, with the index within 1.1% of the level that flips them into sellers. Hedge fund borrowing sits at the 31st percentile of a year and their net market exposure at the 5th, and the AAII bull-bear survey just made new 2026 lows. Against that, $2 trillion of notional exposure settles at today's quarterly expiry, when index options, index futures and single-stock options roll off together (Bloomberg Markets Live), taking with it the $3.8 billion of dealer hedging that has been absorbing moves in both directions. Deutsche Bank has discretionary investors at the 34th percentile while systematic funds sit at their most overweight in eleven months, which is the split that makes the next move violent either way. Cost of being wrong on the upside: missing a squeeze off the most washed-out sentiment reading of the year. Cost of being wrong on the downside: Goldman models $199 billion of systematic selling over a month if prices fall, with no cushion left to absorb it.
