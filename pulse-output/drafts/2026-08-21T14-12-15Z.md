# Buyback bid fades fast

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

One reversal runs through this whole research window. The Treasury's expanded buyback of long-dated bonds bought a single session of lower yields before the bond market took most of it back, and nearly every position in the corpus sits downstream of that. Desks are rotating out of crowded technology momentum into healthcare and defensives after the worst systematic long/short day in more than two years, buying gold as the release valve for a government that wants lower yields and will accept a weaker dollar to get them, and reading Walmart's traffic slowdown as the first hard crack in the US consumer. Fund cash balances are near record lows, hedges are the cheapest in years, and everyone is waiting on Jackson Hole.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Treasury bought one day of calm and the bond market took it back

*The buyback expansion changed who blinks first, not where 30-year yields end up.*

- Treasury doubled its buyback operations in 10 to 30-year bonds from $2 billion to at least $4 billion each, roughly $18 billion more per quarter and $144 billion a year in total (Goldman). That retires about 16% of new 20-year and 30-year supply, and it bought one session of relief.
- The 30-year yield fell 10 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent each) on the announcement and the gap between 2-year and 30-year yields narrowed 9 basis points (Goldman). By Thursday the 10-year had risen 5.8 basis points to 4.71% and the 30-year had given back most of the rally (Deutsche Bank), with the 30-year sitting at 5.25%.
- Goldman's own estimate of what this program can achieve is 20 to 40 basis points of flattening, against the 10 to 20 basis points the 1961 and 2011 versions delivered. That is a rounding error next to a 30-year yield above 5.25%.
- Corporate borrowers have already sold $1.77 trillion of investment-grade debt this year, up 59% from last year (Nomura). Every dollar of that competes with the government for the same buyers of long-dated paper.
- US public debt passed $40 trillion and 2026 interest costs are already above $1.2 trillion (Goldman). The buyback shrinks the stock of long bonds without touching the deficit that keeps creating them.

The buyback is a swap, not a subtraction. Treasury retires long-dated bonds and funds it by issuing shorter ones, so the government's own borrowing shifts toward the short end while the deficit and the AI-driven corporate borrowing wave that compete for the same long-term buyers keep running. The $TLT read flagged in yesterday's pulse, that the buyback expansion would pin long-dated yields, did not hold for a full session, and the direction here has flipped.

Goldman's rates and equity desks read the move as a deliberate signal, a quasi-Twist that anchors volatility in long-dated bonds and is therefore good for stocks and gold. Goldman's own macro team published the rebuttal inside the same 24 hours: twists can change the path, but they rarely change the destination. Nomura calls it a band-aid on a bullet hole against $1.77 trillion of competing corporate supply, and Deutsche Bank's clients are treating it as a stopgap rather than a structural fix. Goldman's desk positioning tells you which argument won, long stocks and gold while avoiding long-dated bonds. If the 30-year is still above 5.25% when Fed Chair Warsh takes the Jackson Hole stage on Friday morning, the buyback was a two-day trade and the next tool has to come from the Fed rather than the Treasury.

### Gold cleared the line that matters

*The dollar is the pressure valve for a Treasury that wants lower yields, and gold is where that pressure goes.*

Gold cleared its 200-day average price at $4,500 and Goldman's desk moved the trade from what it called a 4-out-of-10 conviction toward a regime change with $5,000 in view. Their year-end target is $4,900 an ounce, anchored on central bank buying. Positioning is a top-five reading of the last three years, gold funds are tracking their largest week of inflows since April, and buyers traded more than four $GLD calls for every put. Goldman's explanation of the mechanism is blunt: supporting bond prices means letting the dollar become the adjustment valve. JPMorgan is long gold on the same debasement logic over three to twelve months. Crowding is the risk, and it cuts both ways when positioning is already this heavy. Gold has to hold the $4,500 line it just cleared. Back below it and the regime-change call is a failed breakout, not a new trend. Goldman's equity expression is $EGO, a Buy with five catalysts flagged through year-end.

### The consumer slowed in the same week inflation repriced

*Walmart's traffic number is the first hard evidence that $4 fuel is changing behavior.*

Walmart's US comparable sales grew 2.6%, the slowest in six years and its first miss in at least five quarters, with customer traffic growth halving to 1.5% from 3% in the first quarter (Goldman). Management pointed at fuel above $4 as the psychological threshold, and the stock fell 9.15% on it. Inflation went the other way in the same session: the one-year US inflation swap jumped 16 basis points, its biggest daily move since March, with Brent at $93.78 (Deutsche Bank). Goldman's stagflation basket is up 6.7% on the week. The rebuttal is that this is not a weak economy at all. The Philadelphia Fed's business survey printed 47.4, its highest since April 2021, with capital spending intentions back at levels last seen in the 1970s. Expensive is not the same as recessionary. Goldman frames the Jackson Hole bind precisely: dovish guidance rescues short-term rates but feeds the inflation expectations already moving, hawkish guidance tightens into a consumer that just slowed. Wednesday's core inflation reading at 8:30am is the checkpoint, and Goldman is looking for 2.9% by December.

### The capex math finally works, the pricing math does not

*The AI buildout now has an auditable return, and the thing it sells is getting cheaper by the week.*

JPMorgan put a hard number on the return for the first time: roughly 22% after-tax project return on Alibaba's incremental AI spending against a 10% cost of capital, with outside cloud revenue up 45% last quarter and a path above 50% next quarter. Token volume is doubling every 11 weeks. Cumulative data-center spending through 2030 lands somewhere between $5 trillion and $10 trillion against $1.6 trillion of AI revenue expected by the end of this year, and Goldman sizes next year's spending at $1 to $1.5 trillion. The pressure is on the revenue line. The token expenditure index has fallen 38% since the end of June (Goldman), one customer demo ran an open-weight model at one-seventieth the cost and six times the speed of a frontier model (Deutsche Bank), and OpenAI grew revenue 18% to $6.7 billion last quarter while widening its operating loss to $12.3 billion. Politics is now a second constraint, with Pennsylvania's executive order limiting new data-center permits already pressuring $TLN. Does the price of a token fall faster than the compute bill rises? JPMorgan's answer is to own the operator that can show the receipts, Overweight $BABA to $210.

### Sanctions detail lands Monday

*The oil market is pricing a settlement that the shipping data says is not coming.*

The Treasury Secretary has promised the toughest sanctions ever imposed on Iran with the detail due Monday and has asked China to cooperate. JPMorgan puts the odds of the Strait of Hormuz normalizing before year-end at 30%. Brent is at $93.78, which Goldman reads as near the top of its range with the market fading the risk of real escalation. The trade the desks are actually in is refined fuel rather than crude. Goldman carries a high-conviction call on deferred European diesel spreads with 50 to 60% upside if refinery outages persist, and Nomura flags diesel spreads as the channel through which this reaches global manufacturing. Refining margins are grinding toward triple digits, and Goldman's clients have stopped arguing about historical valuation multiples for $VLO, $MPC and $PBF. The bear case is Chinese product exports and demand destruction at these prices. Monday's detail decides whether this stays a headline or becomes a supply event.

### The unwind was positioning, not fundamentals

*Nothing broke in the numbers this week, which is what makes the systematic damage a positioning event rather than a repricing.*

Systematic long/short funds lost 1.4% in one session, their worst day in more than two years and a three standard deviation move on three years of data, with the momentum factor responsible for 60 basis points of it (Goldman). Goldman's technology momentum pair fell 16% over two days. Their flagship momentum basket has now taken 24 single-day drops of more than 5% this year, more than the prior five years combined. There is no cushion underneath it either, with active mutual funds underweight the largest technology names by 724 basis points and holding 1.2% cash, near a record low. The money that came out went into healthcare, where Moderna closed up 177% on Phase 3 melanoma vaccine data. Protection is unusually cheap into the next two weeks: small-cap three-month volatility sits at the zero percentile of a two-year lookback, Nomura is buying $IWM November $270 puts, and Goldman is recommending September $IWM puts and put spreads into Jackson Hole and the September Fed meeting.
