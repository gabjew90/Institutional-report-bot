## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hyperscalers double down — capex now 100% of cash flow

*The AI capex super-cycle has stopped being a forecast — guide-ups, run-rate revenue, and a buyback drawdown are all confirming the build.*

- Goldman raised 2026 hyperscaler capex to $755B, up 83% year/year — the figure now equals 100% of cash flow from operations
- UniCredit independently triangulates $710-725bn combined 2026 hyperscaler capex, ~75% above 2025 and 5x 2023 levels
- Meta lifted its 2026 capex guide to $125-145bn (from $115-135bn); Alphabet raised to $180-190bn
- Microsoft's AI annualized revenue run-rate is $37bn, up 123% year-over-year
- Hyperscaler buybacks were down 64% year-over-year in Q1 2026 — the cash is being redirected, not added
- BofA puts the AI Big 10 at 40% of S&P 500 market cap, which is at historical bubble-concentration peaks
- Hyperscaler net debt/EBITDA is 0.3x, leaving room for the build to be debt-funded if cash flow can't keep up

The mechanism here is that the spend is being underwritten by demand signals showing up in earnings, not by hopeful forecasts. Microsoft's AI revenue more than doubling tells you the capex is converting to revenue, and the 64% buyback drop is the clearest tell that hyperscaler boards have moved capital allocation away from share repurchases and into the buildout. The picks-and-shovels names — semicaps and memory — have been the cleanest read this week, with Roundhill's Memory ETF (DRAM) raising over $5bn since its April 2 launch and pulling in $1bn in a single day this week.

The bear case from Bank of America's Flow Show is that 40% AI Big 10 concentration is the same level seen at historical bubble peaks — the rally is asymmetric (10% market gains without a meaningful drawdown), and ignoring 30Y yields near 5% and Brent above $100 looks like late-cycle exuberance. Goldman's prime book also flags momentum exposure at the 99th percentile (1-yr) and 100th percentile (5-yr), with high-beta momentum already taking an 8% one-day hit this week — the 5th such drawdown in 2026. That risk is real, but the 1Q earnings cycle came in with 17% S&P 500 EPS growth (UBS), the fastest pace in four years, and the $755B capex figure was raised $80B in two weeks — not a number that implies a rollover. Long $SOXX, paired with long $MU as the cleanest memory expression.

### Momentum cracked, but software shorts are getting buried

*The AI factor is unwinding from extreme positioning while a separate, narrower software short-squeeze gets going underneath it — a true regime where what worked yesterday isn't the same trade as what works today.*

- Goldman's Prime Book momentum exposure is at the 99th percentile (1-year) and 100th percentile (5-year)
- High-beta momentum took an 8% one-day drawdown — top-ten one-day move of the last five years
- AI themes are up 35-55% since the beginning of April; 1m realized vol of momentum is in the 96th percentile
- 1-month momentum-to-AI correlation is 94%, near all-time highs — the factor IS the AI trade right now
- Datadog squeezed 30% in a single session, breathing life into software shorts; IGV broke its $88 resistance and is heading toward the $100 200-day moving average
- Cloudflare cut 20% of staff and dropped 16% from near all-time highs; Coinbase, Upwork, Bill Holdings all running 14-30% layoffs as AI substitutes for labor

The mechanism is two-sided positioning extremes. On the long side, hedge fund books are crammed into momentum/AI and the 1m correlation between those two factors is near a record, so any unwind in one bleeds into the other. On the short side, software was the consensus underweight, and a single name like Datadog beating-and-raising forces an unwind that takes the whole IGV up with it. The fact that hyperscaler buybacks are down 64% means the marginal bid that supported AI mega-caps in 2024-2025 is gone — the build-out is being funded by capex, not buybacks supporting share prices.

The pushback from the long-momentum side is that 8%-a-day drawdowns in a 35-55% rally since April are noise — the trend has been strong enough that even a top-ten one-day move barely dents the year. On the IGV breakout side, the $100 200-day MA is the next resistance and hedge fund short-covering capacity has limits. The cleaner expression for someone with US options exposure is to hedge the factor concentration rather than fight either side outright. Long $IGV calls into the $100 200-day moving-average test, paired with $QQQ put spreads to fade the momentum stretch.

### Hormuz: paper market priced peace, physical market still hasn't refilled

*The MoU framework that pushed Brent from $108 to $98 isn't signed, the export bottleneck hasn't reversed, and overnight headlines are reminding the futures curve that this isn't a one-headline story.*

- Strait of Hormuz oil exports are running at 4% of normal levels (Goldman, 4-day moving average) — the constraint is still binding
- US distillate inventories are 11% below the 5-year average; refinery utilization at 90.1% is slipping when it should be ramping for summer
- Brent fell from $108 to $98 on the MoU headline, a roughly 11% move; SEB notes the framework includes a $270bn Iran reparations demand and is not yet a signed deal
- Goldman's tanker tracking shows 45% of the Persian Gulf export hit being offset by US/Americas/Russia barrels, but that re-route adds days to delivery
- Crédit Agricole sees April CPI at 3.77% YoY headline, with energy CPI up 17.3% YoY driven by an 11-12% MoM motor-fuels rise — the inflation print is going to carry the oil story whether oil reverses or not
- Today's tape: US strikes on two empty Iranian oil tankers and an Iran seizure of the Ocean Koi tanker overnight remind everyone the diplomatic framework is fragile

The mechanism is a standard physical-vs-paper dislocation. Futures and equity markets are pricing the MoU outcome, while physical refined-product inventories haven't refilled — distillate is 11% below the 5-year average and refinery utilization is moving the wrong way. The market is treating an unsigned MoU as a done deal; the supply hole takes months to close even if Hormuz reopens tomorrow. SEB's read is that the binary outcome (deal vs. strike) favors long volatility, since either resolution will be a sharp move from these levels.

The bear case for energy is that 45% of the lost Gulf barrels are already being offset by US/Americas/Russia and Brazil's output just hit an all-time high of 4.25mb/d, up 17% YoY. Plus China's oil demand nowcast is down 1.5mb/d (8%) from mid-March on weak post-holiday consumption. That risk is real, but the rerouted barrels arrive on a multi-week lag while distillate-only product tightness sets the marginal price for refined fuels right now. The bias here is long $BNO outright (cleaner Brent exposure than $USO) into the next set of Hormuz headlines, with the implied vol bid as a separate kicker.

### Rate-cut repricing: UBS sees easing, ING calls it premature

*The bond market is split on whether the central-bank tightening fear has run its course — and Friday's NFP beat just gave the skeptics a fresh data point.*

- UBS forecasts a 3.25% Fed funds rate by December 2026, implying two cuts in 2H26 — diverging from market pricing of no cuts
- ING expects the rate path to be more gradual than the optimistic post-Hormuz-MoU pricing implies, citing oil-driven structural inflation as the binding constraint
- April NFP private payrolls landed at +123k vs the +75k consensus — a clean beat that tilts the data toward the ING side
- Citi (-15k) and MUFG (45k) both went into the print expecting a weaker number than the actual; the dispersion across forecasts (-15k to +75k) was the widest in months
- 10-year Treasury yields are at 4.35%, down 8bps on the day on the geopolitical de-escalation flow, but UBS sees the recent rise in yields as an opportunity to lock in income
- Crédit Agricole expects April CPI at 3.77% YoY headline (May 12 release), peaking at 4.2% in May — well above the level the Fed can comfortably cut into

The mechanism is that two forces are pulling rate-cut expectations in opposite directions. Hormuz de-escalation pressure is taking inflation expectations down (the 8bp rally in 10s today), while the actual labor data is reaccelerating (123k vs 75k). UBS is leaning on the elevated-yield-as-income argument — long-end Treasuries deliver real carry at 4.3-4.4% even if the Fed never cuts — while ING is leaning on the inflation reality of $100 Brent feeding into core CPI on a three-month lag.

The bull case for rate cuts is that growth is softening (Goldman's breakeven job growth model has the labor market needing only 50k/month by year-end 2026 to stabilize unemployment, dropping from 65k now), and the consumer is borrowing through the squeeze rather than spending fresh income — savings rate is at cycle lows. That risk is real, but the binding constraint on the Fed isn't payrolls — it's a 4.2% CPI peak in May with energy CPI up 17.3% YoY. The trade is short $UUP into next Tuesday's CPI print, with $TLT as the separate long-duration expression for the UBS thesis.
