## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI infrastructure spending is reshaping more than just earnings

*The AI capex super-cycle is no longer a forecast, it is showing up in import data, labor cost projections, and inflation prints.*

- Goldman Sachs puts AI investment at 1.1% of US GDP, with $150bn per year in labor costs tied to the AI transition and $800-900bn in workforce reorganization cost over the cycle, this is a real-economy story not just a Nasdaq story
- Bank of America: "spend on AI infrastructure has shown little fatigue" and "demand for AI inputs could lead this wedge to persist" in inflation data, capex is now durable enough to bleed into core CPI
- Goldman flags 5.1% core intermediate goods producer prices, the fastest pace since 2022, the AI build is bidding up the components that feed the inflation index
- Danske Bank notes US imports of AI-related computer equipment are a rapidly growing share of total US import value, the build-out is large enough to reshape the trade balance
- 14.2% TSMC weighting in MSCI Emerging Markets has surpassed India's 11.94%, the entire EM index has tilted into the AI complex

Three forces reinforce each other. The data points show capex widening from chips to power, cooling, software, and second-derivative goods imports. The earnings cycle keeps confirming the spend with names like Datadog and Siemens, and macro spillover into inflation prints means the cycle has structural durability that survives a single hyperscaler guide-down.

The bear case is positioning, not fundamentals. Info Tech allocation in the prime book is at a 5-year high, the Nasdaq is 15% above its 100-day moving average, the 14-day RSI hit 83 (one of the most extreme overbought readings on record), and JPMorgan clients are flagging a possible 3-4% pullback on systematic-fund unwinds. A correction can happen on positioning alone without breaking the structural story. That distinction matters: the picks-and-shovels names (Applied Materials, the cooling and power supply chain) are sold out into 2027, so even a 3-4% Nasdaq pullback does not change the multi-year demand book. NVDA reports next Wednesday after the bell and is the next confirmation test. Long $SOXX into the print.

### Inflation just reaccelerated, and the curve is re-pricing rate cuts into rate hikes

*April CPI confirmed it, May PPI sealed it, the inflation re-acceleration is now broad enough to put hikes back on the table.*

- April headline CPI 3.8% year-over-year, the highest reading in three years, with core CPI +0.38% month-over-month above the 0.3% consensus
- Today's PPI dropped at 1.4% month-over-month versus 0.5% estimate (a triple miss), with core PPI year-over-year at 5.2% versus 4.3% expected, supply-side prices are accelerating not cooling
- Scotiabank flags core services CPI ex-energy and ex-shelter at +0.45% month-over-month described as "explosive", and beef prices +8.8% year-over-year leading the food index higher
- Bank of America forecasts core PCE in the 2.5-3% range, a level which explicitly precludes Fed rate cuts, and warns "this is not a good wedge for the Fed"
- Fed funds futures now show roughly 15-20 basis points of HIKES priced by next April, a full reversal from the cuts that were in the curve a month ago (Deutsche Bank)

This morning's PPI is the eighth straight hot print. Energy is the spark with gasoline +28.4% year-over-year (the steepest since July 2022), but the spread into core services and food is the part that cannot be hand-waved as oil-driven noise. The 8-4 FOMC vote at the last meeting was the most dissents since 1992, the Committee was fractured before this PPI dropped, and the path of least resistance is for the curve to keep re-pricing.

The opposite read: the consumer is wobbling. PNC's May sentiment survey ticked lower, revolving credit growth has slowed, and small-cap names are catching a bid on slowdown hopes. That tension matters because the Fed has cover to look through energy if growth genuinely rolls over. But a 4.6% core CPI annualized pace with shelter still in BLS catch-up mode is not energy noise. Long-end Treasuries cannot rally meaningfully into the 30-year refunding next week, particularly with the UK gilt curve breaking out to 5.10% (the highest since 2008) and dragging global term premium higher. Short $TLT into next week's FOMC minutes.

### The Iran war is a multi-quarter supply story, not a one-week headline trade

*The IEA said overnight that global oil supply will plunge below demand this year because of the Iran war, that does not unwind on a single ceasefire memo.*

- JPMorgan's 3Q26 Brent forecast sits at $104 per barrel, premised on continued Strait of Hormuz disruption and refined-fuel inventory rebuilds
- The national gasoline average breached $4.50 per gallon on Monday, a level JPMorgan flags as the threshold where higher pump prices start offsetting fiscal tailwinds to consumption
- Vietnam is importing more fuel to offset its oil shortfall and India is rolling out emergency measures to shield its economy from the oil shock (Reuters, overnight), the supply scarcity is now showing up in country-level demand-side responses
- Copper +14.4% since May 31 (outpacing the SPX by 84 basis points) and Goldman has broadened commodity exposure, the war-premium trade is spreading across the commodity complex not just oil
- Goldman's commodity desk: "Cooling is critical to data center operations, and water use is increasingly under scrutiny" the AI build adds a structural demand layer on top of the war premium

The Hormuz disruption shrinks deliverable supply, the war footing forces rerouting and inventory drawdowns, and the IEA's overnight call confirms the supply hole has months to refill regardless of headlines. The longer the disruption persists the more the curve back-fills with scarcity premium rather than mean-reverting on resolution chatter.

The pushback case is that Trump just said publicly "no need for China's help on Iran" and is heading to Beijing for the long-rumoured Xi meeting, if a deal lands the futures complex snaps back fast. Risk-on assets are already partially priced for a constructive readout. The Trump-China visit is a real two-way risk this week, but a 6-week ceasefire does not refill refined-fuel inventories that are at 8-year lows. Western Asset's framing holds: "The Iran Risk Premium Isn't Gone". Long $BNO outright at $57.
