# Hormuz reflares

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI capex is no longer a forecast — earnings are confirming the build

*The hyperscaler infrastructure story has moved from thesis to arithmetic, and four of the biggest sell-side desks are pointing the same way.*

- Anthropic hit $30B ARR as of April, an 80x increase year-over-year, with a SpaceX partnership to use Colossus 1 (Goldman)
- Datadog reported +31% afterhours Wednesday on cloud infrastructure software strength, dragging Snowflake and MongoDB with it
- AMD's Q1 raised the server CPU addressable market growth forecast above 35% annually through 2030; Arm could capture 40-45% server share by 2030 (LSEG, Goldman)
- S&P 500 Q1 EPS growth running 24.6% YoY, the fastest in four years; Deutsche Bank raised 2026 EPS to $342 and stuck with a year-end 8000 target
- Institutional flows in tech are 2-3x the year-to-date average; long-only money is rotating into AI infrastructure and taking profits in memory (Goldman)

The signal is the breadth of names participating, not any single print. Tier-1 desks (Goldman, JPMorgan, UBS) plus dedicated capex-watcher commentary at TME all point to revenue lines confirming what guidance promised through 2025. When Anthropic's ARR multiplies 80x while Datadog's software-side print fires +31% on the same theme, that is the second wave landing — not a forecast about it. Q1 EPS growth at 24.6% YoY is the fastest pace in four years, with all 11 S&P sectors positive for the first time in four years.

The bear case is structural rather than cyclical. Goldman's One-Delta desk has flagged déjà vu to the 2000 tech bubble, with SOX 14-day RSI above 80 (a level rarely seen in 30 years) and the SOX 56% above its 200-day moving average. The top 10 NDX names over the last year are up 784% on average — that is above the dot-com peak comparable. SanDisk alone is up 3960% over twelve months. The risk that resolves a bubble is that frontier AI gains become incremental while open-source narrows the gap, collapsing compute economics as supply outstrips demand. That is the right risk to price, not "is the rally too narrow," because the rally is not narrow. The strongest expressions remain the picks-and-shovels names where capex visibility runs into 2027 — Applied Materials and Tokyo Electron are sold out into 2027, so the cycle has more tape to run before any guide-down lands. Long $NVDA into the print.

### The rate-cut path is the real argument and NFP at 8:30 is the next test

*Three sell-side desks reach three different answers on Fed direction, and this morning's print decides which one is closest.*

- UBS sees two Fed cuts in 2H26, calling current "no cut" pricing too hawkish; UBS also raised May headline CPI to peak at 4.44% on gasoline pass-through (April core CPI estimate 0.37% vs Bloomberg consensus 0.33%)
- TS Lombard argues a "Warsh Fed" cuts the funds rate but tightens through the repo channel, which is a cosmetic cut, with inflation expected to exceed 3% in 2027
- The Market Ear flags 30-year Treasuries at 5% as the "Maginot line," with Bank of America's Hartnett warning a durable break opens the "door to doom" for stocks
- Citi forecasts -15k NFP versus consensus +65k; JPMorgan and Natixis are at 50k; UBS at 100k — the dispersion is the trade
- Market is pricing 20-25% probability of a Fed HIKE in 2026, which Citi rates strategists call excessive

NFP today is the catalyst that resolves the disagreement, at least directionally. A weak print (the Citi/JPM/Natixis camp) cuts against the hawkish repricing in the curve and rewards rate-sensitive duration; a stronger print (the UBS scenario) keeps the door open to the no-cut market pricing surviving another month. The dispersion in forecasts — from -15k all the way to +100k — is the cleanest signal that the data has stopped being noise and started being decisive.

The bear case for long duration here is the inflation side: Bloomberg's commodity-versus-Treasury ratio is signaling re-accelerating inflation regardless of the Iran outcome, with 5-10 year breakevens up 40-50bps over the past month, and rising fertilizer proxies (urea, ammonia) flagging upcoming pressure on US food CPI. UBS's own May headline CPI projection of 4.44% is the kind of number that keeps the Fed boxed in. The defense is that rates are too high for current data — a soft NFP this morning lands directly into that argument and you get a fast move in $TLT before the curve recalibrates. Long $TLT into NFP, sized for the dispersion in the consensus rather than the consensus itself.

### Hormuz peace deal cracks overnight

*The market priced 11% downside in Brent on a memo Iran was still reviewing. Strikes on Bandar Abbas in the last twelve hours invalidate the framework before it was signed.*

- US military carried out strikes on Iran's Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas on May 7 evening; Iran accused the US of violating the ceasefire (Reuters, multiple wires)
- Brent had fallen as low as $96 earlier in the week on the 14-point MoU framework, before stabilizing around $100 (ING)
- OPEC production fell to 20.55 million b/d in April, the lowest level since 1990, on Iran and Kuwait disruptions (ING)
- US commercial crude inventories drew 2.3 million barrels last week; OECD product stocks remain well below five-year averages (SEB)
- Even if a deal is signed, physical Strait of Hormuz transit takes weeks-to-months to resume due to mine-sweeping and stranded crews; Iran has demanded $270B in reparations as part of the framework (SEB)
- Polymarket probability of Strait of Hormuz normalization is the cleanest tradeable proxy for resolution timing (SEB)

The cleanest read across the corpus is that the physical setup never matched the financial setup. SEB's note made the case that the MoU could be signed and oil would still take months to flow because mine-sweeping and crew rotations are physically slow. Mizuho's strategist explicitly recommended fading peace-deal optimism, calling the structural concessions (Hormuz access plus nuclear facilities) too steep for Iran to actually accept. Bloomberg's market-based inflation gauges flagged the same thing — longer-dated breakevens reflecting a structural shock, not a transient one. The overnight strikes vindicate that skepticism: even if peace returns next week, refined-fuel inventories at multi-year lows mean the supply hole takes months to refill regardless of the headline cycle.

The risk that breaks this trade is a hard, durable ceasefire announced in the next 48 hours combined with Iran fast-tracking the stockpile transfer — which would compress oil through the headline channel even if the physical scarcity persists. That requires Iran accepting reparations terms and Hormuz access concessions in the current proposal, which Mizuho describes as "structurally difficult to achieve." Long $BNO captures the Brent leg of the supply scar without the WTI distortion.

### Momentum unwind = the rotation tell, not a buy-the-dip

*Goldman's High Beta Momentum factor fell 8% on Wednesday — a top-ten one-day move over the last five years — driven by both legs unwinding simultaneously.*

- GSPRHIMO down 8% on May 7, marking one of the top ten one-day moves in five years (Goldman)
- The unwind hit both legs: software outperformed earnings expectations while secular AI themes sold off after extreme run
- Momentum positioning sits at the 99th percentile on a one-year basis and the 100th percentile on a five-year basis in the GS Prime Book
- Long-only investors showed "capitulatory stop-ins" in semiconductor and AI names following a 50% rally since early April
- High call-volume signals in MU, INTC, and SNDK exceeding $SPY ETF on certain sessions (TME) — speculative chase has compressed the upside path

Goldman's prime-brokerage data shows the unwind was symmetrical — long-only money shifted out of memory and into AI infrastructure on the same tape, while the high-momentum shorts ripped because positioning was crowded. That is a rotation, not a top: when the long and the short legs of a momentum book unwind together at extreme positioning, what you typically get is a redistribution into the higher-conviction names rather than a clean pullback. The historical pattern Goldman cites — aggressive momentum unwinds preceding "buy the dip" opportunities for top themes — applies when the underlying earnings story stays intact, which it has.

The pushback is that 99th-100th percentile positioning is exactly the setup that produces a real correction when the macro catalyst lands. A weak NFP plus the Hormuz reflare plus the SOX RSI above 80 is the combination that takes positioning down hard, not the kind of micro-shake that produces a buy. Even granting that, the LO rotation under the surface is the more durable signal — money is moving from speculative memory ($SNDK +3960% YTD) into platform AI exposure with cash flow attached. Long $MSFT or $GOOGL on this rotation, paired with avoiding the most-extended speculative names.

### Gold squeeze setup is rebuilding into a tight tape

*Light positioning, a technical breakout, and a $5900/oz UBS year-end price target — gold has the cleanest right-tail in this multi-asset stack.*

- Gold cleared a short-term downtrend line and a wedge formation; non-commercial net positioning has been in hibernation since February (TME)
- UBS year-end gold price target stands at $5900/oz, citing central bank buying and physical demand
- Gold has recently traded inversely to the VIX, which is unusual; a reconnection of that historical relationship would force a catch-up rally
- The commodity-versus-Treasury bond ratio Bloomberg cites for re-accelerating inflation is also a structural tailwind for gold
- Central bank gold buying remains the pillar of the bid (UBS, World Gold Council)

The case for gold here is not the geopolitical headline; it is the positioning vacuum on top of the structural inflation read. Non-commercial gold positioning has been in hibernation for two months, which means there is a real pool of capital that has not yet expressed a view. Combine that with the Bloomberg market-based inflation gauges signaling re-acceleration, central bank buying that has not paused, and a technical setup that just cleared resistance, and the right-tail is wide open. UBS's $5900/oz target — already on the books — is the directional confirmation from the Tier-1 side.

The bear case is that a successful resolution to the Hormuz situation removes the geopolitical inflation impulse, which would compress gold through the same channel that took oil from $110 to $96 earlier this week. That risk is real but partial: even before the Iran shock, gold was making its own bid story on central bank demand and dollar-debasement narratives, so the geopolitical premium is incremental rather than load-bearing. Long $GLD as a hedge against rate volatility around NFP, with a small allocation against $SPY index exposure if the positioning and inflation story lines up.
