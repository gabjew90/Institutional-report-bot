## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The Hormuz supply hole is still pricing thin

*Fourteen desks reference the Strait of Hormuz as a live supply risk, but only Rabobank is calling it functionally closed — the cross-asset tape is split between scarcity and resolution.*

- Goldman Sachs flags the strait as an ongoing energy-supply disruption risk without taking a directional view, treating it as a backdrop the Brent curve has only partially absorbed
- Deutsche Bank and ING both reference the Middle East situation in their morning desks as an unresolved overhang on the rates and FX block
- ANZ Research and JPMorgan both put it on their macro watchlist as a supply-flow disruption monitoring item rather than a directional trade
- Rabobank is the outlier explicitly skeptical that flows resume in the near term, which is the only conviction call in the cluster
- The Market Ear's chart pack ties Bitcoin weakness and oil strength to the same Hormuz-driven scarcity bid that hit overnight (bitcoin battered, black gold bid)

Fourteen banks naming the same risk without staking a clean directional call is itself a positioning signal. The desks are watching, refined-fuel inventories in Europe are tight enough that Goldman's commodity team has flagged jet-fuel runs out by June if shipping disruption persists, and physical Brent is still pricing a scarcity premium that the futures curve has discounted. The bull case is more boring than people want it to be: the supply hole is real, refilling takes months even if a ceasefire holds tomorrow, and the curve hasn't repriced for the months-long inventory rebuild. The bear case Rabobank is implicitly arguing the other side of — that paper Brent's quick re-rating on every diplomatic headline means the move fades fast on resolution — runs into the same physical-tightness wall the bull case relies on. The bias here is long $BNO outright into the next Hormuz headline, with the diplomatic-resolution risk acknowledged as the tail.

### AI capex is still ramping, but the bubble call has shown up

*Goldman, UBS, and JPMorgan all want you long the global AI trade; BCA Research has flat-out called it an earnings bubble and The Market Ear is flagging the positioning risk that goes with it.*

- Goldman raised 2026 hyperscaler capex estimates to a new high and called the spending boom "very strong"; JPMorgan keeps the trade on the recommended-long list as the dominant micro tailwind
- UBS notes Alphabet's recent equity raise as a tell that hyperscaler AI capex can no longer be funded purely from cash flow — a regime change from the "self-funded" framing of last year
- BCA Research's report is unambiguous: "The broad conclusion from today's report is that the AI sector is almost certainly in the midst of an earnings bubble"
- The Market Ear flags AI as the source of extreme positioning risk, calling out the crowded long that an air-pocket in chip demand would hit hardest
- One-third of MAG7 profits came from private-equity investment gains rather than core AI revenue, per Goldman's own desk — earnings are more cyclically vulnerable than the headline suggests

Two real conviction reads sit on top of each other in the corpus. The supply side of the AI buildout — $AVGO printed a solid quarter and guide-up Tuesday afterhours, $TSM remains sold out through 2026 — keeps the picks-and-shovels names paid through the next several earnings cycles regardless of what the bear case does. The bear case here: if hyperscaler capex slows even modestly while the AI bubble framing takes hold in positioning, the demand-side names ($NVDA, $AMD) lose multiple before fundamentals turn. The Anthropic-Google partnership and the equity-funding regime change are confirming the bull case at the infrastructure layer while the BCA bubble call is naming the risk at the multiple layer. The bias is long memory and infrastructure ($MU, $AVGO) over CPU-exposed names, with $NVDA traded around the print rather than held through.

### When the consensus is short NVDA, rotate the playbook

*A six-bank cluster of contrarian notes — Goldman, BCA, BofA, Crédit Agricole, UBS, and The Market Ear — is explicitly warning the AI lead trade is set up for a fade.*

- Goldman's Pasquariello note "Peak Growth?" flags rotation rather than continuation as the next theme; BofA's Hartnett Flow Show is on the wealth-effect cycle with a top-of-cycle framing
- BCA Research, in addition to the bubble call, argues a broader macro pricing reset; The Market Ear's "Markets at a Crossroads" piece is calling explicit-top signals
- Crédit Agricole's macro pricing piece pairs with UBS's longer-term investment themes call to argue the AI concentration risk is now the primary equity-market vulnerability
- BofA Global Research has an outright equity-overvaluation note in the corpus; The Market Ear flags a chart pattern not seen outside 1987 pre-crash and recession rebounds
- Multiple desks reference passive-investing concentration as the mechanism that makes the rotation, if it comes, faster than people expect

The contrarian read is not "short tech tomorrow" — it is "the cleanest expression of the rotate-out trade is owning the average S&P stock instead of the cap-weighted top." Six banks reaching the same conclusion through different mechanisms (positioning, valuation, charts, macro pricing) is the corpus equivalent of a flashing yellow light. The bull case against this view: until earnings revisions roll over, the AI concentration trade just keeps grinding higher because the index has nowhere else to lean. The defense: if the rotation does begin, equal-weight $RSP outperforms cap-weighted $SPY for the same reason it usually does — the breadth tax stops being paid. Pair this with the defensive utility tilt $XLU has been quietly building, since both legs of the rotation work if the bubble call lands. The bias is long $RSP over $SPY, paired with long $XLU as the defensive complement.

### Labor market is cooling into the May print

*Most desks see US hiring losing steam heading into Friday's NFP, with MUFG the lone voice for solid momentum continuing.*

- Goldman's May Payrolls Preview pegs the print below consensus, citing the cooling labor-market indicators that ran through Q2
- Deutsche Bank's Early Morning Reid frames the May print as the next test of whether the labor market has actually stalled or just paused
- UniCredit explicitly calls the cooling signal "softening" and ties it to the rate-cut path the curve has been pricing
- UBS goes harder: uncertainty has "frozen" US labor market activity per their note, which is the bearish read
- MUFG's NFP preview is the contrarian, calling for solid hiring momentum to continue and the headline to print at or above consensus; Crédit Agricole's preview lands somewhere in the middle

A 4-1 vs MUFG split with most desks on softening is the real signal. The bull case for a hot print is MUFG's read that hiring momentum is still constructive; the path to that print is small business and services hiring holding up. The defense for the soft-print view: jobless claims have crept higher for six straight weeks, the cooling indicators Goldman and Deutsche cite include the staffing-sector data that has historically led NFP, and UBS's "frozen" framing is the more honest read of what employer behavior has actually looked like in surveys. A miss tomorrow accelerates the Fed-cut path that's been priced out; a beat extends the hawkish reaction function the next theme covers. The bias is long $TLT puts into the print as the asymmetric expression — if MUFG is right, the move is sharp.

### The Fed's reaction function has shifted hawkish

*Goldman's 1-Delta desk, BofA, Crédit Agricole, ING, and SEB all flag the Fed has gotten more hawkish in how it responds to data — but no desk is staking a clear directional bet on what it means.*

- Goldman Sachs flags an explicit shift: "Fed's Reaction Function Has Shifted Hawkish" — the bar for cuts is higher than the curve has been pricing
- BofA Global Research's macro view ties the shift to the labor-market cooling pattern, arguing the Fed will tolerate softer hiring before easing
- Crédit Agricole's May employment preview is built on the assumption that the Fed needs more than one soft print to move
- ING's morning desk frames the hawkish reaction-function shift as the binding constraint on the Treasury curve
- SEB's macro piece references the same reaction-function shift in the context of the European rate path divergence

If the curve is pricing two cuts in 2H and the Fed's reaction function has actually moved tighter than that, the rates trade is short duration into the next softer print, not long. The bull case for the dovish read: the cooling labor data eventually forces the Fed's hand regardless of what the reaction function says, and the curve usually leads the dots in either direction. The defense for the hawkish reaction-function read: the dot-by-dot reaction-function shift is what determines the path from any given data point, and the Fed has explicitly named energy-driven inflation as something they will look through only briefly. The bias is short $TLT into the next CPI print, with the falsifiable window being: if July CPI core prints below 3.5% sustained, the thesis is dead and the trade flips.
