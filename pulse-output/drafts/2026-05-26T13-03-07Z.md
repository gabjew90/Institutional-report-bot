## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hedge fund leverage at three-year fastest pace into AI

*Hedge funds have doubled down on the AI trade right when the smart-money positioning data says the room is full.*

- Tech gross/net exposures hit the 100th percentile as a share of US prime book — a 5-year high (Goldman) — anyone who needed to be long AI already is
- US long-short gross leverage jumped +5.5 points in a single week, the fastest re-leveraging pace in more than three years (Goldman) — that's the unwind risk, not a fresh bull signal
- Hedge fund momentum-factor tilt sits at the 90th percentile (Goldman) — the same crowd is on the same side of the same trade
- BofA flagged a +853bp hedge fund net tilt toward IT entering Q2 2026, the largest quarterly increase on record (The Market Ear) — institutional buying is no longer marginal, it's a stampede
- 25% of S&P 100 stocks have inverted call skew (CBOE) — calls now cost more than puts in a quarter of large-caps, the same single-stock-gamma demand that defined 2021

Three forces compound here. Earnings revisions are accelerating (Jefferies' revisions ratio sits at the 94th percentile, 34% of companies are guiding higher per The Market Ear) so the longs have fundamental cover. The AI capex print is real — Goldman's TMT desk has hyperscaler capex still rising into 2026, ASML is hiring 1,000 more engineers in Taiwan, NAND revenue surged 83.7% QoQ to $38.9B (TrendForce) — so the spend is showing up in the supply chain. And buybacks are running at 1.6x last year's pace with $95.2B of new authorizations last week alone (Birinyi). That's the bull case for staying long.

The opposite read is positioning itself. 100th-percentile tech exposure means the next marginal dollar coming into AI has to come from someone who is not yet long, and there is nobody left in that bucket. Goldman flags AAII bull-bear at NEGATIVE on the same day prime-book tech is at a 5-year high — a not-loved bull rally where the sponsorship is professional, not retail. The 7th-percentile social-media sentiment reading (JPM) confirms it: this is consensus money buying, and consensus money runs for the door together.

The bias here is long quality tech ($NVDA, $MSFT, $GOOGL) paired with downside hedges in $QQQ — calls being expensive to puts inside the index means index puts are the cheap leg of the structure.

### Bond market still pricing hikes — JPM thinks it's the wrong call

*The OIS curve is pricing a Fed hike, not a cut, and JPM is calling that misframe.*

- Market-implied OIS rate-hike probability rose to 75%, up from 62% the prior week (JPMorgan) — the curve is moving the wrong way for risk assets, fast
- US 12-month forward P/E sits at 21x, which JPM calls stretched especially against real yields (JPMorgan) — when long-term yields rise to compensate for inflation, the equity multiple has to compress
- 5% on the US 10-year is JPM's pain threshold — historically the level that triggers concerns over economic and equity-valuation damage (JPMorgan) — we are below that line, but not by much
- US Low-Vol basket is down 6% from February highs (JPMorgan) — the bond-proxy equity bid has been unwinding alongside yields
- Franklin Templeton has 2-year Treasury yield at 4.10%, above fed funds — the 2-year is leading the Fed, and it is leading higher, not lower

Two stories are happening at once. Western Asset has shifted its end-2026 Fed rate expectation to 3% (away from a deeper cutting cycle) — the cut path is being repriced in real time. At the same time the inflation backdrop is grinding higher: Crédit Agricole has US core PCE forecast at 3.3% YoY (up from 3.2%) and Eurozone core HICP at 2.6% (up from 2.2%). The combination is what is pulling the OIS curve toward hikes — a Fed that cannot cut into re-accelerating inflation has to let the front end drift up.

The pushback worth taking seriously is that the OIS move is partially mechanical — repo and Treasury supply are squeezing the front of the curve, not pure rate-expectation repricing. Even granting that, the trade-relevant point is that the equity multiple at 21x forward earnings is priced for the cuts that the OIS market is no longer pricing. If the 10-year breaks 4.5% sustained, the picture changes for multiple-sensitive growth names first.

The bias is long Low-Volatility stocks (the JPM dispersion call) and tactically light on high-beta cyclicals — the trade is positioned for a stable-or-falling-yields regime, not the hike-pricing regime the curve is currently in.

### Hormuz overhang sits unresolved, but the supply hole takes months to refill

*The Iran/Hormuz situation is a live two-way headline risk, but the physical oil market is operating with a 12.8m bpd hole regardless.*

- 12.8m bpd of global oil supply has been lost since February, 12% of pre-war supply (UBS) — this number does not move on a US-Iran headline, it moves on physical resolution
- 50-60% rise in WTI and Brent since Middle East hostilities erupted (Rothschild & Co) — the price has already done the work; the question is whether the supply hole closes or widens from here
- Brent currently around $97.9, down from $103.5 last Friday (SEB) — a 50% retracement from the end-April peak (Crédit Agricole has Brent at $98 1st contract) suggests headline risk is partially priced out
- 67% of global crude moves by sea; 62% of that goes through key chokepoints (JPMorgan) — the structural vulnerability is the spine of the JPM Port Authority piece
- Goldman's scenario projection: SPX dips 3% if war ends AND AI enthusiasm dissipates simultaneously (Goldman) — the two tails are linked in the same trade

Six banks reference US-Iran diplomatic negotiations as a live catalyst (Barclays, Crédit Agricole, Goldman, JPM, SEB, UBS). None of them is making a directional call on whether a deal lands. That is the signal: the corpus is unwilling to underwrite either side of the binary. JPM's framing is the one with the most actionable specifics — a Hormuz Resolution trade in airlines, autos, homebuilders, lodging, retailers if a memo of understanding lands; a continued bid in energy/defense if it doesn't. Rothschild & Co frames it as a 50-60% Brent rally already in the price, meaning the asymmetric setup runs the other way now — a ceasefire-driven Brent drop is more violent than a continued-conflict Brent grind.

The case for staying long oil exposure is physical: refined fuel inventory tightness does not reset on a diplomatic headline. UBS estimates the Iranian tariff per tanker shipment at USD 2 million — an economic rounding error globally — meaning a partial resumption does not reset the price either; the supply hole has to close before the trade reverses. The bear case for $BNO / energy is exactly the headline binary nobody wants to own.

The bias here is small long $XLE or $BNO — sized for the supply-not-headline thesis — paired with a small long $XLY position as the Hormuz Resolution hedge into a ceasefire scenario. The trade NOT to do: pair $BNO vs $USO without sizing, since daily roll noise dominates the dislocation thesis on the daily timeframe.

### Software is the most-shorted, most-hated trade — setup for a squeeze

*Software shorts are at a 6-year high while the group trades at a 39% valuation discount — the asymmetry is mathematical.*

- Software short interest sits at the 100th percentile relative to the past 6 years (Goldman) — the room to cover is enormous
- Software group valuation discount: 39% below the trailing 5-year average (Morgan Stanley) — even adjusting for growth, software is 29% cheap to history (Morgan Stanley)
- Buyback execution flow is +40% vs the prior week, with 1.6x year-to-date pace vs 2025 ADTV (Goldman) — corporate bid is back, and software names disproportionately use buybacks vs dividends
- Discretionary order share is +40% (Goldman) — active money is the marginal buyer, not passive flow, which means single-name selection matters more than index participation
- $NVDA, $MSFT, $GOOGL still anchor index-AI exposure; the software discount represents the underweight name that has not yet been bid

The setup is classic mean-reversion-into-squeeze. The fundamentals have not collapsed — Barclays raised $TENB to $28 (from $27), Elastic ($ESTC) is on a 15% revenue-growth path Q4, $BRZE has its 1Q27 print as the next data point. The crowd has been on the wrong side of software for long enough that any catalyst — a guide-up at earnings, a software-specific M&A print like the $DHERO bid (€38/share from Uber, up from rejected €33) — forces covering.

The smart counter-case: software is cheap because the AI capex flowing through hyperscalers is not landing in vertical SaaS. The risk is that the discount widens as semis get the capex dollars and software keeps getting the funding-cuts. Goldman's GSXULOWQ basket projects 20% downside in a higher-for-longer rates environment, and software low-quality is the heart of that basket.

The bias here is long $IGV (the software ETF) as the cleanest expression of the short-cover thesis, with single-name exposure in profitable software names ($CRM, $ADBE, $MANH) over speculative ones. Skip the unprofitable software bucket.

### Gold trimmed at UBS, but central-bank demand is the floor

*UBS just cut its gold forecast to USD 5,500/oz — and even with the cut, the bull case is intact because central banks are still buying.*

- USD 5,500/oz: UBS year-end 2026 and 1H27 Gold price forecast (down USD 200-400/oz from previous) — the trim is a fair-value reset, not a regime change
- 536 metric tons: Q1 2026 investment demand (UBS) — held up even with prices near record highs
- 244 metric tons: Q1 2026 central bank purchases (UBS) — surprised positively, central banks are still net buyers
- 15% India gold import duty (raised from 6%) — physical demand creates its own supply elasticity
- AI hyperscaler capex equal to a meaningful share of MAG7 operating cash flow (Rothschild & Co flags 2026 AI capex at $0.5-1 trillion, hitting $1T in 2027) — gold's relevance as a non-USD reserve asset is rising as the US runs a structural deficit to fund the buildout

What is happening with gold is that the marginal buyer changed. Investment demand softened recently — UBS notes that explicitly — but central-bank flows did not. Central-bank demand is not interest-rate sensitive in the way ETF demand is; it is reserve-policy driven. As the AI capex cycle forces persistent US fiscal expansion (and as US-Iran tensions push reserve diversification into the conversation), the central-bank bid is structural.

The bear case is that gold has done a lot of work and the rate-cut narrative is pricing out — Western Asset's 3% end-2026 Fed rate vs prior deeper cuts means real yields rise, gold's negative correlation kicks in. That is real, but UBS has already priced it: the forecast cut is the acknowledgment. The structural floor is the central-bank flow.

The bias is small long $GLD as a portfolio hedge, with exposure scaled to the dollar-USD-Iran headline path — bigger if a Hormuz escalation accelerates de-dollarization conversations, smaller if a ceasefire memo lands.
