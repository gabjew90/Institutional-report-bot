# Momentum unwind hits

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The dominant story overnight and into today's open is a violent unwind in the momentum trade rather than a fresh directional narrative. Systematic long/short hedge funds took their worst single-day loss in more than two years, semiconductors and the AI leadership cohort led the drawdown, and the global momentum factor moved 4.7 standard deviations to the downside. Around that, cross-desk research is anchored on two threads: a Treasury that has just doubled its long-dated bond buybacks and what that does to the dollar and to gold, and the run of "everyone owns the same thing" AI capex names finally getting tested. FOMC minutes from the July meeting printed yesterday afternoon and desks read them as tilting toward higher-for-longer, so front-end pricing is grinding out the last of the near-term cuts. Moderna sits on top of all of it after the melanoma vaccine data drove a 175% single-day move, the largest one-day S&P 500 gain of the century.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Systematic funds hit their worst day in two years

*A crowded semiconductor and AI momentum trade cracked hard enough to force systematic hedge funds into their biggest single-day loss since 2024, and Goldman reads it as a positioning unwind rather than a regime change.*

- The global momentum factor moved 4.7 standard deviations to the downside as of 1pm ET August 19, a range where the model would only expect to be once every several years (Goldman)
- Systematic long/short managers lost 1.4% on the day, their worst daily performance in more than two years, with 60 basis points of the drop coming from the momentum factor alone (Goldman)
- The Goldman TMT momentum long/short pair fell 9% over two consecutive sessions and roughly 17% peak-to-trough, semis carrying most of the pain (Goldman Callahan and Contessa desks)
- High-beta momentum has already had twenty separate one-day 5% drawdowns in 2026, more than the last five years combined (Goldman Duttenhoefer)
- The overlap between the 12-month winners cohort and the 3-month winners cohort sits at multi-year lows, meaning the "momentum" bucket funds were selling holds names that have not actually been leading recently (Goldman)

Two things are happening at once, and they need to be separated. The first is mechanical. When a factor prints a move that far outside its historical distribution, systematic strategies whose risk models size positions off recent volatility are forced to cut, which sells the same names harder, which extends the move. That is the loop that made this a systematic drawdown, not a fundamental one. The second is that the composition of "momentum" has quietly shifted, so a fund that owned the 12-month winners was already unhedged against the 3-month winners rolling over. Semiconductors were on both sides of that trade and took the concentrated hit.

The pushback here is that a 4.7 standard deviation factor move usually is the regime change, not just a shakeout, and the two-session 17% pair drawdown is the kind of damage that changes how the next quarter's positioning is built. Goldman's own tension note puts a specific breakpoint on it: if the divergence between 3-month and 12-month momentum bleeds into the 6-month window, this stops being a positioning event and starts being a leadership rotation. That 6-month bleed is the level to watch. Goldman's desk read is that the market has already lived through twenty of these one-day 5% momentum breaks this year and none has held, so the base case is a bounce in the leadership names inside a week. Nvidia earnings after the close on August 26 is the print that decides which read wins.

### Treasury doubles the long-bond backstop

*The Treasury just doubled how much long-dated debt it buys back each operation, and the split on Wall Street is whether that actually pins down long-term yields or just weakens the dollar to pay for it.*

- Treasury raised each buyback operation by $2 billion, implying roughly $72 billion of additional annual purchases and $144 billion in total annual long-end buybacks (The Market Ear)
- Treasury bills are now 22% of outstanding US debt, well above the post-2015 norm — the government has been leaning heavily on short-term borrowing to avoid selling long bonds into weak demand (The Market Ear, TS Lombard)
- Net duration supply for 2026 is running around $550 million per basis point of rate move, down from $750 million, largely because these buybacks are pulling long bonds out of dealer inventory (The Market Ear)
- Hyperscaler bond issuance is on track to push their gross duration supply from around $215 million per basis point to $350 million per basis point in 2026, so private long-end supply is filling the gap Treasury is trying to close (The Market Ear)
- 10-year Treasury yield sat at 4.637% at yesterday's close, and 22 basis points of Fed hikes are now priced by December, the lowest since June's FOMC (JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank)

Citi, Barclays, Goldman and Deutsche Bank all read the buyback expansion the same way at the mechanical level: it takes duration out of the market and signals the Treasury is uncomfortable with long-term yields near 5%. The transmission runs through supply. Every long bond the Treasury buys back is one dealers do not have to warehouse, which loosens the supply-demand pressure at the long end. That is why yields dropped on the announcement.

Where the split opens is durability. Citi and Goldman think the signal is strong enough to hold the long end, arguing Treasury has communicated that it will step in when yields press higher. JPMorgan and Bloomberg push back that the operations are too small versus total long-dated issuance to matter over more than a few sessions, so today's actions will have a fleeting impact on long-end yields. Deutsche Bank and TS Lombard sit in a third camp: the buybacks work, but the mechanism is dollar weakness, because supporting bonds this way lets the currency become the release valve. That is why gold has moved on the announcement rather than the dollar. Barclays is the specific bearish comparison: Japan's experience shows issuance cuts provide only temporary relief and do not address the underlying problem. Citi's own tension note names two long-end yield triggers as the invalidation. Their 30-year payer-spread trade breaks if the 30-year Treasury yield exceeds 4.823%, and their 20-year stop is set at a 20-year yield of 5.4%. Wednesday August 26 delivers the Q2 GDP second estimate (advance print came in at 6.5%, and the second estimate rarely stays at that level) and the July core PCE inflation number, and both are the next hard tests of the buyback backstop.

### The AI capex trade breaks into three pieces

*The money still flowing into AI infrastructure is real, but the pulse in the research is that the debt-funded end is where the risk lives and the picks-and-shovels end is where the survivors are.*

- Q2 2026 cloud revenue growth for the major hyperscalers averaged 48%, up from 40% in Q1 2026 — the demand side of the story is still accelerating (UBS)
- Hyperscaler bond issuance concentration means these names now anchor a growing share of investment-grade credit, and UBS estimates roughly $500 billion of debt issuance from AI-related firms in 2026 alone
- Hyperscaler gross duration supply is projected to rise from $215 million per basis point to $350 million per basis point in 2026, so the capex is landing directly on the long end of the curve
- Systematic short-covering flowed into power and utilities names (VST, CEG, XLU) as the derivative trade of AI compute demand
- SQM guided FY26 lithium sales to 280 to 290 thousand tons and reaffirmed $3 billion of capex through 2028, with $300 million per year of that just sustaining the base business (Goldman Sachs)

The bull side is UBS and J. Safra Sarasin: AI is a very significant growth driver, the money flowing into power and energy came first because compute needs the electricity before the racks land, and second-derivative trades in utilities and grid infrastructure have already begun. The skeptical side, led by JPMorgan on the fiscal-crowding angle and echoed by Deutsche Bank on debt sustainability, is that the same $500 billion of AI-firm debt issuance is now crowding out sovereign borrowing, and hyperscaler credit is starting to trade like a separate asset class. JPMorgan and Deutsche Bank frame the same $500 billion issuance as a fiscal pressure vector; UBS frames it as a valid capital allocation into a growth cycle. That is the split: strong revenue growth on one side, a growing borrowing bill on the other.

The pushback the desks flag is that a single guide-down from META or GOOGL on their capex trajectory would flip the narrative overnight, because the whole picks-and-shovels bid is built on those two names' commitments staying in place. Nvidia earnings after the close on August 26 is the first live check.

### The gold and silver trade the Treasury just handed a bid

*The Treasury's long-end buyback expansion looks less like a bond story and more like a gold story, because the release valve for supporting bonds without tightening is dollar weakness, and gold has already told you that.*

- Gold and silver 3-month daily-return correlation is running at 0.9, a multi-year high, so this is a coupled precious-metals move (The Market Ear)
- Central bank net gold purchases hit 51 tons in June, and China's PBOC added 20 tons of gold reserves in July, its largest monthly increase since October 2023 (The Market Ear)
- Gold spot resistance sits at $4,500 per ounce, the level analyst-flagged as necessary to sustain the technical breakout (The Market Ear)
- BofA highlighted a September GDX 95/110 call spread as the vehicle for the gold miner leverage, and the recommendation is now flagged as exploding to the upside with a suggestion to roll into higher strikes
- JPMorgan flags Long Gold as a longer-term high-conviction position, framed as debasement trade + central-bank buying

If the Treasury is going to double its long-end buybacks and the Fed is not going to tighten to offset the easing that creates, the currency has to take the strain. That is the debasement mechanic and it is why gold caught the bid on the announcement rather than the dollar rallying. Central banks are already telling you the same thing by adding physical gold at a pace that has not softened.

The counter-case is $4,500 resistance. Gold has to clear and hold that level for the trade to survive; a failure to close above $4,500 is the invalidation the desks are watching. Silver is the higher-beta expression through the correlation, but silver still sits under the major downtrend line from the 2020 mania highs and continues to lag gold on any pullback. BofA has the GDX September 95 to 110 call spread on their sheet and is recommending rolling into higher strikes on strength.

### Moderna delivers the biggest single-day S&P gain of the century

*The Moderna melanoma vaccine trial cleared its primary endpoint by a margin that pulled a 177% single-day move out of a stock the market had left for dead, and it reshapes what a biotech catalyst is worth.*

- MRNA rose 175% on August 19, its biggest single-day move on record and the largest one-day S&P 500 gain this century (Goldman Sachs, The Market Ear)
- The single-day gain added roughly $50 billion of market capitalization
- Moderna's mRNA-4157 combined with Keytruda cut the risk of melanoma recurrence or death by 49% versus Keytruda alone, and cut distant metastasis or death risk by 60%
- Goldman moved to Neutral on the stock after the move given the run

The mechanism is that a phase-3 endpoint of this size, in a disease category that had multiple prior failures, resets both the pipeline value and the platform value of the underlying mRNA technology. A single trial does not repair the demand side of the COVID franchise, but it does reprice the vaccine platform's optionality.

The counter-case is a straight profit-take on a stock that was heavily shorted into the print and now sits with a very different valuation. Goldman's move to Neutral captures that: the analyst thinks the news is priced. The single question the trader has to answer is whether the platform read-through pulls up other mRNA and cancer-vaccine names before the profit-take unwinds, or whether the 177% gap gets sold down into an approval-cycle wait that extends for months.

### Momentum inside momentum has broken

*The systematic momentum trade is not just down, it has restructured, because the stocks that led over 12 months and the stocks that led over 3 months no longer overlap the way they used to.*

- The overlap between 12-month momentum winners and 3-month momentum winners is at multi-year lows (Goldman)
- 3-month versus 12-month momentum has outperformed by 15 percentage points over two sessions, an extreme rotation between short-term and long-term leadership cohorts (Goldman)
- Systematic long/short strategies are still up 16.2% year-to-date through August 18, so this is a drawdown from a strong base rather than year-to-date pain (Goldman)

The mechanism matters here because it changes what the recovery looks like. When the 3-month leaders and the 12-month leaders diverge this far, a systematic fund rebalancing next month buys a materially different portfolio than the one it just sold. That is not a bounce, it is a reshuffle.

The counter-case is that this has happened before this year, twenty times in fact, and each time momentum reasserted within days. The falsifiable question is whether the divergence bleeds into 6-month momentum. If it does, Goldman itself says this stops being a positioning event and becomes a leadership change. If it does not, the base case is a mean-revert in the momentum names inside two weeks.
