# AI capex widens

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The tape is digesting two contradictory threads at once. Earnings season has confirmed the AI infrastructure build-out is real, with hyperscaler capex guides moving higher in real time and capex now eating an entire year of operating cash flow. At the same time, the Strait of Hormuz situation flips between peace-deal optimism and live military clashes between US forces and Iranian assets, with oil prices whipsawing on every headline. Positioning is the hinge: hedge funds are offloading the Magnificent 7 at a record pace while retail buys it, and momentum factors are in the 99th-100th percentile by every Goldman Sachs cut of the data, which is what makes the AI-themes complex vulnerable to a sharp unwind even with the fundamental story intact. The macro debate that frames everything is whether energy-driven inflation will stall the Fed from cutting at all this year, or whether central banks look through the supply shock and start easing as growth softens.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### AI hyperscaler capex is now the entire cash flow

*The AI build-out has stopped being a forecast and is now the constraint on hyperscaler free cash flow.*

- Goldman Sachs put 2026 hyperscaler capex at $755B, up 83% year-on-year, equal to 100% of cash flows from operations
- UniCredit pegs combined 2026 hyperscaler capex at $710-725B, roughly 75% above 2025 and three times 2024 levels
- Meta raised its 2026 capex range to $125-145B (from $115-135B); Alphabet to $180-190B (from $175-185B); Microsoft expects June quarter capex above $40B alone
- Hyperscaler buybacks fell 64% year-on-year in Q1 2026, the funding mechanism is the buyback line
- Hedge funds are offloading the Magnificent 7 at a record pace, with net exposure now at roughly 17% of North American net exposure (Syz)

The shift is mechanical. Operating cash flow at the hyperscaler complex is fully absorbed by data center spend, and the gap is being filled by cutting buybacks (down 64% year-on-year) rather than issuing debt, since net debt to EBITDA across the group is still 0.3x. That is a clean balance sheet handing the entire cash engine to one trade. The earnings-side read-through is that 17% Q1 EPS growth at the S&P 500, the fastest pace in four years (UBS), is heavily levered to this capex doing what it is supposed to do.

The bear case is concrete and lives at Syz, which projects hyperscaler free cash flow falling more than 70% as the spend ramp continues. If that math is right, the picks-and-shovels names get paid through 2027 but the hyperscalers themselves see a multiple compression as the market re-rates them away from cash-return stories. The real-time vulnerability sits one layer down in positioning. Goldman flags AI Complex performance up 35-55% since April with momentum at the 99th-100th percentile, and the global semiconductor long/short ratio at 4x versus the prior peak of 2.5x (JPMorgan) is the kind of crowded read that snaps without warning. The setup leans long $SMH on weakness rather than chasing strength, paired with selective long positions in capex-supplier names like $AMAT and $ANET that do not carry the same crowding risk.

### Markets are pricing rate cuts faster than the energy story allows

*Two cuts by year-end is consensus, but the oil channel into core CPI keeps that bet on a knife edge.*

- UBS puts Fed funds at 3.25% by December 2026, implying two cuts in 2H26
- ING expects rates to be cut more gradually than market pricing suggests, citing structural inflation risk from oil
- April private payrolls printed 123k against the 75k estimate (April NFP private release this morning), with unemployment holding at 4.3%
- Brent above $100/bbl on persistent Hormuz disruption is the binding inflation channel; UBS notes a 47.4% year-on-year surge in oil import price index
- Mizuho flags rates selling off as the market re-prices Iran's leverage upward

The mechanism is straightforward and matters for trade construction. Energy-driven inflation feeds core CPI on the standard 3-month lag, which means a Fed that is leaning toward cuts can be forced to wait if the Strait situation takes another month to resolve. UBS's view is that central banks "look through" oil shocks because inflation expectations remain anchored, which is the case for owning $TLT into the next CPI print on May 12. ING's pushback is that markets have already priced the dovish path, leaving rates exposed to a hot CPI surprise. The April jobs print this morning, with private payrolls beating estimate by 48k, cuts in the same direction, the Fed has less cover for an early cut.

The skeptical read is that two cuts is already in the curve and the trade has limited upside without further data confirmation. Where the bull case bites: positioning in front-end Treasuries is light heading into the print, with the 2-year yield at the high end of its recent range and Citi explicitly recommending receiving front-end USTs into NFP looking for the curve to richen 8bps belly-to-front. The cleanest read in plain language is that the Fed is being given the option to cut by softening labor data (June and July prints will be the tell), but the option is contingent on energy not breaking core inflation. Long $TLT is the way to express it, sized for a slow grind rather than a rapid repricing.

### Apple-Intel foundry chatter is the single-stock story this market is mispricing

*The Apple-Intel foundry deal headlines look like leaks ahead of an actual announcement, and the equity setup is asymmetric.*

- CNBC reported this afternoon Apple is in talks with Intel to source iPhone chips, the kind of leak that rarely surfaces unattached to a real deal flow
- Goldman Sachs flagged Cloudflare's 19% after-hours move on a 20% workforce cut and Q2 guidance miss, the broader software complex is showing dispersion, but $INTC has not run with this story yet
- US semiconductor positioning is at the 4x long/short ratio peak (JPMorgan), but the ratio is concentrated in $NVDA, $AVGO, $AMD, $TSM, not in $INTC
- Memory ETF $DRAM has gathered $5B since its April launch (CNBC), confirming the second-leg flow rotation toward neglected semis names
- The Trump-Xi summit on May 14-15 is expected to be transactional, which removes one of the political overhangs on Intel's foundry pivot story

The mechanism matters, and the story is more than a single rumor. Apple sourcing meaningful iPhone silicon from Intel's US fabs would convert a cost line at Apple into a multi-year revenue stream for Intel, and the timing aligns with the broader US semis-onshoring policy push that gave Intel the CHIPS Act funding in the first place. The read-through is not Intel as a sell-off play but as a structural re-rating candidate, because foundry revenue is sticky in a way Intel's CPU business has not been. Memory inflows into $DRAM are the early signal that capital is rotating toward neglected semis, and Intel sits squarely in that bucket.

The pushback is that Apple-Intel chatter has come and gone before, and Intel's near-term execution risk on the foundry side is real. The Q2 guidance ranges across the chip complex have been mixed, and Cloudflare's brutal after-hours print is a reminder that AI is not a tide that lifts everything. Where the bull case wins: the actual cost of being wrong on Intel call options is small, the cost of missing a confirmed deal is much larger, and option-implied vols have not fully repriced the optionality yet. Long $INTC calls into the Trump-Xi summit window is the cleanest expression, with $SMH as the sector hedge if the broader complex unwinds.

### Hormuz supply scar persists even as headlines cycle

*Brent at $100 holds because physical refining markets are pricing a multi-month supply hole, regardless of which way the diplomatic headlines break this morning.*

- US forces struck two empty Iranian oil tankers this morning per CENTCOM, and Iran seized the Ocean Koi tanker in the Gulf of Oman the same morning
- A suspected oil spill near Iran's Kharg Island export hub appeared on satellite imagery overnight, the largest single export terminal in the country
- SEB notes Brent volatility around $99/bbl with physical supply constraints "severe" due to Strait of Hormuz closure, the dated Brent contract has compressed to the paper market
- Goldman Sachs flagged prompt Brent/WTI down 15% and 13% week-on-week respectively earlier this week, on ceasefire optimism, only to reverse on US-Iran clashes
- Barclays upgraded the US Energy Services industry view to Positive, calling it "the best setup in 20 years" with structurally higher oil prices once the supply shock ends

The mechanism is supply-physical versus headline-paper. The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly 20% of global crude flow, and refined-product inventories have been drawing for weeks (108kt week-on-week ARA refined stock draw, ING). When 20% of global crude flow is interrupted, the missing barrels do not get magically replaced by a single ceasefire announcement, refineries need months to rebuild stocks once flow normalizes. UBS's counter-view is that energy is a transitory shock and central banks will look through it, which justifies the equity rally to fresh S&P 500 highs.

The opposing read: SEB recommends long volatility on the rationale that markets are pricing optimism that has not been signed, and the binary deal-versus-strike outcome favors paying for convexity. Where the bull case holds: every leg of physical-market data shows the stock build is going to take quarters, not days, and the Barclays "best setup in 20 years" call on energy services traces directly to that structural read. The Trump-Xi summit on May 14-15 adds a near-term catalyst that could relieve geopolitical pressure broadly, but the oil bid does not need that to hold. The setup leans long $BNO over short $USO (Brent has the cleaner exposure to the supply scar), with $XLE as the equity complement if the Barclays call gets validated.
