# Credit turns on AI

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The July inflation report landed this morning and the desks had spent the last day arguing it was the wrong thing to watch. What the research is actually focused on is credit. Lenders have started charging the AI buildout a visibly higher price than they charge everyone else, insurance volumes on hyperscaler and chipmaker debt are at record levels, and bond investors have moved from hedging the AI trade to betting against it outright. Equity indices are sitting near highs while professional positioning in tech futures has swung to its most bearish reading since ChatGPT launched, and protection against a selloff is priced near the cheapest levels of the year.

Underneath that, two supply stories keep showing up. Corporate borrowing for data centers is competing with the government for the same pool of buyers, which is pushing up what investors demand to hold long-term bonds and sending gold higher as the escape route. Oil is firmer with Iran talks stalled and Hormuz shipping traffic thinning, though the futures market is still priced as if a deal gets done.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The bond market is repricing AI before the stock market does

*Credit desks are charging AI borrowers a premium that keeps widening, and that bill lands on the equity well before the buildout stops.*

- AI-linked bonds have widened 25 basis points this year against 6 for everything else (Goldman Sachs). A basis point is one hundredth of a percent, and a gap that size means lenders now treat the AI complex as its own riskier asset class rather than as blue-chip tech.
- All 10 of the most-shorted bonds in the investment-grade market are AI names (Goldman Sachs). Bond investors are not hedging this trade at the margin, they are positioned against it directly.
- The cost of insuring Nvidia debt against default widened to roughly 77.5 basis points on Monday as its $500B financing plan came into view (Bloomberg). Nvidia default protection is now an actively traded market view, which it was not a year ago.
- Around $1 trillion of AI lease commitments sit off the balance sheet, out of $1.5 trillion tracked this earnings season (Goldman Sachs). Reported borrowings understate what these companies have already promised to pay.
- Corporate bond supply is up about 65% year over year and running at 12 times the 2015-2024 average, driven by data-center issuance (Nomura). That much new paper competes with the Treasury for the same buyers.

Hyperscaler capex is projected to reach $1.1 trillion by 2027 against cash flow that does not cover it, leaving roughly $400bn to be borrowed (Goldman Sachs), and every round of borrowing raises the price of the next one. Trading volumes in default protection on Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom are up tenfold in a month, which is what it looks like when the marginal buyer of AI risk starts demanding to be paid for it.

The case for funding it anyway is that the spending is showing up in results. CoreWeave and Super Micro both beat and pushed the AI trade back toward record highs, and 25 S&P 500 companies now put an explicit number on their AI revenue (Deutsche Bank, JPMorgan). Investors are willing to lend to a business that is visibly earning. Real revenue does not settle the funding question, because the borrowing is front-loaded and the revenue is not. Goldman's credit desk is telling clients to fade any narrowing of the AI-versus-everything-else gap, on the view that multi-year buildout funding keeps the premium in place, and positioning agrees: professional exposure to Nasdaq futures swung from +$9.9bn to -$11.1bn in one week, the most bearish reading since ChatGPT launched, while the index printed record highs. With Goldman's own panic gauge collapsed below 1 and index protection at the cheapest levels of the year, the bias is owning $QQQ puts rather than chasing the rally.

### Memory: the shortage is real, the easy money is not

*The chip shortage runs into 2027 on both desks' numbers, but the part traders get paid for is nearly banked.*

JPMorgan raised its memory market forecast and has the shortage worsening into 2027 before a modest improvement in 2028, with the upcycle running through the end of 2028. Goldman agrees prices keep rising into mid-2027, then splits from that view: the easy gains from scarcity are already behind us. Memory names have already corrected about 25% this quarter on peak-cycle fear and earnings misses, and the bears have cut the multiple they will pay from 5x to 2-3x, which is a bet against momentum rather than against the shortage. Watch in this order: (1) DRAM contract pricing into the September quarter, where long $MU either keeps working or stops, (2) hyperscaler capex guides for any cut to memory content per server, (3) 2028 supply additions, the first thing that ends the upcycle.

### Gold is the release valve for a bond market losing control

*Gold is trading as the escape hatch from a Treasury market being asked to absorb the government's deficit and the AI buildout at the same time.*

US debt-servicing payments are up 200% in five years (Bloomberg), and the extra yield investors demand for holding long-term bonds is nudging higher even as policy tries to hold it down (TS Lombard). Nomura frames gold as the pressure release valve for exactly that, and systematic trend funds have already flipped from an 18% short to a 15% long, roughly $1.5B of buying, with $5,056 the level that takes their model to full size. Nomura also expects the Treasury to lean issuance toward short-term bills to avoid drowning a long end that is already absorbing data-center debt. Does the long bond break first, or does the Treasury blink and shift to bills? Long $GLD does not need that question answered to work.

### Oil is priced for a deal that has not happened

*The oil market has already booked a Hormuz resolution that the shipping data refuses to confirm.*

Brent sits around $88 with barrels for delivery a year out more than $10 cheaper than barrels today (Deutsche Bank), which is the shape of a market genuinely short of physical supply rather than one bracing for a glut. Three desks keep a Hormuz closure on the risk list without taking a side on it, and Mizuho EMEA extends that watch to the Bab el-Mandeb strait. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest level since 1983 and within weeks of operational minimums, so the usual shock absorber is not there. Cost of being wrong: a deal reopens the strait and oil hands back the risk premium. Cost of missing it if right: a chokepoint closes with no reserve left to tap. Long $BNO outright.
