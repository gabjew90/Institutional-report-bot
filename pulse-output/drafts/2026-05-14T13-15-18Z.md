## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Hormuz holds, the supply scar lasts months

*The street is fading Hormuz headlines into the open, but the bigger problem is what's already locked in.*

- 10.5 million barrels per day of crude shut-in during April per the EIA (SEB Iran chartpack), up from 7.5 million in March. Peak shut-in could hit 10.8 million in May. That's already in the inflation pipeline.
- The EIA now models the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed through May, with 2026 inventory draws of 2.6 million barrels per day (LSEG). Bull case for oil isn't fading.
- Brent sits at $99.7 currently. SEB models a $150-200 Brent if the closure runs through June or July, against an EIA $106 average for May-June. The asymmetry is brutal.
- UBS pegs April headline CPI at 3.7% year-over-year (up from 3.3% in March) on the oil pass-through, and global oil demand revised down to +200k bpd from +600k. Demand destruction is starting.

Three forces are running at once. Iran has shifted from full blockade to "controlled corridor" mode, letting Chinese tankers through under IRGC supervision (this morning's Reuters headline), which the equity tape is fading as de-escalation. The EIA already locked in May at peak shut-in, refined-product inventories in Asia are thin enough that Japan and South Korea are pulling 39.7% more coal-fired generation, and US wheat is set for its lowest production since 1972 partly because fertilizer costs ran on the energy crisis. The "peace deal" framing the market wants from this week's Beijing summit doesn't fix any of that on the timeline.

Skeptics will argue Iran allowing Chinese transit is a meaningful pressure-release valve, and the Trump-Xi meeting could produce a verbal commitment that compresses the geopolitical premium another five to ten dollars per barrel. Granting that fully, the supply hole stays through summer driving season regardless of headlines. Refined-fuel inventories do not refill in weeks, and the third consecutive monthly jump in US energy PPI says the pass-through is already in the system. Bias is long $BNO over the next Hormuz headline cycle, sized small given the headline whip.

The fair skeptic case: Xi-Trump summits historically generate vague joint statements the market fades in three sessions. True historically, but this one has an active geopolitical bid attached. What gets verbally committed on China-Iran-energy this week prices through immediately because the oil curve is on edge and the rare-earth supply chain has a hard delivery deadline this month. Trade is long $SPY straddles into Friday's close, with a risk-defined long $FXI add on a clean bullish break.

### AI capex isn't slowing, Cisco just confirmed it

*The build-out got stronger this week, not weaker.*

- Cisco raised FY26 AI orders from north of $5 billion to $9 billion in one quarter, with product orders accelerating to 35% year-over-year (Goldman TMT, Citi confirms). That is contracted revenue, not aspirational guidance.
- 2026 hyperscaler capex consensus at $755 billion, up 83% versus 2025 (Goldman Sachs). The largest single-year capital build in tech history.
- UBS sees 1Q26 cloud provider revenue growth accelerating to roughly 45% from 34% in 4Q25, with aggregate order backlog supporting 45-50% growth into 2Q26.
- DDR4 8Gb 3200 spot prices ran +20% week-over-week on Samsung union strike risk hitting May 21 (Goldman TMT). Memory tightness is back.
- AI-linked software pricing is up 5% month-over-month and 14% year-over-year. UBS calls it out as a contributor to sticky core inflation, which means the build-out is now showing up in the consumer price data.

What the bears keep missing: the hyperscaler build isn't speculative spending anymore, it's contracted revenue showing up at the network and silicon vendors. Cisco lifted the order book by roughly $4 billion in one quarter. Nebius reported +684% revenue growth and upgraded contracted power to north of 4 gigawatts from 3. Even this week's CPI math has AI-linked software contributing measurably to core inflation. That's monetization showing up in inflation data, not just spend showing up in capex slides.

The fair counter Goldman desk has flagged: one-third of MAG7 profits came from private-equity investment gains rather than AI revenue, leaving earnings more cyclically vulnerable than the headline number suggests. And Bloomberg points to record 0DTE call volumes, dispersion at 64%, correlation at 3%. Those are top-of-cycle markers. Granting both, the picks-and-shovels names like $AMAT, $ASML, and memory ($MU) on the supply side are sold out into 2027 and don't depend on near-term momentum staying intact. Bias is long $SMH outright, paired against keeping $SPY broadly hedged.

### Hidden leverage under the SOXX rally

*Goldman is screaming tail hedge on semis, and the options market isn't pricing the gap.*

- Goldman desk: levered semiconductor ETFs now force roughly $2 billion of buying or selling per 1 percent move in the sector, having doubled in six to nine months.
- Aggregate long semiconductor exposure in those levered products is around $100 billion (Goldman Sachs). That's a flow time-bomb sitting on top of fundamentals.
- SOXX has rallied 70% since the end of March (Goldman Sachs). The rubber band is fully stretched.
- 10-delta puts in SMH and the AI-leaders basket look cheap relative to the embedded short-gamma profile of the levered ETF complex (Goldman desk).
- BTIG's base case: an "equal and opposite" SOXX move back to the 50-day moving average, roughly 20% lower, on a momentum reversal trigger.

The mechanical risk is straightforward. Levered ETFs rebalance daily. If SOXX gaps lower in a session, the rebalancing flow forces accelerating selling at the close, which feeds back into spot. That is how a minus three percent session becomes a minus seven percent close. Underwriters of cheap 10-delta puts haven't priced this asymmetry because the spot-up-vol-up regime has trained the market that semis only go up.

What the bulls would say: the rally is fundamentally underwritten by the Cisco, Nvidia, and Broadcom order books, not just flow. True, but mechanics and fundamentals operate on different timescales. A multi-week gap-down on a single bad print can happen even if the multi-year story is intact, and BTIG's whiplash signature (NDX RSI at 82, momentum index +5.4% one day and minus 5% the next) is now visible in the same data. Trade is long-dated tail puts on $SMH funded by selling near-dated upside calls, sized small because the unwind catalyst is still unknown.

### The Fed isn't cutting into this PPI print

*The two-cut-by-year-end view is dead. The next argument is whether the next move is a hike.*

- April PPI surged +1.4% month-over-month versus +0.5% consensus. That's the sharpest topline rise since December 2022 (PNC).
- PPI year-over-year at 6.0%. Energy PPI +7.8% in April and +18.6% since February (PNC). The energy pass-through is broadening, not narrowing.
- Services PPI +1.2% month-over-month, the strongest monthly jump since March 2022 (PNC). Inflation is escaping the goods-only narrative.
- UBS revised the Fed cut forecast to December 2026 and March 2027, from September and December 2026. BofA's RIC report: no Fed cuts in 2026, pushed to 2H 2027. PNC: zero further easing in 2026.
- Retail Sales Control Group printed +0.5% versus +0.4% estimate this morning. Consumer hasn't rolled over.

Three forces argue this print. Energy is still feeding through goods and services PPI, AI-linked software is contributing 14% year-over-year price acceleration, and the consumer is still spending. Three Federal Reserve dissenters at the April FOMC meeting (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) already signaled the hawkish camp wasn't on board with easing. That camp now has a 1.4% PPI print to point at. ING's note frames the dominant market discount as attaching higher probability to a hike than a cut from here.

The fair counter UBS would lead with: yields can still fall even with hotter inflation if growth softens. Unemployment ticked up to 4.34%, and a deeper labor signal could force the Fed's hand by Q4 regardless of inflation. True, but the labor data hasn't broken yet, and supply-side inflation isn't responding to Fed policy anyway. Bias is short $TLT into the May 20 FOMC minutes, paired against long $UUP. The rate-differential support for the dollar widens as the Fed sits.
