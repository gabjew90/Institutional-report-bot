# The rubber band snaps

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP]

The research tape this week is all one story, the crowded AI and mega-cap trade coming apart under its own leverage. The overseas markets that had become leveraged proxies for the AI bet, Korea, Taiwan and Japan, sold off hard, retail money kept pouring into semiconductors just as the system's spare cash ran dry, and the big desks are pointing at forced selling from leveraged funds as the accelerant. Underneath the index pain the rally is quietly broadening, with cash rotating out of the Magnificent 7 into small caps, cyclicals, bonds and gold. The mood is late-cycle and jumpy, with Goldman placing the market at stage seven of an eleven-stage tech bubble and BofA's sentiment gauge stuck on a sell signal.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### Rotate out of the crowded trade, the leverage is doing it for you

*The mega-cap leaders are rolling over, the borrowed money stacked on the AI trade is what cracked overnight, and the cash leaving them is rotating into everything that is not a mega-cap.*

- Korea's KOSPI fell 10% overnight and halted trading, with $2.5B of foreign selling in a single session and margin debt at a record 38.5 trillion won, about $25B (Goldman), so a crowded book of borrowed positions turned a small push into mechanical selling.
- Taiwan's market dropped 4% on the week with a record $6B of foreign outflows on Wednesday alone (Goldman), which tells you the selling is regional and positioning-driven, not one company's bad news.
- US semiconductor ETFs have pulled in $20B this year from near zero (Bloomberg), and that retail money arrived just as excess liquidity, the spare cash in the system above what the economy needs, turned negative for the first time since the 2021 inflation shock, so the late buyers showed up with no cushion under them.
- Leveraged ETFs are now a $200B pile controlling roughly $400B of market exposure, with one CSOP SK Hynix 2x product holding $16.7B on its own, the largest single-stock leveraged fund in the world, and JPMorgan puts forced selling in a real drawdown at $45B to $60B, which is a seller that feeds on itself.
- Mega-cap tech is rolling over, with its worst month since April 2022, underperforming the semis ETF by about 12%, and hedge-fund exposure to the Magnificent 7 has dropped to a one-year low (Goldman) while a record $9.3B left tech funds in a single week (BofA), the classic late-stage topping behavior Goldman maps to stage seven of an eleven-stage bubble.
- 305 of the 500 S&P names still finished higher on the ugly day (Goldman), and weekly flows showed $16.6B into bonds with money moving into small caps, housing and REITs (BofA), so the index pain is concentration pain, not market pain.

The move is plumbing, not fundamentals. Nomura's desk describes a negative-gamma loop, where the dealers and leveraged funds that sold protection are forced to sell more as prices fall (short gamma means they sell into weakness), and zero-day-to-expiry traders pile on by fading every bounce. Korea's national pension fund flipped from buyer to forced seller once its domestic stock holding crossed a 28.8% ceiling, retail came in last, and the spare liquidity that usually backstops a dip is gone.

The bull read here is Goldman's own, that the 10% Korea drop was flow exhaustion and a leverage washout rather than a break in the memory cycle, which it still calls constructive after a strong Micron print and with NAND supply tight into 2027, and Nomura's backtest shows the day after these forced-selling days the semis ETF has averaged a 1.3% bounce. A one-day bounce is not the overhang clearing, though. Retail bought the top with negative excess liquidity beneath it, the $400B leveraged-ETF complex is still there to sell into the next air pocket, and BofA's Bull and Bear gauge at 9.1 has held a sell signal since May with a 60% hit rate for a 2% to 3% drop over the next two to three months. The trade is not to short the AI story, it is to own what the rotation is buying. The bias is long the broadening, equal-weight and small caps ($RSP, $IWM) over the cap-weighted index, with gold ($GLD) added on dips below $4,000 as the rotation hedge BofA is leaning on.

### The capex bill is huge and the suppliers are cashing it

*The AI build is real money, but the market is paying the companies selling the shovels, not the ones digging.*

Goldman puts 2026 hyperscaler capex at $755B to $757B and 2027 at $920B, with a path to $1.1 trillion if spending reaches 2% to 3% of GDP, and semiconductors are now 19% of the S&P 500, double the 2000 peak. The hyperscalers themselves are the ones underperforming, because the market rewards companies harvesting returns over those still pouring cash into construction, and Goldman's reflexivity warning is that if one spender blinks, every board gets asked the same question. Worth weighing the source concentration, most of this capex framing is one house, so treat it as a single view rather than five. The clearest version of the same cycle is memory, where Micron's new customer agreements lock in about 40% of sales for five years, which is why Barclays lifted its target to $2,000 from $1,175 and Citi sits at $1,400. The bull pushback is that hyperscalers are cheap and underowned, with Amazon at a record-low 28 times earnings and a genuine moat, but the supplier gets paid first. The bias is long memory ($MU) over the spenders.

### China's models are 90% as good for a tenth of the cost

*The real threat to the high-spend US AI trade is not a US rival, it is a Chinese model that is almost as good and far cheaper.*

China's frontier models have reached about 90% of the intelligence of the best US models, up from 60% in 2023 (UBS), while their interface prices run below 20% of US peers and some sit under 5% of Anthropic's top tier. Zhipu and MiniMax spent $0.5B and $0.3B on 2025 research, roughly a tenth of Anthropic's budget (UBS), and Zhipu's new GLM-5.2 was trained on 100,000 Huawei Ascend chips with zero Nvidia silicon (Goldman). The stock is up 820% since its January listing and JPMorgan raised its target to HK$1,800 from HK$1,400. The tension Goldman flags is that cheaper intelligence could either pull through more compute demand or speed up the price deflation that destroys model economics. Frame it as an asymmetry. The cost of being wrong, if China keeps closing the gap, is that a chunk of that $755B of US spending is stranded. The cost of missing it, if the US frontier premium holds, is that you underweight the genuine winners. The carry while you wait is cheap, so the clean expression is downside protection on the most capex-levered names through $MAGS puts rather than chasing them.

### The bottleneck is power, and that is the durable trade

*The constraint on AI is not chips anymore, it is electricity, and that points the money at the grid.*

US data centers have over 55GW of behind-the-meter power planned, more than the entire installed capacity of New York state, and 75% of it is natural-gas-fired (ING). 80% of the North American data-center pipeline is going into concentrated clusters to share infrastructure (ING), and the strain is already in household bills, with Ohio retail electricity up 22% year on year in the data-center hubs. Goldman's own scorecard shows the capex-expansion plays, semiconductors, defense, power and capital-intensive names, have outperformed the hyperscalers funding them. The question that decides the trade is whether the power bottleneck bites before AI demand does. If electricity is the real ceiling, the move is not the chip names everyone already owns, it is the utilities and gas supplying them, cleanest through $XLU.

### The oil market is priced for peace that may not hold

*Crude has sold off as if the Iran deal is done, but the deal is the fragile part.*

The Iran-US memorandum is wobbling after an Iranian drone hit a cargo ship near Oman, and Rabobank's base case is now that the deal fails, citing no Iranian follow-through on tolls, uranium or Hezbollah. Energy markets are leaning the other way, with JPMorgan modeling Brent back to $80 in the fourth quarter as the Strait of Hormuz stays open and the shock fades, and Goldman describes only a temporary physical glut rather than structural oversupply. The signing already triggered an energy-equity selloff that RBC calls a buying opportunity in quality names. With four desks watching Hormuz and the whole move resting on Iranian behavior, energy is a cheap hedge against a re-escalation the tape has stopped pricing. The lean is long $XLE into that headline risk.

## 3. WHAT TO WATCH

### Today

It is late Friday afternoon, so few research-flagged catalysts are still ahead today. The one live binary is the memory complex around Micron, where Nomura warned that expectations are stretched enough to risk an AVGO-style selloff on any disappointment.

### This Week / Next

- **Mid-July: TSMC earnings.** The next real catalyst for AI hardware (Goldman). Until then the tape sits in an air pocket and stays vulnerable to positioning unwinds, so expect thin, headline-driven moves.
- **BofA's risk-off triggers.** Watch the Magnificent 7 ETF ($MAGS) breaking $60, or the Australian dollar versus yen pair dropping below 110. Either would confirm the leverage unwind is broadening, not stabilizing.
- **Post-expiry leverage drop.** After the mid-June options expiry, hedge-fund gross leverage historically falls about 5%, and this build was unusually large, up 22% in May and 18% in June (JPMorgan), so the seasonal unwind could be sharper than normal and keep mega-caps offered.
- **Slow burn into 2027: El Niño.** A greater than 95% chance of an El Niño running through March 2027 (Citi, NOAA) threatens crops, power demand and inflation, a tail risk for an equity market already trading near records.
