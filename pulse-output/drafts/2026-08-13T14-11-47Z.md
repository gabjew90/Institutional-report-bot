# Deleveraging done, chase begins

## 1. RECAP

[LIVE PRICE RECAP] The research that landed overnight argues one thing: the forced selling that capped every rally this summer is finished. Systematic funds have already cut what they had to cut, the corporate buyback window is reopening, and passive money keeps arriving on schedule. Yesterday's in-line inflation print gave that story room by pushing the September hike debate further out, and money moved straight back into semiconductors and the AI complex while software and internet names gave ground. The one thing standing against it is hedge fund behavior, which is still defensive. Producer prices came in softer than the desks expected this morning, and the reads on the core measure are still landing.

## 2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

### The forced sellers are done and the buyers are not

*The mechanical selling that has capped every rally is finished, and the money that has to buy is only now showing up.*

- Retail buying is back at the 64th percentile of its own range after sitting at the 4th percentile a week earlier (JPMorgan). The cohort that went quiet in the selloff has already come back.
- $1.6 trillion of ETF inflows year to date, 55% above the previous record pace, with July's $350 billion the largest month ever recorded (Citadel Securities). Passive demand arrives on a schedule and does not wait for the macro argument to resolve.
- Buyback authorizations above $1 trillion, the most ever at this point in a calendar year, and 70% of it outside tech (Citadel Securities). The corporate bid is returning as the window reopens, and it is broad rather than a tech-only story.
- 70% of S&P 500 members trade above their 200-day average price, the strongest breadth since December 2024 (Citadel Securities). The advance has stopped being five stocks.
- Q2 earnings are tracking 33% growth while the index trades at 20.1 times next year's expected earnings against 23.1 last October (Citadel Securities). Prices rose and the valuation came down, which is not what a melt-up looks like.

Volatility has flipped from being the market's output to being its input. Trend-following and volatility-targeting funds size positions off how much prices have been moving lately, so a calmer tape mechanically hands them room to buy at the same moment the buyback window reopens and passive flows keep landing.

The hedge fund book says otherwise. JPMorgan's prime brokerage data has clients still cutting exposure on both sides in the US and Europe, with the amount they have net riding on the market near 12-month lows, and Citadel Securities names its own limit: September seasonality is harder, and if August turns into a chase the buying capacity is spent before it gets there. Cutting exposure is what a fund does before it re-risks, not instead of it, and the option market is already leaning the other way, with S&P call volumes at 20-year highs and 35% of index members showing three-month calls priced above the equivalent downside puts. That is not what a market braced for a drawdown looks like. JPMorgan's desk is holding back full deployment until Friday's retail sales print, which makes 8:30 Friday morning the first hard test of whether the flows keep arriving.

### Everyone called the top in memory a year early

*The memory selloff priced a cycle ending in 2026, and the supply math says the turn is a 2028 problem.*

Morgan Stanley puts memory content per AI server rack above $2 million in the coming VR200 generation against $374,000 in today's GB300, and JPMorgan has memory going from under 10% of cloud providers' hardware spending before AI to 49% by 2027. Jefferies has memory contract prices rising 40-50% quarter over quarter, and DRAM average selling prices are forecast to rise 29% in 2027 and 7% again in 2028. The bear case is what the tape traded last week, that peak margins mean peak earnings and that "less memory" architecture headlines mean demand is cracking. Balancing the market takes another 300,000 DRAM wafers of capacity, which is a fab build rather than a quarter, so August's lows in the memory names are the level that settles whether this was a reset or the top.

### The AI bill lands on whoever spends it

*The market is paying for the backlog and docking the spender, and that split is the trade inside the capex story.*

2027 cloud capital spending now runs above $1 trillion on Goldman Sachs numbers, a 33% jump, with 2026 estimates up $36 billion since earnings season began. Cisco booked $4 billion of new AI hyperscaler orders and guided to $7.5 billion of AI revenue for fiscal 2027, then fell 4% after hours on softer gross margin guidance, and Tencent dropped 4% after capital spending came in 64% above what analysts expected. Nebius rose 23% on a capacity guide of 5 gigawatts. The counter to the return-on-investment worry is JPMorgan's, that hyperscaler order backlog is growing 150% year over year to roughly $1.7 trillion against about 80% spending growth, so the orders are outrunning the bill. Goldman Sachs has one exception among the big spenders and puts a number on it, a reiterated Buy on Alphabet with a $435 target on distribution, monetization and scaled infrastructure.

### Nobody in this research is forecasting a cut

*Producer prices fell again this morning and it still is not enough to put a cut in anyone's forecast.*

Headline producer prices printed at -0.28% month over month, against Deutsche Bank at +0.1% and JPMorgan at +0.2% going in, and the desk reads on the core measure are still landing. Market-implied odds of a September hike had already dropped to roughly 40% on yesterday's in-line CPI (Goldman Sachs), and the split from there is genuine. Deutsche Bank's US economists still call September, JPMorgan has moved it to December and says this data will not sway the FOMC dissenters, and TS Lombard sees the policy rate ending a full percentage point above today's level because shelter has stopped cooling, with rent of primary residence and owners' equivalent rent each running at 0.3% a month. The 30-year yield closed near its highest since 2007 going into this week's $25 billion auction (Deutsche Bank), and that auction is where the hawkish case either gets priced or gets rejected.
