2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

The war premium in oil is draining out

The Hormuz reopening deal is a sell-the-rally regime for crude, and the banks are cutting forecasts to match.

  • Goldman cut its Q4 2026 Brent forecast to $80 from $90 and its 2027 average to $75 from $80, assuming Gulf exports normalize to pre-war levels by end-July
  • Goldman models a 3.2 million barrel-a-day oil surplus in 2027, the glut that caps any rally from here
  • ICE Brent is already down about 30% from its conflict peak (ING), so a large chunk of the war premium has left the price
  • WTI fell 4.87% in a single session June 15, the fourth straight down day, confirming the move is underway not anticipated
  • Hormuz flows still need to climb roughly 12 million barrels a day to fully hit pre-war export levels (Goldman), which is why physical premiums have a floor even as paper sells off

Crude is repricing for a return of barrels, not a shortage, and the forecast cuts moving lower in lockstep across Goldman, ING and Mizuho tell you the desks are treating these prices as an exit rather than an entry. The counter is a timing one, not a direction one. The largest tanker operator says actual Strait transit takes weeks to resume, and spot premiums slipped to pre-war levels without collapsing, so the physical market is normalizing slower than the futures curve assumes. That gap is real, but a 3 million barrel surplus into 2027 swamps a few weeks of shipping friction, which is why the bias is to fade strength. Short $USO into any ceasefire-doubt spike, with energy-sector longs the cleaner thing to be selling.

AI capex is still the spending supercycle, with one crack worth watching

The infrastructure build-out remains the dominant growth engine in the corpus, even as the bill on aging gear comes into view.

  • Goldman lifted 2026 consensus capex for the top 5 hyperscalers to $755B, about $80B above prior consensus and 80% higher than a year ago
  • S&P 500 capital spending grew 38% year-on-year in Q1 2026 versus just 1% for buybacks (Goldman), so companies are plowing cash into building rather than handing it back
  • Total hyperscaler capex plus research spend is set to hit $1 trillion in 2026 across the eight global names (Goldman), the scale that makes this a macro force not a sector story
  • Global data-center power demand is modeled to grow 220% by 2030 versus 2023, with more than half of it in the US (Goldman), the multi-year demand tail under the picks-and-shovels names
  • The capex-beneficiary basket is up about 25% year-to-date versus the global equity benchmark (Goldman), so the market is already paying for durability

JPMorgan frames the AI upstream build as a durable capex supercycle reshaping equity returns, and the spend is still accelerating, not plateauing. The contested point is profitability. Goldman warns that rising depreciation expense, the accounting cost of all this new gear aging, will reverse some of the return-on-equity benefit the S&P has enjoyed, and that is a genuine Goldman-versus-JPMorgan split worth respecting. The defense is timing again, depreciation is a 2027-plus drag while the order books for the equipment makers are full now, with Goldman reiterating Buy on Applied Materials ($AMAT, price target $520) for its chip-deposition and etch exposure. The first place a pullback would show is hyperscaler capex guidance at the next earnings round, then semicap order trends as the real-time demand read. Long $SOXX, with the rare-earth supplier $MP (Goldman Buy, price target $80) as the government-backed supply-chain leg.

Japan keeps tightening and its equity market is leading on it

The Bank of Japan just hiked again, and the next leg is a grind toward a genuinely higher long-run rate.

  • The BoJ raised its policy rate to 1.00% from 0.75% overnight on a 7-1 vote (Goldman), confirming the hiking cycle is still live
  • The rate is seen climbing to 1.5% over the next year as the terminal level (Mitsubishi UFJ Morgan Stanley), so more tightening should be priced, not a pause
  • The BoJ’s own neutral-rate estimate is 1.1% to 2.5% (Goldman), meaning even at 1.00% policy is still stimulative with room to run
  • Japanese AI-semiconductor stocks drove about 60% of the equity market’s gains since April, with ¥16 trillion of buybacks announced year-to-date (JPMorgan), so the domestic bid has a real engine
  • Monthly government-bond purchases hold around ¥2 trillion (Goldman), so the balance-sheet runoff is steady and not a shock variable

A central bank still well below its own neutral estimate can keep tightening without choking an equity market that is being pulled up by the global AI trade and a record wave of share buybacks. The pushback is that a faster BoJ or a stronger yen would pressure the trades where investors borrow cheaply in yen to buy higher-yielding assets elsewhere, and a yen squeeze is the classic way Japan tightening bleeds into everything else. The falsifiable line is the path itself, if the BoJ does not deliver the next hike toward 1.5% by mid-2027 as Mitsubishi UFJ Morgan Stanley expects, the yen-strength worry is dead and those funding trades survive. Until then the buyback and AI-semi bid is doing the work, so long $DXJ, the dollar-hedged Japan equity ETF.

The Fed meets tomorrow with a new chair and a dovish lean priced too lightly

The Fed cuts more than the market expects (Goldman), and Kevin Warsh’s first meeting is the wildcard.

  • Goldman’s probability-weighted Fed path is explicitly more dovish than market pricing, so the house sees lower rates than the curve does
  • Goldman pegs the 2026 median rate forecast at 3.625%, up from 3.375% in March, with the target range expected to hold at 3.50-3.75% tomorrow
  • The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.44% into the meeting, down roughly 0.03 percentage point, so the bond market is leaning the same dovish way
  • The Fed balance sheet is down to $6.7 trillion from a $9.0 trillion pandemic peak (Crédit Agricole), the backdrop for how much runoff room is left
  • Multiple desks flag Warsh’s debut as chair as the event to watch, none willing to call which way he leans, so the uncertainty itself is the trade

This is the first FOMC of the Warsh era, and the projections plus the press conference set the tone for whether Goldman’s dovish case or the steadier market path wins. The counter is that a new chair often plays it safe to establish credibility, which argues for a status-quo hold and no dovish surprise. The defense is that the signal lives in the rate projections, not the decision, and Goldman reads the committee’s own forecasts as having already drifted higher even while its probability-weighted path stays soft. The falsifiable line is the dot plot, if tomorrow’s projections do not move dovish and Warsh sounds neutral, the bet on lower long-dated yields has no fresh catalyst. Until that print, long $TLT into the meeting as the most direct way to play the dovish lean.

Tesla’s delivery number is tracking ahead, but the US is the soft spot

Both desks covering Tesla see Q2 deliveries beating the Street, with Europe and China carrying a weak US.

  • Goldman raised its 2Q26 delivery forecast to 420K from 405K, above the 400K consensus, so the setup is a beat-the-number quarter
  • An independent estimate lands at the same 420K versus a 400K consensus, an unusual convergence that strengthens the signal
  • US deliveries are running down mid-teens percent year-on-year through May (Goldman), the soft underbelly the headline number hides
  • The beat is therefore an international story, with Europe and China demand offsetting the domestic decline
  • No desk in the corpus is arguing the bear side on the delivery total itself, so the risk is in the mix, not the headline

The number that prints will likely top consensus, but composition matters more than the headline for anyone trading the follow-through. The bear case is clean, a beat built on overseas strength while the home market falls mid-teens is a lower-quality beat, and any guidance leaning on a fragile US recovery gets faded fast. The defense is that consensus already sits below both serious estimates at 400K, so the bar to clear is low and the surprise skews positive on the print itself. The open question is whether the beat holds the stock or the mid-teens US decline becomes the story the day after. Long $TSLA into the delivery release if you trust the number over the mix.
