2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

AI infrastructure is the trade and the market

The 2026 capex cycle has gone from upside surprise to the single load-bearing earnings story in the index, and the fade calls are losing the argument.

  • Dell’s F2027 revenue guide jumped to $165-$169B from $138-$142B, with AI server revenue raised to $60B from $50B and EPS to $17.90 from $12.90 (Goldman). The stock ripped roughly 40% overnight on the print, and Lenovo is +50% in two weeks. The capex story is now also a hardware-margin story, which is a different debate than the one the fade desks were having three months ago.
  • Anthropic raised $65B at a $900B valuation (Goldman). Microsoft’s commercial RPO (revenue under contract that hasn’t been recognized yet) sits at $627B, up roughly 99% year over year, and AWS backlog is at $364B excluding Anthropic. Ten-figure forward bookings are the proof that hyperscaler spend is meeting actual demand rather than racing ahead of it.
  • Citadel pegs 2026 AI capex at $750B as the backdrop for elevated US growth pricing. Goldman raised its year-end S&P target to 8,000 on $340 of 2026 EPS, 24% year-over-year growth, on this trade. BofA pegs the AI data-center semiconductor TAM going from $264B in 2025 to over $1.7 trillion by 2030, with high-bandwidth memory (the stacked DRAM chips that feed AI accelerators) alone going to $168B at a 37% CAGR. Wafer-fab equipment is +24% YoY in 2026 to $144B, which is the picks-and-shovels order book.
  • Nomura measures hyperscaler-semis-memory names at 41% of S&P weight and 52% of Nasdaq weight. The trade is the index now, not a sleeve of it.

When a quarter of S&P earnings growth is tied to one capex cycle and the same names are 41% of the index by weight, every incremental backlog announcement is mechanically a positive index move regardless of breadth. The catch-up trade in software (IGV +33% since mid-April on $1.5B of May inflows per LSEG) is the leveraged-beta version of the same thesis. The risk is unit economics: MIT research has 95% of enterprises at zero return on GenAI so far, the Entelligence index puts useful output at 18 cents per dollar of token spend, and $182B of data-center debt issued in 2025 (roughly double 2024) means the build is increasingly debt-funded. Those describe a gap that closes either when revenue lands or when funding breaks, and until $META or $GOOGL guides capex DOWN, supply is the gating factor, not demand. Long $SOXX, with $MU as the cleanest single-name expression of the memory-pricing leg.

Hormuz inventories are draining while the futures curve trades peace

Oil traded on two timeframes at once last month. Spot inventories say the supply hole is worse than the curve is pricing.

  • Tanker transits through Hormuz are down roughly 90% from normal, and 10 million barrels per day of lost Gulf-area crude is the initial shock volume (Goldman). WTI is down 17% on the month, the worst since April 2025, even as the physical market tightens.
  • RBC counts 300 million barrels of lost production since the imminent-MoU story broke three weeks ago, and projects 30-to-40-day inventory cover by October, the lowest reading since 2016. Global inventory drain rate while the Strait stays restricted runs 10 to 14 million barrels a day.
  • Citadel models the immediate reaction if the Strait reopens cleanly: 10-year yields rally by about 0.125 of a percentage point, S&P +1.71%, broad dollar -0.53%. The asymmetry says the pain is shorts-of-oil in a reopening, not longs.
  • Goldman warns the second-order risk is demand destruction. 2% of global GDP runs through Middle Eastern oil under normal accounting, and roughly 27% of global GDP is theoretically exposed under a Leontief-style bottleneck (input-output math where Middle East crude is genuinely non-substitutable).

Twelve banks reference Hormuz as a live catalyst and price has gone the other way, because the futures curve trades headlines while the physical market trades the missing barrels. Overnight US-Iran fire exchange near the Strait and a US strike on Iranian drone-command sites (Reuters) are landing into a market that closed $USO -1.29% Friday on Trump’s “Iran really wants to make a deal” comments. The optimistic read is that ceasefire chatter is increasingly serious, refined-product inventories get one or two reload cycles before the autumn squeeze, and the Trump-Iran deal headlines that lifted equities resolve by July. That is the entire reason WTI is at $54 instead of $90. But you do not refill a 300-million-barrel inventory hole in two weeks of headline diplomacy, and the math says price has to converge UP to the spot reality, not down to the futures fantasy. Long $BNO, with the $USO leg avoided as a roll-cost trap.

Positioning is at the limit on both sides of the book

Hedge funds have rebuilt all the gross they took off in March, and the levered single-stock ETF complex makes any unwind faster than the last one.

  • Hedge fund gross exposure sits at the 100th percentile across all measured lookbacks. The ratio of longs to shorts in semis, hardware, and power names is 3x, versus 1.6x for the rest of the US equity book (Morgan Stanley). The crowd is sized at the limit.
  • Levered single-stock ETF AUM is $187B, an all-time high (Nomura). The complex amplifies daily moves in the very names, $NVDA, $TSLA, $MSTR, that already drive the index.
  • Software short interest is at the 100th percentile vs the past six years, and software gross book exposure sits at the 87th percentile when measured over the past 12 months (Morgan Stanley). The squeeze fuel is still there even after the 7-week run.
  • Retail trading volume is 10% above the January 2021 meme-bubble record. Daily gross options premium is tracking 1.9x its historical average, about 13% above the 2021 peak (Citadel). Call option volume is 70% of total.
  • Only 28% of S&P 500 constituents are outperforming the index over 30 days, a 1st percentile observation across 30 years. Ten names did 67% of the run since end of March.

Pain trades cut both ways from here. With gross at the 100th percentile and 67% of returns concentrated in ten names, the asymmetric vulnerability is a single MAG7 guide-down forcing concentrated unwinds across an enormous levered-ETF wrapper at the same time. Nomura calls out the $187B levered-ETF AUM specifically as the catalyst that makes the next reversal disorderly. The other side is that retail flow and the buyback machine ($865B of 2026 YTD authorizations, annualized to $1.5 trillion per Citadel) keep absorbing supply, and a soft landing plus an Iran deal puts the squeeze fuel in software shorts to work. Long $QQQ Aug puts sized to a 5% drawdown over four weeks, as a tail hedge against an index-long book.

Software is eight straight weeks higher and short interest is still maxed

The thesis that AI would crowd out enterprise software spend was wrong, and the squeeze fuel is still loaded.

  • IGV is up 36% in 7 weeks. $1.5B of May inflows into IGV, the third-largest monthly haul since November 2025 (LSEG), reads as institutional money, not retail. The two-month gain in the S&P Tech sector (April-May) is +36.1%, the third-best two-month tech print since 1990.
  • Software short interest is at the 100th percentile vs the past six years, and software gross book exposure sits at the 87th percentile when measured over the past 12 months (Morgan Stanley). Momentum-factor positioning at hedge funds tracked by prime brokers sits 50% above 2021 highs.
  • The JPM-flagged IGV June 82/92 call spread has returned roughly 4x. The structure that worked the bounce is still cheap because traders are paying more for downside protection than upside calls.
  • The setup: SOX is up 165% over one year while IGV was essentially flat into mid-April. The catch-up is what the May squeeze priced.

The mechanism is that software companies that survived the “AI is going to eat their lunch” narrative now have the same AI-tied revenue tailwind without the heavy spending bill chip makers are running, and margins are levered to that. The risk is that an enterprise IT budget hit from a 4.6% year-end unemployment rate (Goldman) and slowing real income growth (1.3% Q4/Q4 in 2026 per Goldman) bites software guides in the second half. That risk is real for verticals exposed to small business, but the IGV book is dominated by names with multi-year backlogs that pay through next year regardless of macro. Long $IGV through summer, with the JPM call spread the cheapest options expression for the same view.

Breadth is widening and the next 5% does not need the same five names

Equal-weight and small-cap participation is finally joining the rally, and that changes what the index needs to keep working.

  • Industrial cyclicals positioning sits at the 39th percentile, conservative versus the cycle, leaving room for re-engagement. Industrials short exposure is at the 90th/96th percentile on a 1- and 5-year lookback, the same setup that fueled the software squeeze.
  • Q1 S&P earnings growth landed at roughly 25.5% year over year (Citadel), versus the 13% expected at the start of the season. Forward S&P EPS estimates are up to roughly $350 from $235 at the start of the year. Goldman’s 8,000 year-end target is based on 24% EPS growth to $340 for 2026.
  • 28% of S&P constituents outperforming the index over 30 days is a 1st percentile observation across 30 years (Citadel). When breadth bottoms here, it usually mean-reverts UP, not down.

With Q1 prints coming in roughly double the expected rate and forward estimates already lifted across the board, the second-derivative shifts from “ten names doing all the work” to “everyone else catching up.” The risk is that breadth widens on softer data, which would be a structural shift in how markets behave (slowing growth + small-cap participation does not historically end well). But the data we have is a 25.5% earnings beat and a record buyback authorization run, not a slowdown. Long $RSP (equal-weight S&P) versus a partial $SPY hedge captures the catch-up without the concentration risk. Long $IWM July call spreads for the leveraged options version.
