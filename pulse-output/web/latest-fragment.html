2. THE MAIN EVENT

The AI trade is unwinding from the inside

This is a positioning unwind, not a growth scare, and the most crowded corner is where it bites first.

The mechanism is leverage meeting thin liquidity, and the data says the unwind is already underway. AI is now 39% of the S&P 500, the most concentrated the index has been since the dot-com peak (Bank of America), so a wobble in that one trade is a wobble in the whole index. The smart-money bid that caught every prior dip is gone: hedge fund gross leverage (their total borrowed-money exposure) fell to 207.3%, the 4th percentile of the past year, as funds shorted into the move instead of buying it (Goldman). That frequency of violent up-days is what clusters around major tops, and chips have posted 9 separate 5% up-days in 60 sessions while the semiconductor-to-Nasdaq ratio surged 46% in 12 weeks to levels last seen in 2000-2001. The rally has no professional sponsorship either, with momentum stocks running more than five standard deviations above their one-year average versus the broad market on retail buying, not institutional. Korea is the live wire showing what a margin-driven cohort does when forced to de-risk into a market too thin to absorb it: the KOSPI fell 10%, its volatility gauge implies 6% daily swings, and a single 5% index move forces $4.7B of dealer rebalancing in names like SK Hynix.

The bull case is real and worth stating plainly. Growth is fine, Goldman just cut its recession odds to 10% from 25%, and AI server rental prices are slipping, which lowers the cost of using all this compute even as it dents the scarcity story. The trouble is that a healthy long-term buildout and a stretched short-term trade can both be true, and the thing cracking right now is the trade, not the buildout. Hyperscaler gross debt has doubled from 0.9x to 1.8x of cash earnings in two quarters to fund it (Société Générale), and leveraged ETFs have stacked synthetic positions that force selling into weakness. Even Goldman’s desk concedes that with the hardware names this extended, further large gains are getting hard to justify. For US options exposure, this favors owning downside on the most crowded vehicle. Long $SMH puts.

3. BRIEFS

Warsh hands the dollar a tailwind

Warsh’s hawkish debut repriced 2-year yields higher, and the dollar is the cleaner trade off it than stocks.

The new Fed chair opened with minimal forward guidance and a price-stability-only message, and nine FOMC members now pencil rate hikes this year (Goldman). The market has moved to one 25bp hike priced by autumn and 45bp of total tightening through the first half of 2027 (Crédit Agricole), which flattened the 2s30s curve by 19bp as short-dated yields jumped 16bp (JPMorgan) and pushed the dollar to its most overbought reading since the 2022 peak. The pushback comes from ING, which argues two more hikes is overdone and that bonds are the better hedge again now that lower oil has restored the inverse link between bonds and stocks, so longer-dated Treasuries work if the AI drag spreads to the economy. The split is already showing this morning, with 2-year yields braced for a Fed that will not blink while 30-year Treasuries rally hard (10-year yield -0.08 percentage points) on the growth risk the unwind implies. The dollar expression sidesteps having to guess which side of the curve wins. Long $UUP.

Where to rotate when the consensus is short tech

The rotation out of crowded tech is already paying defensives and breadth, and that is where the next leg of leadership sets up.

In the de-risking session, money rotated into consumer staples, health care, financials, materials and energy while it sold technology, momentum and even utilities (Goldman, Morgan Stanley). Breadth is widening underneath the index, with materials, financials, industrials and consumer names all set for double-digit earnings growth rather than the index leaning on a handful of chip names. Hartnett is telling clients to take chips off the table with the BofA Bull and Bear Indicator at an extreme-bullish 9.2, and JPMorgan is pairing its semiconductor and Korea longs against shorts in mega-cap tech and unprofitable software, the same rotation expressed as a hedge. The skeptical read is that retail keeps buying every dip and a defensive rotation has been a losing bet for two years, so this could fizzle like the others. The cleanest concentration-unwind expression is long $RSP, the equal-weight S&P, against $SPY, paired with long $XLU utilities as the defensive complement that gets bid in every risk-off session.

Oil is pricing a recovery that has not arrived

The crowd is record-short oil into a market that is physically still tight, which is a squeeze waiting for a headline.

Managed-money Brent net length has hit a record short after seven straight weeks of selling worth $24.8bn, with new shorts making up about 80% of it and total notional shorts at a 10-year high (Goldman). Prices are grinding lower, and this morning’s 4% drop in $USO is the market pricing a fast recovery of flows through the Strait of Hormuz on the Qatar-Oman diplomacy. The physical reality is tighter than that: traffic is still far below pre-war levels and would need roughly 13 to 14 million barrels a day of additional flow to normalize (Goldman, ING), and ING calls the sell-off overdone, flagging a possible Russian diesel export ban that at 900k barrels a day would bite harder than its earlier fuel restrictions. The counter is that an Iran interim deal could unlock 50mb of Iranian supply and Chinese demand is softer than the official data shows, so the path of least resistance stays lower until positioning flushes. With the crowd this lopsided, the asymmetry is owning a snap-back on the next supply headline. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz disruption.

Biotech breaks out while everyone watches chips

With pharma writing big checks again, biotech is the one breakout that has nothing to do with the AI trade.

The biotech ETF $IBB pushed to fresh 52-week highs and cleared its 2021 record, and $XBI is outperforming the S&P year-to-date. The catalyst is a fresh deal wave, led by AbbVie’s $10.9B purchase of Apogee Therapeutics at a 49.5% premium, with large-cap pharma committing to acquisitions that are dilutive to earnings through 2031, a sign management is looking past the patent cliff. Barclays is flagging argenx as a high-conviction name into its Myositis trial readouts. The risk is that a sector breakout led by M&A can stall the moment the deals pause, and biotech has faked out at these levels before. The bet is that a group with its own catalyst holds up better through the next leg of tech de-risking than the crowded trade does. Long $XBI.
