AI selling resumes

Wednesday, November 25, 2026 · 57 research analyses

1. RECAP

Heading into today’s open, the AI selloff is into a second session and Monday’s rebound in technology shares keeps unwinding. Tuesday’s close left $SPY down 0.49% and $QQQ down 1.99%, with AI stocks doing most of the damage.

Crypto is falling with them: $BTC is down 2.75% today near $58,200, and $SOL is off 4%. Oil rose Tuesday, $BNO up 1.92%, and overnight Iran said ships hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz while Yemen’s Houthis claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery. The hedge funds that pick stocks have already cut their net exposure to the 1st percentile of the past year (Goldman). The selling still to come is from rules-based funds, which BofA projects will sell about $39bn of global equities even if markets go nowhere.

  • October core PCE: 0.3%, in line. The Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, with food and energy stripped out, rose 0.3% against a 0.3% estimate, so it does nothing to move the December hike. Treasury yields are lower this morning, the 10-year down 6 basis points (hundredths of a percentage point) to 4.84% and the 5-year down 7.3.
  • Third-quarter GDP: growth a touch soft, prices hot. The updated estimate showed 3.4% annualized growth against 3.5% expected, but the GDP price index ran at 3.4% against a 2.3% estimate. Bonds have not sold off on the hot price reading, with the 10-year still 6 basis points lower.
  • Dell beat after Tuesday’s close. It earned $9.45 a share against the $6.745 expected, on revenue of $54.94B against $50.30B. The beat did not hold up AI stocks: Nasdaq futures are down more than 2% this morning.

2. THE MAIN EVENT

The AI liquidation is not finished, and the desks disagree on how much is left

The selling in AI stocks has run past what the earnings justify, but light positioning will not end it while the Fed is still raising rates.

  • Goldman’s US Volatility Panic Index hit a record 9.8 out of 10 last Friday. After the four larger weekly jumps in the VIX on record, the S&P 500 was higher a week later every time but lower two weeks later in three of four, so the first bounce settles nothing.
  • Hedge funds that bet on individual stocks both ways cut their net leverage, how much more they own than they have bet against as a share of their capital, to 42.3%, the 1st percentile of the past year. They also sold US single stocks short every day last week (Goldman trading data). Those sellers are mostly done.
  • Rules-based funds are not done. BofA projects they sell about $39bn of global equities if markets go nowhere and $31bn in a falling market.
  • Nvidia beat revenue expectations by about $7B and forecast about $5B above them (Morgan Stanley), then fell 22.7% on the week. Good numbers are not lifting the stocks.

The selling is about the price of money as much as AI. Rates now hit AI demand directly: with the 10-year near 5% (4.84% this morning), holding cloud spending at today’s run rate means a roughly 20% cut to 2027 plans and 35-40% downside to AI chip earnings (BofA), so chip stocks may not find a floor until rates peak.

Supporters of the trade call this impatience with a payoff that is years away, not a top, and Goldman has the Nasdaq 100 at 17 times next year’s expected earnings and chip stocks at 12-13 times, near 10-15 year lows. Skeptics answer that cheap gets cheaper while the Fed hikes. In the five past episodes when Goldman’s panic gauge topped 9 during a hiking cycle, the S&P 500 returned only 0.4% over the next three months. Goldman’s Brian Garrett, replaying the 2018 sell-off, maps another 12% of downside to about 6,294 on the S&P 500 with a low in the first full week of January. Morgan Stanley’s 7,100-7,300 support range on the S&P 500 decides it, and with Nasdaq futures down more than 2% before the open the test comes early. A hold through the holiday week keeps Morgan Stanley’s correction-as-entry-point case alive, and a break with the panic gauge still above 9 puts the index on Garrett’s 2018 path.

3. BRIEFS

Two more Fed hikes are the base case, and October’s inflation did nothing to change it

Today’s 0.3% core PCE keeps the December hike on track.

Crédit Agricole expects quarter-point hikes in December and March, stopping with the top of the range at 4.50%, and Goldman calls a second December hike a near certainty with a third in March. This morning’s prints back them: core PCE matched the 0.3% forecast and the GDP price index ran at 3.4% against 2.3% expected. Markets price about 62 basis points of further hikes over ten months, and in four of five past hiking cycles markets underpriced the Fed by an average 109 basis points. Governor Waller wants a half-point hike in December, but the minutes show only a couple of officials agree. The dissent is narrow: Raymond James expects one final December hike and then a long pause. If the December statement does not signal March, Raymond James’s one-and-done call wins.

Data centers are holding up US construction, and politics is the risk

The buildout is safe for 2027 and exposed in 2028.

US private data-center construction hit $96.5bn annualized in September, 90% above a year earlier, while all other construction fell 4.8% (Allianz Research). About three-quarters of the 33.8 gigawatts of capacity due in 2027 is already being built. The risk is 2028. New governors in Texas, Ohio and Georgia pledged to restrict approvals (Raymond James), states covering about 40% of planned building face fears of a ban on new projects, and Morgan Stanley expects about 5 gigawatts of 2028 capacity to slip to 2029. The first test is any formal approval pause in Texas or Ohio once the new governors take office, which would put Morgan Stanley’s 5-gigawatt slip in play. The second is the big cloud companies’ 2027 spending guidance.

The rotation out of US megacaps has a destination: healthcare

The money leaving AI hardware is finding cheaper defensive and value stocks.

HSBC Asset Management favors rotating out of US megacaps into healthcare and staples, and calls cheap, unloved healthcare the sector where 2027 profit growth speeds up while it slows almost everywhere else. Goldman’s desk that builds stock baskets prefers healthcare over staples. Value stocks back the move: the MSCI World Value index returned 19.97% over 12 months against 2.71% for its Growth counterpart. Morgan Stanley, which raised its S&P 500 target to 8,700, wants high-quality companies tied to the economic cycle, led by industrials. If AI stocks bounce off the record panic reading, healthcare lags by the size of the tech rebound. If Garrett’s 2018 replay plays out, the next 12% of S&P 500 downside lands mostly in the hardware names that led the rally, and healthcare is where HSBC wants the money parked.

AI credit is pricing a different cycle from the rest of the bond market

Corporate bonds have barely joined the selloff, except the debt funding AI.

Since early October the Nasdaq 100 has fallen 19.8%, but the cost of insuring investment-grade corporate debt has risen only 7 basis points (Goldman). Junk bonds had their worst week of 2026, with their extra yield over Treasuries 51 basis points higher. Tech debt is the exception: the extra yield on investment-grade tech bonds rose 30 basis points, and Oracle’s is about 80 basis points above its October low. More supply is coming. Morgan Stanley expects $940bn of AI-related debt in 2027, and Goldman estimates junk bonds pay about 35 basis points less extra yield than the VIX implies. A full 2018 replay means junk yields rise about 98 basis points more over Treasuries, and that move would confirm the stock selloff has become a growth scare.
