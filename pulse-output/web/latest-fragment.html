2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

Hormuz is reopening on the futures curve, not the physical curve

The headline risk is draining out of oil futures while the physical supply hole still has not filled, and the gap is the trade.

  • Brent futures down 22% from the late-March peak even though Hormuz remains contested (Goldman)
  • Front-month oil futures down 12% since US-Iran progress headlines (Citadel Securities)
  • Citadel pegs the probability of normal Hormuz traffic at 22% by July 1 and 50% by August 1, and the curve has priced something closer to 80%
  • BofA puts the 2Q26 oil deficit at 3.4m b/d, returning to balance only in 2027, which does not match a $66 Brent print
  • Saudi-Kuwait-UAE-Qatar-Bahrain-Iraq collectively shut in 10.5M b/d in April per SEB, and global airline chiefs meet today on the fuel shock

Two stories are pulling oil in opposite directions. The futures market is trading ceasefire optimism plus a softer China demand pulse, which is why Iranian crude just slipped to a discount and Beijing is cutting retail gasoline from Friday. The physical market is still short, jet fuel inventories sit at eight-year lows, and the Lebanon-Israel ceasefire has already taken one Israeli strike in Lebanon overnight without breaking, which is exactly how the previous three “deals” this year started before they came apart. Bulls argue the futures curve has the read right, that a real ceasefire pulls Brent into the $60s and the trade is over by the end of June. The risk pushed back on the desks is that the physical scarcity wins out and the futures-physical gap closes the other way when refined-fuel stocks fail to rebuild over summer. Long $BNO outright, paired with short $USO to lean on the WTI-Brent spread widening as physical Brent stays tight while WTI prices the optimism.

AI capex is now funding itself, and that is the problem

The hyperscaler buildout has reached the point where 85% of AI-ecosystem revenue is the spending itself, which is a recycled-money loop rather than a self-sustaining business.

  • TS Lombard: 85% of AI-ecosystem revenues come from recycled capex, with the buildout at 2% of US GDP in 2026
  • Total AI buildout spend hits $700B this year, and Microsoft’s 2026 capex includes $25B attributed to higher component pricing, with $80B in unfilled Azure orders
  • Broadcom guided FY AI chip revenue to $56B vs Street $57.6B, and 3Q26 specifically to $16B vs $17.2B (Goldman TMT desk), the first real airpocket in the build-out narrative
  • Alphabet raised $35B in equity, the largest US equity capital raise on record, plus an $80B secondary that flips Q4 net equity supply from -$44B to +$40B
  • Micron DRAM prices up roughly 60% and NAND up roughly 70% in the latest quarters, and memory is what is keeping the capex math from collapsing right now
  • Goldman 1-Delta desk flags $60B in single-stock levered ETF AUM, doubled since April

The mechanics are uncomfortable when laid out. Hyperscalers are buying their own demand by paying GPU vendors who pay foundries who pay equipment makers. The picks-and-shovels names keep getting fed, and the end-customer revenue line is the part that has to materialize for the loop to self-fund. Citi and Goldman make the bull case that agentic AI raises token consumption 24x by 2030, which closes the loop in time. The skeptic case from TS Lombard is that capex growth at 80% YoY against a backlog of $1.5T means somebody renegotiates a contract first, and Alphabet’s record equity raise is the tell that even the most cash-rich names are running out of organic firepower. The Broadcom guide-down is the first crack visible in the print. Semis own that fragility because they are the most levered to the next quarter’s hyperscaler order flow, while equipment names price longer. Long $AMAT (semi-cap equipment, longer-cycle visibility) paired with short $AVGO into the back-half order season.

Hartnett’s bubble call and the rotate-out has a date

A “bubble” label from BofA’s Hartnett with the Bull and Bear Indicator at 8.5 approaching record, and four other notes hitting the wire arguing what to buy if not AI, says the lead has gotten too crowded.

  • BofA Bull and Bear Indicator at 8.5, up from 8.0, approaching record (Hartnett: “What To Trade When This Bubble Bursts”)
  • 4% of S&P 500 stocks at new highs vs 20 stocks at the March 2000 internet bubble top (Hartnett)
  • 67% of the S&P 500 rally since end-March came from just 10 companies
  • 25% of stocks outperformed the MSCI ACWI in May, and only 39% of active funds outperformed
  • BTIG flags nine consecutive up days and ten up weeks on negative breadth, the longest streak since 1985 and a combination that has not happened before
  • 31 global YTD rate cuts vs 12 hikes, the liquidity prop holding the rally together

The contrarian read is not “short the index tomorrow.” It is that the rotate-out trade has a date. Four separate notes (“Nobody Wants NVDA,” “Sell in May,” “Iran Deal Would Risk AI Rotation,” “The Year the Magnificent Seven Stopped Being Magnificent”) all argue the same thing, that the dispersion between the top ten and the rest of the index has gotten too large for the index to keep working as a single instrument. Bulls counter that put pricing is too cheap and the dealer book is positioned to mechanically buy dips, which is exactly what produces nine straight up days on bad breadth. The bear case wins when an actual catalyst lands (Friday’s payrolls hot enough to push the September cut out, the next MAG7 guide-down, Powell at Jackson Hole), because the same mechanical positioning that is holding the index up flips on a single forced unwind. Long $RSP (equal-weight S&P) over $SPY into Friday’s NFP, with $VIXY as the tail hedge while the Vol Panic Index sits near a record low.

  • BofA Global Research May NFP forecast 95k vs 85k consensus, unemployment held at 4.3%

  • JOLTS job openings 7.618m, up from a revised 6.877m, with 91% of the rise in professional and business services

  • ISM Services PMI 54.5 yesterday (vs 53.8 estimate), prices paid 71.3, employment sub-index still 47.9, so services activity is firm with hiring still soft

The mechanism that matters is the rate path. The market is positioned for two cuts in the second half, and if Friday prints 100k or better with unemployment unchanged and ISM prices paid still in the 70s, the September cut becomes a coin flip and the 10Y has more room to back up. Scotiabank itself flags the labor-data reliability problem (single-month volatility and the professional-services concentration in JOLTS) as a real reason to fade conviction, and the 30Y briefly breached 5% yesterday before settling, so some of this is already in the curve. The defense for the firmer call is that JOLTS does not lie about openings even when payrolls do, and the openings line has reaccelerated. Short $TLT into Friday’s print, paired with long $UUP as the secondary if the print runs hot.

ECB on June 11 is the hike nobody is hedging

The market has priced a 25bp ECB cut at 99% for next week, UBS is calling for a 25bp hike instead, and the long-euro borrowing trade is set up to break on a single phrase from Lagarde.

  • UBS expects ECB to hike 25bp at the June 11 meeting, sees terminal at 2.5%
  • Eurozone CPI accelerated to 3.2% YoY from 3.0% in April
  • 10Y Bund at 3.00%, a move above suggests the market is pricing persistent inflation and energy shock
  • Danske Bank cut 2026 Euro-area real GDP to 0.7% from 1.2% on the Hormuz shock, so the ECB has the inflation reason without the growth cover
  • Hike chatter is showing up across Deutsche Bank, Danske, Mizuho, UBS, and UniCredit

The setup is asymmetric. Consensus is a cut and UBS is the lone hawk on a print. If Lagarde matches UBS, EURUSD breaks higher and short-dated European rates reprice in one session. The pushback is that the growth picture is too weak for any central bank to surprise hawkishly, with Eurozone PMIs soft and Finland trend unemployment at the highest since 1999, so the funding-rate math works because the macro does not justify a hike. UBS counters that this is about the supply-shock inflation impulse, not demand, and the ECB hiked into stagflation before (2008) when the print got too sticky. With the dollar already slipping from a two-month high overnight on the Iran-talks read, the euro side has cover to run. Long $FXE (the US-tradable euro proxy) into the June 11 meeting.
