2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

AI is still the only thing this market rewards, and that is now the bear case

The Mag-7 plus AI-adjacent capex names are the entire equity rally. The desks calling for $1T hyperscaler capex are the same desks telling clients to rebalance.

  • UBS forecasts 2026 AI capex at $820B and 2027 at $990B, with cloud provider revenue running 40% year-over-year in 1Q26. The demand curve has not bent, so AI-tied earnings stay the marginal contributor to S&P EPS.
  • BlackRock Investment Institute projects annual hyperscaler capex hits $1 trillion by 2028, roughly equal to total US corporate capex in 2019, concentrated across five names.
  • Broadcom’s FY27 AI backlog sits above $150B with FY26-28 memory capex at $450B (JPMorgan). The picks-and-shovels names downstream of the hyperscaler order book have multi-year visible revenue.
  • $150B of data-center projects are facing delays or cancellations, plus political-backlash risk where policy shifts from regulation to taxing or owning the rents (Goldman). That is a multiple bear case, distinct from a demand miss.
  • The crypto leg of the AI complex has paused with $BTC -3.32%, $ETH -1.47% and $SOL -2.66% over the past 24 hours, even as cloud-equity AI earnings keep climbing. The long-crypto vs long-AI-equity rotation that worked in April has stalled.

Three facts are simultaneously true and the desks are not pretending otherwise. AI demand is real and the spending plans are real. The market’s reliance on a handful of names to deliver that demand is unprecedented in the post-war record. The funding mechanism for the capex, record corporate debt sales plus AI-tied equity raises, is being flagged as fragile by Deutsche Bank and being labeled “the AI debt bubble” in print. If hyperscaler capex keeps compounding 40% plus, the bulls win. If a single META or GOOGL guide-down lands, the picks-and-shovels names crack first because they are priced for the trajectory rather than the level. UBS itself is the loudest bull on the 2026 capex print and is also telling clients to use the recent strength to rebalance, and when the loudest bull is also flagging concentration risk the asymmetry has shifted. What survives cleanest in either outcome are the picks-and-shovels names with multi-year backlogs over the GPU-leadership names where positioning is already extreme. Long $AVGO and $MU, paired with trims in the most-crowded GPU expression.

Software went from the most-hated growth bucket to a record-setting two-day rip. The 1st-percentile long-term positioning print against semis in the 97th is the structural argument.

  • Goldman: the Software vs Semis basket moved +7.45% in a single session, the largest one-day move higher in over a year, with IGV daily call option volume hitting 280k Friday, the biggest volume day on record.
  • JPMorgan: software has outperformed the S&P by over 10 percentage points across two sessions, $IGV is up 15% over the last three sessions, and long-term software positioning sits in the 1st percentile against semis in the 97th. That mean-reversion gap is the trade even after the price gap has narrowed.
  • Vanda Research flags $46M of retail intraday inflows into $IGV, 40% above the prior record set in early February. Retail is now chasing the move alongside long-onlys.
  • Raymond James pegs 2026 software industry earnings growth at 18%, with the AI-complement thesis (software captures the productivity layer of the AI build-out) finally showing up in earnings revisions.
  • $IGV is up 43% and $SOXX up 45% from the April 14 lows, so the relative-value gap at the index level has already largely closed even as the positioning gap has not.

A 1st-percentile long-term positioning print is the setup for a squeeze in any growth sector, and a single AI capex revision was enough to flip the narrative from “AI is taking software’s lunch money” to “AI is software’s growth engine.” Goldman’s desk is seeing real long-only demand, not just hedge-fund cover. The pushback worth taking seriously is positioning-mechanical, after a historic squeeze and a test of major range highs, with retail setting record inflow days, the marginal buyer has changed and the marginal buyer is the wrong one. The honest read is that the squeeze leg is done but the multi-quarter long-software thesis is intact for anyone willing to size for daily noise. Going long software while shorting semis is too noisy at the index level for a daily signal after this week’s move. Long $IGV outright into next earnings.

Hormuz is still closed, Iran is reviewing a US proposal, and oil prices are whip-sawing

Nine desks treat the Strait of Hormuz as an unresolved supply disruption with no scheduled reopening. A US-Iran ceasefire MoU just hit the wires overnight, dropping front-month oil more than 1%.

  • Natixis: daily ship passages through Hormuz are still under 10 versus a pre-conflict baseline of 125-140 ships per day. The chokepoint is not normalizing on the ground regardless of headlines.
  • UBS: 21 million barrels per day of oil normally moves through the Strait, roughly 20% of global seaborne crude, with no alternative routing for most of it.
  • Rabobank is the lone desk modeling a 7-month total closure, which would knock 2.5-2.8 billion barrels off cumulative production and persist into September.
  • Deutsche Bank: Polymarket probability of Hormuz returning to normal by end-June has fallen to 22% from 26% on Sunday. Even the prediction-market crowd has marked the reopening timeline back.
  • Deutsche Bank pegs $150/bbl as the Brent price in a prolonged-closure scenario and UBS holds a $105/bbl September Brent target. That is the corpus’s implied range to trade against.

The split is the trade. Front-month Brent is pricing the US-Iran MoU headlines, with $USO closing Monday +4.97% before easing more than 1% overnight on the news. Physical ship-traffic data and prediction-market probabilities are pricing closure persistence. When the futures market and the physical market disagree, the futures market eventually re-converges to the physical, historically over weeks rather than days. The opposite read is that an Iran deal genuinely lands in the next two weeks, with Deutsche Bank’s analysts flagging June as the negotiation window, and a single Trump-Iran announcement compresses the entire supply premium overnight. That is real risk and the asymmetry argues against a leveraged outright long-oil structure. UBS’s $5,500/oz year-end gold target ties the same geopolitical premium to a different instrument that is not subject to the same headline whip. Long $BNO sized for headline risk, paired with long $GLD as the hedge into the next Hormuz print.

Nobody wants NVDA, and the contrarian no-bid is now eight desks deep

Eight desks are openly arguing the consensus AI long is the wrong trade. The convergence across macro, equities, credit, and derivatives is what makes it different from one bearish desk getting loud.

  • One desk’s “Speculation Nation” piece argues positioning is extreme, leverage is exploding, and investors are chasing low-quality tech. The framing is a regime call rather than a price call.
  • The only non-recession example since WWII of the S&P 500 rising this fast was a few months before a historic market crash (Deutsche Bank). The analog is explicit and unhedged.
  • State Street and Syz Private Banking both quantify the AI-excluded rally as “significantly less strong.” Concentration risk now sits in the body of two asset-allocator notes, not just trader commentary.
  • Rabobank and RBC BlueBay both have skeptical-stance notes on the post-rally credit setup. High-grade credit spreads are tight enough that an AI capex stumble pushes the whole risk complex wider.
  • Franklin Templeton is the lone bull on broadening into small caps and value, while Deutsche Bank and Goldman push back that US megacap tech concentration stays the regime. The dispersion within the bears is itself the signal that no one has a clean catalyst yet.

The mechanism is unusual. Eight different desks across different specializations have arrived at the same conclusion via different paths. State Street is worried about the asset-allocator’s portfolio. Deutsche Bank is worried about the historical analog. Goldman is worried about political reflexivity. When skeptics converge on the same conclusion from independent starting points, that is qualitatively different from one bearish desk getting loud. The case for ignoring them is simple, the same desks were skeptical at lower prices and prices kept going, and UBS’s $7,900 year-end SPX target plus Goldman and Deutsche Bank’s $8,000 targets are not consistent with the bears being right yet. What the contrarian view actually says is that the marginal positioning is now wrong (retail records, hedge-fund leverage climbing, record-pace AI debt sales), and a marginal-positioning bear case needs a catalyst more than it needs a thesis. The expression for someone with US equity exposure is to fund a rebalance toward equal-weight and small caps without exiting the AI infrastructure book. Long $RSP and $IWM as a partial trim of $SPY, holding the AI infrastructure exposure in $AVGO and $MU.
