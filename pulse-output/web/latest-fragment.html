Chips crack, capex holds

Tuesday, July 28, 2026 · 27 research analyses

1. RECAP

Markets opened lower Tuesday and the damage is concentrated in tech. $SPY is down just -0.30% half an hour into the session while $QQQ has dropped -1.81%, the split that says chips and megacap names, not the broad market, are doing the selling. The positioning backdrop is why it bites: discretionary investors have cut equity exposure to the lowest since early April, the semiconductor index is parked right on the line that held its mid-July sell-off, and the extra yield investors demand to hold big cloud companies’ bonds just pushed to new highs. $BTC slipped under $63k with the risk-off tone but held its range. All of it lands two days before a Fed decision the market still cannot fully rule a hike out of.

  • Apple retook the most-valuable-company crown from Nvidia at Monday’s close. Apple ended Monday as the world’s most valuable company, passing Nvidia for the first time since June 2025. The handoff is a clean marker of money rotating out of AI-chip leadership, the same rotation dragging the Nasdaq this morning.
  • The AI-capex financing question got louder. Reports that Nvidia is in talks to backstop a $500B, 10-gigawatt OpenAI data center in Ohio, on top of a possible $250B OpenAI backstop, have investors asking how the buildout gets funded. The extra yield on big-cloud bonds at new highs shows the credit market is already nervous, and that worry is the through-line under the tech slide.
  • Oil and gold kept unwinding the war premium. $BNO fell -1.31% and $GLD -1.45% as the US-Iran step-back drains the scarcity bid that built during the Hormuz scare, with crude futures “pricing market adaptability, not hopeful Iran peace.” Softer gold takes one inflation input off the table right before Wednesday’s Fed.

2. THE MAIN EVENT

The chip trade is correcting and the crowding says it is not done

The semiconductor sell-off is not a dip to buy yet, because the most one-sided positioning in the market still has to clear.

  • Hedge-fund positioning in chips sits at the 98th percentile and tech hardware at the 86th (JPMorgan, as of Monday), meaning the long bets are about as crowded as they have been in a year, so an unwind has a long way to run before it is clean.
  • The SOX semiconductor index is parked on 11,200, the level that held the July 17 sell-off, with its momentum gauge (RSI, where under 30 is washed out) at 39, the most oversold since the AI melt-up began. A decisive close below 11,200 opens air underneath.
  • BTIG puts 22% of downside in $SOXX to its 200-day average (the long-run trend line traders watch) if that support breaks, and 23% in high-beta momentum names to their March lows, so the technical floor sits well below where the group trades now.
  • Korea’s memory complex has already cracked, with Samsung down 14%, Kioxia off 60% from its peak, and the KOSPI down 35% from its June high, so the AI supply chain is unwinding hardest where it is most levered.
  • The extra yield investors demand to hold big-cloud bonds hit a new high of 1.45 percentage points over Treasuries, right after Alphabet’s earnings, so the credit market began repricing AI-financing risk while stocks were still buying the dip.

Three forces are pulling the group lower at once. The crowding gives an unwind its fuel, the widening in tech credit is the bond market questioning how all this capital spending gets funded, and China’s push into its own chip supply chain caps the long-term revenue of the Western names. The bull case is not weak. Discretionary money has already reset to the 17th percentile, its lowest since early April (Deutsche Bank), the momentum gauge is washed out, and long-horizon valuations look reasonable, which reads as a buying opportunity to some desks. What keeps this from being the bottom is that the reset is only in the discretionary crowd. The systematic funds and hedge-fund long-short books are still at that 98th percentile, and technical damage on this scale usually takes months to repair, not days. Short $SOXX rallies until the index reclaims its 50-day average and holds 11,200 at the close, the level BTIG flags as the line between a pullback and a break.

3. BRIEFS

China is building its own chip supply chain faster than the market priced

China’s home-grown chip effort is now big enough to threaten the Western equipment makers, not just serve its own market.

CXMT, China’s memory-chip maker, debuted up roughly 466% and briefly passed Intel’s entire market value (Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs), a sign of how much conviction sits behind Beijing’s self-sufficiency drive. China is ramping domestic production of DUV lithography tools, the older-generation machines that etch chip patterns, which is the direct reason $ASML broke below its 50-day trend line. Apple is pushing to source memory from Chinese suppliers like CXMT and YMTC to ease a global shortage, while Micron warns that Chinese entry could gut the US industry and points to $250B of planned US capacity (JPMorgan). Memory contract-price growth is already decelerating, with DRAM up 16% quarter-on-quarter after a 56% jump the prior quarter (JPMorgan). Fade $ASML rallies rather than chase them while the China tool story builds, since the risk is a permanent haircut to its largest growth region.

Strip out the positioning noise and AI demand is still real

The actual demand for AI infrastructure keeps showing up in the numbers even as the stocks correct.

Oracle’s finance chief pegs its AI-infrastructure projects at 30% to 40% gross margins and high-20s returns on invested capital (the cash yield on money sunk into a project), with capacity tight and vendors building against booked demand rather than speculatively (Deutsche Bank, $300 target). Rental prices for the newest B200 chips rose 7.3% month-on-month while the prior-generation H100 slipped 1.1% (JPMorgan), so the money is concentrating in the latest silicon, not fading. Amazon heads into this week’s 2Q report with AWS momentum and a $200B capital-spending plan the market wants to see defended, and a raise there confirms the buildout the way Alphabet’s did. Long $ORCL as the direct way to own execution without the crowding, or $AMZN calls into the AWS print.

The Fed holds this week, but a hawkish hold still stings

The Fed almost certainly leaves rates alone, yet the market carries enough hike risk that the tone of the hold matters.

No change is the base case at both JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs for Wednesday’s meeting. Markets are still pricing roughly 38% to 40% odds of a hike, and Goldman lifted its odds of eventual hikes to 35% from 25%. The cover for a patient hold is soft June inflation, with core CPI (prices stripped of food and energy) at -0.02% month-on-month, the weakest since May 2020 (JPMorgan). The risk sits in the messaging, with hawkish (wanting higher rates) dissents expected from Hammack and Logan and a Chair in Warsh who could tip a close call toward a surprise. If the statement reads as a clean hold with soft-inflation cover and no more than the two expected dissents, short-dated Treasuries get relief and long $TLT works into it. If Warsh forces a hike or three-plus members dissent hawkish, that trade is dead and the dollar catches a bid.
