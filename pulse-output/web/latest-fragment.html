2. THE MAIN EVENT

Long-term yields shrug off the soft inflation print

The 30-year Treasury yield kept rising after a soft inflation reading, which means the pressure is coming from the supply of bonds and not from the Fed.

  • Core PCE (the inflation measure the Fed targets, which leaves out food and energy) rose 0.25% in August, below what desks expected, and Goldman pushed its next rate hike from October to December on it. A soft print like that should pull yields down, and it did not.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield was 5.313% on Thursday, the highest since 2002 (Goldman). Long-dated yields are rising while the 2-year yield fell.
  • The 30-year yield reached 5.61% on September 29 while a heavy run of corporate debt, including a $52B package tied to the Paramount, Skydance and Warner Bros. Discovery deal, competed with the Treasury for the same buyers.
  • Japanese investors have been net sellers of foreign bonds for a year (TS Lombard), and a Bank of Japan hike expected in December gives them more reason to bring money home.

Three sources of supply are stacking up at once: Treasury auctions, corporate deals, and Japanese money leaving foreign markets. None of them responds to one inflation reading, which is why a soft print moved the Fed’s expected path and left the 30-year alone.

The case for relief runs through the Fed. New York Fed President Williams sounded dovish and the odds of an October hike dropped sharply, and there is a strong chance that no more hikes are needed (Goldman). Relief in the 10-year and 30-year yields is what Goldman expects would set off an equity rally, but bond funds are taking in money and JPMorgan finds little of it reaching long-dated bonds. That gap is why $TLT sat at $77.71 as of about 8:31 AM ET even with the Fed leaning softer. Goldman’s desk says no one is stepping in to buy long-dated bonds at these levels, and stocks break when yields after inflation rise past what equities can tolerate. Payrolls printed at 8:30 AM ET, and whether the 10-year yield fell or rose on that number decides whether long-dated bonds are still short of buyers.

3. BRIEFS

Micron beat and a sold-out 2027, with two things that can break it

Micron beat and guided higher, and the AI spending behind memory chips is still being raised.

$MU reported Wednesday after the close with $54.23 billion of revenue against $50.95 billion expected and $33.42 of adjusted earnings per share against $31.63, then guided the November quarter to $61.5 billion (Goldman). Most 2027 supply of high-bandwidth memory is already contracted at higher prices, and Goldman keeps a Neutral rating and calls the risk and reward balanced. Separately, customer agreements covering about 35% of revenue through fiscal 2030 cap how much spot-price upside Micron keeps. JPMorgan raised its 2027 hyperscaler spending estimate to $1.1 trillion from $925 billion, the demand side of that sold-out supply. Power limits have held up 60% of the US data centers planned for 2027, which have not started (Goldman), and the Samsung and SK Hynix buyback programs end in early October, a possible gap in demand for Korean memory stocks. December 9 is the date Goldman marks for Micron to start returning all of its excess cash.

Professionals are buying small caps while leverage rebuilds

Professional money bought the Russell 2000 from a multi-year low in positioning, and JPMorgan warns leverage is back near its highs.

The Russell 2000 ($IWM) fell 4.2% in September while the Nasdaq rose 4.6%, and professional money bought into that weakness. Non-dealer buyers added $3 billion of Russell 2000 futures between September 1 and 22, after positioning hit a multi-year low on September 1 (Goldman). The other side of the trade is borrowing: hedge fund leverage through equity futures is near its highest this year and margin borrowing by individual investors remains very elevated (JPMorgan). The small-cap buying gets undone if credit keeps weakening, because asset manager selling has tracked the CDX High Yield index (the price of insuring junk bonds against default), which hit a five-month high on September 30.

Oilfield services sell off with Brent above $100

Oilfield service stocks are falling even with Brent above $100, because the next two quarters of guidance look harder than the oil price suggests.

$NESR fell 11% on September 29 and $SLB, $PTEN and $HAL each fell 3%, while $BKR, $HAL and $WFRD have dropped below their 200-day average price without a bounce (Goldman). The worry has moved from third-quarter results to fourth-quarter and full-year guidance, especially for companies with large Middle East exposure. The same desk is willing to bet against the rally in Brent even above $100 a barrel, and it added $OXY to its US Conviction List with a Buy rating, a $69 target and a $4 billion cash flow improvement target by 2030. The earnings reports in the coming weeks settle it, and a sustained move in Brent below $100 would show whether the desk’s call is right.
