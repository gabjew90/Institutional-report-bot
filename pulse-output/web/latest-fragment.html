1. RECAP

Heading into Thursday’s open, two stories own the session and they pull in opposite directions. Kevin Warsh ran his first meeting as Fed Chair Wednesday and the committee came out more hawkish than anyone was positioned for, with new projections showing roughly half the policymakers now penciling in a 2026 rate hike instead of the cuts the market had leaned on. Stocks sold it: $SPY closed yesterday at $740.96 (-1.25%), $QQQ left the session at -1.01%, and $VIXY jumped +4.03% as hedges got bought. The dollar firmed ($UUP +0.90%) and gold cracked ($GLD -2.27%). Running under all of it, the signed US-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is draining the war premium from oil, with the first tankers already crossing and crude near its lowest since the conflict started. Crypto sat it out, $BTC flat near $64k overnight.

  • Warsh’s first FOMC (2 PM ET Wednesday). Rates held at 3.75% in a unanimous vote, but the dot-plot did the talking: about 9 of 18 officials now expect a 2026 hike versus roughly 3 before, and the Fed lifted its 2026 core inflation forecast to 3.3% from 3.1%. Markets repriced toward tightening, the dollar rose on buying interest, and rate-sensitive tech led $QQQ -1.01% lower.
  • Retail Sales blew out (8:30 AM ET Wednesday). Headline rose 0.88% for May vs 0.5% expected, with the core (ex-autos) at 0.82% vs 0.6%. A consumer this hot hands a hawkish Fed all the cover it needs to stay parked, and it deepened the move: yields up, long-dated bonds down (the longer the maturity, the harder they fall when rates rise) into the close.
  • US-Iran ceasefire signed, Hormuz reopening. Crude fell to its lowest since the war began as the first Saudi-flagged supertankers transited the strait, with $USO -1.07% and $BNO -0.91% yesterday and futures soft overnight on the supply-relief headlines. The deal reportedly carries a $300B reconstruction fund, more than half already committed, though Trump warned he could still resume strikes.
  • BoE held at 3.75% (7 AM ET today), in line with consensus. No surprise, no US-asset spillover.

2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

Warsh’s first meeting was a shot across the bow

The new Fed is telling you it cares about inflation first, and a 2026 rate hike is now the base case, not the tail.

  • The committee held at 3.50-3.75% in a 12-0 vote, but 9 of 18 officials now expect a 2026 hike versus about 3 before, so the center of the committee moved toward tightening
  • The median 2026 forecast jumped to roughly 3.8%, and the Fed lifted its 2026 core inflation forecast to 3.3% from 3.1%, a signal they would rather risk slower growth than let prices run
  • Traders now price about a third of a percentage point of hikes for 2026, up from roughly a fifth before the meeting, and around 80% odds of a quarter-point hike by December (UBS)
  • Warsh said “price stability” 13 times against roughly 3 mentions of jobs (JPMorgan), a tell that the employment side of the Fed’s mandate just got demoted

When the people who set rates signal hikes instead of cuts, every asset whose value leans on low rates (from long-dated Treasuries to high-multiple tech, where the longer the payoff stretches out, the harder it falls when rates rise) has to reprice lower, and the dollar gets a bid from the widening rate gap. Not everyone buys the follow-through. ING reads this as bark over bite and looks for an extended pause of perhaps 12 months, and Rabobank expects a hold for the rest of 2026 because the Hormuz oil shock could still drag on growth. The pushback has teeth, but the data is not helping the doves: May retail sales blew past the half-percent estimate by a wide margin, exactly the cover a hawkish Fed wants. Next Thursday’s core PCE is the real test, and a hot print parks the committee or moves it. Short $TLT, paired with long $UUP, until a soft inflation print forces the Fed to blink.

The Hormuz reopening is bleeding the war premium out of oil

The Iran deal is real supply-side relief, and the path of least resistance for crude is lower even if the headlines stay messy.

  • Roughly a fifth of the world’s oil and gas moves through the Strait of Hormuz (JPMorgan), so reopening it flips the single biggest supply fear of the year from a chokepoint into a flood
  • Crude fell about 5% on the deal and has kept sliding to its lowest since the war started, with Brent back under $78.50 (UBS) and the first Saudi supertankers already transiting per overnight shipping data
  • The framework runs 60 days and pairs a ceasefire extension with a $300 billion Iran reconstruction fund, more than half already committed per reporting, money that buys real follow-through
  • The IEA now sees global oil demand falling 1.1 million barrels a day this year, deeper than its prior call, so the demand side leans the same way as the new supply

Reopen the chokepoint and add softer demand, and the market wants to keep draining the premium it built during the war. The counter is that the relief is thinner than the move suggests. US crude inventories including the strategic reserve just hit their lowest since 1985 after the largest weekly draw since February, so the physical market is still tight, and a deal that defers every hard nuclear question to a rushed 60-day window can unravel on one Israel-Lebanon flare-up (Trump already said overnight he could resume strikes). Restocking a drained system takes months while a headline reprices futures in minutes, so the supply relief is the durable force and the tightness is the trap for shorts who overstay. Fade oil rallies with $USO puts rather than chasing the breakdown, with tight inventories as the reason not to press.

AI infrastructure spend keeps getting revised up, not down

The capex cycle is still accelerating, and the chip-equipment names that sell into it are the cleanest way to own it.

  • BofA now models 2026 hyperscaler capital spending near $800 billion, a 67% jump on last year, and Pacific Asset Management sees $700 billion this year scaling to $1 trillion in 2027
  • JPMorgan raised its chip-equipment market growth forecast to 28% for 2026 (from 21%) and 29% for 2027 (from 18%), marking up tooling demand twice in short order
  • Jabil ($JBL) lifted its full-year AI outlook to $13.6 billion, up 51% from a year ago, with Barclays Overweight and a $426 target, a sign the buildout is landing in real orders
  • Broadcom ($AVGO) is guided to $60 billion-plus of AI revenue in 2026 and over $150 billion in 2027 (JPMorgan), with SK Hynix shares ripping 7% to fresh highs on the same demand

When the dollars get revised up across hyperscalers, equipment makers, and custom-chip designers at once, the whole infrastructure stack is getting bid, not one lucky name. The bear case is financing, not demand: GPU-backed bonds ballooned to $65 billion of new supply in 2026 from a few billion before, average GPU cluster utilization has run near the single digits (Cast AI), and even AI’s own elder statesmen are warning of a bubble (LeCun called xAI a “failure” and flagged the valuation risk this morning). If clusters sit idle while the debt funding them compounds, the collateral story cracks before the revenue does. The defense is timing: the picks-and-shovels names sell into a multi-year order book that does not flinch on one soft quarter, and a capex guide-down from a hyperscaler would be the first real signal, which has not come. Long $AVGO as the cleanest single-name read on the demand.

Semis are the most crowded trade in the market

The chip rally is running on positioning fumes, and the move is to hedge the long, not add to it.

  • The semiconductor index is up about 99% year to date, its second-best run ever behind only 1999, and the equal-weight chip ETF is up roughly 92% (BTIG)
  • The group trades 20% above its 50-day average, the widest stretch since 2002, the kind of extension that has historically preceded drawdowns of 25% to 46% from the peak (BTIG)
  • Hedge fund net exposure rose 6.2 points in a month, one of the sharpest four-week build-ups in five years, with gross leverage at the 98th percentile and net at the 89th (Goldman Sachs prime-brokerage client data)
  • Chips now command a market-cap share six times their revenue share, double the ratio for the megacap tech group (JPMorgan), so the valuation leans on a narrow earnings base

When everyone is long the same names with borrowed money, you do not need bad news to fall, you just need the marginal buyer to be done, and the unwind feeds on itself as leverage gets cut. Bulls counter that the rally is earnings-backed and the demand revisions in the prior theme are real, not froth. That is true, and it is exactly why this is a hedge, not a short: stay long the buildout while owning protection against the crowd, and yesterday’s $VIXY +4% reminds you that protection gets expensive after the move starts, not before. The signal to watch is chip option volatility pressing above its recent range while the index cannot reclaim its highs within two weeks, which is the positioning unwind starting. Long $SMH puts against the equipment longs, cheap insurance on a stretched rally rather than a directional bet.

The bull case for stocks is intact, but dangerously narrow

Banks are constructive on US equities into year-end, and the risk is not earnings, it is how few names are carrying them.

  • Raymond James models 20% S&P 500 earnings growth in 2026, the fastest since 2021, and lifted its 2027 estimate 10% this year, the strongest upward revision in two decades
  • UBS stays Overweight US equities, with JPMorgan and BofA carrying the same constructive year-ahead view
  • The backdrop is firming under them: May retail sales came in well above the half-percent estimate and Q2 growth is tracking near 3%, an economy reaccelerating rather than rolling over
  • The 10 largest tech names are now 40% of the S&P, higher than the dotcom peak, with Nvidia alone near a $5 trillion market value (UBS)

Strong, broad-based earnings growth is the cleanest reason to stay long stocks, and the macro data is cooperating. The risk sits in the same place the upside does. When 40% of the index rides on ten names, the market’s fate is really a bet on AI capex holding, and the New York Fed’s credit-card delinquency rate just hit its highest since the 2008 crisis, a quiet sign the consumer underneath the rally is fraying. Weigh the asymmetry: being underweight here risks missing a strong double-digit earnings year, while chasing the cap-weighted index means owning the concentration risk at its widest ever. Long the equal-weight $RSP over the cap-weighted $SPY, keeping the earnings tailwind without betting the book on ten tickers.
