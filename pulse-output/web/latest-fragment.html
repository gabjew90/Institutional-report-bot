2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

Persian Gulf supply scar isn’t going away

The market is still pricing a fast Hormuz resolution, and the physical data says that’s wrong.

  • ANZ baseline assumes 10.5 million barrels per day of Persian Gulf crude losses, with global product inventory at a 1.55 billion barrel floor (roughly 20 days of cover). The buffer is thinner than headline supply numbers suggest.
  • IEA has drained 164 million barrels from emergency stockpiles as of May 8, and emergency draws are a one-shot fix, not a substitute for Hormuz transit. The gap shows up in physical prices first.
  • Polymarket odds for the Strait of Hormuz reopening by end-May fell to 7%. The political timeline is being priced as unresolved, but the futures curve still carries a resolution premium.
  • AAA national gasoline breached $4.50 per gallon Monday, the level where spring tax-refund tailwind to the consumer gets offset.
  • ICE Brent net long positioning sits at 345,805 lots (ING), cut by 28,400 lots over the reporting week. Speculators are de-risking the long even as supply tightens, leaving fuel for a positioning chase on the next headline.

Three things are happening at once. The physical market is drawing on emergency stockpiles to keep refined products flowing, prediction markets see no near-term political resolution, and futures are still discounting a resolution the same speculators are exiting. Morgan Stanley’s $90 per barrel year-end baseline assumes a quick resolution and pegs $150 as the recession-level threshold (where global supply chains crack). The gap between the two is the trade.

The counter-read is that US rig counts at 415 are the highest since November 2025 and the Primary Vision frac spread count at 184 is the highest since June 2025, both up 5 week over week. US shale is accelerating. The reason that doesn’t fix the supply scar: even in a 6-week ceasefire scenario the refined-product hole takes months to refill, because US shale can replace barrels but not refining throughput, and Pakistan’s weekend deployment to Saudi Arabia is the opposite of a near-term de-escalation signal. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline, with $XLE as the equity-side expression on any retracement.

AI capex super-cycle is still the load-bearing trade

Six banks back the AI build as the structural earnings driver. The cash-flow math is starting to crack.

  • Goldman raised 2026 hyperscaler capex to $680 billion. The 2027 projection is now $1.16 trillion (Morgan Stanley), up from a roughly $450 billion projection a year ago. Capex revisions are accelerating, not slowing.
  • Cloud capex is consuming 95 to 100% of operating cash flow over 2026 to 2028, versus the 65 to 70% peak in the prior 4G/5G cycle (BofA). Almost all the cash being generated is going back into the build, leaving nothing for buybacks.
  • $SOX index revenue is up more than 100% year over year (JPMorgan). Semis revenue IS the AI revenue, and the call-options demand is following it: $3 trillion in call notional traded last week (Goldman), historic by any measure.
  • The S&P 500 2026 earnings expectation has risen to $331 from $308 at the start of the year, a 7% lift. AI capex revisions are doing the work of multiple expansion.
  • $QQQ is trading 15% above its 100-day moving average with a 14-day RSI of 83, one of the highest readings on record (Goldman). $SOX is 62% above its 200-day (BofA), a level the bank only finds parallel for in the Mississippi bubble and the dotcom peak.

The mechanism is consistent across the bull camp. AI revenue is concentrated in the picks-and-shovels names ($NVDA, $AVGO, $AMAT, $CSCO), capex is being underwritten by free cash flow from the same hyperscalers, and the bond market is now extending the funding stack. AI funding needs are reshaping global corporate bond markets as hyperscalers diversify how they pay for the build (JPMorgan). Berkshire’s 224% increase in $GOOGL over the weekend lands directly inside this camp and reinforces the mega-cap concentration story heading into $NVDA’s print Wednesday after the close.

The skeptical case is that hedge funds are hiding in the same trade, and dealers who sold all those calls now have to buy more stock the higher it goes and dump it the faster it falls to stay hedged, which means $9 billion-plus in short levered ETF exposure can amplify a violent unwind. The bull rebuttal is the revenue side: until AI revenue stops compounding at triple digits, the bid is mechanical. Long $SMH on any pullback toward the $QQQ $680 technical zone, with $NVDA calls into Wednesday’s print as the directional bet.

Rate-cut hopes are dying, and equities haven’t priced it

Hot inflation, a 5% 30-year auction, and accelerating data are pushing the Fed wait-and-see camp.

  • Crédit Agricole CIB flags April Core CPI at 0.4% month over month versus 0.3% consensus, and PPI Final Demand at 1.4% versus 0.5% consensus. Both hot, both compounding the Fed’s hold case.
  • ING shows US Final Demand PPI at 6.0% year over year for April, the highest since early 2023, with April headline CPI at 3.8% (ANZ), the highest since May 2023. The inflation print is broadening, not a one-off.
  • The 30-year Treasury auction cleared at a 5.00% coupon for the first time since November 2007 (Crédit Agricole CIB). A 5% yield on 30-year Treasuries is the threshold where bonds reflect fiscal stress and start to weigh on equities (Goldman).
  • The UK 30-year gilt is at 5.86%, the highest since 1998, and Reuters framed the weekend bond rout as global on Iran-war inflation fears. US 30-year Treasuries are not insulated.
  • Fed rate-hike (not just no-cut) expectations are now bleeding into the curve, and Trump told Fortune cuts may have to wait for the Iran war to end. The political pressure-relief valve the equity market was leaning on just got removed.

Three forces are pulling yields higher at the same time. Oil-driven inflation is feeding core CPI on the standard 3-month lag (cost-push from energy takes about a quarter to show up in core readings), fresh long-dated Treasury supply is hitting at a 5% clear, and a Fed that cannot credibly cut into a 3.8% headline print has lost the option of jawboning the curve lower. Goldman makes the equity link explicit: higher for longer is a problem for equities, especially non-profitable tech.

The contrarian read from JPMorgan is that markets are pricing a more hawkish Fed than JPM itself forecasts, leaving room for a dovish surprise into the next soft data point. The catch: even on JPM’s view, near-term rate-cut pricing has been pushed out, not erased, and the 30-year Treasury supply problem is independent of the Fed’s reaction function. The FOMC minutes Wednesday at 2 PM are the next live catalyst. Long $TLT against trimmed index-equity exposure into the minutes.

Breadth is narrow, momentum is at the dangerous part

The same melt-up that is lifting the index is now showing the warning patterns that historically preceded reversals.

  • Bloomberg flagged 30 S&P 500 stocks making new 52-week lows on a day the index posted a 10% monthly return. The historical average forward 1-month return when more than 30 stocks hit new lows is 1.5%, a long way from the recent run rate.
  • The momentum factor (the rolling basket of recent winners) has rallied more than 20% over the past 3 months, the historical threshold for sharp momentum episodes since 1980. Subsequent month gains average 6% but the variance widens.
  • $3 trillion in call notional traded last week and the Goldman Risk Appetite Indicator sits at 1.1, the highest level in over five years. The bid is mechanical because dealers who sold all those calls are now forced to buy more stock the higher it goes to stay hedged, and any spot pullback flips them into selling into weakness.
  • Hedge-fund Z-scores point to a tactical reversal (JPMorgan), with bank-sector positioning at the 8th percentile across investors (Deutsche Bank). Pension and SWF flows are running thin in the rotation laggards.
  • $QQQ has technical support at $680 (Fibonacci retracement) and $660 secondary, the levels every dealer hedging desk is watching and below which the forced-buying flow flips into forced-selling that amplifies volatility.

The setup is mechanical. The index keeps grinding higher on a narrow set of AI-linked names, call-options demand on those same names is forcing dealers to keep chasing stock higher to stay hedged, and the unwind risk is concentrated in the same trade hedge funds are crowded into. Goldman’s counter-read is that narrow breadth alone is not a bearish directional indicator, but the bull case rests on AI revenue continuing to deliver, not on breadth healing.

The skeptical desks aren’t arguing against the AI bull thesis. They’re arguing the entry point is bad. With $SOX 3-month at-the-money volatility at the 100th percentile and stocks still grinding higher, the asymmetry has shifted: the upside is priced and the downside is cheap. Long low-delta $SPY puts as a tail hedge through Wednesday’s $NVDA print, paired with long $SMH on any retracement to the $QQQ $680 zone.
