2. THE MAIN EVENT

The most crowded trade in the world is unwinding, and it isn’t a solvency event yet

The semiconductor crash is hedge funds cutting position sizes, not the AI earnings story breaking, and that distinction is the entire trade.

  • The SOX semiconductor index is down 16% from its June record and its implied volatility spiked to 60, so the options market is pricing chip swings it hasn’t in years and hedges are expensive exactly when everyone wants them.
  • Semiconductors are more than 20% of daily global trading volume across 10,000 stocks, and 82% of fund managers now call long semis the most crowded trade in the world while 81% are still in it, which makes the exit narrow and violent when it comes.
  • Single-stock volatility sits at the 92nd percentile since 1990, a level Bank of America flags as matching the late-90s dotcom peak, meaning the market is trading individual AI names like lottery tickets rather than compounders.
  • Hyperscaler borrowing has doubled, with gross leverage (debt relative to equity) jumping to 1.8x from 0.9x in late 2025 to fund a combined $1.8T AI capex bill (capital spending) for 2026-27 that is turning Big Tech free cash flow (the cash left after spending) negative, and $241B of AI-related investment-grade bonds (the highest-quality corporate debt) have already hit the market this year.
  • Goldman pegs AI infrastructure at roughly 60% of all S&P 500 earnings growth this quarter, so this is not a sector rolling over, it is the index.

Two engines are running in opposite directions. The chipmakers, Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron, are printing cash, with Goldman modeling $430B of free cash flow, while the hyperscalers spending that money, Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle, are watching their own cash flow go negative to fund it. Bank of America reads the single-stock volatility spike as a genuine dotcom echo, while UBS and Goldman call the same drop a de-grossing, hedge funds trimming the most crowded position rather than a break in the earnings story, and the sell-off is the market deciding which half of that trade it still wants to own.

The darker read lives in the bond market, where SpaceX bonds are yielding 7.3% two weeks after issue and leverage doubling in three quarters looks less like discipline than a supply glut chasing thinning demand, and if the high-grade bid for AI debt cracks, the capex bill stops refinancing and the buildout slows all at once. The strain shows in the debt, not the equipment: $ASML just raised full-year revenue guidance to EUR 43-45bn from EUR 36-40bn (Goldman lifted its target to EUR 2,200), while $TSM bumped 2026 capex to $60-64bn from $56bn, and companies sold out of shovels into 2028 are not what the top of a spending cycle looks like. With the crowd this one-sided, the rout live again at Friday’s open and monthly options expiring today, own downside on the crowded index: long $SMH puts into next week’s earnings, with $ASML the better long for anyone who has to be in the theme.

3. BRIEFS

Retail never left, but it stopped buying the market

Retail traders are more active than they have ever been and buying less than they have since COVID, and that gap is a fragility signal.

  • Retail is now 30% of US equity volume with $1.6B in average daily net buying this year (Goldman), and daily options premium hit a $6.7B record in June, 65% above the 2025 average, so this cohort moves prices.
  • Net cash going into single stocks has collapsed to $13B over the past four weeks, the lowest since COVID, even as trading activity sits at the 99.7th percentile, which means they are churning rather than accumulating.
  • 50M option contracts trade daily with calls at 65% of the total (Goldman), so the activity is concentrated in leveraged upside bets on a handful of names, not broad ownership of the market.

Retail is chasing the stories it has the highest conviction in, not the index (Goldman), so the broad bid is thinner than the raw volume suggests. Cost of being wrong: retail re-engages the whole market and this fades. Cost of missing it: the concentrated crowd unwinds and drags the index down with it, because retail is a third of the volume. That gap between record churn and a hollowing bid is where volatility pays. Long $VIXY.

Gold’s correction is too young to be over

Gold’s pullback is only a quarter of the way through the time its prior run took, and the chart just flashed its most reliable sell signal.

  • Gold triggered a death cross, the 50-day average falling below the 200-day and a bearish chart signal, on June 26 at $4,088 spot, and in 30 such signals since 1975 gold was lower 40 trading days later 70% of the time (Bank of America).
  • The current pullback is 24 weeks old against the 121-week advance that preceded it, so by BofA’s own math it needs more time and probably more depth to finish.
  • Gold is heading for a weekly loss as inflation and rate-hike worries firm and real (inflation-adjusted) yields sit at their highest since April 2025, removing the falling-rate support that carried the metal up.

$GLD is up a touch today but still tracking that weekly loss, and the small bounce changes none of the chart math. If spot gold cannot reclaim the $4,088 death-cross level inside the 40-day window, roughly early September, the correction has further to run, and a break below $3,827 kills the last chart argument for a bottom (BofA’s level). Until then, short $GLD.
