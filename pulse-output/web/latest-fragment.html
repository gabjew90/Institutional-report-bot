2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

The AI capex supercycle survives the positioning unwind

The $52B leveraged-ETF flush was a positioning event, not a thesis break. The multi-year infrastructure spend the market keeps pricing in is structurally bigger than one Friday rebalance.

  • JPMorgan models $3.9 trillion in cumulative data-center capex through 2030, a decade-long bid for chips, power, and physical real estate that does not get reset on one weekly de-leveraging cycle.
  • Goldman puts roughly $1 trillion in annualized private-sector AI capex running through the system now, with US power demand growing 3.2% per year through 2030, the utility complex earns this regardless of which hyperscaler wins.
  • UBS has hyperscaler cloud revenue growing 45-50% in mid-2026 and accelerating above 55% by Q4, the demand line is steepening, not flattening.
  • The SOX entered Friday at 76% above its 200-day moving average, the most extended level since the dot-com era (Goldman), with leveraged-ETF concentration in AI tech leadership at all-time highs per Nomura.

Three independent forces are bidding the same infrastructure stack at the same time. Token demand for AI inference is outrunning supply, hyperscalers are now funding the buildout with equity-linked paper (Alphabet-style raises, per Barclays) which deepens the buyer base, and underlying power infrastructure is being repriced on US demand growing 3.2% annually for the next four years. That is a structural cycle, not a positioning trade. The bear case the unwind crowd is making is real, that 76% above the 200-day and record concentrated leverage is a setup for more amplitude on bad prints. Where that breaks: Goldman’s own cloud-growth read is accelerating into year-end and JPM’s $3.9T capex number was raised, not cut, in the latest revision cycle. A positioning unwind on a structurally-rising earnings line is a buy-the-dip setup, not a structural shift in how the market trades AI. Long $MU into the AI build-out, the memory cycle is where the supply tightness is most visible.

Hormuz fragility, not Hormuz resolution

The Iran-Israel halt has oil rolling, but a US Army Apache going down near Hormuz overnight is the kind of detail that says the energy-supply scare is paused, not refunded.

  • 15 desks flag Hormuz transit risk as an active driver of the energy backdrop right now, no bank is calling the all-clear.
  • 9 desks frame the broader Iran-Israel conflict as the upstream catalyst, they are watching escalation, not resolution.
  • Refined-fuel inventories sit at multi-year lows and the LNG-into-Asia recovery is happening because the Strait is still functional, not because the conflict is resolved.
  • Trump warned Netanyahu against renewed war overnight (Axios reporting), the political pressure is for the halt to hold, which is bullish risk and bearish oil at the headline level.

The setup is binary in the wrong way. A clean ceasefire snaps the supply-scare premium out of crude in days. A single incident that recreates the closure-risk narrative, a vessel attack, an IRGC seizure, an Israeli strike, puts it back in hours. The opposing read is that refined-product inventories have been tight for months, the market has absorbed three rounds of Hormuz scare without breaking, and a sustained 4-week halt fully drains the war premium out of the curve. Where that read breaks: the LNG-into-Asia recovery is happening alongside the Apache incident, the Strait is still operationally active even with the diplomatic halt. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline, sized small enough to wear a clean 6-week halt without forced exit.

Wednesday’s CPI is the Fed-hike test

The strip is pricing a 25bps Fed hike for the first time in 18 months, Wednesday’s May CPI is the print that either confirms or unwinds it.

  • Citi has about 28bps of hikes priced through year-end and forecasts May core CPI at 0.22% month-on-month, both consistent with underlying inflation running near 2.5% and gradually easing.
  • Goldman’s May CPI forecast is the dovish corner of the dealer survey: +0.17% core month-on-month, +2.79% core year-on-year.
  • JPMorgan is hotter at +0.27% core month-on-month, the print that would lock in the hike pricing rather than unwind it.
  • UBS reads May payrolls as fully pricing 25bps of hike into the front, but argues the bar for the Fed to actually deliver is high, they need a wage breakout and an inflation re-acceleration that is not in the data yet.

Three forces are arguing about the same data print. Citi and Goldman think the underlying trend is still disinflationary at roughly 2.5% and the May print should ratify that, which would unwind the hike pricing and lift long-dated Treasuries (the longer the bond, the bigger the rally when yields drop). JPMorgan is in the camp where one hot print is enough to crystalize the hawkish path. UBS bridges them: market is overpricing today, but the data has to keep coming in soft for that overpricing to unwind. The skeptic to the dovish call is what Friday’s NFP already showed, a labor market that does not need the Fed to cut and is therefore not stopping the Fed from hiking. The defense per UBS directly is that the hawkish thesis requires both faster wage growth and an inflation breakout, neither of which is yet visible. If May core CPI prints below 0.22% MoM, hike pricing collapses. If it prints above 0.27%, it extends. Long $TLT into the print, the asymmetry favors the curve repricing softer if the data ratifies the disinflation trend.

The rotate-out trade is starting to look real

Six independent bank desks are publishing dedicated notes pushing back against the AI/tech lead, that is not noise, that is the start of a rotation.

  • Six banks (Bank of America, Barclays, Deutsche, Goldman, Morgan Stanley, UBS) are running thirteen distinct PDFs this window arguing for rotation out of the lead theme.
  • BofA’s “Tales from the Biotape, vol 4: Is it time to reassess Biopharma?” is an explicit ask to look at health care as a rotate-into candidate.
  • World Gold Council and Citi both running “Can gold reach new highs?” pieces, the gold-versus-AI substitution trade has bank attention.
  • The wealth-effect that powers the AI trade is itself a concentration risk: 33% of US households are now tied to equities (record high, per BofA), the size of the unwind if consensus breaks.

What is going on here is psychological as much as quantitative. When six different desks all simultaneously publish “what to buy if not AI” pieces in the same week, they are responding to client conversations, not running independent thought experiments. The buy-side is asking for rotation candidates, and the sell-side is delivering. Morgan Stanley publishing on mall sales growth of 4-6% year-on-year for top-quality malls is the same trade in a different dress, quality income outside the AI complex. The bull-case defense is that AI capex is structural and the rotation calls are noise. Where that breaks: the concentration math says even a small rotation creates outsized moves in the equal-weight and small-cap complex, and the rotation calls are clustering ahead of CPI on purpose. Long $XLV paired with long $GLD as the macro hedge.

Semis are in a positioning regime, not a fundamentals regime

Friday’s $52B forced-selling event was a leveraged-product mechanic, semis sold off because semis had been the leveraged longs, not because the AI thesis broke.

  • Nomura: $52B of forced selling from leveraged-ETF rebalancing on Friday, broken down as $23B semis, $18B tech, $5B Mag7, the largest single-day forced flow of the cycle so far.
  • Nomura: leveraged-ETF assets under management sit at $187B all-time high, with 87% concentrated in AI tech leadership.
  • The SOX one-day decline of 10.26% was the worst single-session drop since March 2020.
  • BTIG: roughly 90% probability of breaching Friday’s low in the next session after a 4%+ down NDX day, the path-of-least-resistance call is to expect more chop.

The mechanic is simple. Leveraged ETFs are required to rebalance daily. When they hold concentrated bets and the underlying drops, the forced selling is multiplied by the daily-rebalance arithmetic. The same products are forced buyers on the way up. This is why semi price action this week is going to be more about flow than fundamentals, the capex story is intact, the positioning story is not. The bull-case here aligns with the lead theme, same hyperscaler capex line, same memory cycle, same data-center demand. Where the positioning math overrides fundamentals: until the leveraged-ETF complex normalizes its concentration, semis will move in larger amplitude than the earnings warrant. Long $MU calls into the memory-supply tightness, avoid $SOXX until the forced-flow overhang clears.
