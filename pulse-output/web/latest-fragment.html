2. THE MAIN EVENT

The froth signals are all flashing at once

Positioning in US stocks is stretched to levels that have marked tops before, and several of the Street’s own sell indicators have already tripped.

  • Fund-manager cash fell to 3.6% of assets (BofA’s survey), the lowest since February and below the 4.0% line that flips a widely-followed cash rule to a sell. Low cash means managers are already fully invested, so there is little dry powder left to push stocks higher.
  • The price-to-sales ratio on the broad US market hit 3.57x, the highest in 35 years and above both the 2021 and dot-com peaks. Investors are paying more per dollar of company revenue than at any prior mania, which sets the bar for disappointment very low.
  • Fund managers are running a net 24% overweight in US equities, holding far more than their benchmark weight, the most in over eighteen months and third-highest in five years. The crowd is all on one side of the boat.
  • Retail traders are buying at 3.2x their normal daily pace, per Citadel flow data. The last marginal buyer is the least price-sensitive one, which is what late-cycle chasing looks like.
  • Goldman’s sentiment gauge has sat more than one standard deviation stretched, a statistically extreme reading, for three straight weeks, its own threshold for a sell signal.

When cash is this low, valuations this high, and the last buyers this aggressive all at once, there is no fresh money left to lift the market and any shock has to be absorbed by sellers rather than cushioned by sidelined cash. That is the mechanism behind every one of these gauges pointing the same way. The bull case is that stretched positioning can stay stretched for months in a strong earnings regime, and none of these indicators carries a date. The caution has teeth: sell signals from cash rules and sentiment gauges have fired early before and cost people the last leg of a rally, which is why this reads as a risk-trimming signal rather than an outright short. What it argues against is adding fresh long exposure into a market where every positioning gauge is already maxed out, and it argues for owning some downside protection while it is still cheap. Long $SPY puts as a hedge into the back half of earnings season, not chasing the market higher.

3. BRIEFS

The new regime is higher rates, not lower

A Fed that has to hike into sticky inflation is the macro story that reprices everything, and it kills the classic stock-bond mix.

  • One macro shop sees the Fed starting to hike in September or December and delivering four to five increases over the next 18 months as inflation stays sticky. That is the opposite of the cuts still priced into much of the market, so rate-sensitive assets are mispriced if this call is right.
  • US 2026 growth is pegged at 3.3% versus a 3.1% consensus (TS Lombard), with the reacceleration driven by AI capital spending and government support. A growing economy is exactly what lets the Fed keep tightening without breaking anything.
  • Global AI capital spending is running near $800 billion this year, with the US responsible for over 80%. That spending is a real prop under growth, which is why the hiking case does not need a recession to work.
  • Roughly 85% of AI-ecosystem revenue is money recycling between the AI companies themselves rather than coming from outside customers. That circularity is the soft spot, because the capex boom leans on continued spending rather than durable end demand.

The logic is that more frequent supply shocks, from energy to trade, keep inflation higher and more volatile than the last cycle trained everyone to expect, so bonds lose their role as safe ballast right when rates are climbing. The classic 60/40 portfolio, 60% stocks and 40% bonds, is called dead in this regime, with commodities the preferred inflation hedge. This morning’s data cuts the other way, though: June retail sales cooled and Wednesday’s PPI fell -0.28%, so the immediate prints argue for Fed patience, not hikes. Markets have priced cuts for a reason, and if growth cracks or the energy shock fades, the hiking path evaporates and long-dated bonds become the trade instead. That risk lives entirely in whether inflation actually stays sticky, and the tell is the energy complex, since the same shop flags a Strait of Hormuz disruption as the thing that could push oil past $150 and force the Fed’s hand. Short long-dated Treasuries via $TLT, sized small, with commodity exposure alongside as the hedge.

Tesla’s quarter is a margin test before the story trades

The next Tesla print is a weak-numbers, strong-narrative setup, and which one the stock trades on is the whole question.

Deutsche Bank models second-quarter auto margins excluding regulatory credits, the emissions offsets Tesla sells to other automakers, at 18.0%, down from 19.2% the prior quarter, with adjusted earnings of $0.36 against a $0.47 consensus. That is a real miss, driven by promotional-financing costs and the end of an upfront software revenue item. The bank keeps a Buy and a $465 target anyway, leaning on the longer story: the Optimus robot line ramping toward 2,000 to 2,500 units a week by year-end, Samsung confirmed to build the next AI chip in Texas, and growing chatter about a Tesla-SpaceX combination in the next year or two. The bear case is simple, a margin miss into a stock priced for perfection is enough to break it regardless of the robot narrative. The counter is that the market has repeatedly looked through Tesla’s auto numbers to price the AI and robotics optionality, and this quarter’s headwinds are one-time items rather than demand cracks. Long $TSLA calls into the July 22 print rather than the stock, so the downside is capped to premium.

Payments consolidation just got a live bid

A takeover offer for PayPal puts the whole payments group in play, and the read-through matters more than the one name.

Stripe and Advent have offered $60.50 a share for PayPal, roughly a 28% premium to the prior close and a $53 billion-plus valuation, backed by a reported $50 billion of committed financing, with the buyers planning to hold the company rather than break it up. Deutsche Bank keeps a Hold and a $45 target, arguing management may see more value in selling off pieces than taking the bid. That is the tension: the offer validates the assets but there is no guarantee it closes on these terms. The read-through beyond $PYPL is the signal that a multi-year-low multiple in payments is now pulling real strategic money, which puts $GPN, $FIS, and $PAYO in frame as the next look. Long $PYPL into the board’s decision to play the deal actually closing.
