2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

Hormuz peace headlines on the wires, but the supply scar lasts months

The oil tape is pricing peace; the inventory data is pricing scarcity, and inventory wins on a multi-month horizon.

The Strait of Hormuz closure has cut Persian Gulf production by over 10 million barrels per day, with cumulative supply losses now around 1bn barrels and global crude inventories down nearly 200 million barrels in April — ANZ calls it the largest quarterly drawdown on record for Q2 2026. ING flags 13 mb/d of supply still disrupted as Trump pauses Project Freedom, with US refined-fuel inventories at multi-year lows: gasoline at the lowest seasonal level since 2014, distillates the lowest since 2005. BofA notes regular gasoline near $4.50/gallon with futures pointing to sticky $4+ pricing through summer. Aluminum is the second-order play: the Middle East provides 9% of global primary supply and 20% of non-China supply, Middle East production fell 7% YoY in March, and ANZ flags the aluminum forward curve in its most backwardated state since 2007 — physical metal is tighter than at any point since the financial crisis. The bull case for the peace trade is straightforward: Iran is reviewing a US proposal, Trump floated extending the ceasefire pending Iran’s response, and oil is already giving back the conflict premium today. The pushback we’d anticipate from the room: the convoy plan is on a 48-hour shelf life that’s already lapsed once, refined-fuel inventories don’t refill in weeks, and Mizuho’s house view stays at a 4.50% 10-year Treasury yield if Hormuz remains compromised. Even granting the headline path is now de-escalation, the inventory hole is what binds — Europe’s jet fuel tank runs out by June regardless of ceasefire timing, and any single fresh tanker incident sends the front-month vertical while the back-end barely moves. The asymmetric setup leans long the Brent-tracking ETF $BNO into the next headline; loss of $85 sustained on Brent would be the curve admitting the supply scare is finally over and the trade dies. Refiner exposure and aluminum equities work as second-order expressions if you want to fade peace without owning oil futures direct.

The bond market is pricing rate cuts back in — and the data may not let it

Sticky inflation, a hawkish Fed split, and a $4-gas summer mean the rate-cut thesis is on a shorter leash than the curve thinks.

ANZ projects April headline CPI accelerating to 3.9% YoY on a 40% surge in gasoline prices since February, while core CPI prints a more modest 0.2% MoM (2.6% YoY) — that headline-vs-core gap is exactly the divergence the Fed has to call right. Bank views on Friday’s payrolls span 50k (JPM Feroli, the dovish corner), 80k (BofA, with private at 90k), to 100k (UBS, the hawk) versus 65k consensus — UBS also sees CPI at 4.0% YoY for April, which would be an outright hawkish setup. ING’s piece captures the divide directly: the Fed is split between an upside inflation risk (energy plus tariff pass-through) and cooling growth/wage data, with the Warsh leadership transition adding governance noise on top of policy ambiguity. Mizuho’s call is the most explicit hawk — no more Fed cuts in 2026 with the market mispricing the cut path — versus Goldman’s mid-day view of two 25bp cuts this year and oil eventually receding to $90 by 4Q26. The pushback we’d anticipate: core inflation is locked in a three-month lag from oil prices, and the ISM manufacturing prices-paid index has soared more than 25 points in three months — you cannot credibly cut into a re-acceleration that’s already in the pipeline. Even granting the lag concern, the binding constraint isn’t where core prints in May — it’s the new supply of long-dated Treasuries hitting the auction calendar against a Fed that can’t move credibly. The cleanest read for someone with US fixed-income exposure is that the long-dated Treasury ETF $TLT remains priced for a softer landing than the data supports — a 3.0%+ core CPI print on May 12 would be the bond market’s confirmation that the rate-cut story is dead for 2026, and a sub-50k NFP Friday with a 4.4% unemployment tick would be the dovish counter that puts cuts firmly back on the table.

The AI capex super-cycle is no longer a forecast — earnings are confirming the build

Hyperscaler capex revisions, MAG7 earnings power, and Apple’s foundry pivot are stacking into a tape-defining theme that has multi-quarter legs.

Hyperscalers raised 2026 capex again in this earnings cycle, with $AMZN, $GOOGL, and $META all confirming AI-driven topline strength — Goldman now has 40% of S&P 500 EPS growth tied to the AI infrastructure trade. Goldman’s data center power note projects US data center capacity more than doubling from end-2025 to end-2027 (to 95GW), with annual additions accelerating from 8.5GW in 2025 to 36GW in 2027 and power demand running from 31GW to 66GW — that’s 8.5% of US peak summer demand by 2027. The Semis and Semi Equipment GICS sector — meaning the chip-makers and chip-equipment makers grouped together — is now over $10 trillion in market cap, the largest S&P subsector and bigger than German GDP. Taiwan’s Q1 2026 GDP grew 13.69% YoY, the highest reading since 1987, directly traceable to the foundry effect (Taiwan holds 70% market share in high-precision chips, with exports up 35.25% YoY). The Apple foundry pivot is the cleanest single-stock expression of the next leg — news that Apple is considering $INTC and Samsung as secondary processor suppliers alongside $TSM sent $INTC, $MU, and $SNDK higher on double-digit moves, and Goldman upgraded $AMD to BUY citing agentic AI driving server CPU tailwinds (with 2027/28 EPS estimates raised ~20% above Street). The pushback we’d anticipate is the tension Goldman’s own desk flagged: the risk to the AI trade isn’t a lack of demand but “too much success” — frontier-model gains becoming incremental and commoditizing compute economics. Layer in Morgan Stanley’s data showing hedge funds net selling AI Tech beneficiaries post-earnings, and the picks-and-shovels names — semicap, memory, networking — keep getting paid through July earnings unless a single MAG7 capex guide-down breaks the cycle. The cleanest expression for someone not already long is the memory complex via $MU and $SNDK or the broader semicap basket via $SOXX — an $NVDA close below its current breakout level sustained would be the first signal hyperscalers are pulling back, and a $META or $GOOGL capex guide-down at next earnings would be the harder confirmation.

The melt-up is real, but the positioning data says it’s fragile

Hedge funds are de-grossing into the rally, dispersion is at 17-year highs, and dealers are days away from short gamma — the rally has further to run, but the unwind risk is building underneath.

Hedge funds were net sellers of global equities last week, with US selling concentrated and US long/short net leverage dropping to 53% (39th percentile over 12 months). Morgan Stanley flags HFs actively reducing exposure to AI Tech beneficiaries (MAG7 and Semis) post-earnings, even as AI Power and Infrastructure (Nuclear/Onsite Power) keeps catching net buying. Goldman’s gross leverage came down 5.1pts to 307.8% as the desk net-sold both single names and macro products. Market dispersion in the US hit its highest level since 2009 in April with only 25% of stocks beating the market — first-percentile reading since 2004. Citi flags CTAs (trend-following computer funds that buy strength and sell weakness mechanically) at max-short positioning in 2-year, 5-year, and 10-year futures. JPMorgan describes equities as “synthetically short” with mutual fund managers under pressure to chase, and Goldman’s S&T desk says dealers are currently long gamma (meaning they sell rallies and buy dips, dampening the move) but a 1-3% further rally pushes them into short gamma — at which point dealers mechanically buy strength and sell weakness, amplifying both directions. The Market Ear’s framing is the cleanest: realized vol on up days is running higher than vol on down days — the textbook signature of an unstable rally — with Korea’s KOSPI, up 217% from “Liberation Day” lows, the leading indicator for how this kind of move ends. The bull case for the squeeze having further to run is that mutual fund managers are forced buyers — they cannot afford to stay underweight semis if AMD-style upgrades keep stacking — and retail call buying in $MU, $INTC, and $SNDK has surpassed $SPY, telling you speculative flow is still entering. The pushback is the fragility itself: dispersion at 17-year highs means the average stock isn’t participating, so a single MAG7 air-pocket cracks the index without much breadth cushion underneath. Even granting the squeeze has further to run, the asymmetric setup is owning short-dated index downside protection against the long delta — VIX call spreads (cheap bets that volatility spikes) into early-June capture both NVDA earnings and the NFP/CPI reaction without paying up for premium. A sustained $VIX break below 16 on another quiet week says the squeeze has more legs; an $SPX print below its 50-day with $VIX still under 18 would be the first signal the dealer-gamma flip has started.

$UBER, $APP, $DASH, $DIS, $APO, $MAR, $NRG, $OXY all report after the close — and $APP and $UBER are the cleanest read on whether the AI/secular long bench has fresh ammunition.

Even granting that, the AI-adjacent prints are the cleanest read on whether the next leg of the squeeze has names to chase: an $APP beat-and-raise restarts the speculative-call buying that has already concentrated in $MU, $INTC, and $SNDK — exactly the names Goldman’s TMT spec-sales desk has been pushing. The cleanest options expression is paired earnings volatility — owning $APP and $DASH straddles into the print captures the dispersion regime without picking a side, while short-dated $UBER calls into a beat-and-guide would be the cheapest way to lean long if Goldman’s desk view is right. A clean miss on $APP combined with a soft $UBER guide would be the harder signal that the secular-trade ammunition is thinning, which is the kind of single-day data point that can flip dealers into short gamma at the index level.
