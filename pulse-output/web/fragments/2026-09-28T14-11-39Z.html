2. THE MAIN EVENT

Iran’s ceasefire push just failed, and oil is pricing the war scenario

Iran’s ceasefire offer collapsed over the weekend, and Trump rejected the proposal again this morning. Crude is trading the escalation case research had flagged as the alternative, not the de-escalation markets had been leaning on.

  • Brent crude hit $108 a barrel Thursday, the highest since the conflict began in late February (SEB, Danske Bank), up 36% over that stretch (Goldman Sachs), the level that needs to hold for the war-premium trade to keep working.
  • Iran’s offer traded a seven-day ceasefire for $12 billion in unfrozen assets and gave the US a 4-5 day window to accept. The window closed without a deal, which is why oil didn’t fade back down.
  • Daily oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz have collapsed to 4.9 million barrels a day this year from 21.2 million in 2025 (JPMorgan), the physical supply story underneath the price move, not just headline risk.
  • US retail diesel just hit a record $6.50 a gallon (BofA, citing AAA data), and a possible ban on US diesel exports is now a live risk eleven desks are flagging. A ban would squeeze supply further.
  • Saudi Arabia has pushed crude exports back up to 3.6 million barrels a day this month from just 900,000 in August, ship-tracking data show, proof the chokepoint isn’t fully closed and the only thing keeping this from becoming a full 2026 oil shock.

When a ceasefire offer collapses and Hormuz flows sit at roughly a quarter of normal, oil has to price a sustained war premium instead of a peace dividend, and that premium is hitting core inflation as much as it’s hitting pump prices. Goldman estimates the rebound in oil and refined-product prices adds 3 to 5 basis points (hundredths of a percent) to monthly core inflation through year-end, and Wednesday’s core PCE reading is the first place that pass-through should show up. Saudi exports roughly quadrupling off August’s low is the strongest evidence the supply shock isn’t total, but inventories are still low enough and freight costs still high enough that Brent could push past $150 if the standoff drags on instead of resolving. Trump’s rejection this morning, alongside Saudi Arabia’s foreign minister meeting Rubio in Washington, shows the diplomatic track is still open even as the ceasefire itself failed, which is exactly the setup JPMorgan’s scenario framework was built for: a real deal still flips this to energy prices collapsing and small caps leading a relief rally, but until one lands, the bank’s no-deal case (oil near $100, the 10-year above 5%) is what’s printing. Brent holding above $108 is what keeps that no-deal case printing.

3. BRIEFS

The bond selloff is a Fed bet, and the desks are split on how far it runs

The 10-year Treasury yield broke out of a three-year range to fresh highs this week, and the MOVE index (the bond market’s version of the VIX) has jumped sharply over the past two sessions, a rates-volatility problem as much as a yield-level one that’s starting to spill into how other assets trade. Real yields and Fed-hike repricing are driving the move more than inflation fear (Deutsche Bank). AllianzGI and Goldman disagree on how far it runs: AllianzGI pencils in 75 basis points of further hikes by mid-2027, calling this the first step in a longer cycle, while Goldman calls for a roughly 10 basis point pullback in the 10-year over the next three months. The short $TLT call this pulse flagged on September 22 for a move to 5.2% got there this morning, with 5.5% next. 5.5% is the next level that decides whether the hiking-cycle repricing keeps running.

AI’s power crunch has a $50 billion fix

Global data-center power demand is set to grow 170% by 2030, with US demand hitting 108 gigawatts by then (Goldman), against a 57-gigawatt supply gap building through 2028 (Morgan Stanley). Its fix is a shift to direct-current power systems, which it expects to reach 25-40% of new data-center capacity by 2030 and open a $50 billion market in battery backup units alone. On the demand side, Goldman’s “AI shopping toll” thesis rates $AMZN, $META, $GOOGL, $V and $MA all Buy, betting agentic commerce lets platforms charge a toll on every purchase they route, with price targets of $725 on $META and $375 on $AMZN. Jefferies is the loudest skeptic here, warning the capex bill still needs to show up as actual return on equity before the market keeps funding it unquestioned. Long $META and $AMZN on Goldman’s toll thesis, watching whether earnings guide toward those targets.

The rotation trade: fade the concentration before it breaks

Near record highs, market breadth is quietly deteriorating. Raymond James argues leadership stays this narrow until borrowing costs stabilize, and the 10-year sitting near 5.22% this morning says they haven’t. Goldman’s own chart of the year makes the same point structurally: half the stocks in the S&P now move opposite the index itself, a concentration extreme the bank says raises the odds of a sharp reversal if the AI cohort stumbles. Eastspring is asking the harder question on AI spending specifically, whether the capital being spent is translating into return on equity yet rather than just revenue growth. None of that is a call to sell the AI trade outright, but it is a case for not paying up for the average stock in an index where the gains are this concentrated. Long $RSP over $SPY, paired with puts on Goldman’s own momentum index (GSP1MOMO) as the hedge while this week’s shock is still live.
