2. THE MAIN EVENT

The chip rally nobody trusts

The AI hardware trade is back out front this morning, and the fight between the desks calling demand insatiable and the desk calling it a bubble is the whole game.

  • Capital spending (capex) at the five biggest US tech firms is running at $755B for 2026, up more than 80% from last year (Goldman), and the big cloud builders (hyperscalers) are set to go from $725B this year to $1 trillion by 2027 (UBS). The buildout was never the question.
  • Broadcom’s latest quarter landed on insatiable demand for custom AI chips, and weekly memory-chip fund inflows hit +$2.3B with volume running 60% above its 30-day average. Money is still chasing the picks-and-shovels layer, not fleeing it.
  • Samsung booked a record 89.4 trillion won operating profit but missed on revenue, and the stock fell 9% in Korea. First crack: a great number that still got sold.
  • Even a bull is trimming the crowd. The chips-and-hardware weighting at UBS is down from 76% to 61%, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 4.6% Tuesday before bouncing back in today’s session.
  • US data-center opposition has jumped to 70% of projects from 40% last August, with 20 projects cancelled in the first quarter of 2026 (JPMorgan). The physical buildout is hitting real-world friction.

Memory is the tightest link in the chain. A planned 20% third-quarter price hike plus silicon wafers that stay in short supply mean the supply-constrained names keep their pricing power even if the index chops. That is why a record-profit miss can still sink Samsung 9% while the memory trade underneath stays intact. The market is repricing which part of AI hardware is safe, not whether AI hardware is over.

The bear case is Goldman’s, made out loud: AI hardware is structurally over-earning and highly cyclical, starting to trace the same parabola-then-reversal path silver ran earlier this year. That carries weight from the house whose capex numbers everyone else quotes. The bulls push back on durability, arguing the funding-shortfall worry does not hold and that demand is broadening from a pure chip story into the full supply chain, from power to cooling to memory (UBS, JPMorgan). Both reads can be right at once, with the buildout compounding for years while the most stretched semi names still correct 20% inside it. The play is to own the layers with pricing power and cash flow. Long memory ($MU) and the power names feeding the datacenters ($VST) over the crowded chip designers in $SOXX.

3. BRIEFS

Oil futures are pricing peace the physical market can’t see

Brent snapped from $72 to near $79 on the Gulf flare-up, yet the futures curve barely moved, and that gap is the trade.

Brent jumped roughly 6% Wednesday to the $78 to $79 area (JPMorgan) after Iran said it hit US targets in the Gulf, but the 12-month futures price (what oil costs for delivery a year out) carries only about a $5 premium over pre-conflict levels (Deutsche Bank), and oil ETFs actually leaked lower in early US trading with $USO -1.67%. Physical barrels are pricing scarcity while the paper curve prices resolution. One desk warns the real Battle of Hormuz may just be starting, while several flag a US-Iran memorandum of understanding (a preliminary deal) as the off-ramp. The asymmetry favors the side the curve is underpricing. Long $BNO, or energy equity through $XLE, into the next Hormuz headline, with the $85 year-end Brent target at UBS as the upside marker.

Warsh has the gavel and the bond market is bracing

The curve is now priced for a Warsh Fed that hikes, and whether he delivers is the only thing the bond market cares about.

Kevin Warsh was confirmed as Fed Chair by the narrowest Senate margin in the chair’s history, and the bond market took the hawkish read. Roughly three-tenths of a percentage point of hikes (just over one quarter-point move) are now priced by December, up from almost nothing in late April (Deutsche Bank), and BofA forecasts three-quarters of a percentage point of hikes across 2026 with the 30-year yield already at 5.07%. Cutting against that, HSBC holds its call that the Fed stays parked at 3.50% to 3.75% through 2027, and some desks call the priced tightening overdone. The question is whether Warsh actually raises, or whether that priced tightening bleeds back out first. If the market has over-bought the hawk, long $TLT, and HSBC’s 30-year inflation-protected trade is the same bet that also pays you to hold it.

Gold is coiling under its record

Buyers are back in gold on the Gulf and a softer dollar, and the payoff from here skews to the upside.

Gold tested $4,200 overnight and held firm in early trading, with $GLD +1.08% as the Gulf premium and an easing dollar pulled bids back in. It has already round-tripped hard: a record $5,450/oz clocked in late January (HSBC) before a 22% slide off the late-February conflict start (Deutsche Bank), even as physical jewelry demand softened. The relative-value tell is gold miners priced at a 12% earnings yield (annual profit as a share of the share price, the highest of any sector, per BofA), which is the market paying you to wait. If gold chops while inflation-adjusted bond yields stay high, the miner discount lingers and you’re stuck holding dead money. If the central-bank bid and a live Gulf premium re-rate the metal back toward that January high, you want to already be positioned. Long $GLD on dips.
