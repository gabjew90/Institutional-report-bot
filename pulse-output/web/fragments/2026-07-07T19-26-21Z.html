2. THE MAIN EVENT

The crowded trade cracks, chips first

The forced selling in semiconductors is a positioning unwind, not a broken demand story, and it is the most crowded book on the Street getting sold.

  • Semiconductors logged their worst two-day stretch in over a year, down 10%, closing below their 20-day average two sessions running. A clean technical break like that is what forces trend-followers to cut.
  • Semiconductor ETF volume ran 1.5x the prior day heading into a long weekend. Heavy selling into thin liquidity is how a forced de-risking accelerates, not how dip-buyers behave.
  • Hedge funds and active managers are trimming the momentum factor, the basket of recent winners, across JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley desk data. When the winners get sold hardest and the fundamentals have not broken, the position was crowded.
  • May semiconductor industry sales still grew 119% year over year (JPMorgan). The end market is not rolling over, which is the tell that this is about positioning.
  • Global capital spending is still running at 9.8% annualized in Q2 after 11.3% in Q1 (JPMorgan). The spending that fills chip order books has not turned.

A de-gross is when leveraged funds cut their most crowded positions to pull down risk, and it feeds on itself. The market’s leaders roll over first because the names that ran furthest hold the most weak-handed money, so they fall fastest the moment selling starts, which trips the next fund’s risk model, which sells more. That mechanism is why the drop looks violent while the chip demand behind it keeps compounding. It is playing out live today, with $QQQ down 2% on the session as the crowded book came down again. The bull case belongs to Goldman: broad upside remains as chip demand normalizes, and 119% sales growth says the cycle has real legs left, so this is a dip inside an uptrend. That read is fair on a six-month horizon, but right now the marginal seller is in control. Two straight closes below the 20-day average on 1.5x volume is a book being reduced, and those unwinds usually run for days, not hours, before they exhaust. Short $SMH into the unwind rather than catching it, and the signal it is over is a reclaim of the 20-day average on falling volume, not rising.

3. BRIEFS

AI infrastructure is now the whole earnings story, and that is the risk

The buildout is real and still accelerating, but the market has quietly bet its entire earnings growth on one theme.

AI infrastructure is the most-backed theme in the corpus, and the numbers are big. 60% of the S&P 500’s earnings growth this quarter rides on AI infrastructure alone (Goldman), and analysts have underestimated hyperscaler capital spending by an average of 45 percentage points a year for three years running. The largest tech companies are on track to spend $700B in 2026 (ING), a sevenfold jump since 2021, with $600B of AI-related debt funding it since 2024. The tension is concentration: earnings upside spans most sub-sectors (Goldman), while ING warns the heavy spending is starting to squeeze cash flow and slow per-share earnings growth. When 60% of the index’s earnings growth rides on one trade, the index is only as safe as that trade, and the rotation out of tech into the rest of the market is already underway with room to run (JPMorgan). Own the broadening directly. Long $RSP over $SPY, paired with small-cap $IWM, so the exposure is earnings growth that is not hostage to a single crowded theme.

SpaceX opens a trillion-dollar lane

Four desks put Buy ratings on SpaceX in the same window, and the reusability math is why.

Four desks initiated SpaceX with Buy ratings in the same window, targets running from $200 to $300 (Citi at the low end, Morgan Stanley at $300). The shared thesis is mechanical: Starship carries more than five times the payload of Falcon 9 with full reusability, which collapses the cost of reaching orbit and unlocks a space economy the banks size into the trillions over the next decade-plus. Some go further, framing space-based computing as a genuinely new source of AI capacity as SpaceX stacks Starship, its satellite network, and orbital power. The catch is timing. This is a pre-revenue, catalyst-dependent story where the next two to three years of launches have to deliver, and a single failed campaign resets the clock. For a speculative sleeve, long $SPCX to own the launch-cost collapse.

Hormuz is back on, and the premium came with it

The war premium banks said was priced out just came roaring back into oil.

Research this window had the Strait of Hormuz cooling off, with front-month Brent down near $72 on reopening headlines and most of the war premium already out of the price (Goldman). That framing is now stale. Today Iran attacked tankers using a U.S. Navy route, the strait’s threat level was raised to severe, and Qatar blamed Iran for a strike on a tanker. Oil gapped on it, $BNO +4.86% and $USO +4.49% on the session. The asymmetry the research flagged just paid off: the premium had drained out of the price while the conflict stayed unresolved, so the cheap energy upside was the trade. It is no longer cheap, but the escalation is live and there is no ceasefire holding it down, which keeps energy bid as insurance against the next tanker headline. Long $XLE, and long $USO into the next Hormuz escalation.

Buybacks are the floor under the market

While funds cut risk, corporate treasurers are the steady bid, and that matters most when positioning wobbles.

Goldman’s base case models $31.8B of fresh buyback authorization spread roughly evenly over 2.5 years, and Alphabet has already cut its share count 9.5% from 2020 to 2025 buying its own stock (ING). Buybacks shrink the share count, which mechanically lifts earnings per share even when profits are flat, and they are a price-insensitive buyer that shows up hardest when everyone else is selling. The catch is the blackout window, the weeks before earnings when companies cannot repurchase, so the floor thins out right as the calendar turns. If the buyback bid is holding the market up, own the biggest and most consistent repurchasers. Long $GOOGL, and the thesis breaks if the blackout lines up with the next leg of the unwind and no corporate bid shows up to catch it.
