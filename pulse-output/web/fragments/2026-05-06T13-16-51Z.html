INSIGHTS & ALPHA

Hormuz peace memo collides with a still-broken supply chain

The Iran story flipped overnight, but the supply damage is locked in. ING’s commodities desk: roughly 13 million barrels per day of oil supply remains disrupted, US refined-fuel inventories are at multi-year lows — gasoline stocks at the lowest seasonal level since 2014, distillates the lowest since 2005. Bank of America put a name on it: “the summer of $4 gas,” with futures keeping pump prices sticky above $4/gal through summer regardless of what happens at the negotiating table. ANZ flags aluminum specifically — 9% of global primary output sits in the conflict zone, the futures curve is in its deepest backwardation since 2007 (meaning physical metal right now costs more than metal three months out, which is what a real shortage looks like), and 1.8% of 2026 global production is at risk. Goldman’s basics note adds that Brent December ‘26 futures held firmly above $90 even as the front month sold off — the curve is telling you the scarcity premium is real for months to come, not days.

The bull case Mizuho and Rabobank are leaning on: a credible US-Iran framework reopens the Strait, oil rolls toward Goldman’s $90 4Q26 target, and the energy-driven inflation pulse fades fast enough for the Fed to cut twice this year. The bear case ING and The Market Ear desks keep coming back to is structural: the inventory hole takes months to refill even with a deal, and a single fresh headline (a tanker hit, a rejected memo, a stalled signing) sends Brent vertical because, in Mizuho International’s words, the market is “one oil shock away from retesting yield highs.” The CMA CGM container ship being hit in Hormuz this morning, the same morning the peace-memo headlines stacked up, is exactly that asymmetric risk in real time.

Trade Implication. Long $BNO (the Brent oil ETF) into next week, paired with May puts as protection. Why: even if a peace deal lands tomorrow, US gasoline and distillate inventories are at multi-year lows — refined fuel doesn’t refill in days, so Brent stays bid for months while the supply hole closes. Risk: shipping traffic actually resuming through Hormuz with no follow-on incidents AND product inventories visibly rebuilding — that’s the market saying the scarcity premium is gone, and the puts cap your downside on the long.

The bond market doesn’t believe rate cuts are coming back

Banks are split harder on the Fed path than they have been in months. Mizuho EMEA flat-out says no more Fed cuts this year — the market is mispricing — and pegs a 4.50% 10-year yield target if Hormuz stays compromised. UBS economists forecast 100k April payrolls (well above the 65k consensus) and core CPI at 4.0% year-over-year, both pointing toward a hawkish Fed. ANZ pencils headline CPI accelerating to 3.9% in April on the gasoline surge (gas is up 40% since February), while keeping core CPI at 2.6% — that’s the headline-versus-core split the market doesn’t know how to trade yet. ING’s Fed-watcher piece says the FOMC is genuinely divided: if the Strait reopens and energy flows resume, rate cuts remain more likely than hikes. Bank of America’s labor preview: 80k Nonfarm Payrolls for April, unemployment holding 4.3%. Crédit Agricole’s preview of today’s Treasury Quarterly Refunding pegs $125bn supply ($58bn 3-year, $42bn 10-year, $25bn 30-year) and expects Treasury to maintain the “no need to increase auction sizes” guidance held since February 2024 — any deviation toward bigger long-dated auctions means more 30-year supply hitting at the worst possible time, and yields on the long end break higher.

The optimistic read JPMorgan is pushing: a formal Iran deal triggers lower oil, the long end of the yield curve gets unstuck from inflation fears, dollar weakens, risk runs further. The risk side Mizuho keeps hammering: Treasury issuance plus an oil-driven inflation pulse is the one combination that forces a 30-year breakdown, with UK 30-year government bonds already at fresh post-1998 highs as the canary that says long bonds globally can’t absorb supply when inflation is sticky. JPM’s flow-and-positioning desk notes trend-following computer funds (CTAs) sold bonds aggressively as yields rose — that flow is one-way until an inflation print breaks the other way.

Trade Implication. Long $TLT (the 20+ Year Treasury ETF) into the May 12 CPI release. Why: if core CPI comes in around 2.6%-2.8% like ANZ projects — meaning the gasoline spike is a headline story but isn’t bleeding into wages, services, or rents — the bond market has over-priced the hawkish scenario, and yields fall hard once the headline-vs-core gap is visible. Risk: a core CPI print above 3.0% — that’s the bond market saying the energy shock has bled into the underlying inflation trend, and the Fed-on-hold-forever camp wins.

AI capex is no longer a forecast — it’s earnings

The cross-bank capex revisions this week are the biggest one-week shift the desks can remember. Goldman now forecasts US data center capacity more than doubling from end-2025 to end-2027, hitting 95GW, with annual additions accelerating from 8.5GW (2025) to 13.6GW (2026) to 36GW (2027) — a 4x ramp in two years. Goldman’s mid-day color: 40% of S&P 500 earnings growth is now tied to the AI infrastructure trade, and hyperscaler 2026 capex was raised across $AMZN, $GOOGL, and $MSFT this earnings cycle. Goldman upgraded $AMD to BUY citing AI-agent demand for server CPUs, with 2027/28 EPS estimates roughly 20% above the Street consensus. $ANET printed a clean 1Q26 beat (EPS $0.87 vs $0.81 consensus, revenue $2.71B vs $2.62B), and orders waiting to ship (what banks call “billings”) accelerated to 54% year-over-year growth — Goldman reiterates Buy with a $188 target, while Citi trimmed its target to $173 on advanced-chip supply constraints expected to drag for one to two years. $SMCI gross margin came in at 10.1% versus 6.65% guidance despite a top-line miss, and Citi raised $PLTR’s price target to $225. Morgan Stanley’s chart pack shows hyperscaler capex projections raised again with significant 2027 step-ups — and an AlphaWise survey says US consumer sentiment on AI is net +16 (44% favorable, 28% negative).

The bull case Goldman, JPM, and Citi are all running with: this is a multi-year build, the equipment makers and memory players (the picks-and-shovels) get paid before the chip cycle peaks, and the Semi & Semi Equip GICS sector is now a $10 trillion market cap — bigger than German GDP, and the third-largest “country” by market cap globally. The bear case Morgan Stanley’s hedge-fund prime brokerage data is flashing: hedge funds are actively reducing exposure to AI tech beneficiaries (Mag7 plus chips) post-earnings, US long/short hedge fund net leverage (a measure of how aggressively funds are positioned long) dropped to 53% — the 39th percentile of the last 12 months. The Market Ear adds the kicker: $NVDA has decoupled below the $SMH chip ETF — relative ratio collapsing to early-2024 dislocation levels. When the leader of a rally lags the index, that’s usually how rallies end.

Trade Implication. Long $SOXX (the broad semiconductor ETF) over $NVDA single-name into mid-May. Why: the AI capex trade is broadening — memory ($MU, $SNDK), CPUs ($AMD, $INTC), and networking ($ANET) are all participating, while $NVDA itself is lagging the basket. The ETF captures the breadth without the single-stock funding-trade risk The Market Ear is flagging on $NVDA. Risk: a $META or $GOOGL capex guide-down at next earnings, or $NVDA breaking its breakout level on heavy volume — that’s the signal the picks-and-shovels thesis is cracking.

Squeeze tape with the warning light blinking

Every desk in the corpus describes the rally with the fingerprints of a forced melt-up, not a healthy expansion. The Market Ear’s “Upside Pain” note: realized volatility on UP days is 16.9% versus 14.6% on down days — the inverse of how a stable rally usually trades, because in a stable rally the big moves come on declines and the gains are calm. Downside skew (the price the option market puts on puts versus calls) has compressed to the 14th percentile, meaning almost no one is paying up for crash protection. Retail call-buying in tiny clip sizes (under 10 contracts) is surging, and systematic funds are still net buyers. Morgan Stanley’s hedge-fund data shows US-based long/short net leverage at 53% (39th percentile of the last 12 months) and hedge funds net selling US equities even as the index rips — that pattern is positioning getting squeezed out of underweights, not new conviction money. The Nasdaq 100 is officially in a bull market (more than 20% above its local lows) with the VIX resetting to roughly 17 (late-January levels). Citi notes trend-following computer funds (CTAs) are at maximum short positioning in 2-year, 5-year, and 10-year Treasuries — the rates side is just as crowded one-way as the equity side. Korea’s KOSPI is up 217% from its “Liberation Day” lows in what The Market Ear called an “upside panic,” and SK Hynix’s relative strength index is at peak extreme — the AI mania has gone full vertical in the cleanest expression of the trade.

The bull case Citi and JPM are running: poor breadth and crowded shorts have always been bullish at this stage of a melt-up, mutual fund managers are forced to chase, and the squeeze is structurally one-way until earnings disappoint. The bear case The Market Ear keeps surfacing: realized volatility being higher on up days is the textbook signature of an unstable rally, and when retail call-buying meets compressed downside skew, the unwind is fast and wide. Goldman’s sales-and-trading desk flagged that short-dated option premium is “ownable” given current dealer positioning — translation: dealers are positioned in a way that means they have to amplify moves, so options bought now pay off quickly when something breaks. The desk is telling you to buy protection while it’s cheap.

Trade Implication. Long $VIXY (the VIX futures ETF) calls dated into the May 12 CPI release. Why: downside skew at the 14th percentile means VIX calls are unusually cheap, and the same realized-vol-on-up-days pattern that preceded the August 2024 unwind is back. A single hot CPI print or hyperscaler capex guide-down repositions everyone at once and the VIX repricing is violent. Risk: the VIX breaking decisively below 16 with another quiet week — that’s the market saying the squeeze has further to run before any unwind, and the VIX call premium decays fast in calm tape.

Apple’s foundry pivot is the single-stock story this market is mispricing

Goldman, JPM, and The Market Ear all flagged that Apple is now seriously considering Intel and Samsung as backup chip-manufacturing partners alongside TSMC — and the read-through is unusually clean. Goldman’s TMT Spec Sales note: $AMD upgraded to BUY on AI-agent demand for server CPUs, but the memory rip ($SNDK +12% leading the sector, $MU and $INTC in double digits) was driven explicitly by the Apple-foundry headlines. JPM’s morning desk: $INTC and $MU alone have contributed roughly 350 basis points (3.5 percentage points) to the S&P 500’s year-to-date performance, with the desk calling the market “synthetically short” on these names — meaning so many funds are underweight that any rally forces them to chase. Citi raised $PLTR’s price target to $225 on a separate AI catalyst, but the cleaner read is on the chip-foundry side: if Apple actually splits a meaningful chunk of its silicon orders between TSMC, Intel Foundry Services, and Samsung Foundry, you’ve got a multi-year revenue stream landing on $INTC — a stock the buy-side has spent two years left for dead. Goldman’s broader chip call: the Semi GICS sector is now a $10 trillion market cap, the largest S&P subsector and globally the third-largest “country” by market cap.

The bull case Goldman desk is leaning on: Apple’s foundry split would be a multi-year contract, Intel Foundry Services gets the validation that’s been missing for three years, and the memory complex ($MU, $SNDK) catches the Apple-content-per-device tailwind alongside it. The bear case to take seriously: Goldman reiterated its Sell rating on $SMCI even after the EPS beat — the desk is flagging that not every AI hardware story deserves a rerating, and a “first-take” beat doesn’t fix a stretched valuation. $INTC’s positioning is stale-bullish on consensus underweights — if the Apple deal slips or comes in smaller than the leaks suggest, the unwind is sharp because everyone got squeezed in on the same trade.

Trade Implication. Long $MU (Micron) into UBER’s earnings tonight as a memory-complex proxy. Why: the Apple-foundry leak directly benefits the memory makers (more DRAM and NAND content per Apple device), and Micron is the cleanest US-listed expression versus SK Hynix (Korean-listed) or Samsung (Korean-listed). The Apple narrative supplies the catalyst even on a non-Apple earnings night. Risk: the Apple-Intel/Samsung headlines getting walked back or framed as “exploratory” with no firm volume commitment — that’s the market saying the leak was bigger than the actual deal, and the memory rally fades fast.
