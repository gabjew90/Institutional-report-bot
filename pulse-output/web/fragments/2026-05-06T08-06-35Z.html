INSIGHTS & ALPHA

The Hormuz oil shock has a half-life — and the inflation print outlasts the headline

Brent ripped to $113.9/bbl on Monday’s strikes (US hit Iranian speedboats; Iran hit a UAE port), and the 6-month Brent futures contract printed $91.99/bbl — a high it hasn’t seen since 2022 — meaning the curve is pricing scarcity well past any quick ceasefire. Goldman’s Oil Analyst pegs landed refined product inventories at 41 days of demand, with global stocks on track for an 8-year low by end-May even if Hormuz flows normalize tomorrow. Hormuz crude exports are running at 4% of normal. Polymarket odds of normalization by end-June dropped to 40% per the GS Basics desk note — meaning the market is, if anything, under-pricing the holding pattern. The transmission into US assets is mechanical: 2-year US inflation swaps (the inflation rate priced into the bond market for the next two years) hit 3.13%, highest since Nov 2022, and that’s what put 17 basis points (hundredths of a percent) of hikes by April 2027 into Fed funds futures. Mizuho is now telling clients to expect zero Fed cuts this year and warns the market is mispriced for cuts.

Bulls argue de-escalation is the base case — Pentagon’s Project Freedom is defensive, Trump just paused the reopening operation, and oil sold off 2-3% Tuesday on peace-deal hopes. The bear case Goldman’s commodities desk flags is that even if the Strait reopens, the 8-year-low product inventories (jet fuel, diesel, naphtha) take weeks to refill — the inflation pressure is locked in before the geopolitical fix. Occidental cutting production guidance Tuesday was the single-name confirmation. Trade Implication: Long $USO into any peace-deal headline fade — the curve is pricing scarcity through autumn, not next week. Stop on a confirmed shipping resumption (UKMTO data, not Trump tweets). Pair long $XLE against an $SPY hedge if you want energy exposure without index beta.

Yields are breaking out without a Fed signal — long-dated bonds are repricing on their own

The 10-year Treasury yield punched through 4.44% to a 9-month high; the 30-year is testing 5% per the Goldman Sachs S&T desk. That move happened with no Fed speak and no surprise CPI — just the oil shock plus Treasury supply expectations colliding. Crédit Agricole’s note ahead of today’s Treasury Quarterly Refunding Announcement (the schedule for upcoming Treasury bond auctions) flags a $990B Treasury General Account balance — well above the $850B Q1 and $900B Q2 targets — meaning the Treasury bill (short-term) issuance pipeline is going to expand by mid-May. JPM’s desk thinks Treasury removes the “at least” language from its coupon (longer-dated bond) auction guidance today, which would shove intermediate yields higher. Fed funds is pricing 17 basis points of hikes by April 2027, vs cuts a month ago; ECB June hike is a 99% lock per pricing in DB’s morning note.

Mizuho expects 10Y UST to drift to 4.50% and 10Y Bund to 3.50%. The bull case Mizuho lays out: the US is energy-independent and fiscally cushioned, so the Fed can hold while Europe hikes — that’s a long-dollar trade. The bear case JPM pushes is that core inflation is locked in via energy plus sticky services, and you can’t cut into a re-acceleration. Tuesday’s ISM Services Prices reading at 70.7 — same as the prior month, no relief — is exactly the data point that keeps cuts off the table. Trade Implication: Short $TLT (long-dated Treasury bond ETF) into the QRA — if Treasury signals more coupon supply, intermediate yields take another leg up. Pair with long $UUP (dollar bull ETF) for the rate-differential trade (the gap between US and European interest rates that drives the dollar). Invalidation: a clean ceasefire that sends Brent below $90 — that breaks the inflation impulse and puts cuts back on the curve.

The squeeze is in fragile territory — dealer gamma and CTA flows are now driving the tape

The Market Ear’s “Upside Pain” note nails the structure: realized volatility is asymmetric — 16.9% on rally days versus 14.6% on declines — which is the textbook signature of dealers being short gamma into upside (when dealers are short gamma, they’re forced to buy strength and sell weakness, amplifying both directions; the 1-3% upside zone is where they flip). Put skew (the gap between put and call prices — when puts cost more, traders are paying for downside protection) is at the 14th percentile, meaning almost no one is hedging. Big-tech (Mag7) call option volumes are surging while put activity stays muted. CTAs (trend-following computer funds) are chasing higher; retail call buying under 10 contracts is exploding. Goldman’s prime brokerage data shows L/S gross leverage at 209-211% (96th percentile since 2010) — but net leverage at 51-53% (21st-39th percentile). Translation: hedge funds are running enormous long-and-short books, but the directional bet is small. They are not committed to this rally.

Morgan Stanley’s HF Highlights confirms it: hedge funds cut AI Mag7 + Semis exposure post-earnings while retail soaked up QQQ — the third-largest QQQ inflow on record stacked against the second-largest hedge fund tech selling in a decade per TME. Bulls argue the squeeze has legs into NVDA earnings late May. The bear case: market concentration at 41% has historically preceded crashes (40% threshold per TME), and HFs sitting at 39th-percentile net leverage means a fast unwind has plenty of room. Trade Implication: Own $VIXY calls cheap into the 1-3% upside zone — short-dated convexity (where the position pays off bigger as the move accelerates) is the cleanest hedge against the gamma-driven mechanics. If you want to ride the squeeze, $IGV May 90/98 call spread (TME’s flagged trade) catches the most-shorted, lightest-positioned bucket. Invalidation: NDX rolls below the 100-day moving average — that breaks the squeeze structure.
