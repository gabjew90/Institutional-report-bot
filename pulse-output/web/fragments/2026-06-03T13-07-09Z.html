2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

Hormuz stays closed longer than equities are pricing

The split inside the corpus is about timing, not direction. Banks expect a reopening eventually, but stocks are pricing it sooner than the physical-oil desks.

  • Rabobank assumes the Strait stays out of normal action through September, with 122 vessels already rerouted under US blockade enforcement
  • Rabobank pegs cumulative lost production at 2.5 to 2.8B barrels under a 7-month closure, with the current global oil deficit at 11 million barrels per day
  • Brent is trading $94 per SEB and $97.5 per Mizuho’s morning quote, with a $10 risk premium in the 6-month future
  • MUFG’s base case for two Fed cuts in 2026 is explicitly conditional on the Strait reopening and oil rolling back toward $75
  • SEB’s reopening scenario has WTI converging to $75 and the ECB and Fed pricing in 35bps and 25bps of cuts respectively

Physical oil markets are pricing tightness because vessel reroutes, inventory drawdowns, and a 7-month closure scenario all imply refined-fuel shortfalls inside months. Equity markets are pricing resolution because every overnight ceasefire headline gets bought. Overnight made the split sharper, not softer. Trump claims a nuclear concession from Iran in the same window the IRGC reportedly attacks the US Fifth Fleet and a Kuwait airport. The optimistic read is SEB’s: backwardation in the futures curve already shows the supply scare is in price, and a verified reopening flushes $10 of premium fast. The risk Rabobank presses is that a formal verified reopening is the trigger, not a tweet, and ceasefire headlines have broken inside weeks twice already this year. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz escalation headline. Skip $USO as the hedge, the futures roll noise dominates the spread on any given day.

AI infrastructure consensus keeps moving higher, and the bottleneck is the trade

The biggest theme in the corpus is also the broadest. Capex forecasts keep getting revised up, and the best place to play it is the supply chain, not the hyperscalers themselves.

  • UBS raised 2026 AI capex to $820B and 2027 to $990B, with 85% concentrated in the big four cloud platforms
  • Combined capex across the top four hyperscalers is projected at $5.3 trillion FY25 through FY30, framing the spend as a multi-year structural floor (Goldman)
  • Cloud growth at the major platforms accelerated to 40% year-over-year in Q1 2026 from 34% in Q4 2025, with aggregate backlogs at $2 trillion (UBS)
  • Goldman lifted the Taiwan index target to 51,000 and Korea’s KOSPI to 12,000 on AI hardware exposure and a memory upcycle the market is still underpricing
  • Printed circuit assembly manufacturing prices are up 150% year-over-year, a direct AI-driven inflation signal inside the supply chain (MUFG)
  • Goldman’s TMT desk has ASML capacity expansion debated at 120 to 130 EUV machines (up from 90), potentially triggered by July TSMC capex upgrades

The mechanism is unchanged. Hyperscaler capex is being financed by mid-cycle cash flow rather than debt, and the supply chain in memory, capacitors, substrates, and optical components is still capacity constrained. The bottleneck is now being named explicitly. Bulls argue the $2 trillion cloud backlog is signed contracts hyperscalers will spend against, not speculation. The risk Deutsche Bank presses is the +16% S&P move in two months has happened only four times since World War II (1987, post-oil shock, GFC, Covid), and three of those were post-recession bounces, not mid-cycle rallies. The cohort’s profit covers the debt many times over, so the build is fundable even if rates stay higher for longer. Long $MU as the cleanest single-name expression of the memory bottleneck, and $SOXX captures the broader chip-equipment leg without doubling the mega-cap hyperscaler bet.

ECB hikes next week, dollar should soften, but Hormuz is the wildcard

Banks are now near-unanimous on a June ECB hike. The unresolved question is whether oil-driven services inflation forces a second one.

  • Deutsche Bank: 99% probability of an ECB hike priced into rate futures for next week
  • Eurozone inflation accelerated to 3.2% in May (highest since September 2023), with core CPI at 2.5%, above consensus (KBC)
  • Natixis revised ECB hike expectations up by 65bps. End-2026 rate futures price 35bps of further tightening (SEB)
  • UBS targets EURUSD at 1.20 year-end, with SEB calling 1.19 as a rebound level
  • OCBC frames it as a 25bp insurance hike, with eurozone April PPI decelerating to 0.6% month-over-month from 3.4% in March (Rabobank)

Two things are driving the European inflation re-acceleration. Hormuz-driven energy costs are feeding services and headline simultaneously, exactly what the ECB cannot ignore even with a loose labor market. The dollar leg comes from the rate gap, a hiking ECB against a Fed that probably holds widens it in favor of the euro. The skeptical read SEB presses is that a Strait reopening flushes ECB cut pricing back in by 35bps within weeks, and the whole hawkish euro bid reverses inside days. The defense is that next week’s hike is locked in regardless of what oil does after the meeting, so September is the real decision point. The single question every trader has to answer is whether Hormuz reopens before the September ECB meeting or after. Long $FXE, paired with short $UUP, only works if the answer is “after.”

Labor cooling is the NFP Friday stress test

The strongest cross-desk convergence in the corpus is May payrolls printing below consensus. Until then, short-dated Treasuries are the trade.

  • Natixis: May NFP forecast 75k, versus 115k in April and 85k street consensus
  • JPMorgan: 75k forecast, same 85k street estimate
  • DBS Group has consensus at 85k, with job openings (JOLTS) at 7.6 million, highest since May 2024
  • Goldman has JOLTS at 7.77 million, highest since late 2024 (openings still resilient)
  • LSEG flags ADP private payrolls forecast of 117k for May, versus 109k in April
  • Bank of America: vacancy-to-unemployment ratio at 1.03, highest since January 2025 (more open jobs per unemployed worker)

The setup is a labor market where the openings side has rebounded sharply while actual hiring is decelerating. Natixis expects May unemployment to print 4.4% on the unrounded number versus 4.3% prior, a Fed-cut-friendly print without a recession signal. The risk JPMorgan and DBS press is that the headline-cooling story has been wrong for three months running and one weak print does not make a trend. The defense is the convergence. When Natixis, JPMorgan, DBS, and LSEG all set up sub-trend numbers and the openings data has hard-stopped expanding, the soft-landing print is overdue. The trigger Friday: a print above 110k or unemployment holding at 4.3% cracks the soft-landing thesis and the next two weeks belong to 30-year Treasury bonds getting sold. Until Friday morning, long $SHY (short-dated US Treasury ETF, the cleanest cut-priced expression).

Consensus is short protection, and the rotate-out is the trade

Multiple desks across Goldman, Deutsche Bank, MUFG, and SEB are flagging the same setup. The rally has gone too far, and the trade is to rotate out of the AI lead rather than short it.

  • Deutsche Bank chart of the day: S&P 500 rose +16% over April and May 2026, a pace seen only four times since World War II (1987, post-oil shock, GFC, Covid)

  • S&P downside protection (put options) sits at its cheapest in over a year, dealers are positioned in a way that smooths swings, and a 2 to 4% move either direction flips them into chase-the-move mode that amplifies the next leg

Positioning is extended (hedge fund tech adds, retail call chasing, dealers absorbing swings all stacked), valuations are tight, and the same names that drove the +16% rally are the most crowded. Downside protection at multi-year lows means S&P puts are essentially free. Bulls argue dealers dampen swings right up until something breaks the regime, and nothing has cracked yet. The risk the bears press is the ingredients are already stacked. Complacency, valuation, and one catalyst (NFP Friday, Hormuz news flow, a single mega-cap capex guide-down) is enough to flip dealers from absorbing the move to chasing it, kicking off the unwind. Watch order: NFP Friday, then any single-bank capex cut inside the next two weeks, then the next Hormuz escalation, then the next IPO sentiment tell. Long equal-weight $RSP versus cap-weight $SPY, with a small $SPY put added because protection has rarely been this cheap.
