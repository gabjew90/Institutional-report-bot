Payrolls triple the forecast

Friday, September 04, 2026 · 53 research analyses

1. RECAP

The August jobs report came in three times hotter than forecast at 8:30 this morning, and half an hour into the session the market is rebuilding the case for a Federal Reserve rate increase this month. Equities have barely reacted, with $SPY at -0.14% and $QQQ up 0.49%. The reaction landed everywhere else. $GLD fell 1.15%, $BTC dropped 2.23% overnight to $79,460, and the 2-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since January 2025. The one place the rate-increase story is not showing up is the 30-year, where the yield fell slightly this morning while shorter maturities rose.

  • August payrolls at 162,000, against a 55,000 estimate and a prior month that lost 23,000 jobs. Unemployment held at 4.1%, exactly the estimate. The 5-year Treasury yield rose 2.9 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percent) to 4.54% on it.
  • Thursday’s ISM services report has printed and the first desk reads are still coming in. The prices-paid part of that survey has been running at four-year highs, which is the inflation problem sitting behind this morning’s labor number rather than in front of it.
  • Diesel hit a record price as Ukrainian strikes knocked Russian refineries offline and Moscow banned exports in response. Crude itself sold off into the session, $USO -2.13% and $BNO -1.86%, so the shortage is in refined fuel rather than in barrels of crude. Gulf shipping through Hormuz is still running below its ten-day average.
  • Downside protection stayed cheap straight through the print. $VIXY fell 0.77% on a report that revived rate-increase risk, and the extra amount traders pay for downside protection on the S&P 500 over upside sits at the 2nd percentile of its history (Nomura). A week out from CPI and under two weeks from the September 16 Fed decision, nobody is paying up for insurance.

2. THE MAIN EVENT

The dovish week died at 8:30 this morning

September is a live Fed meeting again, and the labor data just took away the excuse for sitting still.

  • August payrolls printed 162,000 against the Street’s 55,000 and a prior month of minus 23,000. Three times the bar, and the widest gap between forecast and print of any release in this window.
  • Every published forecast in this window was under it. JPMorgan’s Feroli had 50,000, Deutsche Bank 65,000, Goldman 40,000. JPMorgan called 30,000 to 70,000 the range that lets the Fed sit still, and the print cleared the top of that by more than 90,000.
  • Pricing for a September increase had fallen to 51% from 63% at Wednesday’s close (Deutsche Bank) after Fed Governor Waller said he could support holding rates steady if the recent cooling in inflation held. That whole move rested on a soft labor market the official count just contradicted.
  • The prices-paid part of the ISM services survey hit a four-year high of 72.6 in August against 70.0 expected (Deutsche Bank), and the twelve-month average is 68.5, the highest since April 2023 (JPMorgan). Deutsche Bank has that series running about three months ahead of consumer inflation.
  • Unemployment held at 4.1%, exactly where JPMorgan forecast it.

Waller’s case for holding rested on the labor market cooling enough to let inflation finish falling on its own, and Wednesday’s ADP payroll count of 38,000 against 47,000 expected (Goldman) supported him. The official number says the opposite, and it lands on top of a services price survey at a four-year high. The short $TLT read carried here since August 31 rested on a September increase priced at 60% to 70%, Waller broke that on Thursday, and this morning put it back. The market is buying the front of the curve and not the back: the 5-year yield rose 2.9 basis points to 4.54% and the 2-year is at its highest since January 2025, while the 30-year yield fell 1.3 basis points. Traders are pricing a Fed that hikes and slows the economy down, not one that lets prices run.

The counter from JPMorgan’s desk is that next Friday’s CPI decides the meeting rather than today’s payrolls, and that the labor detail underneath is softer than the headline, with Challenger job cut announcements up 58% month over month in August. That cuts less than it looks. The three-month average of those announcements is down 33% year over year and August cuts were the lowest for that month since 2022, both JPMorgan’s own figures, so the layoff data does not carry the dovish case either. JPMorgan’s stated condition for a hold was core CPI at +0.1% to +0.2% month over month with payrolls near 50,000, and half of that condition is already gone. That threshold is the level to trade against on September 11: inside +0.2% keeps the hold alive, anything above it leaves the hold case without a leg.

3. BRIEFS

AI’s problem is the price, not the demand

The AI trade is being repriced on what a unit of usage sells for, and the argument runs through one bank’s own building.

The price of AI usage is falling faster than usage is growing, which breaks the assumption that more demand is automatically good for the companies selling it. The Silicon Data token index fell 29% in August to $0.97 per million tokens, roughly half the May peak, while token volume on OpenRouter rose 47% month over month against a 7% rise in dollar spend (JPMorgan). That combination is a margin problem rather than a growth story (Goldman’s Privorotsky desk). The other side of it, from TS Lombard, is that scarce computing capacity is exactly what has driven the big cloud companies’ outperformance, and that usage pays for itself. The bond market is siding with the first view. Of the 91 bonds the large cloud builders issued this year, 78 traded below their issue price by late August (Goldman), which is investors demanding a better price to fund the build. The same house rates $AVGO a Buy to $540 on a guided $115 billion of FY27 AI chip revenue, a call that cuts against its own desk’s pricing argument.

Diesel is driving the 10-year

The 10-year Treasury is taking its direction from fuel prices, not from the Fed.

Relief on interest rates requires relief on energy first, and the US diesel price has been moving in step with the 10-year Treasury yield (Goldman’s desk). Diesel hit a record this week as Ukrainian strikes took Russian refineries offline and Moscow banned exports in response, so the shortage sits in refined fuel rather than in crude, which sold off this morning. The supply picture underneath is worse than the visible data shows. Counting tanker crossings near Oman that do not appear in shipping records, Goldman puts total Persian Gulf oil exports at 15 to 16 million barrels a day against only 10 million visible, about two-thirds of pre-war flow. Five desks track Hormuz as a live risk and not one will say whether it reopens, which is the honest state of it. The number that decides the trade is Goldman’s Brent fair value of $80 against a spot near $95: $15 of that price is fear rather than barrels, and it comes out the day flows normalize.

Small caps are the election hedge, not the index

Index protection into November’s midterms is priced about right. The mispricing is one layer down.

S&P 500 options are pricing a 0.82% move the day after the midterms, against a 1.3% average one-day move after every midterm since 1975 (BofA). That gap is not the trade, because midterms have rarely produced large moves in the broad index at all. They produce large differences between individual stocks. Regional banks and small caps carry less election premium in their option prices than their 2024 presidential-election moves justify, which is why BofA’s hedge is $IWM puts rather than index protection, put on while the cost of far-out-of-the-money downside protection sits near record steepness. The same screen says the froth has relocated, with US healthcare and cybersecurity now the most stretched price action BofA’s bubble-risk gauge tracks. If the priced 0.82% turns out to be right, the small-cap puts expire worthless for the premium paid. If it is wrong, index protection pays nothing while the damage happens one layer underneath it.

The cheapest hedges in years, two weeks before the Fed

Equity protection has gone historically cheap heading into a live Fed meeting.

What the options market charges for three months of protection on small caps ($IWM) sits at the 0.2 percentile of its own history, the premium for downside protection on the S&P 500 at the 2nd percentile, and the profit available from betting individual stocks move apart from the index at the 5th percentile (Nomura’s McElligott). That last one is the shape of market makers taking real losses, not a market at peace. Underneath the index, fewer stocks are participating: for every 100 CBOE call options traded over the past five days only 82 puts changed hands, among the lowest readings in years, and the share of Russell 3000 stocks trading above their 50-day average price is the weakest since early April (BTIG’s Krinsky, who expects the retest of 7600 on the S&P 500 to fail toward 7200 to 7300). The advice from Goldman’s volatility desk is to hedge while you can rather than when you have to, and it prices a bet on the VIX rising above 20 by October, capped at 30, at about $1.00 into the September 11 CPI.
