2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

AI hyperscaler capex eats buybacks

The 2026 hyperscaler capex number is no longer a forecast, it is the current run-rate, and earnings keep validating it.

Goldman’s raise to $755B (+83% YoY, equal to 100% of operating cash flows) is the headline number, but the structural shift is underneath it: hyperscaler buybacks fell 64% YoY in 1Q26 as cash redirected to AI infrastructure. The post-2010 default use of MAG7 cash flow has flipped, and the cohort doing the flipping is the largest concentration in the index. JPMorgan reads the same data as productivity, not bubble, with AI investment as a key driver of recent economic strength. The $200B Anthropic backlog tied to GOOGL alone is a single-customer commitment large enough to anchor a multi-year build cycle, and net debt to EBITDA at 0.3x leaves the cohort plenty of room to issue debt to fund it.

The bear case is the part the corpus is split on. Goldman’s TMT desk flagged an 8% one-day drawdown in high-beta momentum, the fifth such instance this year, with Prime Brokerage book momentum exposure now sitting at the 99th percentile on a one-year lookback. BofA is more pointed: AI Big 10 at 40% of S&P 500 is the same concentration profile as the late-90s Internet bubble and Japan in the 80s, and previous instances ended in bond-yield spikes that broke the equity narrative. The bull defense is that aggressive momentum unwinds in dominant themes have historically been dip-buy opportunities, not tops, and that compute scarcity is the binding constraint regardless of who is most-crowded long. The way to express the structural call without adding to the most-crowded mega-cap longs is the picks-and-shovels layer where the constraint actually bites. Long $SOXX into the next hyperscaler capex update.

Bond market is pricing the Fed wrong

Markets have repriced the Fed to a near-no-cut path for 2026, and most of the corpus thinks it is wrong in the other direction.

The bond market is “pricing the Federal Reserve to stay on hold for a year” after a -3 standard deviation $TLT impulse, and Goldman has the 30-year Treasury yield support level at 5%, which the desk calls the critical pain threshold for stocks. TS Lombard’s specific call is that you should not look at the funds-rate decision to know whether policy is being eased: the Fed can deliver a political cut and simultaneously tighten financial conditions through the repo market, with $660B of coupons that need to swap into bills to hit a 20-25% target ratio while reserves at 8.5% of bank assets approach scarcity levels. There is one Tier-1 voice genuinely defending the dovish path with two cuts in 2H26 driven by cooling inflation and moderating growth (UBS).

The mechanism most of the corpus is converging on is that an oil-driven inflation impulse plus deteriorating Treasury supply absorption is the combination that prevents the Fed from cutting cleanly. Crédit Agricole has April CPI at 3.77% YoY, just barely above the 3.74% consensus, and projects the headline peak at 4.2% YoY in May, driven by an 11-12% MoM rise in motor fuels. Headline at 4.2% is not a backdrop into which the FOMC can comfortably deliver a rate cut, even if private payroll and services data soften. UBS’s pushback is that the energy shock is transitory enough that a cutting cycle still arrives in 2H, but the mechanical headline number itself becomes the constraint regardless of how the FOMC reads the underlying. Long $TLT into next week’s CPI, sized for the asymmetry that a hot print clears the 5% 30-year level Goldman has flagged as the equity-pain threshold.

Hormuz peace deal is the wrong trade

Each round of Iran-US diplomacy rallies risk and fades as substance fails to materialize, while the physical oil market keeps tightening underneath.

This morning’s Iran seizure of the Ocean Koi tanker in the Gulf of Oman is the latest data point on the side of physical disruption, and it lands the same day Trump is publicly insisting the ceasefire still holds. SEB tracks Brent moving from $108 on May 6 to roughly $98 on the MoU headlines, a typical fade pattern across diplomatic cycles since March. The physical-tightness signal is louder: API crude draw of 8.1mb was the largest in three months versus 2.8mb consensus, and Strait of Hormuz flows are running at 4% of normal levels per Goldman, with 45% of the Persian Gulf hit being offset by US, Americas, and Russian production. Bloomberg’s December 2026 WTI futures price is in the high-$80s, up from roughly $70 in early April, and 5- and 10-year breakeven inflation expectations are at the highest levels since 2023.

The bifurcation is clean. Headline-driven futures markets keep trading the diplomatic optics. An MoU gets signed, Brent drops $10, equity beta lifts, the move fades as the next negotiating round shows the same gap between positions. An 11% Brent decline is on the table if the Strait reopens cleanly (UBS), but the ratio depends on whether shipowners and insurers ratify any framework with actual willingness to transit through a corridor where a tanker just got seized this morning. Goldman’s bear scenario is the durable resolution case where OPEC fragmentation and structural oversupply pull crude back toward $80-90, which is real but not what’s in front of you with active seizures. Polymarket has 50% probability of Hormuz normalization by end of June, which is roughly a coin flip on the optimistic side. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline.

Gold’s squeeze setup is rebuilding

The 50-day moving average and $4800 resistance are the breakout level, and central bank buying plus a Tether overlay is the demand stack supporting it.

The setup is a coiling pattern with a clear technical level and a fundamental demand stack underneath. Central banks bought 244 tonnes in Q1, with the pace stepping up 17% QoQ. Tether is adding a permanent crypto-overlay bid as its gold holdings near $20B and grow, and UBS’s $5900/oz year-end target gives the upside case explicit institutional support. The breakout level is the 50-day moving average around $4800, and the trade pays off bigger the further gold runs once it clears, with a defined-risk option expression.

The bear case is that gold has not been behaving like a fear hedge recently. It is trading inversely to the $VIXY rather than reacting to volatility, which is a sign the haven-bid mechanism is broken. The invalidation is straightforward: sustained failure to break $4800 means the squeeze setup never triggers and the option premium decays. The defense is that central bank flow is structural rather than tactical and the Tether overlay is recurring, so the setup rebuilds even after a failed attempt. Long the $GLD June 440/480 call spread, with risk capped at debit paid and the upside still leveraged to a clean break of $4800.
