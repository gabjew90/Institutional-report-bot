INSIGHTS & ALPHA

Hormuz oil shock is in the “de-escalation tease” phase — and that’s the most dangerous part

Brent ran +5.8% Monday on the UAE missile-and-drone exchange, with 6-month futures hitting a post-2022 high of $91.99/bbl. Goldman’s S&T desk had Brent touching $114 intraday before Tuesday’s pullback to $110, with WTI back to $102 on Trump’s “operation paused” signal and Rubio’s “objectives achieved” framing. The mechanism here matters: the oil curve isn’t pricing the spot crisis, it’s pricing prolonged supply uncertainty. Goldman’s Oil Analyst note flags global oil stocks at an 8-year record low, with landed commercial refined products at 41 days of demand — Europe’s jet fuel inventories are on track to break the IEA’s 23-day shortage threshold by June. So even if Hormuz traffic normalizes tomorrow, the products draw is a six-to-twelve-month overhang. That’s the asymmetry. Spot can fall 5% on a peace headline; the term curve can’t.

The optimistic read is the Citi version: tensions are cooling, Brent is drifting lower, and the 5% intraday vol in oil is a sign the market wants to sell rallies. The risk Rabobank is flagging — and TME echoes — is that diplomacy is stuck because both sides think time is on their side. Oil at 50% above trend has predicted six of the last six recessions, and the U.S. is now seizing Iranian-linked tankers in the Arabian Sea. JPM’s desk model says Brent runs to $150 if the conflict persists into June. Goldman’s “Pirates of the Arabian” framing is darker still: U.S. encouragement of neutral vessels to bypass the blockade is escalation risk dressed as commerce. Brent six-month futures at $92 is the market saying don’t believe the ceasefire yet.

Trade Implication. Long $BNO into next week’s catalysts — the Brent ETF tracks the products tightness story better than $USO does. Stop on Brent six-month futures losing $85 sustained for a week — that’s the curve admitting de-escalation is real. Pair with selective single-name energy: $OXY trimmed production guidance citing Iran war drag, which is the supply-side scar making $XOM and $CVX cleaner expressions than the trimmed names.

Rates broke. The market is now pricing hikes, and the whole growth-vs-inflation regime just shifted

Deutsche Bank flagged it cleanest: Fed funds futures now price 17bps of hikes by next April — full reversal from the cuts that were in the curve a month ago. The 10-year hit a 9-month high of 4.44% Monday. ING says the 10-year SOFR rate just touched 4% for the first time in this cycle, the level where their desk says fixed-rate receiver conversations begin in earnest, but they’re tactically short duration first because breakevens are at 2.5% and pushing higher. Goldman markets/macro has the 30-year testing 5% with $TLT showing a -3 standard deviation impulse. The mechanism is layered: oil at $110 is feeding the 2-year inflation swap (now 3.13%, highest since November 2022), gasoline is rising into the consumer, and ECB hike pricing for June sits at 99% — the dollar can’t catch a sustained bid because the rate differential is moving the wrong way for it.

The bull case for duration is the Fed’s reaction function. Williams was on the wires sounding dovish — willing to look through energy inflation. Mizuho thinks the U.S. is insulated from the war via energy independence and that the market is over-pricing both ECB and BoE hikes. The pushback that JPM and DB are putting up is harder to argue with: oil-driven inflation has a 3-month lag through gasoline into core, and the Fed already faces a Fitch-flagged debt structure problem (deficit ~7.9% of GDP for FY26/27, debt/GDP >120%). Crédit Agricole has today’s Treasury refunding as the immediate catalyst — $125bn coupon supply ($58bn 3Y, $42bn 10Y, $25bn 30Y) and the question is whether Treasury hints at front-end issuance increases that would bear-flatten the curve further (short-term yields rising faster than long-term).

Trade Implication. Short $TLT or long $TBT into Friday’s NFP — JPM’s internal forecast is 50k jobs (vs Street 65k) with U-rate at 4.2%; a hot or in-line print combined with energy passthrough kills the rate-cut bid. Stop on the 10-year cracking back below 4.20% — that’s the energy inflation impulse fading, and the duration trade flips.

Positioning is the most fragile part of this rally — short gamma, retail chasing, HFs shorting strength

Goldman’s Prime Brokerage shows U.S. L/S gross leverage at 307.8% (96th percentile on a 5-year basis), with net at 76% (23rd percentile, 1-year) — translation: hedge funds are running big books with relatively defensive net exposure, which is the configuration that gets violently squeezed on upside breaks. Morgan Stanley’s HF highlights confirm net selling of global equities concentrated in U.S. names, with HFs actively reducing AI Tech Beneficiaries (the MAG7-and-semis cluster) post-earnings. Goldman’s S&T sees fundamental long/shorts at +9.1% in April — the best month on record — but with global books net sold and North American allocation at an all-time low. Everyone wants the upside without paying for it.

The Market Ear’s “Upside Pain” note layers on the vol mechanics: realized vol on up days is 16.9% versus 14.6% on down days (asymmetric in the direction of squeeze pain), put skew is at the 14th percentile (nobody is buying downside), and dealers move into short gamma on 1-3% upside moves — meaning every pop forces them to buy more, which is exactly what fueled Tuesday’s melt-up. Layer on TME’s 41% market concentration (above the 40% threshold that historically preceded crashes), GME’s retail sentiment z-score of 17.3 even on a down week, and the picture is a tape that goes higher until it doesn’t, then falls hard. Goldman’s “Basics” call captures the tension: fast money positioning has moved from -4 in March to +6 now; the market is “no longer under-positioned” and the systematic crowd is “done buying.”

Trade Implication. Long $VIXY into next week’s CPI (May 12) — short-dated vol is cheap because skew is compressed, and the squeeze-up tape will reverse the moment a hot print revives the rate-cut-being-priced-out narrative. Stop if VIX closes below 15 sustained — that breaks the short-gamma fragility argument.

AI capex super-cycle just got upsized again, but the leverage is showing

Goldman raised their 2026/27/28 WFE (wafer fab equipment) estimates to $141B / $186B / $208B (+28% / +32% / +12%) — the upgrade specifically calls $AMAT and Tokyo Electron the cleanest asymmetric expressions of the AI capex extension. U.S. data center capacity is forecast to more than double from end-2025 to end-2027, hitting 95GW, with annual additions accelerating from 8.5GW (2025) to 13.6GW (2026) to 36GW (2027) — by 2027 data centers are 8.5% of U.S. peak summer power demand. AI adoption among U.S. firms reached 19.8% in April (+0.9pp), with 23% expected within six months. And 40% of S&P 500 EPS growth is now tied directly to the AI infrastructure trade. Hyperscaler net income grew 61% year-over-year. Q1 earnings have 61% of S&P 500 names beating by more than one standard deviation — the highest rate in 25 years outside the 2021 reopening. $ANET printed a clean beat (EPS $0.87 vs $0.81 consensus, billings +54% y/y, deferred revenue $6.20B); $SMCI cracked guidance on margins (10.1% gross vs 6.65% guide).

The bull case is structural, and the data backs it. The risk Goldman desk and Morgan Stanley are quietly flagging is more uncomfortable: hyperscalers are funneling near-100% of capital into capex with FCF pressure rising, and the M-cap cluster’s earnings strength is partly cyclical (rising input costs in DRAM, power, cooling are pressuring terminal-value math). Goldman’s prime brokerage data shows hedge funds actively unwinding tech longs concentrated in hyperscalers — meaning the smart-money long is leaning out even as the capex print gets louder. JPM’s “barbell” call — long Tech, long Cyclicals — quietly assumes the picks-and-shovels trade survives any single MAG7 capex guide-down. It’s a thinner thesis than the $751B headline suggests.

Trade Implication. Long $AMAT into July semicap earnings — Goldman’s WFE upgrade is the cleanest catalyst with the tightest research conviction (Buy reiterated, “high” conviction in their note). Stop on a $META or $GOOGL capex cut in 2H guidance — that breaks the picks-and-shovels chain. For the dispersion play, Goldman’s hyperscaler-vs-semis pair (Jul 105% outperformance call on hyperscalers over $SOXX) hedges the exact risk that the pure-semis trade gets unwound.

GameStop’s $20bn move on eBay is the dedicated catalyst the market hasn’t fully priced

$GME submitted an unsolicited proposal to acquire $EBAY at $125 per share — a ~20% premium to May 1 close — with TD Securities supplying a “highly confident” letter for up to $20B in financing. Structure is 50% cash / 50% GME stock. GameStop has already accumulated a 5% economic stake using common stock and derivatives, telegraphing this is real, not a trial balloon. Ryan Cohen would be CEO of the combined entity. Pro-forma diluted GAAP EPS is projected at $7.79, with $2B of opex synergies targeted within 12 months. The strategic logic is to leverage eBay’s collectibles/trading-cards/refurbished-tech focus categories with GameStop’s physical retail footprint for authentication and live commerce.

The optimistic read is the arbitrage: $EBAY closed up 5.1% on the news; the offer is at $125 vs current — there’s still a meaningful spread if you believe a deal happens or a higher bidder emerges. Goldman maintains a positive stance on the eBay franchise standalone. The risk side is severe and named: $GME has ~$12bn equity value trying to swallow a $46bn target; the cost-cut targets ($2bn in 12 months) are aggressive; regulatory and board approval is uncertain; and GameStop’s stock fell -10.1% on the announcement — the bidder is the source of risk, not the target. This is asymmetric enough that the trade has to be paired.

Trade Implication. Long $EBAY / Short $GME as a paired arbitrage trade — capture the spread to $125 if a deal closes or a strategic bidder steps in, while hedging the “GameStop can’t actually finance this” tail. Stop on $EBAY breaking back below the announcement-day low — that’s the market admitting the deal is dead. Avoid pure-direction $GME plays; the financing risk and equity dilution math is too messy.
