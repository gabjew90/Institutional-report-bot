INSIGHTS & ALPHA

AI hyperscaler capex is the earnings driver, and the dips keep getting bought

The AI capex cycle is no longer a forecast. Every print this week confirmed it, and the supply chain is catching the spend in the same quarter the chips ship.

AMD’s beat-and-raise after the bell makes the cycle look operational, not aspirational. Market cap sits around $680bn (larger than Visa or Oracle). The 2026 US tech capex pool is now estimated at $725bn, and Anthropic alone accounts for roughly $200bn of $GOOGL’s backlog (Goldman, via The Information). Goldman reiterated $NVDA as a Buy with a $250 price target into next week’s print, citing 12% average estimate revisions and upside to $1T datacenter guidance. Corning’s long-term $NVDA partnership to grow optical capacity 10x ran in parallel, and Citi raised $GLW to $225 and $VRT to $414, both flagged high-conviction. The Datadog +31% print is the same story showing up in software infrastructure.

The risk is positioning, not fundamentals. $ANET fell 14% on cautious supply commentary even after a beat. The High Beta Momentum factor took an 8% one-day drawdown earlier this week (the fifth in 2026), and Prime Book momentum exposure sits at the 99th percentile on a one-year basis and 100th on a five-year. Bulls argue the $725bn capex pool is structurally underwriting earnings. Bears point at the unwind cascade waiting if any one hyperscaler walks back guidance the way $ANET did. The cleaner expression here is breadth via $SOXX and adding $GLW into pullbacks rather than chasing $NVDA into next week’s print, with a sustained close below $SOXX’s 50-day as the line that says the AI breadth trade has cracked.

Rate-cut repricing meets the Fed’s repo-rate workaround

Two cuts can be priced and policy can stay tight at the same time, if the Fed lets repo spreads widen to do the work.

TS Lombard frames it as a mechanical problem. The Fed needs to swap roughly $660bn of coupons into bills to hit its 20-25% target ratio, bank reserves are at 8.5% of bank assets (close to scarcity), and $200bn has already been added to the balance sheet to keep short rates frozen. UBS reads the same backdrop differently and sees two cuts in the second half of 2026, calling the market “exaggerated” on tightening risk. The 30Y Treasury yield stabilized around 5%, the level The Market Ear has named a “critical pain threshold” for stocks, with Goldman’s desk pointing at a -3 sigma impulse already showing in $TLT.

Both views can be true at once. UBS gets its funds-rate cuts. The funds rate is just not the rate the equity market is pricing off of when 30Y yields are doing the work. Bulls point at 17% S&P Q1 earnings growth (UBS, fastest in four years) and the $725bn capex pool as the offset that keeps multiples elevated regardless of the funds rate. The risk lives in the long bond. Sustained 30Y above 5% is the level that breaks the rally, not the next FOMC. Long-duration via $TLT works against a clean break of 30Y back below 4.85%, with a sustained close above 5.10% on the long bond as the stop.

Hormuz is back live, and accelerating inflation is already in the breakevens

The post-close US strikes flipped the morning’s peace-deal trade, and the inflation market never bought the ceasefire in the first place.

Brent dropped from $108 to roughly $98 intraday on the early peace headlines (SEB) before recovering on the strikes. The futures curve barely moved. December 2026 WTI is still pricing the high-$80s (up from roughly $70 in early April), 5-10 year breakevens sit at the highest level since 2023, and breakevens have widened 40-50 bps since the start of the war. Distillate stocks are at -11% versus the five-year average, this week’s API crude draw printed -8.1mb (the biggest in three months), and the IEA called energy markets “troubled waters” tonight. The pre-strike $7bn oil-options bet Reuters flagged this afternoon is its own confirmation that someone was already positioned.

The asymmetric setup is that headlines can drag spot Brent $10 in either direction in one session, but the December 2026 curve has barely moved off the high-$80s. Refined-fuel inventory is the binding constraint, not Hormuz transit on any given day. Europe’s jet fuel runs near empty by June, and one MoU does not refill that inventory. Bulls on a durable peace deal point at a 20-30% energy-complex flush as the bull case for $TLT and the bear case for crude. The post-close strikes are the rebuttal. The trade lean is long Brent through $BNO into Friday’s open, with a sustained loss of $85 on Brent as the line that says the supply scare is over.

Tomorrow’s NFP is the rate trade for the next month

Citi’s desk is sized for a tail, not a print near consensus, and realized vol heading into the print is mispriced for that.

Private NFP is estimated at 75k versus 186k prior, the headline unemployment rate at 4.3% versus 4.3% prior. Citi DeskTalk flagged “more volatility ahead” and is positioned for a tail. Natixis reads softening as the base case for April employment. UBS’s gasoline-driven CPI preview (May 12) flags higher gasoline as upside risk to next week’s inflation print, and the 10Y closed today at 4.35% (down 8 bps on the day). $VIXY finished at $26.85, well off the 30+ levels that would price true two-sided risk into Friday’s open.

A 75k estimate that beats to 110-130k flips the rate-cut trade entirely, sends $TLT lower, $UUP higher, and the equity rally narrows back to AI breadth. A miss to 30-50k fires the dovish revision, $TLT catches a bid, $SPY breadth widens, and crypto gets a sympathy lift. The unemployment rate at 4.3% is the secondary lever. A 0.1 tick higher to 4.4% counts as a meaningful loosening signal even with payrolls in line. Bulls argue one print is not a multi-month story given the structural capex pool and PE dry powder backstop. The risk is that vol is cheap into a true tail, and the realized vol setup is what matters for the next 48 hours. The cleanest expression is owning a $SPY straddle into Friday’s open or front-month near-the-money calls on $TLT for the dovish miss, with a print inside 60-90k as the muddle that fades both legs.
