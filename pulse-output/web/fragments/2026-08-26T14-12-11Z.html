2. THE MAIN EVENT

Treasury can cap the supply, not the reason for it

The expanded buyback program buys a few weeks of calmer long-term yields and does nothing about the deficit and inflation that put them there.

  • The cap on each 10-to-30-year buyback doubles from $2bn to $4bn through November 4, about $14bn of extra buying, or 13% of the 20-year and 30-year bonds Treasury expects to sell this quarter (Citadel Securities). That absorbs a supply wave. It does not change the size of one.
  • At Goldman’s run rate of $128bn a year, the official bid takes $20bn a month of long-dated risk out of a market that has to swallow $215bn a month. One dollar in ten.
  • Trend-following funds hold their largest bet against bonds in years, sized at roughly $155mm of gain or loss per basis point of yield move, according to Goldman’s futures desk. A two-standard-deviation rally in prices would force about $150mm of that to be bought back, which is what makes a squeeze mechanically possible.
  • Japanese investors hold about $1.12trn of Treasuries, roughly 12% of all foreign holdings (Citadel Securities). Keeping that block from becoming a forced seller is part of what the operation is for.

Buying back long-dated bonds and funding it with short-term bills does not reduce how much the government borrows. It moves the borrowing to the front of the calendar, which cuts how much 20-year and 30-year debt private investors have to swallow this quarter and raises how much they have to roll over continuously.

One reading treats all of this as deliberate. The Treasury’s cash account held $954bn on August 25, and spending it into a market where trend-followers are record short is a way to force those shorts to buy back and drag the 10-year yield toward 4.30% before the midterms, with $80bn to $200bn of that cash plausibly available to fund the operations. What the squeeze case needs is for the inflation data to stay quiet, and this morning’s 0.25% core PCE says it will not. Rabobank’s objection is that unpredictable issuance can raise the risk premium investors demand rather than lower it, and Goldman’s rates strategists do not think the buybacks reset the level of long-term rates at all. Citadel Securities puts it hardest: suppress the bond market’s signal and the pressure moves onto the currency, which shows up as a weaker dollar and more inflation, not less. The 30-year sits at 5.18% this morning with its gap over the 10-year at 52 basis points, which is not the shape of a capped long end. Goldman’s desk says bonds are ultimately a short and is waiting for a better entry toward month-end. Rabobank names the test directly: if long-term yields keep climbing through the first $4bn operation on September 9, the Treasury has lost control of it.

3. BRIEFS

Credit is pricing the AI bill before equity does

The buildout is not slowing, and the market has started charging for the debt it takes on before paying for the profits it produces.

Credit investors have begun asking who ultimately backstops AI financing, a question equity has not asked, and the evidence is Broadcom’s $60bn debt raise last week plus the cost of insuring Nvidia and Broadcom debt against default at record highs (Goldman). Citadel Securities argues the other side of the same balance sheet: hyperscalers are under-earning right now because most of their capacity was contracted in 2024 and 2025 at low rates, so the repricing as those contracts roll off is profit nobody has counted. That is one desk’s read, and the scoreboard so far favors the credit side. Chip stocks trade near 15 times earnings two years out, down from 21 to 22 times, and the price of AI tokens has fallen 49% since May, which is what it looks like when a market marks down the revenue side while nobody dares model a spending cut. Goldman’s own equipment forecast still rises to $150.3bn this year and $217.5bn next. Citadel’s case needs older capacity to reprice higher as contracts roll, and the next round of hyperscaler results is the first place that would show up.

Hedge funds sold the most since April

Funds cut their equity bet to a one-year low while adding hedges, which is preparing for a fall rather than positioning for a rally.

US long/short funds cut their directional equity bet by 3 points to 48.3%, a one-year low, while the total size of the book rose 1.5 points to 205% (Goldman). Growing the book while shrinking the directional bet means funds are pairing longs against shorts instead of backing a view. Technology was the most-sold sector, left at 16.6% and the 25th percentile of the past year. Index futures and ETFs made up 47% of the selling, the signature of cutting the whole book rather than trimming individual names, and Morgan Stanley counts it as the second-largest week of global equity selling in a year, with individual stocks moving more independently of the index than at almost any point in five years. The bull case is that a one-year low in exposure is fuel once a catalyst clears, and tonight’s Nvidia print is the nearest one. If exposure is not rebuilding by mid-September, when JPMorgan’s flow work says these episodes historically turn, this is a trend rather than a flush.

Nvidia reports into its worst run since 2022

Nvidia goes into tonight’s print on a seven-day losing streak with its debt insurance cost at a record, a setup the last four quarters did not have.

The report lands after the close, around 4:20pm ET. The Street wants $2.13 a share on $93.63bn of revenue, the buyside bar sits higher at $94bn to $95bn with a guide of $107bn to $108bn against the Street’s $104.5bn, and the options market prices a 4.6% move, below what the stock has actually delivered on recent results (JPMorgan). The numbers are not the problem. Nvidia has beaten and the stock has still fallen the day after each of the last four reports, and JPMorgan’s own work has it down 3% on average over the following week. At about 17 times next year’s earnings JPMorgan calls that undemanding and keeps a $280 target. The test tonight is not the beat. If the guide lands at or above $107bn and the stock still cannot hold a bid into Friday’s close, what is weighing on it is the financing question, not demand.

The AI bottleneck is now a permit

The limit on new AI compute has moved from chips to power and local permission, and that scarcity protects the biggest spenders instead of hurting them.

Texas’s grid operator is reviewing more than 474 gigawatts of connection requests, roughly 90% of them data centers, more than five times the state’s record peak demand (Citadel Securities). New York and Pennsylvania have paused permits, Texas has ordered an audit of every pending request, and the opposition is bipartisan because the cost lands on a local electricity bill while the benefit is national. Grid hookups and permits run in years, so supply cannot answer demand on the timeline the spending assumes, and BloombergNEF has US data center power capacity falling short of AI chip production starting next year (Goldman). Hedge funds have noticed, and Goldman’s crowding work puts the Grid Revamp basket at the 94th percentile of long positioning and the 97th of short, the most contested pairing in the book. Being early costs you a stretch of power and utility names lagging while yields stay high. Being absent costs you the whole repricing, because if compute scarcity runs for years then every marginal AI dollar accrues to whoever already holds a permitted site. Goldman’s materials desk calls $CCJ a core holding for uranium exposure, which is the same bet one layer back.

Gold is no longer a rates trade

Gold’s run has stopped being a bet on falling interest rates and become a bet against the dollar, and the buyer base has changed to match.

Speculators bought a record $22.2bn of gold futures in the three weeks to August 18, the largest stretch in more than a decade of data, leaving the bullish position at the 93rd percentile of the past two years (Goldman). Trend-followers flipped from betting against gold to buying it, which adds fuel above the current price rather than beneath it. The tell that this is a currency trade and not a rates trade is that gold has run hard while inflation-protected bonds have gone nowhere. The target range is $4,500 to $5,000, with the top of it live if the data cools (JPMorgan), while Goldman is at $4,900 with upside risk but warns that the jump in bullish sentiment sets up a sharp unwind if Warsh sounds uncomfortable with it Friday. $GLD is already down 0.79% this morning on a firmer dollar and a hot core PCE print, which is what the front of that unwind would look like. Copper at a record $6.83 a pound argues the other way, that this is a broad hard-asset bid and not a gold story. UBS marks $4,520 as the support that has to hold, with $4,380 beneath it, and Goldman’s desk names $EGO as its preferred way to own the sector.
