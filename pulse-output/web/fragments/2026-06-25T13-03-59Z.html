Oil cracks, rates cave

Thursday, June 25, 2026 · 26 research analyses

1. RECAP

Heading into Thursday’s open, the whole market is still digesting one shock: the Strait of Hormuz reopened, oil dumped back to pre-war levels, and the entire hawkish-Fed trade that ran earlier in the week unwound with it. Yesterday’s session left $SPY basically flat (-0.05%) and $QQQ down -0.42%, but the real action was in commodities and bonds. $USO sank -4.47% and $GLD lost -3.02% as the war premium drained out, while $TLT rose +1.37% and the 10-year yield slipped to 4.37% (down 0.03 of a percentage point) on a grab for bonds. Crypto is quiet this morning, with $BTC holding the $61k area (+0.42% today) and $ETH near $1,640 (+1.06%). Everything funnels into core PCE, the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, which just crossed at 8:30 with the number still landing.

  • Core PCE inflation (8:30 AM ET, just released). This is the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, expected at 0.30% month-over-month versus 0.2% prior, alongside the final read on first-quarter GDP (est. 1.6% growth). The print crossed minutes ago and the value is still landing. A soft number extends this week’s rate-rally and keeps $TLT bid. 0.3% or hotter snaps the hike trade back on.
  • Oil touched pre-war levels overnight on rising Middle East supply, with reports China’s state refiners are weighing a resumption of Iran oil imports. That confirmed the supply-glut read that drove $USO -4.47% and $BNO -4.23% in yesterday’s session, and it is the single move that pulled the inflation scare out of the curve.
  • JPMorgan unveiled a $50B buyback and Goldman Sachs raised its dividend after the Fed’s annual stress test cleared all 32 large banks. Capital-return headlines landed into a quiet session with prices barely moving, a tailwind for $XLF rather than a same-day mover.
  • Apple is raising prices on MacBook and iPad on surging memory and storage costs, with Tim Cook hinting at more to come. That is the demand-side echo of Micron’s blowout quarter, memory is tight enough that it is now showing up in finished-product pricing.

2. THE MAIN EVENT

Oil cracked the Fed, and the front-end has more to give

Crude’s collapse just did the Fed’s tightening work in reverse, and the market is still priced for hikes the data no longer needs.

  • Brent fell below $75 for the first time since the Iran war began, back near the $72 it traded before the conflict, as Hormuz shipping reopened (Deutsche Bank, Rabobank). The oil-driven inflation scare that justified emergency hikes is gone, so the rate-hike premium has nothing left to stand on.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield dropped to 4.37% and the 2-year fell with it, the biggest one-day move in months as cash piled into bonds for the price gain that comes when yields fall (Rabobank, Deutsche Bank).
  • Fed-hike pricing collapsed from two full hikes earlier in the week to roughly a quarter-point by October (Rabobank). The hawkish trade unwound in 48 hours.
  • The one-year inflation swap, the inflation rate the bond market prices for the next year, fell to 2.16%, the lowest since October 2024 (Deutsche Bank). The market is betting the energy scare is over for good.

One shock did all of it at once. Pulling the supply premium out of oil takes the pressure off headline inflation directly, and a Fed that no longer faces a $100-oil tail has no reason to keep hike risk in the curve. The case for 2-year Treasuries having the most room left to fall rests on growth softening underneath the inflation noise (ING), and a benign core PCE near 0.2% would be the trigger. The loud counter is named: the Fed just marked its own year-end inflation forecast up to 3.3% from 2.7%, and core inflation will not finish below 3% unless monthly prints average 0.20%, which makes today’s hike-pricing fair rather than excessive (Bank of America). Crédit Agricole goes further, looking for headline PCE to accelerate to 4.1% with new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh standing up an inflation task force. Both camps agree the answer lands this morning, and with the print already crossing, this is binary: soft confirms the repricing, hot snaps it back. Long $TLT into the number, sized small.

3. BRIEFS

Micron blew the doors off and made the AI buildout call louder

Micron just turned memory from a boom-bust commodity into contracted AI infrastructure, and the year-end targets moved behind it.

Micron ($MU) reported $41.46bn in quarterly revenue, more than quadrupling from $9.3bn a year ago, and is indicated up over 16% in premarket trading. The number that matters is the contracted-revenue base across strategic customer agreements, which locks demand in rather than leaving it to the spot cycle and is exactly why Apple is now raising MacBook and iPad prices on the memory crunch (CNBC, this morning). Year-end S&P 500 targets moved to 7,800 on the back of AI power-infrastructure growth pegged at an 82% three-year rate through 2028 (JPMorgan), with the optical-interconnect market seen reaching $92bn by 2028 (Citi). The pushback comes from inside Goldman: chasing the beneficiaries at peak earnings is dangerous, and the real tell is that the spenders (Google, Microsoft, Amazon) traded heavy while custom-chip efforts threaten to cut out 75%-plus supplier margins. The contracted backlog is the visibility you are paying for, and the spenders’ price action is the early-warning signal that the cycle is turning. Long $MU on the contracted-revenue floor.

The AI trade is the most crowded long in the market

The buildout is real and the positioning is washed out at the same time, which is why every move whips both ways.

Goldman’s prime desk saw the largest net selling in global tech in about five months (a 2.6-standard-deviation move, far outside the normal range) and calls AI the most crowded long in the market, with hedge funds at record-high exposure in Asia and record-low exposure in the US. The Mag 7 is already down 11.6% from its May 28 peak, in correction territory, and conviction is eroding as funds trim AI to rotate into US cyclicals like industrials and financials (UBS). The counter comes from the same building: gross and net Mag 7 exposure has fallen to one-year lows, which Goldman’s flow desk reads as an entry point rather than a warning, and shorts are getting pressed in the AI-vulnerable names (JPMorgan). Light positioning argues most of the pain is behind, so the cleaner play is rotating into the cyclical beneficiary rather than shorting the AI complex outright, and the bank-capital-return news ($50B JPMorgan buyback, Goldman dividend raise post-stress-test) lands right under it. Long $XLF.

The dollar is breaking out into the rate-cut bets

A dovish repricing should sink the dollar, yet it is the strongest major currency in the world right now.

The dollar index hit its highest level since May 2025 even as Fed-hike bets collapsed, with EURUSD breaking support at 1.14 and USDJPY pushing toward 162, stretched enough on momentum (14-day RSI near 72, a level that flags an overbought, overextended move) that US and Japanese officials are signaling possible intervention (Rabobank). The de-dollarization story is overstated: the dollar’s share of global lending, debt, and payments is stable, and gold’s jump to 29% of central-bank reserves from 11% is mostly the higher gold price rather than dollar selling (JPMorgan). A strong dollar is the headwind under everything priced against it. Gold ($GLD) fell -3.02% in yesterday’s session and bitcoin ($BTC) is pinned near its lows for the year, with year-end gold targets cut to $4,900 (Goldman) and $3,800 downside flagged (Deutsche Bank). The open question is whether a clean break higher triggers the bigger squeeze or intervention near 162 caps it. Until the dollar breaks out cleanly, it keeps a lid on any crypto and gold bounce. Long $UUP, paired with patience on $GLD.
