2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

The Hormuz supply hole is still pricing thin

Fifteen desks reference the Strait of Hormuz as a live supply risk, only Rabobank is calling it functionally closed, and cross-asset price action is split between scarcity and resolution.

  • The strait is an ongoing energy supply disruption without a directional view, a backdrop the Brent curve has only partially absorbed.
  • Rabobank is the outlier, explicitly skeptical that flows resume in the near term, which is the only conviction call in the cluster.
  • Refined fuel inventories in Europe are tight enough that Goldman’s commodity team has flagged jet fuel running out by June if shipping disruption persists.
  • Oman confirmed Mina al Fahal terminal operations are normal overnight, fading the scarcity bid that drove $USO and $BNO higher earlier in the week.
  • Physical Brent is still pricing a scarcity premium that the futures curve has discounted, which is the gap a fresh headline reopens.

Fifteen banks naming the same risk without staking a clean directional call is itself a positioning signal. The bull case is more boring than people want it to be. The supply hole is real, refilling refined fuel inventories takes months even if a ceasefire holds tomorrow, and the futures curve hasn’t repriced for the months-long inventory rebuild. Rabobank is implicitly arguing the other side, that paper Brent’s quick re-rating on every diplomatic headline means the move fades fast on resolution, but that argument runs into the same physical-tightness wall the bull case rests on. With Oman normal overnight and Trump’s rebuke of Netanyahu wobbling the ceasefire framework, the asymmetric expression is owning oil into the next geopolitical headline rather than fading the current pullback. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz or Israel-Lebanon escalation.

Goldman, UBS, and JPMorgan all want you long the global AI trade, BCA Research has flat-out called it an earnings bubble, and positioning is now the risk that goes with it.

  • Goldman raised 2026 hyperscaler capex estimates to a new high and called the spending boom “very strong.”
  • Alphabet’s recent equity raise is a tell that hyperscaler AI capex can no longer be funded purely from cash flow (UBS), a structural shift from the self-funded framing of last year where hyperscalers paid for AI spending out of their own profits.
  • BCA Research’s report is unambiguous, the AI sector is almost certainly in the middle of an earnings bubble.
  • One-third of MAG7 profits came from private equity investment gains rather than core AI revenue, per Goldman’s own desk, so earnings are more cyclically vulnerable than the headline suggests.
  • $AVGO posted a solid quarter and guide-up earlier in the week, $TSM remains sold out through 2026 on the supply side, which keeps the picks-and-shovels names paid.

Two real conviction reads sit on top of each other in the corpus. The supply side of the AI buildout keeps the picks-and-shovels names paid through the next several earnings cycles regardless of what the bear case does. The bear case here is mechanical, if hyperscaler capex slows even modestly while the bubble framing takes hold in positioning, the demand-side names like $NVDA and $AMD lose multiple before fundamentals turn. The Alphabet equity raise and the BCA bubble call sit on opposite ends of the same trade, infrastructure layer confirming the bull and multiple layer naming the risk. Overnight crypto weakness with $BTC -3% and $ETH -6.5% on no specific catalyst is the first crack in the risk-asset complex the bubble call has been waiting for. Long $MU and $AVGO over CPU-exposed names, with $NVDA traded around the print rather than held through.

Labor market dodged the cooling call

Most desks went into the May print expecting a soft headline, MUFG was the contrarian for solid momentum, and the actual 120k beat against an 85k estimate vindicated MUFG.

  • Goldman’s May Payrolls Preview pegged the print below consensus, citing the cooling labor-market indicators that ran through Q2.
  • Deutsche Bank framed May as the next test of whether the labor market had actually stalled.
  • UBS went harder, calling uncertainty “frozen” US labor market activity, the most bearish read in the cluster.
  • MUFG was the contrarian, calling for solid hiring momentum to continue and the headline to print at or above consensus.
  • Actual private payrolls came in at 120k vs 85k estimate, unemployment held at 4.3%, U-6 ticked down to 8.1% from 8.2%, a clean beat against the soft-print consensus.

A 4-1 vs MUFG split with most desks on softening is the kind of consensus that gets paid when it’s right and punished when it’s wrong, and the 120k beat punished it. MUFG’s read that small business and services hiring was holding up is what played out. The bull case for follow-through is that the cooling indicators Goldman and Deutsche cite, including the staffing-sector data that has historically led NFP, take more than one print to reverse. The defense for the hawkish read on this print is more interesting, the Fed’s reaction-function shift the next theme covers means even a modestly better number resets the cut path the curve was pricing. Equity futures sold off mildly on the print, $TLT futures softer, dollar marginally bid. The asymmetric play now is the rates trade rather than the equity trade. Short $TLT into the June 10 CPI print.

The Fed’s reaction function has shifted hawkish

Goldman’s 1-Delta desk, BofA, Crédit Agricole, ING, and SEB all flag the Fed has gotten more hawkish in how it responds to data, and this morning’s NFP beat gives that thesis fresh oxygen.

  • The shift is explicit, the bar for cuts is higher than the curve has been pricing (Goldman).
  • BofA Global Research ties the shift to the labor-market cooling pattern, arguing the Fed will tolerate softer hiring before easing.
  • Crédit Agricole’s May employment preview was built on the assumption that the Fed needs more than one soft print to move.
  • ING frames the hawkish reaction-function shift as the thing that decides where the Treasury curve sits from here.
  • This morning’s private payrolls beat at 120k vs 85k estimate is exactly the data point that extends the hawkish path the curve has been fading.

If the curve is pricing two cuts in 2H and the Fed’s reaction function has actually moved tighter than that, the rates trade is betting against long-dated Treasuries into the next data point, not for them, because longer-maturity bonds get hit hardest when the market reprices fewer cuts. The bull case for the dovish read is mechanical, eventually the cooling labor data forces the Fed’s hand regardless of what the reaction function says, and the curve usually leads the dots in either direction. The defense for the hawkish read is that the dot-by-dot reaction-function shift determines the path from any given data point, and the Fed has explicitly named energy-driven inflation as something they will look through only briefly, which the Hormuz overhang keeps live. The June 10 CPI print is the next test. A core CPI sustained below 3.5% on that print is what kills the trade. Short $TLT into CPI.

ECB hikes while the Fed holds, the dollar story shifts

Nordea, ING, UniCredit converge that the ECB starts hiking June 11 to fight inflation, with more hikes through year-end, and the widening gap between EU and US rates is what bids the euro and caps the dollar.

  • Nordea, ING, and UniCredit converge on the ECB hiking starting June 11, with the full cycle pricing more hikes through year-end.
  • A 25bp hike is fully priced into the June 11 meeting at 2.4% vs 2.15% prior, so the surprise is in Lagarde’s guidance on cadence.
  • Two of the three desks flag the EU energy price surge tied to the Iran conflict as the inflation driver forcing the ECB’s hand, with Commission estimates of 1.3M jobs at risk from the energy shock.
  • ECB hiking while the Fed pauses widens the gap between EU and US interest rates and bids the euro, which weakens the dollar against EUR specifically rather than broadly.
  • The cross-Atlantic divergence is the cleanest long-euro, short-dollar setup in the corpus, and the timing window is the next six trading days into the decision.

The bull case for the euro is that EU rates are climbing while US rates sit still, which mechanically pulls money into the euro. The ECB hiking while the Fed reaction function stays hawkish-but-on-hold widens what holders earn in EUR vs USD. The bear case for the trade is the Iran-driven energy shock that’s forcing the ECB’s hand is also the thing that could break European growth, and a hawkish hike into a slowing economy can paradoxically weaken the currency if growth concerns dominate. The defense is that the next six trading days don’t include that second-order risk, the trade is the rate gap into the decision, and that gap is structural. Hot ECB guidance on June 11 caps $UUP and bids the euro. Long $FXE into the ECB decision, paired with short $UUP as the cleaner expression for a US options account.
