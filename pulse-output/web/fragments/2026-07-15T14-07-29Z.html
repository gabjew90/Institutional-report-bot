The hike is dead

Wednesday, July 15, 2026 · 66 research analyses

A cool June inflation print has pulled the Fed’s rate-hike threat off the table, and the desks are leaning into longer-dated bonds and staying long the AI-infrastructure trade. The two things they flag that could snap the calm: a Middle East oil-supply shock and stretched, low-volatility positioning. Big-bank earnings already landed strong, so attention now turns to whether Fed Chair Warsh sounds as tough in today’s second day of testimony as the market fears.

1. RECAP

Two forces are tugging the market in opposite directions this Wednesday morning. A cool run of inflation data has pulled the Fed’s rate-hike threat off the table, while an overnight escalation in the US strikes on Iran has put a fresh bid under oil. Half an hour into the session the equity read is muted, $SPY up 0.20% and $QQQ flat, with Treasury yields drifting lower across the curve (the 10-year down about 0.02 of a point to 4.56%) as the no-hike view sinks in. $USO is up 0.75% early on the Middle East headlines and $ETH is outrunning the rest of crypto at +1.56%, while volatility keeps leaking lower with $VIXY off 0.77%. Underneath the quiet, hedge funds have been trimming exposure even as the index holds.

  • Tuesday’s June CPI came in cold. Headline prices fell 0.42% on the month against an estimate of just -0.1%, dragging the annual rate down to 3.88% from 4.2%, and core CPI (which strips out food and energy) actually went backwards at -0.02% versus a +0.2% estimate. Core prices falling outright is what takes a July hike off the table, and yields have leaked lower across the curve since.
  • Producer prices cooled too. June wholesale inflation (PPI, the prices businesses pay before goods reach consumers) came in softer than expected, and this morning’s core PPI is still propagating. A soft producer read removes the last easy excuse for the Fed to sound hawkish, reinforcing the bid for longer-dated bonds.
  • Big banks cleared earnings without drama. JPMorgan posted $6.14 EPS on $52.42B revenue and Morgan Stanley printed $3.46 on $21.35B this morning, with Citi ($3.15), Bank of America ($1.21) and Wells Fargo ($2.00) reporting strong numbers Tuesday. The prints cleared a calendar risk rather than setting direction.
  • US struck Iran again overnight, and oil is rising. A new wave of US strikes hit before dawn, with Iran-linked tankers moving through the Strait of Hormuz ahead of a threatened US blockade. $USO is up 0.75% in early trade and crude futures rose overnight. This is a supply chokepoint getting worse, not resolved, and pump prices are already being flagged to climb back toward $4 a gallon.

2. THE MAIN EVENT

The bond market is right that the Fed hike is gone

June inflation cooled hard enough that the Fed cannot credibly hike into it, and longer-dated Treasuries are the way to own that.

  • Headline CPI fell 0.42% on the month against an estimate of just -0.1%, dragging the annual rate down to 3.88% from 4.2%, the biggest downside surprise in months
  • Core CPI, which strips out food and energy, dropped 0.02% on the month against a +0.2% estimate, landing at 2.86% year-over-year, and core prices going backwards is what takes a July move off the table
  • The market is now pricing only about a 10% chance of a July rate hike, effectively pricing the threat out
  • Bank of America’s read is blunt: on core CPI, the “US economy’s allegedly massive inflation problem is grossly overstated”
  • The curve is where the disagreement lives. Bank of America wants to bet on the gap between 10-year and 30-year yields shrinking (a flattener), while the opposite bet, that the gap has room to widen, has JPMorgan behind it, and with the 30-year still yielding 53bps more than the 10-year, that fight is live

When core prices fall outright, a central bank loses the excuse to tighten, and 2-year and shorter Treasury yields move first. That is why yields drifted lower across the curve this morning even before Warsh speaks. TS Lombard is the honest skeptic, warning the medium-term backdrop is more inflationary than one soft June report suggests, and Warsh testifies again at 10 AM with every incentive to sound hawkish and protect his anti-inflation credibility. Even granting both, a Fed chair cannot hike into a negative core print with the market assigning a 10% chance to a July move, so what actually decides it is the data, not the rhetoric. The rhetoric fades by the afternoon while the print stands. Long $TLT into a Fed that stays parked rather than tightening.

3. BRIEFS

AI chip demand is still the engine

The AI semiconductor trade is intact, and the desks are still telling clients to own the picks-and-shovels.

The AI chip demand story is still front and center, and none of the desks is calling the top. ASML delivered a blowout quarter, which Goldman reads as validation of the AI supply bottleneck, and the medium-term demand story looks intact. Bank of America sees the server-CPU market (the processors that run data centers) growing to roughly $170bn by 2030 from about $35bn in 2025, raised $AMD to a $620 target, and reiterates $NVDA as a top pick at $350. The honest caveat is that a chunk of this conviction traces to one loud house rather than many independent reads, and some desks are already flagging chip-sector swings as momentum stretches. The open question is whether the buildout keeps compounding or one hyperscaler capex guide-down cracks it first. Long $SMH, with memory ($MU) as the higher-beta expression, keeps you in the trade while that question stays open.

The Middle East keeps a floor under oil

The whole desk is watching the Strait of Hormuz and none will call the direction, which is exactly why you want the cheap upside.

The Strait of Hormuz supply-and-shipping risk is the most heavily covered theme in the research, and it carries zero directional consensus because the banks are pricing a risk premium, not a forecast. That risk just got louder: US strikes on Iran resumed overnight, Iran-linked tankers are moving through Hormuz ahead of a threatened US blockade, and crude is climbing. A proposed transit fee on cargoes crossing the strait is the live catalyst (Citi, ING), and Goldman likes refined-product margins with a Buy on Valero ($VLO). The asymmetry is the point: being long oil into a chokepoint scare that fizzles costs you a little to hold, while being flat when a transit fee or a naval incident hits is a fast double-digit move. Long $USO, or $XLE for the equity version, is the cheap way to hold that upside.

Rotate toward the protection nobody is pricing

Volatility is priced near record lows while the desks quietly cut exposure, so the move is to own the defensives and the protection the market refuses to price.

The market is complacent and starting to look toppy, Goldman warns, and the options trackers are asking directly whether volatility is priced too cheap, with $VIXY sitting near the floor at $20.50. The tell underneath is that funds are quietly cutting their books: Goldman and JPMorgan positioning data show hedge funds trimming exposure even as the index sits still, the classic sign of leadership rolling over while the headline holds. The bull case for calm is real, since cool inflation and clean bank earnings genuinely remove near-term catalysts, but that is exactly when protection is cheapest and the crowd is most exposed. If the market has not actually started swinging by the time next week’s mega-cap earnings ($GOOGL, $TSLA) print, the complacency read is wrong and the hedge cost only a small premium. Until then, long low-volatility defensives ($USMV, $XLU) paired with long $VIXY as the cheapest crash hedge on the board.
