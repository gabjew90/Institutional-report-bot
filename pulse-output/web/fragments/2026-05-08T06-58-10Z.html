2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

AI capex stopped being a forecast, the prints are confirming the build

Four banks long, zero pushback on the spend cycle itself, with earnings now landing in print form rather than guidance.

The AI infrastructure trade has moved from “this is what hyperscalers say they will spend” to “this is what is actually printing in real time.” US tech firm capex for 2026 is tracking around $725bn. A $200bn Anthropic backlog is now feeding $GOOGL revenue, lifting 2026 regional earnings growth forecasts to 60% from 45% (Goldman). UBS clocks Q1 S&P 500 earnings growth at 17%, the fastest pace in four years. Across April, AI themes have run 35-55%, five weeks of compounding. The validation extended into software with Datadog’s +31% afterhours Wednesday, into chips with AMD’s clean beat-and-raise, and into memory with SanDisk roughly +15% post-earnings and SK Hynix +10% overnight.

The dot-com parallel is the obvious pushback. BTIG’s data shows the top 10 NDX names averaged 784% over the past year, exceeding the 1999 average of 559% and the 622% pre-March-2000 peak. A 25-30% SOX correction back to the 50-day is not a wild scenario. An 8% one-day drawdown in High Beta Momentum was a top-ten move in five years (Goldman), with prime-broker momentum exposure at the 99th percentile over one year and 100th over five. One-third of MAG7 profits coming from private-equity gains rather than AI revenue is a question Goldman’s own partner posed publicly. Even granting all of it, the binding constraint is that earnings keep landing. Goldman’s own work on aggressive momentum unwinds notes they have historically been buying opportunities with a typical one-week recovery window. Long the AI infrastructure stack through $SMH, scale in on the next momentum air-pocket rather than chase the highs.

The bond market is fighting the Fed on cut timing and the bond market is winning

UBS, TS Lombard, and current price action disagree on what the Fed actually does next, but they agree 30-year Treasury bonds are doing the talking.

Three things at once. Markets have overpriced further tightening risk, with two cuts expected in 2H26, fed funds at 3.25% by December, and the 10Y drifting to 4.00% from 4.35%, down 8bps Wednesday (UBS). TS Lombard’s structural read says the Fed will cut funds rate as a political gesture but let repo spreads widen, which is policy tightening through plumbing rather than rate paths. Powell has already added $200bn to the balance sheet to keep short rates frozen, and TS Lombard pegs $660bn of coupon-to-bill swap as the requirement to reach the Fed’s 20-25% target ratio. The binding constraint is bank reserves at 8.5% of bank assets, near scarcity, which is the level below which the Fed loses control of overnight rates regardless of what the funds rate is doing. The $TLT impulse hit -3 standard deviations, an extreme bond market move (Goldman).

The pushback is exactly the UBS framing. Energy-driven inflation is a three-month-lag story that fades as Hormuz resolves, the labor market is softening with private payrolls expected at 75k versus 186k prior, and the curve is overdue to price cuts as growth slows. Norges Bank’s surprise hike to 4.25% is a foreign-bank counterexample, not a Fed signal. Even granting that, the 30Y at 5% is the line the market has flagged as the trigger. A sustained break above forces the Fed to choose between cutting the funds rate to satisfy political pressure and holding 30-year Treasury yields down, and a balance sheet already up $200bn says they cannot do both. The 4.00% 10Y target by December (UBS) is a 35 hundredths-of-a-percent rally from here, the kind of move long-dated Treasuries deliver when growth slows faster than cuts get priced. Long $TLT scaled into NFP, conviction reduced if the 30Y breaks 5% sustained.

The Hormuz peace deal cracked overnight and the supply scar is back in the price

SEB and Bloomberg disagreed on whether the diplomatic round was real, and overnight the strikes settled it.

Brent ran from $108 to $98 on the May 6 MoU optimism, the rally that just unwound. Each round of US-Iran diplomacy since March has rallied risk on the announcement and faded as substance failed to materialize. Reuters’ reports overnight on US strikes against Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas plus Iranian state media claims of missile fire on US navy vessels are substance failing in real time. The physical market is tight underneath: API showed an 8.1mb crude draw versus 2.8mb expected, the biggest in roughly three months, and distillate stocks are 11% below the five-year average. Iran’s 14-point response includes a $270bn reparations demand, the gap to a deal. Polymarket has Hormuz normalization by end of June at 50%. December 2026 WTI is in the high-$80s, up from roughly $70 in early April, and the 5-10 year inflation rate priced into the bond market sits at the highest since 2023, with the 1-year forward headline CPI swap at 2.9% versus 2.4% pre-war.

The bull case is diplomacy resumes within days, the strikes are tactical messaging rather than escalation, and oil retraces toward $98. Trump’s “war will be over quickly” line is that case. The supply scar is the binding constraint regardless. Goldman’s desk has been clear that $120 WTI is where the narrative shifts from “energy is a known problem” to “energy is the problem,” and a sustained move toward that level under renewed strike risk reprices the entire equity rally. Long oil exposure through $BNO, paired with energy single-names like $XOM, rather than buying $SPY into the open.

Memory and CPU catch-up is the cleanest single-name extension of the AI trade

JPMorgan’s Retail Radar names the dedicated theme. Cross-bank earnings reactions confirm the cycle inflection.

The memory and CPU complex was the laggard inside the broader AI trade through April, and earnings are closing the gap. AMD’s beat was clean rather than accounting-driven, the post-earnings price action says the buy side believes the data center accelerator story, and AMD’s market cap at roughly $680bn now exceeds Visa and Oracle, with $MU at roughly $740bn. SanDisk was the top single-name retail purchase last week at +$431M with a 4.5-sigma imbalance, finishing roughly +15% post-earnings. SK Hynix gapped +10% overnight, extending the read into the Korean memory complex, the leading indicator on global memory pricing for the last two cycles. There are 12% average estimate revisions for $NVDA into the 1Q print (Goldman), supply-demand still inflecting. SOX’s 145% best 52-week rolling performance is well below the 264% dot-com peak, leaving room.

The pushback is supply constraints cutting the other way. Arista Networks fell 14% after a cautious supply outlook despite beating headline numbers, signaling supply-chain bottlenecks could turn into an earnings drag rather than a tailwind. Goldman’s momentum-unwind data with prime-broker exposure at the 99th percentile means a single negative print can trigger sharp re-pricing across the complex. Even with that risk live, AMD’s market cap milestone is fundamental rather than narrative, and a 25-30% SOX pullback to the 50-day (Goldman) is a correction, not a regime change. Long $MU paired with $SNDK on dips, sized to absorb a near-term momentum unwind.

Gold is rebuilding the Q1 squeeze setup with Iran back in the mix

UBS provides the fundamental anchor and the price action is rebuilding the technical setup.

Central bank buying is structural at 244 tonnes in Q1, up 17% quarter-over-quarter, and the $4800 50-day moving average is the technical line that decides whether this becomes a sustained breakout. The $5900/oz year-end target is the fundamental anchor (UBS). With Iran tensions re-accelerating overnight, the inflation rate priced into the bond market at 2023 highs, and the dollar steady rather than ripping, the macro backdrop is cooperating rather than fighting the trade. $GLD has held its 2023 breakeven highs.

The bear case is the gold-VIX inverse correlation snapping back, momentum players staying out, and a clean diplomatic resolution stripping out the geopolitical premium. Real risk, but central bank flow is not a positioning trade and runs through diplomatic cycles. The $4800 break is the trigger that confirms the setup. Long $GLD with the bias to size up if $4800 breaks cleanly.
