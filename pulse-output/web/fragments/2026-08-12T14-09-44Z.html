1. RECAP

Stocks opened higher after a July inflation report that landed in line with what desks had expected, and the reaction to it rather than the print itself is doing most of the work in this morning’s positioning. $SPY is up 0.24% about half an hour into the session while $QQQ leads at +0.92%, and $GLD is the biggest mover on the board at +1.32%. $BTC is flat near $63.7k and sitting the equity move out. Under the surface the picture is less calm than the index prints suggest: professional positioning in Nasdaq futures has swung to its most bearish reading since ChatGPT launched while the index holds near record highs, and protection against a selloff is priced near the cheapest levels of the year. Oil is softer even after Iran ruled out ceasefire-extension talks this morning.

  • July CPI, 8:30 AM ET. The July report came out at 8:30 and, per this morning’s reporting, landed close to what the desks had penciled in. Goldman Sachs had estimated core at +0.19% on the month and headline at +0.05%, JPMorgan at +0.22% core and +0.12% headline, and Deutsche Bank at +0.26% core. In line was enough for buyers, with $QQQ +0.92%, $VIXY -1.45% and the 5-year Treasury yield down 3.3 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percent).
  • AI earnings keep confirming the spend. Super Micro reported $1.70 per share on $11.12B of revenue Tuesday after the close, and CoreWeave’s report pointed at the same demand for Nvidia chips. $QQQ +0.92% against $SPY +0.24% is the whole story of the open: the AI complex is carrying the index and the rest of it is barely moving.
  • Gold is outrunning everything. $GLD +1.32% is the largest move on the board while the dollar sat still, so this is not a currency trade. Buyers are paying up for a hedge against the government and the data-center buildout borrowing from the same pool of bond money, and a CPI print that came in as expected did nothing to slow that bid.

2. THE MAIN EVENT

The bond market is repricing AI before the stock market does

Lenders are charging the AI buildout more every month, and that bill hits the stocks long before the spending stops.

  • Investors now demand 25 basis points more yield to hold AI-linked top-rated corporate bonds than they did in January, against 6 for the rest of that market (Goldman Sachs). A gap that wide means the AI complex is being priced as its own riskier category rather than as blue-chip tech.
  • All 10 of the most heavily bet-against bonds in that market are AI names (Goldman Sachs). Bond investors are not trimming at the edges, they are positioned against the trade outright.
  • The cost of insuring Nvidia debt against default reached roughly 77.5 basis points on Monday as its $500B financing plan came into view. A year ago there was no active market in betting against Nvidia’s credit at all.
  • Roughly $1 trillion of AI lease commitments never appear as debt on the balance sheet (Goldman Sachs). What these companies have already promised to pay is much larger than what they report as borrowings.

Capital spending on data centers reaches $1.1 trillion by 2027 against cash flow that does not cover it, leaving about $400bn to be borrowed (Goldman Sachs), and each round of borrowing sets a higher price for the next one. The bull answer is that the money is showing up in results, and 25 companies in the S&P 500 now put an explicit number on their AI revenue (JPMorgan), which is what makes lenders willing to fund the next round. Real revenue does not settle the funding question, because the borrowing arrives years ahead of the revenue it pays for. The standing advice from Goldman’s credit desk is to bet against any narrowing of that AI-versus-everything gap, on the view that multi-year buildout funding keeps the premium in place. Positioning already agrees: professional exposure to Nasdaq futures flipped to a net $11.1bn short in one week, the most bearish since ChatGPT launched, while the index printed record highs and downside protection sat at its cheapest of the year. Long $QQQ puts.

3. BRIEFS

Memory: the shortage is real, the easy money is not

The memory shortage runs into 2027 on both forecasts, but the part traders get paid for is nearly banked.

JPMorgan raised its memory market forecast and has the shortage getting worse into 2027 before a modest improvement in 2028. Goldman agrees prices keep rising into mid-2027 and then splits off, arguing the easy gains from scarcity are already behind us. Memory names have corrected about 25% this quarter on peak-cycle fear and earnings misses, and bears have cut the earnings multiple they will pay from 5 times to 2-3 times, which is a bet against momentum rather than against the shortage. DRAM contract pricing into the September quarter is where this either keeps working or stops. Long $MU, with the September contract prints as the exit signal.

Gold is the release valve for a bond market losing control

Gold is trading as the escape hatch from a Treasury market asked to absorb the government’s deficit and the AI buildout at the same time.

US debt-servicing payments are up 200% in five years, and the extra yield investors demand for holding long-term bonds keeps nudging higher even as policy tries to hold it down (TS Lombard). Data-center borrowing is landing on that same market, with new corporate bond supply running about 65% above last year (Nomura), and Nomura expects the Treasury to tilt its own issuance toward short-term bills rather than drown the 30-year. Gold is the pressure valve for that squeeze, and systematic trend-following funds have already flipped from an 18% short position to a 15% long, roughly $1.5B of buying, with $5,056 in spot gold the level that takes those models to full size (Nomura). Whether the long bond breaks first or the Treasury blinks does not change the trade. Long $GLD.

Oil is priced for a deal that has not happened

The oil market has already booked a Hormuz resolution the shipping data refuses to confirm.

Brent sits around $88 with barrels for delivery a year out more than $10 cheaper than barrels today (Deutsche Bank), the shape of a market genuinely short of physical supply rather than one bracing for a glut. This morning cut against the resolution trade twice over: Iran ruled out ceasefire-extension talks and said the US must return to the interim deal, and Hormuz shipping traffic fell to a one-week low. $USO still traded down 0.89%, so none of that is in the price. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve, the government’s emergency oil stockpile, is at its lowest level since 1983 and within weeks of operational minimums, so the usual shock absorber is not there. A deal reopens the strait and oil hands back the risk premium, which is the cost of being early. A closure with no reserve left to tap is the cost of being absent. Long $BNO.
