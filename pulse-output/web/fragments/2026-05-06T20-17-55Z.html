INSIGHTS & ALPHA

The peace-deal pop is real, but the supply hole takes months to refill

The Iran news is moving oil today, not next quarter. Refined fuel is the part that is actually broken.

  • Brent has retreated from conflict highs, with the 6-month Brent future closing at $90.57/bbl recently (Deutsche Bank)
  • US pump prices for regular gasoline are at $4.48/gal versus $2.98/gal on Feb 27 right before the Iran war began (Bank of America), and futures markets price gasoline sticky above $4/gal through summer
  • US gasoline stocks at 222m barrels, the lowest seasonal level since 2014, with distillates at the lowest since 2005 (ING Commodities)
  • Roughly 13 mb/d of supply is still disrupted with global inventories drawing fast (ING)
  • Saudi Arabia cut its June Arab Light premium to Asia by $15.50/bbl, an aggressive push to move barrels eastward (ING)

The market is celebrating a ceasefire while the physical inventory damage from two months of disruption hasn’t been undone. Crude inventories sitting 1% above the five-year average mask the fact that gasoline and distillate stocks are at multi-decade seasonal lows. A ceasefire pauses new supply shocks. It does not refill the tanks. Until refiners can rebuild product stocks, any fresh Hormuz incident sends gasoline back vertical even if Brent holds.

The bull case is what the tape is pricing today. Peace holds, Brent drifts back to pre-conflict $70s, and the BofA “summer of $4 gas” call gets walked back. The pushback is that “Operation Freedom” was paused, not concluded, and the naval blockade remains in place. Rabobank flags Iran is capable of capping wells and permanently damaging its production capacity if storage saturates, which would create a multi-year supply problem on top of the inventory problem. Even granting the optimistic resolution, second-round effects are already in the pipeline. ANZ expects April headline CPI at 3.9% versus 2.6% core, the highest divergence in years. The asymmetric setup is short refined-fuel exposure into peace-deal rallies, expressed by selling out-of-the-money calls on $USO. A Brent break above $95 sustained is the level that says the supply scare is back. For longs, the cleanest expression is gold via $GLD, which has rallied through every peace-deal headline in today’s session.

The bond market doesn’t believe the rate-cut story anymore

Most banks now see zero Fed cuts this year. The market is still partially pricing them.

  • US 10-year Treasury yield at 4.42%, up from sub-4% before the Iran war (Goldman, Citi)
  • ANZ Research forecasts April headline CPI at 3.9% year-over-year, the highest since mid-2023, driven by a 40% gasoline surge since February
  • UBS forecasts April CPI at 4.0% year-over-year and Nonfarm Payrolls at 100k versus the 65k Street consensus, both hawkish reads
  • ISM Services Prices Paid (the cost component of services activity) held at 70.7 in yesterday’s release, unchanged
  • Mizuho expects no more Fed cuts in 2026, sees the European Central Bank hiking in June and September, the Bank of England hiking twice (June, September), and the Bank of Japan hiking twice (July, December)
  • Mizuho’s 10-year US Treasury target is 4.50% if the Strait of Hormuz remains compromised, and UK 30-year Gilt yields just hit fresh post-1998 highs at 5.78%

The setup is unusual. US gasoline at $4.48/gal feeds core CPI on a three-month lag. The ISM manufacturing prices-paid index has soared 25 points in three months. The Fed has split into two camps, with one side focused on energy passthrough into broader prices and the other on cooling growth and wages. The bond market is being asked to front-run a Fed that has lost its options. ING is the one major outlier, arguing that if Hormuz reopens and energy flows resume, cuts return to the base case.

The bull case for $TLT is the optimistic ING read. Oil drops, headline CPI rolls over by July, and the Fed delivers the cuts the curve still has priced. The pushback Mizuho would deliver is that the 40-year bond bull market is over. The next regime looks like the 1970s, with higher highs and higher lows on yields. Even granting that framing, the binding short-term question is Friday’s jobs report. BofA forecasts 80k payrolls with unemployment at 4.3%. UBS forecasts 100k. A print at 100k or higher with unemployment ticking down forces a hawkish Fed repricing and lifts the 10-year toward Mizuho’s 4.50% target. The setup leans short long-dated Treasuries into Friday, expressed via $TLT puts, while a sub-50k print would invalidate the trade and bid bonds back hard. The May 12 CPI is the second confirmation event.

AI capex went from forecast to receipts this week

The picks-and-shovels names are getting paid in full while the broader tape watches.

  • Goldman raised the 2027 US data center capacity forecast to 95GW, up from 31GW in 2025, with annual additions accelerating from 8.5GW in 2025 to 36GW in 2027
  • Data centers are projected to consume 8.5% of US peak summer power demand by 2027 versus 4.1% in 2025, a structural tightening of the power market
  • AMD beat-and-raised, Goldman upgraded to BUY and lifted 2027/28 EPS estimates 20% above Street, citing Agentic AI driving server CPU total addressable market (the size of the prize the company can sell into) to 35%+ annual growth
  • Arista Networks ($ANET) Q1 EPS came in at $0.87 versus $0.81 consensus, with billings growth (the dollar value of contracts written, a cleaner forward read than reported revenue) accelerating to 54% year-over-year and deferred revenue at $6.20B. Goldman maintains Buy with a $188 price target.
  • Super Micro ($SMCI) F3Q EPS at $0.84 versus $0.62 consensus, with gross margin at 10.1% versus 6.65% guidance. Goldman keeps a Sell at $27, calling the margin beat possibly cyclical.
  • Memory names $SNDK, $MU, and $INTC posted double-digit gains on the Apple foundry partnership leak. The Semis & Semi Equip GICS sector (the chip-and-chip-equipment slice of the S&P) is now a $10 trillion market cap, larger than most countries.
  • The seven largest tech stocks have earnings growth outperforming the rest of the S&P 500 by roughly 42%, the strongest divergence since the second half of 2014 (JPMorgan)

Three earnings reports this week have done what 18 months of forecasts couldn’t. AMD’s server CPU market jumping to 35%+ annual growth means the inference workload is real, not a slide-deck story. ANET’s billings accelerating from 43% to 54% year-over-year means hyperscalers are still ordering hard. Goldman’s data center power forecast tripled in 18 months. The picks-and-shovels names (power, networking, memory, custom silicon) are seeing the order book the bears said wouldn’t materialize.

The bull case is straightforward. NVDA prints May 20, the order book is intact, and the gap between the seven big tech names and the rest of the S&P 500 widens from here. The pushback is the one hedge funds are already acting on. Morgan Stanley’s prime brokerage data shows hedge funds reduced exposure to the AI tech beneficiaries (the seven big tech names and semis) post-earnings. US long-short net leverage (the gap between long and short positions, a measure of how directionally bullish hedge funds are) dropped to 53%, in the 39th percentile of the trailing 12 months. The Market Ear flags that median company earnings growth is in double digits for the first time in four years, but breadth (the share of stocks participating in the rally) is still narrow. Even granting the breadth concern, AI Power & Infrastructure (nuclear and onsite power names) is the one pocket where hedge funds are still adding net long. The cleanest read for someone with US tech exposure is long the picks-and-shovels rather than chasing $NVDA. A memory-and-networking pair via $MU and $ANET captures the order book directly. A sustained $NVDA break of its breakout level (it has already collapsed against $SMH to early-2024 dislocation levels) would be the leading indicator the broader space is rolling over.

The melt-up has turned mechanical, and that cuts both ways

Vol on up days is higher than vol on down days. Dealers are positioned to amplify moves. Retail is buying calls. This is what late-stage looks like.

  • S&P 500 realized volatility is 16.9% on up days versus 14.6% on down days, the inverse of a healthy rally (The Market Ear)
  • VIX at 17.38 today, down 4.98%. Equity vol is “relaxed” while bond vol is “stressed”, a divergence that signals a fragile setup (Goldman, TME)
  • Dealers are long gamma at current levels, but a 1-3% move higher pushes them short gamma. Short gamma means dealers are forced to buy on strength and sell on weakness, mechanically amplifying both directions (TME Fragile Melt-Up)
  • US long-short hedge fund gross leverage at 211% (96th percentile since 2010) with net leverage at 53% (39th percentile). Gross leverage is the total size of long plus short positions, and a record-high spread between gross and net signals crowding (Morgan Stanley)
  • Retail call buying under 10 contracts is surging, and trend-following systematic funds (CTAs, the computer-driven strategies that follow trends) remain max long global equities and max short 2-year, 5-year, and 10-year Treasuries (Citi, JPMorgan)
  • Korea’s KOSPI surged 217% from “Liberation Day” lows, with Korean SSD exports up 715% year-over-year, an AI memory squeeze going vertical (TME)

The mechanical squeeze is doing the work. Trend-following funds are max long. Dealers are positioned to amplify upside moves. Retail call buyers provide constant fuel. The macro hedge funds that could break the rally have already cut exposure. That is the textbook setup that powers the last 5-10% of an AI cycle. It is also the textbook setup for the move that ends it. Once dealers flip to short gamma on a 1-3% rally from here, the same flow that lifted the tape becomes the flow that drops it.

The bull case is the squeeze has further to run before any unwind. JPMorgan flags hedge funds are “synthetically short” (effectively underweight equities relative to their benchmark even as the market rises) with mutual fund managers under pressure to chase. Citi’s vol desk sees short-dated S&P 500 premium ownable. The pushback is the one Morgan Stanley’s data already shows. Hedge fund gross leverage at the 96th percentile is the danger level, not the comfort level, because the pain trade from here is a sharp factor unwind even without a fundamental catalyst. Even granting that fragility, the asymmetric trade is owning what is mispriced, not chasing what is squeezing. S&P 500 three-month puts trading at deep discounts to single-stock vol look ownable. A sustained VIX break below 16 with another quiet week would confirm the squeeze has another leg before any rollover. The level that invalidates the long-vol setup is a $SPY close above $740 with the VIX above 18. That combination would say upside vol panic has arrived and the rally is no longer mechanical.
