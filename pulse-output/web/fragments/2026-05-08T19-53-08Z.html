INSIGHTS & ALPHA

AI hyperscaler capex is now the entire cash flow

The AI build-out has stopped being a forecast and is now the constraint on hyperscaler free cash flow.

The shift is mechanical. Goldman put 2026 hyperscaler capex at $755B, up 83% year-on-year, equal to 100% of operating cash flow. Meta raised its 2026 range to $125-145B, Alphabet to $180-190B, Microsoft expects June quarter capex above $40B alone. Operating cash flow is fully absorbed by data center spend, and the gap is being filled by cutting buybacks (Q1 hyperscaler buybacks fell 64% year-on-year) rather than issuing debt, since net debt to EBITDA across the group is still 0.3x. That clean balance sheet is being handed to one trade. The 17% Q1 EPS growth at the S&P 500 (UBS, fastest pace in four years) is heavily levered to this capex doing what it’s supposed to do, today’s $QQQ +2.19% session is the bid for the picks-and-shovels layer above the hyperscalers.

The bear case lives at Syz, which projects hyperscaler free cash flow falling more than 70% as the spend ramp continues. If that math is right, the picks-and-shovels names get paid through 2027 but the hyperscalers themselves see multiple compression as the market re-rates them away from cash-return stories. The real-time vulnerability is positioning. AI Complex performance is up 35-55% since April with momentum at the 99th-100th percentile (Goldman), and JPMorgan’s global semi long/short ratio sits at 4x versus the prior peak of 2.5x, the kind of crowded read that snaps without warning. Hedge funds are offloading the Mag 7 at a record pace into retail bids, with net exposure at 17% of North American net (Syz). The capex is real, the crowding into the obvious expression is the problem. Long $SOXX on weakness over chasing Mag 7 strength.

Apple-Intel foundry is the single-stock asymmetry this market is mispricing

The Apple-Intel foundry leak this afternoon looks like the precursor to a real announcement, and the equity setup is asymmetric.

The mechanism matters. Apple sourcing meaningful iPhone silicon from Intel’s US fabs converts a cost line at Apple into a multi-year revenue stream for Intel, and the timing aligns with the broader US semis-onshoring policy push that gave Intel CHIPS Act funding in the first place. The read-through is not Intel as a sell-off play but as a structural re-rating candidate, foundry revenue is sticky in a way Intel’s CPU business has not been. JPMorgan’s 4x semi long/short ratio is concentrated in $NVDA, $AVGO, $AMD, $TSM, not in $INTC, the crowding that haunts the rest of the complex doesn’t apply here. Memory inflows into the Roundhill $DRAM ETF ($5B since April launch, $1B in a single day) are the early signal that capital is rotating toward neglected semis, and Intel sits squarely in that bucket.

The pushback is that Apple-Intel chatter has come and gone before, and Intel’s execution risk on the foundry side is real. Q2 guidance across the chip complex has been mixed. Where the bull case wins, the cost of being wrong on Intel call options is small relative to the cost of missing a confirmed deal, and option-implied vols haven’t fully repriced the optionality yet. The Trump-Xi summit on May 14-15 adds a catalyst that could relieve overhang on Intel’s foundry pivot. Long $INTC calls into the summit window.

Hormuz supply scar persists even as headlines cycle

Brent has held the $99-100 area through weeks of headline whiplash because physical refining markets are pricing a multi-month supply hole, regardless of which way diplomacy breaks.

The mechanism is supply-physical versus headline-paper. The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly 20% of global crude flow, and refined-product inventories have been drawing for weeks (a 108kt week-on-week ARA refined stock draw, per ING). When 20% of global crude flow is interrupted, the missing barrels don’t get magically replaced by a single ceasefire announcement, refineries need months to rebuild stocks once flow normalizes. SEB has Brent vol clustered around $99 with physical supply constraints “severe” due to the Strait closure, dated Brent has compressed to the paper market. Prompt Brent/WTI was down 15% and 13% week-on-week earlier this week on ceasefire optimism (Goldman), only to reverse on the US-Iran clashes, that round-trip is the trade.

The opposing read is the one $USO is pricing today (-0.44% even with US tankers being hit and Iran seizing ours), which is that diplomacy holds and the supply scar resolves quickly. SEB recommends long volatility because markets are pricing optimism that hasn’t been signed. Barclays upgraded US Energy Services to Positive calling it “the best setup in 20 years,” premised on structurally higher oil once the shock ends. Today’s tanker exchange and Iran’s expected response to the US peace proposal is exactly the binary SEB is pricing for. Long $XLE into Iran’s response, paired with long $BNO calls for the tail.

Markets are pricing rate cuts faster than the energy story allows

Two cuts by year-end is in the curve, but the oil channel into core CPI keeps that bet on a knife edge into Monday’s print.

Energy-driven inflation feeds core CPI on roughly a 3-month lag, which means a Fed leaning toward cuts can be forced to wait if the Strait situation drags another month. UBS puts Fed funds at 3.25% by December 2026 (two cuts in 2H), grounded in the view that central banks “look through” oil shocks because inflation expectations stay anchored. The pushback comes from ING, which expects cuts more gradual than market pricing because the oil channel is structural, and UBS itself notes a 47.4% year-on-year surge in the oil import price index. Today’s hot private payroll print (123k vs 75k estimate) cuts in the hawkish direction at the margin, the Fed has less labor-market cover for an early cut. Rates are already re-pricing Iran’s leverage upward (Mizuho).

The skeptical read is that two cuts is in the curve and the trade has limited upside without further data confirmation. Where the bull case bites, positioning in front-end Treasuries is light heading into Monday’s CPI, the 2-year sits at the high end of its range, and Citi explicitly recommends receiving front-end USTs into the print looking for the curve to richen 8bps belly-to-front. The Fed is being given the option to cut by softening labor data, but the option is contingent on energy not breaking core inflation. Long $TLT into Monday’s CPI.
