2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

The Hormuz peace rally is mispriced and the supply scar is months long

The market is trading the headline. The tanker data says the supply scar will outlast the deal.

  • The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed: zero inbound transits since May 3, only one outbound tanker (the INTERSTELLAR, naphtha to China) observed yesterday on a 7-day average of 0.7 vessels per day (Morgan Stanley Daily Tracker)
  • TS Lombard puts Hormuz reopening odds at 30%, with operational oil inventory floors set to be breached by September if the closure persists
  • OPEC production fell to 20.55 million barrels per day in April, the lowest level since 1990 (ING), while US API showed crude stocks down 8 million barrels and refined product down 10 million
  • Iran can only service its central wage bill if it sustains 250,000 barrels per day of exports at $85 realized, with inflation projected to hit 100% if the regime monetizes oil through the parallel currency rate (Bank of America)
  • US gasoline above $4.50 per gallon now threatens to offset the entire $200 billion OBBBA tax windfall (JPMorgan Energy)

The peace memorandum is real but the inventory math is brutal. Brent dropped roughly 10% on the MOU headline and refined product cracks compressed alongside it, but tanker traffic through the Strait has not resumed and refined-fuel inventories sit at multi-year lows. Goldman macro pegs 4Q26 Brent at $90, well above the curve’s current pricing of resolution. The “own equities, own oil” trade is the same trade Goldman’s One-Delta desk reiterated this week with deferred Brent futures (the COZ6 contract) as the cleanest expression for institutional accounts.

The pushback we would anticipate is that the UAE has already started routing tankers around the Strait (Reuters this morning), Saudi loadings out of Yanbu remain near 5 million barrels per day, and a deal that delivers in a week could push Brent through $85 on positioning alone. Even granting that pushback, the reopening odds are 30% and operational inventory floors are September, not July. The Brent curve at $90 in 4Q26 is the energy-bond equivalent saying the same thing. The cleanest read for someone with US options exposure is that $BNO captures the Brent carry (the gap between current price and the longer-dated futures it tracks) without the WTI gas-glut drag, with a sustained loss of $85 on Brent the level that says the supply scare is finally over.

The 30-year is testing 5% and the Fed cannot credibly cut into it

Long-dated Treasury yields are at the level that has historically capped equity rallies, and the futures market is repricing rate cuts out of the curve.

  • The 30-year US Treasury yield is testing 5%, the same level that historically brings in buyers (CIBC’s Cloherty) and that UniCredit pegs as the post-2024 cap, with a 4.40% to 5.10% range over the past year
  • Fed funds futures now price approximately 2 basis points of HIKES by March 2027, while the same March 2027 ECB pricing shows 59 basis points of HIKES (Deutsche Bank dislocations note). Translation: the bond market thinks the ECB is hiking and the Fed is doing nothing
  • The MOVE Index (a measure of bond-market volatility) is up roughly 19% since mid-April while equity volatility (VIX) sits below 18, the kind of textbook divergence Raymond James flagged as a warning signal for stocks
  • Fed dissent is hardening: Hammack, Kashkari, and Logan are now openly contesting the easing bias (Citadel Securities), and the Fed funds curve has flipped from pricing easing to assigning a real probability of a hike
  • Friday’s NFP forecasts split wide. Citi sees -15k against +65k consensus, Natixis +50k, Goldman +75k, with unemployment expected to tick up to 4.4% from 4.3%

Three forces are pulling the long end higher at once. Treasury supply pressure remains structural (UniCredit pegs the 2026 deficit at nearly 8% of GDP with debt service consuming 20% of revenue), oil-driven inflation is still feeding into core on the standard three-month lag (Brent up 45% versus pre-conflict per JPMorgan), and a Fed with three openly hawkish dissenters cannot credibly jawbone the curve lower. The 30-year part of the Treasury complex is doing the work the Fed is no longer willing to do.

The pushback we would anticipate is that the long end is overbuilt (Citi’s view that the 20-25% probability of a Fed hike now priced is excessive), a weak NFP print Friday would catalyze a sharp rally in long bonds, and the 5% level on the 30-year has historically marked an attractive tactical entry. Even granting all of that, the binding constraint is the supply-demand mismatch in long-dated paper. Crédit Agricole expects $125 billion of new coupon supply this week with $25 billion in 30-year issuance, and a Fed that cannot cut into 3.2% core PCE has lost the option of jawboning the curve lower. The cleanest read for someone with US fixed-income exposure is $TLT remains priced for a softer landing than the data supports, with a sub-50k NFP Friday the asymmetric upside catalyst and a +75k print confirming the no-cut camp.

AI capex is the spine of the tape and Q1 just printed the strongest beat in 25 years

Hyperscaler guidance, semiconductor revenue, and Q1 earnings are confirming the AI build is structural, not cyclical.

  • Hyperscaler capex is projected to hit $800 billion in 2026 and $1.1 trillion in 2027 (Morgan Stanley), while the big four (Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft) are expected to clear $700 billion in combined annual capex, an 80% increase year-over-year (Citadel Securities)
  • 61% of S&P 500 companies beat Q1 estimates by more than one standard deviation, the highest in 25 years excluding the 2021 reopening (Goldman/Pasquariello). Deutsche Bank raised its 2026 S&P 500 EPS forecast from $320 to $342 and projects all 11 sectors positive year-over-year for the first time in four years
  • Google Cloud backlog jumped from $243 billion to $462 billion, with cloud revenue accelerating to 63% year-over-year vs AWS at 28% (Morgan Stanley). Hyperscaler net income grew 61% year-over-year (Pasquariello)
  • Anthropic’s implied pre-IPO valuation has hit $1.2 trillion (a nine-fold increase since October 2025) on $30 billion ARR, and semiconductors in the SOX index now represent 22% of S&P 500 market cap, up from 6% in April 2024 (The Market Ear)
  • AMD’s beat-and-raise is the proximate catalyst: server CPU TAM guided to 35%-plus annual growth through 2030, and Eaton ($ETN) reported data center orders up roughly 240% year-over-year, the picks-and-shovels confirmation

This is what a capex super-cycle looks like in the financial statements. Institutional flows in tech are running 2-3x year-to-date average, with long-only investors actively rotating into AI infrastructure and using non-AI sectors as a funding source (Goldman TMT desk). Token consumption is projected to grow 24x by 2030 (Goldman), and the bottlenecks have spread from memory to CPUs, substrates, and packaging (UBS). Each new chokepoint is creating its own equity beneficiary.

The pushback we would anticipate is the most-cited yellow flag in the corpus. The median S&P 500 stock is 13% below its 52-week high, the widest gap in 25 years (Goldman’s Ben Snider), and April breadth at 23% of names beating the index is the fourth-lowest reading since 1986 (BofA Global Research). One-third of MAG7 profits came from private-equity investment gains rather than AI revenue, and hyperscalers are moving toward cash-flow negative status with US investment-grade bond supply up 20% year-over-year. Even granting that breadth fragility, the binding constraint runs the other way. The picks-and-shovels names (semicaps, power infrastructure, networking) keep getting paid through July earnings unless META or GOOGL guide capex down, which has not happened. The cleanest read for someone with US options exposure is $SOXX as the broad expression of the trade and NVDA’s May 20 print as the next directional catalyst, with Goldman holding Buy at a $250 target on visibility into upside to the $1 trillion datacenter guide.

The melt-up is structurally fragile and the squeeze still has fuel

Fast money is at +6 of +10, hedge funds are net sellers in tech and energy, retail call-buying is at the 92nd percentile. The rally is running on dealer flows, not new institutional buying.

  • Pasquariello pegs fast-money positioning at +6 (down from +8 at year-start), with the systematic crowd “finished buying” and one-month at-the-money S&P put options trading below 2% of spot (the cheapest insurance available right now)
  • Retail gross flows hit the 92nd percentile (The Market Ear) while hedge funds are net sellers in info tech and energy, a retail-vs-pro divergence that has historically resolved badly for the late buyer
  • The SOX 14-day RSI (a momentum indicator that flags overbought above 70) is above 80, a reading rarely seen in 30 years (Goldman TMT spec sales). Dealer gamma sits at “peak long” with QQQ implied volatility (the option market’s price of risk) running 6 vol points wider than SPX (the spot-up-vol-up pattern, where stocks rally and option pricing rises together, is the textbook signature of an unstable rally)
  • 23 S&P 500 stocks are at new 52-week lows. When more than 20 names hit new lows, the index averages -5.3% over three months and -4.3% over six months (Bloomberg, since 1990)
  • Citi’s equity desk reports SPX options imply a +/- 76 basis point move for Friday’s NFP, the lowest one-day implied move in three months (the market is leaning into the print, not hedging it)

The mechanics of the rally and the mechanics of its potential reversal are the same set of dealer flows running in opposite directions. With dealers short gamma above current spot (meaning their hedging makes them buy more as price rises and sell more as price falls), a 1-3% rally forces them to buy aggressively, but a 1-3% break forces them to dump into weakness. The hedging math lands cleanly here: one-month at-the-money S&P puts cost less than 2% of spot, the cheapest convexity available given how compressed front-end volatility has gotten.

The pushback we would anticipate is that equities are still under-owned by institutions (Citadel’s argument for a continued bid), the relaxed-equity-vol vs stressed-bond-vol setup can persist for months, and dealer positive gamma below spot will support any pullback into the 7,180 SPX pivot The Market Ear flagged. Even granting that under-positioning, the precedent matters. The 23 new-lows reading and the 5-year-extreme large-vs-small divergence (Goldman desk) are both historical yellow flags pointing to a 3-6 month mean-reversion. The cleanest read for someone with US index exposure is to run a long-stock-plus-protective-put structure into NFP, sized so the one-month ATM SPX put offsets the rally extension if Friday’s print breaks the calm.

The earnings beat-and-raise is now broader than MAG7. Uber, Disney, AMD, Lilly are the breadth tell

The single-name dispersion under the index hood is bullish where it should be (consumer discretionary, AI infrastructure, GLP-1) and the misses are concentrated in names hiding from the AI catch-up trade.

  • AMD beat with raised server CPU TAM at 35%-plus annual growth through 2030, the catalyst that has lifted the SOX 36% off the war-start lows (The Market Ear)
  • $UBER printed Q1 above estimates with Q2 Gross Bookings guided to +22% year-over-year FX-neutral despite a Middle East-conflict drag on bookings (JPMorgan Tech Sketch). The read-through to $DASH and $LYFT is positive
  • $DIS beat at $1.57 vs $1.49 estimate with raised 2026 guidance and higher buybacks. $LLY raised FY revenue growth guidance to 28% on Mounjaro international ramp. $APO beat Adjusted Net Income by 3% (Goldman raised target to $134)
  • $ANET fell 14% on supply-constraint guidance despite an EPS beat, the AI infrastructure pinch translating into cautious near-term color from networking
  • Apple is reportedly weighing Intel and Samsung as secondary processor suppliers, sending $INTC +13.85% and pushing TSMC ($TSM) -1.75% intraday (The Market Ear). The foundry diversification narrative is back

The setup that worked in MAG7 (beat consensus, raise guidance, get rewarded) is now working below the surface. Consumer discretionary flows are positive on hedge fund positioning at four-year lows (Goldman desk). $UBER, $LYV, $SPHR, and $DIS were up 5-10% in sympathy on the AMD print, with the squeeze that lower oil prices unlock as cheap gasoline directly subsidizes airline, leisure, and travel margins. JPMorgan’s Retail Radar shows tech hardware took its second-largest weekly retail inflow on record, with $SOXX, $SOXL, and single names ($SNDK +$431 million last week, $MU, $INTC, $LITE, $ANET) doing the heavy lifting.

The pushback we would anticipate is the dispersion warning. $ANET down 14% on supply constraints is the canary that hyperscaler capex enthusiasm hits hardware bottlenecks at the company level, $ARM came in only slightly above guidance with Goldman holding Sell at a $125 target (50x normalized P/E is the valuation problem), and the rising LLM/token costs are a real margin headwind for the internet/software complex (JPMorgan Tech Sketch). Even granting those single-name pinches, the breadth of the beats (UBER, DIS, AMD, LLY, APO, plus AMD’s read-through to ETN) is the structural picture. The cleanest read for someone with US single-name exposure is a long $XLY paired against an AI-disrupted software short like $CRM or $NOW, capturing the lower-oil consumer tailwind without doubling up on the SOXX trade running through the AI capex theme.

Crypto is decoupling from the equity rally and the institutional flow tells the story

Bitcoin and Ethereum spot ETFs have flipped to net outflows, the rally has stalled in the $80-82k range, and the institutional bid has visibly thinned.

  • US spot Bitcoin ETPs recorded $468 million in NET SALES on May 5, the fourth consecutive day of outflows. BlackRock’s $IBIT alone drove 54% of the flow (-$252 million)
  • US spot Ether ETPs saw $95 million in net sales, dominated by BlackRock’s $ETHA (-$69 million) and Fidelity’s $FETH (-$24 million)
  • Aggregate Bitcoin ETP notional volume fell to $2.6 billion, well below the $3.4 billion daily average since launch. The institutional rally has lost participation, not just direction
  • US spot Solana ETPs printed $2 million in net sales but saw the second consecutive day of inflows (Bitwise’s $BSOL the only contributor). The SOL story is the lone bright spot in the institutional flow data
  • The risk-on rally in equities is not pulling crypto with it, even as the tape prints all-time highs in $SPY and $QQQ

The mechanics of the divergence are clear in the flow data. The same hedge funds that drove $BTC from $30k to the cycle highs are now de-risking into the equity rally, taking profit ahead of an uncertain NFP, an uncertain Fed, and a 30-year Treasury yield that is sucking yield-seeking capital out of higher-volatility assets. Spot ETPs were the marginal buyer of $BTC in 2025. Without them, the order book thins and the consolidation tightens.

The pushback we would anticipate is that Solana flows have flipped positive in a small but consistent way ($BSOL +$2 million for two days running), the broader risk-on regime tends to lift crypto with a multi-week lag, and the $IBIT outflows could simply be hedge-fund profit-taking ahead of a tax event rather than a regime shift. Even granting that, the consecutive-day pattern matters. Four straight days of net outflows on the largest spot product is the kind of flow change that historically marks a multi-week consolidation, not a buy-the-dip pause. The cleanest read for someone with crypto exposure is that $BTC at $81k is range-bound until the institutional bid returns. A break of $78k confirms the consolidation has resolved lower, and a sustained move above $84k says ETF buyers are back.
