2. INSIGHTS & ALPHA

AI capex eats the buybacks

The hyperscalers just confirmed the build, and the cash that used to fund repurchases is now buying GPUs.

  • Goldman raised 2026 hyperscaler capex to $755B, up 83% year-over-year and equal to 100% of operating cash flow, so dollars that used to buy back stock are funding GPU orders instead.
  • UniCredit puts combined 2026 capex for Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon at $710-725B, three times 2024 levels, with Meta guiding $125-145B and Alphabet $180-190B, the second cycle of upward revisions in a month.
  • Hyperscaler buybacks fell 64% year-over-year in 1Q (Goldman), so the equity-demand drop is a mechanical effect of capex absorbing cash, not a cyclical retreat.
  • BofA’s “AI Big 10” (MAG7 plus AVGO, MU, AMD) now equals 40% of S&P 500 market cap, a concentration last seen at historic-bubble peaks.

Hyperscaler net debt to one-year cash earnings sits at 0.3x (Goldman), so the build can be funded from balance sheets rather than from cutting buybacks further. Same demand for AI compute, same direction for the trade, but the way it shows up in earnings shifts from EPS-via-buyback at the platform level to revenue-via-capex-recipients at the supplier level. The bear case Goldman’s desk flags: one-third of MAG7 1Q profits came from private-equity investment gains rather than core AI revenue, and Friday’s Cloudflare guide miss took $NET -19% as a reminder of what late-cycle single-name vol looks like. The picks-and-shovels names defend that read with Applied Materials and Tokyo Electron capacity sold out into 2027, so the cycle has further to run before any guide-down lands. This week’s CNBC coverage of Intel, AMD and Micron running double digits while $NVDA lagged shows the bid is actively rotating from GPUs into the rest of the AI stack. Long $MU, paired with a trim on CPU-heavy hyperscaler exposure.

Rate-cut bet got too easy

April jobs ran hot at 123k vs 75k expected, and the bond market priced cuts this print does not deliver.

  • Today’s private NFP printed 123k vs the 65k consensus and the 75k whisper, well above forecasts that had Citi at -15k, MUFG at 45k, and Goldman at 75k.
  • Unemployment held at 4.3% as expected, with U-6 ticking to 8.2% from 8.0%, so under the surface labor is softer but the headline beat is what gets traded.
  • Two Fed cuts in 2H26 are on the table and current “no cut in 2026” pricing looks too hawkish (UBS). The pushback is that rates fall slower than the rally implies because energy-driven inflation is sticky (ING).
  • Crédit Agricole forecasts April CPI at 3.77% year-over-year, peaking 4.2% in May before drifting to 3.5% by year-end, with energy doing most of the work via 11-12% MoM rises in motor fuels.
  • Goldman’s “breakeven” job-growth rate (the pace of hiring that keeps unemployment flat) falls from 65k now to 50k by year-end, so even soft NFPs would not push unemployment higher and the labor market may be less helpful to the dovish case than markets assume.

A hot jobs print landing on top of energy-driven inflation is the combination that keeps the Fed on hold longer, regardless of December’s dot-plot. The bond market’s earlier rate-cut pricing was assembled on the assumption labor was already breaking, and Friday’s surprise pulls that assumption forward. The bull case for $TLT requires UBS’s macro read to be right, Hormuz to resolve cleanly, and oil to unwind the energy push to CPI fast enough for the Fed to act in 2H26. None of that is supported yet by SEB’s data on refined-fuel inventories sitting 11% below the five-year average and mine-sweeping the strait still measured in weeks. Tuesday’s CPI is the next test, and Crédit Agricole’s 3.77% headline call sits above the 3.74% consensus going in. Short $TLT into next Tuesday’s CPI.

Hormuz peace memo, supply scar lingers

Brent fell from $108 to $99 on the MoU headline, but ships still aren’t moving and the inventory hole takes months to refill.

  • Brent dropped from $108 to $99 in a session on US-Iran MoU reports (SEB), a paper-market move ahead of any physical change.
  • Hormuz oil exports remain at 4% of normal levels with Iranian loadings under 30% of 2025 averages (Goldman), so the supply hole is real even with a deal pending.
  • Distillate stocks (jet fuel, diesel, heating oil) sit 11% below the five-year average per SEB, which keeps refined-fuel and gasoline prices supported regardless of where headline crude trades.
  • API crude inventories drew 8.1 million barrels (SEB), the biggest in three months versus a 2.8mb consensus, so physical demand is still pulling barrels.
  • China oil demand nowcast down 1.5 million barrels per day since mid-March, an 8% decline (Goldman), a partial offset to the supply scarcity but only partial.

Even with a signed deal, mine-sweeping the strait and rotating stranded crews takes weeks before tankers move. The MoU traded the futures curve, not the physical market. Until inventories rebuild, the supply premium stays embedded in product prices and feeds into core CPI on the standard 3-month lag. The opposite read: Iran’s $270B reparations demand and the unresolved uranium-stockpile destination are real diplomatic hurdles, and a clean deal could collapse the entire premium fast, especially if China demand stays soft. That asymmetry is exactly why $BNO (Brent exposure) captures the trade better than $USO (WTI), because Brent carries the physical supply scar while WTI prices Gulf of Mexico inventory that is largely unaffected. Friday’s headlines, US strikes on two empty Iranian tankers and Iran seizing the Ocean Koi, kept the headline-risk pulse alive even as the dollar slipped on hopes of resolution. Long $BNO into the next Hormuz headline.

First crack in the AI bull

Momentum just had its biggest one-day drawdown since 2020, and hedge fund exposure is at all-time highs into the weakness.

  • GSPRHIMO (Goldman’s high-beta momentum basket, the long-short factor used to track crowded longs vs crowded shorts) fell 8% on May 7, a top-10 one-day move in five years and the fifth such instance in 2026.
  • Hedge fund momentum exposure sits at the 99th percentile over the past year and 100th over the past five years (Goldman), as crowded as it gets.
  • AI and momentum have run with a 1-month correlation of 94%, so they are functionally one trade rather than two diversified themes.
  • The semis ETF $SMH up 153% over the past year is four standard deviations above its long-term mean, the strongest reading since 2001, so the drawdown was a positioning event waiting for a catalyst.
  • Cloudflare ($NET) -19% on Thursday’s guide miss next to Datadog ($DDOG) +30% on a short squeeze shows late-cycle single-name vol is already showing up.

When momentum positioning hits the 100th percentile and AI is the entire trade, any catalyst, a guide-down, a hot CPI print, a Fed surprise, forces a coordinated unwind. The May 7 drop took both legs, long and short, capitulating at the same time, which is closer to a positioning event than a fundamentals turn. The pushback: BofA’s Bull/Bear indicator at 7.2 (up from 6.6) is rising, not retreating, and weekly cash inflows of $136B were the largest since January. The dip is being bought, not sold. The defense: 7.2 is approaching the 8.0 sell signal, and AI Big 10 concentration at 40% of S&P market cap matches historic-bubble peaks. The setup looks like a bid running out of marginal buyers, and Tuesday’s CPI is exactly the kind of catalyst hedge funds are unhedged against. Long $SPY puts into next Tuesday’s CPI, sized for a 3-5% drawdown.
