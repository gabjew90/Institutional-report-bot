2. THE MAIN EVENT

The AI sell-off is about who pays for the buildout

The AI trade is breaking on the cost of paying for the buildout and not on missing demand, and Wednesday night’s first earnings answers split.

  • Meta is selling $95B of stock, $35B now and $60B in installments from 2027, after its first negative free cash flow quarter since its 2012 listing (Goldman). The biggest borrowers are choosing dilution over bonds, which is what higher funding costs look like in practice.
  • Lenders now charge AI borrowers more. Goldman’s index of 30-year hyperscaler bonds widened 17 basis points last week, and AI-linked junk bonds widened 78 over two weeks against 22 for non-AI junk.
  • Hedge funds sold about $20B of tech, media and telecom stocks in two weeks, near the $23B sold in July’s AI selloff, while their total positions, longs plus shorts, still sit at the 96th percentile of five years (Goldman). Trend-following funds hold $38.5B of S&P 500 futures longs and would sell about $65B if the index breaks its trend line, roughly 1.3% below last Friday’s 7,623 close, whatever earnings say.
  • Same-day options are now about 66% of S&P 500 options volume, and traders buying calls on dips and selling puts on rallies are holding down the index’s actual swings more than dealer hedging does (UBS). That cushion holds only while selloffs stay shallow, and tech same-day flow already pushes moves along instead of damping them. Insuring against a Nasdaq 100 drop costs about 10 volatility points more than the S&P 500 over one month, over two standard deviations above normal (Cboe), so a small miss gets sold hard.

Rising borrowing costs raise the price of every new data center, crowded funds are cutting the same names together, and costly protection amplifies each wobble, so prices fall faster than the demand data changes.

UBS calls October’s selloff structural and unlike July’s forced selling, and Goldman’s credit strategists concede their 93-basis-point investment-grade bear case is no longer bearish enough. JPMorgan reads the same spreads the other way, recommending selling protection on AI-related debt because the widening reflects hedging demand and not damage, with power availability and not money as the real constraint. Demand has not cracked: Celestica guided 2027 revenue to $40B against $34.9B expected, SK Hynix reported 104 trillion won of revenue against 98 trillion expected, and Cadence raised its 2026 revenue guide (all Goldman). The first hyperscaler results split, with Microsoft’s Azure growth beating buy-side estimates by a point while Alphabet’s Search missed by 2% and ServiceNow’s fourth-quarter guide slowed sharply (JPMorgan). UBS’s data shows hedge fund long exposure cut from the 90th to the 15th percentile and shorts raised from the 15th to the 80th, so a good print could squeeze. Amazon reports after tonight’s close, where Goldman’s desk wants flat or higher capex with a clear funding plan (a cut reads as negative) and holds capped $AMZN upside structures out to December and $QQQ 735/755 call spreads for November 13.

3. BRIEFS

Morgan Stanley calls the vote a clearing event and keeps tactical S&P 500 downside at 7,100 to 7,200 until it and two other events pass, AI financing worries and tighter financial conditions. The deciding question is whether $SPY clears Amundi’s +2.1% bar after the vote or RBC’s 5-10% pullback arrives first.

The Fed paused, and the desks disagree on how many hikes remain

The Fed held at 3.75%-4.00%, and a December hike is priced near 75%. Goldman expects hiking to end at 4.00%-4.25% on December 9, against futures pricing a 4.55% peak in 2027, and calls fewer hikes than priced its strongest global view. The market’s 4.5%-4.75% peak is too low and a 25-basis-point hike every quarter until the AI investment cycle ends is the path (TS Lombard). This morning’s data gave neither side much: GDP at 3.4% beat the 3.2% estimate, but core PCE came in at the expected 0.3%, and the 10-year yield fell to 4.99%. ING expects it to snap back above 5% on inflation and deficits, so the level to watch is a close back above 5%, which would fit both ING’s case and TS Lombard’s higher peak.

Rotate out of the lead: desks are moving money from AI growth into defensives and small caps

The contrarian call is to own what the AI leaders are not, and desks are naming the instruments.

Defensive stocks are up 8.2% and growth is down 4.5% quarter to date, and $QQQ is down another 1.38% this morning. RBC cut technology to market weight in mid-October and moved health care to overweight. Bank of America’s Hartnett plans to stay defensive until mid-November, calls the move a tech de-rating and not a macro break, and is adding small caps ($IWM), REITs, utilities ($XLU) and consumer discretionary. The lean is rotation toward $XLV and $XLU for defense, with $IWM for the broader market, the pairing RBC and Hartnett each called.

The pushback is crowding. UBS’s desk sees staples squeezed higher and is betting against them with put options. The rotation is wrong if $XLV and $XLU lose to $QQQ in the week after Amazon reports. Watch in this order: (1) Amazon’s capex tonight, because a cut would feed the rotation, (2) the end of Hartnett’s mid-November defensive window.

Oil is pricing strikes that may come after November 3

Brent set a six-month high of $116.72 last Friday and has eased toward $114 as Qatari and other mediators visit Tehran, with WTI near $101 and $USO down 0.94% this morning. An Iranian official said Wednesday that Tehran sees only unacceptable ideas in the latest mediation, and desks expect US and Israeli strikes on Iran after the midterms. Refinery outages run 5.8 million barrels a day above seasonal norms, beyond Goldman’s own worst case, which favors long oil products and European gas, and Goldman rates $OXY ($76 target), $PR and $DVN Buy on a $73 long-run WTI assumption. The counter comes from BTIG, which points to refiners’ worst day since April 2025, a refining margin that fell from $70 to $51 a barrel after a 157% year-to-date run, and $XOP failing at the top of its trend channel. JPMorgan favors long oil and energy equities as the hedge. The premium fades if the vote passes without strikes.
