Hot CPI Stalls The Squeeze

Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 287 research analyses

1. RECAP

Heading into Thursday’s open, stocks carry a strong Wednesday into a harder morning. $SPY closed Wednesday up 1.32% and $QQQ led at +2.31%, while $VIXY fell 6.77% as the cost of protection against a drop kept falling. The overnight news cuts the other way. A Wednesday night report said Nvidia’s board is preparing to fire CEO Jensen Huang, days after the board opened an independent investigation involving him on November 8. $BTC is down 2.03% this morning at $72,529, and Treasury yields are rising in the first full bond session since Tuesday’s hot inflation report.

  • October CPI (Tuesday). Consumer prices rose 0.4% on the month and core CPI rose 0.3%, each 0.1 point above consensus (ING), the third straight month hotter than expected by PNC’s count. Treasury yields are higher again this morning and the dollar gained on Wednesday.
  • The 10-year yield is at 5.05% with a December hike fully priced. The 10-year Treasury yield is up 5 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent each) this morning and the 5-year is up 6.4 to 4.89%, with a quarter-point hike at the December 9 Fed meeting now fully priced (ING). The bond market was closed Wednesday for Veterans Day, so this is the first full session to trade on the Fed officials who spoke after the inflation report, including Chicago Fed president Goolsbee, who called it “borderline disturbing” (Citi).
  • Dollar up, gold down. $UUP rose 0.69% on Wednesday and $GLD fell 1.13%. A Fed that is expected to raise rates increases the return on holding dollars and makes gold, which pays no interest, less attractive.
  • Two tankers disabled in the Strait of Hormuz overnight. Oil has not repriced: $BNO rose 0.67% on Wednesday and crude edged lower in the evening as rising output offset the risk, while Iran has rejected US claims of negotiations and kept its transit curbs in place (UBS). Continued tanker attacks mean the war premium in crude may have been priced out too early in Goldman’s view, while JPMorgan’s commodities desk puts fair value for Brent at $80 to $85.

2. THE MAIN EVENT

The volatility crush ran out of road

Monday’s rally was machinery, not conviction. Dealers came into the week short puts after Thursday produced the biggest single day of put buying in Cboe history, and as prices rose and the cost of protection collapsed, those positions lost value fast enough that dealers had to buy back the futures they had sold as a hedge. The VIX fell from 28 to 20 in roughly two and a half sessions, 424 of 503 S&P 500 members closed higher, and the Dow, S&P 500 and Russell 2000 erased their post-election losses. Nomura’s McElligott reads the broad advance as the sideshow and the volatility collapse as the actual story.

That engine stalled on Tuesday. October CPI printed 0.4% on the month with core at 0.3% against 0.2% expected, a combination only one of 70 analysts had forecast, and the volatility meltdown stopped, taking the rally with it. A December hike is now fully priced, with 14% odds on a half point, after Waller said on Monday that a hot core reading would put 50 basis points, each one a hundredth of a percent, on the table.

The argument is about whether the selling is finished. In UBS’s view, it is, with Risk Control exposure at 55%, the 1st percentile against the past year, trend-followers net short at -5% of global equity exposure, and an expectation that the S&P 500 sits more than 10% higher in three months. JPMorgan’s Lakos-Bujas calls the momentum capitulation far along and keeps his 8,000 year-end target. BTIG’s Krinsky takes the other side with history. Across eight prior episodes of a 10% collapse and a 5% rebound, seven lost about 10% over the following 30 days and none marked the bottom. He wants the index nearer 7,000 to 7,100 before buying and puts 73% odds on another day where 80% of NYSE volume sits in falling stocks. Morgan Stanley’s Wilson wants a marginal new low into 7,100 to 7,300 to finish the retest.

The level decides it. Goldman’s long-term trend-following pivot is 7,084, with nearer thresholds at 7,685 and 7,550, and its desk models those funds as sellers in every scenario this week, about $31 billion in a flat market. Against that, company buybacks are running near $5 billion a day with 90% of corporates out of blackout, which in Goldman’s view, outbids the supply over a month.

3. BRIEFS

Chip risk passed hyperscaler risk

The quiet part of last week was credit. A basket of Broadcom, AMD, Nvidia and Intel protection traded around 80 basis points in early August, some 20 inside the hyperscaler basket, and has roughly doubled to about 160, crossing above that basket for the first time in three months. Volume followed, with AI default-swap activity at $8-10 billion every week, around seven times the run rate of a year ago. The index barely registered it. Goldman’s CDX investment-grade measure widened 3.5 basis points on Thursday while the Nasdaq fell 6.4% and the S&P 4.4%. Buyers absorbed $76 billion of investment-grade supply in a week, nearly double expectations, including five hyperscaler deals worth $67 billion since October 29 that took year-to-date issuance to $296 billion, past Goldman’s $250 billion full-year forecast. Concessions on AI deals ran above 18 basis points in November. Goldman’s forecast is investment-grade spreads above 93 basis points into early December, overshooting March’s wides.

Permits are the new bottleneck

Goldman’s economists see short-term moratoria potentially touching about 45% of planned US data center capacity. JPMorgan counts 42 of 45 local ballot measures to restrict data centers as passed or leaning that way, with newly elected governors in Georgia and New Mexico running on project pauses and Ohio and Nevada on full utility-cost coverage. JPMorgan still holds 2027 hyperscaler capex at $1.7 trillion against $1.3 trillion consensus, with a floor near $1.5 trillion, and since every $100 billion of capex is worth roughly $9 of S&P 500 earnings per share, even that floor adds 4-5% to 2027 index earnings. UBS puts about 30% of 2028 capacity additions at risk from state pauses, and its analyst Keirstead argues the fight is over permitting rather than projects already under construction, which pushes the damage to 2028 and beyond. Financing already felt it when banks shelved the junk bond sale for Anthropic’s $15 billion Texas site on October 29. Next read: the ERCOT interconnection-queue audit due in December.

Chip multiples fell, estimates did not

The de-rating happened without an earnings cut behind it. JPMorgan’s 61 upstream AI names, worth $12.5 trillion, went from 24.3x to 12.0x forward earnings in under six months, with memory collapsing from 8.0x to 3.2x and optical networking from 49.6x to 16.4x. Syz measures the same gap from the other side. Since June 30 the forward multiple on memory makers fell 50% while their expected earnings for the coming year climbed 32%. UBS’s Arcuri calculates that current semiconductor prices already imply roughly 30% and 35% cuts to consensus 2027 and 2028 earnings, and about 55 gigawatts of 2028 AI chip shipments against an original expectation near 70. The classic warning signs of a cycle peak, rising inventories and shortening lead times, are still absent. Morgan Stanley’s Wilson is not buying the bounce, and his silver-stock analog says the first rally in the group should be sold.

Nvidia’s governance problem has a date on it

On November 8 Nvidia’s board announced an independent investigation after CEO Jensen Huang was reported to be the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Huang admitted an extramarital affair in a release issued the same day, and the board stated confidence in him and the management team. The stock fell 5.5% on Monday. UBS expects no effect on the fiscal third-quarter results due November 17, citing AI compute demand that still outstrips supply, and says investors are not backing away from a January-quarter revenue figure near $135 billion. Its longer-dated worries are key-person risk with no clear internal successor and a very flat organization, and better odds that China chip restrictions persist rather than ease. Position size makes this everyone’s problem: Nvidia is about 7% of S&P 500 weight and around 25% of its variance, and options price roughly a 1% index move on the print.

Hedge funds are out of the way

Morgan Stanley’s prime book shows US long/short net leverage falling 5% on Thursday alone to 42%, the fifth largest single-day decline since 2010, leaving nets 8% lower on the week and at the lowest level all year, though still above April 2025’s roughly 35%. Gross leverage ended at 202%, pushing toward 12-month troughs. Exposure to AI themes has fallen to about 32% of total US net exposure, down from a 47% June peak, leaving funds roughly 2% underweight those names against market-cap weight. Goldman’s book tells the same story with net exposure at five-year lows. Morgan Stanley’s read is that positioning is now clean enough that funds are no longer the driver of sharp reversals, while remaining sensitive enough to accelerate a move either way. The crowded trade that is left is the short in small caps, with Russell 2000 short interest above the 90th percentile.
