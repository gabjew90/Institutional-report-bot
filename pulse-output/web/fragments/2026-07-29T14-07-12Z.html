Bondholders stop funding AI

1. RECAP

Crude is doing all the work this morning. Oil is up nearly 7% on the Middle East, with $USO +6.6% half an hour into the session, and stocks are refusing to care: $SPY sits at $739.79, down 0.14%, and $QQQ is flat at $675.13, with $VIXY up 1.3% the only real tell that anything happened. $BTC is holding the $64k area. The backdrop that matters more than any of it is what is happening in 30-year Treasury bonds, where the yield at 5.10% is the highest since 2007. That is the discount rate every AI capital-spending model now has to clear, and it is grinding higher hours before a Fed decision that is genuinely live at 2 PM ET.

  • Airstrikes overnight, then a dead Hormuz deal. US and Saudi forces hit Iran-backed fighters in Iraq, and Tehran followed by ruling out any regional management of the Strait of Hormuz, which kills the de-escalation trade the oil curve had been leaning on. Brent proxy $BNO is +6.1% and ADNOC cleared crude at premiums to Asian refiners in a seventh tender, so the physical barrel market is confirming the headline rather than fading it.
  • Equities are pricing none of it. With crude up almost 7%, $SPY is down 0.14%, $QQQ is unchanged and $GLD is actually lower at -0.27%. The only thing being bought is short-dated protection at $VIXY +1.3%, which is traders hedging a 2 PM central bank event, not an oil shock.
  • The natural dip buyers are already gone. Hedge fund gross exposure, the total size of their long and short book, sits at the 6th percentile of recent years, with the directional net tilt at the 22nd (Goldman). Semiconductors just came through their worst five-day stretch in nine years with no obvious bid underneath them.

2. THE MAIN EVENT

The bond market is what finally broke the AI trade

The AI complex is not cracking because demand missed. It is cracking because the lenders funding it started charging a lot more.

  • The discount rate moved, demand did not. The inflation-adjusted 30-year yield, what a lender actually keeps after inflation, hit 3% for the first time since November 2008 (Bank of America). Every dollar a data center earns in 2030 gets discounted back against that number, so the return bar on every project currently under construction just jumped.
  • Alphabet raised 2026 capital spending to $200bn and posted negative free cash flow of about $5.9bn in the second quarter (JPMorgan). Cash going out now runs ahead of cash coming in, which is exactly what forces a company to the bond market instead of the checkbook.
  • Lenders are repricing the buildout in real time. Hyperscaler credit spreads, the extra yield investors demand to hold that debt instead of Treasuries, and the cost of insuring it against default both hit record wides (Bank of America).
  • The buyers who would normally step in already cut risk. Goldman’s prime desk, which finances hedge funds and sees their flow, logged the largest single-day sale of global tech longs since September 2024, a 3.6 standard deviation move well outside anything a normal session produces, and its AI long-short pair sits in the deepest drawdown on record at more than 30%.

The transmission runs one way. Higher after-inflation yields raise the discount applied to profits that arrive in 2029 and 2030, which is where the entire AI capital-spending thesis lives, and at the same time they raise the coupon a hyperscaler pays to go borrow the money to get there. Goldman’s read is that this is a required-return shock rather than an inflation scare, which is a distinction with a trade attached: one of those reprices multiples, the other reprices the whole curve.

The bull case has real support behind it. JPMorgan’s demand work still shows supply unable to keep up on both the product and the foundry side, and the Nasdaq at 21.8 times forward earnings, the profits analysts expect over the coming year, is roughly a 10% discount to its ten-year average and the cheapest since this cycle started in early 2023 (Goldman). The catch is that the discount is measured against earnings that assume the capital spending keeps getting funded on today’s terms, and today’s terms are precisely what the bond market is withdrawing. Goldman’s own trigger levels are the ones worth writing down: equities struggle if the nominal 10-year gets to about 5%, or if the after-inflation 10-year reaches about 2.7%, which is roughly a third of a percentage point from here. The 10-year sits at 4.62% this morning and is drifting the wrong way into a live decision, while cross-asset volatility is still priced for a calm macro backdrop. Long $VIXY into the 2 PM decision, sized as a hedge rather than a bet.

3. BRIEFS

Hormuz is a supply problem again, not a negotiation

The de-escalation trade died in Tehran overnight, and the physical oil market is repricing faster than the paper one.

Iran ruling out any shared regional management of the Strait of Hormuz removes the diplomatic off-ramp the oil curve had been discounting, and it lands hours after US and Saudi strikes on Iran-backed fighters in Iraq. Persian Gulf oil flows are running at 41% of pre-war levels on a seven-day average, down from 80% in late June (Goldman), so this has stopped being a risk-premium story and become a barrels-not-moving story. ADNOC clearing crude at premiums to Asian refiners for a seventh straight tender is the same message from the buyer side. The counter is that ship transits through Bab el-Mandeb hit their highest in a week and the Houthis are reportedly weighing transit fees rather than closure, which is the behavior of a group that wants the traffic to keep flowing. Crude at plus 7% already pays for the headline, so the better-paid version of this is owning the producers rather than the barrel. Long $XLE, not the front-month contract.

Own the hyperscalers’ bonds, not their shares

The cloud giants have quietly become credit stories, and credit is the better-paid side of that trade right now.

Heavy AI spending has rewired the link between these companies’ stock and their debt (Goldman): equity value is now tethered to asset values nobody can confidently price, the extra yield lenders demand keeps widening against cash flow that is eroding, and the longer-dated bonds increasingly trade off the share price. Which side wins comes down to what happens to cash. Cutting capital spending protects near-term cash and pays the bondholder. Rising operating income pays the shareholder. JPMorgan has high conviction Alphabet returns to the dollar bond market before year-end on the timing gap between cash going out and revenue coming in, and flags the climbing cost of insuring Nvidia’s debt as the more worrying signal, worse than the hyperscalers’ own. Top-rated tech bonds have handed back their spread improvement and sit near March levels, which means investment-grade paper now competes with equities on yield alone. Long $LQD over adding hyperscaler equity here, and tonight’s capital-spending guidance is the tell that flips it.

Chips: positioning washout, or something structural

The chip selloff is not finished, and the reason is supply arriving from China rather than anything in this quarter’s numbers.

The sector’s refusal to rally on the reported $250bn Nvidia and OpenAI data center financing is the tell (BTIG), and that desk puts $SOXX 22% below current levels at its 200-day average, the trend line most desks treat as the last line of support. The supply story is concrete: Chinese memory maker CXMT debuted up more than 470% on Shanghai’s STAR Market and already holds about 8% of global DRAM revenue (Goldman), while NAND spot prices are falling and the shortage is expected to end by the second half of 2027 as that capacity lands. The counter from the same shop is that China’s domestic immersion lithography output is still a handful of unproven systems a year, so the market is pricing a decade of substitution as though it hits this quarter’s numbers. JPMorgan is the lone bull and still backing the cycle, but global semiconductor long-short positioning at the 98th percentile of crowding means almost everyone owns the same trade, and crowding that extreme clears violently in whichever direction it breaks. Short $SOXX until a memory guide-up from tonight’s hyperscaler prints forces the other way.

The dispersion unwind lands on the biggest earnings night of the week

The violent single-stock rotation is crowded traders leaving at once, and it sets up a squeeze into tonight’s prints rather than a slide.

One-month implied correlation, the price of stocks moving together rather than apart, has collapsed, which says the dispersion unwind is real money exiting and not a chart pattern, and JPMorgan’s desk reads the move as traders repositioning rather than anything fundamental changing. Three-month implied correlation at 13% against 17.5% at twelve months (Goldman) shows the market still paying up for stocks to go their separate ways. The mechanics into tonight matter more than the estimates: Microsoft, Amazon and Meta all sit under dealer hedging that cushions moves below current prices and amplifies them above, so a good print gets accelerated rather than absorbed. Microsoft’s flip level is 360 with 390 the next shelf above, and options are pricing a 6% move for Microsoft and 7% for Meta. If Microsoft cannot reclaim 390 on the print and hold it through Thursday’s core inflation reading, the squeeze thesis is dead and the amplify-downward path opens back up. Long $MSFT calls into tonight’s print.

The rotation into defensives is the trade, not the hedge

Momentum is rolling over and the exit everyone keeps naming is defensives and dividends, not a cheaper version of the same tech risk.

Bank of America’s Hartnett is explicit: long defensives, dividends and long-dated Treasuries, short banks, brokers, tech and industrials, with tighter financial conditions now a bigger headwind than earnings, and he reads the blue-collar semiconductor group, down 21% from its June peak, as an industrial cycle that already turned over. Healthcare is where that money is going. $XLV is trying to break out of its year-to-date range while the high-momentum names that move harder than the market sit right on their 200-day averages, and healthcare is set up for a durable turn on valuation, flows and accelerating 2027 earnings, with post-midterm gridlock as the supporting argument (JPMorgan). The counterweight is not trivial. Second-quarter S&P 500 earnings are tracking 34% year over year against 26% consensus with about 90% of reporters beating so far (Deutsche Bank), and the index has never been lower nine months after any of the last twenty midterm cycles. Being wrong here costs you the tail of a momentum rally already 8% off its June highs. Being wrong the other way leaves you sitting in the exact cohort a higher real-rate regime compresses hardest, while the defensive names that already repriced do the work. Long $XLV, with $XLU alongside for the dividend.
