The fundamental gap from 1999 is that today’s AI names are putting up earnings where the dot-com names were burning cash. For US options exposure, the setup leans long the picks-and-shovels names through Q2 earnings, with $SOXX as the cleanest broad expression and $GLW or $VRT as the cleanest single-stock ways to ride the optical-and-power buildout.

The bond market is pricing the Fed on hold for a year, and the banks can’t agree if it’s right

$TLT just took a -3-sigma impulse and the 30-year yield is sitting at the 5% pain threshold, with three big banks split on what the Fed actually does next.

  • Bond market is pricing zero Fed rate cuts in 2026, a complete reversal from a month ago when two cuts were in the curve (UBS)
  • 30-year Treasury yield stabilized around the 5% level, the critical pain threshold for stocks (Goldman)
  • 5-year and 10-year breakevens (the inflation rate priced into the bond market) are at the highest since 2023, with the 1-year forward headline CPI swap at 2.9% versus 2.4% pre-Iran-war (Bloomberg)
  • TS Lombard expects Powell to cut the funds rate as a political gesture but simultaneously widen repo rate spreads, which is functionally a tightening
  • UBS still sees two cuts in 2H26 as the energy-driven inflation impulse fades and growth moderates

The mechanism here is a three-way tug-of-war. The UBS view is that the energy inflation pulse fades and the Fed eases into softening growth. The TS Lombard view is that the Fed is politically forced to cut the funds rate but offsets it through the repo market, with reserves at 8.5% of bank assets and approaching scarcity. The bond market itself, looking at $660B of Fed portfolio swaps still needed and breakevens at 2-year highs, is calling both groups wrong and pricing pure hold. The breakevens data is the tell that the cut-camp has the harder argument right now.

The pushback we would anticipate from the cut camp is that the Fed doesn’t actually need to cut into a 4.3% unemployment print, the energy-inflation pulse is a 3-month-lag story that fades by August, and historical analogs show breakevens spike then mean-revert when oil settles. Even granting that, the binding constraint is that the Fed cannot credibly cut rates while 30-year yields are stuck at 5% and breakevens are still climbing. Thursday’s NFP print is the next forcing event, with consensus at 75K and unemployment 4.3%. A weak number reopens the cut path, a hot number locks in the hold scenario. Net of all this, the bias is that long-dated Treasuries via $TLT are oversold relative to the realistic path of the Fed, with the asymmetric setup being any payrolls miss to the downside reopening the rate-cut bid into a clean-positioned tape.

Hormuz hostilities reignited, the peace-deal trade is unwinding tonight

The peace-deal premium that compressed Brent from $108 to $98 this week just collided with reports of US strikes on Iranian ports.

  • US military reportedly struck Iran’s Qeshm port and Bandar Abbas around 5:30-6:30 PM ET Wednesday (per Fox News reporter cited by Reuters)
  • Iran is accusing the US of violating the ceasefire with the new attacks, and a US navy vessel reportedly came under missile fire (Reuters)
  • Brent had collapsed from $108 to ~$98 on Tuesday’s MoU headlines, with -8.1mb API crude draw underneath the price drop, the biggest in ~3 months (SEB)
  • Distillate stocks are 11% below the 5-year average, indicating significant physical tightness even before any fresh escalation (SEB)
  • $120 WTI is the desk threshold for a market narrative shift (Goldman), and previous diplomatic cycles since March have rallied risk on the announcement and faded when substance failed (SEB)

The mechanism is that the physical oil market has been pricing tightness all week (the API draw, distillate inventories at multi-year lows, $7B of bullish oil bets ahead of the war news per Reuters) while the futures curve was pricing peace optimism. Tonight’s strikes collapse the peace narrative without changing the physical setup. The asymmetry is in the front-month versus back-month spread, with any escalation sending the front vertical while the December 2026 WTI strip stays anchored in the high $80s.

The pushback we would anticipate is that this is the same diplomatic noise pattern visible since March, where each escalation gets walked back within 48 hours and the curve fades the spike. Each previous diplomatic cycle has rallied risk on the announcement and faded when substance failed (SEB). Even granting that pattern, the difference tonight is that the strikes are a fresh kinetic event, not a verbal exchange, and Iran’s response (missile fire on a US navy vessel) raises the floor on what counts as a credible de-escalation. On balance, the setup looks like long the Brent oil ETF $BNO into the next 48 hours of headline risk, with sustained Brent below $95 being the signal the curve is right that this fades back to the supply-and-demand baseline.

The mechanism is that central bank demand has been a structural floor under the gold price for two years, and the technical setup right now is unusually light on speculative positioning. The break above $4800 would force trend-following systematic flows to add, with the call spread structure capturing the asymmetry while keeping defined risk. The $5900 UBS target implies meaningful upside from current levels.

The pushback we would anticipate is that gold has not behaved like a fear hedge in this cycle, and a Fed that holds rates higher for longer is a headwind via the real-yield channel (the gap between yields and inflation expectations). Even granting that, the central bank demand floor is a structural buyer that does not care about real yields, and the geopolitical risk premium just got reloaded by the Wednesday-night Iran headlines. Where the bias sits is a defined-risk long via the $GLD June 440/480 call spread, with a sustained move on the underlying through $4800 being the trigger for trend systematic flows to lift the trade.
