2. THE MAIN EVENT

The AI trade gets tested, not broken, into big-tech earnings

The buildout is real and still compounding, but the market is repricing how it gets paid for, and the megacap capital spenders are where that repricing bites first.

  • Alphabet has already reported and raised its 2026 capital spending (capex) plan to $195-205B from $180-190B, with Google Cloud growing 82% on a $514B pile of signed orders (JPMorgan). The spend is accelerating faster than the revenue meant to justify it.
  • The hidden funding is the tell: roughly $662B of the group’s data-center commitments sit as leases that never show up as debt on the books, so the leverage under the buildout is real but invisible to anyone reading the balance sheet.
  • The crowd chasing it is retail, not the pros. Retail sentiment has ripped to the 90th percentile from the 5th in under two weeks (JPMorgan), while hedge funds sit at just the 33rd percentile of net exposure, how much they are betting long after subtracting their shorts.
  • Intel is the counter the bulls want, capital spending raised to $20B from $17B and its data-center and AI unit up 59% (Goldman), and it just printed a blockbuster quarter before Friday’s open. The layer selling the shovels is still being paid while the cloud giants spend.

Three things are happening at once. Spending is being pulled forward faster than the cloud revenue that pays for it, free cash flow, the cash left after all that spending, is turning negative at the biggest spenders for the first time, and the money to fund it increasingly shows up as leases and purchase commitments rather than cash in the bank. That mix does not break the growth story, it changes who wins inside it. The bear case is genuine: enterprises are routing work to cheaper open-source models to cut their own bills, what UBS calls token optimization, Chinese open-weight models are closing the gap and making premium capability harder to charge for, and the Street expects Meta, Amazon and Microsoft to print negative cash flow by year-end and lean on debt or new shares to keep spending. The defense is that this same group has beaten the negative-cash-flow calls every quarter so far, and the hardware layer underneath them is sold out into 2027 no matter which model provider wins. Alphabet is already in the books, so the live tests are Meta, Microsoft and Amazon next week. Own the infrastructure layer and let the cloud giants prove the cash math before chasing them. Long $NVDA and the chip-equipment names like $AMAT, where demand gets reallocated toward cheaper models rather than destroyed.

3. BRIEFS

Oil’s supply hole is real and equities are barely hedged

Crude is up because the barrels physically are not showing up, and stocks are treating a 40% oil move like it never happened.

Hormuz flows have collapsed to 5.1 million barrels a day from 12.5 million a week earlier (JPMorgan), Iranian loadings that ran 1.5-2 million a day at the start of the month have gone to zero (UBS), and Brent has added about $28 in three weeks toward $92, with Goldman putting the war’s cost at 0.4 of a percentage point on core inflation. The bear read is that demand destruction of over a million barrels a day plus a Hormuz reopening would crush the fat refining margins driving this (UBS). Inventory is why waiting is dangerous. The margin refiners earn turning crude into diesel and jet fuel sits near record highs on both sides of the Atlantic, and a hole this size takes months to refill even if a ceasefire holds. $XOM reported before Friday’s open and crude eased intraday, yet the physical squeeze runs on its own clock. Long $XLE, or the US integrated majors like $XOM and $CVX that pay you a dividend-and-buyback yield rather than bleeding a crude ETF’s roll cost, the drag from swapping expiring futures for pricier new ones.

The ECB’s next move may be up, and the dollar is how you trade it

The energy war changes the European rate story, and the market’s base case of gentle cuts looks like the wrong side of it.

Consensus pencils in ECB cuts next year off a 2.5% median forecast, but a persistent energy war could flip that into demand suppression and a hike toward 3% by early 2027 (TS Lombard, one house’s scenario). The other side sees the bank near its peak after the move to 2.25% and the euro drifting toward 1.18 against the dollar by year-end (UBS), a call that reads as a firmer dollar. Eurozone headline inflation has already cooled to 2.8% from 3.2%, which supports the peak-rates camp, and Thursday’s ECB hold at 2.4% did nothing to settle it. For a US trader the clean expression is the dollar, not euro rates. Long $UUP on the peak-ECB, weaker-euro consensus, with a hawkish energy-war surprise the trigger that would flip it.

Copper is rising on a real shortage, not a clean macro

Copper can hold through a growth scare because the near-term shortage is physical, and physical beats sentiment until the metal actually shows up.

The bull case is concrete: exchange and Chinese warehouse inventories are shrinking while Chinese power-grid investment keeps rising, so the pull on metal is real even with the macro backdrop murky. The risk is just as concrete. A softer US tariff outcome closes the import arbitrage, the profit gap that has been pulling metal into the US, and kicks off a long destocking cycle, while a stronger dollar or an AI-led selloff would expose a crowded speculative long. Thin inventories cushion the downside of a positioning washout, and missing it means a supply-driven grind higher the macro bears never get filled on. Copper is the hard-asset expression that pairs with the energy trade above without doubling down on the same oil bet. Long copper through $FCX or $CPER.
