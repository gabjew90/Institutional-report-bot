**Q:** why would an options chain show a 1 after the ticker if there was no stock split

**A:**

→ That **"1"** suffix (or other numbers like 2, 3) denotes a **non-standard option contract** that has been adjusted due to a corporate action, not just a split.

→ It usually happens when a company undergoes a **merger, acquisition, spin-off, or special cash dividend** that changes the deliverable requirements of the contract.

→ The OCC adjusts these to keep the contract fair, you might be looking at a contract that now represents a weird number of shares, a cash component, or a different underlying asset entirely, which is why it gets flagged with that suffix to keep it from getting confused with the standard chain.
