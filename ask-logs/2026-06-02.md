**A:**

→ Dark pools are private exchanges where institutional investors trade large blocks of securities away from public order books, the primary goal is to execute massive orders without triggering immediate price impact or signaling intent to the wider market.

→ Transactions are processed by the dark pool operator (usually a large bank or broker-dealer like Goldman Sachs or Morgan Stanley) using internal matching engines, they match buy and sell orders internally before ever touching a public exchange.

→ You’re right on the directionality: a large print in a dark pool is just a record of a completed trade, not a live order. It’s often a institutional rebalance or a hedge, and because the trade is already done, it doesn't carry the same "buy/sell pressure" signal as a public limit order.

→ If you see a massive print, don't assume it's a directional bet, look for the volume relative to the average daily volume (ADV) to gauge institutional interest, but remember that the "why" behind the trade is hidden until the 13F filings drop months later.
