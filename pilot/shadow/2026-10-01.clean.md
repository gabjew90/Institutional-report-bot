# Bonds Ignore The Dovish Print

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The Fed Blinked, Bonds Did Not

August core PCE, the inflation measure the Fed watches that strips out food and energy, rose 0.25% from July and the year-over-year rate came in at 3.01%, both below expectations. Most of that drop came from revisions to earlier months rather than fresh disinflation, and the revision was larger than Goldman Sachs expected, mostly in the portfolio management component of the index. On a three-month annualized basis inflation is running at 2.05%, the softest since July 2024. Traders cut the odds of an October rate hike from 47% to 37%, and short-dated bonds rallied, with the 2-year yield down 4 basis points, meaning hundredths of a percentage point.

Longer-dated bonds went the other way. The 10-year yield rose 2.8 basis points to 5.313%, the highest since 2002, widening the gap between 2-year and 10-year yields to 41.5 basis points. The 30-year closed at a post-2002 high of 5.63%. September was the seventh straight monthly rise in the 10-year, the first streak like it since 2011, and the 53 basis point move was the biggest single month since September 2022.

New supply is doing the damage. Paramount Skydance launched the investment-grade bond sale that is the largest piece of a $52 billion debt package funding its Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition, looking to raise about $32 billion in the current tranche. Strategists call the Treasury market a light buyer's strike, and the read from dealers is that 30-year bonds look cheap against history while the big value buyer has not appeared.

Here is the split. Goldman Sachs now sees an October hike as unlikely, has pushed its second hike to December, and sees a strong chance the FOMC concludes no further hikes are needed. Deutsche Bank's US economists still expect the next hike in December despite the revisions. Goldman's own currency strategist Jenkins points out the market is still close to 50-50 on October, so this repricing is not settled.

What decides it: Waller speaks today and is the Fed official most likely to acknowledge the revised inflation data, and September payrolls land Friday with consensus at +100,000. Goldman Sachs flags the climb in 10-year and 30-year yields as the next headwind for stocks once payrolls are out. If the 10-year holds above 5.313% through Friday, the rate relief that would carry the S&P back to its highs does not arrive.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Micron's Beat Did Not Move The Stock

Revenue of $54.23 billion beat the Street's $50.95 billion, EPS of $33.42 beat $31.63, and November-quarter revenue guidance at a $61.5 billion midpoint came in above the Street's $57.57 billion. The stock still traded down 1% in premarket, after rising only 0.4% after the close. Two things explain the shrug. Gross margin guidance of 86.25% sits below the Street's 87.4%, and the long-term contracts that make earnings durable also cap how large a beat can be. Micron now has 26 strategic customer agreements, up from 16 last quarter, with $32 billion of upfront customer deposits and about $150 billion of committed revenue over five years at floor prices, at gross margins above the prior peak. Goldman Sachs stayed Neutral on balanced risk and reward while raising its target to $1,250 from $1,100. JPMorgan's Harlan Sur puts cumulative free cash flow near $200 billion through the end of 2027.

### Asia Memory Still Trades Below Its Highs

Goldman Sachs cut its third-quarter operating profit estimate for Samsung Electronics by 5% to 106 trillion won from 112 trillion on a stronger Korean won. It still expects profit above 100 trillion won on DRAM and NAND strength and keeps a Buy rating with a 490,000 won target. Pricing supports that view, with TrendForce looking for fourth-quarter DRAM contract prices up 13% to 18% for PCs and NAND up 15% to 20% from the prior quarter. The question is who buys the shares next. Samsung's 15 trillion won buyback is 87% done and SK Hynix's 40 trillion won program about 75% done, both finishing in early October, and foreign and retail investors were joint net sellers of both in September. Regional memory names remain well below their peaks, and Kioxia trades at 3.3 times March-2028 earnings with 116% upside to Goldman's ¥38,667 target.

### Accenture's Best Day Ever

Shares rose 17% in premarket, which Goldman Sachs said would be the largest one-day gain in the company's history. Fourth-quarter revenue of $18.68 billion beat the Street's $18.04 billion, and new bookings of $22.7 billion rose 5% in local currency, well above the $19.90 billion the Street carried. Full-year 2027 revenue guidance of 3% to 6% growth in constant currency, or $76.4 billion to $78.6 billion, sits above the 3.0% to 4.0% Goldman Sachs had modelled. The catch is one line below: adjusted EPS guidance of $14.39 to $14.81 is 3% to 6% growth, under the 6.0% to 8.0% previously carried. Cash is the offset, with free cash flow guided to $11.9 billion to $12.7 billion and about $9.5 billion going back to shareholders. The read-through lifted the rest of IT services, with Cognizant up 8% and IBM up 6% before the open.

### AI Platforms Keep Signing Deals

$GOOGL rose 2% after unveiling Gemini 4 Argon, following a roughly 2% selloff into the prior close on reports of internal doubt about the model. Google says it sets a record in real-world software engineering and ties for first on cybersecurity benchmarks, and is already using it internally to free hundreds of terabytes of data center memory without buying hardware. $ORCL signed Tencent's largest overseas lease, five years across Southeast Asian data centers, worth about $7 billion with roughly 30% paid upfront, giving Tencent access to about 100,000 advanced AI chips it cannot buy in China. Cloud revenue growth across Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Oracle accelerated from 25% in 2024 to 48% in the second quarter of 2026. $SNPS held its roughly 5% Investor Day gain on targets of about 15% annual revenue growth through fiscal 2030, a 50% operating margin and $35.00 of EPS that year.

### France Pays For A Budget Nobody Has Passed

Prime Minister Lecornu's 2027 budget targets €54 billion of adjustment to bring the deficit to 5.0% of GDP from 5.4% this year, against roughly 6.5% with no new measures, and Goldman Sachs expects 5.3% in practice. The French-German 10-year spread widened to 134 basis points intraday, the widest since 2012, before partially retracing after the auction, and Italy's spread over German bonds closed above 100 basis points for the first time in over a year. The arithmetic is the harder problem: debt-to-GDP is projected at 125% by the start of the next decade, and stabilizing it requires a primary surplus of 1%, which France has achieved only in the 95th percentile of outcomes over the past 35 years. With spreads near 120 basis points and positioning heavily one-sided, Goldman Sachs says it would not take much good news to force a squeeze. The tail risk is Melenchon, now second in first-round polling, whose proposal to cancel debt held at the Banque de France could move markets even at low odds of winning.

### The Resilience Puzzle Nobody Has Solved

Morgan Stanley's Andrew Sheets frames it plainly: 10-year Treasury yields are up 100 basis points this year while global equities are up about 13%, which works only if growth expectations are rising as fast as rates, and he argues that is what is happening. UBS data makes that harder to believe outside the data centers, with private non-residential investment averaging 0.1% year over year over the last eight quarters against a 3.2% long-run average and residential investment at -2.0% against 2.2%, while AI-related investment grew 26%. Goldman Sachs reads the puzzle as smaller than it looks. The economy grew 3.4% in the third quarter, little-changed financial conditions imply only a 0.1% hit to growth because higher stock prices, narrower credit spreads and a weaker dollar offset higher rates, and the oil shock nets to a 0.3% hit after the capex offset. Consumption is the beat, cut to 2.0% in the models but tracking 2.2%. What breaks it is equity prices: a selloff plus the hit to household wealth is what would most likely tip the US into recession.

### Oil Trades The Diplomacy, Not The Barrels

Brent is back above $100 and WTI rose 2% to $92 to start the month, after futures fell more than 3% and dropped below $90 on a cluster of de-escalation signals. Qatar and other mediators keep passing messages between Iran and Washington, Saudi Arabia restored about half the capacity of its East-West pipeline after drone attacks, the IEA says it is ready to release more reserves, and the US is offering up to 40 million more barrels from its emergency stockpile. The physical market disagrees with the futures market, with dated Brent still priced at $120 after Iran struck a very large crude carrier in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent rose 42.0% from its late-June lows through the third quarter on the re-escalation and sits at $103.53. The desk read is that the two sides are inching closer to a deal. For the other side of that, Goldman Sachs FX Sales is pitching the December 2026 Brent $120 call at $0.78 a barrel, expiring October 27.

### One Headwind Cleared, Another Ahead

Pension funds were modelled as one of the largest month-end sellers of US equities on record, in the 89th percentile of the past three years, and that selling is now behind the market. Money keeps going to the same place, with the Magnificent Seven ETF taking inflows in 12 of the last 13 sessions and cybersecurity funds on track for one of their best inflow months on record. The S&P 500 has beaten the Russell 2000 for 7 straight weeks, the longest run since 2018, technology has beaten the index for 6, and the equal-weight S&P is heading for a 7th consecutive weekly decline. Breadth argues against all of it, with NYSE new lows exceeding new highs for ten straight days and the NYSE Composite at three-month lows. Goldman's Nelson Armbrust stays constructive on October and bullish into November and December, with the index about 2% from its highs and rate relief the trigger.

### Bitcoin Rallied Without The Leverage

Bitcoin posted one of its strongest third quarters on record, up 43%, with 10-year yields up 100 basis points for the year and Brent up 60%. Goldman Sachs ties that to steady ETF inflows and now calls the technical setup constructive. The quality of the move is the part worth tracking: price is up 35% from the August low while open interest measured in coins is down almost 20% to its lowest since March, and on-chain data shows the rally running on less borrowed money. Bitcoin sits near $84,000 and was flat on the day. Gold is the mirror image, trading at $4,162 after Goldman Sachs cut its year-end target to $4,650 from $4,900 on weaker rate-driven ETF demand while keeping $5,400 for end-2027 on central bank buying. The dollar is back at the top of the range it has held for 17 months, near 102.

### Thursday's Data Is A Two-Sided Test

Goldman Sachs estimates September ISM manufacturing was unchanged at 54.6, against consensus near 55 and Deutsche Bank at 55.1. Goldman's own manufacturing survey tracker fell 0.6 points to 56.1, and it expects the services PMI, running well above other services surveys, to normalize in coming readings. Initial jobless claims are forecast at 200,000, in line with consensus and up from 197,000, with payrolls Friday at a consensus of +100,000. A hot ISM puts October back in play after both Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank moved their next expected hike to December, and a soft one gives short-dated bonds the rest of the move they started this week.

### Desk Moves Worth The Ticket

Goldman Sachs upgraded $OXY with a 12-month target of $69 on debt reduction and valuation, pointing to a plan targeting $4.0 billion of cash flow improvement by 2030. It cut Tourmaline to Sell with a C$49 target on Western Canadian gas weakness. The October Conviction List added Amazon, Burlington, Huntington Ingalls, Johnson Controls and Occidental and dropped Air Products, ConocoPhillips, Golar LNG, Loar and Tyson. Acuity Brands beat on fiscal fourth-quarter EPS at $5.77 against $5.57 expected, on in-line revenue of $1.24 billion, with gross margin at 50.2% against 49.8% consensus. Goldman Sachs reiterated Buy on Quanta Services, seeing growth beyond the guided 7% to 10% organic rate through 2030 as past acquisitions help it win large data center awards. Industrials closed yesterday down 125 basis points and materials down 80, both well behind the broader market, ending the month down 465 and 750 basis points.

### Airlines Are Absorbing The Fuel Bill

American and Southwest both say demand has held even after they raised fares to cover much higher fuel costs. Southwest flagged strong September trends and expects product, network and loyalty work to push margins higher over the coming years. American points to fleet, onboard and airport investment, loyalty growth and network changes as its own margin drivers. The condition both managements name is persistence. If fuel prices stay here, each sees downside risk to its 2027 growth plans, and Goldman Sachs expects the same across the industry. Both are already reviewing capacity, with December schedule changes possible.

### Brazil Votes Sunday

The Bovespa has rallied 10% from its mid-August lows, but domestic stocks outside commodity exporters, rate-sensitive names and small caps are flat to down for the year. Goldman Sachs recommends receiving the belly of the DI swap curve into the October 4 election, a bet that Brazilian interest rates fall, and calls local rates the best risk and reward among Brazilian assets. In equities it prefers rate-sensitive names and index call spreads. The currency is the scoreboard. Its cyclical fair value model puts the dollar against the real at 4.80 if the risk premium left over from the 2024 selloff fully unwinds, at 5.40 under a milder negative fiscal outcome and 5.80 under a clearly negative one.
