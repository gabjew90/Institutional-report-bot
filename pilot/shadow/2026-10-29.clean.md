# Credit Decides The AI Trade

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The AI trade now trades on what the money costs

Monday's rebound was led by the AI names and almost nothing else. The Nasdaq 100 rose 130 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent) and reclaimed its 100-day average price, the Russell 2000 added 81 basis points and the S&P 500 added 67, but 286 S&P names fell and the index excluding its AI-linked members dropped 50 basis points [c626, c627]. Goldman's baskets show where the money went, with neocloud names up 585 basis points, optical networking up 5%, inference chips up 466 basis points and memory up 368 [c221, c222, c223, c224]. Goldman's flow desk says the bounces of the last two weeks are mostly the end of heavy selling rather than fresh demand, and Peter Bartlett on its US desk calls the move supply abatement.

What makes the rebound fragile is that the argument has moved off whether anyone wants AI compute and onto what the money to build it costs. Goldman's delta-one desk in London states it directly: demand is not the problem, the question is how heavily to discount future AI cash flows given uncertainty around supply, competition and financing, and the cost of capital has repriced with no obvious solve. The figures behind that: leases and purchase commitments kept off the balance sheets of five leading hyperscalers now total $2.8 trillion, eight times the 2023 level, per Goldman's chief credit strategist Amanda Lynam. The extra yield demanded to hold the riskiest junk-rated corporate debt closed at 1,118 basis points, the highest in three years, per Goldman's credit sales strategists. Goldman's Jan Hatzius has AI capital spending adding about 1.5% to US GDP in the fourth quarter of 2026 and only about 0.6% by the end of 2027.

Meta already answered the financing question by selling stock instead of borrowing, a plan Bloomberg values at up to $97.5 billion with the common stock portion raised to $25 billion from $22.5 billion. Goldman's Privorotsky calls that the middle ground, spending flat or higher but funded with equity, bad for hyperscaler shareholders and potentially good for their bondholders.

The two desks disagree on what to do with it. Goldman's Lee Coppersmith has a relief rally as his base case given the size of the hedge fund unwind, and prefers call spreads to outright longs because borrowed exposure is still high and systematic funds are still selling [c540, c591]. UBS's flow desk argues the oversold bounce in tech has likely already run its course and wants to cut exposure that only pays on further upside, pointing to software up just 1.1% since Thursday against a 0.8% gain in the S&P 500 while semiconductors ran far ahead [c631, c569, c108, c624, c277]. Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday and Amazon Thursday. Goldman's test is spending flat or up, which it calls fine, against a decline, which it calls disastrous for the suppliers, and a sustained narrowing in AI credit spreads would be the catalyst that turns a bounce into a rally.

## 3. BRIEFS

### A hold today, and an argument about December

Markets put roughly 90% odds on the Fed leaving its target range at 3.75% to 4.00%, with December hike odds near 80%. UBS economist Jonathan Pingle expects Chair Warsh to deliver a hawkish hold, with the vote likely 10 to 2 and dissents from Dallas Fed President Logan and Cleveland Fed President Hammack [c438, c434]. UBS reads the pause as a function of midterm proximity and inflation stuck near 3% rather than accelerating, and still has 25 basis point hikes in both December and January. Goldman also expects a hold followed by a move to 4% to 4.25% on December 9, but its strongest global view is that central banks deliver fewer hikes than markets price. Short-dated rates have stabilised, which Goldman's desk calls objectively good for risk assets. The options market prices a 61 basis point S&P move through tomorrow's close, up from 50.

### Nobody has actually re-risked

The floor was running a 4 out of 10 on Monday with benign flow skews, and the only new buying Goldman's desk could find was limited and confined to the AI names. The momentum factor rose 2.5% after a roughly 20% drawdown and Goldman's high-beta momentum basket is up about 8% over three sessions, yet its prime brokerage team has fundamental long/short momentum exposure near year-to-date lows after the recent cut in gross positions. Hedge fund gross leverage sits at 312%, the 96th percentile over five years, while net leverage fell to a one-year low of 47%, which is defensive positioning without actually shrinking the book. Goldman's model has systematic trend-followers long $38.5 billion of S&P futures, the 91st percentile historically. UBS's own model has Nasdaq positioning roughly halved over two weeks with a further $1.2 billion of selling projected, though improving Dow momentum could pull in $1.5 billion of buying [c571, c548].

### The futures book never got cleaned out

Through the October 13 to 20 selloff, non-dealer net positioning in Nasdaq futures hit a new nominal high, with $9.5 billion bought as the index lost 3.5%, a third straight week of buying for a cumulative $21.8 billion since September 29 [c582, c583, c584]. Meta's 7.0% drop on October 22 brought the first real wave of new bets against the index, with the Nasdaq down 2.9% and outstanding futures positions up $10.6 billion, the largest one-day rise of 2026 outside roll periods. Funding underperformed that day, which is how Goldman identifies the new shorts rather than new longs. From October 22 to 26 the index recovered 1.1% and $9 billion of that open interest unwound as both the tactical shorts and some longs closed out [c585, c586]. Length is therefore still elevated into Amazon, Microsoft and Alphabet, and Goldman's futures team says a disappointment could trigger meaningful selling.

### Stock supply has flipped the wrong way

Net equity supply in the second quarter was the highest on record at over $150 billion. Since the late 1990s that number has been negative in almost every quarter, because buybacks and takeovers absorbed more stock than new issues and listings created, and this year the pattern reversed. The cause is the hyperscalers, whose need for capital to build compute has pushed them toward selling stock alongside borrowing as debt gets more expensive. Anthropic's coming listing is set to be larger than SpaceX's record summer offering. S&P 500 free cash flow in aggregate is expected to fall given the size of capital spending, which could crimp buyback plans and make the supply problem worse [c638, c639]. Goldman adds the market-reaction condition: any further sign of equity issuance or balance-sheet strain from the hyperscalers is unlikely to be well received.

### Korea got paid

SK Hynix's third-quarter revenue beat UBS analyst Nicolas Gaudois's estimate with operating profit in line, and the company announced a KRW100 trillion shareholder return programme for 2026, at least KRW30 trillion in cash dividends and KRW60 trillion in buybacks and cancellations. That was worth about 7% on the KOSPI, its best day in three months, with Samsung up about 8% [c280, c282]. The company also warned that shortages of components vital to Nvidia will persist until 2030, which keeps prices and operating margins high, and Nvidia's Jensen Huang sees little chance of memory oversupply through 2029. Goldman cut its KOSPI target to 9,000 from 12,000 on more conservative assumptions about how the market prices the underlying fundamentals, while still calling Korean equity risk and reward attractive. The index is now the clearest live read on whether AI demand or AI financing wins.

### Oil is the condition for everything broadening out

Brent fell 1.6% to $114 a barrel after Iran's Mojtaba Khamenei called for pursuing the conditions Iranian officials have set to end the war [c512, c495]. Citadel Securities had it above $117 earlier in the week after attacks in Saudi Arabia and around Gulf shipping. The Wall Street Journal reported the US has told several Gulf Arab states to prepare for a short, intense military campaign against Iran after the midterms. Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani delegations are in Tehran with Qatar mediating, and Goldman puts the odds of a breakthrough as low, warning that without tangible progress by Thursday morning escalation risk resumes into the weekend. UBS makes a sustained move lower in oil the explicit condition for rotating into non-tech cyclicals and markets outside the US. Goldman's commodity strategists recommend targeted exposure to refined fuels and European natural gas to hedge the near-term escalation risk.

### Healey's first Budget, and the bank levy

UK Chancellor John Healey's Budget on Wednesday is expected to favour modest household relief over a fiscal reset, with UBS Economics calling it cautious rather than transformational. Room against the fiscal targets may have fallen to around £10 billion from £23.6 billion in March, and the Chancellor is expected to rebuild only about £15 billion through smaller tax rises while deferring harder welfare and departmental decisions to 2027 [c473, c474]. Keeping the fiscal rules should support gilts, per UBS, although dealers polled by Reuters expect an upward revision to next year's financing needs. The live equity risk is the bank levy, where verbal market consensus has it moving to 8% from 3%: UBS models a rise to 6% as a 3% to 4% cut to NatWest and Lloyds earnings and 1% to 2% for Barclays and HSBC [c113, c65]. UBS sees only selective UK opportunities rather than a broad rerating.

### The RBNZ blinked, the RBA did not

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand left its official cash rate at 2.75% against expectations of a 25 basis point hike, on a 4 to 3 vote with Governor Breman among those holding [c338, c339], and the New Zealand dollar fell 1.5%. The committee cited global uncertainty weighing on domestic demand and said more tightening is likely needed but conditional on the outlook. UBS economist Stephen Wu still expects 25 basis point hikes in December and February to a 3.25% end point, well below what the market prices. Australia went the other way, with third-quarter trimmed-mean inflation at 1.0% quarter on quarter and 3.7% year on year and headline at 4.3% [c687, c688]. Governor Michele Bullock said upside risks are materialising and inflation will likely run above the August base case, and November hike odds jumped to 86% from 60%. UBS's desk is buying dips in the Australian dollar against the New Zealand dollar around 1.2475 to 1.2485.

### Brazil repriced in one session

The Brazil ETF fell 20% on Monday, its worst session outside Covid, after Lula won re-election, the real lost another 9% against the dollar and the Bovespa closed down 14%. Goldman's Brazil desk traded around $80 billion of currency volume against a $30 billion to $40 billion average, and local rate futures needed a third trading halt before the market reopened. Goldman's Alberto Ramos says the fiscal starting point has visibly deteriorated after a series of expensive campaign promises, with primary spending up close to 6% a year in real terms over the last four years [c452, c453]. Reports have Lula's team weighing cuts to tax incentives, streamlined social benefits and a change to how the constitutional health and education spending floors are calculated. Goldman's desk is running light risk until the finance minister appointment and the fiscal measures are known, and Thiago Marinheiro expects Brazilian currency to do better than Brazilian rates over the long run.

### The suppliers are still beating

Celestica beat and guided fiscal 2027 revenue to $40.0 billion and adjusted earnings to $21.50 a share, about 11% and 9% above street estimates, which implies 90% revenue growth and came with management pointing to 2028 customer demand meaningfully above 2027 [c56, c54]. The stock rose about 17% after hours. Goldman reads it positively for Flex, Broadcom and Arista in the US. Cadence beat and raised its 2026 guidance with $8.2 billion of backlog, and framed AI as something that increases usage of its core chip-design tools rather than a threat to them [c47, c49, c48]. Rambus beat too and still slipped after hours. Seagate beat and guided revenue up 14% quarter on quarter and fell 7% after the bell anyway [c144, c145]. The reaction pattern, not the results, is what the hyperscaler prints have to fix.

### A construction read nobody expected

Non-residential construction is standing out favourably this earnings season, with previously weak areas including warehouse, retail and commercial office getting better or less bad beyond the data-center-driven strength. United Rentals' commentary, Allegion's organic growth and Fastenal's results all point the same way, and Allegion is now the top-weighted member of the homebuilding index heading into a seasonally favourable November. Goldman's industrials desk thinks that sets Caterpillar up well this week after its large selloff. Defense is also bid on fundamentals rather than defensive rotation, with Lockheed Martin up mid-single digits on a clean print and Northrop Grumman up a similar amount [c246, c112, c116]. Against that, Whirlpool lost $0.21 a share against $0.76 expected and cut full-year revenue guidance to $14.5 billion from $15 billion [c156, c157], and Greenbrier guided 2027 operating margin to 7% to 7.5% against 10% consensus.

### Deposits are the bank story, factors are the price

HSBC fell 5.6% in Hong Kong as competition for deposits in the UK and Hong Kong squeezes the interest income banks earn on loans [c63, c321], and Goldman flags the same competition spreading across Europe as the thing to watch. Inside US financials, large capital-markets banks and trust banks are outperforming regional banks while alternative asset managers trade choppy. The AI rally has created a factor setup that favours high-beta momentum and crowded shorts over low-volatility defensives, which is why senior housing REITs fell 4% to 6% and CME dropped 4% on positioning rather than fundamentals [c554, c94]. Property and casualty insurance bears were vocal on the Ryan Specialty read-through from Brown and Brown and workers compensation trends at Hartford [c86, c105]. No real buying has shown up to defend the defensives, because investors do not want to fight an early-stage factor shift before the Fed.
