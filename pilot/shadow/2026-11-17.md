# Nvidia Loses Its Banker

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The lender at the center of the AI trade just changed hands

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive, president and director with immediate effect [c158]. Chief financial officer Colette Kress took the job on an interim basis while keeping the finance role [c475], and Harvey C. Jones became the company's first chair [c390]. The stock fell as much as 13% during the session and closed about 4% lower [c367], leaving the market value back under $4 trillion and level with Microsoft [c165]. Fourteen sell-side desks cut ratings or price targets within hours [c366].

The chips for the next two years are already sold, so the argument is about money rather than demand [d13][d18]. Capacity is sold out for 2027 and largely for 2028, and the product roadmap is set about three years out [c55], which is why Wolfe Research sees no effect on fundamentals, earnings or orders through at least 2028 [c105]. What Huang ran personally was the lending. UBS puts Nvidia's spare cash at roughly $50 billion to $60 billion a quarter [c442], a large share of which has been recycled into funding the companies that buy its products [c456], and UBS's Tim Arcuri estimates that even a temporary pause could pull $50 billion or more out of the system at a time when borrowing costs are rising [c307]. The last quarterly filing carries $530.5 billion of commitments, buybacks and guarantees, including a $105 billion rent backstop for OpenAI's Ohio site [c363].

That is where the banks split. Goldman's James Schneider expects every signed commitment to be honored and the next round to be harder to arrange, and cut his target to $220 from $300 while keeping a Buy [c176][c177]. J.P. Morgan is Underweight the credit after default insurance on Nvidia hit record highs, and expects new deals to take longer or require more compensation [c195][c197][c204]. Piper Sandler takes the other side, arguing a change of chief executive does not alter the investment case, and keeps a $300 target on 14 times 2028 earnings of about $23 [c49][c57]. On China the gap is wider: J.P. Morgan stripped the roughly 8% of first-half revenue billed to China and other regions out of its fiscal 2028 estimates, cutting earnings about 10% and its target to $215 [c185][c186][c187], while Wolfe calls those shipments immaterial and further export controls irrelevant to the business [c107][c109].

Kress reports third-quarter results on Tuesday November 17, the first test of whether the financing platform stays open to new deals [c487]. Three months after Huang launched it, the $500 billion platform has produced none [c374], and the earliest a deal could plausibly be announced is January 2027 [c380].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Who pays for the data centers now

Non-investment-grade borrowing for AI data centers has stopped, with no new deals in the month since late September against roughly 30 over the prior year [c1815][c1870][d52]. AI high yield spreads, the extra yield investors demand over Treasuries, have widened to roughly 425bp, and one basis point is a hundredth of a percent [c1817]. Comparable non-AI paper sits near 265bp [c1873]. AI investment grade has widened to about 140bp [c1818], roughly 40bp more than non-AI investment grade [c1819]. The blockage is completion risk, since a tenant's investment-grade guarantee only takes effect once the building is finished [c1820], and only 468MW of the roughly 4GW financed by the bonds Goldman tracks has been delivered [c1823]. Goldman's Miriam Wheeler reads that as a split market rather than contagion [c1877], and the top of it never closed, with Morgan Stanley counting about $67bn of unsecured investment-grade bonds priced by the largest cloud operators inside five business days [c1791].

### Funds sold the best chip week since April

Hedge funds net sold US stocks for a second straight week and in five of the last six [c2044][d8], and this time the selling came from fresh single-stock shorts rather than dumping longs [c2053]. The long/short ratio in US information technology fell to 1.5, the lowest in Goldman's prime brokerage records going back to 2016 [c2052]. The share of fund capital actually exposed to the market dropped to 43.3% among US fundamental long/short managers, a five-year low [c2049], after gross exposure fell 8.7 points in midterm week, the largest drop since March 2020 [c2048]. Readings this light have historically preceded an average 6.3% S&P gain over the following three months [c1987], though Goldman's own strategist calls the setup a blank slate with little consensus either way [c1989]. Hedgers are not convinced: the week before the midterms saw 55.8 million puts trade, the most ever [c2276].

### Three hot inflation prints, three hikes

October core consumer prices rose 0.30% on the month, a third consecutive upside surprise [c1564], and the three-month annualized pace reached 3.7%, the fastest since early 2025 [c1543][d75]. Goldman added a third 25bp hike in March 2027, taking the peak policy rate to 4.25-4.50% and pushing the first cut back to December 2027 [c1318][c1319]. Governor Waller said a 50bp December move should be considered at a 0.3% print [c1320], while RBC calls 50bp highly unlikely and now expects back-to-back 25bp hikes in December and January [c1306][c1352]. JPMorgan still expects 25bp in December, with the median committee member projecting multiple further hikes in 2027 [c1302][c1305]. The bond market moved further than either: cumulative hike pricing through September 2027 went to about 90bp from about 47bp in a week [c1337], and the 2-year Treasury yield had its worst week since 2024 [c1742]. Friday's producer prices were softer where it counts for the Fed's preferred gauge, so Goldman cut its October core PCE estimate to 0.30% from 0.35% [c2404].

### Two of every three announced gigawatts are not real

Bernstein rescored every North American data center project in its database and credits only 199 of 586 announced, under-construction or land-held gigawatts, 34% [c898][d1]. Stage of development removes 198GW and tenant quality another 113GW, while state politics takes out just 28GW [c900][c901], the smallest of its four filters and the one Wall Street spent the week modeling [c1147]. Goldman's Alec Phillips puts about 45% of planned US capacity in states likely to tighten regulation, with Texas alone roughly 25% [c996][c997]. Morgan Stanley moves about 5GW of US capacity from 2028 into 2029 and leaves combined 2027 capex unchanged at roughly $1.66tr, up 75% on this year [c1084][c825], which deepens its projected power shortfall through 2028 to 44GW from 38GW [c1085]. Restricting new supply raises the value of compute already running, which is the week's cleanest explanation for cloud operators beating chipmakers [c704].

### Tokyo is where the stress actually is

BofA's cross-asset stress index rose from +0.15 to +0.20 over the week [c1163][d56]. The average hid the shape: it jumped to +0.17 on Thursday [c950], the largest one-day rise since October 10, 2025 [c951], the same session the Nasdaq had its worst day since the Covid crash [c1462]. Japan's regional score jumped 0.50 to roughly 1.7 while the US score rose 0.06 to below 0.2 [c953], leaving Japan about nine times as stressed as the US [c954]. The single most stressed of the 41 gauges is Tibor-OIS, Japan's interbank funding spread [c1803], whose five-day move ranked above 99% of all moves since 2000 [c1804]. The Nikkei fell 6% on Friday into a technical bear market, 20% below its June peak [c956]. The yen rose only 50 basis points on the Nasdaq's worst day [c1645], and Goldman still argues its yen hedge works but needs a bigger and broader shift in expectations than has happened so far [c1581][c1582].

### Bitcoin reconnects with risk

Bitcoin has lost roughly a quarter of its value since October 21 [c2413][d43]. A weekend flush produced close to $2 billion of liquidations after the price broke below its average level of the past 200 days, around $73,000 [c2414][c2415]. Spot Bitcoin funds have sold for seven straight days, with $2.35 billion leaving over two weeks [c2417][c2416]. MARA sold 13,597 coins for about $1.1 billion [c651], 40% of its September-end holdings in six weeks [c652], using $750 million of it to repay loans secured against Bitcoin [c654]. Funding costs turned negative on Binance, OKX and Bybit on November 13, meaning traders now pay to hold short positions [c2420]. One theory circulating is that holders are selling to fund Anthropic listing allocations [c2418], pointing to a 19% Bitcoin decline in the 50 days before the SpaceX listing against a similar 18% drop now [c2419].

### The 2027 argument is about credit, not chips

Goldman's 2027 outlook has the AI investment impulse peaking in early 2027 [c986], with S&P earnings growth slowing from 36% to 11% [c1512], after AI delivered 62% of this year's earnings growth and 92% of the index's market cap gain [c1509][c1510]. Its most constructive call is duration, with the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.6% by end-2027 against roughly 5.1% now [c1724]. Morgan Stanley reads the same capex cycle as intact and went to maximum overweight global equities, raising its 12-month S&P target to 8,700 on 19 times 2028 earnings of $458 [c786][c1443]. J.P. Morgan reiterated 8,000 and treats the drawdown as a positioning reset that makes the next leg more sustainable [c1437][c1990]. Both sides agree on the mechanism that decides it: Goldman says the channel to broader economic downside is financial conditions themselves [c1029], and its base case is that index credit spreads widen further from here [c1841].
