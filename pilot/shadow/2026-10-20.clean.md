# AI Credit Sets The Price

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Credit is now pricing the AI equities

Last week the AI argument stopped being about how much computing power the world needs and became about who pays for it. Goldman's two AI credit baskets, which track the extra yield lenders demand over Treasury bonds to hold hyperscaler and data-center debt, sit at their widest levels of the year. Goldman's Louis Miller points out that the last time that debt traded this wide, the same hyperscaler stocks were 15% lower than they are now. That gap is the argument: either the bond market is wrong or those equities have another 15% to give. The supporting evidence is specific rather than atmospheric. The cost of insuring hyperscaler debt against default hit a record. High-yield bonds funding AI projects trade near $94 after changing hands above $101 over the summer. Seven of the 20 junk-rated AI data-center bonds have exactly one tenant, CoreWeave, and about $14 billion of those bonds sit behind a company whose own debt is distressed. CoreWeave's five-year default insurance closed at a record 1,117 basis points (one basis point is a hundredth of a percent), which implies roughly 63% odds of default within five years.

JPMorgan reads the same numbers as an overreaction. JPMorgan's Nathaniel Rosenbaum measures the move at 12 basis points wider on the week to 148 basis points, the widest since July 2026, with the spread between the best and worst borrowers at 21.2 basis points, the highest since May 2025. His case is that hyperscaler debt is up $117bn this year while the group's average credit rating improved modestly, because the new borrowing came from the higher-rated names, so the widening is overdone unless earnings show real weakness. JPMorgan's own credit desk puts the real stress one rung down, with the borrowers that live on new debt: the companies renting out GPU capacity, the project finance behind individual data centers, and chip-purchase deals. Goldman's Brad Shelofsky lands closer to JPMorgan than to his own equity colleagues, calling the hyperscaler credit basket 70 basis points cheap to the broader market and the year-end setup constructive.

Earnings settle it. 14% of the S&P reports this week and 44% the week after, Meta reports Wednesday, and JPMorgan points out that earnings releases marked the wides in hyperscaler spreads this summer.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Funds cut the bet, not the book

Hedge funds did not trim AI exposure last week, they reversed it. Goldman's prime brokerage book shows every dollar of net AI stock buying since the start of 2025 now undone, global technology stocks posting their largest percentage net selling on record back to 2016, and US long/short net exposure down 3.2 points to 43.7%, the lowest reading in five years. The way they did it matters: managers added short positions rather than selling what they own, so borrowed exposure stayed high at 213.9% of capital, the 83rd percentile of the past three years. Goldman's prime team says that leaves room for more selling if performance pressure continues, and fundamental long/short managers were already down 2.7% on the week. Retail took the other side, with $65bn into global equity funds in the week to October 14 and a record $7bn into the triple-leveraged semiconductor ETF.

### The index finally got hedged

For weeks the damage was in individual stocks while index protection stayed cheap. Friday ended that. The VIX, the market's price of one-month S&P volatility, jumped 21% to 20, VIX futures traded 3.6 times their 20-day average volume, and Cboe index put volume set an all-time daily record. Goldman measures Friday as the largest S&P put session ever by dollar size, with more than $3.3 trillion of notional changing hands, and VIX call volume at the second-highest level in five years, trailing only August 2, 2024, which came days before the VIX spiked above 60. Goldman's Lee Coppersmith calls it the first real sign of panic in index protection buying, and Goldman's own panic gauge went from a one-handle reading at the start of the week to 7.4, one of the sharpest rallies on record.

### Yields broke before stocks did

The bond market read AI credit stress as a growth problem, not an inflation one. JPMorgan reports 2-, 5-, 10- and 30-year Treasury yields falling 11, 17, 19 and 20 basis points on the week, the 10-year's biggest weekly drop since August 2025, taking it to a three-week low near 5.08%. Supply is about to help. Citi expects the Treasury to cut 20-year and 30-year auction sizes by $3bn each at the November 4 refunding, to $13bn and $22bn, removing $6bn of long-dated supply a month, and ties the decision to hyperscaler bond issuance eating into demand for long Treasuries. Goldman moved to Overweight bonds for the next 12 months. JPMorgan's Jay Barry recommends a position that profits if 30-year yields rise relative to 10-year yields, while Citi stays neutral on how much interest-rate risk to hold, because rates keep flipping between AI slowdown fears and energy costs.

### October is off the table

Rate expectations made a round trip inside five days. October hike odds jumped as high as 55% intraday after September's inflation print, from near 20% before it, then collapsed to around 13% once Fed Chair Warsh said the world had broadly conformed to the Fed's expectations without pre-committing to anything. The print itself was hot: headline consumer prices rose 0.6% on the month and 3.7% over the year against 3.6% expected, core rose 0.3%, and gasoline alone, up 9.1%, accounted for more than half the monthly gain. Goldman's David Mericle expects a pause in October and a December hike that ends the cycle, and Citi reads it the same way. BofA's Hartnett is the outlier, arguing the Fed needs to hike in October to restore credibility after 66 straight months of inflation above target. October 28 is the test, and JPMorgan says a hold there would remove the residual near-term risk.

### Breadth at dot-com extremes

Strip the AI names out and last week looks nothing like a selloff. Goldman's S&P ex-AI basket beat the index by 586 basis points since October 6, roughly 350 S&P constituents finished the week higher, and Health Care was the best sector at 5.3%. The problem underneath is that the median S&P stock is 15% below its high while Goldman's preferred breadth measure sits near its narrowest since the dot-com bubble. Goldman's Ben Snider expects S&P earnings growth to slow from 36% this year to 11% in 2027, or 18% once one-off accounting effects are stripped out, and says breadth this narrow means the momentum factor reverses whichever way the gap closes. His trade is long consumer-facing laggards, hedged with defensive healthcare and staples, short momentum, with S&P targets of 8,000 and 8,700 intact. The laggard leg needs oil roughly 30% lower to work.

### Hedge funds bought the oil tail

Brent settled at $109.86 after briefly topping $110 for the first time since May 20. Goldman Sachs Research raised its December Brent forecast to $90 from $85 and expects an $80 average in 2027, on the view that Persian Gulf exports have recovered close to 2025 levels since late September. Against Friday's settle that forecast implies an 18% fall in ten weeks, and Goldman's own estimate of the premium geopolitics adds to the price went from $22 to $25 in a week. Goldman's futures desk reports clients betting the other way, buying $4.9bn of Brent futures over October 6 to 13 as the price gained 5.1%, with 32% of Managed Money's new long exposure built through call options. That leaves dealers positioned to buy into a further rally rather than cushion it. Speculative length at roughly $14bn is still far below April's $38bn.

### One name holds the whole budget

Morgan Stanley put a number on 2027 AI spending and then traced where it lands. Total capital spending comes to $1.58 trillion once $195bn of lease-financed building, which never shows up on a cash-flow statement, is added back. Nvidia alone absorbs about $690 billion of that, roughly 44% of the budget, and Morgan Stanley calculates that excluding Nvidia the entire boom is worth 1.6% of S&P sales. The concentration runs into earnings too: 42 names Morgan Stanley calls core to the thesis deliver 78% of 2027 index earnings growth while holding 37% of index net income. Funding it is increasingly borrowed, with $1.1 trillion of signed-but-unstarted data-center leases, $255 billion of bonds already issued this year, and an illustrative $120 billion raise at 8.5% cutting Oracle and SpaceX's combined interest coverage from 4.0 times to 1.8. Morgan Stanley still calls Nvidia the best risk and reward in chips at 15 times FY28 earnings.

### Semis de-rated to 17 times earnings

The chip complex took the worst of it. The SOX semiconductor index fell 11% for its worst week this year and closed down for an eighth session in a row, while Goldman's measure of what investors pay for a dollar of semiconductor earnings fell from about 29 times this summer to 17. The trigger was CXMT's October 10 disclosure of progress on a new memory cell design, after which Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, SanDisk and Kioxia fell between 11% and 26% and lost over $700 billion of combined value. Goldman argues the technology gap to CXMT is real, especially in advanced memory for AI chips, and that below-book valuations price a 2028 downturn far more pessimistic than its own view. Nvidia is separately pushing the memory makers onto TSMC's 3nm process for its stacked-memory base layers, which Goldman says has limited near-term profit impact. BofA's Hartnett names chip prices, now rising vertically, as the signal that decides whether AI credit stays open.

### Apple went defensive

One megacap sat the week out. Apple trades near all-time highs and is closing in on Nvidia as the world's most valuable company, having now pulled away not just from the chip names but from the rest of the Magnificent 7. The case for treating it as the safe seat inside technology is numerical: free cash flow yield, the cash the business throws off measured against the share price, is about 3.15%, above the Nasdaq 100's, and Apple's rolling two-month correlation with that index has turned negative for the first time on record. Option prices show some stress, with implied volatility rising as investors buy protection, but the move is modest next to the surge in fear elsewhere in technology. The read is a flight to safety within technology rather than away from it, and it rests on Apple continuing to look like an unintended beneficiary of AI spending rather than a funder of it.

### GTA VI lands November 19

Take-Two reports its September quarter with the biggest game launch in years five weeks out. Goldman reiterated Buy with a $285 twelve-month target, 38.8% above the $205.28 share price, after cutting the multiple it applies to enterprise value against EBITDA, earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, to 33 times from 35. Grand Theft Auto VI releases November 19. Goldman argues the 6 million pre-orders booked since June 25, about 90% of them the $99.99 Ultimate Edition, say nothing useful about demand, because digital distribution removed the reason to pre-order that drove the GTA V comparison. Its own numbers assume $1.9bn of download revenue and 34 million units, about 27% of a 125 million install base, a penetration rate it calls likely overstated. Two softer signals sit against it: search interest is running roughly 50% below GTA V's pre-release pace, and NBA 2K27 scored 78 against 2K26's 82, with Goldman expecting first-year units down double digits. No estimate changes until post-launch proof points.
