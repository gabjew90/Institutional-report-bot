# Funds Sold The Rally

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Hedge funds shorted the best chip week since April

The S&P 500 gained 2.5% last week [c852] and semiconductors rallied more than 8%, their best week since April [c331]. Goldman's prime brokerage book shows hedge funds selling into that strength for a second straight week, five of the last six [c1330] [d1]. What changed from the prior week is the form of the selling: fresh single-stock shorts rather than long liquidation [c1339], funded in part by covering index and fund hedges, with US exchange-traded-fund shorts down 8.6%, the largest covering in seven months [c1257]. The ratio of long to short bets in US information technology closed at 1.5, the lowest in Goldman's records going back to 2016 [c1338], leaving those funds more than 20 points underweight technology against the index [c1351]. Net leverage did rise, but it came from the market going up and from covering those hedges rather than from buying [c1358], and it still sits in the 2nd percentile of the past five years [c1359]. October was the worst month on Goldman's records for multi-manager funds, surpassing March 2020 [c1340].

Retail did the buying. Morgan Stanley puts US retail purchases near $25 billion over the week [c1280], including $1.6 billion of $NVDA on Thursday alone, nearly 10x the demand for the next most-bought stock [c24].

The bond market priced something else entirely. The yield on 2-year Treasury notes had its worst week since 2024 [c1456], the 10-year went back to 5.15% [c1452], and all three Treasury coupon auctions drew weak demand [c1459]. Market pricing for a December hike moved from 84% to 113% [c1180] after Fed governor Waller floated a half-point move [c1153] and October core consumer prices rose 0.30% on the month, per JPMorgan [c1513], taking the three-month pace to 3.7%, the fastest since early 2025 [c1500]. Goldman's economists added a third quarter-point hike, placed in March 2027 [c1117].

The disagreement is explicit. Morgan Stanley moved to maximum overweight global equities [c425] with a 12-month S&P 500 target of 8,700 [c863]. JPMorgan reiterated 8,000 [c859] and reads the drawdown as a positioning clean-out that makes the next leg more sustainable [c1277]. Goldman's research sits on the other side: S&P 500 earnings growth slows from 36% to 11% in 2027 [c949], the AI investment impulse peaks in early 2027 [c566], and that peak is manageable for the economy but a bigger test for markets [c606].

Two events settle it. The Fed meets December 9 with a half-point hike on the table [c1151], and Nvidia reports Tuesday, its first result under interim chief executive Colette Kress [c128]. JPMorgan's stated expression stays hyperscalers over high-beta AI names [c1274].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Nvidia reports without its only CEO

Jensen Huang resigned effective immediately, with chief financial officer Colette Kress taking over as interim chief executive while keeping her existing job [c168] [d7]. The stock fell more than 13% intraday before closing about 4% lower [c110], and market cap is back below $4 trillion, level with Microsoft's [c43]. It heads into Tuesday's report at 11x expected earnings for the next twelve months [c124] with a 14-day relative strength reading of 24, the lowest since 2019 [c42], and on JPMorgan's numbers the cheapest large AI name on growth-adjusted price to earnings at 0.2x [c9]. Goldman's James Schneider kept his Buy rating but cut his target to $220 from $300 [c54], expects every signed financing commitment to be honored [c131], and says new deals of that size get harder without Huang's convening power [c55]. UBS's Tim Arcuri estimates even a temporary pause in that recycling could pull $50 billion or more out of the system [c97]. What to listen for is whether Kress stands behind the $500bn of commitments on the call [c151].

### AI borrowing costs are the binding constraint

Credit is where the AI trade is being repriced, not demand [d10]. A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point. Investment-grade spreads for AI-related borrowers have widened to roughly 140bp [c1013], against near 100bp for everything else [c1043]. Goldman's Miriam Wheeler puts AI high-yield paper at roughly 425bp versus about 265bp for comparable non-AI bonds [c1018], and that market has seen no new deals since the end of September [c1019], partly because the investment-grade tenant guarantees only take effect once a building is finished [c1020]. Goldman expects index spreads to widen further in 2027 [c1000] and the gap between AI issuers and everyone else to persist [c1002]. Data-center securitized AAA paper printed at an all-time wide 200bp [c1029], which Morgan Stanley reads as a supply problem rather than a verdict on the theme [c1059] [d26]. The top end still funds easily: hyperscalers placed roughly $67bn of unsecured investment-grade bonds within 5 business days of September 30 [c992], and $103bn of chip financing was announced in October [c994]. Morgan Stanley has removed its underweight on high-quality hyperscaler credit [c990].

### The permit fight moves projects, not capacity

Post-election state politics changes the calendar rather than the total [d5]. Morgan Stanley shifts about 20% of US data-center projects scheduled for 2028 into 2029 [c778], roughly 5GW of capacity [c662], which deepens its projected US power shortfall through 2028 to 44GW from 38GW [c663], and leaves its 2027 capex forecast unchanged at roughly $1.66tr [c464]. Goldman's Alec Phillips estimates the states likely to tighten rules hold about 45% of planned US capacity [c576], with Texas alone roughly 25% [c577], and expects the incoming Texas governor to pause new permits until the legislature passes safeguards [c575] [d35]. Goldman's research still has 2028 capacity roughly in line with 2027 [c364], with more than 50% upside if the additional projects proceed [c365]. Constellation Energy's own read is that the market overreacted to the election [c294]. Morgan Stanley's conclusion is that risk and reward improves for the best-capitalized hyperscalers, which can redirect spending [c208].

### Multiples did the falling, estimates did not

Morgan Stanley's decomposition of the September 30 to November 5 selloff finds the price damage came almost entirely from valuation [d43]. Its AI and Tech Diffusion index fell 26.6% against 3.7% for global equities [c650]. Across 91 members, next-year earnings estimates edged higher while the price-to-earnings multiple contracted by more than the whole price decline [c450]. Of 94 constituents, the 61 that had reported saw estimates rise, with a median increase of 4.5% [c451]. US and European names fell to the bottom quintile of their post-2019 valuation ranges [c452], and Japan de-rated the most, from the 82nd to the 28th percentile, with no member seeing lower estimates [c453]. The rotation inside the theme was valuation-led: adopters returned minus 3.2% while enablers lost 29.0% [c456], even though enablers hold far stronger revision breadth at plus 48% against plus 2% [c656]. Morgan Stanley's response is to cut concentration in capex-heavy upstream names rather than buy the dip broadly [c661].

### The expensive corner is consumer staples

S&P staples carry their widest valuation premium to the index in more than 10 years [c935], while technology's forward price-to-earnings multiple sits near its COVID-crash low [c897]. Staples beat tech by 33% over the last 30 days [c972], the largest such gap in about 25 years [c973], with the only comparable stretch running from April 2000 through October 2001 [c974]. Hedge funds helped push it: short covering unwound gross exposure and left them net long staples for the first time since October 2025 [c1286]. The valuation is not cheap defense either, since the sector's forward dividend yield against the 10-year Treasury sits in the 1st percentile [c1469]. UBS reads the move as the unwind of a crowded funding trade and a flight to safety rather than improving fundamentals [c703], with earnings scores, valuation and expected sales growth near the bottom 5% of their three-year histories [c704], and reiterates its underweight [c706] [d23]. Keith Parker's top 2027 trade at UBS is long Health Care against Staples [c334]. After similar staples rallies, relative returns over the following three months have typically been negative [c937].

### Europe is the AI hedge nobody is underwriting

The Stoxx 600 set a record high last week [c547] and has outpaced the S&P 500 by 9 percentage points since early October [c536]. The mechanical reason is exposure: UBS estimates only about 11% of the index is materially driven by AI enablement [c538], and Goldman measures the index's two-year daily sensitivity to its US AI basket at less than 0.05 [c410] [d36]. UBS expects Eurozone earnings to grow 15% next year and stays positive [c549], and Morgan Stanley raised its MSCI Europe target to 3030, implying 15% upside [c427]. Goldman is on the other side of that trade. It is Underweight European equities into 2027 [c600], sees European earnings growth slowing from 18% to 7% [c599], downgraded European banks after their run [c598], and expects lower 2027 index returns in Europe than in the US or Asia [c603] [d18]. The awkward record is that European banks have beaten the Mag7 over 1 to 5 years [c397] and the CAC-40 has beaten Goldman's own AI-winner basket over 12 months [c396]. The near-term test is S&P's French credit rating review on November 27 [c1009].

### Bitcoin reconnects with the weak side of risk

$BTC has lost roughly a quarter of its value since October 21 [c1782] [d21]. The weekend flush triggered nearly $2 billion of forced closures after the price broke below its 200-day average around $73,000 [c1784]. Fund flows have been one-way, with seven straight days of selling out of spot $BTC exchange-traded funds [c1786] and $2.35 billion leaving over two weeks [c1785]. Miners are supplying coin into that: MARA sold 13,597 $BTC for about $1.1 billion [c313], equal to 40% of its September-end holdings [c314] and part of more than 40,000 sold during 2026 [c315], using $750 million of the proceeds to repay loans backed by the coins [c316]. Price is back below Strategy's average purchase price, which raises the question of whether it can keep bidding [c317], and funding turned negative on Binance, OKX and Bybit on November 13, meaning traders now pay to hold shorts [c1789]. One theory on the timing is capital rotating toward Anthropic allocations [c1787], an offering pricing November 18 with the book reportedly six times covered [c551]. Chinese equities have been red in 13 of the last 14 weeks over the same stretch [c684].
