# Nvidia Beats, 2028 Stays Empty

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Nvidia's beat buys 2027, not 2028

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion against consensus of $109.0 billion and earnings of $2.68 a share against $2.48, with data center revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110% from a year ago [c18][c19][c20] [d5]. Gross margin came in at 74.9% against a 74.0% estimate [c24]. The January quarter is guided to $130 billion, above the $125.3 billion consensus, and management raised next fiscal year's revenue growth guidance to about 75% from 70% [c26][c29]. Shares rose 4.9% after hours, after the Nasdaq had closed 0.7% lower on the same worries the print answered [c39][c546] [d6]. Going in, the stock was down about 35% from its October high with roughly $2 trillion of market value gone, and options priced a 7.95% move, the largest implied earnings move in two years [c152][c206].

The part that decides the next year is the supply line, not the beat. More than $100 billion of the increase in purchase commitments was for 2027 capacity, which UBS reads as conviction in 2027 demand [c50] [d7]. Commitments for 2028 barely moved, in contrast to last quarter's large step up in longer-dated commitments [c117]. Total supply commitments now sit near $430 billion against about $311 billion three months ago [c198]. That is why UBS's worry has moved from a 2027 cut to how hard growth decelerates in 2028 [c118], and why it trimmed its 2028 growth estimate to just under 10% with earnings of about $18.80 a share, cutting its price target to $220 from $240 while keeping its Buy rating [c124][c125][c55].

The disagreement is about what to pay for the far years. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky says the near-term numbers can be strong and the market still has no idea what multiple belongs on 2028, 2029 or 2030 earnings [c171]. Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid points at consensus earnings of $625 billion by 2031, which puts the stock near 6 times those earnings [c1][c2]. JPMorgan's Michael Cembalest argues the competitive position is the bigger risk. Of the roughly 44 gigawatts of compute the frontier labs have announced for 2026 onward, Nvidia takes 28% [c93], down from an 80% to 90% share of AI chip revenue in 2024 and 2025 [c185]. A frontier lab can now get models running on AMD hardware in three days, against six to twelve months a year ago [c227][c233] [d2]. He still calls the stock interesting for long-term holders at a 0.2 times PEG, the cheapest large AI name by a wide margin [c13][c14].

Next quarter's filing settles it. If the 2028 commitment line is flat again, UBS has the right read. The 10-Q is already late, and management told UBS the delay relates to an ongoing investigation [c120][c121].

## 3. BRIEFS

### The Fed is hiking into this, not cutting

Almost all FOMC participants judged further rate increases appropriate at the October meeting, and a couple were already weighing a December move larger than a quarter point [c623][c624] [d25]. Governor Waller has said a quarter-point hike in December paired with clear guidance for another in January does the same work as the half-point December move he floated earlier [c625]. UBS expects quarter-point hikes at both meetings [c622]. Deutsche Bank notes markets now price 113 basis points (one basis point is one-hundredth of a percent) of hikes by the September 2027 meeting, against 95 priced the day before the cycle began [c597][c598] [d12]. Core consumer price inflation ran at a 3.75% annualized pace over the three months through October, the fastest since April 2024 [c836]. TS Lombard's Blitz puts the odds of a half-point December hike near 50/50 against roughly 10% priced, and says the November jobs report is the signal that decides which it is [c616][c890] [d20].

### Dealers are short gamma and the market swings both ways

Nomura puts aggregate S&P 500 dealer gamma at negative $7.3 billion, the deepest since January 2019, so dealers sell into declines and buy into rallies and every move gets amplified [c713] [d4]. Systematic funds have already sold $116 billion over two weeks, cutting exposure to the 10th percentile [c691], hedge fund net leverage sits at zero to low single digits [c690], and the ratio of long to short technology positions in Goldman's hedge fund book has fallen to 1.5 from 2.14 in June, the lowest since that record begins in 2016 [c711][c708]. Nomura's rebalance grid says a 5% drop forces roughly $82 billion of mechanical selling while a 5% rally pulls in only about $66 billion of buying [c716][c717]. McElligott expects the S&P either through 6500 with nothing underneath it or back at 7800 to 8000 within a month [c566]. VIX at 25 with VVIX at 121 is stressed [c879], and he sees the floor rising but no positioning fuel for a move above 30 without a much deeper selloff [c888].

### The Bank of Nvidia is closing its checkbook

Interim CEO Colette Kress is expected to rein in Nvidia's financing of AI deals, and Huang had already started, telling neocloud chief executives in Berlin they would need upfront payments from their own customers to keep their chip allocations [c129][c131] [d10]. The reason sits on the balance sheet, where off-balance-sheet commitments and guarantees reached $530 billion at the end of July, including $279 billion of supply and capacity purchases, more than double three months earlier [c139][c140]. UBS's Tim Arcuri says even a temporary pause in that dealmaking could pull $50 billion or more out of the AI ecosystem [c126]. The cost shows up in bonds first, with the extra yield demanded from AI-related borrowers out to roughly 140 basis points [c751]. Insurance against default on data-center debt is back above 450 basis points [c763], and Blackstone's QTS placed only $300 million of an $800 million bond deal [c764]. Nvidia says it will keep using customer financing but is being more selective about future support, which is the line to watch [c110].

### Goldman's whole 2027 book hangs off one second derivative

Goldman's 2027 outlooks run off a single turn: global AI investment keeps rising, from $1.1 trillion in 2026 to $1.8 trillion in 2027, but its growth as a share of GDP peaks in the first half of next year [c477][c865] [d19]. That matters more to markets than to the economy, because AI has accounted for 21% of US GDP growth against 62% of S&P 500 earnings growth [c869], and as much as 92% of the rise in US market value at the peak [c564]. Goldman expects S&P 500 earnings growth to more than halve in 2027, from 36% to 11% [c540]. It still gets a 12-month index target of 8,700, about 14% above 7,619 [c516], and tells clients to buy the big cloud providers at 19.7 times earnings [c338]. Its credit forecast has investment-grade spreads widening from 88 to 105 basis points next year [c754], and high-yield from 320 to 410 [c755]. Its rates target is a 4.6% 10-year Treasury yield by the end of 2027 [c819], against a yield that ended last week above 5.15% [c825].

### Washington closed the rental loophole

Commerce issued an interim rule on Monday requiring a US export license for advanced AI chips shipped to data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil unless the buyer is US-headquartered, closing the route Chinese firms used to rent overseas compute [c801][c81] [d13]. Existing operators have 90 days to comply [c82]. UBS estimates the rule could strip 1 to 2 gigawatts out of current chip demand, most of it Nvidia's, roughly a 10% demand impact at more than $30 billion per gigawatt, and names Nvidia the most directly hit [c113][c114][c116] [d14]. Nvidia's management told UBS that Southeast Asian licensing requirements are not changing customer plans [c49]. Cembalest puts remote access to US chips at 32% of China's projected AI compute by the end of 2027, three-quarters of it sitting in Southeast Asia [c788][c806] [d2]. UBS treats the demand hit as a longer-term problem rather than a near-term one, unless permitting for powered shells in the US also tightens [c115].

### Texas is about to test who gets to build

Core Scientific registered a $1.3 billion, 341,000 square foot data center shell in Hunt County, Texas for AMD, with construction listed to start November 24 [c231][c264] [d16]. It has power, land, a tenant and most of its permits, but the air permits for its backup generators are still pending at the state environmental agency, which Governor Abbott ordered on September 21 to stop issuing data center permits [c260][c419]. Governor-elect Hinojosa, sworn in on January 19, says her moratorium covers all permits, while conceding she cannot revoke permits already granted [c408][c333][c509]. GLJ's Gordon Johnson says projects with power, permits and steel keep going, and those missing environmental permits slip 12 to 18 months [c332][c406]. Wall Street has sized the risk by excluding anything already under construction: Morgan Stanley counts 8.3 gigawatts at risk through 2030 [c424], Goldman's Jan Hatzius sees short-term moratoria touching about 45% of planned US capacity expansion [c415], and UBS flags 30% of 2028 additions, including 18 gigawatts in Texas [c457]. ERCOT's audit is due December 10 [c511].

### TS Lombard reads its own bull case back from 2000

TS Lombard stays constructive on AI stocks near term, pointing to hyperscaler capex plans that have not been cut and GPU rental prices that show compute demand holding [c354][c355] [d17]. Then it sets that case beside the first year of the dot-com crash. The Nasdaq peaked on 10 March 2000 [c487] and fell more than a third by mid-April as the Fed hiked in February, March and May [c429][c614], then rallied 35% off the lows by mid-June on buy-the-dip behavior and a story that technology was immune to rate increases [c356][c588]. By mid-October it had broken those lows and it finished the year near half its March peak [c433]. The firm has acted on the discomfort, cutting US equities by 3.5 percentage points to a small underweight and raising cash to 14% [c544][c443] [d18]. Blitz calls today a correction rather than a crisis, with the 10-year Treasury yield reaching 6% next year and the real bear market arriving later [c542][c828] [d20].

### UBS's two houses now disagree on the index

With 91.7% of S&P 500 market value reported, third-quarter results are beating estimates by 5.8%, 77% of companies have topped projections, and earnings growth is on pace for 33% [c576][c526][c527] [d8]. The six largest technology companies are the group lagging, at 25.6% against 35.2% for the rest of the market, and the two misses were Alphabet at -5.8% and Amazon at -2.0% [c448][c236][c250]. UBS's chief investment office cut US equities to Neutral and lowered its June 2027 target to 8,000 from 8,400, arguing the drivers shift from accelerating to decelerating growth [c581][c552] [d22]. Its US equity strategists hold an end-2027 target of 8,900 on 18 times 2028 earnings, reading the semiconductor and software derating as skepticism about the pace of the buildout rather than damaged fundamentals [c536][c524] [d9][d21]. Both sides of the house need the same thing, since another beat depends unusually heavily on hyperscalers sustaining the build [c550].
