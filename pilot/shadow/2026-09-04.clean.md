# Diesel Owns The Bond Market

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Rates are taking orders from the refinery

Refinery capacity across the Middle East and Russia has been damaged, and diesel, gasoil, heating oil and European gas have all broken out to the upside. European gas closed at 73.60 euros per MWh, the highest since January 2023, and TTF gas is now trading above where it sat in 2022. With Brent at $95, the US 10-year yield reached 4.80%, and Goldman's desk states the order of operations plainly: rates cannot rally until energy prices fall first. Amanda Ross runs that as a position, trading inverse heating oil against the 10-year.

Europe is where the fuel bill turns into policy. Northern European countries are struggling to refill reserves before the heating season after last year's cold winter, and with gas at these levels markets moved to fully price three more ECB hikes by June 2027. Germany's 10-year yield rose to a post-2011 high of 3.37% and France's 10-year reached 4.25%. Germany's inflation-adjusted 10-year yield also hit a post-2011 high of 0.99%, so investors are not simply marking up expected inflation.

Yesterday broke the pattern for one session, and not because energy fell. Fed Governor Waller said recent data show signs of disinflation and that he would be inclined to hold rates if that continues, and futures-implied odds of a September hike dropped to 51% from 63%. The S&P 500 rose 1.06% and the Magnificent 7 gained 2.38% to a three-month high. The 2-year Treasury yield fell 3.2bps (one basis point is a hundredth of a percentage point) while the 10-year fell 1.1bps to 4.77%. Shorter-dated bonds moving more than longer-dated ones points at Fed commentary, not at a change in the energy picture.

Goldman stays cautious on longer-dated bonds now that month-end buying support has passed and puts energy nearer the high end of its near-term range. JPMorgan reads the same map differently, treating a record 18m barrels moving through the Strait of Hormuz as evidence that the ability to disrupt flows has been reduced, and assigning 55% probability to a face-saving off-ramp against 45% for Washington waiting it out.

George Cole's test says the selloff is fundamental rather than fragile because rates volatility has not broken higher, and Goldman's own forecasts still carry the 10-year 30 to 40bp lower if the energy driver goes away. Against that, the ISM services prices paid index hit a 4-year high of 72.6, a series that correlates with inflation on a 3-month lag. Watch heating oil, not the Fed speakers.

## 3. BRIEFS

### The price of a token is falling faster than usage grows

Cost per token is compressing hard. Silicon Data's expenditure-weighted index fell 29% in August to about $0.97 per million tokens, a record low and more than 50% below the May peak near $2.05. OpenRouter token volume rose roughly 47% month over month while dollar spend rose only about 7%, and rental prices for H100 chips are falling. Privorotsky's point is that more demand at lower prices is not automatically bullish if pricing falls faster than consumption grows, and that unless capability keeps jumping, stretches of compute oversupply become plausible. That collides with borrowed money: five rated hyperscalers are projected to spend around $737 billion of 2026 capex, about 38% of revenue, and hyperscaler bonds issued this year were mostly trading below their issue price by late August. The desk treats OpenAI's unlaunched Astra as the last near-term event that can invalidate the oversupply case.

### $AVGO guided the decade and lost the day

Broadcom reported revenue of $29.6bn in line with consensus and a gross margin of 75.0% against 74.1% expected, with AI semiconductor revenue up 221% year over year to $16.7bn. Management guided AI semiconductor revenue to double to $115bn in FY27 across 10GW of datacenter deployments and double again to $230bn across 20GW, naming Anthropic its largest FY27 customer. The stock fell 5% anyway, on fiscal fourth-quarter guidance that came in below the Street. Goldman raised its 12-month target to $540 from $525 and stays a buyer at 12x management's FY28 earnings guidance. The same session, $SNOW rose 25% on product revenue up 37% against a 30% guide, while $HPE fell 4% after raising FY27 revenue growth to 13-17% and $NTAP fell 8% after raising FY2027 EPS to $9.73-$10.03. The bar each name carried, not the number it printed, is deciding these moves.

### The jobs number nobody wants to be right about

JPMorgan's Feroli forecasts 50k jobs added against the Street's 55k, with the prior print at -23k and unemployment unchanged at 4.1%. Goldman is lower at 40k and below street consensus, Deutsche Bank higher at 65k. The window matters more than the point estimate: JPMorgan defines the good outcome as a print between 30k and 70k and puts 10% odds on a number above 95k, which it maps to the S&P losing 50bp to 1.25%. Options priced an implied move of 1.1% for the day. A strong print is the bad one here, because it lifts yields and weighs on equities, and JPMorgan still views the September 11 inflation report as more impactful than payrolls for the September 16 Fed meeting.

### Volatility got destroyed and the hedges got cheap

Something broke in the options market rather than in stocks. Three-month at-the-money implied volatility on $IWM sits at the 0.2 percentile, SPX put skew is at the 2nd percentile, and profitability on trades that bet stocks move apart from each other has cratered to the 5th percentile. McElligott's explanation is supply: structured product flows leave dealers holding volatility they cannot sell, Citadel's unwind of its legacy options book turned the largest buyer of tech volatility into a seller, and he expects headlines about major trading losses within days. Goldman's desk reports a matching uptick in client hedging ahead of a harder seasonal stretch and quotes the VIX 21Oct26 20/30 call spread at $1.00 as the book hedge, with IWM 30Sep 300 calls at $2.48 and QQQ Sep 725 calls at $4.01 as the upside. Dealer positioning is still estimated very long, which has been holding the index up.

### Momentum has already been sold back to the lows

Global net exposure to the momentum factor is back to year-to-date lows, reversing almost the entire first-half build, with US positioning around the 40th percentile since 2015 and hedge fund net leverage near the 50th percentile. J.P. Morgan's cross-asset monitor has equities at the 70th percentile and bonds at 58%, long but far from extreme. Goldman treats that as an entry point: its momentum basket sold off more than 20 percentage points in a week, leaving hyperscaler and data center equipment valuations near 2-year lows with net exposure in data center names at the lowest since early Q1, and the desk says buy the dip. J.P. Morgan is running the other way, tactically cautious to neutral on global equities, adding cheap European index hedges, and holding high conviction shorts in EU chemicals and Asian consumer electronics.

### Memory prices are rising and the stocks are not

Hyperscalers are paying more for memory instead of ordering less. Amazon attributed part of its latest capital spending increase directly to higher memory costs, which reads as pricing power sitting with the suppliers. The equities have not followed. The DRAM ETF has spent weeks squeezed between its rising trend line from launch and the falling line from its highs, $MU shows the better relative momentum with a close above $1,000 flagged as the trigger for another squeeze, and SK Hynix still trades below its 50-day average price. One way to play a catch-up move without picking a single name is upside in the Korean index, where volatility has been crushed and retail speculation has cooled off.

### The housing market is frozen, not falling

Supply is locked in place. The median US buyer is now 59 years old against 31 in 1981, 95 percent of mortgages are fixed for 30 years, 40 percent of homes carry no mortgage, and the average owner holds roughly $400,000 of equity, so turnover breaks lower permanently rather than pausing. The buyer side is narrow for a different reason, with the median home at $403,000, a median monthly payment of $2,918, and 75 percent of households able to afford only homes priced under $300,000. Money that cannot buy a different house goes into the existing one, and renovations now account for 25 percent of US private construction spending. The rental market is where the damage shows, with negative rent growth in 56 percent of the largest cities, rising multifamily delinquencies and elevated vacancies. Home Depot and Lowe's have lagged while investors stay fixed on weak near-term activity, and the condition that decides it is whether turnover stays frozen while remodeling spending holds.

### Food is where the next inflation scare starts

The Bloomberg commodity index has broken above its 200-day average and dislocated higher after testing that level over the summer, with the grains component moving sharply higher over the past month. The supply shocks stack up. The Gulf accounts for roughly 43% of global urea and 44% of global sulphur exports, both fertiliser inputs, and India's sugar inventories sit at 30-year lows with export restrictions already started. What makes the move violent is policy rather than weather, because exporting governments restrict shipments to protect domestic food supply and prices then move further than the crop loss alone justifies. Positioning is the catch: managed-money net corn longs are back at 317k contracts after collapsing 88% in three weeks during the June washout, and wheat already faded from multi-year highs this week. US 10-year breakevens, the inflation rate priced into the bond market, track grains closely, so a further breakout puts inflation back at the center of the macro debate.
