# Broadcom Doubles, Stock Falls

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The AI trade stopped paying for good news

Broadcom printed the number the AI complex has been asking for. Revenue was $29.6bn, in line, with gross margin at 75.0% against the 74.1% expected, and AI chip revenue grew 221% year over year to $16.7bn. Management guided that line to double to $115bn in FY27 across 10GW of data center deployments and to double again to $230bn across 20GW, named Anthropic its largest custom silicon customer in FY27, and said supply of every key component is secured through FY28. The stock fell about 5%. The proximate cause is fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $34.8bn against Goldman's $36.2bn, but one soft quarter is not why a two-year doubling gets sold.

What is being sold is the price of the product. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky points at the Silicon Data token index, down 29% in August to about $0.97 per million tokens, a record low and more than half off the May peak near $2.05. Volume is not covering the price cut. A JPMorgan data-center note put OpenRouter token volume up roughly 47% month over month while the dollars spent rose about 7%, and rental prices for H100 chips are falling. Set that against five rated hyperscalers projected to spend around $737 billion this year, about 38% of revenue, and 78 of 91 hyperscaler bonds issued in 2026 trading below their issue price by late August. Demand can be enormous and the stocks can still fall if it arrives at a price that does not service the debt raised to build the capacity.

The two big houses split on what to do. Goldman's Jim Schneider stays Buy on Broadcom, its equity sales desk raised the 12-month target to $540 from $525 and calls 12 times management's FY28 earnings guidance compelling value, and Faris Mourad's desk says buy the dip in hyperscalers and data-center equipment, where valuations sit near two-year lows and net exposure is the lowest since early in the first quarter. JPMorgan goes the other way inside tech, favoring the Mag 7 and software over AI, memory and semiconductors, while granting that every data point tracking the AI value chain has accelerated since June even as the group de-rated.

The event that settles it is named. Goldman treats OpenAI's Astra as the last near-term launch that can invalidate the compute oversupply case. OpenAI said on September 1 that Astra meets the Critical cybersecurity threshold under its Preparedness Framework, with no confirmed launch date. Absent a capability jump on that scale, periods of compute oversupply become plausible.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Hike odds halved in three days

Bets on a Federal Reserve hike this month fell from 68% to 63% after private payrolls came in at +38k against +47k expected, then to 51% once Fed Governor Waller said the data show signs of disinflation and that he would be inclined to hold if that continues. He left himself room, saying he would consider a hike if inflation runs hot. The same day's data cut the other way: the August ISM services index hit a six-month high of 55.4, and its prices paid component hit a four-year high of 72.6 against 70.0 expected, a series that tracks inflation with a three-month lag. Forecasts for today's August jobs report run from Goldman at 40k to JPMorgan's Feroli at 50k to Deutsche Bank at 65k, against a 55k consensus after a prior print of -23k. JPMorgan calls 30k to 70k the goldilocks band and puts 10% odds on a print above 95k, which it thinks costs the S&P 500 between 50 basis points, one basis point being one-hundredth of a percent, and 1.25%. It also argues CPI on September 11 matters more for the Fed than today's number.

### European gas is writing the ECB's script

Front-month European natural gas closed 1.96% higher at 73.60 euros per megawatt hour, the highest since January 2023, with the six-month contract at 67.43, so this is priced as a lasting problem rather than a spike. Northern European countries are struggling to refill reserves before the heating season after last year's cold winter drained them, and prices are more than double year-ago levels. Markets responded by fully pricing three more ECB hikes by June 2027, which would put the deposit rate back at 3%. That took the 10-year German bund to a post-2011 high of 3.37%, the French 10-year to a post-2008 high of 4.25%, and the French 30-year above 5% intraday before a 4.98% close. Germany's 10-year real yield also hit a post-2011 high of 0.99%, so this is not only inflation expectations repricing. Goldman plays it directly, holding a 51-stock basket of European gas consumers it expects to underperform as prices rise and a 16-stock basket of power-price-sensitive utilities on the long side. Gas then fell 2.48% to 71.80 euros, which paused the selloff in bonds.

### Two banks read the same Hormuz numbers differently

Brent peaked above $97 overnight after the latest strikes and gave it all back, trading at $95.29, with WTI up 1.5% at $92.30. MUFG frames it as an intact supply shock: crude is more than 30% higher since the conflict began in February, with roughly 8mb/d still crossing the Strait of Hormuz and another 4-5mb/d bypassing it through pipelines. JPMorgan reads the same waterway the opposite way, citing a record 18m barrels moving through it as evidence that the ability to disrupt flows has been reduced, and puts 55% odds on a face-saving exit from the conflict. Goldman splits the difference with a measurement point: total Persian Gulf exports are running 15-16mb/d against only 10mb/d visible, a 5mb/d gap explained by tankers crossing near Oman with their tracking transponders switched off, which still leaves exports at about two thirds of pre-war levels. Privorotsky's condition is the one to hold onto, that markets are stable or higher as long as energy does not rise.

### Momentum has been unwound, and that is the setup

The factor that carried the first half is gone. JPMorgan's momentum index is down more than 21% from its June record and its classic version down nearly 34%, while Goldman's momentum basket shed more than 20 percentage points in a single week, past what the desk expected from a summer slump. Futures flows tell the same story from a different angle: non-dealers sold $11.2bn of S&P 500 futures in the week to August 25, hedge funds accounting for $15bn of selling with $9bn of it long unwinds, the third straight week that liquidation coincided with a rise in Goldman's stagflation basket, which then gained 0.9% while the 10-year Treasury yield rose 12bp. US positioning is back near the 40th percentile since 2015 and hedge fund net leverage near its five-year median. JPMorgan has moved to a tactically cautious stance and recommends cutting net longs, expressed as long the Nasdaq 100 against short the Russell 2000, with short credit ETFs and long VIX as the hedges. Goldman stays overweight equities and underweight credit on a 12-month view while sitting neutral tactically.

### The yen move that Asia has to price

The yen rose 1.86% against the dollar and 1.55% against the euro, so this was yen strength rather than dollar weakness. Behind it, BOJ board member Takata said a 25bp hike is not necessarily set in stone, Bloomberg reported the bank is leaning toward hiking 25bp this month, and the 10-year Japanese government bond yield eased about 4bps to 2.97% after briefly crossing 3% for the first time since 1996. Traders are also watching for intervention around the thin Silver Week holidays that follow the September 18 meeting. JPMorgan calls the move overdone and does not expect the dollar to break materially below the 155-165 range against the yen, with the large outstanding short yen position as the risk to that view. The reason it matters beyond Japan is asymmetry: a sudden 1% appreciation has historically been worth a 0.6% and 0.4% decline in MSCI EM and Asia ex-Japan. Household spending fell 3.6% year on year in July, an eighth straight contraction, which is the argument against hiking at all.

### Memory prices are rising, memory stocks are not

Hyperscalers are absorbing higher memory costs rather than cutting orders, and Amazon attributed part of its latest capital spending increase directly to memory prices, which is what pricing power looks like from the buyer's side. The equities have not followed. The main DRAM ETF has spent weeks squeezed between a rising trend line from launch and a falling one from its highs, with price back above its 21-day average. Micron shows the better relative momentum of the group, and a clean close above $1,000 is flagged as the trigger for another squeeze, while SK Hynix still trades below a 50-day average sitting well above it. Semiconductors more broadly sit below their 100-day average but above the 200-day, with volatility priced cheaply against software. Goldman goes into this earnings season pointing at the historic beats already delivered by Micron and Samsung.

### The bar, not the print, is moving consumer stocks

Goldman's consumer desk framed every move on 3 September against what each name was carrying into the print. VSXY fell 15% on revenue of $1.61B against $1.62B consensus, a miss of one cent's worth of revenue for a stock up 57% this year at a sector-high 18 times earnings. FIVE rose 6% because it guided third-quarter earnings to $1.01 to $1.13 against $0.87 consensus, which cleared a high bar rather than met it. PVH beat at $3.70 against $3.08 on margin, gained 2%, and then capped itself by guiding the third quarter to $2.50 to $2.65 against $2.99. CPB fell 6% after cutting its dividend 35% and guiding 2027 earnings to $1.65 to $1.80 against $1.83, and WOOF rose 10% on a profit beat with 22% of the float sold short. Underneath, Goldman Prime saw gross leverage rise for the first time in three months, consumer discretionary net bought, and defensives sold a fifth straight month.

### Housing is not crashing, it is freezing

The American housing market has locked itself shut. The median buyer is now 59 years old against 31 in 1981, 95% of mortgages are fixed for 30 years, 40% of homes carry no mortgage at all, and the average owner sits on roughly $400,000 of equity. Nobody in that position has a reason to sell, so turnover has broken lower rather than paused, and brokerages, lenders and title companies have already been repriced for it. Prices hold anyway because the buyer pool narrowed to the wealthy: 75% of households can only afford homes under $300,000 while the median sale price is $403,000 and the median monthly payment is $2,918. The spending that used to go into moving now goes into staying, with renovations at 25% of private construction spending. Rental property is the exception, where 56% of the largest cities show negative rent growth, multifamily delinquencies are climbing and vacancies sit above pre-pandemic levels. The gap between repair-and-remodel names and transaction-linked ones widens only if turnover stays frozen while remodeling holds up.
