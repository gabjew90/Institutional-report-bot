# Agents Come For The Banks

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The agent trade turns on the banks

The Nasdaq 100 closed at a record for a fourth straight up day while the S&P 500 finished flat and the Dow fell 36bps to 51,863 [c937][c869][c905]. The split was Financials. XLF fell 2% as the trade that buys semiconductors and sells AI-vulnerable names came back [c162], with the S&P's financial sector down 1.98% [c464].

What is being sold is a specific idea. Goldman's Consumer Inertia basket, built from companies whose customers stay because switching is a hassle, fell 2.58% with 45 of its 55 names lower [c1232][c1238]. Planet Fitness fell 9.45%, the New York Times 7.16%, Schwab 6.11% and Allstate 5.55% [c150][c216][c153][c166]. The claim behind it is that agents which shop, cancel and renegotiate on a customer's behalf compress pricing power, lower retention and cut what each customer is worth over a lifetime [c508], which is why the basket concentrates in telecom, insurance, utilities and auto-renewing subscriptions [c507] [d7][d29].

Two desks read the same selling differently. JPMorgan's O'Dwyer says there is very little in what Muse actually does to suggest it disrupts the unit economics of the banking system [c383], calls the move a positioning unwind rather than a re-rating [c1177], and wants to fade it, sizing patiently and favoring Morgan Stanley, PNC and Fifth Third [c384][c195]. He points at the pattern: the consensus names (Citi, Morgan Stanley, Goldman, USB) dominate the selling volume while regionals and LAZ and JEF outperform [c172][c204]. Goldman's financials desk is not arguing fundamentals back. It calls the move an uncertainty discount while investors work through what agents mean [c505], and reports a complete buyers' strike with no sector defense [c1230]. Its own lesson from earlier AI-driven selloffs this year is that patience beat defending positions on day one [c588]. Smaller banks are already outperforming large ones because they carry less fee and wealth business [c354].

The long side of the same trade is crowded. Hedge fund net exposure to the Mag7 sits near the 95th percentile of the past three years, about 20% against a 14% trough in June [c1167]. Resolution comes from product news, not valuation: Meta Connect Wednesday night, OpenAI Dev Day on 9/29 and Micron earnings on 9/30 [c57].

## 3. BRIEFS

### The hike nobody wants to price

The Fed raised 25bp with no dissents, and 16 of 18 participants signaled at least one more increase by year-end [c730][c731]. Deutsche Bank's case for more rests on how loose conditions still are: US high-yield spreads around 270bps today against above 400bps in 2022 [c946], and the S&P 500 within 2% of its record [c841], so risk-asset resilience argues for a more hawkish cycle rather than a nearly finished one [c723]. In 2022 initial pricing implied 200bps of hikes in the first year and the Fed went well past it [c716]. UBS takes the other side with one further 25 basis point increase in December against three priced by the end of 2027 [c758][c834], and Morgan Stanley expects restraint to land at the lower end of what markets price [c747]. PNC sits between them at two more, December and next March [c788]. Goldman's next-hike call is the October 27-28 meeting [c732], which settles the argument [d39][d45].

### The bond short that only needs yields to stop rising

US and European rates have sold off by more than 80 basis points this year [c711], with three or more hikes from both the Fed and the ECB priced over the next 12 months [c728]. Goldman estimates systematic trend followers are short approximately $171 million of global bond DV01 [c652], meaning that much profit or loss for every basis point move, the largest short since April 2026 and concentrated in Japanese and European rates plus the US five-year [c1118][c653]. No rate cut is required to break it. Yields simply ceasing to rise, on stabilizing oil or a softer inflation print, would force covering [c1014][c1296]. The confirmation is intermediate Treasury yields falling while central banks stay publicly hawkish [c634], and renewed highs in oil, inflation expectations or the five-year yield kill the thesis [c680]. The listed expression flagged for it is a one to two month IEF call spread rather than long-dated TLT [c201][c158] [d31].

### Oil no longer needs peace to fall

Brent closed at $99.02, down 1.09% [c1028], its first close under $100 in more than two weeks [c1025], after a report that Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if the US lifts its naval blockade [c1026][c1027]. Saudi Arabia is restarting its East-West pipeline and aiming for a partial restart of flows by Saturday [c1030]. The physical shortage is already mostly gone. Goldman puts the daily gap between supply and demand (measured in millions of barrels a day) at about 1mb/d in the third quarter, down from roughly 7mb/d in March [c1034], JPMorgan counts non-Middle East production up about 2mb/d in the first half, around 700kb/d more than it forecast [c1059], and global consumption running about 5mb/d below year-earlier levels since the disruption [c1060]. That makes no further escalation, rather than peace, the bearish catalyst [c1073]. JPMorgan models Brent at $87 in 2027 with the conflict intact against $63 if it ends [c1061][c1062], while Goldman's base case is $80 [c1039] and its recommended expression is the USO October 140/120 put spread at about 4x maximum payout [c239].

### The rotation ran to processors, not graphics chips

Meta's Muse launch pulled money into the processor chain. Intel rose 12.17%, AMD 9.95% and ARM 17.16% as names tied to inference demand moved together [c82], and AMD crossed $1tn in market value [c81]. UBS argues the most attractive exposure is that CPU supply chain, since agents that execute tasks lean harder on processors than chatbots do, naming AMD, Intel, Arm, TSMC, ASE and SK Hynix [c445][c87]. JPMorgan's Adler reads the same shift as a modest headwind for the neoclouds, which depend on frontier chips [c515]. Korea's first 20 days of September back the volume story, with exports up 78.3% year over year and semiconductors up 259.4% [c411]. The thing that would break it is a change in spending intentions, and there is little evidence that slower frontier development alters hyperscaler plans through 2027 and 2028 [c415]. Micron reports on 9/30 [c57].

### Fear left faster than the risks did

The VIX fell 1.0 point to end the week at 14.8% [c1458], and the Vol Panic Index recorded its biggest one-day decline in a decade, the third largest in data back to 2006 [c1468][c1470]. Nothing in the backdrop eased to justify that. Options that pay off more the further the market moves now price below the 10th percentile at the index level and at the 0th percentile in some single stocks [c1454][c1455], which is the argument for buying protection now rather than after the fact [c1453]. Goldman's desk likes VIX October 20 calls as cheap protection into a crowded calendar [c1450], and buyers already took the October 17 straddle and October 18 puts 20,000 times each [c1449]. Russell volatility is the preferred vehicle given how extreme the relative reset has been [c95]. Rate volatility decides whether equity volatility stays this cheap [c1472] [d20].

### The crack is at the bottom of the ladder

Investment grade, broad high yield and single-B spreads are all contained [c981]. The damage is concentrated in the worst borrowers, where the CCC minus single-B spread has widened to 799bp, near the top of its three-year range [c963], and Bloomberg's Caa spreads reached 9.2%, the widest since 2023 [c961]. Small-cap equities are carrying it already, with the Russell 2000 down more than 8% relative to the S&P 500 since late June [c96]. BTIG identifies 11 similar episodes since 1996 [c541], where the pattern was not an immediate S&P 500 break but further Russell weakness over the following 40 to 60 trading days [c97]. Options pricing has not registered any of it, with the six-month implied volatility spread between IWM and SPX near the bottom of its two-year range [c102]. The suggested expression is an IWM November 280/265 put spread [c100].

### Five days of more lows than highs

Fewer than 30% of S&P 500 names trade above their 50-day average while the index sits near highs [c899]. The S&P has now logged five straight days with more 52-week lows than highs [c895], and it is up 1.2% over the past month while 9 of its 11 sectors are negative [c896]. BTIG's flag is the banks: the BKX is off more than 2% and more than 10% below its 30-day high while the S&P sits within 1% of a 52-week high, a divergence it last saw in January 2000 [c453]. Inside semiconductors only 3 of the 30 SOX members are closer to their own 52-week highs than the index is to its, and 10 are more than 30% below theirs [c451][c452]. BTIG's instruction for anyone bullish on tech is to own the names near highs rather than the laggards [c249].

### Copper got liquidated, not repriced

Managed money sold $2.6bn of high-grade copper futures between September 8 and 15, the largest weekly sale since April 2023, and $2.2bn of that was liquidation [c138][c139]. The December contract fell 5.6% from record highs on tariff uncertainty and doubts about the pace of the AI buildout [c140]. Copper length tracks the data center equity basket closely, and that basket fell 6.9% in the same AI unwind [c534], which makes the selling a positioning event rather than a verdict on demand. JPMorgan's flow data shows the same sharp cut in Managed Money copper longs week over week [c1243]. UBS stays constructive and treats near-term weakness as a buying opportunity [c135], with LME copper above USD 14,750 per metric ton and near September's record [c134]. The open question is the White House decision on refined copper tariffs [c142].

### France is the euro area's widening problem

The gap between French and German 10-year borrowing costs is back at 104 basis points [c1090], the widest since the euro crisis [c1091]. Goldman has it widening another 7-9bps last week, at or near the widest levels of the past 10 years [c1093], with French corporate bond spreads now catching up to the move in government bonds [c966]. The arithmetic underneath: a 2026 deficit forecast at 5.4% of GDP against a 5% target, and public debt at 117% of GDP [c1099][c1100]. French issuers, banks in particular, are coming to market early to prefund ahead of election volatility next year [c1109]. Goldman's expression is a long in its German fiscal spending basket against a short in its French domestic basket into next year's elections [c1088][c1092].
