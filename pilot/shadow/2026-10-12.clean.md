# Credit Cracks, Equities Shrug

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The level where AI borrowing starts to care

The rise in Treasury yields this year is not an inflation story. Of the 108 basis point (one-hundredth of a percentage point) rise in the 10-year yield, 98 points came from real rates, the part that moves with competition for capital rather than expected inflation. The 10-year closed at 5.23%. Thursday's 30-year auction stopped at 5.618%, the highest yield since August 2000.

What is bidding against the government for that money is the AI buildout. SpaceX raised $40BN in AI compute financing that the market read as crowding out sovereign paper. Broadcom spent the past few weeks working to arrange more than $50 billion in financing for OpenAI's custom AI chip. In the third quarter, the interest-rate risk issued by the AI ecosystem ran about 25% higher than all Treasury issuance. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky says the crowding out effect from that issuance is potentially immense.

The question Goldman's Rikin Shah asks is the one that decides the trade: at what yield do the AI borrowers themselves start to care. His answer is that a 50-100bp rise in the cost of funding does little to slow AI-related borrowing when expected returns run in double digits, which leaves the market hunting for the level where either AI issuance turns rate-sensitive or another part of the economy breaks first. Equity investors have not blinked yet. Since August 27 the S&P 500 is up 1.28% while the same index without its AI names is down 5.19%.

The desks split on timing. Goldman's rates strategists think the selloff has run far enough and recommend a trade that profits if 10-year rates fall relative to 5- and 30-year rates (receiving 10s on the 5s10s30s SOFR fly), entered at 2bp with a target of -4bp and a stop at 6bp. BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky flagged a key reversal day in the 30-year and warns the pain trade from here is lower yields and a lower Nasdaq together. Jefferies will not have the turn at all, holding since March 2020 that G7 government bonds are in a structural bear market after a 39-year bull market.

Credit has already answered. The cost of insuring Oracle's debt against default for five years closed at a record 261bp, up 36% on the month. The same insurance on Broadcom printed a record 136bp. The extra yield demanded from the weakest corporate borrowers, rated CCC, stood at 1,014bp, the widest in at least three years. The VIX closed at 15.4, the 17th percentile of the past year, even with credit at those levels. Next week's CPI print is the near-term trigger.

## 3. BRIEFS

### One trade wearing many hats

An investor told Goldman's Pasquariello directly that the current market is basically just one big trade. The earnings arithmetic backs him, with the top 10 S&P 500 contributors set to generate 68% of EPS growth this quarter, up from 48% last quarter. Hedge funds are already there, Mag 7 at about 22% of total US prime brokerage exposure, the highest since the start of 2022. Underneath the index, 85% of S&P 500 names sit more than 10% below their all-time highs and 59% more than 20% below. Goldman's Adrien Simonet is telling clients that positioning is no longer a cushion against an AI selloff. His desk is pitching $859 million of 6-month best-of puts across 29 AI and AI-adjacent names.

### The $20 billion footnote

The Financial Times reported OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue was approaching $50 billion at the end of September, $20 billion below the $68-70 billion figure that had circulated weeks earlier. Goldman's Sean Johnstone explains the gap as a reporting convention, since OpenAI reports revenue net of what its cloud partners sell while Anthropic reports closer to gross. Equities moved first. The Nasdaq fell 1.25%, the SOX dropped 3.4%, and Oracle lost 5.5%, Broadcom 4.4%, Nvidia 2.9% and CoreWeave 7.8%. Bloomberg then reported OpenAI expects at least $70 billion in annualized revenue by year-end, which lifted the Nasdaq 100 back to unchanged on the week. Credit never took the explanation, with Oracle's and Broadcom's default insurance both printing records once the question landed on their largest customer.

### Waller moves the goalposts

Fed Governor Waller said he likely expects two additional rate hikes to be appropriate, which would raise the funds rate to 4.25-4.50%. He pointed to the unresolved Middle East conflict and signs that the AI buildout is raising tech consumer prices as reasons his faith in inflation's progress toward 2% has weakened. The September minutes read the same way, with all participants backing a 25bp hike to 3.75-4% on insufficient progress. Participants also named surging AI-related investments as a source of inflation pressure. Goldman Sachs still expects one additional 25bp hike in December, raising the funds rate to 4-4.25%, and has lowered its probability that the FOMC skips. The three-month annualized rate of core PCE inflation, the measure Waller had previously emphasized, is now just 2.0%.

### The bar is 27% and rising

Consensus expects S&P 500 EPS growth of 27% year over year for 3Q26, the highest heading into any reporting season since 2021. Goldman thinks most companies will again surpass those estimates. Deutsche Bank forecasts 34% year-over-year EPS growth for Q3. Jefferies has S&P 500 3Q26 earnings rising 30.2% year over year. Financials go first, a sector that closed in the red 16 of 21 trading sessions in September, tied for its worst month in years. Goldman's index desk prefers the 30-October expiry for calls, which captures the Fed decision, the data and about 65% of SPX market cap reporting by then.

### Hormuz still sets the inflation clock

Brent crude traded up to around $106 intraday this week, with WTI topping $93 at the week's highs. Oil still closed up 4% on the day of Trump's ceasefire pledge, and the real decline only arrived overnight as Brent slid back toward $103. The physical market explains the stickiness, with Hormuz shipping flows running at roughly 9.5 million barrels a day, still about 30% below pre-war levels. Goldman Sachs raised its Brent Dec26 forecast to $90/bbl from $85, because a slower decline in the risk premium offsets faster Gulf supply recovery. Nomura's McElligott says it is effectively certain, in his view, that Trump resumes Iran attacks regardless of the midterm election results. Goldman Sachs recommends staying long summer-2027 European gasoline as a hedge against upside oil price risk.

### Brazil's runoff pulls in foreign money

Brazil's presidential election goes to a competitive second round runoff on October 25, after Senator Flávio Bolsonaro's stronger than expected first round. The Ibovespa closed up 7.7% at a record 206,912 on Monday, its best day in more than six years. EWZ traded more than $8bn of volume on Monday, out-trading IWM and TLT. The rally came with $700mm of share creation in that fund, the largest daily inflow on record. Short interest barely changed after the vote, which J.P. Morgan reads as institutional investors being less optimistic than retail. Goldman Sachs thinks a market friendly outcome conducive to a tighter fiscal stance could take USD/BRL down to 4.50-4.80.

### Power is the bottleneck nobody owns

Goldman Sachs recommends buying its Power Up America basket, the GSENEPOW index. Positioning across the basket's underlying categories sits at a two-year low. The price damage is real, with independent power producers down approximately 30% over the last 12 months and gas infrastructure names down 13% over the last three months. Demand forecasts went up rather than down, Goldman Sachs research looking for 108 GW of US data center power demand by 2030, a 23% compound annual growth rate off a 2025 base. FERC is expected to rule on the PJM large-load framework on October 12, the next dated catalyst for data center deal flow. The desk's implementation is to sell the 31-Dec 85% put to help fund a 110%/135% call spread for 1.75% into year-end.

### Spending moves to things you cannot stream

Consumer spending growth on experiences accelerated from 1% in Q1 2025 to 6% in Q1 2026, versus 2% for broader services. The stocks did the opposite, Goldman's Consumer Experiences basket underperforming the S&P 500 excluding AI names by roughly 14%. Live Nation reported more than 10% fan growth and no signs of consumer weakness. Sphere Entertainment's Wizard of Oz show sold 3.6 million tickets and generated $450 million. Viking is already 96% booked for 2026, with 53% of 2027 sold. Goldman's trade is the GSXUCEXP 15Jan27 110%/120% call spread, indicatively 1.61% referenced off 91.12.
