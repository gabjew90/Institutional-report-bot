# Panic Without The Pivot

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Fear is at a record and the Fed is still raising rates

The S&P 500 fell for a seventh straight session into Friday's close at 6,910, down 8.6% on the week [c1220]. A streak that long has happened five times in 25 years [c1224]. The Nasdaq 100 lost 10.4%, its worst week since March 2020 [c1221]. The VIX rose more than 16 points, the fifth-largest weekly jump on record [c1531], and Goldman's measure of panic in the options market closed the week at a record 9.8 out of 10 [c1532].

One group did the selling. Beta-adjusted net exposure across Goldman's hedge fund clients fell 8.8 points to 48.2%, a five-year low, and gross exposure fell to a one-year low of 214.2% [c874, c873]. Short selling was the heaviest since April 2025 [c875], and funds raised gross exposure while getting less long, which means they were adding shorts rather than trimming [c985]. Everyone else kept buying. US equity mutual funds and ETFs have taken in nearly $170bn since the start of the fourth quarter [c696], buybacks ran at 2.2 times last year's pace [c697], and retail order flow showed the clearest buy skews since the first half of the year [c704].

That split is the argument. Goldman's desk thinks equities are near a tactical entry and would rather sell puts at levels where it is comfortable owning stock than chase the rebound [c1128, c1466], noting that panic readings above 9.5 have historically been followed by relief within weeks [c1483]. JPMorgan's Schlegel is not convinced the broad market has given up. His US positioning monitor's four-week change was only about -1z as of Thursday, above the -1.5z he treats as capitulation [c972], and he expects a repeat of 2018, when the index bounced in late November and then fell harder in December [c1260].

What separates this from the four prior panics of comparable size is policy. Every one of those was followed within days or weeks by an intervention [c600], and rates are going up now, not down. Fed Governor Waller pushed for a half-point increase in December [c1361], and Goldman calls a second quarter-point hike close to certain [c1365]. In the five earlier cases where Goldman's panic measure exceeded 9 during a hiking cycle, the S&P returned an average of 0.4% over the following three months [c1191]. Credit is the test that settles it: if Nvidia rallies while borrowing costs for the smaller cloud operators stay under pressure, it is short covering and not a recovery, per Goldman's Privorotsky [c87]. Core PCE on Wednesday, the November jobs report and the December Fed meeting come next [c1402].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Huang is back and the research desks still disagree

Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as President, chief executive and a director, effective immediately [c17], saying input from senior levels of the US government left it confident the move poses no national security risk [c27]. Wolfe Research, which says the Trump administration orchestrated the reversal [c105], keeps Outperform and a $200 target against a $125.53 price, 59.3% upside [c34]. Bernstein upgraded straight back to Outperform at the same $200, roughly 10 times its average CY28/29 earnings estimate [c1, c3], having cut the stock to Market Perform 11 days earlier [c40]. KeyBanc will not follow, holding Sector Weight and a $160 fair value at 9 times its $16.99 FY28 earnings estimate [c95], because the board's own investigation and a separate SEC accounting review are unfinished [c94]. The SEC opened that accounting probe last week [c65]. Evercore ISI's read is blunter: this is AI's first bailout, of the financing architecture rather than of a balance sheet [c276].

### Rising chip rents are a lending story, not a demand story

Silicon Data's B200 rental index rose 23.5% between late August and November 19, from $5.62 to $6.94 per GPU-hour [c662], and the four-year-old H100 rents for 67% more than in July 2025 [c663]. Liquid Compute's Stanley Lee argues lenders explain the move. Advance rates on loans secured by chips fell from about 80% of value over the summer to roughly 70% after Labor Day and 50-60% now [c1709, c1710]. An operator that cannot borrow against a cluster cannot sell a buyer a long contract, so that buyer is pushed into a thin short-dated market and bids it up, while fewer new clusters get funded [d17]. The price of capital is visible in the new deals. The publicly syndicated $25bn tranche of SpaceX's chip financing priced at 345 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent) over the three-year [c1732], north of 8.25% [c1733]. Goldman expects upwards of $300bn of chip financing supply next year to crowd out ordinary high-yield demand [c1689]. CoreWeave and Nebius remain the stress point, with unsecured debt yielding 15% and default protection costing 1,200 basis points [c130].

### Gold was sold to pay for stocks

Spot gold fell 8.9% to Friday's New York close [c1088] and traded below $3,600 for the first time since September 2025 [c1104]. The catalyst was the equity drawdown rather than the dollar or real rates, with speculators selling the most liquid thing they owned to raise cash, per Goldman's Robert Quinn [d36]. Net speculative length has collapsed to roughly $72bn from about $130bn at the September peak [c961]. On November 20, the cost of three-month downside protection relative to upside, and implied volatility itself, both moved 4-5 standard deviations, with that skew reaching a multiyear high [c1558]. Since late 2022, spikes like that have reversed within a week about 70% of the time [c1497]. Two desks push back on calling a floor. The World Gold Council does not think a full washout has happened yet [c1121], and ANZ sees room for another 200 tonnes of liquidation on top of the nearly 200 already sold [c757, c759]. Goldman's end-2027 forecast is unchanged at $4,650 an ounce on central bank buying [c1059].

### Memory marks down 2028 while keeping its buys

The Philadelphia semiconductor index is down about 43% from its June 2026 peak [c482], the fourth-largest drawdown since 1999 and past the range of a mid-cycle correction [c484, c486]. Goldman cut its 2028 price forecasts to -19% year over year for conventional DRAM and -21% for NAND [c488, c489], took Samsung's target to W350,000 and SK Hynix's to W2.0 million, and kept both rated Buy [c492, c493], on the argument that those share prices already discount zero AI capital spending growth in 2028 [c332]. At the same time it raised its estimate of the high-bandwidth memory market to $60bn, $200bn and $237bn for 2026 through 2028 on higher selling prices [c336]. The distinction Goldman is drawing is between a valuation reset and an actual earnings downturn, and it argues only the first has happened so far [d22]. Evercore ISI is less forgiving, with the sector multiple already down from 15 to 13 times earnings and possibly heading below its ten-year low of 11 [c414].

### Asia got official support, and the flows went to India

Korea's Kospi closed down 3.3% after trading as low as -5% intraday [c534], while Taiwan's Taiex finished up 2.2% after swinging between -1.6% and +1.7% [c387]. The MSCI Emerging Markets index is now 21% below its June peak, in a bear market, after its worst week since March 2020 [c530, c531]. Governments moved over the weekend: China lowered margin requirements, Korea eased restrictions on company buybacks and Taiwan tightened the rules on short selling [c328]. Citi puts numbers on the Korean backstop, estimating the National Pension Service could absorb KRW17trn, KRW39trn and KRW62trn of domestic shares if the Kospi fell to 4,500, 4,000 and 3,500 [c267]. Patches like these are not what you want to see marking a bottom, per Goldman's Privorotsky [c478]. The money is going somewhere else anyway. Foreign investors have bought $12.5bn of Indian equities this month, 70% of this year's total inflow [c220].

### Copper ran out of physical buyers

High-grade copper futures fell more than 9% on the week [c455], one of the largest weekly declines in a decade [c463]. Goldman's futures desk attributes it to tighter financial conditions and continued weakness in data center demand, which dragged prices through a momentum threshold and triggered heavy trend-follower selling [c456, c457]. The reason it went so far is that the physical trade had nothing left to give. Producers, merchants, processors and users held gross long positions worth about 20% of open interest on November 10, roughly double the usual level [c462], and Goldman suspects they were positioning ahead of a US copper tariff that has not arrived [c601]. Goldman's Global Copper Basket is down close to 35% from its August peak, against 22% for the metal itself on the LME [c431]. Stan Druckenmiller said at the TomorrowX Summit that he is short copper [c206]. Goldman's Camille Cerisey reads this as a crowded position washing out rather than fundamentals breaking down [c207, c208].

### Two houses at 8,700, and a case for 5,800

Morgan Stanley lifted its 12-month S&P 500 target to 8,700, which is 19 times its 2028 earnings estimate of $458 [c1167], on a view that the earnings cycle has moved from early to mid-cycle, with growth slowing from 34% this year to 14% in 2027 and 9% in 2028 [c1168, c1169, c1170]. It wants investors to stay in quality but rotate more cyclically, with Industrials as its top sector [c361]. Goldman's year-end 2027 target is also 8,700, 21% above current levels [c1157]. The bear case is just as specific. Goldman notes that the AI investment boom has pushed S&P earnings about 25% above their 30-year trend [c1253], and applying the 30-year average multiple of 17 times to trend earnings of $340 gives 5,800 [c1254]. One River's Sparks goes further, arguing US stocks are 40-50% too expensive with earnings 30% above their 50-year trend line [c1271, c1270].
