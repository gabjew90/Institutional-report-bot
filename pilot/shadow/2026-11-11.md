# Machines Sold, Corporates Bought

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The Washout Nobody Sponsored

Thursday's crash did not come from people. The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.36%, its worst session since the Covid crash [c1665], while the S&P 500 with its AI names stripped out lost only 0.3% on the week [c1752]. The selling was mechanical [d15]. Short interest in US-listed ETFs jumped 7% in a day, the third-largest one-day rise in five years, and single-stock selling by hedge funds was a mild 0.5 standard deviation move, which Goldman reads as discretionary managers not being the main driver [c1227][c1434]. Nomura's McElligott counted $47bn of levered-ETF selling and $61bn of supply from volatility-control funds that session [c1316]. UBS measured total forced selling at more than $365bn, the largest on record [c1716], and split it between risk-control funds at $95-125bn, levered ETFs at $50-55bn and trend-followers at $25-30bn [c1348].

Monday went the other way. The S&P 500 closed up 2.3% at 7,549 [c1547], with 423 of its 500 names higher [c1556], and the semiconductor index rose 6.8% even with Nvidia down 4.3% [c1551]. Positioning can snap back that fast because it was emptied first. US fundamental long/short gross leverage fell 8.7 points to 207.5%, the largest weekly drop since Covid, and net leverage, the directional risk left after longs are offset against shorts, fell to 43.3%, its lowest in five years [c1125][c1126].

Goldman is buying the rebound. Its Miller desk likes owning hyperscalers through a January 110/120 call spread costing 1.8% [c349][c350], on the view that positioning is near washout levels [c996]. UBS will not follow. The firm calls the absence of long-only buyers the focal point for flows from here [c1360] and warns a rebound built only on fading forced selling may not carry [c1361]. Its own recommendation is narrower: buy semiconductors and sell the names investors rallied as hedges [c1070] [d18].

Tuesday's inflation print decides which desk is right. Goldman forecasts core consumer prices up 0.16% on the month, under the 0.2% consensus [c2388], and Citi sees 0.19% [c2373]. Goldman's flow desk is explicit about the stakes: a hot number moves its trend-follower estimate from the flat-market case to the falling-market case and puts a December rate increase back on the table [c2395], a path implying $49bn of S&P futures selling over a month [c1239]. A cool number leaves corporate buybacks, near $5bn a day with 90% of companies free to buy their own stock [c420], outbidding the machines [c1006].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Forty-Five Percent, On Paper

The number driving the selloff is a share of planned capacity, not a cancellation. Goldman sees state moratoria potentially freezing roughly 45% of planned US data-center capacity, Texas alone about a quarter of that [c693]. Citadel Securities puts it in megawatts: about 45 GW across 350 projects in Texas, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Georgia at risk through 2030, 24 GW of it in Texas [c590]. Goldman's Alec Phillips expects a temporary pause lasting several months until new rules are written [c662], and Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has said every already-permitted project may finish, while acknowledging limited power to impose a moratorium at all (UBS) [c486][c487]. Goldman raised its US data-center construction spending forecasts anyway, to $80bn for 2026 and $100bn for 2027, and introduced $125bn for 2028 [c383][c384] [d24]. BofA's Hartnett takes the other side, noting Texas is 30% of the 60-plus GW yet to break ground by 2028, which makes the roughly $2tn 2028 capital spending forecast look too high [c583].

### Credit Slept Through It

Equities priced a growth scare and credit did not. On Thursday the 30-year investment-grade credit spread widened 2.2 basis points (one hundredth of a percentage point), against the 8 that the 4.4% equity drop implied, per Goldman [c2307]. Over the week investment-grade spreads finished 1 basis point wider, high yield was 4 basis points tighter, and leveraged loans had their best week since April [c2252]. That calm came in the same week the primary market printed a record $76bn of investment-grade supply, $67bn of it hyperscaler financing, taking hyperscaler issuance to $296bn for the year [c2308][c2309] [d29]. Goldman's explanation is that the selloff hit the companies spending on data centers rather than the ones issuing bonds [c2321]. Its own credit desk is not comfortable: chip credit default swaps now trade wider than hyperscaler ones for the first time in three months [c2270], high-yield AI bonds fell 1.67 points against a 0.36 point gain for the rest of high yield [c2271], and Goldman's Lynam sees near-term spread risk firmly tilted toward widening [c2284].

### Chips At A Staples Discount

The semiconductor index fell 28% over 22 trading days, one of its largest one-month drawdowns on record [c640], leaving AI and semiconductor stocks roughly 40% below their summer highs [c641]. Valuation got strange in the process. Consumer staples now trade at about twice the 24-month forward earnings multiple of the semiconductor index (Goldman) [c642], and the Nasdaq 100's forward price-to-earnings ratio is 18.4x, its lowest since 2020 [c417]. Goldman's research arm calls semis clearly oversold and names where it would buy: Broadcom and Nvidia in AI compute, where current prices imply no earnings growth past 2027 [c6], Applied Materials and Lam Research in equipment [c182], and SK Hynix and Samsung in memory, both below tangible book value [c414] [d4]. UBS's technical work agrees on timing, with the semiconductor index hitting both its daily and weekly downside exhaustion targets at Thursday's low and 63.3% of its members at or near buy signals [c561][c562]. Goldman pairs the long with a short in its staples basket, at its richest premium to the S&P 500 in over a decade [c649].

### The War Premium Priced Out

Brent crude touched $118 on October 30 as the market priced a post-midterm Gulf escalation [c1919] and closed the week just under $98, its steepest weekly drop since the April 2020 Covid crash and its biggest in dollar terms in data going back to 1988 [c1947][c1948]. Brent ended last week down 13.32% at $98.32 a barrel (Deutsche Bank) [c1929]. Crude fell roughly 5% further on Monday to $85 on more Iran de-escalation [c1944]. Goldman's Robert Quinn traced the move to positioning: managed money sold $6.6bn of Brent between October 27 and November 3, $4.9bn of it liquidation of existing longs rather than new shorts, cutting net length from about $28bn in October to about $19bn [c1163][c1165][c1166] [d38]. Citadel Securities reads the options the same way, with the three-month chance of Brent above $120 more than halved since October 28, from 23% to 9% [c1926]. UBS's Dominic Ellis argues the other way, saying the near-700 million barrel drawdown in global inventories will not refill because Congress changed hands [c1909].

### Hot Payrolls, December Back On

October payrolls rose 165,000 against a 70,000 consensus, the prior two months were revised up a net 84,000, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.14% from 4.18% (Morgan Stanley) [c2092][c2093][c2095]. The three-month average gain jumped to 137,000 from 51,000 before the release (UBS) [c2121]. Wages did not confirm the heat. Average hourly earnings growth slowed to 3.1% over the year from 3.9% twelve months earlier, and production-worker hours fell 0.2% on the month (Morgan Stanley) [c2134][c2133] [d70]. Markets took the headline anyway, with December rate-increase odds moving above 80% after the print, per Goldman [c1883]. Morgan Stanley says the report keeps the Fed on track to raise rates in December [c1852], and UBS expects 25 basis point increases in both December and March [c1876]. NISA's read is the awkward one: the labour market itself justifies neither cuts nor hikes [c1888], and the December increase is coming because of inflation, not jobs [c1873].

### Goldman Trims Gold Again

Goldman cut its gold forecast again, to $4,150 a troy ounce for end-2026 from $4,650 and to $4,800 for end-2027 from $5,400 [c2467][c2468]. With spot near $4,130 [c2474], the near-term target implies almost nothing left this year. The cut is a Fed call, not a gold call. The base case assumes a hawkish shift and a December increase contain hedging demand, measured through call-option buying, with net GLD call open interest near 1.9 million contracts assumed to fall back toward 1.0 million [c2224], where each million contracts is worth roughly 5% on the price [c2223]. Under a three-hike path gold ends 2027 at $4,660 instead [c2469] [d39]. Goldman's own traders are less convinced. Rich Privorotsky calls gold an inverse oil trade and a more defensible way to play de-escalation than shorting crude [c2457][c2458], and Adam Gillard notes the firm's trend-follower model is close to maximum short, so the pain trade is higher [c2459].

### Nvidia's Other Problem

Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation on November 8 after reports that chief executive Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, Huang separately admitted an extramarital affair, and the board affirmed confidence in him and the management team (UBS) [c69][c70][c72]. UBS expects no effect on the November 17 fiscal third-quarter results, citing AI compute demand that still exceeds supply [c74][c75] [d13]. The longer-dated risks it flags are different. Investors may start pricing key-person risk, since there is no clear internal successor and the organisation is extremely flat, with press reports putting Huang's direct reports at 50 to 60 [c48][c49][c76]. The intelligence angle also raises the odds that China chip-export restrictions persist rather than ease [c2356]. The stock is down about 26% from its October highs, and most of that is multiple compression rather than any confirmed break in fundamentals, leaving it near 12 to 13 times estimated fiscal 2028 earnings [c44][c45][c10].
