# Bonds Price A Hike

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The bond market prices a hike, stocks price nothing

Government bond yields finished last week at multi-year highs almost everywhere. The US 10 year Treasury yield rose 6.3bps to 4.78% and closed Tuesday at 4.80%, its highest since October 2023. A basis point is one hundredth of a percent, so that is a small weekly move onto a very high level. The German 10 year Bund yield touched a post-2011 high of 3.37%, Japanese 10 year yields sit at their highest since 1996 and UK 30 year yields at their highest since 1998. Futures ended Friday pricing a 62% chance the Fed raises rates at the September meeting, and the same pricing shows up at 60% in Deutsche Bank's and Morgan Stanley's work.

The labour data is only half of why. August payrolls came in at 162k against expectations of 55k, with a further 55k of upward revisions to the prior two months, private hiring firm at 127k and participation up to 61.6%. The price side is what moved rate pricing: the prices paid component of the ISM services survey hit a four year high in August, a level that in the last inflation wave lined up with CPI above 5%. Rates markets still price only a shallow tightening cycle, which is the dislocation Deutsche Bank is pointing at. Inflation at 4-5% has historically been followed by over 200bps of hikes in the first year, and one and done cycles are rare.

Morgan Stanley reads the same Warsh language and lands on hold, because its economists expect core inflation to decelerate in the second half of 2026, and it calls that a close call. TS Lombard splits it: the fundamental case has weakened as monthly core readings softened, but a skipped September hike likely turns into a hawkish flip during the winter.

Equities have not engaged with any of it. MSCI World is up 13% year to date and sits less than 1% below its highs while the VIX prints new lows for Trump's second term. The US 10 year real yield is more than 70bps above its pre-conflict low, and stocks have diverged from that path since the April ceasefire.

Friday's August CPI is the week's biggest event and lands days before the FOMC. Deutsche Bank forecasts headline at 0.38% month-on-month, leaving headline at 3.38% year on year and core at 2.38%. A core print at 0.2% instead of 0.3% takes September off the table in the market's mind, and a halt to the bond sell-off would be unequivocally bullish for stocks, per Goldman.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Oil's curve is betting on a reopening that never comes

Brent rose 7.80% last week to $96.28 a barrel and added 1.05% this morning to $97.29 after tanker incidents around the Gulf. European gas gained 7.42% to 72.00 euros per MWh, a fourth straight weekly rise. The forward curve disagrees with all of it: six month Brent is $83.2/bbl, a price that only works if the Strait of Hormuz reopens, and it has stayed shut for about six months. Morgan Stanley rejects the normalization case and raised its fourth quarter forecasts to $100/bbl Brent and EUR88/MWh European gas. Goldman looks past crude, where total exports run above visible ones and cap the upside, to gas, where Qatari loadings keep missing and European storage is below seasonal norms, so the Dec26 contract could rally toward 100 EUR/MWh from about 71. If the blockage holds, the back of the oil curve has to move up.

### The AI buildout is now a Treasury problem

The five hyperscalers plus $NVDA have issued about $320 bn of debt in 2026, roughly $303 bn in ten year equivalents, equal to 68% of new Treasury long duration borrowing this year. That is the reason JPMorgan expects the Treasury to struggle to cap the 10 year yield, which now trades slightly above where it was when the Twist was announced on August 19. Net buybacks at the hyperscalers have turned into net issuance, and their credit default swap spreads are climbing on supply and hedging demand. Morgan Stanley forecasts over $1.3 trillion of spending across the six largest hyperscalers in 2027, 60% above its 2026 estimate, and says the open question is which market absorbs the financing. Bond investors are already buying protection against a hyperscaler default.

### Records in power demand, price reaction in only one market

PJM peaked above 168 GW gross and 162 GW net of demand response in early July, beating the 166 GW record set in 2006 and its own 154 GW forecast, with real-time prices above $2,000/MWh on July 2 and 3. Forward power prices there held even after the heat passed. Texas set its own record at 91 GW and barely repriced, staying in the low hundreds because renewables and storage covered the load. Texas forwards then fell another 2-5% across the curve last week after Governor Abbott's letter paused the Batch Zero review, leaving later-dated power above nearby power. Equipment orders are the cleaner signal: global gas turbine orders hit a record 38 GW in the second quarter, up 71% year over year. Goldman is buying power infrastructure and calls the fight over who pays for datacenter electricity one of the least appreciated election themes in US stocks.

### Copper's record price is a tariff, not a boom

Copper is at record prices with record US inventories at the same time, which is the shape of a tariff distortion rather than industrial demand. US warehouses took in 225,000 metric tons in July, 51% of London warrants are cancelled with 121,000 tonnes earmarked to leave, and smelter fees are at scarcity levels of negative $200 to negative $270 per tonne while Chinese smelters run below 75% of capacity. The Section 232 report is about two months overdue, and the front-loading bid survives as long as it stays unpublished. Goldman expects mine supply to fall 0.7% this year and says the projected 260kt refined surplus now leans on scrap use rising nearly 5%, so an EU restriction on scrap exports would erase it. Positioning is light at 3 out of 10 in options and 4 out of 10 in futures. A softened tariff decision unwinds the trade quickly.

### Europe hikes into an energy shock the curve ignores

The European Central Bank meets Thursday, and both Deutsche Bank and JPMorgan expect a 25bp increase to 2.50%, with Deutsche Bank looking for another in December to 2.75%. Energy is the driver, not demand. European gas is at its highest since early 2023 and the shock pushed the real deposit rate back below zero. Pricing has not moved with it: expected hikes by the June 2027 meeting have gone from 74bps at the July 23 peak to 72bps now, while gas rose more than 18% and euro area GDP growth was revised up from 1.8% to 2.6% annualised. An ideal backdrop for European banks sits at a 2-3% ECB rate with stronger lending and no deterioration in asset quality, per JPMorgan's Kian. The catch on the growth revision is that it came entirely from Ireland at 47.4%.

### Momentum broke and the leaders changed

The momentum factor has given up close to 50% of its late-June gains and is down 5% year to date. First half leaders should not be expected to lead the second half, per Goldman, and the factor's composition has changed, with software in the long leg on a three month lookback but still in the short leg on twelve months. Hedge funds leaned the other way last week, adding gross exposure on four of the past five days for the largest increase since late June. JPMorgan's desk turned neutral on global equities a week earlier on four risks: terminal G4 rates, energy prices and refining margins, politics, and the forced summer rotation out of tech. It is still buying dips and adding cheap European index hedges rather than cutting exposure. Goldman prefers its S&P 500 ex-AI basket (SPXXAI) as the broad hedge, because AI's index weight blunts a plain hedge.

### Yen risk shows up in currency, not in stocks

The dollar briefly fell below 153 yen overnight and Japanese exporters dropped 3%, with autos down 5%. JPMorgan puts the Bank of Japan risk in currency and rates rather than equities, reckons about half the unwind of long dollar-yen positions is done, and marks the next resistance at 152. The longer picture from Deutsche Bank: the yen is bouncing off 40 year lows, Japan spent 15.4tn yen on intervention from April to August, the most since 1991, and only the Bank of Japan can deliver a durable rally. The Bank of Japan meets Friday of next week, less than 36 hours after the FOMC finishes. JPMorgan's Hong Kong derivatives strategists are positioning for a grind higher in Japanese stocks after that meeting through low premium call spreads on the Topix or Topix versus dollar-yen hybrids.
