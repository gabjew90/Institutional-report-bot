# Oil Clears $100, Yields Follow

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The War Premium Gets Repriced Into 2027

Brent settled at $101.21, up 3.36% [c2284], above $100 for the first time since July [c2283]. The number that matters more than spot is the six-month contract, which rose 1.55% to $86.09, its highest since early June [c2285], because traders have stopped treating this as a spike that fades and started doubting the Strait of Hormuz reopens soon [c2286]. Stocks paid for it. The S&P 500 fell 0.48% for a third straight session [c2216] with 404 names lower, the most since June [c2217], while the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.84%, the highest since 2023 [c2312], after a Treasury buyback capped at $6bn came in below some estimates [c2315]. Odds of a September Fed hike moved from 61% to 63% [c2242]. Energy is now arriving as an inflation problem rather than a headline, which is the channel doing the damage to equities [d68].

The banks disagree about what that price already contains. Goldman raised its Brent and WTI forecasts by $5, to $85 and $80 for December 2026 [c157], on a new assumption that shipping disruption runs into 2027 [c803], and calls its own upgrade modest [c196]. The reason is barrels in storage: of the 543mb of visible global stock draw since the war began, only 26mb came out of the OECD commercial tanks that actually track Brent [c1364], which leaves Goldman's fair value at $79, about $18 under the market [c189][d42]. Societe Generale reads the same conflict and gets a probability-weighted year-end price of $109 against Brent near $99 [c2295], with a 30% chance a stalemate takes Brent to $120 in November [c2293][d65]. J.P. Morgan's Kaneva lands nearer Goldman, arguing crude may not rise much even if the fighting never stops, given spare supply, production outside the region and full inventories [c2302][c2303].

What settles this is inventory and export data, not diplomacy. Goldman assumes Gulf output returns to pre-war levels by the second half of 2027 [c1376], helped by 3.8mb/d of new pipeline capacity by end-2027 [c1377], and China's crude imports are still running about 30% below last year [c1381]. Two things decide it. If OECD commercial stocks start draining fast, the cheap-oil case is dead. If Gulf exports keep adapting and China stays out of the market, the premium bleeds out whatever the headlines say.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Friday's Inflation Print Is Binary

J.P. Morgan's Feroli forecasts August headline inflation at 0.38% for the month and core at 0.21% [c1994][c1995]. He reads a rounded 0.2% as low enough for the Fed to hold and 0.3% as high enough to hike [c2008], and the bond market prices roughly a 63% chance of a quarter-point increase on September 16 [c2009][d44]. Goldman sits a shade hotter at 0.23% core against 0.2% consensus [c1781]. BofA is on the other side of the decision. Its economists put August core inflation at 0.22% [c1606] and core PCE, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, at 0.24% [c1607], which they expect to convince Chair Warsh that higher rates are warranted [c1210], and they still forecast 75 basis points (one basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) of hikes this year [c1211].

### Nobody Is Paying For Protection

Goldman says the market has just traded through the lowest six-month realized correlation between S&P 500 stocks ever recorded [c1916], with the only comparable stretches February 2007 and January 2018 [c1699]. Its derivatives desk argues levels this low have not been sustainable and have previously ended in a burst of index volatility [c936][d14]. One-month implied volatility on the average Nasdaq-100 stock, the option market's price of expected movement, has fallen from roughly 57 to roughly 40 [c1915], and the premium for downside protection over upside is near its flattest in 20 years [c933]. The desk's own hedges are the $VIX October 20/30 call spread at $0.91 [c938] and $IWM 286 puts at $1.70 [c28]. BofA prefers selling the October 16 $VIX put and buying the November 22-30 call spread [c1595].

### Cloud Bonds Are Eating The Treasury Market

About $320bn of 2026 debt issuance comes from the five largest cloud companies plus Nvidia, including the vehicles they backstop for data-centre leases [c1161]. JPMorgan measures $303bn of that in ten-year equivalents, which is 68% of all new long-dated federal borrowing this year [c1162][d33]. Ten-year Treasury yields have risen roughly 50 basis points in 2026 [c1151]. Goldman's George Cole estimates investment equal to about 1% of global output may need redirecting to fund AI-related bonds [c1267], and Rich Privorotsky argues that borrowing competes with the Treasury for one finite pool of savings [c1268]. TS Lombard's standing view is that the US 10-year belongs at 5% or higher [c1515], and Goldman treats long-dated bonds as the bigger risk, since supply and the extra yield demanded for holding them can lift rates with no Fed hike at all [c1264][c1265].

### Copper Is Being Bought For Electricity

Copper set a record above $14,500 a tonne [c302]. Societe Generale raised its fourth-quarter target to $14,750 [c2129] and looks for $15,250 to $15,500 by the end of 2027 [c2130]. The demand story is electrical: the US data-centre pipeline has gone from 136GW to 381GW of planned capacity [c2133], which SG estimates adds 440kt to 660kt of copper demand a year [c2134][d65]. Supply is moving the other way, with Chile's Cochilco cutting its 2026 production forecast to 5.27Mt [c2136], and roughly 70% of exchange stocks now sitting in COMEX warehouses, which leaves everywhere outside the US tighter than the headline totals suggest [c2132]. Goldman expects its Global Copper Basket to outperform as demand rises [c857], while its delta-one desk warns tariff distortions mean this record is not a clean read on growth [c305].

### The Conference Says Demand, The Book Says Fear

Broadcom reaffirmed AI revenue targets of $115bn for its 2027 fiscal year and $230bn for 2028 [c42] and said supply, not demand, is the constraint [c47]. Cisco tied a fivefold rise in network bandwidth to always-on AI agents [c390], and Qualcomm backed a $15bn data-centre target for fiscal 2029 [c478]. Positioning tells the opposite story: Goldman prime brokerage data puts net leverage for US fundamental long/short managers in the 4th percentile [c666], and its tech long/short momentum pair is down about 50% from the late-June highs [c1255]. Goldman's Faris Mourad recommends buying the dip through the AI hardware basket GSTMTDAT [c910][d21]. The one number arguing the other way is customer spend: the top 1% of AI buyers cut spending 9.7% last month, from $7,976 to $7,205 [c2155].

### The Yen Is The Leverage Question

The yen keeps appreciating as the market leans into Bank of Japan tightening and money coming home [c870]. TS Lombard calls the rally sustainable [c1050], pointing to Japan's public pension fund lifting its domestic bond allocation to 27% against a 25% official weight [c1052] and Norges Bank shifting toward Japanese government bonds over Treasuries [c1053][d57]. Goldman's FX strategy team says the case to be tactically long yen has strengthened and that being short the dollar against the yen is a more attractive hedge than it has been in a long time [c641][c642]. Its Tokyo rates team pushes back, arguing BoJ pricing has little hawkish room left [c1250] and the pension fund would need a 10-year Japanese yield near 3.5% before repatriating in size [c1653]. J.P. Morgan's Rie Nishihara expects 155 to 160 per dollar rather than a lasting move below 150 [c1941], and the risk for US holders is borrowed yen unwinding out of American equities [c1388].

### Europe Is Cracking Before Winter

European gas closed up 4.49% at EUR79.25 per megawatt hour, the highest since 2022 [c2287], after a fire at a major Russian gas hub [c2256] and Ukrainian drone strikes on two gas condensate plants there [c2257]. Storage is 67% full [c600], against 79% at this point last year [c601], and the shortfall sits in the north, with Germany at 55% and the Netherlands at 50% [c602]. ABN Amro warns that a cold snap pushing storage toward 30% would turn this into physical scarcity and reprice gas toward EUR100-150 [c573][d30]. The stress is already in bonds and stocks: French 10-year yields hit a post-2008 high of 4.34% [c2319] and the STOXX 600 fell 1.41%, its worst session in two months [c2218]. The ECB is expected to raise rates a quarter point to 2.5% today [c2353], and Goldman thinks September ends the cycle [c1260].

### Bitcoin Is Still Trading The Fed

$BTC reached just under $82.2k on 3 September after encouraging comments from Governor Waller [c858], then gave it back to $78k when the August employment report landed [c1421]. Flows held up through the round trip. Waller's remarks were followed by the largest single-day spot ETF inflow since January at $729m [c859], and US spot $BTC funds took $984m last week [c860], with $ETH funds adding $218m [c862][d55]. The 50-day average price has crossed above the 200-day [c866], and the last five times that happened price gained more than 40% over the following three months [c856]. Perpetual funding near 1% annualized says leveraged longs have been cleared out rather than piled in [c1876]. The next scheduled catalyst is the CLARITY Act procedural vote on 15 September [c1880].
