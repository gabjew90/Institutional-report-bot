# Nvidia's Board Moves, Street Splits

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The Price Of One Person

Nvidia dropped about 9% in after-hours trading and roughly 11% since Monday, against the S&P 500 up 1% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index down 2% [c81][c83], after the Wall Street Journal reported the board is preparing to remove founder and chief executive Jensen Huang [c122][c172]. Huang acknowledged infidelity after the Financial Times reported a US intelligence memo alleging a relationship with a Megaspeed executive whom US intelligence believes China's Ministry of State Security recruited [c125], and the New York Times reported a second relationship, with a direct report [c171]. The board met the evening before and a decision could come within the day [c157][c183]. At the lows the stock sat nearly 40% below its early-October record and nearly 30% under its 200-day average price, matching the extreme of the Liberation Day collapse, which went deeper but took longer [c106][c107][c108].

The question is not whether the chips sell [c25][c70]. It is whether the person who turned that demand into locked supply and financed the customers is replaceable [d9][d13]. UBS puts Nvidia's free cash flow at roughly $50B to $60B a quarter [c163], much of it recycled into financing for newer cloud providers, model developers and suppliers, and estimates a pause under new leadership could pull $50B or more out of that system temporarily, while rates rise and parts of the credit market are thin [c112]. Huang personally secured memory, wafer and optics allocations through prepayments and equity stakes in suppliers including Lumentum, Coherent and Corning [c131], and drove a $30B equity stake in OpenAI plus capacity backstops for newer cloud providers such as CoreWeave [c130].

The disagreement is whether that is a multiple problem or an earnings problem. Wolfe sees zero impact on fundamentals, earnings or orders through at least calendar 2028, because Nvidia is largely sold out that far and its roadmap already runs there, leaving a discount it cannot size and believes is mostly in the price at under 8 times its fiscal 2029 earnings view [c73][c74][c79]. Wedbush holds a $345.00 target and argues that about 10 times fiscal 2028 earnings already prices almost any level of uncertainty [c53][c56]. Oppenheimer cut to Perform and withdrew its price target, pointing to no obvious successor and the risk that a departure drains engineering talent [c88][c90][c91]. William Blair went to Market Perform and will wait for the dust to settle [c124]. RBC kept Outperform but cut its target to $230 from $330, valuing the stock at 15 times calendar 2027 earnings instead of about 20 times [c29][c98][c99]. Mizuho stays Outperform at $250, about 16 times its fiscal 2028 estimate, while expecting the succession to distract the company for six to seven months [c6][c82].

Tuesday's October-quarter report decides the earnings half. Oppenheimer models data center revenue, 93% of sales, up 14% from the prior quarter and 99% from a year earlier to $101.8B [c13], and RBC expects the company to at least repeat its 70% calendar 2027 revenue growth outlook [c34]. The governance half runs past the print to the November 18 closed-door briefing on sales to Megaspeed, a risk Wells Fargo calls unknown and likely drawn out [c120]. A board that names an operator with the roadmap intact leaves the 10 times multiple Wedbush describes [c56]. One that leaves the diversion and export questions open leaves William Blair's overhang in place [c123].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Retail Bought Every Print Of Bad News

Retail met the news with $111mn of net buying in the first 30 minutes, the largest opening half hour Vanda has recorded in the stock, against a $2.7mn median and a 99th percentile of $49mn [c44][c169]. Opens that size have historically turned into four to seven times the figure over the full session, which points to $450mn to $770mn today and a place among the ten biggest retail buying days in any US stock or ETF since 2014 [c45][c46]. Tuesday's $93mn open ranks fourth largest on record, so two of the four biggest have landed this week, and Nvidia topped Vanda's entire traded universe on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday at 9.7, 14.4 and 3.7 times the next most bought security, taking 61% of all US retail net buying [c47][c48][c49]. Buyers put $42.5mn into six long 2x Nvidia funds from Monday to Wednesday, a 99.8th percentile reading and the fifth largest three-day inflow on record, while inverse funds took in nothing despite heavy turnover [c50][c51][c52]. The only comparable stretch of leveraged demand was the DeepSeek decline in January 2025 [c170][d7].

### Three In A Row Moves The Path

US core consumer prices rose 0.3% from the prior month in October, the third month in a row at that pace, and the strength was broad rather than the usual volatile items, with owners' equivalent rent, car insurance and recreation services all firm [c252][c253]. Core inflation is 2.8% from a year earlier and the three-month annualised rate is near 4% and pointing up [c256]. Goldman estimates the report implies a 35 basis point (one hundredth of a percent) rise in core PCE, the inflation measure the Fed targets, and has added a third quarter-point hike in March 2027, which lifts the peak policy rate to 4.25% to 4.50% and pushes the first cut back to December 2027 [c291][c242][c243]. Markets fully price a quarter-point December hike with 15% odds of a double move, and the 2-year Treasury yield rose about 20 basis points on the print [c237][c284], which CA-CIB reads alongside last Friday's hot jobs report as essentially locking that hike in [c235]. Waller said a 50 basis point move should be considered at a 0.3% core print and that going 25 risks Fed credibility [c244][c245], though Goldman expects most voters to resist and sees the December projections signalling the third hike instead, which needs only two participants to move their forecasts [c236][c246][c250].

### The Floor Under Volatility Is Rising

Ten-year Treasury yields came down more than 40 basis points from early-October highs to around 5% after the hot inflation print, and Brent fell more than $20 a barrel from last month's high, yet both sit well above their June lows, so the repricing of macro risk has not reversed [c285][c265][c216]. The VIX has stopped falling around 20 after sliding to roughly 14 in August, and the MOVE index of bond volatility has held near 90, the 85th percentile of the past year, having just ended a 32-session run above that level on November 9, the fifth longest since 2020 [c293][c294][c297]. Bloomberg's Frank Monkam argues the equity drop after the midterms, a five-standard-deviation move, arrived with headline inflation estimated above 4% from a year earlier, so the Fed flexibility that cushioned past declines is less available and an administration past its election has less incentive to answer weakness, which challenges both supports equities have leaned on since early 2025 [c279][c259][c240][c269][c280][d17]. Speculative positioning in oil and Treasuries near year-to-date lows argues for stabilisation, but exhausted positioning is not the same as fundamentals repricing [c292][c295]. Realized S&P 500 volatility is set for its largest weekly gain this year, which is the signal that the year-long pattern rewarding volatility sellers has changed [c300][c302].

### Permits Before Picks

Citadel Securities argues the politics of the AI buildout, not demand for it, now decides who earns from it [d14]. Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has committed to a moratorium on data center construction until stronger protections are legislated, while Pennsylvania's Shapiro administration offers faster and more certain permitting to projects that meet standards on electricity affordability and community benefits [c217][c194]. The base case is longer delays, tougher conditions and selective resumption, with some projects relocated or abandoned [c218]. Holding back new capacity raises the value of compute already running and the hurdle for money committed to future delivery, which is the case for owning hyperscalers with secure power, financial flexibility and several ways to monetise over capacity still waiting on permits and financing [c197][c195]. Hyperscalers beat semiconductor stocks this week, and a Wednesday note described rotation toward a peak AI capex theme [c196][c219]. Slower delivery of powered buildings also leaves component makers facing deferred orders and excess inventory even while usable compute stays scarce [c220].

### Porting Costs Are Falling Fast

The second half of William Blair's downgrade has nothing to do with governance. The firm says CUDA's hold is weakening as frontier work runs through framework layers such as PyTorch, JAX and vLLM, with coding agents taking over the rewriting: Anthropic told its IPO roadshow that bringing up a new accelerator historically took six to twelve months, and its Claude Mythos Preview brought up prompt processing on AMD's MI355X in roughly three days, writing or porting almost all the kernels itself [c136][c137][c138]. Six million CUDA developers still create real stickiness, but William Blair expects that to fade as inference takes a larger share of the work [c69][c139]. On hardware, SemiAnalysis argued Google's Ironwood chip matches or beats Blackwell systems on tokens per dollar for agent-style inference, Anthropic alone could deploy about 5GW of Google's TPU v8i in 2027, and AMD's Helios racks now carry gigawatt-scale commitments from OpenAI, Meta and Anthropic [c134][c133][c135]. Wedbush pushes the other way, saying Google cannot be the primary outside supplier because it competes with its own biggest customers and that custom accelerators have produced more failures than successes, naming AMD first and Cerebras second as the beneficiaries of any Nvidia stumble [c193][c229][c185][c215].

### Europe's Core Keeps Climbing

Preliminary eurozone core inflation printed 2.54% from a year earlier in October, 8 basis points above CA-CIB's own 23 October preview of 2.46%, and the beat was not all noisy items such as hotels and clothing [c199][c200]. CA-CIB expects core to keep rising to 2.6% at year-end and 2.8% to 2.9% in early 2027, even with energy prices easing lately [c201]. Higher gas and oil prices and a weaker euro against the dollar all point to upward revisions in the ECB's own core forecasts, with tighter financial conditions the only real offset [c287]. The firm's 2027 average of 2.9% sits 30 basis points above the ECB's 2.6% projection published in September, which makes the next set of ECB forecasts on 17 December the number to watch [c288][c289]. Market-implied inflation has converged to CA-CIB's own forecast recently, probably on lower energy prices [c230].

### The Growth Gap Pays The Dollar

The US economic surprise index has climbed steadily since late summer to its highest level since August 2023, while the euro zone equivalent has declined and turned negative for the first time since July [c202][c222]. That gap, plus a Fed leaning toward more hikes, has lifted the dollar index to its highest since March 2025 [c286], and Raymond James still prefers US equities on relative growth while expecting fewer hikes than the market prices [c278][c247]. Anthropic's IPO, targeting a market value up to $1.5 trillion and reportedly several times oversubscribed, is reviving appetite for AI names after the post-election wobble, against consensus 2027 earnings growth of 42% for tech versus 16% for the S&P 500 [c203][c204][c205]. The same bid runs through assets with no earnings at all, with auction sales at Christie's, Sotheby's and Phillips up almost 70% from a year earlier to $6.8 billion in the first half of 2026, the strongest start since 2022 [c206][c207]. The cost side at home is barely improving, with the Mortgage Bankers Association's 30-year rate slipping a fourth straight week to 7.21% while still sitting about 90 basis points higher than at the start of the year [c231][c221].

### Saudi Barrels Against The Risk Premium

Saudi Arabia produced 10.6 million barrels a day in October, up 27% from September and about 3% below pre-war levels, which helps explain cheaper gasoline [c266][d16]. Hopes that the war with Iran is drawing to a close have weighed on crude, while new attacks on ships keep part of the risk premium in place, leaving Brent near $92 a barrel [c268][c264]. Saudi exports stay vulnerable to Houthi attacks, as happened in September [c263]. Raymond James carries a 12-month WTI target of $65 a barrel that depends on a US-Iran deal [c267]. Goldman says prices have fallen far enough since the elections that the bulk of the energy pass-through to inflation is now behind us [c262], which is the assumption inside its view that inflation is meaningfully lower by the end of 2027 [c255].
