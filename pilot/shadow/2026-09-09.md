# Friday's CPI Decides The Hike

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### One inflation print settles a Fed that has already changed its mind

August payrolls came in at 162,000 against a consensus of 55,000, with private payrolls up 127,000 and revisions adding another 55,000 to prior months [c799][c800]. Unemployment held at 4.1% because more people entered the workforce as hiring picked up [c1668]. Market-implied odds of a September increase jumped from about 50% to 60% on that number [c804]. Interest-rate futures now put a quarter-point rise at this month's meeting at 63% [c1639]. That leaves Friday's consumer price report as the only thing standing between the market and a rate rise, and Goldman's European sales desk reads it as binary: a mild print lets the Fed wait, a hot core reading likely seals the move [c1489][c1490][d8].

The banks are not split on the data, they are split on what a hawkish Fed chair does with it. UBS flipped from no change through 2026 to two quarter-point increases in September and December, taking the funds range to 4.00-4.25% [c805][c806][d46]. BofA is further out, forecasting 75 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent) of increases this year and expecting August inflation to be firm enough to force September [c901][c898][d1]. Deutsche Bank argues bond markets still underprice what is coming, pointing at the ISM services prices-paid survey at a four-year high, a level that last time lined up with consumer prices above 5% [c925][c927][c928][d33]. Against all three, Goldman's house forecast is still no increase [c1047][c1459][d36], and Morgan Stanley's economists also forecast a hold, calling it a close call because they expect core inflation to slow through the back half of the year [c1663][c1206][d35].

What decides it is a number, not a narrative. JPMorgan's economists read Governor Waller as setting the bar at core PCE, the inflation gauge the Fed actually targets, of 0.30% for the month and 2.8% over the year [c1637][d19]. Goldman's Treasury desk puts the line between a rise and a hold at about 25 basis points of core PCE [c1414]. The forecasts sit under both. Goldman Sachs models core consumer prices at 0.23% for August [c1548]. That translates to a 0.22% monthly rise in core PCE [c1554]. BofA has core consumer prices at 0.22% [c1273] and core PCE at 0.24% [c1274]. JPMorgan's desk says there will be a time to get aggressively long and that the disciplined move is to wait for the print [c1635].

## 3. BRIEFS

### The 30-year is the problem, and AI is helping build it

The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.79%, just under last week's peak and the highest since October 2023 [c982]. The 30-year yield's one-month average is above 5.2% for the first time in earnest since 2003 [c1461], and Goldman's rates strategists call yields near 5% at that end of the curve reasonable given policy uncertainty, energy-driven inflation and the reduced hedging value of bonds [c1005]. Part of the pressure is corporate rather than federal. The five hyperscalers plus NVIDIA have issued roughly $320 bn this year including vehicles where they backstop data center leases [c1142]. JPMorgan converts that to $303 bn in ten-year equivalents [c1143], which equals 68% of all new long-dated Treasury borrowing this year [c1144][d3]. Deutsche Bank reads the same yields as normalisation rather than stress, putting fair value at 4.8% if nominal growth settles at 5.5% [c1289][d2]. BofA gives the falsifiable test this week: if Treasury does not buy back near the maximum, long-dated yields cheapen further [c902].

### Brent touched $100 and the curve stopped believing in a reopening

Brent reached $100 a barrel for the first time since July 24 [c158], after US forces destroyed five Iranian tankers [c455], Saudi Arabia halted operations at several energy sites following attacks [c453], and explosions hit near Kharg Island, Iran's main export terminal [c454]. Tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is the lowest since May [c587], and the US strategic reserve is down to about 287m barrels, a much smaller cushion than in past shocks [c590][d8]. The six-month Brent contract rose 1.52% to $84.78, which Deutsche Bank reads as growing doubt that prices revert over coming months [c163][c164][d30]. European gas hit 75.80 euros per megawatt hour with storage only 67% full against 79% a year ago [c459][c460][c461]. Morgan Stanley raised its fourth-quarter forecasts to $100 Brent and 88 euros gas [c169][c789], and its equity strategists favor energy shares as the hedge [c354].

### Copper's record high is a tariff story wearing a growth costume

Copper made a new all-time high above $14,500 on the London exchange, helped by renewed US tariff risk and a physically tight market [c30]. US warehouses took in a record 225,000 metric tons in July as buyers front-ran the decision [c34]. The tariff report is now about two months overdue, and every week it stays unpublished keeps that buying alive [c33]. On the other side of the ocean, 51% of London warrants are cancelled with 121,000 tonnes earmarked to leave [c35]. Goldman expects mine supply to fall 0.7% this year [c26] and reads its projected refined surplus as dependent on scrap use rising nearly 5%, so an EU restriction on scrap exports would erase it [c45][c27][d12]. Goldman is Neutral at $14,490 and forecasts $13,735 in three months [c40], while JPMorgan keeps copper in the supercycle bucket on constrained supply and secular demand [c717]. A softer tariff decision unwinds the front-loading [c42].

### Nobody is paying for protection into a week that could reprice the Fed

Friday brought the lowest cash equity volume session of the year and the lowest intraday VIX print at 13.8 [c1455][c225]. Realized correlation across S&P constituents over the past six months sits at 11 [c188]. That has happened only twice in 25 years, in February 2007 and in January 2018 just before the volatility blow-up [c1466][d36]. The options market is not braced for much either. The S&P straddle for Friday's inflation session is priced at 100 basis points [c187], and the MOVE index of bond market volatility closed at 73, the 14th percentile over five years [c1462]. Deutsche Bank's read is that complacency is high exactly as the seasonal window for volatility opens, with the low point in VIX seasonality days away [c940][c939][d33]. Goldman's Pasquariello answers both the calendar and the debt math the same way, by collecting cheap convexity while it is still cheap [c1512][d9].

### The corporate bid steps aside in six days

Hedge funds are not carrying this market. US fundamental net leverage sits in the 4th percentile over one year [c1523], with gross at 208.5%, the 27th percentile, and net at 48.4% [c1526][c1527]. Trend-following funds sold $5.5bn of global equities last week, cutting length to about $139bn [c1100], and equity fund inflows slowed to $2.8bn, the weakest in two months, with US funds losing $5.9bn [c960][c961][d11]. What has held the bid is companies buying their own stock. Goldman's buyback desk ran at 1.4 times the 2025 average daily volume in the week to September 4 [c498], with flow concentrated in technology, financials and health care [c1384]. That support steps back around September 15, when roughly 40% of the S&P 500 enters its quiet period [c1386][c1387][d43]. Goldman's own sentiment gauge turned negative at -0.1, its first negative reading in 23 weeks, which the desk flags as possible contrarian support [c1102][c625].

### The momentum trade broke over the summer and conference week is the repair test

Goldman's technology momentum pair printed its worst two months on record and has halved since June [c1087][c1088][d9]. JPMorgan's version tells the same story, with its pure momentum index down more than 21% from the June high and the classic version down nearly 34%, both outside the typical 10-15% drawdown [c1178][c1179][c1180][d18]. Semiconductor implied volatility has collapsed from roughly 65 in July to 36, so the fear premium in chip options is gone [c554]. JPMorgan's read is that the near-term risk to the AI trade is positioning rather than demand [c1647]. Goldman's desk trades the crowding rather than the fundamentals: the ten most in-demand technology names have historically beaten the sector in the week after its conference opens [c501], expressed through the equally weighted GSCBTC10 basket, either outright or via 30 September 105-115% call spreads at 1.68% [c502][c503][d27].

### Oracle reports into a stock that already priced the balance sheet

Oracle reports fiscal first-quarter results on Thursday [c18]. The shares are down 25% since the last set of numbers, which BofA attributes mainly to investor worry about data center profitability as component costs rise and credit spreads widen [c19]. Cloud infrastructure revenue grew 93% over the prior year in the last quarter, so the demand side is not the argument [c5][d4]. BofA's Tal Liani likes the risk and reward into the print, arguing consensus already reflects the balance sheet but not the chance that revenue growth reaccelerates as buildout milestones land, with a $240 price objective [c1][c2]. The bear case is the leverage itself: debt to equity of 500% against 50% at Amazon and 30% at Microsoft, and both the stock and its credit spreads have already sold off sharply since that call was made [c8][c9][d5]. What matters Thursday is how fast contracted backlog converts to revenue and what the funding plan looks like [c20].

### The chip shortage everyone stopped worrying about gets worse next year

JPMorgan met 22 companies at Semicon Taiwan and came back with the opposite of the consensus easing story. Unfulfilled demand for TSMC's 3-nanometer process is close to 600k wafers this year and widens in 2027 [c213]. NVIDIA's Rubin and Vera ramp alone takes 55-60% of 2027 capacity on that node against under 10% in the first half of this year [c99], and the custom processor programs from Google, Amazon and Microsoft all sit on the same node [c72]. TSMC is adding roughly 40% more capacity there and it does not close the gap [c214]. The overflow pushes packaging spending out to the subcontractors: TSMC's advanced packaging reaches 190-200k wafers a month by end-2027 [c221], while the leading outsourced assemblers get to about 100k [c236]. The trade JPMorgan actually names is equipment, with GPTC, Chroma, ASMPT and Hon Precision as top picks [c757][d24].

### The midterm outcome the options market is not pricing

BofA's Michael Hartnett argues November is not a regime-change election in the mold of 1980 or 2016, so the populism and fiscal excess of this decade continue whoever wins [c1262]. A Republican sweep is full risk-on and the conventional split result is modestly risk-on [c371][c372]. The tail is a Democratic sweep, which BofA's prediction-market snapshot puts at 50% odds given a 35-40% approval rating for the president [c1259][c1260]. That outcome means stocks down more than 10%, lower US yields and a weaker dollar, hedged through short financials and short dollars [c882][c883][d37]. S&P options are pricing a 0.82% move after the election [c185], which is why the desk prefers cheap convexity to outright index protection: sell the October 16 VIX put and buy the November 22-30 call spread [c224]. For context, since 1990 the index has risen 5.4% in the three months after midterm day [c712].
