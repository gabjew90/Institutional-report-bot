# Bonds Flash Red, Stocks Shrug

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The bond market's warning that nobody is trading

A 5-year Treasury auction cleared at 5.033% last week, the first time that maturity has priced with a 5-handle since 2007, and it came with the second-largest tail on record and the weakest bid-to-cover since 2018, per BofA [c443, c444]. The 10-year yield briefly rose above 5.3%, its highest since 2004 [c446], the 30-year moved well above 5.6% [c447], and the MOVE index, which tracks how much Treasury investors expect prices to swing, spiked above 100 [c448]. Equities ignored all of it. The Nasdaq rallied more than 3% on the week [c1578] and set a new all-time high [c1632], while the equal-weight S&P 500 fell for a seventh straight week [c1671] and only 23% of index members sat above their 50-day average price [c1702].

BofA has a name for the split. It calls this the AI put: the supply of investor attention is roughly fixed, AI growth stories keep winning that attention away from macro risk, and the result is aggressive dip-buying regardless of what rates do [c713, c714, d2]. The precedent BofA reaches for is the 1990s, when long-end rates rose 200 basis points, one basis point being one hundredth of a percent, and the Fed hiked 100 basis points while the internet bubble kept inflating [c683].

The two tier-one desks split on what the bond market is actually saying. BofA's read is that bonds are flashing red and the equity market is not listening [c889], and it goes further, calling the AI narrative the market's single point of failure [c890], with a downward re-pricing of AI growth expectations the thing that would turn macro stress into real equity risk [c885]. Goldman's Pasquariello takes the other side, treating higher rates as a headwind rather than a wrecking ball absent an inflation surge or a credit seizure [c1787]. His evidence sits in the earnings line. The S&P's forward price-to-earnings multiple compressed from 23 times to 19 times over the past year while the index held its range near 7700 [c1700, c1758].

Goldman's own rule says the speed of the move settles this, not the level. Equities can absorb a rates move until it reaches two standard deviations in a month, roughly 50 basis points today, and Pasquariello says that threshold was just triggered [c501]. The real 10-year yield is up 57 basis points over the past month [c503], and asset managers liquidated another $5.5 billion of S&P futures as real yields jumped 25 basis points [c511]. Privorotsky's checklist for adding equity risk is three items: rates calming, credit stopping its widening, and a clearer energy resolution [c1073]. Credit is the one to watch, because it widened last week even as oil fell [c484].

## 3. BRIEFS

### France is where the rates shock lands first

France is where higher global rates have found something to break. The 10-year gap between French and German government bond yields pushed through 140 basis points, the widest since the 2012 euro crisis [c1438], and France's 10-year yield reached about 4.92% to 4.95%, its highest in roughly two decades [c1437]. France's budget calls for a record EUR340 billion of bond issuance next year [c1439]. Goldman says the latest leg of the widening is driven by political and budget risk premium rather than fresh French fiscal deterioration [c1308], and the move is spilling into Italian bonds [c1309]. BofA puts fair value for that spread at 100 to 110 basis points [c1276] and cut its French growth forecast to 0.3% this year and 0.6% next [c892]. The open question Goldman poses is whether Europe, growing near 1%, can carry the cost of capital the US investment cycle is setting globally [d53]. Budget talks that keep the 2027 deficit near the government's 5% target would settle it [c1446].

### AI's borrowing is becoming a credit story

The money funding the AI buildout is shifting from equity to debt, and the debt is starting to misbehave. Goldman's credit desk puts 2026 hyperscaler bond issuance at $250 billion with $230 billion already done [c1364], raised its 2027 forecast to $420 billion from $400 billion [c1310], and expects 35% of next year's hyperscaler capital spending to be debt funded [c1365]. Hyperscalers are only 40% of the more than $575 billion of global AI-related debt issued this year [c1367], and the borrowing is getting shorter, with the 0 to 5 year bucket rising from 29.9% to 46.4% [c1370]. The stress is showing at the bottom of the credit stack. CCC-rated spreads hit 1,007 basis points, the first reading above 1,000 since the March 2023 bank scare [c1357], and Oracle's 5-year credit default swaps hit a record near 227 basis points while its 2056 bonds yield above 8% [c161, c162]. Goldman's Privorotsky calls the circularity in AI financing undeniably increasing but not today's problem [c1382], which puts the weight on Q3 return-on-capital evidence from the hyperscalers [c1035].

### The jobs print bought the Fed time

September payrolls rose 29,000 against a 90,000 estimate, with the prior two months revised down by a combined 60,000 and unemployment up to 4.2% [c1197, c1198, c1199]. Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% on the month [c1216]. The shortest maturities took the hint, with the 2-year Treasury yield ending the week 3 basis points lower at 4.83% [c583]. Futures-implied odds of an October hike collapsed to 15% from almost 75% a week earlier while December held near 85% [c631, c675], which makes this a postponement rather than a turn. Goldman's economists moved their next and final hike to December [c604], and BofA is the holdout, still expecting hikes at both remaining meetings this year and saying it will revisit that call after the September inflation data [c601, c594]. The September CPI report on October 14 decides whether October comes back into play [c691].

### Everyone is already positioned for the pain

Goldman's prime data puts US hedge fund net positioning in the 0th percentile [c2033] and fundamental long/short net leverage at 46.4%, a third straight weekly decline and the 2nd percentile over five years [c2035]. Non-dealers have sold $63.7 billion of S&P 500 futures over six weeks, and more than 70% of that was long liquidation rather than new shorts [c2039, c2040]. Citadel Securities' Rubner has flipped bullish on exactly that setup, pointing to US equity CTA positioning swinging from plus 2.35 standard deviations at the end of August to minus 0.80, a move of more than three standard deviations in a month [c1979], and to $1.3 trillion of authorized buybacks coming back as blackout windows reopen from October 15 [c1875, c1874]. Goldman's volatility desk likes outright QQQ calls out to November [c205]. Both sides name the same trigger, a reversal in real yields [c417].

### Oil near $100 with a clock on it

Brent traded above $102 late Friday [c1512] even though Goldman estimates Persian Gulf exports have recovered to 23.6 million barrels a day, just above their 2025 average, leaving the global market roughly balanced [c1563, c1517]. Goldman's base case still has Brent easing to $85 by year-end and $80 in 2027 [c1533], and the G7 agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel [c1541]. What keeps the price up is the risk of another disruption hitting a thinner inventory cushion. Citadel Securities frames Iran's position as a long oil call expiring around the US midterms, with the leverage worth more before the vote than after, so the rational move may be to monetize it by reopening Hormuz in exchange for ending the war [c2169, c2172]. SEB reads the path differently, arguing renewed US strikes are more likely to provoke large-scale Iranian retaliation against regional energy infrastructure than to produce a deal, which makes October volatile [c2182, c2203].

### Meta's agent is doing the index's work

Deutsche Bank raised its Meta price target to $820 from $750, moving to 25 times its FY27 earnings estimate from 23 [c6]. The case is distribution speed. Muse, Meta's AI agent, became the number one app in the iOS productivity category with a 4.9-star rating across 38,000 ratings [c7], and drew more than 3 million US installs in its first 16 days against the roughly 35 days ChatGPT took to reach that pace [c8, c9]. Deutsche Bank reads the generous free tier as a user-acquisition phase, seeing little incentive to upgrade to the paid tiers yet [c48], and models Muse at roughly mid-single-digit percent of Meta's 2030 revenue against $25 to $50 of capital spending per user [c20, c50]. Amazon still blocks Muse from shopping on its site [c53]. Goldman has Meta up 25% over the past month on the agentic-AI thesis [c35], so the launch of Watermelon, Meta's most advanced model, is the next catalyst for the stock [c25].
