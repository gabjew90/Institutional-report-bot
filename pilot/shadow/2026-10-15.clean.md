# Hot CPI, Credit Cracks

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The cost of money, not the demand for chips

The S&P 500 fell 111 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent) to close at 7,718 on Wednesday, its first daily move of more than 1% in 53 days. The Nasdaq 100 fell 202 basis points to 29,950 and the Russell 2000 fell 193 basis points to 2,729, now 11% below its peak and in a correction. The damage sat where borrowing costs bite. Goldman's Global NeoClouds basket fell 10.93%, CoreWeave fell 15.5% as its 2032 unsecured bonds moved into distress, and the SOXX semiconductor exchange-traded fund fell for a sixth straight day, a streak Goldman's desk called uncommon.

The money side explains the shape of it. Oracle's cost of insuring its debt against default rose 31bps. Data center borrowers paid 7bps more and the big cloud providers 4-5bps more. The weakest corporate borrowers, rated CCC, sit around 1,250bp over safe debt after widening since September, and some of that is one-off distress in legacy media, cable and building products rather than anything about computing. Goldman's Privorotsky argues the binding constraint on AI spending is now the cost and availability of capital rather than demand, with huge sovereign deficits and the need to finance AI build-out through 2028 competing for the same pool of money. He stays bullish on the technology itself and expects the owners of scarce compute and scarce capacity to win.

JPMorgan is on the other side of the trade, reiterating a tactically bullish view into earnings season. It sees a possible chance to buy the dip even if funds keep cutting exposure into the big tech catalysts, and its positioning work puts US equity exposure at a neutral 46th percentile against about the 19th near the late-July low. It still likes technology as a core long and has dropped the Russell 2000 short that used to sit against it, citing squeeze risk if oil or yields fall.

Credit decides this, not price. If the cost of money keeps rising for the lowest-rated borrowers and the stress broadens, Goldman's case is that the implications reach small businesses, consumers and the wider economy, not just AI, and it points to a few AI-linked names where the cost of default insurance has jumped as the place to watch. If instead BB and single-B spreads stay as orderly as they have been and semiconductor earnings beats start getting paid, which Goldman's desk doubts this quarter, the dip is the right read.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Core inflation reaccelerates and UBS adds a hike

Core CPI rose 0.33% month-over-month in September, above Goldman Sachs's 0.24% estimate, the 0.2% median forecast and the prior month's 0.29%. Owners' equivalent rent, the rent a homeowner would pay themselves, rose 0.35% on the month and contributed 12 basis points to core CPI, about 5 basis points more than Goldman Sachs expected. Core goods inflation rose 0.25% on the month, more than triple its 0.07% three-month average. Core services inflation rose 0.37%, up from 0.33% in August and well above its 0.20% three-month pace. Goldman raised its September estimate for core PCE, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, to 0.32% from 0.20% before the release, and JPMorgan's Reinhart has it at 0.31%. UBS answered by adding a third hike, September and December 2026 plus March 2027, while Goldman and JPMorgan both still expect the second one in December, against market pricing of nearly five in total. For UBS, back-to-back upside surprises make an October move a close call.

### Energy did the work in the headline

Headline CPI rose 0.65% month-over-month, against Goldman Sachs's 0.59% estimate and the 0.6% median. Energy contributed nearly 40bps of the 65bps monthly gain. Gasoline prices surged roughly 9.5% m/m amid Middle East supply concerns. Higher oil and jet fuel costs pushed airfare inflation up 1.2% m/m. Crude was bid up 100bps in the morning, Brent at $106 and WTI at $95, after a flare-up in Middle East attacks. European gas took out its highs this week and sits at 86, which Goldman treats as an inflation problem and increasingly an economic one as fuel costs filter through. The IEA warned diesel stays constrained well into 2027, and Saudi escalation against the Houthis keeps the risk of retaliation against energy infrastructure open, though the latest attack hit Aramco's corporate headquarters rather than oil assets.

### The yield reversal nobody expected

The 5-year, 10-year and 30-year Treasury yields all breached their 2007 highs in the morning. Then the move unwound: the 2-year yield spiked nearly 16bp after the print before closing up 2bp, with the 10-year down 2bp and the 30-year down 4bp. Fast money and real money accounts were better buyers of Treasuries across maturities as stocks sold off. Goldman's research team argues yields are elevated against macro fundamentals, positioning is underweight and technical risks have faded, and the firm recently shifted to overweight bonds in its 12-month allocation while still favoring equities over bonds near and medium term. The cost of yields staying here is about 0.5 percentage points off US growth next year on Goldman's estimates, against a 0.2 point drag in its 2027 baseline that more than doubles if rates persist. Tomorrow's PPI report and Chairman Warsh's IMF speech in Bangkok are what Goldman says will set the timing of the next hike.

### Higher rates are paying for quality

Goldman's Quality Pair Index is up 20% over the last three months, a 12-month high reached on about 13% realized volatility, meaning how much it actually swung. Most of that came from the short leg selling off, not the longs. The factor has now risen in 16 of the last 18 sessions and its RSI reads 83, a level Goldman calls stretched. Because quality runs strongly negative to AI, momentum and beta, Goldman uses it as the hedge against those exposures, favors quality and defensives over cyclicals and shorts low-quality equities to express the same rates view. Momentum positioning sits at the 78th percentile of its five-year range, which is the crowding the pair is short. Goldman names the reversal trigger itself: a softer CPI print or evidence that rates are peaking, and it flags a call option on the short-leg basket as the hedge against exactly that.

### Hedges are cheap into a loaded calendar

Goldman's Vol Panic Index reads 2.7 out of 10. Average three-month implied volatility across the Magnificent Seven, what option pricing says about expected swings, is approaching multi-year lows even as net exposure to the basket sits at the 100th percentile of the last five years. Goldman's read is that hedging those names through earnings and year end costs less than the risk warrants, and its desk is offering 15 January 2027 puts struck at 95% of spot on its Mag7 basket at an indicative 3.05%. Three-month implied correlation, the price the options market puts on stocks moving together, sits below the 2nd percentile of the last twenty years. The S&P 500's implied move through Friday is priced at only 0.76% with PPI, retail sales and earnings from about 7% of market value still due. Goldman also likes QQQ 6Nov26 755 calls at $6.23 against QQQ at 736.24 to carry the catalysts into November 6.

### Bank results gave the index no help

Bank of America fell 5.9% following scrutiny of its operational flexibility going forward. Its top line missed by 3% on fee income, off a base management had already guided lower at an investor conference, and bond, currency and commodity trading missed by 10% with equities 1.5% light. Overall trading revenue fell 5% year-over-year while every large peer grew. $MS fell 3.6% on a miss in income and wealth management, and $STT fell 6.3% on a top line hurt by servicing and currency trading. Money moved to safety instead: healthcare rose 2.44% and consumer staples rose 2.38%. Goldman's own franchise flows closed skewed 8.9% better for sale, with long-only clients at 19.5% better for sale and supply in communication services, macro products and technology.

### Industrials: an HVAC switch and a clean beat

Goldman upgraded TT to Buy and cut LII and CARR to Neutral. The estimate spread carries the call: its FY27 numbers sit 6% above consensus for JCI and 4% above for TT, against 4% below for LII and 3% below for CARR. The argument is that the residential heating and cooling market's structure may be changing, with competition rising and price increases harder to get, while the commercial side keeps the stronger order book. Backlogs are up about 50% on average this year for JCI and TT, and Goldman still expects demand there to hold. The scoreboard already reflects it, with JCI up 47% and LII down 33% over the past year. Elsewhere in the sector, AZZ beat fiscal Q2 earnings at $2.00 against $1.86 expected, beat on revenue at $487mm versus $463mm, and raised fiscal 2027 earnings guidance to $7-7.30 from $6.75-7.15.

### The quarter UBS is counting on

UBS forecasts 28% earnings per share growth for the S&P 500's September quarter, with semiconductor profits alone accounting for about half of that growth. It expects average cloud revenue growth of 58% year-over-year, up from 48% in the June quarter, and puts AI-related spending up 84% this year to USD 900bn. The broadening argument rests on the ISM manufacturing new-orders component, in expansion for nine straight months against a historical average run of about three years. On the rates risk, the 10-year Treasury yield has risen more than 1 percentage point this year, which UBS treats as manageable given a weighted average debt maturity of about 11 years for the index's non-financial debt. The call it actually makes is diversified positioning that combines cyclical and secular exposure, with select Asian tech favored where earnings visibility is strongest.
