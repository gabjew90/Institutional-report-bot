# Oil Blinked, Stocks Squeezed

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The rally came out of the oil market, not out of earnings

Saudi Arabia eased its supply problem on Thursday, bringing the East-West pipeline back online and moving more crude ship to ship, and US crude and Treasury yields fell together on the news. That was enough for the S&P 500 to reclaim its 50-day average price of 7615, one session after it stopped falling at the 100-day average of 7515, with the VIX back to 15 [c620] [c223] [c769]. The buying was not new conviction. Goldman's most-shorted basket rose about 425 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent), its momentum basket about 360 and its AI semis basket about 325, which is the shape of traders closing out bets against the market rather than putting fresh money to work [c687] [c683] [c684] [d6]. Thin liquidity made it easier: the stock quoted at the best price was $2.81 million, roughly half the five-day average, while volume ran 31% above the 20-day average [c727] [c749].

The flow trigger matters more than the headline. Coming into the session the index sat less than 1.1% below Goldman's short-term threshold for systematic trend-followers, and it rose that much on the day [c745]. Those funds hold only $37.54bn of US equities, the 22nd percentile of the past year, and Goldman's model had them selling $5.4bn of S&P futures in a flat market and $54bn in a falling one against buying $17bn in a rising one [c674] [c717] [c718] [c675]. Clearing the threshold removes the selling leg [d38].

Goldman's desk and BTIG disagree on what that buys. Goldman keeps MegaCap Tech as its high-conviction position and reads underweight investor positioning as a tailwind if macro news improves [c680] [c677]. BTIG answers with participation: 49% of S&P 500 members closed above their 200-day average price, the first sub-50% reading since April 2000 with the index within 4% of a record, and says strength is not being rewarded and the correction still needs less complacency and a higher-correlation decline before it is finished [c236] [c237] [c239] [d12].

The condition that settles this is the pipeline, not the Fed. Speculative Brent long positions sit at the 88th percentile of their two-year range, and GS's Quinn says that crowd is exposed to better supply news [c573] [c611] [d37]. If the repairs hold, oil and yields stay lower and the squeeze extends. If the squeeze runs while that 49% keeps falling [c236], the rally is systematic funds covering, not a turn.

## 3. BRIEFS

### The hike was priced, the next one is not

The unanimous move to 3.75-4.00% came with 16 of 18 officials projecting at least one more increase this year and the long-run rate estimate lifted from 3.06% to 3.25% [c384] [c345] [c349]. Goldman read that as reason to pull its next hike forward to October, arguing back-to-back moves fit the Committee's stated aim of a faster return to 2% better than an every-other-meeting pace [c352] [c353]. JPMorgan kept December [c392], Deutsche Bank wants December and March [c323], and Rabobank still thinks one and done is likelier than two [c407]. Markets split the difference at 51% odds for October and 78% for December [c355] [c356]. The figure that decides it is Goldman's own core PCE forecast, the Fed's preferred inflation measure stripping out food and energy, at 3.2% for this year against the Committee's 3.4% [c828] [d35].

### The AI build is now a bond-supply question

JPMorgan has US technology net bond issuance at roughly $335bn this year against $140bn last year, with the US and European increase together near $260bn [c802] [c804]. Measured against global supply that is small. The model puts the effect on world bond yields at 10 to 15 basis points, and 5 to 10 once rate expectations are stripped out, which is why JPMorgan says the build justifies higher yields but does not justify betting against bonds [c537] [c538] [d5]. The livelier signal is that default insurance on AI-linked borrowers has repriced further than option prices on those same companies' shares [c808]. JPMorgan's trade is to buy that mismatch rather than short the story, through three-to-six-month Nasdaq 100 put spreads, and to prefer AI winners that do not have to spend heavily to stay in the race [c17] [c225].

### A year of 33% earnings growth that buys nothing

Earnings are not the problem in BofA's numbers, the discount rate is. It lifted 2026 S&P 500 earnings per share to $365, up 33%, and sees $410 next year [c218] [c219], yet its year-end index target of 7,400 and 12-month target of 7,800 implied about 3% downside and 2% upside at the levels prevailing when it published [c266] [c267]. The gap is inflation. The equity valuation BofA observes is consistent with inflation near 1.7%, while its own economists forecast 3.2% this year and 2.4% next [c656] [c643]. Concentration sharpens it. The five largest companies are a record 27% of next-twelve-month index earnings [c244], and semiconductors supply 9.6 of the 15.4 percentage points of expected 2027 earnings growth [c245]. BofA's own target is the call, and it prices a boom that pays the holder nothing [d9].

### Crude is trading missing barrels, not mood

From August 4 to September 8 speculative accounts added $9.3bn of Brent long positions, 70% of it closing out bets against the price, taking net length to the 88th percentile of its two-year range as November Brent rose 25.7% [c571] [c572] [c573] [c574]. Brent then added 11.1% into September 15 on Houthi advances and the Saudi pipeline closure, and gave back 2.7% when reports said half that capacity could return within days [c575] [c612] [c613]. Goldman puts 3.5 million barrels a day at risk from the outage and values a full year of that loss at $30-40 on the crude price, against just over $5 for a two-month loss [c584] [c587] [c588]. JPMorgan's commodities team says it has no baseline for the endgame for the first time since the Iran conflict began [c634]. A move back to $85-90 needs repairs, calm and weaker demand together, while $110-120 needs one more disruption [c626] [c603].

### The AI trade is splitting on who has to spend

Goldman's Data Centers basket rose 1.28% and its AI Semis basket 89 basis points on FOMC day, while Software At Risk fell 1.83% and AI-Productivity fell 2.19% [c190]. The month's flows run the other way, with software the most bought part of information technology and semiconductors the most sold [c672] [c711], and Pictet says semiconductor earnings revisions are rolling over after a long run higher [c174]. Inside chips the spread is extreme. Hedge fund crowding in analog names reached the 99th percentile with almost nobody short them [c691] [c692], and BTIG points out AMD, Qorvo and Skyworks are back near 52-week highs while Lam Research, Applied Materials and Western Digital sit 30-40% below their June highs [c23] [c22]. BTIG's call is to own the names making new highs rather than the laggards, because selection now carries the return [c217] [c264] [d4].

### Meta's agent knocked the marketplaces, and one desk says wrongly

Expedia fell 5.4%, Booking 9.0%, Shopify 8.9%, Uber 5.2% and DoorDash 3.5% on the fear that an AI agent books the trip, the ride and the dinner without anyone opening the app [c47] [c28] [c87] [c101] [c42]. Wolfe Research rates that fear overdone and the actual risk modest [c145]. The reasoning is subscriptions and structure. Uber has 50 million Uber One members who account for more than half of its bookings, DoorDash has over 35 million DashPass members, and neither platform has reason to hand economics to an agent for an order it was already going to get [c99] [c40] [d3]. On the other side of the same trade, Wolfe puts $30bn of medium-term subscription revenue on Muse, assuming 5% of Meta's 3 billion users pay $20 a month, and rates Meta Outperform with a $700 price target [c7] [c12].

### UK inflation is pulling the Bank of England the wrong way

August consumer prices rose to a five-month high of 3.1% while core held at 2.6% for a fourth month [c207] [c208], and 10-year gilt yields are at multi-year highs, up 115 basis points to 5.38% [c167]. Goldman still expects a hold this meeting on a 6-3 vote, but after running current energy prices through its model it added a November hike and now projects headline inflation approaching 4%, at 3.9% in the first quarter, the level its framework treats as where price and wage expectations start to move [c925] [c926] [c917] [c918]. UBS takes the other side, expecting a hold at 3.75% for the rest of the year and arguing market pricing overstates the chance of renewed tightening [c912] [c914]. Goldman's rates desk names the trigger: the market prices 50 basis points for November if the Bank signals conditions are not tight enough [c919].
