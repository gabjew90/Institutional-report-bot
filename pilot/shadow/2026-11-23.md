# The Unwind Turns Macro

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The AI selloff became a growth selloff

The selling that started in AI stopped being only about AI this week. The S&P 500 fell 8.6% and closed below 7,000 [c191], its worst week since April 2025 [c199], and the Nasdaq 100 fell 10.4%, its worst week since March 2020 [c192]. Friday was the S&P's seventh straight down session, a streak last seen in February 2020 and one of only five in 25 years [c198]. What changed is where the damage landed [d10]. The drawdown that began in October sat inside the AI complex, and over the past week it widened into a broader growth shock [c224]. The S&P 500 excluding AI is now 6% below its high [c225], and cyclical stocks, which had tracked defensives for most of the past month, lagged them by 5 percentage points over the past ten days [c227].

Goldman's read of the mechanism is money getting more expensive everywhere at once [d4]. A 100 basis point (one-hundredth of a percent) tightening in financial conditions at this speed takes roughly 50bp off growth over the following 12 months [c298], and comparable 100-150bp tightenings produced 10-20% S&P 500 drawdowns in 2018, 2023 and 2025, with the current move at the low end of that band [c211].

The two largest houses split on what that implies for the index. Goldman's downside case is roughly 5,800, the 30-year average multiple of 17 times applied to trend earnings of $340 [c222], and the reason it is a real case rather than an arithmetic exercise is that the AI investment boom has lifted S&P 500 earnings about 25% above their 30-year trend [c220]. JPMorgan reads the same selloff as an AI de-leveraging event alongside a more hawkish Fed being priced in, not a change in the underlying macro story [c557], stays constructive on risk assets into 2027 [c157], and expects the market to rebound as catalysts clear while holding quality growth and the big cloud companies building AI capacity [c57].

One level settles it. JPMorgan's base case holds the S&P above 6800-7000 [c159], and a sustained break would open 6150-6315, about a 20% correction from the peak [c230]. Goldman is waiting on one of three bottom signals, renewed confidence in AI earnings [c251], a dovish Fed signal [c334], or a large drop in investor positioning [c541], and the third has not happened: nearly $170 billion has flowed into US equity funds and ETFs since the start of the fourth quarter [c476], and asset manager futures length sits in the 82nd percentile of the past decade [c477]. Mechanical selling is still scheduled, with Goldman's trend-following models forecasting selling in every scenario next week [c469] and triggers at 7596, 7540 and 7119 [c470] after those funds sold $57 billion in a month to a $19 billion net short [c468]. Goldman's Wilson has already switched his hedge, preferring long bonds paired with long equities over this year's short-bond version [c573].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Three hikes where cuts used to be

JPMorgan now forecasts quarter-point hikes at the December, January and March meetings [c304], which would leave the target range at 4.50%-4.75% and more than fully reverse the 75bp of insurance cuts delivered between September and December 2025 [c305]. The October minutes guided to at least a 25bp December hike, with some members open to a larger one [c302]. Santander's Stephen Stanley added a March 2027 hike, making three quarter-point increases in roughly six months, and still expects a January skip rather than back-to-back moves [c319] [c326]. The size argument is live. Waller is advocating 50bp in December [c317] and wants last year's three insurance cuts reversed [c318], while Natixis does not expect 50bp but does expect a hawkish meeting that keeps January well priced [c336] [c314]. The market ended the week with 22bps priced into December [c332] and a 60bp peak by the middle of next year [c333]. Goldman's rates team thinks that path is too hawkish if current inflation pricing is borne out [c291].

### Funds have sold. Nobody agrees that it is enough.

Goldman's prime brokerage book shows beta-adjusted fundamental long/short net leverage down 8.8 points to 48.2%, a five-year low [c427], with the aggregate long/short ratio across the whole book at new 15-year lows [c463]. US single stocks were shorted every day of the week, the heaviest weekly shorting since June 2022 and third heaviest in a decade [c473]. Deutsche Bank's aggregate positioning measure sits 0.9 standard deviations below average, the 11th percentile and the lowest since May 2025 [c385], with systematic strategies at their biggest underweight in 18 months [c387], which it reads as close to levels positioning has typically bounced from [c348]. JPMorgan is not there: funds have cut net risk by a magnitude historically tied to a bounce [c549], but its US positioning metric's four-week change is only about -1 standard deviation against a -1.5 capitulation trigger [c553], and the rebound that usually follows has not arrived [c551]. Goldman's strategists are closer to JPMorgan, calling broad investor positioning still elevated [c475].

### The volatility shock and the relief signal

Deutsche Bank classes this as a volatility shock, a jump of more than 1.5 standard deviations, with implied volatility, realized volatility, intraday ranges, the inverted VIX futures curve and stock correlations all spiking into the top decile [c783] [c784]. In past shocks volatility took 6 to 8 weeks to subside [c795], and outside recessions the S&P 500 fell a further 14% to 16% [c785]. Goldman's equity volatility desk reads the same extremity the other way, with its panic index at 9.8 out of 10 [c787] and readings above 9.5 having historically signaled near-term relief [c782]. The raw numbers are stretched enough for either claim: VIX above 35 with an aggressively inverted curve [c786], the one-week move in VIX the fifth largest on record [c788], the Nasdaq VIX at its highest since Covid [c792], and the index closing more than 4 standard deviations below its 50-day average price, the most extreme reading since August 2015 [c182]. Options price a 3.27% move through November 27 [c797].

### Bonds started hedging stocks again

Treasuries rallied across the curve on Friday as money moved to safety [c568], two-year yields down 12bps, ten-year down 10bps and thirty-year down 7.5bps [c570] [c571] [c572]. That is a change in behaviour rather than one good day. Since the ten-year yield passed 5%, all eight daily S&P 500 declines of more than 1% have come with yields falling, by an average of five bps [c625], where from March through the summer the same declines pushed yields about five bps higher [c626]. Raymond James also has the inflation rate priced into ten-year bonds at its lowest since September 2024 [c578]. Goldman's rates desk is positioned for five-year yields to fall further than two-year yields, entered at 0bp with a 15bp target and a stop at -8bp [c566]. Natixis keeps ten-year yields in a 4.90%-5.25% range into the December meeting [c609] and will buy dips only above 5.20%-5.25% [c594]. Auctions this week total $183bn of two-, five- and seven-year notes [c627].

### Gold and copper broke, and the reason is contested

Gold fell 1.3% on Friday to about $3,575 an ounce, its lowest since September 2025 [c642], and is down nearly 8% on the week [c643], which Morgan Stanley puts inside its biggest drawdown since the 1980s [c641]. High grade copper futures fell more than 9% [c450], and three-month copper on the London exchange broke trend support at 13800-14050 [c99]. JPMorgan's cross-asset team reads both as a rates story, with the dollar and Fed repricing the common driver rather than the AI unwind [c637], and has gold trading below what its regression against one-year-forward one-year SOFR implies, so the fall may have overshot [c630]. It recommends long gold, copper, silver and platinum [c631]. Its own technical team is on the other side, with gold well below critical support at 3800-4000 [c639] and a sustained break there implying a measured move to the mid-$2,000s [c640]. UBS calls the metal oversold, warns against extrapolating the downtrend [c633], and keeps a USD 5,000 target for late 2027 [c635].

### The 2018 comparison has a short inside it

High Beta Cyclicals are up 12% year to date even with beta-adjusted hedge fund net exposure at five-year lows [c48], because growth optimism let money rotate out of AI into everything else instead of pricing equity downside [d13]. Goldman flags that basket as a short candidate if the fourth-quarter 2018 analog keeps playing out and growth downgrades follow [c76]. The arithmetic: 15 times forward earnings, a 40% premium to its 2018 trough multiple [c80], against a 23% fall in the fourth quarter of 2018 that left it 10 percentage points behind the S&P 500 [c78]. Goldman's preferred long against that short is its high versus low free cash flow pair [c49]. JPMorgan would also fade the cyclical bounce, reading it as a product of the AI washout rather than a change in the macro story and wanting confirmation of a patient Fed before staying with it [c229], and says the semiconductor positioning selloff is not over [c485].

### Where the AI bill shows up next

Credit intermediation is JPMorgan's named pressure point, because elevated asset prices rest on earnings expectations that increasingly depend on access to capital markets [c278], and how far this adjustment derails the upturn in business credit is, in its words, the clearest signal of a building growth drag [c277]. Something is moving there already. Goldman's 30-year basket of big cloud company bonds is 15bps wider over the past week [c269] and closed Friday at its widest of the year [c31], while the cost of insuring investment grade bonds against default rose 2.53bps and the high yield equivalent fell 45 cents [c265] [c266]. The broad market has absorbed the supply, with more than $190bn of investment grade issuance this month and over $2tn this year [c287] [c288] against spreads holding between 86 and 92bps [c289]. JPMorgan forecasts $2.1tn of high grade supply in 2027 [c284], including $225bn from the big cloud companies and $225bn from data centers and GPU financing [c285], and high yield maturities rising from $56bn in 2027 to $138bn in 2028 [c273]. Raymond James favors higher quality bonds into year end at index yields near 6% [c263].

### Cheap, or cheap against boom earnings

Raymond James calls the decline technically overdone: third-quarter earnings grew 31% year over year [c165], the forward price-to-earnings ratio (the index price divided by expected earnings) is the lowest since 2023 [c166], the 14-day momentum reading is 31 and close to oversold [c167], and the year's worst drawdown of about 9% [c164] is still under the roughly 13% average annual maximum [c254]. Goldman takes the same multiple to a different place. It is down 25% this year to 16 times, near its historical average [c250], but measured against trend earnings it ranks in the 83rd percentile since 1990 [c221], because the AI boom lifted earnings about 25% above their 30-year trend [c220]. Goldman still forecasts 18% S&P 500 earnings growth for 2027 [c152] while noting only two prior runs of five straight years of double-digit growth, 1928 and 1999 [c212]. Deutsche Bank holds a $430 earnings forecast for 2027 and an 8000 target [c150] [c149], against pricing it reads as consistent with an outright earnings contraction and manufacturing surveys well below 50 [c185].
