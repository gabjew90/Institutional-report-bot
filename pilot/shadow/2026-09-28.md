# Bonds Broke, Stocks Didn't

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The bond market repriced growth, not inflation

The 10-year Treasury yield reached a post-2007 high of 5.20% and the 30-year hit 5.48%, its highest since 2004, with the two-day move in the 10-year of 23.7 basis points (one basis point is one-hundredth of a percent) the largest since last spring's Liberation Day turmoil [c1025][c1026][c1027]. Where the move came from decides how it ends. Ten-year real yields rose 9.8 basis points to a post-2008 high of 2.87% while the inflation rate priced into the bond market fell 1.3 basis points [c1030][c1198]. Across 2026 real yields are up 97 basis points against 9 for inflation expectations, and the extra yield investors demand for holding long maturities has been range bound for about 18 months, which argues against a deficit story [c1199][c1200][d54].

The selling is mechanical as well as macro. The five-year auction tailed 3.20 basis points on weak end-user demand and the five-year yield broke 5% [c1085][c1086]. Foreign and reserve-manager bidders took only 54% of that auction against 61.5% at the prior one [c997]. Treasury then accepted just $4.078bn of a $6bn buyback cap, 68% of it, and yields spiked to a new multi-decade high [c1187][d78][d1].

Here the two largest houses take opposite sides. BofA argues the policymaker panic to cap yields has already begun and tells clients to start buying bonds, projecting 10% on the five-year, 14% on the ten-year and 22% on the thirty-year if yields fall 100 basis points over the next year [c959][c960]. Goldman's Pasquariello says the opposite for anyone carrying significant equity length: pair it with a simple trading short in bonds [c1069]. Goldman's own economists still expect fewer hikes than the market prices, and Kaplan looks for one more move to roughly 4% to 4.25% and then a pause [c1634][c1635]. BofA wants hikes in October and December then a hold, against market pricing of nearly four [c1660][c1661][d46][d17].

The falsifiable condition is Goldman's own speed rule: equities begin reacting once the ten-year makes a two-standard-deviation move, about 50 basis points in a month or 30 in two weeks [c1202]. The ten-year is up about 25 basis points over two weeks and 35 over a month, just inside that line [c1037]. Cutting the other way, trend followers sit at 99% of maximum short in ten-year futures and 100% in thirty-year, roughly $390bn notional, so a cool inflation print forces a squeeze rather than a break [c1076][c1077][c1073][d43][d48].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Underneath the index, the average stock is broken

The S&P 500 sits within 2% of a 52-week high while fewer than half its members trade above their 200-day average, and 52-week lows have outnumbered highs for nine straight days [c1332][c1328][c1329]. BTIG finds only one prior window since 1990 with that combination, December 1999 into January 2000 [c1332]. In that analog the equal-weight index fell 16% while the cap-weighted index rose about 8%, then the two swapped roles and the index fell 17% while equal weight rallied 20% [c1386][c1333]. Goldman reads the same picture differently: the median S&P 500 stock is 16% below its own 52-week high and its sentiment gauge sits at -0.9, which it treats as room for laggards to catch up provided macro uncertainty does not accelerate [c1354][c2241][c1288]. Separately, 213 trading days have passed since the last session where 80% or more of NYSE volume was downside, against an average of 21 a year [c1387][c1388][d25][d81].

### What the AI build has to earn back

Goldman Sachs has put a number on the hurdle: the six largest US hyperscalers need a combined $1.42 trillion of revenue across 2028 to 2030, about $11.6 billion per gigawatt a year, to clear a 15% return on invested capital on their 2026-27 compute spending [c838][c846]. On a simpler breakeven basis the same desk puts the requirement near $300 billion of annual AI revenue [c810]. The three largest cloud providers already carry $1.69 trillion of booked backlog, so only about 59% of it has to convert [c110][c111]. Some of the doubt is priced: the median AI infrastructure stock now trades at 22 times forward earnings, down from 32 times in April [c667]. Morgan Stanley expects combined capex at the four biggest spenders to rise 57% in 2027, while Goldman models growth decelerating from nearly 100% in 2026 to 54% in 2027 and 12% in 2028 [c53][c652]. JPMorgan says a capex cut is the signal that would turn it bearish [c711][d28][d12][d20].

### Oracle's force majeure put a price on AI credit

Oracle's 2056 bonds hit a record 8.16% yield and its credit default swaps reached an all-time wide after it sent a force majeure notice on its New Mexico data center, citing permitting delays that leave it unable to power the site on schedule [c149][c150][c159]. That yield trades where B3/B- junk credits trade, and both major agencies already cut the company to one notch above junk in July, so a further downgrade would reprice a $120 billion bond stack [c151][c152][c154]. Deferring rent does not reduce what is owed: the contract obligates a carry cost in lieu of rent for up to three years, covering lenders' interest in full plus an equity return, and the power delivery risk sits with the tenant [c161][c162][c163]. Barclays calls the lease impact de minimis but still expects roughly $30 billion of hardware capex to slip [c167][c126]. The banks that wrote the $18 billion construction loan cannot syndicate it and are carrying more than $2 billion of paper losses [c158][c157][d26][d31].

### Muse is winning users and not yet dollars

Meta's Muse agent topped both US app stores with more than 902,000 downloads in its first six days, and the stock rose about 11% on Monday, its best single day since April 2025 [c14][c13]. The shares have run 45% in 25 trading days since the launch, while the basket of companies whose business depends on customers not switching has fallen 12% since it was created in late August [c1][c684]. Amazon has blocked the agent from its store and says Muse does not identify itself as an agent when it visits [c104][c65]. JPMorgan puts the eventual addressable market in the tens of trillions of dollars but does not expect monetization beyond subscriptions before 2027 [c47][c103]. Bear Traps expects the other large platforms to ship their own versions within six months, turning four free products into a contest over who can burn cash longest, and likes a put spread on the stock [c70][c622][c72][d18][d22].

### Diesel, not crude, is the inflation problem

Brent closed at $106.60 after touching $108.16 intraday on Iranian escalation warnings and a Houthi missile attack on Saudi Arabia [c1460][c1463][c1462]. The pressure point for the real economy is diesel rather than headline crude, at price levels that have historically echoed inflation spikes above 5% [c1575]. Retail diesel set a record near $6.50 a gallon, and the administration is weighing a 90-day export ban even though US fuel producers warn it raises prices later [c1600][c1601][c1543]. UBS calls the ban counterproductive because it does nothing about the global supply constraint behind the move [c1571]. Goldman Sachs is explicit that the bid in crude is not Chinese buying: China's imports are down 27% from a year ago, and its base case has Brent averaging $80 in 2027 with prices edging lower absent Middle East escalation [c1530][c1539][c1538]. Iran has offered a seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and Trump rejected the ceasefire that plan rests on [c1927][c1865][d83][d35].

### Everyone cut risk before the break

Hedge funds net sold global equities for the first time in five weeks, short sales outpacing long sales 1.6 to one, with gross leverage down to 308.0% and net leverage at 77.3%, the 30th percentile of the past year [c2231][c2229][c2230]. Asset managers cut index futures length from 48% of open interest in May to 36% [c2250]. Goldman's pension model points to $32 billion of US equities for sale into month end, the 95th percentile of its estimates over the past three years [c2206]. About 64% of S&P 500 companies had entered their buyback blackout as of 21 September, heading toward roughly 85% by week-end, which pulls back the market's largest price-insensitive bid just as trend followers are positioned to sell [c2273][c2274]. Goldman's panic index closed below 1 on Friday, so option pricing reflects none of the stress that bond volatility and credit indices are showing at peak-percentile levels [c1289][c2434][d85][d37][d48].
