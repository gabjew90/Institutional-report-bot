# Agents Sort The Market

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The Agent Trade Picks Its Losers

Meta's Muse reached the top of the US free iPhone chart within a week of its September 8 release, and the market has spent two sessions buying the computing that agents run on and selling the businesses whose customers stay put out of habit. Monday was the strongest day in seven weeks, with the S&P 500 up 1.43% (Deutsche Bank) to close at 7,764 (Goldman Sachs), and the Nasdaq up 2.26% with the Magnificent 7 basket up 3.44%, both at records (Deutsche Bank). The processor names led, with $ARM up 17.16%, $INTC up 12.17% and $AMD up 9.95% (Goldman Sachs), while $META rose 11.43% for its best day in 17 months and supplied roughly 15% of the index gain. Tuesday the index went nowhere and the split underneath showed. The Nasdaq 100 closed at a fourth straight record, the Dow ended at 51,863, and S&P financials fell 1.98% (Deutsche Bank).

The reasoning behind the selling is that agents move activity off human-facing screens and onto the plumbing underneath, so any business earning a return from friction, complexity or inertia is exposed while booking, payments and settlement gain volume. Goldman's Louis Miller is short the Consumer Inertia basket on exactly that logic, arguing agents compress the switching costs that used to be counted as a moat. That basket fell 2.58% on Tuesday, with $PLNT, $NYT, $SCHW, $ALL and $INTU among the worst names in it.

JPMorgan's O'Dwyer disputes the read on banks. He says there is very little in the Muse offering to suggest it disrupts the unit economics of the banking system, that the consensus names taking the volume ($C, $MS, $GS, $USB) look like a positioning unwind rather than a re-rating, and that he wants to fade the move, favoring $MS, $PNC and $FITB. Goldman's own financials desk lands closer to the middle, calling the selling an uncertainty discount while investors work through the fundamentals and reporting a complete buyers' strike with no sector defense.

What settles it is whether the inertia names keep falling after the compute names stop rising. JPMorgan turns bearish only on a consumer break or an AI capex cut, and the near catalysts are Meta Connect, OpenAI Dev Day on 9/29 and Micron earnings on 9/30 (Goldman Sachs).

## 3. BRIEFS

### One Hike Down, Nobody Agrees On The Rest

The FOMC raised the funds rate 25 basis points (each one is a hundredth of a percent) to 3.75-4.00%, its first increase in three years, and the vote was unanimous (UBS). Sixteen of 18 participants project at least one more this year (UBS), and Goldman expects it at the October 27-28 meeting. PNC goes further, looking for December and next March for three hikes in total, and says the balance of risk points to more rather than fewer. UBS takes the other side, expecting one more increase in December against market pricing of three more by the end of 2027. Deutsche Bank's case for the hawkish tail is that the tightening burden is not being shared, since US high-yield spreads near 270 basis points against above 400 in 2022 leave financial conditions looser now than when the last cycle began. It reads today's resilience in risk assets as room to hike further rather than a cycle near its end.

### The Biggest Bond Short In Four Years

Trend-following funds hold their largest bet against government bonds since April 2026, about $171 million of profit and loss for every one basis point move in global yields (Goldman Sachs). The concentration is Japanese and European rates, with shorts across the US curve including the five-year (Goldman Sachs). Equity positioning is no friendlier, with Goldman's sentiment gauge at -0.92 and the AAII bull-minus-bear reading very negative, which Goldman says raises squeeze risk into any positive catalyst. Rates have already sold off more than 80 basis points this year, so no rate cut is required. Yields simply stopping their rise, on stabilizing oil or a softer inflation print, would force the covering. Renewed highs in oil, in inflation expectations or in the five-year yield would end the thesis. Goldman's own expression is options that pay off if short-dated rates fall, where it says the entry point looks attractive.

### Oil's Bear Case No Longer Needs Peace

The physical shortage is mostly gone. Goldman puts the global deficit at roughly 1mb/d (millions of barrels a day) in the third quarter against about 7mb/d in March, with Middle East exports back to nearly 80% of prewar volumes despite the partial closure of Hormuz. JPMorgan counts non-Middle East production up about 2mb/d in the first half, around 700kb/d more than it forecast, and global consumption running about 5mb/d below year-ago levels since the disruption. Iran has reportedly offered to reopen the Strait within seven days if the US eases military pressure (Goldman Sachs), and Saudi Arabia has restarted its East-West pipeline. The banks part ways on next year. JPMorgan models Brent at $87 if the conflict persists and $63 in its peace scenario, while Goldman's base case is $80 and it sees $85 by December. Goldman expresses the downside through the $USO October 140/120 put spread, roughly four times maximum payout.

### Nine Sectors Down, Index Up

BTIG counts five straight sessions with more S&P 500 members making 52-week lows than highs even as the index rallied back near records. Over the past month the index is up 1.2% while 9 of its 11 sectors are negative (BTIG). Banks are the sharpest split, more than 10% below their 30-day high while the S&P sits within 1% of a 52-week high, a divergence BTIG last saw in January 2000. Fewer than 30% of S&P members trade above their 50-day average (Goldman Sachs). Morgan Stanley reads the same damage as a mid-cycle transition rather than a top. More than 40% of Russell 3000 stocks are down 20% or more since June, the forward P/E (price divided by expected earnings) has compressed to 19x, and median earnings growth in the mid-teens with revisions breadth near cycle highs is what has contained the index decline. It keeps an 8,000 year-end target and treats 7000-7100 as the tactical downside over the next three to five weeks if oil pushes higher.

### The Crack Is At The Bottom Rung

Investment grade and broad high yield are not breaking. The damage is in the worst-rated paper, where BofA puts the spread between CCC and single-B bonds at 799 basis points, near the top of its three-year range, and the jump from single-B to CCC runs about eight percentage points. Bloomberg Caa spreads have reached 9.2%, the widest since 2023 (BTIG). Small caps are starting to reflect the financing cost, with the Russell 2000 down more than 8% against the S&P 500 since late June (BTIG). BTIG finds 11 comparable episodes since 1996, and the pattern was not an immediate S&P 500 collapse but further Russell weakness over the following 40 to 60 trading days. What has not moved is the price of small-cap protection, and Goldman likes owning three-month Russell 2000 options, an exposure it says has rarely been cheaper over the past five years.

### Fear Reset Faster Than The Facts

The VIX closed at 14.27 (Goldman Sachs), a seventh-percentile level against the past year (Cboe), and one-month S&P skew fell from the 58th to the 29th percentile as investors sold hedges and rotated into calls (Cboe). Options that pay off more the further the market travels now sit below the tenth percentile at the index level and at the zero percentile in some single stocks. Nothing in the backdrop eased to justify that, with a hawkish Fed and the 10-year Treasury yield near 5%. The MOVE index of interest-rate volatility re-bid to 80.6, and rate volatility is the larger macro risk from here. Goldman's desk is buying protection back while it is cheap, liking VIX October 20 calls for the payoff they offer ahead of a crowded calendar.

### Copper Sold The Lows, Not The Story

Funds dumped $2.6bn of high-grade copper futures between September 8 and 15, the largest weekly sale since April 2023. About $2.2bn of that was outright liquidation, pushing fund selling to a roughly three-and-a-half-year extreme. The December contract fell 5.6% from record highs on tariff uncertainty and doubts about the pace of the AI buildout, and the selling landed right on the long-term trend line and the 100-day average. The link that explains it is the data center equity basket these copper longs track, which fell 6.9% in the same AI unwind, so this reads as an AI positioning event rather than a break in structural demand. J.P. Morgan's positioning work shows Managed Money cutting copper longs sharply week over week while trend followers likely bought. UBS would treat near-term weakness as a buying opportunity, with LME copper above USD 14,750 a ton and closing on its September record.

### Meta's Agent Has Users, Not Revenue

JPMorgan reiterates Overweight on $META with a December 2027 target of $820, about 23x its 2028 GAAP earnings estimate of $35.44. Shares are up 21% since Muse launched two weeks ago against a 1% gain for the S&P 500 (JPMorgan). Muse is the most downloaded free app in the US, its daily downloads now exceed every other app Meta runs (JPMorgan), and UBS counts about 1.8 million iOS downloads across the US and Canada in the first 12 days against about 1.3 million for ChatGPT. The gap is revenue. JPMorgan ties the adoption to a free tier of 100 million weekly tokens and does not expect monetization beyond heavy-user subscriptions before 2027. The platform fight has already begun, with Amazon blocking Muse from its marketplace and Shopify partnering for agent checkout (UBS). JPMorgan expects Meta Connect to cover the model and product roadmap rather than launch the next model.
