# No Backstop, No Bottom

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Panic without a policy blink

The S&P 500 fell for seven straight sessions and closed Friday at 6,910, down 8.6% on the week and holding a 0.9% gain for 2026 [c312]. The VIX jumped more than 16 points, the fifth-largest weekly spike in its history [c509], and Goldman's US Vol Panic Index finished at a record 9.8 out of 10 [c510]. Readings that extreme have been buy signals. After the four prior comparable VIX spikes the index rose 3.3% on average over the following week and was higher every time [c274], and 26.5% on average a year later, with the worst case still up 19.7% [c275]. Every one of those four panics ended with a policy rescue [c259]. This one has none in it. The Fed is still hiking [c380], and Goldman expects a second 25 basis point increase in December, each basis point a hundredth of a percentage point, plus a third in March [c374][c375].

That gap is what the week was about [d1][d6]. Goldman's derivatives desk measures the drawdown against the fourth quarter of 2018: the S&P's 8.2% fall from the October 6 peak is 42% of its 19.5% 2018 decline [c306]. The market for corporate debt has barely moved, with insurance against investment-grade defaults only 15% as wide as it got in 2018 and high-yield spreads 28% of the way [c625][c626]. Brian Garrett calls credit spreads the single largest difference between now and then [c627]. Run the rest of the 2018 path and the S&P lands near 6,294, another 12% lower [c307], with the low arriving in the first full week of January [c308]. Until credit or the Fed changes, his stance is to treat rallies as chances to sell [c310].

Morgan Stanley is reading the same decline as a bottoming. The correction has carried the index into the 7,100 to 7,300 range where its valuation, trendline and retracement supports coincide [c357], and Mike Wilson raised his 12-month target to 8,700, 19 times 2028 earnings of $458 a share [c296][c285]. Christopher Metli supplies the detail that decides which read is right: in 2018 it was the market pricing the Powell pivot, not the pivot itself, that stopped the slide on December 24 [c409]. Real two-year yields fell about 8 basis points Friday, which Metli reads as at least a start [c554].

So the condition is explicit. Rallies are rentals while corporate debt stays calm and the Fed keeps hiking. Two-year and shorter Treasury yields starting to discount the end of the hiking cycle, or credit finally cracking, is what turns it.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Hedge funds have already sold

Goldman's prime brokerage book shows fundamental long/short net leverage at 42.3%, the 1st percentile of the past year, and 48.2% once adjusted for how much those holdings move with the market, down more than 22 points since the start of October to a five-year low [c424][c425]. Momentum exposure has gone from a record +70% in June to negative [c426], and last week brought the heaviest single-stock shorting since June 2022, roughly 1.5% of gross market value [c428]. Morgan Stanley counts US long/short net leverage at 41%, the 6th percentile since 2010, and expects Friday's move to push it under 40% [c463][c464]. Trend-following funds are net short $19 billion of US stocks after selling $57 billion in a month, and Goldman's model has them selling again next week whether stocks rise, fall or go sideways [c449][c450]. Deutsche Bank's own gauge sits at just the 11th percentile since 2010, which is why it argues maximum fear may still be ahead [c466][c423][d12].

### Credit has not cracked

The investment-grade index closed 10 basis points wider last week, tied for its worst week of the year, widening on all five sessions at twice its average daily move for 2026 [c616][c617]. High-yield spreads closed 51 basis points wider, also the worst week of the year [c622]. Goldman's credit desk reads the cash move as exaggerated by thin holiday liquidity: it was net lifted on 1.3 billion of risk Friday, credit funds traded an orderly 0 to 1 basis point to net asset value, and default insurance widened 6 basis points against an implied 8.6, which the desk takes as credit investors being positioned correctly [c604][c605][c606][c607]. The $44 billion SpaceX chip financing led by Goldman Sachs and Apollo priced Thursday and closed 15 basis points tighter than issue despite Friday's selloff [c639][c611]. Hyperscaler debt now tracks the index at a 0.93 correlation against a 0.74 average, which the desk puts down to broad risk reduction rather than selling aimed at AI borrowers [c637][c609][d15]. It leans tactically bullish after the widening but would rather sell volatility than own the bonds [c613][c488].

### Nvidia beat and fell anyway

Revenue came in about $7 billion above expectations with guidance about $5 billion ahead [c4], a 17th consecutive beat [c6], fourth-quarter revenue guided to $130 billion against $125 billion expected [c7], and fiscal 2028 growth raised to 75% from 70% [c8]. The stock fell 22.7% on the week to 8.3 times forward earnings, its lowest since the financial crisis and a record 50% discount to the S&P 500 [c11][c13]. Two things did the damage. The SEC opened a civil probe into disclosures with accounting a focus, and the shares dropped 13.8% on Friday as that was reported [c14][c15]. CFO Kress also said the company will be more selective on customer financing, with guarantees kicking in only once projects have land, power and buildings [c21]. Goldman's TMT desk reads that as positive for Nvidia's own credit spreads and negative for neoclouds that depend on residual guarantees or offtake support [c24][d6]. Goldman's 2027 credit outlook counts $985 billion of AI debt coming, including $545 billion of project finance [c629], of which $320 billion needs a guarantor that has just stepped back [c630].

### The biggest IPO ever drained the market

Anthropic priced above its range at $83, raising a record $107.3 billion including a $26 billion placement with its own chip and cloud suppliers [c32], at a fully diluted $1.5 trillion, roughly 12 to 13 times 2027 sales [c33]. It opened at $111.50 and the broad market was sold at that moment [c28]. The S&P shed about $1.3 trillion of value that day, close to what Anthropic was worth at its IPO price [c339], which is the funding-drain mechanism rather than a demand signal [d6]. The stock closed its debut up 17% [c158], then fell to $83.10 by Friday's lows, more than 25% below Thursday's $115.10 high in under a day [c29], after underwriters exercised the greenshoe in full, 147 million extra shares at $83, removing the stabilizing short [c30]. The book was six to seven times covered [c26] and index inclusion on December 21 brings about $19 billion of passive buying [c27]. JPMorgan's Ronald Adler frames the question this way: a clearing event might happen if everyone wants one, but will not if everyone needs one [c31].

### The AI spending level rises, the growth rate stalls

Nobody in the pack disputes the dollars. Morgan Stanley keeps hyperscaler and neocloud capital spending at about $1.6 trillion in 2027 and $1.8 trillion in 2028 [c143], adding 0.9 percentage points to 2027 GDP growth [c122]. Citadel Securities puts 2027 at about $1.5 trillion [c246]. The disagreement is in the rate of change: Morgan Stanley has growth decelerating from 98% in 2026 to 73% in 2027 and 7% in 2028 [c216], while Citadel has it going from 106% to 57% next year, leaving the contribution to nominal growth flat at 0.55 to 0.56 percentage points [c166][c247][d11]. Supply is the other constraint. Newly elected governors could push about 5GW of Texas and Ohio capacity from 2028 into 2029 [c217], MetLife expects new governance rules to slow investment growth modestly with sharper-decline risk elevated [c214], and Bernstein judges only 199GW of the 586GW pipeline credible [c159]. On demand, 44% of S&P 500 companies now cite a measurable AI benefit against 32% a year ago [c137], but 48% of chief information officers who can measure the return on their token spend say it is below that spend [c226].

### Copper and gold washed out together

High-grade copper futures fell more than 9% on the week [c199], one of the biggest weekly drops in a decade [c207]. The setup was crowding, not a demand shock: producers, merchants, processors and users held gross longs near 20% of all outstanding contracts on November 10, roughly double the norm [c206]. The drop pushed prices through a momentum threshold and set off heavy selling by trend followers, which the physical buying community had no room left to absorb [c201][c203]. Goldman's futures desk pins the move on tighter financial conditions and continued data center weakness [c200][d2], and Stan Druckenmiller has said he is short copper [c50]. Goldman's Global Copper Basket is down close to 35% from its August peak against a 22% fall in LME copper [c175]. Precious metals went with it, gold down 9.5% and silver 16.4% on the week [c482], gold back below $3,600 for the first time since September 2025 [c485], and the cost of protecting against a further gold decline moved 4 to 5 standard deviations on November 20 [c486]. Camille Cerisey's read is that copper is washing out rather than breaking down [c51].

### Only Treasuries worked

Two-year Treasury yields fell 20 basis points to 4.65% and 10-year yields 19 basis points to 4.95% on the week [c557], ending the longest stretch above 5% since 2007 [c561]. The rally came against hawkish policy news, not with it: October's FOMC minutes showed almost all participants judged further rate increases likely appropriate [c391], and Governor Waller pushed for 50 basis points in December [c392]. The week's data and the equity decline took that 50 off the table and dragged January hike odds below 60% [c396], leaving about 62 basis points priced over the next ten months [c407]. Citadel Securities' Frank Flight puts his cross-asset growth gauge at -1.85 standard deviations against a 10-year yield at +1.75, a gap of more than 3.5 sigma he calls historically large, and expects it to resolve through a flatter curve first [c563][c564][d11]. In 2018 and 2022 stocks caught down to those internals rather than the reverse [c302]. Goldman's financial conditions index has tightened about 100 basis points this year, all of it since early September [c254][c255], worth a 0.5 percentage point drag on 2027 growth if it sticks, which Goldman does not expect [c670][c256].
