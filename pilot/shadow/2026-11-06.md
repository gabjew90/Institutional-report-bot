# Semis Crack, Machines Follow

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The AI build gets repriced and the selling goes automatic

A day after a clear midterm result sent stocks higher, the selling turned violent. The S&P 500 fell 4.36% to 7,353 and the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.36% to 26,629, its worst session since March 2020 (Goldman Sachs) [c565][c566][c569]. Semiconductors fell 12%, their worst day since 2020 [c389], and Goldman's high-beta momentum basket fell 6.2%, leaving it 51% below its June highs [c388]. VIX closed 67.19% higher at 27.77 [c771], and VVIX, which prices how violently volatility itself moves, rose nearly 45 points, its second-largest one-day jump on record [c773].

What changed is the arithmetic behind the AI build [d14]. Democrats took both chambers and flipped the governorships of Texas, Ohio and Georgia, three states that have backed power-plant construction (TS Lombard) [c492], and Thursday was spent re-cutting data-center capacity forecasts and the spending attached to them [c385]. UBS's Keith Parker flags about 30% of 2028 data-center additions as at risk, 18 gigawatts of that in Texas [c434]. Semiconductor valuations have now compressed roughly 50% from their June and July highs (Goldman Sachs) [c387].

Then the selling went mechanical [d23]. Funds that target a fixed level of volatility have roughly $61 billion to sell (Nomura) [c198], and leveraged exchange-traded funds faced a $47.0 billion closing sell, 86% of it in AI-centric exposure and the second-largest in Nomura's records [c200][c201][c202]. S&P futures closed below the 7,549 level that turns trend-followers into sellers, which Goldman estimates adds about $7.48 billion of supply over the coming week [c176][c177]. Dealers have to sell into weakness at roughly $7 billion for every 1% the S&P drops (Nomura) [c779].

The two desks split on what that means. To Goldman's Privorotsky this is rotation rather than wholesale liquidation, with investor net exposure too low for the broad market to fall easily and a lot of bad news already in the price of AI suppliers [c118][c122][c257]. The opposite case comes from Nomura's McElligott, whose dispersion measure has the average S&P 500 stock moving 13.2% over the past month against 1.1% for the index, a 30-year extreme that has historically preceded a 3.8% median decline over the following three months [c575][c576][c578].

Levels decide it. Futures blew through 27,600, which Vanda Research expects to turn trend-followers outright short, and a clean break of 26,800 would collapse their exposure to Liberation Day lows [c254][c255]. UBS Equity Strategy is buying into the move, recommending semiconductors on multi-year-low valuations and light positioning and hyperscalers on cloud revenue growth [c337][c338][c339].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Payrolls land with a December hike already priced

October payrolls are out this morning, with consensus at 63,000 after September's 29,000 [c79]. Goldman Sachs is above that at 75,000, against its own read of consensus at 65,000 and a three-month average of 51,000, leaning on low layoffs and the rebound that usually follows a late-Labor-Day September [c70][c76]. Oxford Economics is at 40,000, under consensus but above the roughly 25,000 it estimates is needed to hold the jobless rate steady [c92]. The private data ran warm into the survey window, with ADP private payrolls at 86,000 against 69,000 expected [c59] and quits up 321,000 to 3.365 million, the largest rise since May 2023 [c62]. Unemployment is expected to hold at 4.2% [c102] and average hourly earnings to slow to 2.9% from a year earlier, the first reading under 3% since May 2021 [c104]. Money markets price an 83% chance of a 25 basis point hike (one-hundredth of a percent each) in December to 4.00-4.25% [c610].

### Oil is priced as if the war is over

Prompt Brent fell about $8.50 across Thursday's session to roughly $94 (Goldman Sachs) [c641], and physical Brent now sits $2.87 under the Dated benchmark, a record discount against more than $20 over it three weeks ago [c638][c639]. Nearby barrels cost less than later ones, the shape of a market that thinks the conflict ends, and some participants put fair value below $70 [c640][c649]. Energy has been the S&P's best sector this year by a 2-to-1 margin and is down 10.2% on the week (Nomura) [c625][c653]. Working the other way, inventories have been drawn down hard and pledged strategic-reserve releases are largely finished, which UBS says could support prices if undersupply continues [c631], and Iranian officials say a deal is only possible if the US accepts all seven of their points [c709]. Goldman raised its 2027 Brent forecast to $88 a barrel from $80 and still sees Middle East supply risk capable of putting the premium back [c624][c623]. Polymarket odds on the naval blockade being lifted by end-March 2027 rose to 71% from 64% [c706].

### Gold squeezes after a five-week washout

Gold ripped higher after five straight weeks of speculative selling pulled $19.5 billion out of positioning, $8 billion of it in the final week, split between $5.2 billion of longs sold and $2.8 billion of fresh shorts (Goldman Sachs) [c732][c733][c734]. Traders were paying up for downside protection into that low, with the 25-delta put-call skew at its 85th percentile of the past year, and trend-followers held sizeable shorts that have since been partly covered [c735][c736][c737]. Goldman's Privorotsky argues gold is now trading as the inverse of oil, because emerging-market central banks squeezed by energy bills may have paused or sold their buying and cheaper crude relieves that, a theory he calls unproven [c738][c739]. A structural buyer sits underneath it: SocGen estimates Tether held 146.5 tonnes as of June, growing at an annualized 31% against 13.6% for exchange-traded funds [c743][c742]. Gold traded near $4,110 on Thursday, and UBS reads the return above 4,000 as a relief rally rather than a durable breakout, warning another leg up in oil could pressure it again [c748][c754][c755]. The 50-day average at $4,205 is the level that settles it [c762].

### October broke the hedge funds

October was the worst month for momentum in more than 40 years, with cap-weighted momentum lagging the market by 10.8%, a 5.8 standard deviation event that included two 6-sigma unwind days inside a week (JPM) [c179][c180]. Long-short momentum lost 16% as both legs went against it, and with July's unwind the cumulative drawdown reaches 30%, the largest since ChatGPT launched [c181][c182]. By strategy, JPM has equity long-short and multi-strategy funds down around 3%, equity quant 4-5% and Asia-focused quant 7-8%, damage it ranks alongside the dot-com, Lehman and COVID episodes [c183][c185][c186][c187]. Thursday added to it, with Goldman's prime desk estimating global fundamental managers down 1.3% and technology-focused funds down more than 4%, now roughly flat for the year against about 24% before the June and July AI selloff [c166][c167]. JPM's read is that the liquidation reset crowding from the 100th to the 86th percentile of its 40-year history and the next surprise can come from the upside, though it does not expect funds back in AI before late November [c126][c129][c130]. UBS's deleveraging indicator for US equity long-short has now been on for five straight sessions [c223].

### Data centers become a state-level problem

Voters approved 40 ballot measures limiting AI infrastructure, and Democratic governors ran on the issue in the states that host the build (UBS) [c409][c407]. Texas alone holds about 27% of US data-center capacity [c507], and UBS's AI Power basket fell to a 52-week low as that risk stayed in focus [c445]. The split inside technology was clean on Wednesday, with the four largest hyperscalers up an average 4.4% (Raymond James) [c308] against neocloud names down 8.4% and power names down 6.3% (UBS) [c231][c230]. The case for the winners is that slower new builds move the bottleneck from chip supply to existing computing capacity, which the hyperscalers own (Raymond James) [c482]. Hyperscaler credit spreads widened as Democratic Senate odds rose (UBS) [c870], and Goldman expects state and local results to hit select utilities and independent power producers while also expecting the issue to fade and any building bans to be short-lived [c392][c304]. UBS's call is to rebalance concentrated AI exposure toward platforms that fund their own investment out of cash flow [c321].

### The Bank of England joins the hikers

The Bank of England was expected to raise rates 25 basis points to 4.0% on Thursday, with the vote flipping to 7-2 for a hike and Swati Dhingra and Alan Taylor the likely holdouts (UBS) [c842][c854]. UBS then looks for a December pause and one final hike to 4.25% in February, more dovish than the market, which prices a further 67 basis points by September 2027, down from about 75 a couple of days earlier [c845][c846]. Goldman calls this the first hike of the cycle, puts a second in February 2027 at more likely than not, and still sees the roughly 100 basis points priced by mid-2027 as too hawkish [c843][c844][c847]. The tightening is not only British, with Sweden's Riksbank up 25 basis points to 2%, its first hike since 2023 [c359], and Australia's board voting 6-3 to 4.85% [c345]. Goldman pulled its next Bank of Japan hike forward to December from January [c864]. The outlier is Mexico, where UBS expects a 25 basis point cut to 6.25% against 25 of 30 surveyed economists looking for a hold [c454].

### Europe's data worsens under a three-year-high inflation print

German factory orders rebounded in September but landed just short of the 4.5% consensus (UBS) [c356], and that is against a revised 10.1% August collapse, which makes the recovery look smaller than the headline [c529]. German manufacturing sentiment held at 54.2 in October, the second-highest reading in nearly four and a half years [c357], while retail sales fell 2.3% from a year earlier where a 0.2% dip was expected [c446]. France is in worse shape: industrial production fell 1.3% from a year earlier against an expected 0.3% decline [c448], third-quarter GDP shrank 0.1% [c449], and October's composite business survey slumped to 48.4 against 50.3 expected, with new orders the weakest since July 2025 [c450]. Nationwide strikes over living costs are underway and the 2026 budget remains deadlocked in parliament [c815]. Over all of it, Eurozone headline inflation accelerated to 4.0% from a year earlier in October, a three-year high [c424], which UBS says reinforces expectations for an ECB hike in December [c907]. UBS still looks for the euro to recover toward 1.16 against the dollar by year-end [c890].
