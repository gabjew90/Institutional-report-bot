# Oil Sets The Rate Path

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Energy is now writing the Fed's next move

Yesterday started in energy and ended in equities. The US struck Iranian air defense, radar and communications around the Strait of Hormuz, Iran answered with missiles and drones at sites across the region, and Brent rose 4.60% to $94.65/bbl at the close before adding another 0.67% to $95.28/bbl in early trading. European natural gas futures rose 3.30% to a three-year high of EUR72.22/MWh. Crude is only part of the problem, because diesel, gasoil and European gas have broken out together.

That is what moved bonds. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to a post-2023 high of 4.80% and the 2-year to 4.40%, the German 10-year to a post-2011 high of 3.34%, the UK 10-year to 5.22%, and Japan's 10-year above 3% for the first time in 30 years. Higher fuel costs are no longer only a long-dated inflation story. They are dragging short-dated yields up as well, because traders are pricing a real chance the Fed responds to them. Odds of a September hike moved to 69% from 27% in mid-August, with Governor Barr saying the Fed should raise rates if inflation is not moderating enough. Stocks took the other side of it. The S&P 500 fell 71 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent each) to close at 7631, and the basket of economically sensitive stocks measured against defensive ones fell 212 basis points.

Goldman's sales desk reads this as a supply shock hitting growth at the same moment policy is pushed toward tightening, which is a poor backdrop for what investors will pay for earnings, and it says sustained upside in stocks gets much harder if energy keeps climbing. J.P. Morgan's strategy team argues the opposite case: most of the yield move reflects stronger activity rather than inflation expectations coming unanchored, and central banks will deliver less tightening than the market now prices, which is why it stays bullish into year end. TS Lombard lands near J.P. Morgan on rates by a different route. Put the Dallas Fed's six-month trimmed-mean inflation rate of 2.35% into a standard policy rule and the funds rate is already about where it should be.

The condition that settles the argument is Goldman's own. If crude, gas and refined fuel prices come lower, yields can stabilize and risk assets can recover, provided payrolls and inflation cooperate. If they do not, energy prices keep deciding what the Fed does next.

## 3. BRIEFS

### J.P. Morgan cuts its net long

The US Market Intelligence desk moved to a tactically cautious, neutral stance on stocks and expects the next two to three weeks to be choppy while the fundamentals stay intact. The damage so far is inside the market rather than at the index level. Momentum, the crowd of stocks that had been winning all year, is down more than 21% from its June all-time high on one measure and nearly 34% on another, a far deeper drawdown than that group usually takes. September screens as the worst month of the year for the S&P and sits directly ahead of the strongest quarter, and a wave of new corporate bond supply after Labor Day should widen credit spreads. Positioning gives no clean read on direction. The desk's answer is a smaller net long rather than an outright short, expressed as long Nasdaq 100 against short Russell 2000, with short credit ETFs and long VIX products as the hedge.

### Friday's payrolls with the signs flipped

The jobs report matters, but the August inflation report is the print that decides this month's Fed meeting. Michael Feroli looks for 50k jobs added against the Street's 55k, after a prior print of -23k, with the unemployment rate at 4.1% and average hourly earnings at +0.25% MoM, meaning month over month. The reaction is inverted here, so a stronger number pushes yields higher and weighs on equities. The desk puts 10% odds on a print above 95k, which it thinks costs the S&P 50bp to 1.25%, 25% odds on 5k to 35k with the index gaining 25bp to 75bp, and 30% odds on the 35k to 65k middle. Options expiring that day imply a 1.1% move.

### Systematic selling only points one way

These funds are now sized so their selling dwarfs their buying. BofA models systematic strategies selling $163bn in a down market against $9bn in an up market. Deutsche Bank has volatility-targeting funds at a maxed-out 100th percentile of equity allocation, which leaves them nothing left to add. Goldman's one-month projections say the same thing from the other side, modest extra buying if markets rise and a violent unwind if they fall, with its own estimate of systematic length at $146.5bn, up about $2bn on the week. Company buyback activity is near its peak just as the September blackout window expands, removing the other steady buyer. Against all of that, the VIX sits at 16.34, and the muted move in both the VIX and the price of downside protection is what surprises J.P. Morgan's desk.

### AI hardware is the working offset

Dell is the counterweight to the rates story. It reported F2Q27 EPS of $7.04, beating Goldman, consensus and its own guidance, with server and storage revenue up 89% year over year against a 75% guide. It raised its F2027 AI server revenue outlook to $74bn from $60bn and total revenue to $192bn, booked $60.9bn of AI orders in the quarter against a $95bn backlog, and traded 8.4% higher after the bell. Goldman lifted its Dell earnings estimates by 38% on average and stays Buy with a $425 target. Broadcom reports tonight, with consensus revenue up 33% on the quarter and 85% on the year while J.P. Morgan's buyside survey sits at 36% and 89%. The risk is not the number. $AVGO is a crowded long, and the desk warns the results can be spectacular and still fail to lift chip stocks.

### Europe is cheap and long gas at once

European gas above €70/MWh, higher than in 2022, puts the region's cost base under pressure. Goldman trades that through a basket of European gas consumers it expects to underperform, paired against a 16-stock basket of utilities that gain when power prices rise. Its sales desk goes further and says European equities cannot sustainably outperform while gas stays sharply higher, with the conflict spreading into European infrastructure and aviation as a meaningful tail risk. The same firm is constructive on the market itself at 14.5x P/E, the price paid for each dollar of earnings, and a 5.3% free cash flow yield against under 2% for the ten largest US stocks, and it upgraded Deutsche Bank to Buy with a €43.75 target on a better return on tangible equity over three years. J.P. Morgan holds the Eurozone overweight at 11.0% against an 8.7% index weight with a December 2026 target of 420 on the MSCI Eurozone index, while its desk would buy cheap index hedges on Europe now.

### Asia sold first

The overnight damage was worst in Asia, with the Kospi down 3.79% and the Nikkei down 2.85%, after the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index led US declines the session before at -2.14%. J.P. Morgan's positioning measure for Asia has already fallen from the 75th percentile in late June to the 40th, which is why the same desk pairs caution over the next few weeks with an upward medium-term path. Money has been returning underneath that: emerging market inflows resumed in July and total 19 billion dollars, and Korean corporate buybacks have become a new source of demand. The EM team is tactically long China internet stocks through KWEB, long Taiwan as the less volatile way to own Asian AI, long Latin America through ILF as a hedge against AI-theme weakness, and runs long India against short Southeast Asia. Quarterly Bank of Japan hikes keep the yen at 155 to 165 per dollar.

### Gold sold off into the risk it hedges

Gold closed at 4306, down 3.47%, which is the strange part of the session: a war escalation knocked down the asset that is supposed to rally on one. The move is not new either, since gold has given back 20-25% since the Iran conflict began, after starting the year as a consensus long. J.P. Morgan's strategy team treats the selloff as a chance to add and expects the debasement trade to resume. Its market intelligence desk has already taken profit and is looking for a re-entry, and expects that same trade to struggle until the Fed meets this month. The level that decides which of them is early is $4,300, flagged as support.
