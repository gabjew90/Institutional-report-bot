# One Put Showed Up

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The CEO Came Back, The Hiking Cycle Did Not Stop

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, eleven days after accepting his resignation. The stock closed up 31.48% at USD 165.05, at one point up as much as 34%, its biggest gain since 2000, adding $1.1 trillion of market value in a single morning. S&P 500 futures ran as much as 4.6% higher and Nasdaq futures rose far enough that trading was halted. The cash index finished up 4.7% with the Nasdaq 100 up 6.9%.

Most of that buying was machinery rather than conviction. A close above 7,043 flips the 12-month window of Nomura's trend-following model, which carries 46% of the model's weight and forces $16.1 billion of S&P futures buying plus $2.4 billion in Nasdaq 100 futures. Nomura also projected $41.0 billion of leveraged exchange-traded fund buying into the close, every sleeve of it a 100th-percentile reading, for roughly $60 billion of net forced buying before any investor made a decision. Goldman's long-term trend threshold at 7,119 was taken out at the open. Nomura's model leaves trend followers flat until 7,625 and only long again near 7,710.

JPMorgan took the other side of it. Its desk moved back to Tactically Cautious, most of its buyside poll called the move a dead-cat bounce rather than the start of something sustainable, and it reads late 2018 as the template, when the Thanksgiving bounce was faded into Christmas Eve. Its technicians put first resistance at 7,350 to 7,415. Nomura's own authors end up closer to that view, leaning toward a positioning squeeze inside a tightening cycle rather than the left tail being cut off for good.

Credit decides which read is right, not the chip. Goldman's Privorotsky set the test plainly: if Nvidia rallies while neocloud credit spreads stay under pressure, the move is short covering. Goldman's economists put financial conditions roughly 100 basis points (one basis point is one hundredth of a percent) tighter this year, more than 40 of that last week alone, and Pasquariello says the Fed put is not in play. BofA's Hartnett gives the other marker: investment grade spreads above 100 basis points is one of his two hard-landing tells, and they closed Friday at 96.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Credit Finally Followed The Equity Market

Junk spreads widened 51 basis points to 367 last week, the biggest weekly move of 2026 and roughly double the prior record of 27 set in late September. Nine in ten bonds in the junk index lost value. The stress is climbing the ratings ladder: of the 65 bonds that fell five points or more, 45 were rated single-B or double-B, against 18 the week before. Hyperscaler 10-year bonds widened 12.9% on the week and their spreads are up 138% this year, against a 29.6% average for every other segment of credit. Goldman's own desks split on what it means, with O'Connor building positions from the top of the capital structure against the bank's 4% default forecast and Biswas arguing valuations are back to median and recommending getting long through options. Goldman's Lynam targets 105 basis points for investment grade and 410 for junk by end-2027, and one week covered more than half that distance.

### The Fed Is Still Talking About Fifty

Waller wants 50 basis points in December unless the Fed signals a faster pace, citing inflation broadening and the fear that 3% becomes the new norm. The October minutes had nearly all officials expecting further hikes, with two open to more than 25 basis points next month. Goolsbee said he would consider all options including a larger than usual increase and Musalem backed multiple increases, while Williams dismissed the case for an outsized move. Morgan Stanley's Gapen still expects 25 basis points in December and again in March, taking the funds rate to 4.25-4.50% and holding it there through 2027. Markets price about 62 basis points of hikes over the next ten months. Hartnett's trigger for an early stop is the S&P giving back all of 2026's gains, another 1.2% from Friday's close.

### Hedge Funds Sold, Nobody Else Did

Fundamental long/short net leverage in Goldman's prime book sits at 42.3%, the 1st percentile of the past year, and beta-adjusted net leverage is down more than 22 points since the start of October to 48.2%, a five-year low. Momentum exposure flipped from a record +70% in June to negative, the lowest since late 2022. The rest of the market kept adding. US equity mutual funds and exchange-traded funds have taken in nearly $170 billion since the start of the fourth quarter, asset manager equity futures length is in the 82nd percentile of the past decade, and Goldman's sentiment indicator reads +0.7 against -2.6 in December 2018 and -2.5 in April 2025. Coppersmith's conclusion is panic in the options market and capitulation among hedge funds without the broad washout that has historically marked lows, and he would rather sell puts at levels Goldman is comfortable adding exposure than chase stocks.

### Asia Got Its Relief, Not Its Policy Back

Nvidia rebounded 31% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index 14% on Huang's return, which released the pressure that had Taiwan activate its stabilization fund and curb short selling. Natixis reads the leadership risk as the part that cleared, with nothing else back to normal. Huang returns alongside a new board committee on national security and export controls and a chief compliance officer still to hire. He testifies to the Senate Banking Committee on 3 December and answers House questions in private on 1 December, the backlash is bipartisan, and Democrats take control of Congress in January 2027. The remote-access rule effective 19 November already requires licenses for large-scale Chinese access to overseas compute and for covered chip exports to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. Pending bills would ban immersion lithography sales to China and require advanced chips to report their location after shipment.

### The AI Chain Is Being Priced One Name At A Time

Bernstein's study of 66 stocks across nine sectors finds data center exposure explains roughly 45% of the variation in how far AI-linked multiples compressed, with memory and server makers hit hardest and networking holding up better. SNDK closed Friday at 2.5 times forward earnings against a three-year average of 10.4, a 76% compression almost twice what Bernstein's exposure model implies, even though new customer agreements lock in about half of fiscal 2027 and two-thirds of fiscal 2028 bit volumes. Bernstein's top US semiconductor pick is AVGO at roughly 15 times 2027 and 9 times 2028 earnings, with AI customers among the best capitalized in the industry. For CoreWeave the risk is funding contracted demand rather than the demand itself. UBS is using the bounce to cut overlapping megacap technology in favour of equal-weight, Eurozone, industrials and health care, and stays Neutral on US and global stocks.

### Gold Is Set Up For A Bounce, Not A Trend

Gold trades unusually far below its 200-day average. Speculators have cut their net long position aggressively over the past two months, and trend-following funds are running sizeable shorts near levels that have historically turned into short covering. Projections for that group show them needing to buy gold in every scenario modeled. The dollar is stretched against its own 200-day average and just printed its biggest down candle in a while as it approaches a descending trend line in place since mid-2023. On the demand side, SocGen estimates Tether held 146.5 tonnes as of June, level with Libya and ahead of the Philippines, with about 65% of inflows into its gold products judged new money rather than reallocation. Gold lost 9.5% last week then rebounded 3% to USD 3,636 on Monday. UBS keeps it Attractive and forecasts USD 5,000 an ounce by December 2027.

### Breadth Has Not Capitulated Yet

Pictet calls the S&P 500's breakout above its wave-5 target a false breakout and now reads the price action as a wave-4 correction, with support at 6,817 and 6,182 beneath it. Stabilising the structure takes a recovery above the 200-day average and 7,493. The internals argue against a durable low: only 5.57% of index members are at new 52-week lows and 10.16% have a 14-day momentum reading below 30, both well short of the 2022 and April 2025 extremes, and 46.0% still trade above their 200-day average. What fits the setup is a tactical rebound back toward the 7,267 to 7,350 breakdown area, which is the same zone JPMorgan's technicians flag as first resistance.
