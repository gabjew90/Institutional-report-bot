# Panic Without The Pivot

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Fear is at a record and the Fed is still raising rates

The S&P 500 fell for a seventh straight session into Friday's close at 6,910, down 8.6% on the week. A streak that long has happened five times in 25 years. The Nasdaq 100 lost 10.4%, its worst week since March 2020. The VIX rose more than 16 points, the fifth-largest weekly jump on record, and Goldman's measure of panic in the options market closed the week at a record 9.8 out of 10.

One group did the selling. Beta-adjusted net exposure across Goldman's hedge fund clients fell 8.8 points to 48.2%, a five-year low, and gross exposure fell to a one-year low of 214.2%. Short selling was the heaviest since April 2025, and funds raised gross exposure while getting less long, which means they were adding shorts rather than trimming. Everyone else kept buying. US equity mutual funds and ETFs have taken in nearly $170bn since the start of the fourth quarter, buybacks ran at 2.2 times last year's pace, and retail order flow showed the clearest buy skews since the first half of the year.

That split is the argument. Goldman's desk thinks equities are near a tactical entry and would rather sell puts at levels where it is comfortable owning stock than chase the rebound, noting that panic readings above 9.5 have historically been followed by relief within weeks. JPMorgan's Schlegel is not convinced the broad market has given up. His US positioning monitor's four-week change was only about -1z as of Thursday, above the -1.5z he treats as capitulation, and he expects a repeat of 2018, when the index bounced in late November and then fell harder in December.

What separates this from the four prior panics of comparable size is policy. Every one of those was followed within days or weeks by an intervention, and rates are going up now, not down. Fed Governor Waller pushed for a half-point increase in December, and Goldman calls a second quarter-point hike close to certain. In the five earlier cases where Goldman's panic measure exceeded 9 during a hiking cycle, the S&P returned an average of 0.4% over the following three months. Credit is the test that settles it: if Nvidia rallies while borrowing costs for the smaller cloud operators stay under pressure, it is short covering and not a recovery, per Goldman's Privorotsky. Core PCE on Wednesday, the November jobs report and the December Fed meeting come next.

## 3. BRIEFS

### Huang is back and the research desks still disagree

Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as President, chief executive and a director, effective immediately, saying input from senior levels of the US government left it confident the move poses no national security risk. Wolfe Research, which says the Trump administration orchestrated the reversal, keeps Outperform and a $200 target against a $125.53 price, 59.3% upside. Bernstein upgraded straight back to Outperform at the same $200, roughly 10 times its average CY28/29 earnings estimate, having cut the stock to Market Perform 11 days earlier. KeyBanc will not follow, holding Sector Weight and a $160 fair value at 9 times its $16.99 FY28 earnings estimate, because the board's own investigation and a separate SEC accounting review are unfinished. The SEC opened that accounting probe last week. Evercore ISI's read is blunter: this is AI's first bailout, of the financing architecture rather than of a balance sheet.

### Rising chip rents are a lending story, not a demand story

Silicon Data's B200 rental index rose 23.5% between late August and November 19, from $5.62 to $6.94 per GPU-hour, and the four-year-old H100 rents for 67% more than in July 2025. Liquid Compute's Stanley Lee argues lenders explain the move. Advance rates on loans secured by chips fell from about 80% of value over the summer to roughly 70% after Labor Day and 50-60% now. An operator that cannot borrow against a cluster cannot sell a buyer a long contract, so that buyer is pushed into a thin short-dated market and bids it up, while fewer new clusters get funded. The price of capital is visible in the new deals. The publicly syndicated $25bn tranche of SpaceX's chip financing priced at 345 basis points (one-hundredth of a percent) over the three-year, north of 8.25%. Goldman expects upwards of $300bn of chip financing supply next year to crowd out ordinary high-yield demand. CoreWeave and Nebius remain the stress point, with unsecured debt yielding 15% and default protection costing 1,200 basis points.

### Gold was sold to pay for stocks

Spot gold fell 8.9% to Friday's New York close and traded below $3,600 for the first time since September 2025. The catalyst was the equity drawdown rather than the dollar or real rates, with speculators selling the most liquid thing they owned to raise cash, per Goldman's Robert Quinn. Net speculative length has collapsed to roughly $72bn from about $130bn at the September peak. On November 20, the cost of three-month downside protection relative to upside, and implied volatility itself, both moved 4-5 standard deviations, with that skew reaching a multiyear high. Since late 2022, spikes like that have reversed within a week about 70% of the time. Two desks push back on calling a floor. The World Gold Council does not think a full washout has happened yet, and ANZ sees room for another 200 tonnes of liquidation on top of the nearly 200 already sold. Goldman's end-2027 forecast is unchanged at $4,650 an ounce on central bank buying.

### Memory marks down 2028 while keeping its buys

The Philadelphia semiconductor index is down about 43% from its June 2026 peak, the fourth-largest drawdown since 1999 and past the range of a mid-cycle correction. Goldman cut its 2028 price forecasts to -19% year over year for conventional DRAM and -21% for NAND, took Samsung's target to W350,000 and SK Hynix's to W2.0 million, and kept both rated Buy, on the argument that those share prices already discount zero AI capital spending growth in 2028. At the same time it raised its estimate of the high-bandwidth memory market to $60bn, $200bn and $237bn for 2026 through 2028 on higher selling prices. The distinction Goldman is drawing is between a valuation reset and an actual earnings downturn, and it argues only the first has happened so far. Evercore ISI is less forgiving, with the sector multiple already down from 15 to 13 times earnings and possibly heading below its ten-year low of 11.

### Asia got official support, and the flows went to India

Korea's Kospi closed down 3.3% after trading as low as -5% intraday, while Taiwan's Taiex finished up 2.2% after swinging between -1.6% and +1.7%. The MSCI Emerging Markets index is now 21% below its June peak, in a bear market, after its worst week since March 2020. Governments moved over the weekend: China lowered margin requirements, Korea eased restrictions on company buybacks and Taiwan tightened the rules on short selling. Citi puts numbers on the Korean backstop, estimating the National Pension Service could absorb KRW17trn, KRW39trn and KRW62trn of domestic shares if the Kospi fell to 4,500, 4,000 and 3,500. Patches like these are not what you want to see marking a bottom, per Goldman's Privorotsky. The money is going somewhere else anyway. Foreign investors have bought $12.5bn of Indian equities this month, 70% of this year's total inflow.

### Copper ran out of physical buyers

High-grade copper futures fell more than 9% on the week, one of the largest weekly declines in a decade. Goldman's futures desk attributes it to tighter financial conditions and continued weakness in data center demand, which dragged prices through a momentum threshold and triggered heavy trend-follower selling. The reason it went so far is that the physical trade had nothing left to give. Producers, merchants, processors and users held gross long positions worth about 20% of open interest on November 10, roughly double the usual level, and Goldman suspects they were positioning ahead of a US copper tariff that has not arrived. Goldman's Global Copper Basket is down close to 35% from its August peak, against 22% for the metal itself on the LME. Stan Druckenmiller said at the TomorrowX Summit that he is short copper. Goldman's Camille Cerisey reads this as a crowded position washing out rather than fundamentals breaking down.

### Two houses at 8,700, and a case for 5,800

Morgan Stanley lifted its 12-month S&P 500 target to 8,700, which is 19 times its 2028 earnings estimate of $458, on a view that the earnings cycle has moved from early to mid-cycle, with growth slowing from 34% this year to 14% in 2027 and 9% in 2028. It wants investors to stay in quality but rotate more cyclically, with Industrials as its top sector. Goldman's year-end 2027 target is also 8,700, 21% above current levels. The bear case is just as specific. Goldman notes that the AI investment boom has pushed S&P earnings about 25% above their 30-year trend, and applying the 30-year average multiple of 17 times to trend earnings of $340 gives 5,800. One River's Sparks goes further, arguing US stocks are 40-50% too expensive with earnings 30% above their 50-year trend line.
