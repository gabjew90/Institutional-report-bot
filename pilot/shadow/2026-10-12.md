# Credit Cracks, Equities Shrug

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### The level where AI borrowing starts to care

The rise in Treasury yields this year is not an inflation story. Of the 108 basis point (one-hundredth of a percentage point) rise in the 10-year yield, 98 points came from real rates, the part that moves with competition for capital rather than expected inflation [c806]. The 10-year closed at 5.23% [c819]. Thursday's 30-year auction stopped at 5.618%, the highest yield since August 2000 [c818].

What is bidding against the government for that money is the AI buildout [d10]. SpaceX raised $40BN in AI compute financing that the market read as crowding out sovereign paper [c812]. Broadcom spent the past few weeks working to arrange more than $50 billion in financing for OpenAI's custom AI chip [c56]. In the third quarter, the interest-rate risk issued by the AI ecosystem ran about 25% higher than all Treasury issuance [c852]. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky says the crowding out effect from that issuance is potentially immense [c1255].

The question Goldman's Rikin Shah asks is the one that decides the trade: at what yield do the AI borrowers themselves start to care [d7]. His answer is that a 50-100bp rise in the cost of funding does little to slow AI-related borrowing when expected returns run in double digits [c861], which leaves the market hunting for the level where either AI issuance turns rate-sensitive or another part of the economy breaks first [c805]. Equity investors have not blinked yet. Since August 27 the S&P 500 is up 1.28% while the same index without its AI names is down 5.19% [c955].

The desks split on timing. Goldman's rates strategists think the selloff has run far enough and recommend a trade that profits if 10-year rates fall relative to 5- and 30-year rates (receiving 10s on the 5s10s30s SOFR fly), entered at 2bp with a target of -4bp and a stop at 6bp [c782]. BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky flagged a key reversal day in the 30-year and warns the pain trade from here is lower yields and a lower Nasdaq together [c937]. Jefferies will not have the turn at all, holding since March 2020 that G7 government bonds are in a structural bear market after a 39-year bull market [c838].

Credit has already answered. The cost of insuring Oracle's debt against default for five years closed at a record 261bp, up 36% on the month [c32]. The same insurance on Broadcom printed a record 136bp [c52]. The extra yield demanded from the weakest corporate borrowers, rated CCC, stood at 1,014bp, the widest in at least three years [c1253]. The VIX closed at 15.4, the 17th percentile of the past year, even with credit at those levels [c1497]. Next week's CPI print is the near-term trigger [c1547].

## 3. BRIEFS

### One trade wearing many hats

An investor told Goldman's Pasquariello directly that the current market is basically just one big trade [c971]. The earnings arithmetic backs him [d5], with the top 10 S&P 500 contributors set to generate 68% of EPS growth this quarter, up from 48% last quarter [c968]. Hedge funds are already there, Mag 7 at about 22% of total US prime brokerage exposure, the highest since the start of 2022 [c1129]. Underneath the index, 85% of S&P 500 names sit more than 10% below their all-time highs and 59% more than 20% below [c982]. Goldman's Adrien Simonet is telling clients that positioning is no longer a cushion against an AI selloff [c1177]. His desk is pitching $859 million of 6-month best-of puts across 29 AI and AI-adjacent names [c1178].

### The $20 billion footnote

The Financial Times reported OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue was approaching $50 billion at the end of September, $20 billion below the $68-70 billion figure that had circulated weeks earlier [c530]. Goldman's Sean Johnstone explains the gap as a reporting convention, since OpenAI reports revenue net of what its cloud partners sell while Anthropic reports closer to gross [c711]. Equities moved first [d9]. The Nasdaq fell 1.25%, the SOX dropped 3.4%, and Oracle lost 5.5%, Broadcom 4.4%, Nvidia 2.9% and CoreWeave 7.8% [c14]. Bloomberg then reported OpenAI expects at least $70 billion in annualized revenue by year-end, which lifted the Nasdaq 100 back to unchanged on the week [c330]. Credit never took the explanation, with Oracle's and Broadcom's default insurance both printing records once the question landed on their largest customer [c47].

### Waller moves the goalposts

Fed Governor Waller said he likely expects two additional rate hikes to be appropriate, which would raise the funds rate to 4.25-4.50% [c1043]. He pointed to the unresolved Middle East conflict and signs that the AI buildout is raising tech consumer prices as reasons his faith in inflation's progress toward 2% has weakened [c1075]. The September minutes read the same way [d44], with all participants backing a 25bp hike to 3.75-4% on insufficient progress [c1079]. Participants also named surging AI-related investments as a source of inflation pressure [c1083]. Goldman Sachs still expects one additional 25bp hike in December, raising the funds rate to 4-4.25% [c1073], and has lowered its probability that the FOMC skips [c1074]. The three-month annualized rate of core PCE inflation, the measure Waller had previously emphasized, is now just 2.0% [c1614].

### The bar is 27% and rising

Consensus expects S&P 500 EPS growth of 27% year over year for 3Q26, the highest heading into any reporting season since 2021 [c894]. Goldman thinks most companies will again surpass those estimates [c895]. Deutsche Bank forecasts 34% year-over-year EPS growth for Q3 [c884]. Jefferies has S&P 500 3Q26 earnings rising 30.2% year over year [c928]. Financials go first [d12], a sector that closed in the red 16 of 21 trading sessions in September, tied for its worst month in years [c535]. Goldman's index desk prefers the 30-October expiry for calls, which captures the Fed decision, the data and about 65% of SPX market cap reporting by then [c1019].

### Hormuz still sets the inflation clock

Brent crude traded up to around $106 intraday this week, with WTI topping $93 at the week's highs [c1432]. Oil still closed up 4% on the day of Trump's ceasefire pledge, and the real decline only arrived overnight as Brent slid back toward $103 [c1433]. The physical market explains the stickiness [d48], with Hormuz shipping flows running at roughly 9.5 million barrels a day, still about 30% below pre-war levels [c1434]. Goldman Sachs raised its Brent Dec26 forecast to $90/bbl from $85, because a slower decline in the risk premium offsets faster Gulf supply recovery [c1421]. Nomura's McElligott says it is effectively certain, in his view, that Trump resumes Iran attacks regardless of the midterm election results [c1339]. Goldman Sachs recommends staying long summer-2027 European gasoline as a hedge against upside oil price risk [c1426].

### Brazil's runoff pulls in foreign money

Brazil's presidential election goes to a competitive second round runoff on October 25, after Senator Flávio Bolsonaro's stronger than expected first round [c1384]. The Ibovespa closed up 7.7% at a record 206,912 on Monday, its best day in more than six years [c1514]. EWZ traded more than $8bn of volume on Monday, out-trading IWM and TLT [c87]. The rally came with $700mm of share creation in that fund, the largest daily inflow on record [c77]. Short interest barely changed after the vote, which J.P. Morgan reads as institutional investors being less optimistic than retail [c85]. Goldman Sachs thinks a market friendly outcome conducive to a tighter fiscal stance could take USD/BRL down to 4.50-4.80 [c411].

### Power is the bottleneck nobody owns

Goldman Sachs recommends buying its Power Up America basket, the GSENEPOW index [c355]. Positioning across the basket's underlying categories sits at a two-year low [c357]. The price damage is real, with independent power producers down approximately 30% over the last 12 months and gas infrastructure names down 13% over the last three months [c553]. Demand forecasts went up rather than down [d27], Goldman Sachs research looking for 108 GW of US data center power demand by 2030, a 23% compound annual growth rate off a 2025 base [c358]. FERC is expected to rule on the PJM large-load framework on October 12, the next dated catalyst for data center deal flow [c695]. The desk's implementation is to sell the 31-Dec 85% put to help fund a 110%/135% call spread for 1.75% into year-end [c356].

### Spending moves to things you cannot stream

Consumer spending growth on experiences accelerated from 1% in Q1 2025 to 6% in Q1 2026, versus 2% for broader services [c343]. The stocks did the opposite [d35], Goldman's Consumer Experiences basket underperforming the S&P 500 excluding AI names by roughly 14% [c753]. Live Nation reported more than 10% fan growth and no signs of consumer weakness [c241]. Sphere Entertainment's Wizard of Oz show sold 3.6 million tickets and generated $450 million [c260]. Viking is already 96% booked for 2026, with 53% of 2027 sold [c271]. Goldman's trade is the GSXUCEXP 15Jan27 110%/120% call spread, indicatively 1.61% referenced off 91.12 [c349].
