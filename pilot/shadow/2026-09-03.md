# Oil Now Sets Rates

## 2. THE MAIN EVENT

### Energy is running the bond market

Brent crude rose 4.60% to $94.65 at Tuesday's close and traded another 0.67% higher at $95.28 the following morning [c28][c29]. European natural gas futures rose 3.30% to a three-year high of €72.22/MWh [c65], above where they traded in 2022 [c281]. Bond yields went with it. The 10-year Treasury yield hit a post-2023 high of 4.80% and the 2-year reached 4.40%, its highest since July 2024 [c162][c163]. The German 10-year set a post-2011 high of 3.34% and the UK 10-year a post-2008 high of 5.22% [c158][c160]. Japan's 10-year crossed 3% for the first time in 30 years [c164]. The S&P 500 fell 71 basis points (one basis point is one-hundredth of a percent) to close at 7631 [c193], with economically sensitive stocks lagging defensive ones [c192].

The link is not the usual one where long-dated bonds price more inflation risk. Rising fuel costs are pulling short-dated yields up as well, because traders now price a higher chance the Fed answers energy with a rate increase (Goldman's equity sales desk) [c206][d2]. A supply shock slows growth at the same moment policy is pushed tighter, which compresses what investors will pay for earnings [c207]. Heating oil and the US 10-year have been trading as if they were the same asset, so relief in rates needs relief in fuel first [c297]. Odds of a September Fed increase rose to 69% from 27% on August 17 [c166], and Governor Barr said the Fed should raise rates in September if inflation is not moderating sufficiently [c167].

The disagreement runs inside one bank. J.P. Morgan's global equity strategy team argues higher yields are not an obstacle because most of the move reflects stronger activity, expects central banks to deliver less tightening than is priced, and holds equities at 65% against a 60% benchmark [c111][c112][c121]. J.P. Morgan's US Market Intelligence desk has moved to tactically cautious and neutral for the next two to three weeks and wants net longs cut [c314][c316]. Goldman's sales desk sits with the cautious read: while energy keeps rising, the Fed reaction function follows it and sustained equity upside gets much harder [c211]. TS Lombard disputes the hike itself, since putting the Dallas Fed 6-month trimmed mean inflation reading of 2.35% into the Taylor Rule leaves the funds rate about where it already should be [c249][c250].

What settles this is a price, not an argument. If crude, gas and refined product prices come down, yields can stabilize and risk assets recover provided payrolls and inflation cooperate [c83]. If energy keeps climbing, the constructive case does not hold [c211]. The decision point is the August CPI release, which J.P. Morgan's US desk rates above Friday's payrolls for the coming Fed meeting [c317] and TS Lombard calls the deciding data point [c331].

## 3. BRIEFS

### Friday's payrolls invert the usual reaction

Michael Feroli forecasts 50k nonfarm payrolls against the Street's 55k and a prior print of -23k, with unemployment unchanged at 4.1% and average hourly earnings up 0.25% on the month [c318][c319][c320]. A stronger number lifts yields and weighs on stocks, which is the opposite of the normal read [c232]. J.P. Morgan's desk puts 25% odds on a print between 5k and 35k, worth 25bp to 75bp of upside for the index, 30% odds on 35k to 65k for a range of -25bp to +50bp, and 10% odds on anything above 95k, which costs 50bp to 1.25% [c138][c322][c233]. Options expiring September 4 imply a 1.1% move [c321]. Tuesday's data already leaned soft, with ISM manufacturing at 54.6 against 55.2 expected, new orders down to 53.7, job openings at 7.271m and the quits rate at 1.9% [c168][c169][c171][c172].

### The buying side of the market is full

Deutsche Bank puts volatility-control allocations at the 100th percentile, which leaves no room to add [c187]. Goldman's one-month projections show modest further buying if markets rise and a violent unwind if they fall, with its estimated systematic length at $146.5bn, up about $2bn on the week [c188][c189]. BofA sizes the asymmetry directly: those strategies could sell $163bn in a down market against $1bn if markets go nowhere [c157]. Company buybacks are near their peak just as the September blackout window expands, removing the other steady source of demand [c258]. J.P. Morgan's momentum measure is down more than 21% from its June high and its classic version nearly 34%, far beyond an ordinary correction [c237]. Against all that, the VIX at 16.34 [c265] and the muted move in downside protection pricing are the surprise [c298].

### Dell turns AI orders into guidance

Dell reported F2Q27 earnings per share of $7.04, ahead of Goldman, consensus and its own guidance, with infrastructure revenue up 89% against 75% guided and that segment's margin at 15.0% versus an 11.7% estimate [c33][c34][c36]. AI orders were $60.9bn in the quarter against $24bn three months earlier, leaving backlog at $95bn [c48]. Guidance moved with the orders, taking the F2027 AI server revenue outlook to $74bn from $60bn and lifting total revenue by $25bn to $192bn [c43][c44]. Goldman raised F2027 to F2029 earnings estimates by 38% on average and keeps Buy with a $425 target [c45][c47]. Shares were indicated 8.4% higher after the bell [c40]. Management said the binding constraint is unchanged and remains supply, led by DRAM then NAND [c51].

### Broadcom reports into a crowded position

Broadcom reports after the close [c12]. Consensus models revenue growth of 33% quarter on quarter and 85% year on year, while J.P. Morgan's buyside survey sits at 36% and 89% [c7], supported by AI revenue growth above 150% year on year [c8] and an expected 2.2% revenue beat, wider than recent quarters [c17]. Most survey respondents expect the FY27 AI revenue guide to be raised, with $120b the landing zone they have in mind [c9], and the volume behind it is the TPU v8i ramp with Tomahawk 6 essentially sold out [c11]. The live worry is share loss at Alphabet to projects co-developed with MediaTek [c14], in a name that is still a crowded net long [c15]. Goldman expects the print to reinforce the strong compute demand message and act as a counterweight to cycle and rates concerns [c5]. J.P. Morgan's desk sets the trap plainly: results may be spectacular and still fail to lift AI, Memory and Semis [c16].

### Europe is cheap and gas-capped at the same time

The STOXX Europe 600 closed at 647, down 0.56% [c175], with European gas above €70/MWh and higher than 2022 levels [c281]. Goldman is trading the energy shock inside the region as a pair, short a 51-stock basket of gas consumers against long a 16-stock basket of utilities that gain from higher power prices [c201][c282][c73]. The same house stays constructive on Europe at 14.5x earnings and a 5.3% free cash flow yield against under 2% for the 10 largest US stocks [c75][c76], and upgraded Deutsche Bank to Buy with a €43.75 target on about 300bps of return on tangible equity improvement over three years [c32][c30]. Its sales desk takes the other side, arguing European equities cannot sustainably outperform while gas prices stay sharply higher [c208]. J.P. Morgan overweights the Eurozone at 11.0% against an 8.7% index weight [c122] and carries a target of 420 on the MSCI Eurozone index for December 2026 [c120], while its desk buys cheap index hedges now [c219].

### The tightening is not only a US story

The RBNZ raised its cash rate 25bps to 2.75%, its second consecutive increase, and its projections leave room for one more before year-end [c180][c181], with New Zealand inflation now seen at 3.9% in Q3 against a previous 3.3% estimate [c182]. Australian GDP grew 0.4% in the second quarter against 0.3% expected and 2.1% year on year against 1.8% [c66][c67], pushing the 3-year government bond yield to 4.79% as traders extended their bets on further RBA increases [c184]. Euro area headline inflation came in at 3.3% with core at 2.4% [c261][c263], and the ECB's Simkus said one September hike will not be enough [c173], with December pricing at 49bps [c174]. J.P. Morgan expects the Bank of Japan to raise roughly once a quarter and sees USD/JPY held between 155 and 165 absent a change in Fed expectations [c218][c304].

### Gold: same trade, two timelines

Gold has fallen back sharply since the Iran conflict started, after being the consensus long position in January [c18]. It sits at $4,309 [c20], with $4,300 the level J.P. Morgan's desk flags as support [c22]. The split inside J.P. Morgan is about timing rather than direction, with the global strategy team treating the selloff as a chance to add and expecting the currency debasement trade to resume into year end [c19], while the US desk expects that same trade to struggle until the Fed meets [c236]. The desk took profit on its gold position and says it is looking to re-enter [c84]. The dollar index sits at 99.79 [c264]. The currency has traded as a safe haven through the escalation, does not look cheap, and could resume falling (J.P. Morgan) [c226].

### Asia sold off after the positioning already cleared

Asian equities took the worst of it overnight, with the Kospi down 3.79% [c176], and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had led US declines the session before with a 2.14% fall [c25]. Underneath the price move the crowding is gone: J.P. Morgan's aggregate measure of Asia investor positioning has dropped from the 75th percentile in late June to the 40th today [c299], emerging market inflows have resumed since July at 19 billion dollars [c87], and Korean corporate buybacks have become a major new source of demand [c88]. Emerging market equities are up 7% from the end-July low [c100]. J.P. Morgan is overweight emerging markets against developed [c116] and its EM team is bullish on Korea, China, Taiwan, Chile and South Africa [c118], expressed as a tactical long in China internet through KWEB [c54], long LatAm through ILF as a hedge against AI theme weakness [c53], and long India against Southeast Asia through INDA and ASEA [c27]. The team is still cautious on the next few weeks [c216].
