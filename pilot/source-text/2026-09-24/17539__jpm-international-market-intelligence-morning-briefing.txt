IDEAS & INSIGHTS – IN BRIEF 
•         FLOWS & LIQUIDITY: Given the strength of AI-related capex, it might seem 
surprising that overall corporate cash flows have been rising faster than capex . One 


[Page 10]
explanation is that AI-related capex has been crowding out other capex, thus 
containing the growth of overall capex at an aggregate level. These corporate 
financing surpluses reduce the need for funding by the corporate sector at an 
aggregate level and allow corporates to sustain their buybacks, particularly outside 
the tech sector. Momentum signals for bond futures have moved back to more 
elevated shorts for 10y USTs and Bunds after today’s sell-off; they remain in more 
extreme short territory for OATs vs. Bunds and extreme long territory for energy 
product futures. 
•         CHINA AI VALUE CHAIN: We initiate broader coverage of China AI with a 
constructive sector view. We expect exponential intelligence consumption growth to 
support growth across China’s AI value chain, generating US$544bn of cumulative 
operating profit over 2026E-30E, with models + applications contributingone-third 
of industry profits and nearly two-thirds of the increase in annual earnings 
between 2026E and 2030E vs c.14% of the China-AI-attributable listed market’s 
estimated value today. We believe the market is pricing the current revenue 
distribution, rather than anticipating the shift in revenue and earnings that our model 
implies. Our Opportunity × Scarcity × Value Capture framework identifies Alibaba, 
Tencent and Z.AI (Zhipu) 
•         JAPAN BANKS:  The possibility that the policy rate will reach 2.0% sooner than 
the March 2028 timeframe we currently assume for our Japanese bank earnings 
estimates is likely to become more widely recognized than before. Recommending 
megabanks over regional banks, given house view of no significant yen appreciation. 
However, upside risk scenarios for regional bank stocks include BoJ rate hikes 
leading to yen appreciation (a headwind for megabanks’ net profits but neutral for 
regional banks) and credit costs and deposit competition not worsening much even 
as the BoJ continues to raise rates, with regional banks’ positive interest rate 
sensitivity materializing with a lag. Risk-reward could worsen beyond 2.0% policy 
rate and 15% ROEs 


[Page 11]
•         BRAZIL & TECH: (re)introducing a timely monthly measure of investment growth, 
called the Investment Activity Index (IAI). Team finds that technology components — 
concentrated in software services — are becoming an increasingly important part of 
Brazil’s investment. According to the annual GDP numbers, in 2011 software and 
other information services represented less than 5% of investment; in 2021, that 
share nearly doubled to about 9.5%. Including all technology-services related 
components, we estimate a share of total investment of around 15%.  
•         CATALYSTS TODAY:  ECB Economic Bulletin. Germany IFO Business Climate. 
Mexico, Norway, Sweden, Switzerland Rate Decisions. Trump-Xi Summit.

EMERGING MARKETS MONETIZATION – Ellen Wang / Victoria Campos 
The global momentum/tech rebound drove EM 7% higher since the July-end low, 
despite choppy price action. Broadening and AI continue to dominate the narrative in 
EM, with the recent global macro backdrop net supportive. We are bullish on EM 
given (i) improving macro; (ii) weaker USD; (iii) AI CapEx tailwinds, but continue to 
expect divergence in EM performance at the country/sector level and view country-
relative trades as more relevant for the monetization strategy in this environment.  


[Page 13]
•         EM MONETIZATION MENU – Key themes and trades include: (i) 
TACTICALLY LONG China H-shares over A-shares: the pair has been trading well 
in July given the momentum rotation. Despite this outperformance, H-shares still lag 
A-shares YTD and we see further room into China Tech earnings next week. (ii) We 
also express this view via a TACTICALLY LONG in China Internet (KWEB). (iii) 
TACTICALLY LONG Taiwan following the Global Tech selloff in July. Taiwan offers 
a less volatile exposure to AI and Asia Tech relative to Korea. 
• HEDGES – (i) We like long LatAm (ILF) as a diversifying play 
against weakness in the EM AI theme and for relative resilience 
against geopolitical risks. (ii) India (INDA) equity strength has been 
driven in part by a rotation during the AI de-risking phase, but 
foreign investor flows have also turned more supportive again, 
supported by improving macro. You may consider a L/S pair with 
LONG India and SHORT Southeast Asia (ASEA).

Report: Weekly Wrap | Potential for US Crowding to Rebound Soon and 
Tactical EU Upside 
Summary: In some ways, this week was more of the same, despite the FOMC 
meeting being behind us and oil starting to dip.  Quants remain a bright spot MTD 
for HF performance and are driving re-grossing, while broader crowding continues to 
be challenging.  However, we think crowding performance could be close to 


[Page 14]
rebounding and equities could be getting more primed for upside as 
positioning continues to fall globally and sentiment remains cautious. 
Key Points: 
• Performance & crowding — quants resilient and US crowding could 
rebound soon: Global HF performance remains challenged (MTD -0.6%; 
Equity L/S & Multi-Strats -1%) while Quants continue to buck the tape (+1.5% 
MTD). US crowding is still a drag, but the medium-term set-up suggests a 
potential trough (60d crowding return ~5yr lows; 20d improving off earlier 3Q 
weakness). 
• HFs still re-grossing, led by quants: Re-grossing persisted with +2z gross 
added globally and US 20d gross flows at a seasonal high. Quants remain the 
primary source of gross adding, and history suggests gross flows can start to 
soften into October (though Oct–Nov de-grossing has been less consistent 
than July). 
• Broad positioning & sentiment — risk-off tone, closer to lows in US/EU; 
AxJ mixed: US TPM suggests further de-risking this week (-0.5z on 1wk and 
4wk; level -0.4z / ~25th %-tile, nearing July lows).  While global HF net flows 
were modestly positive (+0.7z), they were offset by weak retail flows, 
ETF/futures hedging, and lower CTA exposure (~25th %-tile 12m).  Europe 
also rolled over (EU TPM ~25th %-tile since 2015; EU CTA 20th %-tile 12m) 
with a notable “tactical upside” signal emerging in EU TPM (4wk change 
dipped below -1z then rebounded).  China/HK positioning is risk-off (-1z) and 
sentiment/CTA positioning in HSI is near 12m lows.  Japan remains 
comparatively steadier (combined positioning ~55th %-tile since 2015, but 
CTA ~20th %-tile 12m). 
• Factors — Momentum flows stabilizing, Value being faded in US; small 
caps still pressured: US Momentum flows look like they’re basing, but the 
set-up remains tricky (6m positioning low; limited conviction to re-add to 
winners; shorts still painful).  Even in Europe, HFs have sold Momentum 


[Page 15]
despite the factor rallying MTD. HFs are fading US Value (negative 1m flows; 
YTD back to flat) versus EU Value where cumulative flows remain near YTD 
highs.  Size preference remains large-cap biased with futures/CTA data 
implying bigger risk reductions in Russell vs. S&P/Nasdaq. 
• Sectors/Themes — cyc/def turning up, Energy leads via short covering; 
Banks/Regionals pressured; Semis still a debate: Cyclicals vs. Defensives 
is ticking higher with HF positioning rebounding and retail sentiment snapping 
back in favor of Cyclicals. HF flows are in line with this…Cyclicals mostly 
bought this week (Energy led via short covering, Cons Disc/Industrials also 
bought), while Banks were sold with Regionals seeing shorts added.  
Defensives mostly sold (Telecom most sold, then RE, pockets of 
Staples/Utilities). Semis remain soft in the US (marginal selling) while EU 
Semis saw slight buying this week, but remain meaningfully sold over 20d (-
3z).
