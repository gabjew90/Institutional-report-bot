'A Dynamic Last Seen In Jan 2000': BTIG Warns 
"We've Seen This Before" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, SEP 23, 2026 - 12:50 AM 
As we highlighted in today's EOD, we are starting to see dispersion within Tech and AI (excuse 
us, 'SI'). 
The SOX is ~14% below its June highs, yet only three names in the index are closer to their 
highs than the SOX itself. 
 
10 of the 30 names in the SOX are still off more than 30% from 52wk highs, and the average 
name is off ~26%. 
More notably still is that, as BTIG's chief technical market strategist, Jonathan Krinsky, details in 
his latest note (available here in full for pro subs): 


[Page 2]
 
"We've seen this before." 
Back in 2000 the SOX peaked March 14th and we saw a similar situation after the initial crack, 
as many semi names went on to make new highs while the SOX itself didn't. 
While only 20 of the current 30 SOX constituents were in the SOX back then, 13 of those 20 
names made their 52wk highs AFTER March 14th. 
In other words, it's not unusual to see single names continue to make new highs even if the 
overall index has seen its final high.  
Reiterating what we said last week, for those bullishly inclined we would focus on those names 
near new highs rather than the laggards. 
 
QQQs 
Additionally, as Krinsky highlighted last week,when QQQ starts below its 20 and 50 DMA (but 
above 200 DMA), then opens the next day above its 20 and 50 DMA (like it did last Thursday), 
five-day returns are actually quite positive (median +1.13% positive 73% of the time) but flip 
negative in the 30-40 day timeframe. 


[Page 3]
 
 
It also doesn't preclude a new high like we saw in '07, '11, and '15 before rolling over... 
 
QQQ is up ~4% since that signal and is now less than 1% from a new high. 
The chart looks bullish, and a breakout is likely, but as always the question is can it hold that 
breakout, especially given less than 60% of NDX names are above their 200 DMA.
