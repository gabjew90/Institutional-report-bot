Calm on the surface
Rates have exploded higher, yet SPX and NDX are still holding their ranges and VIX has barely blinked.
Russell is the outlier.
The calm comes with a catch: systematic selling could accelerate a sharp move lower just as the
corporate buyback bid fades into October. The next break of the range matters.
The magic mid
SPX futures are bouncing off the midpoint of the range in place since May. Chasing momentum at the
range highs has been costly, so keep a mean-reversion mindset until we break out. Trading the middle
offers little edge.

Stuck in tech
Tech remains stuck in the same range it has traded in since May. Given the rates move, you would think
NDX had crashed. Instead, it is only 2.5–3% from ATHs.
The main indexes still trade like corks in water: push them under, and they pop right back up.
Imagine rates taking a breather. The upper end of the NDX futures range is around 31,300. A close
above that level would likely set up the main pain trade: more upside.

The outlier
Russell is down around 8% since mid-August, with momentum still weak. The index is now testing its
long-term trend line and 200-day MA. This is the make-or-break zone.

CTA flows go asymmetric into October

scenarios cap out around +88.7b to +89.9b. Translation: the trend-followers are set up to accelerate

Buyback blackout
The largest and most reliable price-insensitive bid is going quiet. Per Goldman, an estimated "64% of
S&P 500 Index corporates had entered the blackout window as of 09/21/26," climbing from roughly
320 corps in late September toward a plateau near 490 by mid-October. Rising to ~85% by week-end
means the corporate bid steps back just as CTAs are set up to sell.
