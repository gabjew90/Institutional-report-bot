The canary
The S&P 500 has finally lost its 50-day moving average for the first time since July. Industrials got there
almost a month ago. The sector peaked in early August and has been quietly breaking down ever
since. Industrials were one of the leaders on the way up. Are they now leading on the way down and do
we need to pay more attention to this out-of-favor sector?
@barchart
Breaking down
Macro forces have driven industrials to sharply underperform the broader market in recent
months. Tactical investors have derisked by meaningfully reducing gross and net exposure. 
Ryan Novak, GS: "We are approaching big technical levels, PMIs remain constructive and we believe
conference season could serve as a reenforcing agent to attract flows back into the space."
Bloomberg
1-year low
This isn't just an absolute breakdown. Relative to the broader market, industrials have fallen to a one-
year low.

5-year low
Zoom out and it looks even worse: industrials are now closing in on five-year relative lows.
Bloomberg
Positioning
At least some of the froth is gone. Large-cap industrial cyclicals have gone from a crowded overweight
back to roughly neutral positioning. In other words, investors have already done plenty of selling.
Deutsche Bank
Not just industrials
And the weakness is not isolated to industrials. The share of Consumer Discretionary stocks hitting 52-
week lows just reached its highest level since April 2025. Industrials may have broken first, but they are
increasingly getting company.

Bespoke
And now, a hike...
As if cyclicals needed another complication, futures are now fully pricing a Fed hike in October.
Industrials don't normally fear the first hike — they fear what forces the Fed to hike. And this time that
distinction matters: tighter policy is being priced just as cyclical leadership is already cracking.
Bloomberg
But here's the problem with the bear case...
Ryan Novak, GS: "We are in a PMI upcycle after an elongated downturn – despite a war –we think this
offers upside the rest of the year."
﻿So we have an interesting divergence. The tape says the industrial trade is breaking, positioning has
been washed out and weakness is spreading across cyclicals. The macro cycle says something
different: PMIs are still turning higher.
Maybe industrials were simply the first crowded trade to crack. Or maybe the sector that led on the
way up is telling us where the broader market is headed next. When the motor starts sputtering, it pays
to listen.
