Geopolitics: Dire Straits 
 
helima.croft@rbccm.com 
Fighting between Houthi and Saudi-backed forces has surged in 
the past week amid a counteroffensive operation, marking the 
most significant violence in Yemen in years. Maritime traffic 
through the Bab el-Mandeb is gravely imperiled by the Houthi 
advances, including those on the port of Mokha, bringing into 
focus our published high Q4 oil price forecast of $121.99/bbl and 
$118.49/bbl for Brent and WTI, respectively. In the past 24 
hours, reports indicate that Houthi forces have seized the port 
city of Mokha in the southern stretch of Yemen’s Red Sea coast. 
The Houthis have controlled the port of Hodeidah further north 
on the coast, which has served as a launch point for attacks on 
vessels and coastal infrastructure throughout the Red Sea. 
However, Mokha lies much closer to the narrow passages of the 
Bab el-Mandeb Strait, and a second port would allow the…  
…group to project its disruptive capabilities further south 
towards the narrowest points of the waterway. The Red Sea 
attacks have triggered a sharp fall in Saudi exports, falling below 
the 2 mb/d mark in recent weeks, and have reduced the efficacy 
of one of the key oil release valves for the 6-month Iran war.
Increased attacks around the Bab el-Mandeb have forced more 
exports to route through the Suez Canal, creating further 
logistical challenges. The northern route adds several weeks to 
Asian voyages, and the shallow canal cannot accommodate a 
fully-laden VLCC, requiring larger vessels to unload and then 
reload volumes through the Sumed pipeline. A resumption of a 
full-blown Saudi-Houthi war would be a potential catalyst for 
our high oil price scenario coming to fruition, as a widening 
regional conflict is a core assumption of that price call. 
Oil: China — Green Shoots? 
christopher.louney@rbccm.com 
Though the prospect of a prolonged Chinese demand recovery 
remains hotly contested in the market, we maintain our 
optimism for the long-term story as potential green shoots 
appear. Since mid-June, refinery runs have crept higher, and our 
latest data puts throughput at over 90% of pre-war levels (up 
around 18% from their early July bottom). Similarly, crude 
imports have ticked higher over the last several weeks, with 
August seeing volumes up 19% from June’s lows. This has 
followed the relaxation of some product market constraints, a 
move which supported August’s 180 kb/d m/m increase in 
product exports (or +600 kb/d from April lows), as well as the 
subsequent return of positive domestic refinery margins at long 
last. While conflict developments and refinery maintenance 
likely mean the trajectory won’t be linear from here, this recent 
strength has illustrated the country’s ability for a rebound, and 
we remain of the view that Chinese demand will prove 
structurally more resilient post-conflict than some anticipate.

Natural Gas: Fresh Highs 
christopher.louney@rbccm.com 
European natural gas prices have hit their highest level since 
January 2023, when prices began to fall from the historic highs 
reached during the Russian invasion of Ukraine. In the midst of 
another conflict driven supply crunch, Europe faces what could 
be another costly winter should injections continue to lag. EU 
inventories sit at 67% (versus nearly 80% this time last year) yet 
are at 54.8% for Germany, potentially creating an environment 
for even higher energy prices come January. A possible move 
even higher alongside the recent state-level victory for the AfD 
in Germany may see a resurgence in headlines about the 
reintegration of the Nord Stream and Russian energy, yet 
political and practical hurdles remain, making this an unlikely 
scenario in our minds. And while Qatar shipped its first LNG 
tanker since July this week, we do not believe a meaningful 
resurgence of gas will exit the Strait before injection season 
ends, setting up TTF to remain well bid through the winter, 
especially if cold materializes in Europe and Asia. 
Gold: Offsetting Forces 
christopher.louney@rbccm.com 
With oil crossing $100/bbl, related inflationary trends, and the 
broader Iran conflict uncertainty, we see the inflationary 
concerns and uncertainty side of the balance continuing to tip 
in gold’s favor despite the shifting inflation case for a Fed rate 
hike. While the US Treasury saying it would increase buybacks 
to $6bln may have trimmed some of gold’s gains yesterday, the 
dollar pushed higher and more importantly, the landscape that 
is leading to Treasury intervention should help offset the 
immediate impact of higher yields (note a deteriorated rolling 
correlation), which leaves us still tilting positive on gold prices. 
We highlighted in our Consistency Matters note how we remain 
of the same price view as we launched last year, and our 
conviction holds. We expect gold to be well within our $4500-
5000/oz range later in 2026, and while the jury is still out on the 
post-midterm outlook for 2027, we are of the view that our high 
scenario may be in the offing next year indeed. 
 
Clean Energy: Playing Their Part 
allison.enck@rbccm.com 
Increasing renewable capacity in Europe has given gas some 
breathing room, even if it can’t ease potential winter highs.
Since 2021, wind and solar grid capacity has surged 70% to 
737GW in 2025, while natural gas fired generation has 
increased by 5% to 405GW over that same time. While the 
seasonality and intermittency of renewables still gives way to 
high demand for gas fired generation (Jan 2026 saw a 9-year
high of 136 TWh), the past three spring shoulder seasons have 
experienced the lowest demand for gas over the past decade, 
allowing for higher flexibility at the start of injection season. We
do not view this structural shift on the grid as a panacea for high 
prices this winter should cold temperatures challenge the 
continent, but it could reprice some aspects of the curve’s 
shape given the undercurrents of gas’s role in the powerstack.
