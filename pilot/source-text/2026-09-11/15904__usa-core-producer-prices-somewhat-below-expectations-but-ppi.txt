USA: Core Producer Prices Somewhat Below Expectations but PPI 
Components Relevant for PCE Firmer; Estimating 0.24% for August Core 
10 September 2026 | 11:07AM EDT
BOTTOM LINE: Core producer prices increased somewhat less than expected in 
August, but the PPI components relevant for PCE were on net stronger than our 
expectations, driven by increases in the domestic passenger airfares and medical 
care components. Based on details in the PPI report, we estimate that the core PCE 
price index rose 0.24% in August (vs. 0.22% before the PPI release), corresponding to 
a year-over-year rate of +3.15% after accounting for our forecast of the revisions 
that will result from the methodological changes and will be implemented with the 
August PCE report. Existing home sales declined by 2.0% to 3.98mn units in August, 
in line with expectations. Following this morning’s data, we left our Q3 GDP tracking 
estimate unchanged at +2.5% (quarter-over-quarter annualized).

somewhat below expectations, and PPI excluding food, energy, and trade services 
increased by 0.3%, in line with consensus expectations. The July growth rates for PPI 
(+0.1pp to +0.1%) and core PPI (+0.1pp to +0.3%) were revised up. Retailer margins 
declined by 0.2%. The ‘old methodology’ core PPI—finished goods excluding food and 
energy—increased by 0.4%. Core intermediate producer prices and the prices of crude 
materials less food and energy increased by 0.5% and 2.1%, respectively. 
2. The PPI components relevant for PCE were on net stronger than our expectations. The 
domestic passenger airfares component increased by 4.2% (SA by GS) in August, and the 
medical care PPI categories relevant for the PCE report also increased. Using PPI and 
interim PPI data (which we seasonally adjust), we estimate a 0.28% month-over-month 
increase in the PCE medical care services category for the month of August. Other PPI 
components which will be incorporated into PCE beginning with the August reading as 
part of upcoming methodological changes but carry smaller weights were mixed: legal 
services PPI increased by 2.3%, while PPI for data processing services and video-game 
software declined by 0.2% and 2.9%, respectively (all SA by GS). 
3. Based on details in the PPI report, we estimate that the core PCE price index rose 
0.24% in August (vs. 0.22% before the PPI release), corresponding to a year-over-year 
rate of +3.15% after accounting for our forecast of the revisions that will result from the 
methodological changes. 
4. Existing home sales declined by 2.0% to a seasonally adjusted annualized rate of 3.98 
million units in August, in line with expectations. August home sales growth declined for 
both single-family units (-1.9%) and condos and co-ops (-2.7%). Sales declined in the 
Northeast (-4.0%), Midwest (-3.1%), and South (-1.6%) and were unchanged in the 
West. The median sales price of all existing homes declined 0.1% month over month (SA 
by GS), corresponding to a year-over-year rate of +1.6%. The imbalance between 
housing supply and demand improved slightly, with the months’ supply of existing 
homes available for sale increasing to 4.4 months in August (SA by GS).  
5. Initial jobless claims decreased by 1k to 206k in the week ended September 5, in line 
with expectations. The four-week moving average of claims decreased by 2k to 206k. 
Claims rose by 3k in Michigan and 2k in Missouri and Tennessee and declined by 5k in 
Texas after large increases last month (SA by GS). Nationwide continuing claims—the 
number of persons receiving benefits through standard programs—decreased by 1k to 
1,774k in the week ended August 29, in line with expectations. 
6. The existing home sales and inventories data this morning were in line with our 
previous expectations. We left our Q3 GDP tracking estimate unchanged at +2.5% 
(quarter-over-quarter annualized).
