Higher energy prices, more inflation risk, more and earlier rate hikes 
Further escalation in the Middle East conflict has driven a renewed surge in oil and 
European natural gas prices as markets increasingly price a prolonged conflict and 
led us to recently raise our December 2026/full-year 2027 Brent price forecasts to 
$85/$80/bbl (from $80/$75/bbl), and our 4Q26 TTF price forecast to €70/MWh 
(from €53/MWh), with the risks to both forecasts still significantly skewed to the 
upside.  
Higher energy prices have stoked inflation fears, and while we expect tomorrow’s US 
CPI report to show enough progress on underlying inflation to keep the Fed on hold 
at next week’s meeting and US monthly core inflation to continue to print around 
0.2% over the coming months, we caution that more persistent oil market 
disruptions would tilt the risks to core inflation and the policy rate to the upside. The 
implications of higher energy prices are even more acute in the Euro area, where the 
renewed price surge, together with the economy’s recent resilience and the hawkish 
message from today’s ECB meeting, leads us to now expect that the ECB will hike 
rates again in December and see a low hurdle for an additional hike (vs. our 
expectation of no more hikes beyond September previously). More broadly, while 
higher energy prices are unlikely to accelerate the BoJ’s rate hike trajectory, we will 
be closely watching policymakers’ assessment of upside price risks at next week’s 
meeting. And we now think that the RBA will bring forward its next rate hike to 
September (vs. November previously), partly owing to the higher oil prices we 
expect.  
Across markets, ongoing energy price risks are helping keep global bond yields high 
while challenging rate-sensitive parts of the equity market, and we think sustainable 
rate relief will require a change in the fundamental outlook. In this environment, we 
see value in remaining tactically neutral across assets, though we maintain a 
modestly pro-risk stance over 12m. To navigate this backdrop, we recommend that 
investors remain invested while mitigating risk through style diversification and 
selective overlay hedges.

What else is on our radar? 
We’re continuing to keep an eye on trends in AI investment, adoption, and funding. 
n
We observe that AI-related investment growth remains strong and that enterprise AI 
adoption continues to rise gradually (to 22.4% in August from 21.5% in July among 
US establishments). We also find that AI-related debt issuance continues to grow, 
which was an important factor behind the lack of a seasonal summer slowdown in 
the USD IG market. In particular, we note that AI-focused companies are increasingly 
tapping the convertible bond market, and think such bonds remain highly attractive 
for total return credit portfolios.
