QQQ Stuck in One-Month Range Into CPI; Energy Hedge Losing Torque
WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW: 
There is little left to say that we haven't said over the prior days and weeks, but one
topic of conversation that has continually come up of late has been the fact that the
'market' hasn't done much despite surging crude and rates. In fact on August 4th, QQQ
traded from a low of 707.53 to a high of 725.66. Since then, so over one month of trade,
QQQ has only spent a few days outside that 8/4 range and is still inside it today. Yet
the equal-weight QQEW is -3%, equal-weight semis (XSD) are -6.5%, industrials (XLI) are
-8.25%, and discretionary (XLY) is -5% since the 8/4 close. This creates the 'potential'
for an island gap should tomorrow open below 702.70. Of course this would likely
require a firm CPI, something we have no view on. Should that occur, however, it would
mean over one month of buyers would be 'left on an island' and thus under-water on
those buys.
■
Shutter Island. QQQ has spent more than a month almost entirely within the 8/4
daily candle. A gap-down tomorrow below 702.70 would leave an 'island' above with
all buyers since 8/4 underwater.
■
What About a Rally Tomorrow? Of course a cooler CPI could allow a relief rally with
volatility coming down and possibly a market pricing out a rate hike for next week's
FOMC (currently ~70% odds). We would then be left with QQQ still inside its 8/4
range, and many areas of the market still vulnerable. In other words, we don't view
a rally tomorrow as likely to change the overall dynamic of the market.
■
Energy 'Hedge' Losing Its Torque. Energy stocks (XLE) are up ~25% QTD, and ~10%
since the big 8/4 QQQ day. It's been a nice hedge to many other parts of the market.
Yet the last two days, WTI crude is up over 8% and XLE is up less than 50bps. At this
point the risk/reward looks asymmetric and any incremental gain in crude is unlikely
to provide the same upside in the equities.
■
Heads Bears Win, Tails Bulls Lose. We also see two meaningful downside risks to
energy: 1) a correlation-one selloff that affects all equities, and 2) headline/de-
escalation that causes an unwind and sees crude/energy move sharply lower. Yes
another rip in crude could see some moderate upside, but things are stretched
across the board.
■
Crude Bullish Sentiment Extreme. As of last night, the Daily Sentiment Index (DSI)
for WTI crude futures was 91. That's the highest since 9/18/23. Crude fell ~3% over
the next two days, went a little higher, then peaked for the cycle and fell -30% into
December.
■
Wouldn't a Top in Energy be Bullish Stocks? From 9/18/23 to 10/27/23, crude fell
~10% yet SPY fell ~8% over that span. So while the narrative: is crude down, stocks
up, it's not always that easy over the short-term. Medium-term yes, lower crude
would likely be supportive broadly.
