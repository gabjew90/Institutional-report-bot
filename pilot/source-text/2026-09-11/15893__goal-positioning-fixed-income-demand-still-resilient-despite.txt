GOAL POSITIONING 
Fixed income demand still resilient despite rising rates
Our sentiment and positioning indicator has turned 
n
more positive, around the 58th-ile. Our Risk Appetite 
Indicator remains elevated, at around 1, supported by 
cyclical pricing, and continued fund flows into risky 
assets (Exhibit 10). Equity and bond volatility remained 
relatively anchored, with the VIX/MOVE Index below 
their 40th-ile (since 1990). Hedge fund net leverage 
remains well below the earlier peaks, while gross 
leverage has modestly increased (Exhibit 64). The S&P 
500 average single stock skew largely reset from the level 
in August (Exhibit 56), and equity call/put volume 
increased - but is far from extreme levels (Exhibit 57). 
Both risky and safe asset calls turned more expensive 
(Exhibit 60). 
For most of 2026, investors steadily allocated capital 
n
to fixed income (Exhibit 1), particularly in short-term 
US government bonds (Exhibit 2). Products such as 
inflation-protected bonds and structured fixed income 
(bank loans, Muni and MBS), also attracted relatively 
high inflows (Exhibit 11). Flows into credit funds were 
also positive but the gap to sovereign bonds widened 
further in recent weeks. Retail investors have continued 
to buy bond ETFs (Exhibit 3), although at a slower pace 
than end of last year.

Investors Intelligence Survey: Created in 1963. The survey is carried out by the investment services provider Investors 
Intelligence and covers the market views of 130 independent newsletters (brokerage house letters excluded), most of which 
are published weekly. The list of advisers is reviewed thanks to Barron’s or web searches. The survey reports the findings as 
the percentage of advisers who are bullish/ bearish/ expect a correction, and is based on the question “are you telling their 
readers to buy or sell?” The survey is released each Wednesday morning, with most of the opinions written prior to the 
previous Friday’s market close. 
AAII Sentiment Survey: Sentiment of individual investors (bullish or bearish) towards the stock market over the next 6 
months. The data are weekly since 1987 and are collected by the AAII (The American Association of Individual Investors) via a 
survey of a subset of their members. Response rates vary, but on average they obtained 315 responses each week from the 
start of 2013, for example, with the weekly period defined as from Thursday to Wednesday. During this window, AAII 
members participate by visiting the Sentiment Survey page on AAII.com and voting. The survey is open to all members, 
although a weekly email is sent to a rotating group of members reminding them to participate. Results are published online 
early each Thursday morning. Prior to the year 2000, members responded by physically mailing a postcard back to the AAII 
offices. The typical AAII member is a male in his mid-60s with a bachelor’s or graduate degree. AAII members tend to be 
affluent with a median portfolio size in excess of $1 million. The typical member self-describes as having a moderate level of 
investment knowledge and engaging primarily in fundamental analysis. AAII has in excess of 160,000 members and, simply
