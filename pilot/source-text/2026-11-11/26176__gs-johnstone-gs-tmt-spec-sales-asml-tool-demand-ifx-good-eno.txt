GS TMT SPEC SALES – Tuesday 10th November 
US equities rebounded sharply overnight, with the S&P 500 up 2.3%, Nasdaq 100 up 3.4% and semis up
6.8% despite Nvidia falling 4.3%; more than 420 S&P names finished higher. Short covering and fresh tech
buying supported the bounce after last week’s sell-off, while oil fell around 5% to $85 as Iran tensions eased.
Asia is also higher, with regional equities up 1.8% and Korea leading at +4%, but this is catch-up with the
earlier US and European rebound rather than a fresh signal. Positioning remains light, with US tech
allocations near multi-year lows and Jim/ GIR describing semis as “clearly oversold”, although investors
remain cautious ahead of CPI and CoreWeave/Nebius earnings this week.

• Semis: Jim Schneider/GIR argues semis are “clearly oversold”: despite post midterm uncertainty, the 35GW of
2027 datacenter capacity is largely already under construction, leaving near term AI capex relatively insulated. At
current valuations, GIR sees compelling risk/reward across NVDA, AVGO, AMAT, LRCX and Memory. Link. 
 
• DRAM: Tesla and SpaceX’s Terafab reportedly targets 700k DRAM wafers per month by 2032. The reported
target is 1m wafers/month, including 700k DRAM and 300k logic, putting the DRAM ambition near
Samsung’sreported 650–700k wafers/month capacity. Terafab has reportedly reopened talks with TSMC over

operating control and investment size, while Intel 14A is under consideration for logic. (ED)  
   
• Apple: Apple demos HALO humanoid control system on Unitree’s G1. HALO achieved a 91% success rate
across 360 single-task trials in six previously unmapped environments and 70% on multi-step tasks. Training data
synthesis used 250 Nvidia H100 GPUs for ~48 hours. The article flags Foxconn Technology (2354) and Delta
(2308) as potential beneficiaries. Their involvement remains speculative. (ED)
 
• Onsemi has abandoned its planned US$2 billion silicon carbide (SiC) expansion in Roznov, Czech Republic,
according to DigiTimes on 9 November 2026. Announced in June 2024, the project would have created an
integrated SiC production chain, but DigiTimes and Czech reporting link the reversal to competition from cheaper
Chinese wafers. Czech outlet Lupa reports that Onsemi intends to source more wafers from Asia and focus on
higher-value activities.
 
• GOAL Kickstart: Post-election uncertainty over energy policy and AI data-centre construction triggered a sharp
sell-off, but lighter investor positioning and cheaper valuations are improving the outlook for equities. Clearer
evidence of AI monetisation, lower interest rates supported by falling energy prices, and a further deterioration in
sentiment are the key factors to watch. GIR remains positive on equities over the medium term but prefers cash
and long-duration bonds in the near term, as volatility may persist. Read the note
 
TELCOS 
• BT is delaying the launch of its Starlink satellite broadband partnership following service disruptions in
Ukraine, despite the service being ready, according to the Financial Times. CEO Allison Kirkby said BT would wait
for the controversy to subside, but subsequently confirmed a launch “in the coming months”, having previously
targeted the second half of 2026. The service will connect customers beyond Openreach’s fibre network, which
covers roughly 25 million homes. Ukraine’s Starlink access has since been fully restored, while the US government
denied ordering the restrictions. Kirkby said Starlink remained well ahead of competitors and intended to partner
with UK telecoms operators rather than launch a rival UK mobile service, despite SpaceX’s plans to compete
directly in the US.
 
• The UK has backed a proposed subsea data cable between Shetland and Norway to strengthen
connectivity and resilience against threats to critical infrastructure, according to the Financial Times.
VodafoneThree is leading a feasibility study, starting next summer, to assess routes and landing points. The cable
could support businesses, public services, defence and data centres while providing an alternative if existing
routes are disrupted. It would be the first new direct Norway–Scotland subsea data link in roughly 25 years and
follows a separate study into a Shetland–mainland Scotland cable after significant internet outages. The proposal
comes amid growing concerns about interference with undersea infrastructure and efforts to strengthen monitoring
and protection.
