Job gains crush expectations, unemployment 
rate falls, in very strong October jobs report
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Headlines
 
▪ 
Job growth crushed expectations in October, increasing by 165k versus a 
consensus expectation of 
70k. Job gains in August and September 
were also 
revised up by a combined 84k jobs, bringing the three
-month moving 
average of job gains to 
a very strong 
137k per month.  
 
▪ 
The breadth of job growth was also quite strong
, with private payrolls up 159k 
in October. Professional and business services employment added 70k jobs 
in October, the fastest pace of job growth since 202
2, and we also saw 
continued hiring in construction that is very likely tied, at least in part, to the 
ongoing AI
-capex buildout.
 
 
▪ 
Strong job growth helped push the unemployment rate down 
slightly to 4.1%, 
matching the year
-to-date low,
 and down from a peak of 4.5% last 
November. Labor force participation rose to 61.9% and the employment
-to -
population ratio to 59.3%, with both improving for a third straight month.
 
 
▪ 
Wage growth, as measured by average hourly earnings, came in stronger 
than anticipated, increasing 0.37% m/m and 3.1% over the past year. 
This 
helped drive aggregate payroll income up at a strong 5.8% annualized pace 
in October, a good proxy for nominal income growth.
 
 
▪ 
Bottom line: This report changes our view on the labor market.
 The strength 
was very broad based, with hiring, the unemployment rate, and wage gains 
all pointing in the same direction. We thought the bar was high for this 
month’s jobs report to matter much given the ongoing jitters around the AI 
buildout, and this re
port likely cleared it. 
Odds of a December rate hike are 
back up to near 80%, which we think is appropriate and aligns with our base 
case that the Fed hikes in December and March.

A very strong jobs report across 
the board 
The economy added 165k jobs in October, 
significantly above the market's 70k 
expectation, and a large acceleration from 
trend job growth heading into October. In 
addition, job gains for August and September 
were revised up a combined 84k, bringing the 
thre e-month moving average of job gains to a 
very strong 137k.

The breadth of job gains also improved in 
October. A large portion of jobs are still being 
added in the healthcare sector, but the surprise 
in October was the 70k jobs added in the 
professional and business services sector of the 
economy, which is the fastest pace of growth 
since 2022. Nonresidential construction 
employment was also strong, which is very 
likely being driven by the ongoing AI capex 
buildout. 
Unemployment rate falls to 4.1%
 
Strong job gains pushed the unemployment 
rate down slightly to a year
-to-date low of 4.1%, 
down 0.4 percentage points from last year's 
4.5% level. In our view, the economy only needs 
to be adding 50k jobs per month to keep the 
unemployment rate steady. If 
job gains 
continue to come in above 100k, we should 
expect further declines in the unemployment 
rate by year
-end and into 2027. The modest 
drop was driven by an increase in hiring, not a 
fall in labor force participation, which 
increased to 61.9%, a third 
straight month of 
improvement.
