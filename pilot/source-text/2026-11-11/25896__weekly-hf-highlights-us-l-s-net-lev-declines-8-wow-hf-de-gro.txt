This Week’s Highlights (as of Thursday, November 5th)
The record de-grossing stretch across L/S HFs that occurred between July and October evolved into an outright reduction in net leverage this 
week, with US L/S net leverage falling 5% on Thursday alone to 42% - marking the 5th largest single-day decline on record since 2010. 
This leaves nets ~8% lower WoW and at the lowest level observed all year, though still above the ~35% lows seen in April of 2025. Meanwhile, US 
L/S gross leverage ended at 202% as it continues to push toward 12m troughs. While midterms cleared a long-awaited political overhang, the 
outcome ultimately did not translate into any willingness to re-risk as HFs used Wednesday’s relief rally to continue de-grossing both sides of 
the portfolio, which persisted into a broader long-side supply event on Thursday. With L/S gross flow over the past few months amounting to -
10.7% of their global GMV at the end of June, positioning imbalances across most sectors, factors and themes have now reached moderate 
levels. With a relatively clean slate, HFs no longer appear to be in the driver seat when it comes to catalyzing these sharp reversals in equity 
markets. 
In N. America, AI remained at the center of the volatility as weakness weighed on long books despite lighter positioning – AI themes (aggregated 
across Hyperscalers, Tech Beneficiaries and Power) still makes up ~32% of total US net exposure but also represents a meaningful reduction from 
peaks back in June (47%) with HFs now tracking ~2% underweight the names relative to their market cap weight. Positioning was also broadly 
lighter across the themes that moved the most between Wednesday and Thursday, as HFs had spent the trailing 4 weeks covering shorts and 
softening underweights in the areas that rallied the most, while actively reducing longs in the areas that sold off the most. As a result, L/S PnL held 
up relatively well through the volatility despite net leverage sitting just under 50% for most of the week. The average US-based L/S fund gave 
back -72bps through the first few days of November with Thursday’s losses relatively extreme in absolute terms (-1.6%), though the group 
managed to mitigate a meaningful portion of the downside risk in S&P, experiencing <30% of the losses in the benchmark. However, YTD PnL 
for the group remains fairly challenged with the average US L/S fund up +5.7%, and ~35-40% of the cohort flat to negative YTD. 
Ultimately, while HF positioning is not ‘extreme’ enough to catalyze any sharp move in equity markets, they can still accelerate moves in 
both directions. Given their sensitivity to additional losses, they can be quick to sell into further weakness, though they can also be exceptionally 
supportive of an upside move given the extremely light positioning setup. Note – US L/S net leverage is in the bottom decile vs. last decade which 
has historically been indicative of more positive forward-returns on the S&P.  
The activity was not only isolated to US equities as APAC also observed meaningful de-grossing that took place across well-owned AI trades 
in Taiwan, South Korea and Hong Kong. Japan also continued to see meaningful gross reductions concentrated in Tech, though a smaller 
fraction of the long side capital was rotated into Discretionary and Financials. EU equities also contributed to the global de-grossing as HFs 
unwound longs across Tech, Energy, Health Care while covering shorts across the Consumer landscape – EU remains the smallest share of 
global gross exposure vs. 15Y history. YTD PnL for both Asia-based and EU-based L/S funds has been challenged – the average Asia-based L/S 
has posted gains of +4.4% (vs. MSCI Asia +14.5%) while the average EU-based L/S fund is up +1.5% YTD, lagging the SXXP +13.9%.
