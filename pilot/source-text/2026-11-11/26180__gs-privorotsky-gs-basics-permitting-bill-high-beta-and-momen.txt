GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Permitting Bill. High Beta &
Momentum. Inflation Expectations Roll Over 10 Nov 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Equities Trading, FICC & Equities
10 Nov 2026
Markets: Strong recovery yesterday. Tech back on the front foot, Nvidia weakness compartmentalized and
energy in freefall. A Middle East that is at least not escalating, enough barrels to balance the physical
market and perhaps the worst of the tech unwind behind us? In hindsight, we’ve just witnessed one of the
most aggressive episodes of net/gross de-risking in our US prime book over the past decade. The final
piece is CPI today. An in-line or softer print that challenges the ~80% probability of a December Fed
hike/or more like the risk of a March hike. Suddenly the setup looks considerably more constructive. Now
we just had violent rally off the lows and we should be vigilant locally as vol tends to be autocorrelated.
The obvious risks are hot CPI and renewed Middle East escalation into the weekend.
Oil: Trump’s discussions with Gulf leaders suggest little appetite for further escalation. Regional
governments appear to favor continued economic pressure on Iran over additional military action. The
assumption is that Iran refrains from targeting Gulf energy infrastructure while diplomatic channels
remain open. Whether that extends to the Houthis is unclear. Containment, not de-escalation. But the
market is increasingly distinguishing shipping constraints from a genuine shortage of barrels. If flows
normalize without further infrastructure damage, the oil risk premium has further to unwind. This one I
see the risk as two sided, think market has repriced a lot quickly so staying balanced.
Tech: First of neoclouds to report post midterms today. The question isn’t demand, but whether contracted
capacity translates into attractive returns after financing costs, delays and depreciation. More encouraging
is the market’s ability to compartmentalize Nvidia’s company specific headlines rather than sell the entire
AI complex. Leadership uncertainty around someone synonymous with a company for 30 years matters,
but it isn’t the same as deteriorating AI demand. The broader complex trading independently is a positive
signal.
DC: The bipartisan permitting bill cleared its first Senate procedural hurdle 73–25. Not final passage, with
the House likely the bigger obstacle. I think the market misread the elections. States want environmental
protections, grid cost accountability and local oversight, but they also want jobs and investment. A bigger
majority means the Democratic Party isn’t controlled exclusively by its fringe. The likely outcome is data
centers getting built with more conditions attached, not a nationwide moratorium. Permitting reform
benefits infrastructure and well capitalized developers. CEG +10% on stronger numbers, higher guidance
and additional generation agreements reinforces that the physical buildout continues.
CPI: GIR forecasts October headline CPI +0.21% MoM versus +0.3% consensus; core +0.16% versus
+0.22%. Shelter disinflation and softer goods prices are the key drivers, -1.5% drop in used car prices,
-2.5% fall in medical insurance...offset by tariffs and transportation costs. With ~80% probability of a
December hike priced, a softer print could materially change the rates narrative. Core at 0.2% would be

constructive; 0.3% would challenge the rally. We also have $42bn of 10yr Treasury supply today,
following a relatively weak 3yr auction, and $25bn of 30yr supply Thursday. Importantly, Brent fell
almost 6% yesterday and the 10yr barely responded...in fairness was a major risk on day for equities but I
think the key point here is that rates are more reflexive to AI growth expectations than energy. That said,
see the side-note...inflation expectations are rolling over quickly...perhaps rate hike expectations are soon
to follow.
Risk: Running through the checklist… impulses are supportive: energy supportive, rates supportive, credit
resilient and meanwhile positioning/sentiment remains shell shocked and diminished. What made this
drawdown especially painful was the sudden convergence of momentum and high beta. Momentum isn’t
always high beta, but when the two unwind together, the impact compounds across some of the market’s
most leveraged exposures, driving dramatic low volatility outperformance. The obvious re-grossing trade
is for rate curve to steepen, growth expectations to recover and high beta/momentum to regain leadership.
If CPI finally gives bonds a reason to rally, we could transition from short covering to a more sustainable
re-grossing cycle. My gut remains we confused the narratives on AI. Undersupply is not the problem its
the fear of potential compute oversupply/efficiency gains that should be worrying us in the MT/LT. For
now if energy and inflation align, the year end setup looks increasingly attractive with positioning so
defensive. Aerospace, transports, select discretionary and high quality/unlevered AI look like the natural
areas to chase. Bottom line: Broadly encouraged. Much of the initial bounce has happened, making
aggressive chasing difficult ahead of CPI but SPX Dec upside back to 13 vol makes it easier to stay
invested.
