US CPI: EASING BUT NOT PLEASING
 
Berenberg Macro View 
Pressure on the Fed remains high:
 Inflationary pressure eased slightly in October, but this does not reduce 
the pressure on the Fed to continue raising the policy rate on 9 December. Following a 0.6% mom rise in 
prices in September, the increase in October was 0.4% (Bloomberg consensus: 0.
3%), which is still well above 
the level at which the Fed is comfortable (Chart 1). This was the last inflation data point available to the US 
central bank ahead of its next 
meeting, and it provides no reason for the Fed to relax. Meanwhile, the stable 
labour market means the Fed can focus entirely on tackling inflation. Markets are pricing in a 98% probability 
that the Fed will raise its benchmark interest rate by 25bp to a t
arget range of 4.00%
–4.25%, and we agree 
with this assessment. For the start of 2027, much will depend on future developments in the Middle East and 
energy prices. However, the likelihood of the Fed raising 
its key interest rate again in the new year
—and 
therefore exceeding our current forecast
—has increased. 
 
Broad -based price pressures persist: 
Although energy prices rose by just 0.7% mom in October, following 
a 5.2% increase in September, inflationary pressure remained high in other sectors. Transportation services 
saw a 0.8% mom increase, suggesting that high fuel costs are being passed on to c
ustomers. Consequently, 
prices for services less energy services rose by 0.3% mom, while food prices 
also increased by 0.5% mom. 
Core inflation (less food and energy) rose by 0.3% mom, matching the September rate. Overall, this repre-
sents a broader -based surge in inflation than the Fed would have liked to see. Notably, no year
-over-year 
inflation figure w
as published. This is due to last year
’s government shutdown, which meant that the Octo-
ber 2025 inflation level was never produced, leaving no comparable baseline from last year.
 
 
Waller’s hawkish signal: 
As the new Fed Chair, Kevin Warsh, has refrained from providing forward guidance 
to the markets, investors are paying closer attention to statements made by influential Fed governors, such 
as Christopher Waller. Waller struck a rather hawkish tone today at th
e “Bank of England and Its Watchers” 
event, speaking ahead of the inflation release. As far as he is concerned, an interest rate rise in December 
seems a foregone conclusion, and the only remaining question 
is whether it will be a 25bp or 50bp hike. In 
his speech in London today, Waller stated: 
“If, as I expect, the monthly rate of inflation improves in October, 
I am leaning toward supporting an increase in our policy rate of 25 basis points. […] But if the October CPI 
inflation data out later today are similar to the past two monthly readings 
— with core CPI inflation around 
0.3% or even higher — then a stronger policy move may be needed.” 
While a 50bp hike normally only occurs 
under exceptional circumstances, and we still consider it unlikely at present, Waller’s comments clearly sug-
gest an upside risk to our current view that the Fed will raise the key interest rate only once more.
