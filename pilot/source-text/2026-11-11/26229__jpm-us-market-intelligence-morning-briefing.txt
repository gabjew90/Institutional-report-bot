JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Today’s CPI print will help solidify December Fed expectations with Waller’s comments on 
potentially needing to do 50 bp a bit surprising given the relatively sanguine commentary 
from the Fed over the last few months. Jay Barry had flagged that the 2Y yield was about 
25bp too low, relative to model-based valuation, so we may see the yield curve flatten in the 
near-term, a move that tends to be supportive of Secular Growth / Mag7. With the market 
trading as though US / Iran is ending, which would create a disinflationary tailwind, how 
much does the market react to a hotter print versus assuming the Fed will look through any 
near-term inflation increases if the Fed thinks it is induced by SoH constraints? In either 
case, following yesterday’s Everything Rally, including a broad-based rally in Tech, we think 
the tactical move in Tech is towards hyperscalers as the preferred long given the on-going 
challenges to the picks & shovels portion of the AI trade (financing / monetization / 
valuation).

•         MIDDLE EAST, BACKGROUND –  JPM Center for Geopolitics team characterized in 
an email last week as: 
o   An Epic Fury 2.0 that revisits the kinds of military targets struck in the 
spring  
o   Broader attacks on energy and civilian infrastructure  
o   A “final blow” aimed at a nuclear target, a largely performative strike that 
would allow Washington to declare “mission accomplished.”  
▪  The team also noted that Washington has had these same options 
on the table since the MOU collapsed in June without reaching for 
them, given all risk Iranian retaliation and an escalatory spiral sending 
energy prices even higher and inflicting considerably more economic 
and psychological damage across the Gulf  
•         MIDDLE EAST SCENARIO ANALYSIS (JPM COMMODITY TRADING) –  
o   50% chance of further strikes, followed by a declaration that military 
action is over – then a 75% chance the US maintains a presence to secure 
Strait of Hormuz passage and continues the blockade and 25% it withdraws 
after declaring its mission complete. These two outcomes would be expected 
to see, respectively, Brent opening ~$3 higher on Monday but ending 
unchanged to $2 lower and prices near $90 longer term, and opening 
unchanged before falling ~$4 as positions are exited, before gradually 
recovering and $105–110 within a week.  
o   50% chance is that diplomacy is given more time – expected to see a 
$4–5 decline in prices and a 7–10 point fall in volatility through the week as 
the market reassesses the possibility of diplomacy and investors exit January 
length.  
o   IAN MITCHELL (ENERGY) – Interestingly the desk sees no chance of 
options 2) or 3) set out by the JPM Center for Geopolitics (i.e. wider re-
escalation) materialising this weekend – I agree 

  
DUBRAVKO – his full note is here 
•         Dubravko reiterates his YE26 SPX price target of 8,000 with $365 EPS, 21.9x. This 
implies 8.4% upside from Friday’s close. 
•         TRADE IDEAS – (i) Long 12M Momentum vs. Short 3M Momentum – which is 
essentially Tech vs. Energy / Defensives; (ii) Low Vol is less compelling – after moving 
form 0%ile to 25%ile, the trade is less attractive as the factor will need to see an increase in 
recession risk to maintain its outperformance. If you want to stick with Low Vol, he sees the 
best opportunity in Healthcare followed by Utilities, and then staples; (iii) AI Upstream is 
attractive – the positioning flush has created an attractive entry point. While hyperscalers 
have less upside in the near-term, their medium-term prospects are attractive with room to 
re-rate higher. The Blue Wave will limited the capex curve, but tighter compute capacity 
should benefit pricing power, margins, and operating cash flow for hyperscalers; (iv) 
Consumer Cyclicals remain challenged – affordability remains the headwind tied to oil 
prices, borrowing costs, and housing shortages. That said, he sees longer-term value in 
Apparel, Restaurants, and Retail vs. Energy. Regarding Housing, mortgage rates need to 
approach 6.0% to see inflows into the group. 
•         WHAT’S THE MACRO VIEW?  Looking ahead to 2027, JPM Economics expects US 
GDP growth to slow but remain trend-like with modest disinflation keeping the broader 
business cycle dynamics intact, while we [Dubravko & team] estimate EPS growth of 
~20%.  
•         WHY THE UNWIND? This pullback is a de-leveraging event. Investor were chasing AI 
and AI-adjacent plays with record high levels of leverage. Leverage is at-risk when liquidity 
dissipate, which began occurring in late-June. Liquidity dries, equity de-leveraging follows 
triggering a rapid reversal of the Momentum trade. The unwind was magnified by systematic 
& vol strategies plus a liquidation of buyside funds. October was the largest single month 
unwind for Long Momentum, falling ~11% or ~6 standard deviations contributing to 
Momentum L/S declining ~40% from its peak. 

•         WHAT ABOUT MULTIPLES? Equity multiple have compressed ~4x since January, 
one of the most extreme periods of de-rating when excluding recessions. This de-rating is 
magnified by the fact that earnings have been growing double-digits this year. AI companies 
have seen multiples compress ~35% since May, including: Semis compressing 52%, 
hyperscalers 14%, Memory 60%, and Processing Units 50%. 
•         HOW MUCH SELLING IS LEFT? He sees the process as being far along pointing to (i) 
a reset in Momentum crowding from 100%ile to 76%ile; (ii) Levered ETF sales of ~$60bn 
on Thursday and $35bn the previous Friday which have dropped exposure below Jan 2026 
levels; (iii) CTA have flipped to net Short as vol targeters have seen leverage decrease 
from 65%ile to 30%ile with maybe $50bn left to sell; (iv) dealer net gamma imbalance is 
12%ile; (v) corporate buyback, with $1.2T in announced buyback, are likely to provide 
support; (vi) QQQ have seen their RSI dip below 30 which has, on average seen ~4% rally 
over the next five trading days. 
•         WHAT ARE THE CATALYSTS? (i) developments in the Middle East; (ii) MegaCap 
IPOs coming to market; (iii) the ERCOT meeting next month, which has AI Capex 
implications; and (iv) December Fed meeting. More generally, he sees US Midterm 
Elections as a clearing event and expected data center headwinds to be less onerous than 
the market is pricing. 
•         WHAT ARE THE SPX EPS RISKS FROM AI CAPEX? Dubravko and team estimate 
that  every $100B increase in capex translates to ~$9 for S&P 500 EPS (~2.5% earnings 
growth) in 2027. Thus, even if next year’s capex spend comes in at the middle of the range 
at ~$1.5T, that would roughly translate to an EPS impact of ~$18 vs. consensus, adding 4-
5% to 2027 EPS growth. While there are concerns around future (2028) data center 
projects after the recent midterm election results, J.P. Morgan credit analysts expect 
hyperscalers to be able to fund their AI capex needs next year, absent any exogenous 
shocks in credit markets. However, the funding mix will need to see more participation from 
other sources including equity.
