• Extensive AI weakness post the US election spurred significant short selling via S&P 500 futures. On
November 5th, S&P Index dropped 4.4%. Hyperscalers (-3.4%) were no longer able to compensate for weakness
in Semiconductors (-12.2%), which suffered from diminishing datacenter buildout expectations. Futures aggregate
open interest skyrocketed $19.1bn - the 5th largest 1 day jump over the past 2 years, excluding roll periods.
• Mere stabilization in AI, plus a drop in oil due to improving prospects for an end to the Iran-US War, forced
quick covering - even without S&P reaching its previous value. During November 5th - 9th, S&P recovered
2.7%. Aggregate open interest fell $16.6bn.
• Interestingly, funding cheapened throughout the entire price chop, potentially due to various supply and
demand factors. Dealers likely regained capacity to arbitrage following unwinds in leveraged ETF exposure.
Moreover, trend followers liquidated. Consider, short and medium-term momentum signals flipped on GS Futures
Strategists' CTA framework. This somewhat offset the aforementioned downside reduction.
• In early November 10th trading, hawkish Fed commentary threatened to derail the
recovery. Waller expressed support for a December hike and opened the door to a half-percentage-point increase.
As a result, US 10 year real yields overcame a continued decline in Brent Oil. S&P E-Mini traded -11bps versus
settle.
• However note the fate of Semi's arguably still holds more weight. Asset Manager S&P 500 shorts remained
negatively correlated to Semiconductors as of the last Commitment of Traders update. The historically positive
relationship versus 10 year real yields was not as strong.
