The panic vs reality
Texas accounts for more than a quarter of planned US data center capacity additions through 2030, so
talk of a potential moratorium has rattled the entire AI complex. But Goldman argues the market is
missing an important distinction: restrictions on new permits don't stop projects already under
construction. The 35 GW of data center additions planned for 2027 are largely already being built, with
limited execution risk. Goldman still sees $1.3 trillion in AI infrastructure capex by 2027.
Meanwhile, SOX has suffered one of its most extreme drawdowns in history. The index has bounced off
its long-term trendline, but remains well below the 200-day MA. The fundamental debate is far from
settled, but with positioning washed out and the physical buildout continuing, the setup is getting
increasingly interesting.

Dis-allocating
The positioning puke in semis has pushed allocations back toward year lows. Hard to imagine just a

The AI valuation reset
Goldman argues that even under an unlikely zero-growth scenario for 2028, NVDA and AVGO trade at
just 12x and 13x earnings, respectively. Not exactly demanding valuations for two of the biggest AI
winners.
AVGO remains below its 200-day MA, but has bounced off a major long-term trendline that has held for
years. A few more days of squeezing and the chase could be on.

Memory: Getting interesting
The memory trade has been hit particularly hard. Goldman notes that Samsung and SK Hynix are
already trading below book value, while Micron is approaching historically attractive valuation territory.
With free cash flow expected to improve, the risk/reward is starting to look compelling. MU is trading
just below its 200-day MA. A close back above this key level could trigger another round of chasing.

Too cheap to ignore?
The valuation reset in Korean memory is extraordinary. JPMorgan highlights SK Hynix trading at just
2.6x 2027 estimated earnings and Samsung at 2.8x. The broader memory group trades at only 3.6x
despite projected EPS CAGR of 98% between 2026 and 2028. Crucially, earnings estimates have not
followed share prices lower, with JPMorgan seeing little risk of cuts over the next six months. The
disconnect between valuations and fundamentals is becoming difficult to ignore.

The bottom line
The market is pricing in an AI spending collapse, while Goldman sees a construction pipeline that is
already largely committed. Longer-term oversupply risks haven't disappeared, but the disconnect
between fundamentals, valuations and positioning is becoming hard to ignore.
With semis washed out and allocations back near year lows, it may not take much to turn this bounce
into a much bigger squeeze.
