Investors have been navigating a tug-of-war between tightening macro 
conditions and above-trend earnings growth. During 1H26, strong AI capex 
guidance overshadowed macro concerns—including higher rates, oil prices, USD, 
and credit spreads—as consensus earnings growth was revised sharply higher 
(2026 and 2027 EPS revised up 9% and 12%, respectively). The growing 
bifurcation between AI beneficiaries and non-AI companies was justified by a 
steady stream of positive headlines tied to rising capex and rapidly improving 
frontier models, which were seen as orthogonal to the macro headwinds facing 
other parts of the economy (e.g., consumer goods & services, rate-sensitive 
housing complex, old economy industrials). While macro risks (resilient growth 
but with sticky inflation, rising yields, and monetary tightening) continued to build 
over the summer, it became increasingly evident that investors globally were 
chasing AI and AI-adjacent exposures with record-high leverage, even as our 
signals were flashing high risk of a potential unwind in Momentum as crowding 
reached a 40yr high in May (see Figure 16M
omentum Crowding in Decline.). 
As liquidity began tightening in late June and steadily worsened into October 
amid insatiable demand for capital (e.g., trillion-dollar IPOs, robust DCM and 
ECM activity, record index rebalance, leveraged index funds), equity deleveraging 
followed, along with a rapid reversal in crowded Momentum trades. The technical 
unwind was amplified by systematic and volatility-sensitive strategies, as well as 
liquidations across equity, quant, and credit funds. The initial wave of selling began 
in Asia and hit crowded Momentum trades globally over the summer (e.g., AI 
upstream versus Software). Since the peak, Momentum (L/S) has declined by 
~40%, the largest unwind since the post-GFC recovery, with October experiencing 
the worst single month decline in the long Momentum (vs. Market) portfolio at 
-11%, or ~6 sigma. At the same time, equity multiples have de-rated by ~4x since 
the January highs—one of the most extreme compressions in modern history 
outside of a recession—despite being in a double-digit earnings growth 
environment. While the pullback has been painful for markets, we see this largely 
as a deleveraging event. This is the third momentum crash episode since the launch 
of ChatGPT (see Figure 18 Largest Unwind in Top M
omentum Crowding Since GFC Recovery ), and it should not be entirely surprising to see mini 
boom-bust cycles within large technological shifts of this scale. Given the constant 
skepticism that the AI cycle has faced and the market penalizing excesses fairly 
rapidly, this should ultimately support the durability of the AI supercycle. Looking 
ahead to 2027, JPM Economics expects US GDP growth to slow but remain 
trend-like with modest disinflation keeping the broader business cycle 
dynamics intact, while we estimate EPS growth of ~20%. As such, we 
maintain our S&P 500 price target of 8,000.
Where are we in the market capitulation process? There are increasing signs of 
investor capitulation after the largest unwind of Long Momentum (vs. market) in 
~40 years, deleveraging metrics back to Liberation Day levels, and shifting 
investor sentiment from bullish in June to now bearish. If we were heading into 
end-cycle dynamics, with significant downward revisions to 2027 earnings, the 
capitulation process would certainly have further room to run. However, we see 
this more as an intra-cycle reset and another deleveraging event in the AI 
supercycle. With that in mind, there are a number of metrics that suggest the

Affordability should remain a hurdle for Consumer Cyclicals. Despite the recent 
drop in oil prices, we cannot fully discount the risk of a geopolitical re-escalation. While 
we see long-term value in consumer segments such as retail, apparel, restaurants—
particularly relative to Energy—consumer affordability remains a structural challenge, 
linked to oil prices, borrowing costs, and the structural housing shortage. While we also 
see a long-term value opportunity in the Housing complex (Home Improvement Stores, 
Home Retail, Building Products, Durable Goods, etc.), we would ideally need to see 
mortgage rates approach 6.0% for structural flows into these names. For investors 
seeking consumer exposure post-midterms, we prefer stocks tied to lower-ticket 
consumer items over those that depend on a sharp decline in interest rates to fund the 
purchase.

Implications for AI Infrastructure buildout. Democratic control of Congress makes 
a comprehensive federal AI regulatory no more likely than before the midterms, in our 
view. A national datacenter moratorium also looks unlikely given the high legislative bar, 
bipartisan support for datacenter investment in many districts and the President’s 
repeated stated support of the buildout. The story of 2026 has been the increasing shift 
of AI infrastructure buildout bottlenecks from AI upstream (e.g. memory, chips, 
etc.) to funding constraints and now to regulatory risks. Importantly, the binding 
constraints have instead moved to the states. Democrats won governor races in key 
datacenter states on platforms that run from an immediate moratorium (e.g. Texas) to 
pauses on new large-scale project (e.g. Georgia, New Mexico) to conditional 
moratoriums requiring datacenters to cover full utility costs (e.g. Ohio and Nevada, 
see Report). At the local level, 42 of 45 ballot measures to restrict datacenters have passed 
or are leaning towards passage. Nearly half of them are in Ohio with multiples likely 
ahead of the 2028 election. Our analysts expect 2027 AI capex to be relatively 
insulated as it supports projects already under construction and mostly funded. 
However, the growth curve will likely flatten ahead of 2028 and beyond as 
permitting becomes more difficult. Moratoriums will likely be temporary as opposed 
to transforming into the worst-case scenario of outright bans. The near-term effects of 
these changes will favor assets already built or fully permitted with new developments

flagging that leverage could still run higher if monetization is delayed. Overall, the AI 
infrastructure buildout trajectory remains intact with key risks around delays 
instead of cancellations or rollbacks.
