Scene of the Crime
High-Beta Momentum's Reclaimed Thursday's Drop, But History Says
Expect More Downside Ahead
WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW: 
Hiistorical data is useful to show us what has happened when similar events occurred
in the past. While you can almost always find a stat to fit your narrative, in many ways
that's no different than what traditional fundamental or technical analysts do. Everyone
has access to roughly the same data, and yet the conclusion depends on how the market
participant analyzes that data. There was no shortage of stats flying around today, and
while everyone has a different way to spin/view it, we consider high-beta momentum
stocks to be the dominant factor of the current market. As such, we tried to isolate the
recent trading by looking at a simple pattern: dates when the high-beta momentum
index saw at least a -10% daily decline, followed by a 5%+ daily gain within the next
three trading days. The results are extremely volatilie but with a pretty negative skew,
and importantly none of them marked the ultimate bottom for the correction or bear
market.
■
The Study: High-beta momentum long index has a -10% or worse daily decline,
followed by a 5%+ daily gain within the next three days. 30-day forward returns
were negative seven of eight times for an average return of -10%. All eight events
saw at least a -9% drawdown over the ensuing 50 days, and seven saw at least a
-16% drawdown.
■
Scene of the Crime. Often times price will return to the 'scene of the crime', which
is where the waterfall decline started last week. Once it re-visits that scene, price
then resumes in the direction of the impulsive move, in this case that would be to
the downside.
■
Overhead Supply. Looking at the volume distribution of SOXX, we can see how much
volume traded above $165. Any purchase made from May through early November
is now underwater. That is a lot of overhead supply and why even if we rally further
from here, price is unlikely to run away to the upside.
■
Where are We Wrong? The 200 DMA for SOXX is ~$160. If it were to trade firmly
above $165, we could then say that clearly demand outweighs supply and perhaps
the recent move was a 'false breakdown'. Short of that, however, we are viewing
rallies as counter-trend and thus opportunities to reduce exposure ahead of what
we think will be a full traverse back towards the April breakout point ($115-$120).
■
Thoughts on CPI. Amidst all the volatility the last few days, it seems as though
Friday's NFP and tomorrow's CPI report are some of the least talked about in recent
memory. To be sure, crude oil dropping ~20% in a few days, and the break in the
data center/AI trade have both been quite helpful to inflation expectations. One-
year swap spreads have fallen ~90bps in the last eight days, the largest such move in
at least the last six-years. Given some of that drop can be attributed to the fall in the
data center/AI space, you have to be careful what you wish for. With that all said,
we can probably say that if tomorrow's number is hot at all it will likely be looked
through, and if it's cool we can expect a knee-jerk reaction higher.
■
Reaction to the Reaction. Given our overall cautious view, we would view any post-
CPI pop as an opportunity to reduce risk. As always, it's the reaction to the reaction
that matters.
