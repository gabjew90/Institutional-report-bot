Summary
We reiterate our BUY rating and $155 Price Target for CoreWeave, Inc. (CRWV) following a
phenomenal Q3 FY2026 earnings print that shattered expectations and explicitly validated the
company’s success-based financing model. CoreWeave delivered Q3 revenue of $3.7 billion
(+168% YoY, adding >$1B sequentially), blowing past the high end of guidance. Adjusted
operating income reached $379 million, propelling operating margins into low-double-digits
(10%) a full quarter ahead of management’s original trajectory.
The primary catalyst emerging from the quarter is the sheer magnitude of CoreWeave's
embedded earnings power as its initial infrastructure fleet rolls off its primary debt facilities.
Backlog surged to an unprecedented $133 billion (up 138% YoY), while active power doubled
year-to-date to 1.7 gigawatts. More critically, management outlined that >300,000 GPUs will
come off their initial contracts across 2027 and 2028; infrastructure that is now fully paid off
and owned free and clear. At current market pricing (which has increased 30% to 50% QoQ
for legacy Ampere and Hopper architectures), renewing this paid-off fleet will generate up to
$10 billion in incremental annualized revenue requiring zero additional CapEx. Supported
by $9 billion in expected near-term cash prepayments, CoreWeave has definitively de-risked
its capital structure and raised its full-year FY26 revenue guidance to $13.0B–$13.3B.

Massive Top-Line Breakout: Q3 revenue surged to $3.7 billion (+168% YoY), adding over

Massive Cash Flow Generation: Q3 operating cash flow was $2.0 billion. CapEx totaled
$14.5 billion as management pulled forward deliveries from Q4 to meet overwhelming
demand.
Key Financial Trends & Strategic Highlights:
1. Full-Year FY2026 Outlook Raised and Path to GAAP Profitability
•
Guidance Raised: FY2026 revenue guidance was raised to $13.0B–$13.3B (from $11.7B–
$12.1B). Adjusted operating income guidance was raised to $1.275B–$1.375B. CapEx
expectations were tightened to $37B–$39B.
•
ARR Reaches >$20B: Management raised its Annualized Run Rate (ARR) guidance,
expecting to exit 2026 at $20 billion to $21 billion.
•
Path to Net Income: Adjusted net loss is expected to reduce meaningfully in 2027,
crossing into positive adjusted net income in the first half of 2028.

•
Self-Amortizing Fleet: CoreWeave’s infrastructure is financed by Delayed Draw Term
Loans (DDTLs) matched to specific customer contracts. By the end of the initial contract
term, the debt is fully repaid, leaving the infrastructure owned outright.
•
Massive Renewal Pipeline: In 2027, over 130,000 GPUs will come off contract, followed
by approximately 170,000 GPUs in 2028. At current pricing levels (which remain highly
elevated), renewing the 2027 fleet will generate approaching $4 billion of incremental
annualized revenue, scaling to nearly $10 billion when including the 2028 renewals, all
with zero additional hardware CapEx.
3. Pricing Power and Capacity Allocation Strategy
•
Pricing Increases Offset Costs: In July, CoreWeave increased pricing across SKUs by ~25%,
followed by another 10% later in Q3, with pricing continuing to move higher in Q4.
•
The Short-Term Premium: CoreWeave will continue allocating a strategic share of
capacity to short-term contracts when pricing warrants. Capturing these premiums could
be particularly valuable if the recent elections complicate bringing capacity online. Short-
term capacity currently commands more than $40 million per megawatt annually, versus
$20 million to $25 million for standard four- to five-year commitments. These premiums
enable CoreWeave to recoup hardware CapEx within 6 to 18 months.
4. Capital Structure De-Risked: Shifting from Unsecured to Asset-Backed Debt
•
$9 Billion in Cash Prepayments: CoreWeave expects to collect roughly $9 billion in
prepayments and deposits in the next three months. Strikingly, >$3 billion of this is
prepayment for existing, fully paid-off infrastructure; pure cash inflow requiring zero
debt service.
•
No Unsecured Debt Needed in 2027: Due to the soaring cost of unsecured debt
(with secondary yields previously spiking), CEO Mike Intrator confirmed CoreWeave will
not use the unsecured debt market to fund the business at current yields. Instead,
the company will rely on investment-grade asset-level DDTLs, prepayments, and OEM
financing. CoreWeave expects to raise less than $5 billion in incremental parent-level
capital through 2027.
5. CoreWeave Omni and the Shift to Enterprise
•
CoreWeave Omni (Asset-Light Growth): Management highlighted CoreWeave Omni,
a new platform allowing customers to run workloads on CoreWeave software while
maintaining direct ownership of the underlying infrastructure, providing a massive lever
for capital-light expansion.
•
Enterprise Penetration (CoreWeave Forge): Demand is rapidly diversifying beyond
frontier AI labs. More than half of new customers this year originated outside the tech/AI
lab sector, with 10% of Fortune 500 companies now CoreWeave customers. CoreWeave
Forge (the unified development environment) is the primary land-and-expand vehicle,
with 70% of new cloud customers in Q3 already utilizing Forge.

CoreWeave (CRWV): Solidifies its position as the preeminent execution layer for the AI

Risks to Our Analysis
While CRWV presents a compelling investment opportunity, several risks warrant consideration:
•
Customer Concentration: One customer makes up 71% of CRWV’s revenue, and even with the large
backlog of RPO driven mainly by OpenAI, that would leave only two customers making up the bulk of
the company’s revenue stream. Additionally, due to current capacity constraints, it is unclear whether
CRWV can maintain its current RPO growth levels, as customers may seek alternative options with
more current capacity (e.g., Microsoft’s deal with Nebius).
•
Investor Concentration: Another core risk to the long thesis is investor concentration. Magnetar, an
early investor in the company, currently owns roughly ~25% of the shares and has been aggressively
trying to sell a portion of its position since its lock-up period ended after CRWV reported its Jun Q
results. The lock-up expiration also served as a short-selling catalyst for some investors as they saw
a forced selling opportunity.
•
Total Addressable Market (TAM) isn’t as Large: Core to the overall thesis, not only to CRWV, but
to the underlying sector, is that the TAM is large and growing, with the potential for inferencing to
be meaningfully larger than training. If the Hyperscalers begin to realize that TAM is not as large as
they forecasted and pull back on spending, that will have a catastrophic impact on the Neocloud
ecosystem, CRWV in particular. Additionally, it would likely force CRWV and others to compete on
price and thus drive earnings revisions lower, coupled with difficulty in securing financing as the
market realizes the TAM is smaller than what the market had underwritten.
•
Competition: CRWV operates in a highly competitive environment. While its platform is designed
specifically for AI, major Hyperscalers like Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, and GOOG
Cloud Platform have extensive resources and may increasingly tailor their offerings for AI workloads,
potentially reducing CRWV's specialized niche. Other AI cloud providers, such as Crusoe, Lambda,
and Nebius, are direct competitors and could ramp up price competition or replicate CRWV's unique
offerings. Additionally, large Hyperscalers (e.g., GOOG's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium/Inferentia) and
major AI labs might develop their own custom AI silicon, decreasing their dependence on third-party
GPU providers like CRWV.
•
Power and Real Estate Constraints: Although CRWV has proactively secured a substantial contracted
power capacity of 3.1 GW, the industry still faces a widespread challenge due to the limited availability
of power and suitable real estate for high-density AI data centers. Future growth may be constrained
if new power sources or sites become too costly or inaccessible.
•
Legislative and Regulatory Impact: Evolving laws related to AI development, data privacy, and energy
consumption could affect the profitability or business models of AI infrastructure providers. Policy
changes might influence customer demand or increase compliance costs, especially as AI becomes
more integrated into critical applications and public discussions.
•
Technology Obsolescence/Rapid Innovation Cycle: The fast pace of AI hardware innovation,
highlighted by NVDA's quick 12-month GPU release cycle, means CRWV must consistently invest
heavily to acquire the latest chips. Falling behind could make its infrastructure less competitive,
forcing significant capital expenses for upgrades. NVDA's rapid 12-month GPU design cycle presents a
double-edged sword for CRWV; while it offers CRWV's "bleeding-edge" advantage, it also necessitates
continuous, massive capital expenditure to avoid technological obsolescence. This requires sustained
access to capital and efficient asset management to maximize the economic life of each GPU
generation.
•
Financing Risk/Capital Intensity: Despite innovative GPU-backed lending, CRWV's business remains
highly capital-intensive. Maintaining its rapid growth will require ongoing access to substantial
debt and equity funding, which could be affected by market conditions or investor interest in
AI infrastructure. Although CRWV has demonstrated unique financing capabilities, its reliance on
substantial debt for expansion makes it vulnerable to interest rate fluctuations and the availability of
specialized lending. A tightening credit market or a shift in investor sentiment toward capital-heavy
AI projects could hinder future growth.
