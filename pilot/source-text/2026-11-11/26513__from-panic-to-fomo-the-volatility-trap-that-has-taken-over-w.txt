The volatility trap
From panic to FOMO in less than a week. VIX has collapsed from 28 to 20, short-dated hedges have
been crushed and the very flows that amplified the selloff are now helping fuel the rebound. Dealer
hedging, vol-control strategies and leveraged ETFs are increasingly dictating market direction. Yet
beneath the volatility collapse, Nomura warns that underlying instability remains extreme, with
realized VVIX volatility hitting record levels. The panic is gone, but the fragility isn't. Welcome
to the volatility trap.
All about vol
The equity rebound has been as much about market mechanics as genuine buying conviction.
Nomura's McElligott highlights the extraordinary collapse in VIX from 28 to 20 in roughly 2.5 sessions,
triggering massive dealer hedging flows. As volatility collapsed, dealers were forced to buy back short
delta, creating a powerful self-reinforcing rally. The catch? As the volatility crush stalled, so did the
equity rally. The market has been trading the volatility, not the other way around.

Anatomy of a fragility shock
Thursday's crash was no ordinary selloff. BofA highlights the 4.4% SPX collapse and 6.4% NDX plunge,
with VIX exploding 11.6 points in a single session. The SPX move was one of the most extreme intraday
drawdowns relative to overnight performance since 2008, while the VIX shock rivaled the fragility
events of February 2018 and August 2024. BofA argues that positioning and reflexive feedback loops,
rather than fundamentals, were the dominant drivers. This was a market structure accident, not
necessarily a fundamental breakdown.
BofA
Overshoot gone
The extreme VIX overshoot during Thursday's crash has now normalized, with the gap between VIX
and SPX basically closed. The volatility panic has faded almost as quickly as it erupted.

The reset
The VIX term structure has seen a massive reset at the short end of the curve. The crowd rushed to
load up on short-term hedges in panic, only to see those hedges crushed by the market rebound and
the violent repricing of short-dated volatility. The cost of panic hedging has been brutal.
Vixcentral
Vol panic sellers
Vol-control funds have slashed exposure as volatility exploded higher. We have already seen a rather
aggressive reset in vols. Keep realized volatility drifting lower, and the vol-control bid should gradually
find its way back into the market. The same flows that amplified the selloff could soon become a
powerful tailwind.
Nomura
The defensive panic trade
The rotation out of tech has reached extraordinary levels. BofA highlights that the one-month
performance spread between defensives and technology has stretched to extremes last seen during
the dotcom era. The rush into safety has become extreme, leaving the trade vulnerable to a violent
reversal. Combine this with the massive positioning reset and collapsing volatility, and the ingredients
for another powerful tech squeeze are firmly in place. The next pain trade could be defensives
underperforming as tech comes roaring back.

BofA
The great AI fragility
Even before the last market rout, BofA highlighted an extraordinary development beneath the surface.
US tech is on track for its second most fragile year in over three decades, surpassed only by 2025.
Earnings reactions are becoming increasingly violent, despite fundamentals not deteriorating at the
same pace. The problem is no longer just earnings. This is what happens when conviction disappears
and positioning takes over.
BofA
Tail wagging the dog
The market's mechanical accelerators are getting bigger. Nomura highlights leveraged ETF assets of
roughly $142 billion, near the 94th percentile, with an estimated $6.5 billion of rebalancing exposure
for every 1% market move, near the 95th percentile. Combine this with volatility-targeting strategies
and increasingly aggressive options flows, and the potential for self-reinforcing moves in either
direction becomes substantial. Modern market structure is increasingly built to amplify volatility, not
absorb it.
Nomura
More violent than Volmageddon
Volatility inside the volatility. Nomura's McElligott highlights an extraordinary development: the 10- and
20-day realized volatility of VVIX have surged to record highs over a 10-year lookback, exceeding even
COVID, Liberation Day and Volmageddon. This is not simply another volatility spike. The volatility of
volatility itself is becoming unstable. McElligott warns that the market may be entering a regime of
increasingly violent two-way moves, with a structurally higher VIX floor. The VIX may have collapsed,
but the underlying instability hasn't disappeared.

Positioning tailwind, Fed headwind
Privorotsky argues that rising yields remain a headwind, but equities have some cushion. Another leg
higher in rates would become more problematic, particularly if oil keeps inflation sticky and the Fed
hawkish. Credit spreads remain the key risk gauge. Meanwhile, gross exposure has been cut, net
positioning is historically low and volatility remains elevated. That creates room for a squeeze as
volatility decays. Positioning is the tailwind. The Fed remains the headwind. The squeeze has room to
run, but the market is still one volatility shock away from reversing the entire feedback loop.
