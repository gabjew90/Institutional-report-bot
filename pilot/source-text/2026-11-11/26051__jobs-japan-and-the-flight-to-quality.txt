Jobs, Japan, and the Flight to Quality
Dimitrios Laloudakis
06-Nov-2026, 07:22 ET (EST)
*Sales desk commentary. Not a product of UBS Research. For institutional investors only.*
• United States: Nonfarm payrolls expected to come in at +70k, mostly sourced from private payrolls (+65k),
with an unemployment rate of 4.2%. The current run-rate for the labor market remains robust (figure #1 plots
the 3m, 6m, and 12m rolling averages). Furthermore, the diffusion of private payrolls is increasing to cycle
highs (see figure #2). Messaging from Fed’s Williams was that the FOMC is on a gradual tightening bias but
that current indicators are not consistent with troublesome inflation, particularly wage growth which is
estimated to print +2.9%, which if materialized, would be the lowest reading this cycle and approaching the
2010s average (see figure #3). Safe-haven demand across the board in FX (nearly +2 std dev move across
most G10 pairs)  
• Japan: Nominal cash earnings rise by 4.6% vs. +3.8% y/y estimates with real cash earnings of +2.2% vs.
+1.3% estimates (see figure #4) . The current run-rate is the highest on record and reinforces the BoJ’s rate
hiking cycle with JPY 1y1y of 1.87% implying slightly more than two hikes over the horizon. However, JGBs
rally today reflects a flight to quality aspect comparable to that seen in USTs the prior day. The entire JGB
curve outside of the 30y space is bid by 10bps+.
• Canada: PM Carney budget release focused on an “investment supercycle” that leverages Canada’s
revenue increases from elevated energy prices into productive business / capex expansion. Limited immediate
implications for CAD given the slow-moving consequences on the exchange rate. The initial level of Canada’s
net debt (% of GDP) allows latitude to spend on an absolute basis and relative basis to G10.
