GS TMT SPEC SALES – Wednesday 11th November 
US trading was choppy after October core CPI rose 0.3% versus 0.2% expected, with broad-based
underlying strength. Tech was mixed Nvidia lower as it fell another ~6% amid an independent investigation,
NYT reporting and a possible House subpoena, with continued selling by long investors and little buying
support. While US semis better Broadcom (+3.8%) and AMD (+0.8%) appeared to benefit, while volumes
excluding Nvidia were light. 
CoreWeave was the overnight focus, raising guidance as demand and backlog remained strong, with
customer prepayments helping fund near-term needs. However, management stepped back from issuing
unsecured debt at current yields, highlighting continued funding concerns for neoclouds despite the equity
bounce. Hyperscaler five-year CDS and bond spreads were largely unchanged. Nebius and Cerebras report
tomorrow, offering a further test of backlog quality and access to financing. Until CDS spreads and
unsecured borrowing costs stabilise or fall meaningfully, credit markets will be the driver.   
For AI bulls, TSMC co-COO Kevin Zhang told the Nikkei Forum that leading-edge chip demand remains
“insatiable”, with customers seeking any available compute capacity (Nikkei Asia).
 
EUROPEAN TOWERS IN FOCUS:
• INWIT flagging that AGCOM and other Italian authorities are seeking to accelerate resolution of its disputes with
Swisscom and Telecom Italia. This could shorten the uncertainty, although their legal powers remain unclear,
and Andrew/GIR cautions that it does not necessarily improve the chances of a favourable outcome.
• CELLNEX/AMT: Telefónica reportedly wants to renegotiate tower rents with American Tower, to which it
sold its infrastructure network for €7.7bn five years ago. According to El Confidencial, the annual bill now exceeds
€2bn, absorbing 21% of operating profit and reducing cash available for dividends—another challenge for
chairman Marc Murtra’s “Transform & Growth” strategy. TEF has already done a bunch of re-negotiations with
Tower names so not clear if this is new until we speak to the company 
 
DATACENTRES: AI capex durability is now a financing and power story. CoreWeave overnight shows the
funding path is improving (equity +7% AH watch the CDS reaction), Top of Mind argues credit quality will
decide the winners, and SoftBank shows power scarcity can still derail timelines. Net, net favour
operators with secured capital and controlled power.
• CoreWeave's beat confirms datacentre demand is strong. ~7% AH; CDS/bond levels (pre-print ~1,150 bp 5Y
CDS, bonds >15–16% yield) New 4–5 year contracts are pricing at $20–25mn/MW, and estimated unlevered IRRs
have risen to 20–30%. Its financing is also clearer: no further parent-level unsecured debt through 2027, with most
remaining capex funded through DDTLs. See GIR Gabriela note 
• The GS Top of Mind note “Is the AI investment cycle durable? “ AI spending will hold up, but credit quality will
decide the winners. Hyperscalers are best placed, while neoclouds funding long-term commitments with uncertain
revenues are more exposed. Gabriela Borges argues CoreWeave's wider CDS spreads reflect hedging rather
than distress, and that leading projects can still secure financing even as high-yield AI issuance slows. Full note

• SoftBank's $3.73bn write-down adds the physical constraint. Even well-capitalised players are limited by
power, and multi-year turbine lead times plus post-midterm uncertainty can delay capacity and make mistimed
equipment commitments costly. Together, the notes point to dispersion. Constrained supply is what supports
CoreWeave's pricing, but it also punishes players that cannot secure power on time. That fits Palihapitiya's view
that operators controlling land, power and buildings will outperform those leasing capacity. 
• The one other point that investors are debating and shouldn't be overlooked is what happens if Jensen is no
longer driving things at Nvidia how does this impact the broader circular finance/backstop debate
 
RESULTS
• INWIT 3Q26: Results were in line with consensus and 2026 guidance was reiterated, but the more important
takeaway was a modest positive on the ongoing contract disputes with Swisscom and Telecom Italia. Several
Italian authorities are looking to accelerate the resolution process, which could shorten the period of uncertainty.
GS stresses this doesn’t necessarily increase the probability of a favourable outcome, and INWIT’s growth
outlook remains heavily dependent on how the disputes are resolved, but faster resolution should nevertheless be
supportive. Should be modestly positive.
 
• Telecom Italia reported 3Q results in line with expectations. Guidance was reiterated, with the FCF miss
due to working capital phasing expected to reverse in 4Q. Full year consensus estimates therefore unlikely to
shift materially. Retail service revenue growth was stable with net adds improving slightly, B2B saw ongoing
momentum again driven by cloud, and the annualisation of MVNO phasing provided additional support. As a result,
we expect investor focus to remain on broader, longer-term strategic questions such as the implications of Poste's
recent stake build. See GIR take
 
• ITV: Missed and guided below as digital ad revenues slowed. 3Q26 revenue missed consensus by 1%, driven
by a 2% shortfall was in Media & Entertainment (M&E – this is the business that is in the process of getting
sold), while Studios matched consensus but missed GS estimates by 3%. TV advertising revenue fell 8% versus
guidance of around 5%, and digital advertising growth slowed to 6% over nine months from 13% in 1H (like
ad supported tiers of competitors); non-advertising revenue was broadly flat. ITV expects 4Q advertising
revenue to fall 5–10%, versus GS/consensus forecasts of 1.3%/1.8% declines, taking the FY26 decline to 2–3% as
weak demand and competition from ad-supported streaming weigh. To cushion this, ITV announced £30mn of
temporary 4Q savings (£25mn in content and £5mn elsewhere), with a full-year content budget of around £1.2bn.
Studios guidance is unchanged: good revenue growth, weighted to 4Q, and margins at the lower end of 13–
15%. GS cut FY26/27 adjusted EPS estimates by 2%/9%. Although consensus 2027 EPS could face double-digit
downgrades, GS sees scope for a more limited share-price reaction because the weakness is confined to M&E,
whose sale to Sky remains on track for 2H27, (the Public Interest Intervention Notice was expected). See GIR
note

TECH
• IFX off the call - Key takeaways are upside to the 2027 margin guide, and supply capping growth not demand.
Margins guided to mid-20s segment margin in 2027. The margin gets help from volume growth, a richer AI mix,
price increases, lower idle capacity and Step Up savings. It is held back by higher input and foundry costs, a
EUR300m rise in depreciation to 2.2bn, and Dresden start-up costs that weigh on Q1 and Q2. Management sees
upside from stronger revenue, unfinished pricing talks and FX. A EUR/USD rate of 1.12 instead of 1.15 would add
50bps to Q1 alone. 
DC revs should reach about EUR4bn, with CPUs a key driver. Auto should grow mid-teens broadly on
MCU share gains and Ethernet, and the high-voltage drag disappears from FY28. SiC rises from about
700m to above 1bn.
Infineon is still supply-constrained, but Dresden is ramping twice as fast as previous fabs. Backlog went
up again (no longer disclosed), but signed and under-negotiation multi-year supply arrangements and
capacity reservations with customers represent a combined revenue volume exceeding EUR 10 billion.
Humanoid robot content was raised to about $1000 but is years from mattering. Flow wise seen HF 2
way. 
 
• NVIDIA -5.5%, US lawmakers are voicing opinions which could trigger increased restrictions on
NVIDIA’s dealings with China. {NSN TOAZZ86EABR5 <GO>}
 
• TSMC (2330) Oct sales came in at NT$586b (+15%MoM, +60%YoY), tracking at 34% of 4Q26 consensus Inline;
besides, board approved to pay NT$9/shr cash dividend for 3Q26 results and will be paid out on 2027/4/8. (ED)
 
• Hon Hai (2317) will partner with Wave Photonics in the UK to focus on quantum computing and new photonic
materials, a step closer to IC design capabilities. (DT)
 
• GIR remains Neutral on TeamViewer post 3Q26, with the key concern being weaker growth: ARR and
revenue missed expectations, SMB remains under pressure, and Enterprise pipeline conversion is expected to
slow in 4Q, while EBITDA was broadly in line. Mohammed Moawalla cuts FY26 ARR growth to c.-1% and
revenue growth to c.-2% YoY pro forma, the bottom end of downgraded guidance, and lowers FY27-30 revenue
growth estimates by c.2-3% to reflect the weaker exit rate. Margin assumptions are also refined, with FY26
adjusted EBITDA margin now at 43.3%, broadly consistent with guidance of >43%. Overall, GIR’s take is that
near-term growth remains challenged despite resilient margins, supporting the continued Neutral stance.
 
TELCOS 
• INWIT 3Q26: Results were in line with consensus and 2026 guidance was reiterated, but the more important
takeaway was a modest positive on the ongoing contract disputes with Swisscom and Telecom Italia. Several
Italian authorities are looking to accelerate the resolution process, which could shorten the period of uncertainty.
GS stresses this doesn’t necessarily increase the probability of a favourable outcome, and INWIT’s growth
outlook remains heavily dependent on how the disputes are resolved, but faster resolution should nevertheless be
supportive. Overall, GS expects a modestly positive share-price reaction.
 

• Telecom Italia reported 3Q results in line with expectations. Guidance was reiterated, with the FCF miss
due to working capital phasing expected to reverse in 4Q. Full year consensus estimates therefore unlikely to
shift materially as a result, in our view. TI has delivered another quarter of broadly consistent fundamental
delivery — retail service revenue growth was stable with net adds improving slightly, B2B saw ongoing momentum
again driven by cloud, and the annualisation of MVNO phasing provided additional support. As a result, we expect
investor focus to remain on broader, longer-term strategic questions such as the implications of Poste's recent
stake build. 
 
• Sunrise getting investor questions after its call about its net cost savings, debt-reduction plans and the meaning of
“inorganic options” – is that assets sales (towever) or a raise or a per divi, not clear and management added that
“we refrain from any speculation about any of those until we have taken a conclusion”. Interesting ahead of a
forthcoming Ziggo spin-off. The short thesis rests on worsening negative cash flow—GIR has FCF declining in
2027 and then remaining broadly flat (the Street has FCF flat in 2027 and beyond). Bears are highlighting: 1)
uncertainty over dividend funding over the next two years, with net debt/EBITDA potentially nearing 5x by the end
of next year
 
• Vodafone call: cost savings were the main focus. Andrew sees significant further upside to the cost-cutting
programme, while I remain more cautious given Germany’s underlying performance. The CFO said net savings
would be well above €200mn, and the CEO suggested they would approach a quarter of the current €1bn four-
year target. Management also left room for further upgrades as visibility improves, but said the planned 19%
workforce reduction in Germany was already included in May’s guidance range. Despite a direct question from
Ratzer, there was no comment on Xavier Niel’s influence: he is not yet on the board, and it remains unclear
whether he has started applying pressure directly.
Germany’s growth outlook remains the key question. Management still expects quarter-on-quarter
improvement, with no change in competition and better customer satisfaction. Business services and
consumer broadband are performing positively, but higher-margin mobile remains weaker. Investors are
therefore likely to remain sceptical until organic service revenue growth turns positive and gross margins
strengthen.
 
• Freenet and TEFD extended their term partnership. They have signed a detailed term sheet with commercial
terms confidential. They are not yet commenting on potential upside from the deal, including revenue and EBITDA.
Both management teams are now working on the final detailed contract, which they expect to conclude by FY26.
FY26 guidance therefore remains intact, including the €50m GP/EBITDA negative impact from Telefonica
agreement. However, a successful and attractive final agreement could create upside to Freenet’s medium-term
guidance.

MACRO AND TRADING 
• US TRADING: Another choppy tape, triggered by an upside surprise in the Oct CPI reading this AM (core +30bps
vs cons 20bps) with a wide breadth of strength contributing to underlying components. This came on the heels of
Waller's overnight comments that raised the possibility of larger moves in the event of a warmer core inflation print,
which gives a bit more runway for the market to reopen the right tail around the policy path following the recent

compression in hike risks. Today’s reading likely locked in a 25bps hike in Dec (mkt currently pricing a %110
probability) and one more 1H 27. As expected yields backed up (10YR +10bps to 4.98%) and rate sensitive
pockets bled lower.   
Elsewhere, performance in tech was mixed with broader semis/AI catching down with NVDA with the stock down
another ~6% today on the back of independent investigation + NYT reporting + GOP house weighing subpoena.
Our desk continued to see Long supply in the name and very little defense with so much uncertainty against
difficult macro/political backdrop. Notable to see AVGO (+3.8%) and AMD (+80bps) continue to trade like primary
beneficiaries of NVDA news. Ex NVDA, market-wide volumes were LIGHT (SPX constituents tracking -15%) with
desk activity, across sectors, on the quieter side. Our floor was a 5 on a 1-10 scale in terms of overall activity levels
with Asset Mgrs finishing moderate net sellers driven by supply in large cap tech. HFs finished net sellers with
supply (short > long) concentrated in tech and macro products. POST BELL: CRWV: +5% .. Q3 Revs: $3.661b
(vs guide $3.45b - $3.6b)… Q3 Adj OI: $379m (vs $200-$260m guide)
