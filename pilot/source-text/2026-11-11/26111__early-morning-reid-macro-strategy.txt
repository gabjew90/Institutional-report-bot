Markets have put in a very strong performance over the last 24 hours, with all the main risk factors fading into 
the background. First, the fears around AI-related growth eased significantly, which pushed the Philly 
semiconductor index (+6.85%) to its best daily performance since July. So in turn, that meant the S&P 
500 (+2.35%) built on Friday’s recovery and posted its strongest day since the Iran ceasefire 
announcement in April. The index is now "only" down -0.25% in the four business days since the 
midterms. Second, there was growing optimism on the geopolitical front, as hopes for diplomatic progress 
between the US and Iran pushed Brent crude (-5.85%) to a 2-month low of $92.57/bbl. And third, the risk-
on tone meant the fiscal concerns in Europe eased, with the Franco-German 10yr spread (-3.4bps) 
tightening to a 3-week low of 147bps. So all round, investors became a lot more optimistic on the near-term 
outlook, and the VIX index (-5.72pts) posted its sharpest daily decline since April 2025 when equities 
rebounded from their post-Liberation Day sell-off. 
This strength was clearest in the most AI-exposed assets, which continued to recover from last 
Thursday’s slump. Indeed, the Philly semiconductor index is up +11.35% in the last two sessions, having 
now unwound almost two thirds of its -15% decline following the midterm election results. There wasn’t 
an obvious catalyst for the bounceback, but as our equity strategists noted, equity positioning had 
become sizeably underweight after last week’s moves, and in tech, positioning was now beneath its 
lows after Liberation Day. So that meant the conditions were in place for a sharp recovery. Indeed, the only 
company which moved lower in the Philly semiconductor index yesterday was Nvidia (-4.27%), which 
hit a 7-month low after the company’s announcement of an independent investigation involving CEO Jensen 
Huang. Remember we have earnings from CoreWeave as well after tonight’s US close, so that’ll be one 
to watch. 
Whilst tech stocks were bouncing back sharply yesterday, the other positive catalyst was the latest decline in 
oil prices, which followed several headlines that raised hopes about a diplomatic resolution. For instance, a 

Qatari official told Bloomberg that both the US and Iran were talking with mediators on the details of a 
deal. Moreover, there was also a reaction to Trump’s weekend comments to CNN that it was “highly 
unlikely” he’d order extensive strikes on Iran, even as he said it would depend on them signing a “very strong 
agreement”. Late in the US session yesterday, Trump also posted that there was “positive momentum 
toward an end to this Conflict” and suggested that Gulf allies “prefer the status quo”, even as the US 
was “locked and loaded” if developments in the region change. 
So that led to optimism that renewed escalation would be avoided, with Brent crude (-5.85%) ending the 
session at a 2-month low of $92.57/bbl and down another -1.21% this morning to $91.45/bbl. 
That combination of tech positivity and oil declines effectively led to the reverse of the stagflation 
playbook, with investors feeling more positive on growth, and less worried about inflation. In terms of 
the reaction in US Treasury yields, it was the risk-on reaction that dominated, with 2yr yields rising +5.0bps 
to 4.56% thanks to a +11.2bps rise in the real yield, even as the inflation breakeven (-6.2bps) fell back. 
Indeed, the optimism on near-term inflation meant that 1yr US inflation swap (-13.6bps) closed beneath 
2% again for the first time since mid-August. However, when it came to the Fed, the easing financial stress 
won out, and investors grew more confident that they would deliver a rate hike in December, with 
market pricing up from 78% on Friday to an 84% chance by the close. Further out the curve, the 10yr 
(+2.6bps to 4.88%) and 30yr (+1.4bps) yields saw more modest increases. 
With all that happening, today will also see the latest US CPI print for October, which is the last CPI print 
ahead of the Fed’s decision next month. Last month’s CPI print for September was a very strong one, with 
the fastest monthly headline CPI in six months, at +0.65%. However, this time, our US economists expect 
inflation to have come down from its September pace, with both headline and core at +0.20% on the 
month. However, this report will be slightly unusual because we won’t get a year-on-year number for 
inflation. That’s because of last year’s government shutdown, which meant the October 2025 inflation level 
wasn’t produced, so there isn’t a number from last year to compare it to. Otherwise, remember that 
markets will also be focused on the read-through to the PCE inflation print (the Fed’s target measure), 
but we’ll have to wait until the PPI release on Friday for some more of the components.  
Ahead of that, Asian stocks are rallying hard again this morning with US futures consolidating. Contracts 
on the S&P 500 (+0.21%) and Nasdaq 100 (+0.08%) are relatively quiet. The big move has come from the 
KOSPI (+4.73%), which is bouncing back after three days of consecutive declines. The Nikkei 225 (+2.82%), 
TOPIX (+2.06%) S&P/ASX 200 (+1.10%) and Taiwan TAIEX (+2.31%) are also advancing.  Elsewhere, the 
Hang Seng (+0.37%), CSI 300 (+0.36%) and Shanghai Composite (+0.33%) continue to eke out gains. This 
morning, the 10yr US Treasury yield is back down -1.0bps to 4.87%. Euro Stoxx futures are +0.84%, 
having closed before the last leg of the US gains last night.  
The rise in European futures carries on from an optimistic session on the continent yesterday, as the tech 
rebound and lower oil prices boosted risk assets. The STOXX 600 (+1.26%), the FTSE 100 (+1.02%), and the 
DAX (+1.73%) all rose strongly. Moreover, France’s CAC 40 (+1.16%) hit a two-month high, whilst Italy’s 
FTSE MIB (+2.69%) posted its best performance since April. Then for sovereign bonds, there was also a 
clear risk-on move, with 10yr bund yields (+0.9bps) edging up to 3.26%, but with 10yr OATs (-2.3bps) and 
BTPs (-2.7bps) falling back. 

Finally, we didn’t get much data yesterday, although there were the latest inflation expectations numbers 
from the NY Fed. They showed 1yr inflation expectations moving up a basis point to 3.91%, the highest 
since May 2023. And the 3yr measure also inched up 4 basis points to 3.29%, which is the highest print 
since June. So that leant against the otherwise disinflationary newsflow yesterday. Meanwhile, there 
were also signs of pessimism on the labour market, as expectations of higher unemployment mounted. 
Indeed, the mean probability that the US unemployment rate would be higher in a year rose to 45.3%, the 
highest since April 2020 during the pandemic. 
Looking at the day ahead now, the main data highlight will be the US CPI print for October, and we’ll also get 
the NFIB’s small business optimism index for October. From central banks, we’ll hear from the Fed’s Waller, 
Logan, Schmid, Bowman and Paulson, along with the ECB’s Sleijpen, Cipollone, Lane and Escriva, and the 
BoE’s Breeden, Lombardelli and Pill. Finally, today’s earnings releases include CoreWeave.
