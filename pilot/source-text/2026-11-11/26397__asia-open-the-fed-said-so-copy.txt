Asia Open - The Fed said so
By Preston Pagli
The hotter-than-expected October US CPI report dominated price action Tuesday, reigniting concerns that inflation is
proving more persistent than policymakers had hoped. Core CPI rose 0.3% MoM, driving a repricing across the front-end
(+17bs) of the UST curve and pushing Jan hike bets over 60%, with Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan (2027 voter)
saying at least two 25bps hikes are needed. The rates selloff weighed across assets with gold -2.5%, DXY +0.6%, and
SPX -38bps. US equity breadth improved into the afternoon with weakness largely being driven by Nvidia (-5.5%) while
the Fed's Bowman flagged the risk of “a large correction in equity valuations” stemming from AI returns. Looking ahead,
expect light liquidity Wednesday with the US bond market closed in observance of Veterans Day while the equity market
will remain open. The macro calendar will be light with the Fed's Paulson slated to speak on the economic outlook at
23:30 GMT and Korea export/import data at 00:00 GMT.

In Focus‌
Rates: US Core CPI printed hotter than expected at 0.3% MoM vs. 0.2% MoM driven by a broad range of subcomponents
suggesting signs of pass through inflation are here. The market repriced accordingly through a "higher-for-longer lens", with
front-end UST yields surging 17bps and OIS now pricing the Jan FOMC meeting at 61%. The most intriguing development
now is that a 50bp hike appears to be on the table, with Fed Governor Waller prior to the CPI print suggesting that a 0.3%
core CPI print or higher could make a 50bp rate hike in December "necessary", while Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan (2027
voter) in remarks said at least two additional 25bp rate hikes are needed. Our US economists are now tracking Core PCE at
+0.33%, but that will change with PPI due Friday. Volumes in US 2s were roughly 150% greater than average with positioning
moving tactically long with risks concentrated close to current prices. Our positioning expert David Bieber suggests larger
moves are needed to force systematic rebalancing flows. Recent longs in 10y futures look vulnerable below 106-00 (ref 106-
06), while key resistance sits at 108-30, leaving the near-term bias skewed toward cheaper yields.
US Treasury Auction Recap: $42 billion of 10-year notes. The auction tailed the 1pm market by 0.1bp to yield 5.02%. The
bid/cover ratio came in at 2.46 which is at the 36th percentile of the past 12 auctions
Equities: Following a round of whipsawing in the morning around the CPI report, price action and participation subdued by
the afternoon with SPX -38bps, NDX -60bps, and RTY -68bps. A sharp deterioration in breadth turned around with SPX
advancers vs. decliners roughly split. Beneath the surface, vol was mostly contained with VVIX dipping below 100. On the

cash equity desk, inflows were skewed toward Consumer Discretionary, Energy, Consumer Staples and Utilities and outflows
in Info Tech, Financials, Industrials, Healthcare, Comm Services, Materials and REITs. Our trading strategy desk notes the
market may face selling pressure from Systematic Accounts, but the team prefers to remain invested by barbelling the
'efficient AI' basket (CGTSTOKN) with the Free Cash Flow Low Risk (CGTSFCFR) basket to ride out the choppiness.​
FX: The dollar followed Dec rate hike odds higher with DXY +0.60% to 102.26. USDJPY was a notable mover, nearly topping
159 for the first time since September as the US and Japan rate differential widened further. The LatAm complex severely
underperformed with BRL and COP both weakening more than 1.2% against the greenback. On the eTrading desk, volumes
were 15% average across the board, most notably in USDJPY (+50%), USDZAR (+60%), and USDTHB (+125%). Net flows
on the desk were skewed CNH, ILS, and NOK, while the leveraged segment were buyers of USD and AUD. Outflows led in
NZD and PLN.
Commodities: WTI crude (-0.5%) and EU Natural gas bifurcated on renewed concerns of the EU supply balance into the
winter months. TTF rose 6% but remains well below the highs of last month. Corn (+2%) and Soybean (+3%) futures surged
after the USDA posted a bigger than expected cut to yield and production. Precious metals gold and silver slumped 2.8% and
3.7% respectively on the back of the firmer dollar.
Elsewhere‌
BRL briefly reached a near 17-month low vs. the dollar after a more dovish than expected meeting minutes among other
headwinds. USDBRL topped 5.60 for the first since the Monday after Lula's surprise run-off election victory in October. Our
trader Gabriel Groh also flagged weaker ToT, a firmer dollar, and season factors for the move. Attention will remain firmly on
the Minister of Finance pick which is expected this month. 
CAD traded mostly unchanged after BoC meeting minutes leaned hawkish, with officials flagging “considerable uncertainty”
about economic headwinds and global developments, but said that uncertainty did not mean inaction. Dec OIS odds jumped
from 40% to roughly 48%. Our economists believe the BoC will remain on hold in December. 
Ahead:
KRW: Exports 10 Days YoY at 00:00 GMT for November, alongside Imports 10 Days YoY. This print offers an early indication
of export trends. Our macro strategist has been warning that outright levels of activity remain resilient but underlying trends
are slowing. This needs to be closely watched especially if we see more broad based AI weakness.
