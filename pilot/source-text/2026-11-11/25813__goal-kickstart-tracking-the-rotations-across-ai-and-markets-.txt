Tracking the rotations across AI and markets post the midterms
After the Democrats won last week’s midterm elections with 
a larger-than-expected majority in the House and Senate, 
markets have remained focused on political and regulatory 
uncertainty around energy and the AI/data center buildout. 
Brent crude prices fell over 16% due to the potential for 
deescalation in the Middle East, while US equity markets 
sold off sharply at the end of last week, with one of the 
largest spikes in Nasdaq volatility since March 2020 (Exhibit 
1). US labor market data was stronger-than-expected with a 
very strong NFP print (+165k), the unemployment rate down 
at 4.1% and initial jobless claims at 57-year lows. 
Our Risk Appetite Indicator (RAI) declined to -0.6, with 
Global Growth expectations (RAI PC1) weakening and 
Monetary Policy (RAI PC2) picking up modestly (Exhibit 14). 
Broad positioning has also turned lighter post-selloff, with 
our sentiment and positioning indicator falling to the lowest 
reading since April 2025 (Exhibit 35). Equity volatility and 
defensive/cyclical factors drove most of the latest decline, 
while all RAI sub-components (except bonds and FX) are 
now in negative territory (Exhibit 2). The broader 
deterioration in risk appetite should start to improve the
