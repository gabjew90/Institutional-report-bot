not been this lightly positioned at any point in the past five years!

In other words, everyone with a fast trigger finger sold: hedge funds (gross and net), CTAs and

The Biggest Degross Since Covid 
Here is how Goldman's Nelson Armbrust laid out the damage in his "What Matters Today" note 
(also available to pro subs): 
Deleveraging: US Fundamental L/S Gross leverage fell -8.7 pts – the largest weekly decline 
since Covid (March ’20) – to 207.5% (26th percentile one-year), while US Fundamental L/S Net 
leverage fell -3.7pts – the largest weekly decline since the week of Liberation Day (Apr ’25) – to 
43.3% (0th percentile five-year). We finally saw gross leverage being reduced drastically. 
Personally, I think this is a strong signal that positioning is cleaning up. 
The full table from Goldman's Prime Services (chart source GS Prime Services, as of Nov 5) is 
brutal. Beta-adjusted gross fell almost 16 points on the week, and gross is now down 11 points 
year-to-date while net is down 11.6 points, giving back half of 2025's 21.9-point gross build and 
far more than last year's tiny 0.9-point net increase. The L/S ratio of 1.527 sits in the 1st 
percentile on both a three- and five-year basis. 
 
And for those who like their capitulation visual, the red line below says it all: the only bigger 
weekly gross drop in the past decade came during the March 2020 Covid crash, and the only 
bigger net drop since 2022 came during Liberation Day. 
 

 
 
Yet there is a wrinkle. Across the entire global prime book (which includes macro and systematic 
accounts), overall gross leverage actually rose 0.1 points to 314.2%, the 85th percentile over 
the past year, while net slid to the 4th percentile and the L/S ratio to the 3rd. So there is still 
plenty of exposure out there, just very little conviction (a theme we first flagged in "Conviction 
Collapse" back on Oct 4, when we thought things couldn't get much lighter. They could.) 
 
Here is the global picture from Saboureault's PB summary, where long sales outpaced short 
covers by a whopping 5 to 1:

De-grossing activity was led in $ terms by Europe and DM Asia. All regions (sans DM Asia) 
were net sold, led by EM Asia and North America. 
 

 
Single Stocks were net sold for an 8th straight week, driven by long sales, while Macro Products

Saboureault's chart of global fundamental L/S leverage shows the same story from a different 
angle: net leverage at 45.3% is the lowest in the trailing twelve months, and the 0th percentile 
on a 1-year and 3-year basis, and the L/S ratio has never been lower over the past five years in 
the GS book.

And while funds dumped single stocks, they piled into index shorts. Goldman's Robbie 
Stankard, whose "Eq Positioning and Key Levels" recap (also available to pro subs)

Put differently, on the day the Panic Index peaked, hedge funds were hedging with ETF shorts

Retail, which bailed out every dip this year, is finally flinching. Saboureault's one-word verdict on

...  and JPMorgan's Mark Schilsky had a perfect acronym for it all: 
Now the exception. Goldman's own US Equity Sentiment Indicator, a composite of nine 
positioning measures across institutional, retail and foreign investors, actually rose to +0.6 from 
+0.2. Light positioning on this gauge means a reading below -1.0; the SI is nowhere near it. How 
to square that with hedge fund nets at a 5-year low? Simple: the "positioning" that matters to the 
SI includes the mutual funds and foreigners who kept buying (more below), and as long as they 
keep the composite in positive territory, the contrarian "everyone is out" bull signal remains, well, 
incomplete. 
 

 
 
Stankard's charts make the same point. CTAs may be close to flat in US equities (just $15BN

risk parity and vol control combined) still hold $181BN of US equities, above their average since 
2020. Diversification at its finest: everyone sold their single stocks and kept their futures.
