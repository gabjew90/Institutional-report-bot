Europe Open - Hitting refresh
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Asia stocks staged a recovery as we start a new week, with Nikkei outperforming peers and US equity futures slightly
higher overnight. USD and UST modestly weaker, but the volumes are lower given onshore holidays in Singapore. NZD
was the best performer despite weekend elections seeing no clear winner. However, as things stand, incumbent National
is expected to form a center right coalition, official results out Nov 27. ILS is next best performer after President Herzog
gave Eisenkot the chance to form a government. Oil prices are ~2% weaker after Trump said military action against Iran
is highly unlikely while negotiations continue. 
Expect conviction to remain low into US CPI tomorrow; for today, watch out for NY Fed inflation expectations and 3y
supply. Goolsbee and Barkin comments will be less influential pre CPI. Eyes on Eurozone Sentix survey with BoE Bailey
on the cards too. ECB President Lagarde warned that inflation will likely remain high in the short-term. Two candidates
are officially running for presidency - de Cos and Knot. At print, FT reports Knot saying he "won't hesitate" to act in a debt
crisis.

Weekly Macro Brief
In Focus‌
Election recaps:
NZD: New Zealand general elections saw incumbent National party and Labour opposition virtually tied on the popular vote.
Preliminary counts place the center-right National, ACT and NZ First coalition on a slim 2-seat majority (67 seats). However,
roughly 400,000 uncounted special votes historically favor center-left parties and could cost the Nationals 2 seats, keeping a
Labour-led coalition in contention. Official results are due Nov 27, and our economists place a 55% chance on the center-right
retaining power. A National-led win would preserve the RBNZ's single inflation mandate and a smaller fiscal footprint,
supporting the NZD and bonds, whereas a Labour coalition would reinstate full employment into monetary policy. Dig deeper.
ILS: Gadi Eisenkot was formally handed the mandate to form a new government by President Isaac Herzog. Eisenkot will
have up to six weeks to try and form a coalition that surpasses the 61 seat majority, failure would mean PM Netanyahu will

get the opportunity. Eisenkot said he is negotiating to form a "national emergency government". Netanyahu continues to
govern in a caretaker capacity. A long coalition process is seemingly in price, so ILS will remain a function of broader risk/tech
sentiment despite overnight strength.
 
What's happening across markets:
FX: Interbank volumes are running -21% below 30d averages given onshore holidays in Singapore, with NZDUSD activity
surpassing peers (our platform saw leveraged buying NZD) but volumes have since tapered off and now in line. The pair is
now up 1% post NZ elections, with impied vol risk premium over AUD implied vol starting to compress. Otherwise, USD is
slightly on the backfoot. G10 implied vols traded heavy to start the week as risk assets prices are recovering from the recent
routs. 1mth JPY implied vols is repriced at about 0.75v lower from last week highs at 6.65v currently, and the entire curve has
seen selling pressure from both markets and franchise. Euro front end vol curve is also trading softer. US CPI tomorrow has
an implied event gap at 25bp.
Equities: Positive sentiment across Asia stocks but Kospi lagging peers down -0.8%. US equity futures trade higher while
European equities are set for a flattish open. Despite AI troubles last week, SXXP is just -2% from all time highs, our equity
trading strategist warns. He remains cautious but also remain alert on Iran deal squeeze risk, the latter which they prefer
playing more directly via their CGDOSOHO basket. Our US team are also not dip buyers yet in this positioning driven sell off,
and expect elevated headline risks in the days and weeks to come around the AI infrastructure cycle. They continue to
recommend strong vs. weak balance sheet (CGP3PROB) stocks, and Health Care AI (CGUSHCAI) as a safer way to own
growth stocks. QQQ puts are the best protection for positioning risks, IWM for credit/rates/policy and SPX for a high
correlation drawdown. Meanwhile, our TMT spec salesperson is closely monitoring Nvidia developments. While Huang
remains CEO, "its hard to see the AI trade marching on in the short term without him", he writes. The company reports on Nov
17. 
Rates: Tad bearish sentiment overnight but its overall a quieter session with Singapore out. US yields started the week higher
amid a modest flattening bias, alongside some recovery in broader Asian equity indices following the global AI stock rout.
Ahead of London open, duration reversed into better bid in sync with the pricing action in the oil sector. Flow-wise, franchise
saw fairly muted activity, with some buying interest concentrated in the front end and intermediates. Volumes were running at
around 0.65x recent averages.
Commodities: Oil prices -2% lower after US President Trump said renewed US military action against Iran is "highly unlikely"
if negotiations produce a strong agreement covering Iran's nuclear program and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. The
US remains prepared for escalation if talks fail, noting three aircraft carriers deployed in the region. US Secretary of State
Rubio said that Tehran's demands to date are unacceptable, and he wouldn't say the US is on the verge of a breakthrough in
talks on ending the conflict.
 
Monday must reads:
Weekly Macro Brief by Jim McCormick. US midterms didn't turn out to be the non-event that markets had been expecting. AI
regulation and more fiscal spending is likely, meaning 10y UST yields below 5% look like a good place to sell. The Democratic
sweep has lowered the likelihood of Iran conflict escalation, which should help cap energy prices from here. This should also
boost EM local bonds and European front-ends. UK inflation also looks expensive, especially with the more hawkish BoE.
With the rates/risk asset implied vol gap closed, it is better to be neutral on vol here. Jim also likes paying front BoE and BoJ
meetings. 
G10 & EM Week Ahead: Picking up the pieces Plenty of (geo)political events to watch out for this week. In New Zealand,
incumbent National Party is expected to form a coalition government with three center-right parties after Saturday's election
saw an inconclusive result. In Israel, opposition Eisenkot has been tasked to forming a government after Oct elections saw no
party secure majorities. Netanyahu remains caretaker PM. Geopolitical headlines will stay prominent as the US, Russia and
Ukraine meet in Abu Dhabi. Expectations for a breakthrough remain limited without Zelenskyy or Putin attending. Oil prices
will also monitor continued US-Iran mediation efforts.
US CPI is the main macro event, with our economists expecting a below-consensus core print of 0.19% MoM, followed by PPI
on Friday. However, neither of the prints should deter market pricing away from an FOMC Dec hike. Eurozone Sentix Survey,

UK Q3 GDP, Norway CPI, Chinese inflation and trade data, are also in focus. Equities remain vulnerable after latest selloffs,
with continued uncertainty around AI capex and data center restrictions likely to keep volatility elevated. It's a heavy supply
week with $125bn issued in the US.
Elsewhere‌
GBP: BoE Bailey speaks in Zurich at 17:25 GMT. Following the Nov MPC, our strategists have entered Dec/Feb MPC SONIA
flatteners. Absent an oil correction, the MPC made it clear that they could hike in Dec. 
EUR: German exports just out beat estimates growing by 2% MoM, though an improvement in imports drives the trade
balance surplus weaker to EUR 15.6bn. More relevant for markets will be Eurozone Sentix Investor Confidence also due at
09:30 GMT for November. Bond and equity market turbulence could have a larger than expected decline this month. Our
economists expect a 1.5 print vs 2.7 prior. ICYMI, it was revealed that Knot and de Cos are the two candidates running for
ECB president. As we go to print, FT reports Knot who said that he "won't hesitate" to act in a debt crisis. President Lagarde
warned that inflation will likely remain high in the near-term, leaving the door open for a Dec hike. 
USD: Fedspeak in the form of Goolsbee (15:00 GMT) and Barkin (17:15), but comments pre-CPI might be less market
moving. In the background, eyes on NY Fed 1y inflation expectations at 16:00 GMT and a US 3y auction at 18:00.
 
Don't miss:
TWD: Trade Balance out shortly at 08:00 GMT for October. Our economists project export growth accelerating to 69.6%YoY
in October, while import growth moderates to 47.6%YoY.
BRL: FGV Inflation IGP-DI MoM for October at 11:00 GMT followed by the Central Bank's Weekly Economist Survey at 11:25
GMT. Our economists expect IGP-DI to remain mostly unchanged at 1.40% MoM, with acceleration in the YoY measure to
5.27%.
CLP: Trade Balance report at 11:30 GMT for October. Watch the trend in copper exports too.
MXN: Bi-Weekly CPI YoY and Headline CPI at 12:00 GMT. Our economists expect bi-weekly inflation at 0.24% MoM driven
by higher non-core inflation (0.67%), reflecting continued pressure from agricultural prices and higher LP gas prices. For
headline, we expect a 0.58% MoM print/3.68% YoY.
CNH: Inflation data was mixed with CPI missing estimates at 0.7% YoY (vs cons. at 0.8%) but PPI beating, coming at 5.7%
YoY vs 5% prior and 5.3% cons. CPI moderated on broad food price weakness, our economists find, with the PPI uptick
largely driven by energy and rising chemical prices. Near-term, we see rising risks of “bad inflation”—tilted to the upside for
PPI and the downside for CPI. However, these prints do not alter our expectation of returning deflationary risks in 2027E,
which we think could intensify further oil price correction. Dig deeper. Also check out China Economics: What Do US Midterms
Mean for China?
PHP: Q3 GDP printed at 2.1% YoY, missing consensus' estimates of a rebound, and slower than 2.3% prior.
