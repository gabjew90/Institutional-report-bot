The correction is entering its final phase, in our view,  as the S&P 
catches down to breadth and approaches our 7100–7300 
downside range. A final retest of last week's lows likely sets up a 
year-end rally. We recommend adding quality cyclicals with 
strong EPS revisions as the market carves out a low. 
We frame our views around a few key questions:
• How did we get to this point? We’ve been through a mid-cycle transition as 
the market has focused on the peak rate of change in EPS revisions and 
growth, alongside less accommodative monetary policy—two factors we 
identified before the market correction began, which prompted our quality 
rotation and Semiconductor top calls in June.
• Why is the correction in its final phase? Our view has been that the last piece 
of the correction that began in June was the index catching down to narrow 
breadth. This is now playing out as the S&P 500 nears our well-established 
7100–7300 downside target (where there is valuation, trendline and 
retracement support) and breadth is well off the lows. Semis have led on the 
downside in this final phase, and are close to the downside targets pointed to 
by our silver stock analog.
• What’s the path into 2027? Technically, we favor a retest in the coming 
week(s) that could reach the low end of our target range, after which we 
think price action resolves to the upside into year-end. Rather than waiting for 
a perfect low, we recommend adding exposure over the next few weeks to 
quality cyclicals that have de-rated materially and are exhibiting strong EPS 
revisions (see our screen here).
• How about the data center risk from the midterms? Our proprietary capex 
mapping analysis shows that data center capex for 2027 represents 5% of 
consensus revenue for the S&P 500. For 2028, we expect this contribution to 
be slightly higher, but still in the mid-single-digit range. Thus, even in a bear-
case scenario where a portion of data center capacity gets pushed out beyond 
this time horizon, we think the impact on S&P 500 revenues and earnings is 
limited and  priced at 18x forward earnings (near Liberation Day valuation 
levels). Finally, keep in mind that policy uncertainty around this dynamic is 
part of the reason Semis and other stocks that benefit from data center 
builds already underperformed materially for  4 months leading up to the 
election.

AI Adoption in Focus  
Michelle Weaver, Head of US Thematic Research  
We leveraged an LLM to analyze approximately 18,000 earnings, conference, and investor 
day transcripts and track how management teams are communicating the quantifiable 
benefits of AI adoption to investors and AI's impact on employment. For more on the note, 
please see AI Adoption in Focus: How Are Companies Quantifying the Benefits and Costs?
Company discussions suggest AI adoption is moving from experimentation toward 
measurable financial value, even as scrutiny of costs increases. On a trailing 12 month 
basis, 44% of S&P 500 companies cited at least one quantifiable benefit from AI 
adoption, up from 32% a year ago. The largest companies are the furthest along in 
measuring AI's impact: on a market cap weighted basis, 54% cite a quantifiable benefit, 
broadly in line with 53% a year ago. Our MS "Adopter" cohort is also ahead of the index 
average, with 49% of companies identified as Adopters by our analysts now citing 
quantifiable benefits, up from 40%. Productivity gain remains the most widely cited 
benefit, quantified by 32% of companies (up from 25%), but financial impact (e.g., cost 
savings or revenue generation due to AI) has risen to 26% of companies from 16%. 
That 10pp increase is the largest of any benefit category and narrows the gap with 
productivity from 9pp to 6pp, a sign that AI discussions are moving beyond efficiency 
gains toward measurable effects on financial performance.

3Q Preview Tracking
Our Morgan Stanley Analysts submit a systematic earnings preview where they forecast

for both KPI and EPS: very negative, negative, in-line, positive, or very positive. We roll up 
these previews into a net score by taking (positive-negative)/total reactions.
