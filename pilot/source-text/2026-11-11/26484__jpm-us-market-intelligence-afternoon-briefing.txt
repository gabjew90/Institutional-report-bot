US MKT INTEL VIEW 

•         US MKT INTEL – I view yesterday’s (Nov 10) moves, catalyzed by the CPI print as 
an opportunity to reset bond yields higher to more directly match economic growth, which 
printed ~3.4% for 26Q3 as the economy remains on track to grow above-trend into 2027. 
US GDP averaged above-trend growth since 22Q2. Combine this with another robust 
earnings season and, from a macro, fundamental perspective the market remains in a 
good place and on a bullish trajectory. We are Tactically Bullish. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU – We agree with Dubravko’s view:  The Tech washout 
appears to be at/near its completion though there remains some risk of another Semis 
decline, we like owning broad-based Tech plays and would consider US Memory and 
Semis plays via options. CRWV, OpenAI Q3 revenue, capital markets reopening are all 
supportive of a return to the AI theme; but the moves may remain choppy as markets 
consider another shift higher in bond yields. The barbell trade (Tech + Cyclicals) makes 
sense but think you can get more upside from playing Cyclicals via European 
names/sectors over their US counterparts due to relatively lower positioning and 
valuation. 
•         BOND YIELDS – As we saw AI / Tech selloff the last few weeks, the market narrative 
shifted towards an increase in recession fears, with many suggesting that AI Capex was 
the sole reason for US growth; this is inaccurate, but it fueled a power risk-off / rotation 
narrative. Our recession basket (JPRECES Index) is +14.9% since Sep 30 while SPX -
1.7%, NDX -8.8%, SOXX -22.7%, and DRAM -32.7%. A decline in AI capex is unlikely to 
drive the US into recession, given it did little to increase jobs. Certainly, there is a wealth 
effect via the stock market, but that is second order and likely not a linear relationship for 
the top income decile which is ~50% of US consumption. AI capex has an offset by 
making Net Exports more negative given the volume of the AI trade that uses imports. 
Consumers remain the engine of the US economy. They have historically large sums of 
cash (checking + savings + MMFs) in a labor market that is essentially at full employment 
with signs pointing to improving wage growth. Rick Shane’s latest Chase Spending Data 
update is here with some charts posted below. That said, there are clear financing / 

financial risks though they are unlikely to be as systemic as what was seen in the lead up 
to GFC.

DUBRAVKO – his full note is here 

•         Dubravko reiterates his YE26 SPX price target of 8,000 with $365 EPS, 21.9x. This 
implies 8.4% upside from Friday’s close. 
•         TRADE IDEAS – (i) Long 12M Momentum vs. Short 3M Momentum – which is 
essentially Tech vs. Energy / Defensives; (ii) Low Vol is less compelling – after moving 
form 0%ile to 25%ile, the trade is less attractive as the factor will need to see an increase 
in recession risk to maintain its outperformance. If you want to stick with Low Vol, he sees 
the best opportunity in Healthcare followed by Utilities, and then staples; (iii) AI 
Upstream is attractive – the positioning flush has created an attractive entry point. While 
hyperscalers have less upside in the near-term, their medium-term prospects are 
attractive with room to re-rate higher. The Blue Wave will limited the capex curve, but 
tighter compute capacity should benefit pricing power, margins, and operating cash flow 
for hyperscalers; (iv) Consumer Cyclicals remain challenged – affordability remains 
the headwind tied to oil prices, borrowing costs, and housing shortages. That said, he 
sees longer-term value in Apparel, Restaurants, and Retail vs. Energy. Regarding 
Housing, mortgage rates need to approach 6.0% to see inflows into the group. 
•         WHAT’S THE MACRO VIEW?  Looking ahead to 2027, JPM Economics expects US 
GDP growth to slow but remain trend-like with modest disinflation keeping the broader 
business cycle dynamics intact, while we [Dubravko & team] estimate EPS growth of 
~20%.  
•         WHY THE UNWIND? This pullback is a de-leveraging event. Investor were chasing 
AI and AI-adjacent plays with record high levels of leverage. Leverage is at-risk when 
liquidity dissipate, which began occurring in late-June. Liquidity dries, equity de-
leveraging follows triggering a rapid reversal of the Momentum trade. The unwind was 
magnified by systematic & vol strategies plus a liquidation of buyside funds. October was 
the largest single month unwind for Long Momentum, falling ~11% or ~6 standard 
deviations contributing to Momentum L/S declining ~40% from its peak. 
•         WHAT ABOUT MULTIPLES? Equity multiple have compressed ~4x since January, 
one of the most extreme periods of de-rating when excluding recessions. This de-rating is 
magnified by the fact that earnings have been growing double-digits this year. AI 

companies have seen multiples compress ~35% since May, including: Semis 
compressing 52%, hyperscalers 14%, Memory 60%, and Processing Units 50%. 
•         HOW MUCH SELLING IS LEFT? He sees the process as being far along pointing to 
(i) a reset in Momentum crowding from 100%ile to 76%ile; (ii) Levered ETF sales of 
~$60bn on Thursday and $35bn the previous Friday which have dropped exposure below 
Jan 2026 levels; (iii) CTA have flipped to net Short as vol targeters have seen leverage 
decrease from 65%ile to 30%ile with maybe $50bn left to sell; (iv) dealer net gamma 
imbalance is 12%ile; (v) corporate buyback, with $1.2T in announced buyback, are likely 
to provide support; (vi) QQQ have seen their RSI dip below 30 which has, on average 
seen ~4% rally over the next five trading days. 
•         WHAT ARE THE CATALYSTS? (i) developments in the Middle East; (ii) MegaCap 
IPOs coming to market; (iii) the ERCOT meeting next month, which has AI Capex 
implications; and (iv) December Fed meeting. More generally, he sees US Midterm 
Elections as a clearing event and expected data center headwinds to be less onerous 
than the market is pricing. 
•         WHAT ARE THE SPX EPS RISKS FROM AI CAPEX? Dubravko and team estimate 
that  every $100B increase in capex translates to ~$9 for S&P 500 EPS (~2.5% earnings 
growth) in 2027. Thus, even if next year’s capex spend comes in at the middle of the 
range at ~$1.5T, that would roughly translate to an EPS impact of ~$18 vs. consensus, 
adding 4-5% to 2027 EPS growth. While there are concerns around future (2028) data 
center projects after the recent midterm election results, J.P. Morgan credit analysts 
expect hyperscalers to be able to fund their AI capex needs next year, absent any 
exogenous shocks in credit markets. However, the funding mix will need to see more 
participation from other sources including equity.
