It's fair to say this was not Michael Hartnett's finest week. 
Last Sunday, BofA's Chief Investment Strategist told us that, a year after saying "short 
hyperscaler bonds," it was time to buy them, specifically the tech bond "diamonds in the rough" 
yielding 6-8% after Meta's $95 billion equity raise signaled "new respect for credit investors." 
CoreWeave's 2031s, at a junk-ish 14.26%, made his list. 
Then the country voted. Tuesday's blue wave sparked market chaos, Thursday's "Fireworks 
Day" handed the Nasdaq its worst day since Covid along with the biggest put day in Cboe 
history, and the hyperscaler bonds Hartnett had just told clients to buy got slammed. As 
incoming Democratic governors promised to freeze the AI buildout, AI junk bonds cratered, with 
CoreWeave's paper leading the way down:

Goldman's Amanda Lynam counted five hyperscaler bond deals worth $67 billion since October

ourselves a modest victory lap.

stocks" is right there in black and white.

half-built campuses and $4.1 trillion in off-balance sheet commitments that still have to be paid, 
 

 
and as Goldman pointed out, the hyperscalers are front-running 2027 supply precisely because 
they know the window may close.

The Last Conviction Standing 
Put it all together and Hartnett's message is that the midterms ended the era that made the AI 
trade possible: a White House with the political capital to bend corporations, foreign 
governments and local zoning boards toward "winning the AI war," and voters willing to tolerate 
a K-shaped boom while their power bills rose. Now the voters want affordability, the bonds want 
less issuance, and the only conviction left is "boom." His prescription: sell oil, buy bonds, wait for 
lower oil and yields before adding risk "bigtime." 
We agree with the diagnosis and, maybe more than Hartnett, we lean toward the bust. The 
Thanksgiving rally needs weak data and a Fed that stops hiking; Friday delivered just the 
opposite. The recession canaries are wobbling (mid-caps already broke), hyperscalers are 
front-running their 2027 bond supply while the window is open, and the 6% of fund managers 
who fear a hard landing look awfully lonely for a market where AI junk bonds just dropped to 78 
cents. And then there's the $41.1 trillion question waiting on February 1, courtesy of a divided 
Washington. If the bond market is going to police the AI boom, it will probably want to see a few 
more arrests first. 
As for those tech bond "diamonds in the rough"... Hartnett said "short hyperscaler bonds" a year 
ago and was right. He may well be right about buying them too. Just not this week.
