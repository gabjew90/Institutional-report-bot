 
Huang-Over: Stocks Jump, Oil Dumps After 
Weekend Of 'Fear And Loathing' 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, NOV 10, 2026 - 04:58 AM 
Tl;dr: The market's split personality over the last few days brought an analogy to mind. Just as 
Hunter S. Thompson hunted the American Dream, finding only the wreckage of the '60s; the 
drug-fueled run up in 'everything-AI' came to an abrupt halt last week and the weekend was full 
of 'fear and self-loathing' articles discussing just how bad shit was (with Jensen's huangover the 
tipping point, NVDA -4.5%). But from those ashes of despair (extremely washed-out positioning 
and leverage lows and spiking panic highs) rose the triumphant dip-buyers with stocks soaring 
(short squeeze) off overnight lows (DOW, SPX, & RTY erasing post-election losses). Why the 
bounce? Anyone's guess (same as the night of the election - as bad as it gets? - but Trump's 
dovishness on Iran strikes helped and IAEA rumors slammed oil). Credit spreads compressed, 
TSYs mixed (short-end lagging), and the dollar dipped as bitcoin volatility spiked. 
 
All-in-all, another of those surreal days (in markets and headlines) with nothing to pin the early 
panic-buying action on but the fever dream analogy is almost too clean... 
 

 
 
Ok, sorry, back to reality... Bonds decoupled from stocks and crude today (lower oil, higher 
stocks as Trump signaled he is unlikely to launch broad military strikes on Iran and dumb IEAE 
rumors late-on) 
 
So, let's start with the energy complex... 
Energy 
The dominant force in crude markets today was growing optimism around a diplomatic 
resolution to the US-Iran war.  
Brent tumbled back to last week's lows after President Trump said over the weekend that it is 
"highly unlikely" he will order extensive strikes on Iran, and the Democratic sweep of last week's 
midterm elections has compounded pressure on Washington to seek an off-ramp from the 
conflict. 
 

 
Then, late in the day, this headline hit: AL ARABIYA SOURCES: ARRANGEMENTS ARE 
BEING MADE TO DISCUSS THE RETURN OF IAEA INSPECTORS TO IRAN, WITHOUT 
CLARIFYING THE SCOPE OF THEIR DUTIES, slamming oil prices lower (WTI $85 handle) to 
six-week lows (down over 4% on the day)... 
 
Money managers had already been trimming their bullish bets heading into today - net-long 
positions in Brent fell to their lowest since early August and WTI longs to their smallest since 
early July in the latest weekly figures - leaving the market vulnerable to a sharp unwind on any 
positive geopolitical signal. 
Physical crude markets continue to signal easing pressures with Dated Brent hovering well 
below $100... 
 
 

 
And falling tanker rates are reshaping physical trade economics, extending their recent plunge 
today. However, they remain at historically elevated levels, with the cost of hiring a supertanker 
from the US Gulf to Asia falling to $45 million from $79.6 million just a month ago... 
 
Diesel prices dipped again today in sympathy with crude... 
 
Interestingly, NatGas prices diverged dramatically on either side of the pond today. 
US Nattie surged today after weekend weather forecasts shifted to show substantially cooler 
conditions across the Atlantic seaboard, boosting heating demand expectations as winter gets 
underway. The move was amplified by short-covering, as hedge funds with bearish positions 
 

 
were forced to unwind automatically, accelerating the price increase beyond what the weather 
shift alone would have warranted. 
In Europe, the picture is more mixed: benchmark TTF gas futures slumped on Iran peace-talk 
optimism, which raised hopes for a rebound in regional energy flows, though the broader supply 
backdrop remains strained - European storage entered the cold season at just 73% full, well 
below the five-year seasonal norm of 89%, a deficit that the latest forecasts suggest could 
persist well into 2027. 
The chart below shows the USD-equivalent for both US and EU NatGas (while they are 
convergent, note that EU NG is still 8x the price of US NG)... 
 
Before we leave energy-land, we do remind readers that oil remains up more than 50% this year 
as the war has hampered flows from the Middle East. But futures have been increasingly 
responsive to bearish geopolitical news since crude exports from the Persian Gulf picked up.  
Output from OPEC last month rose to 88% of prewar volumes, according to a Bloomberg 
survey. However, Iranian production has slumped as the US enforces a naval blockade on its 
exports, and flareups in the region continue.  
A tanker was reportedly hit by an unknown projectile over the weekend while transiting the vital 
shipping route, the Strait of Hormuz, according to UK Maritime Trade Operations. And in 
Riyadh, Iran-backed Houthi rebels damaged the offices of state oil giant Saudi Aramco in an 
attack, prompting Saudi forces to retaliate by destroying two missile launchers. 
So, don't count your MidEast chickens quite yet... 
"President Trump continues to signal no appetite for further escalation with Iran, keeping 
downward pressure on crude oil prices," said Ryan McKay, senior commodity strategist at TD 
Securities. 
 

 
"Constant headlines of deal talks will likely suppress upside for now, but further material 
downside should be limited without a concrete deal." 
And lower energy prices means lower rates, right? Nope... 
Rates 
Instead, TSYs were sold as investor appetite for stocks and corporate bonds recovered 
(dampening the appeal of govvies as a safe-haven hedge). The selling lifted yields across the 
whole curve with the short-end the notable underperformer. 
By the close, with the late-day snap lower in crude (IAEA rumors), 2Y ended up around 4bps on 
the day and 30Y yields down around 1bp... 
 
A heavy auction calendar added concession pressure to the market today. The Treasury is 
selling $58 billion in 3-year notes this afternoon, followed by $42 billion in 10-year notes 
tomorrow and $25 billion in 30-year bonds on Thursday - a front-loaded schedule driven by 
Wednesday's US bond market holiday (Veterans Day). 
That flattened the curve further, erasing the Thursday steepening... 
 

 
 
Several major US dealers since Friday have said yields last week reached levels that were 
unlikely to be breached in the absence of new threats. 
Risk aversion “has taken yields to support, but a sustained break may require clearer evidence 
of deterioration in the growth outlook,” interest-rate strategists Joseph Abate and Monty Gandhi 
wrote. 
Pressure at the short-end was double-fold today: inflation data and FedSpeak. 
Sticky inflation data in the NY Fed's October consumer survey were a reminder that inflation 
psychology remains stubbornly elevated and well above the Fed's 2% target, keeping pressure 
on the front end. 
Additionally, FedSpeak was distinctly non-dovish with two Fed heads reinforcing the view that 
rate cuts remain a distant prospect. Chicago Fed's Goolsbee said policymakers need to tackle a 
potential overheating in the economy from the AI build-out and a broadening of inflation 
pressures, stating bluntly: "We've got to come to terms with this cost side and inflation failure in 
the mandate." Richmond Fed's Barkin echoed that tone, saying the risk lies on the inflation side 
of the Fed's mandate and that while the direction of monetary policy is straightforward, it is "best 
to move policy with care."  
All of which pushed rate-hike expectations higher... 
 

 
 
On top of the Treasury supply, we also saw a heavy slate of new corporate bonds.

The near-term tails remain unusually large, and there’s clearly more positioning to work through.

Not necessarily a reason to rush back into risk, but it’s getting harder to ignore the potential 
opportunity on the other side.

And finally, we return to the scene of the (alleged) crime... As Bloomberg macro strategist, Frank 
Monkam, wrote, the investigation surrounding Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang introduces an entirely 
new dimension of risk for an AI complex already struggling to find its footing following last 
week’s historic drawdown. The market’s sharp rebound Monday in the face of the news 
suggests investors are in a mechanical buy-the-dip mode that is entirely dismissive of the risks 
lying ahead. 
Beyond the personal allegations in the NVDA scandal, the more consequential market 
implications revolve around governance, key-person risk and potentially national security. 
Huang’s central role in Nvidia’s strategic direction and investor confidence means even the 
 

 
remote possibility of leadership disruption could introduce a risk premium previously absent from 
the company’s valuation. 
More importantly, the uncertainty surrounding the investigation could linger for months, leaving 
investors grappling with risks that are difficult to quantify, let alone price. Any substantiated 
national security implications would only compound that risk. 
 
Nvidia accounts not only for 7% of the SPX market weight, but more importantly around 25% of 
its variance. Add to that the macro implications the company has had over broader market 
sentiment and the impact is likely to be widespread. 
The timing could hardly be worse. With liquidity deteriorating, positioning impaired and volatility 
in the upper end of the recent range, the market’s capacity to absorb another shock is 
increasingly constrained.
