The AI disconnect
JPM published its bullish Asian tech roadmap just before the latest market carnage. Its central
argument was simple: valuations had already priced in an earnings downturn that wasn't showing up in
the data. Since then, the selling has intensified. The question is whether this is another violent
positioning reset or the beginning of a genuine fundamental deterioration.
Prices collapse, earnings don't
Asian tech had already suffered its fourth 20%+ correction since 2022 when JPM published its report.
Yet roughly 80% of companies were still seeing positive earnings revisions, while valuations had
compressed toward historical averages. The market was pricing in an earnings downturn that had yet
to materialize. Following the latest carnage, that disconnect deserves even more attention.
JPM
Cheap again?
Asian tech valuations have returned toward historical averages despite continued earnings upgrades.
JPM shows forward P/E multiples for Asian tech excluding memory back near their long-term mean,
while price-to-book valuations have compressed even as ROE remains elevated. The valuation reset is
already significant, and these charts don't even capture the latest carnage. Cheap can, of course, get
cheaper.

The $1 trillion AI machine
While AI stocks are getting crushed, the spending machine continues to accelerate. JPM expects
hyperscaler datacenter capex to rise 85% in 2026 and another 70% in 2027, approaching $1 trillion
annually. Meanwhile, aggregate public cloud revenue growth has accelerated to 50% year-on-year.
The AI infrastructure boom is hardly showing signs of slowing, at least for now.

The AI demand explosion
AI token demand is growing roughly 17x year-on-year, according to JPM, accelerating again after the
August–September slowdown. More importantly, compute remains scarce, with short-term contracts
priced at $40–50 per watt despite mounting neocloud funding concerns. Falling token prices are not
translating into weaker compute demand. Quite the opposite.
JPM
The $45bn problem
The uncomfortable part of the AI boom: spending is exploding much faster than incremental cloud
revenues. In Q3, the four major cloud providers increased quarterly capex by roughly $45bn, versus
just $13bn in incremental cloud revenue. Not a like-for-like return calculation, as capex also supports
internal workloads, but the gap is striking. The market isn't questioning AI demand. It's questioning the
economics of supplying it.
JPM
Where the real cracks would appear
JPM highlights that the classic warning signs of a semiconductor cycle peak remain largely absent:
rising inventories, shortening lead times and collapsing compute prices. Meanwhile, forward net
income estimates for Asian tech continue to surge. The market is trading as if the cycle is breaking, but
the earnings data still tells a very different story. The real danger comes when earnings expectations
finally start catching down to prices.

JPM
The next AI trade
The latest selloff has created a growing disconnect between price action and the underlying earnings
cycle. JPM remains constructive, but the market is clearly challenging that view. Taiwan, the last major
AI market standing until last week, has now joined the liquidation. The distinction between a
positioning-driven washout and a genuine deterioration in AI demand is becoming critical. The charts
are breaking. The earnings cycle, at least so far, isn't.
