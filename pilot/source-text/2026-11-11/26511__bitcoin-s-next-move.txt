BTC and the 200dma
"BTC has now held above its 200-day moving average for 83 days, tracking toward a 90-day return of
8%. Looking back to 2015, instances in which BTC reclaimed its 200-day moving average following a
sustained downtrend have tended to show relatively consistent 180-day forward returns, ranging from
53% to 61%, with an average of 57%. While the historical sample is limited, from a longer-term
perspective, the observation may still offer a useful reference point for assessing the current setup
relative to prior cycles."
Marquee
The ETF exodus
Not everyone is convinced. US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw $1.3 billion in outflows last week, with another
$200 million leaving on Monday. Year-to-date net flows have now slipped to approximately negative $1
billion.
GS
Heading for the exits
It's not just ETF investors heading for the exits. Bitcoin miner MARA has sold nearly $1.1 billion worth of
BTC over the past six weeks, according to CoinDesk, and is planning further sales. Institutional demand
is weakening just as additional supply hits the market.
Retail retreat
Retail participation appears to be weakening too. According to Santiment, the number of non-empty
Bitcoin wallets has fallen by roughly 109,000 since mid-October — another sign of deteriorating
sentiment.
The great leverage flush
Bitcoin's latest plunge toward $73k triggered one of the largest long-liquidation dominance spikes of
the past year, with the metric hitting 19%, according to Checkonchain.
Previous liquidation spikes have often accompanied local lows. Is another leverage reset setting the
stage for the next rebound?

@FrankAFetter
More on the leverage reset
Bitcoin perpetual futures funding rates have fallen from around 10% to 5.5% annualized, suggesting
bullish leverage is being reduced as prices retreat. Funding remains positive, however, indicating that
positioning still retains an upside bias.
CoinMetrics
The bottom line?
ETF outflows, miner selling, retail retreat and a brutal leverage flush. Hardly the ingredients of a bull
market.
But history suggests that some of Bitcoin's most powerful recoveries have followed periods of extreme
pessimism.
The question is whether this is another one of them.
