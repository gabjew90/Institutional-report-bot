Inflation: Well, that was an unhelpful inflation report. CPI hot across the board… headline +0.4%, core
+0.3%, with strength extending well beyond energy into medical services, insurance and other categories.
Combine that with Waller and Logan’s hawkish comments and we’re now pricing a December hike +
some optionality of 50bp and further tightening into 2027. Bear flattening, as you’d expect. Hard to
dismiss this as simply energy pass through… underlying inflation is proving sticky.
Equity Resilience: SPX barely fell yesterday and futures are recovering this morning. AI sentiment
appears to be improving, supporting the core engines of the market. We’ve spent a lot of time debating just
how low nets have fallen, how much gross has been cut and how heavily hedged investors have become.
Even with a hot CPI print, the market struggled to sustain the volatility premium. VIX briefly traded into
the 20s but finished below 20. That’s quite telling… positioning may now be doing more to support the
tape than the macro is doing to undermine it.
AI: Neocloud commentary overnight was broadly supportive, with data center capacity constraints
remaining the primary operational bottleneck. Compute utilization across major providers continues to
track at elevated levels. Markets focus remains concentrated on hardware delivery and developer coding
workflows, while broader edge and enterprise autonomous integrations remain in earlier stages of
deployment. (see below)
Oil: Remarks from Secretary of State Marco Rubio following G7 meetings in Paris suggest the
administration increasingly favors sustained economic pressure and the naval blockade over an immediate
resumption of major combat operations. Per wire reports, Qatari mediators are working within an
estimated two week diplomatic window, though official parameters remain unconfirmed and fluid. Rubio
framed the White House posture directly: “The President doesn’t just have two options. It’s not just return
to war or sign a ridiculous peace deal… The third option we have is to continue to do what we do now”.
The key tactical question is whether Tehran tolerates this blockade status quo over the coming days. While
Brent has bounced off yesterday’s lows, local directional conviction remains low. If Brent consolidates
near $90 with collapsing realized volatility, equities should increasingly look through the headline noise.
Risk: Rates are a headwind but market has built some cushion from prior rally so rates aren’t yet flashing
red for equities… yields need another leg higher before historically they become a more material problem
for equities (it's the rate of change that matters). Cyclicality relative to the market is also hanging in.
Credit still the one to watch with high yield spreads chopping sideways. There’s also the reflexivity with
energy… higher oil, stickier inflation, a more hawkish Fed. The question is whether the Fed is reacting to
energy effects already in the rearview mirror or something more persistent. Gross has been cut, nets are
historically low and the appetite to add risk has diminished substantially. That’s the tailwind for the tape.

Volatility remains elevated relative to the price action and, absent fresh negative information, that
premium should continue to decay. Positioning is a tailwind, the Fed is a headwind. US Veterans Day
today… bond market closed, equities open. Watching the neoclouds and next move in energy.
