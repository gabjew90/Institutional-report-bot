Global equities were net sold (-1.0 SDs 1-year) as the pace of de-grossing
increased (-0.8 SDs 1-year, largest $ de-grossing since July), driven by long
sales outpacing short covers (5 to 1).
De-grossing activity was led in $ terms by Europe and DM Asia.
Single Stocks were net sold for an 8th straight week, driven by long sales,
while Macro Products were net bought for a 3rd straight week, driven by
long buys and to a lesser extent short covers.
Industrials was the second-most $ net sold global sector this week (behind Info Tech)
as the sector was net sold for 4th straight week and saw the largest % net selling in
over 5 years, driven by long sales and to a lesser extent short sales (1.6 to 1).
On a subsector level globally, nearly all were net sold, led in $ terms by Electrical
Equip, Building Products, Aerospace & Defense, and Trading Co’s & Distributers,
while Passenger Airlines and Machinery were net bought.
US equities were modestly net sold this week (-0.4 SDs 1-year), driven by the largest
single stock net selling in 4+ months partially offset by long buying + short covering
in Macro Products.
Macro Products (Index and ETF combined) were net bought for a second straight
week (+1.5 SDs 1-year), driven by long buys + short covers (~3 to 1).
However, US-listed ETF shorts increased +7% on Thursday (Nov 5th) – the
largest 1-day increase since March (3rd largest in the past 5 years) – and
+3.3% week/week, led by shorting in Large Cap Equity ETFs.
US Single Stocks collectively saw the largest net selling in more than 4 months (-2.1
SDs 1-year), driven by long-and-short sales (3.8 to 1).
Info Tech, Industrials, Staples, and Health Care were the most $ net sold US
sectors, while Utilities, Real Estate, and Financials were the most net bought.
Info Tech was by far the most $ net sold global (and US) sector this week and net
sold across all regions, driven by long-and-short sales (2.2 to 1). In % terms, this
week’s selling in global Tech stocks was ~on par with what we saw in mid-October
and Sep ‘24 as the largest net selling weeks on our record (since 2016).
Also worth noting that 4 of the top 5 net selling weeks in global Info Tech on
our record have occurred within the past five months.


Buybacks
Our desk activity accelerated sharply this week, propelled by post-election
momentum and an active corporate earnings calendar. Our desk volumes finished at
1.6x vs 2025 YTD ADTV and 1.7x vs 2024 YTD ADTV, driven primarily by heavy
demand across Industrials, Financials, and Energy sectors. Execution dynamics
shifted notably in the second half of the week, characterized by a sharp uptick in
opportunistic orders. We saw a pronounced pivot toward discretionary open market
orders, pushing the desk execution mix to 33% open market flow vs 67% 10b5-1
flow (compared to 22%/78% in the prior week). This surge in discretionary demand
directly tracks the widening corporate open window as Q3 reporting progresses.
Currently, we estimate ~90% of the S&P 500 are in open window with ~93%
expected by the end of the week. The strong start to November aligns with historical
seasonality, as November consistently marks one of our more active months on the
desk. Supporting this corporate bid is a strong authorization backdrop, with 2026
YTD authorizations reaching $1.5T, already surpassing 2025’s full year total of
$1.37T.
