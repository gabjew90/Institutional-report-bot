1) ADAM CROOK – Connecting you to GS: Thoughts from the Floor – LINK
Key Questions: US Mid-terms: Thoughts from around the floor on takeaways? US Mid-terms:
Implications for Macro - FX, Rates, EM? What is the latest on El Nino + when will Winter forecasts start to
be relevant for TTF + Aggs? How are we thinking about price action in Equities post mid-terms – Buying
opportunity or structural headwinds? What are the major takeaways from US Payrolls for the US
economy, labour market + the AI Labor Force / Productivity debate?
Focusing in on the US Midterms, see below for takeaways from senior thought leaders, paraphrased from
the GS Weekend Macro Call (listen here).
Alec Phillips (Chief US Political Economist) on the implications for the AI Build-Out: The big
surprise out of election night for the data centre and AI issue was not really at the federal level, it was at
the State level… it basically boils down to Texas, where you had something like a 65% chance going into
the election that Governor Abbott was going to win re-election. At the Federal level, we're probably going
to see some focus on data centres, but nothing too extreme… ratepayer protections, and maybe
removing some existing tax incentives… At the State level, it's clearly about moratoria on permitting…
with gubernatorial wins in Texas, Georgia and Ohio, in theory up to around 40% of planned capacity is
impacted… I think we could end up with a temporary moratorium that lasts several months until new rules
can be put in place.
Rich Privorotsky (Head of European One Delta Trading) on the implications for Equities: The initial
reaction post the mid-terms was everything in the data center complex lower, the rest of the market
higher… one of those two things had to be wrong… and the next day everything caught down to the data
centre trade… if you're going to remove the sole engine of the majority of EPS growth by throttling the
build out of AI, it's very hard to rationalize being long the rest of the market… semis down 10% creates a
lot of leveraged supply, you trigger levels in the Nasdaq, then you go into a short gamma environment… I
don't think this means data centres aren't getting built in the US… the structural worry on AI has always
been an oversupply of compute, that's what keeps me up at night… over the last 19 midterm cycles, the
market has been up 19 for 19 on a one year forward… for 2027 you could even see an acceleration of
M&A… it's right to remain constructive on hyperscalers and the higher quality parts of tech.
Tony Kim (Co-Head of EMEA and Asia Commodities Trading) on the implications for Energy: We
saw an unwind of risk premium on Monday and Tuesday due to progress with the Qatari mediators…

post-Midterms, the blue wave showed how opposed to the conflict the general American public was… the
post from Vance distancing himself from the war and arguing for a quick resolution really took the bottom
out… peak to trough, we probably had a $20 sell off… in a full resolution we could go back to the low 80s,
maybe mid 80s, where we find a floor as the world looks to restock inventory… 115 to the upside is when
a huge amount of risk premium is priced in… anything around 100 is probably fair… there's a threat of
escalation, but now a real chance of de-escalation… the market is quite gappy and probably trading right
in the middle of those distributions.
Brad Shelofsky (Head of IG Cash Trading) on the implications for Credit: The down trade was
extremely orderly this week… the consensus takeaway is an increased probability of a slowdown for the
AI build out… but our GIR team just raised their 2028 data centre investment estimates… a more
measured approach would actually be very welcomed by the bond market… I think that plays out with
high quality hyperscalers outperforming the neoclouds and AI equipment suppliers… if demand stays
robust while new capacity is delayed, compute becomes more scarce and hyperscalers with stabilized
assets can extract higher prices… neoclouds face risk of delays where debt servicing is predicated on
delivery… We've all been guilty of using the term clearing event a few too many times this year… and it's
paid to sell those rallies. But the recent Microsoft, Oracle and Meta deals have underwhelmed both in size
and duration versus expectations.

2) Data Centre Forecasts
Given the focus on the risks to the data centre build out, following the US Midterms, see below for two
pieces from GIR on US data centre capacity and construction growth (h/t Giulio Esposito, Head of US
Equities Distribution in EMEA; full note here).
Data Centre Capacity (Michael Ng, Senior Equity Analyst Covering IG Hardware): The US data
centre buildout still looks well supported through 2028 despite post-midterm regulatory risks. Under-
construction capacity alone implies 22/24GW-IT of net adds in 2027/28, broadly matching our forecasts of
20/24GW-IT, while another 137GW-gross of high-likelihood announced capacity provides upside beyond
this. The key risk is timing rather than cancellations, particularly in Texas, which represents 20%/27% of
2027/28 under-construction additions and where a moratorium has been proposed. Delays could raise
costs and push capacity rightward, with IREN most exposed, while CLS/ANET face knock-on risk from
slower AI infrastructure deployment. Full GIR piece here.
Data Centre Construction Growth (Adam Bubes, Senior Equity Analyst Covering E&Cs): Despite
recent narrative shits, there is good visibility on continued US data centre construction growth through
2028, with a ~$900bn high-confidence pipeline and upgraded 2026-28 spend forecasts, while post-
midterm restrictions are more likely to delay and extend the cycle into 2029/30 than reduce the overall
TAM. Full GIR piece here.

3) NZD Update
BOTTOM LINE: NZD has rallied sharply this morning, outperforming the elevated event weight after a
stronger performance from the incumbent National Party in the weekend’s election. Along with the ACT
and NZ First, the tight-leaning bloc secured ~67 seats of the expanded 131-seat parliament, putting it
ahead of the left-leaning bloc which most importantly for NZD FX, reduces the risk of a shift back to the
RBNZ’s dual mandate (including an employment objective). However, an official result will not be
confirmed until the end of the month (expected 27-Nov), which may prolong some of the uncertainty given
the minor majority currently projected.
Today’s Move and Where Next: The bulk of today’s move feels driven by short covering (via both
NZDUSD & AUDNZD) which built-up following the RBNZ’s surprise hold and much weaker-than-expected
labour data – the latter adding to dual mandate risks. Additionally, the soft USD tone as well as broader
improvement in risk sentiment have also benefited NZD this morning. From here, GS Trading prefer to
trade with a long NZD bias, citing exceptionally strong November seasonality (see chart below), but are
somewhat reluctant to chase extremes given still weak economic trends. Given the latter, we will likely
eventually look fade NZD strength.

4) FX STRATEGY (CAHILL) – Global FX Trader: After The Wave – LINK
We think slower AI-related construction, or lower odds of a re-escalation in the Middle East, would be
negative for the Dollar over time. But, in both cases, we see reasons to be sceptical of the degree to
which market sentiment seems to have shifted:
-) Our equity and policy analysts alike do not expect a big policy impact on data centre development.
-) We think energy market moves may under-appreciate the still-significant uncertainties ahead.
Despite its recent resilience, we believe the Dollar is still sensitive to AI-related risks. That often gets
overshadowed in the short-term either by the Dollar’s safe-haven properties in sharp corrections or other
global factors when the changes are small, but we still can attribute medium- and longer-term trends in
the Dollar to US relative return prospects, which have become increasingly dependent on the trends in
tech.
For investors looking to hedge:
-) We think EUR downside options can protect against renewed energy price pressures.

-) Our analysis suggests the Yen is best-positioned to benefit if there was a sharp repricing in capex
expectations.
The AI sensitivity might also help explain MXN’s more cyclical behaviour of late, but most of our more
cautious stance there relates to further asymmetric dovish policy risks.
