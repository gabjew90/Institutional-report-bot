GS on CPI – full details here We estimate a 0.16% increase in October core CPI (month-over-month SA).
On the softer side, we expect deflation in some autos categories, including a 1.5% decline in used car
prices and a 0.3% decline in the car insurance category. We expect a 0.1% decline in medical services
prices, reflecting a large decline in medical insurance prices -2.5%) as a result of the semiannual source
data update in this month’s report that more than offsets upward pressure from potential seasonal
distortions in other medical care services categories. Additionally, we expect the airfares category to be
unchanged on a month-over-month basis—following average monthly increases of 2.5% so far this year—
reflecting the signal from alternative price data. After last month's jump, we forecast more moderate
increases in the shelter categories—a 0.26% increase in the OER category and a 0.23% increase in the rent
category—reflecting the continued slowdown in their underlying trend. On the firmer side, the October
seasonal factors have been distorted by the approach the Bureau of Labor Statistics is using to interpolate
the data that are missing because of last year’s government shutdown for the purposes of calculating
updated seasonal factors. We estimate that this new source of residual seasonality will unduly boost
October seasonally-adjusted core CPI inflation by 5bp, and provide a disproportionate boost to the public
transportation, apparel, and hotels categories. We estimate a 0.21% rise in headline CPI—reflecting higher

food (+0.4%) and energy (+0.5%) prices. Year-over-year calculations will not be available for most
indexes, including the headline and core CPIs, reflecting missing values for October 2025 due to last
year’s government shutdown that hampered data collection. Our forecast is consistent with a larger 0.23%
monthly increase in the core PCE price index in October.

Aerospace and defense opportunities – desk views:
We come away more constructive than in a long time on aerospace, specifically the aftermarket but across
the subsector. Trends across the aftermarket, while more mixed than in recent years, were solid Q3. And
opportunities lie ahead for secular acceleration even if we are in a cyclical slowdown (think the LEAP).
We also believe that, with a bit of patience and considering recent events, the path for geopolitical turmoil
and oil prices is likely more dampened than it has been. GE comes to mind as the bellwether with lowered
expectations after underperformance this year vs. the broader market, industrials and within the sector
itself. On GE specifically, we have consistently highlighted the valuation as extended, stretched or
expensive – however you want put it. With the 10-point pullback in the P/E multiple and 7-point pullback
in the EV/EBITDA multiple and a reminder that historical valuations here embedded GEHC at a much
lower multiple and believe it or not GEV at a headwind to valuation, perhaps the multiple is now in a
much more reasonable place all around. We like HONA as a deeper value turnaround. And BA with
patience now that free cash estimates have rebased.
We also come away positive defense primes as well though acknowledge the very recent outperformance
in the bid to “defensives” creates a slightly higher bar than we wish. However, taking a step back, we
remain in the middle of geopolitical turmoil working down munitions stockpiles amongst other areas,
DoW continues to have interests aligned with higher volume output, Q3 is the cleanest quarter across the
board we can remember – LMT GD NOC LHX RTX – nothing is perfect but Q3 was as close as we’ve
seen in recent memory. The path on the budget appears, with some noise, likely higher not lower over the
short and medium term. As a stock with catalysts we highlight LHX with potential in new management,
tweaks in presentation of financials and catalysts like Golden Dome, Missile Solutions optionality that
rarely come up in conversation anymore. From a portfolio perspective and along with the more positive
outlook for aerospace, we also like RTX with very strong organic growth, idio margin potential and
portfolio levered to growth / restocking areas. On what may have run to far, we look at the ATI vs. CRS
vs. everything gap and wonder if that could pause. HWM HEI WWD all look more expensive vs. GE than
normal. TXT caught a fundamental (no charges) and under owned bid in the last moth but remains an
inexpensive stock – we are torn here but could see this type of name give back. Lastly, should you want
both aero and AI – take a look at what’s happened to BWXT and CW. We remain neutral/slightly negative
the AI catalyst path but acknowledge where we are.
