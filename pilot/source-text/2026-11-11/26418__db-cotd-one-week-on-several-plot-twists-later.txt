DB CoTD: One Week On, Several Plot Twists Later 
It’s now a week since we learnt the results of the midterm elections — a week of extraordinary market 
volatility, but also one in which some clearer trends have begun to emerge. 
First, the volatility. The day after the vote brought the usual post-election rally, with the S&P 500 (+1.58%) 
posting its best day in three months. But that swiftly reversed: the following session saw the S&P 500’s 
biggest daily slump (-4.36%) since the week of the Liberation Day tariff announcements in April 2025. That 
was followed by a strong bounce on Monday (+2.35%), its best day since the Iran ceasefire announcement in 
April. And the volatility hasn’t been confined to equities or election themes. Only yesterday, the post-CPI 
reaction saw the 2yr Treasury yield jump +17.6bps, its biggest move higher since the Liberation Day tariffs 
were postponed by 90 days. 
Amid all that volatility, the S&P 500 is down just -0.6% over the week. But beneath the surface there have 
been much clearer winners and losers. Tech (-4.3%) has notably underperformed, with the stocks most 
exposed to AI doing particularly badly. The Philly semiconductor index is down -5.7% since the midterms, 
partly amid expectations of tougher AI regulation. 
There have been relative winners too. Solar stocks have performed well since the Democratic sweep, with 
the MAC Global Solar Energy Index up +1.4%. In Europe, the continent’s smaller exposure to AI has helped 
the STOXX 600 rise +2.0%. The MSCI EM index has been broadly steady since the midterms, while the Hang 
Seng Tech index rose +5.6% in the week after the vote — perhaps reflecting some rotation from US AI into 
China AI — although it has slipped back again today. 
Oil has been another notable mover. Prices have plummeted as the anticipated post-midterm escalation in 
the Middle East has failed to materialise; if anything, the rhetoric so far has leaned towards de-escalation. 
Brent crude fell -11.7% in the week after the midterms, closing at its lowest level since August, even if prices 
are edging higher again today. 
So, one week on, the market’s initial verdict seems to be that the election result is relatively bad for AI and 
good for lower energy prices. To play devil’s advocate, our equity strategists have pointed out here that US 

tech positioning is now very low relative to recent years, while a deal with Iran still looks a long way off. So 
this one-week trade already looks like it will have plenty of twists and turns ahead.
