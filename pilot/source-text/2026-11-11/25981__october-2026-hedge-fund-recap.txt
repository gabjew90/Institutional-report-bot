Performance Highlights 
Quick highlights for October: 
• 
Based on our MS Proprietary Estimates (Figure 1):  
- The average Global fund lagged the broader market in October, down -2.3% vs. the MSCI AC World's -1.6% (MXWD 
Index -1.6% in Oct). The average Global L/S fund did worse at -3.1%, as PnL was hit by continued volatility in the AI 
complex. On a YTD basis, the average Global fund across all strategies is +5.3% and the average Global L/S fund is 
+4.8%, which compares to the MSCI AC World index +11.6%. Note that ~40% of L/S funds globally are now flat to 
negative YTD as continued weakness in crowded positions has meaningfully eroded the PnL buffer from highs in June.  
- Americas-based L/S funds lagged the broader group, with the average fund down -2.6%, well below the S&P's -87bps. 
The average Americas-based fund held up slightly better at -1.7%, though YTD returns have continued to erode as the 
avg. Americas-based fund and avg. L/S fund are +6.0% and +6.1%, respectively, vs. the S&P 500 +11.8%.  
- October marked the 4th negative month of 2026 for the average Global L/S fund (-3.1%), following March (-4.6%), 
July (-1.8%) and September (-1.5%). It's also the second month in a row in which Global L/S funds lost more than the 
MSCI in a down tape (-3.1% vs. MSCI -1.6%), after holding up better than the index in March. Americas-based L/S funds 
saw a similar pattern at -2.6%, their 4th negative month of the year and well below the S&P. 
- In Europe, the average EU-based HF was down -2.6% and the average EU-based L/S fund was down -2.5%. Both 
lagged the Euro STOXX 600, which rose +2.0%, though the L/S losses partly reflect the group's elevated exposure to 
US equities. For the year, the average EU-based HF and average EU-based L/S fund are +3.8% and +2.2%, respectively, 
which compares to the Euro STOXX 600 Index +12.4%. 
- Asia-based HFs mitigated losses seen in the MSCI Asia, with the average Asia-based HF -5.2% and Asia-based L/S funds 
-4.9% in Oct, which compares to the MSCI Asia Pacific index -6.1%. For the year, the average Asia-based HF is now 
+2.9% and the average Asia-based L/S fund is +3.7% vs. the MSCI Asia Pacific index +16.4%. China-focused L/S funds 
were down -4.7% in Oct but still lead the broader Asia cohort on a YTD basis at +4.2%. 
- Dispersion remains wide on a YTD basis, with the 90th %-tile performer across the Global L/S subset up ~20.5% 
whereas the bottom 10th %-tile is down ~11.2%. The 25th %-tile performer across Global L/S funds is now down ~4.1% 
for the year, and October alone saw a ~10.2% spread between the 90th (+2.3%) and 10th (-7.9%) %-tiles.

Quant & Multi-Manager PnL & Factor Positioning Update 
October was among the more challenging months for the combined Quant / Multi-Manager subset on record, with the median 
Global fund posting losses of ~49bps (all returns quoted are unlevered) for the month, bringing YTD gains to up +89bps.  There 
was once again wider dispersion in the returns with the 25th %-tile fund down ~143bps in Oct while the 75th %-tile fund was up 
+9bps.  The losses were largest in Asia where the median account was down -96bps, though on a YTD basis, returns are still 
+327bps.  In N. Am, losses were just ~14bps for the month of Oct while EU losses amounted to ~74bps.   
At the factor level, much of the losses can be explained by Momentum and Beta, as both factors underperformed and given 
the Quant / Multi-Manager group were long these two factors at the end of Q3, they both weighed on returns.  Momentum 
along contributed -14bps to returns with Beta eroding another ~4bps.  LT Reversal, Size, and Liquidity also detracted from 
returns in Oct.  On the other hand, Analyst Sentiment, Earnings Yield, and Profitability were all positive contributors as each of 
the factors ended in positive territory for the month, which given the fact the cohort was long these factors, benefited returns.

Global L/S Alpha Erodes Through October; Long Alpha Barely Positive YTD  
After September’s brief recovery, October ended as another difficult month for Global L/S alpha driven by pain on both sides 
of the book. Longs fell ~4.8% while shorts were down only ~0.3%, resulting in the L/S spread down -4.5% for the month. 
Relative to the MSCI (-1.6%), this indicates long alpha detracted -3.2% while short alpha similarly dragged -1.3%, marking a 
reversal in the strength on the short side seen through September and a continuation in the long side pain seen through July.   
On a YTD basis, October continued to give back a meaningful portion of the alpha that had built up earlier in the year – long 
alpha is now barely positive YTD (outperforming by ~15bps) while short alpha has similarly declined to +1.6% YTD (vs. +3.1% 
through Sep). This brings the spread between longs and shorts globally to +1.8% (vs. Oct YTD avg. of +6.0%), down from +7% a 
month ago. 
          
 
 
By region, the spread between longs and shorts turned negative across most regions, led by APAC equities (-8.7%) and N. 
America (-3.2%) with LatAm the only region in positive territory (+1.4%).  In APAC, Japan drove the negative alpha (-10.6%) as 
longs lagged benchmarks by ~8.4%, while in N. Am, longs fell ~2.1% more than benchmarks and shorts rose slightly. Europe was 
also negative (-2.8%), hurt by shorts rising +2.5%.  
By sector, the spread was weakest in Info Tech (-8.5%), driven by the long side as longs fell ~6.2% more than the sector – in 
absolute terms, it is worth noting the longs were down ~14% for in Oct. The YTD Tech spread now stands at +13.0%. Materials 
was the strongest (+2.0%), driven by the short side as shorts fell ~3.6% more than the longs, followed by Cons Staples (+1.5%).

GLOBAL POSITIONING AND LEVERAGE 
THEME #1: HFs Unwind Global Gross Exp Led by Long Selling  
HFs ended as net sellers of Global equities through October as the de-grossing was driven largely by the long side. The selling 
flow took place across all regions, led by N. America, followed by APAC, Japan and Europe. Multi-Strat / Macro and Equity L/S 
strategies drove the net selling, while flows from Stat Arb / Quant funds were more muted in net terms. 
The Global L/S ratio (ex. Quants) declined slightly MoM, with current levels sitting at 1.86x.  Relative to history, the current L/S 
ratio sits in the 3rd %-tile over the last 12M and in the 1st %-tile relative to the last 5 years.   
 
 
 
           
 
 
THEME #2: Largest Active L/S Fund De-Grossing Across Global Equities vs. Last Decade 
Amidst further challenges at the factor level through October, HFs continued to unwind gross exposure across global equities. 
The reductions have been extreme when isolating to Equity L/S funds, who have actively unwound an aggregate ~9% of their 
global gross exposure between late June and the end of October. This episode has ranked as the largest active L/S HF unwind 
across global equities seen in the last decade.  
While this metric does not account for the impacts of negative price action on HF gross exposure, this event has been stark 
considering much of the total reduction in HF gross exposure has been driven by HFs actively unwinding risk as opposed to 
deterioration in price. When layering in price impact, this event still falls short of other market-driven declines in HF gross 
exposure like the COVID drawdown (-30%) or the 2021-2022 period (~40%). Importantly, the reductions have not just been 
driven by US equities but also through outsized reductions in both APAC and Europe.

exposure into the mid-terms and then subsequently added to gross exposure following the event. With that said, the lack of a

THEME #2: AI Positioning Risks are Two Ways 
HF net positioning across AI themes has also moderated meaningfully, falling from a peak of ~47% of total US net exposure at

remain along the most bearishly positioned in the trade versus the last year, leaving it susceptible to sharp moves higher.

EUROPE POSITIONING 
THEME #1: HFs Reduce Gross Exp to EU Equities; EU Crowded L/S Spread Worst Month in >10Yrs 
HFs across all strategies have continued to unwind gross exposure from EU equities through October, marking one of the 
sharpest unwinds seen in the region in recent history. These reductions have notably occurred against an already-light 
positioning backdrop – HF net exposure to EU equities sits near 15Y+ lows, while EU equities make up the smallest share of 
global gross exposure in over 15 years (<15% of global gross).   
The selling was broad-based across all strategies (led by Equity L/S), HF regions (both US-based and EU-based HFs), and spread 
across sectors (led by TMT via Software and Semis). Given the long supply has been more concentrated in well-owned pockets 
of the portfolio, PnL has been challenging as alpha across the top 50 longs and shorts in EU was -7.9% through October, with the 
YTD L/S spread sitting at -6.7%.   
 
 
 
Across the EU Top 50 Crowded Longs and Shorts, October marked the most painful month for the spread in 10+ years, down as 
much as -9.4% intra-month and finishing at -7.9%, beyond July’s prior record (-7.6%). Further, the weakness in alpha was driven 
by both longs and shorts – an atypical dynamic as both long and short alpha being negative in the same month has only 
happened eight times since 2016, with three occurring this year (February, July and October). Given the challenging dynamics 
across both the long and short book, 2026 has shaped to be one of the most challenging years for L/S alpha in the top 50 
names in European equities in over a decade.
