1) US ECONOMICS (MERICLE) – October CPI Preview
BOTTOM LINE: We expect a 0.16% increase in October core CPI (vs. +0.2% consensus). We expect a
0.21% increase in headline CPI (vs. +0.3% consensus), reflecting higher food and energy prices. Our
forecast is consistent with a larger 0.23% increase in core PCE in October.
We highlight three key component-level trends we expect to see in the report:
1/ We expect the airfares category to be unchanged month-over-month based on our tracking of online
prices.
2/ We expect a 0.1% decline in medical services prices, reflecting a large decline in health insurance prices
(-2.5%) as the Bureau of Labor Statistics incorporates updated source data on insurance premiums.
3/ We expect a slowdown in autos inflation, reflecting a 1.5% decline in used car prices, a 0.1% increase
in new car prices, and a 0.3% decline in the auto insurance category.
Looking Beyond October: We expect monthly core CPI increases of around 0.1% in November and
December, reflecting the continued slowdown in the shelter categories and shrinking contributions from
tariff-related price increases, as well as continued downward pressure from the health insurance
component.

2) G10 FX SPOT TRADING (BRAUTEN-SMITH) – G10 FX Spot Views
It feels as though the natural USD buying with respect to hyperscaler Eurobond issuance is behind us, and
yesterday’s session stands in stark contrast to those the week prior -- there was no indiscriminate USD bid
anywhere really (previous focused in the US session), and if anything there were suggestions that G10 FX
as an asset class was finally willing to participate in, or at least acknowledge, the energy moves of the past
week.
Participation is still on the light side and this is understandable given the performance strain for
discretionary investors...overwhelming feedback from a trip I took to NYC last week was a desire for 2026
to simply end...), and we expect to continue registering low volumes unless we see a decisive MoM
reading today on US CPI.
Tomorrow is a holiday (stocks open, bonds closed, no USD ccy settlement) which complicates the picture
further, and if anything we are tilting our books towards a weaker USD now vs. JPY & GBP, from tactical
USD length versus EUR last week.

There are idiosyncratic reasons for peripheral USDs to come under pressure -- the NZ elections trimmed
the left tail on NZD (incumbents will continue to form the base of a coalition, no dual mandate), but the
seasonality for AUDNZD-lower in November already has a headstart with the cross -1.6% on the month,
and that’s with reverberations of the end-Oct mis-step by the RBNZ (held vs. +24bps priced-in) and
weaker NZ employment data last week.
GBP longs are favoured over EUR purely from the fact the UK doesn’t include France... Interest to latch
back onto French woes is low for now, but late September/early October price-action has already proved
that an appetite exists to press the French fiscal trade, and “playing a French deterioration” is probably
second on the list of 2027 trades pencilled-in so far (behind short USDJPY/XXXJPY). GBPUSD also has
a much smaller natural offer --> on a USD sell-off GBPUSD should be high-beta. We own mid-December
1.3700 Calls and have a Cash overlay.
There’s no science in this...it’s simply an observation...but if most of the feedback is one of frustration
with markets in 2026, then the most painful move would be for those trade that people have in mind to put
on in 2027 to begin playing-out already over the next 6 weeks...

3) GS FX MARKET STRATS (RASTOGI) – USDCNH: Watching the Fix
Following last week’s FX reserve ratio hike, the key question is whether the CNH appreciation trend
remains intact. GS EM Strategy believe it does and favour adding to short USDCNH positions once the
spot fixing gap narrows back to 200–300 pips. With December exporter repatriation expected to be
stronger than usual, the view is for USDCNH to grind lower towards 6.55–6.57 by end-January. We will
also be watching the fixing closely as a key gauge of the authorities’ intentions. With this in mind, we look
at the fixing pattern so far this year and what it might signal for the path ahead.

4) Equities Update
Rich Privorotsky, Head of EMEA Flow Intermediation and European One Delta Trading, out with his
latest views. Key takeaways below
Overview: Strong recovery yesterday. Tech back on the front foot, Nvidia weakness compartmentalised,
and energy in freefall. A Middle East that is at least not escalating, enough barrels to balance the physical
market and perhaps the worst of the tech unwind behind us? In hindsight, we’ve just witnessed one of the

most aggressive episodes of net/gross de-risking in our US prime book over the past decade. The final
piece is CPI today. An in-line or softer print that challenges the ~80% probability of a December Fed
hike/or more like the risk of a March hike. Suddenly the setup looks considerably more constructive. Now
we just had violent rally off the lows and we should be vigilant locally as vol tends to be autocorrelated.
The obvious risks are hot CPI and renewed Middle East escalation into the weekend.
Tech: First of the neoclouds to report post-midterms today. The question isn’t demand, but whether
contracted capacity translates into attractive returns after financing costs, delays and depreciation. More
encouraging is the market’s ability to compartmentalize Nvidia’s company specific headlines rather than
sell the entire AI complex. Leadership uncertainty around someone synonymous with a company for 30
years matters, but it isn’t the same as deteriorating AI demand. The broader complex trading
independently is a positive signal.
Data Centres: The bipartisan permitting bill cleared its first Senate procedural hurdle 73–25. Not final
passage, with the House likely the bigger obstacle. I think the market misread the elections. States want
environmental protections, grid cost accountability and local oversight, but they also want jobs and
investment. A bigger majority means the Democratic Party isn’t controlled exclusively by its fringe. The
likely outcome is data centers getting built with more conditions attached, not a nationwide moratorium.
Permitting reform benefits infrastructure and well capitalized developers. CEG +10% on stronger
numbers, higher guidance and additional generation agreements reinforces that the physical buildout
continues.
Risk: Impulses are supportive, energy supportive, rates supportive, credit resilient, and meanwhile
positioning/sentiment remains shell shocked and diminished. The obvious re-grossing trade is for rate
curve to steepen, growth expectations to recover and high beta/momentum to regain leadership. If CPI
finally gives bonds a reason to rally, we could transition from short covering to a more sustainable re-
grossing cycle. My gut remains we confused the narratives on AI. Undersupply is not the problem, it’s the
fear of potential compute oversupply/efficiency gains that should be worrying us in the MT/LT. For now, if
energy and inflation align, the year-end setup looks increasingly attractive with positioning so defensive.
Aerospace, transports, select discretionary and high quality/unlevered AI look like the natural areas to
chase.
