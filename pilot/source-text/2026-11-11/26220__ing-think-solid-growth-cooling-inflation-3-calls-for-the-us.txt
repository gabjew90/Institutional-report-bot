Solid growth and cooling inflation: 3
calls for the US  
Our base case assumes solid growth, cooling inflation and the Fed hiking
once more as Middle East tensions subside. However, if President Trump
feels constrained after the midterm elections and hawks within the

different economic outlook. Our bold call envisages an AI revolution

How to read our three calls for 2027

ING's base call: Solid growth and cooling inflation remains
our call
We continue to expect respectable US growth of 2-2.5% over the next couple of years,
but with ongoing bifurcation. Wealth gains are supporting spending among higher-

income households, while weaker wage growth is pressuring middle- and lower-
income households. 
Within business investment, tech-related capex shows little sign of slowing, but
appears to be cannibalising investment spending elsewhere and is contributing to the
low-hire, low-fire economy. Manufacturing is similarly K-shaped: high-value-added
advanced manufacturing is growing strongly and adding jobs, while more
commoditised, labour-intensive manufacturing struggles. The growth story's lack of
breadth is a slight concern and creates vulnerabilities, particularly if asset prices
correct.
Inflation remains above target but should ease as energy costs fall and rents cool.
More significantly, labour costs are the biggest cost input for corporate America. With
wage growth dropping to 3%, we see just one more Fed rate rise – in December –
before a long pause for most of 2027.
Our risky call: Constrained Trump goes all-in for regime change
in Iran
With President Trump constrained by a Democratic-led Congress, foreign and trade policy
remain key sources of risk to our base case. For example, Iran hawks within the Administration
could convince him that diplomacy won't work, meaning regime change in Iran is required.
Disruption in the Gulf would then intensify, with energy prices spiking. At the same time,
President Trump could demand NATO involvement, applying onerous tariffs on imports from
members that refuse to participate in the conflict with Iran.
In this scenario, rising inflation could prompt the Fed to raise rates above 5%. Government
borrowing would rise on military spending, but Treasury yields would be held down by 'safe-
haven' buying as equity markets plunge. Recession risks would then mount through demand
destruction as unemployment climbs. Iran would eventually be weakened sufficiently that
attacks cease and energy flows resume. Oil, natural gas and fertiliser prices would then
subside, with inflation falling rapidly. Monetary policy would be eased aggressively, but it
would be too little, too late to prevent a painful downturn for the global economy.
Our bold call: Party like it's 1999? US growth hits 5% in 2027
US GDP growth averaged 4.5% in the four years before the dotcom crash in 2000. Yet for all
the talk of the surge in AI-related investment spending in the current cycle, the economy has
only averaged 2.7% annual growth since ChatGPT launched four years ago. In our bold call,
that would change in 2027.
