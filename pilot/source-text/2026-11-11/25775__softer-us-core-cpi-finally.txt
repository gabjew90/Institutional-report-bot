Softer US core CPI, finally? 
◼ 
Following last Friday’s strong upside surprise in the payrolls report at 
+165k, well above the consensus of +70k, the focus is now the US CPI. 
◼ 
We namely expect core at 0.26% MoM SA, so a bit softer than in recent 
months. The YoY measures cannot be calculated (no data in 
October 2025). 
◼ 
Looking ahead to late 2026 and early 2027, base effects – tariffs, 
statistical treatment of OER and rents – will dominate the YoY reading. 
We have core around 2.5% YoY at an end-2027 horizon. 
Following last Friday’s strong upside surprise in the payrolls report at +165k, well 
above the consensus of +70k, the focus will turn to the inflation side of the Fed’s 
mandate this week. 
We expect US headline CPI to come out at 0.29% MoM SA in October. We cannot 
provide a YoY number, as the October 2025 index was never published by the 
BLS, courtesy of last year’s government shutdown. There is no YoY consensus 
either of course. We namely expect core at 0.26% MoM SA in October, vs 0.29% 
in August (a number boosted by noise in telephone services) and 0.33% in 
September. Our take is 2bp above the 0.24% MoM SA Bloomberg average 
consensus. The 3M ma annualised core would come out at 3.53% in October, the 
highest level since January 2025. Simply put, we expect a relatively benign 
report with core back around 0.25% MoM SA, so a bit softer than August and 
September. 
Considering the recent sharp decline in Brent crude oil prices, our model now 
suggests headline around 3.5-3.6% YoY until February 2027. The peak was 
reached in May 2026, at 4.25% YoY. We do not expect a major impact on CPI 
from the blue wave result of the midterm election, beyond the lower crude oil 
prices which are already embedded in our model.  
Within core, we have seen very limited signs of second-round effects from higher 
energy prices due to the Iran war, aside from airfares (1.3% of the core CPI basket) 
of course. We have started to see some pass-through in some items in core goods, 
but nothing significant yet. For the coming six months, base effects will 
continue to dominate the core YoY reading.

On the one hand, last year’s tariffs are gradually leaving the YoY calculation, taking 
core goods CPI YoY lower. On the other hand, OER and rent YoY will be 
abnormally strong from November 2026 to March 2027 due to the BLS’ 
methodological choices after the 2025 government shutdown. 
If our forecast is on track, the October CPI report would represent an improvement 
compared to September, though would still be firmer than is ideal for the Fed even 
in terms of core inflation. We continue to expect another 25bp hike in December, 
followed by a final 25bp hike in March as the Fed delivers 75bp of hikes in total to 
reverse last year’s insurance cuts, at an every-other-meeting pace, before another 
pause.
