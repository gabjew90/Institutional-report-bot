Strongest Bullish Setup YTD: Expect SPX 10%+ Higher In 3M
Over the weekend, I wrote “One more pocket of forced selling into Monday, but increasingly few sellers left
afterward. I expect a Monday low followed by a sharp recovery.” I was right about few sellers left, but wrong
about Monday’s low since buying forces simply overwhelmed the lone remaining systematic seller, Risk
Control. After Monday, there should be very little systematic selling left, if any.
Most important point – Systematic positioning is washed out! Risk Control Exposure at 55% is the lowest level
since tariff! The last two comparable washouts were 55% on Aug. 6, 2024, and 30% on April 10, 2025, after
which SPX advanced 17% and 24% in four months, respectively. CTA equity exposure is -5% - negative!
Historically, negative CTA exposure tends to be a bottoming signal, particularly when real-money investors are
not selling, as is now the case. The key implication is that the downside feedback loop has largely been
removed. Negative headlines can still trigger knee-jerk selling from discretionary investors, but systematic
funds are unlikely to become the second-derivative sellers that amplify the initial move lower. With real-money
support from retail, foreign investors and corporate buybacks (returning next week), news-driven selloffs
should be shallow.
That leaves a compelling asymmetric setup: washed-out systematic positioning and limited incremental
systematic selling, resilient real-money demand, short-squeeze risk, and potential CTA and Risk Control
re-risking on the upside. I expect SPX to grind higher in a lower-volatility rally, with at least +10% upside over
the next three months.
(Rebecca Cheong, Structuring Strategist)

AI Trade Political Risk Looks Overdone – Equity Research: Political risk around data center capacity is
rising, but Karl Keirstead argues in his latest note that the market has become too bearish on the potential hit
to the AI buildout. The view follows two days of conversations at DCD Connect in Virginia with more than 15
participants across the data-center ecosystem, including hyperscalers, Meta, Vantage, Stack and GE
Vernova. The key read is that the political debate appears more focused on tightening and balancing the
permitting and approval process than stopping projects already under construction, suggesting the bigger
impact would fall on 2028+ capacity. There is a potential bull case here. Tighter permitting could slow capacity
growth in 2027/28, reducing capex and balance sheet and credit risks while increasing the scarcity value of
existing sites. Karl does not fully buy that argument, viewing any constraint on AI capacity as ultimately
negative for the broader ecosystem. But he thinks investors have moved too far bearish. (Christina Dwyer,
Specialist Sales)
Iran Rejects US Talk Claims, Reasserts War Conditions And Hormuz Curbs – Iran’s security chief
Mohsen Rezaei dismissed US reports of negotiations as “fake news” and demanded acceptance of Tehran’s
seven conditions, conveyed through Qatar in September. Three publicly named demands are ending the war
on all fronts, releasing frozen Iranian funds and lifting the US naval blockade. Tehran says normal Hormuz
traffic will not resume until its conditions are met. The remarks contrast with US President Donald Trump’s
“positive momentum” assessment. While rejecting reports of negotiations, Iran has acknowledged that
messages continue through mediators. The competing positions leave no clear near-term path to restoring
normal Hormuz flows, despite Trump’s assessment that renewed US military action looks unlikely. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Japan Household Spending Slumps; Some BoJ Policymakers Urge Faster Rate Hikes – Japan’s
household spending fell 6.8% y/y in September, more than the consensus forecast for a 3.1% decline, marking
the largest slide since September 2020. Seasonally adjusted spending dropped 2.8% m/m, versus a
consensus forecast of a 0.3% rise. The internal affairs ministry attributed the slump to poor weather that
discouraged outings as Tokyo recorded precipitation every day of the month for the first time. The weakness
contrasted with a ninth consecutive month of real wage gains and the fastest nominal wage growth since

3
January 1997. Separately, the BoJ’s October meeting summary of opinions showed some policymakers
advocating faster rate hikes as underlying inflation reached its 2% target. One opinion argued that financial
conditions remained accommodative and the effects of past hikes had yet to materialise; two others called for
moving the policy rate towards neutral soon. The BoJ held its key rate at 1.25% last month and flagged upside
inflation risks and is widely expected to hike in December. The spending slump adds a reason for caution over
the pace of further tightening. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
China Economic Outlook 2027-28: Transformation Amid Steady Headline Growth – China Economics
expects annual GDP growth to remain around 4.6% in 2027, broadly similar to 2026, saying policymakers
have preferred a more gradual slowdown and are likely to maintain modest support into early next year.
Economist Yu Song sees the growth target being set at "4.5-5%" again. While shifts between growth and
non-economic priorities should continue to generate intra-year mini cycles, there are important disparities
beneath the expected stability in headline growth. Sectoral disparities in fiscal resources, real interest rates
and the regulatory environment will likely persist, further widening sectoral divergence. Yu expects a smaller
net export contribution to GDP in 2027 as energy-transition demand eases and trade tensions weigh on
exports, likely increasing the need to support domestic demand. Meanwhile, consumption growth may stay
broadly stable at a soft level. Yu also points to further RMB appreciation. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

UK Retail Spending Growth Edges Up Even As Consumers Remain Cautious – The British Retail
Consortium and KPMG reported retail sales growth of 1.5% y/y in October, up from September’s 1.3% and the
strongest since June. Food sales rose 3.2%, while non-food sales were flat. While higher household costs and
shoppers awaiting Black Friday deals were constraints, cupboard staples rose modestly after government
advice to stock up for emergencies. The survey suggests modest, uneven growth rather than a broad-based
retail pickup. Separately, Barclays reported consumer card spending growth of 2.3% y/y, up from September’s
2.0% and the strongest since July 2025. Its measure covers a wider range of goods and services than the
BRC survey. Essential spending rose 3.1%, driven by a 22.7% increase in fuel spending, while non-essential
spending grew 1.9%. Meanwhile, Barclays’ survey showed optimism about the UK economy at its lowest since
April. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Australia Outlook: Growth To Slow As Inflation Stays High – UBS Economics: Australia is set to miss the
global growth pick-up, with economist George Tharenou forecasting GDP growth of 1.4% in 2027. Earlier rate
hikes and tax changes are weakening housing, while slower migration adds pressure to demand. AI

RBNZ's Conway Sees Recovery Broadening With Inflation Risks Elevated – New Zealand’s economic
recovery is gaining traction, while persistent inflation risks leave monetary policy firmly data-dependent, RBNZ
chief economist Paul Conway said at the UBS Australasia Conference. He said the recovery is taking hold but
remains uneven. Core inflation is still elevated, consistent with the RBNZ’s October assessment, while cost
shocks could broaden into more persistent inflationary pressure. He stressed that economic conditions, rather
than market pricing, should guide policy following the surprise rate hold in October. Conway said sustained
currency depreciation could complicate the inflation outlook, while strong exports contrast with softer
household consumption. He expects the labour market to strengthen as the recovery becomes more
embedded. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
Semiconductor Selloff Prices In Power Gap – Equity Research: The SOX’s roughly 30% drop over the
past month has pushed semiconductor valuations close to levels that fully discount the AI data-center
powered-shell constraint, according to Timothy Arcuri. With the index down another roughly 12% last week
following the US midterm elections, current pricing implies much of the potential downside from slower
data-center development is already reflected in stocks. Working backwards from historical SOX multiples, Tim
calculates that current prices imply roughly 30% and 35% cuts to Street calendar-year 2027 and calendar-year
2028 EPS estimates, respectively. After adjusting for operating leverage and fixed costs, that equates to about
a 20% reduction in implied calendar-year 2028 revenue. Translated into compute capacity, Tim estimates the
market is effectively pricing roughly 55GW of calendar-year 2028 shipments, versus an original expectation of
about 70GW. That is only modestly above the roughly 50GW of powered-shell availability considered
high-confidence. Tim had previously identified a roughly 20-30GW gap between expected semiconductor
shipments and high-confidence powered-shell capacity in calendar years 2027 and 2028. (Robert Ruple,
Specialist Sales)
