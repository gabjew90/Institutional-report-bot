Outlook 2027: After the derating, how to invest 
Rising risks drove a big derating, which has historically set up strong returns
2026 was a year of regime shifts. The S&P 500  fwd P/E fell ~20%, even as 2026 EPS is on 
track to grow ~33%. AI ROI and regulatory concerns, a spike in yields and credit spreads, 
higher oil and a drop in net buybacks all raised risk premia. The midterms added a new 
risk, as state-level pauses put ~30% of US data center capacity additions in 2028 at risk. 
History is supportive though. In years after a >10% fall in the SPX  P/E, SPX has returned 
a median 20%, with few negatives, as the P/E recouped ~30% of the decline.
Earnings support 2027, but data center risks to 2028 are hard to size
We forecast SPX EPS growth of 18% to $430 in 2027 and 14% to $490 in '28, with 
NVDA, MU and AVGO alone ~45% of next year's growth. Above trend EPS growth 
tends to weigh on the P/E, but big deratings tend to partly reverse. Our S&P 500 target 
for end '27 stays at 8900, or ~18x our 2028 EPS of $490, and our end '28 target is 9600 
on 17.5x EPS of $550 in '29. Data center pauses skew 2028 risks lower, and with policy 
details unlikely before next year, we will revisit forecasts as they emerge.
Drivers of the derating look closer to bottoming/turning
AI capex concerns hit the AI trade even as demand accelerated, with annualized q/q 
cloud  growth nearly tripling to ~$86bn in Q3 from ~$31bn in Q1. Rates vol should fall if 
the Fed hikes twice as UBS  expects, and eventual Iran de-escalation is more likely than 
escalation. Growth worries seem priced as  indicators  point to expansion. Net buybacks 
fell ~$120bn from Q1 to Q3, but surging cash flow points to a $200-400bn rebound 
next year, with the big Semis adding $170bn+. AI capex slowing and a further yield spike 
are the main risks, but after this much derating, more downside looks priced.
Strategy: lean into derated growth, AI demand and the expansion
1) AI demand is accelerating, so buy the Semis derating and application layer.
We estimate AI revenue potential at ~$2.5tr in 5yrs at the midpoint, above the $2tr low 
hurdle for $6-8tr of capex. Yet Semis' P/E has collapsed, pricing  a  fall in profits, so we are 
buyers of Semis over Tech Hardware. We are also o/w Software, now mostly the AI 
application layer, with P/E to growth near 15yr lows. We like Hyperscalers, which own 
scarce compute amid rising capacity headwinds. NDX over RTY is a higher conviction 
view, as NDX EPS has risen ~2x more than RTY's, yet its P/E is back near 2022 lows. AI 
Exposed Semis and AI Software Pioneers baskets can implement the theme.
2) Growth+quality at a discount should lead, with Healthcare most attractive.
Growth and CFROI spreads have widened while valuation spreads are avg. Quality, 
momentum and growth have also tended to lead as expansions mature, so value and 
high beta are funding sources. We are o/w Healthcare, which tops our REVS rankings as 
sales growth rises for BioPharma, and fund it with Staples, as Healthcare trades at about 
half Staples' P/E to sales growth. We reduce Energy after a ~40% rally, as it screens rich 
vs fundamentals. Our Growth+CFROI at a Discount basket over Expensive Fundamentals 
captures the theme, with Longevity & Wellness our preferred Healthcare basket.
3) Expansion is intact, so fade the slowdown with cyclicals over bond proxies.
AI led growth should extend the expansion in 2027. We are o/w Financials, our top 
cyclical, on supportive valuations and solid earnings, and capital clarity could lead to 
payout upside. We favor investment over consumer, as investment ex AI recovers from a 
multi-year recession. Industrials and Materials are closer to neutral but preferred over 
Discretionary, while Utilities and REITs remain funding sources. Quality Capital Providers 
and US Infrastructure over Consumer Cyclicals  can express the views in baskets.

Drivers and risks: where are the asymmetries? 
2026 was a year of regime shifts in which the SPX multiple derated ~4x amid a >30% 
surge in earnings as risk premia increased within equities against a backdrop of historic 
momentum unwinds and rotations. A number of factors drove the derating, 
including: 1) AI monetization, financing and regulatory concerns, 2) the spike in rates 
and rise in credit spreads, 3) Iran escalation and higher oil prices, 4) growth worries on 
tightening financial conditions, 5) less positive equity net buyback dynamics and net 
debt issuance amid full investor positioning. On the other side, SPX NTM earnings are 
set to rise 15%+. As we discuss below, derating years are often followed by reversals in 
relative returns. To understand the setup into 2027, we frame the evolution of equity 
drivers and themes through 2026, and provide our thinking about where we see more 
durable trends vs potential reversals for the quarters ahead:

Big P/E deratings: median 20% SPX returns in the year after. In years 
following a >10% decline in the SPX LTM P/E, SPX has returned an avg 17%, with  
few negative years. The P/E has recouped almost 30% of the prior year's derating 
the next year. With the LTM P/E down ~17% YTD, our 8900 target for end-2027 is 
21% above current and in line with that history, with little re-rating assumed.

AI debates: Capex slowdown potential as AI demand accelerates. H1 2026 
saw Semis double on AI capex upgrades and then fall nearly 40% in H2, as ROI, 
financing and regulatory concerns shifted the narrative to capex sustainability. Yet 
AI monetization is inflecting onto the steep part of the adoption curve, as 
annualized q/q cloud sales growth nearly tripled to ~$86bn in Q3 from ~$31bn in 
Q1. UBS analyst cloud sales estimates are ~15% above consensus for the next four 
quarters. Our monetization work puts AI revenue potential at ~$2.5tr in 5 yrs at 
the midpoint, excluding next gen products, above the $2tr low hurdle for $6-8tr of 
capex. UBS capex estimates of ~$1.7tr in 2029-30 (AI only) sits inside the $0.7-
2.6tr a year that $2-4tr of AI revenue can fund, though sustaining  that likely needs 
revenue above $3tr and next gen products.  Power and regulation remain binding 
constraints, but Semis' P/E near multiyear lows suggests a lot is in the price. 

Bond yields: rate vol falling on benign Fed hikes supports equity upside. 
The jump in corporate bond yields and rates vol was fairly extreme in 2026. The 
market was expecting Fed cuts in Q1 which gave way to hikes as growth was 
strong and inflation was sticky. Rising oil combined with AI debt issuance and 
financing concerns exacerbated the move in rates and credit spreads.  In our view, 
yields are likely to stay elevated as growth remains strong, but rates vol should fall 
if the Fed hikes two more times as UBS econ expects. When the Fed hiked <100bp 
within a year of rate spikes, SPX rallied 18% on avg the next  year. 

Oil and geopolitics: anything can happen but signs of de-escalation. The 
Iran conflict was one of the key drivers of oil, rates and equities this year. While 
escalation remains a risk, we still see eventual de-escalation as the end point and 
lower oil futures prices. With Energy stocks up ~40% YTD, we look to reduce 
exposure and allocate elsewhere with even higher energy prices than recent peaks 
a negative for future demand. 

Regime: fade the slowdown, long expansion. Cyclicals vs defensives did a 
near 180 in 2026 with massive outperformance in H1 nearly fully reversed as yields 
spiked, and the market priced out the recovery regime and priced in a slowdown. 
We see expansion continuing on the back of still strong AI led growth with the 
potential for some broadening.  

Net buybacks: set to jump after big drop from Q1 to Q3. SPX quarterly net 
buybacks fell ~$120bn from Q1 to Q3, which removed a key source of demand for 
stocks. Hyperscalers swung from ~$120bn of net buybacks a year to net issuing ~
$150bn annualized in Q2-Q3 as capex outpaced FCF. The rest of SPX slowed by 
$90bn too. Yet OCF and FCF ex hyperscalers are up $400-500bn y/y, and 
consensus cash flow estimates point to a $200-400bn increase in net buybacks 
next year. Q1 is seasonally the strongest quarter for repurchases too, and 
announcements have picked up in the last 1-2mo.

Strategy:  lean into derated growth, AI demand and 
the expansion
The 2026 derating was uneven led by Semis, Comm Svcs, Energy and Cyclicals while 
Staples, Healthcare and REIT P/Es rose 5%+. Historically, ~50% of sector and IG relative 
return moves have reversed in the year after a derating. Our views lean into that reversal 
and rerating theme, but where earnings have held up and fade overpriced safety/
growth. We group our sector, style and basket views into four themes.
1) AI demand is accelerating, so buy Semis derating and AI application layer. 
Annualized q/q cloud revenue growth nearly tripled to ~$86bn in Q3 from ~$31bn in 
Q1, as AI demand and compute deployment is accelerating into 2027. Our monetization 
work puts AI revenue potential excluding next gen products at ~$2.5tr at the midpoint 
in 5yrs, above the $2tr low hurdle for $6-8tr of cumulative capex. So the debate is about 
timing and the high end, not whether the revenue arrives. Data center pauses after the 
midterms put ~30% of 2028 capacity additions at risk, though a slower buildout would 
also extend the cycle and ease financing pressure. UBS analysts expect MU, NVDA and 
AVGO buybacks to  surge ~$340bn by 2028, with half that equating to 20% of SPX total 
LTM buybacks. That flow should support AI/Tech stocks broadly.

AI Semis at ~10x are cheap vs the growth backlog. Semis' P/E collapse (Arcuri) 
and ~40% discount to SPX  prices mean reversion in  profits.  NVDA, MU and AVGO 
alone account for  ~45% of the $600bn+ rise in SPX earnings in '27. UBS capex 
estimates of ~$1.7tr in 2029-30 (AI only) sit inside the $0.7-2.6tr a year that $2-4tr 
of AI revenue can fund, though sustaining  that likely needs revenue above $3tr 
and next gen products. Chips also need replacing every ~6yrs, which favors 
compute over the building shell. We fund the position with Tech Hardware, which 
scores poorly on REVS (led by AAPL) outside AI servers and networking.

AI Exposed Semis (UBXXSEMA) is our preferred and top scoring thematic 
basket after the selloff. Stocks scoring highest on REVS with Buy ratings include 
AI Semis/Equip (MU, NVDA, AMAT), analog/AI power beneficiaries (TXN, ADI), 
and AI hardware (ANET, CSCO, GLW). 

Software is  the AI application layer, with P/E to growth near 15yr lows. 
About 85% of Software's market cap is AI net beneficiaries, so the ~15% at risk or 
without a clear path to monetize AI is now small. Sales CAGR has risen to ~20%, 
the highest in over a decade, while P/E to sales CAGR has halved to ~1.1x. HOLT 
forecasts Software CFROI at a decade high of 18% in 2027, and new compute 
should turn into application revenue as it comes online. Productivity is the largest 
source of AI revenue in our work, with enterprise adoption worth $0.7-2.1tr of 
compute revenue in 5yrs, and Software is the application layer that delivers it to 
firms.

AI Software Pioneers (UBXXAISP) is our preferred software basket for the 
theme, with NTNX, PLTR, MSFT, SNOW, CRWD and DDOG among the top 
scorers on REVS.

2) Growth+quality at a discount should lead, with Healthcare most attractive. 
Growth and CFROI spreads have widened while valuation spreads have not. The CFROI 
spread between the top and bottom quintiles is in the 99th pctile since 2004, yet the P/E 
spread is only in the 43rd. High value screens cheap but scores negative on both 
earnings level and change, so a value tilt buys cheapness at the cost of earnings 
momentum.

Growth, quality and momentum over value and  beta. Momentum, quality 
and growth have led when ISM has been high but falling, and the expansion will 
eventually slow. REVS spreads agree, with high quality, high momentum and high 
growth scoring well above their low cohorts. Value and high beta have led mainly 
when ISM is rising, and both value and high volatility score negative on REVS. High 
growth quality is preferred as low growth quality behaves more like duration. 

Growth+CFROI at a Discount  (UBEQHGCD) over Expensive Fundamentals 
(UBEQEXFN). The basket trades at 16x P/E vs 21x, with a 14% fwd sales CAGR 
vs 5% and a 33% CFROI vs 6%.

Healthcare over Staples, as Healthcare P/E to sales CAGR is ~3x vs over 6x for 
Staples. Healthcare sales CAGRs of 6-7% are rising, led by Pharma and Biotech, 
and the sector tops our REVS rankings with an 18% CFROI. Its P/E rose this year, 
yet its REVS valuation score remains more supportive than Staples'. Staples' 
earnings, valuation and expected sales growth scores sit near the bottom 5% of 
their three-year histories. So the ~12% rally in the last month looks like an unwind 
of a consensus funding trade rather than better fundamentals.

Longevity & Wellness (UBEQLGWL) is our preferred Healthcare basket. 

Top scoring Healthcare stocks on REVS: Pharma and Biotech (LLY, MRK, GILD, 
AMGN, VTRS), HC Distributors (MCK, CAH) and Managed Care (UNH, ELV). 

Reduce Energy exposure  after a ~40% YTD rally, as it screens rich vs

peaks would hurt future demand.

3) Expansion is intact, so fade the slowdown with cyclicals over bond proxies. 
Cyclicals vs defensives did a near 180 in 2026, as H1 outperformance nearly fully 
reversed once yields spiked. Expansion and slowdown probabilities on the OECD CLI are

Derating of 2026: look for real value 
and reversals
The SPX LTM P/E has declined by 4.3x, or ~17%, YTD, as a number of risks have weighed 
on the multiple. AI concerns have broadened from the pace of adoption to funding to 
data center growth slowing, while the Middle East conflict, higher oil prices, rising yields 
and a more hawkish Fed have added to the derating pressure. Yet earnings growth is set 
to remain strong, creating a disconnect between the multiple and the fundamental 
backdrop.  Heading into 2027, the market is pricing in two Fed rate hikes and a 
prolonged period of elevated rates, alongside heightened geopolitical tensions and 
continued AI uncertainty. The question for returns from here is therefore whether the 
current derating persists or follows the more typical pattern of partial mean reversion. 
After  big P/E declines, median 20% SPX returns 
History has generally been favorable following large P/E declines. In years following a 
>10% decline in the SPX LTM P/E, SPX returns have averaged 17%, with a median return 
of 20% and relatively few instances of negative returns. Multiple recovery has 
contributed to these gains: the SPX P/E has historically recouped almost 30% of the prior 
year's derating in the subsequent year. The relationship also works in reverse with the 
beta in a regression of the subsequent year's percentage change in P/E against the prior 
year's change is -0.26. There are exceptions, including 1973-74 and 2021-22 are 
notable episodes when P/E declines continued into the following year and returns were 
negative.

$6-8tr of cumulative capex requires $2-4tr of

Hyperscaler, neocloud and other AI capex is expected to approach $1.5tr in 2027 on 
UBS estimates, taking cumulative 2025-30 investment to $7.5tr. We exclude non-
AI capex using the prior run-rate and exclude China AI capex. The 2027 UBS estimate is 
9% above consensus while consensus implies cumulative 2025-30 capex of $6.9tr. 
Either path requires >$2tr of annual revenue in 5yrs for the investment to earn an 
adequate return.
Every $1tr of capex requires ~$375bn of revenue. Chips, servers and integration 
represent 67% of AI data center investment and lasts ~6yrs; networking represents 
17% and lasts 6-8yrs; buildings, power and cooling represent 16% and lasts ~20yrs. 
Recovering that investment while earning a 10% unlevered return produces a 21% 
weighted annual capital charge. At a 55% OCF margin, every $1tr of capex therefore 
requires ~$375bn of annual revenue, with $6-8tr of cumulative capex requiring $2.3-
3.0tr of annual revenue. Flexing OCF margins across 45-65% widens the range to $1.9-
3.7tr. Both tails require capex, asset lives and margins to move in the same direction, so 
we use $2-4tr as the hurdle range. 
Required revenue of $2-4tr is 1.3-2.6% of 2030 global GDP, which is not a stretch, 
as 50-100bp of compounded productivity growth would be worth $2.6-5.3tr in five 
years. 
The US spends 2.5% of GDP on software today, so the high-end of the hurdle 
requires a new market of that scale in five years. Software plus R&D took a decade to 
gain 118bp of US GDP in the 1990s. Relative to that, the pace appears demanding but 
most of it does not have to be new spending.

output per worker to maintain profit growth. BLS projects US job growth slowing to 
0.4% a year through 2035, from 1.2% over 2015-25. Against the prior trend, that 
leaves the US ~6mm workers and ~$0.6tr of wages short in 2030. Applying the same 
approach outside the US to working-age population projections points to a ~$2.6tr 
labor income gap by 2030. 
$14-21tr of 2030 global pay is AI-exposed. To size what firms can pay for improved 
productivity from AI, we start with $10.5tr of US private wages across 19 industries 
using data from QCEW (link). Using data from Penn Wharton (Svanberg et al. 2024), we 
score each occupation on the share of tasks that AI can augment and weight those 
scores by employment and wages within each industry based on OEWS data (link). AI 
exposure runs from 8% in agriculture to 68% in finance, with professional services and 
information above 55%. Extrapolating current wages using a 3.5% annual growth, we 
arrive at $4.3-6.4tr of 2030 US pay in AI-exposed work. We scale this globally using ILO 
data which indicates that global income reaches 3.8x of US in 2030, and apply a 15% 
discount to the US exposure rate to account for the larger share of workers in agriculture 
and manufacturing outside the US. That gives $14-21tr of exposed global pay, with a 
midpoint of ~$17tr.
Firms can pay $0.7-2.1tr for that productivity. According to Penn Wharton 
estimates, firms will ultimately find it economically viable to use AI on ~23% of exposed 
tasks. Assuming AI is used on 10-20% of exposed work by 2030 and vendors retain 40-
60% of the value created, enterprise AI revenue can reach $0.7-2.1tr. or 4-12% of 
exposed global pay.
Seats produce a similar range.  As a cross-check, IDC estimates ~934mm knowledge 
workers and developers globally by 2030. We assume 575-770mm paying enterprise 
seats at $150-250 per seat per month. This price is 1.7-2.8x the ~$90 combined 
monthly list price of Microsoft 365 E5 and Copilot, but broadly comparable with 
Salesforce Sales Cloud Core at $195. If compute providers retain 70-90% of enterprise 
AI spending, their revenue reaches $0.7-2.1tr, consistent with the exposed-pay 
approach above. 
Enterprise AI revenue of $0.7-2.1tr covers 36-104% of the $2tr low hurdle. Firms would 
be paying less for AI than they would have paid the workers they cannot hire, while 
funding the spend from the additional output. That mechanism does not require tech 
spending to rise indefinitely as a share of GDP.

Cloud growth shows S curve is steepening
Annualised q/q cloud growth increased 2.5x to ~100% from ~40%.  Cloud sales 
growth q/q tripled to $86bn in Q3 from ~$31bn in Q1, on a ~$540bn revenue run rate. 
The acceleration occurred on a large base rather than a nascent one, which makes the 
change in dollars more important than the change in percentage terms. S curves add 
more dollars each period until adoption is around half complete. After the midpoint, 
dollar and percentage growth begin fading together.
Consensus has dollar growth flattening in 2H27. Consensus effectively treats next 
year as the midpoint of adoption which is conservative for a curve only recently entering 
its steep phase. It is also broadly consistent with constrained power supply at the current 
revenue generated per MW. UBS semis team's chip-based forecast implies ~50GW can 
be deployed in 2027 (up ~2x) if behind the meter solutions can address power 
constraints. At ~$15-16mm of annual revenue per MW, that capacity could support ~
$0.8tr of new annual revenue. Consensus adds only ~$0.5tr to the cloud revenue run 
rate in 2027. The difference indicates that consensus already embeds much of the 
power constraint. If the steep phase runs through 2028, AI revenue can reach the 
midpoint, and markets are likely to price that during 2027.
Consensus 2030 cloud revenue of ~$2.6tr clears the low ROI hurdle, but not the 
central case. Legacy cloud generated ~$200bn of revenue in 2023, before AI cloud 
became material, and will remain a meaningful part of the 2030 total even at a mature 
growth rate. If the steep phase lasts through 2028, the adoption midpoint moves 
towards 2029 and 2030 revenue can exceed the central need. Markets would probably 
discount that extension during 2027, before the associated revenue is reported. 
Risks are two-sided, and next year should show which way. As discussed, on the 
upside, the steep S-curve phase should run into 2028 and can lift 2030 AI revenue 
leading to confidence in cloud cash flow and capex sustainability, with faster behind-
the-meter power key, while new products could arrive sooner as models improve. On 
the downside, the largest risk stems from the democratic sweep in the midterms which 
could lead to bans and limits on new data centers, affecting 2028 growth and beyond. 
AI prices could also fall faster than usage rises amid competition, slowing lab revenue 
before enterprises adopt at scale. In any case, the top of the hurdle likely relies on next-
gen products in 2030s, so the return is more likely to arrive late rather than not at all.

Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Equity market returns are influenced by corporate earnings, interest rates, risk premia, as well 
as other variables influenced by the business cycle. The outlook for any and all of these 
variables is subject to change.
Risks include macroeconomic variables (such as GDP growth rates and inflation), economic 
slowdown, a weakening currency, global economic events, and government policy changes. 
Our quantitative models rely on reported financial statement information, consensus 
earnings forecasts and stock prices. Errors in these numbers are sometimes impossible to 
prevent (as when an item is misstated by a company). Also, the models employ historical data 
to estimate the efficacy of stock selection strategies and the relationships among strategies, 
which may change in the future. Additionally, unusual company-specific events could 
overwhelm the systematic influence of the strategies used to rank and score stocks. Past 
performance is not a guarantee of future performance. Valuation methods across sectors 
include DCF, SOTP and multiples analysis.
The HOLT methodology does not assign ratings or a target price to a security. It is an analytical 
tool that involves use of a set of proprietary quantitative algorithms and warranted value 
calculations, collectively called the HOLT valuation model, that are consistently applied to all 
the companies included in its database. The HOLT valuation model is a discounted cash flow 
model. Third-party data (including consensus earnings estimates) are systematically translated 
into a number of default variables and incorporated into the algorithms available in the HOLT 
valuation model. The source financial statement, pricing, and earnings data provided by 
outside data vendors are subject to quality control and may also be adjusted to more closely 
measure the underlying economics of firm performance. These adjustments provide
