 
"Leaving Vapor Trails": Nomura's McElligott Says 
Monday's Melt-Up Was A Vanna Machine, And The 
Machine Just Stalled 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, NOV 10, 2026 - 06:25 PM 
On paper, Monday was as good as it gets. 
According to Nomura's Charlie McElligott, 424 S&P 500 members closed higher against just 79 
decliners, so 84% of the index finished green, with the rally "broadening-out across sectors and 
industries" after last week's AI purge. Even the Dow, the S&P and the Russell erased their 
post-election losses, as we noted in last night's wrap. 
But in his pre-CPI note this morning ("QUICK MENTIONS",available to pro subs), the Nomura 
cross-asset guru says the breadth was the sideshow: 
But to me, the "far and away" standout -story about yesterday was the absolutely epic "Vol 
Crush"...and what that mechanically meant for "Higher Stocks" 
In other words, a big chunk of Monday's rally was dealers being forced to buy. And as of this 
morning, with the vol meltdown stalled, so is the rally. Then CPI came in hot. Let's walk through 
it. 
Waller Exits The "Clown Car" 
Start with the macro, where McElligott was in rare form about Fed Governor Chris Waller, who 
used a London speech this morning to float a 50bp hike in December if October inflation came 
in hot: 
...Fed's Waller exits his clown car long-enough to try to forward guide us (!) into saying that he 
could support a 50bps hike in Dec if we print a hot number today and effectively moving Dec 
Fed to "fully priced" 
 

 
Somebody was clearly ready for that. McElligott flags "a large +26k Tue Wkly 10Y Put Buyer" on 
Monday as "hot CPI insurance" expiring today, against prior open interest of just 1,080. That's 
roughly 24x the existing OI (napkin math) in a single day-of weekly put. 
The insurance paid. October CPI printed 0.4% MoM vs 0.3% expected, and core rose 0.3% vs 
0.2% expected, a print that only 1 of 70 analysts saw coming: 
The odds of a 50bp hike in December promptly jumped to 14%. Well, Waller did warn us... just a 
few hours before, which is about as much forward guidance as this Fed does these days (as we 
noted this morning, futures had already stalled on his remarks). 
 
Recall that just hours earlier Goldman's Rich Privorotsky had drawn his line in the sand: "Core 
at 0.2% would be constructive; 0.3% would challenge the rally," as we discussed in "Is The 
Worst Of The Tech Unwind Over?". We got 0.3%. 
Meanwhile in rates land, McElligott says vol "got absolutely REKT" on Monday on the oil 
collapse, "continued noise on Iran terms moving closer" and more clearing of election risk 
premium. After the velocity of the recent Treasury buying and short covering, real money has 
tilted to selling further out the curve, while receivers lost momentum "as the rally stalls." And 
nobody wants to own vol to protect against any of it: 
...i would think to tie-in the strong unwillingness to play Long Vol / Long Gamma to the lack of 
Performance out there, just no tolerance for bleed when the performance has already been so 
challenging 
Translation: after the worst momentum crash in years, nobody can afford to pay theta, which is 
precisely the condition under which the next move gets bigger than anyone is hedged for. 
 

 
"Leaving Vapor Trails": How The Vol Crush Bought Stocks 
Now for the main event. The VIX went from 28 to 20 in roughly two and a half sessions, a drop 
of almost 30%. McElligott's point is that the starting conditions made this collapse uniquely 
powerful: 
The real world implications of a "VIX 28 >> 20 move over ~2.5 sessions" are massive on Dealer 
/ Market-Maker -books which had become suddenly short (Customer -bot) Puts and OTM Puts, 
particularly when the starting point of Vol was so high, Skew got so jacked, and "Vol of Vol" went 
absolutely BERSERK after Thursday's Spot Equities "Crash" 
Recall that last Thursday morning Goldman's derivs desk was telling clients to buy tails because 
nobody was paying for them. By the close, everybody was, and Thursday ended up as the 
biggest put day in CBOE history. Dealers who sold all those puts hedged by shorting futures. 
Then came the reversal, which McElligott describes as "absolutely massive"vanna: 
As Spot began to move higher in the morning and Vol got completely scorched (as all those 
Puts lost beaucoup Delta), Dealers had to buy back a BRICK of Short $Delta / Futures into this 
now virtuous feedback loop as Vol gapped lower...and then separately as we rallied further, 
Calls which were playing for a bounce then too moved closer to ITM, eliciting incremental $Delta 
to buy from there, and just leaving vapor trails. 
Put differently, the puts lost their delta twice: once because spot rose, and again because the 
vol input collapsed. Each leg forced dealers to buy back hedges, which pushed spot higher, 
which crushed vol further. A virtuous loop, right up until it isn't. 
And that is where we are this morning: 
But here today, as the Vol meltdown has stalled-out, so too has the Stock rally. 
Which is a polite way of saying that the forced buyer has left the building. Anyone who mistook 
Monday's dealer covering for real demand just found out what's left once the delta is gone. 
A "Powder Keg" Of Combustible Two-Way Moves 
The mechanical flows are also unusually large right now. McElligott stacks up four accelerants: 
record ("ATH") options volumes over the past few days, which inflate the dollar size of every 
positioning swing; the usual vol-scaling strategy behavior on outlier moves; the "Synthetic 
Negative Gamma" of leveraged ETF end-of-day rebalancing; and other "Pro-Cyclical" forces in 
modern market structure. His conclusion: 
...you are simply sitting on a powder keg of "Combustible" two-way moves and "over-realizing" 
 

 
The Nomura dashboard shows why. One-month S&P realized vol is at 22.1, a 100th percentile 
reading, versus 14.7 for three-month. With last Thursday's 4.4% drop locked into that 
one-month window for weeks to come, vol control funds can't add risk back. Their implied equity 
allocation has slid further to $116.1 billion, an 11.2 percentile reading over three years, down 
from $124.6 billion when we covered McElligott's "biblical" $165 billion purge on Friday (chart 
source Nomura Vol, Bloomberg): 
 
Then there are the levered ETFs, now a $142 billion complex (94th percentile) that has to trade 
about $6.5 billion of stock for every 1% move in the underlying. Rolling one-month net flows 
(rebalancing plus creations) are -$82.6 billion, the lowest on record, thanks to last week's 
record-sized end-of-day dumps. Yet look at the red line: creations alone are +$10.5 billion, a 
100th percentile reading. Retail bought the dip, with leverage: 
 
 

 
We flagged that over the weekend: 
Good for the dip-buyers so far. But every fresh dollar in a 3x semis ETF is another dollar the 
product has to sell at the close the next time SOX drops 5%. The fuel for the next mechanical 
waterfall is being refilled by the people who just got burned by the last one. 
Worse Than Volmageddon: The Volatility Of "Vol Of Vol" 
Which brings us to what McElligott calls an "eye-watering observation," one he credits to MRA's 
Dean Curnutt, who made the same point on Monday: the realized volatility of the VVIX itself is at 
a record. 
...we currently see 10- and 20- day realized Volatility of VVIX making record highs (10Y data 
lookback), above that of COVID, Liberation Day and even "Volmageddon" itself. 
The Bloomberg screen (colors inverted) puts 10-day realized vol on the VVIX at 288.8 and 
20-day at 218.5, both highs of the 10-year window. Volmageddon, the February 2018 blowup 
that wiped out the short VIX ETNs, sits below today's reading (chart source Bloomberg, Nomura 
Vol): 
 
Why care about something this far down the derivatives chain? Because McElligott treats VVIX 
as a proxy for "Demand for Wings / Tails," and last week the wings were in demand like never 
before: 
 

 
...which after last week was telling us that there was a real and panicky "Convexity Grab" into 
what looked like a market which was repricing the Macro and Volatility -regime in short-order 
So in one week the market went from too cheap to hedge, to a panicked scramble for convexity, 
to dumping that convexity on dealers all at once. That's not the profile of a market that has 
found its footing. The way vol-of-vol has been whipping around tells you how unstable the price 
of protection itself has become. 
The VIX Floor Is Moving Up 
McElligott knows what the quants will say: when implied vol is this rich, the backtests say sell it. 
He doesn't dispute the backtest, but he does dispute the comfort it offers: 
So I get that the backtest which says "Sell the Rich Vol"...but that doesn't mean we can't keep 
chopping you up as we slip into a wider distribution of outcomes, both "regime" -wise, as well as 
"market"- wise...and that very least, just move into a wider Straddle 
And the reason the distribution is wider is the same one that has dominated our coverage for 
over a year: AI capex. With the "AI Capex" priors now genuinely in question, the impact reaches 
the "Nominal GDP" US economy and, from there, equity vol, skew and correlation, as well as 
rates and credit. His bottom line: 
In other words, it's my view that the floor on VIX is going to be dragged-incrementally higher too, 
as we imply larger daily price ranges / reprice the Straddle into this new "instability" 
We'd go a step further. Monday's rally needed three things: a collapsing VIX, falling oil and a 
benign CPI to let the Fed off the hook. The first has stalled, oil can reverse on the next Houthi 
headline and the third just failed, with core at 0.3% and a voting Fed governor openly talking 
50bps. So we get a Fed hiking into a market whose mechanical buyers are tapped out, whose 
dip-buyers are levered 3x, and whose price of insurance is the most unstable in a decade. 
McElligott's "chop" from Friday's note looks like the optimistic case. 
Then again, when the volatility of the volatility of volatility is the hottest number on the screen, 
maybe the straddle is the only thing left that's honestly priced.
