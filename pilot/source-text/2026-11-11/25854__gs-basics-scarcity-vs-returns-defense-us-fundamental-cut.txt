GS Basics - Scarcity vs Returns. Defense. US Fundamental Cut.
Rich Privorotsky
9 Nov 2026
Asia: Some rebound in semis following last week’s rout but fading through the session. TSMC +4.6%, MediaTek
+9.8%, Tokyo Electron +2.7%, Advantest +1.29%, Taiex +4.9%, Nikkei +2.3%, Kospi -80bps. Taiwan’s tighter
short selling rules and NT$500bn stabilization fund are providing support. NDX futures would probably be
considerably higher without the weekend Nvidia/Jensen Huang headlines. Looks idiosyncratic rather than a
broader AI read through but could impact sentiment. CoreWeave Tuesday and Nebius Thursday matter more for
underlying AI sentiment.
 
Positioning: Extraordinary de-risking last week, particularly across US long/short TMT (see charts below. Largest
cut in gross since Covid and biggest cut in net since Liberation day). Almost everything linked to AI spending
was getting sold. The post mid-term blue tsunami rally lasted 24 hours… short covering followed by the
realization that you cannot be this pessimistic on AI spending and simultaneously this optimistic on the
broader economy. The catch down was violent. SPX moved outside its recent range, gamma support disappeared
and CTA selling accelerated. Double digit semiconductor declines compounded the deleveraging.  Last week saw
">11mm puts trade in a single day, the largest put volume day on record, surpassing the previous high in Apr ’25.
Index puts led the way, while single-stock put activity was the 4th highest day ever." Models suggest another
~$25bn+ of SPX  potential CTA supply this week.
 
This week: Congress returns for its lame duck session, with a bipartisan energy permitting bill potentially
positive for data centers, although passage remains uncertain. 3yr Treasury auction today, 10yr tomorrow and
30yr Thursday. CPI tomorrow is the main macro event. Friday’s strong payrolls (+165k, wages +0.4% MoM,
unemployment 4.1%) add pressure on the Fed, although political uncertainty and AI concerns are currently
dominating.
 
Iran/Oil: Oil lower to start the week given lack of weekend escalation. Conflicting messages over the weekend…
Trump suggesting further strikes are unlikely if negotiations progress, Rubio saying talks have been largely
unproductive. The USS Theodore Roosevelt is not expected in the region before mid-November. Absent
tangible diplomatic progress, I’d expect escalation premium to return to oil by Wednesday/Thursday ahead of the
weekend.
 
Russia/Ukraine: Trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi November 10-11, with prediction markets assigning just 10-15% odds
of a year end ceasefire. My base case remains a prolonged conflict. More interesting is the extraordinary derating
in defense. In the U.S., Washington could ultimately maintain top line defense spending as part of a lame duck
compromise with Democrats on healthcare. Meanwhile, European defense looks increasingly
asymmetric: ceasefire headlines and EU fiscal debates have driven multiple compression, yet structural NATO
2%+ GDP commitments and multi year order backlogs mean a lot of bad news is already priced in.
 
AI: I struggle with the long term bear argument of power constraints/permits/political delays fundamentally
undermine the buildout. Sure in the short term they raise costs and delay projects, but if demand is real, capacity will
migrate to other states, countries or alternative power sources. Think market will get there, look at Mississippi
under Governor Tate Reeves: the state has aggressively positioned itself as America’s 'Digital Delta,' actively
defending xAI’s 2GW Southaven buildout in federal court this month and securing over $25 billion in AWS

commitments by offering speed to market and utility coordination Oversupply, not power or permitting remains
the key MT risk. While headline GPU spot rental rates remain supported by physical scarcity, optimization and
smaller specialized models are rapidly compressing per token inference costs. The bear argument is no
longer that compute cannot be built, but that efficiency breakthroughs could outpace demand growth. 
 
Risk: Semis look painfully oversold. I’d move up the quality curve first… ASML, TSMC  Friday bounce was
inevitable, but whether we buy or sell it depends on incoming evidence around demand and monetization. Beyond
the violence in the price action the macro backdrop has improved… energy lower, rates retracing and credit
spreads tightening. All supportive. Nasdaq has broken below its 200d (backtest of dip buying gets harder),
although the S&P’s broader trend remains intact. CTA supply and signs of retail selling remain headwinds, with
CPI the immediate catalyst. The best outcome is range bound trading, allowing implied volatility to compress and
systematic demand to rebuild. 
 
 
side-note: US Gross and Net Cut - largest weekly decline since Covid
