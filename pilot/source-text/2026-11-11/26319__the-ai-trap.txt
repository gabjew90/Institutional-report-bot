From crowded to crushed
The AI trade has gone from crowded to crushed, and the positioning reset is becoming extreme.
Goldman highlights a massive $19 billion futures squeeze, while semiconductor allocations have
collapsed toward year lows. Nvidia is trading at its most oversold levels since 2019, and JPM's Asian
positioning indicator has plunged to the 8th percentile. Valuations are starting to look compelling, but
technical damage remains severe. The ingredients for a violent squeeze are falling into place.
Whether that turns into a sustainable recovery is an entirely different question.
The $19 billion squeeze
The post-election AI meltdown triggered a massive positioning shock. Goldman notes that S&P futures
open interest surged by $19.1 billion on November 5, the fifth-largest daily increase in two years,
excluding rolls, as investors piled into shorts. But the reversal was equally violent. As AI stabilized and
oil declined, the S&P recovered 2.7% through November 9, while open interest collapsed by $16.6
billion. The message is clear: much of the rebound was driven by shorts getting squeezed rather than
fresh conviction buying.

Semis are running the show
Forget the usual rates obsession for a moment. Goldman argues that the fate of semiconductors may
matter more for the S&P than the latest moves in real yields. During the November 5 selloff,
semiconductors plunged 12.2%, overwhelming the hyperscaler complex and helping drag the broader
index down 4.4%. Goldman also highlights the negative correlation between asset-manager S&P shorts
and semiconductor performance. The implication is straightforward: without a sustained recovery in
semis, the broader equity rebound remains vulnerable.

The semiconductor trap
The SOXX rebound faces a serious obstacle: massive overhead supply. BTIG highlights the enormous
volume traded above $165, leaving investors who bought between May and early November
underwater. The 200-day MA sits around $160, making the $160–165 zone particularly important. A
sustained move above $165 could invalidate the breakdown, but BTIG remains firmly bearish below
that level, viewing rallies as opportunities to reduce exposure. The bank sees potential for a full
retracement toward the April breakout zone around $115–120. That is a brutal downside scenario,
even after the recent washout.
BTIG/Bloomberg
The panic vs reality
Texas accounts for more than a quarter of planned US data center capacity additions through 2030, so
talk of a potential moratorium has rattled the entire AI complex. But Goldman argues the market is
missing an important distinction: restrictions on new permits don't stop projects already under
construction. The 35 GW of data center additions planned for 2027 are largely already being built, with
limited execution risk. Goldman still sees $1.3 trillion in AI infrastructure capex by 2027.
Meanwhile, SOX has suffered one of its most extreme drawdowns in history. The index has bounced off
its long-term trendline, but remains well below the 200-day MA. The fundamental debate is far from
settled, but with positioning washed out and the physical buildout continuing, the setup is getting
increasingly interesting. Full note here.

Everybody out
JPM's aggregate Asian equity positioning indicator has fallen to the 8th percentile, a level that has
historically coincided with important market bottoms. Asian equities have suffered another roughly
15% monthly drawdown, while tech RSI readings have collapsed into the low 20s. Multi-strategy and
tech-focused hedge funds have substantially reduced exposure. The positioning reset is becoming
extreme.

Valuations from another era
It isn't just SK Hynix. The valuation reset across Korean semiconductors has pushed multiples back
toward levels rarely seen in modern market history, according to Goldman. The market is increasingly
pricing a structural earnings downturn, rather than a temporary correction in the AI cycle. If earnings
hold up, the valuation disconnect could become difficult to ignore. More here.
