Yesterday’s CPI beat did more than deliver another sell-off. It shifted the Fed debate towards how quickly it 
tightens, rather than whether and when it tightens. Core CPI printed 0.3%MoM, with breadth also deteriorating, 
while the read-across to core PCE looks consistent with another firm 0.35-0.40% print. Combined with Waller 
opening the door to 50bp, the market pushed December pricing to ~28bp and close to three hikes by mid-2027. 
The reaction was an aggressive bear-flattening, with 2Y UST yields briefly reaching 4.75% while 10Y pushed 
through 4.9% ahead of a slightly soft auction. The DXY rebound towards 102.3 following CPI looks consistent with 
that front-end repricing. Cash USTs are closed for Veterans Day today. Ahead of Friday’s PPI, the focus will 
likely shift to whether Fed speakers validate the market’s more aggressive distribution. Goolsbee calling CPI 
“borderline disturbing” adds to that bias, although Paulson still favours gradual tightening. For us, 25bp in 
December and January looks easier to justify than jumping immediately to 50bp, but yesterday clearly raised the 
bar for fading front-end weakness. Oil also deserves some attention. Brent tested $90pb level yesterday as 
hopes of US-Iran diplomacy reduced the geopolitical premium, with Trump signalling further strikes were unlikely 
for now. But the move has partially reversed overnight, with Brent back around $92pb this morning. That matters 
after CPI showed inflation momentum extending beyond energy-sensitive goods. A renewed move higher in crude 
would make the Fed’s inflation problem harder to ignore. 
Still following, rather than leading. EGBs were again largely passengers yesterday but importantly outperformed 
the UST sell-off materially. German 2Y yields rose around 8bp while 10Y were only ~4-5bp higher, versus a near-
20bp jump in UST 2s. EUR/USD reflected the same divergence, falling below 1.1160 as the US front end repriced 
more aggressively. Today is more domestically focused, albeit without an obvious catalyst capable of 
matching yesterday’s US move. ECB speakers include Nagel, Lagarde, Kazaks and Cipollone, while Germany 
sells €2.5bn across 21Y and 30Y Bunds. With around 63bp of ECB tightening priced through next September, we 
think the bar for another sizeable front-end sell-off is higher without a change in ECB rhetoric. Instead, we would 
watch the long end around today’s supply and any renewed move in oil. The bigger relative-value point remains 
that EUR rates should continue to trade with a lower beta to the Fed repricing unless ECB speakers actively 
challenge current pricing and turn hawkish.  
Yesterday’s early Gilt rally proved short-lived, with Lombardelli’s hawkish comments weakening the front-
end anchor before stronger US CPI pushed yields higher across the curve. 10Y Gilt yields reversed from 
around 5.10% to above 5.20%+, while the 30Y swap rate respected the 200-day moving average and is back near 
5%. GBP/USD also gave back this week’s gains and is back around 1.3340. There is little for the UK market to 
trade off today. December is already around 17bp priced, and without fresh domestic data we struggle to see a 
clean extension of yesterday’s sell-off unless tomorrow’s GDP data beats or the rebound in oil gathers pace. That 
said, the inflation backdrop remains uncomfortable, with NIESR now seeing inflation above target until 2030. For 
now, this makes us reluctant to fade front-end weakness aggressively, even if yesterday’s move invites some 
consolidation and our view that the BoE will ultimately deliver less hikes than currently priced (~80bp into H2-27). 
Risk sentiment held up fairly well in today’s Asian session, with AI optimism continuing to drive the move. 
In Japan, JGBs sold off, catching up with the bearishness in USTs, but here it was the 7-10Y area that 
underperformed on the curve. USD/JPY rose sharply and is now trading around 158.90.
