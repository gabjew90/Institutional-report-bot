European Economic Outlook: Growth To Return To Trend In 2027
European Economics raises its 2026 forecast by 0.3pp to 1.1% and projects growth of 1.2% in 2027 before
slowing to 1.1% in 2028. Fiscal expansion—especially German defence and infrastructure spending,
alongside final NGEU disbursements—should add about 30bp to growth in 2026 and 2027, while resilient
labour markets, lower energy intensity and AI-related activity have cushioned geopolitical shocks. Higher
energy prices are nevertheless likely to lift inflation to 4.3% in early 2027, prompting two further 25bp ECB
hikes in December and March to a 3.0% peak, followed by cuts to 2.25% in 2028. The outlook is asymmetric:
renewed conflict or an energy shock would weaken demand and tighten financial conditions, while Germany
accelerates and France remains the regional laggard.
(Jason Cheng, Macro Sales)
One Liners
Fedspeak Grows More Hawkish On Broadening Inflation - Fed officials remain focused on persistent and
broadening inflation, with commentary growing more hawkish following the hot October CPI print. After
Governor Waller suggested a 50bp hike could be on the table next month, Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan
called for at least two more 25bp increases to move policy into modestly restrictive territory, followed by a
potential pause. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee described October inflation as “borderline
disturbing,” citing stubborn services inflation and risks of broader overheating. Kansas City Fed President Jeff
Schmid similarly said policy remains fairly accommodative and flagged concerning underlying price trends,
while Fed Governor Michelle Bowman signaled she could support a hike next month amid persistently
elevated inflation. Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson struck a more measured tone, favoring gradual
tightening and arguing that inflation expectations, wage pressures and underlying inflation do not currently
warrant a faster pace of hikes. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
Bessent Urges Clear-Eyed AI Cooperation Ahead Of Trump–Xi Meetings - US Treasury Secretary Scott
Bessent urged cooperation with China on shared AI risks while acknowledging differences and competition for
leadership. He said President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping’s planned meeting in
Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Nov. 18–19 APEC summit, would advance a “superintelligence” dialogue
aimed at preventing misunderstandings. This would be their third meeting this year, following a September
agreement on an AI alert system. A fourth summit is expected at December’s US-hosted G20 meeting in
Miami. Bessent reiterated the US aim to “de-risk, not to decouple” from trade with China, despite friction over
the bilateral trade deficit. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Fiscal Conditions Must Deteriorate Much Further To Force Government Action - Fiscal correction rarely
occurs until market and economic conditions have deteriorated to such an extent the public is willing to accept
the country must be put back on track. Panelists discussing public debt at the UBS European Conference felt
such conditions remained distant and hence governments felt no compulsion to act. That lack of willingness
will lead bond yields higher, which in turn will create further fiscal distress. The message from the panel was
that multiple fiscal crises were looming. In the US, lame duck presidencies often resulted in Congressional
Republicans and Democrats agreeing to spend more on a wide range of measures in order to progress the
economic agenda. In France, neither the left nor right of politics nor large swathes of the public seem at all
prepared to take the fiscal measures needed to balance the budget. One panellist suggested such was the
state of the US fiscal situation that the US Treasury yield should no longer be called the “risk free” but rather
just a “global benchmark”. The panellists weren’t especially complimentary of recent central bank decisions,
suggesting monetary authorities were far too quick to leap in and quell volatility. The signal sent by markets
and the price discovery achieved during periods of volatility should be considered a critical component of
market mechanics. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
UBS Sees Australian Home Loans Falling 45%, With Risk Of Dovish RBA Shift Sooner -  Australia
Economics has shifted toward its 'downside scenario' for the housing market, expecting home loan values to
slump 45% through to end-2027 vs. 30% earlier, the largest downturn on record. The level of home loans is
set to completely unwind the covid boom and revert to 2019 levels, dragging down housing credit growth
towards 2% y/y by end-2027. Economist George Tharenou now expects dwelling prices to fall by 10-15% peak
to trough in this cycle. While he still sees the RBA keeping the cash rate restrictive for a prolonged period, the
housing downturn and rapidly weakening sentiment could prompt a dovish flip more quickly. Nonetheless,
George believes 'this time is different', as inflation is still way too high. He is calling for the first RBA rate cut in
November 2027, and a shallow 50bp cutting cycle; but the risks are shifting to an earlier and/or larger easing
cycle. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

NIESR Sees UK Inflation Above Target Until Late 2030, Upgrades 2026 Growth View - The National
Institute of Economic and Social Research forecasts UK inflation will peak at 4.4% in February 2027, up from
July’s 3.8% projection, and return to the BoE’s 2% target only in late 2030—a year later than it had previously
forecast. The think-tank attributes the persistence to second-round wage effects from the Iran war amid higher
energy prices and global borrowing costs. Meanwhile, it raised the UK’s 2026 growth forecast to 1.6% from
1.1%, reflecting stronger government spending and business investment, partly driven by AI, but expects
growth to slow to 1.1% in 2027 and 0.9% in 2028. The NIESR sees just one more BoE hike, in February,
versus market pricing for two or three 25bp increases by end-2027. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Canadian CPI: Inflation Base Effects Signal More Tightening Ahead - UBS Economics: Abigail Watt
expects the Bank of Canada to deliver another 25bp rate hike in December, despite softer October labor and
GDP data. Inflation remains the central focus, with October CPI forecast to rise 0.3% month-over-month and
headline inflation climbing to 3.2% due to base effects. Core CPI is expected to decelerate annually to 1.9%,
reflecting weaker shelter price growth. Minutes from the October meeting revealed concerns about energy
price pass-through and demand-driven inflation pressures, underscoring the need for potential further
tightening. Abigail anticipates November’s sharp drop in gasoline prices could weigh on headline inflation next
month. While uncertainty looms, the BoC’s October deliberations suggest that “uncertainty does not mean
inaction,” signaling a cautious but firm stance on inflation management going forward. (Jonathan Garber,
Macro Sales)

Korea’s Early-November Exports Surge; October Job Gains Uneven - South Korea’s exports more than
doubled in the first 10 days of November, to $33.5 bn from $15.7 bn a year earlier. Chip shipments surged
331.2% to $16.6 bn, accounting for nearly half of the exports. Meanwhile, imports rose 45% to $24.7 bn,
leaving a $8.8 bn surplus. Year-to-date exports reached $963.6 bn, nearing the $1 trn milestone. Separately,
October employment increased 139K y/y, with annual job growth exceeding 100K for a fourth consecutive
month. Unemployment fell 0.2pp y/y to 2%. Manufacturing and construction employment nevertheless
declined by 7K and 42K y/y, respectively, highlighting uneven employment gains. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro
Sales)
TotalEnergies Middle East Production Recovers, LNG Lags - European Equities: TotalEnergies sees
production largely restored in the Middle East and its long-term production outlook well supported, while LNG
remains below pre-conflict levels. Strong gas trading and refining margins should support near-term
performance, while Integrated Power returns are expected to improve and investor focus remains on
deleveraging and shareholder returns. (Shannon Keane, European Equity Sales)
Schneider Growth Broadens As Data Centre Demand Stays Strong - European Equities: Schneider
reiterated the breadth of its growth drivers beyond data centres, with Industrial showing signs of recovery and
Power & Grid demand supported by grid modernisation and energy sovereignty. Data centre demand remains
strong, with 18-24 months of visibility and increasing geographical diversification across China, Southeast Asia
and selected European markets; at the current rate of demand, Schneider does not have enough capacity for
2028 and will keep investing in capex for 2030 if hyperscaler spend continues. Operating leverage, cost
efficiencies and continued pricing should support profitability into 2027, while management reiterated the €800
mn PTC revenue synergies target. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales)
Illumina Demand Remains Strong As Research Headwinds Begin To Ease - Illumina management
remains positive on the business, pointing to strong demand in clinical and early signs of improvement in the
still-weak research market. Government grants “are flowing,” although academic investigators remain cautious
on when and how to deploy those funds. On instruments, X placements continue to more than offset
stabilization in the 6K platform, with sequential placement growth possible, albeit challenging, in 4Q. Looking
into 2027, management continues to expect HSD% revenue growth, with the large X installed base built in
2026 supporting stronger consumables growth. Longer term, proteomics should provide another leg of clinical
growth, particularly in applications such as early cancer detection. Overall, management believes the research
headwind is largely behind them, clinical pressures are beginning to ease, and pricing elasticity is returning to
more normalized levels. (Dimiter Tassev, Specialist Sales)

India Outlook: External Headwinds Pose Downside Risks - India Economics: While economic growth likely
remained resilient at 7.5% y/y in H1 FY2027, the momentum should moderate in H2, taking full-year real GDP
to 7.2% y/y. Economist Tanvee Gupta Jain sees a gradual fading of the tax impulse, tighter global financial
conditions, oil price volatility and the drag from a weak monsoon weighing on activity. She expects real GDP
growth to settle toward trend at 7.0% y/y in FY2028 before recovering to 7.2% in FY2029. Risks remain
skewed to the downside: if Brent crude remains around $100/bbl in FY2028 and global yields rise, Tanvee
flags a 40bp downside risk to her real GDP growth estimate, to 6.6% y/y. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Colombia's October CPI Supports Pause, But December Rate Hike Remains Possible - UBS
Economics: Colombia’s October CPI rose 0.20% m/m, below consensus, while 12-month inflation eased to
6.30% and core inflation declined for a second consecutive month to 5.91%. Stabilizing food and energy prices
helped drive the moderation, lending support to BanRep’s decision to pause in October. The outlook for
December, however, remains finely balanced, according to economist Andrea Casaverde. She says inflation is
still well above target, with elevated services inflation, fiscal uncertainty and potential El Niño-related electricity

US Equities Selloff Masks Constructive Cash Flows - US equities remained firmly macro-driven after the
hotter CPI print, but underlying cash flows were notably more constructive than the tape suggested, pointing to
a rates-led selloff rather than broad institutional de-risking. The desk was net to buy alongside retail, with
long-only investors reversing Monday’s activity to become buyers and hedge funds pivoting from early selling,
albeit with elevated short activity. Rate sensitivity remains the main fault line. Housing, speculative growth and
other long-duration exposures remain under pressure, while banks, insurers, defense and select cyclicals are
holding up better. Within AI, the move increasingly looks like rotation rather than wholesale capitulation:
hyperscalers are acting as a relative safe haven as semiconductors, neoclouds and power and data-center
beneficiaries unwind. Industrials, particularly AI-linked equipment, remain the clearest source of funds. In S&P
500 options, spot down and volatility down took hold as the orderly selloff continued. (Victoria Kalamaras,
Equity Derivative Sales)
Japanese Equities Slightly Higher - The Nikkei 225 is up 0.80% and the TOPIX up 0.79%, as AI enthusiasm

CoreWeave Gains On Beat And Raise - CoreWeave (CRWV) is up 2% after a broadly strong print, well short
