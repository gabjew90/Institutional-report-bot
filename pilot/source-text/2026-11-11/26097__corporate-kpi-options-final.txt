Corporate KPI Binary Options: A New Way to Trade Earnings   
Prediction Markets Expand Beyond Retail 
 
Prediction markets have surged in popularity in recent years. What started as primarily a way to wager on elections 
has now broadened to cover almost anything with a measurable outcome: sports, economic data, cultural events, 
and more recently, performance metrics of public companies. Total monthly volume has grown almost 15x in the 
past year to a high of over $126B in October (see Exhibit 1). Retail traders dominate this market, with Kalshi and 
Polymarket the two most popular platforms in this space. In terms of categories, sports is by far the most popular, 
making up 82% of the volume on Kalshi, followed by crypto (13%). See Exhibit 2.

Corporate event contracts, on the other hand, are a more nascent category within the prediction markets. These are 
primarily split into two types: earnings-mention contracts (i.e. will a word be said on an earnings call?) and corporate 
KPI contracts (i.e. will a reported Key Performance Indicator clear a threshold?). While earnings-mention contracts 
initially dominated in popularity, corporate KPIs have become the fastest growing segment in this space in recent 
months. On Kalshi, for example, corporate KPI contracts have gone from making up less than 10% of all corporate 
event contracts traded in January to over half by July. The shift has happened as Kalshi has substantially expanded its 
corporate KPI offering from 16 active KPI series in January to 248 in August. See Exhibit 3 for a list of the most 
popular series.  
The customer base behind this volume has so far been almost entirely retail. Kalshi’s KPI order count grew 22-fold 
from January through August 2026 versus the same period in 2025, while the median order shrank from roughly $15-
20 of premium to about $5. More people are trading, each in smaller amounts, which is typical of new retail traders 
entering this space. Nine in ten KPI orders on Kalshi are $100 or less in premium. Retail traders also seem to favor

Cboe’s New Corporate KPI Binary Options: 
Cboe is listing cash-settled, European-style binary call and put options on specific company KPIs – such as Earnings 
Per Share, Revenue, and other disclosed financial or operating metrics. Each contract pays $1 if the reported KPI 
metric meets the stated condition at expiration, or $0 if it does not.   
At launch, these binary options will be available on 112 KPIs across 23 actively traded US companies, spanning 
metrics including EPS, Revenue, Segment Revenues, Operating Margins, and company-specific operational statistics. 
See chart below and full product listing details here. More information can also be found on the Cboe pre-launch 
resource hub. 
Unlike the existing KPI contracts on Kalshi, which are CFTC-regulated event contracts, Cboe’s corporate KPI binary 
options are structured as securities options and regulated by the SEC. That brings the full listed-options framework, 
including FINRA oversight and surveillance, a standardized options disclosure document (CARBO), broker suitability 
and options-approval requirements. The contracts will trade on Cboe Options Exchange (C1) and clear through Cboe 
Clear US (CCUS), rather than the OCC.

A securities options structure also puts the product in a regime that risk systems, compliance teams, and investment 
mandates already know how to approve, paving the way for easier institutional adoption. While today the corporate 
KPI market is dominated by retail traders, we see several institutional use cases – from hedging earnings risk to 
relative value spread trades – that could bring larger orders and two-way flow. That would deepen liquidity for every 
participant.
