Third hike added. The October CPI report surprised meaningfully to the upside across several categories and
marked a third month of worse inflation news. We continue to expect a second 25bp rate hike in December and are
adding a third 25bp hike to our forecast in March 2027. A third hike could come earlier in January, but we think
March is a bit more likely because we expect the November and December inflation reports to be softer but then
expect larger start-of-the-year price increases amidst ongoing cost pressures to boost inflation in January. The recent
inflation news has not been entirely one-sided. Oil futures have fallen substantially since the elections, and this
should mean that the bulk of the passthrough from the energy shock to consumer prices is behind us. We remain
confident that inflation will be meaningfully lower by the end of 2027 as the effects of tariffs, the energy shock, and AI
price pressures diminish. But we suspect that the recent inflation prints will lead most FOMC participants to signal a
third hike in the December dot plot and that a firmer January inflation report would make delivering it the path of least
resistance even if the energy shock appears over by then. We have left our forecast for the eventual terminal fed
funds rate unchanged at 3.25-3.5%, but we now expect rate cuts to begin one quarter later in December 2027 (vs.
September 2027 previously), followed by additional cuts in March, June, and September 2028. See David Mericle’s
piece: US Daily: Adding a Third Hike in March.

Miriam Wheeler as well as other GS credit and equity research analysts to discuss the source of the current stresses,

China Strategy. What's ailing Chinese profits, and where to find growth? Speak to Kinger and Si? 
Our clustering analysis enables us to dynamically organize 6,000+ listed Chinese companies from 47 major
industries into 5 growth buckets based on their stylistic growth profile, macro sensitivity, and affinity to key themes,
assisting investors to better express their growth expectations, evaluate performance, and manage risks.
China is still growing, but the Growth universe has shifted and become narrower. We project Chinese companies to
deliver 10% trend EPS growth and 12% normalized ROE over the next 5 years, both pointing to 10-15% of index re-
rating upside from current levels. 
• We’d continue to build our core holdings around structural growth themes such as AI and Going Global
• Remain bullish on Stable and Cash Return Growth for steady incomes and diversification
• Look to re-engage profit turnaround story and free cash-flow generators in De-rated and Cyclical Growth
• Avoid Depressed Growth until property prices stabilize, possibly in 2028/2029 on GSe.

Research in focus this morning
 
China Batteries. Key takeaways from Battery Corporate Day and 3Q26 results
Following our Battery Corporate Day and 3Q26 results, management teams across the value chain expect strong
near-term production to continue into 4Q and guide to robust 2027 demand growth of 20–30% for power batteries
and over 30% for energy storage systems. Management notes that consumption tax pass-through is largely in place
for ESS, while negotiations for passenger vehicles continue with full resolution expected by 1Q27, supporting margin
improvement expectations for tier-2 battery makers next year. For materials, non-lithium components like copper foil
and separators are seeing pricing expansion on tight supply, whereas lithium materials face unit profit headwinds
from lower lithium prices. Within the sector, we highlight Buy-rated Zenergy as an underappreciated high-growth
platform with improving competitive positioning and a favorable growth-return balance
 
Fujikura. 2Q beat/full-year OP trim, NP unchanged, DPS hike: Solid demand and strong business model intact;
cutting TP, but shares undervalued; Buy
We cut our 12m TP to ¥5,000 from ¥7,600 but remain constructive as shares appear undervalued following Q2
operating profits of ¥130.2bn that beat our estimates on solid generative AI data center demand. Management
trimmed full-year operating profit guidance slightly to ¥429bn from ¥432bn due to forex headwinds and anticipated
lower second-half telecom profitability, while substantially hiking the annual dividend forecast to ¥80 from ¥42. We
believe the company's robust business model and client stickiness in ultra-high-density optical fiber cables remain
intact, and we view its strong financial position as attractive in a rising interest rate environment.
