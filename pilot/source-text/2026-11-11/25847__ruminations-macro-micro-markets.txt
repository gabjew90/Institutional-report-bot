Ruminations - Macro, Micro, Markets
Bobby Molavi  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  PMD, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
9 Nov 2026
A violent week...a week of chop and churn...and a week that caused damage.
The S&P fell 2.68% to 7,377, the Nasdaq 3.37%, the Dow 0.94% and the Russell 2.05%...but beneath the
surface it was a week with 3 halves (yes…I know that maths doesn’t add up)….the relief and hope rally
post election (stalemate is good)…the air pocket gap lower Thursday….and then calm after the storm and
some bottom dipping on Friday. However you describe last week…it was a week…the path was violent.
Three completely different markets in three sessions.
Last week saw fundamentals, performance, sentiment, positioning and technicals all coincide. For me, the
primary driver was a negative reinforcing feedback loop between performance and technicals....as 'failure'
in one area (pnl) triggered action in another (technicals). The result of this dynamic was a perpetual game
of 'stop bingo' where short duration tightly managed strategies (often with leverage) were forced to
degross and/or liquidate. Given the concentration and crowding dynamics that have been a feature of
2026...as this happened and the impact of selling/liquidation impacting the very references that triggered
the de-grossing in the first place....they triggered a new wave of selling...and on and on it went with the
primary questions from clients being 'what are you seeing' and 'are we there yet'. Friday did give some
pause....next week will be key in terms of whether that was momentary or a new floor to build from.
Wednesday's rally showed the market digesting the Democratic sweep of both chambers as benign
(gridlock is historically stock-friendly). But Thursday's rout was pinned partly on fears of tighter AI and
data-center regulation under the new Congress — so the election fed the AI anxiety rather than standing
apart from it. Investors spent the week parsing the sweep for AI, data-center-permitting and fiscal
implications.
Thursday was a genuine air-pocket. The S&P fell 4.4%... Dow 1,833 points....Nasdaq 6.3% — its worst
session since the pandemic reaction - with the VIX spiking ~70% and all eleven sectors red. Nvidia alone
shed more than $440bn of market value. A reminder that volumes and liquidity are two very different
things...and a warning signal on what happens when directional flows in a crowded concentrated market
all shift one way....more on that later.
Worth noting….the Phili Semis Index fell over 12% on the week…. and the biggest losers read like whose
who of the H1 AI buildout wnners: Lumentum −36%, Seagate −28%, Marvell −28%, Western Digital
−28%, SanDisk −27% — optical networking, chips and storage. The "AI debt/buildout" complex that led
on the way up is leading on the way down....and that is before you factor todays move in Nvidia on the
back of weekend 'news'.
So where did last week leave us in relation to the major anchors...

1, Ai debate - not smarter but debate rages on, a new dynamic also to digest with Democratic sweep
impact on the theme.
2, Oil - moving right way as a combination of GDP slow down, no escalation in middle east, talk of
talking and Democratic win pointing to calmer waters.
3, Yields - more normalisation on yields as people price potential AI capex slowdown, lower Oil and the
effecting tightening from the last hike.
4, Earnings - still very solid....but...it seems solid (and in some cases blowout) isn't enough.
So...as has been the case for most of 2026....it's the same debates, the same maths but recently the answers
have been wildly different in terms of what that means vs what we were getting a few months ago.
It feels like H1 and Q2 were examples of a market where everyone got paid...a bit like 2021… where
making money got ‘easy’...and perhaps with the benefit of hindsite a bit too easy. Q3 and Q4 so far have
been markedly different...and if anything too hard. Everyone sprinting to....well…. not even
standstill....but in fact to only lose a lot of what they made in H1, rather than all of it or all of it and more.
Anyway...onto the flows...what we saw...and some stabs on what that might mean and what to watch out
for next....
1 Gross and basis - in October one of the tag lines was if you carried gross you lost money...last week
gross and basis became almost impossible to manage. You look at software vs semis spreads, you look at
Apple vs Nasdaq correlation breakdown, you look at Banks vs Insurance, you look at Eur banks vs Eur
defence, you look at Defensives vs Growth, small cap vs large cap, Europe vs US, S&P ex Ai vds
S&P....wherever you look spreads blew out, correlations broke down and carrying risk was like holding a
time bomb.
2, Broadening – we’ve seen several weeks now of rotation to value, small and mid cap, under owned,
defensive, real economy and stable cash flow. We’ve seen the S&P managing to offset big Ai and Tech
drawdowns with unexpected staples, real estate, healthcare, consumer, travel and leisure outperformance.
A clear S&P ex Ai vs S&P with Ai….or a material reversal of what was true only weeks ago…of a S&P
that was up 12 to 13% while the majority of stocks in it were down on the year.
3, Technicals, positioning and performance – I think this was the real story of last week. In reality gross
had come down but concentration had not. Pnl draw down was one of the primary drivers of that gross
lower and then hedging regressed. The problem is that ai vs spx or Nasdaq has not served as much of a
hedge. On top of that - I think basis and spreads are breaking down everywhere - look at software vs semis
or banks vs insurance or even simens energy vs orated. End of day…long short has had an extremely
challenging 4 to 6 weeks and that has gradually ‘infected’ other pockets of capital….multi asset,
systematic, credit, convert to name but a few. The recurring core theme…was one of unwinding. The key
region underperforming (US)…..the main factor hitting recent lows….consensus and crowded longs
suffering higher vol and big corrections…..on top of that the short book suffering as people rotate to safety
and defensive. A pretty painful and in some cases deadly mix. Then finally the deleveraging forced selling

dynamics…..tight stops and high leverage are a feature of today’s aum…..when things good they deliver
high risk adjusted returns and when things go bad you see a lot of stops and degrossing events. That also
has been a feature of October and now early nov price action to say the least.
A few other thoughts……
Indexation, correlation, crowding and synchronisation. The index make up and narrowness embedded
within it, the need for concentration over diversification to drive returns in 2025/26, the fact that
Momentum became embedded in every portfolio and every strategy to some degree is resulting in a
convergence of performance and risks to one factor....and a growing dynamic that saw several
fundamental and systematic strategies increasingly geared the same end themes/factors. To put some stats
to the theory….. our PB risk team highlighted that realized 3m alpha correlations between Fundamental
and Systematic L/S managers were top decile of the past decade….coming out of summer. From a
regional perspective…. correlations between US and Asia Fundamental managers also reached all-time
highs. A lot of this is largely driven by the AI trade.
Indexation and correlation part 2. Some of these moves make me reflect on something I mentioned a few
weeks ago….namely indexation and correlation and what happens when the trend meaningfully shifts
rather than just has a ‘blip’. “The market make up has changed dramatically over the past few years. The
nature of investors shifting more and more to passive and etf. The hedge fund industry increasingly to
market neutral. The emergence of systematic quant where price and price momentum is as important as
fundamentals and valuation. These shifts have likely resulted in a few second order impacts….the
quantum of single stock moves around earnings surprises (driven by stops)….a shrinking of duration from
the overall market…but perhaps the biggest impact of all is the growth of momentum as a factor. What do
I mean by this? Well in a market where the S&P is increasingly narrow and driven by 5 to 10 stocks (and
at times this year only 1 or 2)…..and where over 60% of the ytd returns 6 months into the year had been
driven by 6 stocks (35% by 1 stock) then we live in a market eco system where an ever increasing % of
aum is sat in index tracking or index hugging wrappers/funds…and the higher things go…the more people
‘have’ to buy them….which mean the higher they go. This community is never a seller while these stocks
go up…as they have no view on fair value and whether something has overrun. These ‘winners’ in turn
brings retail to the fore…and they tend to be more interesting in trend and momentum and assets that have
‘performed’ and as such are more likely to by FAANG, MAG7 or Nvidia than to take a bet on a cheap but
underperforming value name. this in part is fuel that drives like Nvidia from $1 trillion to $2 trillion to
$3.5 trillion and at one point $5.8 trillion. But…on the way down….that is what can take you from $5.8
trillion to close to $4 trillion in a matter of weeks. Of course a huge amount of that appreciation if
fundamental and deserved but a huge amount of it is also technical and a new dynamic to the
markets….and that works on the way up….and on the way down.
Technology, Bubbles and Terminal value. It does seem like we confuse the interplay between these three
things. Bubbles and ultimate utility are different things. Technological innovation and revolution can be
extraordinary and yet not create immediate value. Take for example previous bubbles….Internet, Railways
and Tulips. None of those Bubbles resulted in the cause of bubble disappearing. I for one love a Tulip. But
then where the value resided from those things have had a variable end state…perhaps for Tulips it was

the broader flower industry, for Internet it was the Apps that were built on top and for Railways it was the
economies that leveraged the infrastructure. Equally all these bubbles permanently reshaped our societies,
our infratstructures, our cultures, our consumers/workers and our companies. That being said the early
capital that chased each of these previous bubbles was hurt…especially when speculation and hope
translated to ‘valuation’ and decoupled from fundamental revenue and cashflow.
Circular dynamics. In 2000 and the dot com – you had a Tech industry that served primarily the Tech
industry. If one faltered..the knock on effects rippled broad and wide. Nowadays every industry is tech…
and tech is every industry. That being said, in relation to Ai, we have a degree of circularity and
interdependence that is rarely seen. A theme/space that is feeding each other and feeding off each other.
Nvidia and Open Ai deal, Nvidia stake in Intel, Oracle and Nvidia GPUs, hyperscalers with stakes in Ai
startups etc etc. Ultimately we spent much of this year in the midst of a mega fomo trade…where there is a
gold rush in the form of Ai capex and supremacy. The fear of not doing outstripping the risks of doing too
early or too much. Time will tell if this is mania or a rational deployment of capital to sit atop the Ai
waterfall with a dominant stake in Ai infrastructure. But….as previous heavy capex cycles have
shown….the value doesn’t always flow to the ‘builders. The railway boom of the 1800’s in the UK saw
500 new railway companies compete to build out 8000 miles of new track (20x the length of England) at a
cost that was more than the entire national income of the country. The telecom and internet infra boom at
the turn of the millennium saw the likes of Cisco and Qualcomm thrive and then falter….and the likes of
360 networks, Altavista and Webvan shine bright and then not shine at all. one thing is true though…the
rush to innovate did create value. It created the bedrock for the later waves of internet and apps. In the
case of railways it created a super highway for a shift into an industrial age. Both providing new services
and technologies to consumers…..that created efficiency, speed and value. Both unlocking opportunities to
build and create. The value of innovation isn’t in question…but perhaps the price of innovation and the
timing of return is.
Valuation support. Historic trough forward P/E ratios…..March 2009 and GFC 10.4x…..March 2020 and
Covid 13.3x…..October 2022 and inflation/rates shock 15.7x…..and if last week was a trough….then the
S&P settled at 18x. So cheaper rather than cheap would be my summary of where we are. Clearly each of
these isn’t factoring growth rates which is an essential (and missing) part of this mix. Either way…an
interesting stat I thought.
