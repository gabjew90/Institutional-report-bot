Major U.S. stock indexes ended a volatile week lower as investors 
weighed the implications of Tuesday’s midterm elections, which 
saw Democrats gain control of both chambers of Congress. 
Democrats’ win initially fueled optimism that a shift in the political 
landscape could ease geopolitical tensions, particularly with Iran.  
However, equities reversed course sharply on Thursday as 
investors reassessed the implications of Democratic electoral 
gains for corporate earnings and regulatory policy. Concerns about 
potentially tighter oversight of the AI industry, compounded by 
disappointing outlooks from major semiconductor companies, 
triggered substantial selling in technology shares. The Nasdaq 100 
declined 6.4%, while semiconductors plunged more than 12%, 
both recording their worst sessions since March 2020. U.S. stocks 
partially recovered on Friday. Meanwhile, US monthly jobs report 
was stronger than expected, and other data released during the 
week indicated that the economy remained resilient. European 
stock markets recorded slight weekly gains. EU banks 
outperformed while defense names fell. Japan’s Nikkei 225 index 
fell -9.25%. Oil prices declined as markets priced a lower 
probability of near-term escalation with Iran and signs of renewed 
diplomatic activity. U.S. Treasury yields declined across the curve, 
with short-term yields falling more sharply than longer-term 
yields, resulting in yield curve steepening. The dollar gained while 
bullion & bitcoin weakened.
