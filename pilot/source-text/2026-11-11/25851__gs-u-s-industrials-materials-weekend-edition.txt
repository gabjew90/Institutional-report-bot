All references to “we/us/our” refer to the views and observations of the desk.
Industrials were down 280bps on the week slightly unperforming broader markets. Materials outperformed
but were still down 150bps. Violent unwinds under the surface with housing outperforming – builders up
over 400bps and technology down almost 500bps. On the upside were smaller cap, highly levered
consumer names that squeezed like JBLU up 30%, DFH up 27% and GPK up 26%. Also, some straggler
earnings that performed well included VNT up 16%, SWK up 12% falling in similar categories. Downside
included mostly data center related names including STRL ECG LGN -27%, FPS MOD VRT off 22%,
poor earnings results from OEC off 26% and KMT down 21%. Next week starts conference season to hear
thoughts on Q4 and early tidbits for 2027. RPM ENTG WCC WOR KBR MGA investor days, as well as a
set of late earnings with a focus on FERG and TDG below. Please let us know how we can help as we
hobble into the final stretch of the year.
Will be out over the next few days with thoughts on subsectors and individual ideas thinking about the rest
of 2026 and early 2027. We continue to believe that the opportunities are much less thematic, much more
idiosyncratic and require some view of the major moving pieces out there including implications from the
elections, capital raises in the AI/data center space, US rates vs. rest of world and implications for the
dollar and inflation expectations in many ways related to geopolitical turmoil. Reach out if you would like
to get time on the calendar to go through together in detail.
Movers on the Week:
Bloomberg as of 11/6/26. Past performance is not indicative of future results
Prime: US equities were modestly net sold this week -0.4 SDs 1-year), driven by the largest single stock
net selling in 4+ months partially offset by long buying + short covering in Macro Products. Macro
Products (Index and ETF combined) were net bought for a second straight week (+1.5 SDs 1-year), driven
by long buys + short covers (~3 to 1). However, US-listed ETF shorts increased +7% on Thursday (Nov
5th) – the largest 1-day increase since March (3rd largest in the past 5 years) – and +3.3% week/week, led
by shorting in Large Cap Equity ETFs. Single Stocks collectively saw the largest net selling in more than
4 months -2.1 SDs 1-year), driven by long-and-short sales (3.8 to 1). Info Tech, Industrials, Staples, and
Health Care were the most $ net sold US sectors, while Utilities, Real Estate, and Financials were the most
net bought. Industrials was among the worst performing US sectors and the most net sold this week -3.3
SDs 1-year), driven by long and-short sales (~2 to 1). This week’s % net selling in US Industrials was the

largest since Covid (Apr ’20) and the second largest on our record (since 2016). Nearly all subsectors were
net sold (sans Machinery and Airlines), led by Electrical Equip, Building Products, Trading Companies &
Distributors, Aerospace & Defense, and Ground Transportation. US Industrials long/short ratio now stands
at 1.61, the lowest level since Jan ’24 and approaching 5-year lows in the 3rd percentile.

Late EPS previews:
FERG reports pre-market Monday November 11th at 6:45am with the call at 8:30am. 7% implied move in
this one with the stock green three of the last four reports. Short interest notable in this one 7mm shares
short vs. 1.5mm earlier this year likely a function of residential exposures. Valuation has come in but
remains at the high end of historical ranges at 20x EPS and 14x EV / EBITDA. More buying than selling
from top holders in recent periods.
Bloomberg as of 11/6/26. Past performance is not indicative of future results
TDG reports Tuesday November 10th pre-market around 7:15am with the call at 10am. 6% implied move
in this one with the stock down six of the last eight reports. Shares short haven’t really moved – holding
around 1mm shares with 3 days to cover and 2% of the shares short – perhaps somewhat surprising vs.
conversations that continue to lean much more negative. Valuation at the lower bound on an absolute and
relative basis though not extreme. This is fiscal Q4 for TDG so all eyes on 2027 with consensus calling for
an acceleration in EPS growth to 19% from 11% on a topline slowdown to 12% from 19%. Will be
watching commentary on aftermarket, original equipment and defense growth expectations.
