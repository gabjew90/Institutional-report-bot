US CPI in Focus, Riksbank Screens
Hawkish, BoC Minutes Later Today
Dimitrios Laloudakis
10-Nov-2026, 07:26 ET (EST)
*Sales desk commentary. Not a product of UBS Research. For institutional investors only.*
• United States: An even more explicit hawkish message shared from Governor Waller regarding today’s CPI
print with a statement that a core CPI reading of 0.3% or higher warrants a 50bps hike. To contextualize that
move, prior to last month’s reading, the last time a core CPI reading over 30bps was printed was back in April
(see figure #1). These comments ultimately led to the December stub to close the gap and trades at ~25bps
now. UBS Economics has par core readings as consensus has converged closer to UBSe (26bps and 23bps
for core vs. 27bps and 22bps). UBS nowcasting has slightly hotter headline reading (+32bps) but an even
cooler core estimate of +22bps. Figure #2a and #2b plot 3m changes for key core goods and core services
sub-components of interest. A useful alternative measure is the percentage of items that are increasing above
the 2% rate (figure #3), which has been on a steady decline.
• Canada: The summary of deliberations released later today will provide a timely update on the BoC’s
thought process undergoing the first 25bps interest rate hike this year. Given the fact that the BoC is presently
in the lower portion of their neutral range estimates (2.25% to 3.25%), it seems increasingly likely to reach
midpoint (2.75% vs. 2.50% currently). Approx two hikes are cumulatively implied by OIS over the next year
with the Dec + Jan meeting contributing to the first total hike in roughly equal parts. No directional view in the
FX as of now, but if USDCAD extends back near the 1.43 level and diverges from macro regime pricing,
another short expression seems appropriate as outlined in FX Divergence.
• Sweden: Riksbank’s meeting minutes read hawkish with SWESTR curve broadly cheaper by 5bps. There
are several soundbites worthwhile to mention, but just to provide a few “…the slightly lower underlying inflation
in September was due, among other things, to an unexpectedly low rate of increase in prices for travel, and
this hardly changes the assessment of the more fundamental driving forces of inflationary pressure, which are
pointing upwards” and “…it is entirely possible that the policy rate would need to be raised a few times more to
ensure that inflation does not end up clearly above the target of 2 per cent and risk becoming entrenched
there.” Short USDSEK has been a position held and outlined in FX Divergence for quite some time, most
recent weekly here.
