On 8 November, NVDIA’s board of directors announced 
an independent investigation following news that CEO 
Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence 
effort. The company concurrently issued a press release 
in which CEO Huang admitted to an extramarital affair. 
Mr. Huang noted his support of the board’s investigation, 
and the board expressed confidence in Mr. Huang and the 
management team.
NVIDIA is scheduled to report 3QFY26 results on 17 
November after the market close. We do not think recent 
events should have any impact on near-term results or 
guidance. The AI compute market remains robust, with 
capacity in short supply against demand that continues to 
grow.
It’s unclear how much management will comment on 
the recent news other than acknowledging events and 
reiterating the CEO’s cooperation and support of the 
investigation.
We do see several potential intermediate- to longer-term 
impacts.
1. Although a change in CEO seems unlikely at the present 
time, we believe investors will start to price in at least 
some probability that CEO Huang may step aside—i.e., 
key man risk. In our view, there is no clear internal 
successor and a sudden change in leadership may present 
challenges given NVIDIA’s extremely flat organizational 
structure. We note that press reports and interviews have 
discussed CEO Huang having 50-60 direct reports.

3. We believe there is may be a further escalation in 
the geopolitical tensions related to AI. While there are 
many potential outcomes, we do think there could 
be a greater requirement for AI compute vendors to 
know where their chips are ultimately deployed. To this 
end, NVIDIA has begun requiring more customers to 
enroll in its Fleet Tracking program, which tracks the 
physical location of NVIDIA chips. If the intelligence-
targeting allegations are substantiated, we believe it 
would be reasonable to expect tighter restrictions on AI-
chip shipments and/or requirements for greater telemetry 
and tracking of chips deployed in the field. This could 
spur greater international competition in AI chips while 
creating headwinds for AI-chip sales in certain overseas 
markets. If the allegations are substantiated, we would 
also expect increased federal scrutiny, including multiple 
investigations and congressional hearings across the AI 
and semiconductor supply chains.
In our AI TRIO list, we continue to favor hyperscalers such 
as Microsoft, Meta Platforms, Amazon, and Google, where 
tighter future capacity could increase the value of existing 
infrastructure and lower future capex could support free 
cash flow. Within AI "picks and shovels," we would stay 
selective and favor quality names with less margin expansion 
to unwind, alongside defensive tech exposures such as Visa 
and Mastercard and potential memory-cost beneficiaries 
such as Apple and Sony.

List.

CIO Equity Portfolios
CIO Equity Portfolios are published research model portfolio comprising stocks with specified weights that reflect each 
security’s allocation within the portfolio and, in aggregate, total 100%. These weights represent portfolio allocations and 
are not active weights or measures of overweight or underweight relative to the benchmark. Portfolios may focus on 
sectors, countries, regions, investment themes, or another stated objective. Each portfolio is constructed relative to an 
identified benchmark, which is used to define the relevant opportunity set, but the portfolio is not designed to replicate the 
benchmark exactly. The CIO view applies to the portfolio in aggregate, including how the holdings work together, rather than 
each individual holding. It is a research model, not a client-specific portfolio, managed investment strategy, or guaranteed 
outcome. CIO Portfolios do not represent actual accounts, trading or investments and performance will not be published.
Portfolio weights are not security-level ratings or rankings and do not express the relative or absolute investment merit, 
expected performance, or degree of conviction associated with any security or issuer. A higher weight should not be 
interpreted as reflecting a more favorable view, greater conviction, or higher expected return.
A security’s inclusion in or removal from the portfolio, or the size or adjustment of its portfolio weight, does not establish, 
modify, or imply a research rating or CIO Selected designation; constitute a rating action; indicate a change in the standalone 
investment view of the security; establish or imply continuous research coverage; or constitute the initiation of research 
coverage. Such additions, removals, and weight changes reflect portfolio construction decisions only.
