Less AI. More alpha
Everyone is racing to spend more on AI. Everyone except Apple. While the hyperscalers pour hundreds
of billions into infrastructure, Apple has kept capex remarkably flat. Investors don't seem to mind. AAPL
is outperforming the rest of the Magnificent Seven, has recently decoupled from the Nasdaq, and
commands a near-record valuation premium.
King Apple
Apple is the only Magnificent Seven stock outperforming both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 year-to-
date. Not bad for the company supposedly falling behind in AI. Up 25% YTD.
CapIQ
Sticking to the knitting
"The market is obviously rewarding Apple for not investing so much (or really anything) in AI. Instead,
Apple sticks to its knitting, and the knitting has been pretty good" (a16z).
fiscal.ai
Spending or saving
Since 2023, the four major hyperscalers have increased capex roughly 6–9x. Apple's capex,
meanwhile, is actually below pre-AI-boom levels. As a share of sales, hyperscaler spending has
doubled or tripled, while Apple's has fallen by roughly a third.

fiscal.ai
Sitting out the arms race
Indexed to Q1 2023, Big Tech capex tells the story. Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta and Amazon have gone
vertical. Apple has barely moved. Different strategies, very different spending bills.
a16z
A premium for doing less
Apple trades at roughly 36x forward earnings, a massive 17-turn premium to the S&P 500. While the
rest of Big Tech spends aggressively to secure its AI future, Apple is being rewarded with a record
relative valuation for largely sitting out the spending race.
GS/Bloomberg
Still not a darling darling
"Only" 57% of analysts are buy / overweight on Apple. Compare that with NVDA at its peak, when that
figure was >90%. Apple is a market darling, but apparently not quite a Wall Street darling.
