 > Summary of this month's CPI preview
> Beyond this month
> PCE and Fed
jeanfrancois.perrin@ca-cib.com
nicholas.vanness@ca-cib.com
We expect US headline CPI to come out at 0.29% MoM SA in October. We cannot provide a YoY number, 
as the October 2025 index was never published by the BLS, courtesy of last year's government shutdown. 
There is no YoY consensus either of course. The US fixings market – using an arbitrary level of 325.604pt 
for the index in October 2025 – has a YoY mark of 3.56% for October 2026. With this same reference, we 
are 2bp above the market.
•
We expect core at 0.26% MoM SA in October, vs 0.29% in August (a number boosted by noise in 
telephone services) and 0.33% in September. Our take is 2bp above the 0.24% MoM SA Bloomberg 
average consensus. The 3M ma annualised core would come out at 3.53% in October, the highest level 
since January 2025. Simply put, we expect a relatively benign report with core back around 0.25% 
MoM SA, so a bit softer than August and September.
•
We share around 65 components and aggregates as usual. Crédit Agricole CIB research is ranked no. 1  by 
Bloomberg on the US CPI YoY aggregate.
•
As usual, we use the futures curves – natural gas and gasoline – as the main inputs for our energy path. 
Considering the recent sharp decline in Brent crude oil prices, our model now suggests headline around 
3.5-3.6% YoY until February next year. The peak was reached in May 2026, at 4.25% YoY.
•
We do not expect a major impact on CPI from the blue wave result of the midterm election, beyond the 
lower crude oil prices which are already embedded in our model.
•
Within core, we have seen very limited signs of second-round effects from higher energy prices due to 
the Iran war, aside from airfares (1.3% of the core CPI basket) of course. We have started to see some 
pass-through in some items in core goods, but nothing significant yet.
•
For the coming six months, base effects will continue to dominate the core YoY reading.  On the one 
hand, last year's tariffs continue to leave the YoY calculation, taking core goods CPI YoY lower. On the other 
hand, OER and rent YoY will be abnormally strong from November 2026 to March 2027 due to the BLS' 
methodological choices after the 2025 government shutdown.
•
All in all, we expect core CPI – 2.6% YoY on average so far this year – to run around 2.8-2.9% until 
February 2027, before easing towards 2.5% again at end-2027.
•
If our forecast is on track, the October CPI report would represent an improvement compared to September, 
though would still be firmer than is ideal for the Fed even in terms of core inflation.
•
The larger-than-expected downward revisions due to the recent PCE methodology changes likely helped 
buy the Fed some time to stay on hold in October. We continue to expect another 25bp hike in December, 
followed by a final 25bp hike in March as the Fed delivers 75bp of hikes in total to reverse last year’s 
insurance cuts, at an every-other-meeting pace, before another pause.
•
We see recent Fedspeak as supportive of this view, with several Fed speakers advocating some additional 
tightening, but at a gradual pace.

> Our take for this month: 0.25% MoM SA
> Our take looking ahead
jeanfrancois.perrin@ca-cib.com
For October, we expect services to rise by 0.25% MoM SA; slightly below August (0.33%) and September (0.37%) 
but way above the surprisingly low 0.03% MoM SA recorded in June.
•
Among the 'strong' expected MoM SA numbers, we have airfares (1.48% MoM SA, with still some more pass-
through from jet fuel costs to ticket prices). We have relatively firm Owner's Equivalent Rent (0.31% MoM SA). 
Telephone services was very erratic in recent months, we expect 0.3% MoM SA in this component in October
•
Among the 'weak' expected MoM SA numbers, we have hotels at -1.33% MoM SA, following two surprisingly strong 
MoM SA (around 2.5%) in a row. We also expect the ongoing decline in 'Internet services' to continue, at -0.8% 
MoM SA, driven by competition between providers.
•
Because of no data in October 2025 – due to the government shutdown – we cannot provide a YoY estimate.
•
As usual, you can see our 35 component details within services on p11 and p13.
•
Looking past October, base effects will push higher the YoY reading in rents and OER higher between October 2026 
and April 2027 (see opposite table), as the 0% MoM recorded in October 2025 will leave the YoY reading. Medium-
term, a number of conflicting narratives remain, for example lower immigration (potentially inflationary from a labour 
perspective) and AI (potentially deflationary, in the medium term), but they do not dominate the near-term outlook 
(1Y) in our view.

> Our take for this month: 0.25% MoM SA
> Our take looking ahead
jeanfrancois.perrin@ca-cib.com
Our model suggests core goods CPI at 0.25% MoM SA in October, slightly above the average of recently reported 
numbers (0.20% in July, 0.11% in August, 0.25% in September). 
•
Among the relatively strong contributions, we namely have 'Education and communication commodities' (4% of the core 
goods basket) at 0.4% MoM SA, following three strong months in a row, and still boosted by chipset and memory 
prices. We also have recreation commodities (10% of the core goods basket) at 0.51% MoM SA, with this category 
reflecting a gradual increase in prices from the multiple higher input costs (plastics, shipping, logistics, etc).
•
As usual, there are more MoM SA details on p10 and p14.
•
Before the breakout of the Iran war, we expected a slow decline in core goods inflation from around 1.4% YoY at end-
2025 to around 0% YoY in Q427 as the effect of the 2025 tariffs gradually left the YoY calculation.
•
This factor remains intact, but the ongoing energy crisis suggests higher imported costs (core goods) and higher logistic 
costs going ahead, keeping core goods close to 1% YoY until around mid-Q227.
•
All in all, we now expect core goods to return around 0.4% YoY at the end-2027 horizon.
•
You can plot our past forecasts vs realised inflation for each component, on this page of our US CPI dashboard.

We have food at a relatively strong 0.34% MoM SA, following two relatively soft numbers around 0.1% MoM SA in

•

once again emphasise the declining trend in cocoa and sugar wholesale prices, which still stand solidly below their
respective 2024 averages.
•

the coming quarters. The process should be slow though, as multiple idiosyncratic effects continue to pile up.  We 
still expect food CPI to remain not far from 3% YoY for the coming quarters, before running around 3.5% going into 
Q427 and Q128, with El Nino being an upside risk. These are very modest moves compared with US food CPI 
peaking at 11% YoY in late 2022.

> Implications for PCE
> Implications for the Fed
nicholas.vanness@ca-cib.com
As usual, we will be waiting for the October PPI report, released a few days after CPI on 13 November, to finalise 
PCE forecasts. To provide a reminder, the relevant PPI components include airfares (specifically domestic 
scheduled passenger air transportation), a number related to medical care services and a number related to 
financial services.
•
With regards to the latter, since the change is recent we will reiterate our comment from last month that portfolio 
management & investment advice is no longer among the relevant components for PCE as a methodology 
change that took effect in late September altered the source that the BEA uses for this component in PCE.
•
As we have noted in the past, the methodology changes (which also covered computer software & accessories 
and legal services) led to larger downward revisions to PCE data than had been anticipated, with portfolio 
management and investment advice playing the largest role. This component was revised all the way down to 
8.6% YoY in July from 20.8% previously, on its own shaving more than 0.2ppt off of the YoY core rate.
•
The larger-than-expected impact from the methodology changes did paint an improved picture for PCE inflation, 
though the September report was on the firmer side and core PCE is still too high at 3.1% YoY or more than a full 
percentage point above target.
•
If our forecast is on track, the October CPI report would represent an improvement compared to September, 
though would still be firmer than is ideal for the Fed even in terms of core inflation.
•
The larger-than-expected downward revisions due to the recent PCE methodology changes likely helped buy the 
Fed some time to stay on hold in October, though the firmer September data and an October report that is still a 
bit too high would support some additional gradual tightening, in our view. We continue to expect another 25bp 
hike in December, followed by a final 25bp hike in March as the Fed delivers 75bp of hikes in total to reverse last 
year’s insurance cuts, at an every-other-meeting pace, before another pause.
•
We see recent Fedspeak as supportive of this view, with several Fed speakers advocating some additional 
tightening, but at a gradual pace. Aside from the pre-October commentary from FOMC members like New York 
Fed President John Williams, Vice Chair Philip Jefferson and Governor Christopher Waller that we have 
highlighted in the past, even Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan after the October meeting came out in favour of a 
gradual approach. We see Logan’s comments as notable given that she was one of the FOMC members that had 
dissented in favour of a hike in July and was possibly expected to dissent again in October, so if even some of 
the more hawkish members were fine with skipping October, then the gradual pace looks to have fairly 
widespread, though not universal, support.
