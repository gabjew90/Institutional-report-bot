• As the top chart shows, since May there were two distinct market phases with 
respect to beta. During the four months from June to September, while bond 
yields were spiking, Defensive sectors got crushed, together with poor showing 
of Low Vol, bond proxies etc., and Banks strongly outperformed Staples. From 
end September, beta rally reversed, coinciding with the peak in bond yields. 
Banks vs Staples relative performance has round tripped back to the 
starting point of May-June, back to flat on the year. Where next?
• This will likely be determined by Tech, growth and geopolitics directions. Oil 
price remains a wildcard short term; here we believe that even if there is a re-
escalation of the conflict, it will likely be short-lived, and last week’s Blue 
Wave is adding confidence to this view. One should be using any dip on the back 
of potential escalation to add, the same stance we had ever since March. More 
broadly, markets were typically volatile into mid-terms, but historically have 
tended to post stronger performance thereafter.
• Beyond geopolitics, the next stage of beta rotation will likely depend on why 
bond yields have peaked. Is it to do with mounting concerns over where we are 
in the cycle, or is it driven by reducing pressures on long yields, through stable 
inflation expectations, likely less aggressive AI crowding out and more 
appropriate levels of short rates reached. We believe that it will end up the latter; 
we reiterate our call from early October that bond yields have peaked, and 
that the interpretation of this will evolve from what was initially negative, 
to a positive one.
• Other internal rotations that are taking place at present should be seen 
favourably. The peaking in bond yields has instigated a rebound in Value vs 
Growth style, helping our Value OW that we held in Europe for the past 2 years. 
Also, SPW has rebounded vs SPX, tracking the turn in yields. We have been 
arguing entering the summer that there is likely to be a rotation and broadening 
in market leadership in 2H, given the record low breadth seen in Q2 - middle 
chart, and that Tech was unlikely to be the only story in town for the balance of 
the year. Now, after the very strong 1H, the reversal in AI fortunes indeed 
happened, and in a very dramatic fashion, which is turning into a risk to the 
upcycle itself. 
• We stay with the broadening call, however, we do not think AI is a continued sell 
here. In fact, Tech drawdown is offering certain opportunities. The selloff 
was very sharp, and P/E  compression extreme. S&P500 long-short 
momentum factor has lost all the ytd returns - bottom chart.
• If the Tech space stabilizes, upcycle will likely look more sustainable. The 
acceleration phase is behind us, but it doesn’t necessarily follow that we need to 
move into outright contraction next. We can remain in expansion territory, with 
positive earnings growth and activity indicators - ISM & PMIs - staying above 
50 => mid-stage of the cycle, rather than end. As we already had a big growth 
scare within equities, if the activity holds up, oil is down and bond yields more 
stable, then beta sectors will likely rebound.
• Ytd P/E derating stands at 10% for MSCI World. Despite extremely fragile 
investor sentiment and a very tough recent performance, if the above drivers 
show traction, we believe investors should be adding to equities into YE.

Cyclicals sectors typically exhibit a positive correlation to 
bond yields, but that relationship appears to have flipped as 
bond yields spiked. We believe this was driven by a more 
stagflationary interpretation of the move higher in yields.
Consequently, a gap has opened up between the performance 
of Cyclical sectors and bond yields. This is similar to what 
was observed in 2022, but we do not see that period as the 
correct template for the current setup. Back then, Central 
Banks had to resort to aggressive tightening, having fallen 
behind on keeping inflation pressures in check.  We read the 
current gap between Cyclicals and yields as a growth scare, 
not the start of a 2022 style unwind. If inflation stays con-
tained and yields stabilise, we expect the gap to close through 
a Cyclical rebound.

Equity Strategy Key Calls and Drivers
There is potential for a geopolitical re-escalation after mid-terms, which could lead to a bout of market weakness, and a fresh spike in oil prices. If the restart of the conflict does come through, we do not 
think it will be prolonged, it will likely end up as another dip to buy, like all geopolitics-driven dips we saw since March. We maintain the call that bond yields have peaked, made in early October, and 
believe that the interpretation of a turn in yields will soon evolve into a more positive one. If activity and earnings do not take a clear step lower, then yields peaking will allow the upcycle to be more 
sustained, rather than be a signal of an imminent economic weakness. EPS growth is still very strong, resulting in a meaningful derating of equities. As outlined earlier, oil price is a clear wildcard near 
term, where we might end up seeing a proverbial “escalate in order to de-escalate” template. As bond yields peaked in early October, SPW and Value bounced. AI ecosystem is struggling to perform, it is 
back to relative lows seen in June-July. We had the call that market leadership was set to broaden in 2H, that AI will not be the only story in town any more, and we stay with it. That said, we do not think 
Tech is an outright sell here. Hyperscalers in particular have derated significantly, to be the cheapest since 2012. While SPX is just 3% off all-time highs, and SXXP 2%, beneath the surface, risk-off is 
more visible. The sharp rotation into Defensives seen last month has taken out all the gains beta sectors made in the prior three months. If we are right that inflation fears do not worsen, any renewed 
geopolitical escalation is only temporary, and economic activity stays resilient – latest IFO just made a four-year high – then beta sectors should stabilize again. Thematically, we were cautious on Defense 
since summer of ’25, but given the weakness, we two months ago argued to turn more positive on the space, favouring modernization plays.
