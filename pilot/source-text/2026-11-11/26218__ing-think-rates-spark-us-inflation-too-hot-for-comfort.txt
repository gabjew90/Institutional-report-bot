Rates Spark: US inflation likely too hot
for comfort
The direct pass-through from oil to rates has weakened compared with a
few months ago, as markets turn their attention to inflation data. We
expect the US core CPI reading to come in at 0.3% MoM, which is too hot
to support lower rates. The ongoing AI jitters do help a 10yr UST yield of
5% look relatively attractive, supporting inflows into longer rates
Lower oil doesn't have as strong an impact on rates as a few
months ago
Risk sentiment improved a tad on Monday, helped by oil easing lower and a recovery in AI
stocks. But the bid for duration seems to stick and most global 10yr rates managed to tick a
few bp lower. Flows data from the last few weeks already reflected increased demand for
longer-dated bonds, even before the AI jitters started. Concerns about stretched equity prices
can further feed the relative value play whereby a 10yr UST yield of around 5% could look
attractive.
Having said that, we still warn about the upside risks to rates in the near term. Markets are
constantly rotating their attention between AI and inflation concerns, keeping volatility high.
Next up is the US CPI number, which consensus still sees coming in hot at 0.3% MoM for the

Brent oil ticking closer to $90 per barrel helps ease inflation risks but doesn't remove the upside
risks. Rates were much more sensitive to oil prices a few months ago, but the correlation has
diminished significantly. The damage has been done in terms of higher energy costs, and the
focus is rotating towards potential second-round effects. So, whilst lower oil prices take off
some of the pressure from rates, inflation data has become increasingly important. 
Tuesday’s events and market view
The main release of the day is the US CPI which is expected to come in at 0.3% m/m,
still above the 0.17% level needed to average out at an annualised 2% which the Fed
targets. We will not have the annual figure as no data was collected for the yearly
comparison given the government shutdown in 2025. Fed officials Waller, Logan,
Schmid and Paulson will speak on Tuesday.
There is no relevant data out of Europe on Tuesday, but among other things, the BoE’s
Watcher conference will feature a number of central bank speakers from the BoE as
well as the ECB, including chief economist Lane. 
In primary markets, the EU will conduct its last syndicated sale for the year with taps of
the 5y and 20y benchmarks. The Netherlands auctions 10y DSLs (up to €3bn). The UK
auctions 29y Gilts (£1bn), and the US Treasury sells new 10y notes (US$42bn).
