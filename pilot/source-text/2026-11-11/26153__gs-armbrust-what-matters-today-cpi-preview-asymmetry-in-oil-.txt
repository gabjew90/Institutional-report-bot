1) CPI Preview
We expect a 0.16% increase in October core CPI (vs. +0.2% consensus). We expect a 0.21% increase in
headline CPI (vs. +0.3% consensus), reflecting higher food and energy prices. We expect monthly core
CPI increases of around 0.1% in November and December, reflecting the continued slowdown in the
shelter categories and shrinking contributions from tariff-related price increases.
With ~80% probability of a December hike priced, a softer print could materially change the rates
narrative. Core at 0.2% would be constructive; 0.3% would challenge the rally. We also have $42bn of
10yr Treasury supply today, following a relatively weak 3yr auction, and $25bn of 30yr supply Thursday.
Importantly, Brent fell almost 6% yesterday and the 10yr barely responded...in fairness was a major risk
on day for equities but I think the key point here is that rates are more reflexive to AI growth expectations
than energy. That said, see the side-note...inflation expectations are rolling over quickly...perhaps rate hike
expectations are soon to follow. (TY Rich Privorotsky)

2) Asymmetry in Oil
CL1 now back to $85. Trump’s discussions with Gulf leaders suggest little appetite for further escalation.
Regional governments appear to favor continued economic pressure on Iran over additional military
action. The assumption is that Iran refrains from targeting Gulf energy infrastructure while diplomatic

channels remain open. Federal government funding is set to expire on December 11th. If Iran talks derail,
Trump will be in a tough position… does he escalate?
Hard to have strong views when it comes to geopolitics, but thinking of probability scenarios…. If the war
ends, I’d say Oil goes to $70-75.. if it escalates the range is $100-110? So the trade is now skewed to the
upside. Your portfolio probably loses money if the war escalates. I would hedge that scenario by buying
upside in Oil... year end calls, call spreads or dual binaries. Let me know if you want to look at
implementations.
3) 2027 Consumer Outlook
The US consumer has proven more resilient than looked likely when the Iran war began and energy prices
spiked. We estimate that consumer spending will rise 2.5% in 2026 on a Q4/Q4 basis. The two key drivers
of spending growth—real income growth and household wealth—should both be supportive next year. But
we expect real income growth to pick up to 2.3% in 2027. This reflects significant disinflation as the
impulses from tariffs, energy prices, and AI-related prices fade. We are also not too concerned about the
relatively low measured 3.8% saving rate: we estimate that it is closer to 5% after accounting for timelier
source data that will eventually be incorporated with a long lag; a low saving rate is unsurprising when
strong balance sheets and a stable labor market limit the need for saving.
We expect consumer spending to rise at a near-potential 2.2% pace in 2027 (Q4/Q4). We look for a softer
start to the year as higher rates weigh on durables demand, energy prices stay elevated, and nonprofit
spending declines after the midterm elections, followed by a pickup as inflation pressures abate and tax
refunds and the reversal of benefit cuts turn the fiscal impulse modestly positive.

Source: Goldman Sachs FICC & Equities, Marquee, as of 10 Nov 2026.
