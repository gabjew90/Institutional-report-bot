Fed’s Waller Puts 50bp Hike On The Table As CPI Takes Centre Stage
The October CPI report, the last inflation reading before the Fed’s Dec. 8-9 meeting, is due Tuesday, and
ahead of that Fed Governor Christopher Waller laid out a clear threshold for a potentially larger rate move. In
prepared remarks for delivery in London, Waller said he would support a 25bp December hike even if October
inflation improves, while a 50bp increase should be considered if core CPI rises 0.3% or more m/m, according
to Bloomberg. “While I am hoping for an improvement over September, even if this occurs, I would still support
a 25 basis point increase,” Waller said.
UBS economist Alan Detmeister expects headline CPI at 0.26% m/m, below 0.29% consensus and the
0.32% UBS Nowcast. He forecasts core CPI at 0.23%, versus 0.24% consensus and 0.22% Nowcast, marking
a notable slowdown from September. Waller called September’s inflation data “bad” and said he was
particularly concerned about the breadth of price increases. He also cited Fed staff analysis pointing to further
producer-price pressures and potentially more persistent effects from elevated energy prices.
(Simon Penn & Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Nvidia China Risk In Focus As Earnings Expectations Hold – Nvidia was down 5.5% on Monday, but held
up relatively well against challenging news as investors look for clarity on what allegations around CEO
Jensen Huang ultimately means for the stock. There is no definitive answer yet and the unusual nature of the
situation leaves the market watching closely for how the news develops. Most conversations suggest investors
expect the issue ultimately to blow over. If it does not, attention turns to the strength and depth of Nvidia's
management team. Huang has been pivotal to both Nvidia and the broader AI story, but there is also a deep
bench behind him. The debate is also extending to Nvidia's relationship with the US government. Questions
include what Washington ultimately wants from the company, given Huang's push to retain access to China
alongside government pressure for greater US investment, potentially including domestic foundry capacity.
The more immediate fundamental risk is how much Nvidia is shipping into China. The desk's best estimate is
that the exposure is concentrated in a portion of Singapore revenue, where assemblers may ultimately be
serving Chinese companies, while other geographies appear less connected. Earnings are quickly
approaching, and despite Monday's news there has been little indication that investors are backing away from
a January quarter revenue bogey closer to $135 bn. Expectations are still evolving, but for now the earnings
bar appears largely intact. (Robert Ruple, Specialist Sales)

Brent Falls Below $90 For First Time Since August As Iran Fears Ease - Oil extended losses on Tuesday,
with Brent falling below $90 a barrel for the first time since August 31 as fears of further US military action
against Iran eased. The benchmark has retreated around 23% from its six-month high of $118 on Oct. 30. US
President Donald Trump on Monday said he planned to hold off on new strikes against Iran following appeals
from countries in the region. Signs of looser supply conditions are also weighing on prices, with OPEC output
rising in October while high oil prices have dampened demand. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales)
Iran Rejects US Talk Claims, Reasserts War Conditions And Hormuz Curbs – Iran’s security chief
Mohsen Rezaei dismissed US reports of negotiations as “fake news” and demanded acceptance of Tehran’s
seven conditions, conveyed through Qatar in September. Three publicly named demands are ending the war
on all fronts, releasing frozen Iranian funds and lifting the US naval blockade. Tehran says normal Hormuz
traffic will not resume until its conditions are met. The remarks contrast with US President Donald Trump’s
“positive momentum” assessment. While rejecting reports of negotiations, Iran has acknowledged that
messages continue through mediators. The competing positions leave no clear near-term path to restoring
normal Hormuz flows, despite Trump’s assessment that renewed US military action looks unlikely. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Sweden's Riksbank Signals Further Rate Hikes - Sweden's Riksbank expects to raise its policy rate further
over the coming year after lifting it by 25 basis points to 2.0%, with policymakers citing a stronger economy
and elevated inflation risk, according to minutes from the latest policy meeting. Members highlighted higher
energy and producer prices, supply disruptions and the weaker krona as inflation risks. Aino Bunge
nevertheless favoured a “gradual approach”, stressing the need to evaluate each step and noting that
weaker-than-expected household demand could reduce the need for additional tightening. (Matthew Cowley,
Macro Sales)
EuroConf: Fed Going Thru Some ‘Growing Pains’ - The Fed is going through some growing pains as it
goes through the process of developing a new monetary policy framework based on less communication and
less forward guidance, according to panelists at the UBS European Conference. That means there’s a lack of
definition around what the Fed is trying to do, including what it means to return inflation to 2% in a “timelier
manner”. The Fed needs new anchors for what monetary policy is and has yet to define the model it’s using.
These issues should start to be resolved in coming months. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales)
EuroConf: Global Economy Fragmentation Drives Up Cost Of Capital - The fragmentation of the global
economy that has taken place in recent years will lead to a rise in the cost of capital for both equity and credit,
according to panelists at the UBS European Conference. Just as globalization gave rise to an integration of
global markets and lower cost of capital, the reverse is now underway. That is reflected in the higher bond
yields and tighter financial conditions, which are not just nominal but also have a real impact. This is going to
have an impact on fiscal policy, as borrowing costs are higher, and it is having an impact on valuations, and it
will also impact investment and consumption. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales)

EuroConf: Latest Eurozone Oil Shock Very Different From 2021/2022 - The oil shock of 2021/2022, when
Russia invaded Ukraine, came at a time when the Eurozone economy was in a very different place to where it
is today, according to panelists at the UBS European Conference. The ECB had negative interest rates and
was still in QE and the fiscal deficit across the Eurozone was close to 7% of GDP. Then, there was a late
reaction by monetary policy. Now the situation is quite different, with positive interest rates, no QE and a
timelier response from the ECB. That explains why the oil shock this time around has less impact than in the
previous crisis. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales)

Japan Household Spending Slumps; Some BoJ Policymakers Urge Faster Rate Hikes – Japan’s
household spending fell 6.8% y/y in September, more than the consensus forecast for a 3.1% decline, marking
the largest slide since September 2020. Seasonally adjusted spending dropped 2.8% m/m, versus a
consensus forecast of a 0.3% rise. The internal affairs ministry attributed the slump to poor weather that
discouraged outings as Tokyo recorded precipitation every day of the month for the first time. The weakness
contrasted with a ninth consecutive month of real wage gains and the fastest nominal wage growth since
January 1997. Separately, the BoJ’s October meeting summary of opinions showed some policymakers
advocating faster rate hikes as underlying inflation reached its 2% target. One opinion argued that financial
conditions remained accommodative and the effects of past hikes had yet to materialise; two others called for
moving the policy rate towards neutral soon. The BoJ held its key rate at 1.25% last month and flagged upside
inflation risks and is widely expected to hike in December. The spending slump adds a reason for caution over
the pace of further tightening. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

beneath the expected stability in headline growth. Sectoral disparities in fiscal resources, real interest rates

Aussie Consumer Sentiment Hits Four-Year Low As Rate Fears Mount – The Westpac–Melbourne
Institute consumer sentiment index fell 2.7% to 78.2 in November from October’s 80.4, its lowest since late
2022 and down 24.6% y/y. Expectations for the economy over the next 12 months slumped 10.2% to 70.3, the
weakest since August 2020. Westpac attributed the deterioration to persistent cost-of-living pressures, higher
petrol prices and the RBA’s latest 25bp rate hike. Mortgage-holder sentiment fell 11.6% to a record low of
68.1. Among consumers surveyed after the RBA decision, 72% expected mortgage rates to rise further over
the next year. House price expectations dropped 13.4% to 99.6, slipping below the neutral 100 mark for the
first time since November 2022, while unemployment expectations rose 1.6% to 144.4. Westpac said
deteriorating confidence pointed to mounting pressure on household spending ahead of Christmas. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Semiconductor Selloff Prices In Power Gap – Equity Research: The SOX’s roughly 30% drop over the
past month has pushed semiconductor valuations close to levels that fully discount the AI data-center
powered-shell constraint, according to Timothy Arcuri. With the index down another roughly 12% last week
following the US midterm elections, current pricing implies much of the potential downside from slower
data-center development is already reflected in stocks. Working backwards from historical SOX multiples, Tim
calculates that current prices imply roughly 30% and 35% cuts to Street calendar-year 2027 and calendar-year
2028 EPS estimates, respectively. After adjusting for operating leverage and fixed costs, that equates to about
a 20% reduction in implied calendar-year 2028 revenue. Translated into compute capacity, Tim estimates the
market is effectively pricing roughly 55GW of calendar-year 2028 shipments, versus an original expectation of
about 70GW. That is only modestly above the roughly 50GW of powered-shell availability considered
high-confidence. Tim had previously identified a roughly 20-30GW gap between expected semiconductor
shipments and high-confidence powered-shell capacity in calendar years 2027 and 2028. (Robert Ruple,
Specialist Sales)
