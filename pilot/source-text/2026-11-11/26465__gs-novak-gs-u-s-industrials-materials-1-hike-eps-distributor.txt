GS out adding a hike to their March Fed forecast: The October CPI report this morning surprised
meaningfully to the upside across several categories and marked a third month of worse inflation news.
We continue to expect a second 25bp rate hike in December and are adding a third 25bp hike to our
forecast in March 2027. A third hike could come earlier in January, but we think March is a bit more likely
because we expect the November and December inflation reports to be softer but then expect larger start-
of-the-year price increases amidst ongoing cost pressures to boost inflation in January. The recent inflation
news has not been entirely one-sided. Oil futures have fallen substantially since the elections, and this
should mean that the bulk of the passthrough from the energy shock to consumer prices is behind us. We
remain confident that inflation will be meaningfully lower by the end of 2027 as the effects of tariffs, the
energy shock, and AI price pressures diminish. But we suspect that the recent inflation prints will lead
most FOMC participants to signal a third hike in the December dot plot and that a firmer January inflation
report would make delivering it the path of least resistance even if the energy shock appears over by then.
We have left our forecast for the eventual terminal fed funds rate unchanged at 3.25-3.5%, but we now
expect rate cuts to begin one quarter later in December 2027 (vs. September 2027 previously), followed by
additional cuts in March, June, and September 2028. Full note:

US distributors – desk view: Group that has been topical all year with a variety of end markets but one
thing in common – broadly beneficiaries of acceleration inflation – whether you measure that in rates, the
US dollar, CPI and/or PPI.
Thinking ahead to the set up into year end and 2027, there remains a variety of opinions. The desk view is
broadly that the Fed is focused on inflation with one rate increase and another potentially coming in
December, geopolitical backdrop that is more likely to de-escalate than re-escalate and expectations that
are mixed across industrial and consumer end markets as it relates to distributors. We believe the group as
a whole will be a source of funds going forward.
We are broadly surprised that AI disruption / disintermediation / competition from technology broadly has
disappeared from the conversation.
Think customer penetration at the point of sale & physical presence (FAST) remains a competitive
advantage with ecommerce & book and ship business more vulnerable (GWW). Share opportunities under
new management despite year to date gains remain more interesting long (MSM). Upside optionality to
the multiple, business and any moves in management remain (WCC). Remain cautious on municipal end
markets and businesses that almost require inflation (CNM) with competitive pressures remain a multi
year overhang to the multiple (WSO). Despite the move yesterday (FERG) remains the resi levered
distributor we like. Welcome your thoughts.
