1) Privo - Resilient. AI on the watch. Positioning is a tailwind, Fed is a headwind
Equity Resilience: SPX barely fell yesterday and futures are recovering this morning. AI sentiment
appears to be improving, supporting the core engines of the market. We’ve spent a lot of time debating just
how low nets have fallen, how much gross has been cut and how heavily hedged investors have become.
Even with a hot CPI print, the market struggled to sustain the volatility premium. VIX briefly traded into
the 20s but finished below 20. That’s quite telling… positioning may now be doing more to support the
tape than the macro is doing to undermine it.
AI: Neocloud commentary overnight was broadly supportive, with data center capacity constraints
remaining the primary operational bottleneck. Compute utilization across major providers continues to
track at elevated levels. Markets focus remains concentrated on hardware delivery and developer coding
workflows, while broader edge and enterprise autonomous integrations remain in earlier stages of
deployment.
Risk: Rates are a headwind but market has built some cushion from prior rally so rates aren’t yet flashing
red for equities… yields need another leg higher before historically they become a more material problem
for equities (it's the rate of change that matters). Cyclicality relative to the market is also hanging in.
Credit still the one to watch with high yield spreads chopping sideways. There’s also the reflexivity with
energy… higher oil, stickier inflation, a more hawkish Fed. The question is whether the Fed is reacting to
energy effects already in the rearview mirror or something more persistent. Gross has been cut, nets are
historically low and the appetite to add risk has diminished substantially. That’s the tailwind for the tape.
Volatility remains elevated relative to the price action and, absent fresh negative information, that
premium should continue to decay. Positioning is a tailwind, the Fed is a headwind. US Veterans Day
today… bond market closed, equities open. Watching the neoclouds and next move in energy. (Full piece

2) US Econ - Adding a Third Hike in March
The October CPI report surprised meaningfully to the upside across several categories and marked a third
month of worse inflation news. We continue to expect a second 25bp rate hike in December and are
adding a third 25bp hike to our forecast in March 2027. A third hike could come earlier in January, but we
think March is a bit more likely because we expect the November and December inflation reports to be
softer but then expect larger start-of-the-year price increases amidst ongoing cost pressures to boost
inflation in January.

Oil futures have fallen substantially since the elections, and this should mean that the bulk of the
passthrough from the energy shock to consumer prices is behind us. We remain confident that inflation
will be meaningfully lower by the end of 2027 as the effects of tariffs, the energy shock, and AI price
pressures diminish. But we suspect that the recent inflation prints will lead most FOMC participants to
signal a third hike in the December dot plot and that a firmer January inflation report would make
delivering it the path of least resistance even if the energy shock appears over by then. We have left our
forecast for the eventual terminal fed funds rate unchanged at 3.25-3.5%, but we now expect rate cuts to
begin one quarter later in December 2027 (vs. September 2027 previously), followed by additional cuts in
March, June, and September 2028.
3) China - Where to Find Growth?
US$5tn GDP has been added to the Chinese economy in the post-Covid era, but Chinese stocks have
failed to fully convert realized macro growth into equity returns. Those profit inhibitors may stem from
and reflect other deeper economic, policy, and social factors that have suppressed the cost of capital for
Chinese corporates. China is still growing, but the Growth equity universe has changed and become more
concentrated. We forecast Chinese stocks to deliver 10% trend EPS growth and 12% normal ROE over the
next 5 years.

Staying strategically bullish on Structural AI and “Going Global” Growth: AI Tech Hardware industries
and export-oriented sectors, looks remarkably compelling to us given its strong near-term earnings growth
momentum and visibility, and the significant de-rating amid the recent drawdown in AI proxies globally,
we believe that a great deal of pessimism is already well-discounted in equity prices. We’d take the
weakness to build strategic positions on select Structural/AI Growth stocks, notably our Chinese AI
Export Leaders, Domestic Champions, and the more diversified Select AI Portfolio Embracing Stable,
Shareholder Return Growth: From a macro perspective, this group seems well positioned when US (rising)
and China (falling) rates are moving in opposite directions, and/or the US-China yield spreads stay as wide
as they currently are. Focusing on earnings turnarounds and cash (flows) in De-rated and Cyclical Growth
and Avoiding Depressed Growth until the housing market stabilizes.
