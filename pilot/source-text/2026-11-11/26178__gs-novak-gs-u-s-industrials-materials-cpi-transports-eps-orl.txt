GS on CPI: We expect a 0.16% increase in October core CPI (vs. +0.2% consensus). We expect a 0.21%
increase in headline CPI (vs. +0.3% consensus), reflecting higher food and energy prices. Our forecast is
consistent with a larger 0.23% increase in core PCE in October.We highlight three key component-level
trends we expect to see in the report. First, we expect the airfares category to be unchanged month-over-
month based on our tracking of online prices. Second, we expect a 0.1% decline in medical services
prices, reflecting a large decline in health insurance prices (-2.5%) as the Bureau of Labor Statistics
incorporates updated source data on insurance premiums. Third, we expect a slowdown in autos inflation,
reflecting a 1.5% decline in used car prices, a 0.1% increase in new car prices, and a 0.3% decline in the
auto insurance category.

Transports from here – desk view:
Enthusiasm in the group has waned though we still believe there is more length than not which may seem
surprising. Rails are the most owned and also appear the most vulnerable with recent probabilities of
M&A getting done - declining. Rails up 25-35% year to date and remarkably consistently. These names
take time to benefit from the step function higher in spot rates. Volumes have had unique benefits this year
that may not recur. And PSR is a rear view mirror concept. CSX is up the most with easy comparisons,
new management and some M&A premium. UNP CNI CP all up the same. We think this sub category
offers more risk than reward in up and down markets. Parcel remains under owned overall with FDX vs.
UPS a classic pair trade. With a new CFO at FDX, capital deployment could come front and center with
gains artificially boosting UPS US domestic margins year to date. We like the former long and the latter as
a source of funds after this most recent bounce. Trucking has been mixed – KNX sticks out with a better
than feared quarter, better than peers and not reacting. Other truckload and intermodal names appear more
difficult whether it’s the inability to leverage the cycle, price vs. volume trade off and/or valuation
limitations. In less than truckload, FDXF appears to have the most optionality though debates around
where earnings settle remain. XPO consistent execution but seeing pricing slow for multiple quarters in a
row, ODFL needs to see volume improvement but not seeing this yet and SAIA looks binary. Asset light
looks like a tale of two performers. On the upside EXPD has materially outperformed with debates
heading into its analyst day next week whether there will be follow through or start to become sell the
news. CHRW on the other hand at lows also with mixed debates and strong views on both sides. Think
this one will take time to figure out – was RXO the right move, at the right time or not. Desk likes FDX
KNX EXPD FDXF while cautious on UPS CSX UNP XPO ODFL. Welcome your thoughts and debates.
ORLY – Kate hosted the company post earnings: We hosted a virtual group meeting with Jeremy Fletcher,
O'Reilly's CFO, and Leslie Skorick, Senior Director of Tax and Investor Relations. Key discussion points
included the components of elevated SG&A in 3Q and drivers behind the increase in SG&A per store
outlook for FY26, gross margin headwinds related to higher motor oil costs, and accelerating unit growth.
We maintain our Buy rating as we believe O'Reilly is well positioned to take share in the current
environment and outperform peers, particularly in DIFM, and as the company is accelerating share

repurchases while also reinvesting in the business, both of which should aid EPS growth in the near and
longer term.
