GS TMT SPEC SALES – Monday 9th November
A few thoughts as we start the week. The moves have been extreme, but the bigger change is in investors’
willingness to take risk. My conversations late last week were much more about self-preservation (esp. at
HFs) rather than re-engaging in the market: protecting remaining P&L or stopping the bleed lower (feels
like many TMT funds now down on the year…despite the broader market), reducing exposure and waiting
for stability, rather than buying simply because stocks had fallen. Last week as GS data showed was
extremely painful and hedges particularly index and factor hedges have not worked.
Net, net positioning and valuations have reset faster than confidence. The more constructive investors
point to the strong underlying demand, protected near-term projects and scope for relief after extreme
selling. The more cautious investors see unresolved funding and execution risks, limited immediate
catalysts and investors unwilling to buy another false recovery. Cheaper stocks alone are unlikely to
prompt broad re-engagement: investors want a more credible narrative and evidence that volatility is
settling.
The other debate has been about what to rotate into and it been assets less exposed to AI be that Telco or
Media (I have had renewed interest in software (DSY in particular), WPP, classifieds) But now questions
are also on whether this rotation into safety itself has itself become stretched. Moving away from AI risk
does not automatically mean moving into attractive valuations; some perceived safe havens may as have
longer term AI risk.
A couple of good reads from the weekend Tony P piece and his preamble says it all - “I have no idea what
the world will look like in 3-5 years ... no one does.” This especially given AI and the debate with
investors has become increasing more 2 way esp. around the longer term and what to expect given AI no
one really does have an idea what the world will look like in 3-5 year. Plus I think Ryan Sharkey email is
well worth a read and Mark Wilson weekly wrap
THE WEEK IN ONE CHART
From a tech side Semi/SOX has led the market lower and its stabilising is important. The SOX started the
year at a 2-year fwd P/E of ~21x, reflecting elevated AI/semis optimism. Its now pulled back sharply and
is sitting at 10.9x 2-year forward P/E ~ near the lower end of its historical range — comparable to GFC-
era troughs and the 2013 semis downcycle lows. The only period with a sustained lower multiple was the
2012–2014 trough (~9–11x).

The more constructive investors are now debating how big an earnings downgrade is already priced in
(interesting read from a bull The more bearish flagging where the memory name trade vs. where the
semicap (Hynix 1.9x and Samsung 2.3x 2-year fwd PS vs . ASML 17.7 and AMAT 14.7. Admitted
Price/Book or EV/EBITDA might also be a better metric – the point is the pull back has been significant
and does now price in slower earnings). Near term Friday did bounce, but debate with investors on Friday
whether this time to step in – Asia not up much this morning (TSMC +4.6%, Samsung & Hynix up less
than 1%).
NDX valuation bear market. Forward P/E now trades at 18.4x, well below the 5 & 10yr avg and its lowest
level since 2020.
Views on AI had already become far more questioning before the midterms. Concerns around 1)
credit/funding/Insufficient capital to finance the buildout (still the biggest risk and CDS showing little
signs of improving – make the Neocloud reporting this week more interesting), 2) power availability and
now permitting concerns, 3) cash burn and 4) the returns on an increasingly large investment base were
building (ultimately its is whether revenues ultimately justify the capital employed)
The midterm added another layer of uncertainty around permitting costs and delivery timelines. This is not
evidence of an AI demand collapse, but it does challenge the assumption that strong demand automatically
translates into uninterrupted spending and attractive returns across the entire supply chain. Slower capacity
delivery creates its own winners and losers within AI. Permitting and power constraints could make
existing compute more valuable while delaying revenues for equipment suppliers and new infrastructure
projects. That helps explain the divergence between hyperscalers and spending beneficiaries. Constrained
supply is not the same as disappearing demand, but it can still create inventory problems (a pt from semi
bears), cash-flow pressure and earnings risk further down the chain.

• China AI finding a floor. The Asia AI trade followed suit with the US and client activity this week shifted
firmly towards adding downside protection via Taiwan Tech hedges (GSXATTXT, GSXATXTM) and
short selling across Korea and Japan. China Domestic AI stood out on a relative basis through the
volatility. Policy support and national-team buying helped onshore AI initially decouple from the regional
unwind, while domestic names continued to outperform export-oriented peers even as the broader market
weakened into Friday. Following the recent correction, compressed valuations and continued momentum
behind domestic substitution support our view to re-engage via GS China Domestic AI (GSXACADO).
• Oracle: Feedback from meetings with CEO Clay Magouryk and CFO Hillary Maxson was reassuring,
with management emphasizing strong visibility into both AI demand and data-center supply alongside
significant operational flexibility. Oracle sees minimal execution risk to the 7GW of capacity planned
through May 2028, given projects are contracted and already under construction, while management
believes AI demand can broaden materially beyond hyperscalers into enterprise applications, including
agentic software and robotics. Overall, the meetings reinforced confidence that near-term infrastructure
constraints are manageable and underlying AI demand remains robust.
• CrowdStrike: GIR with a update of the view that the latest print was arguably the strongest cyber result
in the group, with revenue +26%, ARR +25% and record net-new ARR +51%, while FY27 net-new ARR
growth guidance was raised to ~34%. The key positive is that Falcon Flex is clearly working, with Flex
ARR +101%, supporting wallet-share consolidation and expansion beyond endpoint into SIEM,
observability and AI security. The longer-term story also looks attractive, with CrowdStrike highlighting
AIDR as a potentially larger product cycle than EDR and the proprietary dataset as a key moat. The debate
is mainly valuation and execution rather than demand: expectations remain high, while bears question
module fatigue and whether CrowdStrike can match Palo Alto's broader platform. Overall, fundamentals
and product momentum look very strong, but the premium valuation leaves less room for disappointment.

• US TRADING: Market bounced Friday with Semis up ~3.5% which felt ‘healthy’ and not just a beta
squeeze. Activity levels were extremely quiet in HT with inbounds very focused on feeling out sentiment
and not much follow through. On the week, LOs finished moderate net sellers (steady drumbeat of semis
supply) while HFs finished net buyers, drive by Friday's buy skew which was predominantly a cover bid
of macro expressions.
• Derivatives: The main story this week was Thursday’s extreme vol repricing. Clients had aggressively
taken off protection into Wednesday’s post-midterm relief rally, leaving the market underhedged heading
into Thursday’s reversal. Our vol panic index surged to 9.57 out of 10 (back in the panic zone of 9.5+).
This move registered as the 3rd largest daily jump ever, which followed Wednesday’s huge decline (4th
largest drop). The combined two-day swing was the largest on record, which shows how quickly the
market went from relief to outright panic.
• ETFs: The sheer trading power of ETFs was on full display on Thursday, as US-listed ETFs experienced
their third largest day of notional volume in our dataset, printing $580 billion worth of volume and
accounted for 33% of the broader tape (28% average). As of Friday morning, we’re observing a
continuation of outsized volumes within the complex, representing 36% of the tape and $ volumes running
+65% vs the 20d average.
