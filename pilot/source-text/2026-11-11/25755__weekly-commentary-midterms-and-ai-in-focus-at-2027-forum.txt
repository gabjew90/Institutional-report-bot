U.S. stocks rallied the day after the elections, sold off sharply on Thursday and 
steadied on Friday. See the chart. The swings illustrate a key conclusion from our 
2027 Outlook Forum: the AI story is moving into a more nuanced phase. In the 
initial phase of the AI transformation, the investment opportunity was about 
pricing the conviction that the buildout needed to happen. Now the focus is 
shifting to assessing the size and speed of the buildout relative to how quickly AI 
is adopted and spreads across different tasks. A material slowdown in 2027, 
though far from certain, could have significant adverse market consequences. 
But it could also ease competition for capital and lower bond yields, while its 
economic fallout could temper policy initiatives. This two-sided uncertainty 
underpinned our move to neutral on U.S. equities and our upgrade of U.S. 
Treasuries last month.

The AI infrastructure boom has been a powerful driver of growth, earnings and market returns. But how long will scarcity 
persist, and where might abundance emerge as constraints ease? These questions ran through our Forum discussions. 
Participants debated how much AI capacity will ultimately be built, whether demand from adoption can justify the scale of 
investment and when opportunities might shift from suppliers to adopters. We heard directly from corporate CFOs about how 
they are deploying AI and assessing its benefits, offering a window into demand that remains difficult to measure. Another 
question was what would happen if capital spending slowed before productivity gains from adoption materialized. And who 
ultimately captures the gains and who gets disrupted – technology providers, businesses adopting AI or their customers?
Competition for capital was another major topic, as the extraordinary financing needs of AI collide with government 
borrowing and investment in energy security and defense. Our portfolio managers debated whether this growing demand for 
funding could crowd out other borrowers, even as AI investment supports economic growth and risk assets. With credit 
markets becoming more selective, participants also discussed whether greater equity issuance could help hyperscalers 
manage leverage and preserve their capacity to borrow as the buildout continues. Another key question was whether stronger 
growth and earnings can continue to offset higher financing costs – and where the pressure might emerge next.
The Forum came at a consequential juncture. After four years of an AI-driven bull market, markets have largely priced in the 
new regime of structurally higher interest rates we’ve talked about for five years. That makes the investment outlook less 
directional and more nuanced. Discussions ranged from shifting global capital flows and changing relationships between 
stocks, bonds and currencies to investment opportunities across Europe, China and emerging markets. Participants also 
explored how evolving market structures could affect market liquidity and volatility. The reliability of traditional portfolio 
hedges and the importance of retaining flexibility were also major topics.
Bottom line: Our 2027 Outlook Forum highlighted the questions facing investors as the supply-driven regime we’ve 
described for five years becomes increasingly priced in. Look out for more in our 2027 Global Outlook next month.

Market backdrop 
U.S. stocks fell sharply last week following the Democratic sweep in the midterm elections, despite a rebound on Friday. For 
the week, the Nasdaq 100 lost 4.7%, leaving it 14% below its October peak, while the S&P 500 dropped 2.4%. U.S. Treasuries 
rallied, with 10-year yields falling 13 basis points over the week to 4.86%. Brent crude fell 17% to $97 a barrel amid hopes for 
an end to the Middle East conflict.

We are neutral. Strong AI fundamentals remain supportive, but policy uncertainty could fuel

We are neutral. We would need to see more business-friendly policy and deeper capital markets

We are neutral. Strong corporate balance sheets and governance reforms remain supportive.

Emerging markets
We are overweight. Strong earnings and cheaper valuations create opportunities. We

China
We are neutral. We see opportunities in physical AI. Cheap, open-source AI could drive adoption, 
but that doesn’t necessarily translate into AI-provider profitability.
Short U.S. Treasuries
We are overweight. Much of the repricing we anticipated has occurred, and expectations for 
further Fed hikes look excessive. We favor the front end for attractive income and a greater 
breakeven buffer.
Long U.S. Treasuries
We are neutral. Higher yields offer attractive income and a bigger cushion against further rate 
increases. We still see room for yields to rise as competition for capital intensifies.

Global inflation-linked 
bonds
We are neutral. We see inflation settling above pre-pandemic levels, but markets may not price 
this in the near term as economic growth could slow.
Euro area govt bonds
We are neutral on short- and medium-term bonds. Current ECB rate pricing looks fairly valued 
given the balance of risks. Higher energy prices could push rates higher. We prefer to deploy 
risk elsewhere. 
UK gilts
We are neutral. We expect periods of elevated volatility given political uncertainty, longer-term 
bonds making up a larger market share, and buyers becoming more price-sensitive.
Japanese govt bonds
We are underweight. The substantial repricing in yields has improved the risk-reward, but 
further rate hikes and heavy issuance could push yields higher.
China govt bonds
We are neutral. China bonds offer stability and diversification but developed market yields are 
higher. A shift in investor sentiment toward equities limits upside. 
U.S. agency MBS
We are neutral and lean into the stable income MBS provide despite tight spreads.
Short-term IG credit
We are neutral. Spreads are tight due to corporate strength, but attractive overall yields help 
offset the risk of increased issuance.
Long-term IG credit
We are underweight. We prefer short-term bonds less exposed to interest rate risk over long-
term bonds. But attractive overall yields help offset the risk of increased issuance.
Global high yield
We are neutral. High yield offers attractive income. We prefer higher-rated U.S. and European 
high yield over investment grade and see dispersion of returns increasing. 
Asia credit
We are neutral. Overall yields are attractive and fundamentals are solid, but spreads are tight. 
Emerging hard currency
We are neutral. Fundamentals have improved, but we see a more attractive risk-reward profile in 
EM local debt.
Emerging local currency
We are overweight. We like the yield relative to its volatility and improving fundamentals.
