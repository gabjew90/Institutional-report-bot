2) US ECONOMICS (MERICLE) – Fed Call Change & CPI Review
Fed: We continue to expect a second 25bp rate hike in December and are adding a third 25bp hike to our
forecast in March 2027. A third hike could come earlier in January, but we think March is a bit more likely
because we expect the November and December inflation reports to be softer but then expect larger
start-of-the-year price increases amidst ongoing cost pressures to boost inflation in January. We remain
confident that inflation will be meaningfully lower by the end of 2027 as the effects of tariffs, the energy
shock, and AI price pressures diminish. But we suspect that the recent inflation prints will lead most
FOMC participants to signal a third hike in the December dot plot and that a firmer January inflation report
would make delivering it the path of least resistance even if the energy shock appears over by then. We
have left our forecast for the eventual terminal fed funds rate unchanged at 3.25-3.5%, but we now expect
rate cuts to begin one quarter later in December 2027 (vs. September 2027 previously), followed by
additional cuts in March, June, and September 2028. Full piece here.
CPI: Core CPI increased by 0.30% in October, above expectations. The upside surprise was driven by
firmer-than-expected readings in a broad set of categories. As in September, the shelter component was
firm. Medical services were also firm despite a decline in health insurance prices following the BLS'

incorporation of updated source data on insurance premiums. Motor vehicle insurance prices rebounded
after running softer this year. Based on details in the CPI report, we estimate that the core PCE price
index rose 0.35% in October (vs. our expectation of 0.23% before the CPI release), corresponding to a
year-over-year rate of +3.30%. Additionally, we expect that the headline PCE price index increased 0.36%
in October, and increased 3.87% from a year earlier. Full piece here.

3) BRL Update
LATAM ECONOMICS (RAMOS) on COPOM Minutes: The Copom minutes were broadly neutral with a
dovish tilt given that the central bank attributes the deterioration of the projected inflation path first and
foremost to the direct first-round impact of supply shocks (which the Copom is not planning to react to),
while the risk of second-round effects should be lower given the outlook for weak below-trend growth and
already “significantly” tight monetary stance. This suggests a high bar for near-term rate hikes. We expect
the Copom to remain on hold until at least 2Q27 given the visible deterioration of the inflation outlook and
see room for only limited cuts in 2027 although considerable uncertainty over the growth and policy
outlook (fiscal in particular) and the propagation of several ongoing supply shocks, makes the scope for
easing, if any at all, difficult to assess. Full piece here.
EM FX STRATEGY (ALVES): USDBRL back at the post-election highs with a stronger USD, lower oil,
lack of clarity on fiscal measures, and dovish tilt in BCB communication all weighing on BRL. The key risk
that we have been flagging is that high rates become increasingly unmanageable for the economy and
that we could be reaching the limit of how much tight monetary policy can do for BRL without a clearer
fiscal anchor. The BCB's messaging has removed the tail scenario of continued rate cuts here, but given
that the market is now pricing hikes, an on-hold stance would not necessarily be supportive for BRL over
the near-term. Overnight, there have been some articles on a potential fiscal adjustment being discussed
that could be introduced in a complementary bill. The measures include the exclusion of certain revenues

from the calculation of spending growth in order to limit the extent of spending growth and meet the fiscal
targets in 2027. These plans fall short of an actual spending cut, but may be enough to support BRL today
after yesterday's selloff.
BRL FX TRADING (ISSA) from the Close: USDBRL broke the 5.53 level it was holding and went all the
way to test the YTD highs, and even break the 5.60 level. At the open it seems like market did not take
well a more dovish tone on COPOM minutes, quickly broke all the levels higher trading at 5.55 before
CPI. Once the stronger number came in Armageddon came upon BRL, currency underperformed peers
with USD buying from CTA names bringing momentum towards USDBRL. Once we approached 5.60s we
saw some USD supply from offshore corporate accounts + cash trading offered meaning we might be
seeing some inflows on that front as well. Around 5.60 seems to be an OK sell especially if you add BRL
on a RV, of course now momentum on USDBRL is higher with CTA names being good USD buyers, the
tactical game here is playing with USD supply from corporates + the resistance around 5.61.
BRL RATES TRADING (CARRER) from the Close: DIs closed the day sideways, with a touch positive
bias in the front-end of the curve. DIs started the day trading with a positive bias following the more
dovish tone from COPOM minutes. However, the move was short lived with the curve widening ~20bps
from the day lows following the external risk-off sentiment following the US CPI data release, with core
rates widening and USD bid across the board. Despite of the risk-off, DI held relatively well close to 14.70
region on Jan29s, and during afternoon hours the curve trimmed the day losses following headlines
mentioning that Fontelles would take FinMin job if Lula invites him.

4) Latest on El Nino and TTF
See below for takeaways from senior thought leaders on the relevance of El Nino and Winter forecasts for
TTF. More details available in Connecting you to GS.
Sam Dart (Co-Head of Global Commodities Research): The so-called Super El Nino is here –
according to the latest NOAA estimates – but this does not guarantee a warm winter for Europe. In fact,
the ECMWF long-range models still call for a pretty average winter (Nov-Mar) across NW Europe (see
Chart 1). Between now and the end of the year we expect TTF to respond primarily to the cross of: 1) Re-
escalation/de-escalation headlines; and 2) Shifts in the short-run weather models. On our side, we like to
track actual Mideast LNG supply closely as well (which has averaged >40% of pre-war levels over the
past week). Our 74 EUR/MWh 1Q27 TTF forecast embeds our 44 EUR estimated fair value for TTF,
consistent with our assumption that Mideast supply will continue to improve, added of a 26/4 EUR
supply/weather risk premium.
Ben Readman (Head of European Nat Gas Trading): Since the start of last week, we have seen a
variety of articles suggesting negotiations are happening about opening Hormuz (changes in stance on
what the Iranians would give up on nukes and the potential release of frozen assets by the US). On top of
this, weather is playing a small part. The Super El Nino is doing its thing in the US but hasn’t affected the

EU so much, and there’s another acronym that has crept up here – the SSW (sudden stratospheric
warming event). Normally this doesn’t come out of the wood-work until much later in the season – this is
the earliest one we have seen. This could bring cold weather to the EU and Asia in early / mid Dec which
has caught the market’s focus, but we will have to see what actually happens. On top of this there have
been mentions of opening up Russian gas flows to Europe again. Given all this uncertainty there has
been an unwinding of risk position, which has led to gas prices selling off to find a new price-level that
buyers are happy to take the gas being sold in to the market.

5) Equities Update
Rich Privorotsky, Head of EMEA Flow Intermediation and European One Delta Trading, out with his latest
views. Key takeaways below; full piece here.
Equity Resilience: SPX barely fell yesterday and futures are recovering this morning. AI sentiment
appears to be improving, supporting the core engines of the market. We’ve spent a lot of time debating
just how low nets have fallen, how much gross has been cut and how heavily hedged investors have
become. Even with a hot CPI print, the market struggled to sustain the volatility premium. VIX briefly

traded into the 20s but finished below 20. That’s quite telling… positioning may now be doing more to
support the tape than the macro is doing to undermine it.
Neocloud Commentary Overnight: Broadly supportive, with data centre capacity constraints remaining
the primary operational bottleneck. Compute utilization across major providers continues to track at
elevated levels. Markets focus remains concentrated on hardware delivery and developer coding
workflows, while broader edge and enterprise autonomous integrations remain in earlier stages of
deployment.
Risk: Rates are a headwind, but the market has built some cushion from prior rally so rates aren’t yet
flashing red for equities… yields need another leg higher before historically they become a more material
problem for equities (it's the rate of change that matters). Cyclicality relative to the market is also hanging
in. Credit still the one to watch with high yield spreads chopping sideways.  There’s also the reflexivity
with energy… higher oil, stickier inflation, a more hawkish Fed. The question is whether the Fed is
reacting to energy effects already in the rearview mirror or something more persistent. Gross has been
cut, nets are historically low and the appetite to add risk has diminished substantially. That’s the tailwind
for the tape. Volatility remains elevated relative to the price action and, absent fresh negative information,
that premium should continue to decay. Positioning is a tailwind, the Fed is a headwind. US Veterans Day
today… bond market closed, equities open. Watching the neoclouds and next move in energy.
