Report: Weekly Wrap | Multi-Strat Alpha & HF Returns Likely to Recover, but 
Uncertainty Persists 
  
Summary: Looking back at what we’ve seen over the past 2 weeks, it seems like the 
risks outweighed the tailwinds that we highlighted from our prior note “Weekly 
Wrap | Painful Under the Surface, But Not a Liquidation” (Oct 23, 2026).  
Looking back, the risks were: “Eq L/S alpha is still deteriorating and YTD returns are 
declining late in the year, CTAs are neutral so could still sell if markets don’t 
rebound…and Retail demand is soft.”   
  
Fast forward to this week and the question is naturally…have we seen 
capitulation?  Will the market or HF alpha rebound?  The market does not equal 
HF alpha, and even among HFs there could be a varied story going forward.  There’s 
potential for both to rebound in the near-term (and history would suggest a rebound 
is more likely than not), but the path forward is still murky.  We covered some of the 
key points in today’s earlier note, while the below is meant to give broader context 

for what might happen going forward…though upcoming market catalysts are still 
likely to have a big impact. 
  
Key Points: 
1. Multi-Strat pain may be easing, though de-grossing has continued. After 
several weeks of negative long-short spreads and the largest 20-day Multi-
Strat de-grossing event since Covid, L-S spreads improved over the past 
week: Tuesday and Thursday were positive, leaving 5-day alpha close to flat 
despite a weak Wednesday. Historically, after Multi-Strat 20-day gross 
flows first fell below the 5th percentile—as they did on Oct. 27—alpha 
was higher over the following 20 days in 9 of 10 cases. Gross flows 
typically began to recover after about 10 days, while alpha stabilized and then 
improved. The main exception was the post-Covid episode. 
2. Broader HF de-grossing may be slowing, but the path is uncertain. From 
a seasonal perspective, US de-grossing is now below its average, and gross 
flows have tended to move sideways from here. The pattern in past midterm 
election years most closely resembles 2022, when de-grossing 
continued into mid-month. Gross flows eventually recovered in December in 
both years, although that recovery coincided with a market sell-off and 
included substantial short additions. 
3. HF returns have often bounced after similar short-term drawdowns—but 
market conditions matter. In the past five years, HFs rebounded over the 
following 5 and 10 days in all eight comparable events in which 5- and 
20-day net returns fell below -2z. Earlier examples, including late February 
2020 and late October 2018, were followed by further losses. A rebound in 
absolute HF returns may depend partly on the market and there’s precedence 
for a rebound post similar extreme 1d events near ATHs. 
4. Selling has not yet signaled broad capitulation; regional flows diverge as 
EMEA shorts covered. The broad net selling needed to drive US TPM to a 

very negative four-week change has not yet appeared. HF flows have instead 
differed by region: EMEA and China/HK saw net buying, while North 
America and Japan saw net selling, with North America sharply de-grossed. 
In Europe, short covering reached an extreme 3z level, last seen in late 
2022. 
5. Exposure has reset in momentum and AI-related themes. HF net leverage 
fell about 4% over the past week, bringing it near 12-month lows; Eq L/S net 
leverage is also at a three-year low, while All Strategy net leverage remains 
higher, around the 40th percentile on a three-/five-year basis. Global 
momentum exposure is near three-year lows and close to neutral; the 
6m z-score of positioning and price is also back near its summer lows, 
suggesting much of the move lower may have occurred for now. APAC 
momentum exposure has also fallen quickly from more extreme levels. In US 
themes, Semis remained the most net-sold and positioning is back near 
early-2026 levels. Software flows have been more neutral recently while 
positioning continues to recover. Broader AI-cyclical exposure has fallen 
quickly, while Defensives ex-Healthcare have begun to rebound.
