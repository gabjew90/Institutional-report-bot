Inflation was back on the agenda over the last 24 hours, as a strong US CPI print and hawkish Fed comments 
drove a major selloff for US Treasuries. In fact, the 2yr Treasury yield (+17.6bps) saw its biggest daily jump 
since April 2025, surging to 4.74%, and markets even priced a modest chance of a 50bp Fed hike in 
December. So it was a very rough day for rates, and that in turn put renewed pressure on equities, with the 
S&P 500 (-0.38%) pulling back as investors priced in faster hikes. To be fair, it wasn’t all bad news, and 
growing hopes for a resolution in the Middle East pushed Brent crude oil (-1.17%) to its lowest since 
August, closing at $91.49/bbl. Moreover, positive earnings from CoreWeave have helped equities 
recover, with S&P 500 futures up +0.65% this morning. But even so, the bigger concern remained that 
inflation was becoming more persistent and couldn’t just be blamed on a few specific categories. 
That US CPI print drove the biggest moves of the day, with the monthly core CPI print for October above 
consensus at +0.30% (vs. +0.2% expected). Moreover, it was the third consecutive month with core CPI at 
+0.3%, and if you take those three months together, the annualised pace for core CPI is running at +3.75%. 
That’s the fastest pace on that measure since April 2024, so it made it increasingly difficult to dismiss 
this as a blip. Meanwhile, the headline CPI numbers were faster still, with monthly headline CPI at 
+0.35% (vs. +0.3% expected), with the 3-month annualised pace at +5.72%. Bear in mind there aren’t year-
on-year numbers in this report, because last year’s CPI print didn’t happen because of a government 
shutdown. But overall, there was little respite in the release, with broad-based gains across the consumer 
basket. 
The CPI release got more attention than usual, partly because of comments from Fed Governor Waller a few 
hours beforehand. He was speaking in London, where he said that “if the October CPI inflation data out 
later today are similar to the past two monthly readings – with core CPI inflation around 0.3 percent or 
even higher”, then he thought “a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the 
December meeting”. Indeed, he also said that in those circumstances, a 25bp hike “would put our 

credibility at risk”. So when the core CPI print was confirmed at 0.3%, that led to mounting speculation about 
a larger rate hike. 
That impact was clear from market pricing, with the amount of hikes for a December up from 21.0bps on 
Monday to 27.7bps by last night’s close, and 28.5bps this morning. So in other words, a 25bp hike is fully 
priced in, with futures also pricing in a 14% chance of a larger 50bp move. But it wasn’t just December 
that was affected, with markets pricing in a more hawkish profile further out the curve as well. Indeed, the 
rate priced in for the September 2027 meeting was up +22.3bps yesterday, so investors had priced in 
almost a full additional rate hike to next year’s path. Meanwhile, DB’s own US economists have updated 
their Fed view, adding a rate hike in January to their profile. So they now expect three more 25bp hikes at 
the next three meetings (Dec, Jan and March), and you can read their latest update here. 
For markets, the clearest impact could be seen in US Treasuries, with yields surging across the curve. In fact, 
the 2yr Treasury yield (+17.6bps) saw its biggest daily jump since April 2025, the day that Trump paused 
his Liberation Day tariffs. And those moves extended to longer maturities, with the 10yr yield (+10.8bps) 
closing back at 4.99%, its highest level in nearly two weeks. Meanwhile, Fed speakers later in the session 
added to that momentum. For instance, Dallas Fed President Logan said in a speech that “the FOMC 
needs to implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases”, and Kansas City Fed President 
Schmid said that the current policy rate was “fairly accommodative”. Moreover, Chicago Fed President 
Goolsbee described the print as “borderline disturbing”. 
With markets pricing in a more hawkish Fed, that also drove a fresh selloff in US equities, though this did 
ease a bit as the session went on. Tech stocks underperformed, with a -5.47% drop in Nvidia leading the 
Nasdaq (-0.74%) and the Mag-7 (-0.83%) to larger declines than the S&P 500 (-0.38%). The broader equity 
mood was a bit better, however, with almost half of the S&P 500’s constituents higher on the day, and the 
equal-weighted S&P 500 only down -0.02%. After the close, we then heard from CoreWeave, which 
delivered a Q3 revenue beat ($3.66bn vs $3.56bn est.) and upgraded its full-year revenue guidance 
($13.0-13.3bn vs $12.9bn est.). So that meant its shares rose by over +7% in after-hours trading, easing 
some of the doubt around the AI cycle. And in turn, that’s boosted optimism more broadly, with Nasdaq 100 
futures up +1.40% this morning, and those on the S&P 500 up +0.65%. 
As all that was happening, oil prices continued to fall, as hopes continued to rise for some kind of diplomatic 
resolution in the US-Iran conflict. There wasn’t a single headline driving that, but several comments have 
pointed away from further strikes, with Trump saying on Monday that “We’re just keeping it the status 
quo, and we’re doing that really at the behest of countries that are their neighbors”. So with fears of an 
escalation receding, that helped Brent crude oil (-1.17%) fall to $91.49/bbl by the close, its lowest since 
August. And longer-dated futures also moved lower, with the 12-month Brent future (-1.68%) at a two-
month low of $77.64/bbl. 
Overnight in Asia, that renewed optimism around the AI trade has boosted equities across the region, 
with the KOSPI (+2.27%) and the Nikkei (+1.33%) posting strong gains. Moreover, the latest data showed 
South Korea’s unemployment rate unexpectedly falling to a 3-year low of 2.5% in October (vs. 2.6% 
expected). However, there’s been a weaker performance elsewhere in Asia, with the CSI 300 (+0.02%) and 
the Shanghai Comp (-0.02%) seeing little movement, whilst the Hang Seng is down -0.83%. Otherwise, the 
hawkish repricing after the US CPI print has led to a fresh bond selloff in numerous countries. For 

instance, Japan’s 10yr yields are up +10.3bps this morning to 2.78%, while 10yr yields in Australia 
(+5.6bps) and New Zealand (+6.8bps) are also higher. 
Earlier in Europe, there was a major selloff for sovereign bonds too, although not quite as big as in the US. For 
instance, yields on 10yr bunds (+5.5bps), OATs (+7.0bps) and BTPs (+7.4bps) all saw a sharp increase. 
And similarly to the US, investors priced in a more hawkish ECB path for the months ahead as well. For 
instance, market pricing for a December hike rose from 83% on Monday to 92% by the close. And looking 
further out, the number of hikes priced in by the June 2027 meeting rose +10.9bps on the day to 58bps. 
For equities, there was a much more resilient performance in Europe, although that partly reflected a 
catchup to the rally late in the US session the previous day. So the STOXX 600 (+0.27%) moved higher, 
alongside the DAX (+0.31%) and the CAC 40 (+0.55%). 
Looking at the day ahead now, it’ll be a quieter one, with US bond markets closed for Veterans Day. Central 
bank speakers will include ECB President Lagarde, and the ECB’s Cipollone, Kazaks and Nagel. Otherwise, 
data releases include Italian industrial production for September.
