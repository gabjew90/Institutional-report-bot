GWIS: datacentres - catalysts, context and next week's
big summit in Texas
Clearly post the US mid-terms, the debate around the construction of US datacenters is one of the most pivotal issues
for APAC equities. The aggregate GIR view is that the market is too concerned. There will be some delays but this will
merely extend the cycle.
 
I decided to compile some GIR publications, largely from the US, and relevant press articles below. One of my team
members in New York is also gathering some investors (please reply for detail) for an event at next week’s TomorrowX
AI summit in Austin, Texas (17-18 November), which I am sure will get press coverage as it is so well timed.
Datacentres is a key topic, inevitably, and the summit will host an extraordinary line-up including Jensen Huang,
Michael Dell, Lisa Su, Stan Druckenmiller, Marc Rowan, Dina Powell McCormick, David Sacks, Brannin McBee, Scott
DeBoer and Rene Haas.
 
Dates to watch:
12 Nov-20 Dec US Senate: Post-midterm consideration of the Ratepayer Protection Act and transmission
permitting reform. 
Why Catalytic: Lame-duck passage starts a mandatory 12-month statutory clock for utilities to implement dedicated
high-load tariff structures. This shifts incremental generation, transmission, and substation capital expenditure directly
onto hyperscalers, preventing grid upgrade costs from being socialized to residential ratepayer
 
31 December: New York Senate: Decision deadline on the Responsible Data Center Development Act
Why Catalytic: Determines whether Executive Order 62 (a temporary pause on discretionary permits for facilities is
codified into permanent statutory law. Enactment would formalize a mandatory statewide moratorium (with proposed
thresholds as low as 20 MW) pending comprehensive environmental and grid impact studies, freezing greenfield
development pipelines across the state.
 
Q1/27: ERCOT / Texas PUC*: Finalization of ERCOT’s Large Flexible Load Grid Reliability Audit.
Why Catalytic: Dictates the resumption and clearance of The Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) stalled
"Batch Zero" interconnection study queue. It establishes binding energization timelines, mandatory curtailment
protocols, and firm on-site backup generation obligations for data centers exceeding 75 MW under Texas Senate Bill 6
rules, defining the commercial and operational viability of greenfield campuses in Texas.
*Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT or PUC)
 
 

Datacentre projects already under construction support strong spending growth through 2027 (86 GW) and
underpin just above half of our 2028 spending forecasts
• Visibility becomes more limited beyond 2028, with the 2029-2030 outlook increasingly dependent on announced
projects converting to construction.
• The states where Democrats are likely to further restrict data center development account for roughly 45% of
planned US data center capacity, with Texas alone accounting for roughly 25%.
• We see a potential outcome as smoothing spending and extending the duration of the cycle, rather than impacting
the TAM
 
Current project timelines imply ~55 GW of capacity energizing in 2028 (total 86 GW is under construction now)
High probability announcements add 171 GW
Medium/low probability announcements add 293 GW

Texas:
Gina Hinojosa (D) won the gubernatorial race in Texas against Governor Greg Abbott (R).
Greg Abbott invited data centers into Texas when he signed into law House Bill 2712 in 2015 to give hyperscale data
centers 20-year tax breaks.
Hinojosa suggested a moratorium until the legislature can put additional protections in place in 2027
• Operators would be legally required to prove that their facilities will not raise electricity or water rates for residential
consumers
• Requiring mega-scale facilities to bring dedicated incremental generation capacity (behind-the-meter or contracted
generation) to the grid, rather than merely drawing from existing reserve margins.
• Establishing statutory provisions that make commercial water delivery contracts legally unenforceable if a project
threatens or depletes a local community’s drinking water or agricultural aquifer supply.
• Codifying statewide setbacks, visual screening, and strict acoustic limits to protect neighboring properties from
continuous cooling and backup generator hum.
• Requiring local community referenda or mandatory public hearings
• Mandating closed-loop, air-cooled, or other low-water cooling technologies
