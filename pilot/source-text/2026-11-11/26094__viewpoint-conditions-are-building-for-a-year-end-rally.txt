IN BRIEF
 • Conditions for a potential year-end rally are building, in our view. Multiple sources 
of uncertainty have converged recently, leading to sharp equity market volatility 
and rotation. Heading into the end of the year, we believe investor attention 
could shift from what could go wrong to what is going right, with solid economic 
growth, record margins and rising productivity back in focus. 
 • We remain constructive on Equities as the positive fundamental backdrop 
remains intact. The sharp downdrafts in Financials, parts of Industrials, 
higher-quality semiconductors within Technology and power-generation 
companies appear overdone, in our view. We would use periods of weakness as 
potential opportunities to rebalance portfolios while continuing to emphasize 
diversification across and within Equities, particularly as market rotation creates 
new pockets of market leadership.
 • Higher yields continue to create attractive potential income opportunities across 
Fixed Income. With 10-year U.S. Treasury nominal yields near 5% and real yields 
above 2.5%, still near multi-year highs, all-in yields are compelling on both a 
nominal and inflation-adjusted basis. We favor moving out of cash and the front 
end of the curve to extend duration toward strategic targets.

We believe the conditions for a year-end rally in Equities are just beginning. The below-
the-surface correction playing out over the last few months in which a majority of 
stocks in the S&P 500 Index fell about 15% to 20% from their peaks became exhausted 
recently, in our view. This recycling of capital has been buckling from too many concerns 
all hitting at once, which include expectations that the Federal Reserve (Fed) would be 
raising interest rates rather than cutting rates (even with the pause in October), the fact 
that oil and diesel prices climbed back toward 2026 highs, the midterm elections coming 
into focus and, most importantly, the new concerns gathering momentum about capital 
availability to drive the next phase of the Artificial Intelligence (AI) buildout. Investors 
have been focusing more on what could go wrong than on what is going right. Therefore, 
corporate earnings season has been more of a sideshow so far than the main stage act. 
Investor sentiment has shifted to “risk-off” in the last two months, which has shifted the 
market’s valuation closer to 18X forward earnings estimates than the 22X at the beginning 
of the year. We believe we have both a time and positioning rotation at work. In other 
words, positioning in the AI buildout beneficiaries became too excessive at the same time 
investors were unable to digest too many concerns all at once. This dynamic can create 
sharp episodes of volatility beneath the indexes in which specific targeted areas, such as 
semiconductors, data center builders and AI architects, overwhelming the broader market.

Given our observations across the capital markets and our macro and asset class views 
overall, we remain overweight Equities. We believe the sharp downdraft in Financials,

3 of 13 November 2026 – CIO Viewpoint
CIO INVESTMENT DASHBOARD AS OF NOVEMBER 3, 2026
In our view, recent market rotation is consistent with a transition from early- to midcycle, where broader participation and greater 
dispersion could increasingly shape market returns. Even amid recent volatility, we believe the bull market remains supported by a resilient 
economy, broadening earnings growth and strong business investment. We continue to see the potential for a more durable expansion 
powered by accelerating productivity, infrastructure investment, energy transformation, and global shifts in defense and technology. 
Long-term investors should remain fully invested and diversified while using periods of volatility as potential opportunities to rebalance 
portfolios and put capital to work.
Current readings on the key drivers of Equities for investors to consider, with arrows representing the recent trend:
Earnings
Our U.S. Equity overweight remains supported by a strong earnings outlook. S&P 500 earnings estimates have 
continued to move higher as Q3 reporting season progresses, with double-digit earnings growth expected in 2026 
and 2027. The BofA Global Earnings Revision Ratio moderated to 1.01 in October but remained above the key 1.00 
threshold, where earnings upgrades outnumber downgrades. Notably, Technology earnings revisions have improved 
despite recent weakness in AI-related stocks.
Valuations
U.S. equity market valuations have compressed from premium levels a year ago but remain elevated relative to 
long-term averages. Relative discounts in areas like Value and Emerging Markets (EM) continue to support potential 
opportunities for diversification. 
U.S. Macro
The U.S. economy remains resilient, with the advance estimate of Q3 real gross domestic product (GDP) showing 
3.4% annualized growth, supported by strength in business investment, consumer spending and government 
spending. Continued AI-related capital spending, positive wealth effects and stable job growth should continue to 
help support economic activity, even as higher energy prices and modest fiscal drag present headwinds. BofA Global 
Research forecasts real GDP growth of 2.4% in 2026 and 2.3% in 2027.
Global Growth
Global growth remains solid but uneven across regions. The AI investment cycle continues to provide an important 
tailwind, with the AI-related export boom supporting growth across Asia, particularly in Taiwan and Korea. China has 
also benefited from stronger exports, though domestic spillovers remain more limited amid structural and cyclical 
headwinds. Meanwhile, the European growth outlook remains softer as higher rates, tighter financial conditions and 
energy prices weigh on activity. BofA Global Research expects global real GDP growth of 3.3% in 2026 and 3.4% in 2027. 
U.S. Monetary 
Policy / Inflation
The Federal Reserve held its benchmark policy rate at 3.75% to 4.00% in October. Recent data suggests the labor 
market remains balanced, while inflation continues to run above the Fed’s 2% target. Price pressures, however, could 
moderate if the U.S. and Iran reach a diplomatic agreement and energy prices move lower. BofA Global Research 
maintains its forecasts for two additional 25 basis points (bps) hikes but now expects them to arrive at a quarterly 
pace in December 2026 and March 2027. Given the economy’s continued resilience, some additional tightening 
should remain manageable.
Fiscal Policy
Fiscal policy remains supportive of corporate earnings and business investment, with tax incentives encouraging 
capital spending, and tariff refunds providing additional support for companies. While elevated defense spending 
and rising interest costs keep U.S. government debt levels in focus, healthy consumer and corporate balance sheets 
alongside resilient nominal growth should help keep debt manageable for now.
Corporate  
Credit
Credit spreads widened in October to their widest levels of the year but remain historically tight, reflecting continued 
economic resilience and healthy corporate fundamentals. While elevated issuance could create near-term technical 
pressure on spreads, credit conditions remain supportive of earnings and the broader Equity market backdrop. 
Yield Curve
U.S. Treasury yields moved sharply higher in the first half of October, with the 10-year yield reaching its highest 
level since 2002 amid strong growth, hawkish Fed rhetoric and elevated AI-related debt issuance. Yields reversed in 
the second half of the month after the Fed held rates unchanged and weakness in AI-related stocks revived relative 
stability demand. The yield curve remains positively sloped but flattened during the month, suggesting the market 
is not overly concerned that the Fed is falling behind the curve. Nominal and real yields remain attractive from an 
income perspective.
Technical 
Indicators
Technicals weakened in late October as the S&P 500 fell below its 50- and 100-day moving averages amid a sharp 
drawdown in mega-cap AI Technology, though its cushion above its 200-day moving average suggests the longer-
term uptrend remains intact. Market breadth improved as capital rotated into other parts of the market rather than 
retreating broadly, with the percent of S&P 500 constituents above their 200-day moving average improving to 
66% from 43% at the end of September. The S&P 500 equal-weight also outperformed its market-cap counterpart 
in October by the widest monthly margin since 2009. The sharp shift in leadership reinforces the importance 
of diversification.
Investor 
Sentiment
Sentiment indicators remain mixed. October American Association of Individual Investors (AAII) survey results showed 
increased retail investor caution, with bears outnumbering bulls, though bearish sentiment remains below extreme 
pessimistic levels. The BofA Bull & Bear Indicator remains neutral at 7.1, below its 8.0 contrarian sell threshold, while 
BofA Global Fund Manager Survey cash levels remain in contrarian sell territory at 3.9%, below the 4.0% threshold.

EQUITIES 
We are overweight Equities. While crosscurrents in the economic landscape and near-
term geopolitical risks remain, positive global growth, stronger-than-expected earnings 
and increased innovation keep us constructive on global Equities. We maintain an Equity 
overweight relative to our strategic targets. 
We are overweight U.S. Equities. The U.S. remains our preferred Equity region, as it 
remains among the strongest and most innovative economies in the world and earnings 
growth continues to beat even the most bullish estimates. The S&P 500 is on track 
for an eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit year-over-year (YoY) EPS growth for 
Q3. Earnings strength continues to broaden, with all 11 S&P 500 sectors expected to 
see earnings growth accelerate for the full year 2026, according to FactSet. Index level 
valuations declined at the start of the U.S./Iran conflict, and, after partially recovering in 
the spring, have grinded slightly lower over the past couple of months. 
Diversification across and within Equities has already proven to be valuable this year, 
especially in recent months as equity market rotations are driving new pockets of 
market leadership. Large-caps continue to exhibit strong fundamentals, strong earnings 
growth and the ability to generate substantial free cash flow (FCF). The recent increase 
in interest rates pared some of Small-caps’ year-to-date (YTD) gains, but Small-caps 
are still well positioned for growth. Small- and Mid-caps are benefiting from a more 
constructive earnings outlook, an uptick in domestic manufacturing and increased capital 
markets activity. We emphasize the importance of incorporating both Large- and Small-
caps into strategic portfolios. 
We believe secular tailwinds related to innovation will continue to support Growth over 
the long term, but short-term volatility is typical of midcycle periods. However, we 
continue to emphasize the importance of avoiding overexposure to any one area of the 
market. Value-oriented Equities continue to trade at a relative valuation discount and 
may offer attractive defensive characteristics against a backdrop of elevated uncertainty. 
We maintain a barbell approach between Large-cap Value and Growth in portfolios as 
market dynamics evolve. 
From a sector perspective, we continue to propose diversification across Equity sectors 
given elevated volatility and uncertainty. As market leadership broadens and capital 
expenditures (capex) trends are debated, we remain overweight Industrials, Financials 
and Healthcare while remaining more cautious on Communication Services, Real Estate 
and slower growth trends in Consumer Staples despite its defensive characteristics. 
Industrials are overweight as capex budgets continue to grow, 100% bonus depreciation 
has been enacted again, strong order backlogs and AI-oriented infrastructure plans 
are not slowing despite concerns about financing the AI capex plans. The overall 
outlook for Financials remains positive, especially for banks with tailwinds from 
deregulation, loan growth, net interest income (NII) growth, capital markets activity and 
reasonable valuations. 
We are neutral Information Technology (IT), Energy, Consumer Discretionary and 
Materials. IT has positive longer-term thematic exposure and earnings growth, but 
there are near-term concerns on capex funding. The outlook for the Energy sector is 
more muted given very strong performance YTD and ongoing uncertainty about the 
Middle East conflict, which has now entered its ninth month. We are neutral Materials 
on improved pricing and demand for commodities in general. We have underweight 
exposure to Utilities, Communication Services, Real Estate (RE) and Consumer Staples 
on weaker growth expectations in those sectors. The consumer backdrop remains mixed 
amid higher energy costs and fading tax refund benefits. Utilities provide the potential 
for multiyear earnings growth and are fairly valued but face local pressures and rising 
interest rates.

We are slightly overweight Emerging Market Equities. We continue to view the 
Asia-Pacific as a major beneficiary of expected growth in IT-related capital spending and 
expanding AI adoption, with the region remaining heavily tilted toward IT hardware in Korea 
and Taiwan, in addition to more diversified exposure in China. Strong balance of payments 
positions also leave the region more resilient to external shocks. Latin America remains 
exposed to longer-term growth expected in demand for key materials used in technology 
and clean energy investment, in addition to fiscal uncertainty in Brazil. And smaller markets 
in Central and Eastern Europe should benefit from increased European Union (EU) fiscal 
outlays, including higher defense and infrastructure spending. The structural rise in EM 
consumer spending remains a big reason why we believe investors should consider 
maintaining a strategic allocation to EM Equities as appropriate, and convergence with 
developed economies should support GDP growth and corporate earnings over the longer 
term. We favor active1 management when investing in EM, as fundamentals differ across 
countries based on fiscal capacity, credit quality, corporate governance and other factors.
We are slightly underweight International Developed Equities. European markets 
remain among the least exposed globally to technology-related market segments and are 
therefore likely to experience limited gains on a relative basis from growth in AI spending, 
while investor concerns remain over the fiscal outlook in France. Manufacturing-led EU 
economies also remain at risk from growing competition from China in key industries such 
as auto manufacturing and volatility in industrial energy costs but should benefit from higher 
fiscal outlays in defense and infrastructure. Corporate sector reforms remain a fundamental 
tailwind for Japan, with additional support from fiscal expansion and ongoing exchange rate 
competitiveness despite official currency market intervention. While we are less constructive 
on International Developed Equities, we believe long-term investors should maintain some 
strategic exposure, as appropriate, given that they trade at a discount relative to U.S. 
Equities, contain more of a balance between Value and Growth sectors, can offer attractive 
dividend yields and provide diversification from mega-cap Technology stocks.

ALTERNATIVE INVESTMENTS 
CIO’s Key Alternative Investment Principles
With their diverse strategies and historical progress, Alternative Investments (Alts) can potentially 
enhance portfolio resilience and long-term performance for qualified investors. However, unlike 
traditional assets, allocating to Alts is a long-term process constrained by liquidity. They should 
be viewed primarily as strategic holdings, with tactical opportunities playing only a secondary, 
incremental role for new investment.
 • Think long-term: Illiquidity demands a multi-year horizon.
 • Invest consistently—even in downturns: Pulling back during volatility can erode benefits and 
lead to underallocation.
 • Diversify: Spread across strategies, managers and vintages.
 • Choose high-conviction managers: Performance varies widely; manager selection matters.

Hedge Strategies: Aggregate performance declined modestly in September (-1.0%) and 
Q3 2026 (-0.6%) but remained positive YTD (+6.8%).2 Performance was mixed across 
sectors in both periods, though macro and market neutral Equity Hedge (EH) strategies 
generally offset losses in other areas. Fundamental EH manager performance dispersion 
was notably pronounced. 
October’s market volatility brought repositioning and intramonth performance estimates 
sharply into focus. Equity market swings increased whipsaw risk among EH managers 
that generally assumed a more defensive posture by adding shorts and later cutting 
longs. Technology, Media and Telecommunications and Asia-focused managers look 
to be particularly exposed to the AI unwinds in October, giving back meaningful 
gains from earlier in the year. Special situations and relative value credit strategies 
contended with basis risk as lower-quality (i.e., CCC-rated bonds) credit continued to 
underperform higher-quality counterparts, especially in Europe where CCCs are on track 
to underperform BB-rated bonds by the widest margin since March 2020. 
On a brighter note, hedged Event Driven strategies (e.g., merger arbitrage) generally held 
in better than the broader market.3 We continue to find a combination of EH and Macro 
attractive as a balance of Growth-oriented, diversifying and market neutral orientations. 
Private Equity: Coming into the year, expectations for lower rates supported hopes 
of a broader recovery in Private Equity (PE) activity. Instead, persistent inflation and a 
more restrictive rate environment have tempered that outlook. Following a 25 bps hike in 
September, the Fed held rates at 3.75% to 4.00% in October, signaling a continued focus 
on price stability. Even so, PE momentum improved in Q3, with U.S. deal value rising 
20.9% quarter-over-quarter (QoQ) and deal count increasing 5.9%.4 Larger transactions 
also reemerged, although add-ons still represent roughly three-quarters of buyout 
activity, reflecting continued selectivity and underwriting discipline.5 Liquidity remains a 
key constraint, but conditions improved during the quarter. Exit value increased 50.4% 
QoQ, supported by stronger strategic mergers & acquisitions, while portfolio company 
performance has generally remained resilient, with recent PE returns increasingly reliant 
on earnings growth rather than multiple expansion.6 The recovery remains concentrated, 
however, with transactions above $1 billion accounting for nearly 78% of Q3 exit value.7 
Continuation vehicles and other secondary solutions remain important liquidity tools, 
increasingly enabling sponsors to retain higher-conviction assets with additional Value-
creation potential. Fundraising remains challenging and concentrated among managers 
with demonstrated realizations, while evergreen vehicles continue to gain share. AI 
is also becoming a more meaningful driver of underwriting and valuation dispersion, 
particularly in software. Following a sharp Q2 pullback, software deal value rebounded 
roughly 50% in Q3, even as deal count declined, highlighting continued selectivity. 
Sponsors have favored businesses with differentiated characteristics, including 
proprietary data or regulatory advantages, that may be less susceptible to AI disruption.

moderated in Q3, elevated levels suggest it may take several additional quarters for 
redemption activity to fall below the 5% caps. Institutional fundraising trends remain 
strong within PC, but inflows across nontraded BDCs have been challenged, with

loss of value. Special risk considerations include natural events (for example, earthquakes or fires), complex tax considerations, and lack of liquidity. Nonfinancial assets are not in the best 
interest of all investors. Always consult with your independent attorney, tax advisor, investment manager, and insurance agent for final recommendations and before changing or implementing 
any financial, tax, or estate planning strategy.
