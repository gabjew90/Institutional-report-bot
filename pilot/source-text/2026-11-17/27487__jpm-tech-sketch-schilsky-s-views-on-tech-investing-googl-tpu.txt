NEWS – DESK COLOR – JPM TECH RESEARCH  
Schilsky’s Views on Tech Investing – from JPM’s All-Equities 
Bootcamp:  
Last week, JP Morgan hosted our All-Equities Bootcamp at our NYC HQ. I delivered a 30-
minute speech that was an overview of how the Tech Investing Landscape is Evolving. I’ve 
lightly edited my remarks and reproduced them below. Please send feedback! Enjoy.  
Tech Investing is Evolving:  
• 
Tech is the Economy; Macro? What’s That?  
o Perhaps I should have titled this bullet point ‘AI is the Economy’, but you get my 
point. Tech has become a bigger and bigger piece of the economy and the S&P 
500 since the Great Financial Crisis. Information Technology now represents 
37% of the S&P by market cap and we’re basically at the highs. So while tech has 
always been important, it continues to gain in importance – and I see no reason 
why this will stop. It’s the singular, secular growth sector.  As for ‘Macro? What’s 
That?’, while I could argue that while tech is the economy, the fact largely 
remains that the average tech investor I speak with tends to mostly ignore the 
overall macro landscape. Rarely do investors I speak with care about interest 
rates, oil, wars, GDP growth, what the Fed’s doing, consumer confidence, etc. 
These are the trappings of analyzing a secular growth industry. Yes, of course 
certain sub-sectors within tech are cyclical, but their cycles tend to happen on 
their own schedules, not always in direct concert with the broader economy.  
• 
Multi-Managers Still Ascendant  
o Multi-managers continue to gain in importance in a variety of ways. On any given 
day, multi-managers and quant funds are increasingly the marginal buyer of 
stocks. As a result, they dominate equity trading revenue across the 
Street. Multi-managers are voracious consumers of talent, AUM, research, 
and corporate access events. As a result, single stock and thematic 
positioning often become extreme as prevailing narratives dominate trading – 
and the echo chamber becomes even louder…  

• 
AI Utilization is Up Massively, but Alpha is Flat?  
o Back in June   emailed investors the following: ‘Investors are Using a LOT of AI: 
Investment analysts, particularly at HFs, are using a ton of AI. I’ve run into 
numerous analysts whose personal token budgets annualize to tens of 
thousands of dollars with some even exceeding $100K. If you’re not using AI in 
your investment process today, you are rapidly falling behind.’ Hedge funds are 
increasingly utilizing AI to make their investments lives easier. AI is very good at 
summarizing the news, spec sales emails, analyst emails, Twitter debates, etc. 
It’s also very good at updating Excel models and enabling analysts to simply be 
more efficient. But it’s not clear to me – or many investors that I speak with – 
that AI is generating alpha for your average fundamental equity 
analyst. That’s because the models still lack true insight into what’s going on in 
the markets. For now.  
• 
Alt Data is Dead! Long Live Alt Data!  
o Alt data from providers like Yipit, M-Science and others used to be able to 
reliably generate alpha as recently as a decade ago, but the data is now so well 
disseminated that all the alpha has been squeezed out of it. Surprisingly, I’ve 
started to run into HF pods that simply no longer subscribe to these data 
sources anymore because the lack of alpha generation is stark. Additionally, 
while plenty of investors continue to ‘complain’ about the estimates Yipit and M-
Science provide to the Street, largely because they are often so wrong, the 
reality remains that whatever estimates Yipit and M-Science put out on a variety 
of KPIs across the tech landscape…invariably, those become Buyside quarterly 
expectations.  
• 
Twitter & Podcasts are the New Alt Data  
o On any given day, Twitter generates a ton of tech news. Whether it’s a CEO 
tweeting news, or a breaking new story blowing up on the platform, Twitter has 
become a much more powerful force in driving tech investor sentiment over the 
past few years. Honestly, besides Bloomberg, it’s my most important resource. I 
literally started a new section of my daily email a few weeks ago called SchilskyX 
where I screenshot and respond to Tweets. It’s that important. One important 
issue to note: the FinTwit community of influencers is VERY small. We’re talking 
less than 50 people that drive a preponderance of sentiment. Most investors 
and sell siders can’t tweet – so only   small number of FinTwit influencers 
with any sort of respectability drive much of the debate. Additionally, 
podcasts really are a new form of alt data. Public company CEOs have really 
ramped up their time spent on podcasts and their commentary will move stocks. 
Luckily for us, tools like NotebookLM now exist that enable AI to transcribe and 
summarize podcasts very quickly. No longer are HF managers required to listen 
to every tech podcast at 1.5x speed!  
• 
Narrative > Value; Earnings Revisions > Price; Growth > Everything  
o Narrative over Value – in the short-term. Once a prevailing company narrative, 
whether right or wrong, takes over for a stock, it can persist for weeks, 
regardless of a stock’s value. The cheap can always get cheaper – and vice 
versa. Revisions > Price. Your average tech analyst has been trained to focus on 
revenue and earnings revisions as their north star. No matter the price of many 
tech stocks, as long as sell side numbers are being revised higher, investors 
believe they can own said stock. Now, this is where investors can get into 
trouble because buyside numbers are well-disseminated by specialists like 

myself and a whisper number miss can take   stock lower, even if sell side 
numbers are revised higher - but it’s still clear to me that earnings revisions 
matter far more than valuation. Growth > Everything. Finally, in a world 
increasingly dominated by AI, topline revenue growth is the other north star for 
much of the tech world. As long as your top-line growth is either at some high 
absolute level (call it 20%+) or accelerating, your stock is ownable – and if not, 
then not.  
• 
Positioning & Sentiment Matter More Than Ever  
o This point is somewhat self-serving as this is the heart of what a sector specialist 
focuses on, but positioning and sentiment can really drive the magnitude of 
short-term stock moves – especially around earnings. Crowding in certain 
stocks/themes feels like it’s never been more extreme. [I then talked about 
APP’s recent Q3 earnings reaction.]  
• 
Corporate Access Matters More Than Ever  
o In a world where alt data alpha has been squeezed dry and all of the world’s 
information can be synthesized quickly by increasingly effective AI models, 
unstructured data like Corporate Access meetings matter more than ever.  
• 
Private Markets Matter More Than Ever  
o Again, in a world where public information is increasingly commoditized due to 
AI, private market information is increasingly valuable. That’s why we are seeing 
more and more public equity hedge funds launch crossover funds to invest in 
private tech companies. Think about how much better an investor would have 
understood the evolving AI landscape over the past few years if they had been 
private investors in several of the biggest private AI companies.  
Internet:  
• 
Capital Light No More (Focus on ROIC) - Analysts Must Evolve  
o Historically, Internet investors never cared much about capital intensity or 
balance sheets. They didn’t have to. Many of the biggest internet companies on 
the planet, GOOGL, META, etc. had pristine balance sheets, generated a ton of 
cash, and capex intensity was low. But no more. The capex intensity of AI data 
centers has complicated the ways in which analysts view these businesses 
which has in turn resulted in heightened volatility related to capex guidance as 
well as announced capital raises. Now, with all that said, the profit focus for 
investors analyzing these companies remains GAAP EPS, not FCF. As long as 
GAAP EPS grows and/or is revised higher, investors will remain long these 
names, no matter what is happening to FCF. For now…  
• 
AI is the Most Disruptive Force During Our Lifetimes  
o I don’t think anyone should be terribly surprised by this statement. The way that 
this has manifested itself in the Internet sector this year has taken many forms: 
GOOGL Search potentially being disrupted by ChatGPT, GOOGL’s shift to AI 
Overviews and AI Mode disrupting the open internet and companies like RDDT, 
META Muse & AI agents disrupting online travel agencies like EXPE and BKNG as 
well as eCommerce companies like AMZN, ETSY, W, & EBAY, AV technology 
potentially disrupting the ride-share companies, and AI being such a big tailwind 
to digital ad behemoths like META & GOOGL that SMID digital ad companies like 
PINS, SNAP & RDDT are being increasingly crowded out. I don’t expect this level 
of disruption to abate anytime soon; nor does the average Internet investor.  
• 
Current Sentiment is Terrible; Worse Than Software  

o While the SaaSpocalypse garnered a majority of headlines in the first half of this 
year, the reality from my vantage point has been that sentiment across the 
Internet sector has been worse than in Software for much of this year. Many of 
2025’s darlings, RDDT, APP, CVNA, NFLX, etc., have had a rough 2026. Combine 
that with all of the disruption-related headlines due to GOOGL Search/AI 
Mode/AI Overviews & META Muse and you have the recipe for   sector that has 
been largely abandoned by generalists this year – especially the SMID names.  
• 
Generalists Have Fled SMID for Semis; Only Specialists Remain  
o I want to reiterate this point; generalists have fled Internet, especially SMID 
internet, over the past two years and have spent a TON of time in Semis. As 
such, only Internet specialists remain. Only internet specialists traffic in names 
like RDDT, APP, EBAY, ABNB, DUOL, etc. One day the generalists may come 
back – but that probably will require the Semis landscape to look a LOT worse. 
[We might be on that path…]  
• 
Alt Data is Dead! Long Live Alt Data!  
o I want to reiterate what I mentioned earlier: analysts often complain about alt 
data moving stocks, and moving expectations – and yet everyone still plays the 
same game. Especially in Internet. Want to know the GMS bogey for ETSY 
headed into a quarter? Check Yipit. The AMZN AWS Revenue bogey? Usually a 
simple average of Yipit and M-Science. RBLX Bookings? Yipit. Now, not every 
Internet stock has ‘solid’ alt data associated with it – but a LOT of them do.  
• 
Alt Research is Ascendant (Cleveland, SemiAnalysis, etc.)  
o Alt Research providers like Cleveland, Edgewater, and SemiAnalysis increasingly 
move sentiment and expectations on stocks. SemiAnalysis had a huge influence 
on buyside expectations for 2027 hyperscaler capex earlier this year. Cleveland 
and Edgewater collectively took APP’s stock down by about half over the late 
summer into the fall on the back of channel checks that ended up being 
completely wrong. Despite their spotty record, their influence continues to be 
felt across the market…  
Software:  
• 
AI is the Most Disruptive Force During Our Lifetimes  
o (Look familiar?) The cost of writing Software is headed towards zero. Now, 
please keep in mind that Claude Code only really got good at writing Software 
late in 2025. Much of the disruption that investors anticipate seeing in 
Software has yet to be observed – but it’s coming. As such, investors remain 
VERY wary of most Application Software stocks. They continually ask ‘What is 
the moat of a Software company that can have its functionality reverse 
engineered by an AI coding agent for a relatively small amount of money?’ 
Expect a TON of volatility in the Software space in the years to come.  
• 
Only Topline Acceleration is Tolerated  
o Given that AI is so disruptive to the software industry, the only KPI that investors 
have really focused on this year to allay their AI-related fears has been Revenue 
growth (and/or cRPO/ARR growth). If Revenue growth is accelerating, like for 
SNOW, CRWD, NET, TEAM, then those stocks are ‘own-able’. And if they are 
decelerating, like for NOW, WDAY, INTU, MNDY, etc., then they are viewed as 
consensus shorts/underweights. This year in Software has really not been much 
more complicated than that at the individual stock level.  
• 
Valuation Doesn’t Matter (Much):  

o Rarely do my discussions with Software investors focus on ‘traditional’ valuation 
metrics like P/E and P/FCF. I’m not here to tell you that Software investors are 
ignorant of valuation, but rather that valuation is one of the least important 
considerations for Software analysts. Especially using near-term metrics like 
NTM P/E or NTM P/FCF. That’s because Software companies are supposed to be 
long-lived assets where a majority of the value is in the terminal value phase of 
the DCF. To give you a sense for how ‘little’ valuation matters, I’ll note that 
CRWD is up nearly +100% YTD, trades over 30x forward sales despite growing 
total revenue in the mid-20s, which is slower than many Software peers, and 
Street estimates for FY28 Revenue are only up +4%. The multiple has expanded 
because CRWD is benefiting from all of the increased threats that advanced AI 
models are enabling in the market. Narrative over price… 
• 
On the Ground Channel Checks Still Work  
o In a world where more and more structured data is easily obtainable and 
parsed by AI models, a great fact remains for Software investors: on the 
ground channel checks still work. Spending time with enterprise customers, 
going to trade shows, getting on the phone still works. You can gain real insight 
into the changing purchasing patterns of major customers and integrators by 
simply speaking with them. But it takes a LOT of effort. But it can definitely be 
fruitful. For instance, once Mythos came out earlier this year, if you were 
consistently taking to enterprise CISOs, you quickly understood that the threat 
landscape had significantly changed and it was time to load up on Cybersecurity 
stocks. You could have generated a ton of alpha if you had followed that 
playbook.  
• 
Alt Data ‘Signal’ is Limited  
o On the flip side, while some alt data providers like M-Science continue to put out 
estimates for quarterly revenue, their ‘signal’ is far more limited than it is in 
Internet. These alt data providers have limited impact on expectations and 
sentiment in Software – relative to Internet.  
• 
Twitter & Corporate Access Drive Sentiment  
o Like I noted before, Twitter can still drive sentiment. Fintwit was dunking all over 
Software earlier this year as IGV went down seemingly every day. Many of my 
investor discussions at the time amounted to ‘I can’t own anything in App 
Software. AI is going to consume the entire sector.’ Well, IGV has bounced about 
one third off the February Citrini bottom. WCLD (the Cloud Computing Index) is 
actually up nearly +20% YTD! There’s a lot of alpha to be made betting against 
Twitter sentiment. Corporate Access can also drive sentiment – especially 
around Conferences. Very often I hear from investors, XYZ CEO sounded bad at 
the latest conference – you can’t own it.  
• 
Infrastructure & Cybersecurity >>> Applications  
o And like I hinted earlier, sentiment in Software is not a monolith. For basically 
this entire year investors have been bullish on Infra names like DDOG and 
SNOW, bullish on Cybersecurity companies like PANW, CRWD and NET and 
bearish on Applications Software names like NOW, CRM, HUBS, ADBE, MNDY, 
and many more. For now, I do not expect this rhetorical positioning to change 
any time soon. Not with AI-driven software-writing capabilities continuing to 
accelerate.  
Media & Telecom:  
• 
AI is a Limited Disruptor  

o In a world dominated by AI, investors have selectively gravitated towards the 
Media & Telecom space in an effort to find companies that are ‘immune’ from AI. 
When I say ‘selective’, what I really mean is that investors have focused on live 
entertainment companies like LYV, TKO, FWONA, SPHR, and CNK this year as a 
place to ‘hide’ from all of the AI-related volatility. It’s largely been a solid trade 
YTD.  
• 
SPCX & Social Media are the Disruptors  
o SPCX is the true disruptor in the space – in telecom, specifically. Cable 
Broadband (CHTR, CMCSA) is actually going to end up being disrupted twice 
in the past 5 years. First by the wireless companies with their FWA technology 
and now by SPCX with Starlink. If you believe Elon, he’s going to launch a ton of 
V3 satellites into orbit in the coming years, saturating the earth with cheap 
broadband connectivity. Additionally, he’s recently laid out plans to build a 
fourth wireless carrier to take on VZ, T, and TMUS. TMUS has been a relative 
darling over the past few years post the Sprint deal. Cozy oligopolies tend to 
attract a certain cohort of long-term value investors. But it sure seems like a 
tough trade when a deep-pocketed and aggressive competitor aims to disrupt 
the space. Expect   lot of striking headlines to come out the in the next few 
years. As for Social Media, NFLX and the other streaming TV companies have 
struggled to meaningfully grow subscriber counts as Instagram, YouTube, and 
TikTok continue to garner more watch time from the public. All three companies 
now have TV-based apps and are offering longer and longer form content that is 
increasingly competing with NFLX. NFLX wanted to become HBO before HBO 
became NFLX. Well, now NFLX is trying to become YouTube because that’s 
where the eyeballs are going. It’s going to be a tough road ahead for them.  
• 
K-Shaped Economy Leads to ‘Live’ Outperformance  
o I just want to repeat that the live entertainment stocks have done well this year 
for two reasons: 1) they are part of the ‘AI immune’ trade and 2) the K-shaped 
economy is resulting in the upper middle class having a TON of disposable 
income. This has really benefited stocks like LYV and SPHR. In all honesty, this is 
likely to continue as long as the stock market is up and to the right. The wealth 
effect is very real.  
• 
Stocks are Cheap; They Can Always Get Cheaper  
o Never start a pitch on any stock – especially Media or Telecom stocks – with 
‘it’s cheap’. What the market is usually telling you when   Media stock is 
‘cheap’ is that the market doesn’t believe the out-year earnings 
estimates. I’ll use SKYD as an example. Management is currently guiding to 
2030 FCF of $10B. The current market cap is ~$40B. Thus, the stock is trading at 
~4x 2030 levered FCF. Is the stock cheap? If you believe in that $10B FCF 
number – yes. But what the market is telling you is that shareholders will never 
enjoy that level of FCF. SKYD’s predecessor, WBD, put out similar far out 
earnings estimate that it missed by a wide margin. Investors expect the same 
here. You want to own SKYD? Prove to your PM that the $10B figure is real – not 
that the stock is ‘cheap’.  
• 
A ‘Fading’ Sub-Sector; Only Die-Hard Specialists Remain  
o Unfortunately, Media & Telecom is a ‘fading’ sub-sector. Growth is hard to come 
by, market caps are dwarfed by lots of other companies in Internet, Software, 
and Semis, and as a result, only die hard investors remain. In some ways this is 
good – there’s less competition amongst Media & Telecom investors to 

analyze, invest in, and talk with management teams of these companies. 
But I must also note that even if you have an elegant long thesis on a stock, 
because the sector is so under-followed, the market will only pay attention when 
a certain catalyst becomes eminently CLEAR to everyone to get paid. Very few 
investors are willing to bet their career on some esoteric telecom stock, no 
matter how cheap it is relative to asset value.  
• 
M&A Will Continue; Like Plugging a Hole in a Leaky Ship  
o As a result of growth being harder to come by, continue to expect M&A to occur. 
In the past two years, NFLX tried to buy WBD; PSKY bought it instead. FOXA 
bought ROKU. NXST bought TGNA. CHTR bought Cox. RKLB bought IRDM. 
Investors believe that FWONK is likely to put it up for sale now that John Malone 
has relinquished his super-voting shares. CMCSA spun off VSNT and is now 
planning to split itself in two again. In other words, expect M&A to continue. It 
always does. It sometimes works – rarely. But given that industry-wide growth is 
increasingly hard to come by, it feels like plugging a hole in a leaky ship. 
Investors WILL NOT pay big multiples for synergy-driven EBITDA growth. They 
crave topline growth above all else.
