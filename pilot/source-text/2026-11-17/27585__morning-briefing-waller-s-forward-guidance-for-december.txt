OUTLOOK 
The rebound in risk assets and recovery of the midterms loses for US indices means the reaction 
function to CPI today should revert to normal after the disruption and FGT bid in bonds on 14th 
October from stocks (Nasdaq -1.9%). This should offer a cleaner read of the market’s opinion on 
positioning and the tactical direction of travel as bets are fine-tuned for the last Fed rate decision 
of the year. This will be the final CPI data set before the December meeting, (one more PCE point 
to come) so there is reason to believe that the reaction could be more impactful than usual. FOMC 
voter Waller (hawk) banged the drum for a hike in his earlier comments as a visitor to the BoE 
watchers’ conference this morning and would even contemplate voting for half a point if core CPI 
comes in at 0.3% or above. Does the Fed want to the invert the curve? In the event of a firm print, 
yields should shift higher with the bulk of any steepening selloff possibly concentrated in the long 
end but with Brent plummeting below $90/b and causing future inflation expectations to snap 
back, it remains to be seen how the different parts of the curve would respond. A downside 
surprise would give bulls fresh impetus to take on the 100dma for US 10s at 4.81%, with bullish 
seasonal tailwinds possibly opening the next front below 4.80%. SG economics pencil in a third 
successive 0.3% m/m increase for core compared to consensus for a softer 0.2%. Used cars rose 
a punchy 0.7% last month and apparel firmed 0.3%. Relief in those two categories could result in 
a friendly print but may still not be enough to make the Fed change course in December. A similar 
approach applies to the ECB. Chief economist Lane spoke earlier and said that based on today’s 
data and without overloading on one factor (cheaper oil), it would still be prudent to tighten in 
December. In FX, European ccies excl the NOK and the NZD are outperforming on cheaper oil. 
USD/INR trades below 94.00 for the first time since April and USD/MXN extends below 18.50 which 
has more to do with the rebound in stocks than slump in energy prices. 
One week after the PBoC raised the FX RRR from 4% to 6%, further signs are emerging that 
Chinese policymakers are becoming increasingly uncomfortable with the pace of CNY gains. 
Reuters reported yesterday that several local banks sharply raised their USD deposit rates, in 
some cases to levels competitive with or above comparable US offerings, to attract dollar 
deposits and stymie appreciation of the yuan. The move comes as exporters complain that the 
currency is starting to squeeze already-thin margins. Higher dollar rates encourage corporates 
and households to retain dollar balances rather than convert them into yuan. The latest increases 
also suggest that authorities are willing to relax the informal constraints that previously kept 
state-bank USD deposit rates capped. Some banks are now offering more than 4.0% on dollar 
deposits above US$50k, roughly 120bp higher than levels seen only a few months ago. By 
contrast, yuan deposit rates have moved lower, with major state banks offering around 0.95%, 
reinforcing Beijing's broader strategy of smoothing the pace of CNY appreciation without 
reversing the longer-term trend. This aligns with our house view that the Chinese policymakers 
are trying to put a floor on USD/CNY.  
The decline in Brent below $90/b is bolstering the terms of trade of India but has also been 
accompanied by a sharp improvement in portfolio flows. FPIs added nearly INR80bn to FAR-
eligible government bonds and purchasing net INR459.7bn of equities last week. Together, those 
factors have helped deflate USD/INR below 94.00 for the first time since April. The next leg lower, 
however, will depend on the RBI tolerance for further currency appreciation given its sizeable net 
short dollar position. A similar dynamic is playing out in South Africa, where lower oil prices are 
helping USD/ZAR to gravitate back toward its 200dma near 16.41 despite a less supportive 
political backdrop following the weaker-than-expected showing by the ANC in municipal 
elections. For now, improving external balances are outweighing other idiosyncratic risks for 
both currencies.
