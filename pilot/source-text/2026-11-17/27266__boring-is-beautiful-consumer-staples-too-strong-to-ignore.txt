Boring is Beautiful
Consumer Staples Too Strong to Ignore
WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW: 
One of the interesting aspects of market psychology is that investors often want to
buy what is cheap and sell what is expensive. Intuitively that makes sense, and that's
what they teach in the textbooks. Of course, cheap and expensive are subjective. For
instance, the consensus in June was that memory names were 'cheap'. They may have
been cheap then, but that didn't preclude a broad basket of memory names from falling
50%. Conversely, the consensus on Consumer Staples is that they are 'expensive' and
have little growth. Again, that may very well be true, but that was also true a month ago,
and they are up 10% since. In fact, staples have beaten tech by 33% over the last 30 days,
the largest such move in 25 years. Sometimes the market is wrong, but other times it's
telling a story for those willing to listen. While we continue to think correlations rise and
thus staples won't be completely immune to a broad-based decline, they are showing
too much relative strength to ignore. Consumer Staples may be boring, but boring can
be beautiful and with XLP breaking out of a multi-month base in absolute and relative
terms, we are warming up to the group here.
■
Stuck Under Supply. One of the key issues we see for tech (QQQ and SOXX) is the
fact that price has fallen below that significant volume zone. Thus, any rallies into
that are likely to be met with overhead supply, like we saw last Thursday.
■
A Trip Down Memory Lane. When the dot-com bubble peaked in March 2000, it
wasn’t a broad bear market right away. In fact, in the one-year following that peak,
seven S&P sectors gained 12% or more, and Consumer Staples were up by over 50%!
■
Biggest Move in 25 Years. S&P 500 Staples just beat S&P Tech by 33% over the last
30 days. That’s the largest such move in ~25 years. The only period we saw similar
such moves was from April ’00 through Oct. ’01. Despite that initial surge, staples
would beat tech by nearly 1000 bps over the next two years.
■
Boring is Beautiful: Staples Too Strong to Ignore. It’s easy to dislike Consumer
Staples. They are considered ‘expensive’ and have just had a 15% rally off the lows.
Because of that, we find that sentiment on them is quite bearish, and yet they
are breaking out of a base in absolute and relative terms. Negative sentiment with
strong price action is a powerful set-up. Given they aren’t particularly extended, we
are warming up to the group here.
