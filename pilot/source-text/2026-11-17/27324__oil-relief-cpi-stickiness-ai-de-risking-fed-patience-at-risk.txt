highest since May. The unemployment rate inched down to 4.1% from 
4.2%, extending the tight range for this year.
•
Resilient labour markets and a still patient Fed remain the critical drivers 
of our broadly constructive outlook for risk assets into 2027, although that 
patience is increasingly conditional on near-term inflation. 
•
US October CPI (0.35% m/m headline; 0.30% m/m core), alongside Fed 
rhetoric, has challenged this narrative. Baseline: one +25bp hike in 
December. Tail risk: faster cadence or larger increments, with the market 
now pricing more than three additional hikes by September 2027. 
•
An offsetting medium-term risk is that AI diffusion could cap wage 
acceleration, creating a regime where low unemployment coexists with 
only steady wage inflation, complicating the Fed’s reaction function.
•
We see the key 2027 risk as a shift from a patient, growth-centric Fed 
willing to tolerate above-target inflation to one committed to restoring 
price stability, even at the cost of weaker growth and a softer labour 
market. This “New Testament” to “Old Testament” shift could be 
validated as soon as the December FOMC meeting. 
•

deleveraging and positioning event, which should ultimately support the 
durability of the AI supercycle by lowering leverage and improving entry

In the US, the Democrats took control of both houses of Congress in the midterm 
elections. Middle East tensions de-escalated after the elections, driving a sharp pullback 
in oil prices, with Brent down almost 20% in the past fortnight, towards our fair value 
estimate, as the physical market moved back into surplus.  Regarding US economic data, 
the October jobs report in the US surprised on the upside, as payroll growth rebounded 
to 159k, lifting the 3M average to 137k per month, the highest since May. 
Unemployment rate inched down to 4.1% from 4.2%, extending the tight range for this 
year. The improvement in labour market is a necessary condition for our constructive 
outlook on risk assets into 2027, provided that central banks remain patient. However, 
strong US October CPI print, with headline and core prices rising 0.35% m/m and 
0.30%, respectively, and Fed rhetoric partly challenged the patient central bank narrative 
this week. We continue to expect a 25bp December hike, but data and Fed’s comments 
raise the risk of a more forceful path, with the market now pricing more than three 
additional hikes by September 2027. 
Across the recent Fed speak, the common thread is a renewed inflation-first bias with 
multiple officials characterizing underlying pressures as persistent/broadening, which 
keeps the Committee leaning toward further tightening. Governor Waller was explicit 
that near-term inflation prints could force the Fed to lean harder to protect credibility 
and avoid an inadvertent easing in conditions, while Dallas Fed President Logan argued 
policy is still not yet modestly restrictive and signaled at least two more 25bp hikes with 
optionality to move faster in winter if the data/conditions require it. Similarly, 
Philadelphia Fed President Paulson emphasized the priority of returning inflation to 
target, favouring “gradual tightening” when conditions allow but “would want to act 
more quickly” if inflation expectations drift higher, wage pressures build or underlying 
inflation rises. Governor Bowman was the key moderating voice: still prioritizing 
inflation, but preferring to wait and assess given that tighter private rates and weaker 
equities may already be doing some tightening for the Fed. Altogether, the baseline 
message is for a slow, gradual tightening path (25bp increments, with pauses to assess), 
but the risk is for an acceleration via a faster pace and/or larger step size if near-term 
inflation data stays “hot” or financial conditions loosen in a way that undermines the 
disinflation trajectory (Figure 1Fed rhetoric remains hawkish with speakers highlighting concerns on inflation ).

In FX, we remain bullish on the USD and EM carry. As discussed earlier this year, USD 
strength against low-yielding DM FX (DXY) can coexist with positive EM FX returns 
(ELMI+). Our updated analysis since 1996 shows that 3-month rolling returns for the 
DXY and ELMI+ (proxy for EM FX) indices have been simultaneously positive 26% of 
the time, on average. 2026 YTD is particularly notable: simultaneous positive returns 
have occurred in 47% of observations, the fourth-highest frequency in our history. The 
only higher readings were in 1996 (76%), 1999 (65%), and 2005 (50%), all pre-GFC 
periods characterized by cyclical uplift and strong carry regimes. To assess the 
persistence of these episodes, we construct a US-EM FX co-movement indicator that 
takes a value of 1 when both DXY and ELMI+ post positive 3-month returns and 0 
otherwise, which we then smoothen out applying a 6-month moving average. 
Historically, high readings have predominantly coincided with low-volatility 
environments, while major volatility episodes have pushed the indicator toward 0
