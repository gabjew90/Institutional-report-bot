JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Last week, the SPX added 3.6%, snapping a 4-week losing streak which saw the index 
shed 5.3%; the index is back to the bottom-end of the trading range it has been in for most 
of Aug – Oct. NVDA was the key story, declining 12.8% on the week and now down 34.6% 
over the last 6 weeks; but, the SOX added 9.2% on the week and memory +7.9%. These 
moves occurred despite hawkish inflation data and Fedspeak that drove yields higher at the 

fastest pace in several years. In terms of establishing the dominant market narrative into 
year-end, this week has a seminal feel to it. Let’s take a look at the Tactical Bull vs. Bear 
Case.  
  
•         BULL CASE – (i) NVDA Earnings – reiterating capex and support for existing 
financing deals is critical, as well as clarity on future leadership given a movement to return 
Huang to the company. Given the speed with which Huang was ousted leads some to 
believe there are more disclosures coming, which that company would need to address. 
Our Equity Derivatives team notes NVDA flows have shifted from downside protection to 
playing for earnings surprises, with little fear of another downdraft (ii) Capital Markets – 
this week’s events need to trade well to improve conviction on both broader equity markets 
and on the potential wave of ECM / DCM / M&A deals which are expected to accelerate 
before the next Congress is seated on Jan 3. (iii) AI / Tech Trade Returns – our JPM 
APAC Tech Conference kicks off in HK with ~250 firms presenting including Samsung, SK 
Hynix, and TSMC. The stabilization / move higher is likely to be led by HFs who have been 
sellers for 6 consecutive weeks, a Positioning Intel record. (iv) Macro Data – the major 
release this week is Retail Sales where another slowdown is expected with the risk that a 
downside print fuels the Recession narrative. Our recession basket (JPRECES Index) is 
+15.8% QTD vs SPX -1.2%. An inline or stronger print would help shift flows back towards 
Beta / Cyclicals supporting markets.  
•         BEAR CASE –  (i) AI / Tech Take Another Leg Lower – this could be a combination 
of NVDA failing to reassure on AI ecosystem financing, a delay to expected capital markets 
events, or perhaps negative pre-announcements are our APAC Tech Conf. Irrespective of 
the genesis, sentiment remains fragile and returns on precarious footing. (ii) A Decline in 
the Incremental Buyer – we have seen the Retail investor step in to buy dips and 
buybacks tend to accelerate in up-markets rather than down-markets, should the first 
domino be another downdraft surrounding AI / Tech, then we may see those buyers save 
their dry powder for 2027. (iii) Hawkish Fed Narrative – while last week showed Equity 
resilience from higher yields / bond vol, one should not expect that to be the norm going 
forward. A hotter Retail Sales print plus continued hawkish Fedspeak, perhaps seeing 
multiple members discussing the potential for 50bp; there are 17x Fedspeakers this week. 

Should the market believe that 50bp is necessary then it seems unlikely that hiking cycle is 
as shallow as the current pricing of 4x hikes (Dec inclusive). (iv) Positioning Purge – 
aggregate US positioning is ~20%-ile in the Tactical Positioning Monitor, dating to 2015; 
and global Semis exposure is 80%-ile, so there is room for another downdraft. Despite the 
recent price action, there has not be the definitive positioning purge that has triggered a 
consensus buy-signal.

POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE –  Weekly Wrap | Is the Semis Sell-Off Over? 
SUMMARY: This past week was still choppy, but ultimately felt like a reprieve as 
performance rebounded a bit and de-grossing slowed. Semis remain choppy and HFs 
were still de-risking the group early this week, but if this were to shift more bullishly, then it’s 
possible that they could rebound in the near-term, given the large reduction in positioning 
(e.g. HF net exposures down 67% from peak globally) and sentiment very depressed. 
Europe and Japan positioning appear to be going in opposite directions, but ultimately this 
just puts them both closer to the global average…i.e., regional skews were reduced further. 
  
KEY POINTS: 
1. Semis saw another heavy week of HF selling. HF net flows in US Semis were 
below -3z for the second week in a row, and HF selling extended to a sixth 
consecutive week—the longest streak on record, with unusually large cumulative 
net selling. Flows varied by region: Japan also saw selling, while AxJ was roughly 
balanced and EMEA Semis were bought. Retail flows suggested dip-buying and 
Tech ETFs saw inflows. 

2. Semis positioning has reset materially, though a rebound may require 
stabilization and renewed HF buying. Global HF net exposure has fallen 67% 
from its year-to-date high to its current level—lowest since September 2025.  
The global long/short ratio declined from 4.4x to 2.1x as well.  Retail sentiment 
towards Semis has also weakened to a very low level.  Looking ahead, with the 
combined 6m positioning + price z-score near -3z, we could see a bounce similar to 
what happened post the same type of reduction in Momentum by late July. However, 
global Semis exposure remains around the 80th percentile versus data since 
2018, so positioning is not especially low on a long-term basis.  Furthermore, as the 
6m z-score of HF positioning remains lower than the 6m z-score relative 
returns, history suggests the rebound might not be that robust unless this 
starts to change (i.e. HFs get more bullish). 
3. Gross flows and HF performance showed signs of stabilization. After near -2z 
de-grossing on Monday, flows calmed over Tuesday–Thursday, with Thursday the 
most positive global re-gross day in about a month. Multi-Strat gross flows also 
appeared to rebound from recent extremes. HF performance gained about 1.5% 
over five days and is now roughly flat month to date globally (-0.3% across All 
Strategies). Europe is up 1.7% MTD, while Asia is down 2%. 
4. Gross leverage dipped; US positioning remains low while Europe has 
rebounded. HF gross leverage fell 4% week over week, while net leverage was 
broadly unchanged. The US Tactical Positioning Monitor was little changed at the 
20th percentile since 2015. Europe rebounded more quickly from depressed levels 
alongside outperformance…European flows have increased by +1.5z relative to US 
flows over the past 1–3 months.  This brings relative EU positioning (vs. US) back 
to neutral. 
5. Japan was broadly sold, with positioning now below average on a combined 
basis. HF flows were -1.4z WoW and -2.3z MoM.  HFs sold nearly all sectors during 
the week and all 11 sectors were sold over the month. HF positioning remains above 
average on a long-term basis, but combined Japan positioning (e.g. HFs, CTAs, 
margin balances, etc.) has fallen to the 24th percentile since 2015, near year-to-
date lows, with a roughly -2z 4wk change.
