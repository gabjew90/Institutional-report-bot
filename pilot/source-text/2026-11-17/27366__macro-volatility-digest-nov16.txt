WEEKLY MARKET COMMENTARY 
Retail Turns Bearish on NVDA Ahead of Earnings 
 
Cross-Asset Volatility: Implied volatilities were mixed 
across asset classes last week as risk sentiment improved. 
Equities jumped higher while the VIX® Index declined, down 
over 6 pts wk/wk to 19.3% (83rd percentile high), giving back 
the entirety of the previous week’s gains. Credit volatility fell 
even more, with the VIXIG Index down almost 8 pts to the 
54th percentile. Oil volatility came in as prices stabilized, 
even as tensions continue to simmer in the Middle East. 
Despite lower oil prices, bond yields jumped last week as the 
Fed signaled a more hawkish stance, with US 10Y real yield 
surging 27 bps wk/wk to ~2.9%. A hike at the Dec FOMC 
meeting is now not only fully priced in, but investors are also 
pricing in a small probability (~7% in the OIS market) of a 
super-sized hike (i.e. 50bps). Gold fell to a new 1-year low 
on the back of higher real yields, while GLD 1M implied vol 
gained modestly, up 0.3 pt wk/wk to 24%. Demand for puts 
in GLD has risen notably in recent weeks, driving GLD 1M 
skew (25-delta ratio) to the 83rd percentile high.  
Equity Volatility: QQQ led the decline in equity vol last 
week as Tech stocks rebounded, with QQQ 1M implied vol 
falling 7.3 pts wk/wk vs. SPX® 1M implied vol down 4.5 pts 
(see Exhibit 1). SPX 1M implied vol ranks as the richest 
index vol currently, in the 77th percentile high. The QQQ-
SPX 1M implied volatility spread narrowed sharply last 
week, down from a high of 11.7% to 6.8%. Meanwhile, the 
RTY-SPX vol spread widened from the lows, up from 1.9% 
to 4.4% over the past week, with small caps seen as more 
vulnerable to higher rates. For more, see our recently 
published report on the growth in the Russell 2000 
derivatives ecosystem, including a deep dive into the drivers 
of small-cap performance and the RTY-SPX volatility 
spread. While skew flattened for SPX and QQQ last week, it 
steepened notably in small-caps, with RTY 1M skew (25-
delta ratio) jumping to near a 1-year high (see Exhibit 2). 
Retail Traders Turn Bearish on NVDA: All eyes on NVDA 
earnings this Tuesday following the ouster of Jensen Huang. 
Options are implying a one-day move of 8% on earnings (vs 
average realized move of 4.8% the past four quarters). 
NVDA 1M implied volatility gained 2 pts last week to near a 
1-year high of 59% (vs. realized vol of 55%). While skew 
flattened on the sell-off in the stock, suggesting overall 
increased bullish sentiment, retail positioning has turned 
more cautious. Looking at the retail flow on Cboe’s 
exchanges, we see a notable uptick in put buying in NVDA, 
which made up 32% of all retail opening trades on Thursday, 
over 10 pts higher than the YTD average of 21%. See Exhibit 
3. Call buying still makes up the bulk of retail activity in 
NVDA, though the share has fallen in recent weeks and is 
hovering near the YTD average level of ~46% (vs. high of 
62% in Sep).

How to read this chart:
The chart shows how various asset class implied 
volatilities are trading relative to their own history, using 
a 10-year lookback. 
The volatilities are ranked by z-scores, which compares 
the current volatility level for an asset versus its own 10-
year history. A z-score of +2, for example, would mean 
that the current volatility level is trading 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average (i.e. rich) while a 
z-score of -2 would mean it’s trading 2 standard 
deviations below average (i.e. cheap)
Because each asset class volatility trades at different 
levels and often defined differently (e.g. lognormal vs. 
normal vol, price vs. bps vol), by standardizing using z-
scores, we’re able to compare different asset class 
volatilities on the same scale - and see at a glance if 
there are any divergences or dislocations in the cross-
asset volatility markets
