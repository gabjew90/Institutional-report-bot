[GSX] Global Reflections

13 Nov 2026

With just under six weeks left to the year, what was meant to be a post-election
relief rally lasted under twenty-four hours, and the AI/geopolitics fatigue resumed.
But despite midterms, the Power theme (GSENEPOW) is now up post-midterms,
IPPs (GSXUIPPS) are +15% off their lows and Hyperscalers (GSXUHYPR) still
offer defensive AI exposure. We think a barbell approach of selective AI trades
and more defensive, immediate FCF/income generators makes sense. Five key
points:
AI darkest before dawn? Last week’s technical washout set the stage for a
strong AI rebound but appetite to digest NVDA specific issues was short lived
and a lot of our investor conversations have characterized price action earlier
in the week as a potential thematic bear market rally. Determining whether this
sell-off ultimately proves an attractive ‘buy the dip’ or the first signs of a regime
shift in equity leadership will depend on whether (or not) we are in an earnings
bubble. What we do know is that certain pockets of the market are clearly
‘over earning’ and the discovery process of where medium-term earnings
growth will eventually settle is has proven painful. But valuations in certain
pockets are now calling for excessive downward revisions to 2027 earnings,
meaning any reignition of fundamental AI confidence could drive a doubling of
memory stocks (visual #2 below). To balance that AI exposure in portfolios, we
also like adding longs on SPXXAI.
 
Cash is King, but where is he hiding? The market’s biggest index weights
now lack free cash flow, and non-cash earnings are a bigger portion of
earnings growth, so free cash flow is now scarce and matters more. We
expect the market to continue rewarding high FCF generators given greater
capital flexibility versus peers in a higher-for-longer rates environment. Our
preferred expression is to go long our High versus Low Free Cash Flow pair
(GSPUFCFP) where recent outperformance has been equally driven by the
long and short leg. Worth noting strength in a similar factor like Quality has
been mostly driven by the short leg. While our European High versus Low
FCF pair (GSPEFCFP) lagged the US expression over summer, it has started
to aggressively catch up, which we expect should continue in coming weeks. A
related short theme would be our Lending Sensitive basket (GSXULEND)

which comprises businesses and areas of the economy that have capital
needs and may get crowded out by other capital market needs.
A Long China trade that’s working. China’s high dividend trade remains a
differentiated way to stay invested while reducing reliance on the growth cycle.
Stable cash generation, attractive yields, and predominantly domestic
earnings have provided a buffer through recent volatility, while National Team
buying has added support to the SOE/high-dividend complex. The backdrop
for shareholder return also remains constructive with listed Chinese
companies returning more than Rmb2tn of cash annually via dividends and
buybacks in each of the past three years. In a backdrop of wide US-China
yield differentials, our Asia colleagues continue to favor Value, Dividend and
Cash Return exposure over longer-duration growth. Their China High Dividend
basket (GSXACHHN) targets onshore companies with high and stable
dividend yields, concentrated in Banks and Energy/Utility stocks. The basket
has delivered +8pp of performance over the last 3m while CSI 300 has
declined -15pp over the same period.
 
Europe’s lack of AI serving it well. Globally, Europe displays one of the
lowest betas to the AI trade (SXXP 2y daily beta to GSPUARTI <0.05). While
that meant Europe was, at best, a marginal allocation to global equity
portfolios for most of the last few years, we expect investors will increasingly
return to the region for much-needed AI diversification. Ultimately, the bull
case for European equities still hinges on a US-Iran resolution, but in the
interim, there are AI-uncorrelated pockets worth parking capital in. Our EU
High and Stable Margins (GSXEMARG) basket offers an attractive overlap of
low/no AI correlation and a defensive skew to protect against any potential
geopolitical shock. The basket has just recently started to break out versus the
broader market. On the flip side, there are pockets of European equities
where we see recent strength as premature. French Domestics (GSXEFRDO)
have rallied 10% from October lows as French politics took a bit of a backseat.
We see scope for the downward trend to resume given credit conditions

remain difficult, political uncertainty is still high, and the near-term catalyst is
S&P’s upcoming credit rating review (Nov 27th).
 
What’s momentum made of? A lot of the points we discuss above point to a
momentum regime shift, with AI pain extending since summer and the
willingness to pay upfront for long-duration growth significantly waning. As a
result, correlation between 12m momentum (GSPRHIMO) and 3m momentum
(GSPRHMO3) is now negative and the number of AI names in the short leg of
3m and 6m momo (GSPRHMO6) have skyrocketed. This is consistent with net
sector flows based on momentum tenor, where infotech was net sold in both
3m and 6m momentum, but still slightly bought in 12m momo. With
momentum volatility still elevated (60-day vol is 3.5x vs SPX), rotations and
reversals are likely to stay a feature of the factor until the market settles on
new long-term leadership.  
SPXXAI has now outperformed vs SPX over the last year, and has been
more resilient through last week’s sell-off

The market is now at the left tail of potential Memory outcomes for 2027,
implying significant upside if confidence in the trade resumes
