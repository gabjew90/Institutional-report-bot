It is that time of year again. Every year, our economists and strategists around the 
world step back from the daily flow of data and market moves to debate what 
comes next. Our year-ahead outlooks, published later today, distill their collective 
judgment. In 2026, geopolitical shocks reshaped the global economy and monetary 
policy, while AI infrastructure investment remained a defining theme across markets. 
In 2027, an AI capex cycle we view as intact, a resilient global economy, and solid 
earnings growth extending into 2027 provide a constructive foundation. With major 
risk catalysts behind us and risk assets having cheapened over the past six months, 
we enter 2027 with a broadly positive stance on risk. In this week’s Sunday Start, we 
trace the  contours of our outlook for the global economy and markets.
Our economists see a resilient global economy confronting a more difficult inflation 
backdrop. Growth has modestly exceeded their mid-year expectations, led by US 
strength and an investment cycle that is broadening globally. AI capex is critical for  
growth in the US and globally through exports. Despite policy uncertainty following 
the US midterm elections, our equity analysts have maintained their baseline 
investment projections. The energy shock, however, has renewed price pressures, 
complicating the policy response as central banks balance inflation risks against the 
costs of tighter policy. We expect global growth to soften modestly in 2027 before 
returning to its 2026 pace in 2028, as energy headwinds recede. Our expectation of 
a brief tightening cycle assumes that relief materializes, while persistent inflation 
remains the key risk.
Against that global backdrop, regional drivers differ. The US remains the principal 
engine of global growth. Accelerating AI infrastructure investment and robust 
consumer spending supported by wealth and full employment sustain the growth 
momentum in US and contain downside risks to global growth. German fiscal 
support and growth in the periphery underpin Europe’s moderate expansion, while a 
gradually fading deflationary cycle leaves China's growth modest. Across Asia, 
industrial investment is broadening and the cycle is becoming more durable. 
Energy is the principal complication and a clear challenge to our global economic 
outlook. Wide spreads between crude and refined products, alongside differences in 
delivered prices across regions, mean crude prices alone offer an incomplete gauge 
of  inflation. Combined with food and supply-chain pressures, energy costs could 
feed into core inflation, prompting tighter policy even where growth is vulnerable—
particularly in emerging markets. We expect these headwinds to recede by end-
2027, but more persistent inflation crimping growth is a clear risk that we 
contemplate in our downside scenario. 
Continued strong earnings growth underpins our constructive stance on global 
equities. AI remains central to this outlook, as infrastructure investment and 
accelerating adoption support revenue growth and productivity gains. But elevated

That preference extends across credit: we raise US investment grade to equal

equities, we retain a neutral allocation to US Treasuries, maintaining duration 
precisely because our equity exposure is high. With uncertainty and tail risks still 
significant, a constructive stance on risk strengthens the case for portfolio 
protection.
2026 was a difficult year to navigate, and our constructive stance on risk assets for 
2027 does not imply an easy ride. The challenges ahead look different: fewer macro 
shocks in our base case, but greater weight on shifts beneath the surface, with 
increasing focus on evidence of returns to AI capex in hyperscaler earnings and 
broader AI adoption. Our global outlooks, published later today, present the full 
picture, with deeper dives into individual asset classes to follow. Stay tuned.

related spending in October. Warmer weather is also likely to have dented 
clothing stores sales. Among the other components, we expect sales of

building materials were flat. We expect another strong month for restaurant 
sales (up 0,4%m/m). This would be a soft start to 4Q real consumer 
spending: No m/m change in total real spending and a 0.2% m/m decline in 
real goods spending. It's consistent with the slowing we expect: our 
underlying forecasts have consumption growth slow from its 3.8% q/q 
annual rate in 3Q to 1.6% in 4Q, with 4Q GDP up at a 2.2% pace.

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)
Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including 
standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages 
in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, 
as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment 
speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the 
MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even 
though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any 
premium paid.
