Reassessing the Balance of Risks, Neutralizing Growth
After a rare period of index-level calm and violent rotation, global equities have tumbled in past days. While the selloff has been most 
aggressive in Tech, volatility has been broad-based. Against this backdrop, we make several changes in our regional and sector recommendations. We 
tactically downgrade the US,  Global Tech, and European Tech to Neutral (from Overweight); while fundamentals look attractive, positioning remains 
relatively neutral and could therefore adjust further into net short territory. We remain constructive on AI over the medium-term but would look for 
cleaner positioning and more stable AI sentiment (along with steady EPS revisions) before reestablishing our Overweight call. We upgrade Europe ex-UK 
to Overweight (from Neutral); the market remains less sensitive to Tech and is more levered to falling oil prices than peers.  Our global sector strategy is 
now a barbell of Cyclicals and Defensives, with Overweights in Materials and Health Care (upgraded from Neutral). Our market targets remain 
constructive into next year, as lower rates, still-resilient economic data, and potential easing in geopolitical risks should remain supportive. However, 
our work on decelerating EPS growth years (akin to 27E) would still point to incremental Defensive/Quality rotation, even in an up-market environment.
Until recently, global equities had largely moved sideways despite violent rotation beneath the surface. In both the 
US and Europe, Defensives outperformed Cyclicals by as much as 15pp since in a month, while equal weight indices have also outpaced traditional market cap 
weighted peers. We had found  that “steady market, violent rotation” events of similar magnitude are incredibly rare, and typically only occur during major historical 
stress episodes (‘08, ‘20, ’22). Volatility has now turned more broad-based, with a sizable sell-off across regions and sectors in past days.
The investing environment remains tenuous, but risks appear balanced, rather than significantly skewed to the downside. On the 
positive side, oil prices have fallen amid signs of easing geopolitical risks, bond yields (and Fed pricing) are well off highs, and macro data has remained resilient. More 
negatively, though, credit spreads have risen, earnings revisions have decelerated, and AI sentiment has plunged—especially as the result of the US midterms calls into 
question the longer-term trajectory of the AI buildout and capex spend. But ultimately,  valuations have also reset lower across the equity space while 27E EPS forecasts 
remain largely unchanged, and potentially lower energy prices help balance out earnings risks.
We worry that positioning on key indices, such as the Nasdaq, could reset lower into net-short territory (from roughly Neutral levels  as of 
yesterday’s close). For Nasdaq positioning to move to properly negative territory of -3 (on Citi Quant normalised positioning scale of +5 for max long to -5 for max short), we 
would likely need to see another c5% price unwind.
Global earnings should remain strong next year, but markets will need to face down sizable deceleration in the pace 
of growth (expected to fall by c20pp in 27E). We find that global stocks have risen c4% on average in “positive but decelerating" EPSg years akin to 27E, albeit with 
rotation in favor Defensives and Quality. Concentration is another concern, as the share of global EPSg from Tech is rising in 27E, not falling.
We make several changes to the regional and sector recommendations. We first downgrade the US,  Global Tech, and European Tech to Neutral (from 
Overweight). We remain constructive on AI over the medium-term but would look for cleaner positioning and more stable AI sentiment (along with steady EPS revisions) 
before reestablishing our Overweight call. We upgrade Europe ex-UK to Overweight (from Neutral); the market remains less sensitive to Tech and is more levered to falling oil 
prices than peers.  Our global sector strategy is now a barbell of Cyclicals and Defensives, with Overweights in Materials and Health Care (upgraded from Neutral). In Europe, 
we also upgrade Food & Bev to Overweight (from Neutral).

European earnings revisions (ERI) have been consistently strong in past months, remaining in net upgrade territory for 24 consecutive 
weeks. Revisions have also bucked the usual seasonal trend of weakness in the pre-reporting season for the second consecutive quarter. 
This has only happened six times over the past 25y, and points to a modestly more constructive backdrop for European equities than usual..

Bear Market Checklist
Our Bear Market Checklist (BMC) is not intended to be a market-timing tool. Instead, it advises on what to do when the inevitable dips come along. It 
does that by measuring how excessive the previous peak was. The frothier the market high, the less inclined the BMC is to suggest buying into the 
ensuing dip. Our global BMC recently reached 10/18 red flags, its highest point since the GFC. Several factors are contributing to the recent rise in BMC 
flags, including elevated capex growth and megacap IPOs. However, the BMC does not yet signal “overexuberance”, remaining below levels seen ahead of 
past bear markets.
Source: Citi Research, DataStream, MSCI, FactSet, Dealogic, Lipper

Allocation Model Factor Definitions
Correlation to Rates – Regions/Sectors with lower correlation to 2Y UST rates rank higher.
Weak DXY - Regions/Sectors with high price correlation to falling DXY rank higher.
Lower Oil – Regions/Sectors with high price correlation to falling oil prices rank higher.
CESI – Regions with high 3m momentum in CESI ranking higher. Sectors with high price correlation to improving Global CESI rank higher.
Earnings Revisions – We estimate trailing consensus earnings revisions (over 4 weeks), calculated as (upgrades-downgrades)/(upgrades+downgrades).
Improving ERI – Regions are ranked based on 4 week change in 4 weekly ERI. Regions with highest change are ranked higher.
EPS Mo – We measure the percentage change in 12m forward IBES consensus EPS. Regions and sectors with growing/improving earnings rank higher.
Cons EPSg 26E – We calculate expected EPS growth over 2026 based on FactSet consensus. Regions and sectors with greater expected growth rank higher.
Cons DPSg 26E – We calculate expected DPS growth over 2026 based on FactSet consensus. Regions and sectors with greater expected growth rank higher.
12m Fwd PE – We use Citi equity analysts’ recommendations for the underlying companies in Regions and Sectors. We aggregate by Market cap. Those most favored rank 
highest.
Fwd PE vs Median – We compare IBES consensus 12m forward PEs of Regions and Sectors to their long-run median. Those cheaper vs history rank higher.
Cons DY 26E – We calculate expected DPS growth in 2026 based on FactSet consensus. Regions and sectors with greater expected growth rank higher.
FCFY 26E – We calculate 26E FCF Yield using Citi fundamental analysts’ data. We use FactSet consensus data where Citi estimates are not available. Financials and Real 
Estate companies are excluded. Cheaper regions and sectors rank higher.
What’s Priced In – We use our proprietary “What’s Priced In” model to estimate 12m fwd EPS growth embedded in current valuations. Markets with lower expectations 
rank higher.
Positioning – We consider Net positioning calculated by Citi Quants for Regional Indices. Regions with the most net long positioning rank poorly. For Sectors, we 
calculate Market Cap Weighted Long/Short Crowding score using Citi Quants data. Sectors with the highest Net Long Crowding rank poorly.
Citi Analysts – We use Citi equity analysts’ recommendations for regions and sectors underlying companies, weighted by market cap. Those most favored rank highest.
Citi Strategists – We use Citi equity strategists’ regional market targets and sector recommendations. Markets with greater implied upside for mid-2024 – or more cap-
weighted Overweight recommendations – rank higher.

Guide to Citi Research Fundamental Research Investment Ratings:
Citi Research stock recommendations include an investment rating and an optional risk rating to highlight high risk stocks.
Risk rating takes into account both price volatility and fundamental criteria. Stocks will either have no risk rating or a High risk rating assigned.
Investment Ratings: Citi Research investment ratings are Buy, Neutral and Sell. Our ratings are a function of analyst expectations of expected total return 
("ETR") and risk. ETR is the sum of the forecast price appreciation (or depreciation) plus the dividend yield for a stock within the next 12 months.  The 
target price is based on a 12 month time horizon. The Investment rating definitions are: Buy (1) ETR of 15% or more or 25% or more for High risk stocks; and 
Sell (3) for negative ETR. Any covered stock not assigned a Buy or a Sell is a Neutral (2). For stocks rated Neutral (2), if an analyst believes that there are 
insufficient valuation drivers and/or investment catalysts to derive a positive or negative investment view, they may elect with the approval of Citi 
Research management not to assign a target price and, thus, not derive an ETR. Citi Research may suspend its rating and target price and assign “Rating 
Suspended” status for regulatory and/or internal policy reasons. Citi Research may also suspend its rating and target price and assign "Under Review" 
status for other exceptional circumstances (e.g. lack of information critical to the analyst's thesis, trading suspension) affecting the company and/or 
trading in the company's securities. In both such situations, the rating and target price will show as “—“ and “-“ respectively in the rating history price 
chart. Prior to 11 April 2022 Citi Research assigned “Under Review” status to both situations and prior to 11 Nov 2020 only in exceptional circumstances. 
As soon as practically possible, the analyst will publish a note re-establishing a rating and investment thesis. Investment ratings are determined by the 
ranges described above at the time of initiation of coverage, a change in investment and/or risk rating, or a change in target price (subject to limited 
management discretion). At times, the expected total returns may fall outside of these ranges because of market price movements and/or other short-
term volatility or trading patterns. Such interim deviations will be permitted but will become subject to review by Research Management. Your decision to 
buy or sell a security should be based upon your personal investment objectives and should be made only after evaluating the stock's expected 
performance and risk.
