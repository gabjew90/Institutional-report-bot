Europe Open - Reboot pending
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Weak tech sentiment continued to weigh on equity markets as we start a new week, with most indices trading on the
backfoot. Kospi is the worst performer down -3.5% while HK shares relatively outperformed, Nasdaq futures down -1.1%
at print. USTs drifted, while front end JGB yields dropped after a miss in Japan Q3 GDP and BoJ hawk Takata pushing
for a more nimble approach. Oil rally picking up at print - Iran attacked a Bahrain air base, the first since Sep, but still
signaled that the path to end the conflict remains open. A US-Russia-China trilateral meeting with Trump/Putin/Xi has
been confirmed for Thu. FX rangebound, with 160 still offering decent resistance in USDJPY. 
Ahead, Fed Chair Warsh will be of center attention - though he's speaking at a labor market conference (17:00 GMT),
any comments on monetary policy will be closely scrutinized. Also keep an eye on ECB Zigman, Stournaras and
Riksbank Bunge. Canada CPI due at 13:30 GMT with soft trends reiterating expectations of a Dec hold. In EM, activity
data dump in China overnight was net soft. Brazil economic activity, Israel GDP and Poland core CPI are other metrics to
monitor. It's a local holiday in Colombia and Mexico.

HK/China FX Trading: Weekly Traders' Take
In Focus‌
Equities: Tech sentiment remains on the backfoot, with most Asian indices trading in the red barring Hang Seng, which is up
0.5%. Our Asia desk notes that some of the selling could also be driven by funding for upcoming IPOs, with HK shares
resilient thanks to short covering and funds rotating back into laggards. Nasdaq futures lags peers down -1.1% at print. CETS
highlights that while market risk/reward is incrementally better, they are still not dip buyers as positioning driven moves are
unpredictable. Nvidia earnings (Tue after US cash close) is now the largest SPX event priced by options for the next month at
+/- 1.01%. Elsewhere, FT reports that ousted Nvidia CEO Huang is discussing a potential comeback. US Treasury Secretary
Bessent said that Nvidia needs a “dramatic shakeup” in leadership and should go outside the company to find new leadership
Rates: USTs drifted overnight, with street-wide technical issues to start the week keeping volumes slightly lower. Duration
showed little conviction amid a modest rebound in oil prices alongside weakness in broader Asian equities. Flow-wise, the

franchise saw better selling in the intermediates and the long end. Hawkish Fedspeak continued over the weekend, with
Schmid saying that there was nothing restrictive now about rates, and that "inflation is running too hot, we have work to do".
Fed Chair Warsh's speech is in most focus today - Dec OIS pricing stands at ~27.5bp (Bloomberg WIRP) with 88bp priced
into the curve by Sep 2027. 
JGB front-end slightly stronger - Q3 GDP missed estimates coming at -0.1% QoQ vs 1.4% prior, with nominal GDP also
softer at 0.4%. Softer growth trends should unlikely deter the BoJ hiking in Dec (our macro strategist stays paid front-BoJ
meetings) though BoJ hawk Takata also pushed for a more nimble monetary policy approach. "2026 represents the start of a
new phase, in which the Bank should not carry out rate hikes at a fixed pace and size but instead needs to raise the rate
nimbly," he said.
FX: MXN and ILS, well correlated with equity sentiment are on the backfoot, with G10 FX more rangebound. Interbank
volumes in the Asia morning session were running -39% below 30d averages but franchise flows did see leveraged names
buying USD, JPY and HKD, net selling EUR, GBP, AUD, NZD, CHF and THB. USDJPY trades at 159.46 - 160 remains the
key short term barrier and has produced jitters every time its tested. Meanwhile ur Asia spot trader sees more scope for AUD
weakness and continues to like short AUDUSD and AUDJPY. Softer China growth data overnight with soft risk tones is not
helping, with first support to watch at 0.6750. A break would see us test the 200wMA at 0.6650-60. Key events to watch this
week are RBA Nov minutes, followed by the Oct employment report. 
Commodities: Oil prices rally picking up after being largely flat overnight. Iran targeted an air base in Bahrain in a direct
strike, the first since early Sep. Bahrain said that it intercepted and destroyed multiple Iranian aerial attacks on Sunday,
prompting its first public threat alert since Sep too. However, Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said
the path to ending the conflict is open if the US accepts Iran's "logical conditions". Risk sentiment will be tuned in on US-
Russia-China trilateral meeting scheduled Thu. Trump has previously said the meeting would focus on denuclearization, but
he also intends to discuss the war in Iran with Putin and Xi.

Cheatsheet - G10 & EM Week Ahead: Word on the street Headline risk will be elevated this week, with APEC and tech
earnings keeping geopolitical and AI uncertainty high. APEC summit in Shenzhen, China takes centre stage on Nov 18-19,
with Trump, Xi and potentially Putin attending. US-China trade, chip restrictions, Taiwan and Russia-Ukraine and Iran
developments firmly in focus. Nvidia earnings (Tue) should beat though questions on leadership should persist.
The macro calendar is also busy. UK jobs and inflation data will determine whether the MPC will hike in Dec. FOMC minutes
and a heavy Fedspeak (20x), including Chair Warsh will be closely watched after Waller put a 50bp December hike on the
table. Also on the radar: Japan GDP/CPI, Canada CPI, Australian wages and employment, and China’s activity data. Major
US and China retail earnings feature also.
Weekly Macro Brief by Jim McCormick. For this week our senior macro strategist has mainly focused on the still strong case
for selling USTs, with a growing bias toward UST steepeners. Value, inflation and fiscal policy all point to higher UST yields.
The Fed is behind the curve and will stay there, which further points to a steeper curve, higher inflation and, most likely, a
weaker dollar. Globally, BoE hike in Dec is becoming more likely - stay paid front-BoE meetings, stay long back-end gilts
cross-market. BoJ is also looking to hike in Dec - stay paid front-BOJ meetings and look for further 5s-30s JGB flattening. If
odds are rising that De Cos is the next ECB President, it should add further dovish pricing into the front-end of the EUR curve.
Australia's wage and employment data should clarify whether the RBA hiking cycle is done: if it is, there should be further
outperformance of 10y AUD bonds vs USTs.
Elsewhere‌
SEK: Riksbank's Bunge speaks in Stockholm (17:40 GMT). 
EUR: ECB's Vujcic speaks in Frankfurt (08:00 GMT); Zigman at 14:15 and Stournaras at 18:10 GMT. We continue to keep an
eye on ECB President selections, with German leader Merz stating that they intend to make proposals for the top ECB jobs,
which includes replacing Schnabel and Chief Economist Lane, this week. On the data front, Eurozone Industrial Production
SA MoM out for September (both 10:00 GMT). Our economists are above consensus at +0.7% MoM.

USD: Fed Warsh gives opening remarks at the Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference (17:00 GMT) is the most
important event of the day. Unclear whether he references monetary policy, but his comments will be under more scrutiny
after Waller put 50bp on the table for Dec. Barr and Williams speak prior at 09:00 GMT and 14:05 GMT respectively, Empire
Manufacturing for November at 13:30.
CAD: CPI YoY and CPI Ex Food and Energy YoY for October (13:30 GMT). Our economists anticipate a slight acceleration in
headline towards 0.4% MoM (cons. 0.3%) vs 0.1% prior, core trim and median measures are most relevant. With inflation
trends remaining soft, BoC will likely keep rates on hold in the near-term (Citi house view).
CNH: Data dump with activity indicators weakening across the board for Oct. Retail sales dipped to 0.4% YoY on payback
effect from earlier Mid-Autumn Festival and the slump of jewelry sales. Investment growth contraction deepened, as the
monthly rate of -16.1%YoY, creating an all-time low outside Covid. The new economy remained the sole bright spot, with IC
and industrial robot output accelerating. Our economists see building downside risks to our 4.6% YoY forecasts for both
26Q4E and 2026E as domestic demand weakness drags on. Chances of more significant easing still rise in our view despite
the strong consensus of a high bar for policy pivot. Dig deeper.
ILS: Oct CPI decelerated towards -0.1% MoM/1.1% prior, missing consensus estimates at 0.2%/1.4% respectively. Out later -
GDP Annualized for Q3 A (both 11:00 GMT). Bloomberg consensus expects a sharp correction towards 4.5% YoY vs 15.4%
prior.
BRL: Watch trends in the Central Bank Weekly Economist Survey (11:25 GMT). Economic Activity MoM for September
follows at 12:00 GMT. Consensus expects a small moderation towards -0.2% MoM vs 0.34% prior.
KZT: Real GDP NSA YoY for January-September (11:00 GMT). Our economists forecast real GDP growth to accelerate to
4.3% YoY from 4.1% in January-June, reflecting continued strength in non-oil sectors, particularly manufacturing and
construction, as the drag from weaker oil production gradually fades.
PLN: CPI Core YoY for October (13:00 GMT). Our economists estimate core inflation declined to 2.8% from 3.1% on the
partial breakdown of the flash CPI release, suggesting little spillover from high commodity prices to consumer prices.
THB: Q3 GDP jumped to 2.9% YoY vs 1.9% prior, though QoQ growth remained soft at 0.3% against consensus of 0.7% and
a prior of -0.20%.
