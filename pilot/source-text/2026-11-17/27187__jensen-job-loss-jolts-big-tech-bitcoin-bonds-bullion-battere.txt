with a wave of retail dip-buyers dragging stocks into the green, but the selling returned leaving 
stocks red on the day (RTY & NeoClouds ugly, Semis o/p). The initial bounce in stocks

dollar extended its recent gains. As one trader said "today was everything bad, everywhere, all 
at once..."

closed yesterday) and then extending their gains today (as bond yields and brent crude rose). 
The retail dip-buyers ran out of short-squeeze ammo and the afternoon saw stocks fade (but 
remain in their own world from bonds and crude)...

Energy 
The dominant headline driver across the energy complex today continued to be geopolitical risk 
centered on the Strait of Hormuz as oil pushed higher after Trump said that the US could forgo a 
deal with Iran, dimming prospects for a breakthrough to end the war that has roiled Middle East 
supplies. 
“We don’t need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed. We’ve got their money, 
hundreds of billions of dollars, and we’ve got their strait!” Trump wrote in a post on Truth Social 
on Thursday. 
Shortly after, Iran's Army claimed two oil tankers struck mines and caught fire east of the Strait 
of Hormuz, with Iran saying the vessels had switched off their radar systems and ignored 
warnings. 
Bearish supply data from the DOE provided a brief offset to the geopolitical bid, but that didn't 
last, leaving WTI back above yesterday's highs to $89... 
 

 
 
US and EU NatGas rallied today (with the latter notably so) on the back of cold weather 
forecasts and accelerating storage drawdowns. US natural gas futures rose independently of 
the crude complex, driven by a sustained dip in production output. Producers continue to hold 
back supply until regional prices rise to levels that make lifting production more economical.  
 
Higher oil prices meant higher rates and they were glued together in their own (non-equity) 
world today... 
 

 
Rates 
With bonds closed yesterday, it seems traders had some catch-up to do (after yesterday's hot 
CPI, ignoring today's ugly 16 year lows for existing home sales) and dumped bonds as Jensen 
resigned?! In addition to CPI (which reignited fears that inflation remains broad-based and 
sticky, prompting markets to question whether the Fed's gradual pace of hikes is sufficient), 
today also saw the hawkish chorus of Fed officials continue to reinforce that view. 
Cleveland Fed's Hammack, who dissented in favor of a hike last month, reiterated this morning 
that policy needs to move to a restrictive stance to bring inflation back to the 2% target. 
Rate-hike expectations rose once again with a 15% chance of 50bps hike in Dec and a 75% 
chance of a Jan hike too... 
 
But finally and most importantly today, geopolitical developments added fuel to the bond-fire 
with Trump's above comments sending oil prices higher (reinforcing the inflation-risk narrative 
and accelerating the Treasury selloff). The result of those three things (sticky CPI, hawkish 
FedSpeak, higher oil) was a 12-13bps surge in TSY yields on the day (long-end modestly 
outperformed, but was still up 8bps on the day)... 
 

 
 
The 10Y yield surged back above 5.10% (not helped by an ugly 30Y auction)... 
 
Real yields are also jumped today, with the 10-year TIPS yield up bigly today (on track for its 
fifth consecutive daily rise and up 90 basis points year-to-date)... 
 

 
 
...suggesting the move is driven by genuine tightening expectations rather than inflation 
breakevens alone. 
All of which drove the dollar higher...

One more thing from Goldman's Chris Hussey, who offers an interesting historical analog: Steve 
Jobs.  
The Apple CEO went on medical leave in January 2011 and passed away in October 2011. 
Over the ~18 months leading up to that point, Apple's stock de-rated from ~17X to ~12X NTM 
P/E - and the stock traded as low as ~9 NTM P/E in 2013 before recovering to ~15X in the 
following quarters once the company's ongoing continuity of execution with its iPhone and other 
products became apparent. 
We would suggest that the Apple/Jobs analogy overstates the role of key-person risk in the 
multiple compression and understates structural factors. 
 

 
 
Apple’s experience shows that execution continuity can support the stock and that selling purely 
on founder (health) news was a mistake. However, it does not cleanly demonstrate that a 
temporary multiple de-rating caused by key-person uncertainty will reliably reverse once 
continuity is shown, because the multiple itself was already on a longer downward path driven 
by more fundamental reasons.
