US Equities: Framing Risk Into Year End
1. The Fed's continued tightening: Last week’s 0.30% core CPI print put a 50bp hike on the table for
December. While UBS and the market’s base case is for consecutive 25bp hikes in December and January,
there is now a risk of additional hikes given persistent inflation and strong growth, which has been
accelerating. While nuanced, a pivot in the narrative from a three-hike adjustment towards a hiking cycle would
be meaningful, particularly against a backdrop of prolonged Middle East tensions and the AI repricing.
2. AI repricing: The AI trade has shifted from AI beta to AI alpha as the market increasingly questions capex
spending vs. future monetization. This has translated into a good vs. bad balance sheet trade, with funding at
the center. The market has favored companies that can self-fund AI capex vs. those that need capital markets
to stay open. Jensen Huang stepping down as Nvidia CEO has added further uncertainty to the funding
backdrop, given his central role in orchestrating many of the creative financing arrangements supporting the AI
ecosystem.
3. Positioning: Bearish, but not short: Systematic funds have de-risked, discretionary investors have reduced
exposure, and portfolios have rotated away from AI with a safety tilt rather than putting on large directional
shorts. With the Fed, AI funding concerns and geopolitical risk all headwinds, light positioning creates
asymmetric risk around good news. Investors do not need to turn bullish for the market to squeeze; the feared
outcomes simply need not materialize. On the flip side, the bearish undertone has caused investors to reduce
risk, stopping short of actually being short. Full note here.
(Michael Romano, Head of Hedge Fund Derivative Sales, Americas)

Fed Debate Is About The Pace Of Hikes Now - Fed Governor and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said late
Friday he was concerned that higher energy prices were leading to persistent inflation. He said inflation was
the most important signal for policy. Note, alongside John Williams of the NY Fed, Jefferson is the other senior
advisor to Chair Warsh. Both Williams and Jefferson are openly discussing rising inflation risks. The debate
within the Fed is now about the pace of hikes rather than the need to. Last week Governor Waller put the
option of a 50bp hike out there; Dallas Fed’s Logan suggested Dec/Jan 25bp moves might be more
appropriate; Hammack said that it should be more gradual. Over the weekend, Boston Fed President Collins
(non-voter in December) said the direction of monetary policy was clear and she could see broad inflationary
pressures, but she preferred the gradual approach. Kansas City Fed’s Schmid sounded closer to Logan, with
his comments that inflation was running far too hot, there wasn’t anything restrictive about rates and there was
a risk of reinflation is the Fed didn’t do its job. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)

Japan's Q3 GDP Unexpectedly Contracts; BoJ’s Takata Calls For Nimble Rate Hikes  - Japan’s Q3 GDP
contracted 0.1% q/q on an annualized basis, vs. a 0.2% gain forecast and a revised 1.7% increase previously,
marking the first contraction in a year. Consumer spending, the largest GDP component, fell 0.3%. The BoJ
said in its latest Outlook report that household spending had been weak due to adverse weather, including
typhoons and prolonged rainfall. Corporate investment provided a bright spot, rising 1.6% as AI-related
demand continued to support spending. Seperately, hawkish BoJ board member Hajime Takata, said the
central bank should take a more flexible approach to rate hikes as policymaking enters a new phase. "2026
represents the start of a new phase, in which the bank should not carry out rate hikes at a fixed pace and size
but instead needs to raise the rate nimbly," he said. Takata said the policy rate should be moved early to a
“good” level near the midpoint of the estimated neutral rate range, providing room to raise or cut rates when
needed. He added that the BoJ should not be constrained by market expectations over the pace or ceiling of
hikes, but should remain data-dependent.  (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

Huw Pill: BoE Has 'Further To Go' - Bank of England Chief Economist Huw Pill said in an interview with the
Financial Times [paywall] that last week’s 25bp hike to 4% was a “significant step”, but that the bank “has
further to go”, signaling he expects another hike in December and further tightening in 2027. Pill said market
pricing for rates approaching 5% next year was “not outrageous”, with the risks potentially validating some of
the tightening currently priced. The key concern is second-round effects from elevated energy prices. Pill
warned that the absence of evidence is not “evidence of absence”, particularly with inflation expected to peak
around 5% next year and UK growth proving more resilient than anticipated. The comments are in line with his
hawkish lean, with Pill also arguing that the BoE “waited too long” to tighten and that. A Dec. 2026 hike is
priced at a 76% probability, with around 75bp of tightening priced by Jul. 2027 (Wayne Gouro, Macro Sales)
China's October Activity Data Disappoint Across The Board - China’s retail sales rose 0.4% y/y in
October, below the 0.8% forecast and slowing from 1.1% previously. Industrial production increased 4.8% y/y,
also missing the 5.3% expected and slowing from 5.5% prior. Fixed asset investment fell 8.1% y/y in
January-October, worse than the 7% decline forecast and the 7.2% drop in the first nine months. Property
investment remained a key drag, falling 21.6% y/y year-to-date vs. a 21% decline forecast. China’s new and
used home sales also extended declines during the month, falling 0.15% and 0.24% m/m, respectively. China
Economist Yu Song thinks the slowdown in October data shows underlying domestic growth momentum
remains weak. Even the strength in exports was insufficient to offset domestic weakness and maintain broad
stability. Yu expects policymakers to take more administrative measures to ensure Q4 growth remains stable
on a year-on-year basis compared with Q3. He expects data to improve in November and December and
continues to forecast 4.6% growth for the full year. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
