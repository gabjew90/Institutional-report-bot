Chart #4
Could Nvidia’s role in financing the AI 
buildout be about to change?
Goldman expects Nvidia to honour its existing 
commitments. The open question is what happens to 
new deals. If lenders are asking for backstops of 25% 
or more, will Nvidia keep providing them on the same 
terms? Or could new deals get smaller, take longer to 
close, or cost more?
If Nvidia pulls back, how much more would neoclouds 
and AI labs have to pay to borrow, with CCC spreads 
already near the widest levels of this cycle?
The first clue could come on Tuesday, 17 November, 
when Colette Kress reports Q3 results. If she’s asked 
about the $500bn, her answer could give investors 
a sense of how willing Nvidia is to make fresh 
commitments.
Source: Zerohedge, GS, Yahoo Finance

Chart #5
Who really owns Nvidia?
Nvidia has no controlling shareholder. Vanguard and 
BlackRock manage the largest reported blocks of shares, 
while Jensen Huang is the largest individual shareholder 
disclosed in Nvidia’s 2026 proxy, with a 3.41% beneficial 
stake. That figure includes shares held through trusts and 
the Huang family foundation.
 
Source: Kondwelani Kalinda, Kukula Capital
Chart #6
Can SpaceX catch up with Telecom 
giants?
SpaceX’s connectivity revenue grew 69.5% year over 
year in Q3 2026, far ahead of AT&T at 7.2%, T-Mobile 
at 6.3% and Verizon at 2.9%. The business brought in 
$5.25 billion in revenue and $3.49bn in adjusted EBITDA. 
Consumer revenue rose 57%, while government and 
enterprise revenue jumped 91%.
Starlink’s subscriber base doubled over the period, 
even as average revenue per user fell from $83 to $69. 
The telecom giants are still much larger, generating 
roughly four to six times as much revenue across their 
connectivity and service businesses. Starlink’s next 
move could put it in more direct competition with them, 
though, since SpaceX has agreed to acquire nationwide 
low-band spectrum for Starlink Mobile. Can it turn its lead 
in growth into a meaningful share of the mobile market?
Source: Sergey, SergeyCYW
