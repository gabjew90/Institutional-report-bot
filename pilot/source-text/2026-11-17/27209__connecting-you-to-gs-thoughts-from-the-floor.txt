• Policy : What was the market base-case for US data-centre giga-watt production in 2026-28 pre-election,
and how much is at risk after the mid-term political shift?
Geoff Okomoto (GS Office of Government Affairs) paraphrased from the GS Weekend Macro Call… On
the one hand, we are probably past the point at which the US government has no restrictions and no
hurdles to AI … Both at the R&D level and at the physical infrastructure build out… That probably started
to turn about a year ago with the off-cycle elections in Virginia and New Jersey, where power costs arising
from data-centre build-out was cited as an emerging issue... When you look at what the Democrats seized
on as a campaign message in the mid-terms, they really focused on this affordability message …. And they
basically connected that in voters' minds to two things…. One was Trump's tariffs… and that the cost of
their utility bills were linked to the data centre build out …. And that means that there are going to be
some policy headwinds, potentially emerging regulatory headwinds to the build out…
… But it's not a 0 or 1 phenomenon … The Democrats aren't necessarily irrational when it comes to
designing the frameworks for this.. .. A lot of folks have looked to what the incoming governor in Texas
will do… What the new Democrat Governor. has basically said is that she is going to put a pause on new
permits for data centres until the legislature enacts some kind of safeguards and regulatory changes in this
area … . And if you look at what that list is, she wants union labor to be used for the construction of these
facilities…. She wants certain protections in place for the conservation of water…. And either power
plants, or data centres that bring their own power with them, or engage in contract negotiations with the
utilities so that they don't raise the power costs of households… . She wants a repeal of state tax subsidies
that are going to come to these facilities.

… When you look at that list… that's a big difference from what you have today, which is none of those
requirements are currently in place. … But in actuality, some of the hyperscalers have actually begun to
agree to these terms in Texas and in other places… So the threshold for them to comply with some of the
upcoming, foreseen requirements for data centres is actually not too dramatic …. And so to that end, I
don't think there's this dramatic interruption that happens for data centre building as a result of the
midterm elections…
Michael Ng (Media, Cable, Telco Analyst) paraphrased from the GS Weekend Macro Call… Taking a step
back, I think we view data centre moratorium as a temporary pause as communities assess the
environmental impacts and the other impacts to the constituents in that area…. And I think for the
developers themselves, I think it's incumbent upon them to make sure that they do all the community
engagement ahead of and during development to make sure all the constituents that they're impacting are
well aware around what a data centre is , how it might or might not affect the environmental dynamics of a
community, and making sure that they come in and become an active member of the community .. with the
community benefits package that helps to improve the state of the residents that live there in terms of
investments in infrastructure and labor programs…
… And I think ultimately it may cost a little bit more for some of these data centre developers to stand up
their data centres, and in the case of tax breaks and property taxes, potentially adding to the incremental
costs…. I think in the grand scheme of things, that's a very marginal cost relative to standing up the data
center itself …. So I would consider more risk to timing , rather than the capacity coming online…. From
all our checks, the demand side of the equation, from hyperscalers to other types of tenants, is very
strong…
Alec Phillips (Chief US Political Economist) from this piece “On AI and data centre regulation,
Democratic wins at the state and local level are likely to have significant implications . Republicans lost
the gubernatorial elections in Texas, Ohio, Nevada, Georgia, and Michigan, and Democrats gained a
trifecta in Pennsylvania and likely in Arizona as well.
In each of these states, the Democratic side explicitly campaigned on stricter data centre regulation , and
we expect Democratic governors in Texas, Georgia, and Michigan to impose conditional moratoria
pending new legislation restricting data centres. That legislation could end certain tax breaks (a priority for
Hinojosa), tighten environmental, water, and electricity rules, and make it easier for localities to impose
their own moratoria . The states where Democrats are likely to further restrict data centres account for
roughly 45% of planned US data centre capacity , with Texas alone accounting for roughly 25%.
However, since data centres are low on the list of priorities for voters this election, we expect that the issue
will fade somewhat in importance in the ensuing months and that these moratoria will not be long-lived if
they are enacted.”

• Inflation : How bad has the recent trajectory been + is it broad-based enough to seriously question our
benign inflation outlook?
Mike Cahill (Head of FX Strategy) paraphrased from the GS Weekend Macro Call… Cumulatively over
the last 3-4 months, inflation has surprised significantly to the upside, versus both our forecasts and
consensus. .. And I think the most concerning part of those developments is that there's not any particular
element of it that you can really point to and say it's this category that's driving it - it's really been sort of a
lot of small beats along the way.. . And also on the inflation news, some signs of pressures in the pipeline
as well…
… I think what we're seeing is policymakers responding to that and also somewhat responding to the
footsteps they hear from the echoes of 2021 and 2022… I think that does set up for further dollar strength
in the near term .. I do think that there is more to run on this theme as we switch and start to, for right now,
focus on just how much more adjustment policy needs to do, and how the composition looks a little bit
different in the US versus the rest of the world…
… It's going to be a little while before markets can start to be more comfortable that we're safely on the
downward trajectory on inflation. .. That probably is at least until we see the turn-of-the-year inflation
trends at the start of next year with the annual resets - what those look like and just how much pressure
that will bring…. I think until we can get more comfortable around that trajectory, then probably we
continue to see this tailwind in the dollar .
.. And what I'm especially watching for over that period of the next few months is: Does the policy
tightening that we get in the US start to expose those vulnerabilities that we saw a few months ago in
places like Europe.. As tighter financial conditions in the US spill over, do we start to see signs of
divergence picking up again… That's really what has kept FX returns and FX volatility more suppressed
all year…. I think that if we have this dynamic where energy prices are moving lower, but some of the
other inflation pressures are still there, which is what we've had over the last month or so - then that is
something that I think can create more dollar strength , particularly against G10, where these policy
differentials tend to have a much bigger influence…
Ronnie Walker (US Economics) from the GS Weekly FX Trader Call… On Tuesday’s CPI, what was
probably most concerning to me in terms of the composition of the details...was that the noisy categories
we spend a lot of time forecasting did print to the downside … You had benign airfares after having, for
most of this year, very large increases in airfares…. You had a large decline in the health insurance
component…. You had a large decline in used car prices,... So despite all this downward pressure from the
noisy components, you still had an overall print that came in at 30bp..
.. Going forward we're still a way from where we were several years ago when we're talking about
sequential inflation, but nonetheless this is where you would say this is unacceptably high inflation ... I'm
not sure you'd call this the kind of report that makes you say you should abandon this view that inflation is
ultimately going to be a somewhat transitory process, but it makes it harder to fully embrace that…

… Over the next few months, I think you should be moving things a little bit higher from what I had
thought would be favourable reports… I had a mid-teens run rate for November and December core CPI..
and now I'm thinking maybe I should pencil in a little bit more shelter strength , maybe there's a little bit
more upward pressure from oil prices.. and so, instead of mid-teens, you're talking maybe high teens, low
20s-ish … And so, again, it's not really a sea change, but you are moving it in a hotter direction …
Andrew Lavenberg (US Inflation Trading) : “The two most recent inflation prints have certainly been
hotter than both forecasters and the market expected, with signs of strength showing up across the index .
For the September print there was upside in both primary housing (Rent/OER) as well as lodging, which
combined with broad-based firmness across the rest of the index. In October, we again saw high OER but
also got hotter than anticipated vehicle insurance, recreation goods/services, and medical services.
On net we're tracking ~10bps higher on a Core MoM basis compared to consensus forecasts and the
market for each of the last 2 months. In terms of the overall trajectory we see from here and the challenge
this presents to our prior baseline view, I think that it really hinges on the path for shelter inflation. If we're
moving towards a 30bps MoM run rate for OER versus a 20bps run rate, that's pretty material and will put
a floor on where we can realize heading into the start of 2027 where we get a lot of annual price resets.
However, if we revert to our baseline shelter trend from here, we still think that the risk of a material
reinflation is muted next year .”
Guillaume Banderet (Head of European Inflation Trading) : “We believe European December holiday
period and Q1-27 to be key turning points for underlying inflation dynamics , as cost pressures are set to
broaden to processed food, annual services contract resets, and further non-energy industrial goods.
Airlines – low on jet fuel hedges for most of next year – are expected to offload part of the higher costs,
with fare hikes up to 20% in yoy terms. German inflation, with a weight of over a quarter of the European
basket, should converge higher as utilities pass 2026 wholesale energy increases into retail household
tariffs. Overall, we project Eurozone core inflation to average 2.9% next year , with headline numbers
reaching 4.4%, the highest since Q3-23.
Finally, we observe that the share of the xEnergy basket growing above 3% is growing, mainly led by
goods but food is not far behind. This is typical of the early stage of the cycle . Usually services follow 6-
12m later but that remains uncertain as we don’t have enough information about wage forward wage
growth. We continue to think the market is too complacent about inflation .”
• Brazil : Are investors still in the trade, what is important to watch, and any change on outlook for
Brazilian assets?
Alberto Ramos (Head of Latam Economics) on the Econ/Politics : “We are focused on three areas: (1) The
credentials and policy preferences of the new Finance Minister ; (2) The fiscal strategy for the next 3-4
years, including the quality (revenue increases or spending cuts) and nature (short-term or permanent) of
the measures needed to deliver the primary fiscal path; and (3) The new central bank directors , and
whether their appointments tilt the Copom toward a more dovish, heterodox composition.

The fiscal package submitted to Congress on Wednesday failed to impress the market , as the measures
were mostly short-term and skewed toward raising revenue. With fragile fiscal and debt dynamics and an
unsettled BRL, the market is looking for strong, credible policy signals.”
Fabiano Ribeiro (MD, Macro Sales Brazil) on Macro : “Most investors have stepped back from the trade
and the mood is defensive . Several large macro funds have closed their Brazil FX, rates and equity
positions and are waiting on clarity around the economic team; some have zeroed long BRL positions ,
keeping only received real rates; and the big asset managers are reluctant to re-add risk after equities lost
over R$700bn in market cap. Foreigners are still net sellers in the futures exchange.
What I'm watching : the Finance Minister pick, the credibility of any fiscal package, and — increasingly a
bigger risk — the Central Bank board nominations , where heterodox names would hurt BCB credibility.
On top of that, a hawkish Fed and increasingly weaker domestic growth / higher inflation outlook keeps
the backdrop tough.
Net, our outlook is more cautious : Brazil looks cheap but risks being a value trap. A re-rating really needs
external help (weaker dollar, lower oil) plus credible appointments — not domestic momentum.”

FX/EMFX Carry : How it fits: Growth is the dominant driver for strategy performance as we see in the
below regime analysis that the strategy outperforms in periods of stable/increasing growth.
FX carry remains an attractive source of return in the current environment. The key conditions for carry
remain broadly supportive: (a) Realised FX volatility is relatively contained and might further decrease
once rates vol starts normalizing; b) US + global growth remains robust and; c) Wide, persistent rate
differentials continue to provide meaningful carry between high-yielding currencies such as BRL, MXN
and ZAR and lower-yielding funding currencies.
Brent Vol RV via Front-to-Back Expiries : How it fits: A continuation of the status quo would mean that
the still elevated implied vol levels for short-dated ATM strikes offer an attractive premium versus benign
realized moves that fail to outpace the implied breakevens. For protection against a Brent tail move in
either direction we like buying longer dated put+call wings.
Brent volatility offers an attractive asymmetric setup. While outright volatility remains elevated, realised
moves have struggled to justify current breakevens , making short gamma positions attractive to own. A
continuation of the status quo in the Middle East should keep this premium elevated. Also, near term stress
remains priced in the market as the front-to-back 1m vs 6m term structure continues to trade at 11 vols in
backwardation . This makes owning put+call wings on longer dated 6-month maturities an interesting
opportunity to pair with the short ATM gamma position. Therefore, we like trading the Brent front-to-back
vol RV by selling ATM straddles on 1m and buying OTM strangles on 6-month expiries .
