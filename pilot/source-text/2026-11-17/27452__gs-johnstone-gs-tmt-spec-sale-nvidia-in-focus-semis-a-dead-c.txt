SEMIS mixed investor views late last week on whether this is a dead cat bounce and we will go lower or
whether we have now bottomed. Post-Huang, the semiconductor debate has shifted beyond Nvidia itself to
the AI ecosystem’s dependence on its funding capacity, multi-year pre-purchase commitments, and
supplier relationships. The primary concern is no longer near-term physical supply disruption—backlogs
and TSMC allocations remain intact—but whether the company under interim CEO Colette Kress (and
any permanent successor) will sustain the same aggressive balance-sheet deployment that has underwritten
demand at neoclouds (Nebius Q3 guide cut on power delays did not help sentiment around “execution
risk), independent labs, and inference providers. See Jim’s take. Nvidia has functioned as lender of last
resort through large capacity backstops, residual-value guarantees, and equity stakes; a more conservative
stance could expose financing-dependent demand. Parallel risks include tighter China controls (including
potential MATCH Act revival, which is more negative for ASML than ASM) and any Chinese retaliation
that hits the broader hardware chain. Continued reliance on Chinese open models remains a secondary
vulnerability for some inference businesses.
The core investor debate is now structural. Nvidia’s strategic edge rested heavily on Jensen’s personal
relationships, willingness to pre-commit capacity aggressively, deep Asian supplier ties, and credibility to
underwrite long-duration demand. The key uncertainty is whether new leadership will have the same
appetite or authority to deploy the balance sheet at similar scale. Physical supply is unlikely to break near-
term, but the durability of the pre-commitment model is less clear (2028 is the key focus point). Spying-

related allegations raise the odds of broader US/China measures that could further pressure equipment
names. Longer term debates also on the impacts of custom silicon and what happens as ASIC share rise.

This week: Several key 'AI' catalysts including NVDA (Tues), Tencent (Wednesday and reads to Prosus)
and BABA (Thurs), Nvidia reports on Tuesday night, with sellside consensus looking for $108.8–109.3bn

14-day RSI at 24, the lowest since 2019, the key question is whether results and guidance can restore
confidence. Investors want a clear revenue beat, gross margins at or above the 74% guide and reassurance
on the prior expectation of roughly 70% revenue growth in FY2028. Vera Rubin’s rollout is central: local
press reports suggest HBM4 shortages could push volume shipments from year-end into the first half of

commitments are also in focus.
See AJ write up on Alibaba and Tencent that have both been under pressure, with both shares down ~36%
YTD and investor engagement subdued, but fundamentals around AI/cloud are improving. For Alibaba,
the near-term debate is dominated by chip access, heavy AI infrastructure spending and funding
requirements, although GS expects cloud growth to accelerate to 53% with margins improving to 12.5%,
while Quick Commerce losses remain a drag. Tencent faces a similar capex/funding debate, with investors
also focused on its HY strategy and WorkBuddy AI monetisation; GS expects 3Q revenue +9% YoY,
supported by resilient gaming, advertising and accelerating cloud, but AI investment weighing on margins.
More broadly, GS sees an improving setup for China Internet into 2027, with H-share Internet profit
growth accelerating from-3% in 2026 to +18% in 2027, contrasting with slowing earnings growth in
Korea/Taiwan, while renewed KWEB/FXI inflows suggest positioning may finally be starting to turn.
