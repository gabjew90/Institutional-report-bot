fell 1% vs. SPY’s -1.9% decline. Mag 7 (-2.9%) were the main laggard: NVDA -4.1%, 
GOOG -3.8%, TSLA -2.7%, AMZN -3.3%, META -2.4%. The AI trade took another leg

Huang's ouster fuels doubts about Nvidia's support for neoclouds and the wider pool of AI

Thematically, Cyclicals vs. Defensives -4.6%, Value L/S +2.6%, Recession L/S +2.0%

2.0bp, 2y -1.0bp, 10y -0.4bp to 5.13%, 30y +0.9bp), reversing part of last week's CPI-
driven rise in yields at the front and intermediate maturities, while DXY rose 0.2%. Fed's 
Warsh said staff are gaming out contingencies on AI but stayed away from policy, and 
Hammack said inflation is moving the wrong way. WTI fell 3.6% to $85.36, silver 3.6%, 
copper 1.4% and gold 0.8%.

broad ex-AI rotation made Europe a relative outperformer from the tech-led selloff, 
coming off the SXXP’s best week in more than four months and a fresh all-time high last 
Thursday. EU Defence and Luxury led; Healthcare outperformed with NOVO +2.4% on

floated the Donbas-for-NATO idea (The Hill) ahead of Thursday's Trump/Xi/Putin 
meeting. UKX -0.2%, SX5E -0.6%, SXXP -0.3%, DAX -0.6%.

TOMORROW 
•         RETAIL SALES PREVIEW (here) – Feroli expects October headline retail sales to 
be flat MoM vs. +0.1% survey vs. +0.8% prior, with gasoline station sales slowing to 
+0.5% from +6.4% and vehicle dealer and parts sales falling 1.1%, “informed by a 2.7% 
decline in unit auto sales”. He added that “Excluding autos and gas, retail sales grew 
0.2% in September, which we noted as a return to normal after some volatility from the 
change in timing of Amazon’s yearly Prime Day event. Overall, as the earlier boost to 
spending from the tax stimulus continues to fade and consumers remain pressured by 
high gas prices, overall consumption could begin to tick slightly lower after the strong 
prints in the recent quarter. Food services have been a steady boost to headline sales, 
and our Chase card data points to a decent 0.4% rise in October, a slightly lower pace 
than recent months. The control group (which excludes food services, autos, gas, and 
building material stores) is expected to rise 0.2%, also informed by our card data. The 
weakness in the September report was mostly from declines in general merchandise, 
sporting goods, and miscellaneous retailers, most of which had risen sharply in August. 
Building material sales could rebound after a 0.6% contraction in September, but remain 
at a muted overall level, as employment in the sector fell over 15k through the month.” 
•         NVDA THOUGHTS (JOSH MEYERS) – There’s a tremendous amount of frustration 
with Jensen for his behavior/indiscretions, and many people believe there may be more 
negative headlines to come (and that perhaps the board already knows). The view is not 
universal, however: there are now petitions to bring Jensen back, and some folks are 
painting this as an ambush by vested interests who want (among other things) to ensure 
the primacy of closed models.  
o   Still lots of questions about who will succeed Jensen, with the list above 
covering the most common suggestions. Rene Haas – a former NVDA 
employee who’s not afraid to think big – and Lip Bu Tan have also come up 
on a lot of conversations. I’ve actually also heard an unexpected view from 
a few bulls that the bench is deeper than we think, and that “it’s not like the 
stock was some great outperformer this year.”  

o   Many folks are concerned that any successor will have an uphill battle. 
Internally, NVDA has a deeply personal and unique culture, and many key 
engineers are now quite wealthy; retaining them and keeping the culture 
are going to be a balancing act for the board. Meanwhile, Jensen has left 
big shows to fill, best explained by the Ming-Chi Kuo tweet I pasted below. 
Additionally, many articles over the past two days have underlined Jensen’s 
role in leveraging circular financing to build and support the ecosystem; that 
seems unlikely to continue at the same rate under a new CEO.  
o   There is a real sense that there are larger geopolitical factors at play 
behind the scenes - including a strategic move by China hawks (led by 
Rubio?) to shut the door on exports to China – and that Trump will be under 
pressure to out-hawk the hawks. The expectation is that this will be bad for 
open source models, and potentially also bad for NVDA. Separately there 
some optimism that NVDA’s grip on the supply chain may wane – 
potentially a positive for competitors (also viewed as a potential positive for 
HBM prices, which were reportedly lower for NVDA).

US MKT INTEL VIEW 
•         US MKT INTEL – We are Tactically Bullish. Adding to the bullish bullets from 
above, we think positioning is cleaner and thus entry points are more attractive. While the 
AI trade has given the market more to think about, demand is insatiable but the pace of 
the build out may be slower, which is not necessarily a bad thing as the commercial 
adoption rate and productivity gains increase. Assuming this week’s events unfold in 
bullish fashion, we look at inflation as being the primary risk but view that as more of a 
problem for early 2027 as we think it is highly unlikely that the Fed moves to hike by 50bp 
in Dec, which would create a tactical headwind to the Equity bull case.  
•         MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 16) – The Anti-AI theme / ex-Tech rotation has been 
dominant in 26H2, even more so over the last month as Energy gains have flipped, the 
recession narrative has emerged, and investors have positioned more defensively. We 
think moving back toward Tech makes sense and like the cautious approach detailed in 
the previous Monetization Menu with primary exposure to Mag7 and then using options to  
play a rebound in the AI themes. It seems most likely that the market will be patient with 
seeing how the catalysts play out rather than getting ahead of those events. Look for 
earnings, capital markets, and the JPM APAC Tech Conf to be a positive catalysts. In 
terms of ex-Tech plays, we prefer Cyclicals over Defensives as the growth narrative 
reboots and markets digest an incrementally more hawkish Fed. Recall, SPX earnings 
are more tied to nominal GDP growth than to real GDP growth meaning increases in 
inflation are not a bad thing for earnings and that profit margins near record levels 
suggest that pass-through is not a problem in the near-term. We continue to like Europe 

for some Cyclical / Value exposure given relative positioning and valuation, though 
another surge in the USD may temper tactical outperformance.
