U.S. Markets Recap 
US -0.40% | Nasdaq -0.91% | Dow +0.10% 
Synopsis 
 
US equities finished mostly lower on Friday but ended well off session lows, with the S&P 500 down 0.40%, the Nasdaq down 0.91% and 
the Dow up 0.10%. Major indices still posted solid weekly gains and breadth was positive, with the equal-weight S&P outperforming the 
cap-weighted index by more than 60 bp. The AI trade was broadly lower, with neoclouds, memory and semiconductors weaker and Nvidia 
down again after Jensen Huang's removal as CEO. Rotation rather than risk-off dominated, with energy and defensives gaining. Treasury 
yields rose 3-4 bp at the long end, the dollar index rose 0.2% near a fresh 19-month high and WTI crude settled down 0.2%. 
 
October core PPI rose 0.3% m/m, in line with forecasts, while headline PPI rose 0.3% against a 0.4% consensus. The cooler print prompted 
downward revisions to October core PCE estimates and supported expectations for a 25 bp Fed hike in December, with futures implying 
roughly a 10% chance of 50 bp. Fedspeak leaned hawkish, with Collins and Goolsbee flagging broadening inflation, while Williams said the 
economy does not appear out of balance.

 
Software, networking, aerospace and defence, and quantum computing lagged, while energy, larger-cap banks, payments and pharma out-
performed. Liftoff Mobile rose 30.6% on a Q3 beat and above-Street guidance, Modine rose 27.9% on a Bloomberg report of acquisition 
talks with Samsung Electronics, and Cerebras rose 11.2% after guiding Q4 well ahead of expectations. Cisco fell 6.2% despite a beat and 
above-consensus guidance as analysts flagged margin pressure from hyperscaler mix, and CommVault fell 8.5% after its CFO's departure. 
Macro 
 
Nvidia removes Jensen Huang as CEO; Kress named interim 
Nvidia's board removed Jensen Huang as CEO in a widely expected move following revelations of extramarital affairs. CFO Colette Kress 
was named interim CEO, with the company saying it will consider internal and external candidates for a permanent replacement, and Nvidia 
reportedly approached ARM CEO Rene Haas as a potential successor. The scandal widened the stock's weekly decline to 11%, with the 
shares hitting their lowest since July 2025 before ending off session lows. Lack of succession planning had been a discussion point this week 
alongside Huang's importance to the broader AI ecosystem, and the development raised concern about Nvidia's increasingly prominent role 
as a source of funds, with Nvidia-backed neoclouds coming under pressure on Thursday. Nvidia's nearest competitors AMD and Broadcom 
are seen positioned to capture any share gain. 
 
October PPI cooler than expected, core in line 
October headline PPI rose 0.3% m/m versus a +0.4% consensus and September's revised +1.4% (was +1.3%). Core PPI rose 0.3% m/m, in 
line with consensus, while September's core was revised to +0.8% (was +0.7%). Annualized headline PPI increased to 6.5% versus 6.6% 
consensus, the highest since Nov-22, and annualized core was +5.0%, in line with consensus. Goods prices rose 0.5%, with food +1.3% and 
energy -0.1%, including a 7.9% decline in diesel. Services prices increased 0.2%, a fifth consecutive advance, driven by transportation and 
warehousing (+2.0%), particularly truck freight (+3.9%). PCE-relevant components were mixed, with airline passenger services down 1.2% 
(September +4.8%), hospital outpatient care up 0.8% (flat), physician care up 0.1% (unchanged) and portfolio management up 0.7% 
(+0.9%). 
 
NY Fed's Williams says more tightening needed, but economy not overheating 
Speaking in New York, New York Fed President Williams reiterated that further rate hikes are necessary to reach the 2% inflation target, 
though he did not say when the next hike will come or how long the cycle will last. He cautioned that the Fed must not harm its maximum 
employment mandate, said the labor market seems to be strengthening, and said he does not yet see signs of the economy overheating. His 
comments were more measured than other recent hawkish Fedspeak, including Governor Waller earlier this week, who said he is open to a 
50 bp increase in December in a hotter inflation scenario. Markets have priced in a sharply steeper hiking path this week, with a ~13% 
chance of a 50 bp hike in December and 85 bp of hikes through year-end 2027, up 39 bp w/w. Elsewhere, Chicago's Goolsbee said inflation 
releases are going the wrong way and price pressures are spreading due to overheating demand, Boston's Collins said additional hikes may 
be needed soon, SF's Daly said it is hard to say policy is restrictive enough and that her bias for rates is up, and Dallas Fed's Logan, a voter, 
stressed keeping purchase operations separate from the policy stance. 
 
Oil heads for second weekly decline on US-Iran stalemate hopes 
WTI crude is on track for a second consecutive weekly decline amid expectations that both the US and Iran will avoid renewed military 
conflict. A prolonged stalemate is emerging as a potential path, with Trump maintaining economic pressure while midterm losses weigh 
against further escalation. However, hopes for easing tensions have been offset this week by continued attacks on Hormuz shipping, Houthi 
strikes on Saudi oil infrastructure and Trump's assertion that the US does not need an Iran deal. October saw 36 attacks on commercial 
vessels, the most since the war began. Meanwhile, the IEA said Friday that the oil market is expected to return to surplus this quarter for 
the first time since the war began, citing a significant hit to consumption this year and a faster recovery in Gulf exports. OPEC+ reported 
Wednesday that its crude output recovered to 97% of pre-war February levels last month.

Synopsis 
 
European equity markets ended mixed on Friday, off their best levels of the session, with the DAX down 0.1%, the CAC up 0.1% and the 
FTSE 100 up 0.4%; the STOXX 600 was unchanged but recorded its largest weekly gain in almost four months. Afternoon trading came 
under some pressure from doubts over the AI trade, with UK-based neocloud Nscale warning of widening losses and the need for more cash 
before an IPO. Government bonds were broadly weaker, with the French 10-year yield up 8 bp against 3 bp for Bunds, widening the OAT-
Bund spread to around 154 bps, while the UK 10-year gilt yield rose 2 bp to 5.34%. 
 
The eurozone's second Q3 GDP estimate confirmed growth of 0.4% q/q, double expectations, and UK GDP grew 0.7% q/q, its fastest in 
more than two years. Higher energy prices lifted hawkish rhetoric, with ECB chief economist Lane saying a December hike would be prudent 
if data are unchanged and warning inflation could exceed 4% by year-end, and BoE Deputy Governor Lombardelli calling December and 
February live meetings. European gas prices rose above €85/MWh. 
 
Utilities, oil and gas, and food and beverage outperformed, while technology, basic resources and industrials lagged. Wise rose 7.7% after 
settling a Belgian prosecutor probe without admitting wrongdoing and announcing partnerships with two major North American banks. 
Richemont fell 3.8% as slower-than-expected growth in its Jewellery Maisons division outweighed a modest group-sales beat, ams-OSRAM 
fell 9.1% on a Q4 revenue guide below consensus, and Eramet fell 10.6% after launching a rights issue of about €500M. 
Macro 
 
Energy shock lifts hawkish ECB and BoE talk; euro-area and UK growth beat 
Ongoing energy shock influenced European macro talking points this week, with European gas prices rising above €85/MWh and expecta-
tions of higher-for-longer energy prices. This kicked up hawkish rhetoric among central bankers, with ECB Chief Economist Lane saying a 
December hike would be prudent if the data remain unchanged, warning inflation could exceed 4% by year-end, while BoE Deputy Governor 
Lombardelli described December and February as live meetings. Improving growth data offered some reassurance, with Eurozone Q3 GDP 
expanding 0.4% q/q, double expectations, and UK GDP growing 0.7% q/q, its fastest quarterly expansion in more than two years. Press and 
economists highlighted technology and AI investment becoming increasingly important UK growth drivers, but hiring has slowed and hous-
ing-market sentiment has deteriorated as higher mortgage rates weigh on affordability. KPMG warned that further energy shocks could 
undermine the recovery as households and businesses have already drawn down financial buffers. On fiscal and political pressures, Germa-
ny's 2027 budget envisages €210.4B in new borrowing, while France's fiscal and political uncertainty continued to weigh on sovereign 
spreads, though nowhere near October's pressure. 
 
UK close to breakthrough on EU reset deal ahead of next week's summit 
INews reported that the UK is close to reaching a historic new deal with the EU at next week's summit. While talks will likely go to the wire, 
a source said there is increasing optimism on both sides for the UK's inclusion in the EU's Made in Europe legislation. UK PM Burnham has 
made this a priority amid warnings that exclusion could damage some sectors of the economy. One EU source said inclusion was all but 
guaranteed if the UK agreed to lower tuition fees for EU students and a hike in tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles. The wider issue in focus 
is whether next Friday's summit will include any discussion on the future relationship after Burnham floated the possibility of rejoining the 
EU and free movement after the next election. Burnham has signaled his intention to give a speech after the summit to set out possible 
options on moving closer longer-term (Bloomberg). 
 
Europe gains a seat at Ukraine talks, but Putin's conditions raise trap fears 
President Putin agreed to include European representatives in the next round of US-brokered Ukraine peace talks (Reuters). Envoy Kushner 
described the step as important for addressing postwar security alongside territorial issues, while noting progress toward a prisoner ex-
change (FT). Ukrainian President Zelenskiy welcomed the prospect and hoped for a de-escalation agenda (Bloomberg). Yet the opening is 
conditional and contested (Euronews): Putin urged Washington to clarify whether Europe seeks 'peace or war', warned that Western troops 
in Ukraine after a deal would be seen as a threat and restart fighting, and continues to press for a redesigned European security architecture, 
with limits on NATO expansion, eastern-flank forces and drills, as a precondition. European leaders rejected the push, with EU Council 
President Costa insisting Europe already had a functioning security order that Russia destroyed by invading Ukraine. Officials warned that 
linking a seat at the table to broader architecture talks risks a trap that diverts focus from a ceasefire. Divisions persist over who should 
represent the EU, and significant gaps remain on territory and guarantees. 
 
Italy's 2027 election race tightens as centre-left coalition sets timetable 
Italy heads into the 2027 general election with two main coalitions locked in a near-dead heat under the new Stabilicum electoral law. 
Recent polls put PM Meloni's Fratelli d'Italia at ~28%, the Democratic Party at ~20.5% and Five Star Movement at ~12.5-13%, while Rob-
erto Vannacci's Futuro Nazionale slipped to ~6%. Aggregators show the centre-left 'campo largo' at 43-45%, just ahead of or level with the 
traditional centre-right without Vannacci (Repubblica). PollingForecast gives the centre-left a projected 224 seats and a 63% chance of a 
majority if the campo largo holds together; the outcome would reverse if Vannacci sides with the centre-right. Meloni has ruled out an 
alliance with Vannacci (Politico), whose stance on Ukraine and Europe could be politically costly for her coalition. Markets are already 
discounting some political risk into Italian bond spreads, albeit much less than France (Teleborsa), and prediction markets still see Meloni

 
UK government weighs welfare cuts for under-25s to tackle youth unemployment 
Senior Labour MPs are signaling willingness to consider welfare reforms that could reduce benefit payments for young people to incentivize 
them into work (FT). This comes ahead of the Milburn review, which is expected to recommend upfront investment of billions in support, 
including placements, job subsidies and personal advisors (Guardian, Telegraph). Under the recommendations, under-25s would no longer 
be eligible to claim Pip and the health element of universal credit and would instead get a new youth allowance paid at the standard rate. 
The London Times reported that PM Burnham is expected to back the key recommendations, which will apply to both existing and new 
claimants, and that early indications are that any rebellion is limited to the hard left of the party. Ministers are preparing to respond to the 
Milburn report next Tuesday. The issue is sensitive: former PM Starmer had to abandon his welfare reform plans due to backbench oppo-
sition, though moderate Labour MP Hiller, who played a key role in that rebellion, has said the plans set out so far are very different to

Japan +0.53% | Hong Kong -1.55% | China -0.59% 
Synopsis 
 
Asian equities fell again on Friday. Japan's Nikkei and Topix and Taiwan's Taiex stayed positive, but the Kospi fell. Greater China boards

 
The yen stalled just short of 160 per dollar, with Japan's finance minister and currency diplomat warning over intervention, while a dovish

activity, with industrial production seen at 5.3% y/y and retail sales at 0.8%. India's October CPI rose to its highest in two years, breaching 
the RBI tolerance band. 
 
Chinese internet and AI stocks fell sharply on concerns about tighter US chip export rules, with Z.AI, MiniMax and Alibaba among the 
decliners in Hong Kong, while the tech giants in South Korea and Taiwan proved more resilient. Samsonite rose 5.3% on Q3 results ahead 
of estimates, Advantest rose 2.1% on plans to lift inspection equipment capacity, and Citizen Watch rose 1.2% after raising FY guidance.

Macro 
 
US lawmakers move to tighten chip exports to China 
GOP House Speaker Johnson noted a growing consensus among lawmakers on tightening chip exports to China (Reuters). Congress is 
looking to pass four bills on chip exports, which would give it more oversight of advanced chip exports, force tech companies to track the 
location of advanced chips, pressure US allies to curb exports of advanced chipmaking equipment to China, and give the Commerce Depart-
ment power to prevent Chinese firms from renting offshore data centers to access advanced chips. Reuters sources noted China CSPs and 
smaller AI labs are racing to secure Nvidia chip capacity ahead of any tightening of rules. There is some concern that any tightening could 
affect US-China ties after Beijing warned against using the affair as a pretext for imposing a 'tech blockade.' The bipartisan push comes 
amid growing scrutiny over the White House's ties to Huang, with the spotlight on Commerce Secretary Lutnick following reports that his 
department refused to add companies including Megaspeed to a trade blacklist and was lax in overseeing chip diversions to China in viola-
tion of export controls. Reuters sources said senior White House officials were discussing whether Lutnick should be pushed out, though a 
White House official said Trump retains confidence in him. Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott called on Huang and Lutnick to testify before 
the panel next month (Politico). 
 
China activity data seen off to a sluggish start to Q4 
Bloomberg consensus looks for softer growth in manufacturing output and consumption in October, with the lone consolidation being a 
slightly narrower decline in investment. Industrial production is expected to rise 5.3% y/y versus 5.5% in the previous month, following 
soft PMI data. Concerns about the sustainability of the AI buildout are raising questions about China's dependence on exports given the 
absence of other growth drivers. Retail sales are also expected to slow to 0.8% from 1.1%. Weak auto demand was again cited as the key 
drag on private consumption, with CPCA data showing passenger car sales tumbled 27% in October, the 13th straight slide. Waning subsi-
dies compound underlying soft consumer sentiment. Fixed asset investment is projected to fall 7.0% YTD, following a 7.2% decline in Jan-
Sep. The data are seen as significant for measuring the impact of policy support measures announced in recent months, and attention is 
already shifting to the Central Economic Work Conference to see the policymaker response to fading growth momentum.

Hon Hai Precision Industry (2317-TW) Q3 headline metrics beat consensus. Overall FY guidance remained positive, underpinned by Cloud 
& Networking, while other segments were upgraded. The company expects 'high double-digit growth' in AI rack shipments both q/q and for 
2027. In the wake of recent AI volatility, Chairman Young Liu said on an earnings call they see no signs of changes in AI demand for the

ronment as well as volatility in inflation and FX. It noted industry implications from US midterm elections are limited in the short term, 
but longer-term effects need to be monitored, and expressed caution about supply chain tightness, mainly for the PC segment, which could 
persist for some time.

Yen stalls near 160 on Fed hike bets, dovish BOJ speak and verbal intervention

the latest Fedspeak that put a potential 50 bp move into market consciousness. Overnight price action is suspected to have been driven by

 
BOJ's Asada remains cautious on rate hikes 
In a speech, BOJ board member Asada's dovish stance was largely based on the lagged and cumulative effects of prior rate hikes, which he 
has stated take at least a year to emerge. He implied this alone was sufficient to balance the board's consensus view of upside risks to 
inflation from the Middle East, AI and FX factors. He also noted that with the policy rate now in the estimated neutral range (1.1-2.5%), 
each additional rate hike will have a greater impact, especially compared with the start of the rate hike cycle. Asada did not raise major
