ETD Rates Watch: Treasuries Muted; Fed
Inflation Fears Build As Warsh Remarks
Loom
Daniel Noorian
16-Nov-2026, 03:10 ET (EST)
Asian equities were weaker as fears over tech continued to dominate price action. The Nikkei dropped 2.42%,
while the Kospi was off 3.59%. The largest fall came in Taiwan, down 6%. Hong Kong bucked the trend, with
the Hang Seng and China H shares both up around 0.5%. Events at Nvidia and Microsoft continue to cast a
shadow over the sector, with the former reporting quarterly results this week. None of this seems to have
bothered analysts elsewhere, who have recommended “max overweight” on global stocks due to an intact AI
capital spending cycle. 
It was a fairly quiet session for fixed income markets, with TY futures trading in a 5+ tick range, stuck between
105-10 and 105-04+. Despite the narrow range, activity was decent, with a number of block trades posted.
There was a seller of 3,165 TYZ6 and 6,164 FVZ6 early in the session. This was then followed by a 2s10s
steepener around 0630 GMT (8,500 TU v 5,000 TY). There wasn’t that much of note in options space, with the
largest trade a seller of 4k TYF7 106/104.50 put spreads.
There was a raft of Chinese data on Monday, which came out weaker-than-expected with the exception of the
unemployment rate, which fell from 5.2% to 5.1%. Retail sales and industrial production were both weaker
than expected. UBS Economics believed the weakness was to be expected, as part of the strength in the
September data came from front-loading. However, the slowdown shows underlying domestic growth
momentum is weak, while “administrative tightening measures have offset loosening measures.” The CSI 300
was down just over 1%.
St Louis Fed President Schmid, a non-voter, focused on inflation in his speech on Sunday, laying out four
reasons why it had remained elevated. Those were energy, rising durable goods prices, spillover from the AI
infrastructure buildout and, finally, rising healthcare costs. The one area he said wasn’t causing inflation was
the labour market. He also pushed back on narratives that inflation was down to “unfavourable supply shocks”
or “one-time shocks.” He concluded that the Fed has the tools to address inflation and reiterated Chair
Warsh’s comment that “we have work to do.” 
Fed Governor and Vice Chair Jefferson said late Friday he was concerned that higher energy prices were
leading to persistent inflation. He said inflation was the most important signal for policy. Note, alongside John
Williams of the NY Fed, Jefferson is the other senior adviser to Chair Warsh. Both Williams and Jefferson are
openly discussing rising inflation risks.
The debate within the Fed is now about the pace of hikes rather than the need to hike. Last week Governor
Waller put the option of a 50bp hike out there; Dallas Fed’s Logan suggested Dec/Jan 25bp moves might be
more appropriate; Hammack said it should be more gradual. Over the weekend, Boston Fed President Collins,
a non-voter in December, said the direction of monetary policy was clear and she could see broad inflationary
pressures, but preferred the gradual approach.
As the cost of energy debate in the UK continues, the Financial Times [paywall] picks up on recent work from
Cornwall Insight. The report warns that electricity costs for businesses are set to rise by around 13% on

2
average by the end of the decade. This increase is largely driven by the costs of investment in electricity
networks. However, increased costs for renewable energy schemes and charges for the new Sizewell C
nuclear plant will also add pressure to bills. The report also warned that “sustained electricity cost increases
will deepen the challenges already being faced by businesses across the economy.” 
The FT [paywall] also published a transcript of their recent interview with Bank of England Chief Economist
Pill. He warned the Bank had further to go, blaming the risks of higher energy costs. I think the most interesting
part was on market pricing. A few weeks ago he pushed back on the amount of hikes priced. In the interview
he now says: “I don’t think it’s outrageous where we are now in terms of market pricing.” Markets are still
pricing 100bp of hikes in the next 12 months.
