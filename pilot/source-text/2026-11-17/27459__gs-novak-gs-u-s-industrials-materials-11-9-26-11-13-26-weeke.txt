GIR a couple of notes out Friday to flag:
HWM CFO meeting takeaways – full details here Aerospace. Aerospace demand remains strong, with
price expected to be an enduring theme. HWM expects each capacity addition to drive share gain, and sees
further volume upside from additional scope. Engine Products drive the margin portfolio with an adjusted
EBITDA margin of 39%, and management noted ~90% of sales are under LTAs. Fastening Systems
carries a lower LTA mix and margins are currently diluted by the CAM acquisition, which runs at an
adjusted EBITDA margin of ~20%. Management expects to drive CAM margins closer to 30% over the
next two years with yield and throughput improvements.
Industrial. HWM is targeting ~$2.5bn in IGT revenue by 2030, driven by data center buildouts and rising
electricity demand. IGT demand is trending toward H-class and newer-style turbines which are more
content intensive. Similar to aerospace, HWM's IGT pricing ability is strong, noting it has taken price
annually since 2019 even in weaker demand environments. Management also noted that the sophisticated
generators in data centers strain turbines, likely resulting in more replacements.
Competitive Landscape. Management considers its manufacturing scale to be a core competitive
advantage. A few peers can produce many of the products made by Howmet, but few can do many at
scale. Howmet believes recent strategic action in the industry is a testament to the constrained
environment rather than new direct competition.
Capital Allocation. Management noted its deliberate approach to capacity build-out, requiring strong
contract terms and sticking to high returns on capital thresholds. Management is targeting the first wave of
capacity build-out for the next 3-4 years, expecting capex to remain elevated through 2028 at ~5-6% of
revenue. After capex, capital allocation priorities include M&A opportunities, continued share
repurchases, and the current dividend.
HEI site visit – full note here Hollywood, FL side visit. Touring Heico's facility presented us an
opportunity to see how parts of the PMA process works. Heico has a number of engineers and labs
dedicated to precision measurement, materials identification, and performance testing in order to develop

and manufacture PMA components. More complex parts can take a number of months to reverse engineer,
while more simple parts can take a month or less. We also toured a parts repair wing of the facility, where
Heico is able to repair aircraft components for less and on a faster timeline versus the OE, often due to the
availability of in-house PMA components.
Top-line drivers. Heico has seen solid double-digit organic growth across the business in recent quarters,
with growth across aerospace & defense, OE & aftermarket. Heico is seeing underlying end-market unit
growth, price, and market share gains. On a quarter to quarter basis, growth can be somewhat lumpy given
the timing of orders and shipments throughout the year, and that has occurred to a degree in the current
fiscal year.
Margins. Heico incremental margins in several recent quarters have been higher than the historical
average. Heico is pricing less than others in the industry on existing and ongoing products under existing
contractual terms. But when it has new contracts rolling in, or new product introductions, it is marking
prices to market, in a higher aerospace pricing environment. Broadly speaking, new products are more
profitable for Heico. The company also sees future margin expansion from volume leverage and cost
performance.
M&A. There remains a large pool of M&A opportunities across the A&D supply chain, but Heico noted
that there is more competition for businesses that sell components with substantial pricing power (sole-
source, high IP). HEI is focused on businesses that fit its value-based business model, and remains a
disciplined buyer within the supply chain.
