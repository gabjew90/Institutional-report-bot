JGBs - Following the sharp rise in overseas yields, futures opened significantly lower. After that,
futures were sold further, touching 82 sen lower at one point, but they quickly recovered from
there, perhaps due to dip buying. Futures then traded in a range at around 60 sen lower for a
while. The super-long sector held up in the early morning, and the curve bear flattened.
However, perhaps partly because swaps beyond the 10yr rose in yield almost in parallel, selling
rushed into cash bonds, and super-longs were gradually sold. In the afternoon session, super-
longs were sold further, while futures held firm. In the end, looking at the whole day, the curve
closed with a bear steepening. The sell-off over the past few days has already erased the
November rally. Since shorts appear to have been covered to some extent, investors' buying
capacity has probably declined, and the market seems to be vulnerable to selling pressure.
Unless overseas yields turn lower again, auctions will continue from here, so it would not be
surprising to see the upside stay heavy across the curve.

Global Economics Outlook
The global economy grew 2.7% in 2026, in line with potential, with strong AI capex, easier
financial conditions, and fiscal expansion offsetting the energy supply shock. We expect 2.8% in
2027, as lower energy prices and still-strong AI capex outweigh tighter financial conditions and
more neutral fiscal policy.
But the risks are tilted to the downside. First, renewed escalation in Iran could result in another
energy price surge. Second, large fiscal deficits could push up long-term yields more sharply,
especially in France. Third, AI capex could disappoint because of capacity constraints,
regulation, credit market stress, or weaker demand. We also see early signs that AI is weighing
on the job market.
We expect two more rate hikes from the Fed and one from the ECB, but see both returning to
cuts in late 2027. With most of our forecasts for other central banks also below market pricing,
we expect longer-term rates to come down modestly despite large government deficits and
heavy AI-linked borrowing.
Global Markets Outlook
1. A maturing market cycle: Stable global growth and declining inflation make for a friendly
macro baseline, but risks are to the downside as the market cycle ages.
2. The AI investment impulse peaks: AI has contributed more to market gains than economic
growth, so a bigger test for markets than the economy.
3. Passing—or dropping—the baton: Equity gains move from AI infrastructure to other areas,
but we see risks if tighter financial conditions thwart the transition or markets look further
ahead.
4. Energy—balancing risk and risk premium: Our baseline is for a gradual decline in energy
prices, but an escalation-driven spike remains one of the largest near-term risks to markets.
5. Bonds reclaim macro and hedging value: Higher running yield, more paths to relief, and
cheap valuations argue for higher bond allocations, as supply shocks give way to demand
stresses.
6. A more cyclical Dollar, Asia’s revaluing currencies: Lower inflation and less tech
exceptionalism should make for a softer Dollar through 2027, especially versus Asia's
revaluing currencies (JPY, CNY).
7. Europe & UK—global shocks and local shifts: European markets are under pressure from
global shocks and local concerns, which may persist until the French election, keeping EUR
downside attractive.

8. The stress of financing the boom: Record credit and equity issuance has been digestible so
far, but with discounted pricing needed in credit, and further widening in US credit spreads is
likely.
9. EM—resilience and diversification: The case for EM is exposure to high, diversified and
resilient yields in EM fixed income and a regionally diversified earnings mix in EM equities.
10. Growth risks and tail hedges: A benign baseline and tech has de-rated, but diversification
benefits from bonds and commodities, and equity index volatility has scope to rise further.
Global FX Outlook
We expect the Dollar’s solid but mixed performance in 2026 to eventually give way to a softer
trajectory over the course of 2027. We believe markets will gradually see the sources of recent
Dollar support—stubborn inflation, relative resilience to higher energy prices, and surging AI-
related investment expectations—turn from a boost to a drag. However, this will likely be a
gradual shift given the recent data trajectory and the time required for these factors to decisively
turn. As a result, we expect continued mixed performance in the months ahead, with the Dollar
strengthening somewhat further particularly against DM currencies. For the year as a whole, we
expect a reversal in commodity-sensitive currencies, continued AI spillovers in Asia, a still-solid
carry contribution to returns, and the Yen to finally see more durable policy support.
