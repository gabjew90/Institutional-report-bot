1) What Matters in Macro Equity
Charts We Are Watching:
• Chart 1: After the second painful unwind this year, AI positioning looks cleaner, but
is not yet washed out. The crowding score spread between our AI Winners and Ex-AI
400 baskets {UBXXAIW vs. UBXXX400} has dropped back to February levels. The
magnitude of the derating now prices an overly pessimistic outlook for AI
fundamentals, skewing risk-reward increasingly to the upside, though sentiment
remains fragile.    
• Chart 2: Hyperscalers {UBXXHYPE} have been the hiding place of choice, whilst our
Neoclouds+ basket {UBXXNEOC} sits at the epicentre of leverage and data-center
buildout concerns. Stronger operating cash flows, healthier balance sheets and
ownership of valuable existing compute capacity explain the rotation. This remains
the best defensive expression for those with less conviction on broader AI beta.
• Chart 3: The broadening trade has largely relied on AI weakness. That leaves
pockets where fundamentals have not supported recent strength vulnerable to a
correction. Consumer Staples {UBXXSTAP}, for example, still show very poor
earnings revisions breadth. Health Care looks better. One of Keith Parker’s top trades
into 2027: XLV over XLP.
• Chart 4: China is again attracting attention as a portfolio diversifier, but mind the
fundamentals. CSI 300 vs. S&P 500 earnings revisions breadth is at multi-year lows.
A more sustained recovery would need that line to turn higher, as well as more
forceful policy support and a stabilization of the property market.

3) What Matters in Macro Equity (Europe)
Charts We Are Watching:
• Chart 6: The Stoxx 600 is making new highs, outpacing the S&P 500 by 9pp since
early October. European equities have benefited disproportionately from fading
macro headwinds across rates and energy prices. More importantly, the index
remains well-insulated to AI jitters: we estimate only ~11% of the Stoxx 600 is
materially driven by the AI enablement trade.
• Chart 7: Earnings revisions breadth for European Banks {UBXEBANK} has dropped
sharply. Q3 was a softer reporting season than previous quarters with smaller
average beats and more misses. The house view stays constructive, however, with
Gerry Fowler expecting a sustained re-rating through 2027. Valuations and rising
lending growth remain the most compelling upside arguments.
• Chart 8: Energy stocks {UBXEENER} are down 8% against Metals & Miners
{UBXEMETL} in the last three weeks as Brent has priced out ~$15-20 of risk premium.
That rotation has more room to run should flows continue to normalize and a major
re-escalation be averted. Net exposure in Energy stocks has started to roll over but
remains at the 85th percentile on a 2-year lookback (well above summer lows).
• Chart 9: UK Domestics {UBXEUKEX} have been on a surprisingly strong run ever
since this summer’s change in Prime Minister. Last month’s steady-as-you-go
Budget allayed near-term fiscal worries, and growth has proved resilient, with AI
making a rising contribution.
