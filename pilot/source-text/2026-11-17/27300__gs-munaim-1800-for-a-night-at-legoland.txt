Faraz Munaim  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
Sat 14 Nov 2026, 7:58am ET
Good morning – It is pretty incredible how much inflation has dominated both the economic and political
spheres globally over the last few years. It literally has the power to influence elections. Case in point
would be the recently concluded US mid-term elections where the top issue for voters was the economy
and within that “cost-of-living” leading to the results we saw. What the voters expressed at the polls has
been showing up in anecdotes for awhile now and as of the last couple of CPI prints, the data.
Very recently we had a weekend with few activities so thought it would be a good idea to take the kids
upstate to Legoland, an easy drive from the city. When we went to look for rooms, we were absolutely
stunned that not only was the on property hotel almost sold out but the few rooms available were going for
$1800 a night! The fact that it was both sold out and also charging the prices it is a little confounding
given that it is a very family oriented place and not for the influencer set – people are clearly deciding that
they are still willing to pay for experiences even if it means potentially putting it on their credit cards. But
these stories seem to hold true in other places too. For Christmas this year, we are flying to Asia – I had
booked the tickets a few months ago and I just did a price check to see where they are now and the exact
route was sold out and flights leaving a day before were almost 40% more (with only a handful of seats
remaining). The upward pressure on prices just seems relentless – obviously geo-politics and external
circumstances not helping but even the demand side continues to be resilient. Hardly surprising then that
50 bp hikes for the Fed have entered the dialog at a time when anxiety about the economy and the future is
reaching a fever pitch.
GS research has now added a third hike in March (from earlier in the week):
The October CPI report this morning surprised meaningfully to the upside across several categories and
marked a third month of worse inflation news. We continue to expect a second 25bp rate hike in December
and are adding a third 25bp hike to our forecast in March 2027. A third hike could come earlier in January,
but we think March is a bit more likely because we expect the November and December inflation reports
to be softer but then expect larger start-of-the-year price increases amidst ongoing cost pressures to boost
inflation in January.
Markets / FX
The post mid-term price action in other asset classes didn’t really bleed into FX (the trade was not obvious
and positioning was light) but the surprise US CPI number certainly got things moving. However, the
flows didn’t really back up the moves to the same extent thanks to the mid-week US holiday, low
conviction and positioning and painful shocks recently. However, to the extent that there were flows we
did see USD buying which was spread out across various currencies. USDJPY was an interesting one –
positioning in recent weeks had been building up in the long JPY trade and we saw some of that washed
out with the latest move but for those who continue to like it as a fundamental position / AI equity hedge,

was a good opportunity to add mainly via options. There was also short dated protection buying against
this core view.
