The global Outlook outage turned Friday’s EMR into the EAR instead. It reminded me how much things have 
changed over my career. When I started in the mid-1990s, I used to fax handwritten trade ideas individually to 
tens and tens of clients every morning, carefully typing each number to make sure they got the fax rather than 
the local Chinese restaurant. I promise to personally fax the EMR every day to any client who sends me their 
current fax number. I’m pretty confident none of you still have one. Maybe it’s time to bid for one on eBay as a 
backup. 
Assuming you’re reading this and nothing else has gone wrong, the week ahead has plenty to keep markets 
occupied after Waller’s 50bps thunderbolt last week and the stronger-than-expected inflation data 
which, as we'll see at the end, led to the largest rise in US 10yr (+27.9bps) and 2yr (+33.0bps) yields 
since April 2025 and October 2024 respectively. Nvidia's results tomorrow may outweigh everything though 
and will largely put a bow on a bumper earnings season in what has been a bumpy period for markets. 
Tomorrow also brings US October retail sales and industrial production, Wednesday has US housing 
data and the minutes from the October FOMC meeting, while a heavy run of Fed speakers should help 
frame how much the inflation surprise has changed the policy debate, especially given the reaction to 
Waller's speech last week. Watch out for Warsh speaking today. Globally, the UK has labour-market data 
tomorrow and inflation on Wednesday, while Japan has Q3 GDP today and national CPI on Friday. The APEC 
leaders’ meeting in Shenzhen on Wednesday and Thursday adds a geopolitical angle. 
Digging into the US, tomorrow’s October retail sales report should be particularly insightful after a strong 
+0.8% MoM increase in September. Our economists expect headline sales to be unchanged in October, 
with the step-down in auto sales weighing on the total. Ex-autos, our economists expect a +0.3% MoM 
increase after +0.8% previously, with lower gasoline prices acting as a drag. But the underlying consumer 
picture should look firmer than the headline: our economists expect the retail control measure to rise 
+0.4% MoM after just +0.1% in September. That will be an important cross-check on consumer momentum 
after recent data have continued to paint a picture of resilient US growth. 

Tomorrow also brings October industrial production. Our economists expect a +0.3% MoM increase, 
moderating from September’s strong +0.7%. So between retail sales and production, tomorrow should give 
us a useful snapshot of whether the economy retained momentum at the start of Q4 even as financial 
conditions remained relatively tight. 
The backdrop to those numbers has become more hawkish after last week’s inflation data came in 
stronger than expected. That prompted our US economists to revise their Fed call: they now expect 
three further 25bp hikes, in December, January and March, taking the fed funds rate to 4.625% by the 
end of Q1 2027. Wednesday’s October FOMC minutes therefore risk looking a little stale given they pre-date 
the latest inflation surprise, but they should still provide a useful benchmark for where the Committee’s 
policy views stood before those data. There are also 12 Fed officials appearing over the week, so markets 
will be looking for any evidence that the latest inflation news has shifted the broader reaction function. 
Fed Chair Warsh is in focus today, moderating a panel in San Francisco as the first of the Fed’s five task 
forces — on productivity and jobs — presents its findings. The discussion should be particularly relevant to 
the current debate around AI, productivity and the labour market. Our economists’ baseline is that the task 
force will focus primarily on AI’s potential to lift productivity and sustain strong growth without 
necessarily generating the same degree of inflation pressure, although possible disruption to hiring and 
implications for r-star could also feature. 
Housing returns to the agenda on Wednesday, with October housing starts, building permits and pending 
home sales all due. That will give another read on a sector that remains sensitive to elevated borrowing costs 
and should help fill out the picture of domestic demand alongside tomorrow’s consumer and production 
numbers. 
Outside the US, focus will be on the APEC economic leaders’ meeting in Shenzhen on Wednesday and 
Thursday. In Europe, the UK has a busy week: labour-market data arrive tomorrow, followed by October CPI, 
RPI and PPI on Wednesday. Our UK economists expect headline CPI to rise to +3.80% YoY from +3.48% in 
September and core CPI to increase to +2.87% from +2.78%. Friday then brings October retail sales and 
November GfK consumer confidence. Germany’s November ZEW survey is tomorrow and October PPI is on 
Friday, while the UK-EU summit also takes place on Friday. 
Japan’s trade balance comes out on Wednesday, before October national CPI on Friday; our economists 
expect core CPI to accelerate to +2.4% YoY from +2.1% in September, while core-core CPI is expected 
to slow to +2.3% YoY. 
Finally, earnings season is drawing to a close but until Nvidia reports tomorrow it's hard to move on from it. 
Elsewhere the slower trickle of earnings sees Walmart, Home Depot, TJX, Lowe’s and Target providing a read 
on the US consumer. Walt Disney and Palo Alto Networks are among the other US highlights, while Alibaba, 
Tencent and JD.com report in China. 
As we start a new week, a tech stock sell-off is hurting Asia. Across the region, Taiwan’s TAIEX (-5.74%) is 
the worst performer, with the tech heavy KOSPI (-4.00%) and Nikkei (-2.09%) also weak. The CSI 300 (-0.81%) 
and Shanghai Composite (-0.74%) are also lower after weaker-than-expected October activity data 
(more below). In contrast, the Hang Seng (+0.84%) is outperforming. S&P 500 (-0.49%) and Nasdaq 100 (-
1.00%) futures are notably lower although European futures are flat. 

In terms of data, China’s October activity data highlighted ongoing weakness in domestic demand, with 
industrial production and retail sales both missing expectations while investment remained under 
pressure. Industrial production rose +4.8% YoY, below forecasts of +5.3% and down from +5.5% in 
September. Retail sales increased +0.4%, undershooting expectations for +0.8% growth and slowing from 
+1.1% previously. Fixed-asset investment fell -8.1% in the January-October period, worse than the 
expected -7.0% decline and a deterioration from the -7.2% contraction recorded over the first nine 
months of the year. Meanwhile, new home prices declined for another month in October, although the 
pace of declines seems to be moderating. Prices fell -0.15% month-on-month after a -0.14% decline in 
September.  
Japan’s economy unexpectedly contracted in Q3 2026, reflecting softer export momentum and weaker 
consumer spending despite ongoing government support measures. GDP declined -0.1% YoY in the three 
months to September, missing expectations for +0.2% growth and reversing a revised +1.7% expansion in the 
previous quarter. On a quarter-on-quarter basis, GDP was unchanged, compared with expectations for a 
modest +0.1% increase. 
Recapping last week now and the main story was a rates sell-off sparked by hawkish remarks from Fed 
officials and a higher-than-expected US CPI print, while volatility in tech and energy markets continued. So 
that led 10yr Treasury yields (+27.9bps to 5.14%) to post their largest weekly rise since April 2025, while 
2yr yields (+33.0bps to 4.84%) saw their largest rise since October 2024. 
For equities, what started off as a strong rebound ended with a Nvidia-driven tech sell-off late in the week. 
This still left the S&P 500 (+2.51%, -0.40% Friday) and the Nasdaq (+2.27%, -0.91% Friday) posting solid 
weekly gains. The Philly semiconductor index (+8.05%) rebounded from its early November slump, despite a 
-3.43% slide on Friday.  Nvidia was at the centre of the late selloff, posting its largest weekly decline 
since April 2025 (-12.75%, -2.01% Friday). In Asia, the Nikkei (+6.46%) and the KOSPI (+4.56%) joined in with 
the weekly tech gains, but the Hang Seng (-3.45%) lost ground. European equities also advanced, with the 
Stoxx 600 (+2.44%, +0.02% Friday) and DAX (+3.60%, -0.08% Friday) reaching new highs. Credit markets 
also benefited from the overall risk-on mood, with US HY spreads -20bps tighter, while EUR HY (-25bps) 
saw its biggest tightening since April 2025.   
Turning to rates, the main catalyst of the sell-off was a strong US CPI print on Tuesday, with monthly core 
inflation at +0.30% (vs. +0.2% expected). This was also accompanied by hawkish Fed commentary, most 
notably Governor Waller opening the door to a 50bps December hike, which left markets pricing +27bps 
of hikes for the December meeting (+7.2bps on the week). So that drove a painful bond sell-off, although 
2yr yields did pare back their rise on Friday (-3.8bps) after October PPI came in below expectations 
(+0.3% m/m vs +0.4% m/m expected). In Europe, sovereign bonds also weakened, with yields on 10yr 
bunds (+12.3bps), OATs (+16.5bps) and BTPs (+9.4bps) all higher over the week. And investors priced in a 
more hawkish path for the ECB, with the amount of hikes priced by next June rising +13.5bps to 59bps.   
The repricing in US rates boosted the US dollar, with the dollar index (+1.09%, +0.24% Friday) reaching its 
highest level since Trump announced the Liberation Day tariffs on April 2nd, 2025. By contrast, gold 
posted its biggest weekly decline since June (-4.58% to $3,884/oz). And crypto assets faced headwinds 
from higher rates and tech volatility, with Bitcoin posting a sixth consecutive weekly decline (-12.24%).   

Finally, oil prices saw a sizeable roundtrip. Brent crude ultimately ended the week -2.52% lower at 
$95.84/bbl, but this was well above the lows below $90/bbl on Tuesday as hopes for Iran resolution again 
faded.
