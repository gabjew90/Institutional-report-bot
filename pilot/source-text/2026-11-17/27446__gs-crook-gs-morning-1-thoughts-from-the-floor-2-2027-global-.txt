1) ADAM CROOK – Connecting you to GS: Thoughts from the Floor – LINK
Key Questions: Post CPI + Fed policymaker comments last week, how should we be thinking about Fed
pricing + Terminal Rates? What was the market base-case for US data-centre giga-watt production 2026-
28 pre -election, and how much is at risk after the mid-term political shift? How bad has the recent US
inflation-trajectory been + is it broad-based enough to seriously question our benign inflation outlook? Are
investors still in the Brazil trade, what is important to watch, and any change on outlook for Brazilian
assets?
Focusing in on the Fed, see below for key takeaways from senior thought leaders…
Brian Bingham (Short Macro Trading): The composition of 2027 pricing, which in tandem with the equity
move, has pivoted from a fairly even distribution through Q3 towards a much more truncated path of hikes
in Q1 over the past week – we see this front-loading as more likely than not to continue, given the lack of
near term progress on the inflation front coupled with (what seems like) a pivot from the Trump
administration away from what had been a well-telegraphed path towards escalation into November.
While near term risks to the policy outlook are clearly skewed towards the upside as focus turns to the Dec
SEP and presumptive increase in the 2027 dot from 4.125, and we see little rationale to fade the kneejerk
retracement particularly as risk stabilizes, terminal rate pricing remains precarious given the starting point
– and as last week clearly illustrated, will react violently on any wobble in AI sentiment. Our preferred
implementation of this narrative is receiving structures like SFR H7H8.
Praveen Korapaty (USD Rates Vol): With yet another strong core CPI print, markets would’ve solidified
additional hike pricing regardless of Waller’s (and other Fed officials’) comments. And there’s still
unlikely to be a 50bp hike in December – in my view, there won’t be enough data, or for that matter votes
on the FOMC, to support such a move. That said, the combination of data and Fedspeak has laid some
groundwork for hikes in consecutive meetings rather than the quarterly pace markets had settled on and
allowed markets to push terminal rate pricing higher. My base case would still be two additional hikes and
a pause thereafter, i.e., essentially reversing the 75bp of cuts from last year. That’s under the assumption
that core inflation will gradually move towards the Fed’s target over the course of the next year. On
Terminal: If you are asking where the current adjustment hikes will stop, I’d think that range is 4.25-4.5%.
If you are asking about neutral, I think we are there, i.e., 3.75-4%.
Ilir Xholi (US Swaps Desk): The market now prices a full Fed hiking cycle again with peak terminal rate
around 4.75% by next fall. We have added 1.5 hikes since the CPI report, and the market is reluctant to

take much back on softer PPI components on Friday I think this speaks to market participants getting
chopped up in positioning – many chased the rally as AI capex slowdown fears dominated the price action
in the last few weeks, and are now having to reduce those positions. I think a December hike is done but I
don’t believe 50 is on the cards a la Waller (the market seems to agree as only 27 priced). The terminal
rate, however, is priced above where I think the Committee ultimately ends up and speaks more to the
positioning I highlighted above. I think U7 is a buy here.

2) FX STRATEGY (TRIVEDI / CAHILL) – 2027 Global FX Outlook: Timing the Turn – LINK
USD: We expect the Dollar's solid but mixed performance in 2026 to eventually give way to a softer
trajectory over the course of 2027. We believe markets will gradually see the sources of recent Dollar
support - stubborn inflation, relative resilience to higher energy prices, and surging AI-related investment
expectations - turn from a boost to a drag. As a result, we expect continued mixed performance in the
months ahead, with the Dollar strengthening somewhat further particularly against DM currencies. For the
year as a whole, we expect a reversal in commodity-sensitive currencies, continued AI spillovers in Asia, a
still-solid carry contribution to returns, and the Yen to finally see more durable policy support.
EUR & GBP: We expect the Euro to be kept under pressure in the near term, and forecast further
depreciation against the Dollar to 1.08 in 3m. Over the second half of 2027, we expect more resilient
growth, fading headwinds, and relatively synchronized policy to take the Euro back towards levels that
prevailed for much of this year before political headwinds came to the forefront; we expect EUR/USD to
rise to 1.15 by the end of 2027. We forecast EURGBP at 0.86 in 12 months, largely on a relaxation in
European sovereign credit stress, and GBPUSD at 1.34 over the same horizon on energy relief and AI
capex deceleration.
JPY: We expect JPY to appreciate in 2027 as its deep undervaluation starts to erode, with the scope for
even sharper appreciation if AI-related risks materialize. The BoJ's shift to quarterly rate hikes removed
that source of downward pressure on the Yen, and our economists now have hawkish expectations relative
to current market pricing, with a baseline of three more 25bp hikes to 2% by June 2027 (vs. the around
1.75% priced through June 2027). We are therefore expecting a gradual decline in USDJPY over the
coming year to 158, 155, 150 in 3, 6, and 12 months as nearer-term cross-currents fade.
Asia FX: Our forecasts envisage USDCNY moving towards 6.30 by end-2027, matching the lows in 2022.
We expect the low-yielding, tech-exposed KRW, TWD and MYR to outperform within Asian FX in 2027.
We expect the CBC to become more tolerant of TWD appreciation as rising corporate margins provide a
larger cushion against FX strength. We expect SGD and THB to moderately underperform other low
yielders. We therefore maintain our long MYRTHB recommendation. With the MSCI decision pending,
we see further IDR underperformance and maintain our long INRIDR recommendation.

3) US EQUITY DERIVATIVE SALES (COPPERSMITH) – AI Confidence is Fading - LINK
BOTTOM LINE: Equities can grind higher after the recent positioning reset, but we like being more
selective from here. Parts of AI are starting to look interesting again at these levels, and we’d use
weakness to add rather than chase rallies. We also expect more rotation beyond the infrastructure trade as
we move into next year. With AI spending growth peaking and financing getting more expensive, we’d
stay disciplined on hedges.
