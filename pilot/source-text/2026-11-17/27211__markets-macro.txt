just when you thought that 2026 couldn’t get any more interesting, this week took things to yet another
level.
outside of short-cycle periods when there was genuine trauma in the markets, this year has featured the
most dynamic ecosystem for the capital markets that I’ve ever seen.
given all of the action and activity, one of the attendant challenges is an overload of information, where
separating the SIGNAL from an abundance of NOISE is particularly difficult right now.
in addition, the daily rotations within the market have been brutal, particularly for those deploying
significant leverage (be it professionals or civilians).
with all of that said -- and with appreciation for how difficult it has been to manage money in H2’26 -- we
walked out of the office yesterday with equal weight S&P sitting just 1% from the all-time highs.
here’s another observation that speaks to the disjunction between how the market “feels” day-to-day and
how the big indices are “performing” point-to-point:
if you had told me that THE bellwether stock of this entire era would incinerate over $500bn of market
cap ... and that inflation concerns would drive a 33 bps backup in US 2-year note yields ... I would not
have guessed that NDX would have rallied 3.5% this week.
what follows from here is a short check-down of my current directional framework (skip to point #3 for
the punchline).
///
1. risk / reward skews towards the positive side for the remainder of 2026 :
i. the technicals: as detailed in point #4 below, after a period of intense position cleansing, near-term flows
should be supportive.
ii. the seasonals: with the midterms behind us , we’ve entered one of the best vol-adjusted periods of the
entire year (since 1952, the last seven weeks are up in 69% of occurrences, for an average rally of 2.1%).
iii. while this week was a rollercoaster in the AI infrastructure stocks, it also provided fresh evidence that
AI demand remains some form of insatiable (witness TSMC, CRWV).
iv. most fundamentally, the economy is performing well as nominal US GDP growth continues to run
above trend (and appears to be accelerating in the second half, which was not expected).
2. looking further up the road, however, 2027 sets up to be more complicated :

i. over the past four years, the largest infrastructure project in the history of the United States has unfolded.
not only was the scale of the buildout consistently bigger-than-expected, but the path was essentially
uninterrupted. flashing forward to today, the market has taken a lot of credit for the first factor, and the
second factor is increasingly at risk of disruption from US politicians.
ii. as the market contemplates that political cycle, there will also be risk that more regulation and higher
taxes are coming to a theater near you.
iii. it’s not just that core inflation is sticky, but the latest CPI report suggests that it’s broadening.
remember, bull markets typically don’t die of old age -- they die because the central bank chokes off
liquidity.
iv. a reminder: 2027 will likely see the largest increase in NET supply since the year 2000; for the
executive summary, see exhibits 6 and 14.
v. set against these risks, stock operators are supposed to ask “what could go right” as often as “what could
go wrong.” that’s the axiom that connects to this unimpeachable fact: despite all manner of things to worry
about along the path, NDX is trending to be up in 17 of the past 18 years (total returns).
vi. I should also note that the biggest dynamics in the game should remain generally favorable (again,
sturdy GDP growth = healthy earnings growth), which is supportive of the broad bull uptrend (see the
chart at bottom).
3. here’s where I’m going with all of this :
i. the balance of risks has a positive skew through year-end.
ii. and the primary trend in S&P remains higher (see the chart at the bottom).
iii. as 2026 becomes 2027, however, I would expect more volatility and less upward convexity -- which
points towards lower risk-adjusted returns.
iv. in effect, I think we’re setting up for more of a seesaw -- where very powerful, yet countervailing
forces leave the market fighting hard to grind out more modest gains.
v. for a formal take on the framework for the year ahead, see here from Peter Oppenheimer and our global
portfolio strategy team.
4. a quick level set on flow-of-funds / positioning :
i. since the start of October, the market has absorbed a very significant liquidation of risk from the trading
community (I say that with regard to both net AND gross exposure).
ii. to put a line under it: last week our franchise witnessed the biggest sale of global AI stocks since the
new world was revealed four years ago.
iii. in turn, if you take stock of our entire PB book, and measure current positioning vs the past year of
history, net exposure registers in the 3rd percentile.

iv. looking ahead, corporate supply-vs-demand should improve meaningfully as November moves along.
5. a handful of other points :
i. this week’s podcast zeros in on the recent action in the HEALTHCARE space with Asad Haider.
ii. I was surprised by how many responses I got to last week’s mention of gathering concerns over the US
credit market. for what it’s worth, the vast majority were tinged with significant negativity.
iii. for all of the absolutism that one finds in political forecasting these days, I heed the wisdom of Alec
Phillips: an enormous amount changes in the US political narrative in two-year increments (or even in
two-month increments).
iv. I find myself thinking about this more and more: the US economy has never been more sensitive to --
tethered to, really -- the performance of the stock market (see exhibit 11).
