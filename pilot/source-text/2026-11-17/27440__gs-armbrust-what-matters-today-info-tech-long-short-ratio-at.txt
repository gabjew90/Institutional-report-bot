1) Info Tech Long/Short Ratio at the Lows
HFs net sold US equities for a 2nd straight week (5 of last 6), driven by short sales in Single Stocks
partially offset by short covers + long buys in Macro Products. While 8 of 11 sectors were net sold, % net
selling in US Financials was the largest in 8 months. Info Tech was by far the most $ net sold global (and
US) sector again this week – US Info Tech long/short ratio now stands at 1.5 (vs. YTD peak of 2.14 seen
in early June), the lowest level on our record (since 2016).

Fundamental L/S Performance Estimate rose +1.16% this week. Overall book Gross leverage -2.1 pts to
312.1% (74th percentile 1-year) and Net leverage -0.4 pts to 71.4% (3rd percentile 1-year). Overall book
L/S ratio +0.1% to 1.593 (4th percentile 1-year). Global equities were net sold (-0.7 SDs 1-year) as de-
grossing activity continued to increase, driven by long sales outpacing short covers. Energy was the most
$ net bought and the most risk-on global sector this week as the sector was net bought at the fastest pace in
3 months, driven by long buys.
2) NVDA Flows and News
We had previously witnessed mainly long liquidation and de-grossing, but this past week saw a more
pronounced pickup in new shorts. Our cash desk also saw persistent NVDA supply from the LO
community, with money rotating into hyperscalers and other pockets of large cap tech.
This weekend Bessent urged NVDA to pursue a “dramatic” shakeup and look externally for its next CEO.
Also, the FT reported that Jensen Huang is discussing a possible return. And we have earnings
tomorrow… unlikely to resolve the debate around future AI commitments. Probably reinforces that the
stock is heavily discounted on next years numbers but impossible to know what is correct multiple for

28+. Personally, I think the noise continues and overall its a headwind for the stock. Feels like it will be
some time before we get an “all clear” which allows the stock to breath again.
As Rich Privo put it, reduced AI commitments could be negative for equity growth expectations but
positive for credit as balance sheet risk diminishes. Less spending, better credit, weaker equity multiples.
Meanwhile, AMD could emerge as a key beneficiary of the uncertainty surrounding Jensen Huang’s
departure, as hyperscalers accelerate efforts to diversify away from Nvidia. AMD has just launched its 6th-
generation EPYC CPUs, specifically targeting three critical agentic AI workloads: enterprise applications,
GPU orchestration, and isolated environments where AI agents execute tasks—claiming up to 3.3x better
agents-per-watt efficiency versus Nvidia’s Vera CPU. Combined with its expanding GPU and rack-scale
offerings, AMD has an opportunity to capture incremental AI infrastructure spending.
3) Enough Semis Underperformance?
My colleague Lee Coppersmith argues that Semis have underperformed the S&P by >30 percentage points
since June (sharpest relative drawdown since 2002) though still well short of the 90s extremes. GS
Research makes the case that much of this has gone too far, with some stocks now discounting something
close to zero earnings growth in 2028
On a side note, CTA positioning in NDX is back near max short levels. With positioning this light, it
probably doesn't take much positive news to spark a squeeze higher.

4) US Economic 2027 Outlook
2027 GDP growth at an above-consensus 2.4% and the labor market stable, while allowing core PCE
inflation to fall substantially to 2.4%.
• Consumption should grow a bit over 2%, driven mainly by firmer and more evenly balanced real income
growth as inflation moderates and the boosts from last year’s tax cuts and wealth effects diminish.
• AI drives half of our 8% forecast for business investment growth but contributes only modestly to
measured GDP growth because much of the equipment is imported or undercounted in the GDP statistics.
• Housing investment will likely contract again as poor affordability continues to depress demand.
• Labor market: the low fire and low hire environment equilibrium should last for another year before AI
displacement begins to have a larger impact in subsequent years.
• Rates: We forecast two more 25bp rate hikes in December 2026 and March 2027 to a peak of 4.25-4.5%,
followed by gradual cuts back to 3.25-3.5% starting in November, the final meeting of 2027, once
inflation has declined meaningfully.

Source: Goldman Sachs FICC & Equities, Marquee, as of 16 Nov 2026.
