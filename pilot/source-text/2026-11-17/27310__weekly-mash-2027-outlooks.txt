After last week’s volatile price action & Thursday’s largest ever notional 1-day stock market
decline, equities had impressively recovered those losses by Wednesday – only to be rocked
again by worsening inflation news, higher yields, and the ejection of the founder/CEO of the
world’s largest company
Yesterday we published our major outlook pieces for 2027 ::
We expect stable growth (2.8% global GDP vs 2.7% in 2026) & slowing inflation
We expect 2 more Fed hikes & 1 from the ECB, decently below what markets now price
Importantly we think both those central banks will be cutting by the end of the year
Uncertainty & “downside risks” loom large through these reports, mostly because of Middle
East/energy uncertainty, & a slowing AI investment boom
Our global equity outlook piece is about “Finding value in growth”, while our European view
focuses more on the necessity of growth …
We’re overweight US & Japanese equities, but now go underweight Europe and also we
downgrade European banks after a great run
On the topic of European equities, it leaves many incredulous when I point out that as of last
week even France’s CAC-40 was outperforming our basket of AI-winners over the last 12mths
(chart 1), and that European banks have now outperformed the mighty ‘Mag7’ over 1yr, 2yrs,
3yrs, 4yrs & 5yrs (chart 2) ….


Although I find it difficult to defend either of those positions from here, Europe is going to be
highly topical in the coming the 12mths : there’s no clearer economic beneficiary of declining
energy prices than Europe; similarly, the surge in govt bond yields is creating clear
vulnerabilities for numerous European countries, but at the same time – just as the US mid-
terms moved the US politically from Right to Left - Europe looks increasingly likely to be in the
midst of a meaningful political shift in the other direction (starting with Spain in 2 weeks).
Arguably, the range of possibilities for European markets in the coming 12mths is wider than in
many others.

1.Stable growth and falling inflation, but growing headwinds as the market cycle matures
 
Global growth remains stable at 2.8% in 2027, but the earnings growth will experience a slow
down: US EPS growth falls from 36% to 11%, Europe from 18% to 7% and Japan from 24% to
9%. Higher real rates, wider credit spreads and peaking AI investment (see: Growing Pains)
therefore matter more for profits and equities than GDP, even as lower inflation, rates and
energy should provide some offset

link
2. US leadership should continue, supported by both growth and earnings
GIR is above consensus on US GDP growth at 2.4% vs. 2.2%, supported by resilient
investment, while US EPS still grows 11% in 2027 despite slowing sharply from 36%. US
equities have de rated while profitability remains superior, so we are Overweight US and
Japan, Neutral Asia/EM and Underweight Europe heading into the year
Link

3. AI capex continues to grow, but the growth impulse peaks in 1H27
Global AI investment rises from $1.1tn in 2026 to $1.8tn in 2027, adding 0.4pp to global GDP
growth, but the incremental investment impulse peaks in 1H27. However, equity markets have
already moved to price this in following concerns around the DC buildout in recent months,
compounded by the Dem midterm sweep. The question will now be how much of the recent
acceleration in profits can persist as the investment cycle matures
link
4. Duration is becoming attractive again
GIR sees 10y UST yields at 4.6% by end 2027, with central bank pricing too hawkish versus
its baseline, bond valuations cheap and positioning underweight. As supply shocks give way to
demand risks, bonds should regain hedging value, with greater scope for yields to fall if AI
spending slows faster than expected

Link
5.Europe remains a good AI hedge, and global investors are already rotating into the
region
GIR expects lower 2027 index returns in Europe than the US or Asia, but European indices
have been resilient through recent AI sell offs. US investors are already increasing European
exposure, while a 5.5% FCF yield, continued buybacks and shrinking net equity supply
provide additional support.
Link

6. In a scenario of further deescalation, lower energy and yields should be a
disproportionate helpful for Europe
GIR expects Brent to fall towards $78/bbl by end 2027, a meaningful tailwind for Europe
given a 10% rise in oil has historically taken c.1% off valuations. Combined with lower yields,
this supports Retailers, Utilities, Telecoms, UK Real Estate and the DAX, while unhelpgul
against Energy, Banks and Insurance
Link
7. Value is emerging within Tech after the de rating
Global Tech now trades around the 20th percentile of its 20 year valuation range, while
Hyperscalers trade on 19.7x forward P/E, with their premium to the rest of the S&P 500 almost
eliminated. In Europe, Tech still offers c.28% 2027 EPS growth and trades below 1x PEG,
while traditional Value itself is close to the 90th percentile of its historical valuation range

Link
8. Broader call for Growth with valuation discipline, not a broad defensive rotation
With earnings growth becoming scarcer, GIR favours Growth, selective Defensives and
beneficiaries of lower oil and yields. That means areas where earnings durability and
valuation align: US Hyperscalers, Healthcare, Power and Buyback Aristocrats; European
Tech, Telecoms and Utilities; and selective Asian AI exposure, rather than simply buying
traditional Defensives, many of which have already re rated.
