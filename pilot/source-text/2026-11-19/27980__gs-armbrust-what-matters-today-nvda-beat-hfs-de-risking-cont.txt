1) NVDA Beat
Nvidia rose +3% after hours as stronger results and guidance eased growth concerns. FY2028 revenue
growth guidance increased to around 75% from 70%, implying roughly $741bn of revenue and close to
$17 of EPS—above the Street’s $16 but below buyside expectations of $18. Demand still exceeds supply,
with better visibility on orders and secured memory and packaging capacity supporting growth. Vera
Rubin generated nearly $20bn in its first quarter, while CPU revenue is expected to more than double in
FY2028, driven by agentic AI. Gross margins are expected to bottom at 71–72% in January as memory
costs rise, before settling at 72–73% in FY2028. Management also provided a fulsome update on its
financial commitments in support of customers and supply chain partners, which now total $518bn (up
from $366bn last quarter). These span supply chain/capacity commitments ($390bn, up from $279bn prior,
largely memory), cloud service agreements ($28bn, down from $29bn prior), datacenter leases ($74bn, up
from $25bn prior), equity investments ($20bn, down from $25bn prior), and CapEx ($6bn, down from
$8bn prior).
Nvidia saying its more selective on customer financing: investments, commitments and guarantees will
prioritize projects with land, power, buildings and clear demand, with guarantees only taking effect once
projects are ready. Against roughly $390bn of supply commitments, this is positive for Nvidia’s balance
sheet but could make funding harder for leveraged neoclouds reliant on its backing, widening the gap with
investment-grade hyperscalers. Buybacks remain a priority, with around $227bn of authorization
remaining, while export restrictions remain a risk. See Jim/GIR take (TY Sean Johnstone)
2) HFs De-risking Continues
Performance: October’s performance drawdown has given way to a cautious November. Global
Fundamental L/S managers are down slightly by -0.2% so far in November (+8.9% YTD as of Nov 16th)
and Systematic L/S returns are up +1.1% MTD on the back of a sharp recovery last week.
Large net selling: Hedge funds have heavily net sold global equities so far in November, driven by long-
and-short sales (~3 to 1). On a trailing 6-week basis, the pace of selling in global Info Tech since early
October is by far the fastest in the past decade (-4.4 z score), led by selling in Semis & Semi Equip stocks.
Industrials is the second most net sold US sector MTD.

Leverage: Fundamental L/S net leverage has declined by another -7.6 pts MTD to the lowest level in more
than five years (now below Liberation Day lows).

Momentum Unwind Continues: Factor exposure to Medium-Term Momentum is on track to decline for a
6th straight month to the lowest level since Dec ’22 (20th percentile 5-year), while factor exposure to
Value has risen sharply to 5-year highs amid increased stagflation concerns.

3) Cyber Concerns Rising
Recent disruptions worth noting:
• Microsoft 365 outage affecting Outlook, Teams, Copilot, and related services from November 12-14,
2026; Microsoft reportedly said it did not suspect malicious activity.
• FAA-related disruptions, JPMorgan Chase transaction issues, and Apple service outages reported in the
same broader window.
• NSA classified internal communications network outage reported on November 17, 2026; officials
described it as linked to routine maintenance/software upgrades, with no immediate signs of a cyberattack.

One theory I heard from investors is that some Eastern countries could be behind some of these
disruptions as a pressure tactic ahead of President Trump’s meetings in Shenzhen. Tonight, Trump and Xi
meet at 11:45pm EST followed by a Trump-Xi-Putin trilateral meeting at 1:30 a.m. EST on Thursday,
November 19. That said, I am skeptical: the timing is peculiar, but the evidence does not point to them
being cyberattacks.
Panic buying from everyday people is extending into generators, emergency supplies and pantry loading..
several people are getting worried. My take on this is that Cybersecurity services demand will continue to
rise quickly… watch CRWD, PANW, ZS, FTNT, OKTA, S, CYBR.
