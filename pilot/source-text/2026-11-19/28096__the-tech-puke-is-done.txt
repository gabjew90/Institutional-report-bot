Are we there yet?
The tech trade has gone from everybody's favorite to almost nobody's favorite in record time.
Hedge funds have slashed exposure to historic lows, the momentum trade has been dismantled, and
institutional AI positioning has collapsed. Yet the AI spending machine keeps humming, earnings
expectations are resetting, and the first signs of a bounce are emerging.
Has the great tech puke finally run its course?
Lowest on record
Goldman's prime book has a remarkable number: the US technology long/short ratio has collapsed to
1.5, down from 2.14 in June. That's the lowest reading since the series began in 2016.
The fast money hasn't just trimmed tech. It has gutted the trade.
GS Prime
Gets gutted
The crowd that couldn't get enough Tech just sprinted for the exit. The net % overweight Tech dropped
"to roughly -27 ppt, one of the steepest monthly declines in the series," rivaling the 2004 washout. That
is one month erasing a lot of conviction, and it maps neatly onto the survey's finding that 52% now
expect AI hyperscaler capex cuts.
BofA
Momentum gone
The momentum unwind is almost complete. Global hedge fund net exposure has flipped slightly
negative as equity long/short funds and multi-strats abandon the factor. Only quants remain net long,
and even they are well off the highs.
One of the market's biggest consensus trades has been dismantled.

JPM PI
AI positioning
The positioning purge extends well beyond tech hedge funds. Institutional exposure to AI-related
equities has dropped to its lowest level since April 2025. The AI trade is suddenly looking a lot less
crowded.
Vanda / Sober Look
Floor for now
The first bounce is in, and a potential double bottom is taking shape. With positioning this washed out,
the bulls finally have something to work with. Now the lows need to hold.

Trend friend
NDX is back near its massive long-term trendline. The short-term picture looks ugly, but do you really
want to bet against this long-term trend?

GS on NVDA
Goldman sees roughly 40% upside to Nvidia in its base case and an extraordinary 175% in its bull case.
Meanwhile, Nvidia says it intends to return all excess cash to shareholders, with approximately $227
billion in buyback authorization remaining. Not exactly the behavior of a company preparing for the
end of the AI boom.
GIR
AI EPS growth cools
Goldman expects AI infrastructure earnings growth to slow from nearly 90% to below 20% by 2029.
Sounds bearish? Perhaps not. A reset to more realistic expectations could be exactly what the sector
needs to start delivering upside surprises again.
GIR
Never stop
For all the panic about an AI spending slowdown, the absolute numbers remain staggering.
Morgan Stanley Research sees hyperscaler capex rising from $951 billion in 2026 to more than $1.6
trillion in 2027 and almost $1.8 trillion in 2028. Growth is slowing, yes. But from extraordinary levels.
The AI spending machine isn't stopping anytime soon.
Morgan Stanley

NDX in bear market: No problem
More than half of Nasdaq 100 constituents are in bear-market territory. Sounds terrifying. But
historically, when the 10-day average crosses above 51.5%, the Nasdaq has gained an average of 5%
over the following three months, with positive returns 59% of the time.
Better than a coin toss. Not bad for a market everyone seems to hate.
