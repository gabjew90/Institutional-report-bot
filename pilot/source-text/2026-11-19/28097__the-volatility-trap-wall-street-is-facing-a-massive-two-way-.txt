Stressed (again)
SPX closed at its lowest level since May, while VIX is once again showing aggressive signs of stress. We
saw full-blown panic in volatility during the November 5 sell-off, but the latest spike in VIX looks
different.
VIX is once again overshooting the move in SPX, yet the past three sessions have hardly been
characterized by outright panic in spot. A lot of fear is being priced into volatility, even as the
underlying market has been grinding lower in a relatively orderly fashion.
Don't forget that markets can trend lower without volatility needing to rise much.

The gamma trap
Nomura estimates aggregate SPX dealer gamma at approximately negative $7.3bn, near the bottom
of its historical range.
Dealer hedging can amplify moves in either direction, keeping the market unstable. McElligott sees the
VIX floor shifting higher, but limited positioning fuel for a genuine volatility shock above 30 without a
much deeper equity selloff.

The $116 billion liquidation
Nomura's McElligott estimates systematic equity strategies have sold a massive $116bn over the past
two weeks, taking aggregate exposure down to the 10th percentile.
The deleveraging has been extraordinary. Prime brokerage data also shows net leverage down to zero
or low single digits, while Mag7 call skew sits at the 12th percentile.
A significant amount of risk has already been taken out of the market. McElligott sees potential for a
painful "buyers are higher" squeeze if volatility starts to cool.
Nomura
Two-way acceleration

Nomura's latest model shows just how sensitive equity flows have become to market direction. A
further 5% decline in equities could trigger approximately $81.8bn of mechanical selling from options
dealers, leveraged ETFs and volatility-control strategies. A 5% rally, meanwhile, would imply roughly
$66.1bn of buying.
The market remains vulnerable to substantial mechanical flows in either direction.
