JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Yesterday’s sell-off occurred on low volumes, finishing as one of the slowest day over the 
last two weeks. Investors area seemingly waiting for NVDA earnings and to see how capital 
markets activity shakes out this week. As the market awaits direction updates on AI / Tech, 
the macro driver is about the Fed and its reaction function as growth remains above-trend 
and sticky inflation resumes its acceleration higher, calling into question the level of R-star 
and whether the Fed will find itself behind the curve in 2027. Fed tightening risk transforms 

into liquidity risk, a headwind for risk assets. Below is color on NVDA, Retail Sales, and Day 
1 of our JPM APAC Tech Conference.

KEY TAKEAWAYS 
•         Investor sentiment is “neutral” on growth. In our audience survey, investors were neutral 
on the outlook for the global economy with expectations coalescing around “trend” growth 
for next year, and only 5% worried about a recession. Two things are clearly front of mind 
for investors in terms of the expected big “swing factor” (good or bad) - the AI capex cycle 
was cited by 50% as most important, followed by geopolitics at 30%. Only 2% thought fiscal 
concerns or oil was the big variable.  
•         Investors are uncertain on tech leadership. When asked to characterize the current tech 
backdrop, >70% said they expect more near-term volatility or they see the correction as 
healthy and calibrated to fundamentals. 11% see more downside. Interestingly, just 13% 
said they see pockets of value and only 2% thought tech was oversold. Investors are also 
divided where future AI leadership comes from - 34% said software/apps, 25% 
hyperscalers, and 14% each said semis, power or digital infrastructure 
•         Nvidia was front of mind, ahead of results tomorrow. Concern of a cautious 27 guide 
from interim CEO Kress was widespread, effectively a post-Jensen “kitchen sink”. Today’s 
weak price action in tech clearly didn’t help (e.g. Advantest -11%, Hynix -8%, TSMC -6%). 
Polymarket now has 16% odds of Jensen returning as CEO - rising alongside an open letter 
petition to Nvidia’s board calling for his return - now at 175k. That said, I didn’t come across 
many who thought this was likely, given the national security debate.  

•         Corporates are more upbeat vs investors. While investors were downbeat on tech, 
companies were not. Robust demand was commonly cited, underpinned by AI scaling laws, 
and S/D is expected to remain tight in many parts of the supply chain (read: high margins). 
This aligns with our fundamentally constructive research view on the tech supply chain into 
1Q27 - with token demand booming (17x y/y), hyperscaler backlogs massive (>$2tr), and AI 
supply chains likely to get tight tighter, not looser, next year 
SNIPPETS FROM PANELS / SESSIONS – mostly bullish 
•         We are living in an era of industrial intelligence - Intelligence is now scalable and 
geographically and linguistically agnostic. As this stage, it’s still hard to comprehend the 
impact that institutionalizing intelligence will have - once the application layers are 
effectively and efficiently built on top of this foundation.  
•         The AI tech wave will exceed all others- Tech Wave 1 was the PC & Enterprise 
revolution (1970-00) and Wave 2 was the Internet & Mobile revolution (1995-25). The AI 
wave will likely exceed these 2 in terms of impact - as intelligence is a capability multiplier 
that can lift both sides of the P&L (i.e. lower costs and boost revenues).  
•         Coding is just the tip of the iceberg. Coding is estimated to account for roughly 1% of 
the overall value-add in knowledge work. This is where AI has had the greatest impact so 
far. So the other 99% is the (still to be tapped) addressable TAM opportunity.  
•         The build-out of the intelligence infrastructure layer is expensive. The hyperscalers are 
likely to spend close to $10tr on building out the necessary compute infrastructure. This is 
roughly equal to 10x the total cost of the internet infrastructure build-out (was $1tr for the 
telcos).  
•         AI scaling laws continue. Frontier models have been released every 16 days this year… 
and token costs have fallen by 98% due to model & silicon efficiencies. However, GPU 
prices continue to rise because of the same efficiency gains alongside supply scarcity of 
chips (leveraging the more efficient models and architectures).  
•         Eventually, value accretion will go to the application layer. As scaling laws continue, the 
greatest value-accretion will go from the infra layer to the application layer. We saw this in 

the Internet wave (e.g. Google’s cost per click: $0.38 in 2005 to $5.42 in 2025), and it will 
inevitably happen in the AI wave too. But it’s still early days.  
•         Compute is set to become a tradable commodity (on CME). Carman Li, founder/CEO of 
Silicon Data, presented a keynote panel. SD will soon launch compute futures, making 
compute a tradable commodity similar to oil or gold. This could add another boost to GPU 
rental prices - as financial investment adds supplements end-user demand? It will also 
change how companies buy, sell and hedge compute.  
•         AI in healthcare is productivity story - AI has compressed healthcare timelines, but it’s 
not yet a novel-molecule story. For Wuxi Bio, the company’s “protein service” was +40% y/y 
in 1H and 2H is guided even stronger. This segment has recently been stripped out from 
total revenues given high investor interest. 
  
 
DELTA-ONE FLOWS & POSITIONING – Bram’s full note is here 
•         Equity ETFs drew their strongest inflows in at least 5 years (2.7z), led by the US, 
though roughly 20% of US inflows reflected CIB-related creations that may reverse this 
week. Equity futures were weaker (-1.6z), with heavy selling in EM, while Fixed Income 
flows were weak in both futures (-1.7z) and ETFs (-2z). Tech flows were mixed: QQQ led 
ETF inflows (+$11.3Bn), and investors bought SMH (+$1.9Bn) and NVDL (+$0.7Bn), even 
as levered Semis ETFs saw $5.5Bn of net outflows across SOXL and SOXS. Defensive 
sectors attracted inflows, while CIBs and a large issuer model rebalance skewed style 
flows. 
•         ETFs 
•         Asset classes: Equities ($74.6Bn, 2.7z) saw strong inflows, Fixed Income was weak 
($2.1Bn, -2z), Commodities were near-average ($0.6Bn, 0.1z), and Currency/Multi-asset 
saw outflows (-$1.3Bn, -1.3z). 
•         Regions: US equity ETFs posted their strongest inflows in at least 5 years 
($65Bn, 2.7z). We estimate that about 20% came from CIB-related creations, which are 

likely to reverse this week; underlying inflows remained strong even excluding them. Buying 
was also strong in CEEMEA (4.5z), Europe (2.2z), India (1.9z), China (1.2z), and Canada 
(1.1z), while Brazil and Korea saw outflows. 
•         Sectors: Defensive sectors led, with strong inflows into Real Estate (3.4z) and Staples 
(2z). Tech (-$2Bn, -0.8z) recorded outflows, driven largely by levered Semis products, while 
Utilities also posted outflows. 
•         Styles: Style ETF flows were distorted by CIBs and a large issuer model rebalance. 
Quality drew strong inflows (3.8z), led by $3.5Bn into QLTY as the fund passed its 3Y 
anniversary. Multifactor (4.4z) and Growth (1.5z) posted strong inflows, which appear to 
have been fueled by CIBs. Dividend and Low Vol each drew ~1z inflows. Thematic ETFs 
saw strong inflows (2.5z), driven by model-rebalance demand for THRO and likely CIB-
related buying across ARK funds; those ARK flows may reverse this week. 
•         Levered ETFs: Levered products saw heavy outflows (-$5.2Bn, -2.2z), driven by 
degrossing in semis (SOXL -$3.7Bn, SOXS -$1.8Bn). NVDL bucked the trend, attracting 
~$0.7Bn as investors bought the dip after NVDA’s recent underperformance. 
•         Fixed Income: Treasuries saw heavy outflows (-$4.2Bn, -3.1z) as the issuer model 
rebalance reduced broad and short-term Treasury exposure, with only part of the proceeds 
reallocated to long-term bonds. Munis (-$0.8Bn, -1.5z) saw their first outflows in nine weeks 
after a period of strong inflows. Investors also rotated out of HY (-0.8z) and into IG (1z) 
bonds. 
•         Commodities: Broad Commodities (-1.1z) were net sold. 
•         Currency/Multi-Asset: Crypto (-$1.6Bn, -1.3z) recorded another week of meaningful 
outflows. 
•         Futures 
•         Flows: MSCI EM saw strong selling (-3.8z). There was also large net selling (<-1.5z) 
in MID, DAX, Germany 5y/10y and SOFR, and large net buying (>1.5z) in AUD futures. 

•         CTAs: CTAs likely remain neutral to short US equities, with SPX/NDX/RTY all trading 
~3-4% below key momentum signals that could trigger releveraging. They likely sold long-
term USTs as yields surged following the hot CPI print last week, and added to shorts in 
precious metals and crypto w/w. CTAs likely remain generally long European equities, short 
APAC (ex-Australia and India), short global rates (ex-China and Korea), short Energy (ex-
natgas), and long USD vs global FX. 
•         CFTC Positioning: Asset Managers sold EAFE, covered shorts in VIX (+2.8z), and 
added to UST 10y futures w/w. Leveraged Funds cut shorts in SPX, flipped long EAFE 
(+2.3z), and sold USTs w/w. Managed Money sold oil and bought gold. 
  
US MKT INTEL VIEW 
•         BULL CASE – (i) NVDA Earnings – reiterating capex and support for existing 
financing deals is critical, as well as clarity on future leadership given a movement to return 
Huang to the company. Given the speed with which Huang was ousted leads some to 
believe there are more disclosures coming, which that company would need to address. 
Our Equity Derivatives team notes NVDA flows have shifted from downside protection to 
playing for earnings surprises, with little fear of another downdraft (ii) Capital Markets – 
this week’s events need to trade well to improve conviction on both broader equity markets 
and on the potential wave of ECM / DCM / M&A deals which are expected to accelerate 
before the next Congress is seated on Jan 3. (iii) AI / Tech Trade Returns – our JPM 
APAC Tech Conference kicks off in HK with ~250 firms presenting including Samsung, SK 
Hynix, and TSMC. The stabilization / move higher is likely to be led by HFs who have been 
sellers for 6 consecutive weeks, a Positioning Intel record. (iv) Macro Data – the major 
release this week is Retail Sales where another slowdown is expected with the risk that a 
downside print fuels the Recession narrative. Our recession basket (JPRECES Index) is 
+15.8% QTD vs SPX -1.2%. An inline or stronger print would help shift flows back towards 
Beta / Cyclicals supporting markets.  
•         BEAR CASE –  (i) AI / Tech Take Another Leg Lower – this could be a combination 
of NVDA failing to reassure on AI ecosystem financing, a delay to expected capital markets 

events, or perhaps negative pre-announcements are our APAC Tech Conf. Irrespective of 
the genesis, sentiment remains fragile and returns on precarious footing. (ii) A Decline in 
the Incremental Buyer – we have seen the Retail investor step in to buy dips and 
buybacks tend to accelerate in up-markets rather than down-markets, should the first 
domino be another downdraft surrounding AI / Tech, then we may see those buyers save 
their dry powder for 2027. (iii) Hawkish Fed Narrative – while last week showed Equity 
resilience from higher yields / bond vol, one should not expect that to be the norm going 
forward. A hotter Retail Sales print plus continued hawkish Fedspeak, perhaps seeing 
multiple members discussing the potential for 50bp; there are 17x Fedspeakers this week. 
Should the market believe that 50bp is necessary then it seems unlikely that hiking cycle is 
as shallow as the current pricing of 4x hikes (Dec inclusive). (iv) Positioning Purge – 
aggregate US positioning is ~20%-ile in the Tactical Positioning Monitor, dating to 2015; 
and global Semis exposure is 80%-ile, so there is room for another downdraft. Despite the 
recent price action, there has not be the definitive positioning purge that has triggered a 
consensus buy-signal. 
•         US MKT INTEL – We are Tactically Bullish. Adding to the bullish bullets from above, 
we think positioning is cleaner and thus entry points are more attractive. While the AI trade 
has given the market more to think about, demand is insatiable but the pace of the build out 
may be slower, which is not necessarily a bad thing as the commercial adoption rate and 
productivity gains increase. Assuming this week’s events unfold in bullish fashion, we look 
at inflation as being the primary risk but view that as more of a problem for early 2027 as we 
think it is highly unlikely that the Fed moves to hike by 50bp in Dec, which would create a 
tactical headwind to the Equity bull case.  
•         MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 16) –  The Anti-AI theme / ex-Tech rotation has been 
dominant in 26H2, even more so over the last month as Energy gains have flipped, the 
recession narrative has emerged, and investors have positioned more defensively. We think 
moving back toward Tech makes sense and like the cautious approach detailed in the 
previous Monetization Menu with primary exposure to Mag7 and then using options to  play 
a rebound in the AI themes. It seems most likely that the market will be patient with seeing 
how the catalysts play out rather than getting ahead of those events. Look for earnings, 
capital markets, and the JPM APAC Tech Conf to be a positive catalysts. In terms of ex-

Tech plays, we prefer Cyclicals over Defensives as the growth narrative reboots and 
markets digest an incrementally more hawkish Fed. Recall, SPX earnings are more tied to 
nominal GDP growth than to real GDP growth meaning increases in inflation are not a bad 
thing for earnings and that profit margins near record levels suggest that pass-through is not 
a problem in the near-term. We continue to like Europe for some Cyclical / Value exposure 
given relative positioning and valuation, though another surge in the USD may temper 
tactical outperformance.   
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 11) – We agree with Dubravko’s view:  The Tech 
washout appears to be at/near its completion though there remains some risk of another 
Semis decline, we like owning broad-based Tech plays and would consider US Memory and 
Semis plays via options. CRWV, OpenAI Q3 revenue, capital markets reopening are all 
supportive of a return to the AI theme; but the moves may remain choppy as markets 
consider another shift higher in bond yields. The barbell trade (Tech + Cyclicals) makes 
sense but think you can get more upside from playing Cyclicals via European 
names/sectors over their US counterparts due to relatively lower positioning and valuation.
