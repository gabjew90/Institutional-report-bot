Bet against the trend?
NDX is back near its massive long-term trendline after another round of selling. We are well below the
200-day moving average, momentum remains poor, and the 21-day is crossing below the 200-day.
The short-term picture looks ugly, but do you really want to bet against this long-term trend?

Record bearishness
Goldman's prime brokerage data shows US technology long/short positioning at its lowest level since
records began in 2016. More importantly, the nature of the selling has changed. What started as long
liquidation and de-grossing has now turned into outright short building. CTAs have also aggressively
sold technology, with Nasdaq positioning back near maximum short levels. The market has moved
from investors reducing AI exposure to actively betting against it. That leaves the door open for a
potentially violent squeeze.

The great Nvidia disconnect
Nvidia has lost 35% from its October peak despite an extraordinary upward revision in long-term
earnings expectations. As DB's Jim Reid points out, NVDA now trades at roughly 6x projected 2031
earnings.
Either the market is dramatically underestimating Nvidia's future earnings power, or those forecasts
are far too optimistic. Something has to give. Full note here.

Goldman cuts Taiwan
Goldman has downgraded Taiwan from Overweight to Market Weight, cutting its TAIEX target from
57,500 to 50,000.
The new end-2027 target still implies almost 20% upside, with EPS growth projected at 32% in 2027
and 25% in 2028. The problem is valuation. Goldman has cut its target forward P/E to 14x.
Strong earnings growth, but less room for multiple expansion.

The leverage problem
Goldman highlights record leveraged-ETF exposure and elevated margin balances in Taiwan.
Domestic liquidity has been a powerful driver of the rally, but the market is increasingly leveraged.
That leaves equities vulnerable to forced selling as prices fall, particularly through leveraged ETF
rebalancing and margin calls.

The anti-AI trade is working
We highlighted India as an attractive way to diversify away from the crowded AI trade several weeks
ago. That call has worked spectacularly.
MSCI India has outperformed MSCI EM by 13.7% so far this month, the strongest relative performance
since May 2009. Goldman has raised its 12-month NIFTY target to 28,500, citing strong domestic
demand and low foreign ownership.
We remain constructive on India, but after such an extraordinary move, chasing the market looks
increasingly unattractive. More on India here.

Too far, too fast?
The medium-term India story remains attractive, but the short-term setup is increasingly stretched.
Relative performance is at its strongest since 2009, foreign buying has accelerated sharply, and much
of the index short-covering has already taken place.

JPMorgan also flags rising input costs, margin pressure, weaker rural prospects following a poor
monsoon and tighter monetary conditions.
We prefer to rebuild exposure on a consolidation rather than chase India near the top of its range.

The oil connection
India is a major oil importer, making its equity market particularly sensitive to crude prices. The latest
rally has coincided with oil reversing lower, continuing the strong inverse relationship between the two.
However, Indian equities have now overshot the move in oil by a wide margin. The divergence is
becoming increasingly stretched.

Defensives pricing a growth scare
Privorotsky highlights the extraordinary rerating in defensives, with relative multiple expansion
approaching levels last seen decades ago.
XLU has outperformed TLT by roughly 5 percentage points over the past 20 sessions, a 3-standard-
deviation move. With nominal and real yields still elevated, investors are paying an enormous
premium for safety in equities.
The equity market appears to be pricing a growth scare that the bond market has yet to
acknowledge. More here.
