FX Daily: Dollar showing resilience to
tech stock instability
A poor start to the week for tech stocks has caused some dovish
repricing in the USD curve, but the dollar has retained its good
momentum. We still feel the AI story has the best potential to hit the
dollar at this stage. Nvidia’s earnings will drive market sentiment today.
EUR/USD may hold up around 1.100 despite a brief dip lower yesterday
Nvidia enters a new chapter without Jensen Huang at the helm

USD: Staying strong
The drop in US tech stocks has done little to dent the dollar’s strong momentum. That is
probably because the sell-off has spilled over sufficiently into broader US and global equities
for the dollar to benefit from its safe-haven role, despite some dovish repricing in front-end
USD rates.

Today’s main focus is Nvidia’s quarterly earnings, with the company’s long-term prospects
under increased market scrutiny following CEO Jensen Huang’s departure. Speculation that he
might be reinstated was dismissed yesterday. Nvidia’s results are always a key test for the AI
trade, but even more so in the current environment. We still think further weakness in tech/AI
stocks should make additional greenback appreciation less sustainable as it drives more dovish
Fed repricing.
On the data front, October US retail sales are due today. Expectations are for a slowdown from
September, but still slightly positive MoM growth: 0.1% in the headline and 0.3% ex auto and
gas. October industrial production is also expected at 0.1% MoM. These aren't tier-one
releases, and equity market dynamics still have a greater chance of driving FX moves today.
There is also more Fedspeak, after Kevin Warsh refrained from monetary policy comments
yesterday and the hawk Beth Hammack left the door open for outsized rate hikes. We’ll hear
again from Chris Waller, alongside Musalem (hawkish leaning) and Williams (dovish leaning).
We are sticking to our view of some dollar stabilisation rather than another major leg higher.
Still, the macro nature of the dollar’s bullish momentum means corrections may be more
easily faded.
Francesco Pesole
EUR: Holding at 1.10 for now
EUR/USD briefly dipped below 1.100 yesterday but lacked enough bearish fuel for a sustained
break lower. The short-term EUR:USD rate differential actually tightened by some 5bp in favour
of the euro, making us a bit more comfortable with not calling another leg lower in the pair just
yet.
The German ZEW survey is due this morning: both the expectations and current situation
indices are expected to rise modestly, broadly underpinning the narrative of eurozone
resilience. Still, the impact on ECB pricing should be limited.
Markets continue to price in 70bp over the next 12 months but are less aggressive on timing
relative to the Fed’s curve, with a follow-up hike after December expected only in March.
Crucially, we continue to see the EUR front end as more vulnerable to dovish repricing if oil
prices take another hit.
Elsewhere, French presidential frontrunner Marine Le Pen is set to present her pensions plan
today. Pensions are a fundamental issue given the difficult budget situation, but also a highly
politically charged topic. She may opt for a less drastic plan compared to the spending cuts
plan announced in early October (and raises some deliverability concerns).

GBP: Slightly softer jobs figures not impactful
UK jobs figures released this morning were weaker than expected, but unlikely to have much
impact on Bank of England tightening plans. October payrolls surprisingly declined by 34k, but
the September figure was revised higher from 10k to 20k. September unemployment was also
a tad lower than expected at 4.6%. The closely monitored wage figures were also a bit softer in
September, with weekly earnings ex-bonus down from 3.7% to 3.5% in September.
Expect much greater market sensitivity around tomorrow’s CPI report for October. The
consensus is for a small acceleration from 3.5% to 3.6% in the headline.
Pricing remains aggressive on the Bank of England, with a December hike having an 80%
implied probability and 90bp of tightening in the next 12 months. Our baseline is for the next
move only in February, but we admit the risks are of an earlier hike. EUR/GBP continues to face
some upside risks, but the 0.83-0.84 range may dominate as a range into the new year.
Francesco Pesole
NOK: Caught between an oil correction and higher rates
The Norwegian krone has been the worst-performing currency in the G10 since the start of
November, losing 4.7% vs USD and 3.3% vs EUR. The correction is primarily due to the sharp
drop in oil prices, but also mirrors an unwelcoming environment of hawkish Fed repricing and
risk sentiment instability for the least liquid currency in the G10.
The very attractive carry should protect the krone once global risk sentiment finds some more
sustainable stabilisation. But for now downside risks persist, especially as Norges Bank has
signalled greater caution in further tightening, removing some short-term protection for the
currency.
Our EUR/NOK target for year-end is 10.65, but risks are skewed towards exploration above
10.70 -10.75 in the coming weeks. Anyway, NOK continues to face greater downside vs USD.
Francesco Pesole
