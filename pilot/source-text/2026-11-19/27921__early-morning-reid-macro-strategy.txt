After a torrid couple of sessions, markets are breathing a sigh of relief this morning following Nvidia’s 
earnings after the US close. The company delivered a decent earnings and revenue beat and signaled 
confidence in future demand for its chips, projecting sales of $130bn for the current quarter (vs. 
$125.3bn est.) and upgrading its forecast of revenue growth for the upcoming fiscal year from 70% to 
75%. Meanwhile, Nvidia’s chairman said during the conference call that the board “does not intend to 
reinstate” Jensen Huang as CEO. Nvidia’s shares rebounded by nearly +5% in extended trading, after 
ending yesterday’s regular session at their lowest since July 2025 (-0.44% on the day). The earnings 
expectation for the company still remains astonishing. See my CoTD from yesterday here for a spotlight on 
this.  
Those earnings have driven an improvement in the equity mood overnight, with Nasdaq (+0.60%) and S&P 
500 (+0.24%) futures trading notably higher as I type. Tech heavy Asian indices like the KOSPI (+1.79%) and 
Nikkei (+0.55%) are moving higher but markets are more mixed elsewhere in the region. The Shanghai 
Composite has just turned positive (+0.18%) but the Hang Seng (-1.00%), and the S&P/ASX 200 (-0.69%) are 
lower. European futures are flat after big declines yesterday.   
Indeed, prior to Nvidia’s release, it was another rough session for markets, with fresh losses pushing the S&P 
500 (-1.03%) to its lowest level since July. Coupled with Monday’s -1.92% slump, this marked the first 
time since March that the index posted consecutive declines of more than -1%. While fears of an AI-
related slowdown were an initial driver, the sell-off broadened as the session went on. By the close, there 
were 427 decliners in the S&P 500 as the equal-weighted index fell by -1.45%. The mood wasn’t helped by 
ongoing reports of technical disruptions, with an outage in Apple’s systems after an internal update and 
CNN reporting an outage in the NSA’s internal communication network. It’s been a strange week for 
outages if you include Outlook and the grounding of all planes in the US on Friday. They appear to be all 
coincidences but you would understand some people being concerned about their sudden increased 
frequency. 

In the end, tech stocks fared better than most even before Nvidia’s earnings, with the Nasdaq (-0.74%) 
posting a more moderate decline and the Philly semiconductor index (+0.34%) stabilising after falling 
by -8.5% over the previous two sessions.    
The risk off cascaded across multiple asset classes, with US Treasuries also rallying as the equity selloff 
gathered pace. Indeed, there were fresh questions about how far the Fed would be able to keep hiking over 
the year ahead, with the rate priced in for September 2027 (the peak on the curve) falling -4.2bps on the 
day. So markets are now pricing in 81bps of further hikes from the Fed at the peak. And for a sense of how 
the risk-off mood was driving fixed income, the decline in Treasury yields actually reversed an initial jump 
earlier in the session after St Louis Fed President Musalem made some hawkish comments. He said that 
the economy “will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027 to achieve 
price stability in a timely manner.” So even as we had a clear signal of future hikes, markets still moved in a 
dovish direction on the back of the tech selloff. In fact by the close, the 2yr yield (-2.6bps) was down to 
4.80%, and the 10yr yield (-2.4bps) was down to 5.11%. They are down another -1.5bps and -1.8bps 
respectively overnight. 
Otherwise, sentiment also took a hit from the latest rise in oil prices, as fears grew about some kind of 
escalation. That came as Iran’s Mehr News Agency said that the US attacked locations on Qeshm Island 
on Tuesday. And earlier in the day, Iran’s negotiator Ghalibaf said on X that “Any dam, port, or power plant 
near a US or Israeli base is dual-use”. That came in response to a NYT report saying that water projects in 
Iran “may have been developed to support the country’s military buildup”. So given the latest rhetoric 
and various headlines, that helped push Brent crude oil prices (+0.76%) up to $93.14/bbl by the close.   
As all that was happening, the latest US economic data painted a mixed picture of economic performance. 
On the bright side, retail sales grew by +0.2% in October (vs. +0.1% expected). But industrial production 
unexpectedly fell -0.4% (vs. +0.1% expected), whilst the NAHB’s housing market index also fell 
unexpectedly to 25 in November (vs. 29 expected). Indeed, that was the lowest print for the housing 
market index since 2012, when the economy was still recovering from the aftermath of the GFC. So with 
all that in hand, the Atlanta Fed’s GDPNow estimate was little changed, with the Q4 estimate moving down 
very slightly from an annualised 3.5% to 3.4%.   
Earlier in Europe, markets had also put in a rough performance, with declines across the major indices. In 
fact, the STOXX 600 (-1.75%) posted its worst daily performance since March, alongside losses for the 
DAX (-2.08%), the CAC 40 (-1.62%) and the FTSE 100 (-1.76%). And in turn, there was a more consistent risk-
off across other asset classes, with yields on 10yr bunds (-1.5bps), OATs (-8.0bps) and BTPs (-2.1bps) all 
falling back. The outperformance of OATs came as Marine Le Pen discussed potential pension reforms, 
contributing to a more sanguine take on the RN candidate’s fiscal proposals.    
Here in the UK, it was a slightly different pattern where 10yr gilt yields (+0.5bps) rose on the day. That 
followed a mixed set of labour market data, but it did show the unemployment rate falling to 4.6% in the 3 
months to September (vs. 4.7% expected). That’s the lowest since April 2025, so it worked against 
several of the other headlines pushing yields lower around the world. Nevertheless, it was still a mixed 
report, as the number of payrolled employees fell -34k in October (vs. +10k expected).   
Across the channel, the German ZEW survey surprised on the upside in November, with improvements in the 
expectations and the current situation readings. The expectations reading was up to an 8-month high of 

35.6 (vs. 28.8 expected), whilst the current situation reading hit its highest since April 2023, at -33.2 (vs. -
35.3 expected). 
Early morning data showed that Japan's exports expanded for a 14th straight month in October, rising 
20.5% YoY (vs. 20.4% expected) and marking a record monthly level, driven by continued strength in 
semiconductor-related demand. Imports increased 21.6% YoY (vs. 25.9% expected), reflecting higher 
energy and freight costs. Meanwhile, a weaker-than-expected 20-year JGB auction reminded us of the 
current nervousness towards duration, with the bid-to-cover ratio falling to 3.64 from 5.84 previously and 
below the 12-month average of 3.92. 
Looking at the day ahead now, and there’s plenty of central bank speakers, including ECB President Lagarde, 
and the ECB’s Vujcic, Dolenc and Kocher, the Fed’s Daly, Barr, Williams and Kashkari, along with BoE 
Governor Bailey. We’ll also get the Fed’s meeting minutes from the October decision. Otherwise, US data 
releases include housing starts and building permits for October, along with pending home sales, and there’s 
also the UK CPI print for October.
