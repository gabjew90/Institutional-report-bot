High earnings bar, lower valuation bar
We have published our Global Economics & Markets Outlook 2027/2028. One of the 
things we discuss is how much is already priced into S&P 500 earnings: consensus 
expects growth of 17.8% in 2027 and 16.9% in 2028.  A key risk is that forward 
earnings revisions start to roll over. Historically, the market has struggled when revisions 
to 12- and 24-month forward earnings growth turn negative, especially when starting 
expectations are as elevated as they are today.
Earnings can, of course, surprise to the upside. This year began with 12-month forward 
growth expectations of 15.1%, against likely realised growth of 34.7%. But a repeat 
now depends unusually heavily on the hyperscalers' ability to sustain the AI 
infrastructure build-out.
Valuations, by contrast, are becoming a potential source of support. In the US and EM in 
particular, Tech and Semis have derated sharply despite strong earnings, and they 
account for nearly three-quarters of the market's derating (see today's chart). We read 
this as scepticism about the sustainability of the speed and magnitude of the investment 
boom, but it is not clear to us that company fundamentals have deteriorated:  cloud 
revenue growth was annualizing at ~100% in Q3, enterprise AI adoption is still low, and 
only part of the compute needed to meet future demand is in place. That said, our end-
2027 S&P 500 target of 8,900 conservatively assumes no re-rating.  See the full report 
here.

Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Risks of multi-asset investing include but are not limited to market risk, credit risk, interest rate 
risk, and foreign exchange risk. Correlations of returns among different asset classes may 
deviate from historical patterns. Geopolitical events and policy shocks pose risks that can 
reduce asset returns. Valuations may be adversely affected during times of high market 
volatility, thin liquidity, and economic dislocation.
