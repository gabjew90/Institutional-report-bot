Energy 
Oil prices are somewhat surprisingly lower today (down 2-3% with Brent ~93/𝑏𝑏𝑙𝑎𝑛𝑑𝑊𝑇𝐼~
86/bbl today, even as Iran reported six tanker strikes in the Strait of Hormuz since Sunday and 
launched fresh drone attacks on a US air defense site in Bahrain. 
 

 
 
The core reason prices are falling is that the physical oil market is well-supplied, and that supply 
cushion is increasingly outweighing geopolitical risk premiums. The IEA reported that global oil 
supply exceeded demand for the first time since the conflict began, and Goldman is now 
forecasting a sizable surplus heading into 2027 (flagging a potential glut of around 2 million 
barrels per day). That bearish fundamental backdrop has proven more powerful than the 
headline risk from ongoing attacks. As Capital Economics noted, "the energy supply backdrop 
has improved markedly since the height of the Hormuz crisis," with Gulf oil exports having 
largely recovered despite the shipping disruptions. 
 
 

 
A secondary factor (as Goldman highlighted) is market adaptation to persistent risk. Traders and 
shippers have rerouted around the most dangerous Hormuz corridors, and the physical market 
has absorbed the disruptions without a meaningful supply shortfall materializing. With a third US 
aircraft carrier strike group now in the Persian Gulf - raising the prospect of a negotiated or 
military resolution - some of the tail-risk premium that had been embedded in prices appears to 
be unwinding. The net result is a market that is pricing in the supply glut more heavily than the 
geopolitical threat, at least for now. 
The drop in crude also helped refined products with wholesale diesel prices dropping on both 
sides of the pond... 
 
And US retail diesel prices tumbled back below $6 for the first time since Sept... 
 
 

 
Nevertheless, tumbling stocks and oil prices did nothing for bonds... 
Rates 
After last week's volatility, today felt like another market holiday in bond-land with yields barely 
lower on the day with some late-day buying rousing interest. By the close, the 30Y was unch, 
and the belly down 2-3bps... 
 
While the rest of the week is heavy with FedSpeak, only Warsh, Barr, and Hammack spoke 
today. Barr warned that autonomous AI agents could accelerate bank runs, and Warsh outlined 
a range of scenarios for how AI could reshape the economy (Upside: AI boosts productivity 
broadly; Downside: AI drives substantial labor market disruption). Hammack reiterated she 
favors smaller and steady increases in interest rates to help fight inflation pressure she sees 
broadening across the economy.  
At the short-end, we saw Dec hike odds dip modestly (basically taking 50bps off the table)... 
 

 
 
But 2027 expectations lifted a little more... 
 
So, overall marginally hawkish at the short-end... 
Fiat & Anti-Fiat

And finally, as Bloomberg macro strategist, Frank Monkam, notes, the 'Fed Put' - or the 
expectation that monetary policy would cushion equity corrections - appears to be missing. That 
puts equities in an uncomfortable reality amid the current spike in volatility. 
Despite the recent drawdown in equities, markets continue to price in a more hawkish Fed, with 
June 2027 rate expectations moving higher even as the SPX tests the bottom of the recent 
range. Historically, equity corrections have prompted investors to expect a policy backstop in the 
form of monetary easing. This time, persistent inflation risks are complicating that calculus. 
 

 
 
For retail investors more specifically, the implications could be particularly dire. Dip alpha, or the 
alpha historically generated by buying market dips, is becoming increasingly difficult to harvest 
when the prospect of a monetary policy rescue keeps fading. 
So much for the record retail buying last week...
