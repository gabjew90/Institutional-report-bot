Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
17 Nov 2026
Asia: Under pressure again as the market digests Jensen’s departure and the prospect of tighter US AI
export controls. The potential implications of the MATCH Act and RASA are twofold… lost chip export
revenues could redirect supply into US domestic markets, while economies dependent on US technology
face weaker near term capex and potentially slower LT productivity growth. Much depends on the
eventual scope and implementation of these measures, but Asia’s supply chain is particularly exposed.
Nvidia remains central to global AI investment, and the market is beginning to price the risks of a more
fragmented ecosystem.
Defensive rotation: Magnitude has been extraordinary. Relative multiple expansion in defensives is
approaching levels last seen decades ago. More striking is the divergence between utilities and long
duration Treasuries… XLU has outperformed TLT by roughly 5 percentage points over the last 20 sessions
(3sd over 20d...see below). As I posted yesterday, what exactly are these stocks pricing? If bonds aren’t
going to rally, why pay such an immense premium for safety and quality? Perhaps it’s just that simple, but
with real and nominal yields where they are, the persistence of this rerating is surprising. The equity
market is pricing a growth scare that the bond market has yet to acknowledge.
Rates: Speaking of bonds there was ample opportunity for a rally yday given oil down/stocks
down/growth down but back end remained highly subdued. In fairness, Williams reiterated a cautious,
hawkish leaning stance, emphasizing that monetary policy must remain sufficiently restrictive to guide
persistent inflation back to the 2% target. Warsh spoke yesterday and offered a more balanced perspective
on AI, acknowledging the potential for an investment bust alongside the productivity upside. Remember,
Warsh has a deeply rooted history of focusing on FCI, particularly during the GFC. Think the reflexivity is
all around the equity market... If equities continue to fall, the distribution of potential rate outcomes should
shift quickly away from hikes.
Cyber: Recent IT disruptions across cloud infrastructure, commercial airlines, and bank...none confirmed
as malicious cyber events. Think this all underscores systemic operational fragility across complex
enterprise stacks. Feels like enterprise cybersecurity budgets will beginning to behave as non discretionary
capex. Key dynamic is spending durability: budget priorities are tilting aggressively toward automated
threat detection, real time telemetry, and identity verification to safeguard against higher velocity synthetic
threats. More spending here feels baked for 27-30.
Robotics: When are we going to start talking about this? While everyone is throwing their semi exposure
and 2028/2030 forecasts in the bin, perhaps the bigger AI unlock is happening in the physical world.
Advances in robotic training are extraordinary...GPU-accelerated simulation (e.g., NVIDIA Isaac Lab) and
differentiable world models (like SuperMimic, which compressed 100,000+ whole body motions into

three hours on a single GPU) have effectively commoditized lower level balance and locomotion. When
are the robots coming?
Risk: Retail sales the main macro event, alongside industrial production and another round of Fed
speakers. Nvidia earnings after the close… I suspect the near term numbers could be robust. But the
market simply has no idea what multiple to put on 2028, 2029 or 2030 earnings. For broader risk the
disconnect is increasingly stark… equities pricing a growth slowdown while rates and FX barely respond.
Either equities are overreacting or rates and FX are underreacting. The recent NDX lows are now the
technical line in the sand. Hold and a base can form; break and we risk another mechanical leg lower as
CTAs sell, volatility rises and risk reduced.
