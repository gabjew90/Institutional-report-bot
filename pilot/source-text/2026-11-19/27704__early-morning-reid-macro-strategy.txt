After getting over the disappointment of Robert Pattinson ruining my dreams of becoming the first bald James 
Bond, it’s back to markets. And yesterday certainly felt like “no time to buy”, as a rough start to the week 
continued amid renewed fears over an AI slowdown. First, there was another big slump for chip stocks 
ahead of Nvidia's earnings tonight, with the Philly semiconductor index down -5.28%. So that dragged 
equities lower more broadly, with the S&P 500 (-1.92%) posting a third consecutive decline. On top of that, 
fears about a slowdown put significant pressure on commodities, with Brent crude oil (-3.55%) coming 
down to $92.44/bbl. So it was a rare day of late where falling commodities were not a good sign. And for 
bonds there was little respite either, even though we had a decent risk-off move and lower oil prices. Indeed, 
the 10yr Treasury yield (-0.4bps) barely budged, whilst 10yr bund yields (+1.2bps) posted a slight 
increase. So it was a poor session once again, with weakness across the major asset classes. 
That tech selloff was the main driver of the day, as last week’s rebound failed to sustain itself into the new 
week. Admittedly, we’d already seen some declines on Thursday and Friday, but the move gathered pace 
yesterday, meaning that the Philly semiconductor index is now down by -9.03% over those three 
sessions. Moreover, it was announced yesterday by the US Commerce Department that data centre 
operators across multiple countries would now require a US government licence to purchase advanced 
AI chips. 
The tech story is set to stay in the spotlight today, as Nvidia’s earnings are being announced after the US 
close tonight. In recent quarters, Nvidia has often seen a negative share price reaction on the day after 
its earnings release. However, the most recent results in August ended that sequence, as the share 
price was up +8.74% the next day, breaking a run of 4 consecutive declines after the previous earnings 
reports. We’ll have to see what tonight brings, but clearly there’s been huge attention on the company over 
recent days, and President Trump himself said yesterday that he “would not mind” if Jensen Huang were 
reinstated as CEO. This was a slightly different emphasis to Treasury Secretary Bessent's interview on 
Sunday where he said the company needed a "dramatic shake-up". The stock closed -4.12% yesterday and 
is now down -27% over the past three weeks.   

That tech selloff has continued in Asia this morning, where all the major indices have lost ground. South 
Korea’s KOSPI (-1.95%) is leading the declines, but the Nikkei (-1.38%) has also posted a decent decline. 
Meanwhile, the Hang Seng (-0.73%) and the Shanghai Comp (-0.30%) are also lower, and the CSI 300 (-
0.20%) is currently on course for its lowest close since July 2025, if the current move is sustained. 
Nevertheless, there are signs that the selloff has started to stabilize, with futures for the S&P 500 (-0.10%) 
only slightly lower, whilst those on the NASDAQ 100 (+0.14%) are pointing to a modest gain. 
Whilst the tech fears have continued to mount, yesterday did bring more relief on the energy side. Indeed, 
Brent crude oil (-3.55%) fell to $92.44/bbl, and this morning is down another -0.81% to $91.69/bbl. That 
comes as investors have focused on the continued oil flows out of the Middle East and the potential for a 
supply glut to emerge next year. In addition, Vice President JD Vance said in a Fox News interview that “We 
are not going to allow this to become a 10- or 15-year commitment, or, frankly, even a two-year 
commitment”. So that eased fears about an extended conflict and supported the pullback in energy prices. 
Moreover, European natural gas futures (-6.82%) also saw a sharp pullback yesterday to €84.75/MWh. 
On top of that, another factor behind the oil decline was growing concern about economic demand, 
particularly as fears of an AI slowdown rose and tech stocks struggled. Indeed, yesterday saw broad-based 
declines across all the major commodity groups. For example, in the metals space, copper (-1.36%), silver (-
3.55%) and gold (-0.72%) all fell back, with the latter down to its lowest level since September 2025 at 
$3,856/oz. And even for several agricultural commodities, we saw declines for sugar (-1.68%), cotton (-
2.05%) and wheat (-0.95%). So that helped to lower inflation expectations on both sides of the Atlantic, with 
the 1yr Euro inflation swap (-9.4bps) falling to 3.13%, whilst the US 1yr inflation swap (-9.3bps) fell to 
2.07%. 
Yet even as oil and inflation expectations fell, this didn’t offer much respite for US Treasuries considering the 
scale of the moves. In fact, the 10yr yield only fell -0.4bps to 5.13%, as the 10yr real yield rose +3.1bps to 
2.92%, only a couple of basis points away from the post-2008 high in early October. Then at the front end, 
2yr yields (-1.3bps) saw a slightly larger decline as the amount of hikes priced in by September 2027 (the 
peak point on the curve) pulled back by -2.1bps on the day to 85bps. We also heard from Cleveland Fed 
President Hammack, who said she supported moving in “slow, steady increments, consistently, 
successively at meetings to make sure we’re getting that policy restriction back in the economy”. 
Earlier in Europe, there were further headlines on the ECB Presidency, as the de Volkskrant newspaper 
reported that former Dutch central bank governor Klaas Knot would withdraw from the ECB race this 
week. They cited EU diplomats, although that isn’t something we’ve had official confirmation about. So if 
that happened, it would clear the way for Pablo Hernández de Cos, who is currently the general manager 
at the Bank for International Settlements, and was previously the Spanish central bank governor. This is 
to fill the vacancy when incumbent President Christine Lagarde departs, as she’s standing down on February 
7, a few days after the ECB’s first policy decision next year. 
Otherwise in Europe, markets also struggled to gain traction across different asset classes. In equities, the 
STOXX 600 had initially been on course for another record high but then reversed course to close down -
0.28%. Similarly, sovereign bonds lost ground across the board, with yields on 10yr bunds (+1.2bps), 
OATs (+3.7bps) and BTPs (+2.7bps) all moving higher. And on top of that, the Euro itself continued to 
weaken, falling beneath $1.10 for the first time since April 2025 and closing at $1.0997.  

Finally, there was limited data yesterday, although we did get Canada’s CPI print for October, which came in 
softer than expected. It showed headline inflation remaining at +3.1% (vs. +3.2% expected), which meant 
Canadian government bonds outperformed their global counterparts. Indeed, the country’s 10yr yield 
fell -3.4bps to 3.82%, and market pricing for a rate hike at the Bank of Canada’s December meeting fell 
from 62% on Friday to 46% by the close. Otherwise, the New York Fed’s Empire State manufacturing survey 
for November came in weaker than expected at 7.8 (vs. 18.0 expected). 
Looking at the day ahead now, and data releases include US retail sales and industrial production for 
October, along with the NAHB’s housing market index for November. Meanwhile in Europe, we’ll get UK 
unemployment for September and the German ZEW survey for November. From central banks, we’ll hear 
from the Fed’s Musalem, Waller, Hammack and Williams, along with the ECB’s Moulin, Cipollone and Lane. 
Finally, today’s earnings releases include Nvidia and Home Depot.
