AI-linked equity sell-off deepened; Fed’s Hammack backed steady 
hikes; Empire survey missed; Canada CPI undershot; UK 2y gilt 
yield hit 5%; EUR fell below $1.10; BRL led EM losses on fiscal 
concerns; DXY at 103.58 (+0.2%); US 10y at 5.13 (-0.4bp).
A third day of selling in AI-linked equities drove safe-haven demand for 
Treasuries and JPY, while hawkish central bank pricing kept the dollar strong 
and pushed front-end gilt and Bund yields higher.
Developed Markets
• US Treasuries  twist-steepened, with the belly leading the rally (2y: -1.3bp; 
5y: -2.2bp; 30y: +0.9bp), as a risk-off rotation out of AI-linked equities 
supported safe-haven demand. The November Empire State manufacturing 
index fell to 7.8 (C: 18.0; P: 25.5), below all estimates, with prices paid easing 
to 45.9 from 59.5 even as the employment index rose. Fed Chair Warsh 
avoided comment on monetary policy at a San Francisco Fed conference, 
instead saying staff had begun mapping out contingencies for the economic 
impact of AI and cautioning that the technology could disappoint. Cleveland 
Fed President Hammack reiterated her preference for small, steady rate 
increases, while noting that larger moves may be needed if inflation 
pressures worsen. Markets continued to price 27bp of hikes into the 
December 9 decision, with SOFR options flows skewed toward additional 
hawkish protection. On the supply side, 3m and 6m bill auctions stopped 
through WI yields (3m: 4.155%; 6m: 4.310%), pointing to aggressive bidding.
• US equities sold off for a third straight session (S&P 500: -1.9%; 
NASDAQ: -2.5%; VIX: +18.7%), led by large-cap tech and semiconductors. 
Investors reduced AI exposure ahead of this week's events. The 
administration’s new AI-chip rule, which expands US oversight of overseas 
compute rented by Chinese firms, added to pressure on the sector. Selling 
broadened through the session, with IT (-3.0%), Communications (-2.8%), 
and Consumer Discretionary (-2.1%) lagging, while only Consumer Staples 
(+0.3%) and Utilities (+0.1%) posted gains. Bank stocks also weakened after 
reported technical issues. Bitcoin fell 4.5%.
• USD strengthened against most G10 peers (DXY: +0.2%) as hawkish Fed 
pricing held, with JPY the only gainer on safe-haven demand (USD/JPY: -
0.2%). Risk-sensitive currencies underperformed (AUD/USD: -0.5%; NZD/
USD: -0.5%), with weaker-than-expected Chinese activity data adding 
pressure. EUR/USD fell 0.2%, trading below $1.10 for the first time since 
April 2025, as one-month risk reversals moved to about -0.56 vol from -0.16 
on November 10. CAD touched its weakest level since 2025 (USD/CAD:

• Following the rise in UST yields on Friday, JGB yields moved higher across 
the curve during the Tokyo morning session. 3Q GDP data showed that

terms of clues on the BoJ’s rate hike path, the market is  focusing more on 
Governor Ueda’s speech on November 30th. Price action was largely driven 
by supply-demand dynamics, with the front end strengthening into the 
morning close while the super-long end remained under pressure. There

early London hours, and USD/JPY has since recovered to ~159.70, pointing to

• The German yield curve bear-flattened today, with 2y yields closing ~2bp 
higher and the 2s10s cash curve flattening ~1bp. Within EGBs, French, 
Italian and Spanish 10y spreads to Germany widened by ~3bp, ~2bp and 
~1bp, respectively. The sell-off was led by the short end despite declining 
energy prices and lower inflation swap rates, with the 5y EUR CPI inflation 
swap rate closing ~3bp lower. Policy pricing remained little changed, with 
markets pricing a ~92% probability of a December hike and a ~57% 
probability of a further hike in March. EUR swap rates were little changed 
and swap curves closed the day modestly flatter. 
• UK gilts underperformed Bunds across the curve and also bear-flattened, 
with 2y yields closing ~3bp higher and the 2s10s cash curve flattening 
~1bp. GBP swap rates rose ~2bp across maturities, with little change in curve 
slopes.

• Fed Chair Kevin Warsh focused his remarks on the potential 
economic implications of AI, noting that the Fed's staff has begun 
modeling scenarios in which AI either lifts productivity and growth or 
triggers widespread job displacement and a stock market downturn.  
He warned that the widespread adoption of AI - including what the 
federal government terms "superintelligence" - could fundamentally 
reshape the economy and carry significant monetary policy 
implications. Warsh did not comment on the near-term outlook for 
interest rates.
• Cleveland Fed President Beth M. Hammack reiterated her 
preference for moving in slow, steady increments on interest rate 
increases to restore policy restriction and bring inflation back to the 
Fed's 2% objective. She noted that inflation has broadened across 
more sectors of the economy and that businesses are becoming more 
accepting of passing on price increases, while the labor market 
remains broadly in balance. Hammack stated she believes the 
economy can withstand more restriction and would like to see the 
Fed reach a restrictive policy stance.
• In the Euro Area, ECB Vice President Boris Vujcic  said market 
expectations for an ECB rate hike in 2027 do not seem unreasonable, 
while reiterating that policy remains data-dependent with no 
predetermined rate path and warning that euro-area financial stability 
risks remain elevated amid stretched AI equity valuations and high 
public debt.
• ECB Governing Council Member Simkus said he is not sure that 
December's planned rate hike alone would be enough, and that 
further tightening in 2027 may be necessary depending on the 
inflation outlook and the strength of policy transmission.
• In Japan, BoJ Board Member Takata said 2026 marks the start of a 
new phase in which the Bank should raise rates nimbly rather than at 
a fixed pace and size, as the economy returns to a normal state. He 
called for moving the policy rate quickly to a "good" level close to the 
midpoint of the estimated neutral rate range of 1.1–2.5%, warning 
that continued low real rates could weaken the yen and drive up 
inflation through higher import prices.
• In Canada, BoC Governor Macklem announced he will not seek 
reappointment for a second seven-year term when his mandate 
expires in June 2027, with the Board of Directors having begun the 
search for his successor. He stated he remains fully committed to 
fulfilling his responsibilities and delivering for Canadians through the 
remainder of his term.
• In New Zealand, RBNZ said in its six-monthly Financial Stability

Emerging Markets
• In Taiwan, CBC Governor Yang said the central bank had intervened 
more frequently in the FX market from October through early 
November. He noted that recent TWD fluctuations were linked to 
local stock market movements and foreign capital outflows rather 
than the Taiwan–US interest rate gap. Yang reiterated that imported 
inflation remained under control and price pressures were relatively 
mild compared with other countries, adding that modest increases in 
fuel and electricity prices would have little impact on inflation, 
although large increases would be a different matter. On the 
proposed sovereign wealth fund, Yang said it would initially be small, 
with the proposed TWD30bn a starting point that could be increased 
annually. He added that the CBC was not suited to issuing foreign 
currency government bonds to finance the fund. Separately, CBC 
board member Chang said that he does not rule out a pre-emptive 
rate hike in December, though any increase could be limited. He 
expects monetary policy to remain neutral to slightly tight, with US 
interest rates, foreign capital flows and potential changes in domestic 
electricity and fuel prices among the key considerations.
• In Thailand, BoT Governor Vitai said there was no internal dispute at 
the central bank, following Chair Somchai’s comments of slow 
progress on regulatory reform and action to address a prolonged 
contraction in SME lending. Vitai added that he was not involved in 
the review of the BoT Act as potential changes directly concerned his 
position as governor.

Economic Releases
Developed Markets
• US November Empire Manufacturing: 7.8 (C:18; P:25.5). Manufacturing 
activity moderated in November, with slower growth in demand and 
shipments. Prices paid and received both eased but remained elevated. Labor 
demand strengthened. Delivery times lengthened at a slower pace, while 
inventories continued to grow, also at a slower pace than in the prior month. 
The Empire ISM-equivalent index fell to 55.3 from 57.8. We are tracking the 
November ISM Manufacturing PMI at 54.4, down from October’s 55.5.
• Euro Area September Industrial Production excl. Construction: 0.2% m/m 
(MSe: 0.2%; C: 0.6%; P: -0.5%). The print came in below consensus but in line 
with our estimate. Solid gains in Germany, Italy, and Spain were offset by 
declines in France and a drop in Ireland, which weighed on the aggregate 
figure. The y/y reading came in at -1.5% (C: -0.2%).
• Switzerland 3Q Preliminary Sport Event-Adjusted GDP: 0.2% q/q (C: -0.1%; 
P: 1.5%). The print beat consensus, coming in positive despite expectations of 
a slight contraction, though it represents a meaningful step-down from the 
prior quarter's pace.
• Switzerland Domestic Sight Deposits, week to Nov 13: CHF 425.3bn (P: 
CHF 429.2bn); Total Sight Deposits: CHF 443.7bn (P: CHF 452.9bn). Both 
measures declined week-over-week, continuing a gradual drawdown trend in 
SNB sight deposit balances.
