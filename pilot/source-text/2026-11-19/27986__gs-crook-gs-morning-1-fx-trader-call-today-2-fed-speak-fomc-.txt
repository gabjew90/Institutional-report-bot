2) US ECON (MERICLE) – Recent Fed Speak and FOMC Minutes Preview
Fed Speak: New York Fed President John Williams said yesterday that “inflation is stubbornly high and
we need to get it down,” but added that “I don’t think the situation calls for dramatic action” when asked
about the possibility of a 50bp hike at the December meeting. Commenting on the firmer-than-expected
October core CPI print, Williams said the data reflect a “continuation,” not a “broadening,” of the
passthrough from tariffs and the energy shock into prices. In other recent comments, Vice Chair Jefferson
repeated earlier remarks that further hikes will depend on “trends in the data,” which hints at a more
gradual pace, while other FOMC participants have sounded more hawkish. Full piece here
FOMC Minutes: The FOMC left the funds rate unchanged at its October meeting, and the meeting seemed
consistent with a compromise among Fed officials to hike every other meeting Since the meeting, the
inflation and labor market data have been on net firmer than expected. Following last week’s CPI report
we added a third 25bp rate hike to our forecast in March 2027, following a second 25bp hike in December.
In the minutes, we will look for details on the assumptions underlying participants’ economic outlooks and
views of the balance of risks at the time.

3) CEEMEA ECON (DALY) – NBH Review – LINK
BOTTOM LINE: The MNB kept its policy rate unchanged at +5.50%, in line with our forecast and
consensus expectations. The accompanying press release that inflation has been increasing in line with the
Bank's forecasts. The Bank expects inflation to increase in the coming months, driven by fuel prices and
policy measures (e.g., the increase in the excise duty on tobacco and the retail tax), whose impact will be
assessed in the December Inflation Report. The MNB also noted the positive reaction from markets to the
fiscal path released last month as well as the government's commitment to Euro adoption outlined in the
medium-term fiscal plan The Bank previously stated that a credible commitment to meet the Maastricht
criteria and a favourable market reception of the fiscal announcements would create scope for further
easing.
Policy Outlook: Looking beyond the short term, we maintain our view that the outlook for Hungarian rates
is more dovish than market pricing implies, and that the market has overreacted to potential inflation risks.

While elevated wholesale energy prices previously warranted caution on monetary policy, the sharp fall in
oil prices in recent weeks (if maintained) should mitigate further inflationary concerns. Moreover, we
forecast the lagged effects of past HUF strength to keep inflation subdued. On balance, we believe the
combination of weak price developments and the better-than-expected 2027 fiscal deficit target will give
the MNB room to resume easing in the coming months.

5) CNY Fixing Trend Analysis
Sun Lu (EM Market Strats): USDCNY midpoint fixing came in at 6.7118, down 14pips vs last and passed
through 24% of BBG survey implied daily fixing change. This is new YTD low in fixing again. Spot-
fixing gap is 456pips. This week's fixing started to move stronger, clearly a signal for APEC leaders
summit (Xi-Trump meeting tomorrow lunch time). We have historically observed stronger fixing into and
after leader's summit. We think USDCNY NDF remains a sell given these dynamics. Meanwhile, given
hawkish Fed + AI concern, USD has been bid. Long USDCNH-USDCNY NDF basis is also a good way
to express the grind lower in fixing vs strong USD for the near term. 6m USDCNH-USDCNY basis long
is near flat carry now while level is attractive to be long.
