NVDA – Harlan Sur’s full note is here 
•         Amidst all the noise and persistent headlines over the past two weeks, NVIDIA 
Corporation (NVDA) delivered a solid set of F3Q results with a strong F4Q guide that came 
in well ahead of consensus expectations (~5% beat) and slightly ahead of the buyside client 
expectations we had heard coming into the quarter (~1% above). More importantly (and 
rather surprisingly), the company raised its FY28 revenue growth outlook, with the team 
now guiding for ~75% Y/Y growth (up from ~70% prior) off what is now a higher FY27 base, 
citing both sustained demand and incremental supply support from its partners (this implies 
total revs could reach ~$741B in FY28, ~6% above consensus estimates). 
•         Strength in the quarter itself was primarily driven by the Vera Rubin ramp, which added 
~$20B of revenue in its first full quarter of shipments (fastest product ramp in NVDA’s 
history) and pulled forward the reacceleration in the co’s hyperscale business a quarter 
earlier than management had expected. 

•         On gross margins, management reaffirmed F4Q as the trough (71-72%) and indicated 
that GMs would improve to the 72-73% range in FY28, underpinned by supply 
commitments. 
•         On leadership, the Board confirmed that Jensen Huang would not be reinstated and 
that the search for a permanent CEO was underway, with interim CEO Colette Kress 
emphasizing that the company’s product roadmap remains on track (Vera Rubin Ultra on 
track for next year with Feynman slated for 2028). 
•         Although management has navigated a difficult backdrop admirably, we think three key 
points remain outstanding: 
1)    NVDA has halted licensed Hopper shipments to China and excluded 
China from its outlook, but that doesn’t necessarily resolve the regulatory 
overhang risk, as the ongoing investigations are likely to extend well beyond 
China (given the focus on diversion); 
2)    management didn’t clearly address financing / DC buildout risks for CY28, 
with the team striking a more measured tone on ecosystem financing and 
noting that allocation now hinges on customer land / power / shell readiness; 
3)    execution remains a key risk, as delivering on an annual product cadence, 
scaling the supply chain, and retaining top talent is a difficult task for any 
company, let alone one in the midst of a leadership transition. 
•         All told, the near-term outlook remains strong, and while the company’s roadmap 
remains on track, the question of who will lead the company through that roadmap (and 
beyond it) remains open. When you combine that with all the headline / regulatory risks 
(CEO search, execution risk, probes from the SEC and DoJ, etc.), we think there’s a strong 
case to be made that shares will remain highly volatile in the near-term. Nonetheless, on the 
back of tonight’s results and management’s stronger near-to-mid-term outlook, we are 
raising our estimates and adjusting our PT slightly higher to $230 (up from $215 
prior). Maintain OW. 
  

INTERNET LATERALS FROM NVDA PRINT – Doug Anmuth’s note is here 
•         NVDA’s earnings point to durable, multi-year AI infrastructure demand across our 
coverage & the broader industry. NVDA highlighted that Cloud industry backlog rose to 
$2.5T, up from $2T just 3 months ago, & its hyperscaler customers’ cloud revenue growth 
accelerated for the 6th straight quarter. NVDA compute remains fully utilized at every 
hyperscaler & neocloud it serves. GPU rental prices are also rising, w/Hopper GPU re-
contracting at up to 2x its original price, and B300 rates up 60%+ since launch. Key 
highlights for our Internet coverage include: 
1)    Vera Rubin racks are landing at every major hyperscaler, with GOOGL 
among the first to go live; 
2)    META is expanding across NVDA’s stack after early traction with its Muse 
agent; 
3)    SPCX is every bit a hyperscaler w/its strong ramp in AI compute; 
4)    all buyers get the same pricing, with new pricing coming in the new year as 
supply costs rise; and 
5)    agentic workloads (~80% of tokens) are pushing CPU demand well above 
supply. Beyond cloud, NVDA’s broader autonomous driving toolkit is a L-T 
positive for UBER. While rising compute costs remain a risk to cloud margins & 
capital intensity, we expect the clearest winners to be platforms with proven 
monetization, internal silicon, and captive demand. 
•         Among the mega-caps, we believe META reflected the biggest positive changes in 
NVDA’s disclosures. NVDA highlighted the Muse agent passing 10M setups in the U.S. & 
Canada within its first 6 weeks. META now plans to expand its use of Vera Rubin, Vera 
CPUs, Spectrum-X networking & BlueField-4 across training, inference & agents. We 
believe Muse reflects early, but real proof of product returns on META’s capex, which 
should help address FCF concerns over time. 

•         We believe GOOGL is well-positioned as a dual-track winner. Google Cloud is among 
the first to run live Vera Rubin NVL72 racks, and its A5X instances are now generally 
available. Google also joined NVDA’s group building open infrastructure for Vera Rubin. We 
believe pairing NVDA racks for cloud customers with internal TPUs gives GOOGL a margin 
edge, since NVDA indicated that all buyers pay the same price. That edge grows as frontier 
labs lean further into custom ASICs. 
•         We view AMZN’s readthrough as more indirect. AWS was not named among the live 
Vera Rubin NVL72 rack partners. However, it is a leading Vera CPU partner and works with 
NVDA on OpenShell, its agent security software. NVDA also expanded frontier lab access 
to Grace Blackwell & Vera Rubin capacity through cloud providers. We believe this supports 
growth at both AWS & GCP. 
•         SPCX is a featured NVDA customer, w/Vera Rubin NVL72 racks coming online soon at 
SPCX. NVDA also called out the continued buildout of SPCX’s infrastructure, w/SPCX a 
leading Vera CPU partner. SPCX is also NVDA’s #1 equity holding per its latest 13F. 
Importantly, NVDA sends supply first to customers with land, power & buildings ready and 
capital in place. It is also staying “disciplined and selective” on financing support. We 
believe that favors well-capitalized buyers like SpaceX over smaller neoclouds. 
•         UBER should be a beneficiary as NVDA introduced new Alpamayo tools and its Halos 
SAFE framework, expanded its Cosmos world models, and is investing in its own AV 
software. Mercedes-Benz, Volvo Cars, VW Commercial Vehicles & Bolt with Lucid are all 
using or showcasing NVDA’s Hyperion platform for automated driving & future robotaxi 
fleets. We believe a more turnkey AV stack lowers the barrier for many Level 4 suppliers to 
plug into UBER’s network. That weakens the bear case of a Waymo or Tesla robotaxi 
monopoly. While the same stack also equips rivals like Bolt in Europe, we believe UBER’s 
network liquidity & utilization remain its key edge. 
  
US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the November 16th Morning Briefing 

•         BULL CASE – (i) NVDA Earnings – reiterating capex and support for existing 
financing deals is critical, as well as clarity on future leadership given a movement to return 
Huang to the company. Given the speed with which Huang was ousted leads some to 
believe there are more disclosures coming, which that company would need to address. 
Our Equity Derivatives team notes NVDA flows have shifted from downside protection to 
playing for earnings surprises, with little fear of another downdraft (ii) Capital Markets – 
this week’s events need to trade well to improve conviction on both broader equity markets 
and on the potential wave of ECM / DCM / M&A deals which are expected to accelerate 
before the next Congress is seated on Jan 3. (iii) AI / Tech Trade Returns – our JPM 
APAC Tech Conference kicks off in HK with ~250 firms presenting including Samsung, SK 
Hynix, and TSMC. The stabilization / move higher is likely to be led by HFs who have been 
sellers for 6 consecutive weeks, a Positioning Intel record. (iv) Macro Data – the major 
release this week is Retail Sales where another slowdown is expected with the risk that a 
downside print fuels the Recession narrative. Our recession basket (JPRECES Index) is 
+15.8% QTD vs SPX -1.2%. An inline or stronger print would help shift flows back towards 
Beta / Cyclicals supporting markets.  
•         BEAR CASE –  (i) AI / Tech Take Another Leg Lower – this could be a combination 
of NVDA failing to reassure on AI ecosystem financing, a delay to expected capital markets 
events, or perhaps negative pre-announcements are our APAC Tech Conf. Irrespective of 
the genesis, sentiment remains fragile and returns on precarious footing. (ii) A Decline in 
the Incremental Buyer – we have seen the Retail investor step in to buy dips and 
buybacks tend to accelerate in up-markets rather than down-markets, should the first 
domino be another downdraft surrounding AI / Tech, then we may see those buyers save 
their dry powder for 2027. (iii) Hawkish Fed Narrative – while last week showed Equity 
resilience from higher yields / bond vol, one should not expect that to be the norm going 
forward. A hotter Retail Sales print plus continued hawkish Fedspeak, perhaps seeing 
multiple members discussing the potential for 50bp; there are 17x Fedspeakers this week. 
Should the market believe that 50bp is necessary then it seems unlikely that hiking cycle is 
as shallow as the current pricing of 4x hikes (Dec inclusive). (iv) Positioning Purge – 
aggregate US positioning is ~20%-ile in the Tactical Positioning Monitor, dating to 2015; 
and global Semis exposure is 80%-ile, so there is room for another downdraft. Despite the 

recent price action, there has not be the definitive positioning purge that has triggered a 
consensus buy-signal. 
•         US MKT INTEL – We are Tactically Bullish. Adding to the bullish bullets from above, 
we think positioning is cleaner and thus entry points are more attractive. While the AI trade 
has given the market more to think about, demand is insatiable but the pace of the build out 
may be slower, which is not necessarily a bad thing as the commercial adoption rate and 
productivity gains increase. Assuming this week’s events unfold in bullish fashion, we look 
at inflation as being the primary risk but view that as more of a problem for early 2027 as we 
think it is highly unlikely that the Fed moves to hike by 50bp in Dec, which would create a 
tactical headwind to the Equity bull case.  
•         MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 16) –  The Anti-AI theme / ex-Tech rotation has been 
dominant in 26H2, even more so over the last month as Energy gains have flipped, the 
recession narrative has emerged, and investors have positioned more defensively. We think 
moving back toward Tech makes sense and like the cautious approach detailed in the 
previous Monetization Menu with primary exposure to Mag7 and then using options to  play 
a rebound in the AI themes. It seems most likely that the market will be patient with seeing 
how the catalysts play out rather than getting ahead of those events. Look for earnings, 
capital markets, and the JPM APAC Tech Conf to be a positive catalysts. In terms of ex-
Tech plays, we prefer Cyclicals over Defensives as the growth narrative reboots and 
markets digest an incrementally more hawkish Fed. Recall, SPX earnings are more tied to 
nominal GDP growth than to real GDP growth meaning increases in inflation are not a bad 
thing for earnings and that profit margins near record levels suggest that pass-through is not 
a problem in the near-term. We continue to like Europe for some Cyclical / Value exposure 
given relative positioning and valuation, though another surge in the USD may temper 
tactical outperformance.   
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 11) – We agree with Dubravko’s view:  The Tech 
washout appears to be at/near its completion though there remains some risk of another 
Semis decline, we like owning broad-based Tech plays and would consider US Memory and 
Semis plays via options. CRWV, OpenAI Q3 revenue, capital markets reopening are all 
supportive of a return to the AI theme; but the moves may remain choppy as markets 

consider another shift higher in bond yields. The barbell trade (Tech + Cyclicals) makes 
sense but think you can get more upside from playing Cyclicals via European 
names/sectors over their US counterparts due to relatively lower positioning and valuation.
