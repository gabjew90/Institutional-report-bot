 
Bitcoin Erases Its Entire "Debasement" Rally As 
Goldman Says Every August Catalyst Has Reversed 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 16, 2026 - 05:25 PM 
It took bitcoin three months to climb out of its summer range. It took about three days to give the 
whole thing back. 
After losing its 200-day moving average on Thursday and its 200-week moving average (around 
$68k) on Saturday, BTC slid in thin weekend trade to a low of roughly $62k, while ether hit 
$1,711, before both stabilized near $65k and $1,826 in Monday's Asia session. That leaves 
bitcoin down 16% in November and 26% year-to-date, and, as Goldman's FICC crypto desk put 
it in its latest note this morning, aptly titled "GS Crypto: Unwind" (available to pro subs), the 
round trip is now complete: 
"Both assets have now fully unwound the price inflection that began on 19 August. BTC is 
trading back at levels seen before that, while ETH is slightly below its August range." 
In other words, the entire "debasement trade" in crypto is gone, and judging by how the 
weekend went, it didn't leave quietly. Which, for regular readers, should come as no surprise: 
two weeks ago we warned that while the blue wave was "largely priced" into bitcoin, "the flush 
probably isn't, especially if ETH's $2,213 cost basis gives way." It gave way. So did the flush. 
Gold Went First, Bitcoin Just Caught Down 
To understand the unwind, start with what kicked off the rally. On August 19, with 30Y yields at 
20-year highs, Bessent's Treasury unexpectedly doubled the size of its long-end buybacks, 
which we promptly dubbed "QE Lite", and gold spiked. Crypto followed, with an assist from the 
White House crypto event where the President urged Congress to pass the CLARITY Act.

Goldman's point is that the two "debasement" assets have now unwound in the same order they 
rallied. Gold moved first on the way up and first on the way down: by mid-September, bullion 
 

 
was already trading below its August 19 range. Bitcoin, as the desk puts it, "has now caught up 
with that adjustment." 
That gold leg is something Goldman's commodity team effectively conceded earlier this month, 
when it cut its gold target again in "Long-Term Constructive Gold, But Just Not Today" (available 
here for pro subs), which we summarized as Warsh killing the "debasement" trade (for now). 
Bitcoin, being bitcoin, just took a few extra weeks (and a lot more leverage) to get the memo. 
Every Catalyst Reversed (And Some Got Worse) 
The desk doesn't mince words: "In many respects, the conditions and catalysts that drove the 
August rally have now reversed, and in some cases worsened." Start with macro, where last 
week's hotter-than-expected CPI print was the third straight month of bad inflation news, and 
Goldman's economists promptly added a third rate hike to their forecast: 
"Markets now price in three hikes into early Q2 next year. The Fed's hawkish shift and rate hikes 
are likely to keep 'debasement concerns' contained - thus removing the narrative that had 
supported the moves in gold and BTC in August." 
And the price of money confirms it. Per Goldman, the 10Y yield is at 5.14% versus 4.70% in 
August, the 30Y at 5.47% vs 5.28%, and the DXY has gone from a 98 handle to a 103 handle. 
Translation: a stronger dollar and much higher yields, which is about the worst possible 
combination for anything that pays nothing. Funny how "digital gold" behaves exactly like 
regular gold when real rates go up. 
Then there's Washington. The August rally was partly a bet that CLARITY would pass. Instead, 
the Senate blocked it in September, and the midterm blue wave, including Sherrod Brown's 
comeback in Ohio that some in the industry view as a harder backdrop for crypto, means House 
Democrats are now drafting their own market structure bill with a bigger role for the SEC. 
Goldman notes regulatory progress "continued to progress through agency channels," but the 
legislative momentum "ultimately did not materialize." 
For context, Bloomberg's survey of pros back in October put the median year-end BTC call 
under a Democratic sweep at $76,456. Bitcoin is now about 15% below the "worst case." So 
much for the market having done the work. 
The ETF Bid Has Left The Building 
Which brings us to the marginal buyer that powered the last leg higher, and who has gone 
missing. Per Goldman, "outflows from U.S.-listed spot crypto ETFs accelerated through 
 

 
November," with US spot BTC ETFs losing $1 billion last week alone, bringing year-to-date net 
flows to -$1.9 billion. 
The chart shows cumulative flows down to roughly $57 billion from a peak of about $65 billion in 
October 2025, while price has lost almost half from last year's highs (chart source GS FICC, 
Bloomberg): 
 
Ether is worse. US spot ETH ETFs saw $398 million of outflows last week, taking year-to-date 
flows to -$804 million: 
 
Recall that at the start of the month, ether's realized price, the market's aggregate cost basis, 
was $2,213 per Goldman's monthly data pack. At $1,826, the average ether holder is now 
roughly 17% underwater. Diamond hands tend to get a lot less shiny at that point. 
Weekend Liquidity, Weekday Problems 
The technical break did the rest. As the desk describes it, BTC "lost its 200-day moving average 
on 12 November and subsequently fell below its 200-week moving average, which was around 
 

 
$68k, on 14 November," a level widely watched as bitcoin's cycle floor. Then, Saturday 
happened: 
"With lower weekend liquidity, the breakdown triggered a cascade of forced selling, with BTC 
recording $1.78bn of liquidations across 13-15 November. Perpetual futures funding has now 
fallen back to flat as of Monday, reflecting the reduction in net long positioning." 
We covered the carnage in real time on Sunday in "Liquidation Nightmare Sends Bitcoin 
Plunging As Market Gripped By "Extreme Fear"", but the chart below tells the story best: 
annualized funding, which was still running near 10% after the Binance hack, has collapsed to 
zero alongside price (chart source GS FICC, Coinmetrics): 
 
Two weeks ago, when funding was still at 10% into a falling market, we wrote that the 
"positioning is clean" argument looked "a lot like positioning is long and hasn't noticed yet." Well, 
it noticed. Naturally, we'll allow ourselves a modest victory lap. 
The good news, such as it is: zoom out to five years and the leverage wasn't remotely as 
extreme as the 2021 or early-2024 blowoffs, when funding spiked to roughly 90-125%. Then 
again, that cuts both ways: this was a relatively modest amount of leverage, and it still took out 
the 200-week moving average: 
 

 
 
Fade It? Goldman Is Happy To Sell You The Puts 
So what now? In options, Goldman notes that downside protection "continues to be better bid 
over upside," with BTC 1-month 25-delta risk reversals at -5.09%, the most bearish of all tenors 
(chart source GS Marquee PlotTool Pro): 
 
Which, Goldman says, creates an opening for the brave: 
"Those keen on fading the move might find it advantageous to fund calls by selling puts, given 
where positioning is now." 
 

 
Put differently, with funding at zero and longs flushed, the desk sees room for a squeeze, and 
the skew means the market will pay you to wait for it. Fair enough. But the five-year view is 
worth a look before anyone writes those puts: at -5%, today's skew is nowhere near the panic of 
2022, when 1M risk reversals plunged to roughly -37% (chart source GS Marquee PlotTool Pro): 
 
In other words, this is fear, but not yet capitulation. And the drivers of the unwind have not gone 
anywhere: the Fed is still hiking into December, 10Y yields are above 5%, the dollar is grinding 
higher, ETF holders are heading for the exits, and Anthropic's IPO next Thursday may be pulling 
speculative capital away from crypto (for now). 
The bulls will say the leverage is gone, the August froth has been fully digested and bitcoin is 
back where it started before Bessent's "QE Lite." All true. But the August rally was built on 
debasement, and the Fed is now doing the exact opposite. Until that changes, or until the bond 
market forces it to change, selling puts below the 200-week moving average in the middle of a 
hiking cycle looks a lot like picking up pennies in front of a steamroller. We'd wait for the ETF bid 
to come back first, or more likely, for the Fed to blink.
