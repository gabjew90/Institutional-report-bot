The minutes from the October 27-28 meeting showed that most members supported the decision to
hold rates steady but that almost all expected that the Fed would raise rates further. A couple of

Unchanged rates was a close call for a few members
The minutes from the October 27-28 meeting shows that most members supported the decision to hold
rates steady, awaiting more information and (according to a couple of members) to give businesses and
households time to adjust to higher interest rates. However, it was a close call for a few participants, while
some favoured a rate increase. Two members, Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack, voted to raise rates by 25 bp
at the October meeting and the language in the minutes suggest that their views were shared by other
members. However, a few who favoured a rate increase would, according to the minutes, have supported
unchanged rates provided it was “clearly understood” by market participants that further tightening was
likely. This could be a sign that some members are critical against Chair Warsh’s new communication
policy, including his resistance to forward guidance. Judging from his recent comments and speeches,
Governor Christopher Waller is likely to be among those.
A couple saw a larger step as appropriate in case of unfavourable inflation data
Almost all participants thought that the Fed would likely have to raise its policy rate further. The minutes
also shows that the possibility of a larger 50 bp hike at the December meeting was discussed at the
meeting and that “a couple” believed it could be appropriate if incoming inflation data were “unfavorable”.

Most members expected inflation to return to target over time
The minutes did otherwise not contain much news. Inflation was expected to remain elevated in the near
term, but most members expected it to return to the target over time, reflecting waning effects of tariffs, a
reduction in the contribution from energy and a moderation of price pressures from the AI buildout. At the
same time, the “vast majority” saw upside risks to this outlook. This suggests that tighter policy could be
seen as a precautionary step by the Fed rather than as necessary.
AI build-out remains in focus for the Fed
Much of the discussion focused on the risks from the AI buildout for inflation. The labour market was seen
as stable and close to maximum employment, though many saw signs of a modest tightening. A majority
noted that financial conditions had tightened since the September meeting – as pointed out by Chair Kevin
Warsh at the press conference – though several members believed that financial conditions remained
supportive of growth. Economic activity was generally seen as strong, reflecting resilient household
spending and robust investment.
Strong support for another rate increase in December, split thereafter
We view the minutes as strongly supportive of another rate increase in December, but with members more
split about the path thereafter. The discussion in the October minutes is, however, outdated since it took
place before recent stronger data for both the labour market (the higher-than-expected payroll growth in
October) and for CPI inflation (which surprised on the upside in October and with relatively broad-based
upside surprises). Recent comments from Fed members have in general been hawkish though Waller has
been alone in discussing 50bp steps.
We expect another rate increase in December and possibly also in March
FOMC members will not get much more data ahead of the December 8-9 FOMC meeting. There will be one
more employment report, for November, released on 4 December, while November CPI will only be
released the day after the FOMC meeting. Developments in financial conditions, including equities, and
geopolitical developments in the Middle East, may also affect the decision. But barring any large surprises
we expect the Fed to raise rates by another 25 bp at the December meeting. Members will also release
new projections for the economy and rates, and we see it as likely that these will signal a large probability
for at least one more rate increase in 2027, given that many members are now talking about further rate
increases (in plural), though most seem to prefer a gradual pace rather than a larger 50 bp move. We
continue to expect the Fed to raise rates again in December and possibly also in March 2027. Markets
continue to price a small probability of a larger increase in December (27 bp) and at least two more rate
increases until summer (81 bp by September).
