Markets at a breaking point
NDX is testing major long-term support after another brutal round of selling. Hedge fund liquidation in
tech has reached decade-long extremes, leverage has collapsed, and the latest positioning data show
just how much risk has already been taken off the table.
The similarities to previous tech sell-offs are increasingly uncomfortable. The 2018 pattern is striking,
while the lessons from the dotcom crash remind us that strong fundamentals don't necessarily prevent
further downside.
Meanwhile, gold is breaking major long-term technical levels, the euro remains remarkably weak, and
the dollar continues to attract support despite the turmoil in equities.
Make or break
NDX is trading very close to major make-or-break levels. The long-term trendline from the Liberation
Day lows comes in here, alongside the key levels from last year.
We are well below the 200-day moving average, and as we pointed out yesterday, the 21-day has just
crossed below the 200-day (a light death cross).
A similar cross earlier this year coincided with the local lows, but the same negative cross in March last
year was followed by the Liberation Day puke.

Tech liquidation at extremes
Hedge funds continue to aggressively dump global tech. On a trailing six-week basis, selling has been
by far the most aggressive in a decade, with a staggering -4.4 z-score. The global tech long/short ratio
has collapsed from a YTD peak of 2.9 to just 1.79, approaching 10-year lows.

The puke
Fundamental long/short funds continue cutting exposure. Gross leverage has fallen 4.4 points so far in
November to 206.3%, putting it at the 23rd percentile over the past year. Net leverage has dropped an
even sharper 7.6 points to 41.6%, the lowest level in five years.

The gamma trap
Nomura estimates aggregate SPX dealer gamma at approximately negative $7.3bn, near the bottom
of its historical range.
Dealer hedging can amplify moves in either direction, keeping the market unstable. McElligott sees the
VIX floor shifting higher, but limited positioning fuel for a genuine volatility shock above 30 without a
much deeper equity selloff. Latest on vol here.

2018 déjà vu?
The tech sell-off has been brutal, but we have been getting a strange sense of déjà vu. It took us a
while to figure out why, until we went back and looked at the nasty autumn 2018 sell-off. So far, tech is
following that pattern remarkably closely.
There were plenty of opportunities to catch the falling knife back then, but the market kept falling
much further than most thought possible. NDX eventually lost around 25% from its peak to the late-
2018 lows. More here and here.

The first warning
The Nasdaq peaked on March 10, 2000. By mid-April, the index had lost more than a third of its value.
But the broader market barely noticed initially. The S&P 500 remained resilient and was still trading
near record highs six months after the Nasdaq peak. The real pressure on the broader market only
arrived when the economic outlook started deteriorating.

TS Lombard
The fundamentals looked fine
One of the more interesting observations from TS Lombard concerns the timing of the deterioration in
tech demand.
Semiconductor sales continued to rise after the Nasdaq had peaked. Order backlogs remained
elevated, and companies were still struggling with shortages and bottlenecks.
Even in the autumn of 2000, Greenspan was pointing to strong capital spending and robust technology
demand. But the market was already telling a different story. Orders and backlogs took months to
adjust. Within six months, shortages had turned into a glut. More here.

Breaking
Gold is breaking below the major trendline that has been in place since 2024. RSI is already oversold,
but oversold markets can stay oversold much longer than most expect.
More importantly, gold is also breaking below the 100-week moving average, a level it hasn't traded
below since late 2023. The ultra-long-term 200-week moving average comes in just above $3,000.

The ETF risk
Gold ETF holdings increased by 211 tonnes in Q3, with Europe accounting for 92 tonnes of inflows. That
buying helped support gold despite weakening demand elsewhere.
ANZ now warns that North American ETF holdings could decline in Q4 as monetary conditions tighten.
Investment demand remains a key pillar of support, but that pillar is increasingly vulnerable. Latest
gold note here.

The dollar's unusual support
The dollar continues pushing higher despite the brutal tech sell-off, overshooting the widening in US
interest rate differentials.
Deutsche Bank notes that foreigners have been aggressively buying US equities into the weakness. Its
tracker of more than 100 foreign-domiciled ETFs shows the largest two-week buying spree of US stocks
this year.
DB questions whether the aggressive equity buying and widening rate differentials can both continue if
