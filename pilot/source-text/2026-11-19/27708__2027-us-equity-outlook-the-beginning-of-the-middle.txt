Theme 2: The great re-leveraging, continued 
 
 
Corporate leverage has increased as the AI investment cycle has become 
increasingly reliant on external financing. Hyperscaler capex spending exceeded cash 
flow from operations in Q3 and consensus estimates point to free cash flows becoming 
more negative next year. Our credit strategists expect total US IG and HY debt issuance 
will reach $2.8 trillion in 2027, up from $2.4 trillion in 2026. In addition to debt on 
balance sheets, the hyperscalers have entered into lease commitments and 
unconditional purchase commitments that represent roughly $3 trillion of additional 
liabilities.  
 
Strong capex spending has come in part at the expense of buybacks. S&P 500 
buyback growth stalled in Q3 on a year/year basis. The S&P 500 buyback yield now 
registers 1.4%, the lowest in two decades outside of recessions.

Theme 3: Value in Growth 
 
 
Our outlook for positive but decelerating earnings growth in 2027 indicates an 
environment that has historically favored stocks with secular earnings growth and 
“quality” attributes. During the past few decades, environments of slowing earnings 
and economic growth have favored stocks with price momentum, upward earnings 
revisions, and “quality” attributes such as low volatility and strong balance sheets. 
Among sectors, Info Tech and Health Care have typically performed best in these 
environments, while cyclicals have generally lagged.  
 
Valuations add to the argument for owning stocks with secular earnings growth. 
Valuation dispersion across S&P 500 companies has narrowed to its tightest level in 10 
years. Periods of similarly narrow dispersion have typically been followed by the 
outperformance of Growth as a factor relative to Value. Similarly, the valuation of our 
Growth factor currently ranks in the 11th percentile relative to the past decade and the 
38th percentile vs. the past 40 years.

Theme 4: Safety in diversification 
 
 
Although our base case is for continued earnings growth and equity market 
appreciation, the downside risks associated with extended earnings and valuations 
argue for diversifying portfolios with select defensives. Two examples are the Health 
Care and Consumer Staples sectors. In contrast with the S&P 500, earnings for those 
sectors register modestly below their respective trends. In addition, each sector trades 
at a relatively undemanding multiple on trend earnings. 
 
Defensive sectors have also outperformed during the unwinds following past 
overextended bull markets. In addition, Health Care and Consumer Staples have 
recently been inversely correlated with the AI trade, offering a counter-balance during 
periods of volatility. These sectors have also historically outperformed during periods of 
weakening earnings and economic growth (Exhibit 38).  
Within Health Care, our analysts are particularly positive on biopharma and parts of 
managed care. 
In Consumer Staples, our analysts see the most opportunity in beauty, energy drinks, 
and nicotine.
