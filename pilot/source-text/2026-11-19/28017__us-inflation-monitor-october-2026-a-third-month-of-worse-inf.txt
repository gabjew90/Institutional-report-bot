Core CPI rose by 0.30% month-over-month in October. Based on the 
o
details of the October CPI and PPI reports, we estimate that core PCE 
prices increased by 0.29% month-over-month and 3.24% year-over-year 
in October.    
Headline CPI rose by 0.35% month over month in October. We estimate 
o
that headline PCE prices rose by 0.32% month over month and increased 
by 3.83% year over year in October. 
Our measure of the breadth of high inflation remains above the average 
o
over 1990-2019, when inflation averaged roughly 2%, but well below the 
pandemic peak. The share of PCE categories above 3% rose after the start 
of the Iran war when sequential passthrough of higher commodity prices 
was at its peak, but has moved slightly lower on net in recent months. On a 
scale from 0 (the 1990-2019 average) to 10 (the 2022 pandemic peak 
year), the share of categories above 3% is 2 inclusive of tariff effects or a 1 
excluding tariff effects, and the share above 6% is a 2 on either basis.  
We estimate that core goods prices increased by 0.36% in October (vs. 
o
+0.20% in September) and 2.4% over the prior year in the PCE price index. 
This compares to an average year-over-year pace of -0.5% in 2018, when 
year-over-year overall core PCE inflation ran at a roughly 2% pace. 
We estimate that core services prices increased by 0.28% in October (vs. 
o
+0.36% in September) and 3.5% over the prior year in the PCE price index. 
This compares to an average year-over-year pace of 2.5% in 2018, when 
overall core PCE inflation was roughly 2%.

AI-related effects: 
n
Strong demand for AI infrastructure has raised the prices of some key 
o
electronic inputs, including digital memory and batteries. The producer price 
index for electronic components and accessories rose by 4% on a 
month-over-month basis in October. Import prices for computer peripherals 
and parts (which includes digital memory) and for semiconductors rose by 4% 
and 7%, respectively, on a month-over-month basis in September.  
We estimate that computer software and accessories prices rose 16% on a 
o
year-over-year basis in October, contributing 0.20pp to year-over-year core 
PCE inflation. This component is very sensitive to changes in memory prices, 
which have risen around 250-300% on a Q4/Q4 basis in 2026. Looking ahead, 
our equity analysts expect memory price growth to slow to a 5-10% pace in 
2027 (Q4/Q4), slightly below consensus expectations for 10-15% growth in 
memory prices next year. 
AI-related cost pressures for electronic inputs have boosted prices for 
o
computers (+9.7% year-over-year in September) and other consumer 
electronics such as phone services (+0.4%) and video games (+14.4%) and 
consoles (+9.6%). We estimate that consumer electronics categories added 
0.15pp to year-over-year core PCE inflation in October.  Incorporating our 
equity analysts’ forecasts, we expect the year-over-year rates of inflation in 
the computers (-11%), phone services (-1%), and video games (9%) and 
consoles (7%) categories to slow notably by the end of 2027.  
Strong demand for data centers is boosting electricity demand and power 
o
prices. We estimate that higher electricity prices will boost headline PCE 
inflation by 0.1pp in 2026Q4, with half coming via core components and the 
other half coming from food and energy.  
We estimate that AI price pressures are currently boosting core PCE inflation 
o
by 0.45pp, about half of which reflects genuine impact rather than 
mismeasurement, and we expect this to peak at 0.55pp by end-2026 before 
declining to 0.15pp by end-2027. But the largest effects on monthly inflation 
are likely behind us.

Inflation expectations: 
n
Inflation expectations increased notably following the start of the Iran war but 
o
have since retraced some of the initial increases. In the latest readings, 
short-term inflation expectations declined in the UMich survey (-0.4pp to 
+4.3%, 1yr ahead) and were unchanged in the NY Fed survey (at +3.9%, 1yr 
ahead). Long-term household inflation expectations declined in the University 
of Michigan survey (-0.1pp to +3.4%, next 5-10yr) and were unchanged in the 
New York Fed survey (at +3.0%, 5yr ahead). 
Financial market-implied expectations for annualized headline CPI for 2027 
o
were unchanged at 2.2% and expectations for 2028 declined by 0.1pp to 
2.4%.  
GS inflation forecast: 
n

o
to 2.4% in December 2027 as the effects of tariffs, the energy shock, and AI 
price pressures fade. 
We expect year-over-year core CPI to stand at 2.9% in December 2026 and 
o
2.2% in December 2027.
