Asia Open - Taking cover
By Preston Pagli
A broad risk-off tone carried across asset classes to start the week, with flows rotating back into USTs after last week's
historic selloff. US rates partially retraced ahead of a packed macro calendar including Nvidia earnings (Tue), new
issuance (Thu), Trump-Xi summit (Thu), and the arrival of a third US carrier strike group in the Middle East. SPX fell
nearly 2% with every sector lower aside from consumer staples while Nvidia (-4%) extended losses after CNBC reported
the company is not considering reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO. In FX, the dollar firmed with DXY (+0.18%) to 103.59
with Cleveland Fed President Hammack reiterating her preference for slow, steady rate hikes. In commodities, Brent
slumped 3.5% while gold weakened 90bps to trade firmly below the $4,000 handle. Looking ahead, RBA meeting
minutes will print at 00:30 GMT, followed by New Zealand inflation expectations at 02:00 GMT. Later on, UK labor data
will come at 07:00 GMT which will be closely watched for implications for December.

In Focus‌
Equities: The continued unwind of AI picks and shovels cratered headline indices with SPX -2% and NDX -2.7% as liquidity
begins to thin ahead of the US holiday season. Nearly every SPX sector was lower with more than 380 constituents lower. On
the cash trading desk, selling was skewed toward Info Tech, Financials, Healthcare, Consumer Discretionary, Comm
Services, Industrials, Energy, Consumer Staples, Utilities, Materials and REITs. In derivatives, vols were bid with Jan ATM:
SPX +0.2v, QQQ +0.65v, IWM +0.2v. Customer flow were two way directionally but generally better buying of options in SPX
and QQQ, with QQQ activity concentrated in 1-2 week expiries. The premier event this week is priceed as the trading session
after Nvidia earnings with NVDA Earnings: SPX @ 1.05%, QQQ @ 1.77%, IWM. Nvidia fell as a CNBC report put out rumors
Jensen Huang could return as CEO against mounting calls and a broad based petition. 
Rates: USTs strengthened to start the week following last week's sharp repricing that began with a hotter-than-expected CPI
print. The 5y (-2bps) was the biggest gainer Monday with a light US data calendar this week putting Fedspeak into focus. Fed
Chair Warsh provided little guidance as expected, instead giving his thoughts on AI, going on to say the Fed's role is neither
advocacy nor opposition, and “After significant capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns could turn down
and equity valuations could fall, leading to an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment,” Warsh said. “In
time, the economy would recover, but without the productivity gains.”

FX: The dollar remained well-supported with DXY +0.18% to 103.59, underscoring the flight to quality theme within markets
today as US equity weakness was countered by inflows into USTs and the dollar. Over the past week, USD saw net outflows
as bank and RM selling outweighed leveraged buying. EM aggregate flows were close to flat, with small net outflows as RM
buying was met with bank and leveraged account selling. CAD weakened roughly 0.2% after headline CPI rose +0.2% MoM
and 3.1% YoY in October, both below consensus. Our economists continue to expect rates to be unchanged in December.
Commodities: Crude oil slumped 3.5% despite fresh Middle East tensions and the arrival of a third US carrier strike group in
the region. Our sales trader notes participation remains light compared to the 10 day average while the market has been
leaning on the IEA's view that supply exceeded demand last week for the first time since the war began, on higher OPEC+
output, and on hopes of a diplomatic breakthrough. Gold hit its lowest level since October 2025 earlier today, dropping as
much as 1.3% to near $3,830/oz, before paring losses
Elsewhere‌
BRL weakened 2.3%, bringing USDBRL to a new cycle high of 5.79 as the market remains focused on Brazil's deteriorating
fiscal narrative and uncertainty surrounding Lula's new cabinet. Our trader Gabriel Groh notes that November and December
have historically been challenging months for BRL. At the same time, exporters have been notably timid in their USD selling
despite the sharp rally in USDBRL, contributing to lighter overall market volumes. Desk flows have remained skewed toward
the RHS, led primarily by offshore real-money accounts and CTAs.
