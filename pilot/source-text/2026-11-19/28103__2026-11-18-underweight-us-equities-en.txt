EGBs are in a pincer between energy prices and the gravitational pull higher of USTs. 
Energy 
prices remain a key source of downside risks for European bonds and currencies. In the run
-up to 
the midterms, the market had largely priced in the risk of the US launching another attack on Iran 
immediately after the election. Instead, the US Admin
istration chose more diplomacy, while JD 
Vance’s post -vote tweet putting the Iran war on top of the list of reasons for the GOP defeat 
triggered a mighty crude bear market, with Brent dropping below $90/bbl. In general, though, 
according to Kpler data, crude flows across Hormuz had already recovered to pre
-war levels and 
through October LNG flows had rebounded to close to 50% of pre
-war levels. To be sure, the 
crunch on refined products remains powerful, but shipping rates 
– one of the chief factors 
behind high energy prices lately 
– have dropped significantly, dragging down crack spreads. 
 
This reduction in risk premia has recently been reflected in part in lower rates expectations and 
EGB yields, including for OATs, how persistent this 
improvement proves depends
 on the odds of a 
more durable solution to the US
-Iran conflict. Crucially, though, the gravitational pull higher of 
USTs is likely to counteract marginal positive developments on the energy front in the short run. 
This is likely to be the case for Gil
ts in particular . Therefore, we remain neutral on Gilts, while 
preferring EA periphery govvies.
