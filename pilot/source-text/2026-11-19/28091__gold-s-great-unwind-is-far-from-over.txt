Breaking
Gold is breaking below the major trendline that has been in place since 2024. RSI is already oversold,
but oversold markets can stay oversold much longer than most expect.
More importantly, gold is also breaking below the 100-week moving average, a level it hasn't traded
below since late 2023. The ultra-long-term 200-week moving average comes in just above $3,000.

The gold hangover
The gold mania that peaked earlier this year attracted far too many investors chasing a narrative they
barely understood. Flushing out that excess speculation takes time. We have seen the same
psychology play out countless times following parabolic moves.
Gold continues to trade inside the broader downward-sloping channel that has been in place since the
all-time highs. The lower end of that channel comes in considerably below current levels.

The liquidation isn't over
ANZ estimates that nearly 200 tonnes of gold have already been liquidated through CME speculative
positions. Despite that selling, net longs remain at 533 tonnes, compared with lows around 200 tonnes
in 2022 and 2023.
ANZ sees scope for another 200 tonnes of speculative liquidation. The positioning washout could have
considerably further to run.

The other fear
Regular TME readers know that gold typically trades with an upside skew. During the bull market, we
saw some extreme examples of the spot-up, vol-up dynamic.
Reversals following parabolic moves fueled by heavy options speculation often come with another
feature: falling volatility suppressing deltas. We saw the same dynamic play out during the KOSPI AI
mania.
Worth noting is that gold volatility has recently started picking up as spot moves lower, a relatively rare
dynamic for gold. The crowd is increasingly pricing in downside fear.

The Fed isn't coming to the rescue
ANZ expects the Fed to hike rates by 25bps in December, January and March, with a 50bps move in
December still a possibility.
The market is already pricing around 80bps of tightening over the cycle. Stronger US growth, sticky
inflation and a resilient labour market are keeping pressure on non-yielding gold.

"But I trade gold, not the dollar"
If you trade gold, you are implicitly trading the dollar. The inverse relationship has been extremely
strong this year, with gold and the dollar moving almost perfectly in opposite directions.

The ETF risk
Gold ETF holdings increased by 211 tonnes in Q3, with Europe accounting for 92 tonnes of inflows. That
buying helped support gold despite weakening demand elsewhere.
ANZ now warns that North American ETF holdings could decline in Q4 as monetary conditions tighten,
adding another potential source of selling pressure.

The fear hedge myth
We have pointed this out many times, but gold is not a reliable fear hedge, despite the crowd
repeatedly returning to that narrative. For global market stress, you look at VIX, not gold.
Contrary to popular belief, gold has actually sold off during several of the biggest VIX spikes in recent
years.

Gold and China tech
China is a huge part of the gold trade. We have been wondering whether there is a connection
between Chinese tech and gold. ChiNext and gold continue to move in remarkably close tandem.
Could the washout in Chinese tech be spilling over into gold, with investors forced to sell gold to
finance losses elsewhere? You decide.

India's gold demand is collapsing
Indian bar and coin demand fell 40% year-on-year to just 55 tonnes in Q3. Global jewellery demand fell
to 313 tonnes, the weakest third quarter on record. The physical market isn't providing the support
many gold bulls might expect.
ANZ
The CB bid
While speculative investors are reducing exposure, central banks are doing the opposite.
ANZ has raised its 2026 central bank gold purchase forecast from 850 to 950 tonnes after buying
reached 321 tonnes in Q3. China accelerated purchases to 26 tonnes in October, the highest since
2023.
ANZ expects central bank buying to remain strong at lower prices, providing a potential counterweight
to investment liquidation.
