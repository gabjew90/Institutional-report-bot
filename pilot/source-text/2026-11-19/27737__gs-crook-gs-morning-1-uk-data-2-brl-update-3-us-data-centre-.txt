1) UK ECON (MOBERLY) – UK Data
Labour Market Data Review: The unemployment rate dropped to 4.6% in September, in line with our
forecast but a tenth below consensus. We had noted that a cohort with a particularly high unemployment
rate was dropping out of the sample at this print. Vacancies dropped to 690k in October, after the
September figure was revised up to 697k. Small business vacancies continue to show a negative trend. The
flash payrolls data for October showed a 34k contraction, notably weaker than consensus expectations for
a 10k increase. The September data were revised up, but August was revised lower. Private sector regular
pay growth edged up to 3.1% (from 3.0%), in line with expectations. The sequential pace for August was
revised up, but the September month-on-month figure was softer at +0.13%.
CPI Preview: We think the inflation numbers will show headline CPI inflation rising to 3.7% (from 3.5%).
Core inflation is likely to rise to 2.9%, driven by firmer core goods prices and an increase in volatile
services components. Energy inflation should accelerate given higher road fuel prices and an increase in
the Ofgem price cap, and we expect tobacco inflation to rise on the back of higher duties. Full preview
here

2) BRL Update
See below for takeaways from senior thought leaders on the outlook for Brazilian assets. More details
available in Connecting you to GS
Alberto Ramos (Head of Latam Economics) on the Econ: We are focused on three areas: (1) The
credentials and policy preferences of the new Finance Minister; (2) The fiscal strategy for the next 3-4
years, including the quality (revenue increases or spending cuts) and nature (short-term or permanent) of
the measures needed to deliver the primary fiscal path; and (3) The new central bank directors, and
whether their appointments tilt the Copom toward a more dovish, heterodox composition. The fiscal
package submitted to Congress on Wednesday failed to impress the market, as the measures were mostly
short-term and skewed toward raising revenue. With fragile fiscal and debt dynamics and an unsettled
BRL, the market is looking for strong, credible policy signals.
Fabiano Ribeiro (MD, Macro Sales Brazil) on Macro: Most investors have stepped back from the trade
and the mood is defensive. Several large macro funds have closed their Brazil FX, rates and equity
positions and are waiting on clarity around the economic team; some have zeroed long BRL positions,
keeping only received real rates; and the big asset managers are reluctant to re-add risk after equities lost
over R$700bn in market cap. Foreigners are still net sellers in the futures exchange. What I'm watching:
the Finance Minister pick, the credibility of any fiscal package, and — increasingly a bigger risk — the
Central Bank board nominations, where heterodox names would hurt BCB credibility. On top of that, a
hawkish Fed and increasingly weaker domestic growth / higher inflation outlook keeps the backdrop
tough. Net, our outlook is more cautious: Brazil looks cheap but risks being a value trap. A re-rating really
needs external help (weaker dollar, lower oil) plus credible appointments — not domestic momentum.
Rafael Fonseca (Brazil Rates Trading) on DIs: Given the exceptionally high bar for the BCB to resume
rate hikes in a slowing economy, combined with sticky inflation, we favour positioning for a
decompression of premium in the front end: i) Front-End Receivers (up to Jan28): Capitalizing on priced
hike-premium at the short end of the curve; ii) Real Rate Receivers (NTN-B/Linkers in the 2028–2030
sector): Protecting against inflation while capturing high real yields; iii) DI Steepeners: Expressing the
view of a long-term fiscal risk premium shift.
Lucas Baladi Chohfi (BRL FX Trading) on FX: We think there’s too much uncertainty around which kills
any carry trade for the short term(<1m) as 1) Global assets trading on a long USD bias -> We don’t know
how hawkish fed can be; 2) Seasonality is for stronger USD towards the year end -> Historically outflows
goes past 15bn USD due to Remittances/dividends/etc; 3) It’s unclear if market will take the fiscal
adjustment they proposed and what this implies to the economy as it’s basically raising taxes. If we walk
towards to a recession path that can get quite dangerous. We’re walking slowly for a scenario that we have
a bear-trend like 2024 year end, where the market flips its bullish trade towards global and local issues and
selling USDBRL gets erratic. We see a good entry point for 1h27 positive seasonality and carry
compounding. We have been trading tactically on USDBRL buying dips and a short USDBRL would
depend on a resolution on the first three points mentioned above.

Ernesto Mello (Latam Equities Trading) on EWZ: EWZ is down 23% since the election, vs. EEM and
ACWI mostly flat. The question our trading desk is getting the most – have the inflows from the pre-
election hype been already erased? Quick answer is NO, we are about halfway there. EWZ
creations/redemptions shows that the election hype started in late September – there is still a US$1.5bn
overhang to be sold. At the current rate of about US$150mn in redemptions per day we should have a
much cleaner position by mid Dec. Volumes have been drying up quite fast from the peak election days to
US$5bn/day from US$15bn. So, the issue is that the exit door is also getting narrower. In Orange 2026
volumes vs. Min/Max of the last 5yrs in shaded gray (see chart below); the bottom line is that money
rushed in when the doors were wide and will have to trickle out from a much more narrow exit.

Key excerpts on the implications of the Midterms for US datacentre giga-watt production across 2026-
28…
Geoffrey Okomoto: On the one hand, we are probably past the point at which the US government has no
restrictions and no hurdles to AI, both at the R&D level and at the physical infrastructure build out… but
it's not a 0 or 1 phenomenon… the Democrats aren't necessarily irrational when it comes to designing the
frameworks for this… the new Texas Governor has basically said is that she is going to put a pause on new
permits for data centres until the legislature enacts some kind of safeguards and regulatory changes in this
area… in actuality, some of the hyperscalers have actually begun to agree to some of these safeguard
terms in Texas and elsewhere, so the threshold for them to comply with some of these upcoming foreseen
requirements is actually not too dramatic… to that end, I don't think there's a dramatic interruption that
happens for data centre building as a result of the midterm elections.
Michael Ng: We view data centre moratoriums as a temporary pause, as communities assess the impacts to
the constituents in that area… ultimately it may cost a little bit more for some of these data centre
developers to stand up their data centres, in the case of tax breaks and property taxes, potentially adding to
the incremental costs… in the grand scheme of things, that's a very marginal cost relative to standing up
the data centre itself… so I would consider more risk to timing, rather than the capacity coming online…
from all our checks, the demand side of the equation, from hyperscalers to other types of tenants, is very
strong.

4) NVDA Preview

Jim Schneider (Head of US Semiconductors Research): We expect Nvidia to deliver solid 3Q results, with
4Q guidance above the Street driven by better-than-expected margins. Our 3Q/4Q EPS estimates are both
6% above the Street. We expect investor focus to centre on three key debates: (1) management’s visibility
into datacentre power/shell capacity visibility through 2028; (2) the sustainability of funding for AI
infrastructure; (3) broader AI demand health, including agentic-driven CPU upside, customer ROI, and
enterprise adoption. Full preview here
Rich Privorotsky (Head of European One Delta Trading): Nvidia earnings after the close… I suspect the
near-term numbers could be robust. But the market simply has no idea what multiple to put on 2028, 2029
or 2030 earnings. For broader risk the disconnect is increasingly stark… equities pricing a growth
slowdown while rates and FX barely respond. Either equities are overreacting or rates and FX are
underreacting. The recent NDX lows are now the technical line in the sand. Hold and a base can form;
break and we risk another mechanical leg lower as CTAs sell, volatility rises and risk reduced. Full note
here
Sean Johnstone (TMT Specialist Sales): Ahead of Nvidia’s results tonight, a minority see downside risk to
guidance, but most see limited near-term risk and are more focused on how the company addresses CEO
succession—although some question how much it can say this early. Overall, the bigger debate remains
calendar 2028, rather than the next couple of quarters. Full note here
