US Economics 
  
Time, time is not on your side 
We have added a 25bp hike in Jan ’27 to our Fed forecast. We now expect hikes at the 
next three meetings (Dec, Jan, Mar), for a terminal rate of 4.5-4.75%. After Oct CPI, the 
Fed looks further behind the curve on inflation. It no longer has the luxury of hiking at a 
quarterly pace. Risks to our new call are balanced. The Fed might delay or skip the last 
hike if there’s a soft patch in the data or significant tightening in financial conditions. A 
surge in wage inflation or large annual price resets could push the policy rate above 5%. 
The week ahead: Focus on retail sales & FOMC minutes 
Oct retail sales (Tue) and the FOMC minutes (Wed) are the highlights of this week’s 
calendar. We also get Oct import prices (which will help refine our Oct PCE estimate), 
Oct industrial production & the Nov NAHB housing maket index (Tue), Oct housing starts 
(Wed), and Nov Univ. of Michigan consumer sentiment (Fri). We will again have plenty of 
Fedspeak this week, including Warsh (Mon), Williams (Mon, Tue, Wed), Barr (Mon. Wed, 
Thur), Waller (Tue, Fri), Musalem (Tue), Hammack (Tue, Thur), Kashkari (Wed), Goolsbee, 
Barr, Barkin & Paulson (Thu) and Cook (Fri). 
Oct retail sales preview: Looking past a tepid month 
Total BAC aggregated credit and debit card spending per HH rose by just 0.1% m/m in 
Oct, with outright declines across a number of categories. However, the rebound in 
online retail (card not present) should provide some offset. Seasonal factors for Oct are 
also likely to be supportive. Therefore, we forecast a trend-like retail sales report, with 
both ex-auto and control group sales rising 0.4% m/m. Meanwhile, holiday spending is 
off to a strong start, though a decline in the discretionary wallet share in Oct is a point 
of concern. 
See note: BofA on USA: October retail sales: looking past a tepid month for 
methodology, limitations, and disclaimers related to BAC aggregated credit and debit 
card data. 
Oct FOMC minutes preview: A heated hold 
We expect the Oct FOMC minutes to reveal a deeper divide over the decision to be on 
hold than the 10-2 vote suggested. Those favoring a hold likely argued the rise in yields 
was doing some of the Fed’s work and pointed to financial market concerns. Those 
opposed likely emphasized the economy’s underlying strength and the risk of falling 
behind the curve. The subsequent data flow should reinforce the latter camp’s concerns, 
and lead them to push for picking up the pace of hikes going forward. 
 
See note: US Economic Weekly: Fed call change: Adding a hike in Jan ’27

Fundamental Equity 
Retailers: WMT, TGT, HD, LOW 
 
Walmart: Well positioned for durable growth 
Walmart reports Thu Nov 19 before market. We think WMT is poised to deliver 
continued share gains across both grocery and gen merch (see note: CEO meeting 
takeaways). We forecast 3Q EPS of $0.63 (roughly in line with consensus) and expect 
WMT to update its FY sales and EPS guidance to the upper end of its prior range.  More 
in: Walmart Inc: 3Q Preview: Well positioned for durable growth 
Target: Risk/reward becoming more challenge 
Target reports Wed Nov 18, and we hold a cautious view. TGT has delivered an 
impressive improvement in sales this year, but we remain wary about the pace of EPS 
revisions and sustainability of strong comp trends heading into 2027. We see risk that 
the stock will begin entering crossroads of decelerating sales trends (on tougher laps), 
worsening industry margin dynamics (freight, promos), and limited SG&A flexibility 
versus others in our coverage given the company’s need to lean into this investment 
cycle to drive sustainable comp. More in this note: Target Corp.: 3Q Preview: Risk/reward 
becoming more challenged 
Home Improvement Retailers 
HD: Higher Pro exposure should drive outperformance 
Our 3Q EPS est of $3.83 is relatively in line with consensus $3.85. HD laps the GMS mix 
headwind in September, shifting the focus to the magnitude of supply chain costs and 
management’s ability to offset these headwinds as tariff refunds roll off. A lack of 
storms in 4Q could add risk to sales, particularly SRS, but we note the company has 
proven to gain share/grow comps in tough backdrops before. HD’s share gains should 
translate into stronger comps and provide a catalyst for multiple expansion. 
LOW: FY EPS guidance feels derisked absent a 4Q surprise 
Low’s reports Wed Nov 18 (before mkt). Our 3Q EPS estimate of $2.84 (cons $2.90) is in 
line with management’s guidance calling for a 7% EPS decline. Despite the worsening 
supply chain backdrop, we think LOW derisked its full year EPS guidance last quarter and 
think potentially better sales (and refunds) could offset the tougher margin backdrop and 
challenging new home construction market. Tariff refunds are not included in guidance 
and we think on a full year basis this could amount to roughly $400mn or 53c in EPS. We 
see potential risk from a milder winter as LOW laps winter storm benefit in January last 
year (50bp comp benefit in 4Q25).

Options Risk Statement 
Potential Risk at Expiry & Options Limited Duration Risk 
Unlike owning or shorting a stock, employing any listed options strategy is by definition 
governed by a finite duration.  The most severe risks associated with general options 
trading are total loss of capital invested and delivery/assignment risk... all of which can 
occur in a short period. 
Investor suitability 
The use of standardized options and other related derivatives instruments are 
considered unsuitable for many investors.  Investors considering such strategies are 
encouraged to become familiar with the "Characteristics and Risks of Standardized 
Options" (an OCC authored white paper on options risks). U.S. investors should consult 
with a FINRA Registered Options Principal.  
For detailed information regarding risks involved with investing in listed options: 
http://www.theocc.com/about/publications/character-risks.jsp 
 
 
  
Price objective basis & risk 
Lowe's Companies, Inc. (LOW) 
Our PO of $251 is based on 20x P/E (F27) estimate, a slight premium to the company's 
LT average valuation of 18x given its higher Pro mix which has a better backdrop than 
DIY and structural margin improvement. 
 
Upside risks to our PO are improving consumer sentiment and other macro metrics tied 
to renovation spending, better-than-expected margin expansion from sales growth 
coupled with cost-saving and productivity initiatives, and upside from favorable weather 
events. Downside risks to our PO are rising interest rates which may continue to dampen 
investor sentiment towards housing, whether acquisition-related dilution masks 
underlying earnings power, a slower than expected improvement in comps, and slower 
than anticipated progress towards margin improvement goals. 
Target Corp. (TGT) 
Our $141 PO is based on 15x our 2027E EPS, relatively in line with historical multiple 
given near term revenue inflection, balanced by potential downside risk to 2H comp and 
margin in F27. 
 
Downside (upside) risks to our price objective are higher (lower)-than-expected margin 
pressures from labor costs, markdowns, investments, and digital profitability as well as 
more (less) aggressive competition from competitors. 
The Home Depot, Inc. (HD) 
Our 12-month price objective of $397 is based on 25.5x P/E (F27E), a premium to LT 
avg of 20x given our forecast for an acceleration in multiyear EPS growth. We believe a 
multiple above the home improvement average (21x) and HD's long-term average of 20x 
is warranted given the relative resilience of the home improvement retailers in the 
current macro backdrop, likely market share gains, and consistent execution at the 
company. 
 
Downside risks to our price objective are a weakening in the housing market/consumer 
backdrop beyond our forecasts, whether acquisition-related dilution masks underlying 
earnings power, unfavorable weather and poor execution in supply chain upgrades, and a 
significant increase in promotions by HD and/or competitors. Upside risks are a
