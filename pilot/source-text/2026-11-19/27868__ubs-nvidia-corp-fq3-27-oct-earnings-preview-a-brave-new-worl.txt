Summary Thoughts
We expect NVDA to beat FQ3 (Oct) revenue by ~$3-4B (e.g. as much as ~$111-112B) 
and we see FQ4 (Jan) revenue guidance in the ~$130B range  (Street ~$125B) and 
revenue ultimately coming in even higher than this as Rubin remains on track to ramp 
substantially in FQ4. This is despite delays for some rack components like rail kits – in 
other words, a similar situation to prior product generations where compute boards 
shipped in well ahead of the inflection in rack shipments (which should come in 
CQ1:27). With the company having already soft-guided FQ4 gross margins to 71-72% 
(before rebounding starting in FQ1), there should be no surprises on margins. In the 
wake of this recent management shake-up last week, we heard many investors 
concerned about NVDA’s longer-term product roadmap – concerns that we find 
overblown as the ecosystem will continue to pull the roadmap along and any new CEO 
would almost certainly not want to risk competitive standing by moving away from the 
annual product cadence (though we still believe the new more dense Kyber racks are 
now off the table). As for a new CEO, we believe the company will likely look to the 
semis and tech world, including potentially trying to hire from a competitor. In addition 
to the potential for semis to undergo some correction given the  mismatch between 
semis build plans and powered shell availability that we first highlighted in late 
September, our biggest concern now is new scrutiny that may restrict some of its sales 
into certain geographies such as Singapore/Malaysia. In a worst case, we estimate this 
could cut  up to ~10% out of revenue - in the near term though, we see no effect to 
estimates as demand still significantly exceeds supply. This could, however, be an issue 
for C2028 – especially as we are hearing more about delays in data center readiness/
availability. As a result, we are cutting C2028E revenue/EPS from $985B/$22.08 to 
$885B/$20.01 – a cut of ~10% in terms of revenue. As for C2027, the company already 
soft-guided it to be up at least 70% Y/Y and we could see this maybe walked a bit 
higher as a symbolic show of strength (UBSe ~80%). Lastly, in terms of valuation, 
everybody understands the stock is inexpensive, but when viewed through the UBS 
HOLT framework, this is particularly jarring. In essence, the market is assuming C2028 
will be the peak, there will be no growth thereafter, and EBITDA margins will compress 
~1,500bps - this seems incredibly draconian to us. Net, we are trimming our PT on the 
back of the C2028 revenue cut, but we believe the near-term damage to the stock has 
been done and we don't think it makes sense to give up on the stock after being down 
this much (as some of our peers have done last week) especially ahead of January 
hyperscaler earnings calls where we think CapEx will be guided higher than current 
Street expectations. On a lower C2028E EPS, our PT goes from $300 to $240, obtained 
by applying a 12x  NTM P/E multiple to our $20.01, and we view this pullback as an 
attractive buying opportunity, albeit one that may require some patience to let the 
management dust settle.

Modeling Details and Other Key Items

We are lowering our revenue estimates from ~$420B to ~$418B in C2026 and 
from ~$744B to ~$739B in C2027, which assumes ~$391B and ~$710B in Data 
Center revenue, respectively. Our latest NVDA GPU unit estimates are ~8.2MM in 
C2026 (vs. 8.1MM prior, +1%) and ~8.4MM in C2027 (unchanged), including 
CPX chips but excluding CPUs and LPUs. Including CPUs and LPUs, we expect total 
chip volumes of ~9.8MM in C2026 and ~14.3MM in C2027. We continue to 
model gross margin in the ~72-73% range through at least C2027 (i.e., the Rubin 
generation), while we believe out-year margins will increasingly depend on factors 
including memory pricing and the Rubin Ultra configuration. We model ~$182B 
and ~$355B of FCF in C2026 and C2027, respectively, as NVDA plans to return at 
least 50% of FCF to shareholders, including ~$235B of share repurchases over six 
quarters through FY2028, while continuing to invest in the ecosystem.

We now estimate C2026 product mix at 67% Blackwell Ultra, 26% Rubin 
(including CPX, LPX), 6% Blackwell, and 1% Hopper (vs. prior 65%, 28%, 6%, 
and 1%, respectively). For C2027, we model ~81% of revenue from the Rubin 
platform, 14% from Rubin Ultra, and 5% from Blackwell (unchanged from our 
prior ests). Importantly, with Vera Rubin now ramping in production and the 
broader ecosystem deploying systems at scale, we expect the transition to become 
increasingly pronounced through C2027 as Rubin scales across GPU, CPU, and LPX 
configurations and becomes a larger contributor to overall DC compute revenue.

From a supply chain perspective, the UBS Global team maintained its TSMC 
CoWoS capacity estimates at ~130k wafers per month (WPM) by YE26, while 
continued expansion in non-TSMC capacity is expected to push total industry-
wide CoWoS capacity toward ~270kwpm by YE27. On a full-year basis, our latest 
industry CoWoS capacity forecast stands at ~1.59MM wafers in C2026 
(unchanged vs. prior) and ~2.8MM in C2027 (+7%), while UBSe HBM estimates 
continue to imply supply for ~11MM NVDA units in C2027, also unchanged. 
Taken together, the latest CoWoS and HBM data leave us increasingly constructive 
on available supply into C2027, with both supporting slightly more units than we 
currently model and providing additional capacity headroom relative to our current 
GPU unit assumptions. While our updated GPU unit forecast moves ~1% higher in 
C2026 and remains unchanged in C2027, we view our C2027 estimates as 
reflecting conservatism around the timing of deployments rather than available 
supply.

On the demand side, the latest hyperscaler earnings cycle reinforces our view that 
AI infrastructure spending remains on a much steeper trajectory than current 
investor expectations imply. Our updated framework now points to ~$1.76T of 
hyperscaler/CSP capex in C2027, +69% Y/Y, with the Big Four alone reaching ~
$1.13T. Importantly, we see the breadth of spending as increasingly relevant - the 
build-out is no longer being driven solely by the traditional hyperscalers, as other 
AI infrastructure programs become a much larger component of the overall 
investment cycle. Net, we believe the combination of a substantially larger capex 
pool and broader participation in AI infrastructure spending provides a more 
durable underpinning for C2027 demand, with further upside possible should the 
magnitude of capex revisions seen over the past several years continue.

Based on our cloud accelerator study (Figure 32Key Acelerators' # of locations), we see growing Blackwell supply 
getting absorbed without meaningfully disrupting the existing GPU base. October 
data shows continued build-out of B200/B300 capacity is occurring alongside 
relatively steady availability across H100/H200 and older NVDA GPUs - suggesting 
the cloud market is adding Blackwell as incremental compute capacity rather than 
simply replacing prior-generation instances, a potentially constructive read-
through for aggregate GPU demand. Pricing is admittedly less clean, with variation 
across architectures and providers, but we would put more weight on the physical 
expansion of Blackwell across the cloud ecosystem than on monthly spot-price 
volatility. And while purpose-built alternatives increasingly look competitive on 
raw $/FLOP, NVDA still offers something harder to replicate: multiple generations 
of accelerators deployed at meaningful scale across multiple clouds, giving 
customers considerably more choice around capacity, workload placement, and 
migration. 

On Rubin, our checks reinforce our view that risks are increasingly weighted 
toward the timing and cadence of deployment, but with relatively limited change 
to underlying full-year unit potential. The hardware outlook remains consistent 
with ~80K/95K racks (C2026/C2027); however, based on the full-year chip supply,

there appears to be sufficient support for upside to current rack forecasts while 
also maintaining ~6 weeks of inventory exiting C2027. That said, downstream 
component signals point to a more back-end loaded ramp:  VR substrates remain 
on plan, but rail-kit shipments, which typically lead ODM assembly by ~1-1.5 
months, have shifted meaningfully to the right, with King Slide now expecting the 
majority of VR volumes in 1H27. At the same time, Hon Hai's latest feedback 
suggests assembly bottlenecks have improved, leaving the timing of NVDA chip 
availability as the primary near-term constraint on the ramp. Longer term, supply 
commitments remain supportive, with NVDA targeting a ~2.5x increase in 
substrate capacity through 2028 across Ibiden, Unimicron, and SEMCO, including 
Unimicron's KF fab beginning production in 2Q27 and scaling through mid-2028. 
Net, we see limited change to full-year rack shipment potential but greater 
quarterly timing risk and a more back-end loaded Rubin ramp. Ultimately, the 
continued build-out of substrate and broader supply capacity leaves us more 
constructive on C2027 unit potential; however, we believe the cadence of DC 
deployments could introduce some slippage in when that supply translates into 
recognized GPU volumes.

availability guarantees than spot. They fit production or time-sensitive workloads where 
interruptions are costly and offered at a premium to spot.

Spot Pricing
Spot instances are spare, interruptible capacity that clouds sell at variable prices that can 
change quickly and can be reclaimed with little notice. They are best for flexible, fault-
tolerant workloads where you can checkpoint and restart, and the tradeoff is lower cost 
in exchange for reliability risk.
Note that Trainium2 and B300 spot pricing is not yet publicly available on cloud pricing 
tables but may be available through sales representatives.

Company Description
NVIDIA Corp. designs and manufactures graphics processing units, add-in boards, and 
related low-level software. Its Graphics Processing Unit segment includes GeForce for high-
end video games and professional graphics workstations; Quadro for designers; DGX and 
Tesla Accelerator cards for data center and AI scientists; and GRID for cloud-based visual 
computing users. The Tegra Processors segment provides processors that enable embedded 
computer vision applications in automotive video surveillance end markets, as well as 
delivering multimedia experiences on mobile gaming platforms, while consuming minimal 
power.
Valuation Method and Risk Statement
NVDA: Our price target is based on applying a P/E multiple. NVIDIA company-specific risks 
include: 1) competition from AMD in graphics processors (GPUs) and professional 
visualization products, which historically have driven the bulk of the company’s gross profit, 
2) intense competition in ARM-based applications processors, where rivals seek to leapfrog 
NVIDIA's early lead in dual-core technology, 3) emerging competition from Intel with its 
many-integrated core (MIC) processor family vs. the company's professional GPU computing 
products, where Intel's approach could be more flexible and easier to develop software. In 
addition, NVIDIA faces the semiconductor sector risk where revenue trends have historically 
been linked to corporate profitability, which could be challenged for robust growth, as the 
economic recovery has been more subdued vs. prior deep downturns.
