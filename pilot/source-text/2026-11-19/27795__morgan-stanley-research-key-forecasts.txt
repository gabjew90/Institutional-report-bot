Next 12-Months Outlook: Our High-Conviction Calls
Our US and global economic outlook remains constructive, although higher energy prices are keeping inflation elevated for longer. In the 
US, resilient consumption and AI-driven capital spending support robust growth across the forecast horizon, while a “slow-hire/slow-fire” 
labor market keeps unemployment below 4.5%. We expect productivity growth to improve gradually, but disinflation comes primarily from 
fading energy and tariff effects. Against that backdrop, we expect the Fed to raise rates in December and March, taking the policy rate to 
4.25-4.50% by 1Q27, before remaining on hold through 2027 and beginning to ease in 2Q28. In Europe, higher energy costs generate a more 
persistent inflation impulse, with headline inflation peaking above 4% in early 2027 and core inflation remaining above target until mid-2028. 
We therefore expect the ECB to hike in December and March to 3.00%, before holding until June 2028. In Japan, higher energy prices and 
tighter financial conditions weigh on near-term activity, but structural investment remains resilient; we expect the BoJ to raise rates twice, 
reaching 1.75% in March 2027. More broadly, emerging markets face a greater near-term slowdown from higher energy prices and tighter 
financial conditions, but should benefit as those headwinds fade and the US AI investment boom continues to support global trade.
Embracing Risk: An intact AI capex cycle gives us confidence that resilient macro and earnings growth will extend into 2027.  With risk assets 
having cheapened, we embrace risk, but with tails remaining long,  pair with quality.  We recommend a maximum OW in global stocks, EW 
in core fixed income, and UW in other fixed income, commodities, and cash. Lean into quality cyclicals, keep bonds as a hedge, and move 
up the quality curve in most corporate and securitized products. We add to equities in every region and lift Japan and Europe to OW. We 
close out of our IG corporate credit UW given the repricing that has already occurred, and fund all of these changes by paring risk in the 
rest of fixed income, commodities, and cash.
 Morgan Stanley Research: Key Forecasts
In our baseline, we remain broadly constructive on global growth through 2028, with the AI 
infrastructure build-out, particularly in the US, providing an important engine of activity. Resilient 
US growth generates positive spillovers globally, while Asia’s broader capex cycle continues and 
Europe benefits from sustained strength in the periphery and additional fiscal support from 
Germany. Outside the US, however, higher energy costs produce a modest soft patch around the 
turn of the year, most notably in Japan and the UK, before growth gradually firms through 2027. 
Emerging markets face a more persistent slowdown, reflecting the combined drag from elevated 
energy costs and restrictive monetary policy.

Next 12-Months Snapshot
Equities: Risk vs Reward Is Favorable; We are OW US, Japan, & Europe and EW EM
 We remain constructive on US markets as positive operating leverage, stabilizing rate and oil volatility, and continued AI investment and 
adoption drive mid-teens earnings growth. For Japan, we expect earnings growth to slow gradually as growth in AI investment tapers, with 
the deceleration centred on Electric Appliances & Precision Instruments, while banks take over as a key driver of EPS growth as interest 
rates normalize. We believe the forward P/E can rise above its  long-run average, supported by structurally higher ROE and shareholder 
returns, and by our economists' view that the BoJ will raise rates less than the market prices in. A sharper correction in AI capex is the key 
downside risk. In EM, base case upsides to target prices are significant as the secular earnings cycle likely continues, with risk/reward 
favouring Japan versus EM & China. We continue to anticipate above-average volatility. For Europe, despite continued relative caution 
towards Europe from market participants, MSCI Europe has actually slightly outperformed the S&P 500 since November 2024 in total return 
terms –  a structural break with the prior 10Y+. Our new end-2027 MSCI Europe index target implies +15% upside, in line with our US 
strategists' target upside, & slightly ahead in total return terms (+19% vs +16%, respectively). This trend is supported by continued 
diversification flows, earnings rebalanced toward structurally growing sectors, buyback yield ahead of the US for first time in history, and 
demonstrated corporate resilience following lessons learned in 2022.

In the US, we forecast 10y UST yields to peak at 5.05% in 2Q27 before ending 2027 at 5.00% and 2028 at 4.75%, as Fed cuts begin 
in 1H28. Across G10, we expect yields below forwards and steeper curves, particularly in 2028, with the most value in gilts and 
Bunds. We forecast modest USD strength as carry demand, wider USD-RoW rate differentials, and the US’s investment appeal 
support the greenback. We see DXY rising to 105 and EUR/USD declining to 1.08 ahead of the French presidential election before 
stabilizing through end-2027. We forecast USD/JPY at 162 and GBP/USD at 1.27, while AUD and NZD outperform within G10.

Commodities
Oil - Physical Softening vs Lingering Supply Uncertainty:  The US elections outcome now makes a resolution in the Middle East 
now looks more likely, but not imminent. Physical oil market conditions have also steadily weakened as more Middle East oil hits 
the market and freight rates come down. However, flat price remains very geopolitics dependent.  We return to $75/bbl Brent by 
4Q27 but keep 1Q27 at $100/bbl. 
Gas - Too Early to Call the All-Clear:  European gas prices continue to be supported by tight fundamentals. European gas storage 
is at a record low for early November of 69% on cold weather  and Qatar's LNG flow at only 40% of pre-war levels, meaning Europe 
will have to continue to compete for supply in a tight global LNG market. We forecast TTF at ~€90/MWh in our mild winter base 
case, with risks still skewed to the upside. 
Metals:  In gold,  the current macro backdrop poses challenges amid dollar strength and expectations of higher interest rates. 
Near-term physical indicators are pointing to tight availability in copper although US tariff policy remains the key uncertainty. In 
aluminium, drawing inventories and limited near-term availability comes against expectations of a meaningful surplus in 2027. 
Zinc mine supply remains constrained, while nickel may find some cost support at current levels. See: metal&ROCK: Copper 
Signalling Scarcity.
G4 Central Bank Monitor
In the US, resilient growth and persistent inflation keep the Fed focused on preventing higher energy prices from generating second-
round effects. We expect two further hikes, in December and March, taking the policy rate to 4.25-4.5%, followed by an extended 
hold through 2027 as inflation gradually recedes. As disinflation becomes more firmly established, we expect easing to begin in 
2Q28, with rates reaching 3.625% by year-end. The ECB faces a similar but more persistent inflation challenge, as higher energy 
costs pass through into wages and services. We expect 25bp hikes in December and March, taking the policy rate to 3%, before 
the ECB holds in restrictive territory until June 2028, when improving inflation dynamics allow quarterly cuts to begin. In the UK, 
the energy shock keeps near-term inflation elevated despite a softer underlying growth backdrop. We expect the BoE to hike again 
in February and remain on hold through 2027, before easing begins in 2028 as headline and core inflation move more convincingly 
toward target. The BoJ continues its gradual normalization, with hikes in December and March taking the policy rate to 1.75%, before 
holding as the energy impulse fades and inflation settles below 2%.
