Following the departure of cofounder and CEO Jensen 
Huang, NVIDIA’s 3QFY27 results came in ahead of 
expectations, with revenue of USD 115.8bn versus 
consensus of USD 109.0bn and non-GAAP EPS of USD 2.68 
versus USD 2.48 consensus. Data center revenue reached 
USD 107.6bn, up 110% y/y and comfortably ahead of 
expectations. Within the data center segment, hyperscaler 
revenue of USD 60.1bn was well ahead of expectations and 
grew 71% y/y off a difficult prior year comparison of 125%. 
ACIE revenue (AI Cloud, Industrial & Enterprise) revenue of 
USD 41.5bn grew 195% y/y, well above consensus as Vera 
Rubin shipments ramped to leading neocloud customers 
and frontier labs began to build out their own data 
centers. Non-GAAP gross margin was 74.9%, above 74.0% 
consensus and up from 73.6% a year ago, while non-GAAP 
operating margin was 67.2% versus 65.8% consensus and 
66.2% a year ago.
Guidance for the January 2027 quarter of USD 130.0bn 
±3% was also comfortably above the consensus of USD 
125.3bn, driven by robust AI infrastructure demand and 
the continued Vera Rubin ramp. We note that guidance 
excludes any data center compute revenue from China. 
Non-GAAP gross margin guidance of 71.0-72.0% brackets 
the 71.6% consensus, with management expecting 4QFY27 
to mark the trough before margins recover to 72-73% in 
FY28. The midpoint of guidance points to upside in non-
GAAP operating income driven by higher revenue, with 
non-GAAP operating margins slightly below expectations on 
higher costs due to a 14-week quarter and transition costs.

More importantly, management raised its FY28 revenue 
growth outlook to approximately 75% y/y from 70% 
previously, citing rising demand and additional supply 
availability. Putting it all together, we expect earnings 
estimates to be revised higher in the mid- to high-single-
digit percentage range.
We see five key takeaways from results and the earnings call:
1. Moving 
forward 
in 
the 
post-Jensen 
era.
Management stressed the depth of NVIDIA’s technical 
and leadership bench, continued employee commitment, 
and ongoing customer support following the CEO 
transition. The board plans to consider both internal 
and external candidates and highlighted the importance 
of navigating advanced silicon and national-security 
dynamics in selecting a permanent CEO. Management 
also cited continued engagement from key suppliers, 
including TSMC, and said memory and advanced-
packaging capacity had been secured.
2. 2028 guidance revised higher on both demand 
and supply visibility. Management raised FY28 
(January 2028) revenue growth expectations to 
approximately 75% from 70%, reflecting both continued 
demand growth and improving supply availability. The 
company emphasized that demand remains well above 
available supply, with customers continuing to seek 
additional capacity. Pushing back on investor concerns, 
management also expressed confidence that agentic AI 
and new model development can sustain demand into 
2028.
3. Confident in pricing and a gross margin trough.
Management expressed confidence in longer-term 
pricing, emphasizing the value of its full-stack systems 
despite growing competition from ASICs and increased 
focus on compute efficiency. In our view, management 
signaled that they see no need to trade margins against 
market share and competitive positioning. Furthermore, 
management reiterated that 4QFY27 (Jan 2027) is 
expected to represent the gross-margin trough, as that 
quarter will absorb higher supply costs that the company 
chose not to pass onto to existing customer purchase 
orders before transitioning toward new pricing.
4. More selective on customer financing. NVIDIA will 
still utilize customer financing, but management seems 
to be a bit more selective going forward, highlighting 
that future support will be evaluated carefully, with land, 
power and shell infrastructure an important potential 
priority and third-party financing partners expected to 
play a larger role. We’re not sure if this is a major 
change, but we believe investors will take some comfort 
in a thoughtful process. Management also clarified that 
neocloud guarantees are contingent upon customers 
securing the necessary financing and infrastructure to 
deploy compute, and that this condition is not a new 
policy. Management continues to allocate supply based 
on factors including capital availability, secured demand, 
and data-center readiness.
5. Capital allocation focuses on supporting the 
supply chain and shareholder returns. Management 
highlighted the critical role the supply chain plays in 
NVIDIA’s success and the need for the company to 
support its vendors with capital when and as needed in 
the form of prepayments. With the newly authorized USD 
150bn share repurchase program, total buyback capacity 
stood at USD 227bn. Management expects to fully utilize 
all of its buyback capacity by the end of 2028.
While we remain constructive on the AI theme, we recognize 
we are in the fourth year of the AI rally. Looking ahead, 
incremental AI capex alone may not be sufficient and the 
risk is that investment by NVIDIA's large cloud service 
providers could be uneven; investors will likely focus more 
on monetization.We continue to believe that diversification 
across the three layers of the AI stack (Enabling, Intelligence, 
and Application) is critical.
Among our AI TRIO Selected stocks, we continue to favor 
platform and application beneficiaries that are well-placed 
for AI-related use cases. Within the enabling layer, we 
also like infrastructure firms with strong pricing power and 
competitive positioning in their respective supply chains.
