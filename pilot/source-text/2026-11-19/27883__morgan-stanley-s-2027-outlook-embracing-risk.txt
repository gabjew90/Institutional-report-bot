 
Morgan Stanley's 2027 Outlook: "Embracing Risk" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 16, 2026 - 07:15 AM 
By Vishwanath Tirupattur, global head of Quantitative Research at Morgan Stanley 
It is that time of year again. Every year, our economists and strategists around the world step 
back from the daily flow of data and market moves to debate what comes next. Our year-ahead 
outlooks, published later today, distill their collective judgment. In 2026, geopolitical shocks 
reshaped the global economy and monetary policy, while AI infrastructure investment remained 
a defining theme across markets. In 2027, an AI capex cycle we view as intact, a resilient global 
economy, and solid earnings growth extending into 2027 provide a constructive foundation. With 
major risk catalysts behind us and risk assets having cheapened over the past six months, we 
enter 2027 with a broadly positive stance on risk. In this week’s Sunday Start, we trace the 
contours of our outlook for the global economy and markets. 
 
 

 
Our economists see a resilient global economy confronting a more difficult inflation backdrop. 
Growth has modestly exceeded their mid-year expectations, led by US strength and an 
investment cycle that is broadening globally. AI capex is critical for growth in the US and globally 
through exports. Despite policy uncertainty following the US midterm elections, our equity 
analysts have maintained their baseline investment projections. The energy shock, however, 
has renewed price pressures, complicating the policy response as central banks balance 
inflation risks against the costs of tighter policy. We expect global growth to soften modestly in 
2027 before returning to its 2026 pace in 2028, as energy headwinds recede. Our expectation of 
a brief tightening cycle assumes that relief materializes, while persistent inflation remains the 
key risk. 
 
Against that global backdrop, regional drivers differ. The US remains the principal engine of 
global growth. Accelerating AI infrastructure investment and robust consumer spending 
supported by wealth and full employment sustain the growth momentum in US and contain 
downside risks to global growth. German fiscal support and growth in the periphery underpin 
Europe’s moderate expansion, while a gradually fading deflationary cycle leaves China's growth 
modest. Across Asia, industrial investment is broadening and the cycle is becoming more 
durable. 
 

 
 
Energy is the principal complication and a clear challenge to our global economic outlook. Wide 
spreads between crude and refined products, alongside differences in delivered prices across 
regions, mean crude prices alone offer an incomplete gauge of inflation. Combined with food 
and supply-chain pressures, energy costs could feed into core inflation, prompting tighter policy 
even where growth is vulnerable—particularly in emerging markets. We expect these headwinds 
to recede by end-2027, but more persistent inflation crimping growth is a clear risk that we 
contemplate in our downside scenario. 
Continued strong earnings growth underpins our constructive stance on global equities. AI 
remains central to this outlook, as infrastructure investment and accelerating adoption support 
revenue growth and productivity gains. But elevated funding costs and record borrowing needs 
call for a quality bias. In US equities, our strategists favor strong balance sheets, durable 
margins, and cash generation—even the expected shift toward cyclicals remains a rotation 
within quality. 
 

 
 
That preference extends across credit: we raise US investment grade to equal weight, cut high 
yield to underweight, and move up in quality within both hard-currency emerging market 
sovereigns and securitized credit. Credit markets should also play a larger role in funding AI 
capex in 2027, bridging the gap between investment and monetization. High-quality issuers 
retain access to multiple financing channels and can absorb higher borrowing costs. 
Lower-rated participants increasingly rely on asset-backed financing, often with support from 
higher-rated counterparties. As financing needs grow, these differences will matter more. 
 

 
 
Diversification remains essential. Alongside our maximum overweight in global equities, we 
retain a neutral allocation to US Treasuries, maintaining duration precisely because our equity 
exposure is high. With uncertainty and tail risks still significant, a constructive stance on risk 
strengthens the case for portfolio protection. 
2026 was a difficult year to navigate, and our constructive stance on risk assets for 2027 does 
not imply an easy ride. The challenges ahead look different: fewer macro shocks in our base 
case, but greater weight on shifts beneath the surface, with increasing focus on evidence of 
returns to AI capex in hyperscaler earnings and broader AI adoption. Our global outlooks, 
published later today, present the full picture, with deeper dives into individual asset classes to 
follow. Stay tuned.
