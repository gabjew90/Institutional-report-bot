From panic to premium
We highlighted XLU at the recent washout lows (here), when utilities were massively oversold, bond
volatility was exploding and the 10-year yield looked increasingly stretched. Fast forward, and XLU has
surged around 13% from early October.
The move has been impressive, but the setup is now looking increasingly stretched. XLU is
approaching major technical resistance, the divergence versus Treasuries has reached extreme levels,
and defensive valuations are approaching levels last seen decades ago.
The rates relief trade worked beautifully from the lows. After a 13% rally, the risk/reward looks
considerably less attractive.
Back from the dead
The rally has taken XLU right back towards its 200-day moving average and the downtrend line in
place since March. Two major resistance levels after such an aggressive move. Not exactly the ideal
place to chase.

The TLT gap
XLU has left TLT well behind lately, despite the strong relationship between the two since March. That
relationship has broken down sharply, with utilities increasingly disconnected from the bond market.

Defensives pricing a growth scare
Privorotsky highlights the extraordinary rerating in defensives, with relative multiple expansion
approaching levels last seen decades ago. The divergence between utilities and bonds is particularly
striking. XLU has outperformed TLT by roughly 5 percentage points over the past 20 sessions, a 3-
standard-deviation move.
With real and nominal yields still elevated, investors are paying an enormous premium for safety in
equities. As Privorotsky points out, the equity market appears to be pricing a growth scare that the
bond market has yet to acknowledge.
