AI Exports, Copper, and France - GS Daily Monitor
Good morning,
 
Bottom-line: The Trump Administration issued an interim chip export rule. SHFE has stepped in to try to ease
a historic copper squeeze. French bonds dropped across the curve on Le Pen's pension reforms discussions.
Taiwanese equities fell on back of retail liquidation & AI chip curb headlines. A Kremlin aide suggested the
trilateral Putin-Xi-Trump meeting later this week should be greatly influential. 
 
If you were forwarded this email, reach out to subscribe. 
 
• In Equities, US equities are choppy this AM as market attention remains on AI and NVDA's EPS post-close
today. 
• The Trump Administration issued an interim chip export rule requiring data center operators in certain
countries to obtain US government license before exporting AI chips from NVDA/other US
manufacturers. 
• In Commodities, the Shanghai Futures Exchange has stepped in to try to ease a historic copper squeeze
with pressure to settle positions financially vs delivery.  
• An aluminum supply squeeze is intensifying with the Tom/next spread jumping more than $15 a ton. 
• In Europe, French bonds dropped across the curve after Le Pen's discussions on pension reforms.
•  Le Pen's proposal included plans to drop the retirement age to as low as 60 years and to introduce an
individually funded pension pillar. 
• In Asia, Taiex fell as much as 3.1% early Tuesday as AI chip curb & forced retail liquidation weighed on the
index. 
• In China, PBOC delivered a large 254bn net liquidity injection with credit bonds stabilizing and
redemption pressure easing. 
• In Emerging Markets, a Kremlin aide said that the trilateral Putin-Xi-Trump meeting later this week "should
have great influence on development of international situation." 
• In Lebanon, Lebanese banks escalated their dispute with the government over the proposed taxation of
unrealized gains & provisions against defaulted Eurobonds. 
• Chart of the Day: The build-up of copper inventory in the US has been of significant focus this year as the
metal continues to leave Shanghai. Recent headlines have focused on the SHFE's reported intervention to
encourage long & short positions to settle their contracts financially vs to seek physical delivery.
