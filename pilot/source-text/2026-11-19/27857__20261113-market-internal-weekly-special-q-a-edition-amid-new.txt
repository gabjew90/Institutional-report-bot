Quick Takeaways: 
 
I will publish only 2 more Market Internal Weekly over the next five weeks, today and Nov 27, before returning Dec 17, due to upcoming business trips to California, Toronto, 
and Asia, as well as vacation. During the off-weeks, Megan and Stella will publish the Market Internal Weekly – Abridged Version, with the latest Model Details Update. 
 
Given the recent surge in client questions, this week is a Special Q&A Edition addressing four of the hottest topics. 
 
Near-term pressure may persist, but the setup becomes increasingly bullish after mid-November. Some residual capital raising ahead of next week's anticipated IPO 
could continue to weigh on the market, but the bulk of the typical pre-IPO liquidity adjustment appears to be behind us. Meanwhile, real-money demand has strengthened 
further, tax-loss selling typically fades into late November, and the real-money backdrop is dramatically stronger than during 2018Q4. Foreigners have bought almost 
100% of days since October, UBS RMM clients bought the dip 75% of days and bought the rally 83% of days so far in Nov. Target Date Fund inflows are on track to be one 
of the best inflow months in 2026. 
 
Taken together, some near-term pressure from pre-IPO liquidity raising, tax-loss harvesting, and news-triggered uncertainty may persist, but I see much 
greater upside potential after mid-November, particularly post-IPO. Importantly, the weakness is being met with aggressive real money buying rather than 
de-risking: UBS RMM clients have bought semiconductor stocks heavily over the last few days, including today, at an even stronger pace than ETFs.

Q2: What do you think about the potential market impact from the anticipated IPO next week? (Charts 13 & 14) 
A: The typical pre-IPO liquidity-driven selling pressure should be largely behind us. While some selling may persist into next week and weigh on the market, I don’t 
expect significant incremental pressure from this channel. On the other hand, the historical post-IPO pattern for SPX has been strongly positive, with SPX +9.5% on 
average in 3M.  
     Based on the four largest U.S. Company IPOs relative to prevailing market cap since 2008: Visa (‘08), General Motors (‘10), Facebook (‘12), and SpaceX (‘26) (Chart 13): 
• 
Chart 14: Pre-IPO liquidity pressure has historically weighed on the broader market. During the three weeks preceding these IPOs, SPX average max 
drawdown @ -5.8%, potentially reflecting investors raising capital ahead of the offerings. The recent -5.0% drawdown is broadly consistent with this historical 
pattern. Given current low HF leverage, I believe there should be sufficient liquidity to fund next week's IPO.  Some pre-IPO selling may continue, but incremental 
pressure should be limited from here 
• 
Chart 14: The historical post-IPO pattern has been much more constructive. SPX rallied in all four prior cases, gaining an average +9.5% in 3M. The IPO stocks 
themselves were considerably more volatile, with average 3M low/high returns of -20.2%/+24.8% 
 
Q3: Has retail tax-loss harvesting started? Will it have a negative impact on the market? (Charts 15 – 17) 
A: Yes, retail tax-loss harvesting is likely underway, but the selling should be more stock-specific than a significant broad-market headwind. Importantly, the timing of 
tax-loss harvesting appears to have shifted from being concentrated in Q4 to being spread more evenly throughout the year, likely helped by zero-commission trading. Since 
2019, the flow picture has typically mproved into December (Chart 16) 
     Based on UBS RMM flow data: 
• 
Chart 17: Tax-loss harvesting typically takes in the form of selling individual stocks vs buying ETFs, so the market net impact varies by year and doesn’t necessarily 
result in net selling 
• 
Chart 17: Before 2019:          Q4 net selling pressure was significant, with December typically seeing the heaviest selling, as retail investors tended to 
concentrate tax-loss harvesting late in the year 
• 
Charts 15 - 17: From 2019:   Q4 net selling pressure has declined significantly, with December typically flipping to inflows, as more retail investors appear 
to have spread tax-loss harvesting throughout the year. Zero-commission trading likely contributed to this shift by enabling investors to trade more 
frequently and realize losses opportunistically throughout the year. While October and 1st half of November have continued to see selling, December recorded 
net inflows in 4 of the last 6 years
