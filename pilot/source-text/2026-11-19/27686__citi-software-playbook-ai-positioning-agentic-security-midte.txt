Key Themes Looking Ahead to 2027
Software remains investable, but the burden of proof for AI tailwinds has risen: Following a volatile Q3 earnings season, we 
believe investors should prioritize companies driving tangible AI-driven acceleration, with durable distribution advantages, and 
mission-critical product. We have begun to see discrepancies arise in results as the consequences of strategic positioning and 
execution become manifest. The burden of proof has risen and we believe sustained re-ratings will now require meaningful 
improvements in growth expectations with sufficient disclosure to validate AI tailwinds.
• We maintain a preference for the infrastructure, data, and security layers: AI adoption is creating direct demand for cloud 
capacity, data platforms, observability, networking and security. We favor companies where expanding AI workloads can 
translate more directly into consumption and revenue. Recent post-midterm policy concerns around data-center development 
appear more likely to create near-term sentiment and positioning risk than alter long-term AI demand, while potentially 
increasing the value of existing capacity. Meanwhile, AI-assisted vulnerability discovery, agentic attack surfaces and 
disappearing patch windows expand the long-term opportunities in “AI for Security”, even if major vulnerability events do not 
drive immediate budget acceleration.
• Staying selective in application software: We believe the best-positioned companies at the application layer combine 
proprietary data, domain expertise, regulatory/compliance value, strong distribution and a credible path to AI upsell or 
consumption revenue. With pockets of enterprise deal cycle challenges, we have a relative preference for companies with 
demonstrated execution consistency. Additionally, AI-driven disruption of traditional search is pressuring customer 
acquisition across SMB and self-service software, leaving companies more exposed until alternative channels prove scalable.
• M&A can provide valuation support as strategic buyers become active: We see M&A activity providing valuation support, 
particularly for companies with scarce AI, data, security, and workflow assets. Strategic acquirers have started to become 
more active, with consolidation potentially widening the competitive gap between scaled platforms and point solutions.

• Enterprise Apps/Hyperscalers (Radke, Wong): MDB, SNOW, ESTC
• Infrastructure Software (Boolani, Zhang): NET, TEAM, DDOG

Sentiment Across Sub-Sectors
• Following the bounce in performance mid-way through the year, we exit a volatile Q3 earnings season with a software 
sentiment backdrop that is more selective. 
• At the application layer, the bar for outperformance has risen from simply proving a right to existence to demonstrating a 
cohesive AI strategy with tangible adoption translating into material growth contribution. While the more extreme AI 
displacement fears have faded, concerns remain on the impact to enterprise deal cycles and SMB top-of-funnel, while 
agentic monetization broadly remains nascent. 
• Within infrastructure and security software, DevOps/Observability sentiment is bullish despite recent earnings blemishes, as 
AI/Agentic adoption tailwinds increase  observability needs, with non-traditional players tapping into this market via M&A. 
Cybersecurity and CDN sentiment both remain mixed/bullish, with AI/agent security use cases materializing, web security 
increasing in importance, but vendor execution in these sub-sectors remaining highly bifurcated.

Q3 Results: Idiosyncratic or Symptomatic?
4
Source: Factset, as of 11/13/2026
Coming out of a volatile Q3 season, we assess which of the following earnings results we view as idiosyncratic, reflecting 
company-specific issues, and which are symptomatic of broader industry trends with relevant readthrough to a larger group.
• NOW (10/29/26; -27%): Idiosyncratic/Symptomatic—We view NOW results as largely idiosyncratic given lower-than-
expected Q4 cRPO guidance was impacted by early renewals, a higher mix of on-prem deals, and greater use of termination-
for-convenience (T4C) contracts that aren’t counted in cRPO. The rebucketing of subscription revenue into PS with greater 
use of FDEs could be more symptomatic of broader enterprise apps’ struggles to demonstrate value to customers.
• FIG (11/4/26; -17%): Idiosyncratic/Symptomatic– FIG’s drop post-earnings appears likely weighed down by top-of-funnel 
sluggishness which we view as a more symptomatic issue across front-office app software, especially those with exposure to 
non-enterprise customers. While FIG doesn’t regularly report total customer adds, NN clients across both $10K+ and $100K+ 
ARR slowed y/y and q/q. This trend was also evident in HUBS and KVYO Q3 reports. 
• TEAM (11/6/26; -16%): Idiosyncratic/Symptomatic. We see the TEAM result as largely idiosyncratic. Hybrid deals impacted 
in-Q Cloud vs DC revenue allocation, and relatively new CFO is likely guiding conservatively with 3 Qs remaining in the FY.
• DDOG (11/4/26; -14%): Idiosyncratic/Symptomatic. Investor focus on largest-customer usage rationalization in our view 
overshadows otherwise healthy underlying business.
• NET (11/6/26; +10%):  Idiosyncratic/Symptomatic. NET is benefitting from the explosion in AI/agent adoption, driving 
workload growth in its Workers developer platform.
• BL (11/6/26; -23%): Idiosyncratic/Symptomatic - Q3 deal delays appear symptomatic of rising budget and governance 
scrutiny across the OCFO category, particularly for AI-enabled solutions. Decision-making is extending beyond the controller 
org to CIOs, CISOs, legal, audit, AI-governance teams and boards, creating more approval gates and extending cycles. 
 

Assessing the Blue Sweep Midterm Implications
5
Source: Citi Research
•
The midterms have heightened concerns about data-center development, including tighter permitting, greater scrutiny 
of power consumption and potential restrictions on the pace of AI infrastructure expansion
•
The market reaction likely overstates the fundamental risk. AI demand, cloud migration and workload growth remain 
durable, while infrastructure already under construction, existing capacity and efficiency improvements should limit the 
near-term impact of policy changes.
•
Owners of existing cloud and network capacity (MSFT, ORCL, DOCN, NET) could see higher value assigned to existing 
capacity if new development capacity becomes more difficult. Application software companies could see rotational 
benefits to the extent sentiment headwinds linger, as well as possible valuation tailwinds if inflation expectations cool 
and drive down long-term interest rates.
•
Most exposed (Sub-Scale Neoclouds) could see a longer road to permitting and datacenter development, especially 
among more restricted states. Additionally, financing and potentially paying higher rates to fund the additional capacity 
remains the larger headwind. We expected unsecured and convertible markets to become harder to borrow against and 
secured/ATM issuances will likely be the more common funding route. We wouldn’t be surprised to see these companies 
pass through the higher costs of these issues to customers. We continue to expect a strong DC buildout nonetheless 
given strong AI demand trends and desire for the US to not fall behind global peers.
•
Bottom line: The midterms have introduced another perceived constraint on AI infrastructure, but we view the concern as 
primarily a sentiment and positioning risk rather than a meaningful change in long-term demand. Any slowdown in 
development should increase the value of existing capacity and encourage greater software-led optimization of compute, 
data and workloads. We reiterate our preference to own the infrastructure layer.
 

Addressing the Vuln-pocalpyse: Patch Windows Have 
Disappeared
6
Source: Citi Research
• A slew of critical vulnerability headlines have caused significant concern around the security industry, with major vulnerability 
breaches such as PHP2Shell highlighting major weaknesses in the software supply chain and raising concerns around 
security hygiene in an agentic age. 
• PHP2Shell 101: PHP is a programming language which powers ~70% of the global web that sits on web servers ingesting user 
requests to build requested pages before delivering them to the user’s browser. Security researchers using Gemini Argon and 
an unreleased frontier model discovered vulnerabilities allowing remote code to be injected into PHP, allowing malicious 
actors to hijack a website and obtain admin-level controls. 
• Due to the widespread, public facing nature of PHP, many government agencies and public facing websites were taken 
down while web-security vendors rushed to patch the vulnerability, with major patches being pushed out by edge 
security players within 24 hours of public disclosure. 
• Despite the quick fixes, the overall pace of severe vulnerabilities disclosed (multiple “once-in-a-decade” vulnerabilities 
found every several days) and potential severity (given public facing nature) raise concerns around broader security 
hygiene given increasingly large and complex attack surfaces. 
• Conversations with Citi’s CISO org note that headlines like these will not unleash a budgetary flush but are part of an ongoing 
posture shift to assuming vulnerability and to harden security estates. 
• Despite headline fears, many organizations have noted that AI can be a net benefit for defenders, given faster patching, 
more robust red/purple-teaming, and an emerging multi-billion “AI for Security” TAM. We note that traditional security 
vendors have already begun to make inroads, such as CRWD quantifying a $216B AI security TAM (assuming an MSD 
cyber attach on AI spend) and launching several AI security products, and ZS highlighting 1000% growth for their Security 
for AI segment.
• Bottom line, while security budgets do not see an immediate acceleration, both providing agentic security, as well as 
protecting against malicious agents is expected to be a rapidly growing, multi-billion-dollar opportunity.

Infrastructure Software: Debates & Where We Stand
•AI-assisted vulnerability discovery headlines have caused significant volatility in security stocks, where investors are debating how much and 
how quickly accelerating volumes of CVEs discovery and previously "once in a decade" breaches catalyze a broad-based increase in 
cybersecurity budgets. This is also stimulating debate on the competitive commercial value props/opps/encroachment risks for Frontier Labs 
as initiatives such as Daybreak, Glasswing and Gemini Cyber expand. 
•Conversations with Citi’s CISO Org reaffirm our view that while headline-generating vulnerabilities such as PHP2Shell do not immediately 
translate into higher spending given budgeting cycles and procurement timelines, it does continue to incentivize CISOs, CIOs and other key 
decision makers to prioritize infrastructure modernization and harden security hygiene with a far more regular cadence. Here, we see security 
organizations beginning to make inroads with both "Security for Al" and "Al for Security" products, enlarging long- term security TAM and 
attach to overall IT budgets.
PHP2Shell: Does This Catalyze Budgetary Spend?
•Infrastructure and cybersecurity budgets converge as deal activity continued to pick up into 3Q across both categories IonQ bought Elisity 
($1.3B), DT acquired Arize ($915M), PANW acquired Console ($500M), CRWD  bought Dreadnode and Fiddler AI ($500M), and DDOG 
reportedly made a bid for Upwind and remains interested in larger M&A.
•AI adoption and governance is another driver of convergence, where we emphasize comments made by MSFT’s CEO on how models and 
their outputs should be governed as insider risks, with zero-trust principles applied, where agentic governance and control requires 
cooperation between both the observability and security stacks
•We continue to see the trends of platformization and tool consolidation play out, driven both by customer demands for operational and 
procurement efficiency (also familiarity with/lean on incumbents as AI adoption ramps), as well as vendors pressing their incumbencies to 
drive revenue scale (multi-disciplinary cross-sell) across large existing installed bases. As a result, we continue to favor large platforms 
PANW, CRWD as share consolidators with proven M&A and execution playbooks, against point solutions challenged with scale.
Continued Consolidation Trends Blur Budgetary Lines
• DDOG, NET and others (like SNOW, and even DT to lesser extent) have seen a visible inflection in growth momentum as their usage models 
remain uniquely tethered to broader AI adoption, with these names enjoying a growth torque from being directly in the "path of 
tokenization". Yet this has fomented concerns around spending/customer concentration (voracious-users, very large hyper-growth AI-
natives) and spending optimization risks, analogous to the post-COVID "hangover" where names like DDOG witnessed deceleration, with 
these factors partially influencing DDOG's recent near-term guide. We maintain these are noisy temporal idiosyncratic factors and continue 
to view DDOG, NET, and even TEAM broadly benefitting from software creation proliferation across multiple product areas in the medium-
term.
