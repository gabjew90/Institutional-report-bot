GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Commodity Disinflation. Technicals.
Hynix Multiple. 18 Nov 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
18 Nov 2026
Markets: Tech related markets recovering off the lows. Nvidia did pretty much everything you could ask
for overnight … beat and raise, $130bn guide vs approx $125bn expected, zero China data center compute
in the numbers. The spending isn’t stopping. The one thing the market would’ve liked was a hint that
Jensen could come back. They shut that down. Kospi holding lows of prior sell off and now
+2%...encouraging (SK Hynix on 1.79x blended 24m P/E...see below). Tape felt different yesterday. High
beta momentum was up, AI outperformed the AI losers and the short leg of momentum got hit. A lot of
expensive defensives that had simply gone too far finally caught down alongside small and mid cap
cyclicals. From a gross standpoint, I actually thought that was somewhat constructive. Similar pattern in
Asia, with Korea leading the bounce.
Cyber: I think a lot of the anxiety in the market was around the idea that all these outages were connected.
But as WIRED put it, “Yet there’s still no evidence that any one of these clustered technological failures
were related, much less caused by hackers.” On the bottled water and generators, apparently I’m late.
Costco had sold out of 21 of its 26 high ticket generator models online by Sunday, including a $7k Kohler
(Bloomberg). Panic buying extending into generators, emergency supplies and pantry loading. Quite a
read on sentiment. Hard to deny that cyber incidents rising globally and will continue to do so but think
we've hit some local levels of anxiety on the topic.
APEC: Trump and Xi are due to meet Thursday in Shenzhen, with Putin reportedly joining for a separate
three way conversation. On trade, I’m watching whether the truce holds after the new US chip curbs.
Maybe something on AI safety. On Russia/Ukraine, I’m bearish on a nt ceasefire. Reports that Ukraine
took out the central road span of the Kerch Bridge two days before Putin sits down with Trump and Xi
hardly make a deal easier.
Oil: The US hit an Iranian missile launch site on Tuesday after Iran said it targeted a US radar installation
in Bahrain. Oman and Qatar are still talking to Tehran. Nearly nine months in, the incentives to do a deal
seem obvious, especially after the midterms. Still hard to see a path. Meanwhile, the US 3-2-1 crack
spread is down 36% in six weeks, from $65.56 to $41.90/bbl. Gasoline and diesel are losing ground
relative to crude, even with Hormuz keeping a premium in Brent. API numbers showed US crude and
gasoline stocks each building by around 5m barrels.
Metals: London copper near 5m lows, Shanghai testing 100k and gold still can’t hold a bounce even with
some relief in yields. Starting to run out of excuses for gold here … maybe there’s a liquidation element to
it. Objectively China tape has been awful. Perhaps commds just telling us policy is way too tight.

Technicals: The velocity of CTA selling may have peaked, but the magnitude can still be considerable if
we sit around here, especially in Europe. Vol stabilizing matters for vol control, while spot gamma remains
very level dependent. SPX hanging on to the 200d by a fingernail. Nasdaq broke the early month lows
intraday before clawing them back into the close.
Risk: I expected more chop given how defensive positioning already was. The last couple of days
surprised me, but outside of cyber I’m struggling to point to much of a catalyst. My contrarian muscles are
starting to flex a little on AI. I get why you haircut 2028 and why people worry about a peak in 2029. But
look at the sheer dollars still to be spent, how far multiples have come in and what people are paying for
defensives. Some of these names could go up pretty quickly. On rates one day isn’t a trend, but look at
what’s happening across commodities … copper, crude, gasoline, diesel, parts of the grain complex. If that
sticks, it’s a meaningful disinflationary impulse. We can debate supercore… I think we overcomplicate
how commodity prices feed through. I like long front end bonds alongside equity here.
side-note:
Big picture this should probably make us worried about earnings sustainability (cheap get cheaper) but
equally it feels very difficult to press lower. If the market is bearish on AI/growth perhaps that is better
expressed in other pockets as the margin of safety in things like this is quite meaningful now.
