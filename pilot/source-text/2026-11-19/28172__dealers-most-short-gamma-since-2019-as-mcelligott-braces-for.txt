 
Dealers Most Short Gamma Since 2019 As 
McElligott Braces For NVDA-Anthropic-OpEx "Risk 
Quad-fecta" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, NOV 17, 2026 - 06:35 PM 
Nomura's Charlie McElligott titled his latest note with a single word: "WHEWWWWW!" 
Looking at the chart he leads with, we get it: after a "spicy multi-day drawdown," aggregate SPX 
dealer gamma has plunged to negative $7.3 billion, by a wide margin the deepest reading on 
Nomura's chart going back to January 2019, and well below the 2023 and 2024 troughs that 
marked the last two "slippery tape" episodes. 
 
Translation: dealers are now selling into every dip and buying into every rip, which is how you 
get a market that moves 1.5% on a Monday "relief rally" and gives most of it back by Tuesday 
 

 
lunch. And it lands right in front of what McElligott calls the "Risk Quad-fecta": Nvidia earnings 
tonight, VIX expiration Wednesday, the Anthropic IPO pricing Wednesday and trading Thursday, 
and monthly OpEx Friday. 
His conclusion, in his latest note (available to pro subs), is that the S&P has entered a 
"Two-Way Accelerant Risk" regime where the straddle has to stay wide, because a month from 
now we are probably either "through 6500 and 'without a net' or 7800 going to 8k." 
With futures around 7,320 when he wrote, that's roughly an 11% air pocket on one side and a 
9% melt-up on the other (napkin math). Pick your poison. 
"Tape Feels Slippery Bc It Is" 
Regular readers will recall that last week, in "Higher Floor, No Fuel", McElligott argued that vol 
would stay elevated but lacked the fuel to properly explode. Tuesday's note is the "as I was 
saying" sequel: 
As I was saying... the Equities Market has simply evolved into a higher Realized Vol / wider 
Straddle -regime as Macro has tilted "wrong-way" on risk sentiment, hence my view that the VIX 
floor had moved-up alongside Vol of Vol resetting higher too on "Demand for the Wings," 
alongside a blast of Skew Steepening (!) and the ultrarare rip higher in Correlation (!!) all of 
which helped put us in such a prolific "Short Gamma vs Spot" location with this spicy multi-day 
drawdown. 
So why not a proper VIX 30+ blowout? Because there's "No Juice to Squeeze." McElligott still 
doesn't see the big vol shorts or dealer VIX gamma that would provide the "accelerant flow" for 
a true vol shock, "at least without Spot Index prices absolutely crapping-out and crashing further 
from here in a 'Dec 2018' re-do." 
The numbers back him up. Leveraged VIX ETN assets are down to $1.3 billion, a 25th 
percentile reading... 
 

 
 
...while the total VIX vega that would need to be bought on a 10-point vol spike is a measly $25 
million, a 4th percentile reading, versus well over $100 million at several points over the past 
two years. 
 
Hence McElligott's verdict that front-month VIX futures at "kinda 20-22," spot VIX in the low 20s 
and VVIX at 110-120 "feels appropriate." 
For now. 
The "Risk Quad-fecta" 
The catch is the calendar. NVDA tonight, VIXperation Wednesday, ANT on 
Wednesday/Thursday and OpEx Friday "could in-fact then begin acting as a Vol inflection point, 
 

 
with potential for substantial 'Vanna' impact." (Regular readers will recall McElligott described 
Monday's melt-up of the prior week as exactly that, a "Vanna Machine".) 
And Friday's expiry won't clean the slate, because the hedges dealers are short to customers sit 
further out: 
So despite Options expiration and a lot of $Delta rolling-off, I think there will still be some chunky 
residual sensitivity on any Vol reset potential bc a lot of SPX Downside hedges that Dealers are 
Short to Customers are for Dec expiration, as these risks still feel quite "amorphous" and are 
stacking in non-linear -fashion. 
Which brings us to the two-way setup, in his own words: 
IF we get that Vol cool-down after the flurry of risk-catalysts this week (NVDA and ANT 
especially), a move even back to just the 17-18 location in VIX could see lumpy mechanical 
Equities BUYING impact that would hurt in a world of many investors having their hands-tied on 
VaR (hence, "Buyers are Higher" pain-trade)... but there remains a lot of Downside that is 
keeping Dealer "Negative Gamma" at extremes as potential "Accelerant Flow" in Spot Index, 
especially with the list of "Fat Tails" brewing (below) that feels totally unstable at this point (for all 
of us "Preppers" out there, let's just say the stockpiles are BUILDING). 
Nomura's rebalance grid shows just how lopsided the mechanics have become: options dealers, 
levered ETFs and vol control funds would need to sell roughly $82 billion on a 5% down day, but 
"only" buy about $66 billion on a 5% up day. Small moves, in other words, no longer stay small.

Why The Vibes Turned: The Fat Tails List 
McElligott says the options market's implied distribution is now pricing "Fatter Left-Tails" on a 
"holistic tightening in FCI in recent weeks." His list reads like a ZH homepage from the past 
month: 
 

 
●​ Higher real rates and wider credit spreads. 
●​ A "Negative Wealth Effect" risk, since stocks have overtaken real estate as the largest 
contributor to US household wealth, with equities at $74 trillion, or 48% of household net 
assets (nearly $10 trillion of that "gain" arrived in Q2 alone). 
●​ A "Hawkish" CB repricing as "inflation stays too damn high," with a Fed speaker "turf 
war" against Chair Warsh adding nearly two hikes to Spring '27 pricing, on top of an Iran 
supply shock with "no offramp" (refinery shortages, too few VLCCs, "gaping-holes" in 
product inventories). 
●​ Debt and equity supply from AI capex financing plus record G10 deficit spending. 
●​ A "different kind of 'singularity'" as AI and geopolitics converge into a stream of agentic 
outages, hacks and emergency patching, creating what he calls "WEAPONIZED FEAR" 
as the preferred front of a "New Cold (World) War." 
On the wealth-effect point, McElligott reaches for the Fed's own data: households' equity share 
of financial assets just hit a new all-time high near 48%, comfortably above the dot-com peak 
(chart source: Federal Reserve via FRED, Financial Accounts of the US). 
 
 

 
In other words, equities are now "THE" transmission mechanism from financial conditions to the 
real economy. A Fed that wants to tighten FCI only has to wait for stocks to do it for them. 
Spoiler alert: so far, they are. 
The "Central Bank Of AI" Loses Its Governor 
The final, and probably biggest, fat tail is Nvidia, which McElligott has long described as the 
"Central Bank of AI" and the perceived "Put" across the entire AI ecosystem. That prior just took 
a beating: 
...but now with the loss of Jensen having previously driven the so many of the commitments with 
their customer "circular financing" -deals (with his son in charge of spinning many of those 
plates, mind you), while leadership downshifts on the risk-spectrum to a CFO... as customers 
sense pricing power leverage in the moment of weakness, and all as the entire industry resets 
AI "enabler / bottleneck" -companies' revenue expectations for China (indirectly, of course LOL) 
to ZERO in the years-ahead... you can kinda get your arms around the -35% move over the 
past month and a half. 
For those who spent last week in a bunker (with their stockpiles), Jensen Huang resigned on 
November 12, with CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO. And in a twist that landed just as 
McElligott hit "send," Huang broke his silence this morning to say he'd return if the board asks, 
although per the WSJ the board isn't considering it: 
Some on the Street hope interim management "kitchen sinks" tonight's quarter to lower the bar. 
McElligott doesn't buy it: 
Honestly, however, I don't think that's possible, as in reality, the company was already too far 
down the qtr rabbit-hole and the numbers, guides and language was already "set"... while 
STRUCTURALLY, they ARE committed and need to keep the "bullish" story intact. 
Translation: the quarter matters less than the Q&A, which will focus on how aggressive Nvidia 
stays on "circular financing" going forward and what sales bans to "China contingents" across 
Asia do to forward numbers. 
Meanwhile, some brave souls are already buying the megacap dip with upside: Nomura's 
options desk flagged a buyer of 15.5k AMZN Feb 295/350 call spreads, 20k INTC Sep 95 calls 
(the third 20k INTC call buy in a week), and 8.8k MU Feb 920/1000 call spreads. 
 

 
Boomers Buy, Pros Puke 
The most interesting part of the note comes from a macro dinner McElligott attended, where the 
consensus was "remarkably clear" for higher stocks AND higher Treasury yields into year-end 
and a year out, with just one "stocks down" and one "rates lower" view in the room. 
"Problematically," crude was the same: only one person saw it lower. 
Perversely, everyone agreed FCI tightening is accelerating. Yet: 
...the common refrain around the room was that passive flow mechanics and a well-conditioned 
"fallback" on "moral hazard" from Authorities... then continues to dictate "Buy-the-Dip" behavior 
from many Investors in the passive / target allocation / 401K class, who aren't feeling anywhere 
close to the "pain" of the professional community which has suffered through the "acute" 
Momentum and Crowding -factor deleveraging events... which then means significantly 
"Washed-Out" position in the Leveraged -space... then risks creating a "Buyers are Higher" 
-dynamic into rallies. 
How washed out? Per McElligott: 
●​ "ZERO to Low Single-Digit" net leverage per Street prime brokers 
●​ Systematic equity exposure at the 10th percentile after selling $116 billion in two weeks 
●​ Mag7 call skew at the 12th percentile, SPX call skew at the 22nd 
 
That purge is the same one McElligott flagged in real time two weeks ago in "My Goodness, 
Was That WRONG", when a "biblical" $165 billion mechanical unwind sent AI momentum to its 
5th worst crash on record. 
 

 
The 401(k) crowd, meanwhile, is doing just fine, thank you. With the S&P +8.1% YTD, equal 
weight +14.1%, a 60/40 portfolio +5.6% and "Balanced Risk Allocation" +16.4%, McElligott's 
verdict on what the average retirement statement looks like is: "zzzzzzzz." (Or, double-gasp, 
"Bonds Work As Your Hedge Again"!) 
 
So the retail bid keeps coming. Last week's EPFR data showed $98.3 billion of global equity 
inflows (99.9th percentile since 2000) and $63.6 billion into US equities (99.7th percentile): 
 

 
 
Historically, that's been a bullish signal: when 1-week global equity inflows top the 97.9th 
percentile, the S&P has averaged a 13.8% gain over the next 12 months with an 85% hit rate, 
and when asset manager length in ES1 tops the 92.2nd percentile, 6-month and 12-month 
returns have been positive 100% of the time (in a sample of just six, so don't bet the farm). 
 

 
 
Then add the buyback bid, which "perpetually shrinks / absorbs the float, bids the market and 
suppresses volatility." Exhibit A per McElligott: Apple, which has bought back $840 billion of 
stock over the past decade and is up 30% YTD "bc it's not really playing the AI game + 
buyback." Bloomberg's tally still shows over $114 billion of remaining authorization at Apple, and 
$99 billion at Nvidia. 
 
Nothing says "free market" like a bid that never has to ask for a price. 
Buyers Are Higher... Until Sellers Are Lower 
McElligott ends by flipping the bull case on its head. The same "BTD conditioning" that keeps 
saving the market is the danger: 
Of course then, this assumption and then "BTD Conditioning" creates the danger, waiting for the 
bounce that doesn't come, averaging down and lighting money on fire, which becomes "Sellers 
are Lower" -risk... especially the case into the previously mentioned "non-linear" / "asymmetric" 
risk stacking that feels like "LOSS OF NARRATIVE CONTROL"from NVDA to AI to "Hawkish 
Repricing" to civilizational stability. 
 

 
And just as he was about to hit send, the "conditioned" crowd showed up on cue: someone sold 
13k SPX Nov 7500 puts at 203, handing dealers back roughly $1 billion of gamma and 
coinciding with a solid 50-handle rally in futures off the lows. McElligott called it a seller 
"fulfill[ing] their patriotic duty," trying to mark a "line in the sand." 
We'll take the other side of the patriots, at least on probabilities. The pros are flat, the 401(k)s 
are all-in at a 99.9th percentile pace, dealers are shorter gamma than at any point since at least 
2019, and the one stock that has acted as the "Put" for the whole AI complex has an interim 
CEO, its general counsel heading to Congress and a former CEO lobbying to come back. 
"Buyers are Higher" is a fine trade if NVDA and ANT both clear tonight and Thursday. If either 
stumbles, a market with $82 billion of mechanical selling queued up on a 5% down day probably 
finds out what "without a net" means. 
Then again, there's always the Fed... if only Warsh weren't busy pricing in hikes.
