1) US Equity Outlook
We forecast the S&P 500 will rise by 17% to 8,700 by year-end. This compares with a 21% annualized
return during the past four years. Earnings will continue to drive returns, and a solid economy and
continued AI investment should generate robust but decelerating earnings growth in 2027. We expect EPS
growth will slow from roughly 30% in 2026 to 18% in 2027. Our base case is that elevated interest rates
and AI uncertainty will keep the P/E near its current 18x.
Four investment themes at the start of the year:
• The next leg of the AI relay race: Decelerating AI capex growth suggests the “AI trade” will continue to
transition from AI infrastructure stocks to the beneficiaries of monetization and adoption, including the
hyperscalers. As adoption continues, we recommend focusing on AI productivity candidates with
physically-intensive business models that reduce disruption risk (see stocks in Exhibit 50).
• The great re-leveraging, continued: AI investment spending has increased leverage and weighed on
buyback growth for parts of the equity market. We expect “buyback aristocrats” will outperform as
investors seek companies with strong free cash flows that return cash to shareholders (Exhibit 51).
• Value in Growth: Decelerating S&P 500 earnings should favor companies with secular growth,
accelerating earnings, and upward revisions. Tight valuation dispersion adds to the case for Growth as a
factor. Among industries, Energy infrastructure firms offer rare earnings acceleration in 2027 while
Transports represent an attractive cyclical exposure for a macroeconomic upside scenario.
• Safety in diversification: In contrast with the S&P 500, Health Care and parts of Consumer Staples trade
at discounted valuations to trend earnings and provide diversification value for portfolios exposed to the
AI trade. Mid-caps have outgrown large-caps during the past 30 years but currently trade at discounted
valuations and are not over-earning.




2) Three US Consumer Themes
Staples in Focus – Leaning into Shorts?: The XLP hit another all-time high yesterday. The group is +10%
the last month and +21% YTD. It seems unnecessary to explain what’s happening, as it’s largely all 1 big
momentum trade in the market. There has also been some small help from better 3Q earnings in much of
HPC and Beauty, though that seems like a secondary explanation. The biggest question we are getting
from investors is around the flows in this trade. As the group rallied early on (1 month ago), we had
buying. We have been much better for sale though the last 2 weeks, as investors have tried to short the
strength. This was especially true last week, where we were 6-1 better for sale in Staples, led almost
entirely by shorts (GS PB data). It was across Staple sub-sectors. Bottom-line, investors are not embracing
the move in Staples, but actively fighting it.
Solid EPS Likely To Come in Retail: We do not think the rest of EPS will be perfect but we are likely to
see a lot more beats (vs minimal misses) the next 2 weeks. We think the AS and HD beat this morning was
expected (both also raised the sales guide) . We think a string of strong results are also expected out of
TGT, LOW, WMT, WSM, ROST & BJ. We think the biggest hill to climb this week in terms of client
sentiment for 3Q will be TJX (consistent feedback that the quarter was choppy). Into next week, we think
expectations are for upside at BBY, KSS & ANF. It is worth noting that last week, Discretionary was one

of the only 3 sub-sectors bought last week. November is historically the best month of the year for Retail,
with the bulk of that outperformance the last 10 years coming in the last 2 weeks of the month (Black
Friday).
Home Improvement – Stock Resiliency: HD earnings are on the tape this morning (beat and raise sales
guide to top of the prior range) and LOW is reporting tomorrow. The feedback we had gotten from
numerous investors was that small top-line upside was expected for both, along with generally in-line
EPS. Much of the data feedback has been surprisingly stable for much of 3Q, despite a very tough housing
environment. What we find interesting from the last few days is that HD and LOW have both held in very
well, despite some very high profile sell-side downgrades (away from us). The big question is why?
Expectations of today’s 3Q beat? Optimism around housing policy relief (doubtful) or just positioning?
(TY Scott Feiler)

3) CTA + Vol Control + Risk Parity Positioning
CTAs: In the past week, CTAs have sold over $22bn of S&P alone (having sold almost $2.5bn of Nasdaq
and pretty much flat on the week for Russell). This has brought overall positioning in US equities to short

almost -$10bn – the lowest we’ve seen since April. For the first time in weeks, flows from the cohort are
skewed asymmetrically to the upside.
Over the past week, our model estimates Vol control + risk parity have sold over $30bn combined. For
sub-strategies that have made generally little impact this year, these are stark figures. For risk parity,
following the CPI print, stocks and bonds have fallen together which typically has the cohort cut exposure.
We do not expect this to be all that dramatic given how gradual these flows can be. The more interesting
story lies in vol control which may be set to re-risk in December/January should the market calm. (TY
Gail Hafif)
