Rates Spark: Can AI confidence bring
rates back up?
Nvidia will announce its quarterly earnings today, followed a day later by
Anthropic's IPO, two major AI events. Macro has become closely
intertwined with AI sentiment and therefore positive and negative
surprises should not be ignored by the bond market. Having said that,
we think fading spillovers to euro rates will often make sense 
We’ve just come through a period of fragile AI sentiment, which has had a direct impact on rates
Corporate earnings announcements now part of the macro
realm
We’ll be keeping an eye on Nvidia’s earnings announcement as these results have become an
important bellwether for the broader AI story. The stakes are high, with Nvidia making up 5%
of the S&P 500 market capitalisation. We’ve just come through a period of fragile AI sentiment,
which has had a direct impact on rates.
The 10yr UST yield is still down from the previous highs in October on the back of AI jitters. But
with the NASDAQ up again, we think both US and EUR rates can continue heading back to their
previous peaks. If Nvidia earnings don’t disappoint and Anthropic manages to pull off a
successful IPO, bonds are unlikely to come out as a winner.
On the other hand, failing to meet high AI expectations can trigger a significant move lower in
rates too. AI-driven investments have become an integral part of the US growth story. Markets

contemplating a 50bp hike in December goes hand in hand with confidence that AI will
continue to support the economy. Any signs of setbacks could make markets more cautious
and tilt expectations in a more dovish direction.
An AI-driven risk-off event will likely also pull down euro rates, but we would suggest fading
such moves, especially at the front end. First, the ECB’s reaction function is more driven by
inflation than growth at the moment. Second, AI doesn’t have a big footprint (yet) in eurozone
growth. An exception would be if worsening risk sentiment also hurts French government
bonds. In that case, a more dovish market position could be warranted.
Tuesday’s events and market views
After employment data from the UK, we'll turn to the ZEW survey outcomes for
Germany and the eurozone. Consensus doesn't expect much change, but, if anything,
maintains a slightly positive bias. From the US, we have retail sales, import prices,
industrial production and capacity utilisation numbers for October. In particular, retail
data will be watched for signs of price developments and the strength of US
consumers.
The UK will auction £3.25bn of 14y Gilts. Germany will auction €5bn of 2y Schatz.
Finland will auction 4y and 10y RFGBs for a total of €1.50bn.
