The macro is screaming. The S&P isn't
listening
Bond volatility has exploded, credit is pricing stress and macro trading has migrated into fixed income.
Meanwhile, SPX sits near record highs and VIX barely moves.
Something has disconnected.
Let's look at the great macro divide.
Time to MOVE on
Back on September 10, with MOVE around 77, we wrote:
"One of our core vol views in recent weeks has been that bond volatility is pricing far too little risk...
Rates price action increasingly looks like the market is betting against the house. Bond vol remains
attractive, especially if the market decides to make a run at it."
Well, the market made a run at it.
MOVE has since exploded from 77 to 113.
Thank you very much for the long vol logic. Time to move on.

Macro trading has moved somewhere else
If SPX no longer reacts to macro, traders need somewhere else to express the view.
Fixed-income ETF option activity has exploded. GS says FICC ETF option volume has more than
doubled over the past three weeks, as traders increasingly use instruments such as HYG, LQD, TLT and
IEF rather than equity indices to trade macro risk.
The chart shows fixed-income ETF option volumes surging toward roughly 2.2–2.4 million contracts on
a 10-day average.

SPX may have stopped trading the macro. Macro traders haven't.

SPX has become an island
Something unusual is happening to the world's most important equity index.
GS notes that correlations between the S&P 500 and traditional cross-asset signals have broken down.
Bonds are telling one story, rates another, oil another and sentiment another.
Meanwhile, SPX keeps grinding around with implied volatility in the low double digits.
The desk puts it neatly: "the equity market is trading the Fed, the bond market is trading the regime."
The S&P is increasingly behaving like a self-contained asset class.
GS
The index is hiding the market
The S&P 500 remains near record highs, but underneath the surface the picture looks very different.
The spread between members making 52-week highs and lows has collapsed to around -20, even as
SPX sits near 7,700.
Meanwhile, correlations between the index and its own sectors have fallen sharply.
SPX isn't just disconnected from macro. It's increasingly disconnected from the market underneath it.

Where's the panic?
Cross-asset stress is everywhere. Equity volatility still doesn't care.
GS's US Vol Panic Index remains below 2/10, despite the huge moves across rates and credit.
More interestingly, the ratio of VIX implied vol to HYG implied vol has fallen to a 3+ year low. Credit is
pricing considerably more stress than equity vol.
The message from the options market is clear: investors aren't expecting the rates and credit stress to
spill materially into equities.
For anyone who disagrees, GS says VIX upside convexity remains extremely attractive.

The problem with the Fed put
Financial stress has once again pushed markets toward a more dovish central-bank path.
But DB highlights the problem: this isn't the 2010s.
Back then, inflation was generally below target, giving central banks plenty of room to respond to
market stress. Today inflation is still above target.
We saw the same pattern repeatedly in 2022-23: Ukraine, the UK mini-budget and SVB all triggered
dovish repricing. Each time, persistent inflation ultimately forced central banks back toward tightening.
Financial stress doesn't automatically equal a central-bank rescue anymore.
