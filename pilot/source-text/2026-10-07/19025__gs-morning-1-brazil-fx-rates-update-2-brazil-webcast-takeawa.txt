1) Brazil FX & Rates Update
Teresa Alves (FX Strategy) on our Morning Call: “I would split the potential FX move into (1) the initial
reaction and then (2) any actual policy-led moves. On (1) we probably saw the bulk of the move yesterday,
on (2) after this initial reaction it becomes much more about actual policy implementation to drive the next
leg of an eventual rally, or even a potential reversal if fiscal measures are not delivered. Beyond that initial
~10% move, we'd have to see clear signs either through cabinet appointments or policy ideas that
something's being done to address the underlying fiscal issues to then get further appreciation…
… the 2018 election is a good parallel because the initial first round move and the final result went in the
same direction, which is not the case in 2022, or even in 2014. When you look at the 2018 election, the
overall move over this similar window was close to 10%. When compared to 2018 -- governability is
much better for Flavio, given how the Congress has come in this time (vs for Jair) and that was eventually
one of the reasons BRL didn't manage to rally further in 2018, because the divided Congress was
complicating reform (see Charts below). Here, we don't have that issue, but we do have more pressing
fiscal concerns than we did back then…
… in this context, this idea that second round vol comes down substantially but then vol further out goes
up makes sense to me, because after the election is over, we'll have cabinet appointments, like the finance
ministry, and the 2027 budget that has to be finalised by year end. There will be quite a lot of policy-led
noise into year-end…”
Heloise Carrer (BRL Rates Trading) from the Close: DIs closed ~125bps tighter with a flattening bias,
long-end outperforming as expected. We thought the trade turned more symmetric Friday given polls
showing Lula ahead – clearly there was still decent asymmetry to a Flavio win (unlikely on polls, but it
happened), especially in the long end, which hit limit-down twice with the curve ~150bps tighter at one
point. The question now is whether the rally has legs. Long-end FRAs are already ~12.50, and honestly, to
break below the 11.5-12% region we'd need Flavio elected plus a credible fiscal package / transition bill.
That said, never underestimate market excitement – we're still talking about a double-digit long-end FRA.
Flows: net receivers in large size in the long end. Some profit-taking, but small relative to the receiver
community.

2) Brazil Webcast Replay & Takeaways

Senior equities/macro traders and our head of Latin America Economics shared key takeaways and market
implications from the first round of the Brazil presidential election.
Speakers:
Alberto Ramos, Head of Latin America Economics Research
Carlos Loureiro, Head of Latin America Equities Local Trading
Thiago Marinheiro, Head of the Brazil Macro Trading and ALM Desk
Key paraphrased takeaways below…
Alberto Ramos: “If Flávio wins, the market is anticipating a more conservative and conventional fiscal
strategy that delivers more of a traditional fiscal adjustment, but I'm not going to bet on a front loaded
large fiscal adjustment; rather, a gradual multi-year type of adjustment… making fiscal adjustments
requires a lot of political capital – we have seen in previous congresses the difficulty in approving things
that are needed but unpopular… I think they have to hammer the iron while it's hot…”
Carlos Loureiro: “My view is that EWZ is going to trade in the range of 45-50 for the next few days…
there's a lot of room for the upside in Brazil given the P/E ratios and the technical setup… I have been
trading markets for 25yrs and I have never seen an alignment like this across Congress, Senate,
Executive…”
Thiago Marinheiro: “Rates overall have not been very supportive for a rally… whilst in the first stage you
might have a reset in terms of pricing, it will then probably trade more correlated to the external market
which may prevent another pronounced rally… if by any chance you see a relief in the external market,
then I think we could go forward with a much more sustainable rally…”

3) TONY PASQUARIELLO – Markets/Macro
Tony Pasquariello, Global Head of Hedge Fund Coverage, out with his latest thoughts on the market.
Some key points below.
Market Direction: The trading environment is certainly not uncomplicated, but the primary trend clearly
remains higher, and the tactical setup is net positive. I say that for four core reasons: nominal GDP growth
is strong ... earnings growth is superb ... the flow-of-funds is supportive ... and the investment super cycle
continues apace. Looking forward, the core bullish framework remains intact, and the technical setup is
locally improving (sentiment, positioning, seasonals). In the end: I expect that Q4 will remain choppy, but
S&P will be higher on December 31st than it is today.
Stocks And Bonds: Higher rates are a headwind, not a wrecking ball. I don’t think that stocks are
complacent about the risks -- again, the P/E multiple has fallen from 23x to 19x over the past year.
Furthermore, you can clearly see impingement where you should (e.g. small cap, homebuilders, utilities,
REITs, biotech, non-profitable tech stocks). Set against that, earnings have been a bulwark -- again, the
spread of interest rates vs the earnings yield of S&P has been notably steady. Said another way: interest

rates matter, but so do profits. As we move forward, sustaining double-digit EPS growth -- alongside a
healthy credit market -- is the key to S&P absorbing more punches.
The Fed: At the risk of being too clever by half, however, I believe this market is very aware of what the
interest rate strip prices today: one more hike this year, and ~ two more hikes next year. If that is what the
Fed ultimately delivers, I expect that stocks will be able to tolerate it (this is where a $2tr budget deficit
and $1tr of AI capex come in uniquely handy). No matter what, taking stock of the last two paragraphs,
I’m still a believer that core length in risky assets should be accompanied by a trading short in US Fixed
Income.
Earnings: Consensus expects S&P EPS to grow 27% in Q3 -- that’s a slight deceleration from Q2, but still
some form of terrific. Here’s what I find so interesting: tech and energy are expected to drive 80% of the
growth (of course they are). AI infrastructure stocks are expected to drive half of the growth. NVDA and
MU alone are expected to drive 1/3 of the growth. Stick with the fastest horses in the race that are
supported by truly superb (and compounded) earnings growth.
US Tech: The freight train keeps chugging along -- note NDX is now +23% YTD -- as AI remains the
clearest and most powerful theme in the market. I expect more air pockets and volatility as the dollars
stack up, but US mega cap tech continues to be both the sword and the shield.
