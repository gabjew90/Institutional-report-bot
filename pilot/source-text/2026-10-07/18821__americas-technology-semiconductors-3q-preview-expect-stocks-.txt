work on better numbers following sector-wide pullback
5 October 2026 | 12:01AM EDT
Most estimate upside for SemiCap/Compute/Storage - risk/reward more favorable 
We see upside to estimates across most sub-sectors of the semiconductor ecosystem 
heading into 3Q earnings.  Following a significant pullback in the sector over the past 
two months driven by significant de-risking (SOX down 11% vs. SPX up 4%), we see a 
more constructive trading setup ahead of earnings.  This stands in stark contrast to 
the 2Q setup, where we signaled a more cautious tactical outlook heading into 
results.  We expect significant positive estimate revisions for SemiCap companies 
given pull-ins to WFE spending, and long-term visibility into 2028.  In compute, given 
an upward bias to hyperscaler CapEx, we see upside for both server CPUs (increasing 
agentic AI attach) and key ASIC programs.  We are favorable on analog given upside 
to estimates - and we prefer stocks with the greatest industrial, aero/defense and 
datacenter exposures. Finally, we see fundamental upside for the memory/storage 
complex, but prefer HDDs and NAND given a lack of near-term supply additions.  
Tactical Ideas: See upside for AMAT, STX, MCHP; downside for QCOM, KLAC, WDC 
AMAT (Buy) – We expect Applied to raise its margin targets and provide a robust 
growth outlook at SEMICON West in October, ahead of earnings - where we expect 
the company to deliver a strong report driven by DRAM and advanced logic. 
CDNS (Buy) - We expect Cadence to tick up guidance for 2026 and provide positive 
commentary on its momentum in recurring revenue growth tied to agentic AI, in line 
with peer Synopsys’ positive commentary at its Investor Day.  
MCHP (Buy) - We expect upside to the quarter driven broad industrial demand, plus 
upside in aerospace/defense and datacenter.  We believe the Street continues to 
under-model the analog recovery; MCHP is among our favorite names. 
QCOM (Neutral) - We expect modest upside to the quarter, but we think the stock 
may be ahead of itself on a tactical basis, given very strong investor expectations 
tied to agentic AI and its benefit to CPU vendors such as Qualcomm. 
KLAC (Neutral) - Despite upside to the quarter/guidance, we expect results to lag 
peers, as WFE spending is skewed toward DRAM, where process control - despite 
expectations for mid-20% revenue growth through 2027. 
WDC (Neutral) – Although we expect WDC to deliver modest upside to the quarter, 
we expect results to under-perform peer STX as its HAMR ramp is likely to drive 
stronger near-term exabyte growth and gross margin expansion.

Items on the call that could move the stock: (1) Potential server CPU upside: We

readiness, could be meaningful drivers of stock; (3) Margin progression: Commentary

Key stock takeaways: We expect investor focus to center on incremental supply 
procurement for ARM’s AGI CPU plus FY27 royalty revenue guidance, as muted 
smartphone market conditions are partly offset by strength in Server CPUs and 
datacenter demand. We believe expectations are elevated in light of a resilient AI 
infrastructure spending environment. 
Setup heading into the print: We believe expectations are elevated as (1) investors look 
for details on supply procurement for ARM’s AGI CPU; (2) AI related spending remains 
strong, which should benefit ARM’s datacenter royalty business.  
Our view on key metrics and our estimates: We expect an in-line quarter with EPS 
guidance above consensus, driven by better margins.  We would highlight near-term risk 
to the royalty business driven by persistent smartphone weakness, which is likely to 
offset strength in CPUs and datacenters. 
Items on the call that could move the stock: (1) Supply availability and potential 
upside to FY27 AGI CPU revenue: Management previously cited $2 bn of AGI CPU 
demand in FY27-28, with supply constraints restricting fulfillment of an incremental $1 
bn of demand; progress on securing supply and / or improving margin visibility for this 
business could drive the stock; (2) Agentic AI and ARM / X86 market share trends: 
Given the emergence of Meta’s Muse agentic product, we expect investors to focus on 
market share trends between ARM vs. X86, given our checks which suggest that Muse 
runs predominantly on X86; (3) Smartphone demand and impact on royalty revenues: 
Thoughts on persistent smartphone market weakness, visibility into end market 
recovery, and its impact on ARM’s royalty business could move the stock. 
Coming out of the print: Key areas of focus are likely to be incremental supply 
availability for the AGI CPU opportunity, traction with new customer engagements for 
AGI CPU, and visibility into the smartphone market recovery. 
 
Price Target Methodology and Risks 
We are Sell rated on ARM. Our 12-month price target of $160 is based on 50X our 
normalized EPS estimate of $3.20. Key upside risks: (1) stronger-than-expected 
Datacenter ramp; (2) better-than-expected operating leverage; (3)

hardware emulation momentum.

growing AI monetization opportunities.

revenue upside versus the Street in the quarter and raise full-year guidance for CY26 to

and hardware businesses, along with ~30% SD&A growth (~10% ex-Hexagon). For 2027, 
our EPS estimates are ~4% above Street consensus as we expect near-term EPS dilution

incremental AI revenues over time.

associated recurring revenue momentum; (2) EDA and IP momentum: We expect

monetization, sustainability of non-traditional customer demand, and long-term 
physical AI opportunities.

restrictions; (2) market share losses; (3) fewer custom chip designs.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
