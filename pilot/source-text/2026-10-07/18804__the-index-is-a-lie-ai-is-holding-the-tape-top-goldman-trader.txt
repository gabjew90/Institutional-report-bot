 
The Index Is A Lie, AI Is Holding The Tape; Top 
Goldman Trader Warns Midterms 'Probably Won't 
Be Risk Positive' 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SUNDAY, OCT 04, 2026 - 11:30 PM 
As growth becomes scarcer, the composition of growth matters increasingly, according to 
Goldman Sachs global head of equity custom baskets Louis Miller. (pro subs can read the full 
'Global Reflections' note here). 
The market is likely to favor compounders with durable growth profiles over high-growth 
cyclicals, which may face slowing momentum as higher rates, elevated oil prices, and rising 
energy costs create headwinds. 
De-escalation-driven investment narratives are becoming harder to underwrite in an 
environment where the Fed is still tightening, oil remains near $100, and 10-year yields are 
above 5%, although these themes remain compelling from a narrative perspective. Our Call on 
AI over cyclicals/SPXXAI Index has worked well, and we still like it. 

 
 
Ultimately, midterms beckon and the result probably won’t be risk positive. 
​
​
A softer PCE and NFP print have helped the market push out the next Fed hike, but exposure in 
Europe feels increasingly uncomfortable as fiscal risk is building. Japan is the standout region in 

 
Asia with a $140bn AI datacenter push driving renewed focus on regional AI capex 
beneficiaries. 
Below are five key points (and a bonus one of Brazil): 
Rates-sensitive equities have felt the pain. 
At the index level, equities have continued to grind higher amid the rates market meltdown, but 
the market is (once again) being held up by the AI trade. 
 
Beneath the surface, rate-sensitive pockets have been under pressure. Our US Homebuilders 
(GSXUBLDR) basket has declined -17.5% over the last 3 months, and while Non-Profitable 
Tech (GSXUNPTC) had a strong start to the year, it has drawn down -11.4% since early July. 
Lending Sensitive Equities (GSXULEND) have underperformed in tandem while Regional Banks 
(GSCBRGBK) have been more resilient relative to their rates beta and could have downside if 
rates remain elevated. 
A dose of accommodation has been removed... 

 
 
A softer PCE and NFP are pushing yields lower, which could drive significant relief in these 
rates-sensitive pockets if sustained. Weak market breadth also implies scope for a broader beta 
rally, which we like expressing via our sector/industry-neutral beta basket (GSPUBETA). 
Our Beta-ex-AI pair (GSPUBETX) has underperformed the broader Beta factor (GSPUBETA) 
while maintaining consistently high correlation 
 

 
For more cautious investors, we recommend hedging via our Beta-ex AI pair (GSPUBETX). 
Fundamentals on POWER remain intact. 
Demand forecasts continue to be revised higher. GS research sees 108 GW of datacenter 
power demand by 2030 (23% CAGR off a 2025 base) and a 3.5% if total US power demand 
CAGR, of which datacenters contribute just over 2.5% (1.8pp front-of-the-meter and 0.8pp 
behind-the-meter). Equipment demand remains exceptional. GEV is guiding to 125 GW of 
backlog plus slot reservation agreements by year end, roughly 6x current capacity. Construction 
on the ground is materially stronger than the headlines suggest. 
Valuations have de-rated across the complex: IPPs are down ~30% over 12m, gas 
infrastructure names are down ~13% over 3m. Behind-the-meter is the key structural shift to the 
4.5 year interconnection queue and is expected to make up ~20% of datacenter power demand 
by 2030. State-level permitting friction has, on the margin, increased the preference for 
“bring-your-own-power”. We are monitoring the upcoming catalysts in this area: FERC ruling on 
PJM large-load framework, 3Q Earnings, November midterms, and ERCOT datacenter 
interconnection audit.​
​
We like owning upside in this theme into year-end, particularly as rising agentic AI adoption 
drives higher compute and power needs. 
Power Up America (GSX1POW1) has lagged other AI themes YTD but upcoming catalysts and 
strong fundamentals imply scope to catch up. 

 
 
The AI power story in Asia is no different and we’ve just launched a basket to gain exposure to a 
critical component of next-gen AI datacenters with our Asia Battery Backup Units (GSXABBUS). 
The basket provides exposure to the BBU supply chain across batteries, modules and energy 
storage systems. Note our research colleagues expect the global BBU TAM to expand 23x 
between 2025 and 2030E. 
French fragility becoming a Euro problem? 
OAT-Bund spreads hovering around 150bps with signs of contagion into BTPs this week 
implying the market is now pricing broader European sovereign risk. In equities, European 
Banks (SX7E) have been hit hard following an exceptional 18m rally and given significant net 
length in the trade. 
OAT-Bund spread widening is showing signs of contagion to BTPs and EURUSD reflecting 
European fiscal anxiety 

 
 
If French fiscal risk remains contained, we would be buyers of dips in EU Banks given a solid 
structural earnings outlook, but we remain cautious on stocks with weak balance sheets and 
upcoming debt maturities (GSXECLFF). We also note a pick-up in investor interest on equities 
vulnerable to Euro weakness. Our European Domestic vs. US exposed pair trade 
(GSPEDOAM) has historically been most reactive to moves in EURUSD. 
Japan separates itself from the rest of Asia. 
Japan’s strong regional performance is being driven by AI leadership and BoJ normalization that 
remains gradual enough for equities to absorb. A less hawkish BoJ summary of opinions has 
supported sentiment on Japan equities, while flows in recent weeks have increasingly favored 
Japan AI versus EM AI beneficiaries. 
Japan AI (GSXAJPAI) is breaking out from the broader Asia AI Datacenters (GSXADATA) trade. 
The latest $140bn AI datacenter investment push should drive further upside 

 
 
Our favorite Japan AI trade is our Optical Networking basket (GSXAJONE) as the key 
beneficiary of the next leg of AI infra spending. 
Pulse Check on China Consumer. 
Golden Week consumer data will be the key thing to watch when China reopens next week. 
Another disappointing print would reinforce concerns that stimulus remains incremental rather 
than transformative. 
The macro data shows a widening production-consumption gap, which our economists argue 
reflects a structural pivot to high tech manufacturing, supportive of headline output, but less 
labor-intensive, and negative for employment, household income, and consumption. 
China Consumer Sentiment needs to inflect for EU Luxury (GSXELUXG) stocks to work. 
Feedback from Golden Week will be key. 

 
 
We like positioning via shorts on Asia Consumer Inertia stocks (GSXASWCH) that face 
pressure from weak discretionary spend and the longer-term risk of AI-enabled switching 
behavior. Inflecting China consumer sentiment is also key for a turnaround in European Luxury 
stocks (GSXELUXG), though Liberation-Day low valuation and stable EPS momentum have 
driven a pick-up in interest ahead of mid-month 3Q updates. 
Bonus point – Brazil first round elections this weekend. 
We’ve been flagging Brazil Rate Sensitive (GSBZRATE) as the key equity trade into elections. 

 
 
A Bolsonaro win scenario should be bullish equities, and in particular rate sensitive equities 
which could re-rate up to 30% in a 200bp rate cut outcome. 
 
What Miller is really describing is two discount rates living in one market.  
AI CapEx is being underwritten as if power, batteries and fiber are the new duration: scarce, 
long-lived, and worth paying for even with the 10-year above 5% and oil near $100. 

 
Everything else is being taxed by that same cost of capital. 
Homebuilders down 17.5% in three months and non-profitable tech down 11% since July are not 
a side story. They are the market that exists once you stop letting the AI bid speak for the index. 
That split only lasts if the power buildout monetizes before the political calendar forces one price 
of money back onto the whole tape. 
French spreads at 150 basis points, a US midterm that he already calls not risk-positive, and a 
Brazil first round this weekend are all bids to do exactly that. 
Scarce growth is not the constraint. The constraint is how long the market can pretend the cost 
of capital applies to everyone except the trade holding the index up.
