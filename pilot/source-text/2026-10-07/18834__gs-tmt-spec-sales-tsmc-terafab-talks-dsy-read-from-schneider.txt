• DSY read from Schneider’s acquisition of PTC: Schneider Electric has signed a definitive all-cash
agreement to acquire PTC at $205/share. Equity value ~$22.6B (€20.1B), EV ~$23.7B (€21.1B). Premium
is ~42% to last close. Schneider’s acquisition of PTC creates a scaled industrial software platform that
spans product design/PLM through operations, data, and AI, accelerating sector consolidation around a
handful of end-to-end “design-to-operate” stacks. Hardware-centric players are buying high-quality
recurring software to lock in customers, raise switching costs, and monetize AI/digital-twin demand; the
result is higher competitive intensity, sustained elevated multiples for strategic assets, and pressure on
mid-tier or point-solution vendors that lack comparable breadth or distribution. For DSY the deal raises
the bar without being existential. Schneider + PTC + AVEVA + Cognite becomes a more formidable rival

with stronger go-to-market reach into discrete manufacturing, but DSY retains clear differentiation in deep
multi-physics simulation, complex systems engineering, and long-cycle verticals (aerospace, automotive,
life sciences) where 3DEXPERIENCE switching costs remain high. Near-term sentiment positive for the
space as it now supported by M&A validation of the space (plus DSY is a consensus short – so risk of a
squeeze); however medium-term the pressure mounts of DSY to accelerates its own data/AI layer or faces
share shifts in contested deals and the ~€800M of rev synergies needs to come from somewhere. Also
remember for DSY a takeover is not simple as control rest with the Dassault family (~39.9–40.5%).

• BT/TalkTalk: BT has now confirmed the acquisition of TalkTalk and PlatformX Communications out of
administration on a debt-free basis, moving the situation beyond the weekend press speculation
underpinning my earlier view. The estimated FY27 cash impact is c£400m, including consideration,

AI credit Adam Crook full email worth a read he is flagging that AI-driven capital spending is increasingly
becoming a credit and rates market story, not just an equity story. Credit strategists estimate hyperscaler
debt issuance will reach roughly $250bn in 2026 (versus less than $20bn in 2024) and rise further to
$420bn in 2027, reflecting the enormous funding requirements of the ongoing AI infrastructure buildout.
Importantly, hyperscalers account for only about 40% of the more than $575bn of global AI-related debt
issuance this year, with additional supply coming from data centre financing, semiconductor projects and
broader AI ecosystem companies. While issuers appear largely insensitive to higher borrowing costs given
the strategic importance of AI investment, issuance has increasingly shifted toward shorter maturities.
The broader macro implication is that AI investment is creating a persistent source of debt supply that
could keep long-end bond yields structurally higher and more disconnected from traditional economic
fundamentals. Goldman Sachs rates strategists argue that the scale of planned borrowing is reducing
investor appetite for duration risk and increasing the likelihood that long-term yields overshoot fair value.
For equity investors, this reinforces the importance of upcoming hyperscaler earnings, as confidence in the
AI capex cycle increasingly depends on companies demonstrating improving returns on invested capital
through stronger revenue growth and cloud profitability. The note also highlights growing investor
concern around concentration risk, rising AI-focused credit trading activity, and the market’s ability to
absorb the rapid increase in both investment-grade and high-yield AI-related debt issuance.

• SEMIS SIA DATA: Jim’s view is that semiconductor demand continued to improve in August, with SIA
data showing integrated circuit shipments (excluding memory) rising ~2% month-on-month, ahead of
normal seasonal patterns. Most end markets tracked above seasonal levels, while weaker trends in
discretes, MCUs and DSPs were attributed to ongoing consumer electronics softness. Importantly, overall
semiconductor shipments are now running about 10% above long-term demand trends on a three‑month
moving average basis, up from 7% in July, reinforcing management commentary that inventories are
normalising and demand is recovering. Schneider highlights that analog semiconductor trends have
improved meaningfully, with shipments also ~10% above trend, supporting a more constructive industry
outlook. Given the prolonged period of below-trend shipments over the last four years, Goldman expects
the recovery to continue and remains most positive on Microchip, NXP and Analog Devices, which were
among the companies that under-shipped the most during the downturn and therefore stand to benefit the
most from the upcycle.
• US Semis Q3 preview: Jim is constructive, arguing that after a sharp sector de-risking (SOX down 11%
over the past two months despite the broader market rising), earnings expectations are now more
achievable and most semiconductor sub-sectors should deliver upside to consensus estimates. The firm is
especially positive on SemiCap, where stronger wafer fab equipment spending and improved visibility
into 2028 could drive meaningful estimate revisions, while compute should benefit from rising hyperscaler
capex, growing agentic AI adoption and strength in both server CPUs and ASIC programmes. GS also sees
upside in analog semis, particularly names with industrial, aerospace/defence and datacentre exposure, and
remains constructive on memory/storage, preferring HDD and NAND plays given limited near-term
supply additions. Tactically, it highlights Applied Materials (AMAT), Seagate (STX) and Microchip
(MCHP) as preferred longs into earnings, while viewing Qualcomm (QCOM), KLA (KLAC) and Western
Digital (WDC) as less attractive on a relative basis despite expecting generally solid quarterly results
across the sector.
• AWS data centres: Amazon Web Services plans to invest more than $1bn over five years in US
communities that host its data centres, funding education, job training, energy affordability, and water and
energy conservation efforts. The commitment is aimed at addressing local concerns around the impact of
AI infrastructure on electricity and water use, with AWS also pledging to become “water positive” by
2030 by returning more water to communities than it uses. The move comes as opposition to new AI data
centres remains high, with a Gallup survey finding that 71% of Americans oppose having one built in their
local area because of concerns over power demand and other local impacts.

• US carriers push back on Starlink: AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon are creating a joint venture to pool
spectrum and common technical standards for direct-to-device satellite coverage, with the aim of reducing
US mobile dead zones, improving backup connectivity when terrestrial networks fail and making satellite
service easier for handset makers and rural carriers to support. The move matters because it turns satellite
coverage from a set of fragmented carrier partnerships into a coordinated industry response, giving the big

three US operators more control over how satellite-to-phone connectivity develops rather than leaving the
narrative — and potentially the customer relationship in rural or uncovered areas — to Starlink. It is still
early, with no service date and no venture name yet, but strategically it is a clear attempt by the
incumbents to close the coverage gap before Starlink can use dead zones as a wedge into mobile
connectivity.
MEDIA & INTERNET
• Europe Media: Key Takeaways from Adam on Agencies - The advertising agency sector has rebounded
strongly in 2026, with investors becoming more comfortable that AI is not materially disrupting agency
business models, particularly in media. Media growth at the large holding companies has accelerated back
towards ~6% in 2Q26 versus ~3% in 2025, while consolidation among the three major global media
groups is seen as helping maintain pricing power and client relationships. Importantly, there has been very
little evidence of advertisers bringing media buying in-house, with only one significant media account
moving in-house this year. However, Adam highlights a growing competitive threat from a select group of
“digital-native” independent media agencies. While independents overall are not gaining share, some are
successfully winning smaller and mid-sized accounts from the large holdcos. These agencies tend to focus
on advertisers with media budgets below $200m, making companies with greater exposure to smaller
clients more vulnerable. Adam sees Publicis as the least exposed due to its larger-account mix, while WPP
and Omnicom have greater exposure. Against this backdrop, GIR upgrades coverage of Stagwell to Buy
with a $13 price target (54% upside), arguing recent pitch wins, strong momentum in marketing services
and digital transformation, and improvements to its underperforming media business should drive faster
growth. Goldman also initiates coverage of Havas at Neutral with a €19 price target (8% upside). While
Havas is expected to deliver solid organic growth and margin expansion, GIR is more cautious on the
outlook for its media business given its smaller scale and increasing competition from newer entrants.
Bottom line: Adam remains constructive on the agency sector as AI concerns recede and media growth
improves, but sees the key debate shifting toward whether high-growth independent agencies can
increasingly challenge the large holdcos for smaller accounts. Stagwell is viewed as the most attractive
beneficiary of this trend, while Havas faces a more balanced risk/reward profile. more balanced
risk/reward profile.
• Outdoor Advertising: Global out-of-home trends remain favourable across most major markets, with
2026 year-to-date growth accelerating versus 2025, while the competitive backdrop has also become more
rational as lower valuation multiples push operators to focus more on profitability. Recent M&A and
newsflow around assets such as oOh!media, Stroeer, Clear Channel and Adams Outdoor Advertising
highlight the continued strategic interest in the space, and JCDecaux looks best positioned to benefit given

in line with consensus on revenue and EBITDA
• Auto1’s 3Q pre-close update disappointed versus expectations, with group units sold coming in ~4%

EBITDA forecasts by c.4%/6%. The weaker quarter was driven by slower growth in both Merchant and
