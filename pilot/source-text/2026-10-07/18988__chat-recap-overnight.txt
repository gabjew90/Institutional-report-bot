"Treasury yields are set to test higher levels as evidence mounts that companies are not 
only aggressively expanding headcounts, they’re also defending them. September’s weak 
payroll print looks less convincing as a sign of a broad labor-market break after both ISM 
and S&P Global data signal there’s a floor for labor demand. The ISM employment index 
flipped to expansion from contraction in the prior month, while S&P Global data showed the 
strongest job creation since June 2022. Importantly, both signaled they are filling long-
standing vacant jobs. 
  
"Replacing a retiring or departing worker who left months ago is a hire — and it does add to 

the payroll count — but it says more about unfilled backlogs than about new demand for 
labor. These vacancies are being filled while orders and backlogs are rising, suggesting 
replacement hiring is being driven by actual workload, not just hiring sentiment. ISM’s 
backlog index reached 56.6, the highest in over four years, with one respondent to the 
survey saying workload had exceeded available manpower. 
  
For Treasuries, this is bearish at the margin, but not as bearish as widespread hiring for 
newly created positions. It says the labor market has shifted toward maintenance hiring -- 
something that reverses the trend seen in the aftermath of the pandemic for fear of a 
recession. Combined with rising selling prices, it leaves the bond market with resilient 
employment demand and inflation pressure, without the compensating growth scare." -- BN
