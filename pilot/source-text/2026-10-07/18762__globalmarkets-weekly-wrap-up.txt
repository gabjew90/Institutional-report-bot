U.S. equities ended mixed as weaker employment data reduced 
expectations for an October Fed hike, while elevated Treasury 
yields, volatile oil prices, and U.S.-Iran tensions weighed on 
sentiment. The Nasdaq and S&P Mid Cap 400 advanced; the Dow 
and S&P 500 declined. On the macro data front, September 
payrolls rose just 29,000, versus 90,000 expected. Prior months 
were revised down by 60,000, and unemployment increased to 
4.2%. Treasury yields fell following the release but remain higher 
on the week. Other macro indicators were mixed: private hiring 
improved, vacancies declined, and jobless claims remained low. 
Inflation stayed sticky, with headline PCE at 3.4% annually and 
core PCE at 3.0%. Second-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 
2.2%, while underlying private demand expanded 4.6%. 
Manufacturing grew for a ninth consecutive month, with the ISM 
PMI at 54.5. However, its prices index surged to 77.9, highlighting 
renewed cost pressures. Slower hiring strengthens the case for a 
Fed pause, although resilient demand and inflation complicate the 
policy outlook. The pan-European STOXX Europe 600 Index ended 
the week down 1.14% in local currency terms. French fiscal 
concerns weighed on sentiment and pushed OAT-bunds spread 
higher and the euro lower. Japan’s Nikkei 225 Index advanced 
2.93%. Oil prices extended the week's rise, with Brent above $102 
by late Friday on the lack of any sign of an off-ramp in Iran. Gold 
stalled while bitcoin flirted with $87k. 
U.S. job growth cools as inflation remains persistent
