 
Mega-Cap Tech Tries To Stay "Duration Resistant"; 
Goldman Says Brace For A "Spirited Q4 In The 
Shooting Gallery" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SUNDAY, OCT 04, 2026 - 09:30 PM 
"Three down, one to go," says Goldman Sachs head of hedge fund coverage, Tony 
Pasquariello, warning traders to "limber up for what should be a spirited Q4 in the shooting 
gallery" in his latest note (available here in full for pro subs). 
Below, he offers a set of short points on what he's seeing / hearing / thinking: 
Another week of uneven news flow and gathering pressure across global bond markets, yet, 
S&P keeps rolling with the punches, and NDX just made a higher high.  Set against that, 
equal-weight S&P is down seven straight weeks...  
 
One could read this as a marker of patently terrible market breadth.  

 
One could also read this as yet another example of how hard it is to beat plain old cap-weighted 
S&P, led by US mega cap tech.  
In that context, Pasquariello struggled to find the word choice to characterize Q3 - "tricky" was 
the best he came up with- and offered the following framing: the Magnificent Seven was UP 
11%.  in turn, cap-weighted S&P was UP 2%.  in contrast, equal-weight S&P was DOWN 2%.  in 
addition, the Russell was DOWN 8%.  alongside all of this, of course, was a surge higher in US 
rates (e.g. 10s backed up -- ahem -- 82 bps).  
At the single stock and sector levels of the market, the leaders are leading (driven by superb 
earnings growth) and the laggards are lagging (given the impingement of rates and oil).  
On one hand, you can argue the market is rational, the game is straightforward and one should 
attack the obvious trends with high gross exposure.  
That gives short shrift, however, to the volatility of momentum - a factor that was belted in July 
(-30%) and August (-14%), before ripping in September (+27%).  
 
Said another way, the fast money game is harder than it appears. 
Again, US tech has reasserted leadership of the market.  Again, it’s been in the doing.  Which is 
to say: META is up 25% over the past month given the presumed birth of agentic AI for the 
almighty US consumer.  Another example: MU demonstrated that a stock with a huge market 
cap -- and a stubborn multiple -- can still rally on superb earnings.  or this: NVDA added $150bn 
to their buyback plan, which now totals $235bn (that’s not something I’d look to pick a fight with 
-- note nearly $300bn of market cap expansion since the announcement).  

 
So, if you’re wondering why NDX has rallied in 11 of the past 13 days despite the charge higher 
in interest rates, I’d argue the answer lies in fundamental news flow of these monstrous 
companies.  Part-and-parcel of that, it’s the one part of the market where capital clearly flowed 
in September. (read Goldman's full Weekly Prime Brokerage flows report here) 
 
Asia 
I thought this was a good take - from one of my partners who has a front row seat for the action 
in the Asian equity markets: 
“it is a sign of the times that portions of the global investment community remain underweight a 
region where some of its largest market cap companies have sell-side price targets implying 
~90% upside.   
The willingness to add risk remains remarkably measured”. 

 
 
Speaking of Asia, the Japanese equity market continues to impress.   
Here’s another good take from a local colleague that resonated with me: 
“Japan has outperformed the S&P YTD, but it doesn't feel like it.  TOPIX is up 20.0% in local 
terms against roughly 11.8% for the S&P, with the Nikkei up 36.6%.  between JPY swings, sharp 
rotations and the wait for a fresh TOPIX high, it hasn't always felt like a winning tape.  the path 
matters.  so does what you own”. 

 
 
Europe 
While repeating my respect for the doggedness of European equities in the face of so many 
headwinds, given the gathering pressures across EU sovereign debt stacks, I struggle to work 
out how it can compete with US and Asian equities in the next round. 
Iran/Oil 
Lots of talk, no progress.  if anything, it feels like the temperature is set to rise.  with that said, 
Persian Gulf exports of oil are back to the average level of 2025 (link).  
I’m not arguing that the war isn’t an open risk for stocks -- I’m simply pointing out that the oil 
market has quickly adapted to a seemingly intractable situation.  it’s here that I’ll return to a line 
from the great Neil Mehta in the depths of March: “oil usually finds a way to flow.” 
Q4 Setup 
I will circle back next week with a full directional framework, but for the moment, I’ll register this: 
there’s a ton of things to worry about right now, and S&P has been stuck in the mud at 7700 for 
a good while now, but I like the technical setup for the market.  

 
Why?  
A decent chop of quarter-end / month-end supply is behind us.   
Both long only funds and the levered community have clearly shed length.   
 
As the Q3 reporting period moves along, buybacks should kick fully into gear.   
And, the US retail investor was lazy as a fox in September.

If you’re curious where capital is flowing within an ETF complex that’s growing like a weed, this

In the context of rampant calls for de-dollarization at various turns over recent years, I find it a 
little interesting that foreign investors are still pouring into US equities... 
 
Two charts for the road: 
On one hand, global rates are melting higher ... here I’m zeroing in on 10-year yields in the US 
(black line) and Japan (red line)... 

 
 
And, yet, US mega cap tech companies are fighting through it to make higher highs ... perhaps 
the biggest question into Q4 is whether these stocks can remain “duration resistant”: 
 
So, with all that in mind, we see Pasquariello's message as not that bonds are rising, it is that 
the market has already picked a winner inside that rise, and that winner is a handful of cash-flow 
machines that do not need the rate to come down. 

 
Equal-weight down seven weeks and the Russell down 8% in a quarter when the ten-year 
backed up over 80bps is the normal transmission. Nasdaq up in 11 of 13 days is the exception, 
and it is being paid for with earnings and buybacks, not with a multiple.  
The risk is that this gets mistaken for the market being fine. It is not fine. It is narrow, and the 
momentum factor’s −30%, −14%, +27% path says the narrowness is violent. 
The useful Q4 setup is the one he almost buries: Length has been shed, quarter-end supply is 
done, buybacks reopen, and retail sat out September. 
That is a better tape for the index than for the median stock.  
If mega-cap tech stays duration-resistant, 7700 is a pause before higher highs.  
If it stops being duration-resistant, there is no second engine: Europe is pinned by its own 
sovereign stack, Asia is underowned but unloved, and oil has already adapted. 
The shooting gallery he opened with is not the level of the S&P. It is whether five stocks can 
keep doing the work of five hundred.
