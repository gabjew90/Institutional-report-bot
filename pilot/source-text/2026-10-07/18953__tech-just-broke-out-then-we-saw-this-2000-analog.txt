The breakout dilemma
Tech has finally done it.
After months of consolidation, NDX is breaking into fresh air just as bearishness has been building and
the seasonal backdrop turns increasingly supportive.
But underneath the index, the picture is much less clean. Breadth remains weak, semis are tracking an
uncomfortable historical analog, and NDX is already overbought.
The breakout is here. The question isn't necessarily whether to fight it, but how to own the upside from
here.
Teflon Tech
On Sep 10 (here) we outlined our most recent logic on tech and we wrote: "...the ingredients for an
upside vacuum are falling into place."
NDX is up roughly 7% since then and is now breaking above the final resistance, entering pure vacuum
territory. Note that the 50-day moving average has turned positively sloped again.
Yes, we've come a long way since mid-September, but zoom out: NDX has essentially gone nowhere
since May and is only now breaking out of a massive consolidation.
A lot of complex psychology has built up inside that range, with bearishness actually intensifying over
the past few weeks.
That makes this breakout increasingly interesting.

Can the NDX breakout hold?
NDX has finally broken out of its multi-month range, but the internals remain far less impressive than
the index.

BTIG points out that just 59% of Nasdaq names are trading above their 200-day moving averages,
while the advance/decline line continues to make new lows.
That doesn't necessarily kill the breakout. The late-1990s showed just how long a narrowing market
can continue higher.
But this remains a breakout driven by the generals, not the army.
BTIG/Bloomberg
The uncomfortable SOX analog
While NDX is breaking out, BTIG highlights an uncomfortable setup underneath the surface.
SOX continues to track the 2000 analog remarkably closely. On their roadmap, semis are now roughly
where the sector was around the March 2000 peak.
Importantly, that doesn't imply an immediate crash. The 2000 analog actually points to a period of
choppy consolidation first.
NDX may be breaking out. The semiconductor analog says this could be much later in the cycle than it
looks.
BTIG
A broadening attempt
There is still a possible bullish resolution to the breadth problem.
Equal-weight SPX continues to hold above a rising 200-day moving average and has recently started
improving relative to cap-weight SPX. That's exactly what bulls want to see next.
The breakout has been narrow. The question now is whether the rest of the market finally starts
catching up.
