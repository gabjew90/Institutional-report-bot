Mkts/Macro – US Equites were mixed last week with S&P (-0.27%), Nasdaq +0.45%, Russell 2000
(-0.16%). All eyes still on the Yields as Treasuries were mostly weaker with the curve steepening. Key
datapoints were a surprisingly soft NFP print (+29k vs. ~90k consensus; -60k net two-month revisions;
unemployment rate rising to 4.2%) paired with a cooler core PCE (+0.2% m/m, 3.0% y/y via
methodological adjustments) . Combined, they took October FOMC hike probabilities from 64% down to
sub 25% with short-end yields dipping while the 10Y/30Y saw yields hit ~24-year highs before pulling
slightly back off those levels on Friday. A couple of Fed speakers also helped drive the FOMC hike prbs
lower including NY Fed President Williams ("no urgency") and Vice Chair Jefferson ("judgment may take
more time"). GS Econ also took out the Oct hike from their forecast and now expect the Fed to go in
December instead.
Geopolitics/Energy - WTI crude -1.40% on the week post Friday announcement of a coordinated G7/IEA
emergency release of up to 100m barrels of diesel and crude over 4 months. However, newsflow around
physical supply dislocations continues to feel less than benign with UKMTO confirming 2 separate
commercial crude tanker projectile strikes in and around the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, while
Iranian Parliament Speaker warned that the waterway will remain closed until Tehran's Memorandum
conditions are met. The focus is on physical barrels clearing to help drive an energy-driven disinflation

Flows/Positioning – GS execution team highlighted US equities were modestly net bought on the week,
driven by short covering in Macro Products partially offset by continued shorting in Single Stocks. 8 of 11
sectors were net sold, led by Comm Svcs, Consumer Discretionary, and Materials, while Health Care was
by far the most net bought driven by aggressive short covering (still net sold month/month). While oil
prices remain near multi-year highs, Energy is the most net sold US sector in the past month in both $ and
SD terms, driven almost entirely by short sales as HFs have net sold the sector for 6 straight weeks. GS
Prime team also confirmed HFs aggressively de-risking single-stock long portfolios. Fundamental L/S

Gross leverage dropped 1.7 percentage points to 206.2% (21st percentile 1-year) while Net leverage shed
2.1 percentage points to 53.0% (7th percentile 1-year). In addition, GS Sentiment Indicator latest read of
-0.7 SD (vs -0.9 SD last week) continues to point to light positioning at best. Given such lean exposure
and with single stocks now on the 3rd consecutive week of net shorting, the pain trade is the upside bleed
higher (more below on Risk Views).

Key Takeaways below from GS Research, Trading Floor/Execution, Prime & beyond:
Updated Econ Forecast – GS econ team pushes their second expected Fed hike from October to December
after softer-than-expected core PCE inflation and sizeable downward revisions to prior inflation data,
while acknowledging a growing risk that the tightening cycle is already over and no further hikes are
needed.
Jan Hatzius continues to have a more dovish policy outlook. Softer August inflation, combined with
larger-than-expected downward revisions to prior core PCE data and recent comments from NY Fed
President Williams, leads Jan to push his second expected rate hike from October to December. Jan also
emphasizes that there is now a meaningful possibility the Fed ultimately decides that no additional hikes
are required if disinflation continues.
On the inflation picture, August core PCE rose 0.25% MoM and 3.01% YoY, both below expectations,
while benchmark revisions lowered prior inflation readings more than anticipated. The revisions were
driven largely by portfolio management costs and imply inflation is running materially below the Fed's
latest projections. As a result, Jan cuts his 2026 Q4/Q4 core PCE forecast to 3.0%, versus the median
FOMC participant expectation of 3.4%.
Finally, on GDP, US growth data remained resilient despite the softer inflation backdrop. Q2 GDP was
revised up to +2.2% annualised, with stronger consumer spending (+3.8%) and investment (+4.6%),
including support from data-centre-related capex. However, a much wider August trade deficit offset some
of the strength, leading GS to trim Q3 GDP tracking slightly to +3.3%, which still points to above-trend
economic growth heading into Q4.
Earnings Preview - The US Strategy Team previews the upcoming Q3 earnings season , expecting a
reinforcement of the AI investment cycle, with S&P 500 EPS growth remaining exceptionally strong and
increasingly concentrated in AI infrastructure, while investors focus on evidence that surging capex is
translating into revenue, productivity gains and durable monetisation.
Ben Snider and team note that consensus looks for 27% YoY S&P 500 EPS growth in Q3, the strongest
pre-season growth expectation since 2021, with GS expecting most companies to beat estimates again as
economic data and earnings revisions remain supportive.
AI infrastructure beneficiaries are expected to contribute more than 50% of S&P 500 earnings growth,
while MU and NVDA alone account for more than one-third of index growth this quarter. Hyperscaler
spending also continues to accelerate: Consensus expects 116% YoY hyperscaler capex growth in Q3, with

cloud revenue growth forecast to accelerate from 48% to 55%, providing further evidence that AI
investment is beginning to monetise.
Info Tech and Energy are expected to generate ~80% of total S&P 500 earnings growth, while market
breadth has fallen to its weakest level since the Dot-Com era, highlighting how concentrated both earnings
and market returns have become and even with the S&P 500 sitting close to ATHs, the median stock is
17% below its peak.
Ben Snider and team note that last quarter, only 2% of S&P 500 companies quantified an earnings benefit
from AI, but investors will increasingly scrutinise commentary for signs of productivity gains and tangible
financial returns as adoption broadens.

Earnings Preview – Semis and Banks in focus. Jim Schneider sees stronger AI capex, DRAM/HBM and
advanced packaging demand are driving broad earnings upgrades, with AMAT, LRCX, AMD and STX
among the highest-conviction names into 2027. On the banks side, Richard Ramsden notes NII remains
the key earnings driver as loan growth, asset repricing and a steeper curve support profitability, with WFC,
BAC and C offering the best mix of earnings upside and return improvement.
Jim Schneider sees the best 3Q setup in semis since the correction, with broad estimate upside across
SemiCap, compute, analog and storage driven by stronger hyperscaler AI capex, server CPU demand,
WFE pull-ins and improving visibility into 2027-28. Key themes are accelerating DRAM/HBM and
advanced packaging spending, agentic AI boosting CPU and ASIC demand, analog cyclicality recovering
through industrial/aero-defense/datacentre exposure, and tight HDD/NAND supply supporting storage
earnings. Tactical preferences are skewed toward companies with positive estimate revisions and
favourable positioning into 2027, with highest-conviction ideas AMAT, MCHP, STX, LRCX, ONTO,
TER, AMD and SITM, while Jim is more cautious on QCOM, KLAC, WDC, TXN, ARM, ENTG and
MKSI where expectations, valuation or relative growth appear less compelling.
On banks, Richard Ramsden sees NII as the standout earnings driver for large-cap banks, supported by 7-
9% loan growth, fixed-rate asset repricing and a steeper yield curve, with funding cost pressure from
deposits still more than offset by asset sensitivity and NIM expansion. While capital markets revenues are
moderating from exceptionally strong levels and capital return is likely to slow as excess capital falls,
valuations remain undemanding at ~11x FY+2 EPS and ~60% of the S&P 500 multiple. The highest
conviction ideas remain WFC, BAC and C, where the team see the clearest NII upside, strongest operating
leverage, improving capital returns and meaningful scope for ROTCE and profitability expansion over the
next 2-3 years.
US Conviction List – Our GIR team updates their Conviction list for October – highlighting a shift
towards high-conviction and structural growth stories across AI, data centres, defence, energy and
consumer, with GIR adding AMZN, BURL, HII, JCI, and OXY.

GIR add AMZN to the Conviction List, with Eric Sheridan seeing the company as well positioned to
produce a strong mix of compounded revenue growth and operating margin expansion on a multi-year
horizon while continuing to make critical investments in long-term growth initiatives. Three growth
drivers to focus on: (1) the AI revolution's need for AMZN's compute power; (2) operating leverage
combining with cost optimization to drive eCommerce profitability; and (3) a still nascent but growing
advertising platform.
JCI is another addition to the Conviction List. For JCI, Joe Ritchie sees the company in the early stages of
a dual top and bottom-line transformation that is poised to drive consistent, highly visible earnings growth,
>500bps improvement in adjusted operating margins, and a more than doubling of EPS through 2028.
Now led by CEO Joakim Weidemanis – who’s prior 13-year executive experience at Danaher is
increasingly visible in the rollout of a proprietary business system -- JCI is executing a structural shift
from a cyclical commercial construction company to a mission-critical thermal management platform.
This continuous improvement operating model is already unlocking tangible improvements in
manufacturing throughput, innovation velocity, customer-facing time, and cash conversion.
Simultaneously, JCI’s AI-driven data center cooling business is scaling rapidly, with organic order growth
exceeding 30% for 3 consecutive quarters and pushing the total backlog to a record $21 bn (up 32%
organically in F3Q26). As these dynamics play out, Ritchie believes FY27 organic revenue growth can
accelerate into the LDD-MT% range, significantly outperforming management's longer-term HSD%
framework and current consensus expectations.
For BURL, Brooke Roach expects the company’s idiosyncratic topline drivers combined with self-help
initiatives to boost margins in the years ahead. Look for continued strong store growth combined with
steady 3%+ same-store-sales growth to drive a consistent 10%+ topline trajectory through 2028. Look also
for continued EBIT margin improvement as the company progresses toward its long-term ~10% operating
margin target, supported by supply chain productivity, occupancy savings and other cost efficiencies.
For HII, GIR add the stock back to the list after taking it off in March and following a period of
underperformance tied to margin disappointment. But Noah Poponak continues to highlight the company’s
unique position in the US Defense sector - HII is a pure US Navy Shipbuilder as the Pentagon and US
government continue to focus on the domestic shipbuilding base. Labor and supply chain investments
from the DoW are now proving out, with Shipbuilding revenues +15-20% for four consecutive quarters
and translating to higher margins, with contract tailwinds as well. Poponak models HSD% shipbuilding
growth, several points of margin expansion, and better free cash generation over the medium term.
Finally, on OXY, Neil Mehta highlights the company is employing advanced recovery techniques to
extend the life of depleting shale oil reserves at the same time as it ramps up its focus on capital efficiency
and deleveraging under new leadership that arrived in March. Advanced recovery efforts could add 2
billion barrels of oil equivalent (BBOE) to OXY's reserves. And cost containment initiatives could also
drive as much as $4bn of incremental cash flow by 2030. Look for excess cash to be delivered back to
shareholders as a rising dividend or opportunistic buybacks.

Single Stock Highlight – Neil Mehta is out with 2x rating changes, where he upgrades OXY to Buy on the
view that the market is underestimating a multi-year improvement story, with enhanced recovery & AI-led
operational gains, while also downgrading TOU to Sell as weakening AECO gas fundamentals, elevated
storage and supply growth outpacing demand leave its premium valuation difficult to justify.
OXY upgraded to Buy (PT $69): Neil believes the market is underappreciating the structural improvement
underway at OXY, driven by differentiated enhanced recovery capabilities, AI-driven completion
optimisation, and a >4bn boe advanced recovery opportunity. Strong Permian execution, with Delaware
productivity running ~12% above peers, creates scope for production and FCF to outperform over time.
The upgrade is also driven by a rapidly improving financial profile, as debt has fallen from ~$29bn at its
peak to ~$11.8bn today with a clear path to <$10bn over the next year, while management’s $4bn cash
flow improvement plan by 2030 should lower sustaining capital, lift margins and support materially higher
dividends, buybacks and FCF generation. At ~14% average 2027/28 FCF yield, Neil sees attractive
valuation with significant self-help still to come.
TOU downgraded to Sell (PT $49): Neil has become more cautious on Western Canadian gas
fundamentals as AECO pricing continues to weaken, storage remains elevated and supply growth is
expected to outpace new demand and egress capacity over the next several years. Given TOU’s continued
exposure to AECO and a premium valuation versus gas peers despite a softer macro backdrop, Neil sees
better risk/reward elsewhere in the gas E&P space, particularly AR.
