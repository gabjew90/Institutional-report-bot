 
Higher Yields Keep Traders Paying Up For 
Protection 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, OCT 06, 2026 - 07:40 PM 
Authored by Michael Ball, Bloomberg macro strategist, 
Treasuries are getting some relief today, but elevated rate vol and rich payer skew show the 
market is wary of another move higher in yields. 
 
The dominant theme remains bear steepening despite Tuesday’s pause. Softer payrolls and 
more patient Fed rhetoric have pushed the probability of an October hike below 25%, allowing 
the front end to outperform. 
That has done little for the long end, where a less-urgent Fed doesn’t imply a lower terminal rate 
or ease concerns over fiscal deficits and heavy Treasury supply. 

 
Inflation also helps explain traders’ reluctance to embrace the more-dovish shift. ISM Services 
Prices Paid jumped to 74, the highest since July 2022, while uncertainty around energy costs 
and AI-related capex demand reinforce the possibility that yields will continue to grind higher. 
 
Options markets reflect that uncertainty. 
The MOVE Index is near its highest this year, while one-month implied volatility on TLT, the 
long-duration Treasury ETF, sits around the 98th-100th percentile over the same period -- a 
stark contrast with subdued equity volatility. 
Recent Citi research finds long-expiry payer skew and Treasury put skew rich, showing 
investors are paying an unusually high premium for protection against a further rise in yields. 
The backdrop can become self-reinforcing: rising yields tend to lift long-dated volatility and 
extend mortgage duration, prompting additional hedging that can add further selling pressure. 
The market is technically oversold, yet the catalyst for a durable reversal is unclear. 
Positioning muddies the picture: Deutsche Bank shows CTAs heavily short bonds, while 
JPMorgan’s Treasury clients are net long. 
This week’s 10- and 30-year auctions therefore matter more than normal.  
A clean absorption of supply offers a route toward stabilization; another weak reception would 
validate what options already imply -- yields have yet to hit cycle highs.
