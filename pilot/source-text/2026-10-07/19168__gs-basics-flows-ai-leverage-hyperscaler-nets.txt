Markets: Market’s a little soggy overnight and leadership remains extremely concentrated in Mag 7…
S&P/Nasdaq held up yesterday while Russell struggled. Asia has failed to take the baton despite fresh US
highs… Japan industrials remain firm while financials lag, Korea struggling -2%, and Taiwan flat/HK tech
fading despite fresh US highs. Feels less like generic risk off and more like higher oil + higher real rates
increasingly biting the duration sensitive parts of the tape. Mainland China reopens tomorrow, another
useful test of risk appetite.
Flows: At least in Europe, yesterday felt pretty heavily for sale… long selling plus ETF supply into the
rally. Despite some oil relief and support for OATs/rates, follow through was limited. Perhaps that is
increasingly a function of the real rate/equity valuation differential becoming pretty glaring. Either way,
the flow picture was not particularly constructive, which was interesting given the index move.
AI: Samsung prelim Q3 tomorrow… they are going to be enormous, the question is simply are they
enormous enough? If fundamentals keep delivering, I'd argue they just earn into their already low
multiple. The bigger story overnight is SpaceX looking to raise $40bn to buy Nvidia chips… another
enormous endorsement of compute demand, but more leverage in the AI ecosystem is not exactly what the
market wants to hear right now. Shout out to Mistral, which finally has a genuinely competitive European
open weight model… Europe badly needs a champion. Marvell’s guidance yesterday was another huge
endorsement of the custom silicon/interconnect infrastructure chain, supportive for Broadcom along the
way. TSMC fundamentals remain very strong.
Oil: No resolution… if anything the regional conflict is broadening, with Houthis attacking Aden and
Saudi intercepting another missile near Riyadh, even as physical flows improve materially. Vitol estimates
~12mbd of crude and 2mbd of products have actually left the Middle East over the last 7–10 days, so this
is increasingly a freight/insurance/logistics problem as much as a barrels problem. It feels like maybe Iran
itself is drifting toward frozen conflict… we are late in the electoral cycle and the odds of a durable
solution ahead of the midterms feel pretty slim but it appears they are still talking.
France: Le Pen clearly listened to the bond market. She is now proposing €140bn of net savings by 2032, a
constitutional “golden rule,” deficit <3% by 2030 and a primary balance within 18 months… enough to
take some immediate fiscal tail risk out of OATs, which the market loved as yields fell sharply and spreads
tightened. But the more interesting point to me is her plan to cap France’s net EU contribution at €5bn.
That helps the domestic arithmetic, but creates a different left tail… a future French government explicitly
prioritizing national fiscal consolidation over EU transfers is potentially much more consequential for the
euro than for OATs. So perhaps the trade is cleaner now… less near term France sovereign tail, more mt
European institutional tail.

Rates/Risk: RBI hiked 25bp to 5.5% and shifted to “calibrated tightening”… another datapoint that the
energy shock is forcing a global tightening impulse even as US front end expectations calm down. Fed
minutes later today, October hike odds are now only ~20% versus ~85% for December, yet the 10yr is still
around 5.30%… front end increasingly anchored, long end still refusing to rally. The 3yr was well
absorbed yesterday; 10yr auction today, 30yr tomorrow, an important litmus test given how sensitive the
market remains to duration supply. Gold and crypto softer overnight are another small confirmation that
real rate pressure is still biting.
Risk: Positioning interesting people have added tech and taken Mag 7 nets higher (see below), but overall
nets remain extremely low and sentiment pessimistic. The risks are obvious and well understood. My
problem with bonds/Russell/Europe is still the same… it is hard for them to sustainably work without a
durable energy solution even though positioning is a major tailwind. Constructive AI and think the
leadership is justified by the fundamentals in the inference economy/compute shortage. For the rest we
need rate relief. Watching Fed min/Samsung tomorrow/10yr auction.
