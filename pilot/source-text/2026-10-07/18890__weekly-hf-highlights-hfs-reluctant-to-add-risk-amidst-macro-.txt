This Week’s Highlights (as of Thursday, October 1st)
HFs were reluctant to deploy further risk through this week amidst the macro and geopolitical overhang, leading to a quieter week from a 
volumes and thematic perspective. US L/S fund net leverage remained relatively flat WoW at 52% below 12m medians, while gross leverage 
also ticked marginally higher to 207% - remaining near 12m troughs (2nd %-tile). HFs ended as small net sellers of global equities, with the selling 
spread across N. Am, Europe, and APAC. Oil prices remain very much in focus, with HFs continuing to trim their exposure to the broader US 
Energy space – in fact, the sector has been net sold in 13 of the past 15 sessions after having been bought consistently in July and August. 
Crude Levered equities continue to drive much of the supply in the space, though HFs have also been net sellers of E&Ps – notably, positioning 
between these two groups looks quite different with Crude Levered Equities net exp still quite elevated whereas E&P’s net exp is at ~12M lows.  In 
addition to Energy, HFs were also net sellers of Health Care, much of which was driven by MedTech and Life Science Tools. However, HFs 
continued to trim gross across the crowded Biotech longs (MSXXBIOC) and the crowded shorts (MSXXBIOS) (see attached for more detail on this 
dynamic). TMT flows were more mixed, however, as HFs continued to buy Infrastructure Software (MSXXINSW) and Cybersecurity (MSXXCYBR), 
while they were smaller net sellers of the key AI verticals – Agentic AI (MSXXCLAW) and AI Tech Beneficiaries (MSXXAIB) broadly ended net 
sold. At the factor level, flow was relatively muted, though there continued to be a small bias towards buying Quality and Size, consistent with flow 
over the trailing ~2 months.  Notably, positioning to SMID-caps remains near recent lows given heightened levels of short exposure across all 
HF strategies on the back of the pressure in the rates space.  
Outside N. America, HFs continued to be net sellers of EU equities through this week via short additions – HFs across all regions were net 
sellers, though both US-based HFs and EU-based HFs added to short exposure as opposed to unwinding longs from the region. HF net exposure 
to EU equities remains near record lows driven by EU-based HFs now running along the most overweight US equities relative to EU equities 
over the last 5Y history. While the group has not yet shifted to ‘buyers’ of EU equities, they have begun to sell US longs and have reindexed some 
of the capital into short ideas in Europe (see attached). APAC equities also tilted sold in lighter amounts led by supply in China and India, while 
HFs were smaller buyers of Korea and Taiwan. However, it’s worth noting that HFs did not re-engage in Global Memory (MSXXGMEM) names 
and have continued to be net sellers of the theme. Japan also contributed to the supply observed globally as HFs reduced directional risk across 
the Consumer space in the region, while they were smaller buyers of TMT. 
As for performance, September ended as a particularly challenging month for HFs, with the average Global fund ending down ~60bps, 
leaving them up ~7% for the year.  Equity L/S funds in particular were under the most pressure in September, with L/S funds in the US, Europe, 
and in Asia all down roughly ~1.5%.  Americas-based L/S funds (-1.6% in Sep) are now +8.4% for the year through the end of the 3rd quarter, 
though dispersion remains quite wide with the 75th %-tile find +16% while the 25th %-tile fund is actually down ~2.2%.  In Europe, the average fund 
is now +5.4% YTD while EU-based L/S funds are +3.5%, which compares to the Euro STOXX 600 Index +10.2%.  Asia-based funds (all strats) 
and Asia-based L/S funds are both up ~8% for the year through the end of September.
