JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE
TODAY
With Huang’s return to Nvidia dominating today’s headlines and NVDA adding back 33%, we take a step back
and look at what comes next on the tech calendar. Huang’s reinstatement resolves the immediate question of
who runs Nvidia. For the rally to extend from here, we think investors will need more clarity on the following
catalysts:
•  PUSHBACK FROM LAWMAKERS – Lawmakers in both parties have criticized Huang’s reinstatement
(The Hill, RTRS). The questions now extend to the rules of corporate governments and political
involvement. Comments from Ron Adler (Global Co-head Equity HT Trading) summarized it well:
“Removing the Jensen Huang uncertainty around NVDA is clearly positive. However, the broader
uncertainty around the rules of corporate governance and political involvement just got A LOT worse
(recall it was only ~a year ago where Trump called for Lip-Bu Tan’s resignation, only to renege on that a
few weeks later). Over time, this should weigh on equity multiples, particularly with a Democratic
Congress and the prospect of a Democratic president in 2028.”
•  DEC 3 TESTIMONY – Huang is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee at 10:00 a.m.
ET at a hearing on U.S. export controls.

•  INVESTIGATIONS REMAIN OPEN – Reuters reports that the SEC is examining potential accounting
issues at Nvidia. Separately, California Attorney General Rob Bonta has launched a state-level
investigation into NVDA, including potential criminal violations (BBG).
•  PENDING CHIP-CONTROL BILLS – Beyond Huang’s December 3 testimony, we’ll be watching (i) the
MATCH Act, which would prohibits the sale and servicing of critical chipmaking tools to China, and (ii)
the Remote Access Security Act, which would give Commerce explicit authority to regulate remote access
to controlled AI chips hosted abroad.

◦  It is unclear how much macro matters given Huang is returning to NVDA and everything is surging
higher. The Fed still matters, to an extent, but right now the euphoria surrounding the resumption of
the Tech trade is palpable. The NVDA news sets the stage for a meaningful squeeze higher and
potential re-risking. While the return and Trump statements may not fully assuage concerns on the
acctg investigation & California investigation today’s news appears to de-risk those inquiries.
Perhaps this acts as the clearing event that many hoped would have materialized post-NVDA earnings
and last week’s cap mkt event. The NVDA news is causing an active re-think of our tactical view but
it would appear that in the near-term this bounce has some legs despite the residual concerns on
NVDA, though that may no longer be a 2026 story.
◦  TRADES – AI, Tech, Cyclicals, Beta, Momentum appears to be the best plays vs. shorts/hedges in
Defensives and our Recession basket. Precious metals are flying.

aren’t ready to cut their defensive exposure again. Even as investors re-gross the AI trade, it no longer
feels like the either/or decision between growth and defensives that we felt for most of the year.

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the November 23rd Morning Briefing
•  Last week, we offered our view on the Bull vs. Bear cases, thinking that the Bull Case was more likely;
however, the Bear Case played out in remarkable fashion pushing sentiment toward excessive fears and
markets to oversold conditions.
◦  BULL CASE – (i) NVDA Earnings; (ii) Capital Markets; (iii) AI / Tech Trade Returns; (iv) Macro
Data.
◦  BEAR CASE – (i) AI / Tech Take Another Leg Lower; (ii) A Decline in the Incremental Buyer ; (iii)
Hawkish Fed Narrative; (iv) Positioning Purge.
•  We are moving back to a Tactically Cautious view. In hindsight, it seems obvious that we should have been
on the sidelines but we, similar to the market, had viewed NVDA and capital markets activity as the
necessary support for Tech to form a bottom and thus whiffed on the tactical bullish call. Now, the
uncertainty surrounding NVDA and peers and opacity over 2028 and beyond makes Tech longs highly
uncertain in the very near-term. Given the aggressive nature of the selloff, a tactical bounce seems likely,
but we are not yet of the view that a bottom forms in the very near-term given the recently introduced
uncertainty in Tech fundamentals.
•  Thus, the comparison to 2018 appears to be the best historical comp where the late-year tactical bounce
was faded. We do anticipate a further rotation away from Tech, trades which are getting increasingly
crowded creating another layer of potential pain should a tactical Tech bounce come to fruition. If Tech
continues to falter, it is unclear if the balance of the SPX can rally enough to boost the index materially
higher.
•  It is worth mentioning that the underlying economy remains strong, AI capex has less of a direct impact on
the economy, and the AI risks are more tied to financing and the consumption wealth effect. The earnings

outlook remains positive irrespective of the AI outlook. SPX median earnings for 26Q3 was ~12%, which
is in line with the 10-year average EPS growth of 12.6%, per FactSet. Further, Mislav tells us that median
profit margins made fresh ATHs.
•  We do think a bounce is coming but we are not yet convinced that any bounce can turn into a sustainable
rally given headwinds from AI / Tech, uncertainty over the nascent hiking cycles, and the subsequent
impact on the earnings outlook for FY27 and FY28.
•  As for what would make us flip back to being bullish? We do not have a high conviction answer for that,
but it seems that (i) positioning reset / reversion would be the start; but, we likely also need to (ii) see
support for the US economic growth story proxied by Cyclicals outperforming Defensives or a selloff in
our Recession / Stagflation baskets. Plus, (iii) a reduction in vol. It may be as simple as (iv) investors /
market needs to travel through time to digest the news and going through another earnings season, which
kicks off in mid-Jan. A positive tail-risk would be (v) if the Fed pauses in December to go in Jan / Mar.
•  MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 23) – We think a market-neutral approach is prudent. Within Tech, Mag7
names and Security Software appear to be the safest areas though there is now squeeze risk for Memory /
Semis and would suggest using options for those looking to play a rebound. Assuming we see an
aggressive near-term bounce, longer-temr we think +SPW vs. -SPX makes sense or +SPW vs. -NDX for
those looking for more gearing. Regionally, UK / EU are likely to outperform especially if we see the
Dollar see profit-taking. From a factor perspective, Value will continue to outperform both Beta and
Momentum.
•  OLD MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 16) – The Anti-AI theme / ex-Tech rotation has been dominant in
26H2, even more so over the last month as Energy gains have flipped, the recession narrative has emerged,
and investors have positioned more defensively. We think moving back toward Tech makes sense and like
the cautious approach detailed in the previous Monetization Menu with primary exposure to Mag7 and
then using options to play a rebound in the AI themes. It seems most likely that the market will be patient
with seeing how the catalysts play out rather than getting ahead of those events. Look for earnings, capital
markets, and the JPM APAC Tech Conf to be a positive catalysts. In terms of ex-Tech plays, we prefer
Cyclicals over Defensives as the growth narrative reboots and markets digest an incrementally more
hawkish Fed. Recall, SPX earnings are more tied to nominal GDP growth than to real GDP growth
meaning increases in inflation are not a bad thing for earnings and that profit margins near record levels
suggest that pass-through is not a problem in the near-term. We continue to like Europe for some Cyclical /
Value exposure given relative positioning and valuation, though another surge in the USD may temper
tactical outperformance.
