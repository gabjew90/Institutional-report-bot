#Markets : EStoxx +50bps, bouncing with broader equity gauges after Friday's capitulatory style move into the
close - The S&P (-3.7%) and Nasdaq-100 (-5.6%) closed out their worst week since Mar-20, majorly led by a
fragile AI/Capex narrative while cross asset price action showed symptoms of signalling a broader growth
slowdown. NVDA (-14%) hit an 18-month low on a report of an SEC accounting probe and dragged SOXX
-10%. AAPL (+0.5%) and GOOGL (+0.4%) were rare bright spots, and Staples was the only sector in the
green as Defensive rotation continues. US futures bouncing this morning (Nasdaq +3%, S&P +2%), helped by
Bessent warning that the Fed risks overtightening. Asia is stabilising as authorities step in. Taiex (+2%) has
shrugged off Friday's futures drop Kospi (-3%) is faring better than signalled after Seoul said it stands ready to
deploy stabilisation funds immediately. SK Hynix is in focus on reports that SoftBank's Son weighed a $1tr bid
before shelving the idea. Japan and cash Treasuries are closed for a holiday. The Dollar trades easier while
precious and base metals bounce. The market increasingly is drawing similarities with the market
setup/dynamics of Q4 2018: pronounced market rotation followed by a correction into a hawkish December
Fed. This time the correction is of a similar magnitude and has also come with a Fed seen as hawkish, though
the AI fear is new. Thanksgiving week typically supports markets (S&P +71bps average return over the last
30yrs, 63% hit rate), though in 2018 the S&P finished the same week -3.8% before rallying +28.8% in 2019.
See charts below overlaying performance., courtesy of UBS US Head of Risk Jeffrey Yu. Call for a low
participation/volume recovery in risk this week though the market, particularly the AI Capex story lacks a
definitive short-term catalyst. Beyond a technical rally, I think the Dec Fed meeting is of increasing importance
(93% priced for a 25bp hike) and the market could test the durability of the FOMC’s conviction into the event
given how much of a focus comparisons with 2018 are now becoming. Waller has clearly been the dissenter,
so it will be interesting to see any increase/decrease in his vocality here – Fed blackout period 28 Nov – 10
Dec.

#WeekAhead : A holiday-shortened US week puts most of the macro risk into Wednesday, when October
PCE lands alongside GDP, personal income, durable goods, new home sales, jobless claims and the Beige
Book. That makes it the last major inflation read before the Fed's December decision. Monday opens with
flash PMIs across the Europe, UK and the US, with Japan closed and Williams speaking in New York.
Tuesday brings German IFO and US consumer confidence, Macklem in Toronto. Wednesday adds Australia
CPI and German GDP ahead of the US data. On Thursday US markets are shut for Thanksgiving. The ECB is
scheduled to publish its October accounts, and the two-day European Council opens in Brussels to discuss
Ukraine financing and the next ECB president. Friday packs in France and Spain CPI, German unemployment
and Tokyo CPI, then the US early close for Black Friday, with France's sovereign rating review keeping OATs
in focus. Earnings are led by US tech hardware and software (Dell, HP, HPE, Analog Devices, Autodesk,
Workday), plus Deere and Medtronic, with Xiaomi, Meituan and Li Auto in Asia and Compass and Prosus in
Europe.
#Flow: Flows on Friday finished marginally better for sale (54/46) with turnover broadly in line with the HT YTD
average. Healthcare was the busiest sector (20% of turnover) and finished better to buy with demand across
the defensive sector. Industrials were also busy  (12% of turnover) and finished balanced with demand in A&D
and logistics offsetting supply in transport. Technology (11% of turnover) saw supply across semis and
software as the complex sold off while Energy, Consumer Discretionary, Telecoms, Banks, Basic Resources
and Real-Estate also saw supply.
