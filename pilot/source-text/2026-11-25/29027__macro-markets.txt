following one of the most bruising weeks in recent decades, here’s a take on the key market dynamics,
broken into ten short points:
1. for all of the introspection and blame game around what’s been pressuring the tape, let’s identify the
most salient problem: in the wake of an all-time “bolt from the blue,” the biggest stock on planet Earth
shed $2.7tr of market cap over the past six weeks. in fact, most all of the S&P breakdown from the
October 6th highs through November 11th can be blamed on NVDA. while the selloff broadened since
then, I don’t think you can overstate the significance of this singular story.
2. as we approach the mother of all sentiment indicators -- the Thanksgiving dinner table conversation -- it
seems clear that the broad AI narrative has flipped from where it was just a few months ago. one
component of this is an inflection in the growth rate of AI capex. another is a shift in the political winds. a
third is the parabolic increase in cyber-attacks. in the end, our basket of AI leaders is now down on the
year -- if you had forecasted that five months ago, when it was up 53% YTD, you’d have been laughed out
of the room.
3. my point so far in this note: the market has shown its hand regarding its dependency on the AI story.
after nearly four years of uninterrupted advancement -- during which time the AI theme was rocket fuel for
the market and, thus, the wealth effect -- we’ve entered a far more complicated period. in this context, the
past few weeks have been tinged with a growth scare across the asset complex. and, to repeat a line from
last week, I believe the de-rating of the multiple is the stock market signaling this is a massive cyclical
boom that won’t last forever.
4. this selloff has also been paved with very significant risk reduction by the trading community. both
fundamental and systematic funds have been dumping exposure, and lower prices have only drawn out
more supply. this has all taken place in a moment when option market makers have been offsides (both
gamma and then vol). with a nod back to recent points on changes to industry structure, the market has
clearly struggled to absorb this risk transfer.
5. the Fed is not the primary driver of the correction. if anything, the front end rallied noticeably hard last
week. with that said, to be in the midst of a tightening cycle right now certainly doesn’t help. while I have
confidence that the economy is more durable today than it was between the GFC and COVID, you could
generally count on the cavalry to arrive back then -- the inconvenient truth today is the Fed put isn’t in
play.
6. what are the signposts for a tactical bottom? while I worry that most market actors are still carrying
plenty of risk, particularly households -- e.g. there’s been $150bn of inflows to US equity mutual funds
and ETFs over the past month -- the hedge fund de-risking since early October has been huge. this also
stuck out from the weekend reading: the most recent wave of hedge fund selling has been marked by very

large short sales, particularly in single names, and the CTA community is now meaningfully short index
futures.
7. for the first time this spell, Friday brought elements of panic. markers include deep inversion of the
S&P vol curve, the highest reading for VXN since the teeth of COVID and a surge in ETF volumes. for
texture, these two notes from my former summer interns are superb. I’d also add that client sentiment in
my travels has gotten exceedingly negative. all said another way: there’s a lot of fear in the market today,
considerably more than at any other point over recent months.
8. I tend to avoid talking about valuation in the middle of a tornado, but we’ve just experienced one of the
most pronounced de-ratings of the past decade. for good order’s sake, S&P began the year on a 22x P/E,
and today it’s marked at 16x, which places it below the average level of the past three decades. also note
that NDX is trading at the lowest multiple in the entire COVID / post-COVID era.
9. for a formal take on things, Ben Snider and our US Portfolio Strategy team identify their three
conditions to support an S&P 500 bottom (earnings, the macro backdrop, positioning).
10. at the end of this exercise, I’ll put myself on the dart board: while the tails still feel admittedly fat, my
instinct is the high in volatility has been seen for this episode.
