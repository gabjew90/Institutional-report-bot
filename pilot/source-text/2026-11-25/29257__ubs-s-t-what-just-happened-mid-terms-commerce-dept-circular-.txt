What Just Happened? Mid-terms,
Commerce Dept, Circular Financing,
Unfortunate Set-Up
Simon Penn
23-Nov-2026, 01:47 ET (EST)
The S&P dropped 8.6% last week for a loss of around 12% from its early October high, thus entering
corrective territory. The market set up was as much to blame for the unwind as anything else. Investors of all
types have found the last few months challenging. From changing Fed guidance, unexpected reversals in
Brazilian politics and the extent of the Democrat’s mid-term election victory. In the background has been the
war in Iran and the months-long record dispersion in equity – a seemingly calm VIX completely
mis-representing the churning within.
As P&Ls were repeatedly challenged, selling begat selling.
Last week brought things to a head.
The markets had been worried enough about tech spending and RoI as it was. Just a week ago a report in
Brookings made headlines by revealing that tech spend for the next five years was estimated at $10.3 tln (and
that accounted for likely delays and cancellations).
On Monday, as President Trump flew to China, the US Commerce Department unveiled tighter restrictions on
the export of advanced chips. The rule closed a loop hole that Nvidia had used to supply offshore data
centres.  That ruling crystallised long held fears - AI capex, increasing funded by leverage, was going to be
increasing difficult to payback in an increasingly aggressive regulatory and geopolitical environment.
The Nasdaq 100 posted its worst week since March 2020, losing 10.4% over the five sessions. The
Philadelphia Semiconductor Index was the epicentre: it fell 10.7% on Friday alone, erasing $1.3 trillion in
market capitalisation in a single session, and ended the week down 17.1% — its worst weekly performance in
25 years.
The UBS Hyperscaler Basket (UBXXHYPE) fell 10.3% on the week, confirming that the selloff was not
confined to semiconductors but extended to the entire AI infrastructure complex.
Compounding the AI-specific distress was a hawkish macro backdrop. The Federal Reserve's October
meeting minutes, released mid-week, showed near-unanimous support among officials for further rate
increases. Discussion the week before and also in the minutes that a 50bp hike was worth discussing, didn’t
help.

US credit conditions have tightened for vulnerable sectors. US CCC spreads have widened for six
consecutive months, the longest run in 32 years outside a recession or acute crisis. CCC spreads are
1,109bp, 47bp wider over the last month, after +149bp in September and +109bp in October. Stress is
concentrating on the weakest borrowers. Four forces are at work. First, rates: the 10-year Treasury yield has
risen 127bp since February to 5.09%, while median CCC interest coverage has collapsed from 0.73x in 2023
to 0.43x. The median CCC issuer generates less than half the earnings needed to meet its interest bill.
Second, competition for capital: US HY issuance is heading for $410bn in 2026, total US corporate issuance is
$2.79trn and the four largest technology companies have financing plans approaching $1trn next year. Third,
refinancing: only $57bn of HY debt matures in 2027, rising to $146bn in 2028 and $282bn in 2029. CCC
maturities jump from $4bn to $13bn between 2027 and 2028. Finally, CCC issuance has collapsed from $61bn
across 78 deals in 2024 to $19bn across 32 deals this year. Distressed exchanges, maturity extensions, and
liability-management exercises increasingly become the alternatives.
Defaults tell a more nuanced story than CCC spreads. Private-credit defaults have risen every quarter
since Q3 2025 to a record 6.3%. With 21% of US private-credit debt maturing over the next two years, UBS
expects defaults to approach 7% by end-2027. Public credit looks considerably healthier: UBS expects US HY
defaults around 1.5% in 2027 and leveraged loans around 2.5%. Higher rates are exposing weaker borrowers,

4
and the market is reflecting that distinction. BB spreads are 195bp, 48bp wider from the August low of 147bp,
while US HY is 337bp versus IG at just 91bp, not distressed, but no longer complacent either. If BB widening
stalls while CCC distress continues, the story will remain dispersion rather than contagion. If BB follows CCC
wider while IG stays anchored, the distinction between weak and merely lower-quality credit will blur. There is
also less scope for a policy rescue. In 2015-16, credit deterioration was eventually answered by easier
monetary policy. Today, inflation is around 3.5%, the 10-year Treasury is at 5.09%, and policymakers are
discussing further hikes. The paradox is that the more concentrated stress remains in CCCs, private credit,
and leveraged loans, the easier it is for the Fed to ignore.
No clearing event, hedge funds stay defensive. Neither NVDA earnings nor recent tech news proved
sufficient to reset risk appetite. Hedge-fund activity across the UBS Prime Brokerage book remained decisively
defensive in both the US and Europe, with Health Care/Pharma the most net bought across both regions. In
the US, selling was concentrated in high-momentum Semis and Hyperscalers, while in Europe it focused on
Industrials, both risk-off. US gross exposure was broadly unchanged, while European funds added +1% to
GMV as Health Care/Pharma long buying outweighed Industrials selling. More broadly, Prime Brokerage
positioning and client conversations suggest little appetite to re-gross into year-end, particularly given tough
performance. UBS Cash Trading desks globally and the Market Structure Strategy team are seeing similarly
limited buying appetite from retail, CTAs and Long-Onlys at current levels, a notable contrast with the summer,
when Long-Only buying helped catalyze and sustain the August rebound.
• Software vs. Semis. Net exposure to Software has now surpassed Semis for the first time this year and
returned to the 2024/25 average. This comes as the Semis/Software correlation has flipped firmly positive this
month after being strongly negative for most of the year. Software flows were muted this week, but hedge
funds continued to net sell Semis (-0.4std).
• Systematic exposures are washed out except Risk Parity. VIX Cross-Asset Rank hit 100% intraday and
closed at 99%, the highest since April 4 and 8, 2025 (Tariff). CTA Global Equity Exposure is -8%, at the 19th
percentile versus five years. Risk Control Exposure is 57%, at the 24th percentile versus five years, while Risk
Parity Equity Exposure is 26%, at the 64th percentile. UBS RMM clients bought the dip on six of eight down
days. Foreign-listed US ETF inflows remain strong, with buying every day since October 13 and flows at +2.9
standard deviations.
VIX above 30v shifts the options discussion. The equity correlation spike has finally taken VIX above 30v,
meaning investors need to consider part-funded option structures for both hedges and upside chases. Earlier
this week, client discussions centered on low-risk, high-convexity ways to chase the market higher into
year-end once this week’s catalysts had passed. That optimism has tailed off as the week progressed, with
discussions increasingly focused on attractive downside hedges, favoring volatility knock-out (VKO) puts given
the spike in implieds, or convex crash puts on lower-vol indices such as SMI or UKX. For those still looking to
add right-tail exposure to an AI-led bounce, with Nasdaq down more than 17% from the recent spot high,
upside vols are arguably a little too high to buy outright. QQQ 31Dec26 680 / 107% calls cost 8.5 / 1.3% indic
(25d, 28.4v). Investors can halve this outlay by selling a 630-620 put spread on ~34v, risking just a 1.5%-wide
slice of additional downside. The overall outlay could be reduced to zero by selling a similar strike/maturity call
on Russell, in line with Keith’s RV call on NDX>RTY into 2027. Alternatively, UBS’s basket of heavily shorted
stocks has underperformed significantly, down nearly 50% since June and nearly 20% this month alone, with
positioning potentially a tailwind in any rebound. Investors can buy 2m calls or call spreads on UBXXOSHR, a
liquidity- and volatility-optimized version of UBXXSHRT.
USD rally may pause, longer-term path higher. Over the last ten trading days, the USD rally has extended
sharply against G10 peers, with the median G10 peer down 260bp, ranking below the bottom 10th percentile
over the past decade. However, positioning in USD longs remains light. UBS’ proprietary FX Flow Monitor
shows hedge fund and asset manager ten-day cumulative flows at +0.7σ and +0.3σ, respectively, while
option-implied positioning based on risk reversals still favors calls but remains below the five-year average and
has not moved in tandem with spot. The initial USD rally was fueled by inflation and reinforced by Fedspeak,
but markets now require further validation from data for another leg higher. The December meeting is fully

priced at ~25bp, while the one-year, one-year forward has moved from its local peak of ~4.80% to 4.50%,
reflecting only 50bp of implied hikes. Over the coming weeks, 3Q26 GDP, November ISM readings, and the
industrial sector could reaccelerate rates volatility if underlying growth remains robust. UBS FX Strategy’s view
is that the USD rally still has legs, with updated forecasts and more detail on the 2027 outlook here.
China equities: clients are asking, “what’s the catalyst?” Client sentiment on China equities has been
increasingly challenged. The Knowledge Network Asia Derivatives Strategy team has been meeting clients in
the US and China over the past two weeks, with a significant focus on China markets. There was broad
agreement that equities have come under significant selling pressure and are now trading at levels that
historically would represent an attractive buying opportunity, but many struggled to see what would turn the
market around. The most consistent question was: “What’s the catalyst?” Expectations for stimulus are low,
while the concentration of earnings growth around AI is viewed as unhelpful given deteriorating global
sentiment around the theme. Uncertainty over the impact of US chip restrictions was also an emerging talking
point in US discussions this week. At the same time, most clients agreed that CSI option structures offer an
attractive, low-premium way to position for a potential rebound and were surprised that outperformance rates
have remained elevated, although many wanted a clearer view on a potential catalyst before pulling the
trigger.
Taiwan equities fall 20% in November. Taiwanese equities have fallen 20% so far in November. This has
exceeded the worst monthly returns even during the GFC, despite 2027 consensus earnings rising 7% since
October. While US hyperscalers went sideways through the summer and the APAC memory trade came
unstuck from June, Taiwanese equities made new highs into late September. This created a vulnerability as
one of the last remaining tech areas with large profits as the AI trade came unstuck. It wasn’t helped by
elevated retail margin and positioning. The November unwind has eviscerated the sense of being a tech safe
haven and brought the longer-term relative performance of Taiwan and the UBS Hyperscaler basket back in
line: both have now delivered exactly the same return since the start of the AI trade in November 2022. Put
differently, the market is essentially saying there is no difference between being the spender-on or supplier-of
AI capex. If UBS Tech Research’s views hold, that AI spending will continue and the APAC/Taiwan supply
chain remains tight, short-term liquidity concerns aside, this brutal November creates an opportunity for
Taiwan equities.
French fiscal metrics in reverse. Finance Minister Lescure’s 2027 budget was based on GDP growth of
1.0%, but he warned this week that growth is unlikely to reach that pace. The European Commission forecasts
just 0.6% for France next year. The Senate and National Assembly are also at loggerheads over the planned
2027 social-security budget after the Senate put the rejected 2023 pension reforms back into the plan. Neither
the left nor right in the Assembly will accept them. Rassemblement National (RN) leader Le Pen has proposed
cutting the retirement age to 60-62 at a cost of more than EUR18bn, arguing that over time the system could
be funded by a near-EUR1trn national pension investment scheme. That would be years in the making. With
its lower growth forecast, the Commission sees the French deficit at 5.4% in a year’s time, from 5.5% now,
versus the government’s 5.0% target. The German/French 10-year yield spread is all but unchanged this week
at 150bp. Clients remain convinced it will move much wider: 88% of respondents in an audience poll at last
week’s UBS European Conference thought the 185bp spread seen in mid-October would not prove to be the
high.
Gold remains vulnerable to further near-term downside. Gold’s 31% drawdown is already comparable with
previous major corrections, but momentum and flows remain weak. The break below 3,800 failed to attract
fast-money buyers, while all 12 CTA strategies are now short for the first time since September 2022, leaving
scope for further selling toward 3,600. This also looks like gold weakness rather than simply USD strength:
XAU is down around 3% WTD against EUR, GBP, CHF, and JPY as well as USD. Physical demand has yet to
provide a floor, although longer-term economic or financial stress could bring gold’s defensive characteristics
back into focus. Read The Bullion Report.
