Sales & Trading Commentary For Institutional Use Only Not a Product of Research
Macro and derivs trades over the last week:
• US institutional flows remained dominated by selling, with Semis, Neoclouds and crowded
AI/momentum exposures the main sources of funds. Midweek saw selective LO dip-buying and HF
buying/covering, but Thursday’s bounce was sold as investors returned to de-risking and adding shorts.
Buying was selective rather than a broad re-grossing, with demand for Pharma/Biotech on PB and
pockets of buying in Cyber, Consumer and non-AI Industrials. Retail also remained net sellers of single
stocks, although ETF demand held up better in QQQ and Healthcare.
• US options flows included sizable tactical upside demand, consistent with positioning for a potential
squeeze. QQQ upside was added through December call condors and January outright calls, alongside SPX
call spreads. This coexisted with SPY downside hedging and some closing sales of upside structures.
• Korea and Taiwan remained focal points of Asia AI de-risking. Korea saw KRW5.2tn of foreign selling
over the week, partly absorbed by retail buying, while Taiwan’s weekly visible pad was 65% seller-skewed.
The derivatives desk saw protection buying in Korea early in the week, while Taiwan protection was unwound
towards the end of the week.
• Japan cash activity was quieter and moderately seller-skewed (56% better to sell), while derivatives were
more two-way. Early-week protection buying gave way to downside unwinds and NKY upside demand
midweek, followed by put rolls, volatility selling and overwriting into Friday’s weakness.
• HK/CN cash flows turned seller-skewed, with Internet and Metals/Miners under pressure, while domestic
semiconductor substitution remained a pocket of buying. By contrast, the derivatives desk continued to see
CSI300/CSI1000 upside demand. Feedback from US client meetings continues to point to these structures as
a relatively cheap way to express China upside.
• In FX, AUD selling remained the clearer conviction trade: HFs sold AUD on all ten trading days in the
Flow Monitor’s rolling window, with NOK selling also accelerating. Broad USD buying was more modest
despite the dollar rally, while the options desk saw interest in AUDUSD downside structures over the past
week.
• The gold selloff saw neither broad holder capitulation nor much demand from marginal buyers.
Executed physical demand remained subdued and ETF holdings were relatively resilient, alongside
downside-protection demand and short CTA positioning. Friday saw options hedge close-outs and some
topside structures at lower spot levels.
