•
Equity markets sold off last week on the back of AI concerns. Nvidia slumped despite strong 
earnings after fresh reports of an accounting probe by the SEC, while Anthropic’s record IPO gave 
back much of its debut gains. Fed minutes showed almost all officials expect to hike in December. 
Mixed data releases in the US with retail sales holding up while manufacturing missed 
expectations. Elsewhere, Eurozone inflation is at a three-year high, with UK inflation higher. 
Japan’s Q3 GDP slipped on weak consumer data while China October activity data came in weaker 
than expected.
•
Major global equity markets sold off last week while bond yields rallied. Oil prices further eased, 
with the dollar ending the week higher.
•
The sharpness of gold’s sell-off warrants a deeper dive (p2). Suffice to say, the technical picture 
suggests a bounce could be on the cards (C.O.T.W), and we’re seeing the early innings this 
morning. But how long it lasts is anyone’s guess. Further pressure on leveraged equity/credit 
positions should the AI stock rout continue, a further rotation into bonds, hawkish Fed minutes, a 
potential upside PCE surprise on Wednesday and the absence, so far, of a clear positioning flush-
out leave risks to gold roughly balanced over the coming days.

All about gold..
3
A tumultuous week saw the XAU gold price fall -8.9% to Friday’s New York close. AI/tech valuation 
concerns triggered broader de-leveraging across markets. With the Fed’s Waller still ringing in its 
ears, and being a highly liquid asset, gold appears to have been caught in that risk reduction. 
What the data tells us:
•
Gold ETFs: November selling had been led by Europe (-16t), but last week North America 
dominated (-12t). However, the outflows from ‘sticky’ gold ETFs have been modest (Chart 1). 
•
Trading hours: US hours contributed around -4.5% and Asian hours -3.5% (p5)
•
COMEX positioning: managed money, other reportables and non-reportables fell a combined -
75t by Tuesday 17 November (p18). Open interest then fell another -35t by Friday, although 
possible changes in spreading positions complicate the attribution of that drop.
•
China: Shanghai Futures Exchange (SHFE) net positioning fell -11t last week. Not dramatic.
•
Options: 25-delta risk reversals fell across 1m, 3m and 6m maturities, signalling increased 
demand for downside protection (p24). But the moves look orderly.
•
Sentiment: During the week, Polymarket odds of gold reaching US$3,500 in November 
jumped from ~10% on 16 November to 85%; But as of this morning, a bounce to US$3,700 is 
seen 60% vs a fall to US$3,500 at 35%*
•
Drivers: US and European 10-year yields fell between 5–20bp, commodities dropped -4%. The 
US dollar indices DXY and equal-weighted BBDXY climbed, but very modestly (c.+0.5%).
What’s next…
•
Commodities positioning looks stretched and a bounce could help gold (Chart 2)
•
Fewer rate hikes are now priced in for 2027. 13 November had a peak implied Fed Funds rate 
of 4.7%. As of Friday, the implied rate had dropped to 4.5%**
•
Our caution is that gold sentiment is weak but not extreme and we don’t view the equity sell-
off as sufficient to change the Fed’s course, as per the recent Fed minutes.
Bottom line: the evidence suggests a broad reduction in risk exposure, dragging liquid assets 
with it. While a decent chance of a bounce lies ahead, we urge some caution for now

The decline in Gold accelerated sharply last week with the market breaking key Fibonacci support at US$3,858/oz as suspected - 
the 38.2% retracement of the entire 2015 – 2026 rise to leave the market already testing next key flagged price support from the 
top of the April/August 2025 range at US$3,500/oz, which represents a fall of 37% from the peak seen at the beginning of this 
year. Whilst our bias is to look for some near-term stabilisation here following such a fall, our broader concern remains negative 
with the USD having in our view completed a major base to warn the core trend has turned higher (see appendix) which we see as 
a major headwind for gold. Additionally, although positioning has fallen, we do not yet think we have yet seen a “washout”.

to look for a potential floor here. Should weakness extend, we would see support next at US$3,249/oz.
Resistance is seen at US$3,595/oz, with the immediate trend seen staying lower whilst below the 13-day exponential average and 
late October low at US$3,835/oz – US$3,870/oz. Above US$4,149/oz though is seen needed to turn the immediate trend neutral.

Gold Drivers - The USD maintains a large base to 
suggest the core trend has turned higher
12
Another strong week for the USD as the Bloomberg DXY (BBDXY) (which has a lower EUR weighting than the “classic” DXY) reinforces its break above important resistance from the top of the 
range from last summer, 38.2% Fibonacci retracement of the 2025/2026 fall and long-term 200-week average at 1229/1233 which in our view marks the completion of a large base to warn 
of a significant change of trend higher for the USD. Resistance is seen next at the 50% retracement of the 2025/2026 fall at 1249 and whilst we would look for this latter level to cap at first, 
we would see no technical reason not to look for a move above here also in due course, with resistance then seen next at 1261. The immediate trend for the USD seen staying higher whilst 
above the low of last week and 13-day exponential average at 1234. Only below the 1217 early November low though would be seen to neutralise the base.
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council

Gold Drivers –10yr US Real Yields see a pullback as 
broader “risk off” takes hold
13
10yr US Real (and bond) yields fell last week after being capped ahead of their 2.975% y-t-d high as equity markets fall sharply which has resulted in a broader cross-asset “risk off” and a 
flight to safety and a move into bonds. With equity markets still likely seen under pressure this suggests to us the near-term risk for yields is for a continuation of the tone seen last week and 
a further decline. Support is seen next at the 55-day average at 2.72/2.71% and with yield support seen not far below at 2.68% our bias would be to look for a potential hold here. A break 
would be seen to expose trend support from May, currently placed at 2.62%. Big picture though with a large “triangle” continuation pattern still seen in place our bias would be to view such a 
phase of weakness/consolidation as temporary ahead of the broader yield uptrend eventually re-establishing itself. 
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council

The sell-off in US equity markets and indeed global equity markets (see overleaf) has accelerated with the Nasdaq 100 now in what is typically seen as bear market territory – 20% below 
its peak – and with the broader S&P 500 now in what is typically seen as corrective territory, with the market now over 10% lower from its October peak. Near-term the S&P 500 has fallen

with support seen next at 6817 ahead of the 38.2% retracement of the 2025/2026 uptrend at 6695. Resistance is seen at 7238 initially, then 7350.

Appendix 3
Glossary of Technical Analysis terms
27

Technical Analysis Glossary
28
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council
Advance/Decline Line 
A popular type of Breadth Indicator (see below) which represents the cumulative number of individual stocks in a broader index that have risen during a session, 
 
against those in the index that have fallen.
Bar chart 
 
A bar chart shows the open, close, low and high of the price of an instrument over a specific time-period. A vertical bar shows the low to high move, with the open 
 
a small horizontal bar to the left of the vertical line and with the close a small horizontal bar to the right.
Bollinger Bands 
Shows bands that represent 2 standard deviations above and below a central move moving average, typically a 20 period average. The bands are expected to 
 
 
typically capture 95% of price action under normal conditions. 
Breath Indicators 
Breath indicators describe a range of indicators that aim to show the internal strength of a specific equity market index (see Advance/Decline line).
Candlestick chart 
A method of representing open/high/low/close data, originally from Japan. The candlestick (or candle) is formed of a rectangle which represents 
 
 
the open to close move, called the real body, with this shaded different colours depending on whether a higher or lower close was seen for the session. The low 
 
and high are shown as vertical lines above and below the real body/rectangle.
Continuation Pattern 
A pattern that indicates a consolidation phase which is a pause within the direction of the current prevailing trend.
Divergence 
 
When two separate measures behave differently. For example, when a new high or low in price is not confirmed/matched by a corresponding new high or low in
 
 
a momentum indicator, hence showing a divergence.
Double Top/Bottom 
A Double Top is a type of Reversal Pattern (see below) formed during an uptrend when two price highs occur at approximately the same level. Completion of the
 
 
pattern is signalled when the “neckline” to the pattern (see below) is broken. A Double Bottom is the exact opposite setup.
Fibonacci retracements 
Horizontal lines that can indicate where support and resistance can potentially be found when a market retraces following a trending move. The percentage 
 
 
value shown is how much of the prior trend the price has retraced. The Fibonacci retracement levels typically shown are 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6%.
Fibonacci projections 
Horizontal lines that can indicate where support and resistance can potentially be found in the direction of the current trend. The percentage values are applied 
 
to the prior trending move, projected off the low/high of the subsequent corrective counter-trend move. The Fibonacci projection levels typically shown are 50%, 
 
61.8%, 100%, 150% and 161.8%.

Technical Analysis Glossary
29
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council
Flag 
 
A Flag pattern in a classic continuation pattern, characterised by a sharp rise or fall (the flagpole) followed by a short-lived counter-trend move (the flag). They are
 
 
expected to be resolved in the direction of the prevailing trend.
Head & Shoulders Top/Bottom A Head & Shoulders price pattern is a classic trend reversal pattern that appears with three peaks, where the outside two are seen closer in height and the
 
 
middle peak is the highest. Completion of the pattern is signalled when the “neckline” to the pattern is broken (see below).
Measured Objective 
Most technical patterns, regardless of whether they are reversal or continuation patterns come with a “measured objective”, which is typically based on the size
 
 
or height of the pattern. The objective is a potential indication of where the price may move to after a pattern has been completed.
Momentum 
 
Momentum is the rate of acceleration or velocity of the underlying instrument/security. It is thus the speed at which the price of the security is changing.
MACD 
 
Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) is a trend-following indicator, often also used as a momentum indicator. It shows the relationship between two
 
 
exponential moving averages of a security’s price, know as the MACD line, with an exponential average then taken off this line (the Signal line).
Moving Average 
A classic statistical moving average of the underlying price data of the security to give a guide to the direction of the prevailing price trend. Different periodicities
 
 
are used to define short-, medium- and long-term trends. Also used to identify potential areas of support and resistance.
Moving Average Envelope 
Shows bands which represent the percentage distance from a selected moving average, which can be used to identify potential support and resistance.
Neckline 
 
A trendline which marks the point where a reversal pattern is confirmed, typically found by connecting the lows/highs of the pattern.
OnBalanceVolume 
A cumulative volume indicator constructed by comparing the amount of volume traded seen on positive sessions to those on negative sessions.
Overbought 
 
An overbought condition occurs when a price rally has extended too far to fast and is seen unlikely to extend further and a pause is likely to be seen.
Oversold 
 
An oversold condition occurs when a price decline has extended too far to fast and is seen unlikely to extend further and a pause is likely to be seen.
Pennant 
 
A Pennant pattern is a type of continuation price pattern, formed when there is a sharp rise or fall (the flagpole), followed by a short consolidation period within
 
 
converging trend lines, similar in shape to a small triangle (the pennant). They are expected to be resolved in the direction of the prevailing trend.
