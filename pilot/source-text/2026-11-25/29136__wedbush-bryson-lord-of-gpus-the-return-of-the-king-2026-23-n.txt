Nvidia  (NVDA)
Lord of GPUs: The Return of the King (2026)
  
This morning, Nvidia announced the Board of Directors has voted unanimously
to reinstate Jensen Huang. The Board noted: 1) it is satisfied with Mr. Huang’s
candor and ability to lead NVDA (following a series of meetings) and 2) input from
senior levels of the United States government leads the Board to believe Jensen's
reinstatement poses no national security risk.
We would also note several other factors that may have influenced the board in
making its decision including:
●
US Administration preferences - Over the weekend, we saw not only President
Trump advocate for Jensen's return, but also Scott Bessent (one of the more
vocal administration critics of both Jensen and NVDA) note that Jensen Huang
was now an "outsider" and as such met his prior preferred criteria.
●
Arm CEO Rene Haas (the most likely external candidate in our view) committed
to Arm, announcing a new contract that extends his time with Arm through
2033 as of this morning.
This announcement should be viewed as positive for NVDA (and the broader
AI ecosystem in our view) as the decision likely results in: 1) Jensen's personal
importance to NVDA (whether in dictating technology direction, ensuring supply
chain support, etc.), a continuation of many of NVDA's policies (e.g. startup and
data center financing, supply chain support and sourcing agreements, etc.) as well
as 2) the continuation of NVDA as a strong proponent for the AI industry with access
to the highest levels of government to voice opinions that should presumably
benefit the entirety of the AI industry.
While we do see some uncertainty around how Jensen's reinstatement might
impact internal dynamics, we tend to believe the longstanding relationships
between many of NVDA's execs should help to recreate a positive company
dynamic. The question now, in our view, is how much US government decisions/
policy might shift in conjunction with this announcement:
●
Will the US minimize its apparent investigation into NVDA's accounting
practices, which could still affect NVDA AI investments and supplier/customer
relationships depending on the focus of the inquiry (circular investments and/
or illicit shipments of GPUs)?
●
Could we see the US moderate its new regulations around Asian chip exports
and restrictions on Chinese companies using data centers outside of China?
We believe concerns around both these developments have weighed on investor
sentiment the last week given both threaten broader AI ecosystem ramifications
should AI demand falter (whether due to capital constraints or US restrictions) and
supply outpace end markets. Any more mild resolution in either case (which we
see as more probable with the return of Jensen) should be viewed as favorable for
markets. Finally, if concerns around the AI trade moderate from here, we would
expect commodities (memory, HDDs, optics, etc.) should be among the greatest
winners (just as they were substantial underperformers as AI concerns proliferated)
given the increased probability forward pricing and margins hold combined with
valuations that have moved well below typical peak cycle levels.

Net, all of our conversations through last week (ex eSSDs and data center financing at the margins)
have generally suggested that the broader AI industry is still operating in a business as usual fashion
with supply (rather than demand) being the primary constraint. At the same time, for the past few
months, there has been an underlying fear that this situation could potentially be derailed (despite
no signs of demand deterioration) if availability of financing and/or US policy shifted in ways that
curtailed growth. And we see US policy as the greater risk, in light of the significant demand for
data center resources (as suggested by continued upward moves in GPU rental pricing). As such,
we view the administration's apparent support of Jensen as shifting the forward risk profile around
government policy in a favorable manner. As such, we remain constructive on NVDA.

FY28 EPS estimate of $16.59 (from $15.53) plus net cash of $2.70/share.

Company Description
NVDA is a fabless semiconductor company that designs and sells GPUs for gaming, data center, and other markets.
Risks to the Attainment of Our Price Target and Rating:
Potential acquisitions; Data Center customer investment softens; AI demand deteriorates; government regulations weigh on the growth
of AI; gaming demand remains weak; competitors (e.g., AMD, INTC etc.) are successful in taking share in AI silicon; customers shift to
internally developed products; tensions between the West and China grow culminating in NVDA losing share in that market and/or
disruption to NVDA's supply chain; NVDA misexecutes on future product development or roll-outs.
