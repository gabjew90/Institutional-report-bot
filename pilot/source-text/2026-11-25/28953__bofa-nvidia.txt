SEC probe compounds 10-Q delay, leadership uncertainty 
Media reports suggest NVDA is under SEC investigation related to its accounting 
practices, with no details yet on the practices, transactions or periods under review. 
Alongside the delayed Form 10-Q and CEO transition, this adds another layer of 
uncertainty with investor confidence already fragile. Without further disclosure, we think 
it is premature to draw conclusions on scope or financial impact. Investors will likely 
scrutinize NVDA’s exposure to emerging/neocloud customers (~25-30% of sales, our 
est.) and direct/indirect China demand (another ~10-15%), together ~40-45% of 
revenue. However, scrutiny is not equivalent to revenue impairment. Even if 25-50% of 
this revenue slowed, NVDA would still generate $10-11 in pf-EPS (vs. our $15 NTM), or 
~13x PE vs. ~30x historically. We await clarification from the company and the SEC, but 
maintain Buy on the valuation cushion in our forecasts. We expect volatility to stay 
elevated until there is greater clarity on the SEC inquiry, the 10-Q, and CY27 cloud capex 
plans, likely from early next year. 
Where investors are likely to focus: neoclouds and China 
NVDA’s Data Center business serves hyperscalers and AI cloud/enterprise/industrial 
(ACIE) customers. Over the last twelve months, hyperscalers were ~54% of total revenue 
and AI cloud/enterprise/industrial ~39%, with neoclouds a meaningful portion of the 
latter (~25-30% of total sales). Direct China Data Center revenue is de minimis (<2% last 
quarter, effectively zero in our forecasts), but China retains compute access through 
cloud infrastructure legally operated outside the US. Of the ~30% of revenue shipped 
overseas, we estimate perhaps half could be accessed by China-based users, putting 
combined direct/indirect China exposure at ~15%. This is not unique to NVDA: INTC, 
AMD, AVGO and QCOM also serve China directly or via global cloud customers under 
prevailing BIS/export-control rules. The key question is less whether these end markets 
exist than whether revenue recognition, customer economics, financing arrangements 
and end-user exposure withstand heightened regulatory scrutiny. 
Neocloud commitments are not the same as revenue loss 
Neoclouds play a real economic role in the AI ecosystem, providing incremental compute 
capacity when hyperscalers are constrained by land, power, shells, or networking. NVDA 
has supported this ecosystem through direct equity investments, GPU-backed financing 
(accelerators as collateral), capacity arrangements, and efforts to attract third-party 
capital. As of FQ3’27, NVDA disclosed ~$32bn of agreements extending across FY28-32 
(<1% of cumulative revenue over that period, our est.), plus ~$3.5bn of land/power/shell 
guarantees. Management has emphasized that relevant commitments and guarantees 
become operative only when counterparties secure financing to purchase DC products 
and associated compute capacity is deployed. NVDA has separately disclosed ~$105bn 
of credit support associated with land and power development in Ohio, available across 
customers rather than exclusively to neoclouds. The distinction matters: these structures 
warrant examination, particularly around counterparty risk and the economic linkage 
between financing and product demand, but headline commitment values should not 
automatically be treated as financed revenue or losses to NVDA.

Price objective basis & risk 
NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) 
Our $280 PO is based on 16x CY27E PE ex cash, within NVDA's historical 15x-56x 
forward year PE range, which we believe is justified by NVDA's leading share in fast-
growing AI compute/networking markets, offset by lumpiness in global AI projects, 
cyclical gaming market, and concerns around access to power. 
 
Downside risks are: 1) Potential for enhanced reputational and financial risk and 
leadership transition due to ongoing US government scrutiny around Nvidia CEO, 2) 
Media reports suggest SEC probe of NVDA accounting practices that could produce 
unexpected scrutiny or impact to reported sales and other financial statements, 3) 
Competition with major public firms, internal cloud projects and other private companies 
in AI and accelerated computing markets, 4) Larger than expected impact from 
restrictions on compute shipments to China, or additional restrictions placed on activity 
in the region, 5) Lumpy and unpredictable sales in new enterprise, data center, and autos 
markets, 6) Potential for decelerating capital returns, and 6) Enhanced government 
scrutiny of NVDA's dominant market position in AI chips.
