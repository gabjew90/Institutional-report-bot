WEEKLY MARKET COMMENTARY 
VIX® Jumps to 1-Year High, but No Panic as AI Trade Unwinds  
 
Cross-Asset Volatility: Implied volatilities jumped across 
asset classes last week as risk assets sold off, led by 
equities. The VIX® Index gained over 16 pts wk/wk to a 1-
year high of 36%, though as we show below, most of the 
move was driven by idiosyncratic risk rather than macro risk. 
This can also be seen in Exhibit 1, which compares cross-
asset volatility based on their z-scores. While implied vols 
rose across every asset class, equity vol (red line) far 
outpaced the rest, suggesting the rise in equity risk was more 
a story of equity positioning than broad macro de-risking. 
Bonds rallied as investors sought safety, while interest rate 
volatility (MOVE Index) gained another 7nms to 106 bps vol. 
Credit spreads continued to widen, driving credit volatility 
higher, with the VIXIG Index up 12pts to 38bps vol, though 
still far below its March highs. Commodities sold off broadly, 
with gold down over 9% while oil prices fell to near a 3-month 
low. Gold implied volatility jumped higher, up 8.5 pts wk/wk 
to 32%, and continuing to screen as the richest cross-asset 
vol, trading almost 3 std dev above average (see Exhibit 1).  
Equity Volatility: Friday’s VIX move, while large, was 
surprisingly orderly. The sell-off in SPX® spot (-3.7%) and the 
increase in fixed-strike vols (+3.2 pts) accounted for almost 
90% of the 7.4pt VIX increase. Very little came from demand 
for downside puts. In fact, changes in skew and convexity 
contributed just 0.9pt to the VIX move (see Exhibit 2). In 
contrast, demand for downside protection made up almost 
half of the Nov 5th VIX spike. This can also be seen in 
measures of SPX 1M skew (25-delta ratio), which spiked to 
the 95th percentile high after the Nov 5th sell-off but only 
steepened modestly to the 80th percentile last Friday. Also 
notable was the lack of demand for upside calls on Friday, 
with call skew falling and call convexity unchanged. It seems 
that while there was little panic on Friday, investors were also 
not positioning for a quick rebound.  
Friday’s move was about idiosyncratic risk, not macro: 
Single stock vol outpaced the increase in index vol on Friday, 
with the VIXEQSM Index jumping over 11 pts to a 1-year high 
of 58% (vs. VIX Index up 7pts). In other words, expected 
dispersion increased even as the market sold off almost 4%, 
as can be seen in the DSPXSM Index rising 5 pts on Friday 
(see Exhibit 3). This comes as investors rotate out of the AI 
theme into other sectors, rather than selling everything 
indiscriminately. As tech stocks fall out of favor, Tech vols 
have risen the most, with QQQ 1M implied vol jumping over 
20 pts last week, the most of any global index. In contrast, 
US small cap volatility, which has very little exposure to the 
AI theme, saw its volatility trail that of its large-cap 
counterparts. The RTY-SPX 1M implied vol spread narrowed 
to a 6-year low of 1.4% as a result.

How to read this chart:
The chart shows how various asset class implied 
volatilities are trading relative to their own history, using 
a 10-year lookback. 
The volatilities are ranked by z-scores, which compares 
the current volatility level for an asset versus its own 10-
year history. A z-score of +2, for example, would mean 
that the current volatility level is trading 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average (i.e. rich) while a 
z-score of -2 would mean it’s trading 2 standard 
deviations below average (i.e. cheap)
Because each asset class volatility trades at different 
levels and often defined differently (e.g. lognormal vs. 
normal vol, price vs. bps vol), by standardizing using z-
scores, we’re able to compare different asset class 
volatilities on the same scale - and see at a glance if 
there are any divergences or dislocations in the cross-
asset volatility markets
