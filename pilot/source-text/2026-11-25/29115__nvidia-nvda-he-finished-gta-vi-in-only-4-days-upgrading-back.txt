This morning the NVIDIA Board of Directors reinstated Jensen Huang as President
CEO, and board member of the company, effective immediately following a
unanimous vote. Harvey Jones will remain Chairman while the board continues its internal
investigations into Mr. Huang’s recent conduct; however they believe his reinstatement is
“in the best interests of stockholders, employees, partners, and customers.”
Government AI backstop? The BoD indicated they had “received input from senior levels
of the United States government” giving them confidence that the reinstatement poses no
national security risk, and a follow-on Trump Truth suggested “the U.S. Government does
not possess evidence that [Jensen] has engaged in any unlawful conduct”
We downgraded NVIDIA's stock to Market Perform 11 days ago when it looked likely
that his conduct would see his removal from the company (a fear that was realized
the next day), and cut our target price in half. Since then, the stock fell almost 30% (closing
at ~$125 on Friday); as of this writing it feels set to bring it back to that point today. As we
remain positive on AI buildout fundamentals, and with Jensen back in the seat, we upgrade
today back to OP (as his exit was the primary reason for the downgrade). That being said,
this whole mess has brought up some legitimate questions and will leave some overhangs
in place that may limit NVDA’s multiple for now. These include the following:
Investigations ongoing; any concessions required?. While Trump et al seem
comfortable with Jensen returning, there are many on both sides of the aisle who may
be less enamored, hence we wonder if any concessions were required (China related
perhaps?) Coupled with the democrats about to take power, we suspect Jensen and NVIDIA
will remain in the crosshairs, and we don’t know what will turn up as inquiries continue.
Weakness in NVIDIA’s board and governance has been clearly highlighted. While we
are happy to have Jensen back, the on-again-off-again response from the board is, frankly,
clown-like (they were adamant just last week that he was gone for sure) and brings up
legitimate questions as to their judgment and process which has to make one wonder how
they come to their other important decisions.
Jensen’s judgment remains under question. We don’t actually care what Jensen does on
his personal time. However, he clearly does keep questionable company on occasion, and
his willingness to lie to the board and other key stakeholders is troubling to us.
Broader AI investor concerns remain. The last week has been brutal for the entire AI and
semi space, and it would have been interesting to see what it would have been like with
Jensen still there. However, the concerns driving the move were not all Jensen-related, with
investors increasingly worried about AI spending achievability and sustainability.
Nevertheless, current valuation, with Jensen at the helm, seem supportive enough
to us for now. We are going to leave our price target at $200 (~10x our CY28/29 avg EPS),
which seems to reflect a decent amount of potential downside. We upgrade to Outperform.
