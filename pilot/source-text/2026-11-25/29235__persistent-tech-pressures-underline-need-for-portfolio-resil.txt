Thought of the day
What happened?
Technology shares extended their decline on Friday. The Nasdaq 100 was 
down 5.6%, while the S&P 500 fell 3.7%, posting its seventh consecutive 
daily decline. The Nasdaq is now 19.8% below its recent high, reached on 
6 October, putting it close to bear-market territory, defined as a decline 
of 20% from a recent high. The S&P 500 is in correction territory, defined 
as a decline of 10% from a recent high, having fallen 11.5% since 6 
October. US Treasuries rallied, with the 10-year yield falling 10 basis points 
to 4.95%. The VIX, a measure of US equity implied volatility, reached nearly 
36, its highest level since the market turmoil surrounding the rollout of 
Liberation Day tariffs in April 2025.
The selling reflects mounting questions about the sustainability of spending 
on AI infrastructure considering physical and financing constraints as 
well as whether the revenues generated by AI will justify the investment. 
The leadership transition at NVIDIA and a Wall Street Journal report that 
the SEC has opened a civil probe into the company have stoked further 
uncertainty across the industry.
Markets are now pricing a near-100% probability of a 25-basis-point 
Federal Reserve rate increase in December, with further tightening expected 
in the first quarter of 2027. This reflects a broader hawkish sentiment in 
recent Fed communications, with several voting members, including two 
governors, signaling support for additional tightening in recent weeks. 
The October meeting minutes reinforced this message, indicating a strong 
expectation of a rate increase at the next meeting and heightened concern 
about the broadening of inflation pressures in the US.

Daily Asia
In commodities, gold dropped to a 14-month low of USD 3,532 an ounce 
on Friday and was down around 9% for the week. Higher real rates and a 
stronger US dollar have been important headwinds, while some investors 
may also have liquidated gold positions to cover losses elsewhere. Copper 
declined amid the broader risk-off move and recorded its steepest weekly 
fall since April 2025, as the reassessment of the AI trade spread beyond 
equities toward other beneficiaries of data-center investment.
What do we think?
The latest sell-off is largely positioning-led, in our view, ahead of a short 
trading week this week in the US, with Thanksgiving on Thursday.
In our base case, we expect economic growth to be close to trend in 2027, 
corporate earnings to rise, AI investment to increase, and inflation to 
moderate later in the year. This is consistent with equity gains and positive 
fixed income returns.
But we recently downgraded global equities from Attractive to Neutral due 
to elevated expectations, concentrated equity indexes, and uncertainty 
around AI monetization. The volatility over the past week shows how these 
risks can manifest and supports our decision.
The AI debate is shifting from whether investment will continue to whether 
it can generate adequate shareholder returns. Cumulative US technology 
investment in AI infrastructure could approach USD 5tr by the end of 2028, 
and we estimate that annual AI model and application revenues may need 
to reach at least USD 1.5tr by 2030 for the industry to earn an adequate 
return. Competition, price cuts, financing constraints, and difficulties 
monetizing mean that the gains are unlikely to be evenly distributed.
How do we invest?
While the sell-off means the balance of risks for equities has improved 
relative to last week, and our base case outlook remains constructive, we 
see few catalysts in the coming weeks that would substantially lift equities 
and expect volatility to remain elevated.
Periods of volatility are a reminder to maintain discipline in investing and 
having a clear financial plan. Aligning investments with goals, liquidity 
requirements, risk capacity, and risk tolerance can reduce the likelihood of 
abandoning a strategy under stress to seek perceived safety in cash.
First, investors should keep a long-term perspective and avoid overreacting 
to price action alone. Market moves can be amplified by systematic 
strategies, forced selling, and shifts in positioning. Missing a recovery after 
selling into weakness can be more damaging to long-term outcomes than 
enduring a temporary drawdown.
Second, diversification matters. A portfolio concentrated in a narrow 
group of AI-linked stocks will naturally experience greater volatility when 
expectations change. Diversification across regions, sectors, bonds, and real 
assets can reduce reliance on any single return driver.
And third, investors should maintain updated “shopping” and “disposal” 
lists. A shopping list should identify markets or asset classes where the 
portfolio is underinvested relative to its strategic allocation, as well as high-
quality assets that would become attractive at lower prices. A disposal list 
should include excessive single-stock or regional concentrations, assets with 
weaker long-term return potential, and surplus cash that could be deployed

Selected Year Ahead Messages in Focus
Refocus equity exposure. We forecast around 15% earnings growth 
in both the US and Eurozone in 2027 but favor broader exposure rather 
than simply adding to concentrated technology positions. The Eurozone 
is our preferred region. At the sector level, we favor industrials and 
health care, while US equal-weight strategies can help reduce technology 
concentration. Within AI, we favor financially strong hyperscalers 
with visible monetization and selected leaders in compute, foundry, 
semiconductor equipment, and infrastructure software.
Use volatility, rather than fear it. Investors can use shopping and 
disposal lists to rebalance toward strategic targets and build positions 
gradually. Structured strategies may help investors participate more 
deliberately or generate income when implied volatility is elevated, 
although capital-loss, issuer, liquidity, and opportunity-cost risks require 
careful sizing and diversification.
Build resilience with gold and commodities. Gold’s current decline 
reflects high real yields, US dollar strength, and liquidity pressures, rather 
than a repudiation of its longer-term diversification role. We recommend 
maintaining a strategic allocation and forecast gold to reach USD 5,000 
an ounce by December 2027. We also favor actively managed exposure to 
energy, industrial metals, agriculture, and precious metals. Copper remains 
our preferred industrial metal, supported by investment in power networks, 
electrification, data centers, and AI infrastructure.
Caught our attention
China cloud growth accelerates. Alibaba on Thursday reported cloud 
revenue growth of 53% y/y for the third quarter, with external cloud 
revenue rising more than 60% y/y. The division’s margin reached roughly 
14.5%, up from below 10% two quarters earlier. Management retained 
its USD 100bn cloud revenue ambition for 2030 and highlighted greater 
use of domestically produced processors. Earlier last week, Tencent said 
its cloud growth accelerated to around 35% y/y, although higher capex 
contributed to a second consecutive quarter of negative free cash flow. 
Management said spending was near a quarterly peak but could remain 
elevated for several more quarters. While both companies indicated that 
the latest US chip restrictions should not pose a material impact on capex 
or cloud revenue, they emphasized increasing reliance on domestic chips.
Our view: The results provide further evidence that Chinese demand for 
AI computing remains robust, and that cloud monetization is improving, 
even as the investment required to build capacity weighs on near-term 
cash flow. In our view, rising capital intensity and uneven returns reinforce 
the need for selectivity. Within China tech, we favor areas where earnings 
visibility, policy support, and demand fundamentals align, particularly 
onshore semiconductor equipment vendors, foundries, and other hardware 
companies benefiting from AI chip localization and technology self-
sufficiency. The latest spending commitments from large internet platforms

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
In line: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
