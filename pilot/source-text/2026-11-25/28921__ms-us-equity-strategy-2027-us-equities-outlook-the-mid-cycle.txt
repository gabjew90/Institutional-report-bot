• Mid Cycle Is Here. We were early in identifying the robust and broad 
earnings recovery that followed the end of the rolling recession in April 
2025. In fact, the median Russell 3000 stock is delivering mid-teens EPS 
growth, validating the breadth of that recovery. Building on the call we made 
in July, we see the mid cycle transition now ending with a more stable mid 
cycle backdrop evolving in 2027.  Growth remains healthy though its rate of 
change moderates as the initial operating leverage benefit matures, and the 
Fed normalizes toward less accommodative policy. We reiterate  our 
preference for Quality and believe the next phase of margin expansion shifts 
toward efficiency gains through AI adoption.
• Recent Price Action Sets Up an Attractive Entry Point. Our view dating 
back to late September has been that the correction associated with the mid 
cycle transition would conclude with the S&P 500 catching down to narrow 
breadth. That process is now well advanced as the index has approached our 
established 7,100–7,300 support range (where there is valuation, trendline 
and retracement support). While a retest could still lie ahead, the recent 
correction provides the springboard for the next leg higher toward our new 
8,700 base case. Healthy EPS growth, lower oil,  more stable interest rates 
and reduced bond volatility support the 2027 backdrop. Broadening AI 
adoption and an ongoing AI capex cycle, reinforced by our thematic research 
team's latest work, provide additional tailwinds. At ~18x forward earnings, 
below the March lows, valuation also offers a more attractive starting point. 
• We Raise Our 12-Month S&P 500 Price Target to 8,700 (19x forward EPS 
of $458). We see 2026 EPS of $369 (34% growth), 2027 EPS of $420 (14% 
growth), and 2028 EPS of $458 (9% growth). Healthy nominal sales growth 
and a broadening contribution from AI adoption should support further 
margin expansion as the early cycle  operating leverage matures. Earnings 
growth remains the primary driver of our target, with modest valuation 
expansion providing additional upside as financial conditions ease. At ~18x 
forward earnings, the recent selloff has taken the market multiple below our 
estimate of fair value, making a 19x target multiple achievable without 
demanding valuation assumptions. The main risk to our constructive call is 
more hawkish monetary policy than expected, through both the policy rate 
and the balance sheet, resulting in renewed tightening in financial conditions 
that pressures multiples.

The Mid Cycle Transition Evolves
 
An earnings recovery we identified around Liberation Day
A year ago, our 2026 outlook was built around the view that the rolling recession had 
ended with the Liberation Day capitulation in April 2025 and  that a new economic and 
earnings cycle was well underway. We were early in identifying that turn, particularly for 
the average stock, where several years of weak growth and compressed cost structures 
had created the conditions for a meaningful recovery in profitability. The combination of 
improving revenue, positive operating leverage and a sharp rebound in earnings revisions 
breadth underpinned our above-consensus earnings forecasts at that time.  It also explains 
the strong early cycle  sector (i.e., Semis, Financials, Autos, and Media) and lower quality 
factor leadership we enjoyed from April 2025 to June 2026. 
Our NFIB framework ( Exhibit 1 ) showed nominal sales improving relative to 
compensation expense, a combination that has historically preceded a steep recovery in 
earnings. We also saw pent-up demand across areas that had spent much of 2022 through 
early 2025 in a rolling downturn. Our view was that better pricing would meet leaner cost 
structures, allowing the recovery to spread beyond the largest index weights. The NFIB 
relationship shown below illustrates that recovery and the notion that the early cycle 
rebound in margins is now in a more mature state as the series in the top panel 
approaches prior highs. As we discuss below, margin expansion is now likely to come more 
from AI adoption, lower energy prices and the continued re-industrialization of America.

Despite having a higher  growth forecast than consensus a year ago, the earnings recovery 
has been even stronger than we anticipated, although non-operating gains for major index 
weights increased the overall growth number for this year. More related to our framework 
is the breadth of the improvement: the median Russell 3000 stock is delivering mid-teens 
earnings growth and high-single-digit top line growth, a feature we have been highlighting 
all year.  
An expansion moving into mid cycle
We think the economy and earnings backdrop are now in a mid cycle phase, a call we 
initially made in July as the transition from early cycle began. Growth remains healthy 
even as its rate of change moderates. The earliest beneficiaries of the recovery saw 
revisions breadth and growth momentum peak around June—areas such as Semis and the 
most cyclical parts of Consumer. These were the same groups where operating leverage 
and/or stronger pricing had provided the greatest benefit coming out of the rolling 
recession. As those comparisons became more demanding, the market naturally became 
more selective about the quality of earnings. More broadly, this incremental focus on 
quality typically comes as the rate of change on EPS growth for the overall market 
decelerates. We still expect very healthy earnings growth of 14% in 2027, but it's not as 
strong as the 34% experienced in 2026.
The Fed’s normalization toward less accommodative policy is the other defining feature of 
the recent mid cycle transition which has been ongoing for over 4 months. Expectations 
for future hikes, slower balance sheet growth, and higher energy product prices have 
coincided with strong demand for capital from the real economy, producing a larger 
tightening in financial conditions than the policy-rate adjustment alone would suggest. 
This has further reinforced our preference for companies with durable margins, strong 
balance sheets and resilient cash conversion, as reflected in the breakout in our quality 
factor ( Exhibit 2 ).

The correction is setting up the next leg higher
The correction that began in June is now in its final inning, in our view. Much of the 
adjustment occurred beneath the surface well before the S&P 500 began to reflect it. 
~65% of Russell 3000 stocks have experienced a drawdown of more than 20% since June, 
with considerably more damage in several early-cycle groups, such as Semis.  This was a 
bear market that many didn't fully acknowledged along the way, which is why some may 
be reducing risk at an inopportune time—i.e., as the headlines confirm the clearing events 
that the market has been processing for months (i.e., the election outcome). 
Our call in June for Semis to underperform was our central expression of this transition. 
Revisions breadth had reached levels that were unlikely to be sustained, and the group 
had experienced the kind of parabolic price appreciation that left little room for a 
moderation in expectations. The subsequent correction has followed the silver stock 
analog we have been highlighting since that time. We now think Semis are in a bottoming 
process, although a retest and break of the recent lows may still be required. Selectivity 
within the group remains important.

AI adoption and a broader opportunity set in 2027
AI adoption remains a high conviction theme from us into 2027 and fits naturally with the 
higher quality earnings environment we expect where operational efficiency is in focus. 
We think the enterprise application layer will account for a larger share of value creation 
as this process develops, and we remain early in that evolution.
Our latest adoption work provides tangible support. Across earnings, conference and 
investor-day transcripts, 44% of S&P 500 companies cited at least one quantifiable AI 
benefit over the past year, up from 32% a year earlier. The share citing a measurable 
financial benefit, such as cost savings or incremental revenue, rose from 16% to 26%.

The broader AI investment cycle remains in its early-to-middle innings, in our view, even as 
leadership within it changes. Our preference is to broaden exposure to application-related 
stocks and adopters while remaining selective in infrastructure. Hyperscalers continue to 
offer attractive optionality through their strong core businesses, ability to lead in the 
application layer and means of improving their own cost structures through AI adoption.
The latest work from Stephen Byrd, Brian Nowak and our TMT colleagues reinforces the 
view that the capex cycle remains intact. Their post-midterm forecasts retain aggregate 
hyperscaler spending of approximately $1.6 trillion in 2027 and $1.8 trillion in 2028.
We also point out that our proprietary capex mapping analysis shows that data center 
capex for 2027 represents 5% of consensus revenue for the S&P 500. For 2028, we 
expect this contribution to be slightly higher, but still in the mid-single-digit range. Thus, 
even in a bear-case scenario where a portion of data center capacity gets pushed out 
beyond this time horizon, we think the impact on S&P 500 revenues and earnings is 
limited and priced at 18x forward earnings (near Liberation Day valuation levels). Finally, 
keep in mind that policy uncertainty around this dynamic is part of the reason Semis and 
other stocks that benefit from data center builds already underperformed materially for 4 
months leading up to the election. 
Midterms bring risks and potential offsets
The midterms have removed an important source of event uncertainty, although the 
implementation of the resulting policy agenda will take time. We had argued that a 
narrow Democratic sweep was well anticipated, while a more decisive Senate outcome 
could cause investors to look further ahead to the policy environment in 2028. For 2027, 
the more immediate questions concern regulation, data center approvals and the 
administration’s use of executive authority. Tariffs, foreign policy and much agency-led 
deregulation remain largely within the executive branch’s control.
The election result brings potential positives. Efforts to delay or reverse Medicaid and 
SNAP cuts could support consumption, while the prospect of a different policy 
environment after 2028 may pull forward some investment and M&A activity. Local 
opposition to data centers often reflects incumbent and community dynamics rather than 
a simple party divide, making project status and the details of any new approval process 
more informative than the election headline alone. The selloff in several AI-related and 
defense-linked stocks appears to have discounted a more immediate deterioration than we 
expect, which creates opportunities into next year.
History provides an encouraging backdrop as election-related uncertainty is resolved. 
Since 1982, our large-cap universe has gained about 15% on a cap-weighted basis and 16% 
on an equal-weighted basis in the six months following midterms, with both measures 
rising roughly 22% over the subsequent year. The industry detail also shows a tendency 
for cyclicals to outperform relative to defensives. We would use this as context for the 
opportunity created by the correction, with earnings and financial conditions ultimately 
determining the durability of the next advance. The more notable policy risks should they 
play out would be a renewed escalation with Iran, additional tariffs that pressure growth

one in which price pressures are accelerating broadly or one where the Fed is set on 
getting inflation to target quickly, which is also not our view. From an equity perspective, 
the key is how the Fed weighs the firmer October CPI print alongside the tightening that 
certain markets have already delivered.
In our view, the most consequential part of Warsh’s latest FOMC communication was that 
he did not intensify the message investors had taken from Jackson Hole about returning 
inflation to 2% quickly, irrespective of the near-term cost. He acknowledged tighter 
financial conditions, although he stopped short of describing them as outright tight. We 
view that as preserving flexibility to pause while the existing restraint works through the 
economy. Our constructive view on equities into next year does not require an immediate 
easing cycle or a material decline in back-end yields; we believe a more stable expected 
policy path would itself remove a source of pressure and allow multiples to expand 
modestly from 18x (below fair value) to 19x.
The pace and composition of the bond-market move remain more important than the 
level of yields alone. Our October work showed that nearly all of the roughly 130bp 
increase in the 10-year yield from February had come through real yields. That is a harder 
adjustment for equities to absorb than an increase driven by inflation breakevens. Longer-
term nominal growth provides context for where yields can settle, but a renewed, rapid 
rise in real yields would still be disruptive, in our view. The risks would be more hawkish 
commentary from the Fed after the October CPI print, and that inflation prints in coming 
months show a broadening of pricing pressures.

Bull case scenario
Our bull case of 10,000 assumes that the earnings recovery proves even more resilient 
than in our baseline and that financial conditions are more supportive. We apply a 20x 
multiple to 2028 EPS of $500, with EPS of $374 in 2026 (36% growth), $439 in 2027 
(17%) and $500 in 2028 (14%).
Key to this scenario is that the early cycle operating leverage that fades in our base case 
extends further into 2027. AI adoption tailwinds are also more significant in this outcome. 
Lower oil and stabilizing rates would reinforce that strength. Additional Middle East 
supply and near-term easing in geopolitical risk would reduce costs, support real spending 
and give the Fed room to remain on hold in 2027. Treasury’s cash drawdown would 
release liquidity and the growth of the Fed's balance sheet would expand again, while the 
real-yield shock behind most of the rise in the 10-year since February would fade and bond 
volatility would remain contained.

including lower quality areas. Credit market risk would recede as this plays out and as

persistent agents become more prevalent and support enterprise consumption. The 
power issues would be worked around with data center capacity sourced outside the US 
as needed and political pushback against data centers fading in TX and OH. Token pricing 
would stabilize and rise modestly, supporting top line for many of the compute providers,

Long Quality Consumer Discretionary with Divergences between Strong Fwd. EPS

We believe Consumer Discretionary presents a compelling opportunity where relative 
performance appears disconnected from the earnings outlook. Our framework places 
significant emphasis on EPS revisions and the rate of change in expectations, but an 
equally important distinction is how much of an improving growth trajectory is already 
reflected in prices.  Exhibit 41 below illustrates the extent of pessimism embedded in 
Consumer Discretionary, with the sector’s equal-weighted relative performance back to its 
2020 lows. The economic backdrop is materially different today: the economy is not in 
recession, and we do not expect one over the next 12 months.
Several potential catalysts could help close that dislocation gap, including stabilization in 
rates and oil prices, potential relief from planned Medicaid and SNAP cuts, and continued 
resilience among higher-income consumers. Earnings growth and improving revisions 
should also support a recovery in relative performance. While the extent to which these 
drivers benefit individual companies will vary, the depressed starting point suggests that a 
more constructive earnings path may be underappreciated across parts of the group.

characteristics: stocks whose performance has lagged the path implied by earnings growth

Long Hyperscalers
We remain constructive on the Hyperscalers, where the valuation reset has improved the 
risk/reward while leaving several sources of AI upside underappreciated. As discussed 
previously, we think the group was early to discount the market’s renewed focus on capex 
discipline and has already absorbed a meaningful repricing of  the risks. The group's 
multiple recently declined to 21x, in line with the March lows, creating a more attractive 
entry point into businesses with strong core earnings franchises and multiple avenues to 
monetize AI. As Exhibit 46 shows, hyperscalers' relative earnings strength remains quite 
attractive. Their existing customer relationships, distribution and ability to embed new 
capabilities into established products position them to participate in—and potentially lead
—the development and implementation of the agentic application layer. This provides 
opportunities to improve monetization within existing businesses while creating new 
revenue streams as adoption broadens. We also see an underappreciated internal cost-
cutting lever, with AI adoption offering scope to improve productivity and support margin 
expansion alongside revenue growth.
The evolving, potential constraints on data center development could further reinforce 
the advantages of scale. While delays are a risk, we think they also increase the strategic 
value of operating compute capacity and favor companies with the financial resources,

One question we frequently receive from investors is: "Are companies seeing tangible 
efficiency gains from AI adoption?"  Last fall, in collaboration with the AlphaWise team, we

Following AI's $1.6T Money Trail
 
Tracking Hyperscaler and Neocloud Capex to Stock-Level Revenue. Our colleagues have 
regularly published models for capex per GW, GW capacity added per year, ROIC on AI 
capex (latest models published here), and power demand and supply estimates (here). But 
tracking all this capex spend to revenue dollars is a difficult exercise. While many 
companies have highlighted areas of their business that benefit from the AI infrastructure 
boom, AI revenue is rarely quantified. 
Quantifying capex is also not straightforward–some companies are financing a substantial 
portion of their capex with leases, which bypasses capex on the cash flow statement. We 
create new estimates of lease capex across the hyperscalers. We then aggregate our lease 
capex estimates with  hyperscaler and neocloud cash capex estimates for a comprehensive 
view of AI infrastructure spend, regardless of funding source. 
Tracking capex dollars spent into revenues generated is further complicated as 
components pass through different entities within the AI infrastructure supply chain 
before landing in a data center. For example, our analysts estimate more than $130bn of 
HBM is sold from Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron into Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD and other 
AI chip designers. 
Then, Original Equipment Manufacturers (Dell and HPE) and Original Design 
Manufacturers (Wistron, Hon Hai, and Quanta Computer) assemble components–AI chips, 
memory, networking, power, cooling–into AI servers and racks. The OEMs and ODMs earn 
a 4% to 6% margin on the total component cost. To avoid double counting within the 
server supply chain, we include HBM revenue within accelerator revenues, and we include 
only OEM and ODM margin to capture their assembly contribution to AI servers. 
We map $1.6T of capex to $1.7T of supplier revenues. This difference could be attributed 
to a number of variables, including (a) the challenge of splitting supplier revenues 
between hyperscaler and public neoclouds vs private neoclouds and bitcoin miners, (b) 
different expectations for timing of capex spend across companies, (c) price increases 
incorporated in supplier revenue estimates that have not been incorporated into capex 
estimates yet, among others.

AI earnings are outpacing non-AI earnings. Both the AI cohort and the SPX ex-AI cohort 
are growing earnings in the double digits, and both cohorts show 2Q26 as the peak 
quarter for earnings growth ( Exhibit 75 ).  This peak could extend depending on how 3Q 
earnings season plays out relative to expected beat rates. Regardless, we appear to be 
near peak earnings growth — and markets trade on rate of change. The picture is further 
complicated by private AI investments showing up in non-operating profit, which skews 
the 1Q, 2Q, and full-year 2026 earnings picture.
With earnings growth now high but decelerating, market focus may shift toward who is 
incrementally improving EPS growth into 2027 — a dynamic that favors the AI cohort, 
where that growth is notably stronger. Taken together, AI remains a dominant theme in 
2026's market, and we expect this to continue into 2027. That said, we think leadership 
will shift away from pure Enablers toward more blended Enabler/Adopters and Adopters, 
even as the AI theme broadly remains highly prevalent.
AI companies are growing 2027 sales at 28% versus 5% for non-AI companies ( Exhibit 
76 ). Sales for the S&P 500 broadly are very strong and in line with historical regressions 
versus nominal GDP. S&P 500 2027 sales growth is running at 9%; stripping out AI 
investment (assuming a 65% SPX absorption rate) would bring that down to roughly 7% 
— still in line with historical regressions of nominal GDP and historical sales growth. 
These points support our view that S&P 500 broadening is fully underway: growth would 
already be strong without AI investment, and AI spending is fueling incremental growth on 
top of that.
The growth numbers are significantly higher within AI names, but they're working off 
a lower absolute sales base. Sales for our "AI names" cohort represent 20% of S&P 500 
total sales in 2026, rising to 23% and 26% in 2027 and 2028, respectively. The AI names 
category is heavily skewed by the Magnificent 7, which account for 14 of those 20 
percentage points — roughly 70% of the cohort's sales. In terms of sales growth 
contribution, the AI cohort is driving 60% of 2027 S&P 500 sales growth ( Exhibit 77 ).
