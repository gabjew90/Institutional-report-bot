It's very hard to know where to start this morning after a tumultuous week. But for anyone looking for some 
respite after Friday’s savage US close, which saw the S&P 500 fall -3.70% and the Nasdaq -5.28%, the good 
news is that futures are pointing to a sizeable rebound, up +2.25% and +3.05% respectively as I type. 
Asian markets have been a bit all over the place this morning. The KOSPI had dropped more than -5.5% but 
is now -2.28%. The Korean authorities have announced a KRW10.7tn market stabilisation fund and have 
included other measures like easing the size of company buy-backs for three months. 
Elsewhere, Chinese equities are rallying off their session lows as markets digest a fresh round of policy 
support. The Shanghai Composite (-0.32%), Hang Seng (-0.37%) and CSI 300 (-0.32%) are now only slightly 
lower. Beijing announced lower margin requirements and state-backed funds to continue to support the 
market. Japan is closed for a public holiday, and the ASX 200 (-0.26%) is also bouncing from its earlier lows.   
This follows a huge risk-off move last week as fears grew around the AI trade and spread to a general hunt for 
liquidity across the globe. The S&P 500 fell -8.61% (-3.70% Friday), its worst week since the Liberation Day 
tariff announcements in April 2025, leaving it in technical correction territory at -11.62% from its early-
October peak and at its lowest level since April and only +0.95% for the year now. It has now fallen 
seven days in a row, the longest run since the pandemic began in February 2020. If we fall again today, 
which futures at the moment don’t point to, that would be the longest run since just before the US 
Presidential election a decade ago.  
The most AI-exposed assets were hit hardest. The Philly semiconductor index fell -17.13% (-10.73% 
Friday), its worst week since 2001, while the Nasdaq lost -10.02% (-5.28% Friday), its worst week since 
March 2020 and is now -2.69% lower for the year. Nvidia fell -14.01% on Friday (worst since May 2025) and 
had its worst week since the peak of the GFC in autumn 2008 (-22.92%), leaving the stock down -47.5% from 
its early October peak. There were late headlines on Friday suggesting that the SEC are investigating the 
company, with the NYT on Saturday suggesting it’s over accounting practises. 

But this was no longer simply a Tech selloff. The equal-weighted S&P 500 fell -6.25% (-2.04% Friday), also 
its largest weekly decline since the Liberation Day tariffs, while the rout went global, with the STOXX 
600 down -5.80% (-2.00% Friday), its worst week since April 2025, and the MSCI EM index down -8.40% (-
3.10% Friday). 
Our equity strategists Parag Thatte and Binky Chadha commented over the weekend here that what began 
in October as a textbook rotation out of Tech from extended levels has morphed into a broader risk-off 
move amid a succession of negative catalysts — the US mid-term blue wave, the issues surrounding 
Nvidia, fears of large equity supply and a cluster of seemingly concurrent tech outages that have raised 
wider concerns. And with the S&P 500 now more than -10% below its recent peak, this is no longer a 
routine pullback: -3% to -5% declines occur every 2-3 months, but -10% drawdowns happen only 
around once a year on average and are generally associated with a clear shock or meaningful growth 
concerns. 
They believe that a lot of bad news is already being priced. Equity positioning is now in the 11th percentile 
and close to the bottom of its usual +/-1 standard deviation range, with discretionary positioning in the 
4th percentile and systematic strategies in the 15th. They believe pricing and positioning are effectively 
consistent with an outright contraction in S&P 500 earnings and the ISM and global manufacturing PMIs 
falling well below 50. 
If they are correct that this is ultimately a correction rather than a recessionary bear market, the process may 
still take time. Implied and realised volatility, intraday ranges, the inversion of the VIX futures curve and 
implied correlations are all in the top decile. Similar volatility shocks have typically taken 6-8 weeks to 
subside, with equities tending to bottom over that period. Outside recessions, the S&P 500 has fallen an 
average -14% to -16% in such episodes and taken 4-6 months to fully recover. So history would argue that 
further downside and volatility remain plausible before a durable bottom forms, but positioning is already 
washed out and they believe Tech has moved to unusually depressed technical levels with the sector at 
the bottom of its decade-long absolute channel and well below its relative channel versus the rest of 
the S&P 500, while the Nasdaq 100 is more than -10% below its 200-day moving average, only the fifth 
such occurrence in the last decade. Read their piece for much more on how it’s left positioning. 
Moving on to the main highlights of the coming week, if we can take our eyes off equity markets, it will kick off 
with the global flash PMIs today and a very important Fed Williams speech, followed by US PCE inflation 
(Wednesday) ahead of Thanksgiving on Thursday. Inflation will also be due in Europe and Tokyo on Friday.    
Going through the week in a bit more detail, and starting with the US, the October PCE inflation, along with 
personal income and consumption, will be the main focus. Those are due on Wednesday, alongside durable 
goods orders, ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday on Thursday. Our US economists expect core PCE to 
come in at +0.29% MoM (+0.3% in September), with income and consumption both up +0.4% MoM. For 
durable good orders, our team see headline growth of +2.5% (+1.9%). 
New York Fed President Williams’ keynote today at the US equity close will also be especially 
important, as it is currently scheduled to be the final Fed communication before the blackout ahead of 
the December 9 FOMC meeting. Williams was firmly hawkish last Tuesday, stressing that the Fed still has 
work to do to return inflation sustainably to 2%, while pushing back on the need for “dramatic action” 
at the December meeting. Despite the sharp selloff in risk assets since then, we doubt his core message on 

the need for further tightening will change materially. But with the October FOMC minutes highlighting 
financial-stability risks from rapid AI-related debt growth, it will be interesting to see whether Williams 
gives greater weight to the recent tightening in financial conditions — even if, so far, Fed officials appear to 
view conditions as still too accommodative rather than restrictive enough to alter the policy path. 
Elsewhere, a further read on households will come from Conference Board’s consumer confidence reading 
for November (tomorrow, DB forecast 92.0 vs 91.7 in October). The Fed’s Beige Book on Wednesday will 
also be watched for a qualitative assessment of economic conditions. 
Before that the global week will kick off with a snapshot of business activity as November flash PMIs are 
released across the US, UK, Germany, France and the Eurozone today, followed by Japan tomorrow. Our US 
economists see the manufacturing gauge at 55.1 (54.9 in October) and the services one at 56.4 (56.1). 
In Europe, the focus will also be on preliminary November CPIs from France and Spain on Friday, with prints 
for Germany and the Eurozone due the week after next. Our European economists preview the releases 
here and forecast a 3.53% YoY HICP reading for France and 4.57% for Spain. From the ECB, there will be 
the account of the October meeting (Thursday) and the October consumer expectations survey (Friday). 
Rounding out the European calendar, in Germany, sentiment indicators due include the November Ifo survey 
(tomorrow) and December GfK consumer confidence (Thursday). Q3 GDP reports will also be out in Norway 
(Thursday) and Sweden (Friday). 
Inflation releases are also due in Asia, including Japan’s November Tokyo CPI (Friday) and Australia’s 
October CPI (Wednesday). For the former, our Chief Japan economist sees an acceleration in both the 
core index to 2.9% YoY (2.6% in October) and the core-core index to 2.7% YoY (2.6%). Elsewhere in the 
region, China’s October industrial profits will be out Friday. Finally, notable companies reporting next week 
include Dell, Analog Devices, Deere and HPE. See the rest of the day-by-day calendar at the end. 
We recapped last week’s equity moves at the start but rounding off the week now and the risk-off also had a 
big impact on bonds, especially US Treasuries. Indeed, the 2yr Treasury yield fell -19.5bps (-11.5bps 
Friday), marking its biggest weekly decline since August 2025, back when an underwhelming jobs report 
led to mounting expectations that the Fed would start cutting rates again at the next meeting. Further 
out the curve, the 10yr Treasury yield also fell -19.1bps last week (-10.1bps Friday) to 4.95%. However, 
there were smaller declines in yields elsewhere, with the 10yr bund yield falling by -7.4bps (-9.9bps Friday) 
to 3.30%. 
As all that was going on, there was a significant pullback in the commodity space, driven by renewed concern 
over economic demand. That included oil prices, with Brent crude down -3.41% last week to $92.57/bbl, 
marking its third consecutive weekly decline. Meanwhile, copper prices fell -9.94%, marking their 
biggest weekly decline since August 2025 as well, whilst gold prices fell -8.91% to $3,538/oz, their 
lowest level since September 2025.   
Finally, the moves last week drove a fresh widening in credit spreads, with US IG (+10bps), HY (+51bps), and 
Euro IG (+6bps) and HY (+30bps) all sharply wider. Meanwhile in FX, the dollar index was up +0.38% last 
week, strengthening for a 4th consecutive week. Conversely, the Euro weakened -0.50% against the 
Dollar, closing at $1.0968.
