 
Hawks Squawk, Doves Dote, Dario In, Jensen Out: 
Bonds Jump As Stocks Dump Most Since Liberation 
Day 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SATURDAY, NOV 21, 2026 - 10:30 PM 
Oh 'deer'! 
 
Tl;dr: Every piece of good news the bulls asked for arrived this week, and stocks fell anyway. 
Nvidia beat and raised in its first quarter without Jensen Huang, but the board said he isn't 
coming back and the "Bank of Nvidia" is closing its open checkbook. Anthropic priced the 
biggest IPO in history and opened 34% higher, then slid back toward its $83 offer price on 
Friday, while its record raise drained cash from gold, bitcoin, copper and chips. The S&P fell for 
seven straight days, dropping over 9% in its worst week since Liberation Day (NDX Down over 
10%), and broke its 200-day as record short gamma met new China chip curbs, smashing VIX 
>32. The Peace Summit promised production and crude prices plunged amid burning tankers. 
 

 
The Fed stayed hawkish, with Waller wanting to "shock people," but the bond market is now 
pricing a slowdown: the 10Y fell to 4.90%, UMich inflation expectations plunged, oil fell 6%, 
copper about 8% and gold broke $3,600 as bitcoin puked below $60,000 as safe-haven flows 
bid the dollar to 18mo highs. Asia had its worst week since March 2020. Equity funds still drew 
$59 billion, the most since June, as investors bought the AI dip. Goldman's Privorotsky calls it 
2018 again. Next: PCE, CPI and a December 9 FOMC that may be overtightening. 
 
The week in a chart: Stocks, commodities, and yields all fell together for the first time in this 
cycle, which is what a market pricing a slowdown rather than inflation looks like... 
 
 

 
The best framing of the week came from Goldman's Delta One head Rich Privorotsky on Friday 
morning, after a Thursday session he admitted had "blindsided" him ("'Every Chart, Every 
Asset'", Nov 20): 
"In hindsight, the November/December 2018 parallels feel increasingly close. The Fed risks 
looking in the rear view mirror at inflation and the AI trade, effectively overtightening into a 
market already showing considerable stress. 2yr inflation breakevens have collapsed towards 
their lows, leaving 2yr real yields effectively near the relative highs." 
Translation: for two months the bond market priced inflation while stocks priced a slowdown; this 
week commodities, consumers and bonds all joined the stock market's side of the argument, but 
the Fed hasn't, which is exactly the setup that produced the December 2018 crash. As 
Privorotsky put it, "you can't break this many charts, across this many assets, and pretend we're 
still trading a bull market tape." The difference from 2018 is that the Fed is still talking about 
hiking by 50bps, and the only thing between the market and a repeat may be how much bad 
news is already in positioning that is now the lightest in five years.  
Below we walk through the Anthropic IPO and the liquidity it drained, Nvidia's post-Huang print, 
the six-day slide and the positioning behind it, the Fed and data, the bond rally, credit, the 
commodity rout, the dollar and bitcoin, Asia, the flows, and what comes next... 
"If Everyone Needs One, It Won't": The Biggest IPO Ever 
Becomes The Biggest Supply Shock 
Anthropic was supposed to be the AI trade's vindication. The Claude maker priced above its 
marketed range on Wednesday night... 
...raising a record $107.3 billion including a $26 billion placement with its own chip and cloud 
suppliers, beating SpaceX's record at a fully diluted valuation of about $1.5 trillion, or roughly 
12-13x 2027 sales ("Anthropic Opens 34% Above IPO Price", Nov 19). Nasdaq futures ripped 
2% overnight. Then, just after the cash open, came the headline "*ANTHROPIC IPO SAID TO 
GIVE NOTHING TO MOST INSTITUTIONAL BUYERS." Either the book was so strong that 
bankers could guarantee a pop by filling retail first, or, as we suspected, they "couldn't find 
institutional investors who were willing to pay $83 a share so they crammed it into retail bidders 
who were less discriminating on price." As the indicated open rose, the indices fell... 
...and the moment ANT opened at $111.50, pushing its market cap above $2 trillion, it was sold, 
and so was everything else. The Nasdaq 100 swung from +2% to -2%, the AI basket from +4% 
to -2%, neoclouds from +6% to -4%, and all 11 S&P sectors closed red, with the S&P losing 
 

 
about $1.3 trillion of market value on the day, roughly what Anthropic was worth at its IPO price 
("Markets Gag On Biggest IPO Of All Time", Nov 19). It was the worst opening hour for a large 
IPO in at least 20 years... 
 
As one market wit put it to us, "I love the smell of negative gamma in the morning." 
 
The IPO was the week's real story because of what it pulled out of everything else. As we noted 
on Wednesday, the market spent days "making room," just as it did before SpaceX's debut, 
when Vanda showed retail net selling single stocks for three days to raise cash for allocations 
("Gold & Crypto Dumped As Hawkish Fed, Kress Cross, & Anthropic Overhang", Nov 18). Desk 
chatter said gold, crypto, copper and Nvidia were sold to fund allocations, and Bloomberg's 
equity analysts called bitcoin's pre-IPO slide "statistically unusual, consistent with investors 
raising cash ahead of the offerings." Nomura's Charlie McElligott summed up the deal in six 
words: "foaming at mouth, impossibly low allocations." Seaport's verdict on the prospectus may 
be the line of the year: 
"Anthropic could develop AI systems which become fully autonomous and wipe out humanity 
which may lead to a serious decline in earnings." 
By Friday the round trip was nearly complete... 
 

 
...with ANT touching $83.10 at today's lows (down over 25% from Thursday's $115.10 high in 
less than a day), after the underwriters exercised the greenshoe in full, selling 147 million extra 
shares at $83 and leaving no stabilizing short to buy back stock if the price breaks the offer ("'If 
Everyone Needs One, It Won't'", Nov 20). Nearly half of the record day-one retail volume was 
selling.  
 
The bull case is mechanical: the book was six to seven times covered, so underweight 
institutions still need stock, Anthropic is on track to join the Nasdaq 100 on December 21 with 
about $19 billion of passive demand, and listed options start trading on Monday, the day 
SpaceX and SK Hynix popped. JPMorgan's Ronald Adler summed up the standoff: "If everyone 
wants a clearing event, one might happen. If everyone needs one, it won't. The market has 
been unforgiving - and one might say almost misanthropic - over the past few months." 
The whole AI trade crashed into the red YTD today (while SPX Ex-AI is up around 10%)... 
 

 
 
The bigger point is the one Bloomberg's Simon White makes: the US is now more dependent on 
equity financing than ever, and this week the equity market was asked to finance ~$120bn of 
one company in one go. SpaceX, for its part, sold $32bn of bonds at yields above 8% to buy AI 
chips, and DayOne put its data-center IPO on hold. Supply is the story. 
And that all came after the real show that shut the 'bank of AI' down... 
Nvidia Did "Pretty Much Everything You Could Ask For"... Except 
Bring Back Jensen, Suffers Worst Week Since DeepSeek 
Lehman 
The first Nvidia print without its founder was, on the numbers, a blowout... 
...beating every sellside estimate for the 17th straight quarter, with a fourth-quarter guide of 
$130 billion (in a 14-week quarter) against $125 billion expected and fiscal 2028 growth raised 
to 75% from 70% ("Nvidia Surges After Blowout Q3 Earnings", Nov 17). But the call opened with 
new chair Harvey Jones shutting the door: 
That came hours after Huang told the WSJ he was open to returning if the board allowed it, and 
after the WSJ reported an SEC probe into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. 
Privorotsky's verdict: "Nvidia did pretty much everything you could ask for overnight … beat and 
raise, $130bn guide vs approx $125bn expected, zero China data center compute in the 
numbers. The spending isn't stopping. The one thing the market would've liked was a hint that 
 

 
Jensen could come back. They shut that down" ("When Costco Sells Out Of $7,000 
Generators", Nov 18). 
 
The stock rose about 5% after hours, gave it all back on Wednesday to close down 2.9%. Shit 
continued to get even worse and in the last hour or two today, the SEC announced yet another 
probe (this time of accounting practices), yanking the not-so-giant tech company down further 
(down 20% for its worst week since Oct 2008 -Lehman)... 
 

 
 
Smashing the stock to its lowest since May 2025 and well below its 200DMA (NVDA's 7th 
straight losing week - the worst streak since 2008)... 
 
The more important change was in what Nvidia will no longer do. It is no longer shipping 
licensed products to China... 
...and while total commitments to customers and suppliers soared, Kress said Nvidia will be 
"more selective on customer financing," with guarantees only kicking in once projects have land, 
power and buildings.  
 

 
 
Goldman's TMT desk read that as Kress "explicitly dialling back the 'bank of AI' role," which is 
"positive for NVDA CDS/spreads, negative for any pure neocloud reliance on residual 
guarantees or offtake support."  
 
Citadel Securities' Nohshad Shah, in a note titled "Decapit-ai-tion," explained why that matters: 
founders like Huang commit their companies to bets that "a professional manager could never 
justify to a board," and the risk now is that caution spreads to the long-dated projects that kept 
expanding Nvidia's market ("Decapit-AI-tion", Nov 18). McElligott put it more bluntly: "the NVDA 
'Central Bank of AI' was previously viewed as a LONG Put for the ecosystem, but is now 
completely turned upside down and being viewed as de facto SHORT Put for the market." 
Privorotsky had flagged the bigger question on Monday: "I worry the real debate is a much 
bigger 2nd order question around the duration of the entire capex cycle" ("Equities Are Pricing A 
 

 
Slowdown, Rates Are Pricing Inflation", Nov 16). By Wednesday, the board had approached 
Arm's Rene Haas about the CEO job, and Moody's had cut Nvidia's outlook to stable. 
Just one more thing!! NVDA has now lost $2.65 trillion from its highs up near $6 trillion and is 
now smaller than GOOGL, MSFT, and AAPL... 
 
All of which crushed the broad market... 
Seven Days Down, Below The 200-Day, And Nobody Left To Buy 
The tape started breaking on Monday, when Asia greeted life after Jensen with a 6% drop in the 
Taiex and the S&P fell 1.9% with only staples higher ("Huang-Over & Hormuz Headlines 
Hammer Stocks", Nov 16). On Tuesday, a CNN report that the NSA's classified network had 
gone down sent stocks sliding (the NSA later blamed "routine maintenance")... 
...and SPX closed below its 200-day moving average for the first time since April ("Hormuz, 
Hacks, & Huang-less NVDA", Nov 17).  
 

 
 
All the majors were down massively this week with NDX leading the plunge (down over 10%!)... 
 
The Nasdaq tumbled into the red YTD... 
 

 
 
Tech was unsurpisingly the worst hit sector this week but even defensives (like Staples) ended 
red... 
 
Positioning should have cushioned it, and didn't. Goldman's Prime book shows fundamental 
long/short net leverage has fallen another 7.6 points this month to the lowest in more than five 
years, below the Liberation Day lows, and tech selling since early October is "by far the fastest 
in the past decade" (a -4.4 z-score). Privorotsky's hedge fund net weight in tech is about 18% 
against a 29% index weight, an 11-point underweight near five-year extremes, which led him to 
hope on Thursday that "merely not falling could be enough to spark a squeeze." Goldman's Lee 
Coppersmith set out the same case on Sunday in a note titled "AI confidence is fading": 
"We've seen the AI debate change pretty dramatically over the past month or so. Judging by 
conversations with clients last week, sentiment around the theme is about as negative as we've 
seen in quite some time... The nature of our flows has also shifted: we had previously witnessed 
 

 
mainly long liquidation and de-grossing, but this past week saw a more pronounced pickup in 
new shorts... With positioning this light, it probably doesn't take much positive news to spark a 
squeeze higher." 
Goldman's trading desk noted that US Single Stocks collectively were shorted every day this 
week and saw the largest weekly shorting activity since Jun ’22 (3rd largest in the past 
decade)... 
 
Nasdaq is the most oversold since the Liberation Day lows... 
 

 
 
He added that semis have underperformed the S&P by more than 30 points since June, the 
sharpest relative drawdown since 2002, and that Goldman's AI semis basket trades at 11x 2028 
earnings versus 16x for the S&P, so "either the selloff has gone too far, or investors are already 
pricing in cuts to earnings estimates."  
With the overall AI basket crashing into the red YTD (as above), it was NeoClouds that were 
clubbed like a baby seal this week, down over 20%... 
 
Which dragged the NeoClouds down into the red YTD too (after being up over 200% in June)...  
 

 
 
Semis were also destroyed this week, but remain higher on the year (for now)... 
 
As the DotCom analog holds up... 
 

 
 
The problem, as we noted on Thursday, is that "an underweight crowd chases strength, but it 
doesn't buy dips." Dealers were also running record short gamma into Friday's OpEx 
(SpotGamma), so every leg down was amplified, and liquidity evaporated: Goldman saw S&P 
futures top-of-book depth average just $4.2 million on Wednesday, "one of worst liquidity 
sessions in the last four years."  
 
Goldman's Gail Hafif noted that "spot VIX traded more than 12v over UX1 - the most inverted 
term structure since April 2025," with the VIX surging above 40 briefly. McElligott's warning on 
Thursday was the most ominous: if stocks can't rally with every systematic buyer primed, "it's 
not a positioning problem" ("'Somebody Wants Out!'", Nov 19).  
This was the highest VIX close since Liberation Day. 
 

 
 
BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky found just three prior six-day losing streaks in 30 years that closed 
below the 200-day but within 3% of it: September 2000, October 2018 and February 2020. All 
three saw the S&P higher five days later, and all three saw "meaningful downside after that 
small pop." He was targeting 7,000-7,100, and today's low broke it. 
Privorotsky's post-mortem on Thursday's reversal was brutal: "Broke just about every level 
possible. Bearish high/low close, SPX reversing 3.3% intraday and taking out virtually every 
reasonable area of support. Breadth was bad, with only 38 S&P constituents advancing and 464 
declining. Negative gamma into expiry likely amplified the move." What worried him most was 
that even the hiding places stopped working: "utilities and staples sold off alongside everything 
else, despite some relief in Treasury yields... What had been a rolling rotation increasingly looks 
like outright equity liquidation." He also blamed the new China chip rules, which took effect on 
Thursday and leave China facing "pressure on three fronts: direct access to advanced chips, 
remote access to overseas compute and its ability to manufacture leading edge technology," 
with the uncertainty "potentially enough to freeze incremental investment decisions." S&P 
futures fell further Friday as Intuit tumbled on an agentic AI threat to Credit Karma ("Futures 
Tumble As Anthropic IPO Hangover Turns AI Unwind Into Global Rout", Nov 20). 
By the close, all the majors were in correction (down over 10% from their highs) and Nasdaq the 
worst (down over 17% from its highs)... 
 

 
 
By the Friday close the index was down seven straight days, its longest losing streak since Feb 
2020, dropping over 7% near 7,000, its worst week since Liberation Day.  
 
And a Fed backstop is precisely what Warsh and his pals are not offering... 
 

 
The Fed: "You Might Need To, Like, Shock People..." 
The Fed did nothing to help. Waller went on CNBC on Wednesday and reprised his 50bp call... 
...saying about 70% of core services items are rising more than 3%, the Fed may have to take 
back all three of last year's "insurance" cuts "and maybe more," and "you might need to, like, 
shock people" out of thinking 3% inflation is the new normal ("Waller Wants To 'Shock' People", 
Nov 18). That afternoon the minutes of the October meeting confirmed the hold was about 
timing, not direction... 
...with "some" participants favoring a hike in October, "many" saying AI demand "was continuing 
to strain supply and raise prices," and a few hawks warning that delay risked "a larger and more 
disruptive tightening later"("Hawkish Minutes Show 'Almost All' FOMC Members See More 
Hikes", Nov 18). Musalem wants "several" hikes before spring, Hammack prefers "slow, steady 
increments," Jefferson said the AI buildout "is exerting upward pressure on demand and 
inflation," and Goolsbee warned it could overheat the economy. Only Williams pushed back on a 
jumbo move: "I don't think the situation calls for dramatic action." Bloomberg's Fed-speak gauge 
is the most hawkish since 2023, and Goldman still sees hikes in December and March to a 
4.25-4.50% peak. 
Yet the market stopped adding hikes.  
Retail sales beat on Tuesday, with the control group up 0.6% and sales up 6.8% on the year, the 
most since 2022 ("US Retail Sales Growth Fastest In 4 Years", Nov 17), but the Empire survey 
collapsed to 7.8 from 25.5, industrial production fell 0.4%, building permits dropped 7.4% to 
below every forecast, and on Thursday the Philly Fed plunged to 6.3 against 20.1 expected. As 
Bloomberg's Frank Monkam put it on Monday, the Fed put "appears to be missing," and "dip 
alpha... is becoming increasingly difficult to harvest." 
Friday's UMich survey added to the case that the Fed is fighting the last war... 
...with year-ahead inflation expectations plunging a full point from 4.7% to 3.7% and long-run 
expectations easing to 3.3%, as sentiment rose for a second month on the back of 
independents and Democrats expecting a divided government to slow the White House agenda 
("Inflation Fears Plunge As 'Independents' Lift UMich", Nov 20). 
The fundamentals combined with today's carnage in stocks took the 50bps hike off the table for 
December and dragged January's rate-hike odds down belw 60%... 
 

 
 
By the end of the week, 2026 rate-hike expectations had fallen very modestly, rotating into 
2027... 
 
Notably, those with the biggest stockholdings reported a decline in their finances, "consistent 
with continued AI-related agitation in stock markets." Privorotsky says Waller's 50bp talk 
"certainly helped shock the market," and that "financial conditions have tightened extraordinarily 
quickly," with Goldman's US FCI basket at its tightest in a year. 
 

 
 
But the tighter financial conditions may get a little reflexively easier... 
Bonds: The Haven Bid Beats The Hawks 
The week's most telling move was in Treasuries, which rallied for five straight days despite the 
hawks.  
The 10Y fell from 5.17% to 4.90% and the 2Y plunged over 20bps....  
 
Privorotsky: "Hawkish Waller, Fed minutes and ECB regulatory noise, yet bonds rallied. Back 
end well bid, curve bull flattening, front end broadly unchanged." By Wednesday he was buying: 
"I like long front end bonds alongside equity here." Supply went smoothly after last week's three 
tailing auctions: the $16 billion 20Y stopped on the screws at 5.459% with the most indirect 
demand since August ("Average 20Y Auction Prices On The Screws", Nov 18), and Thursday's 
 

 
$19 billion 10Y TIPS reopening drew strong demand at 2.903%, possibly the highest real yield 
since 2008, with dealers left with just 6%, the least since July 2025. 
Privorotsky flagged on Tuesday that utilities had outperformed long bonds by about 5 points 
over 20 sessions: "The equity market is pricing a growth scare that the bond market has yet to 
acknowledge." By Thursday, the bond market had started to acknowledge it, helped by a shock 
from Australia, which lost 43,500 jobs in October against an expected 25,000 gain, sending its 
unemployment rate to 4.9% and its markets pricing rate cuts. On Friday the rally accelerated... 
...ending the 10Y's longest run above 5% since 2007, with the 5Y down 10bps to 4.90%.  
 
Privorotsky thinks the long end "may already be starting to price a policy mistake… yields falling 
and breakevens compressing even as equities and gold liquidate. Increasingly looks like a 
transition from an inflation problem to a growth/liquidity problem." 
 

 
 
 His trade: "Receiving rates and owning duration remains the one trade that feels misaligned 
with what equity internals have been telling us… and I still think that's relatively attractive." 
Credit: The AI Divide Widens 
Credit followed stocks lower, though it held up marginally better (LSEG Lipper data showed 
high-yield funds losing $2.3 billion in the week to Wednesday, the most since mid-September). 
IG Spreads blew out around 10bps to their widest since May 2025...  
 
 

 
...with Goldman noting that 7 of the 10 widest names in the IG index are now AI-related, pulling 
hyperscaler credit risk to a new record high...  
 
Goldman's credit team asked whether AAs are the new junk because the highest-rated 
borrowers are now the biggest AI issuers. 
Goldman's 2027 credit outlook counts $985 billion of AI-related debt coming (including $545 
billion of project finance, $320 billion of which needs a guarantor who has just stepped back), 
and urges investors to "use year-end strength to reload systemic hedges."  
HY Spreads surged to their widest since April (notably underperforming IG spreads, but also 
considerably tighter than VIX would imply)... 
 
 

 
The 'barometer' of the market before the Midterm - Data-Center credit - was smashed wider this 
week (as one would expect with the Central Bank of AI closing its doors... 
 
Those aren't crisis moves, but they came in the week the market had to absorb the largest 
equity raise in history, and the AI funding cracks are spreading up the quality ladder. 
Banks paused a $480 million chip loan tied to ByteDance after the new export rules, and 
Blackstone's QTS cut an $800 million IG deal it couldn't sell. Privorotsky sees a silver lining: 
"reduced AI commitments could be negative for equity growth expectations but positive for 
credit as balance sheet risk diminishes. Less spending, better credit, weaker equity multiples." 
The worse the quality, the bigger the spread widening this week with 'triple hooks' smashed 
back above 1100bps - the widest since 2022... 
 
 

 
When credit wobbles and the Fed won't blink, the liquidation shows up first in whatever is liquid 
and still up on the year... 
Commodities, Currencies, & Alternatives: The "Sources Of 
Funds" Week 
If you needed cash to pay for Anthropic, or to meet a margin call on Nvidia, you sold what was 
liquid and still up on the year: precious metals, commodities, and crypto.  
On paper this was a terrible week to be short oil: Iran struck a US radar installation in Bahrain, 
tankers were set ablaze in Hormuz, the US struck IRGC missile sites on Qeshm Island, Houthis 
hit the Saudi PIF's headquarters in Riyadh, and Iran's attacks on shipping have more than 
doubled compared with prior months. But crude prices plunged around 5% on the week, with 
WTI below $85 for the first time since August... 
 
The IEA said the market has flipped to a surplus for the first time since the war began, Goldman 
warned of a 2 million barrel-a-day glut in 2027, Hormuz exports hit a wartime high of 11.4 million 
barrels a day in October, and US crude inventories rose for a second week. The Trump-Xi-Putin 
summit in Shenzhen added a pledge to "increase the production and supply of refined 
petroleum products," though, as Bloomberg's Javier Blas noted, "the US is maxed out; China 
can do more but only if it buys more crude; Russia is limited by the war in Ukraine" ("Trump, Xi, 
& Putin Unveil Four-Point 'Declaration Toward Peace'", Nov 19). Privorotsky noted the US 3-2-1 
crack spread has fallen 36% in six weeks, and pump prices are at eight-month lows. 
 

 
 
The metals were worse... 
...with Druckenmiller reportedly short and CTAs dumping, sending Silver down 15% on the week 
and clubbing copper and gold down 10% on the week... 
 
Gold broke back below $3600 for the first time since Sept 2025... 
 

 
 
We argued there's no reason to panic, and Goldman's 2027 gold playbook says the same thing 
in fewer words: central banks keep buying, everyone else is selling. 
UBS's Justinus Steinhorst warned clients to "watch for AI fears to morph into a broader growth 
scare across assets. Yields and commodities are starting to move that way" ("Copper 
Slammed", Nov 20).  
Privorotsky's explanation: "Strong dollar, elevated real rates and attractive all in credit yields are 
providing competition for gold/precious. Not a savings glut...it's a spending glut, a less 
supportive backdrop for precious metals and multiples with the Fed tightening." His conclusion 
is the line of the week: "Precious and industrial metals, grains, gasoline and diesel all under 
pressure. A broad disinflationary impulse that could materially change the rates narrative." 
Bloomberg's Simon White offered gold bugs some comfort: "It is not, and has never been, a 
liquidity hedge," and bullion now trades below fair value. 
The dollar dipped today to end a hot week (up 7 of the last 9 days), but still ended at its 
strongest against its fiat peers since April 2025... 
 

 
 
Hartnett's King Dollar "wrecking ball" did most of its damage in EM (with BRL suffering its worst 
week since November 2024, as high-beta and carry currencies were sold). But today - amid all 
the chaos in the US markets - we did see a shift as the haven bid shifts to the yen... 
 
Bitcoin was a bloodbath (starting the week with a Sunday liquidation cascade and never 
recovered. ending down 7 weeks in a row), erasing its entire post-August "debasement" rally, 
crashing to its lowest since Sept 2024... 
 

 
 
Glassnode noted that coins bought in the past six months are now underwater. 
So where did the money go?

What Comes Next 
The market goes into Thanksgiving week below its 200-day, with record short gamma about to 
roll off and the lightest positioning in five years. What we are watching: 
●​ OpEx (today) and the gamma reset: Goldman estimates dealers are "right around max 
dealer short vega" going into expiry, and Nomura's CTA buy triggers sit about 2% and 
4% higher. Privorotsky says "the sheer depth of current pessimism and one sided 
de-grossing suggests the market may be approaching a tactical local low". 
 

 
●​ Macro & The Fed: October PCE comes Wednesday, then payrolls, November CPI 
(which Privorotsky thinks is "potentially the one that forces a recalibration from the Fed") 
and the December 9 FOMC. 
●​ Nvidia: Treasury Secretary Bessent has reportedly given the board about 10 days, until 
around November 25, to name a new leader, Huang is due to testify before House 
panels, and the SEC and California probes continue. Arm's Rene Haas has been 
approached. 
●​ Anthropic: whether $83 holds now that the greenshoe is gone, listed options from 
Monday, and Nasdaq 100 inclusion on December 21 (about $19 billion of passive 
demand). 
●​ Treasury supply: $69 billion of 2Ys, $70 billion of 5Ys and $44 billion of 7Ys in a 
holiday-shortened week, with the 10Y now back below 5%. 
●​ Oil and Iran: Trump is reportedly weighing another strike campaign but says "we'll see 
what happens after New Year," while the IEA's surplus and the Shenzhen fuel pledge 
weigh on prices. 
●​ Yen and global central banks: USDJPY at 160 with the BoJ about 85% priced for a 
December hike; the ECB is also expected to hike in December. 
●​ Seasonality: Goldman notes most of the Santa rally comes after December 15, and that 
when the VIX starts mid-November above 18 it has ended the year lower 14 times out of 
17. 
Bottom Line 
This was the week the AI trade's three pillars all wobbled at once: the hardware leader lost its 
founder and its appetite for financing the ecosystem, the flagship IPO priced as a liquidity event 
rather than a vindication, and Washington tightened the export screws. But the more important 
shift was outside AI. For two months the bond market has argued with the stock market about 
whether the problem is inflation or growth; this week commodities, credit and Treasuries all

Nvidia passed the demand test. Anthropic failed the supply test. And the Fed wants to shock a 
market that's already in shock. 
In short, the AI trade didn't fail a demand test, because Nvidia passed it. It failed a supply test: 
~$120bn of new stock in one week on top of a vendor-financing book nobody can see through, 
absorbed by retail and long-only buyers while the fast money sold, with the underwriters' safety 
net already gone by day two. The good news is that bonds finally acted like a hedge. The bad 
news is that they did it because breakevens, UMich and the Philly Fed are pricing a slowdown 
the Fed says it doesn't see.
