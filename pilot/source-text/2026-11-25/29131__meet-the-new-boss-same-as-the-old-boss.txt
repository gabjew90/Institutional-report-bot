Meet the New Boss, Same as the Old Boss
 
We are pleased to see Huang back at the helm of Strong Buy rated Nvidia, but Nvidia does
not get a do-over. We reduced our valuation multiple with the recent earnings because risk
and uncertainty changed; Huang’s return reduces some uncertainties, but overhangs remain
including the Dec. 1 and Dec. 3 Congressional hearings, questions about Nvidia’s corporate
culture, the board’s independent investigation, a lack of succession planning, and customer
financing. But Huang is not a one-man-band and objectively, fundamentals appear to have
changed very little in the AI market, with Nvidia even increasing its FY28 forecast. While Jensen
Huang has been a stand-out CEO closely associated with the company, we argue that Nvidia is
more than just its CEO. The AI evolution continues and Nvidia remains an important player.
●
The board announced Huang’s return as CEO, as well as the formation of a committee
of independent directors to investigate actions by foreign intelligence, and a committee
of independent directors to oversee trade compliance and national security risks. Outside
council’s investigation continues.
●
The executive branch weighed in. While not explicit, the president implied that he advised
Nvidia’s board that from a national security perspective that it would be safe and advisable
to bring Huang back.
●
Succession is among the board’s responsibilities, and we consider recent events a catalyst
to establish a formal succession plan. Nvidia’s FY26 10-K acknowledges dependence on its
longstanding executive team and the risks of inadequate succession planning. Its proxy
describes management development and board engagement with senior executives, but
identifies no CEO successor. While succession oversight rests with the board, it is not required
to publicly disclose a detailed plan. We believe Nvidia has considerable management depth,
but it has limited investor engagement beyond Huang, the CFO, and IR team, which makes
that depth difficult to assess. We think the company should broaden investor engagement.
●
Nvidia has established a long-term product roadmap, and its customers have plans
that dovetail with this evolution. We doubt the fundamentals supporting the AI industrial
revolution are altered by Jensen Huang’s departure from Nvidia and/or his prominent return.
●
Top Tin Foil Hat Theories Engulfing the AI Trade: The administration’s increasingly
aggressive restrictions on China’s access to AI accelerators, followed by signs of a thaw
in its relationship with Nvidia and Jensen Huang, suggest an internal struggle between
national security hawks and more business-friendly factions, with the former initially holding
the upper hand. Adding to the intrigue is the divide between proponents of open AI
models and those championing proprietary frontier models, raising questions about whether
competing industry interests have helped shape the administration’s posture toward Nvidia
and its leadership. Recent developments suggest the political winds may be shifting, with
commercial interests regaining influence, but the underlying tensions are far from resolved.
