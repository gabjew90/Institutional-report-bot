The anatomy of a market panic
What a market. SPX is back at extreme oversold levels, tech volatility has exploded, and hedge funds
have been dumping exposure at a historic pace.
GS's panic indicator has reached 9.8 out of 10, hedge fund positioning is approaching decade lows,
and some of the biggest extremes since COVID are showing up across markets.
The conditions for a violent bounce are increasingly in place, but the Fed is hiking, and the 2018
playbook continues to haunt this market. Add short gamma to the mix, and moves could get explosive
in either direction.

The great hedge fund unwind
According to GS PB, hedge funds have been aggressively cutting risk, with US single-stock shorting
reaching its highest weekly level since June 2022 and the third highest in a decade. ETF shorts surged
11% in a single week, one of the largest increases in five years. Importantly, the data only runs through
Thursday and does not capture Friday's selling.
GS
1st percentile
Fundamental L/S net leverage has dropped to 42.3%, the 1st percentile over the past year. Beta-
adjusted net leverage has plunged more than 22 percentage points since early October to 48.2%, a
five-year low.

GS
Hedge funds: Near decade lows
According to GS, hedge fund net exposure entered last week at multi-year lows, with global positioning
near 10-year lows. HFs have sold tech at the fastest pace on record over the past six weeks, while
systematic selling has been aggressive. CTA positioning in NDX futures is near the bottom of its 10-
year range. GS believes the unwind is closer to the end than the beginning, although CFTC non-dealer
positioning remains near the highs.
GS
Tech: Oversold, but buyers missing
GS's Peter Callahan notes that tech-heavy indices are firmly oversold, with NDX trading at 17x forward
earnings and semis at just 12–13x. Conditions for a bounce are in place, but risk appetite remains
weak. The big disconnect: charts are pricing in earnings cuts, while fundamentals remain solid and
estimates are still moving higher.
Callahan also highlights the extreme drawdown in tech relative to SPX. Over the past 20+ years, we've
only seen a larger drawdown once.
GS
Extreme panic, but a different Fed
GS's Vol Panic Index ended last week at 9.8/10, historically a strong signal to start adding risk. The
catch is the Fed. During previous panic episodes above 9 with the Fed hiking, SPX returned just +0.4%
on average over the following three months.
