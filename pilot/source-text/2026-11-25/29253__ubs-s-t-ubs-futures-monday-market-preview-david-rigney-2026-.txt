Key Overnight Developments
• Risk sentiment deteriorated sharply through Friday’s European and US sessions as anxiety surrounding the
viability of the artificial-intelligence trade developed into a broad positioning unwind.
• The S&P 500 fell 3.70% and entered a technical correction, recording its worst week since the April 2025
tariff rout. The Nasdaq 100 declined 5.61% on Friday.
• A closely watched chipmaker index fell 11%, while Nvidia declined 14% following a Reuters report that US
regulators were investigating its accounting practices.
• Nvidia had beaten estimates and raised its outlook in its first results since Jensen Huang’s resignation, but its
shares fell to their lowest level since June 2025.

2
• Anthropic’s record IPO initially supported sentiment, but its first-day surge partially reversed and contributed
to a violent reassessment of AI-linked valuations and positioning.
• Equity volatility rose to its highest level since April 2025. The risk-off move spread to metals, Bitcoin and
emerging markets.
• The University of Michigan’s final November consumer-sentiment reading was 51.5, against 51.6 expected.
One-year inflation expectations fell to their lowest level since February.
• Traders ceased to fully price a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate increase in December. Fed Governor Lisa
Cook nevertheless reiterated that inflation remained “unacceptably high” and that the FOMC “does not have
the luxury to wait.”
Rates: Flight to Quality Drives Bull Steepening
US Treasuries rallied sharply as the equity selloff generated flight-to-quality demand and the market unwound
part of its December rate-hike premium.
Shortly after 3pm ET, Treasury yields were 10bp to 15bp lower across the curve. The move was a bull
steepening, with 2s10s and 5s30s widening by approximately 2bp and 5bp respectively. The session
commentary places the US 10-year yield near 4.92%, down 12bp on the day.
European sovereign bonds followed Treasuries higher:
• The German 10-year Bund yield fell 10bp to 3.30%, its lowest level since 12 November and its strongest
daily performance since early April.
• The UK two-year gilt yield declined 8bp to 5.23%, with gilts heading for their largest weekly gain since early
August.
• ECB Governing Council member Joachim Nagel said the central bank should raise rates next month into a
range where they begin to constrain the economy.
Equities: AI Unwind Broadens Globally
European equities suffered their worst week since April 2025, with the Stoxx 600 down 2.00% on Friday.
Technology recorded its largest weekly decline in more than six years as the AI-led selloff spread across
global markets.
Investors accelerated a rotation towards defensive sectors, including personal care, food and healthcare.
Poland’s WIG20 fell 4.5% after a Russian incursion into Polish airspace refocused attention on geopolitical
risks arising from the war in neighbouring Ukraine.
Commodities and Cross-Asset Volatility
Copper was on course for one of its worst weeks of the past decade as concerns over AI-related demand
spilled into industrial metals.
Oil headed for a third consecutive weekly decline. Brent held below $92 a barrel after a volatile Thursday
session and was down approximately 4% over the week. Traders balanced the possibility of renewed US
strikes on Iran against signs of ample global crude supply.
APAC Opening Setup
SPI futures closed at 8,556, down 49 points, or 56.9bp, from the previous futures close and 60 points, or
69.6bp, below Friday’s 8,616 cash close.

Overnight SPI volume was 20,862 contracts, equivalent to 138% of the 10-day average of 15,112.5 contracts.
Friday’s AS51 traded value was AUD9.305 billion, or 122.3% of its 10-day average.
At the time of publication:
• E-mini S&P 500 futures were 304.8bp below their level at the Australian cash close.
• CME Nikkei futures were 367.5bp below their level at the Australian cash close.
• Japan is operating under a partial Labour Thanksgiving Day holiday schedule.
Desk-level read: The APAC setup is defensive, with AI-linked equities and cyclicals exposed to the global
positioning unwind. Duration may retain a flight-to-quality bid, although thin Japanese holiday liquidity could
amplify price action.

Desk Takeaways
• Positioning, rather than economic data, is driving the immediate move. The acceleration in equity
losses followed an unwind in crowded AI and technology exposures despite a broadly uneventful US
sentiment release.
• Cross-asset correlations have shifted decisively towards risk reduction. Equities, metals, Bitcoin and
emerging markets weakened while developed-market sovereign bonds rallied.
• Australian futures point to a materially weaker open. Elevated SPI volume suggests active repricing
rather than a low-volume marking exercise.
• Holiday liquidity is a consideration. Japan’s partial closure may reduce regional depth and increase gap
risk.
• Preliminary PMIs are the next macro test. Releases from India, Europe, the UK and US will indicate
whether the market’s growth assumptions validate or challenge Friday’s positioning-led move.
