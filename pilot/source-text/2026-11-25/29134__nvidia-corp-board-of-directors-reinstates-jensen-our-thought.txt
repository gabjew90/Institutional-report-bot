Summary Thoughts
This morning NVDA announced Jensen Huang’s reinstatement as CEO (here), a clear 
positive and consistent with our view that the story was not necessarily over and in many 
ways framed  our decision to keep the faith and not downgrade  the stock. As we 
highlighted previously (here), his departure created meaningful uncertainty around 
financing, supply-chain strategy, and talent retention. While these should now recede, 
the issue of land powered shell is not going away - especially if President Trump follows 
through on comments from Friday dissuading companies from building data centers 
outside the US. The question we are getting now is what are the other bank shots from 
this - e.g. how will things change for NVDA and the industry post all of these 
developments. With respect to NVDA: 1)  it seems likely that the management structure 
of the company could change with more of a hierarchical structure to enable clearer 
management succession plans; 2) it is tempting to say that the company could pull back 
a little bit on financing of the ecosystem, but it also now has ~$390B  of supply 
commitments, so to some degree it needs to keep this flywheel operating in full force to 
stimulate demand offtake. With respect to the broader industry read-throughs: 1) the 
fact that US intelligence established  connections at Megaspeed to Chinese intelligence  
could drive more hawkish export control actions, the clearest of which could  be new 
additions to the US Department of Commerce Entity List or passage of the MATCH Act 
(we have written about this here and here, though we continue to cite that MATCH 
would have broader potential negative implications across the entire tech hardware 
landscape as it could produce "matched set" issues similar to the COVID timeframe). For 
NVDA, the focus should now shift back toward execution, the product roadmap, and 
the broader AI opportunity. Our $220 PT remains unchanged.
Valuation: PT $220 (unchanged)
Our PT remains unchanged at $220, based on a ~12x C2028E P/E multiple (unchanged) 
applied to our C2028E EPS of $18.80 (unchanged).

Company Description
NVIDIA Corp. designs and manufactures graphics processing units, add-in boards, and 
related low-level software. Its Graphics Processing Unit segment includes GeForce for high-
end video games and professional graphics workstations; Quadro for designers; DGX and 
Tesla Accelerator cards for data center and AI scientists; and GRID for cloud-based visual 
computing users. The Tegra Processors segment provides processors that enable embedded 
computer vision applications in automotive video surveillance end markets, as well as 
delivering multimedia experiences on mobile gaming platforms, while consuming minimal 
power.
Valuation Method and Risk Statement
NVDA: Our price target is based on applying a P/E multiple. NVIDIA company-specific risks 
include: 1) competition from AMD in graphics processors (GPUs) and professional 
visualization products, which historically have driven the bulk of the company’s gross profit, 
2) intense competition in ARM-based applications processors, where rivals seek to leapfrog 
NVIDIA’s early lead in dual-core technology, 3) emerging competition from Intel with its 
many-integrated core (MIC) processor family vs. the company’s professional GPU computing 
products, where Intel’s approach could be more flexible and easier to develop software. In 
addition, NVIDIA faces the semiconductor sector risk where revenue trends have historically 
been linked to corporate profitability, which could be challenged for robust growth, as the 
economic recovery has been more subdued vs. prior deep downturns.
