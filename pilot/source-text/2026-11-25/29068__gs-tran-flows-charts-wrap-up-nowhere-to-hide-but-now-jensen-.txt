We have now the largest IPO ever behind us, another blockbuster listing in

It could have been all so nice toward the weekend, had we ended there...
Some had hoped this to be a catalyst for the start of a constructive year
end (raising my hand here).... After a great start into the session, we saw
an aggressive and ugly intraday reversal with large peak-to-trough bands.
NDX -4.6% & SPX -3.26% and VIX at around 28 handle. No clear single
catalyst, most point to an AMD short report from Hunterbrook, mixed with
some technicals such as forced liquidation, trigger levels, selling to fund or

profit taking. HFs continue to de-gross, institutional investors, from my seat,
have been rather idle. Sentiment is very cautious, but not yet panicked.
Hawkish Fed-speak beforehand was mostly ignored by markets, possibly
overshadowed by the anticipation and excitement towards the IPO. NY
Fed’s Williams comment certainly pointed more hawkish: “inflation is
stubbornly high and we need to get it down”. Waller had already added a
50bps hike comment earlier. This gives 2018 vibes when the Fed was
hiking into crowded tech positions as yields broke out.
Oil lower with continued, though result-less talks to end Iran & Ukraine
wars, but currently not helping stocks. First ever Tri-lateral meeting in
Shenzhen between Trump, Xi and Putin showed no real takeaways. Plan to
increase petrol supply, that helped. Trump is scheduled to brief on the
90min meeting later on.
What the current price action shows is that the market continues to be
extremely fragile, with little conviction and a steep wall of worry to climb.
Pessimism has fully taken over. There are voices, however, that pockets of
the market look oversold. The issue though: no one wants to be the first to
step in meaningfully... everyone looking for a big buyer to go first. Right
now, that is not the case. Retail has tried its luck but felt the pain
immediately. As of the time of writing NKY -7,7% / TAIEX -6,2% / KOSPI
-8% for the day. Nasdaq is holding onto its 2.74% ytd gains, SPX +4.8%.
HSI & CSI are down double digit for the year. Gold now at $3.5k and with
that -37% from its peak. Bitcoin trending above $62k level. This is not only
ugly to look at, but also puts many investors ytd margins to a test,
institutional as well as retail (again, raising my hand here).
Are we there yet...? Almost. I hope. Maybe soon. We are now at a true
testing point of risk sentiment once again, with most performance having
been wiped out in the past few weeks. The question is, what can be the
catalyst to turn this around again from here? I am looking at Nov CPI / Fed,
geopolitics (some sort of peace deal in any region), clarity towards NVDA
and AMD... As hopeful as I want to be, it is hard to ignore the pressure
across assets right now with lots of overspilling effects.

Having said all that... I continue to power through the pain. Positioning
continues to be very light, we are in oversold territory in many pockets of
the markets, the overall AI trade is still intact with demand being much
higher than capacity, fundamentals are strong. Stock pickers can go to
work. Currently though, there are not many places to hide. I expect us to go
another leg lower before things get better, though the tape will continue to
be volatile with aggressive swings to be prepared for (both ways). More
dovish data points or Fed comments would certainly help. GIR stays bullish
S&P.

More discipline... While the equity market outlook points to a smaller
tailwind from beta in 2027, alpha opportunities remain substantial. One
striking contrast with past overextended bull markets is the lack of
speculative mania in today’s market. Recent equity returns have generally
tracked near-term earnings outlooks, both at the index level and across
stocks. Active fund managers faced a difficult backdrop in much of 2026.
But earnings-driven returns, elevated dispersion, and improving equity
market breadth bode well for stock pickers heading into the new year.

CTAs... Are continued overall seller. We estimate that global equity length
has been cut from ca. $410bn to $290bn vs 3m ago. This is roughly a 5/10
rank on a simple min-max scale (and lower as a true percentile). S&P and

Mutual Funds... 42% of large-cap mutual funds are outperforming their
benchmarks YTD vs. the historical average of 37%. 81% of large-cap
growth funds are outperforming, the highest share in over a decade. Value
funds have posted the strongest YTD returns (+14%) but just 4% of large-
cap value funds are outperforming as they struggle to match their
benchmarks. Large-cap mutual funds are most overweight Industrials
(+200 bp), followed closely by Health Care (+199 bp, a 10-year high) and

Financials (+196 bp). Funds cut their Financials tilt by 35 bp, the largest
reduction across sectors, led by Banks. Funds remain most underweight
Info Tech (-631 bp), but they lifted their tilt by 29 bp. Funds across styles
trimmed tilts to Consumer Staples and Utilities. Large-cap mutual funds
were 722 bp underweight the Mag 7 at the end of Q3 2026, effectively
unchanged during the quarter. The average fund is underweight each of the
Mag 7 except AMZN, which is now an average fund overweight for the first
time since 2016. A majority of funds reduced ownership in most of the Mag
7 stocks last quarter. The largest reductions were in GOOGL and AAPL.
Hedge Funds... MTD, the Global Prime book registered heavy net selling,
driven primarily by long liquidations concentrated in North America and
Asia. A distinct risk-off tone led emerging markets to see sharper net selling
and de-grossing relative to developed markets. Asian equities experienced
aggressive selling across the Information Technology and AI sectors, where
long sales outpaced short additions by a 2.4-to-1 margin. South Korea and
Taiwan led the de-risking as hedge funds simultaneously trimmed long
positions and initiated new shorts, resetting net exposure in Taiwan to
February levels and Korea to early January levels. Japan and China saw
more modest net selling contributions. Broad de-allocation occurred across
9 of 11 sectors. Info Tech led the outflows, followed by Industrials and
Consumer Discretionary. Conversely, defensive sectors—specifically
Utilities and Consumer Staples—logged slight net buying.
US ETF Inflows... Inflows into US-listed equity ETFs accelerated to $59.3
billion last week (surpassing the prior week's $55.2 billion), outperforming
91% of the 46 weeks recorded year-to-date and more than doubling the
2026 weekly average of $26.5 billion; in stark contrast, fixed-income ETF
allocations tumbled from $13.7 billion to just $2.6 billion, sliding into net
redemptions on Thursday and Friday following a hotter-than-anticipated
CPI inflation report.
Global Fund Flows... Global equity funds saw inflows accelerate to $98
billion for the week ending November 11 (up from $89 billion the prior
week), driven by returning foreign capital to the US that lifted the four-week

moving average by ~$5 billion; meanwhile, money market fund assets
contracted by $18 billion as strong risk-asset performance and appealing
longer-dated yields continued to divert capital from cash, setting up a key
trend to monitor as risk-asset returns potentially moderate in the coming
year.
GIR Bullish S&P500... We expect positive but moderating US equity
returns in 2027 alongside slowing earnings growth. We forecast the S&P
500 will rise by 17% to 8,700 by year-end. This compares with a 21%
annualized return during the past four years. Earnings will continue to drive
returns, and a solid economy and continued AI investment should generate
robust but decelerating earnings growth in 2027. We expect EPS growth
will slow from roughly 30% in 2026 to 18% in 2027. Full outlook here.
AI’s – who’s next... One candidate for the next runner in the AI relay race
are the hyperscalers omce again, which have increasingly shown signs of
monetization of their AI investments. Based on investment spending
through 2027, the "required revenues" that need to be generated for
sufficient returns appear optimistic but potentially achievable. Cloud
revenue growth for several of the hyperscalers has accelerated from 25%
in 2024 to 54% in Q3 2026. These companies have also announced
revenue backlogs totaling $1.8 trillion, offering line of sight in their ability to
meet the required revenues.
Credit financing... Our credit strategists expect total US IG and HY debt
issuance will reach $2.8 trillion in 2027, up from $2.4 trillion in 2026.
