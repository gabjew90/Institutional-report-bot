1) ADAM CROOK – 2 Mins Read: Volatile Week Ahead, Risk Thoughts – LINK
Ahead of what promises to be a volatile Thanksgiving Week in markets, some reflections from GS
thought-leaders in the Tech, AI, Semis + broad Equities space + some thoughts on risk-reward, and
potential catalysts from here. Takeaways below – full piece available here.
Ben Snider (Chief US Equity Strategist): “Ultimately, the key question for the market is where earnings
will come from next.. who is going to come along and take the baton from the AI infrastructure and CapEx
build out that has really driven earnings growth in 2026… that can come from an improvement in the
macro backdrop, if we see lower oil and lower rates, or it can come from a continued advancement in the
AI story, but we do need that new source of earnings, both to avoid a broad earnings downturn for the
market and to provide new leadership for the market. We should acknowledge we're just wrapping up the
Q3 earnings season right now, and S&P 500 companies reported earnings growth of over 35% in the
quarter, so this is not about earnings today or tomorrow. The market is forward looking and that is
certainly what it's wrestling with right now.”
Pete Callahan (US TMT Sector Specialist): “Exiting the week, Tech-heavy indices are now firmly in
oversold territory (NDX, SOX, Info Tech GICs), valuations at >10 year lows (NDX = 17x fwd P/E +
SOX/Semis = ~12-13x fwd P/E) & ‘drawdown’ charts suggesting a lot of work has been done (see Chart 1
below). In turn, the conditions for a ‘bounce’ feel present, that said, optimism around a year-end rally
appear to have faded as even if one feels good about the health of fundamentals (firm compute prices,
strong bottoms up company soundbites, big beat/raise prints from KEYS / NVDA / PANW this past week),
price action has put a thaw on risk appetite as the lack of identifiable catalysts to improve sentiment +
lack of confidence = buyers strike (with some ‘rotational’ (unwind) dynamics at play under the hood).
To put an underline on the above point – what makes this moment so hard is that many AI supply chain /
semiconductor stock charts appear to be suggesting ‘cuts’ are coming .. the ‘problem’ is, most investors
we speak to don’t see that as a likely near-term catalyst (in fact, estimate revisions are likely to move
higher in the near-term with earnings from DELL, MU, AVGO, HPE around the corner), which, almost
makes stepping in a harder dynamic given the lack of clarity re: who is right - charts or companies?. From
here, keep an eye on industry developments (NVDA headlines, US data centre policy, AI adoption trends)
as investors at least try to build some fundamental confidence into 2027.”

Dom Wilson (Senior Macro Advisor): “The big worry has really been around the potential cracks in the
AI story… that's a mix of the financing needs, signs that the investment impulse might be peaking +
worries about the forward earnings picture.. that's come against a backdrop where the market's put a lot
of value into that space over the last few years, and there's a lot of concentrated exposure…
Unlike the 1990s, the current earnings picture is very good, so the worries are much more about how
persistent these record earnings are going to be and how to think about that profitability a couple of years
out from here after the investment cycle peaks. A number of things have come together: signs that
upgrades to the CapEx cycle are slowing, pressure on credit, a fresh Fed tightening cycle, a shift in the
political backdrop – that’s leading people to take some of the weight off the deep upside in that
distribution and put more weight onto the downside stories…
But there isn't a clear single macro worry yet – measures of growth don't look different to our forecasts at
the moment, and there isn't a single source of external pressure… that makes it trickier in terms of what
provides the floor… the generic lessons are that you get that either because there's reassuring news or
there's a shift in the driver / the policy reaction… if you don't get those things, it may be that there is
enough cheapening to encompass a lot of the risks people are worried about.”

2) Equities Risk Views
Rich Privorotsky, Head of EMEA Flow Intermediation and European One Delta Trading, out with his latest
views. Key takeaways below; full piece here.
Overnight: Markets bouncing after a weekend of support measures across Asia… China lowering margin
requirements, Korea easing buyback restrictions and Taiwan tightening short selling rules. Helpful on the
margin, but these are the sort of sticking plasters you don’t really want to see marking the bottom (Japan
closed).
Positioning: Prime brokerage data suggest nets came down aggressively gross increased materially and
investors added substantial shorts into the decline. "Fundamental L/S Gross leverage -3.6 pts to 204.8%
(13th percentile 1-year) and Net leverage -3.1 pts to 42.3% (1st percentile 1-year). Beta Adjusted
Fundamental L/S Gross leverage -8.3 pts to 214.2% (1-year low) and Beta Adjusted Net leverage -8.8 pts
to 48.2% (5-year low)… This week’s shorting activity was the largest since April 2025." Breaking the 200d
triggered further de-risking. The difference now is that investors aren’t simply defensively positioned…
they’ve now got meaningful shorts in the book. Arguably the missing ingredient. The setup for a bear
market rally is glaringly obvious. The question is whether you’re meant to sell into it or let it breathe.
Vol/Sentiment: Vol reached extreme levels into the weekend, with the VIX kissed 40 and genuine left tail
stress emerging. Normalization should itself be a tailwind. CNN Fear & Greed around 10, put/call ratios
near the 95th percentile, CFTC leveraged funds reportedly net short Nasdaq futures and the VIX curve at
its steepest inversion since April 2025. Historically, VIX closes above 35 have often preceded positive
equity reversals over the following weeks. Small samples, not a forecast, but the asymmetry looks
compelling. A lot of bad news has been priced into a very short period.
Risk: Beyond the AI noise, the pivotal question for equities is how the Fed assesses the balance of risks
between growth and inflation. The distinction now is between a positioning squeeze and a genuine
recovery in risk appetite. Credit is the tell. If Nvidia rallies but neocloud spreads remain under pressure,
we’re probably just covering shorts. The broader concern is that AI infrastructure has become increasingly
dependent on external capital precisely as the cost of that capital rises. We’ve moved beyond a routine
correction into something resembling a bear market in parts of AI. The structural questions won’t
disappear overnight, but the combination of extreme positioning, elevated vol, a potential Nvidia policy
pivot and a more visible Trump put creates a compelling tactical setup.

3) FX STRATEGY (CAHILL / TRIVEDI) – Global FX Trader: Tightening Tremors – LINK
Policy Shocks & The USD: From an FX perspective, policy shocks now spilling over into growth
concerns has meant that Dollar strength rotated from DM to EM. It’s true that over the medium-term
faltering market expectations for AI-intensive capex fueled growth should be negative for the Dollar. But in
the near-term, a hawkish US policy shock boosts the Dollar against the G10 (the post-CPI moves). When
that then weakens growth expectations, it is often the most painful type of Dollar strengthening for cyclical
assets like EM FX, and that is what developed last week.
2027 Outlook: Many of our client discussions regarding our 2027 FX Outlook have focused on whether
markets will be able to look through the inflation peak and trade the turning tides ahead of time. We are
finding client views on this timing question to be more mixed than on the shape of the macro trajectory,
but we think these recent challenges support our view that the Dollar still has some fight left, and should
soften only gradually in 2027. Historically, markets typically only realize the peak of hiking cycles with the
benefit of hindsight.
Opportunities Across EM & G10: Cyclical models go a long way towards explaining relative FX moves
lately, together with a few idiosyncratic domestic developments. That helps inform where we see
opportunities in currencies from here. We think COP and ZAR still have attractive domestic fundamentals
for the most part. In G10, we’re focused on relative policy developments in AUD and CAD.

4) US ECONOMICS (MERICLE) – What Does the Tightening in Financial Conditions Mean for
Growth and the Fed? – LINK
Tightening of Financial Conditions: Our Financial Conditions Index (FCI) has tightened by about 100bp
this year, with all of that coming since early September and over 40bp coming last week alone. The key
contributors to the tightening are the rise in interest rates over the course of the year and the more recent
decline in equity prices and appreciation of the dollar.
Implications for Growth: We estimate that if the tightening persists, it will impose a 0.5pp drag on 2027
Q4/Q4 GDP growth, a few tenths more than is implied by the interest rate and other assumptions
underlying our 2027 forecasts. If instead our market strategists’ forecasts prove right, the incremental FCI
tightening would reverse next year.
Implications for the Fed: A second hike in December is a near certainty, and while the implications for
potential hikes beyond December are more uncertain, we continue to expect a third hike in March, for a
few reasons. First, the tightening is equivalent to the usual impact of 100bp of rate hikes, and most FOMC
participants likely now expect 75-100bp of hikes in total. Second, some of the tightening has come from a
rise in long-term interest rates driven largely by expectations of additional hikes. Third, unlike in some
recent episodes when FCI tightening stopped the FOMC from hiking, today inflation is high and growth is
a bit above potential, so FOMC participants are less likely to worry that hiking might do more harm than
good. That said, further tightening—especially if it is led by a deterioration in risk sentiment or is
connected to concerns about a pullback in AI spending—could displace rate hikes next year.
