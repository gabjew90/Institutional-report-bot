UK’s October Budget Deficit Comes In Smaller Than Expected - The UK's October public sector net
borrowing (PSNB) came in at £17 bn — below the Bloomberg consensus estimate of £18 bn and down from a
revised £22.9 bn in September. The smaller-than-forecast deficit was supported by a resilient economy, which
helped offset upward pressure on spending from rising inflation, leaving the cumulative deficit for the first
seven months of the fiscal year at £111.3 bn — £7.3 bn (or 6.1%) below the same period last year, according
to the ONS. Debt costs rose to £11.2 bn last month from £8.4 bn a year earlier, with around a quarter of UK
government debt is linked to RPI inflation, which hit 4.2% in October and is forecast to exceed 5% early next
year. While the October print offers some near-term relief for Chancellor Healey, the fiscal backdrop remains
tight: elevated inflation continues to drive up debt interest costs and public spending pressures, and the
year-to-date borrowing figure still runs ahead of official projections. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
UK's October Retail Sales Beat Estimates; Consumer Confidence Edges Up - The UK's October retail
sales figures came in well ahead of expectations across all measures on Friday. Retail sales including auto
fuel rose 0.7% m/m (versus Bloomberg estimates of 0.0%) and 2.3% y/y (versus Bloomberg estimates of
1.7%), while the ex-fuel measure also beat, rising 0.9% m/m (versus Bloomberg estimates of 0.1%) and 2.5%
y/y (versus Bloomberg estimates of 1.8%). The ONS said Food stores sales volumes rose as supermarkets
reported increased sales of long-life items such as tinned goods and UHT milk following government advice,
while art galleries and clothing stores also rose on the month. The stronger-than-expected print arrives
alongside a modest improvement in consumer sentiment — GfK's consumer confidence index edged up 2
points to negative 22 in November, with the index of big-ticket purchase intentions rising 5 points to negative
17 after two consecutive months of declines, suggesting some recovery in appetite for discretionary spending
ahead of the festive season. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)

France’s Pension Problem - Pensions now account for one in every four euros the French government
spends on all public goods and services. John Burn-Murdoch, writing for the Financial Times [paywall], does a
deep dive into the global pension problem, where retirees are more affluent than ever, but workers’ pay is
increasingly being eaten up by state pension spending in high-income countries.  In 2024, the French
government spent more on pensions than on education, policing, housing, infrastructure and defence
combined. In fact, almost a quarter of what the French government spends on education each year goes on
pensions rather than actually delivering education. Far-right candidate Marine Le Pen, the frontrunner in next
year’s French election, has promised to reverse President Emmanuel Macron’s pension reform, which
increased the retirement age from 62 to 64, and bring it to as low as 60. In fact, her idea this week was to take
more money away from education to use in her pension fund. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
SpaceX At 8.25% Is A Problem For All Of Credit - SpaceX sold $32 bn of debt at 8.25% on Thursday. The
company is rated BBB. The average for the whole BBB sector is 5.70%. This is the problem being faced by the
credit market and more specifically the underlying companies – tech is (and can afford to) offer yields far
above the rest of the corporate space. It’s not the Treasury market that’s being crowded out, it’s the rest of
credit. For example, SpaceX at BBB is offering he same yield one could currently only achieve by dropping
down two full notches to singe B, in which case one is also dropping from investment grade into high yield.
(Simon Penn, Macro Sales)

UK-EU Reset Takes Another Step Forward - UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham meets EU leaders in
Brussels on Friday to formalise the next stage of the post-Brexit reset, centered on agreements covering
agri-food trade, carbon markets and youth mobility. Earlier this week, Burnham said the package could add
around 0.4% to UK GDP, according to the Guardian. Burnham has described the summit as part of a longer
process, with any more fundamental change in the UK-EU relationship likely to take time. Important fault lines
remain: the UK has resisted dynamic alignment in financial services, while differences over China policy could
complicate access to the EU’s Made in Europe procurement programme. Electricity-market integration,
migration, security and defence cooperation have largely been deferred to future negotiations. Politically, the
direction of travel has clearly shifted towards closer UK-EU ties, although both sides remain cautious about
how far the reset can go. Burnham has openly raised the prospect of eventual re-entry, while France’s
Emmanuel Macron and Spain’s Pedro Sánchez have signalled openness to closer ties. Brussels, however,
continues to stress there can be no cherry-picking, with deeper integration requiring greater acceptance of EU
rules. (Wayne Gouro, Macro Sales)

UBS CIO Remains Constructive On Copper Despite Lower Prices - The benchmark three-month LME
copper price fell over 7% over the last two days to the lowest level since April amid concerns about AI’s growth
trajectory. UBS Wealth Management CIO thinks the sharp decline reflects a combination of weaker sentiment
and rising exchange inventories rather than a deterioration in copper’s underlying fundamentals. Mine-supply
growth continues to disappoint, tight concentrate availability is reflected in deeply negative treatment charges,
and demand from electrification and power-grid investment remains robust. China’s physical market is also
tightening, with Shanghai inventories near multi-year lows and the Yangshan copper cathode premium over
LME spot prices rising to a record high this month. CIO views periods of weakness as opportunities to add
exposure as part of a broader commodity allocation that is managed actively, maintaining its $15,500/mt price
forecast by mid-2027. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
