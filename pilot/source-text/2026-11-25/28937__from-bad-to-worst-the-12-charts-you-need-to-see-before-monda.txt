From bad to worst
It's been quite a week on Wall Street. From collapsing tech to hedge fund deleveraging and GFC-era
short interest, the selloff has produced some truly extraordinary readings. Here are our twelve
favourites, counting down from bad to downright extreme.
12 - Tech trend trashed
Tech is now well below its channel relative to the rest of the S&P 500.
Deutsche Bank
11 - Systematics
And the systematic crowd has joined the selling. Positioning has turned modestly short, but remains
well above previous extremes. In other words, the machines still have plenty of room to sell.
Deutsche Bank
10 - Illiquidity
And just as selling pressure builds, liquidity disappears. S&P futures liquidity – measured by top-of-book
depth – is at the lowest level since Apr’25 and mirrors other stressed episodes.
GS trading desk
9 - Shorts smell blood

Goldman's prime brokerage desk reports the heaviest week of single-stock shorting since June 2022,
with short flow reaching roughly 1.5% of gross market value.
Goldman
8 - Humiliated VIPs
 Recent drawdown of Hedge Fund VIPs among worst on record. Performance fee bonus at risk of being
cancelled.
Goldman
7 - As far as HF leverage is concerned...
The decline from 12m highs is now in line with some of the most extreme episodes we’ve seen since
2018.
JPM PI
6 - Fundamental L/S net leverage

Fundamental L/S net leverage has fallen to 42.3%, the 1st percentile over the past year. On a beta-
adjusted basis, net leverage has fallen more than 22 pts since the start of October to 48.2%, a five-year
low.
GS Prime
5 - MoMo goes from hero to zero
Momentum exposure has also completely reversed, from a record +70% in June to negative today, the
lowest since late 2022.
GS Prime
4 - Sigma
As the chart below shows, the S&P finished today 4.10 standard deviations below its 50-day moving
average. The last time it was this oversold was more than a decade ago on August 25th, 2015.
Bespoke
3 - Contraction
Here's where things get interesting. Equity positioning has fallen to levels historically associated with
outright earnings contractions. Investors are already positioned for an earnings recession. Whether
earnings will actually oblige remains to be seen.

Deutsche Bank
2 - Short interest at GFC levels
The shorts are as loaded as they have been in over a decade. Median short interest ended at 3.1% and
the 90th-percentile stock at 8%, both having "risen sharply" from the 2021 lows back toward the 2008-
2010 peaks. The last time the tape looked like this, the squeezes were violent.
Goldman
And finally, the chart that spoils the
capitulation party.
Despite all the extremes above, Deutsche Bank's consolidated equity positioning is only at the 11th
percentile since 2010 — less than one standard deviation below average and still well above previous
capitulation lows.
In other words, investors are bearish. Very bearish. But nowhere near as bearish as they have been.
The scariest part of these twelve charts? We might not have reached maximum fear yet.
