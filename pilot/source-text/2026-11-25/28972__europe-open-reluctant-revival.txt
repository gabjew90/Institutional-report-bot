Europe Open - Reluctant revival
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
US equity futures and gold stage a strong rebound with Nasdaq futures up nearly 3% and Gold +2.5%. Asian stocks still
point negative, but have recovered from overnight lows, Taiex is best regional performer. Last week's FX worst
performers top the list overnight (ZAR, ILS) with JPY leading losses as safe haven demand eases. USTs were closed
overnight thanks to Japan holiday, the recovery in risk points to a weaker open, despite AU/NZ bonds holding gains
overnight. Oil prices flat at print - US President Trump indicated that talks could last into year-end, but the situation on
ground remains shaky as Iran attacked an industrial facility in Bahrain on Saturday. 
Global PMIs due shortly with France starting at 08:15 GMT, Germany, Eurozone aggregate at 09:00. Resilience should
persist wih EZ manufacturing coming at 53. UK and US PMIs due later. In EM, Brazil inflation data, Mexico and Peru
GDP, ILS rate decision (hold) due. IDR rallied after reports that MSCI are in talks with the Indonesian government to find
alternatives in avoiding standalone downgrade. 20d South Korean export data remained robust.

In Focus‌
Equities: Asian equities mostly traded negative with Kospi -3% but traded as low as -5% while Taiex was in the green
(+2%), after stabilizing measures were introduced. Sharp rebound in US equity futures with SPX e-minis up 2%, Nasdaq
futures up 2.9% at print. Our futures desk is watching for a close above 7016 as the first step, 7149 the first congestion level.
CETS remain wary and are still not active dip buyers since positioning-driven moves are unpredictable. We expect elevated
headline risk in the weeks to come around the AI infrastructure cycle and continue to recommend strong vs. weak balance
sheet (CGP3PROB) stocks on a beta neutral basis. 
Today at 13:00 GMT: Citi Equity Risk Update. Equity markets face increasing pressure from macro, cross-asset, geopolitical
and earnings growth risks. We have not been dip buyers here. Please join us to explore how those risks are impacting client
portfolios, sentiment and crowded positions with senior members of our equity and derivatives sales & trading teams.
Rates: USTs were closed overnight due to Japan holiday, but futures fell overnight as equity sentiment corrected, with AU/NZ
bonds still holding gains. US Rates Strategy thinks that concerns around the AI selloff will likely restrain the Fed from hiking

too quickly. 5y should outperform the curve, but don't have a strong view on when the selloff will stabilize, keeping them
neutral on duration in the near-term. 
FX: USD losses picking up, EURUSD back above 1.10, JPY leading losses trading at 158.96 against USD. Last week's worst
performers, ZAR and ILS outperform overnight thanks to the stock stabilization, but participation remains on the lighter end
with overall interbank volumes in the Asia session -14% below 30d averages, with EURUSD and USDJPY volumes -35%
lower. G10 gamma a touch off Friday's highs, but vol remains supported amid broad uncertainty, with JPY and CHF risk
crosses outperforming. 
Commodities: Gold jumps 2.4% after its biggest weekly decline in 8 months, but copper was more choppy, now trading 1%
at print. Our strategists warned on Friday that they wanted to wait before dipping back into risk-exposed assets. Gold should
be a reasonable medium to long-term buy at $3,000/oz-3,300/oz, with perhaps $500/oz downside from those levels and up to
$2,000/oz upside taking a 12-24 month view. Meanwhile oil prices trade flat. In an interview on Sunday US President Trump
indicated that he might let Iran talks continue into year-end, which somewhat reduces chances of near-term significant
escalation. However on Saturday, Iran struck an industrial facility in Bahrain, so the situation on ground remains fragile. 
 
Monday must reads:
Cheatsheet - G10 & EM Week Ahead: Shopping for direction Risk sentiment will continue to take cues from equities with the
week ahead relatively lighter on major catalysts. Macro data should remain constructive, with flash PMIs across G3 (Mon) and
Germany's Ifo survey (Tue) expected to reinforce resilient growth trends. US data dump on Wed includes core PCE, GDP
second release, jobless claims and durable goods orders. Elsewhere, Australia CPI, Tokyo CPI and preliminary Eurozone CPI
will be monitored for further signs of disinflation. Fed is on blackout but ECB speakers and BoE Pill feature at a conference in
Vienna, BoC Macklem speaks elsewhere.
BoK will likely deliver a 25bp hike, with expectations of another in Feb. Eyes on Korean 20d export data and Taiwan IP for
reads on semiconductor demand. US equities and bond markets will be fully closed on Thu for US Thanksgiving holiday and
closing early on Fri which should result in thinner activity and liquidity into the weekend.
Weekly Macro Brief by Jim McCormick. Markets are pricing a potentially big growth slowdown, but data are not yet showing
this. This week's heavy batch of growth sentiment data will be key to watch. Recent volatility in bond markets validates my
neutral view, but there is now scope to tilt risk a little more bearish. The front-end rallies in USD, JPY and GBP look
excessive. The rising core inflation gap between the US and other G10 countries is one of many reasons to sell USTs cross-
market. Commodity price risk should be more two-way from here.
Elsewhere‌
EUR: Preliminary S&P Global PMI’s starting with France (08:15 BST) followed by Germany (08:30 BST) and Eurozone (09:00
BST). French weakness could persist; peripheral strength could keep the EZ aggregate print strong. Robust manufacturing
PMI at 53 (Citi e.) for the Eurozone will support that a slow manufacturing recovery remains in place. Central bankers feature
at a conference on European economic integration, with ECB Kocher speaking with NBP Szalamacha at 11:30 GMT. Simkus,
BoE Pill speak at 13:00 GMT with Kocher featuring again at 14:45.
GBP: S&P Global UK Services PMI at 09:30 GMT for November P. A steady print should reinforce expectations of a Dec MPC
hike. Citi Economics expects services at 53, composite at 52.7
USD: S&P Global US Manufacturing PMI at 14:45 GMT for November. PMI should remain solid at 55.7 for services, 54.5 for
manufacturing (Bloomberg cons.). Beyond headline strength, anecdotes within the report will be more interesting to monitor,
such as output and input prices. Also watch - US 2y auction (69bn) at 18:00 GMT and Fed Williams speaking at 21:00 GMT.
 
Don't miss in EM:
KRW: 20d export data remain robust at 101.9% YoY thanks to semis demand. New note from Citi Economics highlights that if
KOSPI unexpectedly falls further to, 4,500, 4,000 and 3,500, we roughly estimate the NPS would have capacity to purchase
KRW17trn, KRW39trn and KRW62trn of domestic equities, respectively. We continue to believe the financial authorities could

deploy further market-stabilizing measures including the Stock Market Stabilization Fund (~KRW10trn) should market volatility
persist. The financial authorities could temporarily lift the daily caps on corporate treasury share repurchases to facilitate
corporate-led price support.
IDR: Knee-jerk rally overnight after reports that MSCI are in talks with the Indonesian government to find alternatives in
avoiding standalone downgrade. USDIDR traded as low as 18200 and the local index JCI staged sharp reversal into lunch
break. INDOGB also rallied, but in a more cautious fashion. Our FX trader still prefers buying USD dips but waits for larger
dips to re-enter. USDIDR should trade within the 18100 - 18300 range. 
SGD: Oct CPI printed in line with expectations at 1.9% YoY with core slightly softer at 1.5% YoY (prior 2.20% and 2.00%). 
BRL: FGV CPI IPC-S at 11:00 GMT.; Central Bank Weekly Economist Survey at 11:25 GMT. Current 2027 Selic and inflation
forecasts stand at 12.75% and 5%/
MXN: Final Q3 GDP print at 12:00 GMT for September. Our economists expect the final print at 3% YoY vs 3.2% prior. INEGO
will also publish monthly GDP for September which should print at 3.5% YoY.
ILS: Base Rate Decision at 14:00 GMT. Our economists expect the BoI to leave the base rate unchanged at 3.25%. Lower-
than-expected CPI has opened the possibility of a cut but recent FX depreciation and uncertainty about geopolitics and
domestic government formation should lead the BOI to push the decision on lowering rates to another date.
PEN: GDP YoY for Q3. Bloomberg cons. expects a pickup towards 3.2% YoY vs 2.6% prior.
