GS Basics - Nvidia. Positioning. Fed Hawkishness
Rich Privorotsky
23 Nov 2026
 
Markets: Markets bouncing after a weekend of support measures across Asia… China lowering margin
requirements, Korea easing buyback restrictions and Taiwan tightening short selling rules. Helpful on the
margin, but these are the sort of sticking plasters you don’t really want to see marking the bottom (Japan closed).
 
Nvidia/Trump: More meaningful are the supportive comments  from Trump administration on Nvidia. Bessent was
out on CNBC Friday and CBS Sunday, making some important distinctions around selling compute to China
versus supplying the Chinese military. Notably, he also left the door open to Jensen’s return, conditional on
the compliance investigation. Trump’s public support for reinstatement feels entirely purposeful and will leave
some to wonder if the Trump market put appears to be back? The structural issues remain, but the acute
concerns driving last week’s selloff were Nvidia export restrictions, remote compute access, TSMC licensing
uncertainty and an already troublesome AI backdrop compounded by growth deceleration and accounting
questions. For now, this is an Nvidia trade, and a more business friendly administration helps. But let’s not confuse
a rhetorical pivot with regulatory relief. Bessent continues to defend the remote access restrictions and the
investigations remain active. The real test will be whether licenses are granted for offshore data centers,
particularly existing facilities.
 
Positioning:  Prime brokerage data suggest nets came down aggressively gross increased materially and investors
added substantial shorts into the decline. "Fundamental L/S Gross leverage -3.6 pts to 204.8% (13th percentile 1-
year) and Net leverage -3.1 pts to 42.3% (1st percentile 1-year). ..Beta Adjusted Fundamental L/S Gross leverage
-8.3 pts to 214.2% (1-year low) and Beta Adjusted Net leverage -8.8 pts to 48.2% (5-year low)...This week’s
shorting activity was the largest since April 2025." Breaking the 200d triggered further de-risking. The difference
now is that investors aren’t simply defensively positioned… they’ve now got meaningful shorts in the book. Arguably
the missing ingredient. The setup for a bear market rally is glaringly obvious. The question is whether you’re
meant to sell into it or let it breathe.
 
Vol/sentiment: Vol reached extreme levels into the weekend, with the VIX kissed 40 and genuine left tail stress
emerging. Normalization should itself be a tailwind. CNN Fear & Greed around 10, put/call ratios near the 95th
percentile, CFTC leveraged funds reportedly net short Nasdaq futures and the VIX curve at its steepest
inversion since April 2025. Historically, VIX closes above 35 have often preceded positive equity reversals over the
following weeks. Small samples, not a forecast, but the asymmetry looks compelling. A lot of bad news has been
priced into a very short period.
 
Rates: I will continue my overtures about the mispricing of the Fed. The hawkish rhetoric is clear, but the market is
pricing a growth shock and an AI capex slowdown, commodities have collapsed, inflation expectations are
near their lows for the year and real rates look far too high for this environment. Financial conditions have
tightened materially. My hope rests with Kevin Warsh, who has historically been attentive to financial conditions. My
prediction is that the first FOMC member to acknowledge the tightening will provoke an outsized reaction.
Interestingly, Bessent has already raised the risk of a Fed policy mistake. The question is when the Fed itself gets
there.
 

Risk: The parallels with November/December 2018 are increasingly clear. Energy fell further back then and inflation
started lower, but so did rates. Financial conditions have tightened faster this cycle, although the equity
drawdown was deeper before bottoming in 2018. Beyond the AI noise, the pivotal question for equities is how the
Fed assesses the balance of risks between growth and inflation. If November CPI comes anywhere near
expectations, the hawkish rhetoric could quickly feel backward looking.  The distinction now is between a positioning
squeeze and a genuine recovery in risk appetite. Credit is the tell. If Nvidia rallies but neocloud spreads remain
under pressure, we’re probably just covering shorts. The broader concern is that AI infrastructure has
become increasingly dependent on external capital precisely as the cost of that capital rises. The weekend’s
administrative pivot helps sentiment, but it doesn’t solve financing or oversupply. Flash PMIs today and the Beige
Book Wednesday are important tests of whether growth is deteriorating faster than the Fed acknowledges. Core
PCE Wednesday is the marquee inflation release ahead of payrolls, the December FOMC and November CPI,
potentially the most consequential number for the market. Dell reports Tuesday. Thanksgiving also means a
shortened US trading week, potentially amplifying the squeeze in thinner liquidity. It’s adult swim out there. We’ve
moved beyond a routine correction into something resembling a bear market in parts of AI. The structural questions
won’t disappear overnight, but the combination of extreme positioning, elevated vol, a potential Nvidia policy
pivot and a more visible Trump put creates a compelling tactical setup.
