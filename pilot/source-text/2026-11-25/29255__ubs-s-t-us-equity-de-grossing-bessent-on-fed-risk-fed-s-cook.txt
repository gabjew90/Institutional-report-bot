US Equities: Trading Desk Sees De-Grossing, Not Capitulation
The character of the selling in US equities changed meaningfully Friday. After a week of poor liquidity,
composite volumes surged 60% above average, while my colleague Rebecca Cheong similarly flagged
outsized excess sell flow across both S&P and Nasdaq. The trading desk saw this as de-grossing rather than
wholesale capitulation, even if it felt like the latter in semiconductors and AI stocks. The desk saw increasing
dispersion within tech rather than indiscriminate selling: software/cyber and select hyperscalers were relatively
better sponsored, while semiconductors and neoclouds remained the primary funding sources. This suggests
investors were increasingly differentiating on balance-sheet strength, funding requirements and returns on AI
capex rather than abandoning the theme outright.
Retail finally became a seller of weakness. UBS's US retail market-making net flow fell to the lowest 7th
percentile versus the last three years, with outflows across every sector and led by Tech, while the historically
high Nvidia inflows highlighted last week subsided significantly. Hedge funds remain decisively defensive, with
prime broker data showing continued selling in high-momentum semiconductor names and client
conversations suggesting very little appetite to re-gross into year-end. Long-only investors have largely
remained on the sidelines, an important difference versus the summer sell-off. While long-onlys bought into
the midweek weakness/event reset, the desk saw them quickly monetise the bounce, with little evidence of
investors chasing strength.
Systematic positioning is becoming increasingly clean. Both CTA and risk-control funds are approaching their
lowest quintile of exposure over the past five years, while levered ETF exposure has effectively been wiped
out. So while discretionary conviction remains extremely low, systematic supply should become less of a
downside driver. Trading sees the burden of proof now resting on buyers beginning to chase strength rather
than simply absorbing weakness.
(Davide Montoni, Head of Institutional Equity Derivatives Flow Sales America & Torsten Sippel, Head of Voice
Cash Trading – Americas)

Governor Cook Warns Fed Policy Remains Too Loose - Fed Governor Lisa Cook delivered a hawkish
message, arguing policy is not restrictive enough and further tightening is needed to prevent high inflation
becoming embedded in the economy. “The current monetary policy stance is not providing needed restraint,”
Cook said, adding that September’s interest rate hike “was not enough to curtail high inflation.” With inflation
still of “great concern,” she said high inflation does not give the Fed “space to be patient.” Cook said data since
September point to tighter monetary policy and warned that holding off on rate hikes increases the risk inflation
becomes embedded in the economy. Financial conditions are “too accommodative right now,” she added.
(Antonya Allen, Macro Sales)
Nvidia Extends Decline After Reuters Reports SEC Accounting Probe – Nvidia shares declined by more
than 12% after Reuters late Friday reported that US SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting
issues at Nvidia. Two people briefed on the matter told Reuters that accounting practices are a focus of the
inquiry. Reuters could not determine when it began or which potential accounting issues are under
examination. The SEC declined to comment, while Nvidia did not immediately respond. An investigation is not
evidence of wrongdoing and may close without enforcement action. The inquiry follows Nvidia’s change last
year to reporting geographic revenue by customer headquarters rather than billing address. Nvidia said the
change better reflected its revenue profile. It resulted in Singapore being removed from the company’s
geographic breakdown, even as sales linked to the country had increased. The US Justice Department is also
reviewing Nvidia’s $12.9 bn acquisition of Hugging Face as part of a broader antitrust investigation into its
acquisitions and business practices. US authorities are separately investigating Megaspeed over possible
export-control violations involving alleged shipments of Nvidia AI chips to China. (Antonya Allen, Macro Sales)
Eurozone PMIs Stable as Manufacturing Offsets Softer Services - November’s Eurozone PMIs point to
slower but still positive growth, with the Composite PMI unchanged at 51.7 as stronger manufacturing offset
weaker services. France remained the regional weak spot, while Germany picked up. Services softened, with
weaker employment, outstanding business and expectations, while manufacturing improved on firmer output,
employment and future expectations. New orders and export orders were less encouraging. The inflation
picture was more supportive, with both input and output price indices falling, signalling some easing in price
pressures. The Q4 Composite PMI average now stands below Q3 levels, broadly consistent with UBS
Economist Anna Titareva’s expectation for Eurozone growth to slow to 0.2% q/q in Q4. Anna Titareva’s full
take is available here.
UK PMIs Soften, But Q4 Activity Remains Resilient - November’s UK PMIs pointed to softer momentum but
continued activity resilience, with the Composite PMI falling to 52.3 as weaker services offset broadly stable
manufacturing. Services were the main drag, with employment and new business both weakening, while
manufacturing output and new orders improved despite softer export demand. Encouragingly, both input and
output price indices declined, pointing to some easing in inflationary pressures. Despite the November
slowdown, the Q4 Composite PMI average remains above Q3 levels, signalling greater activity resilience than
UBS Economist Anna Titareva’s forecast for growth to slow to 0.1% q/q in Q4. Anna Titareva’s full take is
available here.

Aussie Flash PMI At Five-Year Low - Australia’s flash composite PMI fell to 46.1 in November from 48.2, its
lowest since August 2021, remaining under the neutral 50 level that separates growth from contraction.
Services PMI dropped to 46.2 from 48.5 while manufacturing PMI eased to 48.7 from 49.6. Higher borrowing
costs and weak demand weighed on new orders, which fell for the first time in five months. Firms resumed job
cuts and business confidence hit a three-year low. Input and selling price inflation eased but remained above
long-run average, complicating the RBA’s policy path. Final November PMI will be out on Dec. 1 for
manufacturing and on Dec. 3 for services and the composite. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
NZ Retail Sales Beat Expectations; UBS Raises Q3 GDP View - New Zealand’s seasonally adjusted retail
sales volumes rose 2.2% q/q in Q3, beating the 0.5% consensus forecast and reversing a 0.4% dip in Q2,
taking annual growth up to 3.5% from 3.3%. Fuel (+9.5%), pharmaceuticals and other store-based retailing
(+6.4%) and vehicles (+3.8%) led gains. Excluding fuel and vehicles, spending rose 1.4% q/q from 0.7%,
suggesting underlying resilience. Retail sales values rose 7.1% y/y, to new cycle highs, significantly stronger
than monthly electronic card spending, which in nominal terms slowed to 0.6% y/y in October. UBS
Economics has raised its Q3 GDP forecast to 0.5% q/q from 0.4%. It still sees the RBNZ hiking 25bp in
December, with high inflation and apparently surging retail spending. NZDUSD edged higher following the
release, trading near 0.5475. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Financial Conditions Have Tightened For Parts Of The Market - Network Thoughts: US CCC spreads have
widened for six consecutive months, the longest run in 32 years outside a recession or acute crisis. CCC
spreads are 1,109bp, 47bp wider over the last month, after +149bp in September and +109bp in October.
Stress is concentrating on the weakest borrowers. Four forces are at work. First, rates: the 10-year Treasury
yield has risen 127bp since February to 5.09%, while median CCC interest coverage has collapsed from 0.73x
in 2023 to 0.43x. The median CCC issuer generates less than half the earnings needed to meet its interest bill.
Second, competition for capital: US HY issuance is heading for $410 bn in 2026, total US corporate issuance
is $2.79 trn and the four largest technology companies have financing plans approaching $1 trn next year.
Third, refinancing: only $57 bn of HY debt matures in 2027, rising to $146 bn in 2028 and $282 bn in 2029.
CCC maturities jump from $4 bn to $13 bn between 2027 and 2028. Finally, CCC issuance has collapsed from
$61 bn across 78 deals in 2024 to $19 bn across 32 deals this year. Distressed exchanges, maturity
extensions, and liability-management exercises increasingly become the alternatives. (Greg Poole, Global
Head of Knowledge Network)

Precious Metals Relieved, But Demand Is Still Hesitant – The precious metals market seems to be in what
feels more like a relief rally than any renewed demand for metals. Gold and silver are higher, but equities are
tearing higher and safe havens are being sold, so it is hard to read much demand into it. More likely, there is
some short-covering after a fairly turbulent clean-out. Cleaner positioning can allow room for a bigger bounce,
but it does not necessarily provide a new bull case. Precious Metals Research sees futures as the main
source of supply, with open interest dropping sharply and systematic shorts likely adding to pressure. ETF
holdings have so far stayed steady, making them the next test; near-term stabilisation, and ETF flows in
particular, will indicate whether recent inflows withstand the drawdown. (Laurie Kirby, Precious Metals
Distribution & Jason Cheng, Macro Sales Trading)

FX Divergence: More Room For The Dollar - Over the last ten trading days, the USD rally has extended
sharply against its G10 peers. The median G10 currency is down 260bp against the USD, a move that ranks
below the 10th percentile of ten-day returns over the past decade (figure #1). Cross-sectional rank correlations
indicate that this week's price action reflected a genuine risk unwind: both carry and equity-market
performance were strong drivers of G10 FX returns. USD strength has also begun to permeate
emerging-market currencies, most visibly in LatAm and parts of EEMEA. Even so, USD-long positioning still
appears light. The initial USD rally was fueled by inflation and reinforced by Fedspeak, prompting a rapid
repricing in rates. A further leg higher now requires validation from activity data and wider US rate differentials.
