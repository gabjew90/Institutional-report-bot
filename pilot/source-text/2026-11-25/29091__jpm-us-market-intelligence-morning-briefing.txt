US MKT INTEL VIEW 
•         Last week, we offered our view on the Bull vs. Bear cases, thinking that the Bull Case 
was more likely; however, the Bear Case played out in remarkable fashion pushing 
sentiment toward excessive fears and markets to oversold conditions. 
o   BULL CASE – (i) NVDA Earnings; (ii) Capital Markets; (iii) AI / Tech Trade 
Returns; (iv) Macro Data.  
o   BEAR CASE –  (i) AI / Tech Take Another Leg Lower; (ii) A Decline in the 
Incremental Buyer ; (iii) Hawkish Fed Narrative; (iv) Positioning Purge. 
•         We are moving back to a Tactically Cautious view. In hindsight, it seems obvious that 
we should have been on the sidelines but we, similar to the market, had viewed NVDA and 
capital markets activity as the necessary support for Tech to form a bottom and thus whiffed 
on the tactical bullish call. Now, the uncertainty surrounding NVDA and peers and opacity 

over 2028 and beyond makes Tech longs highly uncertain in the very near-term. Given the 
aggressive nature of the selloff, a tactical bounce seems likely, but we are not yet of the 
view that a bottom forms in the very near-term given the recently introduced uncertainty in 
Tech fundamentals. 
•         Thus, the comparison to 2018 appears to be the best historical comp where the late-
year tactical bounce was faded. We do anticipate a further rotation away from Tech, trades 
which are getting increasingly crowded creating another layer of potential pain should a 
tactical Tech bounce come to fruition. If Tech continues to falter, it is unclear if the balance 
of the SPX can rally enough to boost the index materially higher. 
•         It is worth mentioning that the underlying economy remains strong, AI capex has less of 
a direct impact on the economy, and the AI risks are more tied to financing and the 
consumption wealth effect. The earnings outlook remains positive irrespective of the AI 
outlook. SPX median earnings for 26Q3 was ~12%, which is in line with the 10-year 
average EPS growth of 12.6%, per FactSet. Further, Mislav tells us that median profit 
margins made fresh ATHs. 
•         We do think a bounce is coming but we are not yet convinced that any bounce 
can turn into a sustainable rally given headwinds from AI / Tech, uncertainty over the 
nascent hiking cycles, and the subsequent impact on the earnings outlook for FY27 and 
FY28. 
•         As for what would make us flip back to being bullish? We do not have a high 
conviction answer for that, but it seems that (i) positioning reset / reversion would be the 
start; but, we likely also need to (ii) see support for the US economic growth story proxied 
by Cyclicals outperforming Defensives or a selloff in our Recession / Stagflation baskets. 
Plus, (iii) a reduction in vol. It may be as simple as (iv) investors / market needs to travel 
through time to digest the news and going through another earnings season, which kicks off 
in mid-Jan. A positive tail-risk would be (v) if the Fed pauses in December to go in Jan / 
Mar.

POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE –  Weekly Wrap | Extreme HF selling...watching for 
broader capitulation 
SUMMARY: Abandon all hope, ye who trade tech (and risk assets more 
generally)…that would seem to be the mood after another bruising week for the 
markets and HF performance.  In fact, based on today’s index returns, we’d estimate HFs 
were down 1.5% today and could be down >3% over the past 5d, which would be the worst 
since Apr 7, 2025.  The silver lining is that, unlike what we were seeing in Oct and early 
Nov, the losses continue to be driven by beta rather than alpha, which makes the past week 
also similar to what we saw in early Apr 2025.   
  
The clear answer to last week’s question of whether the Semis sell-off is over is “no.”  
Increasingly, the 2018 analog appears to be in focus given the 4Q selling and reversal 
in performance to basically flat YTD (we did see S&P briefly bounce in late Nov ’18 
before ultimately selling off more in Dec).  More generally, we continue to see HFs reduce 
net risk at a magnitude that has historically been associated with a bounce, but we’re still 
waiting to see whether there’s a broader capitulation.  Perhaps that happened today and 
we’ll keep an eye on how positioning continues to evolve from here. 
  
KEY POINTS: 
• 
HF performance was hit again, while gross flows continued to recover. Based 
on today’s index performance, HFs are estimated to have fallen another 1.5%, 
putting MTD returns at -4%. 5d and 63d returns are now below -3z, though the 
negative overall returns and more neutral HF alpha (partly due to prior de-risking 
by HFs) makes this quite similar to Apr ’25 and early Aug ’24. Gross flows have 
rebounded from prior lows and are back in line with average US seasonality, with 
additions across most regions this past week. 
• 
HF net selling and the decline in net leverage are approaching extreme levels. 
Global HF net selling is around -2z over 20 days and exceeds 2z in North America. 
Historically, periods of large HF net selling that coincided with a markets sell-

off, have been followed by a rebound over the next month, but so far that’s not 
been the case.  The decline in net leverage from 12-month highs is comparable to 
some of the most extreme episodes since 2018 and 8wk change is around -2z. Net 
leverage is at the 26th percentile over the past five years for All Strategies. 
• 
Broader capitulation is not yet confirmed by US TPM (prior to Fri at least). 
Despite low positioning, the US TPM’s 4wk change was only around -1z as of 
Thursday, above the -1.5z trigger.  The positioning level is at the 13th percentile

GOLD THOUGHTS – Brendan Henrici 
To give some flavor on how unusual the gold price action is. Dollar down, real rates down, 
equity vol up (all seemingly gold +'s) and gold...for sale. Took a look how frequently that has 
happened in this magnitude. Answer? Not frequently. It happened about a month ago and 
then you would have to go back to '08 to find similar price action. Only has happened 4x 
since 2000 (including the 2x this year). Pure cash raise it looks like ... 
 
 
Source: Bloomberg; JPM Equity Spec Sales 
  

For those that have asked, gold was up 15% 10 days after that '08 instances and down -3% 
after the '07 instance. No real trend. One thing that folks don't talk about (that probably is an 
unappreciated driver of gold weakness) is Russia returning to SWIFT if the war ends. 
  
Let’s not forget that the gold bull market/dollar debasement trade was basically forged in the 
immediate wake of Russia/Ukraine when Russia was removed from SWIFT in March '22. 
Russia explicitly named it was seeking a return to SWIFT not only in '22 but also as recently 
as '25 when it explicitly asked for its agricultural bank Rosselkhozbank to be added back to 
the payment system as part of implementing what was then the Black sea maritime 
agreement (which was an announced but never enacted ceasefire). 
  
As to whether the EU (or hawkish US leaders) would allow this, that remains to be seen. 
But clearly there has been a laying of western ties between Russia/US lately. No idea if that 
is it (re: weakness) but if it was, it would be a material change to the gold bull case. It is a bit 
coincidental that it is coinciding with these peace headlines. Spitballing ... 
  
THE JP MORGAN VIEW – the x-asset note from JPM Research is here 
•         Both resilient growth and sticky inflation were reinforced by this week’s data 
releases. Retail sales underscored an upbeat consumer. Meanwhile, import prices 
signaled building goods inflation pressure, challenging the view that inflation is 
moving back to target. 
•         The October FOMC minutes turned more hawkish: guidance for at least a 25bp 
hike next month, with some members open to greater than 25bp and a few hinting at 
a more restrictive path ahead. The October pause reads more like process than 
conviction, raising the risk of a reaction-function shift into late ’26/early ’27, with 
December pivotal. 
•         We now anticipate the Fed will accelerate the pace of re-tightening with a further 
three consecutive hikes in December, January and March. This would leave the fed 

funds target range at 4.50%–4.75%, 25bp above the mid-2025 level (4.25%–4.50%), 
more than fully reversing the 75bp of “insurance cuts” delivered between September 
and December 2025. 
•         Our 2027 US Economic Outlook projects growth to remain broadly in line with 
this year’s pace, with real GDP growth of 1.9% Q4/Q4 for 2027. Core PCE inflation 
is expected to moderate only partially and settle around 2.5% Q/Q saar in 2H27. 
•         We keep a constructive outlook for risk assets into 2027 on expectations of 
resilient labour markets and a Fed unwilling to re-test previous highs in policy rates. 
However, we highlight that tighter Fed policy, if inflation stalls, increases the risk of 
“collateral damage” via financial conditions and funding stress. 
•         Cross Asset Views – We reiterate our view that the recent pullback in risk assets 
is mostly an AI de-leveraging event amidst weak sentiment, coupled with a more 
hawkish Fed reaction function being priced in. We keep the faith in the durability of 
the AI supercycle, supported by lower leverage and improved positioning. 
•         We expect the market to rebound as key catalysts clear. We continue to favour 
Quality Growth and hyperscalers in the AI space, but also highlight relative value in 
memory and select semis. NVIDIA delivered strong 3Q results and better-than-
expected 4Q guidance. Despite the strong near-term outlook, we flag risks around 
CEO succession, regulatory investigations/export controls, financing/datacenter 
buildout, and execution. 
•         A more aggressive Fed hiking path remains a key challenge to the broadening 
narrative. However, the recent wobble in AI/Tech space has left Cyclical stocks 
outperforming despite the higher repricing of the Fed terminal. We believe a 
continuation of the Cyclicals rebound requires confirmation of a patient Fed. The 
Goldilocks narrative of immaculate disinflation is not shaping up, challenging the 
durability of the recent outperformance. 
•         Downside risk to equities could arise from an even-more-aggressive Fed pivot 
shift, which would challenge our current call for a terminal rate below 5%. 
Additionally, a regime where the Fed cannot hike despite sticky inflation because 
growth deteriorates materially and the AI trade wobbles, or because inflation 
credibility is questioned, would be risk negative. 

•         Upside risk for Equities, conversely, could stem from dovish and patient 
guidance in December, validated by downside inflation surprises and clearer 
evidence of AI-productivity gains. 
•         In DM rates, we note that the cumulative tightening priced in for the Fed is less 
than our revised call. However, the risk scenario is a December pivot validating 
consecutive hikes, which would push terminal pricing higher and drive a bear-
flattening of the front-end. We hold 2s/5s UST curve flatteners. 
•         In FX, we stick to a procyclical outlook supporting high-yielding currencies 
across DM (AUD, NOK) vs. low-yielders (EUR, SEK). We stay positive on USD, 
which should benefit from a faster rate of and larger cumulative rise in Fed 
tightening, with risks skewed to higher terminal to be priced in. A less patient Fed 
remains the key risk for EM FX vs. USD while not derailing the EM carry theme vs. 
DM low yielders. 
•         In credit, we remain constructive overall with a preference for the high-quality 
credit spectrum. Tighter financial conditions continue to weigh on credit, with 
pressure increasing as credit quality declines. Vulnerability to higher rates is higher 
in US High Yield credit and Leveraged Loans, where the maturity wall and 
deterioration in credit quality are increasingly backloaded into 2028. 
•         In commodities, the pullback in gold and copper is mostly a rates story, with the 
dollar and Fed repricing the common driver. Copper's weakness also reflects tariff 
uncertainty, softer China demand and shifting trade flows, and not just the AI 
wobble. The risk is a December pivot that validates consecutive hikes, which would 
add to the pressure.
