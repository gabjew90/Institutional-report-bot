Mutual Fundamentals analyzes the quarter-end positioning of 453 large-cap active mutual 
funds with a combined $3.9 trillion in equity assets. 
1. PERFORMANCE AND FLOWS: 42% of large-cap mutual funds are outperforming 
their benchmarks YTD vs. the historical average of 37%. 81% of large-cap growth 
funds are outperforming, the highest share in over a decade. Value funds have 
posted the strongest YTD returns (+14%) but just 4% of large-cap value funds are 
outperforming as they struggle to match their benchmarks. Mutual fund cash 
balances register 1.1% of assets, matching the record low from December 2025. 
2. THEMES IN FOCUS: 
(A) AI: Mutual funds were underweight AI-exposed equities ahead of recent poor 
performance across the AI complex. However, the average mutual fund underweight 
across AI-exposed equities narrowed to 130 bp from 175 bp across Q3. Despite their 
underweight to AI-exposed stocks overall, mutual funds added exposure to select 
pockets of the AI complex in Q3. The top 20 most popular mutual fund increases in 
Q3 include AI infrastructure stocks AMD, WDC, STX, and SNDK as well as Software 
stocks PLTR, SNOW, TWLO, PANW, and CRWD. The 20 most popular decreases 
include AMAT, LRCX, CSCO, and KLAC. 
(B) Magnificent 7: Large-cap mutual funds were 722 bp underweight the Mag 7 at 
the end of Q3 2026, effectively unchanged during the quarter. The average fund is 
underweight each of the Mag 7 except AMZN, which is now an average fund 
overweight for the first time since 2016.  A majority of funds reduced ownership in 
most of the Mag 7 stocks last quarter. The largest reductions were in GOOGL and 
AAPL. 
(C) Dispersion and market breadth: High return dispersion and an improvement in 
market breadth have supported mutual fund returns. Mutual funds typically deliver 
the best excess returns relative to their benchmarks when return dispersion is wide 
and market breadth is not narrow. Since the start of the quarter, market breadth has 
improved through both a “catch-up” from laggards and a modest “catch-down” from 
the largest stocks while return dispersion has remained high.  
3. SECTORS: Large-cap mutual funds are most overweight Industrials (+200 bp), 
followed closely by Health Care (+199 bp, a 10-year high) and Financials (+196 bp). 
Funds cut their Financials tilt by 35 bp, the largest reduction across sectors, led by 
Banks. Funds remain most underweight Info Tech (-631 bp), but they lifted their tilt 
by 29 bp. Funds across styles trimmed tilts to Consumer Staples and Utilities.
