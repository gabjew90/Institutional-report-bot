NDX Saw Largest Weekly Fall Since Tariffs, But Not Broad Capitulation
The NDX fell over 10% last week, its worst week since March 2020 (the start of covid), as crowded
AI/momentum bore the brunt of systematic, long-only and hedge fund selling. However, selective Cyclical
buying and Value leadership suggest this is not wholesale equity risk capitulation. Positioning is increasingly
oversold, and options investors are starting to look for a squeeze. But the AI/Tech debate has shifted: good
news is being monetized rather than chased as investors demand greater forward visibility and financing
conditions tighten. European equities also struggled, suffering their worst week since April 2025 (the start of
tariffs). SX5E was down close to 7% as the Tech/AI sell-off broadened into a sharp Cyclical correction. Full
note here.
AI weakness is spilling into broader risk assets, with sharp declines spreading beyond Tech into US banks,
copper, gold and credit. Equity valuations and technicals are becoming stretched enough to suggest value is
rebuilding, but a convincing stabilization trigger is still missing. Risk-reward remains poor in attempting to time
the exhaustion of forced/systematic selling. Beyond the negative Tech headlines, the hawkish macro backdrop
has also been a headwind, although Fed hike pricing has already been reduced from 80bp over the next 12m
to around 60bp. US Treasury Secretary Bessent’s comments are supportive of sentiment, noting that Fed
hikes risk jeopardizing the US economy by overtightening and that financial conditions have tightened
“substantially” over the past few months.
(Jamie Briggs & Jason Cheng, UBS Knowledge Network)

Financial Conditions Have Materially Tightened For Parts Of The Market - Network Thoughts: US CCC
spreads have widened for six consecutive months, the longest run in 32 years outside a recession or acute
crisis. CCC spreads are 1,109bp, 47bp wider over the last month, after +149bp in September and +109bp in
October. Stress is concentrating on the weakest borrowers. Four forces are at work. First, rates: the 10-year
Treasury yield has risen 127bp since February to 5.09%, while median CCC interest coverage has collapsed
from 0.73x in 2023 to 0.43x. The median CCC issuer generates less than half the earnings needed to meet its
interest bill. Second, competition for capital: US HY issuance is heading for $410 bn in 2026, total US
corporate issuance is $2.79 trn and the four largest technology companies have financing plans approaching
$1 trn next year. Third, refinancing: only $57 bn of HY debt matures in 2027, rising to $146 bn in 2028 and
$282 bn in 2029. CCC maturities jump from $4 bn to $13 bn between 2027 and 2028. Finally, CCC issuance
has collapsed from $61 bn across 78 deals in 2024 to $19 bn across 32 deals this year. Distressed
exchanges, maturity extensions, and liability-management exercises increasingly become the alternatives.
(Greg Poole, Global Head of Knowledge Network)
Nvidia Extends Slump After Reuters Reports SEC Accounting Probe - Nvidia shares are slumping by
more than 12% after Reuters reported that US SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting
issues at Nvidia. Two people briefed on the matter told Reuters that accounting practices are a focus of the
inquiry. Reuters could not determine when it began or which potential accounting issues are under
examination. The SEC declined to comment, while Nvidia did not immediately respond. An investigation is not
evidence of wrongdoing and may close without enforcement action. The inquiry follows Nvidia’s change last
year to reporting geographic revenue by customer headquarters rather than billing address. Nvidia said the
change better reflected its revenue profile. It resulted in Singapore being removed from the company’s
geographic breakdown, even as sales linked to the country had increased. The US Justice Department is also
reviewing Nvidia’s $12.9 bn acquisition of Hugging Face as part of a broader antitrust investigation into its
acquisitions and business practices. US authorities are separately investigating Megaspeed over possible
export-control violations involving alleged shipments of Nvidia AI chips to China. (Antonya Allen, Macro Sales)

Where Is Gold Selling Coming From? - Precious Metals Research: Gold’s slide toward $3,500 reflects a
stronger dollar, hawkish Fed repricing and broader risk-off, but strategist Joni Teves sees futures as the main
source of supply: open interest has dropped by more than 6m oz since last week, with systematic shorts likely
adding to pressure. ETF holdings have so far stayed steady, making them the next test; roughly 166 tonnes of
prior-quarter inflows were accumulated with gold at $4,000–$4,500, leaving many investors underwater after
the pullback. Dip buying remains limited as FOMC uncertainty and the speed of the decline deter investors
and physical buyers, although lower prices and China’s early-February demand season could support
purchases. Central banks are also likely to wait for volatility to ease. For long-term allocations, the
diversification case remains intact, but near-term stabilization—and ETF flows in particular—will indicate
whether recent inflows withstand the drawdown. (Jason Cheng, Macro Sales Trading)

FX Divergence: More Room For The Dollar - Over the last ten trading days, the USD rally has extended
sharply against its G10 peers. The median G10 currency is down 260bp against the USD, a move that ranks
below the 10th percentile of ten-day returns over the past decade (figure #1). Cross-sectional rank correlations
indicate that this week's price action reflected a genuine risk unwind: both carry and equity-market
performance were strong drivers of G10 FX returns. USD strength has also begun to permeate
emerging-market currencies, most visibly in LatAm and parts of EEMEA. Even so, USD-long positioning still
appears light. The initial USD rally was fueled by inflation and reinforced by Fedspeak, prompting a rapid
repricing in rates. A further leg higher now requires validation from activity data and wider US rate differentials.

Aussie Flash PMI At Five-Year Low - Australia’s flash composite PMI fell to 46.1 in November from 48.2, its
lowest since August 2021, remaining under the neutral 50 level that separates growth from contraction.
Services PMI dropped to 46.2 from 48.5 while manufacturing PMI eased to 48.7 from 49.6. Higher borrowing
costs and weak demand weighed on new orders, which fell for the first time in five months. Firms resumed job
cuts and business confidence hit a three-year low. Input and selling price inflation eased but remained above
long-run average, complicating the RBA’s policy path. Final November PMI will be out on Dec. 1 for
manufacturing and on Dec. 3 for services and the composite. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
NZ Retail Sales Beat Expectations; UBS Raises Q3 GDP View - New Zealand’s seasonally adjusted retail
sales volumes rose 2.2% q/q in Q3, beating the 0.5% consensus forecast and reversing a 0.4% dip in Q2,
taking annual growth up to 3.5% from 3.3%. Fuel (+9.5%), pharmaceuticals and other store-based retailing
(+6.4%) and vehicles (+3.8%) led gains. Excluding fuel and vehicles, spending rose 1.4% q/q from 0.7%,
suggesting underlying resilience. Retail sales values rose 7.1% y/y, to new cycle highs, significantly stronger
than monthly electronic card spending, which in nominal terms slowed to 0.6% y/y in October. UBS
Economics has raised its Q3 GDP forecast to 0.5% q/q from 0.4%. It still sees the RBNZ hiking 25bp in
December, with high inflation and apparently surging retail spending. NZDUSD edged higher following the
release, trading near 0.5475. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

UST Futures Retracing Lower After Friday's Huge Safe Haven Bid - UST futures are lower Monday
morning in APAC, giving back some of Friday’s gains but still remaining well above Thursday’s close. On
Friday, USTs surged with yields 9-11bp lower across the curve as dire risk sentiment amid plunging stocks
triggered a huge haven bid. Gains were led by the front end, with the curve bull steepening as the market
priced out rate hike premium; 2s10s and 5s30s were 2-3bp steeper. SOFR futures rallied aggressively Friday,
led by reds and greens, which settled ~20bp higher, as the market finally took a run at what was an extremely
flat forward curve. Immediate front-end pricing ended the week at ~24bp of hikes into December, a cumulative
39bp into January and a cumulative 65bp into the middle of next year, while the big shifts came in back-end
reds and greens as the market ran for risk premium for cuts further out the curve on the back of the equity
sell-off and should the front-end hikes land. (David Robinson, Macro Sales Trading)
