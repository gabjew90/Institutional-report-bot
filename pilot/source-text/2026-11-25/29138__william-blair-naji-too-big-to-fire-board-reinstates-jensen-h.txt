NVIDIA Corporation
Too Big to Fire; Board Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO
What You Need to Know: Nvidia’s board of directors announced this morning its decision to
reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, just 12 days after his resignation following reports of multiple
affairs, including a relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, which
raised additional concerns about the company’s chip exports to China. With shares rising
33% on the news, Huang’s return removes a significant overhang on the stock, restoring a
leader whose unique management style, technical vision, and relationship-driven approach
to deal-making would have been difficult to replace. Moreover, reported assurances from
the U.S. government surrounding his reinstatement could signal a more favorable regulatory
outlook, potentially easing concerns about Nvidia’s exposure to chip sales in Southeast
Asia. While Huang’s return clears some of the uncertainty surrounding Nvidia, the events
of recent weeks have exposed deeper questions around corporate governance, leadership
succession, and regulatory oversight that could have lasting implications for the company’s
competitive position. Against this backdrop, we maintain our Market Perform rating as we
await greater clarity on these issues and assess what Huang’s second act at Nvidia will mean for
the company’s long-term outlook.
Huang’s Return: During the company’s earnings call last week, interim CEO (prior CFO)
Colette Kress and members of the board emphasized investors should not expect Huang to
return to Nvidia as his actions did not align with the company’s code of conduct. In a turn of
events, Huang was reinstated as president, CEO, and board director through a unanimous
vote by board. Kress will return to her position as CFO. Chair of the Board Harvey Jones noted
“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is
in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers.” The decision
followed President Trump’s renewed call that Huang should return as CEO. Investigations into
Huang are still ongoing; however, the board believes Huang’s return does not pose any national
security risk. In efforts to mitigate further risk, Nvidia formed a National Security and Export
Controls Committee comprising independent directors and is in search of a chief compliance
officer.
Stock Thoughts: Shares of NVDA are up nearly 33% Monday morning following Huang’s
reinstatement as CEO. At a price of roughly $168 per share, NVDA trades at a price-to-earnings
multiple of 10 times our fiscal 2028 estimates.
Risks: Risks to Nvidia include China exposure, competition from other merchant GPU
vendors and custom ASIC projects, increased regulatory constraints and tariffs, supply chain
constraints, the potential for deployments to be pushed out, and key-man risk surrounding
long-time CEO Jensen Huang.
