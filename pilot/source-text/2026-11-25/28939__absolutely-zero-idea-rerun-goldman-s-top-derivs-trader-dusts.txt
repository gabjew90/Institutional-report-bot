 
"Absolutely Zero Idea" Rerun: Goldman's Top Derivs 
Trader Dusts Off 2018 Playbook... And It Ends At 
S&P 6,294 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SATURDAY, NOV 21, 2026 - 01:30 PM 
Wall Street has a new favorite year, and it's 2018. 
With the S&P 500 down almost 3% on Friday, on track for a seventh straight loss, and the 
Nasdaq 100 shedding more than 3% as the Anthropic IPO hangover turned into a global tech 
liquidation, just about every desk has reached for the same comparison: the fourth quarter of 
2018, when a hiking Fed, broken technicals and a wave of forced selling took the S&P down 
almost 20% in three months, all the way to a Christmas Eve low. The Market Ear asked on 
Wednesday if the great tech liquidation of 2018 was about to repeat, and by Friday morning 
both Barclays and Goldman's Rich Privorotsky were drawing the same parallel. 
Now Goldman derivatives guru Brian Garrett has put numbers on it. In a rare pre-weekend 
edition of his Weekend Prep note (available to pro subs), sent early Friday afternoon while 
stocks were puking, Garrett says the Q4 2018 comparison keeps coming up with clients. He 
was in the seat for the original, and he remembers exactly what he told the people who called 
on Dec 24 to ask why stocks were down so much. Here is how he opens: 
1/ a lot of client conversations are drawing comparisons to q4 2018​
2/ if you were in the seat, you remember that 24dec session … q: “bg why are we down so 
much today? … a: “absolutely zero idea” … hawkish fed, technical breakdowns, extreme 
volatility, systematic supply, etc… sound familiar? 
The ingredients he lists from that day will be recognizable to anyone who traded this week: a 
Fed that is still hiking (with one governor openly floating 50bps), an index that lost its 200DMA, 
dealers short gamma and a tape that couldn't rally even with every machine primed to buy. 
So Garrett ran the numbers. Using Goldman's tools, and with the standard caveat that past 
performance says nothing about future returns, he measured every major market's move since 
the S&P's Oct 6 peak against what it did between Sept 24 and Dec 24, 2018. His summary: 
 

 
4/ equities are close (especially tech) … credit is not (has barely moved relative) … rates have 
done half of the heavy lifting (10y and 5y) … fx has seen massive outperformace vs 2018 … 
havens (gold and jpy) have gone completely wrong way 
Translation: stocks have done roughly half of the 2018 job, credit has barely clocked in, and the 
two assets that were supposed to cushion the fall are falling too.

Next Stop On The 2018 Map: S&P 6,294 
The damage so far is in tech. Through Thursday's close the Nasdaq 100 was down 15.1% from 
the Oct 6 peak, which is 69% of its entire 2018 drawdown (21.8%). The S&P, down 8.2%, had 
done 42% of its 19.5% drop, and the Russell 2000, down 6.2%, just 24% of its 25.7%. 
Then comes the part nobody wants to read. 
Run the rest of the 2018 path from Thursday's close and Garrett's math says another 12% on 
the S&P, to about 6,294, another 8% on the Nasdaq 100 and another 21% on the Russell. 
 

 
Friday's drop has already made a down payment: from around 7,020, S&P 6,294 is now roughly 
10% away (napkin math, ours). 
 
Garrett's drawdown charts, which appear to include Friday's leg lower, show the S&P 9.5% off 
its 52-week high against a 2018 trough of -19.8%... 
 
... and the Nasdaq 100 down 17.4% against a 2018 trough of -23.0% (both charts Goldman 
Sachs). 
 

 
 
And the calendar? The 2018 low came 65 trading days after the peak. Friday was day 33. On 
that clock the low is due in the first full week of January (again our math, and all the usual 
caveats apply). 
Regular readers will also recall the stat from "Nothing Good Happens Below The 200-Day 
Moving Average" earlier this week: six straight down days plus a close below the 200DMA has 
happened only three times since 1996, in September 2000, October 2018 and February 2020. 
Each one was followed by a five-day bounce, and then by drawdowns of 26%, 17% and 30%. 
Yes, 2018 was the gentle one. 
Credit Hasn't Shown Up Yet (And Neither Has The Fed) 
This is where 2026 and 2018 part ways. CDX IG is just 5bps wider since the peak, 15% of the 
2018 move, and HY is 38bps wider, or 28% of the 136bps it widened back then. A full replay 
would mean roughly 27bps more in IG and 98bps more in HY, and Garrett calls credit spreads 
the single largest difference between now and 2018. 
There are two ways to read that. Either credit is right and equities are having a positioning 
accident, or credit is late. 
Having warned since last October that the AI buildout runs on debt, with the highest-rated 
borrowers now drowning in itand Dan Loeb saying this week that the next wave of opportunity 
comes from credit, we know which one we'd bet on. 
Rates are the other tell. 10Y swaps are 23bps lower (64% of the 2018 rally) and 5Y swaps are 
17bps lower (42%), but 2Y swaps have moved all of 2bps, or 7%. As Garrett reads it, the front 
end is not pricing a policy response the way it did in 2018. 
 

 
And why would it? In 2018 Powell hiked on Dec 19, watched stocks crater into Christmas and 
was "patient" by Jan 4. This time the Fed is hiking into a hot CPI, Goldman's own economists 
expect a third hike in March, and as Privorotsky put it this morning, the market probably pivots 
when the central banks do. Barclays made the same point: the bigger problem for stocks is that 
nobody sees a Fed backstop. 
The Hedges That Didn't Hedge 
In 2018 at least the havens worked: gold rose 5% and the yen gained 2.1% against the dollar. 
This time gold is down 10.8% and the yen is 1% weaker, which on Goldman's scorecard prints 
as -215% and -49% of the 2018 move (The Market Ear did warn on Wednesday that gold's 
great unwind is far from over). 
A hedge that falls 11% alongside the thing it was hedging is just another long. 
The rest of the cross-asset tape is a mix of overshoots and no-shows: 
●​ Copper is down 13.1%, more than twice its 2018 decline. Call it the AI data center trade 
in metal form. 
●​ Crude is down just 7%, against a 41% collapse in 2018 (Hormuz will do that). 
●​ High-beta FX has already done more than 2018: AUDUSD is at 160% of its 2018 move 
and USDMXN at 127%, with EURUSD at 85%, USDCHF at 59% and USDCAD at 17%.

Panic Index At 9, VIX Curve Most Inverted Since Liberation Day 
The vol market, at least, is taking 2018 seriously. In its latest Vol Views note (also available to 
pro subs), published Friday morning, Goldman's vol desk says client conversations suggest the 
path from here looks increasingly binary: 
Uncertainty persists, sentiment remains impaired, and client conversations suggest the path 
from here looks increasingly binary. The vol market is showing clear signs of stress; our Panic 
Index registers ~9/10. We see dealers short gamma here (~$1bn) and while some will roll off 
with today's reg expiry, we model dealers remaining short vega in this range. 
The Panic Index is back near 9 out of 10, two weeks after it exploded the first time (chart 
source: Goldman FICC & Equities). 
 
The desk's other stress gauges: 
●​ Thursday's S&P advance-decline spread of -426 was the worst of the year, and 
one-month implied vol on the average S&P stock is about 38, in the 93rd percentile of 
the past decade. 
●​ The S&P's implied move for this week was 1.9%. The index has now realized that entire 
weekly move inside a single session twice in the last four days. The implied move 
through next Friday, with Thanksgiving in the middle: 3.00%. 
●​ Spot VIX sits about 6 vols above the front-month future, the most inverted the curve has 
been since April 2025. 
 

 
 
Remember last Sunday, when we asked "One And Done?" as the options market priced seven 
weeks of calm for 4.2%? The S&P took out that straddle's lower breakeven in four sessions. So, 
no. 
Garrett's own VIX chart makes the 2018 point from a different angle: at 30.3, the VIX is already 
within 6 points of its Christmas Eve 2018 peak (36.1), with the S&P only about halfway through 
the 2018 drawdown. Maybe that's the hopeful part, since fear is running well ahead of price. 
 
Which brings us to Garrett's one piece of live desk color: 
desk anecdote - today is one of the first days we are seeing offense being played via equity 
volatility but its not the standard "buy calls" its more "sell skew" ... fade the bid to the wings on 
both sides 
 

 
In other words, clients are finally leaning against the panic, and they are doing it by selling the 
rich wings on both sides. Nobody is paying up for upside. 
The vol desk saw the same thing on Thursday, when clients faded the VIX spike with puts and 
put 1x2s (one bought the Dec 16 20/18 put spread 30K times as a 1x2). In Nvidia (NVDA), now 
down about 39% since early October, long-only supply keeps hitting the cash desk while the vol 
side sees clients lifting call spreads and call spread collars to keep some upside on. 
As for what Goldman's desk would do here: 
●​ Rent the snapback: QQQ Dec 31 680/720 call spreads for $7.40 (ref 637.04), with the 
QQQ RSI near 33, positioning washed out and dealers short gamma. 
●​ Hedge what has worked: XLP Jan 15 85/100 bearish risk reversals for $1.15 (ref 89.86). 
Staples put-call skew is in the 3rd percentile of the past 20 years, and Goldman Prime 
shows staples among the most net sold sectors this month as hedge funds fade the best 
staples run since the dot-com peak (Nov 2). 
●​ Still a believer in an AI bounce? Feb 19 120% worst-of calls on NVDA/AVGO/INTC cost 
1.41%, about 60% less than the cheapest vanilla call. 
 
One Year, Two Very Different Halves 
Garrett closes with two bonus charts on just how bifurcated 2026 has been. Across 2,258 
Goldman baskets, the better the first half, the worse the second (slope -0.33, R² 0.40, data 
through Nov 19). Baskets that more than doubled in 1H have a median 2H return of about -50%. 
 

 
The top decile of first-half performers shows a median second-half return of -32.7%, and the 
ninth decile -16.5%. 
And the bottom four deciles? Between -0.4% and +1.8%. The winners got liquidated, and the 
laggards got... nothing. 
 
So Which Christmas Is This? 
The bull case writes itself: positioning is washed out, the Panic Index is at 9, clients are starting 
to sell vol and the QQQ is nearly oversold. All three of those 200DMA episodes began with a 
bounce, and we could well get one into a holiday-shortened week. 
The bear case is everything on Garrett's table that hasn't happened yet. In 2018 it took a credit 
scare and a Fed surrender to make the low. Today IG has done 15% of its move, the front end 
7%, and the Fed is still hiking. Until one of those changes, we'd treat rallies as rentals 
(Goldman's vol desk is quite literally recommending call spreads) and assume the 2018 map still 
has plenty of road left on it. 
On Christmas Eve 2018, Goldman's top derivatives trader had "absolutely zero idea" why stocks 
were down so much. Eight years later everyone has an idea... and stocks are down anyway.
