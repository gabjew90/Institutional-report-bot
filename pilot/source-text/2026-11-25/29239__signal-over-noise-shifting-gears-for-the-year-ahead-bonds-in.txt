Signal over Noise: Shifting gears for the year 
ahead – Bonds in the lead, equities to Neutral

Hello and welcome to Signal over Noise.
I'm Ulrike Hoffmann-Burchardi, CIO for the Americas and Global Head of 
Equities for UBS Wealth Management.
Today, we are diving straight into our main playbook for the year ahead.
We are at an inflection point. We are seeing the highest yields in over 20 
years and, at the same time, the highest disruption risk to megacap tech.
Our advice? Lock in medium-term bond yields and use stock market rallies 
to diversify away from concentrated index holdings. We see securing yields 
and broadening equity exposure as a way to shield against higher volatility.
We're seeing a turning point in the market: elevated borrowing costs, 
slowing economic momentum, and profound shifts across big tech 
leadership.
In this situation, focusing exclusively on a single metric like earnings growth 
can leave blind spots. That's why we look at the markets using a three-
lens framework: big-picture economic data, company fundamentals, and 
structural trends. Combining these three distinct angles makes it easier to 
separate the signal from the noise.
Let's kick things off with lens one: the big macroeconomic picture.
Overall economic momentum is cooling down compared to last year's 
expansion. The US GDP impulse from government spending is stepping 
down from 0.4% to 0.1% next year. Meanwhile, inflation remains 
stubbornly high, near 4%.
The culprits? Sticky service costs and shelter disinflation slowing down.
On top of that, consumer savings rates have fallen to 3.8%, down 
from 6% a year ago, and benchmark interest rates remain almost a full 
percentage point higher. The clear takeaway here is that both households 
and government budgets have much smaller safety margins.

Moving to lens two: corporate fundamentals.
The S&P 500 is currently trading around 18 times forward earnings. This 
follows a 17% upward revision to profit estimates over the past year, the 
largest upward adjustment we have seen since 2020-2021.
With a more tepid macro outlook, we see limited room for additional 
upside from our 15% earnings growth forecast for next year.
Finally, let's examine lens three: structural trends, particularly artificial 
intelligence.
Their budgets are also getting tighter. Aggregate free cash flows across 
the five largest tech hyperscalers are set to turn negative next year. Instead 
of funding new data center expansion out of organic cash flows, these 
companies will have to rely heavily on debt and external financing.
We are also tracking a shift in software usage. AI agents now account for 
over 75% of total AI token usage, up from less than 5% just a year ago.
Here's the catch. AI agents are not influenced by advertising. Consumers 
are. They don't need software with dashboards and user interfaces.
We are moving from B2C, business-to-consumer, to B2A, business-to-
agents. This poses risks to the largest and most profitable markets in tech: 
advertising and software as a service.
At the same time, we are seeing an unprecedented number of large-cap 
entrants into the public market. These private companies are entering at a 
size and scale that already rivals the largest public companies, and they're 
not only competing for a place in portfolios, but also for economic profits.
So, bringing these three lenses together, how should you structure your 
portfolio today?
First, actively reduce concentration risk. Equal-weighted market strategies 
have already outperformed broad market index benchmarks this year, and 
we expect this trend to continue.
Second, lock in high fixed income yields. Yields are at the highest levels in 
over 20 years, with benchmark 10-year Treasury yields hovering around 
5%. Securing these yields guarantees income and positions the portfolio 
for price appreciation when interest rates eventually come down.
With this, stay well and stay ahead.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
In line: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
