NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA)
NVDA - Quick Take - Return of the Jedi
NVDA reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO. Earlier this morning, NVDA announced that the
BoD unanimously voted to reinstate Jensen Huang. We congratulate Mr. Huang on his return
to the company he co-founded in 1993. This announcement removes a significant overhang
from the stock. Now, it seems appropriate for us to consider the upside and downside risks
to the stock, especially in light of our recent downgrade, which was not entirely related to
Mr. Huang's departure.
The upside is mostly about technical leadership, and culture. We've long said that
NVDA's success is driven less by the performance of its chips, and more by its culture of
innovation, ecosystem of incumbency, and massive investment in software, models, and
services. We believe the culture of innovation was driven by Mr. Huang's technical brilliance
and magnetic personality. Mr. Huang's reinstatement as CEO will certainly fuel the "ride or
die with Jensen" dynamic. Further, we believe the BoD's additional changes offer potential
improvements in culture to mitigate certain risks. These changes include:
•
Independent investigation committee will continue its work. The BoD's independent
director committee investigating aspects of Mr. Huang's position as target of a foreign
intelligence effort will proceed to conclusion.
•
New National Security and export Control Committee. The BoD recently formed
a National Security and Export Control Committee comprised solely of independent
directors to oversee trade compliance and national security risks. The committee is
engaging counsel with experience in national security matters, and such counsel will be
retaining advisors with expertise in national security matters.
•
New Chief Compliance Officer. The BoD established a new executive position - Chief
Compliance Officer - and is commencing a search to fill that role. This person will oversee
compliance across NVIDIA and report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance
Committee.
The downside/risks that remain are legal/regulatory, including KYC, and cycle related.
We cannot claim to possess real expertise in legal or regulatory matters. Still, it appears to
us that downside risks in this regard have not been removed.
•
Congressional investigations. On Dec. 1 Mr. Huang has agreed to a closed-door
interview before several House Committees. On Dec. 2, NVIDIA General Counsel Tim
Teter has agreed to the same. Perhaps more important, Mr. Huang has agreed to testify
publicly in front of the Senate Banking Committee, but a date has not been scheduled yet.
•
New KYC rules. On Nov. 16, the Commerce Department's BIS issued an interim final
rule covering remote access to AI chips intended to stop restricted Chinese entities
from accessing U.S. computing power through cloud services. We cannot forecast with
accuracy what impact this would have on GPU sales, but we expect there will be some
downside pressure to revenue from this action.
•
Megaspeed. On Nov. 17 a joint statement from the U.S. Dept of Justice and Dept of
Commerce described the Megaspeed (private) matter as ongoing, and said the DoJ is
now leading a criminal investigation. NVDA's supply to Megaspeed seems to introduce
some risks to the company, in our view.
Furthermore, the cyclical concerns we highlighted in a recent industry note: Anatomy of a
Bubble - have not gone away. Let's walk through a few of these.
•
Cycle positioning looks beyond peak. A typical semi cycle peaks at +29% y/y growth
7 qtrs into improving growth. The Tech Bubble peaked at +50% y/y growth 9 qtrs in. The
AI super cycle appears set to peak at +76% 15 qtrs in (4Q26). It's the strongest cycle in
our lifetime and probably the strongest ever in semis. The other telltale signs of a peaking
cycle (bubble, or not) are significantly rising leadtimes and rising prices that lead to multi-
ordering ("greed"). While we believe those dynamics have been somewhat concentrated
in areas outside our coverage (memory, optical, package substrates), they appear to be
present.
•
Suspended and canceled IPOs. Australian AI infrastructure company Firmus
Technologies (private) planned IPO in October was scrapped following weak investor
interest at the planned valuation. Other similar examples include the canceled or delayed

IPO plans for Solodigm (private, SSD memory), SB Energy (private) and Holtec Nuclear (private).
•
One capex push-out. While other major AI spenders have been maintaining or even raising capex forecasts recently, Microsoft (MSFT,
Tillman, Buy) lowered its 2026 capex guidance by $5B. MSFT also canceled Nscale (private)'s flagship Loughton project after power
delays pushed availability into the 2030s.
•
Recent elections. Perhaps most important, the recent U.S. midterm elections brought to power officials who have called for moratorium
on DC buildouts, most notably Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa in TX and Amy Acton in OH. Perhaps even more important, 40 of 41
DC related local ballot measures across 13 states
•
Potential inventory build. Business Insider reported that internal communications at Amazon (AMZN, Buy, Squali) indicated some
sites had physical room for additional racks but insufficient power to turn them on, forcing equipment to be staged in warehouses.
At least one supply chain team reportedly lacked a “clear forecasted path” to move those racks into operating datacenters. Amazon
created an “Early Rack Receipt” process so it could receive NVDA systems before facilities were ready. This is indistinguishable from
a channel or customer inventory build, potentially denting future demand, in our view.

Valuation and Risks
Our $174 price target is based on ~16x (a ~12x discount to Datacenter peers) applied to our CY28 EPS estimate of $11.02.
Risks to our rating and price target:
Upside risks are now focused on the company's ability to hire an inspirational leader who can resume the position vacated by Mr. Huang
in November 2026.
Furthermore, the AI ecosystem - from suppliers to competitors to customers - are generally highly constructive about future growth
opportunities and forecast growth higher than we model for NVDA today.
Downside risks are now focused on how NVDA might navigate the departure of its co-founder and former CEO Jensen Huang, considering
the incredible talent and culture that he has been able to develop at NVDA.
Further downside risks include the potential that the company has shipped GPUs to companies that have either sent directly, or allowed
access indirectly to, its GPUs to entities in China that are prohibited from buying NVDA's GPUs by the U.S. Commerce Department.
Additional downside risks include the potential for an unexpected downturn in demand for AI infrastructure.
