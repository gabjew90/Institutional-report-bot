Weekly Commentary with Professor Jeremy J. Siegel 
 
Fed Tightening Fears Overshadow Falling Energy Prices 
11/23/2026 
 
 
The market sell-off last week was particularly disappointing because it extended well beyond the technology sector. Most of 
this year, when we saw a decline in growth stocks value stocks held up relatively well, supported by falling interest rates. Last 
week, however, both growth and value were hit hard. What surprises me most is a backdrop of sharply declining oil and 
gasoline prices and an economy that continues to show virtually no signs of deterioration. 
One important factor was the bond market's reaction to Governor Waller's call for a 50-basis-point rate hike in December. I 
believe his comments reflect frustration with Chairman Warsh's reluctance to provide forward guidance. Waller might have 
preferred 25 basis points in December followed by another 25 in January, but without that guidance, he has pushed for the 
full 50 immediately. This spooked investors and helped push the 10-year Treasury yield back toward 5%, removing the 
interest-rate support that previously helped value stocks withstand technology sell-offs. I do not believe the Fed will 
ultimately deliver 50 basis points in December. With oil prices declining, headline inflation should look considerably better in 
December and January, reducing the pressure for aggressive tightening. 
The collapse in energy prices is an important positive that the market is largely ignoring. Gasoline has fallen to around $3.69 
per gallon from its $4.50 peak, and wholesale prices suggest we could see $3.50. That would put nearly a dollar per gallon 
back into consumers' pockets, providing hundreds of dollars in additional purchasing power during the holiday shopping 
season. With the labor market remaining resilient and little evidence of weakness in the real economy, I believe the economic 
fundamentals are considerably stronger than market sentiment. 
The bigger question is whether the AI trade is undergoing a more fundamental reassessment. Nvidia's earnings were strong, 
but earnings are backward-looking, and investors are increasingly questioning whether the enormous investment in AI 
infrastructure can generate sufficient returns. Anthropic's IPO, which was priced at high end of ranges, quickly surrendered 
most of its initial gains, reinforcing those concerns. Some believe the tech selloff was caused by this latest IPO taking the 
oxygen from the rest of the market. I don’t believe that to be the case. The real issue is whether future demand and 
monetization will justify these extraordinary valuations, particularly as token prices continue to fall. 
AI is absolutely a transformative technology, but that does not mean every company investing in AI will earn an attractive 
return. We saw something similar with the internet, when collapsing fiber-optic costs eventually created enormous benefits 
for businesses using the technology, even as many infrastructure investors suffered. Falling AI costs could produce the same 
outcome, with substantial opportunities for companies benefiting from cheaper computing power. 
Despite the weakness, the S&P 500 remains positive for the year, and I expect value stocks to regain their relative strength as 
investors recognize that this is primarily a technology-driven correction rather than a deterioration in economic 
fundamentals. Lower oil prices should eventually bring interest rates down and provide additional support to the broader 
market. We may see more volatility before year-end, but I remain constructive on equities heading into 2027. Stocks will 
probably snap back this week, and end the year higher than what we saw Friday. The lower the market finishes this year, the 
greater the potential for gains next year.
