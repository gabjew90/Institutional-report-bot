CONCLUSION
In an unexpected move, Jensen Huang has been re-instated as CEO of Nvidia. Colette
Kress to remain as CFO and Harvey Jones as board-chair. The board voted unanimously to
reinstate Jensen following a series of meetings. The board also received input from senior
levels of the US government (USG), and based on those discussions, it is confident that
Jensen's reinstatement does not pose any national security risk. The board added that
Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and his reinstatement is in the best interests
of stockholders, employees, partners and customers. The stock and AI complex are up
significantly following Jensen's reinstatement. It removes the uncertainty around the CEO
position and future direction of the company, as well as the AI ecosystem, since Jensen's
removal 12 days ago, Nov 11th. We remain O/W on Nvidia and continue to view the stock
as attractive at current levels.
•
Jensen reinstated as CEO: In a press release pre-market, the Nvidia board has
unanimously voted to reinstate Jensen as CEO of Nvidia, adding that they are satisfied
in his ability to lead the company, following a series of discussions and meetings with Mr.
Huang. It appears the USG was quite involved in the decision, as the board statement
mentions receiving input from senior levels of the USG. The investigation committee,
formed by the board to probe the targeting of the CEO by foreign intelligence, will
continue to conclusion. The board also formed a National Security and export control
committee (formed of independent directors) to oversee trade compliance and national
security risks. Furthermore, Nvidia is pursuing the addition of a Chief Compliance Officer
to oversee compliance. Down the line, succession planning will also likely be a greater
focus for the board, given there was no obvious candidate or succession planning on who
would succeed Jensen over the last 2-weeks. This would be welcomed by shareholders
and likely make the internal governance of the company stronger.
•
More compliance on the horizon, but fundamentals remain unchanged: NVDA is
likely to face increased scrutiny going forward coming out of the events these past few
weeks. Short term, Congress will likely want their answers from the CEO directly, and
his appearance before Congress next month (closed doors) is likely still going-ahead.
However, with the USG having given input to the board already on national security
concerns, we don't think this will translate into anything meaningful to the stock.
•
Medium-term, GPU chip shipments to Asia are likely going to face increased
pressure: As we move from an environment where 'GPUs are actively being shipped'
to 'what are GPUs are being used for.' That said, we note that ~70% of the AI market is
driven by US companies, EU ~10%, Rest of Asia ~10% and China ~10%. With demand
well ahead of supply, we don't see any impacts to estimates from potentially lower
shipments to Asia, as it can be easily offset with demand elsewhere.

Read across to AI supply chain: The news of Jensen's reinstatement will be welcomed by
the wider supply chain. Jensen is in a unique position as founder/CEO of Nvidia for propelling
the AI market, given its significant GPU market share and through its annual product cadence.
The CEO has built key relationships with customers and supply chain partners, which is
paramount in getting the supply chain to invest in capacity. Lastly, financial support for the
wider AI ecosystem, while the industry is developing, has been primarily driven by Jensen.
His reinstatement will likely remove concerns of a change in the financing strategy.
