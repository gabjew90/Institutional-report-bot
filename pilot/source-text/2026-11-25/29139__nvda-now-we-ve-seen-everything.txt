November 23, 2026
NVDA: Now We've Seen Everything
This morning, NVDA’s Board announced the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as CEO.
Huang’s return was clearly orchestrated by the Trump Administration, and as we
write this the President is on TV discussing his involvement in the Board’s decision.
The Board’s statement noted that based on input from senior levels of the US
government, “the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national
security risk.”
Some quick implications of Huang’s return below:
●Huang’s return is important for NVDA strategically. Naturally, Jensen was
irreplaceable as a strategic visionary. His return brings back his expertise,
relationships, and will help NVDA employee morale.
●The Administration’s involvement likely to calm investor fears regarding
fallout from improper sales. While the press has been focused on the potential
for illicit NVDA sales into China, there has thus far been no evidence presented
that NVDA shipped product in violation of regulations. In this morning’s press
release, the Board indicated that it would continue its investigation related
to Huang being targeted by foreign intelligence. The Board also formed a
National Security and Export Control Committee, and would hire a Chief
Compliance Officer. This, as well as Huang’s strong endorsement from the
Trump Administration, will help to calm investor fears. Nonetheless, the
incoming Democratic Congress is not likely to drop the issue, and we do expect
a Congressional inquiry. With regard to our view, while we only know what they
company has disclosed, it’s been our view that since demand from legitimate
customers is so far ahead of NVDA’s ability to supply, it would make no sense
for NVDA to purposely ship where it was not permitted to do so.
●More disclosure regarding geographic revenue would be welcome. Another
investor concern has been NVDA’s exposure to SE Asia and Brazilian data
centers, which have been subject to new export controls since early last week.
We note that such shipments were not prohibited until last week, but are
now subject to new controls depending on the ownership of the data center
and the data center's customers. NVDA’s position following the earnings call
is that, since demand is so far ahead of supply, any impact from these new
regulations would be insignificant. Nonetheless, we encourage the company to
over disclose in this area, to make it abundantly clear about both the geographic
ship to location and the customer HQ location for all of its revenue. We think
disclosure is the factor that will reassure investors with respect to this issue.

●We still expect a higher level of scrutiny regarding financing deals. We believe NVDA management intended to
send a subtle message during the earnings call that future financing deals by NVDA would be subject to increased
scrutiny going forward. We don’t think that necessarily changes with Jensen’s reinstatement, and we don’t think
that’s a bad thing. We think tension between the CEO and CFO on such financial matters is necessary and proper
– it’s the CFO’s job to scrutinize such deals. Given that demand remains well above NVDA’s ability to supply,
it’s in NVDA’s interest to assist with financing for only the most financially sound deals. Similar to the approach
from AVGO, we get the sense that such financing is only intended to be a short-term endeavor, as neocloud and
frontier model customers businesses mature.
