AI inflection
As Tony P writes, the “agentic revolution is here.” Meta’s Muse marks another inflection in applied AI,
with markets wasting little time pricing it.
GS’s Rich Privorotsky sees Muse as only the first of many products bringing capabilities previously
reserved for AI super-users to the mass market. As large corporates provide the safety, infrastructure
and distribution, adoption could explode. The winners and losers will take time to sort out, but
Privorotsky sees the aggregate impact as positive for equities and structurally disinflationary.
Did anyone say resilient stocks?
Investors may be looking beyond the original AI winners, but GS argues it is too early to move on.
Hyperscaler earnings have caught up with performance, while the median AI infrastructure stock has
de-rated from 32x forward earnings in April to 22x today. The original AI trade is a lot less expensive
than it looks.

Superglue
DB’s Jim Reid argues that growth remains the “superglue” holding markets together. It is hard to
explain surging yields alongside equities near record highs, tight credit spreads and subdued cross-
asset vol without strong underlying growth.
The key driver is an unusually powerful global capex cycle, fueled not just by AI but also government
spending on strategic autonomy across multiple industries. These booms are rare and tend to last: the
largest historical capex cycles averaged around three years.
DB estimates the boom could run for five years and add 3–6% cumulatively to GDP, potentially making
it the second-largest post-war capex cycle. Global data surprises are already on track for their
strongest positive run on record.
Consensus may still be underestimating the growth impulse.

The $1.4 trillion question
AI capex is getting so large that the debate is shifting from how much will they spend? to can they
actually earn a return on it?
GS’s Eric Sheridan estimates Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle and SpaceX need to generate
a combined $1.42 trillion of cumulative revenue in 2028–30 to earn a 15% ROIC on their 2026–27 AI
buildout.
That sounds enormous. The next number makes it look less crazy.
GS
The backlog is already there
The three big public cloud players, Amazon, Microsoft and Alphabet, already have a combined $1.69
trillion backlog.
GS calculates they need to convert only around 59% of that existing backlog into future revenue to
clear the 15% ROIC hurdle on their AI investments.
The AI capex debate looks rather different when much of the required demand is already sitting in the
order book.

From tokens to agents
The next leg of AI monetization is moving from conversational AI toward AI that actually does things.
GS sees enterprises shifting from experimental “token-maxing” toward productive “token-optimizing,”
while agentic AI accelerates the move from chat interfaces to action-driven platforms.
More productivity per token + more use cases = a potentially much larger revenue pool against all
that infrastructure spending.
Capex now, cash flow later
The hyperscalers are spending aggressively, but the payback periods may be shorter than feared.
Amazon, for example, points to roughly three-year breakevens on server and networking capex.
That makes the near-term ROIC compression important: returns can look ugly while the infrastructure
is being built, then improve rapidly as utilization and revenues catch up.
The real AI question is no longer whether capex is exploding. It is whether revenues can catch up fast
enough. GS thinks the math works.
Cheap hedge
Not a clean arb, but the relative-value setup is getting hard to ignore. Hyperscaler CDS spreads have
blown out relative to QQQ put vol.
Credit is pricing much more AI stress than equity options. We like to listen to the tape, and the tape
remains resilient. Run the longs, hedge them with cheap QQQ puts.
