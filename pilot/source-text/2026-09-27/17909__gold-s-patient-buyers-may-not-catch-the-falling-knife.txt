Gold’s buyers face the rate shock
Gold first rose despite a stronger dollar and higher Treasury yields. It has now surrendered that
rebound. The live test is whether patient official buyers can provide support while faster-moving
investors adjust to the rate shock.
Gold resisted. Then rates kept going.
The week of the Fed hike created the puzzle. The US 10-year yield moved above 5% and the dollar
strengthened, yet gold rebounded after the decision. Higher yields raise the cost of holding a metal
that pays no income, while a stronger dollar makes it more expensive for buyers using other
currencies.
By 24 September, gold was back near its Fed-day low while yields and DXY had climbed further. Gold
resisted the first shock; it has not become immune to the next one.
LSEG Workspace
The patient buyer
GS estimates central bank gold buying at a seasonally adjusted pace of 91 tonnes a month over the
recent three months, versus an average of 17 tonnes before 2022. Its forecast assumes 60 tonnes a
month through 2027.
Reserve managers diversifying their holdings can keep buying through a Fed tightening cycle. That is a
plausible source of support beneath gold, though it does not tell us who bought on any particular day.
GS/TME
The faster buyer
ETF investors can change course much faster. Global physically backed gold ETFs shed 74 tonnes in
June, added 23 tonnes in July and then added 121 tonnes in August, according to the World Gold
Council. Funds were buying heavily before the September rate shock.
Gold’s decline since 18 September is an early warning, not proof that ETF money has left. The next
holdings data matter: official buying may support the longer horizon without setting the price on every
trading day.

WGC
A floor can still give way
GS cut its end-2026 gold target from $4,900 to $4,650 after the hike but kept its end-2027 target at
$5,400. The near-term path depends more on investors responding to rates; the longer one depends
heavily on continuing official purchases.
There is also positioning to clear. GS says gold call option positioning is about three times its
historical average. Its more hawkish Fed scenario takes gold toward $4,070 before a partial recovery
toward $4,200 by year-end. That is a downside scenario, not its base case.
GS
The patient buyer may not catch the falling
knife
Central banks may still want gold at these prices. That does not mean they will absorb every ETF sale
or options unwind. The fast money can leave long before the patient buyer shows up.
Gold has already given back its post-Fed rebound as yields and DXY pushed higher. If it keeps falling
against those headwinds, the official-buyer story is offering less protection than bulls hoped. The signal
to watch is gold holding up again against rates and the dollar.
