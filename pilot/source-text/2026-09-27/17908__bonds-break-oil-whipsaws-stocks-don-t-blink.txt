Bonds break. Stocks haven’t blinked
The bond market is setting the pace. The 10-year has broken out of a three-year range, MOVE has
surged, and small caps are feeling the pressure. Equities and VIX still look calm, but that calm faces a
test as buybacks fade into October.
Oil is causing pain of its own: crowded longs have been flushed, yet oil vol shows little stress. Across
assets, the question is how long equity calm can survive faster moves in rates.
Vacuum
Yesterday’s surge in the US 10-year took yields to levels we have barely seen in two decades.
After three years of consolidation, the breakout has opened up a vacuum above the range. Watch the
5.2% area first. Beyond that, the next major level is 5.5%. More here.

Gradually, then suddenly
On September 10 (here), we argued that bond vol was pricing far too little risk. Bond vol was starting to
rise, but rates were still moving in an orderly fashion. Our read was that the market was betting
against the house, and bond vol looked attractive if traders decided to press the move.
Yesterday, they did. The 10-year surged and MOVE exploded higher. The orderly phase may be over.

Painful in oil
Quinn notes that speculative WTI gross longs rose $6bn from August 11 to September 15, lifting gross
positioning to the 93rd percentile. Short selling offset much of that build, leaving net positioning up just
$2.1bn, at the 85th percentile.
The crowded trade then met a 10.2% plunge in November WTI over the next five sessions as Saudi
Arabia repaired its East-West pipeline, some oil moved through Hormuz, and US-Iran talks showed
signs of progress.

Buyback blackout
The largest and most reliable price-insensitive bid is going quiet. Per Goldman, an estimated "64% of
S&P 500 Index corporates had entered the blackout window as of 09/21/26," climbing from roughly
320 corps in late September toward a plateau near 490 by mid-October. Rising to ~85% by week-end
means the corporate bid steps back just as CTAs are set up to sell.
