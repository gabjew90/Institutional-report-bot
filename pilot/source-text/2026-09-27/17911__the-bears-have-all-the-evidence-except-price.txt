Rates are screaming, equities are refusing to
listen
The 10-year is technically stretched, bond vol has exploded and tech CDS are flashing stress, yet NDX
remains remarkably resilient. Part of the answer may be that the rates shock is being driven by growth
and real yields rather than inflation, while the AI demand story continues to hold up.
The disconnect is getting extreme. For now, the tape still wins, but the cost of hedging that resilience
remains unusually cheap.
Extreme
The rates surge has pushed the 10-year yield’s RSI to levels we haven’t seen in a long time. The macro
backdrop is different, but RSI was similarly stretched around the local yield high in January 2025.
Thursday’s late move had the feel of someone big getting stopped out. That is a warning against
chasing yields higher here, not a call that the top is in. More here.

Why stocks haven't blinked
Jim Reid at Deutsche Bank points out that almost all of this year’s rise in the 10-year yield has come
from real yields, with little change in inflation expectations. His read is that bonds are repricing growth
and the path for rates more than an inflation shock.
Our equity read: stronger growth may be helping SPX absorb higher yields through earnings. But that
is an index-level story. Russell’s weakness shows that more rates-sensitive stocks are already feeling
the squeeze. If real yields stay up here, the gap between calm equities and panicked bonds becomes
harder to ignore.

The $1.4 trillion question
AI capex is getting so large that the debate is shifting from how much will they spend? to can they
actually earn a return on it?
GS’s Eric Sheridan estimates Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle and SpaceX need to generate
a combined $1.42 trillion of cumulative revenue in 2028–30 to earn a 15% ROIC on their 2026–27 AI
buildout.
That sounds enormous. The next number makes it look less crazy.

The backlog is already there
The three big public cloud players, Amazon, Microsoft and Alphabet, already have a combined $1.69
trillion backlog.
GS calculates they need to convert only around 59% of that existing backlog into future revenue to
clear the 15% ROIC hurdle on their AI investments.
The AI capex debate looks rather different when much of the required demand is already sitting in the
order book. More here.
