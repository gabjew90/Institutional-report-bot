Half The Stocks In The S&P Have A Negative Beta 
To The Index Itself: Goldman's "Chart Of The Year" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, SEP 24, 2026 - 01:45 AM 
Goldman's derivatives guru, Brian Garrett, is out with his "chart of the day" (and "possibly chart 
of the year"), which shows that almost half of the stocks in the S&P500 have a negative beta to 
the index itself (all time high by significant margin) . 
 
The Goldman trader notes that the FT described this phenomenon quite accurately yesterday: 
“pick a stock, just not any stock”  
Referring to the latest note by Goldman strategist Christian Mueller-Glissman (report available 
here to pro subs), Garrett writes that index concentration as a function of both AI capex and 
tech dominance is a clear explanation (but not the only one).  
More ominously, he makes the following observations:  


[Page 2]
 
●​ this dynamic can increase risks of a momentum unwinds at the factor level (remember 
July?)   
●​ the index itself is providing more diversification against a risk-off unwind than historical  
The negative beta (ie, diversification aspect) can also help explain the relative spread between 
index vs single stock convexity (.vixvixeq index on bbg)  
Should this phenomenon revert, the Goldman trader says that long S&P index wings should 
work, and long correlation trades will work. 
To hedge for the coming reversal, the trading desk likes playing RSP > SPX outperformance 
options (~65% implied corr) ... 
 
... as well as puts on the GS momentum index (gsp1momo) which imploded during the July 
factor momentum unwind.
