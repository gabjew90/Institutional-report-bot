agreed on a new joint arrangement on trade.

2) Banxico Recap


[Page 2]
LATAM ECONOMICS (RAMOS): The MPC kept the policy rate at 6.50% (unanimous decision), in line
with GS and market consensus. The forward guidance was modified and became more fluid (less neutral)
in a nod to FOMC rate hikes. The 2027 headline/core inflation forecasts did not change. In our
assessment, the 2026/27 inflation forecasts remain too optimistic. The balance of risks for inflation
remains skewed to the upside and for growth to the downside. The MPC expects the economy to operate
with slack (idle capacity) over the forecasting horizon. We expect the MPC to keep the policy rate
unchanged at 6.50% over the foreseeable future, but a potential intensification of hawkish global monetary
winds could force the central bank to react given the current very thin Mexico-US interest rate differential.

EM FX TRADING (FERNANDO ALVARADO AGUILAR): At current levels, we have seen some

3) Brazil Election Trade Ideas
Trade ideas across FX, Rates, Credit and Equities to position for Brazil's presidential elections (h/t
Fabiano Ribeiro, Brazil FICC Sales).
Our polling average has President Lula holding a narrow lead in the first round while the runoff scenario is
effectively a toss-up. The gap between the two frontrunners has continued to compress: Lula and Flávio
Bolsonaro now command ~89% of first-round valid votes vs. ~86% a week ago (AtlasIntel Poll, 23-Sep-
26), and the market is starting to price a non-trivial probability of a first-round decision. Support for third-
way candidates has steadily eroded, reinforcing the perception that this is increasingly a binary choice
between President Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro. Lula's approval ratings have stabilized somewhat after the
15% Bolsa Família increase, but disapproval still exceeds approval by ~5% (AtlasIntel Poll, 23-Sep-26).

to 5y rates, trading US$75mn/day, historically ~3x the Ibovespa when the market prices a cutting cycle.

rallies double digits post-event when vols should fall.

4) US PORTFOLIO STRATEGY (HAMMOND) - AI Capex, Required Revenues, and What's Priced in
the AI trade - LINK
BOTTOM LINE: The largest US AI hyperscalers are on track to spend $800 billion in capex in 2026 and
consensus expects $1.1 trillion in 2027. Our baseline forecast is that capex spending will surpass
consensus in 2027 but the rate of both capex growth and upside surprises will diminish relative to recent
quarters. Based on average annual capex in 2026 and 2027, our equity analysts estimate hyperscalers need
to generate annual AI revenues of roughly $300 billion in the next few years to break even. Hyperscaler
cloud revenues have accelerated sharply, annualizing roughly $70 billion above the pre-AI trend in Q2
2026, with announced revenue backlogs exceeding $1.5 trillion. We estimate AI users would need to spend
roughly $1 trillion annually on AI applications for hyperscalers to generate solid ROI and the application
layer to generate strong profit margins - comparing with roughly $1.5 trillion in global software spending
in 2026 and $1.8 trillion in potential annual US labour productivity gains our economists estimate will
eventually accrue to capital. Market skepticism about AI infrastructure earnings durability is at least

partially reflected in pricing, with some semiconductor stocks and hyperscalers trading at low earnings
multiples.
Capex Outlook: GS equity analysts estimate hyperscaler capex will register $1.2 trillion in 2027 and $1.4
trillion in 2028, $130bn and $100bn above consensus respectively, but nonetheless implying deceleration
from nearly 100% growth in 2026 to 54% in 2027 and 12% in 2028. Hyperscaler capex now exceeds cash
flow from operations, meaning further growth will increasingly need funding via debt and equity issuance.
Concentration in the investment grade market is a more relevant bottleneck than balance sheet capacity,
requiring additional funding from private credit and equity issuance.

5) US ECONOMICS (MEI) – Do the PMI Surveys Signal That Growth Is Surging? - LINK
BOTTOM LINE: The S&P US PMIs rose to their highest levels since early 2022 in September's
preliminary report, raising concerns about overheating and prompting a sharp rise in Treasury yields. We
think the bond market response was an overreaction for two main reasons. First, the PMIs have been much
firmer than most other business surveys released so far this month, which have increased more modestly
or even declined. Second, part of the strength likely reflects nominal bias - the boost to survey index levels
from respondents who think in dollar rather than real terms. We estimate the S&P PMIs' September index
levels would be roughly 2pt lower without this bias. Our composite business survey tracker remains close
to its long-run average, pointing to a healthy pace of activity but no sign of a surge to a far above-trend
pace. We continue to expect 2026 GDP growth of 2.2% on both a Q4/Q4 and full-year basis.
