'Short Unprofitable Tech'; Top Goldman Macro 
Trader Says Market's Still Slave To Energy & Rates 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SATURDAY, SEP 26, 2026 - 07:25 PM 
The S&P is still a rates-and-oil derivative with a Nasdaq wrapper. 
Goldman Sachs macro equity trader, Faris Mourad, spent Friday night reminding clients that this 
does not mean every other narrative is dead - it means the thematic book only gets to breathe 
when energy vol and the 10-year give it permission. 
On the week, the house AI pair (GSPUARTI) was +7.7%, the HALO trade (GSPUHALO) +6.0%, 
and megacap vs. unprofitable tech (GSPUMENP) +3.9% - all extending significant gains in a 
month where the S&P has gone nowhere. 
 
The debate has migrated from "does AI exist" to "who owns the application layer", with the 
market dragging the highly-volatile AI infra beneficiaries back for a second look. 
Five items stand out to Mourad as potential opportunities this week... 


[Page 2]
 
AI agents have entered the conversation. 
Agentic AI adoption is ramping across consumer product discovery, research, and choice 
optimization, which our analysts estimate will be a 3-5+ years transition, analogous to the 
development of eCommerce. 
As AI agents become a more important starting point of consumer shopping experiences, the 
strategic question shifts from who owns the digital storefront, to who owns discovery, intent and 
infra required to fulfill a trusted transaction.  
Investor focus over the last week has largely been on the Agentic (GSXUAGNT) vs Consumer 
Inertia trade (GSXUSWCH) which we expect will continue as the market discovers incremental 
agentic application proofs. 
The AI debate is all about application. GSPUAGNT has rallied amid emerging agentic use 
cases 
 
Note, GSXUSWCH has declined -12% since launch in end August. 
But increased agentic adoption will also likely drive increased demand for cyber security and 
fraud prevention

Investors should also pay attention to a growing cyber security TAM – and our Cybersecurity 
basket (GSTMTCYB) - as additional risks and unknowns around machine-to-machine 
transactions unfold. 
Our Cybersecurity basket (GSTMTCYB) has rallied >130% from YTD lows, but a growing TAM 
will likely drive significant further upside in coming months

Alternatively, lowering friction via agents could lead to more consumer demand and thus higher

Its time to short Non-Profitable Tech.  
US 10Y yields have been higher for 7 months in a row, inclusive of September, tied for the 
longest streak in 50 years.  
With the equity market at its highs, investor conversations reflect increased anxiety of what a 
downside scenario could be if the 10Y approached 6%. 
Non-profitable Tech (GSXUNPTC) tends to be the most vulnerable pocket of the equity market 
to higher rates, but over the last year that rates correlation broke down, with increased retail 
participation and still solid nominal growth supporting the Cyclicals vs. Defensives trade. 
Non-Profitable Tech (GSXUNPTC) tends to be the most vulnerable pocket to higher rates and 
performance is starting to break as we breach 100bp+ 
 
With a 100bp+ expansion in the 10Y and GSXUNPTC outperforming SPX by more than 20pp 
over the last year, we think the setup to short is now very compelling.

The basket has started to underperform the market this month (-11.8%) and we note a sharp 
decline in retail participation YTD. 
Power trade getting its SPARK back. 
The US DOE committed to $5.25Bn spend to help fund 31 grid-improvement projects across 26 
states in order to improve grid reliability and lower electricity costs. 
The plan estimates driving an additional 20+GW of grid capacity. Power (GSX1POW1) has 
been a laggard in the AI rally as energy inflation and regulatory intervention risks weighed on 
the trade.  
We’re now seeing a narrative shift towards greater power grid investment over price cap 
policies, while valuation and positioning have cleaned up to offer a solid ‘catch up’ opportunity 
into year-end. 
 
Europe’s Power (GSXEPOWR) and Renewable (GSXERNEW) trades also stand to benefit from 
increased power investment rhetoric in the US, and incremental datacentre announcements in 
Europe.

Fiscal charges the European bull. 
To be a bull on European equities, you need fiscal stimulus to deliver. But potential for a positive 
fiscal impulse differs significantly across countries. We are bullish German Fiscal Beneficiaries 
(GSXEGFSC) as Germany executes its fiscal spend above expectations and with a healthy 
<65% debt-to-GDP ratio. While the equities have derated on the energy shock, we think they’re 
now ripe for inflection amid solid manufacturing PMI growth and earnings upside into 3Q 
reporting based on supportive corporate commentary from our German conference. 
In contrast, France is entering an election year with limited progress on deficit reduction, while 
borrow costs are on the rise. Our economists expect debt-to-GDP to increase to 125% by 
2030E, driving a negative feedback loop of higher debt and lower growth. In this environment, 
we expect French domestics (GSXEFRDO) to continue underperforming, beyond the -10% 
decline since early August particularly given a significant disconnect in equity and credit risk 
pricing (OAT-Bund +100bp). 
French Domestics vs. Internationals (GSPEFRDI) have historically moved with credit spreads, 
implying significant equity downside if spreads remain stretched 
 
But very near-term, there is scope for relief as the narrative around Le Pen’s agenda shifts more 
bond market favorable, particularly evidenced by the move in French Banks (GSXEFRBK) 
today. We prefer expressing our cautious medium-term view via Dec put spreads on 
GSXEFRDO. GSXEFRDO Dec 95%/85% put spread costs 1.35%, 20d, 7.4x pay-out.

Brazil goes to the polls next week. 
Ahead of the first round of Brazil’s presidential elections (Oct 4), polls suggest the gap between 
the two front-runners has continued to narrow and waning support for third-way candidates 
reinforces the perception of an increasingly binary choice between President Lula and Flavio 
Bolsonaro. 
Across assets, our recent investor survey indicates Brazil equities are the least owned, but also 
the pocket that could see the largest upside in a Bolsonaro win scenario (h/t Nelson Armbrust). 
 
We like owning upside in our Brazil rate sensitive basket (GSBZRATE), which have potential to 
re-rate 30%+ (P/E from 9x to 12x) if local rates price in 200bps of cuts. Our investor 
conversations have indicated a range of 200-300bps of rate cuts in a Bolsonaro win outcome. 
So Goldman's house view, stripped of the polish is that the index is still collateral for energy and 
duration, but the book is being rebuilt around four crowded-to-uncrowded rotations: agents vs. 
legacy consumer, short the unprofitable-tech zombie that outran a 100bp bond selloff, own the 
grid now that Washington is writing checks instead of price caps, and split Europe into German 
fiscal winners and French domestics you hedge with puts. 
Brazil is the weekend lottery ticket: under-owned, binary, and a 200–300bp local-rate fantasy if 
the ballot breaks one way. 
None of that survives a 6% 10-year or another oil spike.
