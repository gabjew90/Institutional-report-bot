Amazon blocked the bot
Amazon has blocked Meta’s Muse from shopping on its site. Yet Amazon Ads is testing a way for
selected advertisers to reach people in ChatGPT.
The bigger question is no longer which bot can enter Amazon’s store. It is who gets paid when an AI
assistant helps decide what a customer buys.
There is a large business to defend. Amazon reported $68.6bn in global advertising revenue in 2025,
while EMARKETER forecasts $56.71bn in US Amazon retail-media spending in 2026.
Emarketer/TME
Discovery moves. The checkout may not.
Brands pay Amazon because shoppers arrive ready to compare and buy. An AI agent could move part
of that decision earlier: the customer asks for a shortlist and sees a recommendation before ever
opening Amazon.
That could reduce the value of a sponsored search result on Amazon.
But the recommendation does not have to finish the purchase. The customer may still visit Amazon for
reviews, price, delivery and availability. Amazon’s fulfilment and checkout remain valuable.
That creates the interesting part: a brand could end up paying for influence in the agent’s shortlist and
then paying again for placement when the shopper reaches Amazon.
The double toll
AI shopping may not eliminate the advertising toll. It may simply add another one.
If the AI recommendation does not bring a new buyer, the merchant is potentially paying twice to win
the same customer. Someone then has to absorb that second toll: the merchant takes a smaller profit,
cuts spending elsewhere or raises the price.
TME
Amazon wants both sides
Amazon isn't simply waiting to see where discovery goes. Alexa for Shopping can already display
sponsored products, while its ChatGPT pilot extends selected campaigns into an outside AI experience.
In other words, Amazon is trying to earn ad revenue upstream as well as at the point of purchase.
Shopify has a different opening. It helps merchants make products available across AI channels and
complete transactions, including through Muse when available. It could benefit from more agent-
driven orders without having to own the ad slot. 
AMZN: defend/extend the toll.
META: potentially create a new toll.

The market isn't waiting for the revenue
Muse has become part of the bull case as Meta rallies, but investors are not waiting for the
monetisation numbers.
Vanda’s options-flow model points to one of the largest weeks of institutional META call buying in two
years. That shows how aggressively investors are positioning for upside, even though it does not tell us
how much of that demand is specifically about shopping ads.
Vanda
Who pays?
Amazon’s reviews, delivery and checkout give it ways to keep earning even when a shopper arrives
with an agent’s recommendation.
For Amazon, watch advertising revenue relative to marketplace activity. For Meta, watch whether Muse
turns usage into disclosed revenue. For merchants, watch whether paying across both channels brings
more orders, or simply raises the cost of the same sale.
AI shopping may add an advertising toll rather than remove one. The harder question is whether the
next dollar of ad revenue comes from a new customer or a second charge on the old one.
