Bonds are panicking, stocks are not
Bonds are flashing panic. Stocks are still acting as if this is a growth story. The 10-year looks stretched,
MOVE has exploded, and bond books face a VaR shock. Yet SPX has barely blinked. The question is
whether this stays a bond-market event or forces equities to catch up.
Extreme
The rates surge has pushed the 10-year yield’s RSI to levels we haven’t seen in a long time. The macro
backdrop is different, but RSI was similarly stretched around the local yield high in January 2025.
Thursday’s late move had the feel of someone big getting stopped out. That is a warning against
chasing yields higher here, not a call that the top is in.

Pure panic

move, adding selling pressure even after yields start to look stretched.

The great vol dislocation
Bond vol is screaming while VIX is barely responding. The MOVE–VIX gap has blown out to extreme
levels. One market is pricing a shock; the other is still trading as if it can be contained.
LSEG Workspace
No care mode
SPX has also held up against the MOVE surge. That divergence can last if stocks see higher yields as a
sign of stronger growth. It gets harder to sustain if bond-market stress starts forcing broader risk
reduction.
LSEG Workspace
Why stocks haven't blinked
Jim Reid at Deutsche Bank points out that almost all of this year’s rise in the 10-year yield has come
from real yields, with little change in inflation expectations. His read is that bonds are repricing growth
and the path for rates more than an inflation shock.
Our equity read: stronger growth may be helping SPX absorb higher yields through earnings. But that
is an index-level story. Russell’s weakness shows that more rates-sensitive stocks are already feeling
the squeeze. If real yields stay up here, the gap between calm equities and panicked bonds becomes
harder to ignore.
