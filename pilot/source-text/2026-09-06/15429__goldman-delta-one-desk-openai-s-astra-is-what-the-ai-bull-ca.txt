Goldman Delta One Desk: OpenAI's Astra Is What 
The AI Bull Case Has Been Waiting For 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, SEP 04, 2026 - 09:45 PM 
"Astra looks like it matters," says Goldman Sachs Delta-One desk-head Rich Privorotsky in a 
must read note this morning (available here for pro subs). 
 
SoftBank ripped over 10% higher overnight (as a proxy for OpenAI through its holdings) and 
Oracle jumped about 5.5%, but the stock pops are the least interesting part of the story. 


[Page 2]
 
 
Interestingly, OpenAI's ecosystem notably outperformed Anthropic's this week... 
 
What the market is really reacting to is the idea that a leading model has taken a genuine step 
away from the pack - and that is exactly the outcome the AI bull case has been hoping for. 
Astra benchmark scores of AGI - where artificial general intelligence surpasses human 
capabilities - gives OpenAI confidence to claim it has overtaken Anthropic on some measures. 
As OpenAI President Greg Brockman said yesterday, Astra is a "generational leap" and said it 
could eventually be viewed as the moment AGI arrived. 
"I think it might be about this model," he said when asked whether Astra meets that definition. 


[Page 3]
 
While there is no agreed-upon test for AGI, and Brockman said that he will leave the final 
judgment to users, he ended the call with reporters by saying, "Welcome to the AGI era." 
 
Greg Brockman Photographer: Jordan Vonderhaar/Bloomberg 
Cheaper usage is one thing.  
A real jump in capability is another. 
As Privorotsky explains this morning: 
When the models get meaningfully smarter, companies do not just use the same amount of AI 
at a lower price. They find new reasons to use it, and the whole demand picture shifts again. 
That forces every other lab to chase, keeps the spending cycle from going stale, and revives the 
sense that there is still something meaningfully better to build toward. 
Privorotsky says that some of his comments from yesterday, which we detailed here (on the rate 
of increase in token demand not keeping up with the pace of token costs decline), were taken 
out of context. 
His exact comment was: 
"More demand + lower prices is not automatically bullish if pricing falls faster than consumption 
grows. Unless capability keeps making radical jumps (looking at you, Astra)… periods of 
compute oversupply become plausible. " 


[Page 4]
 
 
Today he clarifies he was not a bearish call on AI (though he admits that the hardware complex 
has been getting cheaper relative to earnings, and it is worth understanding why that is 
happening). 
 
However, as he states, Astra, if anything, points the other way on the core thesis: 
...a breakthrough in intelligence is what brings back the desire - and the necessity - to keep 
spending. 
Privo concludes by noting that the September calendar also looks helpful rather than hostile.  
Oracle reports next week, the conference circuit is busy, and there are plenty of places for 
bullish implications to leak into the rest of the complex. 
We have five words for all this exuberance: "Do you believe in miracles?"
