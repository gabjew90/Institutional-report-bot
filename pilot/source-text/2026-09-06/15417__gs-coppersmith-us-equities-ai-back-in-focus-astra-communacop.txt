Lee Coppersmith  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director · FICC & Equities

A lot of investors seem to be holding their breath for what next week’s inflation data might bring, but the
latest on the AI front may be more important for stocks. The release of OpenAI’s Astra model, lighter
positioning and a big round of micro updates - headlined by our marquee Communacopia + Technology
Conference - make for a pretty interesting setup... All with the SPX straddle for the upcoming week at
~1%...
On the macro front, our research team has flagged that further increases in risk appetite likely need lower
rates more than stronger growth expectations; Our Risk Appetite Indicator’s growth component is already
near the top of its post-COVID range, while the monetary policy component remains negative. Lower
energy prices with a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, alongside further progress on US inflation, would
be a friendly combination (i.e. more Goldilocks pricing). But with a decent amount of growth optimism
already embedded across assets, there is less room for growth disappointments or another negative
geopolitical shock.


[Page 2]
The earnings/valuation dynamic is also worth keeping in mind. It’s no secret a lot of this year’s earnings
strength has come from Tech, supported by AI capex and gains on equity investments. Investors have been
less willing to pay higher multiples for increasingly cyclical earnings, particularly with leverage rising
across the AI ecosystem. Higher long-end yields have also pressured valuations outside Tech. Our strategy
team expects lower equity Sharpe ratios from here, though lower rates alongside resilient growth could
help multiples again - especially as AI adoption broadens beyond the infrastructure buildout;
The market needs fresh catalysts, and next week could deliver. OpenAI kicks off our Communacopia
conference Tuesday at 9:30am PT, with 40 public/private companies across our research team’s lineup.
The focus: AI spending, returns and competition into 2027 and beyond. Our team still sees upside to
capex, with compute demand outstripping supply well into 2028. Beyond AI, expect updates on the digital
consumer and the trade-off between growth, investment and margins;


[Page 3]
Our PB data shows exposure levels are still quite friendly. Gross leverage for US fundamental long/shorts
is in the 27th percentile over the last year, and net leverage is in just the 4th percentile. There has been
some rebuilding recently, but positioning looks very different versus two months ago;


[Page 4]
The reduction in positioning is also reflected in the options market, namely in tech vols. Semis volatility in
particular has seen a dramatic reset, in what has been a painful stretch for long-vol positions. VXSMH - a
proxy for semiconductor implied volatility - has fallen from roughly 65 in July to 36. Nearly cut in half,
and back toward where we started 2026.
But the market hasn’t really pivoted on its micro optimism. Put-call skew on the average S&P stock is still
very flat, near the lows of the last three years. The vol reset has compressed the implied range of
outcomes, particularly across AI, without much of a bearish tilt taking hold. Less being priced for how far
stocks can move, but still very little premium for downside relative to upside.

Speaking of things cut in half - our Tech Long/Short Momentum pair has now seen a ~50% drawdown
since the late-June highs. That’s even after Friday’s near-7% bounce. It’s been anything but easy for a lot
of investors trying to navigate recent rotations in the equity space.


[Page 7]
Some interesting charts here too. MU, SNDK, EWY and DRAM are starting to break above their recent
downtrends after spending much of the summer consolidating…


[Page 8]
On Korea specifically, the last four weeks of equity flows have been supported purely by buybacks despite
net selling from the rest of the investor base. Still room for broader participation. It will be interesting to
see if demand returns post summer;


[Page 9]
The takeaway for me: owning right-tail expressions through options makes a lot of sense here given these
dynamics. Light positioning, a dramatic reset in implied vols and plenty of potential catalysts in the
coming days. It’s worth noting Friday started to show a little of this excitement returning to our space,
with decent upside buying.


[Page 10]
If we’re framing where to hedge risk at the moment, downside on our SPX ex-AI index (SPXXAI) offers
really attractive convexity. It’s still sitting at the highs as investors were forced to rotate away from AI for
most of the last two months. A reversal of those flows could leave it vulnerable.
Even pairing long Broad AI (GSTMTAIP) vs short SPXXAI looks compelling at these levels in our view.
Separately, Brazil has been getting more attention. Brazil’s stock market RSI is now 76 – rare territory, but
not totally uncommon – with another ~7% rally needed to get back to the April highs near 200k. Inbound
interest has picked up from macro hedge funds and value investors, in addition to the traditional
LatAm/EM investor base. Outsized upside prints continue across EWZ and Ibovespa. On elections, the
latest PoderData/Aya poll has Flávio Bolsonaro ahead of Lula for the first time in the runoff scenario, 45%
vs 44%. That compares with 44% vs 45% in the August 27 poll. A narrow gap, but driving a lot of the
interest.
