2) COMMODITIES RESEARCH (GRIGSBY) – Oil Tracker – LINK
BOTTOM LINE: Brent crude has risen to nearly $110 as Saudi Arabia closed its 7mb/d East-West
pipeline following Thursday's attacks and as a meeting between Iran and other Gulf nations on the future
of Hormuz shipping has been postponed. The affected volumes and outage duration remain highly
uncertain — the April East-West pipeline attack reduced flows by 0.7mb/d for four days, but this attack
may be more severe and could threaten the remaining 2mb/d of recent Yanbu exports, with repair
assessments ranging from "very soon" to eight weeks. Saudi exports dropped 2.8mb/d m/m in August as
Yanbu loadings fell below 2mb/d, but have recovered to June averages over the last two weeks, likely
reflecting redirections from Red Sea to Eastern ports. The attacks mark a meaningful escalation and


[Page 2]
increase the probability of our price upside scenario, where Brent exceeds $120 (if 2027 average Gulf
output remains 4mb/d below pre-war levels).
Persian Gulf Production: Recent estimates suggest Persian Gulf crude output decreased by 1.2mb/d in
August from July, implying crude production losses of 7.1mb/d. Production diverged: Saudi slipped on
attacks on both Eastern and Western shipping routes, the US blockade weighed on Iranian production,
while Iraqi and UAE production continued to ramp on growing dark crossings and potential US assistance.
The market has likely shifted to a larger deficit in August (~3mb/d, wider than our balance estimate of
~1mb/d) after being nearly balanced in July. We now estimate a net hit to Persian Gulf exports of 6.7mb/d,
with total recent exports at ~16-17mb/d (~70% of pre-war levels).
Buffers: OECD SPR draws slowed to 0.3mb/d over the last month, with the US accounting for all draws
while Japan and South Korea began rebuilding reserves. China crude imports remain down 4mb/d y/y but
may increase somewhat despite firm prices given security incentives to refill domestic gasoline/diesel
inventories (down 9% yoy) and economic incentives to increase refined product exports. Trump
announced Ukraine and Russia agreed not to hit energy targets, sending European diesel 5% lower, though
Ukraine later warned no deal is finalized. Russian refinery outages remain at 4.6mb/d.

4) Gold Update
See below for a compilation of views on Gold across Trading and Research.
COMMODITIES TRADING (KIM): Higher highs are probably in the distant future, but locally it's going
to be a very choppy path. The CPI print has obviously increased the chances of a Fed hike, which is a
negative outcome for gold, but a lot of that had been priced in over the past week. Another shorter-term
challenge is that the Middle East conflict has spread to the Red Sea, which could further complicate the
logistics of re-routing energy flows. The higher energy goes, the more inflation concerns there are, the
higher the yield curve goes. The longer-term trends are still in place: central bank accumulation by the
Emerging Markets as well as the fiscal challenges faced by the West. It doesn't appear that those are going
to be changing. Ultimately, I think it's very hard to know when the inflection point is going to occur, but at
the $4000/oz price point, we did see quite a bit of institutional and sovereign sponsorship. We think that
prices eventually will take out the ATHs. We need to get through what the Fed is going to do with this data
and how aggressively they signal on a forward basis, as well as getting some clarity around how the
Hormuz situation is going to resolve, before the market can really shake off those concerns and potentially
go to new all-time highs. Listen to more on the [ GS Weekend Macro Call + view our curated Gold
Monitor].
COMMODITIES RESEARCH (THOMAS): Our GS nowcast estimates central bank purchases of 44
tonnes in July (vs. a pre‑2022 average of 17 tonnes), with a large contribution from China. On a 3‑month
seasonally adjusted basis, the trend stands at 91 tonnes/month. We continue to see net upside risk to our
$4,900/oz end-2026 gold forecast, but also greater two-sided volatility along the path. Our fair-value


[Page 4]
forecast of $4,900/toz by end-2026 assumes continued strong central bank demand -- with average
purchases of 50 tonnes/month in 2026 and 40 tonnes/month in 2027 -- alongside a recovery in private
investor ETF demand as the Fed remains on hold in 2026. It does not incorporate elevated demand for
global macro-policy hedges through gold call options. If ETF investor inflows recover and the current
elevated call option positioning persists, dealer hedging could mechanically amplify the rally and drive
gold prices well above our forecast. Conversely, a renewed increase in Fed-hike expectations could
likewise trigger dealer hedge unwinds and produce a sharper-than-usual correction. If the Fed were to
hike, demand for gold as a macro policy hedge might (partially) unwind as market concerns about DM
central bank independence ease. Combined with rate-sensitive ETF holders net selling into higher rates,
gold prices could then reach $4,440/toz by end-2026 -- materially below our base-case of $4,900, but
slightly above today's levels, as continued central bank buying eventually more than offsets.
