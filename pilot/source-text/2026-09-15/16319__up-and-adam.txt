Monday, September 14, 2026
September’s Michigan Sentiment Index came in well below expectations, falling to 47.8, the second-lowest reading on record behind 
May’s 44.8. Elevated fuel prices and trade tensions continue to weigh on consumer confidence, though actual spending has held up 
better than sentiment surveys suggest. Meanwhile, inflation expectations moved higher, with the one-year and five-year measures 
rising to 4.6% and 3.4%, respectively. Combined with recent PPI and CPI reports, the data adds pressure on the Fed to raise rates.

August’s headline CPI matched expectations, rising 0.4% MoM and holding at 3.4% YoY. Core CPI increased 0.3% MoM, slightly above 
forecasts, while the YoY rate moderated to 2.4%. Higher energy prices were the key driver of the headline increase, while lodging costs 
contributed to shelter inflation and broader core pressures. On balance, the data could reasonably support a wait and see policy 
stance, but we now believe recent inflationary pressures will lead FOMC policymakers to raise rates by 25 bps this Wednesday.
