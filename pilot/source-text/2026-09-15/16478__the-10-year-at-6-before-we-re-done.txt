Higher for longer
The 10-year has spent the past three to four years consolidating “up here.” The 200-week MA is now
positively sloping, and a decisive break and close above 5% risks triggering a major vacuum move.
Take out 5%, and this could become the supertanker finally turning, from a multi-year consolidation
into a structural acceleration higher.

Oil Is the Engine, AI Is the Fuel
10-year USTs sit at 5.04% as of writing—not testing 5%, but holding above it, past the July '07 mark.
Brent is back well above $100 after Houthi strikes shut the Saudi East‑West pipeline, with Riyadh's
output at a 30‑year low (~6m b/d) and Hormuz/Bab el‑Mandeb now a two‑front chokepoint risk. This is
a supply shock, which means it doesn't fade on its own, and the WTI/10Y correlation has hit 0.96. Every
dollar higher in oil is another basis point in the long end.
Then there's the quiet second claim on the same buyer pool: the AI build‑out. Hyperscaler capex now
runs >100% of operating cash flow, implying ~$1.3tn of debt by 2028, with tech leading the $380bn
2026 bond supply, competing directly with Treasuries while bidding up chips, power and labor. The
trade funding the equity bull is now financing the tightening cycle. More on this theme here.

6% before it is over?
Once the Fed starts tightening, it rarely stops after one move. Historically, hiking cycles persist for
some time, with the 10-year yield generally rising by more than 100bp along the way.
Judging by the history surveyed by DB’s Jim Reid, investors should brace for the 10-year to reach 6%
before the Fed is done.
DB/Authers
Bond vol
Bond vol, as measured by MOVE, has spiked recently, but pricing remains relatively calm. The last
sharp rise in rates, in March, spilled over into serious bond volatility stress. This time, the move higher
in MOVE has been far more orderly, especially considering the Fed premium already embedded.
LSEG Workspace
SPX struggles
Using the past seven tightening cycles as a guide, GS notes that the S&P usually struggles at the start,
falling an average of 2% over the first three months. The pinch typically does not last long, however,
with the index gaining an average of 9% over the following 12 months. The bruising exception was
2022.
Ultimately, earnings growth determines the market’s trajectory, but the speed and volatility of the
move in rates can heavily influence stocks along the way.
