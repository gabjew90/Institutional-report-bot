"Going Nowhere Fast": BTIG Warns New Lows 
Soaring Below Calm Index Surface 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, SEP 14, 2026 - 01:00 AM 
Markets continue to churn, says Jonathan Krinsky, BTIG's chief technical market strategist, in 
his latest note "Going Nowhere Fast" (available here in full for pro subs). 
It’s been 27 trading days since QQQ’s +3.4% surge on 8/4. 
 
Yet outside of a few days, trade has largely remained entirely within that 8/4 range.  
Bollinger Bands are as tight as they were in March. 


[Page 2]
 
 
At some point this will see a resolution, and Krinsky's view remains that it will be to the 
downside... 
New Lows Surge Despite Calm on Surface.  
Nearly 5% of the Russell 3k made a 52wk low, with the RAY itself less than 3% from a 52wk 
high. 
 


[Page 3]
 
Historically that’s a coin flip looking out 20 days, but there have been some notable dates 
preceding meaningful drops with this set-up including: March ’00, Oct. ’07, July ’15, Sep. ’18, 
Jan. ’20, Dec. ’21, and Jan. ’26 
Semis Have Unfinished Business Lower.  
Semis (SOXX) continue to trade below a declining 50 DMA. 
 
We remain in the camp this has unfinished business on the downside, with an eventual test of 
the 200 DMA (-17%) likely. 
Small-Caps Breaking Trend.  
IWM has now spent ten days below its 50 DMA, and the slope is inflecting lower. 


[Page 4]
 
 
The long-term trend is still firmly bullish, but the risk is a test of the 200 DMA later this month. 
Real Talk.  
While rates may be short-term extended and sentiment on bonds is quite negative (Daily 
Sentiment Index is single digits), 10yr real rates are just now emerging from a three-year trading 
range. 
 
Hard to say this is a major top for rates. 
Aussie/Yen Rolling Over.  
While not a perfect correlation, AUD/JPY has generally tracked the SPX quite well.  


[Page 5]
 
 
It has recently pulled back towards the lower end of its range, which could be another headwind 
for equities. 
Crude Shipping Costs Go Parabolic.  
The Breakwave Tanker Shipping ETF (BWET) tracks the future cost of transporting crude oil via 
tankers. 
It’s up nearly 500% since the end of June, and ~4k% YTD. 
 
Like all parabolic moves, this likely unwinds in equal and opposite fashion, but timing it is 
uniquely difficult. 


[Page 6]
 
Energy Stocks Look Tired.  
Despite WTI crude surging above $100/bbl and shipping costs going parabolic, the Oil Service 
ETF (OIH) closed down on the week. 
 
As we have previously stated, we don’t view falling energy stocks as bullish overall market 
initially and think everything can fall together in a correlation-one event.
