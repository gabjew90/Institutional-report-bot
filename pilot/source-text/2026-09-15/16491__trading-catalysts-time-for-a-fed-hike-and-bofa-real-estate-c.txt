Time to hike (or risks to the long end could rise) 
All eyes will be on the Sep FOMC meeting on Wed, where our economists expect the Fed 
to hike by 25bp. After a stronger than expected CPI last week, markets are pricing over 
20bp of hike for this week’s meeting, and over 80bp of Fed hikes over the next 12 
months. Chairman Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks, together with current market pricing, 
have raised the cost of disappointing expectations significantly. Warsh can also point to 
the renewed twist flattening of the Treasury curve as evidence that tighter policy is 
reinforcing inflation-fighting credibility, providing further justification for a hike. If the 
Fed were to refrain from hiking after the latest data, it could risk a significant selloff at 
the long end, potentially larger than the move following the July FOMC meeting. 
Equity strategists continue to favor Value 
Our equity strategists continue to favor Value, which has outperformed during Fed 
hiking cycles, offers inflation-protected income, benefits from broadening profits 
growth, is neglected and may have newfound appeal with formerly high multiple growth 
stocks shifting from Growth to Value post Russell rebalance. 
REITs: strong funda., light positioning into BofA conference 
BofA Real Estate conference happens Tue-Thu this week. BofA analyst Jeffrey Spector 
highlights strong fundamentals and light positioning continue to provide a good setup 
for the group, even though higher rates for longer remain a risk. He remains ahead of 
the Street in both 2026E and 2027E. New construction is slowing across most property 
types, supporting landlord pricing power and improving the outlook into 2027. The team 
observe the strongest compounding capacity for healthcare and retail REITs, and 
highlight AHR and WELL among their top picks. To express the view via an ETF, see 
Exhibit 2 and views in the ETF Research section on page 2. 
Derivs: How big a risk are rates to the AI boom? 
As US equities confront long-term yields hitting multi-year highs ahead of the Sep 
FOMC meeting and against a backdrop of fiscal & inflation concerns, our derivs 
strategists highlight that macro risks have historically faced a high bar to disrupt tech 
euphoria (e.g., Nasdaq soared in the late 90s amidst 30y yields rising 200bps and the 
Fed hiking 100bps). In their view, bond stress spilling over into equities would likely need 
rates vol re-pricing meaningfully higher, making the FOMC meeting a near-term test to 
see if a tightening path can be achieved without causing panic. See the latest GEVI 
report.

FCF Quality: With free cash flow (FCF) becoming scarcer, investors are willing to pay 
more for it. We prefer measuring quality through FCF yield, as it is difficult to 
manipulate and easily comparable across firms. This measure of quality is up almost 
40% YTD, and on pace to equal its best year ever going back to 2000. One reason: S&P 
500 FCF yield is at record lows, despite the median S&P 500 company FCF yield 
remaining steady. VictoryShares Free Cash Flow ETF (VFLO; 1-FV) is up 37.5% YTD, with 
a 13.7x forward P/E vs. 21.3x for the S&P 500, & 40% exposure to energy and health 
care sectors.

Fed: Time to Hike 
Last week's data all but lock in a rate hike this week. Chairman Warsh's Jackson Hole 
remarks, together with current market pricing, have raised the cost of disappointing 
expectations significantly. Warsh can also point to the renewed twist flattening of the 
Treasury curve as evidence that tighter policy is reinforcing inflation-fighting credibility, 
providing further justification for a hike. Beyond this week's meeting, uncertainty around 
the policy path remains. However, there will be only one additional employment report 
and one CPI release before the October meeting. Unless incoming data soften materially 
or Fed communication turns unexpectedly dovish, we expect additional rate hikes in both 
October and December. 
The week ahead: All eyes on the Sep FOMC decision 
All eyes will be on the Sep FOMC meeting on Wed, where we expect the Fed to hike by 
25bp. Additionally, we get retail sales for Aug on Wed as well. We also get import prices 
(Wed), the NAHB housing market index (Wed), housing starts (Thu), pending home sales 
(Thu) and industrial production (Fri). The Fed is in communication blackout until Sep 17 
(Thu). There is no Fedspeak on the calendar yet for Fri, but we will be on the lookout for 
any updates.

2026 Global Real Estate Conference Preview 
This week is BofA’s 20th Global Real Estate Conference. We’re hosting more than 130 
real estate companies from over 15 countries at Two Bryant Park from Tuesday through 
Thursday. See report: U.S. REITs: Latest Bull/Bear debate across subsectors ahead of 
BofA’s Global Real Estate Conf. 
Strong fundamentals + light positioning => good setup 
REITs have outperformed the broader market year to date and fundamentals continue to 
come in better than expected. We have now had two unusually strong earnings quarters. 
In 2Q, 72% of REITs beat the Street versus a historical average of 62%, and 87% raised 
guidance versus a historical average of 60%. This followed a strong 1Q, when 78% beat 
and 60% raised. While we recognize that higher rates for longer remain a risk, we believe 
strong fundamentals and light positioning continue to provide a good setup for the 
group. We remain ahead of the Street in both 2026E and 2027E. 
The recovery is broadening 
New construction is slowing across most property types, supporting landlord pricing 
power and improving the outlook into 2027. As a reminder, this is a multiyear tailwind 
we highlighted in our Year Ahead report, and it is playing out earlier than expected. 
Retail leasing remains near record levels, senior housing continues to deliver double-
digit property-level earnings growth, and industrial demand improved more than 
expected during the first half. Industrial, apartments and self-storage are all benefiting 
from the decline in new supply from peak levels, with declines ranging from 20% to 50% 
and expected to continue for several years. New supply also remains limited in senior 
housing and retail. 
Strong compounding capacity for healthcare and retail REITs 
Our new proprietary BofA REIT Compounding Score (see reports: REIT Compounding 
Engine and following REIT Compounding Engine part II) highlights the REITs with the 
strongest capacity to compound steadily over the coming years. Healthcare and retail 
rank as the strongest compounding sectors. AHR and WELL, which both remain on 
BofA’s US 1 List and our list of top picks, also rank among the leaders. WELL ranks 
second overall and AHR ranks fifth.

Price objective basis & risk 
American Healthcare REIT Inc. (AHR) 
Our $71 price objective is derived using an AFFO multiple. We apply a 31x AFFO multiple 
our 2027 AFFO estimate. Our applied multiple is based on AHR's relative mix of 
healthcare real estate exposure and our view on its portfolio quality relative to publicly 
traded peers. 
 
Risks: AHR's reliance on Trilogy & govt reimbursement risk via SNF exposure. 
Welltower (WELL) 
Our $292 price objective for WELL is derived from applying a 26x AFFO multiple to our 
2031 estimate and discounting back at 6.6% discount rate to the present value of $292. 
Our $292 PO is based on our assumption that a monetization begins to drive additional 
EBITDA in 2028/2029. Upside risks to our PO are better-than-expected senior housing 
or medical office building performance, higher-than-forecast dividend growth and lower 
interest rates. Downside risks to our PO are further public-pay reimbursement cuts, a 
more competitive acquisitions environment, weaker-than-expected senior housing 
fundamentals, increased tenant credit risk, and rising interest rates.
