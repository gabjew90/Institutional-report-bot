The AI right tail gets repriced
Privorotsky sees yesterday as a de-grossing event. Hardware fell around 500–800bp more than he
expected, depending on the vertical. Electrical, data center, optical and anything tied to the right tail of
AI capex got obliterated.
Meanwhile, Meta gained 2.5% and Alphabet 2.7% as the market effectively concluded that they may
not need to spend quite as aggressively. The cross-sectional moves were extreme: Software versus
Semis +12.5%, AI Winners versus AI at Risk -9.96%, three-month momentum +6% and High Beta TMT
Momentum -11.5%.
His read is that the market is cutting the highest incremental infrastructure growth assumptions, with
the biggest damage concentrated in names whose earnings and multiples depend most heavily on
continued acceleration.
Asia complicates the idea that the AI trade itself has fundamentally broken. SoftBank is up 7.5%, TSMC
is green and was barely touched throughout the episode. Net/net, this looks more like a repricing of
the right tail of infrastructure growth than a break in AI demand itself.

Spending is not slowing
Privo thinks the market is confusing slower frontier development with slower spending. He sees little
evidence that capex plans through 2027/28 will change. Compute remains scarce, workloads are
growing and hyperscalers are still raising spending intentions.
The real constraint is permitting (outlined here yesterday). Broader local opposition and proposed
moratoriums could limit how quickly and where capacity gets built, justifying lower multiples for
electrical, power, cooling and data-center exposures.
The derating has already been savage, however. On Bloomberg blended 24-month forward numbers,
SOX has fallen from around 23x to 13.85x, close to April’s peak oil-shock levels. Privo’s bottom line: a
lot is now priced, and treating all physical AI infrastructure as one trade is too simplistic. Grid
constraints can make behind-the-meter power more valuable, while rising inference demand still
requires enormous optical bandwidth and interconnect capacity.

Just in time
GS PB shows HFs bought US TMT in 10 of the past 11 sessions, led by semis and semi equipment. The
two-week pace of long buying now sits in the 97th percentile of the past five years.
That rebuild matters because dispersion remains extreme. The 200-day vol of GS TMT Momentum
Winners versus Losers is around 76, compared with just 13 for SPX. Tech price action has been
almost 6x more violent than index vol.
Net/net: newly rebuilt tech longs remain vulnerable to AI and Iran headlines. According to Lee
Coppersmith, near-term vol makes sense, but a break in the bigger AI story still does not.

Scared of SOX?
SOX vol spiked at yesterday’s open but was crushed throughout the session. There is very little fear
embedded in current vol pricing.
Perhaps this is simply a sell-SOX-spot market, with little interest in options. For anyone genuinely
concerned about a much larger unwind, however, hedging the downside through options makes a lot
of sense.
