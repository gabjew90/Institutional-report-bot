GS TMT SPEC SALES – Tuesday 15th September

Overnight US markets were weaker overnight, though off the lows, as investors rotated sharply out of AI
infrastructure and semis and into software/cybersecurity after weekend AI-safety headlines, including Anthropic
CEO Dario Amodei’s call for a slower pace of frontier model development. Semis took the brunt of the pain, with
SOX down c.6%, while software, cybersecurity and cloud names outperformed, helped by moves in CrowdStrike,
BUG, Google and Microsoft (Cyber was the bigger theme winner at the GS Tech conference last week). The
Software > Semis pair closed +12.6%, a 2.5 standard deviation move, while AI-at-risk baskets had their worst day
since DeepSeek. ASML’s c.$40bn fall yesterday was equivalent to the entire ASMI market cap. See all GIR notes
from the tech Conference and a 45min podcast with our entire TMT research team as they discuss the top
takeaways from our Communacopia + Tech Conference.
Macro was also in focus ahead of Wednesday’s FOMC, with 10-year yields briefly touching 5%, oil still firm
despite easing Russia/Ukraine energy-target headlines, and US desk activity quiet at 3/10 with net selling from
both asset managers and hedge funds. Incoming investor debate was that voluntary AI slowdowns look unlikely
without global coordination, especially with China still pushing ahead. Calls for tighter regulation and recent
safety headlines may create near-term noise, but the bigger practical constraint is likely power and permitting,
which could extend the AI capex cycle rather than end it.
Some debate on whether over the long term, pacing the frontier lowers compute demand. While this is likely to
have little short term impact as both OpenAI and Anthropic are unquestionably compute constrained today (see
SemiAnalysis piece flagging as Frontier model employ more safety checks is drives even more compute).
Interesting President Trump and his former tech adviser David Sacks both firmly reject aggressive AI regulation
and calls for an industry slowdown, advocating instead for a “light-touch” approach to ensure the U.S. outpaces
China. The positive this morning Asia looks more stable with Hynix and Samsung only slightly down and TSMC
up.
Jensen at the All-In Summit: Huang remains highly bullish on AI as a new industrial revolution requiring major
investment across compute, data centres, energy and applications. He argues AI safety should be managed through
engineering, testing and monitoring rather than slowing innovation, and sees fears around runaway AI and job
destruction as overstated. He also believes more hyperscalers, neoclouds and open-source models will expand the

While Elon Musk proposed that leading AI labs (including competitors and potentially labs from China) test each
other's models with shared security harnesses before public release, similar to the MPAA rating system. Microsoft:
published its “Humanist AI” Code of Conduct as primary governing document for training its MAI models from
2027 onward; core philosophy is that AI must remain subordinate to humans, never resist shutdown or develop
independent goals. . OpenAI also disclosed that their enhanced chain-of-thought monitoring overhead uses 20% as
much compute as the underlying rollout. Put differently, if they spend X compute having the agent attempt a task,
they now spend another 0.2X checking there was no problematic behaviour. This doesn’t include the compute
needed for other types of safety work.
While the EU is set to propose ban on social media and AI chatbots for under-15s. Reuters According to European
Commission policy documents leaked on September 14, 2026, a major child safety initiative will be formally
unveiled on Thursday, as part of the new EU Kids Act. The restrictions target "high-risk" social networks (such as
Instagram and TikTok), video services (like YouTube), online gaming ecosystems, and consumer AI services
including ChatGPT and AI companion apps. Purely educational tools are excluded. The draft is not yet law. To
take effect, it must navigate intense upcoming negotiations and win approval from both the European Parliament
and EU member governments

TELCOS
| • | SFR takeover: Unions are taking legal action, alongside a planned strike, to obtain documents and
management responses from Altice France on the proposed €20bn+ sale of SFR to a consortium of competitors,
amid concerns the deal could eventually lead to the disappearance of the SFR brand. Altice says it has provided
comprehensive information in line with competition law, while the employee consultation period has been
extended to 6 October and a strike remains scheduled for 24 September. (Alloforfait). This will not come as a
major surprise. | | • | Telefónica has acquired a 10% stake in Seville-based OGA to strengthen its AI capabilities,
particularly in industrial/logistics optimisation, digital twins and defence. Financial terms were not disclosed and
there is no change of control, with OGA’s founder retaining 90%; the deal also includes a commercial partnership
that lets Telefónica add OGA’s solutions to its enterprise and public-sector offering, while OGA gains access to
Telefónica’s distribution network. (Cinco Dias) |
