We hosted ~40 Software companies at our Communacopia + Technology Conference 
last week. Investor sentiment was better than at any time in the prior eight months 
and has moved to be significantly more micro/idiosyncratic focused, with questions 
and commentary becoming much more product and tech stack specific. Generally 
speaking, for companies with exposure to the inference economy, there are good 
reasons to believe that the best part of AI product cycles are still ahead, given how 
early enterprises are in scaling production inference use cases. At the same time, 
there is a fair amount of investor discomfort in a) the move higher in application 
software stocks, without a commensurate move higher in fundamentals or good 
visibility into how the next 12 months will unfold from an estimates revisions 
standpoint; b) the potential for a wildcard change in the security narrative or 
underlying architectures, such as from frontier models entering the security TAM. 
Most notable incremental positive datapoints in our view were for CRWD, DDOG, 
DOCN, DT, SHOP and SNOW; more mixed datapoints/low visibility in our view were 
for CHKP, HUBS, INTU and NAVN.

Platform 
 
 
AI is changing the economics of data modernization, shifting historically 
n
lengthy, services-intensive projects into automated, outcome-driven, faster 
processes.  
Snowflake discussed AI coding agents accelerating both migration execution 
o
and problem resolution e.g. a large Teradata migration now moves to <3 
quarters from 2-3 years prior. Partners are increasingly willing to deliver 
migrations using fixed-price, outcome-based models rather than traditional 
time-and-materials engagements, all of which is enabling customers to ramp 
consumption faster, reach production more quickly, and generate value sooner 
than in prior cohorts. While faster migrations theoretically lower switching 
costs in both directions, platform vendors noted that easier onboarding 
outweighs any incremental churn risk, with the understanding that continued 
innovation is key to customer lifetime value.  
MongoDB is increasing its investment in AI-assisted tooling with the ambition 
o
of compressing modernization projects from years to months and eventually 
weeks. Enterprises already have ample reason to retire aging applications and 
data estates; the practical hurdle has been the cost, disruption and scarce 
expertise required to do it. Automating more of the conversion, testing and 
remediation work could turn modernization into a more immediate source of 
workload growth across databases, data platforms and infrastructure. 
As data gravity weakens and models become interchangeable, value is shifting 
n
higher up the stack toward governance, semantics, and enterprise context.  
Databricks emphasized that governance is becoming a central buying 
o
criterion as enterprises face three converging trends: increasingly rapid model 
proliferation, growing scrutiny around AI spending, and rising security 
concerns around autonomous agents. In this context, Unity Catalog is one of 
its most important products and frequently the primary differentiator in 
competitive evaluations.  
Snowflake acknowledged that open formats and AI-assisted migrations are 
o
reducing traditional switching costs at the storage layer, making openness 
increasingly standard; as a result, differentiation is shifting toward governance, 
disaster recovery, observability, agent orchestration, and application services 
that sit above the underlying data platform. Snowflake’s view is that if 
customers can move data more freely, long-term value creation must come 
from providing the control, visibility, and operational capabilities needed to 
run AI at enterprise scale. 
Dynatrace argued that confidence remains one of the primary bottlenecks to 
o
broader enterprise AI adoption. As organizations move AI workloads into 
production, they need visibility not only into whether systems are running, but 
whether models are generating reliable outputs and agents are behaving as 
intended. Management believes this increases the value of platforms that can 
bring together logs, metrics, traces and business events into a shared

Applications 
 
 
The conversation has moved from “existential risk” to a more nuanced 
n
discussion of NTM product cycles, tech stack maturity, and value capture vs. 
value abstraction. Companies continue to describe a future where software serves 
as the orchestration layer connecting agents and delivering outcomes, rather than 
simply acting as the system of record. Management teams generally argued that 
durable differentiation will come from owning workflow context and vast amounts of 
historical data, not simply access to frontier models. Shopify, HubSpot and Intuit 
each discussed how they are adapting to marketing channel fragmentation (e.g. 
AI-driven impact to SEO). Intuit noted it is not yet seeing impacts from AI native tax 
competition, but investing heavily in AI features in TurboTax and sharpening their 
price points to ensure they remain competitive. 
Monetization continues to be a work in progress. Most vendors continue to 
n
prioritize adoption and usage, while experimenting with new forms of 
pricing/packaging. At the same time, there are headwinds from elongating sales 
cycles, app budget pressure to fund token projects, and more uneven competition. 
As companies tie their products to quantifiable outcomes, there does seem to be 
some willingness to source some portion of apps budgets from labor budgets.  
Microsoft described AI as accelerating a shift from seat-based licensing 
o
toward pricing aligned with units of work, outcomes, and productivity 
delivered. The company pushed back on the notion that AI simply reallocates 
existing software budgets, arguing instead that AI enables new workflows, new 
business processes, and entirely new revenue opportunities that were 
previously uneconomic or impossible to deliver. In this framework, AI is not 
just an efficiency tool but a mechanism for expanding software spend by 
creating incremental demand for new outcomes. At the same time, Microsoft 
emphasized that enterprise adoption increasingly depends on the ability to 
measure, govern, and optimize AI usage. Agent 365 is the control plane that 
helps customers monitor consumption, manage costs, and validate ROI across 
models and workflows, reflecting a view that observability and spend 
management may become as important to enterprise deployment as model 
performance itself. 
Salesforce sees a sizable upgrade opportunity within the installed base, with 
o
only ~5% of customers currently on the AE1 premium edition which carries a 
~60%-80% uplift. Rather than positioning AI as a standalone product sale, 
Salesforce framed the strategy around consolidating sales enablement, 
performance management, analytics, collaboration, Data 360, Agentforce, 
and ClaudeForce into a single higher value offering (AE1). Salesforce further 
quantified the value proposition, noting that sales organizations often spend 
~$1,300 per seller across point solutions vs. $550 price point for Salesforce’s 
bundled premium edition. This monetization framework could extend well 
beyond traditional seat licenses; with agents, consumption, workflow 
execution, and business outcomes becoming incremental pricing vectors as 
agents represent a larger share of enterprise activity. The combination of

Security 
 
 
Several industry experts described AI as compressing time: for the first time, 
CrowdStrike noted that security is being viewed as an accelerant to business 
outcomes rather than a break; while Palo Alto noted that new AI headline attacks 
have been a more useful marketing tool than the marketing efforts it drove 
internally for the last eight years. At the same time, Palo Alto acknowledged that AI 
value-added services are being built as we speak, and no vendor today has a complete 
stack. At speed, it takes 3-6 months to build the right security hooks into new agentic 
stacks, such that a complete agentic stack with a robust accompanying security stack is 
still very much a moving target today. 
Against this backdrop and the Security stocks trading at elevated multiples, 
investors are spending an outsized amount of time looking to understand where 
there could be disruption, such as with a frontier model entering the security TAM. 
CrowdStrike further detailed its introduction of SafeMind at Fal.con last week as a 
n
cyber frontier model that can spin up continuous penetration testing, built on 
Nvidia’s Nemotron paired with CrowdStrike’s 15+ years of proprietary kill chain data. 
CrowdStrike believes that frontier models have a role in understanding 
vulnerabilities and patching - but do not have the benefit of being in line and are not 
taking action real time, as is the case with CrowdStrike.  
Palo Alto noted the following enduring moats: 180mn sensors deployed globally 
n
(endpoint and network), which can expand both organically and inorganically and 
19PB of data run through Google Cloud every day. Observability (Chronosphere) and 
operational IT (Console) are good new examples of Palo Alto further inserting itself 
into token flow, and subsequently compounding on Palo Alto’s traditional 2-5% take 
rate on cloud spend.  
Across security subcategories, network security screened more positively as the AI 
debate translated into a more concrete traffic and throughput argument. There is 
also an underlying push to modernization, which we believe has the greatest impact 
in SIEM. The consensus view in Identity is that a rising tide lifts all boats; we believe 
the more nuanced answer to whether there is a step function change in 
architectures because of the ephemeral nature of agentic identities will become 
clearer in 2027. 
Fortinet shared that customers are buying larger capacity firewalls, as AI-driven 
n
east-west traffic, machine-to-machine activity and internal segmentation create 
higher capacity needs. In other words, AI could help the existing firewall refresh and 
SASE cycle, not only create a separate set of AI-security products. Sovereignty came 
up in the same context, with Fortinet tying demand for on-premise and sovereign 
SASE to customers that want more local control over AI infrastructure and traffic. 
Okta and SailPoint highlighted identity as an increasingly critical control plane for AI 
n
adoption, with enterprises becoming more focused on agent discovery, 
authorization, and auditability as deployments scale. While views differed on the 
extent to which agentic identities require new architectures, both companies 
emphasized the growing importance of understanding what agents exist, what they

Rubrik added another dimension to the identity debate, arguing that resilience

complex workflows. 
Resilience is moving closer to the center of the security discussion as 
machine-speed attacks compress the time available to respond and recover. Boards 
and CEOs are increasingly evaluating cyber risk through downtime, financial 
exposure and the ability to keep the business running. 
Rubrik is building a broader resilience platform across data, identity and agents as

Physical AI 
 
 
Physical AI could become one of the more consequential shifts in vertical software 
as digital intelligence begins to connect with real-world execution. These vendors 
already sit inside critical workflows and increasingly control the operating data 
generated across those workflows. AI creates an opportunity to move beyond systems of 
record and into systems of action, expanding the role of software from documenting 
work to helping execute it. While adoption remains early and will vary by workflow, 
customer readiness and accuracy requirements, we came away increasingly focused on 
how these platforms are expanding the scope of data, context and operational 
responsibility they control. 
Procore highlighted a pragmatic adoption path through tiered Digital Coworker 
n
packages, allowing customers to start with narrowly defined workflows before 
expanding into more sophisticated automation. Management’s approach suggests 
the market may develop incrementally rather than through a single step-function 
adoption curve. 
Samsara pointed to roughly 4x usage growth across certain AI capabilities over the 
n
past two months and highlighted measurable use cases in maintenance, safety and 
asset utilization. The common thread across these examples is that success can 
often be tied directly to labor productivity, downtime reduction or improved asset 
efficiency. 
Autodesk sees AI connecting design, construction and operations into a more 
n
automated project workflow, helping customers address persistent capacity 
constraints across AEC and manufacturing. Management argued that using fewer 
resources per project can free customers to work through backlogs and pursue 
additional bids, supporting seat demand across a larger volume of work. Given the 
accuracy requirements and long project cycles in these industries, monetization 
should develop gradually, with task automation increasing seat value and broader 
automation creating incremental consumption over time. 
Across the broader ecosystem, companies are also expanding the context they 
n
control. DroneDeploy gives Procore greater visibility into jobsite conditions, 
MaintainX connects Autodesk’s design and construction workflows to operational 
performance, and Samsara continues to deepen its understanding of physical 
operations through its connected network. We see this expansion of context as an 
important part of the long-term opportunity, potentially allowing vertical software 
vendors to extend beyond task automation and into larger portions of the operating 
process.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
