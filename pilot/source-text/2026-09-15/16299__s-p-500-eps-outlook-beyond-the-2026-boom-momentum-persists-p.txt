S&P 500 EPS Outlook 
Beyond the 2026 boom: momentum 
persists, pace moderates 
     
   
 
 
Raising 2026E to $365 (+33%) after another beat & raise 
Corporate America delivered another outsized beat in 2Q, with S&P 500 EPS growing 
+50% YoY including GOOGL and AMZN’s one-time investment mark-ups and 32% 
excluding those gains, blowing past consensus of 22%. Guidance was also stronger than 
usual, particularly in Tech and Industrials. Following 2Q results, we raise our 2026 EPS 
forecast by 6% from $345 to $365 (+33% YoY) vs. $362 bottom-up consensus. Slightly 
over half of the increase reflects one-time gains at GOOGL/AMZN, with the remainder 
driven by stronger underlying growth in 2Q and robust guidance. 
Launching 2027E at $410 (+12%): good but less great 
We expect S&P 500 EPS growth to decelerate but remain above trend in 2027. Our 
forecast of $410 (+12% YoY) is slightly below bottom-up estimates and above top-down 
(Exhibit 1). Although our economists' 2.2% real GDP growth forecast (see Global 
Economic Viewpoint report) would historically imply high-single-digit EPS growth, we 
believe the AI investment cycle, manufacturing strength, and productivity gains should 
keep growth elevated next year.  
Risks: More earnings eggs in one AI basket… 
Five stocks (NVDA, GOOGL, MU, MSFT, AAPL) now account for a record 27% of NTM 
S&P 500 earnings, while Tech and the Mag 7 represent 50%. Semis alone are expected 
to contribute over 60% of the total consensus EPS growth in 2027 (Exhibit 13). We 
believe the capex cycle has more room to run (see report: High Yield & Loan Strategy: 
Funding the Future #2, 16 July 2026), but the growing dependence on a highly 
interconnected AI complex amplifies two-sided risks. Case in point: 2026 consensus 
Semis EPS growth has been revised up by 70ppt over the past year from ~30% to 
~100%, adding 6ppt to S&P 500 EPS growth. 
… plus: margin pressures, earnings quality 
Some of the S&P 500's recent net margin expansion reflects factors unlikely to persist, 
including one-time investment gains and a timing asymmetry in the AI buildout, where 
capex beneficiaries recognize revenue before spenders recognize the full cost through 
depreciation, leading to lower FCF relative to net income (Exhibit 48). Strong but slowing 
sales growth should support further margin expansion from here, but rising hyperscaler 
D&A (12% of sales in 2027 vs. 10% in 2026) raises the bar for AI monetization, while 
memory, tariff and commodity costs remain swing factors. We forecast that the S&P 
500’s net margin (ex. Financials) reaches 16.3% in 2027 (vs. 16.6% consensus).  
Strong earnings may not be enough for strong returns 
Although we are encouraged by the fundamental backdrop, worsening liquidity, 
geopolitical tensions, sticky inflation and unfavorable seasonal trends keep us cautious 
on equities in the near term, particularly given that the S&P 500 has gone nearly six 
months without a 5%+ pullback. Moreover, history suggests returns tend to slow when 
EPS growth remains above trend but decelerates, as we expect in 2027. Our year-end 
S&P 500 target (report) implies 3% downside, while our NTM target implies 2% upside.

2027: $410 in 2027 (+12% YoY) 
We expect S&P 500 EPS growth to decelerate but remain above trend in 2027, 
supported by the AI investment cycle, manufacturing strength, productivity gains and a 
stable macro backdrop. Our forecast of $410 (+12% YoY) is slightly below bottom-up 
estimates and above top-down (Exhibit 1). While this points to another year of healthy 
earnings growth, equity returns have historically moderated when profits remain above 
trend but are decelerating as we expect in 2027 (Exhibit 12), suggesting that strong 
earnings alone may not be sufficient to drive outsized market gains. For more on our 
market outlook, see S&P 500 target (report).

On watch: earnings quality

Dividends: healthy growth ahead

is at a record low of 25% (see Exhibit 53).

Buyback contribution to EPS: past the peak but still in play 
Buybacks accelerated during the post-GFC zero interest rate era, adding roughly 
1ppt/year to S&P 500 EPS growth as companies took advantage of low financing costs. 
While net buybacks remain near record highs in absolute terms, they have declined over 
the last year excluding Financials and have declined as a share of market cap for the 
overall index since 2022. Financials currently have the highest LTM net buyback yield, 
while hyperscalers have shifted from net repurchasers to net issuers as AI-related capex 
has weighed on free cash flow. We assume buybacks add roughly 0.5ppt to EPS growth 
per year going forward.

Appendix 
Guidance Ratios 
Earnings guidance: We track the number of above-consensus and below-consensus 
guidance instances and compute the ratio of above- vs. below-consensus guides over 
the last three months and one month (note: FFO is used rather than EPS for REITs). If a 
company changes its outlook more than once in a one-month period, we incorporate all 
instances of guidance into our aggregate ratio. The ratio includes both quarterly and 
annual guidance. The data source is Bloomberg prior to 2006 and FactSet thereafter. In 
FactSet’s data, guidance is classified as above-consensus when the guidance midpoint is 
more than 1% above the consensus estimate and below-consensus when the guidance 
midpoint is more than 1% below the consensus estimate. In Bloomberg’s data (used 
prior to 2006), guidance is classified as above-consensus if the company’s lower range 
of guidance is greater than consensus and below-consensus if the company’s upper 
range is less than consensus.
