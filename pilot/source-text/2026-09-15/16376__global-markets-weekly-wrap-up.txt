The S&P 500 looks calm. Beneath the surface, it isn't.
Six-month realized correlation has fallen to just 0.11—seen only 
twice in 25 years. Low correlation allows traders to sell index 
volatility, buy single-stock volatility and capture the spread. But 
when stocks suddenly move together, that trade can unwind 
everywhere at once. This is not necessarily a crash signal. It is a 
fragility signal. Your portfolio may contain many stocks, but far 
less true diversification than the number of holdings suggests.
