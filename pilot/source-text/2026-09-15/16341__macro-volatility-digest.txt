WEEKLY MARKET COMMENTARY 
Macro Uncertainty Fuels Hedging Demand Ahead of FOMC 
 
Cross-Asset Volatility: Risk assets sold off last week as oil 
prices jumped to a 3-month high on escalating Middle East 
tensions and US core inflation surprised to the upside. Oil 
volatility jumped the most across asset classes, with the 
OVX Index up 14 pts wk/wk to 59%. Notably, while oil prices 
have surged back to near their Mar/Apr highs, oil volatility 
has been much tamer this time around, with the OVX Index 
trading at just half the levels we saw in March (see Exhibit 
1). Similarly, demand for oil calls has also cooled off 
compared to when the Iran conflict first erupted. The 
difference likely reflects that while the risk of a prolonged 
conflict has risen (hence higher spot prices), the risk of 
severe disruption has fallen (less tail risk/hedging demand). 
Traders are pricing in a significantly smaller tail premium for 
oil, with the WTI 1M 5-delta/25-delta call wing still trading 
below its 1-year average. Higher oil prices are adding fuel to 
the sticky inflation story in the US, with the odds of a Fed 
rate hike at this week’s meeting rising from 58% to 90% in 
the OIS market. Interest rate volatility (MOVE Index) gained 
almost 10 pts last week to the 92nd percentile high. SPX® 
weekly options are pricing ~1.1% implied move for 
Wednesday’s FOMC meeting. 
Equity Volatility: The VIX® Index gained 1.3 pts last week 
to 15.8%, with more than half of the move coming from a 
steepening in SPX skew and convexity (see below). Single 
stock volatility diverged notably from index vol, as VIXEQSM 
Index fell 1.3 pts to 34.5%. The VIXEQ-VIX Index spread 
has now halved to ~17% since it hit a record high of 34% in 
July as risk in the equity market has shifted from the micro 
(earnings & the AI trade) to macro (inflation & rates). As 
fears over the AI trade have subsided, so has the volatility 
premium in Tech stocks, with the QQQ-SPX 1M implied 
volatility spread now trading near a 1-year low of 3.6% after 
hitting a high of 13% in July. See Exhibit 2.  
Skew: Hedging demand has picked up notably in SPX 
options, with SPX 1M put skew (25-delta vs. 50-delta ratio) 
jumping to the 73rd percentile high from the 36th percentile 
low a week ago. See Exhibit 3. Tail hedging has increased 
too, particularly via VIX options, as VIX call volumes have 
also picked up. Three of the four largest VIX trades year-to-
date all occurred in the past two weeks, with a customer 
buying over 120k contracts of outright VIX calls in each 
instance (each trade ~$12M in premium, with strikes ranging 
from 28-34 in the Oct and Nov expiries).  
Term Structure: With the bid to short-dated implied 
volatility, SPX term structure flattened last week, with the 
1Y-1M vol spread narrowing from 5.6% to 5.0% (75th 
percentile high).

How to read this chart:
The chart shows how various asset class implied 
volatilities are trading relative to their own history, using 
a 10-year lookback. 
The volatilities are ranked by z-scores, which compares 
the current volatility level for an asset versus its own 10-
year history. A z-score of +2, for example, would mean 
that the current volatility level is trading 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average (i.e. rich) while a 
z-score of -2 would mean it’s trading 2 standard 
deviations below average (i.e. cheap)
Because each asset class volatility trades at different 
levels and often defined differently (e.g. lognormal vs. 
normal vol, price vs. bps vol), by standardizing using z-
scores, we’re able to compare different asset class 
volatilities on the same scale - and see at a glance if 
there are any divergences or dislocations in the cross-
asset volatility markets
