Rates/Fed – All eyes this week on the FOMC Meeting and Chair Wash presser on Wed (more below).
Notably, GS Econ Research officially added a 25 bp rate hike to our forecast for this Wednesday's
September FOMC meeting, which would bring the federal funds rate to 3.75%–4.00%. While our team
does not see a strong economic case for raising rates—as the overshoot of 2% can be attributed to one-
time factors—the August CPI report pushed market pricing of a hike to nearly 90%. The FOMC will likely
want to avoid the market reaction that would follow from remaining on hold. GS continue to expect two
cuts in 2027, but now expect them in September and December (versus June and December previously),
and have raised our terminal rate forecast to 3.25%–3.50%. Worth noting that implications for Equities
show the S&P 500 has historically generated an average 3-month return of -2% at the start of seven hiking
cycles during the last few decades ( see chart below). However, as of today, the rates market is already
pricing more than three 25 bp hikes by the middle of 2027, lifting the bar for policy to surprise in a
hawkish direction and therefore downside to the current tape ( more below).

to 90%, making it difficult for the Fed to stay on hold without disrupting expectations. David believes the
Fed will try to avoid signalling a sustained hiking cycle, likely making only minimal statement changes
and emphasizing a data-dependent approach to any further tightening. The base case remains just one hike
in 2026.
David argues inflation is not strong enough to justify a hawkish Fed. He believes most inflation above the
2% target reflects temporary factors such as tariffs and oil-related shocks, both of which should fade over
time. Core PCE has already slowed toward a ~2.5% annualized pace, inflation expectations remain
broadly anchored, and the economy does not appear overheated outside a handful of AI-linked industries.
As a result, several FOMC participants may be reluctant to endorse additional rate hikes after September.
The GS medium-term rate outlook remains less hawkish than markets are pricing. David expects only one
hike this year, followed by two cuts in 2027 (September and December), resulting in a terminal Fed Funds
rate of 3.25-3.5%. The key debate at this meeting is whether the Fed’s dot plot shows one or two hikes in
2026. While risks lean toward a somewhat higher path if policymakers view AI-driven investment and
energy pressures as persistent inflation drivers, David still believes markets are pricing a more aggressive
tightening cycle than is ultimately likely.
Higher Rates & Equities –US Strategy Team argue that while higher rates are creating a valuation
headwind, strong earnings, healthy corporate balance sheets and growth investments such as AI should
allow the equity bull market to continue despite Fed tightening and elevated bond yields.


[Page 7]
Ben Snider argues higher rates are creating a valuation headwind, but earnings remain the dominant driver
of equities. While the S&P 500 forward P/E has compressed from 22x to 19x this year as Treasury yields
approached 5%, the index remains within 2% of record highs because earnings growth and corporate
fundamentals have stayed resilient. Importantly, equity valuations relative to bonds have remained broadly
stable, with the equity risk premium little changed over the last two years.
History suggests equities often struggle around the start of Fed hiking cycles, but the market has already
priced a meaningful amount of tightening. While the S&P 500 has averaged a -2% return in the three
months following the first Fed hike, it has historically delivered roughly +9% over the subsequent 12
months. Ben believes the bar for a hawkish surprise is now relatively high, with markets already
discounting multiple additional hikes and corporate earnings and balance sheets remaining robust.
Long-duration growth stocks and housing-related equities are most sensitive to rising long-term yields,
with homebuilders already underperforming the equal-weight S&P 500 by 16 percentage points since


[Page 8]
June. In contrast, Financials have historically benefited from higher rates, while broader corporate balance
sheet risks remain limited given fixed-rate debt structures, long maturities and historically strong interest
coverage ratios.
A key message is that companies can offset higher discount rates by driving faster growth. Ben notes that a
1 percentage point increase in the cost of equity can be offset by roughly 2 percentage points of additional
long-term growth, reinforcing the strategic rationale behind elevated AI investment, rising M&A activity
and other growth-oriented capital allocation decisions. In this framework, AI capex is not just a technology
trend but also a response to a higher-rate world where growth is increasingly valuable

Communacopia Feedback – Our US TMT Research team & Sector specialist, Pete Callahan recapped our
Tech + Communacopia conference last week, noting that fundamentals remain exceptionally strong across
internet, software and semiconductors, with sustained infrastructure spending, growing enterprise adoption
and improving monetisation opportunities supporting a bullish long-term outlook, even as investors
remain cautious around valuation, macro risks and the pace of capital deployment.
Eric Sheridan’s key takeaway from Communacopia is that AI adoption is moving from experimentation to
measurable implementation, while investor focus continues to shift toward returns on AI investment and
the durability of current capex cycles. AI infrastructure spending remains elevated into 2027, digital
advertising and cloud demand remain healthy, and the consumer backdrop is largely resilient. The core
debate remains whether AI-driven productivity, market share gains and execution can continue to offset
macro uncertainty while reshaping consumer discovery and competitive dynamics across the internet
ecosystem.
Gabriela Borges highlighted a much more constructive tone at Communacopia, with investors increasingly
focused on company-specific AI winners rather than broad thematic debates. AI adoption is moving from
experimentation into production, supporting sustained infrastructure spending, cloud demand and new
software monetization opportunities. Across software, security and applications, management teams
emphasized that the biggest value creation opportunities remain ahead as enterprises scale inference


[Page 12]
workloads, invest in governance and observability, and increasingly tie AI deployments to measurable
productivity, cost savings and business outcomes.
Jim Schneider’s key message from Communacopia was that AI demand remains exceptionally strong
across the semiconductor ecosystem, with companies broadly reinforcing confidence in a multi-year AI
infrastructure buildout. There are persistent supply constraints across compute, power and datacentre
capacity, increasing visibility into 2027 spending, and growing evidence that AI adoption is driving real
productivity and monetisation outcomes. Across semis, equipment, foundry and memory, management
teams struck a notably bullish tone on AI capex, datacenter demand and a structurally stronger growth
outlook extending beyond the current cycle.
Pete Callahan described investor sentiment toward AI infrastructure as constructive but cautious. Bottom-
up fundamentals remain extremely strong, with tight supply-demand dynamics, robust token demand and
accelerating product innovation making it difficult to find meaningful negatives in the space. However,
investors remain hesitant to fully re-engage with the trade given lingering macro and geopolitical
uncertainty, policy risks around the midterms, and questions around how much further a rapidly growing
AI infrastructure ecosystem can scale. The result is a healthy tension between very strong company-level
fundamentals and more cautious top-down positioning.
Consumer Conference Preview – Scott Feiler (our US Consumer Spec Sales) previews our US Consumer
Conference this week, noting that positioning is near multi-year lows which can create scope for better-
than-feared conference commentary and potential outperformance.

Scott notes consumer sentiment and positioning are at depressed levels, with PB data showing exposure
near multi-year lows despite earnings revisions generally moving higher and many companies reporting
stable-to-improving 3Q trends. The market remains focused on the post-tax-refund slowdown, higher rates
and oil, while repeated failures of stocks to hold gains after earnings have reinforced the negative
sentiment cycle.

The key setup for the conference is exceptionally washed-out positioning and valuation compression. The
10 most requested meetings are in stocks averaging 32% below their 52-week highs, versus just 2.7% for
the SPY, with names such as HD, LOW, DKS, BURL, OLLI and FND all seeing significant multiple
compression. Scott Feiler believes company commentary may prove better than stock price performance
implies, creating the potential for positive surprises. The most requested meetings are: HD, TJX, WMT,
DKS, LOW, BURL, OLLI, FIVE, DG, FND.
Scott highlights that investor interest remains concentrated in large-cap quality names such as HD, TJX
and WMT, but the key debate is whether incrementally better business trends will finally be enough to
drive stocks higher after a period where even earnings beats have struggled to gain traction. He expects
many companies will sound a better tone than recent stock price action has suggested, which should result
in some outperformance, particularly as the consumer group has outperformed during the conference in 7
of the last 10 years
