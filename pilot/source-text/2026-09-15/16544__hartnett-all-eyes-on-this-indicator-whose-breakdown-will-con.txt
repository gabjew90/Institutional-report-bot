Hartnett: All Eyes On This Indicator, Whose 
Breakdown Will Confirm A Fall Stagflation Event 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, SEP 14, 2026 - 03:35 AM 
One week after issuing a warning about a future market crash should Democrats sweep the 
midterms, BofA's Michael Hartnett is back to his more familiar ways, and in his latest Flow Show 
report titled "Those yields might not hurt yet, but diesel", he analyzes the current state of the 
market, where - in an echo of what Goldman said earlier this week - he falls back to one of his 
more popular maxims, namely that "markets stop panicking when policymakers start 
panicking"... and yet, there is no panic anywhere despite the highest 30yr yield since Jun’07 and 
spiking commodities. 
And while oil back at $100/bbl takes headlines, it is diesel - which as we said several weeks ago 
- is the key real-economy pressure point: shipping, trucking, agriculture, construction, mining all 
rely on diesel. 
As Hartnett notes, crude is well below Ukraine peaks while - thanks to the abovementioned 
record crack spread - diesel at a $6 record. 


[Page 2]
 
 
Diesel aside (and for all of industrial America it will be impossible to put the biggest input cost 
aside) Hartnett warns that blasé markets + bravado policy = recipe for volatility. 
But before we get the real volatility, market will test how far they can get away, or as Hartnett 
puts it, "markets usually test policy resolve" to wit: 
●​ the July 31st US-Japan intervention saw yen test 160 before "success" pulled it down  
<154; 
●​ 30yr Treasury yield jumped +16bps the day after QE3 news (2012), +51bps in days after 
QE4 (2020); 
In this particular case, however, a test is unlikely to show weakness: according to the BofA 
strategist, a big surge in yields is too harmful to both Main St & Wall St… which is why the $1tn 
Treasury General Account, which Bessent told CNBC is where dry powder for more buybacks 
will come from, is waiting. 
Hartnett next picks up on a theme he discussed last week, and writes that “peak yield” trades 
are working ahead of central bank hikes to restore credibility; but note the long-duration rally in 
XBI, KRE, REITs, small caps is unmatched by flows; Hartnett's conclusion, "it is not too late to 
hedge AI-soaked stock indexes." 


[Page 3]
 
 
But - since Diesel is the "real-economy pressure point" -  watch transportation stocks (IYT), 
which are now flirting with the 200-day moving average @ 80; should the transportation ETF 
break below, it will confirm the summer "as good as it gets" macro derisking is set to become an 
autumn stagflation event. 
 
Hartnett then takes his usual detour looking at the latest weekly fund flows, which are as follow: 
$17.5bn to bonds, $12.9bn to cash, $9.8bn to stocks, $1.3bn to crypto, $0.6bn to gold, with the 
following key "flows to know"

●​ Global bonds: $17.5bn inflow, averaging $18bn weekly inflow past 4 weeks; 
●​ IG bonds: $5.0bn inflow, 23 rd week of inflows, 
●​ Global equities: $9.8bn inflow, averaging $7bn weekly inflow past 3 weeks down from 
$52bn average weekly inflow in Jul’26…inflows slowing, 
●​ US equities: $3.9bn outflow, with biggest 3-week outflow since Jan'26 ($14.2bn), 
●​ China equities: $1.1bn inflow, first inflow in 6 weeks  
●​ Materials: $1.9bn inflow, 10 th week of inflows  
●​ Crypto: $1.3bn inflow, with strong $6.8bn inflow past 6 weeks  
 
Harnett also updates that his proprietary sell indicator, which last week hit an all time high, 
eased back just a fraction down to 9.5 from 9.6 on slowing inflow to stocks and healthcare 
outflow; still despite the modest stock drop last week, since the "sell signal" was triggered on 
May 2; the S&P is +1.0% and the broader, world ACWI, is up 1.5%.  
Hartnett then moves on to the second part of his Flow Show, starting with a discussion of asset 
allocation, where he ominously predicts that the diversification benefit of holding bonds is fading 
fast, so yields may stay high even if Treasury buying caps the surge. As a reminder, in the 
2000-2019 “secular stagnation” era of rapid globalization & record-low inflation, bonds & stocks 
did not move together…back then a 2-3% yield was almost a bonus. But today, returns are 
correlated again and allocators may require higher yields before moving wholesale into bonds.

Something else unexpected: while momentum and memory is all the crazy, the next chart from 
Hartnett shows that stocks that can generate big free cash flow without excessive debt have 
been the best-performing factor of the last 30 years, better than momentum, growth, dividends

Today, when the money pit that is the AI supercycle craze has pushed the Free Cash Flow yield 
of the S&P 500 to record lows, high-FCF stocks (VFLO) are up 37% YTD; better - in a 
stagflation year like ‘22, the index rose 9% vs. SPY -18%.  
But it is Hartnett's final point that is also the most disturbing as it hints that the AI bubble will 
burst soon enough with little to short for it, i.e., despite $1.5tn spent in past 3 years, scant 
evidence yet of economy-wide productivity gains; in fact, total factor productivity is falling below 
trend, a measure highly correlated the past 50 years with consumer confidence; sometimes 
Main St knows what Wall St doesn’t.
