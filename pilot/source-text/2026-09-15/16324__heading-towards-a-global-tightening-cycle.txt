Heading towards a global tightening cycle? 
MARKET LINES 
Interest rates, oil prices, the actions of central banks and artificial intelligence (AI) remain the key 
market themes. Interest rates rose further last week, with 2-year yields in the U.S., Germany and 
Britain climbing by almost 25 bps and the U.S. 10-year yield approaching the 5% threshold. Last 
week's US inflation figures, which were higher than expected, failed to calm the markets, 
especially given the almost 9% increase in oil prices due to renewed tensions in the Middle East 
and the closure of the Saudi East-West pipeline. Following last week’s ECB’s 25-bp rate hike, the 
Fed, the Bank of England (BOE) and the Bank of Japan (BOJ) will be the key macroeconomic 
events this week. The market is pricing in a 25-bp rate hike for both the Fed and the BOJ. The key 
question remains whether further hikes will follow, and at what pace... but the prospect of a 
global tightening cycle is certainly taking shape. Finally, AI continues to underpin optimism 
surrounding risky assets, with earnings and investment momentum remaining robust. However, 
OpenAI, Anthropic and Elon Musk's call to slow the development of AI models serves as a 
reminder that this foundation could be fragile. We remain tactically defensive on risky assets as 
the cost of protection remains attractive. 
Rates 
► Despite a weak start, EGBs recovered driven by declining oil prices, leading to steeper curves and 
10y spreads tightening by 0.5–2.5bp, led by OAT and BTP.  
► The 10y German Bund yield remained range-bound throughout the session, ultimately settling 
within its tight 3.50%–3.52% range at the close. Last week, the overall increase in the Bund 10y yield 
was +17bps. 
► In the U.S., the 10-year Treasury yield ended the week at 4.967% (overall +18bps last week), close to 
its November 2023 high (4.991%), and the 2-year yield at 4.62% (+26bps last week). The market is 
pricing in an 88% probability of a 25-bp rate hike by the Fed this Wednesday; see our view Irregular 
and Unpredictable - Across The Americas. 
► Rates weekly: Higher yields ? Yes, please. 
► Our take away from last week’s ECB meeting : ECB September Meeting Higher for Longer: we now 
expect an additional policy rate hike of 25 bps by year-end; ECB Monetary Shifts: Tracking R-Star - 
EMEA Macro Insights. 
► Meeting BOE 17/9: BoE Preview: Balancing rate holds and QT pressures - EMEA Macro Snapshot. 
► Meeting BOJ 18/9: BoJ to move forward hike to September given accelerated inflation and 
government acquiescence after US joint intervention. 
FX 
► The dollar moved selectively, with the DXY closing at 99.07. It retreated against the Japanese yen 
while advancing against the Swiss franc, Canadian dollar and Scandinavian crowns.  
► EUR/USD closed virtually unchanged at 1.1601.  
► The yen posted the strongest appreciation of the day among G10 currencies, with USD/JPY falling 
-0.54% to 153.58, amid risk aversion fueled by soaring oil prices and concerns about global inflation 
ahead of US CPI data.

Equities 
► Stock indices rose on Friday, with the SPX and SX5E gaining ~0.9%, buoyed by falling oil prices.

Credit 
► Slight tightening of the iTraxx indices yesterday (-0.9 bps for the Main, -4.8 bps for the X-Over) 
following Oracle's better-than-expected results the previous evening. These allowed Oracle's 
spreads to tighten by an average of 4 bps in USD, while its 5-year CDS compressed by 10 bps to 
fall back to 183 bps. Meta, Broadcom, and CoreWeave benefited from the spillover effect, with their 
CDS tightening by 5, 6, and 25 bps respectively.  
► In contrast, € credit spreads continue to suffer from upward pressure on rates, particularly 
banking AT1s, which shed an average of 0.4 points in price.

the 100$/bbl for the 1st time since last July on Tuesday and briefly topped $109.9/bbl Thursday amid 
Strait of Hormuz tensions and the Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown. This morning Brent is up 
2.5% again to 107.3$/bbl.
