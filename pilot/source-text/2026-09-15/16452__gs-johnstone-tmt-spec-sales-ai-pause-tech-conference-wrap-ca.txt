Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei on Saturday (see link) is urging leading AI labs to pace frontier model development
rather than halt progress, arguing that capability gains are moving faster than safety controls. His proposed
framework centers on embedded independent evaluators, industry-wide coordination on safety standards, and
international cooperation—while also preserving the U.S. AI lead over China through tighter controls on chips,
model theft, and distillation. The key tension is that AI labs face rising safety and regulatory pressure just as
investor, funding, and IPO incentives push them to keep accelerating. OpenAI and SpaceX are reported to have
signalled openness to slowing AI development in coordination with rival labs amid growing safety concerns.

TMT Conference Final Wrap
Bottom line: corporate messaging remained bullish on AI datapoints and consistent with Q2. AI hardware demand
is still strong, compute remains tight (investor debate on this pt and esp. memory is split), and software companies
are shifting from “token maximisation” to more disciplined model routing and cost optimisation (while a near term
help showing revenue inflection remains key as AI has commoditised building software).
Investor feedback: demand is not the issue; conversion is. The debate is whether hyperscaler, Oracle, frontier-
model and infrastructure backlogs translate into durable, profitable enterprise revenue rather than another
construction cycle. Sentiment remains constructive, but not euphoric, particularly after many TMT funds gave
back a large share of YTD outperformance in July and August. US Treasury yields at two-decade highs do not help
and there are repeated questions whether credit markets can fund the scale of AI infrastructure issuance required
(even if Nvidia helps).

1. AI infrastructure: demand intact, supply remains the bottleneck
| 1. | Corporate message: last week TSMC (August revenue +53.3% YoY) and Oracle’s prints supported the AI
infrastructure bull case: compute is scarce, pricing power is real, and demand visibility remains strong. Microsoft’s
reported internal capacity plans of more than 38GW by 2032, versus around 12GW today, fit the broader
hyperscaler message. ASML said its sold out for 2027 and 2028 is already selling fast. Recent datapoints from
NVDA, AVGO, GPT-6 and the conference were broadly supportive of the AI infrastructure trade, helped by
frontier-model progress and cheaper inference driving more usage. | | 2. | Semi read-across: companies continued
to highlight datacentre buildouts as a multi-year growth driver, with orders and backlog at historically elevated
levels. Semi-cap equipment vendors stopped short of giving 2027 guidance, but commentary around 8–10 new
fabs coming online next year supports an acceleration in WFE spending. DRAM, foundry/advanced logic,
advanced packaging and NAND are expected to be the biggest 2027 growth drivers. In analog, datacentre and
aerospace/defence remain key idiosyncratic growth drivers, while broader industrial and automotive demand is
improving on lean inventories, content growth and better end demand. See Jim’s note |
2. Software: from token maximisation to token optimisation
| 1. | Corporate message: the clearest shift versus three months ago was on the application side. SAP, HubSpot,
ServiceNow and Microsoft talked less about unbounded compute bills and more about routing: frontier models for
the hardest work, cheaper closed tiers for high-volume tasks, and open-weight models where cost and control
matter. | | 2. | Proof points: HubSpot is using OpenAI’s Luna saying it gives them all they need and there is no need
for the most advanced models, and OpenAI said an 80% Luna price cut drove roughly a 10x usage jump. SAP said
routing HR queries to Mistral was around 5x cheaper than current frontier models. (GS CIO put it most succinctly
saying that the not every task needs a frontier model: use cheaper/open models for “good enough” work such as
developer tools, licence checks and routine reporting, but reserve frontier models for high-value areas like trading,
alpha generation and cybersecurity where small accuracy gains matter. Put simply, it is Formula 1 versus trucking:
pay for maximum performance only where it changes the outcome; elsewhere, optimise for reliability and cost.)
The message has shifted from “AI will break software gross margins” to “model routing can grow usage without
blowing up opex.” | | 3. | Investor feedback: investors are not fully convinced. They see the opportunity/upside in
Data platforms and security software sleeves (have a look at the multiples), while sentiment (despite bounce) on


[Page 3]
enterprise SaaS is still mixed until corporates can show an AI revenue reacceleration, not just lower unit costs.
Open-source and open-weight models were widely discussed as cost-control options; cheaper inference should lift
usage, but falling token prices are less automatically positive for anyone selling undifferentiated inference. Here
the perceived investor perception is that the hyperscalers are the winners |
3. Security and agentic AI: strongest software theme
| 1. | Corporate message: security was the strongest theme of the week as agents move from answering questions to
taking actions. ServiceNow, Databricks, Cloudflare, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and SAP made the same point:
security cannot sit in a sidecar. Identity, permissions, logging and tool access need to be enforced at the moment an
agent acts. Jensen whet further “the fact of the matter is cybersecurity will likely be the next major use case of AI,
and it's going to run continuously’ (bbg transcript) What I struggle with is that everyone wants in on the act
ServiceNow saying its currently the 8th largest enterprise security company in the world and has the ambition on
been no.1. | | 2. | Open-source reality check: Capgemini was useful on the practical constraints. Downloading
weights is easy; building the harness, keeping models current, evaluating them and staffing the expertise is not.
The operating layer remains scarce, which should keep specialist vendors relevant in the stack. |
4. Emerging opportunity: “AI to watch AI”
| 1. | There is a large, still-emerging opportunity in tools that monitor and align agentic systems — effectively AI
watching AI. The analogy is early detection: spotting dangerous or unintended behaviour before it spreads through
enterprise workflows. Palo Alto, CrowdStrike and others are already trying to productise real-time governance of
agent behaviour. If this scales, it becomes the next major cybersecurity category rather than a feature inside
existing suites. Palo Alto’s CEO argued that the AI security layer is still being built. New AI products are hard to
secure given the pace of change, integration gaps, missing context and immature industry practice. This supports
the case for more consolidated platforms, continuous adaptation and a new category around behavioural
monitoring of agents. |
5. Consumer vs enterprise AI platforms
| 1. | Visibility on the AI platform landscape should improve in 2027, and beyond as more cards get flipped with
the Frontier LLM potentially going public and giving more details on enterprise adoption curves and financial-
services use cases. | | 2. | Consumer AI: a fourth major platform beyond Alphabet, Meta and OpenAI looks unlikely
given distribution and daily-use integration. The question is less relevance and more whether all three can earn
acceptable returns on the capital now being deployed. That said SPOT presentation to me was one of the more
interesting on what its doing to better monetise it business with the help of AI. Meta Muse is an interesting new
monetisation opportunity for Meta. See GIR’s take on Muse and Conf wrap. | | 3. | Enterprise AI: the map is less
settled. The live debate is horizontal platforms versus vertical, workflow-specific systems. Proprietary data,
regulation and embedded process should protect some incumbents, but investors still need evidence that AI
adoption expands budgets rather than simply rearranging them. |
