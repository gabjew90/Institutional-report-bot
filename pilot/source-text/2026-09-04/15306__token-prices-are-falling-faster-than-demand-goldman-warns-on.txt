Token Prices Are Falling Faster Than Demand, 
Goldman Warns On The Broad AI De-Rating 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, SEP 03, 2026 - 04:30 PM 
The AI Trade Just Ran Into Its Unit-Economics Problem 
Amid all the optimism around Broadcom's outlook (which did nothing to help the stock, down 5% 
today)... 
On Goldman's research side, Jim Schneider reiterates Buy on Broadcom after management 
guided AI semi revenue to double to $115bn in FY27 (10GW of datacenter deployments) and 
double again to $230bn (20GW), naming Anthropic as its largest FY27 customer with OpenAI 
and Meta "material" in FY28 — a note that pre-emptively waves away the two biggest bear 
points (Google diversification, margin dilution). 
But, the AI complex is still losing ground fast.  
It is not de-rating because the models stopped working. It is de-rating because the price of the 
work is collapsing faster than anyone sized the plants for... 
 


[Page 2]
 
That is the narrative from Goldman Sachs One-Delta desk-head Rich Privorotsky (full note 
available here for pro subs), whose latest note is less a model-launch rumor and more a 
warning about what happens when token prices, open-weight competition, local inference, and 
a wall of financed capex show up in the same month. 
Lots of speculation that Astra drops imminently, though no confirmed launch date. Meta’s new 
Muse Spark 1.3 is another reminder that competition at the frontier is accelerating (extremely 
competitive on benchmarks). The bigger point is massive compression in cost/token. If unit 
prices keep collapsing, consumption has to grow extraordinarily fast just to offset it (Silicon data 
Token index down -29% in month of August). Add more inference moving toward cheaper/local 
hardware and I struggle with per token pricing as a durable end state model. That matters for 
equities. More demand + lower prices is not automatically bullish if pricing falls faster than 
consumption grows. Unless capability keeps making radical jumps (looking at you, Astra)… 
periods of compute oversupply become plausible. Beyond the repricing of credit that helps 
explain why the space has been de-rating. 
Strip out the Astra gossip and the desk is saying one thing: the last two years of equity 
underwriting assumed a world where more tokens meant more revenue. August printed the 
opposite. 
The number that actually matters 
Silicon Data’s LLM Token Expenditure Index - the usage-weighted price the market is paying per 
million tokens, published on Bloomberg as SDLLMTK - fell 29% in August and printed about 
$0.97 per million tokens at month-end. That is a record low, and more than 50% off the May 
peak near $2.05. 


[Page 3]
 
 
That index is not "token demand." Silicon Data itself has had to remind people it is an 
expenditure-weighted price. A crash in the line can mean list-price cuts, a mix shift into cheap 
open-weight models, or both. 
It does not require that anyone stopped using AI. 
Which is why the bull talking point - "look at the volume" - is starting to miss the trade. 
Additionally, JPMorgan’s data-center note had OpenRouter token volume up roughly 47% 
month-over-month in August. Dollar spend was still only up about 7%. 
Look again at the chart above, the green line shows rental pricing for H100s is falling (not what 
you'd expect if 'demand' for tokens is soaring). 
And our best proxy for actual demand appears to be rolling over... 


[Page 4]
 
 
There is no official global token meter. The cleanest public series is OpenRouter routed volume 
- a real API-gateway tape, but not ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude first-party apps. 
Volume up a lot. Price down more. Spend barely holding. 
That is the problem: Equities do not get paid in tokens, they get paid in dollars, and CapEx was 
underwritten off the dollars. 
Muse Spark is the present. Astra is the option. 
As Privorotsky notes hopefully, OpenAI’s next model, Astra, is the potential narrative 
circuit-breaker. 
Meta’s Muse Spark 1.3, which started rolling out September 2 in Muse Code and the Meta 
Model API, is the thing already on the tape. 
Independent benches put Spark 1.3 in the same neighborhood as GPT-5.6 Sol and next to 
Claude Opus 5 / Fable on agentic and coding work, with long-context retrieval that looks like a 
different product.  
Frontier lead measured in weeks is not a moat. It is a product cycle.  
Every time the second-best model is close enough and cheap, enterprises route around the 
premium tier. 
The token index is that routing decision, aggregated. 
Astra can still reset the mix - if it is a capability jump people will pay a multiple for. On 
September 1 OpenAI said Astra meets the Critical cybersecurity threshold under its 


[Page 5]
 
Preparedness Framework, the first model it has put in that bucket, and that it plans to make the 
system "available soon." The most advanced cyber tools get gated to testers and then Daybreak 
Blue. There is still no confirmed date. 
A radical model that is access-restricted, monitored, and friction-heavy does not automatically 
refill the token-revenue bucket.  
It can even reduce billed tokens if the valuable work never hits the public meter. 
Privorotsky’s parenthetical is doing a lot of work: looking at you, Astra. The desk is treating the 
launch as the last near-term thing that can invalidate the oversupply case. Everything else is 
just more competition at a lower clearing price. 
Per-token pricing is a phase, not an end state 
This is the line in Goldman's note that the equity complex should have tattooed (hopefully) on 
the monitor. 
Cloud inference priced per million tokens only works while the model is too big to run locally, the 
buyer cannot substitute to an open-weight peer, and the workload is bursty enough that owning 
the silicon is dumb. All three are rotting at once. 
On-device and "personal cluster" inference is no longer a hobbyist footnote.  
RTX Spark-class laptops, DGX Spark boxes, Mac Studios running 70B-class weights, and 
NPUs in the 40–75+ TOPS band have pushed a large class of tokens toward the cost of 
electricity. 
Once a shop’s monthly API bill crosses the amortization line on a five-to-fifteen-thousand-dollar 
box, they stop being a token customer and become a hardware customer - once. That is a GPU 
sale. It is not a 40% software annuity. 
So the durable commercial models start to look like seats, agents, and outcomes; intelligence 
bundled into ads, cloud, and devices; owned silicon for steady-state decode; and a thin 
premium tier at the true frontier. 
Per-million-token list prices racing toward a dollar, then toward dimes, are not a bug in that 
world. They are the tell that the unit of account is wrong. 
Privorotsky, again: "I struggle with per token pricing as a durable end state model. That matters 
for equities." 
More demand is not automatically bullish... And Credit Noticed 
The sentence that should end the "insatiable demand" slide: 


[Page 6]
 
More demand + lower prices is not automatically bullish if pricing falls faster than consumption 
grows. 
CapEx in this cycle is not a flexible OpEx line. Hyperscaler spend has been revised into the 
high-hundreds-of-billions to roughly a-trillion zone. Moody’s has already flagged free-cash-flow 
compression, a shift from asset-lite to asset-heavy balance sheets, and lease commitments that 
do not show up as tidy bonds but bind the issuers all the same. Direct debt across the tracked 
cohort is already in the hundreds of billions. The five rated hyperscalers were projected to spend 
on the order of $737 billion in calendar 2026 capex - about 38% of revenue. 
As always, credit noticed first.  
Broad investment-grade OAS can look calm while a hyperscaler-specific basket quietly gaps 
wider than the index. Seventy-eight of 91 hyperscaler bonds issued in 2026 were already 
trading cheaper than at issuance by late August, in one compilation of the move.  
 
That is the "repricing of credit" Privorotsky is pointing at. Equity multiples were the lagging 
indicator. 
The arithmetic is not complicated: 
Tokens 30% cheaper and volume up 20% means inference revenue falls. 
Inference revenue falls while depreciation and interest from the 2025–27 buildout are still 
ramping, and ROIC collapses. 
ROIC collapses and the market does not need a cartoon "bubble pop," it just needs a multiple 
that prices a utility with a lot of stranded useful life. 


[Page 7]
 
Oversupply does not require empty halls. It only requires utilization that cannot clear at the price 
the financing assumed. Every GPU can be busy running twenty-cent tokens and the people who 
borrowed against two-dollar tokens still have a problem. 
But... 
Of course it's not all doom and gloom and below we offer three counters that are often heard:  
1.​ Agents eat the discount. A model that uses 25% fewer tokens per task and then runs ten 
times as many tasks is still a volume machine. Meta’s own Spark 1.3 comparisons claim 
roughly 20% fewer tool calls and 25% fewer tokens versus 1.2. Efficiency can increase 
the set of viable jobs. The question is whether those jobs get metered and paid for, or 
bundled into products that were already sold. 
2.​ Training and memory are not the same trade as decode tokens. HBM, power, and 
training clusters can stay tight while merchant inference commoditizes. August already 
sketched the split: software ripped, the semiconductor complex wobbled. Shorting “AI” 
as one blob is how you get the factor wrong. The weak link is inference margin and the 
second-tier lessors, not necessarily every watt of training silicon. 
3.​ The frontier tier can still be redefined upward. Every prior collapse in fixed-capability 
price coexisted with a new expensive flagship. The bull case was never “tokens stay at 
five dollars.” It was “the five-dollar tier keeps moving up the capability curve fast enough 
that mix offsets mix-down.” That worked for three years. August is the first clean month 
where it looks like it stopped. 
...none of which rescue the multiples by themselves. 
The Bottom Line 
Demand can be infinite and the stocks can still go down if the infinite demand arrives at a price 
that does not service the debt issued to build the plants. 
The market is rolling from a capability bull market into a price-taker tape. The CapEx was sized 
for the first regime. Credit has started to charge for the second. Astra is the last near-term 
narrative that can flip the mix back up the stack. Until it does - and unless it does by enough to 
matter after the gates and the monitors - August’s token print is the tell. 
Simply put, more tokens and lower prices are not automatically bullish. Goldman’s desk already 
wrote the headline. The index just printed it.
