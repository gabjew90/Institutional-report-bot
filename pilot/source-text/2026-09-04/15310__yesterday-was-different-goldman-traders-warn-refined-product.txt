"Yesterday Was Different": Goldman Traders Warn 
'Refined Product' Is The Rates Trade 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, SEP 02, 2026 - 05:20 PM 
The tape is Brent at $95 - a May-2026 print; markets are pricing a 66% chance of a September 
hike, suggesting the path of least resistance is another crude-and-FOMC panic (TACO & 
WACO?). 
 
Goldman Sachs one-delta desk-head, Rich Privorotsky, has been watching the same chart all 
week (as we highlighted yesterday), and yesterday the United States joined it... 
“Yesterday was different… the US escalated while oil was already rising and during market 
hours.” 
Not overnight; not the usual cool-it reflex that leaks a corridor rumor into Asia and lets the barrel 
dump before New York. Instead, Iranian air defense, radar, comms all hit around Hormuz, all 
while crude was already going up, in the US cash session. 
Inverse heating oil is still the 10-year. TTF is through €70. 
“Trading as if they were the same asset. if we want relief on rates we first need relief on energy.”

The financial press will file $95 and a 66% hike. The reality is energy complex's plumbing is 
clogged (MidEast and Russian refinery capacity damaged) for refined product, and a White 
House that just hit the tape instead of talking it down. 
Diesel, gasoil, heating oil, European gas have all broken out. 
As Goldman's Johannes Duttenhoefer noted, TTF climbed through €70/MWh yesterday, “even 
higher than 2022.”  
 
Russia's Novak has squeezed (or been forced by Ukraine) to push EU gas to $74, +25% in a 
month. US 10-year 4.80%. Japan 10s through 3% for the first time since 1996. 
Let that sink in...

European gas is above the war-in-2022 print, Japanese tens are at a 30-year high, and the 
United States chose yesterday — market hours, oil already rising — to escalate around the 
Strait. 
Wright claimed 17 million barrels transited Monday. Heavily debated. 
The official restoration number versus a product complex that has already voted. You can argue 
the crude count. You cannot argue TTF through seventy euros and a US diesel crack that made 
the 10-year its twin.  
 
Relief on rates first requires relief on energy.  
Yesterday went the other way. 
Tanker-for-tanker is the color. Product is the rates trade. 
Goldman's Industrials specialist, Amanda Ross, summarized the chaos: US forces struck about 
100 targets Tuesday night, including two Iranian tankers, under Trump’s new “tanker-for-tanker” 
policy. Germany blamed Russia for sabotage on a Ukrainian cargo aircraft and a power plant. 
NFP is next. All of that is the ticker.  
She reiterated: The desk trade is still inverse heating oil versus the 10-year.  
Iran hit two tankers into a rising barrel and the 10-year does not care what Wright’s Monday 
transit count was. It cares that TTF is above 2022. 
Goldman's head of European Rates Strategy, George Cole's overlay is the uncomfortable one. 
The sell-off is fundamental, not fragile. 
“The tell for us is what isn't happening: rates vol hasn't broken higher.”  
Forecasts still have 10 year yields down 30-40bp if the driver goes away.
