Pressure points
Markets are setting up around a few increasingly interesting pressure points. Tech risk has been cut
just as NVDA starts squeezing, SOX is approaching must-hold territory with optionality unusually cheap,
and memory continues to compress despite fundamentals remaining strong.
Away from tech, commodities are sending another signal. BCOM has exploded higher and rates are
paying attention. Another leg higher from here could quickly put the inflation narrative back at the
center of the macro debate.
Teflon tech
NDX has been stuck in consolidation mode since May. We just tested the longer-term trend line again,
bouncing right around the 100-day MA. A proper close above the shorter-term negative trend line and
things could start squeezing again.
The vol reset has been aggressive, with the long weekend starting to add further pressure as of writing.
Tech optionality is getting cheap, especially if realized starts picking up from here. More on tech here.

The SOX debate


[Page 3]
There is a fundamental reason to take the latest SOX weakness seriously. The AI debate is shifting from
pure demand to unit economics: token volumes are exploding, but prices are falling even faster. As
Goldman's Privorotsky puts it, “More demand + lower prices is not automatically bullish if pricing falls
faster than consumption grows.”
That does not mean the entire semiconductor complex is broken. Training, HBM and power can remain
tight even as inference economics deteriorate. That makes the approaching SOX must-hold
particularly important, especially with semiconductor vol this cheap.
Much more on the changing AI economics here.

Memory keeps getting paid
Memory remains one of the tightest links in the AI supply chain. Amazon explicitly attributed part of its
latest capex increase to higher memory costs, yet hyperscalers continue absorbing those prices to
secure HBM, DRAM and enterprise SSD capacity.
That's a powerful signal. If customers are raising capex partly because memory costs more rather than
cutting demand, memory suppliers still have serious pricing power.
