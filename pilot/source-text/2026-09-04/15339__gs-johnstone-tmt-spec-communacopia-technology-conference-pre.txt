GS Communacopia + Technology Conference preview
Next week’s Communacopia + Technology Conference in San Francisco should be an important post-Q2
checkpoint on AI capex, early monetisation and macro resilience. The agenda offers broad read-throughs
across semis, cloud, software, internet, media and telecom (over 260 companies will be in SF), with many
sessions webcast and available on GIR website — see Event link and agenda:
https://events.gs.com/2026/cmntch
Most requested in order – Datadog, CoreWeave, Microsoft, Shopify, Broadcom, AMD, Nvidia, Nebius,
TSMC and Snowflake. The pattern remains clear: investors are focused on the AI capex-to-revenue
bridge, compute-demand durability and which software companies can show credible AI monetisation.
The conference stats are all up on last year – more companies are coming with more investors (over 2k)
and hence more meeting requests.
Note the European Communacopia Conference in London on 15–16 September, with 44 corporates and
c.75% C-suite participation: https://events.gs.com/2026/cmncp/client
The three big themes I will be watching:
1. Can infrastructure spend turn into software revenue outside the Frontier names?
The central AI debate is whether record investment in chips, power, data centres and networking is
translating into visible revenue for software, cloud and application-layer companies. Semis and
infrastructure names can still point to strong demand, but investors now want clearer proof of enterprise
adoption, measurable AI revenue and sustainable returns for hyperscalers and software companies. The
risk is that Nvidia’s profits are being supported by investor capital flowing through model labs rather than
self-sustaining customer economics further down the stack.
Q2 offered more constructive evidence. Palantir posted 149% US commercial revenue growth, Cloudflare
flagged rising agentic activity, and Snowflake pointed to faster AI-enabled migrations. In internet and ads,
Tencent and Meta both showed AI improving monetisation, while security remains a key beneficiary as
more capable models raise the importance of alignment, oversight and protection.

2. SaaS disruption versus agentic AI monetisation
With Prometheus presenting on day one, the tone could matter for how SaaS companies frame AI as both
an accelerator and a source of disruption. The key investor question is whether AI agents extend software
value or pressure seat-based licensing. For SAP, listen for updates on Joule, agentic solutions, token
consumption and any framework for monetising usage, outcomes or work units. The private AI sessions
and VC panel should also help frame how quickly AI-native models could pressure public software
incumbents.
3. Capital discipline, useful lives and the cost of AI buildout
With AI investment still rising, investors will focus on how companies protect margins, free cash flow and
balance sheets. Key watch points are useful-life extensions, depreciation and inference costs, and whether
companies can fund growth without materially diluting returns.
CoreWeave’s Q2 call was notable for management’s comments that “as our earlier generation fleets roll
off their original contracts, they offer the potential to deliver strong returns in the subsequent years. We are
seeing this across our Ampere and Hopper fleet. As an example, we recently signed an A100 contract that
extends into 2029 at an attractive price. As a reminder, this SKU was introduced in 2020.” Google has also
said that its seven-to-eight-year-old custom TPUs are still running at 100% utilisation.
Sector playbooks
Semis and semicap: the focus is the durability of the AI capex flywheel. Key questions are whether
hyperscaler and neocloud demand is still accelerating into 2027, whether power and cooling constraints
are slowing deployment, and whether advanced packaging, CoWoS and HBM tightness continue to
support pricing. Investors will also look for more colour on large-scale compute projects, especially
SpaceX/Terafab, and the read-across for semicap names. On ASML, the key question is whether planned
Low-NA EUV capacity increases are enough if Terafab-scale demand materialises (Elon Musk suggested

we could see 6–8GW of capacity in 2027 alone, potentially well above 10GW, versus GIR estimates of
c.9.3GW added between YE25 and YE28 and cumulative AI segment capex of c.$375bn over that period.)
Software: the key question is whether AI is moving from pilots to production, with hard evidence in usage
growth, ACV uplift, seat expansion or consumption credits. Investors will also focus on inference costs,
gross-margin pressure and whether agentic AI cannibalises seat-based models or creates a new pricing
layer.
Investor interest remains strongest in data infrastructure, developer tools and security, while broader SaaS
sentiment is still more muted despite the recent sector bounce. Reported PE interest in Workday was
largely viewed as opportunistic following the sector’s sharp de-rating and raised questions around
financing, rather than signalling a broad re-rating catalyst.
Mo’s note on software moats, business models and valuations in the agentic era is helpful here. The key
takeaway is that prior trough valuation levels may provide support, but not necessarily a re-rating trigger,
unless companies can show revenue reacceleration. Workday suggests that at around $120, or c.3x
EV/recurring subscription revenue, PE will at least engage, but that is different from a durable public-
market catalyst.

Telecom: all three major US telcos are attending, alongside SpaceX, which should keep satellite risk and
spectrum strategy in focus. The main US debate remains competition, particularly at T-Mobile, where
investors have been watching for any pick-up in churn following recent price rises. With SpaceX also
present, listen for comments on spectrum auctions, willingness to spend and whether Starlink could
become a bidder or competitive reference point. Recent wildfire activity across Europe and the US also
reinforces the use case for Starlink as an emergency or add-on connectivity service.
Media, internet and consumer platforms: the debate will focus on whether AI is improving ad targeting,
creative tools and advertiser ROI, while digital ad pricing remains healthy and streaming profitability
improves. OpenAI’s ads business is reportedly scaling quickly, with a $1bn annualised revenue run-rate in
under 200 days. For consumer internet, investors will look for signs of demand resilience, trade-down and
whether AI is changing discovery, search and commerce behaviour.
European names that stand out into the conference:
• ASML — Q2 was one of the most constructive updates in some time, with 2026 capex guidance lifted
and 2027 capex still expected to rise significantly. The debate is whether higher lithography intensity,
tighter capacity and Low-NA/High-NA productivity gains can sustain better pricing and visibility if
Terafab-scale demand materialises.
• Adyen — 2Q26 results were solid, with stronger net revenue growth and revised FY26 guidance that
looks de-risked given it does not require a 2H acceleration. The key debates are integration execution,
medium-term revenue opportunities from recent acquisitions and whether broader payments consolidation
could become a sector catalyst.

• SAP — The debate is more balanced after a better Q2 print and >40% share-price move since results.
The key positive was stronger cloud backlog, with CCB up 26% YoY ex-FX and organic CCB up c.24.7%,
helping de-risk 2H cloud growth. At Communacopia, listen for H2 CCB colour, any update on mega deals,
early feedback on agentic solutions, token-consumption metrics and whether inference costs are beginning
to affect margins.
• Wise — for the stock to work, investors likely need greater clarity on Brussels and confidence that Wise
can reapply for a US bank charter under the Genius Act. A further major platform win would be the other
obvious catalyst.
• Scout — Adam’s note highlights that the ImmoScout24 Tenant Network has nearly tripled pre-market
rental listings since May, which should improve the Search+ proposition and support ARPU. The key
question is how quickly exclusive inventory converts into paid subscriber growth and pricing power.
