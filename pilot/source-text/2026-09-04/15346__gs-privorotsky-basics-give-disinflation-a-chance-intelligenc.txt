GS Privorotsky - Basics Give Disinflation a Chance. Intelligence
Improvements. NFP Downside 4 Sep 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Equity Sales
Fri 4 Sep 2026, 3:12am ET
Markets recovered yday. Waller gave us a real break in the clouds on Fed policy and basically
corroborated Williams…the center of the committee hasn’t shifted materially. They remain data
dependent. Waller might hold in September unless inflation comes in hot, and made pretty clear that NFP
matters less than CPI next week. Payroll consensus is only ~+55k, so barring a genuinely huge upside
surprise, difficult to see today changing much. “Give disinflation a chance.” Event risk has effectively
migrated from payrolls to CPI.
AI: Astra looks important. SoftBank +10–11% overnight, while Oracle was +5.5%. The bigger point is
that a frontier model making a genuine step away from the pack is exactly what the AI bull case wants.
Falling token prices are one thing…a breakthrough in intelligence changes the demand curve again. It
forces everyone else to chase, keeps the spending cycle alive and revitalizes the idea that there is still
something meaningfully better to build towards. I think some of my comments yesterday got taken out of
context…I’m not bearish AI (but important to acknowledge hardware has been de-rating and we need to
understand why). If anything, Astra reinforces the core thesis...breakthrough intelligence revitalizes the
desire/necessity to spend. The September AI calendar also looks pretty constructive…Oracle next week,
conferences and plenty of opportunities for bullish read throughs.
Japan: Keep an eye on the yen…it continues to strengthen with no obvious sign that intervention is driving
it. BOJ pricing has become aggressive: jut over 25bps for September and close to 100bps over the next
year. At the same time, Takaichi’s fiscal push is getting bigger…FY27 budget requests have reached
¥143.1tn, back towards pandemic era scale. That is an interesting mix: looser fiscal policy, tighter
monetary policy and a stronger yen. Exporters should struggle at the margin, domestic banks should trade
better (Topix banks highs of the year). The stronger yen also bites the carry trade, which is slightly
punitive for broader risk, although it fits neatly with the weaker dollar impulse coming from Waller/Fed
repricing.
Energy: Remains the biggest unresolved macro risk. Iran’s latest retaliation has focused on US regional
bases rather than energy infrastructure, which is at least incrementally positive, but shipping through
Hormuz remains heavily impaired. The US has demonstrated it can escort large crude convoys through the
Strait, but that doesn’t solve the broader problem…product and LNG flows remain disrupted and
inventories stay under pressure. The status quo doesn’t feel sustainable. Heading into a long weekend, I
worry about another escalate to de-escalate episode, but equally this feels like an increasingly obvious
window to find some sort of off ramp before domestic political considerations become even more
important.

Russia/Ukraine: Some more encouraging rhetoric. Putin said there is a chance of reaching an agreement
and Zelensky said US negotiators are expected in both Moscow and Kyiv in the coming days. Wheat
reversed hard yday. Hard to square that with continued attacks on Russian refining capacity, so I wouldn’t
get carried away on peace prospects…but there does appear to be a renewed diplomatic track despite the
military escalation. Polymarket odds on ceasefire haven't moved off the lows but something to keep on
radar.
Risk: The market is starting to see a narrow path where the Fed simply stays on hold. Today’s payroll
hurdle is low…the real requirement is that CPI doesn’t come in hot. Positioning also feels lighter after a
difficult August where consensus and momentum trades repeatedly struggled. Combine that with Astra re-
energizing the AI narrative and a catalyst heavy tech calendar next week, and you can see the ingredients
for a melt-up into the weekend. Dream state is all that + some Hormuz off ramp over weekend. Think set
up is good but Energy and CPI remain the obvious things that can spoil it. Watching Saxony-Anhalt
election in Germany over weekend.
