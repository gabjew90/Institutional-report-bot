JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Waller’s dovish press conference was the main driver of yesterday’s bond market rally and the 
move higher in stocks. Jay (here) highlighted three factors that drove the bond market 
reaction: (i) Waller and Warsh have different gauges of the trend in inflation, (ii) Waller outlined 
an explicit data-dependent reaction function, and (iii) his speech was also reinforced by 
Williams and Barr. We don’t think his speech changed much of the narrative and remain of the 
view that next week’s CPI will be a critical catalyst to watch compared to today’s NFP, given 
that wage inflation has not been the driving force for inflation and Warsh’s assessment of labor 
market productivity.

On equities reactions, we shared the below immediately after the ISM release yesterday: 
“Equities seem to be looking for a reason to move higher. Macro / GDP is good and getting 
better. Micro / Earnings are historically strong. AI has some fears but that seems more about 
stock selection than a theme that faces insurmountable challenges. Seasonality is unfavorable 
but whether one is concerned about September or Midterms, the next period tends to be 
awesome. Post-midterms, on average, SPX rallied +5.4%, +11.4%, +12.5%, and +14.7% at 3 
month (78% hit rate), 6 month (100% hit rate), 9 month (100% hit rate), and 12 month (100% 
hit rate) tenors after midterm election days since 1990. If you go back 1950, then the returns at 
the 6-, 9-, and 12-month marks maintain their 100% hit rate for positive returns and the 3-
month hit rate increases to 89%. Risks are primarily geopolitical which can still impact inflation, 
esp. diesel prices which are used by truckers and shippers, so more important to the inflation 
picture than gasoline. Keep an eye on Russia/Germany and Russia/Norway; on the latter the 
target would be Svalbard island as another way for Putin to test NATO.”

•         US MKT INTEL ON NFP – Post-Jackson Hole, Warsh has flagged that the economy is at 
full employment and that inflation is too high. With 1x NFP and 1x CPI ahead of the Sep 16 
Fed meeting, we view CPI as more impactful and that NFP is operating in a ‘Good News is 
Bad News’ type of environment. A stronger NFP print will likely push bond yields higher, 
weighing on Equities; the logic is that more jobs leads to more spending and that combined 
strength should embolden businesses to hire further. The cycle is self-reinforcing, especially 
consider loose financial conditions which was also flagged by Warsh at Jackson Hole. That 
said, a downside miss of a large magnitude, say another negative print, could reignite 
stagflation concerns. Goldilocks is like in the 30k – 70k range.

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the Aug 24, 2026 Morning Briefing 
We are moving to a Tactically Cautious / Neutral view, which is to say that Equity market 
fundamentals remain strong but that near-term variables are likely to cause markets to chop 
sideways. Here is what is driving the change in our tactical view: (i) The Fed – Equity bull 
markets tends to end with either a hiking cycle or a recession. Currently, a recession is highly 
unlikely to manifest over the next few quarters, but Warsh’s comments now make Sep a live 
meeting. What is unclear is if the Fed hikes, what is the scale / length of the hiking cycle, e.g., 
would he go in Sep / Dec and conclude the hikes or potentially go 50bp in Sep? NFP and CPI 
are the critical inputs with CPI on Sep 11 the more important of the two prints given Warsh’s 
view that the US is at full employment ad Friday’s print is unlikely to drive a material change in 
the U.3 unemployment rate. (ii) Positioning – make sure to read the weekly update from 


[Page 7]
Positioning Intel but the TL; DR is that positioning is not giving a clean read on market 
direction and history suggests a choppy next 3-4 weeks. The team also have flagged that a 
sharp rebound in Momentum factor is unlikely given recent activity. (iii) Credit Spreads – 
Historically, there is a spike in issuance in the two weeks post-Labor Day driving spreads wider 
which can pressure Equities. Given the recent headwinds to the AI trade stemming from CDS 
spread widening, this is another variable to monitor. (iv) Seasonality – September screens as 
the worst performing month for the SPX followed by Q4 being the strongest quarter of the year. 
Seasonal moves may be exacerbated by midterm election seasonality, which tends to be 
negative into the election and then strongly positive post-election. Since 1990, the SPX rallied 
+5.4%, +11.4%, +12.5%, and +14.7% at 3 month (78% hit rate), 6 month (100% hit rate), 9 
month (100% hit rate), and 12 month (100% hit rate) tenors after midterm election days. (v) 
Equity Rotation / Momentum Unwind – Momentum (JPMPURE Index) is down more than 
21% from its June ATH and ‘Classic’ Momentum (JPPQMO Index) is down nearly 34% from its 
June ATH. These moves fall outside the typical drawdown ranges of 10-15%. (vi) AVGO 
Earnings – may prove spectacular and fail to drive meaningful stock appreciation in AI / 
Memory / Semis. 
  
In summary, we think market fundamentals (econ & earnings) remain intact but see the next 2-
3 weeks being choppy. The index may not see a dramatic pullback, but it would not surprise to 
see additional turmoil at the sub-index and single stock level. This argues for reducing net-
longs and potentially adopting a $-neutral or market-neutral approach. Keep an eye on (i) US / 
Iran to see if a deal is possible or if we see actions that drive oil prices higher and (ii) does the 
US / Canada trade debate expand into something more serious.
