Freya Beamish
Asset mini-cycles
Tech will not capture the proportion of earnings implied by current forecasts. The equilibrium at the
end of last week was not sustainable. For tech valuations to make sense, we need to see progress in
increasing the share of revenues coming from outside the tech complex. That's difficult with energy
cost and interest rate headwinds. To stay in risk on from a macro perspective, we need to see a
broadening of profits, which we think is happening. But if profit broadening is the condition for risk on,
it's time for another rotation into the rest of the economy and another investigation of who are the
winners and losers from deploying this technology. In contrast, if profits fail to broaden then the rest of
the economy can't afford to pay for tech and we'll anyway be going into risk off, with bonds finally
catching a break. Alternatively, the weak links in the tech chain pop under the strain of higher rates,
leaving open the threat of a risk off moment even if the macro is improving. Bonds would probably
benefit in this scenario as well, though we still prefer non-US sovereign debt, where there is more to
gain if the energy shock reverses, while the rise  in UST yields has been driven mainly by domestic
inflation pressures.
The asset mini-cycle is now becoming an established feature of the new regime, which kicked off with
a lot of equity after a decade of deleveraging and asset price inflation. Demand-side resilience has
preserved that equity, but negative supply shocks are also a feature of the new normal and the
combination creates periodic rotations as long as the energy shocks remain small enough. Demand
resilience keeps investors in risk on but cost-push shocks periodically crowd capital into tech and the
dollar, creating the conditions for the next rotation. The bar is high for profits to concentrate further on
a secular basis and right now, it seems high from a mini-cycle perspective too.

A bet on rotation within the US, which is less exposed to the energy drag seems safer, though any
retreat in energy prices is a positive for global equities and weakens the dollar. And we see little upside
in the dollar from a US outperformance perspective at this stage. Warsh has restored the right hand
side of the dollar smile - the dollar now rallies with the US is expected to outperform and the Fed
expected to hike as a result. But our hawkish call is now close to priced and if anything Fed speak
seems to be trying to walk back. It made strategic sense for Warsh to prove his hawkish credentials at
Jackson Hole and in September. But there seems still to be a debate to be won on the FOMC as to
whether the economy really is moving towards overheating or just needing a couple of insurance
hikes. We think the economic data will come in suggesting the former - wage growth has bottomed for
low income earners, and even more importantly for job switchers and possibly more broadly in the
AHE data, stripping out education and health. And profits do appear to be broadening. And inflation
once again is showing its inability to progress towards 2%. So we stand by our call for quarterly hikes.
But we remain concerned around US risks around the midterms. At the same time, another feature of
this mini-cycle dynamic is that hedging ratios have dropped right down again, leaving dollar investors
exposed to another episode of “post hedging” (which is dollar negative) if another US centric shock
hits.
The bitter pill to swallow is the following: the more air that is knocked out of tech now, the greater is
the chance of a sustained expansion and genuine improvements in productivity trends. If investors
remain complicit in the run up of a bubble in tech, fearful of being left on the sidelines, leverage will
build from levels that are currently threatening for certain weak links in the AI chain but not
systemically problematic. The problem to deal with in one or two years time would then be
significantly bigger and more likely to bring about a significant recession. In the meantime, we are
watching for triggers in the tech space - see Dario's upcoming note - and short of that prefer to look for
opportunities elsewhere.
