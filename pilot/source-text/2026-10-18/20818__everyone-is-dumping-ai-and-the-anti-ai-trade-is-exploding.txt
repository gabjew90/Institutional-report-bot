The great AI unwind
The AI selloff is turning into a full-blown positioning washout. Credit spreads are widening, hedge funds
are aggressively cutting exposure, and Korean tech is facing relentless foreign selling.
Yet beneath the carnage, memory prices continue to rise, valuations are back near AI-cycle lows, and
India is emerging as an unlikely winner.
Is the market anticipating a fundamental downturn, or has the selling simply gone too far?
Paying attention
There has at least been some logic to the latest tech selloff. The previous spike in tech CDS spreads
was met with a surging NDX, leaving many scratching their heads. This time around, rising credit
spreads have been accompanied by falling tech stocks.
At least credit and equities are finally speaking the same language.

Everybody wants out
Foreign investors are dumping Korean tech at an extraordinary pace.
According to Goldman, foreign selling has reached $11.6 billion over eight consecutive sessions. SK
Hynix has endured 15 straight days of foreign selling, while Samsung has seen five consecutive days of
outflows.
The selling has become increasingly mechanical, with hedge funds cutting exposure and foreign
investors rushing for the exits.
Interestingly, domestic institutions and retail investors have started absorbing some of that supply. The
question is how much more they can take.

The great AI liquidation
This is no longer just a Korean memory story.
Goldman reports that hedge funds have been net sellers of global AI stocks for five consecutive
sessions, taking net allocation down to 26.1% from a June peak of 31.7%.
Meanwhile, the aggregate long/short ratio has collapsed from 4.34 to 3.27, putting it in the seventh
percentile over the past year.
A remarkable amount of bullish positioning has already been flushed out. The obvious question is: who
is left to sell?

Stocks down, prices up
Here's the fascinating divergence.
While memory equities have been hammered, DDR5 spot prices have continued moving higher. In
other words, the stock market is pricing a deteriorating memory cycle that has yet to materialize in
spot pricing.
Goldman notes that Samsung trades at approximately 1.2x book value, while SK Hynix is around 0.9x,
suggesting investors are already discounting a significant downturn by 2028.
The gap between the physical memory market and the equity market is becoming increasingly difficult
to ignore. More on the latest memory meltdown here.

The anti-AI trade
There is little fundamental connection between NIFTY and KOSPI, but the two markets have moved in
an almost perfectly inverse relationship this year. The latest leg higher in NIFTY has coincided with the
KOSPI puke, although a wide performance gap between the two indices remains.
India's software services sector has surged 7.5% this week, emerging as the anti-AI trade of choice for
regional allocators.
As investors dump AI-linked Asian technology exposure, Indian IT has become an unlikely beneficiary
of the rotation. The latest leg higher was also supported by optimism following Accenture's investor
day.
