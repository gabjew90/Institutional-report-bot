• Indian IT services delivered a better-than-expected quarter, led by Tech Mahindra, where revenue growth
beat by about 90 bps and is now running roughly twice as fast as large-cap peers, with healthy deal wins
supporting near-term momentum. Wipro also beat on growth and margins, but its deal wins fell 26% year

a meaningful acceleration. Estimates rise modestly for Tech Mahindra and Wipro, but both remain Sell-
rated. See GIR

• Satellites: EU fast-tracks SpaceX rival (Bloomberg): Project Bromo, the Airbus–Leonardo–Thales
satellite joint venture, could secure rapid Phase 1 approval as Brussels seeks a European champion to
reduce reliance on US providers such as SpaceX. The Commission has spent more than a year working
through antitrust concerns, although clearance may still require concessions and regulators insist the deal
will not receive a free pass.
• Google Quantum AI: Google says it has overcome a major bottleneck in quantum computing by
demonstrating “magic state cultivation” on its Willow processor, reducing errors by around 40 times and
achieving 99.99% logical fidelity. Magic states are essential for useful, error-corrected quantum programs
but can consume up to 95% of system capacity, so producing them more cleanly could significantly reduce
the physical qubits, cryogenic infrastructure and capital required for a commercial machine. The result
turns a long-standing theoretical concept into an experimental reality and helps de-risk use cases such as
materials discovery, logistics and financial modelling, although Google still needs to prove the technique
works at much larger scale before practical quantum computing becomes viable.

• GLOBAL TECHNOLOGY: SEMICONDUCTORS – MEMORY: CXMT’s 4F2 announcement triggered
an 11–26% sell-off across major memory stocks and erased more than $700 billion of market value,
although weakness in CXMT itself and NAND names suggests broader AI-financing, credit and inflation
concerns also contributed (report). China is making genuine progress in DRAM capacity and CXMT’s
planned 4F2 server DRAM launch by end-2026 warrants attention, but meaningful gaps remain in
customer qualification, high-yield production, advanced-node DRAM and HBM versus Samsung
Electronics and SK Hynix. With Korean memory leaders trading below book value on 2027–28 estimates,
the correction may be pricing in an overly bearish downcycle, supporting attractive risk/reward despite
limited near-term catalysts.
• Besi / ASML- SemiAnalysis has a pop at BESI - note this thesis has been circulating in the market for
over a week: the SemiAnalysis note was initially behind a paywall but has now been released more
broadly, while two other brokers have downgraded Besi on the same concerns that hybrid bonding
adoption in HBM is running later than expected and that ASML could enter die-to-wafer hybrid bonding.
SemiAnalysis argues that ASML could leverage its precision alignment, motion, metrology and
contamination-control expertise to challenge Besi, initially in advanced logic at customers such as TSMC
and Intel, which would be strategically positive for ASML but a meaningful medium-term competitive risk
for Besi. However, no product has been officially announced and technical hurdles remain, so this is an
increasingly established market thesis rather than confirmation of a competitive shift. I think that is the
growing consensus is this thesis is right
• SEC(005930): Construction for SEC's second plant in Taylor will begin next month after Samsung
announced its expansion plans, to meet the growing demand for chips. The first Taylor fab is also said to
be on track, expected to be online by the end of this year. Separately, the Co has scaled back its Q4 mid-to-
low-end smartphone production plans due to supply shortages and price hikes in glass fabric for copper
clad laminate (CCL), a core raw material for PCBs. Compounded by surging memory chip prices, rising

component costs have severely squeezed margins, rendering higher production volumes unprofitable for
the vendor.
