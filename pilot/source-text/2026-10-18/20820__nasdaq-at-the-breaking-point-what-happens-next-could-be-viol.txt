Must hold
NDX is sitting at a critical crossroads after yesterday's violent reversal. A massive hammer candle right
at key trend support and the 100-day moving average makes this a must-hold zone for tech.
Meanwhile, semiconductor volumes have exploded, long/short books are getting crushed, and credit
stress continues to build.
With valuations back near the lows of the AI cycle and positioning increasingly washed out, the stakes
are high.
Hold these levels, and the squeeze could be powerful. Break them, and things could get ugly fast.

Risk
Privorotsky highlights just how brutal conditions have become for long/short strategies. Global
systematic L/S fell 1.4% yesterday, a nearly four-standard-deviation move.
The pain is no longer about net exposure. It is about gross. Violent short squeezes alongside equally
aggressive selling in longs point to widespread de-grossing.
He expects more choppiness but is increasingly optimistic that semis have reached levels where much
of the bad news is priced in. One potential relative-value trade: short hyperscalers versus semis.
Lower yields are providing some relief, but stubbornly high oil prices remain a headache.
The bottom line: AI demand remains strong, but financing and power constraints are not going away.
AI leverage/capex
Privorotsky points to some encouraging signs of stabilization, with the most leveraged parts of the AI
complex reversing sharply off yesterday’s lows. He suspects this may ultimately prove to have been a
local bottom, as credit-related fears had become overdone in the short term.

However, the structural problem remains. Too much capital is chasing too little investable, power-
ready compute capacity. The repricing process is far from over and could unfold gradually or through
violent dislocations, potentially spilling into broader markets.
The interesting contradiction? Spot compute pricing remains remarkably strong. B200s, H200s, H100s
and even ageing A100s continue to command attractive rental rates despite rapid technological
depreciation.
The takeaway: AI financing may be under pressure, but actual compute remains scarce. That scarcity
continues to favor those who already own the infrastructure.

Falling yields, falling tech
Contrary to popular belief, NDX and the US 10-year yield have moved in remarkably close tandem
since September. Tech and yields rising and falling together.
This suggests growth expectations and risk appetite are dominating the traditional discount-rate
narrative. Lower yields are not necessarily bullish for tech if they are falling for the wrong reasons.
The market seems more concerned about growth than rates.
