Bloomberg as of 10/16/26. Past performance is not indicative of future results.
Prime: US equities were net sold on the week (-0.9 SDs 1-year), driven by long-and-short sales in Single
Stocks which saw the largest net selling in 8 weeks. Macro Products (Index and ETF combined) were
marginally net sold (-0.1 SDs 1-year), driven by risk unwinds with long sales modestly outpacing short
covers. US-listed ETF shorts decreased -1.9% (-1.5% month/month), driven by covering in Small Cap and
Credit ETFs partially offset by shorting in Financials and Large Cap Equity ETFs. Single Stocks
collectively saw the largest net selling in 8 weeks (-1.2 SDs 1-year), driven by long-and-short sales (1.5 to
1). 8 of 11 sectors were net sold, led in $ terms by Info Tech, Consumer Disc, Industrials, and Financials,
while Health Care, Comm Svcs, and Staples were net bought, suggesting an incrementally defensive
posture. Info Tech, by far the worst performing US (and global) sector this week, was also the most net
sold in both $ and SD terms (-1.6 SDs 1-year), driven by long sales and to a much lesser extent short sales
(6 to 1). All subsectors were net sold on the week, led by Semis & Semi Equip, which made up 57% of the
$ net selling within Info Tech, and to a lesser extent Software and Electronic Equip, Instruments &
Components. Semis & Semi Equip stocks now make up 9.5% of total US net MV on the Prime book, in
the 39th/88th percentiles vs. the past 1-year/5-years.

Stock down three quarters in a row and down four of the last five quarters. Valuation has retraced off peak

industrials over the last month heading into the results.
