1) US Rates Lower on Hot Inflation Prints?
US Rates caught a proper bid yesterday and held overnight. Pretty impressive given the market has now
taken both a hot CPI and a hot PPI on the chin. There are some nuances in the PPI composition,
particularly energy, but the underlying print was robust. I think rates are finally beginning to reflect the
same AI capex growth shock that equities have spent the week repricing. If AI capex disappoints, growth
expectations fall and bonds rally. If spending meets expectations, growth remains robust and yields
probably go higher. A somewhat reductive framework, but an important shift in the market’s reaction
function.
AI Stocks: There is too much capital seeking a home relative to the available supply of investable,
energized compute. Repricing is a process the market will continue to go through, sometimes gradually,
sometimes acutely, with the potential to become systemic. There is an undeniable scarcity of available
compute, which is structurally bullish for those who already own it.
Hyperscalers: Perhaps the ultimate solution to the market’s funding concerns. Heading into earnings, I
expect robust numbers, and I wonder if the FCF inflection could be pulled forward materially, reducing
the need for incremental leverage. With relatively resilient equity valuations, they also retain considerable
financial flexibility. The interesting question is whether stronger earnings and cash generation can
ultimately restore confidence in the financing of the broader AI complex. (TY Rich Privo)
2) A Higher Cost of Capital for AI Credit
USD HY AI-related spreads widened by 25bp between October 13 and 14, reaching a new high of 360bp
over Treasuries (Exhibit 1). By contrast, non-AI-related spreads were only 2bp wider and remain below
their March peak and USD IG AI-related spreads are only 3bp wider.

We remain underweight USD IG Technology. In USD HY, we remain neutral, as we see scope for some
near-term reversal of the recent sell-off and valuations are more attractive, especially given the structural
nuances for project finance bonds. However, dispersion across AI-related issuers is likely to remain high.
USD IG and HY bond markets, project finance issuance has already surpassed $106 billion, which is
nearly three times the full-year 2025 level of $37 billion. For 2027, we are expecting $300 billion of
global project finance issuance across the IG and HY markets to fund data center and compute
investments. This is in addition to the $420 billion of direct issuance from the hyperscalers that we
forecast for next year.
3) Warsh Hints at Pausing in October
In comments at the IMF’s annual meetings, Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh did not specifically address the
FOMC’s upcoming October meeting but said that “in the intermeeting period, the world has broadly
conformed to our expectations [at the September meeting].” Alongside other recent comments from
FOMC participants, Warsh’s comments indicate that the FOMC is set to pause in October. We continue to
expect a second and final hike in December

Odds of an October hike dropped from ~30% to ~17% after the remarks (reminder that the Fed enters
blackout tomorrow).
