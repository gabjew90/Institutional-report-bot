It's been yet another fascinating 24 hours with CoreWeave 5yr CDS initially widening another 76bps 
intraday to 1194bps, its widest level yet, before turning around and ending -30bps tighter at 1086bps. 
This turn around was seemingly sparked by Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang coming to the AI computing 
company’s defense amid its sharp selloff in recent days. The comments likely contributed to a broad dip 
buying in Tech seen during the US trading session. The Philly Semi index was down over -4% in early 
trading before rebounding to closing down “just” -0.88% lower but still for its 7th consecutive loss. 10yr 
US Treasury yields were down -12.7bps due to the risk-off and yet 378 names in the S&P 500 were higher 
on the day and the index managed to end up with a +0.33% gain and stay within a percent of its record 
high. Even the NASDAQ itself pared back its losses, having hit an intraday low of -1.44% at the open, to 
scrape a slight +0.08% gain. The KOSPI is back up over 4% this morning as well.   
Drilling deeper into the continued AI volatility, CoreWeave's equity also recovered from being down -
9.54% at the lows to close up +5.94% yesterday. In terms of other comments from Nvidia CEO Huang, 
which turned around the session, he noted that “CoreWeave’s strength is improving every day”, before 
adding “I’m optimistic. Their cost of capital is actually decreasing.” While that is true given when the 
company issued its current debt, the market is pointing to a higher cost of capital now and increased 
refinancing costs in the future, so it's one to continue to watch. I explored some of these moves in more 
depth in my chart of the day yesterday (link here), which showed that for now at least, AI-linked financing 
costs aren’t excessive relative to the rest of the credit stack but there are outliers starting to emerge. 
Indeed, Oracle is the other main outlier and the stock was down -8.83% at the lows yesterday before 
recovering to close +2.02% higher. 
Overnight, Kevin Warsh has just spoken at the IMF conference and perhaps the most important thing 
was what he didn’t say. With the Fed about to enter its blackout ahead of the October 27-28 meeting, 
Warsh declined to push back against rate expectations, saying he doesn’t “prejudge meetings”, 
despite this week’s hotter inflation numbers. Instead, he was strikingly upbeat about the US economy, 

talking about a “massive investment surge” and the potentially huge productivity benefits from AI. Markets 
took the lack of hawkishness as good news, with October hike pricing dropping to around 15% from 
roughly 30% as he spoke, while S&P and Nasdaq futures are both +0.15% higher having been negative 
for most of the session before he spoke. Stoxx futures are +0.68% higher. 
A recovery in Asian equity markets had already been in full force before Warsh but has continued after. 
South Korea's chip-heavy KOSPI (+4.28%) is leading gains after tumbling nearly 8% yesterday, a move 
that pushed the index to its lowest close since April. Elsewhere, the Nikkei (+1.83%) is also trading firmly 
higher, recovering a portion of Thursday's steep losses as semiconductor stocks have regained ground. 
Chinese equities are likewise advancing, with the Hang Seng (+1.25%), CSI 300 (+0.75%) and the Shanghai 
Composite (+0.95%) all higher. In contrast, Indonesia's Jakarta Composite is plunging nearly -11.0% in its 
steepest one-day decline since January 1998 after MSCI proposed reclassifying the country from 
emerging-market to standalone-market status. 
Warsh's comments overnight actually followed another hawkish round of Fed speakers ahead of the 
upcoming Fed blackout period. Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco President Daly said that she had 
changed her view from earlier this year that the Fed should “look through” supply shocks, saying “Do 
the conventional pieces of wisdom — where our tool doesn’t really work for supply shocks and they 
dissipate on their own — does that really apply when the shocks are overlapping and they’re long-
lived?”  Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland President Hammack joined her in these concerns, adding that “at 
some point businesses start building in an expectation that more of these are going to come, and that prices 
are just going to keep going up.”    
However the theme of the day was a big global rate rally, as the prospect of slower AI growth pushed yields 
lower across the board. So, the 2yr yield (-5.9bps) fell to 4.74%, whilst the 10yr yield (-12.7bps) was down 
to 5.06%. And this was a global pattern as well, with yields on 10yr bunds (-5.3bps), OATs (-8.5bps) and 
BTPs (-6.7bps) all falling over the session.   
But even as bonds were rallying, a striking feature was it took place despite another batch of hawkish US 
data. So in other words, AI growth concerns continued to outweigh the inflation impulse when it came to 
yields. Most notably, there was a huge upside surprise in PPI inflation, with headline inflation up +1.3% 
in September (vs. +0.6% expected). In fact, it was the strongest monthly jump in producer prices since 
March 2022, shortly after Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. Moreover, the core measures were also stronger 
than expected, with the measure excluding food, energy and trade services up +0.5% on the month (vs. 
+0.3% expected). So as with the CPI, the release helped to fuel concerns that inflation was broadening out 
beyond energy. Taking into account the PPI and CPI components, our US econ team is projecting a 
+0.37% monthly increase for PCE. 
The hawkish data impulse was also clear in the labour market, as the weekly initial jobless claims also 
remained low in the week ending October 10, coming in at just 199k (vs. 200k expected). So that kept the 
4-week moving average broadly steady at 198.5k, only up slightly from its 198.25k level the previous 
week. In addition, the retail sales number was also stronger than expected, rising +0.8% in September (vs. 
+0.3% expected). So with the latest data, that left the Atlanta Fed’s GDPNow estimate at an annualised 
pace of +3.6% for Q3. 

Earlier in Europe, markets put in a relatively stronger performance, as they remained more insulated from the 
AI-related issues. So the STOXX 600 (+0.38%) actually put in a second consecutive gain, taking the index 
to a 3-week high. That included particularly strong gains for the FTSE 100 (+1.08%) and France’s CAC 40 
(+0.78%), although Italy’s FTSE MIB (-0.72%) was a relative underperformer. In the meantime, gilts saw a 
relative underperformance, with the 10yr yield only down -5.0bps on the day which came as the UK GDP 
release for August was stronger than expected. That showed GDP up +0.1% (vs. -0.1% expected), which 
meant the rolling 3-month growth rate was at +0.7% compared to the previous three months (vs. +0.5% 
expected).   
Looking at the day ahead, and it’s a quiet one ahead. However, we will get data on US industrial production 
and capacity utilization for September. In the Euro Area, there’s also the final CPI print for September.
