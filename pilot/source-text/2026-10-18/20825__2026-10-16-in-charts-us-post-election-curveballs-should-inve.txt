US (POST-)ELECTION CURVEBALLS: 
SHOULD INVESTORS WORRY?
Grace Fan
16 October 2026
Global Watch | In Charts

Should investors worry? Wall Street has, by and large, been mum on this issue, with markets poised to 
look through the noise so long as the duration of such risks is short. But in the event of an extended bout of 
post-election uncertainty, market volatility is likely, given the corollary knock-on effects linked to 
concurrent political developments (e.g., Iran war, two planned Trump-Xi meetings that could be delayed, 
a looming government shutdown if Congress fails to pass funding by 11 Dec) all before yearend.
Grey rhino or tail risk? Prediction markets give Democrats better than 60% odds of sweeping the Nov 
midterm elections, yet six out of 10 Americans say Trump won’t accept such election results. 
Key takeaways
We map not one but three asymmetric waves of electoral risks to monitor. From a procedural 
standpoint, the election does not end on Election Night, but extends from Election Week to Jan 3, 2027 
(or beyond) when the new Congress convenes. Potential curveballs for investors to monitor range from 
the by now routine (e.g., new ICE/FBI/National Guard deployments) to the maximalist by design (e.g., 
invocation of the Insurrection Act, seizure of voting machines/federal ballots, disruption of state 
certifications, a constitutional crisis). For markets, joined-up federal action is the key variable to track.
The bigger the blue wave, the less likely post-election disruptions will weigh on investor sentiment. 
Our base case, in line with consensus, is that Democrats will win control of at least one and more likely 
both houses of Congress. But with tight margins for key races and should the blue wave fizzle 
(remember, big polling misses dogged key primary races), election-related disruption risks would 
proportionally rise, adding to market volatility, even as the AI trade could still benefit.
For investors looking to hedge against tail risks, tactical long positions in gold remain the cleanest way to 
play the trade. Profit-taking also makes sense; and with spot VIX remaining relatively low despite the 
recent chip sell-off, expected higher equity volatility can still be played via long VIX futures as well.

Sending in the troops?
An unexpectedly large federal deployment of armed federal agents or troops, should it materialize, could temporarily rattle markets
•
Sending the National Guard, ICE and/or the US military 
to battleground districts around Election Day especially in large-scale 
deployments could unnerve markets, as US federal law (18 U.S.C. Secs. 592-595) 
prohibits federal officials from deploying armed federal agents (incl. ICE) to 
election sites, except to “repel armed enemies of the United States” – a high bar.
•
Steve Bannon, Trump’s former adviser and a MAGA influencer, has long called for 
Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) personnel to “surround the polls”. 
Dept. of Homeland Security (DHS) Sec. Markwayne Mullin dismissed such talk as 
“fearmongering” but noted targeted cases could occur, while Attorney General 
Todd Blanche has previously indicated sending ICE to polling places is ok by him.
•
‘Nuclear’ option: Declaring a state of emergency and invoking the Insurrection 
Act to actively deploy US military forces at or near polling sites to ‘secure 
elections’ is the biggest bazooka in the executive branch’s toolkit. In a Time 
magazine interview published 1 Oct 2026, the President said of the Insurrection 
Act: “I could use it”, after saying, “I don’t rule anything out or in. We’ll see.”
