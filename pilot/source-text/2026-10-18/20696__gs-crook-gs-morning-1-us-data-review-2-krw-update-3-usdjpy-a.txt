1) US ECONOMICS (MERICLE) – Warsh Commentary and PCE Estimation
Warsh Commentary: In comments at the IMF’s annual meetings, Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh did not
specifically address the FOMC’s upcoming October meeting but said that “in the intermeeting period, the
world has broadly conformed to our expectations [at the September meeting].” Alongside other recent
comments from FOMC participants, Warsh’s comments indicate that the FOMC is set to pause in October.
We continue to expect a second and final hike in December. Full note here.
PCE Estimation: Core producer prices increased more than expected in September, and the PPI
components relevant for PCE were on net slightly stronger than our expectations. Based on details in the
CPI and PPI reports, we estimate that the core PCE price index rose 0.34% in September (vs. our
expectation of 0.32% prior to today’s PPI report), corresponding to a year-over-year rate of +3.12%. Full
note here.
Retail Sales & Business Surveys: Core retail sales increased 0.1% in September, somewhat below
expectations. The core retail sales reading is consistent with our view that consumer spending growth is
set to slow in 2026Q4, as the boost to consumer cashflow from larger-than-usual tax refunds that
supported spending earlier this year fades and the headwind from higher energy prices remains. The signal
from the business surveys was mixed. The Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index decreased by 16.9pt to
20.9 in October, below expectations for a smaller decline, while the Empire manufacturing index
increased by 17.9pt to 25.5, well above expectations and its highest level since December 2021. Taken
together, the broader signal from the business surveys continues to point to a healthy pace of economic
activity but no sign of a surge to a far above-trend pace. Full note here.
2) EM MARKET STRATS (SUN LU) – Updated Views on KRW
More Bullish on KRW FX: Large trade surplus and high repatriation should keep USDKRW heavy, but
BOK may accumulate reserves at 1300, making it hard for KRW to rally further. However, BOK governor
Shin’s recent FX comments indicate authorities may be open to appreciation below 1300. This week, Shin
mentioned KRW at 1330 level remains weak vs history, and that further KRW appreciation is desirable
given the boost to household purchase power. This means authorities may not be drawing a line in the sand

near recent levels. Earlier measures to allow NPS unwind hedges may be intended to control speed of
KRW appreciation mainly. This means KRW appreciation below 1300 is likely in the medium term.
Flow Math Update: With now over US$40bn trade surplus per month, assuming a 75% conversion ratio,
this translates into US$30bn/month exporter USD selling. FDI+NPS outbound investment is ~US$7bn per
month. Retail equity outflow is now close to US$3bn/month. For NPS buy back hedges we assume
US$5bn per month. Unhedged WGBI inflow US$2.5bn per month till Nov. This gives us US$17.5bn
surplus per month without considering foreign equity flow impact. Foreign equity outflow since Jul
averaged US$13bn/month. Considering the steady state of foreign equity outflows, Korea still runs
~US$4bn of surplus per month.
KOSPI–USDKRW Correlation: We think the positive correlation weakens mainly due to FX hedging of
offshore single-name leveraged ETF is a lot lower than earlier, given much lower marketcap of these
ETFs. However, we do expect some RHS hedge unwind as KOSPI goes lower. With US$1.8tn foreign
ownership of Korea equities and 10% average FX hedging ratio, this means each 5% drop in KOSPI may
lead to RHS hedge unwind of US$1800×10%×5%=US$9bn. Such potential RHS hedge unwinds on
KOSPI drop is likely to partially offset the equity outflow (US$13bn month-to-date). We think KRW is
likely to continue to outperform TWD in tech selloff scenario.

3) G10 FX OPTIONS TRADING (PRANEET SHAH) – USDJPY Downside as a Growth Hedge
Buy 2m USDJPY 150 Digital Put – 9.55% USD offer
Buy 6m USDJPY 144 Digital Put – 9.60 % USD offer
Spot ref 157.95. Max Loss = Prem Spent
What initially started off as a sell-off in yields has morphed into a bull flattener. A signal that markets are
now pricing a growth/financing scare rather than just sticky inflation.
The cost of financing the AI buildout is starting to collide with how much the market can comfortably
absorb. We're seeing this across three different channels simultaneously:
/ Credit: Wider spreads across the AI stack,

/ Equities: Nasdaq (AI CapEx demand — hyperscalers) and KOSPI (AI CapEx supply — memory/semis)
selling off together, as pressure on hyperscaler spreads hits both ends,
/ Rates: Today's bull flattening is consistent with the market pricing growth/credit concerns rather than just
persistent inflation.
The FX moves post CPI are also telling… the currencies most exposed to AI (USD, KRW, TWD) are all
underperforming, whilst funding currencies (EUR, CHF, JPY) are all mostly outperforming. Rather than
'classic risk off', this looks more like a Terms of Trade repricing as currencies tied to AI capex financing
and supply chains are being sold together, regardless of their usual risk-off/risk-on betas.
To help frame this, it's worth also dusting off the 2022 concept of R** — the level of rates above which
financial stability/credit concerns start to bite.
Given the scale of leverage now embedded in the AI CapEx ecosystem, we may be approaching a level of
yields (R**) where that ecosystem becomes materially more rates-sensitive than it was even six months
ago.
We think the "equities lower / yields lower" tail of the distribution is under-hedged by clients – implied
correlation before this week was marked at ~0%. In hybrids space, these structures have remarked
significantly, making it much more expensive hedge. Instead, to hedge underpriced growth risk premia we
think FX vol looks very attractive given that it typically outperforms rates vol when recession/credit risks
build.
Our pick to hedge this scenario: USDJPY vol. If these moves extend, JPY's role as a funding/haven
currency, combined with USD's AI sensitivity, could make it an efficient way to express the hedge.
Additionally, Japan holds a lot of tech-sensitive US assets, raising the potential for asymmetric moves
should the repatriation story pick back up.

4) FX STRATEGY (KANTER) – Implications of Relative Equity Returns for the USD
In light of the recent AI selloff, we flag two pieces published by Lexi Kanter (FX Strategy) earlier in the
year, which analyse the implications of the AI boom for the USD. Takeaways below; see the full pieces
here and here.
Relative Equity Performance Matters: It is the relative backdrop that is key for Dollar performance and its
relationship with US equities. The “Deepseek” episode represented a potential shift in the global
beneficiaries of AI from the US to the rest of the world and, in turn, had clear negative implications for
future relative asset performance, growth prospects, and ultimately for the Dollar. However, the software
selloff in February was less clearly a US-specific negative shock, and hence did not trigger a “Dollar
down, equities down” episode. While this year’s AI-driven outperformance, particularly vs other DM
markets, may overstate the extent of US outperformance and demand for Dollar assets, the broader equity
impulse has clearly shifted from an expected drag to a support for the Dollar
Equity Market Breadth Limits FX Spillovers: The concentration of the recent market rally has compressed
US equity market breadth to one of its tightest levels in recent decades. We find that more concentrated
market rallies tend to coincide with larger Dollar underperformance relative to what equity performance
would imply (see Chart 2).
Overarching Importance of Macro Drivers: Earnings expectations are only a part of the story. Generally,
we expect other macro drivers, including rate differentials and terms of trade, to matter more. This was

clearly the case in the 2011-2014 period, when dovish Fed policy weighed on the Dollar despite
supportive relative equity performance.

5) EM STRATEGY (KOUL) – Brazil Equities: Benchmarking the Election Rally; Scope for Further Gains
if Policy Delivers
The Rally So Far: Brazil’s first-round election has raised hopes of a more market-friendly policy outcome,
driving sharp moves in Brazilian assets. Since the October 4 vote, the Bovespa index has gained c.8% and
MSCI Brazil (EWZ) is up nearly 15% in USD terms. Our cyclical macro model attributes c.85% of the
Bovespa rally since the election to lower long-end yields. The 15–25% gains in small caps and rate-
sensitive stocks have eroded their undervaluation that had built up ahead of the election, but the moves
remain consistent with their historical rate betas given the c.150bp decline in long-end rates.
Valuations at Mid-Cycle Levels: Despite the recent market rally, equity valuations in Brazil have risen
from inexpensive to mid-cycle levels. Domestic equity valuations (MSCI Brazil ex-Commodities) traded
at -0.8 s.d. below 10-year averages prior to the first round of elections and are now mostly in-line with the
10-year historical average of 12x. The Bovespa local index overall also trades at 9.6x NTM P/E, in-line
with our macro model implied fair valuation levels (9.5x). While foreign flows have picked up in recent
weeks – with the election week alone seeing over $2bn foreign net buying - overall flows remain low
relative to the trend in prior election cycles and could improve as election uncertainty continues to fade.
Price Target & Upside Risks: We keep our Bovespa target unchanged for now at 215,000 driven by
30%/5% EPS growth forecasts in 2026/27 and a target P/E of 9.5x, but note upside valuation risks from

any fiscal policy announcements over coming months, that could drive a further relief in rates and PE re-
rating in equities - akin to the 2018 election episode where the Bovespa rose c.15% over six months and
small caps/rate-sensitive stocks gained c.30–40%. As an example, if 10y local rates decline 100-120 bp
further bringing the cumulative change since first round election to 290bp similar to the 2018 election
episode, Bovespa could re-rate to 10.1x PE, reaching 225,000 levels.
