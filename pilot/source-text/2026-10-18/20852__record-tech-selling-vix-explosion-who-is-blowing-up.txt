The NDX puke
NDX has sold off sharply, closing below its 100-day moving average yesterday. Zooming out, however,
the index is still trading within the range that has been in place since May, and we haven't even
reached the lower end of that range yet.
Note that the longer-term trendline and the 200-day moving average sit slightly lower, providing
another potential area of support. Meanwhile, RSI is approaching extremely oversold levels.
Will this turn out to be yet another frustrating positioning washout near range lows?

Size selling
The biggest percentage net selling in Info Tech since GS records began in 2016. That's what we just
witnessed.
Tech was by far the most aggressively sold global sector this week, with the largest percentage
reduction in gross exposure in two months. Long liquidations drove most of the selling, with additional
pressure from short sales.

The VIX panic
Aggressive buying in VIX futures sent the VIX to its highest level since July, while VVIX surged 17 points,
one of its largest single-day moves in four years. Front-month VIX futures outperformed their implied
sensitivity to SPX by 1.5 volatility points, the biggest divergence since April according to GS.

Vol momos
Very few people actually understand trading volatility. The boring, resilient equity market heading into
Friday's carnage had attracted plenty of investors selling volatility. Why? Because it worked. Until it
didn't.
The problem with selling vol for "income" is that many have little understanding of what happens when
volatility suddenly explodes, as we saw on Friday.
Adding fuel to Friday's volatility explosion, asset managers entered the session with their largest net
short position in VIX futures on record, according to GS.
GS
Panic, what?
SPX is barely off its all-time highs, yet we've seen outright panic in volatility markets. The index has
only pulled back to its 100-day moving average and remains comfortably within the broader trading
range. The longer-term trendline, 200-day moving average and range lows all sit roughly 250 points
lower.
As we outlined yesterday, this has all the hallmarks of somebody blowing up. The resulting stress
appears to have spilled into volatility markets, triggering increasingly outsized moves.
All this panic, and SPX hasn't even tested the bottom of its range.
