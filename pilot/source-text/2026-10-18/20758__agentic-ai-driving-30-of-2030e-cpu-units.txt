We share our updated thoughts on agentic AI tailwinds post META's Muse and OAI's Dots
introduction. Key points: 1) Agentic AI adoption accelerating with Muse, reaching >5M
users in 22 days (Ex-1), driving strong demand for Agentic Server CPUs, 2) OAI debuted
Dots (agentic AI assistants) for deep integration of daily enterprise/work processes, 3)
we estimate Agentic CPU TAM growing to $80B by 2030E, 123% 4Y CAGR, with units
reaching ~30% of server CPUs, and 3) key beneficiaries ARM with AGI CPU, AMD (seeing
~$220B CPU TAM by 2030E), and NVDA CPU revs ~$20B and 2x in 2027E. Raise PTs for
AMD (OP) to $705 from $580, SNDK (OP) to $2,050, and DELL (OP) to $650, INTC (N)
to $114 from $92, and SMCI (N) to $43 from $35. We see ARM, AMD, INTC, and NVDA
benefiting strongly, with MU/SNDK seeing agentic AI memory demand.
Key Points
Agentic AI driving CPU TAM to >$200B by 2030E as agents drive the next phase of
AI adoption. We see agentic workload penetration accelerating with user adoption and
tasks per user growing rapidly as we believe: a) expanding agentic capability with tool
calls, coding, multi-app processes, b) agent intelligence improving rapidly and can run
24/7, c) multi-agent workflows add a strong scaling factor. We took a bottom-up look
(Ex2-5) at agentic CPU demand, and see penetration growing from ~4% of CPU demand
in 2026E to ~30% by 2030E. We estimate the CPU TAM growing from ~$68B in 2026E to
~$209B by 2030E (32% 4Y CAGR) with agentic CPU reaching ~$80B (123% 4Y CAGR). We
believe NVDA standalone CPU revs estimated at $20B in 2026E and could grow >100%
y/y in 2027E and ARM a key LT beneficiary with its AGI CPU potentially gaining share
with 2027E ramps while royalty growth remains strong from Vera/Graviton/Axion/Cobalt
deployments.
Close Agentic AI integration with Muse and Dots assistants as the key AI layer. META
(covered by Lloyd Walmsley, Outperform, ) introduced Muse and OAI debuted Dots, its
Agentic AI assistants, with the aim of being integrated into daily consumer/enterprise
workflows, with deep 3rd party SW (incl. ~4,000 apps). We see AI assistant agents playing
into the strong tailwinds, as we see: a) agent ecosystems and close SW stack integration
potentially limiting migration between providers, b) CSPs competing to bring versatile
LLM capabilities, and c) headwinds to scheduling, travel, organization SW.
Reiterating ratings and raising PTs for key Agentic AI-exposed players. AMD - reiterate
Outperform, raise PT to $705 from $580, while reiterating Neutral for INTC and raising

AI by 2030E.

Agentic Potentially Driving ~30% of CPU Demand by 2030E
We took a bottom-up projection of the Agentic AI CPU shipment forecast where agentic-enabled
MAU (monthly active users) grows at a 51% 4Y CAGR, reaching ~4.2T by 2030E and agentic tasks/
user/day reaches 21.5K by 2030E (86% CAGR), with more agents, tool calls, code runs, checks, and
advanced multi-app tasks, often running in the background 24/7. Our key driving assumptions are:
1) agents become the default interaction layer by users with other platforms and environments,
2) agents are reliable and smart enough to run, complete, and improve tasks on its own 24/7 for
a majority of workflows, and 3) multiple agents can be invoked by the user to run multiple tasks,
or to drive faster improvement on a singular task. We estimate Agentic AI CPU deployments will
grow at 92% 4Y CAGR to 14.6M by 2030E, up from 1.1M in 2026E. We categorized Server CPUs
by agentic, head-node, and traditional and note some overlap between the head-node CPUs and
agentic only CPUs may exist (we used 5-25% of idle capacity for head-node CPUs).

CPU TAM Accelerating with Agentic/Head-Node Tailwinds and Driving Memory Demand
We split the Server CPU TAM by three segments: agentic, head-node, and traditional. We estimate
agentic CPUs reaching ~$80B by 2030E (~123% 4Y CAGR), head-node CPUs to $68B (~61% 4Y
CAGR), and traditional CPUs to $60B (~2% 4Y CAGR). Total Server CPU TAM we estimate reaches
~$209B by 2030E (32% 4Y CAGR), and conservatively below AMD's target of >$220B. Our bottom
up projection sees Agentic AI driving CPU, DRAM, and NAND growing to 22-30% of their respective
total markets (number of bits shipped). Our model estimates Agentic AI CPU demand as a % of
total server CPUs grows from ~4% today to ~30% by 2030E. Our blended ASP (agentic, head-node,
traditional) for Server CPUs is expected to grow from ~$3K/unit in 2026E to >$5K/unit in 2030E.

Agentic CPU Landscape - AMD, NVDA, and ARM as the Performance Leaders
Near term, we see four leading edge CPUs, targeting growing Agentic AI workloads: 1) ARM's
AGI CPU (136 cores, 272MB cache, 6TB DDR5, 300W) potentially offering the best cores/watt
of the group as ARM's first HW product, but with supply potentially limited by volume ramps, 2)
AMD Venice (256 cores, 1,024MB cache, 4TB DDR5, 600W for Zen6c compute dense), with cache-
capacity ~2x vs Turin, but the highest TDP of 600W, 3) NVDA Vera (88 cores, 1.2TB/s via LPDDR5X
SOCAMM, 450W), which sacrifices core count for per-core bandwidth and NVLink-C2C integration,
but potentially could be best suited for small numbers of latency-sensitive agents rather than
hundreds of slower agents running in parallel, and 4) INTC's Diamond Rapids (256 cores, 1.28GB
cache, 1.6TB/s w/MRDIMM), which potentially leads on raw core count and cache, as we believe
it could be launching in 2027E with potential delays and more performance metrics undisclosed.
Broadly, we see ARM gaining CPU share at the cost of x86 with ramping ARM-based NVDA Vera
and AGI CPU potentially ~doubling from by 2030E, while it also sees tailwinds from in-house CPUs
at major CSPs also ramping.

Token Demand Accelerating >4x y/y with Pricing Normalizing, as Agentic AI Potentially Needs
5-30x More Tokens/Task

windows. We note token pricing is moderating (Ex-9), being down >50% since mid-2026E as we

note a stronger push towards improving task efficiency with CSPs implementing prompt caching,
routing to smaller LLMs, and latent-reasoning architecture to helps answer the token demand
surge and drive more efficient usage with higher TCO.

Price Target Calculation and Key Risks
Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD)
Investment Thesis
We see AMD well-positioned as a second-place AI accelerator competitor after enjoying continued strength in DC compute with share
gains as it ramps its Zen 5 Turin with missteps from its competitor. We believe AI accelerator demand remains strong as MI350-series
ramps with MI400X on track 2026E, and beyond driving into the ~$1T TAM by 2030E (>40% 24-28E CAGR), currently dominated by
NVDA, with upside from an estimated ~MSD% market share. 2026E could see continued strong roadmap execution with Zen 6 Medusa
and Venice.
Base Case/PT
With AMD gaining server share, a potential PC rebound and upcoming AI product launches, we see a base case PT of $705, ~41x our
F27E EPS.
Bull Case/PT
If AMD can win significant server/DC market and capitalize on growth in AI, AMD could drive EPS higher with accretive GMs and better
than expected Instinct ramps, implying a bull case PT of $900, trading at ~47x our F27E upside EPS.
Bear Case/PT
If AMD is unable to maintain current roadmap execution momentum and loses server market share, AMD could see growth decline,
leading to a downside ~34x our downside F27E EPS implying a bear case PT of $550.
Risks
AMD could see challenges with executing on its AI roadmap into 2026/27E.
The PC market could continue to be slow into 2026E.
We would also note AMD remains a distant second supplier to NVDA in the AI GPU race, with key software stack ROCm trailing CUDA.
Arm Holdings plc (ARM)
Investment Thesis
ARM is a global leader in semiconductor intellectual property (IP), powering >99% of the world’s smartphones and has shipped 300B+
Arm-based chips to date. Arm leverages a broad IP platform across client (smartphone, PC, XR and wearables), infrastructure (servers,
cloud, networking, and 5G), automotive, and Internet-of-Things (IoT) end-markets to bring energy-efficient central processing units
(CPU), graphics processing units (GPU), and system IP to its customers. We see ARM well positioned as the company should see
increasing royalty revenues driven by 2-3x core counts gen/gen, increasing v9 penetration (~5% rate), 2-3x vs v8, CSS at 8-10%+ rates.
ARM is expanding its TAM 10x+ with AGI CPU and potential AI ASIC products longer-term. We see strong growth opportunities in AI
with CSPs AMZN/META/MSFT/GOOGL expanding Capex while ARM dominates efficient edge compute in Auto/Client, IoT.
Base Case/PT
We see ARM as a global leader in semiconductor IP, and increasing opportunities in DC, client and automotive to drive better units/
content. We rate ARM Outperform with a $400 PT, ~2.5x F28E PEG.
Bull Case/PT
ARM could see stronger than anticipated demand and share gains driven by AI ramps, improved IoT, and faster transitions to v9. In our
Bull case scenario, we see Arm share prices rising to $700, at ~4.4x our F28E PEG.
Bear Case/PT
If ARM sees slower v9 adoption, or is impacted by geopolitical headwinds with Arm China, we could see weaker earnings growth and
softer ~0.9x F28E PEG, resulting in a Bear case scenario of $150.
Risks
ARM could see competition from emerging open-sourced architectures such as RISC-V, but could also see impacts from slower AI/
handset markets as well as challenges sustaining higher royalty rates. We would also note that ARM’s current revenue mix is highly tied
to the handset market, where it has ~100% market share, while its top 5 customers make up >50% of its revenues and sees investor

Cerebras Systems Inc. (CBRS)
Investment Thesis
CBRS is a differentiated AI enabler and Fast Inference leader. It has established a technology moat via its wafer scale engine (WSE)
which embeds massive SRAM on chip, enabling up to 15x faster AI inference vs leading edge GPUs. Cerebras focuses on the fastest
growing subsegment of the AI TAM in AI Inference (53% CAGR to $2T by 2030E), which we believe positions CBRS for significant upside.
It has an expanding pipeline of customer engagements, anchored by key partnerships with OpenAI and AWS, with potential additional
customers driving future upside into 2027/28E. CBRS is executing on its multi-generation roadmap from CS-3 to CS-5 and beyond, which
we expect could continue to advance its leadership in fast inference. We believe Agentic AI proliferation with >1000x token generation
(vs. reasoning-only tokens) could widen CBRS's advantage enabling faster LLM response times.
Base Case/PT
Our base case assumes CBRS executes on its roadmap and gains share as the AI Inference TAM grows fastest and customer ramps
expand. We rate CBRS at Outperform, with a PT of $300, ~12x F28E P/S.
Bull Case/PT
If CBRS is able to penetrate meaningfully into hyperscalers and see adoption accelerate ahead of expectations, while also being able
to secure the power capacity required, revenue could inflect significantly higher, and driving our bull case PT to $465, or an ~14x bull-
case F28E P/S.
Bear Case/PT
Significant slow down in adoption for CBRS's product and a collapse in AI infrastructure spend could lead to a bear PT of $165, an
~7x bear-case F28E P/S.
Risks
• Technology Roadmap – CBRS must continuously execute on its roadmap to maintain and widen its WSE technology moat.
• Customer concentration – OpenAI is a key customer and growing to >80% of revenue.
• Cloud revenue and execution - Cloud revenue is growing to >80% of revenue and will require significant Capex and timely execution
throughout the AI Datacenter supply chain.
• Geopolitical Risks – while we believe now de-risked, the Middle-East entities G42 (UAE) and MBZUAI accounted for a significant portion
of 2025 revenue (~86%), but we note mix decreasing to <40% in F26E and <5% in F27E+ as OpenAI ramps.
Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL)
Investment Thesis
We see DELL as a growing player in the AI server market, with broad exposure to Enterprise/Sovereign/CSP AI demand and with a
growing pipeline (growing q/q, and we believe still multiples of its current backlog of $95B). We see DELL benefiting as Blackwell ramps
DLC and Air-cooled servers with strength across enterprise, sovereign and CSP customers. We do see near-term headwinds from lower
initial AI server OMs, but we highlight offsets from traditional compute server strength and its strong storage margins. We see potential
tailwinds from DELL with increasing storage needs tied to AI server cluster ramps, as well as benefit from an expected corporate PC
refresh, which could lead to higher adoption of AI PCs as refresh cycles have elongated to 3-4 years post-Covid vs. historical 2-3 years.
However, we note increasing memory costs could weigh on near-term unit sales, but we think strong execution and corporate PC focus
could help DELL take share. We believe DELL has negative WC due to strong supplier relationships and a strong balance sheet, which
helps drive strong FCF to support its growth into the AI server market.
Base Case/PT
With a strong compute server market share, growing AI server share, and a pending corporate PC refresh, we rate DELL Outperform
with a $650PT, ~18x our F28E EPS.
Bull Case/PT
If DELL can build off its strong AI server backlog and capture more of its continuously growing pipeline, as well as see strong ramps
of AI PCs and corporate PC orders, we believe DELL's could trade at a ~22x P/E on our upside F28E EPS of $38.79, indicating a bull
case PT of $850.

Intel Corporation (INTC)

We see Sandisk as a leading player in the NAND flash market with strong positioning across key end-markets, including Consumer,

centers, including edge devices such as smartphones and PCs, is driving strong demand for Sandisk's products, driving mid-to-high
teens CAGR in NAND bit shipments. We also see tight wafer supply supporting NAND ASPs through C2027E. We believe Sandisk
attractively positioned to capitalize on secular growth in data storage given its strong portfolio of current and next-gen NAND flash
memory products.
Base Case/PT

products; 3) a slower-than-expected rollout or failure to commercialize next-gen memory products by SanDisk which allows competitors
to take share from the company; and 4) increasing competition from domestic suppliers in the Chinese market potentially leading to
share loss in a key region.
