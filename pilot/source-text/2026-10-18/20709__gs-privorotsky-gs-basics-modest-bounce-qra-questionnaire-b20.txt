GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Modest Bounce. QRA Questionnaire.
B200 Pricing Near Highs 16 Oct 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
16 Oct 2026
Asia: Finally a bounce, although feels a little lackluster relative to the scale of the selloff. KOSPI +2-3%
after losing nearly 20% over seven sessions, with Samsung and Hynix recovering 1–3% after yesterday’s
9–11% declines. Japan firmer, Kioxia +3-4%, Tokyo Electron and Advantest +1-2%. TSMC closed near
the lows of the session (ADR had already taken a much bigger hit). The memory complex is finding some
relief, but after the scale of the liquidation this feels a bit subdued.
Rates: Caught a proper bid yesterday and held overnight. Pretty impressive given the market has now
taken both a hot CPI and a hot PPI on the chin. There are some nuances in the PPI composition,
particularly energy, but the underlying print was robust. I think rates are finally beginning to reflect the
same AI capex growth shock that equities have spent the week repricing. If AI capex disappoints, growth
expectations fall and bonds rally. If spending meets expectations, growth remains robust and yields
probably go higher. A somewhat reductive framework, but an important shift in the market’s reaction
function.
QRA: Today’s Treasury primary dealer questionnaire (5pm London) could signal a reduction in long end
issuance ahead of the 4 November refunding. Some on the Street expect Treasury to cut both 20Y and 30Y
auction sizes by $3bn each, replacing supply with T-bills, with speculation that the 20Y could eventually
be eliminated altogether. Explicit questions about reducing long-bond supply would be bullish for duration
and potentially curve flattening; no mention would likely disappoint. This is a consultation, not an
issuance decision.
AI Leverage/Capex: Some signs of stability yesterday, with the most leveraged corner of the complex
reversing sharply off the lows. I suspect in hindsight we may find this was a local low, with some of the
credit driven concerns becoming locally overblown. That said, the structural math is right. There is too
much capital seeking a home relative to the available supply of investable, energized compute. Repricing
is a process the market will continue to go through, sometimes gradually, sometimes acutely, with the
potential to become systemic. The interesting contradiction is that spot compute pricing remains incredibly
strong. B200s (see below), H200s, H100s, even ageing A100s continue to command meaningful rental
rates. Compute that should theoretically be depreciating rapidly remains valuable. There is an undeniable
scarcity of available compute, which is structurally bullish for those who already own it.
Hyperscalers: Perhaps the ultimate solution to the market’s funding concerns. Initially sold on FCF
worries and the transfer of economics to memory producers and the broader capex supply chain, yet the
scarcity of energized compute has only intensified. Heading into earnings, I expect robust numbers, and I
wonder if the FCF inflection could be pulled forward materially, reducing the need for incremental
leverage. With relatively resilient equity valuations, they also retain considerable financial flexibility. The

interesting question is whether stronger earnings and cash generation can ultimately restore confidence in
the financing of the broader AI complex.
Risk: It’s been a difficult period for L/S books. Yesterday’s global systematic L/S print was down -1.4%,
nearly a -4 sd move. Real tough sledding. What was particularly notable yesterday was that it was no
longer about net exposure. It was gross. Multiple Sd squeezes in shorts alongside equally violent declines
in longs, including through the European session. All indicative of aggressive de-grossing. Feels like it
will remain choppy, although I’m increasingly optimistic we’ve found a level where much of the concern
is priced into semis. I do wonder whether selling hyperscalers versus semis becomes the relative trade
here. For everything else, rates are objectively lower, which is a good thing. Suspect much of the move is
technical and positioning driven, but will take the W. The problem remains oil, which is stubbornly high.
With upcoming political events in the US, geopolitical risk is increasingly difficult to ignore. Think the
demand picture remains excellent for AI spending but the bottlenecks of financing and electrification
remain real. Watching QRA questionnaire today and US HY spreads which found their feet in yday's
session.
