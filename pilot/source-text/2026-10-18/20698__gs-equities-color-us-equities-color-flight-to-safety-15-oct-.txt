Volatile session (despite SPX finishing green / NDX nearly flat on the day) that started with an early
Tech/AI sell-off following a tough overnight session in Korea -7%. Our US HT desk saw a notable uptick
in Long Only activity with an early wave of supply in Semis around the open (this slowed as the session
wore on) and a clear 'flight to safety' bid in pockets of Mag7, Staples, MedTech & Tools, and Financials.
The mid-day drift higher felt like an abatement of supply in tech + a continued broadening out with RTY >
SPX > NDX. We did NOT see any meaningful defense in AI / peripheral pockets. Thoughtful take by
Privo this AM which felt front and center during today’s session: In hindsight, the pain trade was long AI/
short bonds. Now we’re watching both sides unwind. Credit concerns have infiltrated the AI spending
equilibrium, and the market increasingly seems to be interpreting this as a growth shock.
Our floor was an 8/10 during the AM session, with LOs starting out -40% better for sale. Activity levels
stabilized into the afternoon, settling at a 6/10 with LOs finishing -25% better for sale driven by supply in
tech vs demand in financials and healthcare. HFs were more benign on the day, finishing -3% better for
sale driven by small supply in tech, HC, and consumer discretionary.
Callahan said well: Risk appetite = not great with increased signs of risk off price action and flows as
investors balance a number of moving parts right now as we sit in a compressed calendar window into
year-end. Unlike the momentum unwind in July, which felt mostly ‘technical’ in nature after the huge run
in April-June, the current episode has created some more debates on which 'wall of worry' is most ‘acute’
for investors to navigate into year end -- earnings, elections, rates/credit, ROIC, inflation, et al? On the
positive side, the fwd P/E of the NDX is back to ~21x – the lows since the AI cycle kicked off in 2023 –
perhaps a supportive backdrop into earning season for the “E” to do some work?
These moves really speak to positioning right now across the market. Our GS sentiment indicator has been
negative for 6 straight weeks and last reading was its lowest at -1.3 (as of Friday). Aka overall length is
not max long. The AI trade is still long but now a 6 out 10 in terms of length and much cleaner than it was
in Jun/July. Yesterday was the 2nd largest net selling in the past year in our Global AI basket
(GSXGGLAI). Two points on AI length from PB:
1) Net allocation to Global AI stocks (as % of total global single stock net MV) now stands at 26.1% (vs.
peak level of 31.7% in early June), in the 49th percentile vs. the past year and 83rd percentile vs. the past

three years.
2) Aggregate long/short ratio (MV) across Global AI stocks now stands at 3.27 (vs. peak level of 4.34 in
early June), in the 7th percentile vs. the past year and 64th percentile vs. the past three years.
DERIVS: Squeezy tape as the major indices swung back off their intraday lows. In the vol space, skew
opened the session offered and continued to get smashed as the day progressed and spot stabilized. Fixed
strike vols trickled lower on the rally back with gamma tenors offered most aggressively. On the spot
outperformance, RUT vol caught a bid in the belly and back-end of the curve. Flows wise, we saw a tilt to
sellers of skew following yesterday's heavy bid. In the singles/thematic space, we saw clients come in to
monetize hedges and defend positioning while rolling down strikes to adapt to the moves in spot -
particularly in MAG7. NVDA gs cust bought 70,000 Nov 230 260 call spread and sold 70,000 Nov 245
295 call spread neutral. Worth noting, the squeezy activity was well evident with our High Beta 12m
Losers (GSCBLMOM) and Liquid Most Short (GSXUMSAL) baskets at the top of the leaderboard today.
