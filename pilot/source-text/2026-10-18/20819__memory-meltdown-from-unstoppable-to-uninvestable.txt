Memory meltdown
Let's recap the memory meltdown that has been unfolding over the past several sessions.
What was one of the hottest trades in the market has turned into a bloodbath. Memory stocks are
getting crushed, hedge funds are aggressively unwinding AI exposure, and fears of Chinese
competition are adding fuel to the fire.
Yet beneath the selling, an interesting divergence is emerging. Memory prices continue to rise even as
equities collapse, while bullish positioning has been aggressively flushed out.
Is the market anticipating a fundamental downturn in the memory cycle, or has the selling simply gone
too far?
Here are the charts behind one of the most violent unwinds in the AI trade.
Memory massacre
The memory trade has gone from unstoppable to uninvestable in a matter of days.
Goldman's memory basket surged from around 600 to a June peak of 2,675, before reversing sharply
toward 1,900. That's almost 30% off the highs. Samsung and SK Hynix have been hit even harder, with
the two Korean giants down roughly 22% and 26%, respectively, over just five sessions.
ZH
Everybody wants out
Foreign investors are dumping Korean tech at an extraordinary pace.
According to Goldman, foreign selling has reached $11.6 billion over eight consecutive sessions. SK
Hynix has endured 15 straight days of foreign selling, while Samsung has seen five consecutive days of
outflows.
The selling has become increasingly mechanical, with hedge funds cutting exposure and foreign
investors rushing for the exits.
Interestingly, domestic institutions and retail investors have started absorbing some of that supply. The
question is how much more they can take.
GS

The great AI unwind
This is no longer just a Korean memory story.
Goldman reports that hedge funds have been net sellers of global AI stocks for five consecutive
sessions, taking net allocation down to 26.1% from a June peak of 31.7%.
Meanwhile, the aggregate long/short ratio has collapsed from 4.34 to 3.27, putting it in the seventh
percentile over the past year.
A remarkable amount of bullish positioning has already been flushed out. The obvious question is who
is left to sell.
GS
Stocks down, prices up
Here's the fascinating divergence.
While memory equities have been hammered, DDR5 spot prices have continued moving higher. In
other words, the stock market is pricing a deteriorating memory cycle that has yet to materialize in
spot pricing.
Goldman notes that Samsung trades at approximately 1.2x book value, while SK Hynix is around 0.9x,
suggesting investors are already discounting a significant downturn by 2028.
The gap between the physical memory market and the equity market is becoming increasingly difficult
to ignore.
GS
The China scare
China's CXMT has emerged as the latest nightmare for memory investors, with its 4F² technology
announcement raising fears of another supply shock.
But the distinction between commodity DRAM and high-bandwidth memory matters.
CXMT is making genuine progress in conventional DRAM, with its share of global DRAM revenue
reaching approximately 10% in Q2. However, commercial qualification of the new technology remains
uncertain, while the company continues to face challenges with HBM yields.
The competitive threat is real, but the market may be treating very different memory businesses as
one trade.

The next big squeeze?
After the carnage, the technical picture is starting to look interesting.
The SOX has puked, with the RSI trading at 34 and the index testing the lower end of its trading range.
Meanwhile, Goldman's memory basket has crashed, selling in Korean tech has become increasingly
extreme (not impressed by the mini bounce overnight), and CRWV is holding on for dear life.
None of this guarantees a bottom. But with positioning heavily reduced, valuations compressed and
the physical memory market still holding up, the ingredients for a violent countertrend squeeze are
starting to emerge.
