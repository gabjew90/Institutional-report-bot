Weekly Market Guide
We are maintaining an upward bias on equities as we enter a seasonally strong period for stocks, which, combined with
improving fundamentals, supports our constructive outlook into year-end.
●
Beneath the surface, market internals have weakened. Despite the S&P 500 trading near all-time highs, strength in mega-
cap stocks has masked weakness in the average constituent. In fact, approximately 74% of S&P 500 constituents have
declined more than 10% from their respective 52-week highs. This divergence highlights the increasingly rotational nature
of the market and the importance of monitoring breadth for signs of stabilization or further deterioration.
●
Our base case favors continued sideways consolidation and rotation rather than a broader market pullback. While we
cannot rule out a scenario in which weakness in the average stock eventually weighs on the headline index, this is not our base
case. Instead, we believe any stabilization in interest rates could encourage capital to rotate back into recently pressured
areas, potentially funded by profit-taking in mega-cap growth and high-beta stocks. Although this rotation could result
in continued near-term choppiness and sideways consolidation in the broader index, it would also reinforce the market's
underlying resilience as leadership broadens.
●
Earnings remain the primary long-term driver of equities, but the bar is elevated heading into earnings season.
While we anticipate another quarter of positive earnings surprises, replicating the magnitude of last quarter's historic 26%
upside surprise could prove challenging. With consensus already projecting approximately 28% year-over-year EPS growth,
expectations are elevated, leaving less room for outsized beats. Corporate guidance and the market's reaction to results will
be particularly important in determining the markets reaction.
Bottom Line: Despite weakening market breadth, elevated interest rates, and geopolitical uncertainty, we continue to favor
upside for equities into year-end, supported by improving economic momentum, resilient earnings growth, and favorable
seasonality. Our base case remains a rotational market, with stabilization in interest rates potentially providing the
catalyst for capital to flow back into recently pressured areas rather than triggering a more meaningful correction in the
broader index. Given our constructive outlook, we would continue to view periods of consolidation and market weakness as
opportunities to selectively add exposure.

Watching for Support: Small-Caps and EW S&P 500
One potential scenario we envision is a rotation back into recently pressured areas, funded by outflows from mega-cap growth and high-
beta stocks. While this could lead to continued near-term sideways consolidation in the broader index, it would also reinforce the 
underlying resilience of the market as leadership broadens. We will be closely monitoring small-caps and the equal-weight S&P 500 to 
see whether they can hold their respective 200-day moving averages (200-DMA), which could be an important signal of market health. 
Alternatively, if selling pressure persists in already beaten-down areas and both indices break below their 200-DMAs, it could signal a 
deterioration in market breadth and resilience, ultimately increasing the risk of a more meaningful pullback in the S&P 500.

Index Definitions
The S&P 500 is an unmanaged index of 500 widely held stocks that is generally considered representative of the U.S. stock market.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA) is a price-weighted average of 30 significant stocks traded on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and the NASDAQ.
The NASDAQ Composite is a stock market index of the common stocks and similar securities listed on the NASDAQ stock market.
The MSCI World All Cap Index captures large, mid, small and micro-cap representation across 23 Developed Markets (DM) countries. With 11,732 constituents, the index is
comprehensive, covering approximately 99% of the free float-adjusted market capitalization in each country.
The MSCI EAFE (Europe, Australasia, and Far East) is a free float-adjusted market capitalization index that is designed to measure developed market equity performance,
excluding the United States & Canada. The EAFE consists of the country indices of 21 developed nations.
The MSCI Emerging Markets Index is designed to measure equity market performance in 23 emerging market countries. The index's three largest industries are materials,
energy, and banks.
The Russell 2000 index is an index measuring the performance of approximately 2,000 smallest-cap American companies in the Russell 3000 Index, which is made up of 3,000
of the largest U.S. stocks.
The NYSE Alerian MLP is the leading gauge of energy infrastructure Master Limited Partnerships (MLPs). The capped, float-adjusted, capitalization-weighted index, whose
constituents earn the majority of their cash flow from midstream activities involving energy commodities, is disseminated real-time on a price-return basis (AMZ) and on a
total-return basis (AMZX).
The Barclays Intermediate Government/Credit Bond index measures the performance of U.S. Dollar denominated U.S. Treasuries, government-related and investment grade
U.S. corporate securities that have a remaining maturity of greater than one year and less than ten years.

The Euro Stoxx 50 Index is a market capitalization weighted stock index of 50 large, blue-chip European companies operating within Eurozone nations. Components are

The S&P 500 Futures is a capitalization-weighted index of 500 stocks. The index is designed to measure performance of the broad domestic economy through changes in the

The DJIA Futures is a stock market index futures contract traded on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange`s Globex electronic trading platform. Dow Futures is based off the Dow
30 stock index.
The Nasdaq 100 Futures is a modified capitalization-weighted index of the 100 largest and most active non-financial domestic and international companies listed on the
NASDAQ.
Europe: DAX (Deutscher Aktienindex (German stock index)) is a blue chip stock market index consisting of the 40 major German companies trading on the Frankfurt Stock
Exchange.
Asia: Nikkei is short for Japan's Nikkei 225 Stock Average, the leading and most-respected index of Japanese stocks. It is a price-weighted index composed of Japan's top 225
blue-chip companies traded on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.
