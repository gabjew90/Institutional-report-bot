The wall of worry push back this week. Hot CPI revived debates on Fed hike risk (though Oct feels like a
hold). CXMT breakthrough claims triggered a sell-off in Korea memory names which spilled over to the
global tech supply chain. As sentiment weakened, the sustainability of AI capex spending, ROIC
outcomes, and funding requirements are back in the spotlight.

In Asia, the spotlight was firmly on the AI unwind, while Indonesia faced an additional overhang from
potential MSCI downgrade risks.
Geopolitical tensions and higher oil only reinforced the negative feedback loop around rates vs funding
costs vs global growth and put the lid on risky assets.
Worth noting, though, our US economists actually raised 3Q26 tracking GDP by 20ps to 3.3% on
industrial production strength.
With AI/Momentum rotation at play it was perhaps not too surprising to see European equities up 2% for
the week. The 4ppt outperformance vs SPX is the largest in more than a year - see below. NIFTY +2.8%
for the week - don't forget it was briefly the top performing Asian market during the June/July Momentum
sell off. We continue to pound the table on India private banks. In US we saw rotation to defensives
including Healthcare and Staples as the markets broadened out. SPW closed the week +48bps.
This week's % net selling grossing in Global Tech was the largest on record. GS Prime book is now UW
Tech vs MSCI World Index by -8.4%, which ranks in the 23rd/29th percentile on 1-year/5-year look back.
Net allocation is in the 48th percentile on 1-year look back.

Using our Global AI basket (GSXGGLAI) as a benchmark, the cumulative selling over Oct 8-15 was
roughly on par with the late-July episode and the largest since Mar '22. In terms of positioning we think it
stands at roughly 6/10 on length and materially cleaner than Jun/July. But while the July unwind felt
largely technical after the April-June run, this episode has opened a real debate on fundamentals across
rates/credit, earnings, ROIC etc. Below plots GSXGGLAI vs regional tech proxies
QQQ,Star50,KOSPI,TWSE.

In earlier notes I have noted that net positioning in Asia Tech looked relatively rich compared to the US.
This week we saw North Asia bore the brunt of the risk-off move. As % of Global Net MV, Asia Tech
stands at 18% versus 27% June peak vs 10% at start of year.

GS was net to sell $7.8bn in Asia this week. This was led by Taiwan (-$2.75bn), China ($-2.1bn), Japan
(-$1.9bn) and followed by Korea (-$700m). Australia and India both caught a bid.
Outside of GS and more broadly Taiwan saw the largest net FII selling in history on 15th Oct, ending the
week -$19.2bn. Korea, on other hand, now sits on cleaner positioning with FIIs sold $110+bn since May.
Per GS Prime net allocations in Korea now sit in the 30th percentile vs past 12 months vs Taiwan still
peakish. This trend of Taiwan selling vs. moderate selling in Korea is one to watch in the week to come.
HF positioning in Taiwan still closer to the highs. We had active engagement on Taiwan hedging ideas this
week: GSXATWSM is our Taiwan Semis basket (10 names) and GSXATTXT is Taiwan ex-TSMC Tech
basket (41 names). Details. Ask for color.

Korea net allocations now sit in the 30th percentile vs past 12 months, but still 85th percentile on 5-year
look back.

FII ownership in Korea Semis now sit at -2.5 SD below longer term history. Exposure to leveraged ETF
has also fallen sharply – AUM fell from $53bn peak to now $15bn, or 1.3% of free float.
KOSPI has also fallen by more than 20% over the past eight trading days, a magnitude of decline
previously seen only during the Asian Financial Crisis, the Global Financial Crisis, and the COVID-19
outbreak. If anything, history books say the risk reward looks very favorable from here
Updated chart of KOSPI vs CSI1000 in 2015. Had a few good discussions this week on this. Question is
we are at the yellow or the blue spot. But either way looks like we are due for a tactical bounce?

CXMT’s '4F2' - one of the smoking guns. Giuni Lee is happy to chat on his views. Bottom line, while this
could narrow some of the capacity gap, we think markets could have overestimated the ability for CXMT
to close the technology gap. 4F2 architecture consists of smaller memory cells which are more complex
and costly to manufacture. There is significant ground to cover from customer qualifications, to mass
production at high yield levels, and to further expansion into advanced nodes without direct access to EUV
equipment.
