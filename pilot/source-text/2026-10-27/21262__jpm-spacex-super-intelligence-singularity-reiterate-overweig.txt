Style Exposure
Another SpaceX earnings report and another series of significant upward 
estimate revisions. While SPCX beat on the top line across all 3 segments in 3Q—
growing revenue 161% Y/Y and 87% Q/Q—the primary driver going forward is 
Super Intelligence (SI, fka AI), and in particular an accelerated pace of compute 
buildout. SPCX now expects to end 2026 w/between 2-3 GW of compute & to build 
an incremental ~10 GW in 2027 to ~13 GW total, well above the previously 
expected 5-10 GW range. SPCX noted that constraints in power, cooling, & infra 
are bigger than in chips, but that the company is aiming for 20 GW of power by the 
end of 2027. SPCX is looking at solar & wind, in addition to gas, and also believes 
that its advanced casting capability for turbines & vanes gives it a significant 
advantage in bringing power online. Importantly, we believe the 10 GW of 
incremental compute in 2027 is almost all Vera Rubins, which should 
command significantly higher monetization than GB300s. According to 
SemiAnalysis, VR monetization could be ~40% higher than the strongest GB300s. 
An elevated level of monetization—from $30B-$50B/GW today—on accelerated 
compute is what supports SPCX’s view that it can reach $500B-$1T in ARR ending 
2027. Importantly, we believe that still assumes most of the capacity goes towards 
raw compute deals in 2027, with Grok/Cursor inference & intelligence increasing 
monetization & becoming a growing use of compute over time. With bigger 
compute, of course, comes bigger capex. SPCX now expects 4Q26 capex of $35-
$40B, well above our previous $19B estimate. More significantly, building out 
toward 13 GW of compute next year (we actually model 10 GW ending 2027) takes 
our 2027 capex estimate from ~$200B to ~$300B. Capital requirements are front 
and center across the market, and SPCX indicated it has a number of available pools 
of capital on the debt side. Importantly, in our discussion w/management, 
SPCX also said that it is not considering raising equity. At a higher level of 
monetization, operating cash flow will play a bigger role in supporting capex, and 
SPCX also believes it has advantages in the cost of building out a GW of compute 
in the mid-$30'sB range, potentially well below the costs of others. That 
combination of lower costs & outsized VR monetization in a strong demand 
environment is how SPCX believes it could have a 6-month payback on compute 
sometime next year. Shifting to Space, the company confirmed that it will attempt 
to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the upper stage Ship on the next Starship 
flight (Flight 15), which will likely be in November. While SpaceX has caught the 
Super Heavy three times, the Ship catch would be a first and it is a critical milestone 
on the path to full reusability. Shifting to Connectivity, Starlink added 1.6M 
consumer subscribers in 3Q26 to reach 13.6M, while consumer ARPU rose 5% q/q 
to $69/month. Both metrics reflect a focus on higher-ARPU developed markets as 
SpaceX worked through kit inventory constraints during the transition to V5 user 
terminals. However, management expects higher activations in 4Q, driven by the 
V5 production ramp and retail channel prioritization ahead of the holidays.

Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation
Investment Thesis
SpaceX’s ambitions are bigger than any company’s we’ve seen
—to make life multi-planetary, leverage the Sun to build out AI 
in space, & build bases on the Moon and cities on other planets. 
In our view, no other company is as well positioned to go after 
the combined anticipated TAM of $28T+. Launch is the key 
enabler & differentiator, with 670+ orbital launches, a 99%+ 
success rate, & 80%+ of all mass to orbit since 2023. Starship 
should deliver a 10x cost improvement & ~4x higher payload vs. 
Falcon 9 and enable the company to pursue entirely new 
markets. Connectivity drives current financials via Starlink, the 
largest LEO constellation, where we project broadband 
subscribers growing from 9M in 2025 to 94M+ in 2030. 
Importantly, AI is expected to be the long-term driver, as SpaceX 
is modeled to ramp terrestrial compute from 2.8GW in ‘26E to 
19.8GW by the end of 2028 & pursue orbital compute towards 
~78GW by the end of 2031. We believe significant upside 
potential remains as the company quite literally builds out the 
next frontier.  
Valuation
We increase our December 2027 price target from $240 to $500, 
based on 20x our 2028E GAAP EPS of $24.41, reflecting our 
higher estimates and a lower multiple. We apply an in-line 
multiple to mega-cap peers (Mag7 + PLTR) because we see 
greater execution risk around SPCX’s magnitude of P&L ramp 
across 2027–2028 vs. the broader group’s earnings trajectories. 
Importantly, we believe SPCX’s multiple could rerate higher as 
it continues to show progress toward its targets & de-risks the 
out-year model.

SUPER INTELLIGENCE (SI): Management highly confident in 10 GW of 
incremental capacity by YE27, power infrastructure build for 20 GW; high 
monetization driven by Vera Rubin premium leading to $500b-1T of ARR by YE27. 
SpaceX is adding capacity at a rapid pace, ending 3Q26 with 1.7 GW of nameplate 
compute, expecting 2.2 GW by the end of October, and 2.5-3.0 GW by year-end. The 
company is adding ~200k GPUs/month for the remainder of the year, or ~600k in 4Q26. 
SpaceX has previously discussed ~220k GB300 deployments equating to 110 MW, 
implying an all-in power draw of ~2 kW/GPU. 600k additional GPUs in 4Q26 would be 
~1.2 GW of incremental nameplate capacity, allowing SpaceX to end the year just shy of 
3.0 GW. We model 1.1 GW of incremental capacity coming online in 4Q26, ending the 
year with total nameplate capacity of 2.8 GW. While the pace of additions is impressive, 
SpaceX is looking to continue the acceleration into 2027, with CEO Elon Musk confident 
in the company’s ability to bring an incremental 10 GW of compute online during 2027 
(practically all VR generation). If achieved, SpaceX would be exiting 2027 with 13 GW 
of total nameplate compute. Next year, SpaceX will begin deployment of Nvidia’s Vera 
Rubin platform, and the company expects to have 440k VR GPUs deployed by the end 
of 2Q27, “bringing online the most powerful training cluster in the world” as noted by 
Musk. While Musk is highly confident in 10 GW of incremental capacity in 2027, he also 
mentioned the company could deliver 20 GW of power cooling infrastructure and a small 
chance of it being converted to compute capacity. As another example of SpaceX’s 
extreme vertical integration, Musk highlighted the company’s advanced casting 
capabilities, which are used for the rocket business, but also now being used for casting 
the blades and vanes for gas turbines. With current equipment lead times, this capability 
can allow SpaceX to bring power online faster than the rest of the industry with turbine 
suppliers noting blades and vanes are 12-18 months behind the rest of the turbine parts. 
Additionally, SpaceX is looking at utilizing alternative sources of power beyond just 
natural gas such as wind and solar, and management also discussed modular compute 
deployments to help scale capacity for inference workloads. While details are limited, the 
approach seems to be prefabricating an entire data center offsite which includes compute, 
power, and cooling within a single modular design, and then deploying the cluster 
nationally and/or globally. Together, these approaches provide a more concrete path to 
the 10+ GW of incremental capacity the company is targeting by YE27. Importantly, 
management is seeing pricing for compute “slightly above the high end” of the $30-50/W 
range discussed in 2Q and expects a premium for Vera Rubin capacity. The $500B-$1T 
ARR target by the end of 2027 depends on accelerated compute build & elevated 
monetization for Vera Rubin capacity. SpaceX management noted it can build in the mid-
$30b/GW vs others at $50b+/GW, with savings mostly coming from the data center build 
side, not GPUs.

CONNECTIVITY: Consumer net adds soften as mix lifts ARPU; 2027 growth now 
hinges on V3 deployment. Starlink added 1.6m consumer subscribers in 3Q26 to reach 
13.6m, while consumer ARPU rose 5% q/q to $69/month. Both metrics reflect a focus on 
higher-ARPU developed markets as SpaceX worked through kit inventory constraints 
during the transition to V5 user terminals. Management expects higher activations in 4Q, 
driven by the V5 production ramp and retail channel prioritization ahead of the holidays. 
We now model 4Q consumer broadband net adds of 2.2m and ARPU of $66 (-12% y/y), 
with ARPU supported by the developed-market mix carrying over from 3Q. We also 
suspect limited network capacity may be constraining sales in certain geographies, 
consistent with management's comment that V3 deployments should “enable new sales 
in high-demand areas.” Flight 14 carried 26 V3 satellites, and management expects 
Starship to deliver twice as many satellites per mission over time. However, Shotwell 
noted that “a few hundred” V3 satellites will be needed in orbit before users see 
meaningful network benefits, which she expects by mid-2027. As V3 capacity comes 
online and Starship cadence increases, we forecast 2027 net adds of 11.5m and a 17.4% 
y/y ARPU decline to $55.42, as faster back-half growth led by emerging markets dilutes 
mix.
•
CONNECTIVITY: With spectrum largely in place, Mobile V2 satellites become the 
gating item for Starlink Mobile’s early 2028 launch. Following the agreement to 
acquire Grain's 800 MHz low-band spectrum, satellite deployment is now the key 
constraint on launching U.S. service, which SpaceX still expects in early 2028. On 
network architecture, President & COO Gwynne Shotwell noted that 85–93% of mobile 
connectivity runs over Wi-Fi. She expects the S-band spectrum SpaceX is acquiring from 
EchoStar to carry most of the remaining traffic, with 800 MHz primarily providing in-
building penetration. On the terrestrial side, Shotwell focused on smaller radios placed 
on existing Starlink homes and businesses, while towers appear less central to the 
company’s network deployment plan. Musk believes Starlink connectivity can 
ultimately generate several hundred billion dollars in annual revenue, noting that outside 
observers are “missing some of the pieces that we know about internally,” and suggested 
a separate, longer presentation, with timing yet to be determined, to lay out the “game 
plan.” Consistent with our prior view, we expect these radios to use satellite backhaul 
rather than fiber. That said, while we acknowledge SpaceX’s engineering prowess, we 
continue to believe the company will need to deploy terrestrial macro towers over time 
to compete with the Big-3 incumbents.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
SpaceX (Overweight; Price Target: $500.00)
Investment Thesis
SpaceX’s ambitions are bigger than any company’s we’ve seen—to make life multi-
planetary, leverage the Sun to build out AI in space, & build bases on the Moon and cities 
on other planets. In our view, no other company is as well positioned to go after the combined 
anticipated TAM of $28T+. Launch is the key enabler & differentiator, with 670+ orbital 
launches, a 99%+ success rate, & 80%+ of all mass to orbit since 2023. Starship should 
deliver a 10x cost improvement & ~4x higher payload vs. Falcon 9. We expect launches to 
go from a handful in 2026 to ~5,000 in 2031. Extreme vertical integration & an engineering-
first culture let SpaceX build faster & cheaper. Connectivity drives current financials via 
Starlink, the largest LEO constellation. We see broadband subscribers growing from 9M in 
2025 to 94M+ in 2030. On mobile, we see optionality over time, with an MVNO more likely 
to emerge in international markets first, where Starlink Mobile may convert existing 
revenue-sharing agreements. Importantly, AI is expected to be the long-term driver, as 
SpaceX is modeled to ramp terrestrial compute from 2.8GW in ‘26E to 19.8GW by the end 
of 2028. From 2029 on, we expect SpaceX to pursue orbital compute towards ~78GW by 
the end of 2031 at a significantly cheaper cost than could be done on Earth. We believe 
significant upside potential remains as the company quite literally builds out the next 
frontier.  
Valuation
We increase our December 2027 price target from $240 to $500, based on 20x our 2028E 
GAAP EPS of $24.41, reflecting our higher estimates and a lower multiple. We apply an 
in-line multiple to mega-cap peers (Mag7 + PLTR) because we see greater execution risk 
around SPCX’s magnitude of P&L ramp across 2027–2028 vs. the broader group’s earnings 
trajectories. Importantly, we believe SPCX’s multiple could rerate higher as it continues to 
show progress toward its targets & de-risks the out-year model.
Risks to Rating and Price Target
Key downside risks include:
•
Everything Depends on Starship: Rapid & complete reusability is the critical enabler 
underpinning SpaceX’s long-term growth drivers, expected to structurally lower launch 
costs ~80%+ vs. legacy; any delays, technical setbacks, or regulatory hurdles would 
impede growth across multiple business lines given Starship’s scale & complexity.
•
Supply Constraints, Especially in AI: Bottlenecks in power availability & silicon/
chip supply could delay constellation buildout, moderate launch cadence, & weigh on 
gross margins, with reliance on advanced chips adding geopolitical/trade exposure; the 
industry-wide nature of these constraints makes them hard to fully mitigate, though 
Terafab first-party chip manufacturing is anticipated in the outer years.
•
Extremely Strong Execution Required: Continued execution across existing & more 
ambitious initiatives will inevitably bring unforeseen challenges given the scale, 
complexity, & frontier nature of what SpaceX is pursuing; while many years ahead, 
competition is picking up—from Blue Origin, AMZN, & international players—which 
could pressure pricing, win rates, & R&D spend over time.
