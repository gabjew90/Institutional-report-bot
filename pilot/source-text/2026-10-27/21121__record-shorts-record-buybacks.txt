Buyback authorizations hit a record
The corporate bid has never been bigger on paper. Per GS's Ranganath desk, 2026 YTD repurchase
authorizations reached a record "$1,305bn," up from $1,151bn in 2025 and well above the old $959bn
(2022) and $951bn (2024) peaks. Translation: corporate America has authorized a record amount of
buying, potentially providing substantial support into year-end.

The memory meltdown
The three major memory stocks, Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron, have fallen an average 42% from
their recent peaks, according to JPMorgan. For perspective, the average drawdown across the previous
four major memory downcycles was approximately 49%.
In other words, the market has already delivered much of the punishment normally associated with an
actual industry downturn. The interesting part? JPMorgan sees little evidence that such a downturn has
begun. Full note here.
JPM
Pricing in disaster
The market appears to be discounting a memory earnings peak as early as the first half of 2027. Yet
Samsung and SK Hynix are trading at just 2–3x estimated 2027/2028 earnings, with forward price-to-
book multiples around 0.9–1.5x.
Those valuations reflect enormous skepticism about the sustainability of current profitability. The
question is whether investors are correctly anticipating a sharp earnings reversal, or simply
extrapolating the latest market panic too far.

France is the problem, not Europe
French sovereign stress continues to intensify, but the contagion story is surprisingly muted according
to Authers. While French OAT spreads have widened sharply, Italian and Greek spreads versus
Germany have actually narrowed this month.
This is a very different setup from the 2010–12 eurozone crisis. Investors are increasingly treating
France as an isolated sovereign problem rather than a systemic European event.
However, France is part of Europe's core, making it dangerous to dismiss the potential for broader
contagion. More here.
