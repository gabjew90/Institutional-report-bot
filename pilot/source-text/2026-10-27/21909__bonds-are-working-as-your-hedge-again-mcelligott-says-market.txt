 
Bonds Are "Working As Your Hedge" Again: 
McElligott Says Market Is Now Repricing An 
AI-Driven Growth Scare 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 22, 2026 - 07:30 PM 
Two weeks ago, Nomura's cross-asset guru Charlie McElligott told us that the market's only fear 
was of the right tail. 
Today, the title of his latest note (available to pro subs) says it all: "REPRICING THE 
LEFT-TAIL...FAST." 
 
And this time, he isn't blaming positioning. 
 

 
Tuesday: "Not Yet." Thursday: Yet. 
Regular readers will recall that just two days ago, in his "DE-TRENDED" note, McElligott laid out 
why the Treasury short squeeze had just blown up every "High Sharpe" trend trade. His 
diagnosis at the time was mechanical: crowded bond shorts taking profits around the hot 
inflation prints, which then snowballed into a broader de-grossing. What it was not, he stressed, 
was a macro call: 
"I do not believe that last week's escalation in said 'Momentum' / 'Trend' -reversals was a 
capitulative narrative pivot into some sort of 'Growth Scare / Recession' -outlook as 'Chalk' / the 
base-case just yet." 
He did, however, leave the door open: "we could absolutely still get to that 'Growth Scare' place 
in short-order." As it turns out, "short-order" meant about 48 hours. Here is McElligott this 
morning: 
"But this 'positioning / crowding dynamic' being said, it is becoming increasingly clear that we 
are indeed 'Repricing the Left-Side' of 2027+ the US Economic Growth implied probability 
distribution, DUE TO this 'Rethink' on AI trajectory... and that is certainly now contributing to this 
feedback loop where suddenly, we've witnessed USTs 'working' again as Risk-Asset hedges 
into this micro-cluster of thematic 'AI Equities' Vol expansion, as short-term 'Bonds : Stocks' 
correlation reverses course." 
In other words, the market has stopped treating the AI selloff as a positioning wash and started 
treating it as a growth problem, and it is hedging accordingly. 
Hedges Are Back, And Spot Is Finally Talking To Vol 
For weeks, the only options anyone wanted were upside calls. That changed today. According 
to McElligott, equity vol, skew and vol-of-vol (what he calls "Demand for Wings") are: 
"...suddenly jacking higher on outright hedge buying after having been a total afterthought in 
recent weeks, as Spot Equities accelerate through some of the MegaCap Tech / Nasdaq / QQQ 
'Negative Gamma' flip (e.g. $715 in QQQ) as AI is under the cosh, and we see a flip in 'Spot / 
Vol Correlation' to the more standard NEGATIVE form (Spot Down, Vol Up)." 
You can see it in the QQQ vol curve, where front-month implied vol jumped roughly 3 points 
from yesterday's close to around 24, above even the late-February curve, while a month ago the 
front end sat around 19-20 (chart source Nomura Vol): 
 

 
 
And in the Nasdaq, the spot/vol relationship has snapped back to textbook form: NDX down, 
VXN up, right on the regression line. 
 
The hedging flow on the tape this morning, per the Nomura desk: 
●​ SPX Jan 5900 puts: paid 18.3 on 1,300 contracts 
●​ VIX Nov 25 calls: 18,150 committed at 0.85 
 

 
●​ SPX Dec 7400 puts (25-delta, tied to 7730): 2,000 bought at $84.50 
The reason the QQQ $715 line matters: below it, dealers are short gamma, and the mechanical 
selling starts to feed on itself. Nomura's rebalance projection for options dealers, levered ETFs 
and vol-control funds shows roughly $34 billion to sell on another 1% drop, $134 billion on a 3% 
drop and $225 billion on a 5% drop. On the upside, the same crowd would buy just $23.5 billion 
on a 1% bounce, and on a 5% rip vol-control selling actually leaves them net sellers. 
Asymmetric doesn't begin to describe it. 
 
Bonds Are "Working As Your Hedge"... Again 
The bigger shift, however, is in rates. For most of 2026, owning duration against your stock 
portfolio was a great way to lose money twice. Today, with Treasury yields at fresh one-month 
lows, McElligott notes that USD rate vol is also bid into the risk-off rally, "as UST Bonds are 
*gasp* 'working as your hedge' again." 
And the flows say this is more than short-covering. Overnight and this morning, Nomura saw a 
wave of bullish Treasury option trades: 
●​ TYZ 108.5 calls: paid 6 on 49,999 
●​ TY week-5 105.25/105.75 strangle: paid 36-37 on 20,000 
●​ FVH 106 calls: paid up to 27 on 15,000, then another 20,000 
●​ TYZ 107.5 and 107 calls: 23,000 and 17,000 
 

 
The swaption vol grid tells the same story: 1-month expiry vol on 1y and 2y tails jumped 10.1 
and 11.2 on the day, while 10y-and-longer expiries barely moved. When the front end of the vol 
surface is what's moving, the market is pricing what the Fed does next, and in a growth scare, 
that means cuts.

The "AI Trajectory Rethink" Charge Sheet 
Here is his list of what has gone wrong since last week, and it's quite a list: 
"The escalation of the 'AI Trajectory Rethink' which kicked-off last week in earnest and has 
continued into the overnight (Anthropic 'finger in air' ARR disappointment and questions of IPO 
viability >> Coreweave / Neocloud / Infrastructure -dooming from Gartner >> a smattering of 
poorly received 'AI ecosystem' IPOs around the globe >> disappointing guidance from LRCX >> 
generic take of 'Capex flattening vs lofty estimates' -impulse, despite META staying 'full tilt' and 
raising Capex vs priors, while too pushing-on with a massive Equities Secondary which iterates 
that Hyperscalers are de facto 'Overwriting' / 'Selling Calls on Themselves') have all 
cumulatively and suddenly reset the calculus LOWER on what had been spectacular 'steep' 
projections of both AI / Hyperscaler profitability & 'resumption to positive FCF' -expectations, 
especially now as 'AI Financing Costs' are increasingly realizing as 'Non-linear,' as Credit 
Spreads widen in somewhat stressy-fashion, and with a lot more paper / issuance coming down 
the pipe (Debt, Loans and Equities at that!)" 
We covered pretty much every item on that list as it happened: Anthropic's run-rate math hitting 
the brakes, Gartner's warning that "over 80% of neoclouds will fold", the Korean data center IPO 
faceplant and Lam Research (LRCX) struggling to clear elevated whispers overnight. 
But the item worth lingering on is Meta, and McElligott's "Selling Calls on Themselves" line. As 
we detailed this morning, Meta's own 14C filing admits it needs to raise "hundreds of billions of 
dollars" starting as early as this fall, via a new class of non-voting stock (because Zuck won't 
give up the votes) and a lot more bonds (because that much debt would hurt the ratings). 
Translation: the hyperscalers are now funding the AI arms race by selling their own upside to 
the public, and credit is pricing what happens if the upside doesn't show up: 
 

 
That's the "Non-linear" part. When the most profitable AI spender on the planet has to pay up to 
borrow, everyone downstream of it pays more, which is why the neoclouds were the first to 
crack. 
Both "Sector Generals" Down: Goodbye Barbell, Goodbye 
Trickle-Down 
McElligott is clear this is real de-risking, and the scoreboard backs him up. The two leaders of 
the S&P this year, Tech and Energy, are the two legs of what he calls the "Core Constraints of 
the AI Cold War," and the trade built on them has been a monster: 
"...the thesis behind the 'Semis / Energy Barbell' which has been +60% YTD on a 3 Sharpe-are 
both getting TOSSED today (Energy -1.7%, Tech -2.9%)" 
Diversification at its finest. 
Why does this matter beyond one crowded trade? Because, as McElligott argues, the AI capex 
boom has been doing a lot of the heavy lifting for the whole economy. Along with G10 
governments' deficit spending ("Fiscal Dominance") and policy rates that apparently still aren't 
restrictive enough, the "AI Capex Trickle-Down" has helped keep US nominal GDP: 
"...'Running Hot' in a 6-10% band for much of the year, which of course has then contributed to 
the both 'real and perceived' bullish Earnings Growth -backdrop for Corporate America" 
Pull one leg out of that stool, and the earnings story, the "run hot" story and the "higher for 
longer" rates story all wobble at the same time. Which is, presumably, why the bond market is 
suddenly paying for calls. 
Of course, none of this is news to longtime readers: we have been warning since last October 
that the AI buildout runs on debt, and that sooner or later the financing bill would come due. It 
now has, and it's getting more expensive by the day. 
From Right-Tail To Left-Tail In Two Weeks 
McElligott's closing thought is the one to frame: 
"...again, bc those prior assumptions of 'Forever Capex Trickle-Down' are no longer what they 
once were" 
The optimists will point to the reflexive loop Goldman described: lower yields ease the 
hyperscalers' funding bill, which could eventually put a floor under the AI trade. Maybe. But that 
floor only exists because the market is pricing less AI growth, and Meta alone needs "hundreds 
 

 
of billions" from the same investors that Alphabet, SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI are also 
hitting up. We side with the left tail: the bond market is late to this party, not early, and with 
dealers short gamma below QQQ $715 and $225 billion of mechanical selling waiting on a 5% 
drop, it won't take much to find out. 
Then again, two weeks ago the only thing anyone feared was missing the rally.
