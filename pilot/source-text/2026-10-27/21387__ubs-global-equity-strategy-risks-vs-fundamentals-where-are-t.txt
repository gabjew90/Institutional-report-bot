De-risking accelerated but signs of resilience waiting for circuit breakers
Market internals showed signs of accelerated de-risking with large relative moves in 
defensives>cyclicals, high quality>low and low momentum>high, Europe>US, among 
others. The AI 100 was down 5% last week as Coreweave's CDS surged but the ex-
AI 400 was up 2.5% with every equal weighted S&P sector up except Tech as bond 
yields fell even though CPI was "hot" and WTI oil was up 10% on Iran escalation fears. 
Risk premiums vs fundamentals: using our frameworks to find asymmetries 
We see rising odds of both a correlated rally and  selloff with key catalysts the next 2+ 
weeks (earnings, FOMC, US midterms) determining the path for stocks into 2027, and 
Iran an increasing overhang. Implied correlations are too low if Tech and ex-Tech / rate 
sensitive segments can rally or fall at the same time. After the rate spike, the beta of risk 
premia to moves in rates seems to be higher now as highlighted by CRWV CDS. Amid 
now higher risk premia and lower P/Es, we look for relative asymmetries to position amid 
uncertainty using a number of our frameworks including: 1) ML model to identify what 
overshot macro moves, 2) REVS scores vs rates betas, 3) PEG ratios vs EPS momentum.

Trades for market exposures - Buying cheap growth and hedges
Buy SPX December OTM calls: Helps position for a broader rally if rates/oil pressures ease, midterm-election risk passes without a 
major shock, AI demand remains strong and earnings and buybacks provide additional supports - without pretending the macro 
downside has disappeared. The  S&P 500 has historically rallied by an average of 6% from end-September to end-December in 
midterm-election years and by 14% through the following March. OTM calls add upside exposure while keeping downside 
limited amid still-elevated macro risks.
Buy HYG downside for the yield-plus-spread 'double whammy': We prefer using  HYG downside structures to hedge the tail risk 
that Treasury yields and high-yield spreads rise together. Credit spreads have remained unusually calm despite higher rates 
volatility, but an unexpected tightening cycle could expose lower-quality high yield and leveraged loans first. (link).
Buy SPX puts, funded by selling XLU puts: Index protection remains expensive to carry bas high levels of dispersion continue to 
mute index-level moves. Utilities have already underperformed by more than expected by our macro model implies at a time 
where power demand remains supported by the AI build-out. XLU downside volatility is relatively rich versus SPX, and hence 
selling XLU puts to fund SPX puts therefore provides protection against a broader growth or AI disappointment. 
Short KOSPI2/SMH/SOXX puts to buy SXXP calls: Take downside risk in Korea (and primarily Korean semis), where strong pricing, 
cash flow and buybacks are underwritten by DRAM undersupply through 2028 and a multiple already discounting considerable 
margin mean reversion that we don't expect. Use the rich implied vol to buy more than two times the notional of upside in 
Europe, where most industry groups are growing earnings above 10% and those expectations are still rising.
Long SPX vs FTSE 100: SPX has derated ~15% relative to the FTSE despite delivering 22% stronger relative earnings growth 
during the same period. US equities offer superior exposure to Technology and Industrials, the areas with the strongest growth 
and revision support, while the FTSE’s defensive mix lacks the earnings growth offset required in a high real yield environment.
Long KOSPI versus NIFTY 50: Korea offers stronger two-year earnings growth and a lower valuation than India. The position also 
has appeal across rate scenarios: both markets are rate sensitive, but Korea’s current-account surplus provides a buffer if rates 
and/or oil increase further, whereas India is more reliant on foreign capital and is more exposed to slowing IT services.
Trades for risks stabilizing/reversing  - Buying asymmetric recovery potential
Buy XLI calls, funded by selling XLP calls: Consumer pockets (e.g. XLP, XRT) have rallied aggressively amidst the yield retracement 
despite oil remaining elevated – upside convexity to yields + oil falling in tandem now appears reduced. We would favor selling  
XLP upside to fund XLI upside. Fundamentally, Industrials are more attractive amid stronger revision momentum. Investors may 
consider XLI Dec vs. XLP Dec  25-delta ratio call switches for flat premium, which rewards a scenario in which rates pressure eases 
and the industrial part of the market recovers alongside an already aggressive Staples recovery.
US Utilities over US Staples. As rates have risen, Utilities P/E has fallen toward 15x, near 10+ year lows, despite rising medium-term 
EPS growth expectations on increased capex and power demand. Staples P/E is a lofty~25x, despite falling EPS growth potential. 
Utilities earnings yields plus EPS CAGR is nearly 16%, compared to just ~11% for Staples. Both are rates sensitive, but Utilities are 
also less crowded than Staples. In the case of rate pressure abating, we see investors reallocating back to Utilities given its higher 
growth is now at a substantial discount.
Long EU renewables (UBXERNEW): Positive revisions, light crowding and a valuation held back by real yields. Renewables trade 
below our Narrative Radar fair value having underperformed their macro-sensitivities where bond yields going up is negative but 
gas prices going up is positive but under-priced currently.
Long US Industrials and Capex Broadening (UBEQCPXB). We believe AI related industrial demand will remain robust amid higher 
rates. On the other side, capex had been turning up beyond AI/data centers as mfg recovered from a ~3yr recession, while 
operating cash flow  is up ~$825bn y/y (+25%) and capex to OCF is near historic lows ex hyperscalers. The correction provides an 
attractive entry point to a secular story related to AI, combined with the potential for a cyclical recovery in other segments. 
Long EU Industrials (SXNP) vs Consumer (UBXECCYC): AI capex in Europe is moving from product suppliers to the industrials that 
build capacity. Revisions for these 'industrial capacity beneficiaries' are now as strong as for the 'AI Capex beneficiaries', from 
cheaper valuations and lighter positioning. Industrials lead European sector revisions while EU Consumer Cyclicals are being cut.

Rates driving equities: what next? 
Our machine learning (ML) model assesses the key drivers of equities on an absolute and 
relative basis (see Tactical Dashboard). Our model currently shows that Fed policy 
expectations have increased in importance in explaining returns, with AI still at the top 
but down from the highs. While there have been many drivers of the 10yr rise, the shift 
from expectations of Fed cuts last fall to now pricing more Fed hikes has  been the main 
driver. The first phase of the bond selloff was fundamental driven by the repricing of 
growth while the "spike" since early Sep has been exacerbated by positioning unwinds. 
Investors have been asking what level of US 10y yield breaks the market/cycle. While 
equities were relatively resilient to the rise in yields given such strong earnings, the SPX P/
E is down ~20% since late last year and risk premium more broadly have been repriced 
across sectors and factors with the spikes in France and Coreweave spreads the most 
visible casualties of the rates spike.

AI demand  key amid AI capex risk on higher rates
AI demand remains the first principal driver of equities.  CDS spreads fell sharply on Q2 
cloud beats amid the momentum unwind. Again, hyperscaler beats and commentary 
are the needed circuit breaker to refocus on accelerating AI demand and OCF. 
UBS ~15% above consensus for N4Q cloud revenue. UBS analysts  expect US 
hyperscaler cloud revenue to grow 60% y/y in 3Q, accelerating from 50% in 2Q and 
ahead of consensus. More important, over the next 4 quarters, UBS expects cloud 
revenue to reach $574bn, ~15% above consensus and ~73% growth versus prior. Thus, 
we see the potential for notable upgrades to 2027 cloud sales through Q3 reporting and 
into next year, which can hopefully act as a circuit breaker for the AI selloffs and reignite 
interest in the AI infrastructure theme as investors get more confidence in the 
sustainability of spending amid accelerating OCF. 
Neocloud 2027 capex share is ~7%. The spike in Coreweave's bond yield further 
fueled a selloff in the AI complex on worries about financing costs and structure 
weighing on the ability to fund capex commitments. Though neoclouds are a bigger 
share of GPU demand, they account for 7% of AI capex in 2027 and neocloud 
enterprise value is ~$130bn. Compute demand far exceeds supply, so compute will get 
allocated in the event capital is an issue, with low risk to the broad AI build out in our 
view  if AI demand is there given core hyperscaler OCF and borrowing capacity. 
Low bar for Q2 reporting after derating. We see a low bar for Q2 earnings with 
nearly 70% of companies expected to deliver slower EPS growth than in 2Q and the 
median stock having derated 7%+ since the start of 2Q reporting. Revisions remain 
strong, with estimates up 1.8%, including 3.6% for Tech+ and 0.8% for ex-Tech+.

Benign hiking cycle points to further rally 
The US10yr has risen +1.5 stdev above its 1yr moving average only seven times in 40 
years. Many catalysts will affect the path of rates (oil, employment, inflation, midterms), 
but 10y yields did not meaningfully decline for 2+ months post  this stage of10yr spikes, 
and yields increased further in full Fed hiking cycles (Rates spike & equities). 

Fed hikes moderate -> equities rallied. When the Fed hiked <100bp within a 
year of rate spikes in the 2013, 2016, 2021 and 2023 episodes, the US 10yr yield 
settled at elevated levels for ~2mo on avg before falling. After treading water 
those ~2mo, ACWI and SPX rallied 8-10% on avg the next 6mo and 15-18% the 
next year. We see a case for the 10y to return toward 5% if data/catalysts fall the 
right way and rates vol falling further fueling a rebound in rate sensitive equities. 

Fed hiking cycles -> equities weak. When the Fed hiked by 100+bp within a 
year of the 10y surge  in 1994, 1999 and 2022, ACWI and SPX returns were 
negative or flat over the following year. The 10y also took 100 trading days on avg 
to reach the peak after crossing the +1 stdev threshold (vs 43 for other cases), and 
took much longer to get back to the moving average (288 days versus 197).

Hedges for tightening tail, growth/AI spillovers
We discussed in previous sections how non-benign tightening cycles have historically 
been a large headwind for equities. The three main mechanisms  which rates affect 
equities are higher real rates, weaker growth, and higher credit spreads, to which every 
major market has a very large negative beta. An aggressive tightening cycle is not our 
base case. We also think equities are likely to stay more resilient than rates because of 
the growth offset and suppressed correlations.
Rising rate hike probabilities into early 2027 have driven sharp moves across the 
yield curve. Our Rates Strategists see these moves as difficult to fade as yields remain 
highly sensitive to oil, the Fed is giving energy prices unusual weight, and the 
geopolitical backdrop is unlikely to resolve quickly. History further suggests rates stay 
elevated for at least a few months after similar yield spikes recently witnessed. Rate 
volatility has already risen notably to reflect this, in line with our view.
Our credit strategists suggest that spreads have remained unusually 
complacent (see here), as the link between rate volatility and spreads has historically 
strengthened during tightening cycles. They see an unexpected tightening cycle as the 
main downside risk for credit, with stress emerging first in lower-quality HY and 
leveraged loans. We have already seen early signs of this with yield vs. HY spread 
correlation just off all-time highs, a 'double whammy' we think is not being fully priced 
into HYG volatility.
Trade idea: HYG downside for yield + spread 'double whammy'
We continue to favor HYG downside structures to hedge an environment in which 
Treasury yields and HY spreads rise in tandem. Rate risks are more pronounced for 
companies with debt and will come through in credit spreads more quickly than equity 
markets skewed heavily toward profitability rather than leverage. Investors may consider 
HYG 1x1.5 put ratios to position for further downside, exploiting recently higher skew 
amidst the initial drawdown – see here.

What time-varying betas say about the impact of yields on 
equities
We have built a weekly Kalman-filter model in which equity sensitivities are allowed to 
change over time as new information arrives. Four US macro factors are estimated 
together: real rates, inflation breakevens, credit spreads and growth. Each coefficient is 
therefore a partial beta, holding the other factors constant. Absolute betas translate an 
actual factor move into an equity sensitivity; standardized betas put returns and factors 
on comparable volatility-adjusted scales.

The HOLT methodology does not assign ratings or a target price to a security. It is an analytical 
tool that involves use of a set of proprietary quantitative algorithms and warranted value 
calculations, collectively called the HOLT valuation model, that are consistently applied to all 
the companies included in its database. The HOLT valuation model is a discounted cash flow 
model. Third-party data (including consensus earnings estimates) are systematically translated 
into a number of default variables and incorporated into the algorithms available in the HOLT 
valuation model. The source financial statement, pricing, and earnings data provided by 
outside data vendors are subject to quality control and may also be adjusted to more closely 
measure the underlying economics of firm performance. These adjustments provide 
consistency when analyzing a single company across time, or analyzing multiple companies 
across industries or national borders.
The default scenario that is produced by the HOLT valuation model establishes a warranted 
price that represents the expected mean value for a security based upon empirically derived 
fade algorithms that forecast a firm's future return on capital and growth rates over an 
extended period of time. As the third-party data are updated, the warranted price updates 
automatically. A company’s future achieved return on capital or growth rate may differ from 
HOLT default forecast. Additional information about the HOLT methodology is available upon 
request.
