Asia Open - The beat goes on
By Preston Pagli
A strong risk-on tone returned to markets as SpaceX's blowout earnings and projections reinforced confidence in the
durability of the AI capex cycle. Underneath the surface, risk remained sensitive to developing geopolitical headlines with
Washington ordering the return of US embassy personnel in Saudi Arabia, offsetting new tri-lateral diplomacy efforts in
the Russo-Ukraine war. Elsewhere, global FI traded weaker with front-end US yields +3bps and the OAT-Bund spread
topping 160bps. The dollar traded mostly unchanged and weaker than G10 while USDMXN topped its highest level in a
year with CPI due Thursday. Earnings remained in focus with Meta topping estimates and surging more than 5% after-
mkt. Looking ahead, New Zealand CPI prints at 22:45 BST, then the BoK rate decision, with our economists expecting a
hawkish hold.

In Focus‌
Equities rebounded sharply driven by renewed optimism around the durability of the capex cycle after SpaceX's blowout
earnings. SPX rose 0.58%, and NDX +1.13% with momentum flows on the desk back into semis. Despite the strong headline
performance, breadth took a step back with A/D nearly split. Both SPX and NDX closed back above the key CTA levels which
would see systematics adding back longs into Thursday's session. In derivatives, despite subdued vols, there was still
demand for downside hedges, most notably via put spreads across SPX and ES. 
Earnings: Meta (+5%) as Q3 revenue printed +26% while 4Q revenue cleared the high end of the buyside bar $76bn, and
opting not to provide a 2027FY outlook. "Our investments are paying off, the core business is strong, and we're seeing
velocity across the board."  Meta increased the low end of its capex expectation by $5bn to $135bn, while the top end
remains at $145 billion.
Texas Instruments (-1%) posted a beat and a rise with gross margin upside, lifted by demand from automotive and industrial
customers. Revenue to $6.3bn
IBM (+3%) jumped as sales increased to $17.1bn vs. $16.82bn expected. Adjusted EPS to $2.95. 

Rates: UST yields moved higher in a bear-flattening fashion, with the front-end and belly leading the selloff. Stronger risk
sentiment and the AI-led equity rally pushed near-term rate expectations a touch higher just one week until Oct FOMC, with
+4bps priced. The $13bn 20y bond reopening added modest duration supply pressure during the session, though long-end
yields underperformed to a lesser extent, long bond roughly flat at 5.415%. In Europe, UK gilts rallied despite hotter-than-
expected CPI, suggesting the inflation print was largely taken in stride, while spreads widened further with OATs
underperforming Bunds.
US Treasury Auction Recap: $13 billion of 20-year bonds. The auction tailed the 1pm market by 0.9bp to yield 5.469%. The
bid/cover ratio came in at 2.73 which is at the 73rd percentile of the past 12 auctions.
FX: Activity across the e-trading desk was relatively subdued overall, with EURNOK standing out as the exception in terms of
volume. Net flows were skewed toward EM and high-beta FX buying via leveraged segment demand in CNH, SEK, and ZAR,
while TRY and MXN saw the largest net outflows. Attention now turns to tonight's New Zealand CPI, with AUDNZD in focus
with our traders looking for potential dip opportunities to add AUD exposure. 
Commodities continue to send mixed signals beneath the surface. Brent and WTI diverged, with Brent supported by ongoing
concerns around potential supply disruptions in the Gulf. The widening Brent-WTI spread remains one of the more notable
cross-asset developments on the day. Elsewhere, natural gas (-6.0%) is the largest mover across asset classes after sharp
profit-taking followed yesterday's surge above €90.
Elsewhere‌
MXN unperformed sharply with USDMXN +1.3% to 18.66, a yearly high. The move came amid no clear catalyst with retail
sales printing +1.2% MoM, well above expectations. Flows on the desk were driven RM and corporate segment outflows, with
our trader holding a bearish MXN conviction heading into CPI on Thursday. 
Ahead:
NZD: CPI YoY at 22:45 BST for Q3. Lower fuel prices should help drive a softer print at 0.8% QoQ/3.9% YoY vs 1.5%/4.1%
prior (Citi e.). This is in line with RBNZ forecasts so shouldn’t surprise markets. Upside risks remain for a higher terminal OCR
in the near future.
PHP: BSP Overnight Borrowing Rate Decision at 07:30 BST. Our economists expect the BSP to raise the policy rate by 25bp
to 5.25%.
KRW: BoK Base Rate Decision. Our economists expect a hawkish hold at 3.00% as the central bank assesses its back-to-
back summer hikes, maintaining a view for 25bp hikes each in November 2026 and February 2027 toward a 3.5% terminal
rate.
