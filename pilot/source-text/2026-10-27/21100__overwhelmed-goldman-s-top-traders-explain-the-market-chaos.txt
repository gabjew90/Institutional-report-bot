 
Overwhelmed? Goldman's Top Traders Explain The 
Market Chaos... 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, OCT 20, 2026 - 02:15 AM 
Across equities, credit, and derivatives last week the common theme was a sharp reassessment 
of risk as traders moved from celebrating AI-driven growth to questioning the cost of funding it. 
 
The volume of research and reports around this sudden shock to reality has been 
overwhelming, and so, Goldman Sachs trader Miti Goel attempts the impossible, synthesizing 
all of the banks' top trading floor thoughts below... 
Zoomed-Out: 
As Tony Pasquariello put it, "we've entered a new phase in the AI sequence, one that is 
unequivocally more complicated" and markets are now engaged in an "intense debate around 
both the availability AND the cost of capital." AI credit spreads have widened to the worst levels 
 

 
of the year while the market's AI leaders-vs-laggards basket has given back all the gains 
accumulated through the first half of 2026. Importantly, this isn't simply an equity story. The 
widening gap between high- and low-quality assets is now evident across both credit and 
equities, reinforcing the market's defensive shift toward quality and balance sheet strength. 
Root Cause:  
Mark Wilson quoted the credit team directly: "we view AI-related corporate bonds from 
neoclouds or those with frontier lab exposure as being at the epicentre of the funding cost 
debate." The trigger appears to have been a combination of heightened scrutiny around AI 
funding models, CoreWeave-related commentary, and a growing debate around the economics 
of frontier AI labs and value capture across the ecosystem. However, Mark argues investors 
should not confuse this debate with a collapse of the secular AI thesis. In his words, "the 
mega-theme of AI development & investment is very much intact," while slowing growth rates 
and funding requirements are now being priced more realistically. He points to OpenAI CFO 
Sarah Friar's recent comments that "we're still short compute, so I should have bought more of 
it" as evidence that underlying demand remains robust even as financing questions intensify. 
Transmission Mechanism: 
Brutal positioning unwinds. Lee Coppersmith notes that Friday brought "the first real sign of 
panic in index protection"as investors aggressively sought downside hedges. VIX futures 
volume reached 3.6x normal levels, CBOE index put volume hit an all-time record, and VIX call 
activity surged to the second-highest level of the last five years. At the same time, dealer 
gamma moved substantially lower and hedge funds aggressively cut risk. Prime data showed 
US long/short net leverage falling by 3.2 percentage points, the largest weekly decline since 
Liberation Day, while Global Tech experienced its largest percentage net selling on record since 
2016. Despite this, Lee emphasizes that managers largely increased shorts rather than exiting 
longs, suggesting portfolios are more defensive but not outright capitulating. His conclusion is 
that "the market is moving into a different volatility environment but not necessarily a more 
bearish one." 
 

 
Fundamental Impact: 
The technology complex absorbed most of the damage. Peter Callahan cites the Nasdaq 
suffered its worst week since Liberation Day, led by semiconductors which posted their longest 
losing streak since 2008. Investor conversations increasingly revolved around whether earnings, 
elections, rates, credit, inflation, product momentum or AI build-out execution represent the 
primary overhang into year-end. Unlike July's purely technical momentum unwind, this decline 
has triggered a genuine debate over the durability of the AI growth story. Nevertheless, several 
valuation metrics have reset materially. Semiconductors have de-rated from roughly 29x forward 
earnings this summer to 17x today, while the Nasdaq now trades near the lowest multiple seen 
since the AI cycle began in 2023. The question for investors is increasingly not whether 
expectations have fallen, but whether they have fallen enough. 
Silver Lining: 
One of the more striking observations was that the weakness beneath the AI surface has not 
translated into indiscriminate selling elsewhere. Louis Miller argues investors should "buy the 
broadening." AI-related spreads may be at their widest levels of the year and AI beneficiaries 
are down roughly 25% from their highs, but the broader market continues to rotate rather than 
collapse. SPX ex-AI has substantially outperformed the headline index, quality factors are 
reaching new highs and non-AI growth opportunities continue to attract capital. Louis notes, "we 
wouldn't confuse skepticism and uncertainty with overvaluation." His team continues to favour 
quality, healthcare and selected international opportunities while viewing the current 
environment as one of rotation rather than market-wide deterioration. 
Catalysts: 
Similarly, Brian Garrett highlights that positioning has already been reset dramatically. US 
long/short net exposure is now at five-year lows, and sentiment has deteriorated sharply. Yet 
breadth is improving, with roughly 350 S&P constituents finishing higher last week and SPX 
ex-AI outperforming the benchmark by nearly 500bps. His central message is straightforward: 
"the next move is more dependent on earnings confirmation than anything else." With 14% of 
the S&P reporting this week and another 44% the following week, earnings are likely to 
determine whether investors regain confidence in AI spending, corporate returns and the 
broader growth outlook. 
 

 
Synthesis: 
Taken together, the message remains markets are no longer debating whether AI matters; they 
are debating how it should be financed, who captures the value and what returns justify the 
scale of investment currently underway. Positioning has been aggressively reset, sentiment is 
near washed-out levels and volatility has surged. The market has shifted from a 
momentum-driven phase to a fundamentally driven one, where earnings validation, capital 
discipline and demonstrable AI returns matter far more than narrative enthusiasm. The coming 
weeks of earnings season will likely determine whether this becomes a temporary growth scare 
or a more lasting regime shift.
