JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Wednesday's session closed near its highs: AI/Tech led and Momentum outperformed, 
while cyclical groups such as machinery, banks, credit cards and hotels also rallied. That 
tone reversed overnight. META first traded higher on its revenue beat and Q4 guide, but it 
has since given back those gains and is -1.9% pre-market. On the headline level, the main 
catalyst was the chatters over renewed Iran kinetic-risk post US mid-term election (NYT 

article “U.S. and Israel Draw Up Plans for Renewed Strikes on Iran After Elections”). 
However, underlying price actions and feedback from our trading desk also suggested the 
still fragile narrative around AI financing concerns and the lack of conviction / re-grossing 
from the recent tech reversal (concerns about how AI spending gets financed are still 
unresolved despite solid prints).  
  
•         META EARNINGS FIRST TAKE (MARK SCHILSKY) – [Mark published below 
immediately after the initial earnings release] C You in January: META is currently trading 
up +3% after hours after producing a Q3 result that I would characterize as inline with 
investor expectations (+26% Y/Y FXN Revneue growth). The Q426 Revneue guide is 
decidedly better than expected – and the high end of the range contemplates a 2-point FXN 
acceleration. That is VERY good. More importantly, Susan provided some 2027 ‘guidance’ 
that will leave some investors wanting as we really got nothing concrete on 2027 Opex or 
Capex. She did state: “Looking ahead, although we are not providing a full-year 2027 
revenue outlook, the benefits our AI investments are delivering to the core business, 
combined with emerging opportunities from model advances and product launches, position 
us to sustain strong overall revenue growth throughout 2027.” Strong overall revenue 
growth sounds good. But how strong? Finally, as leaked by Semafor before the close, 
META is issuing non-voting C-Class shares to all shareholders in January which will 
effectively become a 2:1 stock split. While I think this still means we are likely to get an 
equity raise in the future, this would seem to suggest that we won’t get one until early 2027. 
The overhang lives on! But the Q4 Revenue growth really is great. Focus on that. 
o   Q326 Earnings Results: 
▪  Revenue of $64.6B grew +26% Y/Y reported & FXN. This is slightly 
better than investor expectations which I characterized as ‘~$64B with 
a slight upward bias.’ Call it inline. 
▪  Ad Impressions grew +10% Y/Y. Ad Prices grew +14% Y/Y. 
o   Q426/2026/2027 Guidance: 

▪  Q426 Revenue was guided in the range of $73B to $76B (+22% to 
+27% Y/Y reported – that’s +23% to +28% Y/Y FXN). This is decidedly 
better than investor expectations of a guidance range of $72B to $75B 
with the high end representing+25% Y/Y FXN growth. Put another 
way, the high end of the guide is a 2-point acceleration! That’s a great 
number. 
o   2026 Opex was upped ~$10B due to the legal charges that we all knew 
were coming. Call this inline.  
o   “At a segment level, we now expect Reality Labs losses to decline 
compared to 2025 to a range of $18-19 billion.” 
o   2026 Capex was upped at the lower end of the range by +$5B as 
expected. Now it’s $135B to $145B. Again, inline. 
o   2027 ‘Guidance’: “Looking beyond 2026, we are still developing our budget 
and capacity plans for next year, but we expect to continue investing 
aggressively to support both our core business and emerging AI initiatives. 
Our current capacity and what is scheduled to come online gives us the 
flexibility to allocate resources across model training, core business growth, 
and the development and support of new products. Our AI investments are 
already delivering substantial incremental advertising revenue through 
stronger engagement, better recommendations, and more efficient 
monetization. Building on this progress, our MSL investments are creating the 
technology and infrastructure needed to unlock significant new revenue 
opportunities and strengthen capabilities across the business. While these 
investments will take longer to generate returns than those in our core 
business, the breadth of monetization opportunities and internal efficiencies 
gives us confidence in their long-term potential. Looking ahead, although we 
are not providing a full-year 2027 revenue outlook, the benefits our AI 
investments are delivering to the core business, combined with emerging 
opportunities from model advances and product launches, position us to 
sustain strong overall revenue growth throughout 2027. Separately, we 

remain focused on improving Reality Labs profitability and expect losses to 
narrow next year to around $15 billion as we reallocate investment toward 
other priority areas, primarily AI.” 
o   As leaked by Semafor before the close, the Board approved a new Class C 
non-voting share: “The Board of Directors has approved the establishment of 
a new class of publicly listed, non-voting Class C common stock, with an 
effective date in January 2027…Meta intends to file an amended and restated 
certificate of incorporation in Delaware to establish the Class C common 
stock, with an effective date of January 19, 2027. The Board has also 
declared a one-time stock dividend of one share of Class C common stock for 
each outstanding share of Class A common stock and Class B common 
stock, contingent upon, and to be paid after, the effectiveness of the 
amended and restated certificate of incorporation. The record date and 
payment date of the stock dividend will be determined by the Board at a later 
date. After the payment date of the stock dividend, we expect the market 
price for our Class A common stock to reflect the effect of a two-for-one stock 
split. The stock dividend will not change the voting power of any shareholder.”

•         Market Backdrop:  
o   Hyperscaler spreads are retesting their July wides heading into earnings, 
but the move this time is idiosyncratic rather than broad. HY AI is leading the 
sell-off with the J.P. Morgan HY AI basket trading at all-time wides and 97bps 
wider than its widest level in July. Most of the move is led by CoreWeave.  
o   The selloff has fed through to IG unevenly, with Oracle widening far more 
than the rest given its lower rating, pointing to a reactionary move around 
broader AI-ecosystem fears rather than credit concerns specific to the 
hyperscalers themselves (JPM Daily Credit Strategy Update, 20-Oct-26).  
o   Supply is not driving the widening, with IG hyperscalers largely absent 
since the $25bn Google deal on 6-Aug. Unlike July, this is not an anticipation-
of-supply widening as in that case all hyperscalers would be widening, which 
would have shown up across all hyperscalers rather than the uneven move 
seen now.  
o   Rates added to the pressure with last week’s hot CPI print raising concerns 
that the Fed would tighten faster than anticipated. This impacts the more 
debt-dependent segments first, namely HY neoclouds and by implication HG 
hyperscalers (JPM Daily Credit Strategy Update, 19-Oct-26).  
•         Trade Rationale:  
o   Long side: Hyperscaler fundamentals are robust. Hyperscaler debt is up 
$117bn YTD, yet the cohort's average rating improved modestly because the 
new debt went mostly to higher-rated borrowers. J.P. Morgan Research sees 
the widening as overdone unless earnings show fundamental weakness. If 
the selloff is contagion from HY rather than a hyperscaler-specific problem, 
solid results should let IG spreads retrace.  
o   Short side: Earnings releases set the hyperscaler wides over the summer. 
Hyperscaler issuance could also return after earnings, which would make 
supply a driver again. With HY AI at all-time wides, further contagion into IG 

would run through the weaker links, Oracle first. A short protects against 
capex guidance or jumbo deals that renew funding concerns.  
  
US MKT INTEL VIEW – we published below in the October 19 Morning Briefing 
•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – Last week's sell-off ran against our tactical bullish call, so we 
did a reassessment on our view and risk factors. In our Oct 12 Morning Briefing, we flagged 
below as key risk factors “(i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI 
Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer 
Data”. Of the risks we flagged, the Momentum / Tech Unwind and AI Credit Fears were the 
main drivers for last week’s selloff, while the fundamental picture remained encouraging. 
ASML and TSMC delivered healthy earnings and consumer data stayed resilient. We 
therefore retain our tactical bullish stance. The underlying support from earnings and 
macro resilience remains intact, and the AI sell-off has not come with evidence of 
deteriorating earnings in the results reported so far. That said, we don't expect the 
rotation out of Tech to end abruptly, though its pace could moderate. We favor 
expressing our constructive growth and earnings view through Cyclical exposure outside 
Tech, while selectively buying Tech weakness, particularly in Semis and Memory, 
potentially through defined-risk options structures. Large-cap Banks are another area of 
interest. Earnings were generally positive, and the recent sharp pullback could offer an 
opportunity to participate in a recovery in XLF. Heading into a much busier reporting week, 
broadening and AI concerns remain the two key themes for investors, and all eyes are on 
META earnings and NVDA's GTC Berlin. 
•         RISKS (Unchanged WoW) – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; 
(iii) AI Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker 
Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – Following last week's sell-off, we revisit our three key catalysts: (i) 
Macro / Fed: We think the inflation component of this week’s PMIs will be critical. On Oct 
28 Fed (note that the Fed has entered its blackout period), a hold could provide relief by 
removing residual near-term hike risk, but the bigger focus will be any remarks on the FY27 
policy path. (ii) Rates Stabilization: Lower Treasury yields have already delivered part of 

our anticipated catalyst. From here, we would look for sustained moderation in rates vol and 
no renewed yield spike to support the broader rotation. (iii) Tech Earnings / AI 
Monetization: META's results and NVDA's GTC Berlin will be important tests of revenue 
delivery, spending plans and demand visibility. Beyond the hyperscalers, evidence that AI 
investment is broadening and generating measurable revenue across other sectors would 
strengthen the case for a more durable AI trade. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 19) – Following last week's sell-off, we favor a more 
selective approach to expressing our bullish view. We retain a preference for Tech and for 
exposure to a potential rebound through both pairs trades and derivatives. Within Cyclicals, 
we expect the rotation to continue. We remain constructive on Financials and Retailers, 
given consumer resilience and their relatively limited direct exposure to AI-related concerns. 
Internationally, we continue to favor Brazil and Japan. We are also monitoring Reopening 
opportunities that could benefit from lower energy prices and rotation away from AI. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with 
a preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 
Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your 
shopping list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and 
Retailers given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy 
prices. Other elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower energy 
prices. Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and Japan. 
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long 
but would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / 
yields, which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, there 
is likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the Tech tape. 
Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus bullish play. The 
AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we like Tech in the 
near-term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we think you need to 
see the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; excluding AI-related 
plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper yield curve, and 
favorable capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real Estate, Telecom, and 
Utilities may participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most likely to lag the SPX, 

ex-AI plays. The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which would squeeze EU and 
RTY higher; plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, Cruise Lines, 
Discretionary, Hotels, etc) is another area of interest with a preference to play via options.
