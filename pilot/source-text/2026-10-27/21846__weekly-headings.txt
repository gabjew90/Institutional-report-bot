Another Sports Equinox! On Monday, October 26, baseball, football, basketball, and 
hockey will all take the field on the same night. For sports fans, it’s a rare occurrence, 
happening only 32 other times since 1971, with all four major sports leagues competing 
for fans’ attention and creating the ultimate channel-surfing bonanza. Investors are 
heading into a “Sports Equinox” of their own. The next two weeks bring an unusually 
crowded lineup of potential market-moving catalysts, including a Fed policy meeting, the 
busiest stretch of 3Q26 earnings season, the Treasury’s Quarterly Refunding 
Announcement, and the midterm elections. Together, these events could shape 
expectations for economic growth, earnings, interest rates, and Fed policy in the weeks 
and months ahead. Below, we highlight the key developments we’re watching and why 
they matter for financial markets.
• Fed Officials ‘Face Off’ Over The Next Rate Move | Despite hotter-than-expected inflation data, markets are pricing just a 10% probability 
of a rate hike, down from ~70% following last month’s hike, heading into next Wednesday’s FOMC meeting. While recent remarks from Fed 
officials Williams and Jefferson suggest little urgency to tighten further, solid economic momentum (consensus expects 3Q GDP to 
accelerate to 3.0% SAAR) and broadening price pressures could complicate an October pause. Under the previous Fed, odds this low would 
have made a decision-day surprise unlikely. But with inflation above the Fed’s 2.0% target for more than five years and several 
policymakers signaling further tightening may be needed, the October meeting could be more ‘live’ under the Warsh-led Fed than market 
pricing implies. Assuming no change in policy, the post-meeting press conference will likely be brief. Our base case remains one additional 
hike in December, followed by a prolonged pause. Still, a hawkish dissent in October would not be surprising: four FOMC members penciled 
in two additional hikes by year-end in the September dot plot, while persistent inflation and oil at ~$100/barrel keep risks tilted hawkish.
• Major Tech-Related Earnings ‘On Deck’ | Next week marks the busiest week of 3Q26 earnings season, with 172 companies representing 
37% of the S&P 500 market cap reporting. While still early, Q3 results are off to a strong start, with S&P 500 earnings up ~29% YoY, marking 
the fifth consecutive quarter of double-digit EPS growth, the longest streak since 2018. Meanwhile, 82% of companies are beating EPS 
estimates, above the previous 10-year average of 77%. The spotlight now shifts to the three largest hyperscalers, Alphabet, Amazon, and 
Microsoft, as investors assess whether AI monetization can keep pace with rapid investment, with cloud revenue, capex guidance, and free 
cash flow in focus. With hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending expected to surpass $1T in 2027, strong results and guidance will be critical 
to sustaining confidence in the AI ecosystem amid elevated expectations and growing financing concerns. The results will also test the 
earnings growth underpinning our 8,450 12-month S&P 500 target. Beyond tech, Visa, Mastercard, Hilton and Starbucks will offer a read on 
consumer resilience, while UPS and FedEx should provide a real-time pulse on broader economic activity.
• Will The Treasury Go On The ‘Offense’ With Bond Supply? | The November 4 Quarterly Refunding Announcement (QRA) could be an 
important catalyst for rates as the Treasury Department updates its long-end liquidity buyback program and upcoming borrowing needs. 
While the Treasury remains committed to “regular and predictable issuance,” recent market chatter suggests it could trim longer-maturity 
issuance and potentially eliminate new 20-year bonds, which were reintroduced in 2020. Less long-end supply could temporarily support 
bond prices and lower yields, but it would not reduce overall government borrowing needs. Instead, issuance would shift to bills or other 
shorter maturities, potentially increasing refinancing risk. While supply can influence short-term yield movements, economic growth and 
inflation remain the more durable forces shaping the rate outlook.
• Republicans Apply A ‘Full Court Press’ To Salvage The Midterms | With less than two weeks left before the midterm elections, early 
voting results point to strong enthusiasm for Democrats. That is not entirely surprising, as the party not in power typically picks up seats in 
the House and Senate during the midterm elections, particularly when the President’s approval rating is low. And with President Trump’s 
approval rating near a record low of 39%, Democrats are in a solid position to at least regain control of the House. Polling suggests 
Democrats have the advantage amid broad voter dissatisfaction with the economy and ongoing affordability issues. As a result, our 
Washington Policy team sees Democrats well positioned to win the House, while Senate control remains a toss-up, leaving a Democratic 
sweep as the most likely scenario. Regardless of the outcome, the S&P 500 has historically performed well after the midterms. In fact, the 
index has gained ~15% on average in the 12 months following the midterms and was positive 100% of the time dating back to 1942.

Economy
•
Builder sentiment fell more than expected in October to 29, its lowest level since June 2012, while the share of builders cutting prices 
reached 43%—the highest share since COVID. Sentiment remains pressured from elevated mortgage rates and construction costs.
•
According to the National Retail Foundation, while 56% of consumers say economic conditions will influence their holiday spending, 
that spending is projected to exceed $1T, up 4.2% YoY—the fastest growth since 2021, suggesting consumer resilience.
•
Initial jobless claims remained below 200k for the sixth consecutive week, signaling continued strength in the labor market. Despite 
signs of moderating hiring activity, historically low claims reinforces the labor market’s underlying strength.
•
Focus of the Week: The first estimate GDP growth for the third quarter of the year is on deck this Thursday, and we expect an 
annualized quarterly growth rate of 3.5%, matching the Atlanta Fed’s latest GDPNow estimate and above FactSet consensus estimates 
of 3.0%. Meanwhile, we expect the September headline and core PCE price index to have risen 3.6% and 3.1% YoY, respectively. 
Equity
•
Market leadership has reversed sharply from September to October. September's rise in Treasury yields fueled a narrow equity rally 
during the month concentrated in Tech and Communication Services as investors gravitated toward the earnings visibility and secular 
growth profile of AI-related companies. In contrast, the reversal in rates in October in addition to building AI spending concerns have 
supported a rotation in market performance. As a result, Tech and Comm Services have each declined more than 4% month-to-date, 
while the S&P 500 Equal Weight Index has outperformed the cap-weighted index by nearly 4%, its strongest monthly outperformance 
since February. The shift suggests the historical relationship between interest rates and market breadth may be reasserting itself, with 
falling yields encouraging broader participation rather than continued concentration in a small group of mega-cap growth stocks.
•
Despite the recent volatility, we remain constructive on Tech and expect it to regain leadership moving forward. The recent weakness 
in Technology share prices has not been accompanied by a corresponding deterioration in fundamentals. 2027 EPS estimates have 
been revised ~2% higher since the start of the month, and EPS growth for the Technology sector is still expected to outpace the S&P 
500 through at least 2Q28. We expect this theme to be reinforced as earnings season ramps up over the coming weeks which should 
help improve sentiment for the sector, particularly as tech valuations relative to the S&P 500 are near the lowest level since 2020.
•
Focus of the Week: Next week, earnings season shifts into high gear with 172 companies representing 37% of the S&P 500 market cap 
reporting results highlighted by three hyperscalers (MSFT, AMZN, GOOG) to gauge AI monetization and AI infrastructure spending.
Fixed Income
•
The rolling 90-day correlation between WTI crude and the 10-year yield has retreated from its historic peak in late summer. While the 
correlation remains positive, renewed risk aversion and haven demand are supporting Treasuries, pushing the 10-year yield below 5% 
for the first time in a month. With little economic data and the Fed in a communications blackout, the 10-year yield saw its largest daily 
drop (-17 bps) since August 2024. After seven months of rising yields, the 10-year yield is set for its steepest monthly decline (-32 bps) 
since February 2025. The reversal is a reminder that Treasuries can still provide diversification when stocks come under stress.
•
Credit spreads have widened modestly, but within investment grade, weakness remains concentrated in Tech and AI-related issuers. IG 
spreads are +7 bps on the month to 87 bps, still historically tight and in line with their three-year average, while IG Tech spreads have 
widened 15 bps to 102 bps, their widest since June 2023 and the biggest gap to the broader index on record. Still, falling Treasury yields 
have cushioned the impact of wider spreads, with the Bloomberg US Corporate IG Index up 2.0% on the month. 
•
Focus of the Week: In addition to the Fed, the BOC, BOJ and ECB are set to meet with markets pricing in a hold by all but the BOC.
Washington Policy
•
The US-Iran conflict remains a key geopolitical risk facing markets heading into the midterms, with expanding US military preparations 
and heightened coordination with Israel reinforcing concerns about resumption of strikes after the November 3 elections. Reports this 
week indicated that additional US military assets are being positioned in the region. Defense Secretary Hegseth's recent visit to Israel 
and discussions with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu further underscore the heightened military readiness. The situation may be 
further complicated by next Tuesday's election in Israel, as current polls point to Netanyahu's coalition losing.  The risk of escalation is 
consequential for markets as Iran continues to threaten retaliation against energy sites and other economically critical infrastructure. 
Iranian officials have warned that renewed strikes could trigger retaliation against data centers and AI facilities in the region.
International
•
As France’s 10-year government bond yield topped 5% for the first time since 2002, in the backdrop are persistent fiscal pressures. For 
the fourth straight year, France is set to post a budget deficit exceeding 5% of GDP, much worse than the 3% cap that all euro zone 
members are obligated to abide by. According to France’s independent fiscal watchdog, even if GDP growth were to pick up from 0.5% 
this year to 1.0% in 2027—more optimistic than the IMF’s projected 0.7%—the budget deficit would barely improve to 5% of GDP. 
•
The lack of political will to tackle the deficit reflects gridlock ahead of the April 2027 presidential election. The candidate who leads the 
polls—Marine Le Pen—is a far-right populist who supports high levels of social spending. Her main opponent, Jean-Luc Melenchon, is 
on the far left but shares Le Pen’s lack of fiscal restraint. Regardless of who wins, the next president will need to deal with a parliament 
that is splintered, in roughly equal portions, between left, right, and center parties. This makes it difficult to obtain a majority for either 
spending cuts or tax hikes. If France continues to run deficits on par with current levels, we would expect yields to drift higher.

LEADING ECONOMIC INDEX | The Leading Economic Index (LEI) is a monthly composite statistic published by The Conference Board that predicts future shifts in the business cycle, typically looking six to

they become evident in the overall economy.
MICHIGAN CONSUMER SENTIMENT INDEX | The Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index (MCSI) is a monthly survey-based economic indicator measuring US consumer confidence regarding their personal
