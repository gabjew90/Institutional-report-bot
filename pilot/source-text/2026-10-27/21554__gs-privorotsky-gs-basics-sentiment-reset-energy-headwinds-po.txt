GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Sentiment Reset. Energy Headwinds.
Positioning Color 22 Oct 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
22 Oct 2026
Sizeable reversal overnight. KOSPI down ~3.5%, China flirting with a bear market and significant
weakness across the semiconductor complex. A little surprising following yesterday’s rally on SpaceX and
Supermicro, with what felt like a series of supportive data points for AI.
What’s gone wrong? A few things. 1.) LRCX delivered strong numbers but struggled to clear elevated
whispers, with guidance offering limited upside (semi capex under pressure Tokyo Electron -6.99%). 2.)
Tesla actually cut FY capex guidance from >$25bn to ~$20bn, completely out of sync with what SpaceX
was telling us earlier this week. Could be sequencing, but the market doesn’t like capex going backwards.
3.) Meta was objectively pretty good, lifting the bottom end of its FY capex guidance to $135bn, but that
implies an extraordinary ~$49–59bn of spending in Q4. Presumably they have visibility, but an ambitious
ramp nonetheless. 4.) TI looked very good, with broad based analog recovery and robust data center
demand, yet the stock isn’t doing much. 5.) DeepSeek’s open source DeepInfer promises materially better
inference efficiency on existing infrastructure, effectively unlocking capacity and raising questions around
how much incremental compute is really required. None of this feels thesis breaking, but it is enough to
unsettle a fragile tape.
Cybersecurity: Two critical vulnerabilities discovered in PHP, the foundational software underlying a huge
portion of the internet. Both carry 9.2/10 severity scores, with remote code execution possible and
emergency patches released. This is pretty foundational stuff, and potentially another complication for risk
sentiment. Curious whether cybersecurity finally catches a bid.
Rates: Bonds firmly bid as AI sells off. We’ve seen this playbook repeatedly now… AI weakness reprices
the capex cycle, takes some pressure off prospective issuance and increasingly becomes a growth shock.
The reflexivity is striking. The very thing creating the equity problem is helping solve the rates problem.
Feedback: Spent the last few days meeting with institutional accounts in New York, and the prevailing
mood around tech is defined by fatigue and caution. It has been tough sledding. AI has been virtually the
only narrative driving equity performance and managing through the chop since mid year/particularly over
the past few weeks has proven challenging. Aggregated prime flow data indicated one of the most
pronounced net tech reductions in recent memory, reflecting a swift institutional de-risking. My sense is
that managers have cut net exposure while trying to keeping gross intact; participants are far less heavily
allocated to AI names than earlier this year, though I met none that are outright short. Heading into year
end, the setup is dominated by capital preservation and locking in performance. Most still believe the
demand side of AI is robust, but questions around capital availability, the ability to energize capacity and
the sustainability of spending have all entered the conversation.

Energy: Clear signs of de-escalation hopes fading, diplomatic pathways narrowing and press reports
suggesting renewed escalation could follow the midterms. This complicates the usual playbook.
Historically equity markets have traded positively coming out of midterm elections, but we’ve now
effectively stapled another potential escalation event to the calendar. Hard to see how simply clearing the
election removes the risk premium when retaliation against energy infrastructure remains a very real
possibility. Equities have done their best to ignore punitive energy prices, but at these levels they remain a
significant headwind and one more reason for people to stay cautious.
Risk: Confidence has clearly been shaken, yet the demand side of AI still looks remarkably robust. Token
consumption continues to grow, B200 compute pricing remains extraordinarily firm (breaking out in spot
mkt) and SpaceX is talking about sub one year paybacks on new compute deployments. Meanwhile, we
have a series of almost unfalsifiable concerns. First, people worry the industry simply cannot deliver the
projected gigawatts… some estimates are in the high 40s for 2027, while actual delivery could be closer to
the low 20s. Then the exact opposite concern, that we deliver too much capacity and inference demand
cannot absorb it. I’m not sure how we can simultaneously be worried about both, but somehow we are.
Add midterm/energy risk, rates, credit stress and sovereign spreads in France, and there is plenty for the
wall of worry bucket. I would have thought the hyperscalers remain the critical anchor for equities. They
continue to tell you demand is extraordinarily robust, monetization is accelerating and the bridge towards
more organic revenue and free cash flow may be arriving faster than expected. But it’s a difficult risk
environment, with credit increasingly setting the tone. At a bigger picture level, we’ve moved from a
savings glut to a spending glut, with capital competing for returns across the economy. That probably
demands lower multiples and greater certainty around future cash flows. Neither is particularly helpful for
equities in the short term. Credit spreads and the availability of financing are becoming increasingly
important signals to watch.
side-note: AI exposure very low on 1yr lookback but still above historical baseline
