Network Thoughts: Strong Q3 Earnings
Overwhelmed By Positioning Unwind
Simon Penn
23-Oct-2026, 11:10 ET (EDT)
• Strong Q3 earnings are being overwhelmed by positioning unwind
• Equity index volatility is finally starting to move higher
• US AI positioning is more selective, with hedge funds trading relative value within the AI winners
• French budget fears elevate yields and spreads
• French GDP is insufficient to withstand much higher OAT yields
• APAC tech’s sell-off looks more like a low-volume confidence shock than a fundamental break
• G10 FX volatility will like rise into the next wave of central bank decisions
(Contributors: Greg Poole, Kieran Diamond, Josh Guilliard, Simon Penn, Niall MacLeod, Dimitrios Laloudakis

Strong Q3 earnings are being overwhelmed by positioning unwind. Nearly 25% of S&P market cap has
reported. 78% of companies are beating expectations. Revenues are growing 12.5% and earnings are tracking
to 31% higher y/y. Every sector is showing positive growth, but only Energy and Technology are outpacing
S&P growth. This earnings strength has been overwhelmed by hedge fund unwinds: they are selling AI
Winners/Momentum. The size of the derisking is comparable to July and has been overshadowed by a big
surge in short exposure. Since September, long exposure has fallen from 90th to 15th percentile, while shorts
have risen from the 15th to 80th percentile. Nets are now in the 25th percentile, and performance has suffered:
UBS Knowledge Network estimates that in October so far, equity long/short strategies are down 7% and quant
funds are flat. Despite the intense unwind, the flow picture has been supportive: on Thursday, there was $52
bn of excess S&P buy flow and only $25 bn of excess sell flow in the Nasdaq (mostly from systematic funds),
and, importantly, UBS retail market making bought the dip for the fifth time this month, mostly in ETFs, Tech,
and Semiconductors. Looking forward, US equity strategist Keith Parker thinks circuit breakers include:
hyperscaler cloud beats, no hike from the Fed with less hawkish rhetoric, Iran and oil and buybacks; Keith likes
names with higher growth and CFROI that trade at a discount (UBEQHGCD).
Equity index volatility is finally starting to move higher. Having remained surprisingly calm through the
September bond-market disruption, equity implied volatilities have started to re-awaken, with the VIX this week
breaking back above 20 vols, and the VSTOXX rising to a five-month high. Index implied volatility has been
rising faster than stock volatility, corresponding to a rebound in implied correlation measures. This suggests
that the market is pricing for a more correlated end to the year, relative to the extreme de-correlation seen
through the first three quarters. Put-wing skews have also started to re-steepen, alongside a pick-up in
hedging interest, as traded notional in S&P puts hits an all-time high. With the US index still trading less than
2% from all-time highs, investors looking to hedge against a break lower into year-end should consider put
spreads.

US AI positioning is more selective, with hedge funds trading relative value within the AI winners. Long
selling of semiconductors drove this month’s degrossing, but the past two days have been all about long
selling of hyperscalers on concerns about overspending on capex. Buyers have started to return to
semiconductors, memory and hardware stocks, but a significant rise in shorting of names within AI Winners
suggests a reluctance to reload on "AI Beta". Total US Net Exposure has fallen to 25th percentile, driven by a
collapse in long positioning (15th percentile today from 90th percentile in September) and a rise in short
positioning (15th percentile to 80% percentile this month). Estimates show losses of 6.7% so far this month for
US discretionary equity hedge funds, while moves of the past two days cost quants all their gains for the
month.
Europe: Short exposure has remained close to the 100th percentile (over five years) for much of the year,
while long exposure is at less than 5th percentile, despite overall gross exposure remaining above the 90th
percentile. This leaves European positioning particularly susceptible to squeeze risk from a covering rally,
geopolitics and the macro backdrop permitting. Equity long/short funds are down 2.8% for the month.
Japan: De-grossing remains broader, with 3.5% net outflows this week and Tech the most sold sector as the
unwind continues. Tech is a significant drag on performance and continues to contribute approximately 35% of
Japan net exposure. Equity long/short funds are down 8.2% for the month.
French budget fears elevate yields and spreads. The French 10y yield added around 20bp this week (and
at one stage was up 34bp). The OAT spread over Bunds widened as much as 40bp on the week, and OATs
yielded more than 100bp over Spain. The French parliament’s objections to Prime Minister Lecornu’s budget
have been far more aggressive than expected. A number of critical budget articles have already been
amended, deleted or rejected. By late week more than €4 bn of planned budget improvements had been
discarded, and with the anticipated rejections within the social security plans, that’s likely to finish as more
than €10 bn. Instead of dropping the deficit from 5.4% of GDP to 5.0%, the deficit is likely to remain
unchanged – based on 1.0% GDP growth next year. This year GDP is likely to be only 0.5%. The remaining
risk is execution. Further parliamentary concessions could weaken consolidation again, while resorting to
Article 49.3 would expose the government politically. Even with all the amendments it remains uncertain if
Lecornu’s government can pass a 2027 budget. The worst case would be rolling over the 2026 budget, which
would likely not contain enough spending cuts to satisfy markets.
French GDP is insufficient to withstand much higher OAT yields. France’s trend growth is around 1.0%
and possibly less, and this year’s GDP is likely to be just 0.5%. The 10y OAT real yield, however, is 3.20% --
some 220bp higher. That’s a huge mismatch. By contrast, the 10y real rate in Spain is 1.90% against potential
growth of around 2.0% (and expected growth of 2.6% this year). The ECB, meanwhile, is expected to raise its
deposit rate in mid-December and again next spring to 3.00%. For Spain, with GDP not much less than that
and inflation at 5% such ECB policy makes sense. But for France, one-size-fits all policy, where inflation is 3%,

APAC tech’s sell-off looks more like a low-volume confidence shock than a fundamental break. Korean
cash returns could provide the next catalyst. This week’s APAC decline has been unusually severe relative to
the news: positioning was less stretched than during the summer unwind and, beyond concerns around AI
financing and data-centre investment, fundamental deterioration has been limited. Hedge funds remain
reluctant to re-engage after repeated P&L reversals. UBS prime brokerage estimates October equity
long/short performance is down 8.0% in APAC and 8.7% in Japan. That loss of confidence, combined with
thinner liquidity, has amplified the downside and raised questions about who provides the bid. There are two
potential sources: Korea, where both memory companies report next week, and China’s ‘National Team’. For
Korea, UBS estimates Samsung Electronics’ free-cash-flow yield at 22% for 2026 and 35% for 2027; SK Hynix
is at 20% and 34%, respectively. Together, those forecasts imply unusually strong cash generation. Evidence
that both companies will convert that cash into higher shareholder returns could bring investors back. For
China’s National Team, UBS estimates they have been steady buyers since mid-October. Friday’s 7% intraday
swing, as an early sell-off became an end-of-day recovery, was widely attributed to ‘National Team’ buying.
This remains a potent force in a risk-averse market.
G10 FX volatility will like rise into the next wave of central bank decisions. EM FX implied volatility spiked
to levels last seen in March, when US-Iran tensions were last at a peak, fueled mostly from the LatAm
components, primarily the election-induced volatility in BRL and the long MXN unwind given a dovish pivot
from Banxico. As outlined in the FX Flow Monitor, short USDMXN has been crowded given its quality carry
profile and still has room to grind higher given the sizable legacy MXN longs combined CTA signals flipping
long. It could now be the turn of G10 currencies: they typically lag rises in EM FX vol and as the G10 central
banking period begins (RBNZ on Oct. 27, FOMC and BoC on Oct. 28, ECB on Oct. 29, and BoJ on Oct. 30)
with divergent policy paths and updated monetary policy reports/forecasts to be delivered, the likelihood for
G10 implied volatility to rise appears likely. All G10 FX 1m and 3m implied volatilities come in at 6.7vols each
(below the 5y average of around 8.1vols. Interestingly, the ATM vol curve is flat all while rest of 4Q 2026
encompasses a litany of macro catalysts such as the UK fiscal budget, OATs induced stress, and US midterm
elections.
