Asia Open - Look out below
By Preston Pagli
A sharp flight-to-quality theme dominated markets as investors aggressively de-risked amid a deepening tech-led equity
selloff and escalating geopolitical tensions. The move sparked a rotation into USTs, driving 10y yields more than 16bps
lower and back below 5% Notably, the rally in rates came despite another surge in crude oil, with breakevens little
changed as markets appeared far less focused on renewed inflation. In FX, USDJPY fell ~0.82%, a two-week low while
the euro retraced after a sharp overnight drawdown with the OAT-Bund spread reaching ~175bps. Looking ahead, global
PMI's begin with Australia at 23:00 BST, then to Japan at 01:30 BST and CPI at 00:30 BST. UK retail sales prints at
07:00 BST, and France and Germany PMI at 08:15 and 08:30 respectively.

In Focus‌
Rates: USTs rallied sharply, with 10y yields moving ~16bps lower and below 5.00% for the first time in nearly a month as the
tech selloff deepened and investors sought safety. Brent crude surged 4% though break-evens traded nearly unchanged,
reinforcing a market repricing growth rather than inflation. Positioning suggests the move still has room to run. Citi's
David Bieber notes structural shorts are at risk of a squeeze above 106-00 in TY futures, with 10s currently trading above that
level at 106-08. On the flow side, activity remains skewed toward upside duration exposure with call buying in the belly and
call skew flipping bullish. France was the major stress point today with the OAT-Bund 10y spread surging ~15 bps to ~175
bps, the widest since 2011.
US Treasury Auction Recap: $26 billion of 5-year TIPS. The auction tailed the 1pm market by 0.7bp to yield 2.367%. The
bid/cover ratio came in at 2.41 which is at the 27th percentile of the past 12 auctions 
Equities: The NDX (-2.9%) faced its worst day dating back to June after a sharp sell off across semi's with SOX -6.7%, and
27% below the ATH. In CTA's the NDX flipped back short with the max short level at $28,489 not too far away. SPX (-1.21%)
teetered around the flip short level at 7638, with >65% lower. On the desk, buying flows were skewed to Industrials, Energy
and Materials.and selling in Info Tech, Financials, Healthcare, Consumer Discretionary, Comm Services. Citi AI Supply
Bottleneck (CGUSBOTL) basket dropped 7%. Our equity trading strategists Vishal Vivek flags just how meaningful some of
the moves were, with Citi's Growth basket (CISUGROW) declining -6%, the second-worst day in the past 5 years, and among
the worst in 10 years. OpenAI is close to finalizing an up to $30bn funding around at a $1.25 trillion valuation. 

Commodities: Crude oil surged to a five-month-high amid escalation fears with the US ordering an evacuation of its Saudi
Arabia embassy. Despite oil flows out of the region recovering, price action is clearly pricing in a tighter for longer view. Brent
trades +4% to $113 while WTI is 2% higher to $99. Our commodities research desk covered their recent discussions with
clients and found a lack of compelling risk-reward across the broader oil complex as geopolitics is widely viewed as too binary
to position with high conviction. In the precious metals complex, silver slumped nearly 6% while gold fell 3% on a move
toward the critical psychological support at $4,000 (ref $4,022). 
FX: USD traded nearly unchanged, underpinning headline price action with a deep rotation out of US equities and into US FI,
supporting DXY at the 101.6 level. USDJPY was the best performer of the day, reaching 156.80 on RM flows. EMFX
displayed notable weakness, particularly oil importing currencies.Overall interbank FX volumes were approximately in line
with the average with increased activity in EURNOK and EURSEK, while in Asia, USDHKD trading activity was 100% greater
than average. Net aggregate flows during that time frame were shifted towards buying of EUR and ILS and selling of HKD
and JPY.
Elsewhere‌
GBP: GfK Consumer Confidence at 00:01 BST for October; Retail Sales at 07:00 BST for September; S&P Global UK PMIs
at 09:30 BST for October P. Bloomberg cons. expects retail sales to moderate t 1% YoY with ex-auto also easing towards
1.5% YoY vs 2.7% prior. PMIs should remain in expansionary territory (services at 51.8), so more interesting will be
anecdotes on price and labor trends within the report.
JPY: National CPI YoY at 00:30 BST for September. Bloomberg consensus expects a slight acceleration towards 2.1% YoY
with core (ex-fresh food, energy) also picking up towards 2.3%. Recent BoJ speak have been warning of upside inflation risks
but our base case remains for a move in Dec. This suggests rangebound trading in the short term.
EUR: Preliminary CPI starting with France (08:15 BST) followed by Germany (08:30) and Eurozone (09:00). Strong PMIs in
Sep were largely driven by surprise upticks in the services sector, with Eurozone composite jumping to 53.1. Watch whether
upside trends continue, or whether reports highlight any recent bond/fiscal woes. ECB's Cipollone speaks in Turin at 15:00
BST.
SGD: CPI YoY at 06:00 BST for September. Our economists expect an unchanged headline print at 2.3% YoY with core a tad
higher, also at 2.3% YoY (vs 2.2% prior). After MAS’ surprise decision to hold, our economists think risks remained skewed
slightly towards another extended steepening in H1 2027. Sticky inflation should reinforce this view.
TWD: Industrial Production YoY at 09:00 BST for September. IP is expected to print solid at 24.6% YoY, vs 23.47% prior (Citi
e.).
HUF/THB: Local holiday.
