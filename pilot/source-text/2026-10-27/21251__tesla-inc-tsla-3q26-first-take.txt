Tesla Inc. (TSLA): 3Q26 First Take
21 October 2026 | 5:05PM EDT
Synopsis 
We expect investors to have a positive initial view of the report given progress in 
several areas including autonomy (e.g. FSD attach rates on new vehicle deliveries 
in North America reached over 75%, and robotaxi unsupervised miles continued 
to grow double digits week on week), demand (e.g. comments about having 
strong backlog in both Auto and Energy) and gross profits (with the gross margin 
better than our expectation in all three segments). Opex was higher than we had 
estimated, Energy revenue was lower than our estimate, and the company is still 
iterating on Optimus, but on balance our first take is positive. 
We expect key questions on the call to relate to the pace of deployments in physical 
AI related businesses, the trajectory of capex/FCF, and margins.  
Results 
Tesla (TSLA; Neutral) reported 3Q26 revenue of $28,046 mn (down 1% qoq and 
about flat yoy) which was 4% below GS at $29,330 mn, about in line with the Street 
(FactSet) at $28,057 mn, and 1% below company-compiled consensus at $28,341. 
The total company gross margin (including SBC) was 19.6%, above GS at 18.5%, 
company-compiled consensus at 17.9%, and the Street (FactSet) at 17.5%. The 
2Q26 margin was 16.8%, and 3Q25 was 18.0%. 
The automotive non-GAAP gross margin (including SBC, excluding regulatory credits) 
was 18.1%, above both GS at 16.3% and the Street at 16.8%.  
EBIT (including SBC) of ~$718 mn (2.6% of sales) was below our forecast of ~$1,043 
mn (3.6% of sales), and the Street at $1,473 (5.3% of sales), and about in line with 
company-compiled consensus of $721 mn (2.5% of sales). The earnings deck cites 
higher opex and lower regulatory credit revenue as the main drivers of lower EBIT, 
partially offset by improved mix and growth in FSD and Services. 
Company-reported non-GAAP diluted EPS (excluding SBC and non-cash mark to 
market adjustments) was $0.48, $0.04 above GS at $0.44 and $0.03 above both the 
Street and company-compiled consensus at $0.45. 
Cash, cash equivalents, and investments were up by $1.9 bn qoq to $45.4 bn, with 
FCF of $1,531 mn in 3Q. Capex was $4.7 bn, below our estimate of $8.0 bn. Recall 
Tesla had expected over $25 bn of capex for the year.

Equity Research
 
 

On automotive, the company highlighted its strong deliveries as being supported by 
record deliveries in several markets including Canada, Australia, Japan, and Taiwan. It 
also highlighted the start of volume production of Tesla Semi.  
On FSD, Tesla FSD active subscribers grew to 1.74 mn (up 18% qoq and 67% yoy), and 
the company noted FSD adoption reached record attach rates of >75% on new vehicle 
deliveries in North America (up from >55% in 2Q). Tesla also highlighted several 
European approvals received for FSD in the quarter.  
On Robotaxi, Tesla highlighted the roll out of its Cybercab purpose-built AV in Austin. 
Total paid unsupervised/fully driverless robotaxi miles were 3.1 mn as of October 18th 
(up from 1 mn as of September 3rd 2026 and ~380K as of the 2Q earnings call). Tesla 
added San Antonio and Jacksonville to its upcoming cities list (in addition to Phoenix and 
Las Vegas, which were previously disclosed). 
On robotics, Optimus began production in Fremont on high-volume lines. Tesla believes 
its manufacturing process for Optimus is suitable for a commercial product, although it 
continues to iterate on the hardware and manufacturing process.  
On Energy, Megafactory Texas started production. Recall Megafactory Texas will 
produce the Megablock product and Megapack.  
On compute, the company expects to reach 400 MW of compute by year-end (from 
~265 MW currently). Tesla also started construction on its Austin Fab with equipment 
commissioning expected in 2027. 
On battery efforts, the company highlighted that battery availability remains a limiting 
factor in the global vehicle production ramp but is continuing efforts for pack capacity in 
Berlin, cathode production and lithium refining in Texas, and LFP cells in Nevada for 
Energy.  
Outlook 
Tesla commented that its passenger vehicle backlog is the highest in four years, and its 
Energy backlog is at a record high.  
Tesla noted that it has begun production of Tesla Semi in Nevada and Optimus in 
Fremont. Additionally, capacity build-out and ramp of multi-year initiatives including 
compute, solar, battery material, and semiconductor manufacturing are underway.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
