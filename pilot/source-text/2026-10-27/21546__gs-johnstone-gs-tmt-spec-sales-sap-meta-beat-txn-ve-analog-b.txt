pushes back; TELIA solid; RELX solid; PSON inline; NOKIA debate

22 Oct 2026
GS TMT SPEC SALES – Thursday 22nd October 
US market overnight: US equities moved higher, with the QQQs up 1.1% and semis outperforming by 2.2%
on positive updates from SpaceX and Super Micro alongside constructive AI commentary, although the
rebound felt modest after recent weakness. Software gained 1.8%, led by cybersecurity stocks following an
AI-discovered PHP vulnerability. Note this did not follow through is Asia as KOSPI down ~3.5%, China flirting
with a bear market and significant weakness across the semiconductor complex. US telcos down after AT&T
missed phone-add expectations but driven by SpaceX comments on mobile (seems we will have a
presentation on their plans in the near term, Google fell on mixed checks. Trading activity was relatively
muted, with modest net buying from hedge funds and broadly flat asset-manager flows.
 
Loads of results overnight and this morning - broadly solid: Meta, SAP, TXN (+ve analog) and IBM (+ve
CAP) beat expectations, reinforcing healthy AI-led advertising, cloud migration, analogue recovery and
enterprise software demand. While LRCX, Nokia, BESI and Pearson produced respectable numbers, but
softer guidance or already-high investor expectations limited upside; Telia and RELX were steady. Meta to
me while not raising capex did not answer the question on 2027 capex intensity and rather pushed that out
to Q4 and also did not answer on the topic that SpaceX raised that power, cooling and electrical
infrastructure, rather than chips alone, increasingly constrain AI deployment. 
• Meta’s beat with revenue above the top end of guidance, advertising growth of 26% and underlying
profitability ahead of expectations despite a $9.9bn one-off legal settlement. Q4 revenue guidance of $73–
76bn brackets consensus of $73.6bn and the roughly $75bn buyside bar, while 2026 capex guidance was only
modestly tightened to $135–145bn from $130–145bn, easing fears of a larger increase. The core Family of Apps
remains healthy, supported by AI-led engagement and ad performance, while Muse is gaining early traction with
around 10mn downloads, strong shopping usage and encouraging user economics, although monetisation is still
developing and capacity remains constrained. Management expects aggressive AI investment to support strong
revenue growth through 2027, partly offset by Reality Labs losses declining towards roughly $15bn, but rising
capital intensity means investors will focus on 2027 spending, compute efficiency and whether returns continue to
justify the build-out (more details on this promised on the Q4 call). Up 3% AH as market focused revenue beat,
solid outlook and improving evidence that AI investment is driving returns in the core advertising business- See
Eric take – he has made small raises to his revenue for 2027 & beyond on higher advertising revenue but trimmed
his EPS   
 
• SAP’s CCB (current cloud backlog) beat—delivering 26% CC growth versus consensus of 24.6% and buy
side expecting at least 25%, while organic CCB growth of around 25.2% showed no deceleration for a third
consecutive quarter and reinforced confidence in the S/4HANA migration cycle. Cloud revenue rose 25% to
roughly €6.7bn, around two points ahead of expectations, helped by about one point of one-off benefits, while total
revenue of €10.27bn and operating profit of €3.03bn were also ahead, with lower sales and marketing spend
supporting margins and free cash flow. SAP now expects FY26 cloud revenue towards the top end of its €25.8–
26.2bn range, but left operating profit and free-cash-flow guidance unchanged, which looks prudent given tougher

Q4 comparisons, macro uncertainty and CFO saying on call that there are early signs that higher interest rates are
affecting customer behaviour. Up in the US AH (~3.5–4%) on the CCB beat and print confirms strong cloud
execution key debate remains the pace of CCB deceleration into year-end (messaging is still a slight deceleration
in CCB versus FY25 of 25% yoy ex-fx. On call still saying that Q4 decelerates but not giving clarity on scale mostly
attributing to multiple macro related cautiousness. Personally think -street CCB is likely to trickle up from the 23%
CC ). SAP should be up on these numbers today. See GIR take 
 
• TXN delivered a strong Q3, with revenue, gross margin and operating margin ahead. Provides the 1st read
through to the analog group (Including IFX & STM) that should benefit, . Demand improved across all end
markets, led by industrial and automotive, both up around 10% quarter on quarter, while data-centre revenue grew
20%; Q4 guidance was also better than expected, with revenue of $6.05bn and EPS of $2.62 at the midpoints, well
above typical seasonality. Free cash flow improved sharply to $8.4bn over the trailing 12 months from $2.4bn a
year earlier, supporting the view that the heavy capex cycle is largely behind the company, while lower inventory
days and continued margin expansion are encouraging. Overall, the results confirm a broadening analogue
recovery and should support a positive initial reaction across the sector, although expectations were high and
TXN’s gross-margin recovery may lag peers such as Microchip and NXP, where near-term earnings revisions
could be stronger. See Jim’s take 
• BESI – Mixed – a solid Q, Q4 guide light due to pushouts (-ve), pushing back on ASML risk(+ve) and better
than expected order book (+ve) while being a consensus short – my view its up of this today give
positioning. The quarter is solid with small beats at gross & operating profit plus big orders beat (15% ahead of
street, given ASML and VAT somewhat expected). Besi pushing back on analysts concerns that ASML could be
competitor in HB saying that ASML is interested in a roadmap for 3D transistor stacking inside the chip over the
next decade and not in competing with Besi in the area of die to wafer hybrid bonding per mgmt. The catch is Q4
guide is light due to some pushouts as certain customers request delivery dates for Q3-26 orders in alignment
with H1-27 production capacity plans with Revenue expected to be flat to +/-5% vs. Q3-26 (street was
+4%). Gross margin expected to range between 63-65% vs. in line with street (but lower revenue implied 5%
below at Gross profit). Operating expenses expected to increase by 5-10% vs. the € 55.1 million reported in
Q3-26 due primarily to higher strategic consulting costs – inline with street.   
 
• LRCX 7.5% after an in-line quarter and outlook fell short of elevated expectations following stronger beats
from ASML and VAT. F2Q gross margin of 52.4% was slightly ahead of consensus at around 52%, while Q4
revenue guidance of $8.60bn was broadly in line with the Street at $8.62bn but below GS at $8.87bn; EPS
guidance of $2.33 at the midpoint was also in line with consensus. China rebounded to 35% of revenue from 26%
last quarter. The longer-term outlook remains constructive, with Lam raising its 2026 WFE forecast to the
mid-$150bn range and guiding to more than $200bn in 2027, led by DRAM, foundry/logic and NAND as 10 new
Tier-1 fabs come online. Management also reiterated a path to mid-50% gross margins and expects services
growth above 30% in 2026 and 15% in 2027. See JIM’s take
 
• IBM rose 6% after a solid quarter (supportive of CAP this morning) , with software revenue growth
accelerating to 9% year on year at constant currency from 5% last quarter, driven by a sharp recovery in
Transaction Processing to 10% growth from a 9% decline, while Red Hat/Hybrid Cloud remained strong at 11%.
Consulting also showed early signs of stabilisation, supporting the view that the business may be troughing. IBM
reiterated its full-year constant-currency revenue growth outlook of 4–5% and raised free-cash-flow guidance to
growth of more than $1bn year on year, from around $1bn previously. Overall, the improved execution should help

rebuild confidence after the Q2 guidance cut, although several more quarters of consistent delivery may be needed
for the shares to fully re-rate. See Jim take
 
• NOKIA: Mixed as while Q3 beats FY guidance is only reiterated (implies street Q4 is too high) and I think bulls
expected more. On AI orders QoQ slowed but its guiding 2027 ahead of street (optical and IP Networks business)
to grow 25-30% in 2027 vs. street at 20%. Mixed incoming on this bulls that its good enough and bears that its not.
 
 Q3 beat with net sales of €5.15bn +2% vs cons, with comparable EBIT of €605mn +34% vs cons. Q3 revenue
increased 9% in cc and portfolio basis driven by 15% growth in NI where optical/IP grew 18%/43% yoy. For Q4
Nokia expects 20-24% qoq sales increase (vs cons +26% qoq), and expects typical strong qoq increase in
operating profit in 4Q26 (vs cons estimate €1,210mn). As such, Nokia’s reiterated its 2026 comparable EBIT guide
of €2.1 to €2.6bn (cons has €2.398bn) and stated that it is on track to deliver FY26 operating profit above the
mid-point of the range. Nokia received €1.5bn orders in its AI and cloud business and sales more than doubled
yoy (Q2 was 2.8bn). The company stated that it expects its combined optical and IP Networks business to grow
25-30% in 2027 (cons is already modelling 20% yoy increase for these segments combined). 
 
• Telia: Good set of number and guidance raised albeit street is already there. In Q3 beats on topline – service
revenue 1.2% ahead, EBITDA 0.9% ahead helped by further cost cutting. Lower capex means big FCF beat. On
Sweden much more constructive than tele2 – saying no change in trends and B2C remains good and mobile
growing. On B2B price pressure saying that there is some. Norway saying market remains competitive and
pushing back on Telenor claims of higher competition. On Finland like Elisa saying August was competitive and
September a little better. It raising guidance now sees FY Adjusted EBITDA growth, like for like, of 3 -4%, saw
around 3% (street is 3.4%) and free operating cash flow at least SEK9bn, saw about SEK9bn vs cons SEK9.27bn.
So only catch is street is there See GIR take
 
• Pearson: Solid and Q3 organic beats at 6% vs. street at 5.5% the mix driven by virtual schools and A&Q
(+10% driven by the AI contract signed in July) while Higher Ed and ELL looks light but consensus only consisted
of 2 brokers. The FY guide is reiterated at the group level however note its trimmed Higher Ed from >2% to
>0%. Near term not sure this is enough esp. given it run and valuation multiple. On the group guide its reiterated -
Mid-single digit organic revenue growth (vs. GSe +4.6% and Visible Alpha Consensus Data/ VUMA Consensus:
+4.7%/+4.8%); Adj. operating profit of £650-675mn (mid-point of £662.5mn – this is narrowed from 640-685) at
1.35 USD/GBP). Note it is also streaming the business to 4 units that comes with £50m cost – ½ non cash, and
any savings will be reinvested. See James takev 
 
• RELX – No surprises 9M org growth 7% in line with GSe and FY26 guide reiterated. In the mix only weak point is
Exhibitions coming in at 4% vs GSe 4.4% but this is expected given the ME. Call at 8:30am see Adam’s take
 
OTHER KEY NEWS/EVENTS 
• A critical flaw in PHP, the software underpinning much of the internet, prompted government agencies and
other organisations across Europe and elsewhere to take websites offline as a precaution after reports
that hackers were already exploiting the vulnerability. PHP developers have now released a fix, but cyber
agencies are urging users to update immediately or remain offline, as temporary firewall protections may not be
sufficient and automatic WordPress updates will not patch the underlying server software. The risk is significant
because PHP powers roughly 75% of websites with an identifiable programming language, including WordPress,
and attackers can scan for vulnerable systems at scale, potentially aided by AI. Government portals, hospitals,

schools and other services in countries including the Netherlands, Finland, Denmark and France were disrupted,
while smaller sites and organisations with limited technical resources may remain exposed for longer. See Reuters
and BBC
 
• hardware: interesting a16z letter on CPUs, GPUs and hardware specialisation; inference is growing incredibly fast;
demand for tokens will increase by 24x from 2026 to 2030 but available power will only increase by about 2x; we
need to run these workloads more efficiently and performantly, because we will likely still have scarcity in the
available physical infrastructure; GPU has been following in the footsteps of the CPU. Link

TECH 
• Meta was ordered to pay New Mexico $25bn following a Cambridge Analytica privacy trial, below the state’s
requested $35–40bn but above the $18bn settlement agreed by 48 other states in August. Meta plans to appeal,
which could reduce both the number of violations and the final financial penalty.
• AWS / NVDA: AWS reportedly planned to receive US$5bn of GB300 racks upfront, followed by ~US$3bn monthly,
under an early-delivery program. Some equipment is being stored in warehouses because destination data centers
lack sufficient power, highlighting a gap between hardware deliveries and operational capacity. (Business Insider;
related CT report)
• PC / memory: Lenovo, HP and Dell reportedly signed memory LTAs to secure 2027 supply, after DRAM/SSD
deliveries met only 70–80% of demand last year. Citing Omdia, the article reports 3Q26 PC shipments −21% YoY
and forecasts another 7% decline in 2027; PC system prices could rise 10–20% next year. (ET News)
 
TELCOS 
• Elisa’s call suggested a weaker near-term outlook, with competition, Q4 earnings recovery and dividend
sustainability the key debates. Consumer mobile voucher promotions have eased somewhat (seems to be why
the stock is rallying) , but corporate pricing remains tough, leaving full-year telecom service revenue growth
tracking towards the low end of 0–2% guidance and suggesting downside risk to consensus of +0.2%.
Management still targets stronger Q4 EBITDA growth, although weaker revenue mix, inflation and reinvestment
are absorbing cost savings. Dividend policy is unchanged, but year-to-date cash flow is down 12% and leverage is
1.9x versus a 1.5–2.0x range, putting greater focus on Q4 data-centre inflows and working-capital improvements to
support cash conversion and payouts. Finland’s recovery and a €100mn data-centre connectivity order book offer
upside, but the latest commentary points to macro benefits towards the end of H2 2027 rather than mid-2027,
suggesting a softer Q4 recovery and weaker momentum into 2027 than expected.
 
• AT&T’s update the Q was broadly in line, with the main miss being postpaid phone net additions of 379k, around
10k below consensus. It’s reiterated that 2026 FCF > 18bn while it sees 2026 adjusted EBITDA growth at the

upper end of its 3–4% range, rising to at least 5% in 2028, while reiterating low-single-digit annual service revenue
growth and a double-digit adjusted EPS CAGR through 2028. The key change is lower capital investment of $22–
23bn in 2027 and 2028, versus the previous $23–24bn range, which is a negative read-through for Ericsson. The
bigger drive of the roll was on the SpaceX call - they talked up a US mobile network promising update in the near
term
 
MEDIA & INTERNET 
• Coinbase’s efforts to move beyond cryptocurrency prompted a review of its media agency—and a shift from
WPP’s Wavemaker to a far smaller independent shop: Noble People. For Coinbase, the shift reflects a changing
dynamic in which the competitive edge offered by massive media companies is shrinking. Link  
 
• Legora announced it has raised a $550 million Series E at an $8.8 billion post-money valuation. Legora went
from $1 million to $100 million in ARR in less than 18 months, faster than any other enterprise software company
selling through a direct sales team, and the next $100 million took less than six months. ARR now stands at more
than $200 million. The new valuation compares with $5.6 billion at the company's previous round.Link
