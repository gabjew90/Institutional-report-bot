NY Close - Exhale
By Preston Pagli
Risk remained contained to close the week with oil back to a five-month high, driving global yields higher and the market
into "wait-and-see" mode, as cross-asset risks collide next week. The 10y UST yield retested the 5% level as markets
unwound part of Thursday's sharp rotational rally, while stronger October PMI and U. of Mich. data, re-affirmed the reality
of a stickier backdrop. Equity weakness continued with lighter participation and weaker breadth, with the SPX (-0.47%)
finishing lower as volatility remained elevated. Commodities remained the key macro driver, with Brent up another 3.5%
to $117 on escalating Iran-related supply concerns, while gold slipped below $4,000. In FX, the dollar was modestly
firmer weighing on EUR and GBP. Attention now turns to a pivotal week ahead, beginning with Sunday's Brazilian
presidential runoff election where the latest Datafolha poll shows Lula and Bolsonaro effectively tied. A packed slate of
central bank meetings will follow including the Fed, ECB, and BoJ, as well as earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft (Wed)
and Amazon (Thu), with roughly 41% of SPX market cap set to report and AI spending commentary likely to be the key
focus for investors.

DeskTalk - Brazil Election: The setup into Sunday
In Focus‌
Rates: UST yields reversed a portion of Thursday's extraordinary rally, with the 10y once again retesting the important 5%
level. Thursday's 16bp rally was driven largely by CTA short-covering rather than a shift in fundamental demand. Today's
move higher in yields was fueled by another sharp advance in crude oil alongside flash October PMI data, which pointed to
the strongest increase in the manufacturing component since 2022, reinforcing concerns that inflation pressures could remain
sticky. Heading into next weeks 7y auction, our US Rates Strategy desk is fading the recent richening by shorting the belly in
a 5s7s10s fly. 
Equities: Breath weakened in the aftermath of Thursday's sharp draw-down which saw multiple factors +2dev. SOX, gained
46bps and flirted with the 200dma at $10,689 (ref $10,781), while trading 26% off the ATH of June. Flows were mixed on the
desk. Buyers were concentrated in Consumer Discretionary, Industrials, Consumer Staples, and REITs, while selling pressure
remained focused in Financials, Tech, Communication Services, Healthcare, Energy, Materials, and Utilities. In derivatives,
volatility continued to hold firm despite markets finding a floor early in the session, reflecting lingering anxiety ahead of next
week's key event risk. January ATM vol was marginally higher in SPX and QQQ, while VVIX pushed back above 110,

highlighting persistent demand for hedges. In credit, weakness in TMT spreads continued on spending angst and additional
heavy issuance expected. Flows were skewed toward HF downside protection. 
Commodities: Commodities remained at the center of the macro narrative. Brent crude (+3.5%) climbed to $117, its highest
level since May amid reports of potential US and Israeli strikes on Iran, raising concerns over disruptions to flows through the
Strait of Hormuz. In contrast, gold continued to struggle, falling below the $4,000 level and trading near three-month lows.
FX: In FX, the dollar was modestly firmer, benefiting from both the rise in yields and its role as a risk-off hedge. The euro
remained under pressure amid ongoing political uncertainty in France, while GBP (-0.21%) also lagged. USDCAD traded
unch. despite a WSJ report President Trump is expected to remove 50% tariffs on some Canadian goods. Desk flows saw
demand for MXN following Thursday's sharp weakness, alongside buying interest in ZAR and CHF. Outflows were
concentrated in PLN, TRY, and leveraged selling of NZD. Overall activity was relatively subdued across G10 and EMFX.
Elsewhere‌
BRL: Flows were mostly subdued on the desk despite the real gaining roughly 1% to break 5.00 on lighter liquidity heading
into Sunday's runoff presidential election, suggesting investors are preparing for a Bolsonaro victory. Thursday's Datafolha
poll showed an even tighter race than the recent Quaest poll, with Bolsonaro and Lula virtually tied at 50/50. Tonight, TV
Globo will host the last Presidential Debate, with our trader noting we are at a point where votes are unlikely to be changed.
Citi positioning remains skewed towards a Bolsonaro victory. 
Cheatsheet - G10 & EM Week Ahead: When it rains, it pours: Prepare for a blitz of catalysts as its a mega week for central
bank decisions, fiscal updates, elections and top-tier data releases. Oct FOMC is widely expected to be on hold, but Warsh's
guidance amid a fragile macro climate will be key. BoJ, BoC and ECB also expected to hold, latter more in focus given French
fiscal noise. Lagarde faces the delicate task of acknowledging market concerns without adding to them.. Meanwhile, RBNZ is
expected to raise rates by +25bp.
Aussie CPI is the last metric heading into an expected Nov RBA hike. UK Autumn Budget will be presented Wed where a
larger fiscal headroom at £15bn could be a benign outcome for UK assets. EUR inflation in gas-sensitive countries set to rise,
while GDP is expected to slow. Other data to monitor in the US: conf. board, Q3 GDP, core PCE and ECI.
Elections in Brazil (second round Sun) and Israel (Tue) will dominate market focus with polling indicating a tight outcome for
both. T-1 week into US midterms with President Trump rallying in key Senate battlegrounds. Earnings season will be critical
with 41% of the SPX reporting, AI financing remains a top concern. It's a heavy week of US supply, with $213bn scheduled.
Finally, UK daylight saving time ends this weekend. Clocks move back one hour, reducing the US-UK time difference to 4
hours and reverting the UK to GMT.
