A sharp hedge fund derisking this week and an ongoing increase in equity 
n
supply underscore the importance of household demand for US equities. 
Hedge fund gross leverage fell sharply this week but remains above the 90th 
percentile vs. the past 5 years. While very recent increases in retail trading 
activity and leveraged ETF flows have signaled an ongoing “buy the dip” 
mentality, retail trading volumes in US equities have declined by 20% since the 
summer and 45% since late 2025. Likewise, after surging in mid-2025, our 
Speculative Trading Indicator has turned lower in recent months. 
Weak consumer sentiment and elevated energy prices likely explain most of 
n
the reduction in retail trading activity. In addition to leveraged ETFs, equity 
options and prediction markets have become fast-growing alternatives for retail 
traders. Monthly prediction market volumes have grown to $78 billion in 
September from $5 billion a year ago. However, this increase remains small 
relative to retail equity volumes, and the decline in trading activity appears 
broadly consistent with the recent downturns in consumer sentiment and 
personal saving rates alongside rising energy prices. 
Despite a decline in retail trading, broader household demand for US 
n
equities has been extremely strong this year. Households directly own 42% of 
the US equity market and control the majority including indirect ownership via 
investment funds. The top 10% of households by wealth own 87% of total 
household equity assets. Federal Reserve data suggest that households bought 
$840 billion of US equities in H1 2026, more than offsetting a decline in net 
equity demand from US corporates. Higher-frequency mutual fund and ETF 
flows show continued strong demand in recent months. 
Going forward, the trajectory of the labor market and interest rates will be 
n
key determinants of household equity demand, which in turn will help 
determine the near- and long-term path of US equities. Household equity 
demand is sensitive to employment, wealth, and the competition from yields on 
cash and bonds. Our baseline forecasts suggest household demand will remain 
solid in 2027, although recent macro volatility is a reminder of the risks around 
those forecasts. Looking further ahead, a secular shift in retirement asset 
allocation is a tailwind for equity demand, while interest rates and AI-related 
disruption are key sources of uncertainty.
