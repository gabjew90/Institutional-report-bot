## Marketcolour + UK Budget Pre / SAP / Maersk (dg) / Schindler /
Galderma / SEB ##
Ioannis Blekos
22 Oct 2026
Good Morning,
US equities finished higher on Wednesday amid momentum chase: SPX +58bps to 7757 (7/11 sectors +ve,
Industrials +0.91%, Tech +1.61%, Comm Svcs. -1.33%), NDX +113bps to 29,822, RTY +27bps to 2781. Big tech
mostly higher, with GS AI Software basket (GSTMTAIS, +3.98%), and the GS Cyber Security basket (GSTMTCYB,
+4.55%). Space names also caught a bid led by SPCX +7.8% strong Q3 earnings, GS Space and Satellites basket
(GSXUMOON, +3.31%). In Europe, EU Semis (GSSBSEMI +2.7%) as SpaceX and Super Micro blowout prints
overnight plus Jensen Huang's bullish Berlin keynote drove the re-rate
 
Tech Colour: Mag7 lagged which felt driven by increased competition concerns following SPCX SI results, rotational
dynamics into SPCX, Cleveland checks weighing on GOOGL, possible source-of-funds trading into expected future
issuance. Semis bounced but felt underwhelming (particularly coming off 10 of 11 sessions lower), despite positive
news flow & constructive global tech px action overnight (SMCI +10% on positive pre).
 
Earnings Snippets: META +5%... Solid Q3 Rev beat, guiding Q4 $73-76bn nicely above $73.57bn. New class of
stock coming Jan '27. // TSLA (+) +4%... 3Q deliveries were a clear beat and we already got that news at the
beginning of October. Qualitative commentary seems more positive vs recent quarters. // LRCX -7.5% .. beat on
quarter with F2Q GMs 52.4% vs cons ~52% and guides slightly above street.
 
HF Colour: HFs have heavily net sold global equities so far in October, driven by short sales far outpacing long buys
(~5 to 1). Overall Long leverage now sits in the 73rd percentile vs. the past year (94th percentile 5-year), while Short
leverage has risen sharply and is approaching 5-year highs in the 99th percentile.
 
UK Budget Preview: Economic forecast changes will likely lower the Chancellor’s fiscal headroom from £24bn to
around £15bn before new policies at next week’s Autumn Budget, as a stronger wage projection only partly offsets
higher rates and weaker growth estimates. Tax rises are likely, but we expect the magnitude of the changes to be
notably smaller than in 2024 and 2025 at around £5bn. While the Budget looks set to be a relatively low-key affair, it
will probably set up a difficult Spending Review next year. Link
 
SAP (Buy / PT EUR230 / 10% Upside): We expect consensus forecasts to remain broadly unchanged for SAP
following a stronger 3Q print across all KPIs and anticipate a positive share price reaction given the better-
than-expected CCB. CCB (current cloud backlog) beat—delivering 26% CC growth versus consensus of 24.6% and
buy side expecting at least 25%, while organic CCB growth of around 25.2% showed no deceleration for a third
consecutive quarter and reinforced confidence in the S/4HANA migration cycle. Cloud revenue rose 25% to roughly
€6.7bn, around two points ahead of expectations, helped by about one point of one-off benefits, while total revenue of
€10.27bn and operating profit of €3.03bn were also ahead Link
 
BESI – Mixed: A solid Q, Q4 guide light due to pushouts (-ve), pushing back on ASML risk(+ve) and better than
expected order book (+ve)while being a consensus short – my view its up of this today give positioning. The quarter is
solid with small beats at gross & operating profit plus big orders beat (15% ahead of street, given ASML and VAT
somewhat expected).

 
Maersk – Dg to sell (PT DKK18 / 25% downside): Much of the current tightness in the container market has been
driven by temporary factors: subdued supply growth in 2026, geopolitical and climate-related disruption and US re-
stocking. We believe the impact of all three will likely now start to fade. Link
 
Galderma: 5% topline beat with Nemo 7% ahead ($302m vs $283m). Guidance up to 22.5% - 23.5% (ahead of our
expects for 21-22%), drives ~2% upgrades to street FY26.
 
SCHINDLER Q3 5% beat: Long awaited better order and margin print from Schindler, after several lacklustre growth
prints orders today beat consensus by 6%, printing >10% OOG, a material acceleration from 2Q. Feedback has been
turning a little more constructive from low levels, in part as defensive & quality names have been coming back into
vogue of late, and these results look good enough for SCHP to benefit should that rotation continue. 
 
SEB: 5% miss driven by weaker-than-expected core income and trading income, while cost discipline was good. NII
missed Consensus by 1%. NII was supported by loan growth being strong up +4% q/q and solid performances in
Swedish BRB (+3% q/q), CIB (+6% q/q) and the Baltics (+10% q/q), partly offset by NII headwinds from Funding &
Other
