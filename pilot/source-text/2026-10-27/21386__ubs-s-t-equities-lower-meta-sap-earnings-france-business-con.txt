Equities Heavy Amid Mixed AI Earnings, Higher Oil Prices
Equity markets are edging lower amid mixed signals from AI earnings and further Middle East tensions, with
S&P futures down 0.95%, and the Nasdaq and Russel 2000 losing 1.66% and 0.84%, respectively. Treasuries
are rallying across the curve, with the 10y yield down 4.8bp.
The AI trade is still struggling for clarity after a slew of earnings after the Wednesday’s close. Among the
highlights, Meta’s Q3 earnings were strong, beating analyst expectations, and the company raised its capex
plans for this year by $10bn to $145 bn. But Tesla’s net income fell 43% y/y to $787 mn, below consensus,
and it cut capex, and guidance from Lam Research, which supplies wafer-fabrication equipment, fell short of
analyst expectations. UBS analysts have buy recommendations for Meta and Lam at buy, and are neutral on
Tesla.
In the Middle East, the conflict between Saudi Arabia and Houthi Militants in Yemen continues to escalate,
while The New York Times [paywall] reported the US and Israel have developed “specific plans” to attack Iran
after the US midterm elections on Nov. 3.
(Aditi Samajpati & Wayne Gouro, Macro Sales & Ben Huh, Advisory Sales)
One Liners
Meta: Better Earnings Trajectory, But AI Bill Keeps Rising -- Meta delivered a strong Q3, with revenue
ahead of expectations and an encouraging outlook for the final quarter. UBS analyst Stephen Ju reiterates
Buy, arguing that the pressure on earnings growth may be starting to ease. The bigger debate is now around
Meta’s heavy investment in AI and how quickly it can generate a return. Early adoption of Muse has been
strong, while improving efficiency and plans to generate revenue from its AI businesses from 2027 provide
some encouragement. However, investment levels remain high and cash generation has come under
pressure. The key question for investors is therefore shifting from whether AI can drive growth to whether
monetisation can keep pace with spending. A sizeable privacy judgment, which Meta plans to appeal, provides
an additional risk to watch.
SAP: Watching Cash Generation – SAP delivered a strong Q3, with cloud growth ahead of expectations and
profitability also slightly better than forecast. UBS analyst Michael Briest, who has a neutral recommendation
on the stock, highlights particularly good visibility into the year-end, with SAP’s existing cloud business
providing solid support for its full-year targets. Management has also become more confident about finishing
towards the upper end of its growth guidance, he said. The main area to watch is cash generation, where SAP
still has work to do in Q4 to hit its full-year target. Longer term, the strength of the current cloud business is
encouraging, although it could make the planned acceleration in growth next year harder to achieve.

French Business Confidence Up In October; Better Manufacturing And Services - French business
confidence according to INSEE rose in October by 1.1 points to 97.5, better consensus of 96. This was driven
by large gains in manufacturing (+4 to 105.3; consensus 101), with INSEE noting strong global demand, and
services (+1.5 to 99.3). By contrast, construction (-1.4 to 94.9) and especially retail trade (-2.5 to 93.2) fell
sharply. The survey period was 25 September and 19 October 2026 and INSEE notes that most companies
answered during the first two weeks of collection. (Felix Hüfner, Research - Economics & Strategy - European
Economist)
BoK Holds, Keeps Tightening Bias – Bank of Korea held the key rate at 3% on Thursday, as widely
expected, in a unanimous decision following pre-emptive back-to-back hikes in Q3. Governor Shin reiterated
the need for patience in assessing the impact of tightening so far, while keeping the bias amid persistently
above-target inflation. He said the current account surplus would exceed BoK's prior forecast while growth this
year and next were also likely to be "considerably higher". On credit conditions, the statement flagged that
housing-related loans continued to increase and house prices pressure picked up. On FX, Shin said that KRW
is weaker than past in years on FX measures, aligning with his recent comments that he sees room for further
gains and that it's desired. Overall, the assessment points that conditions are incrementally supportive of a
longer tightening bias. (Jason Cheng, Macro Sales)
Singapore Emerges As Gold Haven For World’s Wealthy - Wealthy individuals from the US, Europe and
Middle East have looked to diversify where their gold is being held and Singapore has been a beneficiary of
these flows, Bloomberg reports [paywall]. They find that a growing number of these individuals have been
unsettled by wars, government debt and political uncertainty in their home nations, leaving them to look for
reassuring places to hold their gold. Private gold holdings in Singapore surged by about 16x from 2014, to over
800 metric tons last year, with the majority of these flows into Singapore coming from Europe. (Nana Antiedu,
Macro Sales)
An Elastic Fed Reserves Regime - The Federal Reserve’s new balance-sheet policy should seek to meet the
demand for reserves with a wide range of liquidity providing facilities. In that way, reserves will be supple and
elastic, expanding and contracting with demand but without friction. Chris Hughes, a non-resident fellow at The
Hutchins Center on Fiscal and Monetary Policy (with Brookings), has written a working paper on how the Fed
should approach balance sheet shrinkage.  He recommended the Fed maintains the ambitions of ample
reserves but rids itself of the complications of the current system. Read my full note here. (Simon Penn, Macro
Sales)
UBS Brings Forward Timing Of RBNZ Hike To October Following Inflation Data – New Zealand’s Q3 CPI
inflation slowed to 0.7% q/q vs. consensus expectation of 0.8% and 1.5% last and to 3.7% y/y vs. 3.8%
consensus after 4.1% ahead of the Nov. 7 general election as fuel prices eased. This was below the RBNZ’s
3.9% projection, but still above its 1–3% target band. Petrol remained the largest contributor, despite easing
from Q2, while house-building costs increased. NZDUSD traded to 0.5692 after the data. New Zealand
Economics has revised its RBNZ call, now seeing a 25bp hike at next week's meeting as the most likely
outcome rather than a hold. Market pricing for the October meeting has firmed further to 90%, prompting
economist Stephen Wu to bring forward the hike he had previously expected in December. He retains his
terminal OCR view of 3.25%, likely to be reached in February 2027. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Brazil Election Polls Suggest Narrow Victory For Flávio Bolsonaro – LatAm Economics: Brazil's latest
polls show Senator Flávio Bolsonaro leading President Lula in the presidential runoff with 51%-49% of valid
votes. Aggregated data from 17 recent surveys indicate Flávio's vote share has edged up slightly, maintaining
a stable trend over the past 10 days. Adjusting for historical polling deviations, UBS estimates the final result
could lean closer to 52%-48% in Flávio's favor. Prediction markets also remain steady, assigning a 77%
probability of a Flávio victory. While recent rallies, debates, and interviews may influence voter sentiment, the
polling landscape appears largely unchanged. (Jonathan Garber, Macro Sales)

Energy To Push Up UK, Swiss, Sweden; Eurozone Unchanged On German Fuel Tax Cut - European
economist Anna Titareva expects higher energy prices to push inflation higher in October across the UK,
Switzerland, and Sweden but to temporarily remain unchanged in the Eurozone, due to a temporary fuel tax
cut in Germany. Eurozone headline and core inflation is expected to remain unchanged at 3.8% y/y and 2.5%
y/y, respectively (due Nov. 4). Despite higher oil prices in October, Anna expects aggregate Eurozone energy
prices to fall 0.2% m/m, entirely due to a temporary fuel tax cut in Germany. In the UK, she expects headline
inflation to rise by 0.2pp to 3.7% y/y in October (released Nov. 18). (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
SpaceX Lifts Asia AI Demand – UBS Research: SpaceX's plan to add 10 gigawatts of compute in 2027
sharply expanded the addressable demand for Nvidia's Asia supply chain. Analyst Randy Abrams notes about
33K Vera racks could represent 35% of his 95K-rack Nvidia estimate, supporting foundry, packaging, memory,
substrate, rack and power suppliers. However, power availability could constrain deployment and Randy
estimates $170 bn of financing will be needed. Fast cloud payback strengthens the AI narrative, but execution
and funding remain key. (Kevin Cheng, Equity Sales)
South Africa’s MTBPS: CPI Target, Issuance Lowered; Cautious Framework - EMEA Economist Gyorgy
Kovacs thinks South Africa’s 2026 Medium Term Budget Policy Statement (MTBPS) confirmed the recent
strong fiscal track record of the National Treasury in South Africa, with the 2026/27 budget on track to
outperform the primary surplus target set in the February 2026 Budget. Firstly, The MTBPS continues to call
for a stabilization (and reduction) in public debt, with public debt seen falling from the 2025/26 peak of 78.4%
of GDP to 76.6% of GDP in FY 2029/30. Second, the government over next year will put a fiscal rule into law
that will "create a durable commitment to sound public financial management". Third, net SAGB issuance
would be largely flat around ZAR150 bn for the next two fiscal years and then fall further in 2029/30, which is a
somewhat lower issuance plan (mainly for 2028/29) than in the February Budget - aided by the decision to
lower cash balances. Fourth, Gyorgy thinks the MTBPS macro and fiscal framework is prudent and views the
macro and fiscal assumptions as not too demanding. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)

French Yields Higher, Spreads Wider After Budget Rejections - The French 10-year yield has opened
Thursday higher, at 5.05%, up 3.6bp on the day so far. That’s pushed the spread to Germany out another
5.5bp. The news flow has moved away from street protests and back to the roots of the budget. The
government isn't finding any takers for its budget proposals so far. Though full formal votes on the entirety of
the budget are still to take place, this week has already seen MPs reject reduced spending measures. News
that the rejections mean some EUR10 bn of deficit reductions have already been cast to one side, and the
most contentious budget matters are still to be discussed has raised further the prospect of budget failure. A
week ago the working assumption in the markets had been that MP’s would vent their frustrations with budget
measures but stop short of outright rejection. That is not proving to be the case. The bond market is hence
reacting accordingly. The next threat comes later Thursday afternoon if MPs indicate today’s proposals to cut
spending are also to be rejected. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
