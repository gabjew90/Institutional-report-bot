META did not draw incremental inflows ahead of its earnings announcement (-0.2z).

Similarly, retail investors showed only limited buying in TSLA on the day of its 
announcement. The company’s deliveries were already reported on 10/2, coming in 
well above consensus, powered by RoW (ex-China/US) and better-than-expected 
trends in Europe. Broader market approvals and a prospective EU-wide approval are 
still pending, as a bloc-wide vote has now slipped to December from the previously 
expected October timeline, report. 
•
SPCX beat on the top line across all 3 segments, with AI the primary driver going 
forward. Retail investors initially added to positions earlier in the week, but took 
profits as the stock surged ~8% on Wednesday. The company now expects to end 2026 
with 2-3 GW of compute and to build an incremental ~10 GW in 2027 to ~13 GW 
total, well above the previously expected 5-10 GW range. Regarding a potential 
SPCX-TSLA merger, Elon Musk noted that both companies have formed special 
committees to discuss a potential deal and that there are significant merits to a 
combination, but it would require shareholder approval, report. 
•
Retail investors net sold Aero stocks GE, RTX and NOC on Tuesday post-earnings; 
the former two underperformed post-announcements despite solid beat and raises 
while NOC was the exception. Note that all Aero stocks are coming up on half a year 
in which flight activity isn’t growing much, and while that is not yet affecting 
maintenance activity, the market is wary of the potential impact down the road, 
report. 
•
Retail investors were net sellers of UAL (-0.8z) after an earnings miss driven by 
higher jet fuel costs and softer-than-expected 4Q guidance. Despite this, the stock 
rose 4% as United pointed to strong operating revenue and reaffirmed next year’s 
margin outlook. Airlines are still benefiting from resilient demand from high-end 
consumers willing to pay up for premium seats.

Retail Trading Activity in Numbers for the Week: Oct 15 to Oct 21
•
Retail flows were $4.3B this week, well below the 12-month average of $6.7B/week. Retail investors continued to favor ETFs 
(+$4.0B) over Single Stocks ($0.3B).
•
Outside of Broad Based Large Cap Equity ETFs (+$1.6B), retail ETF buying was influenced by Equity Sector Technology 
(+$539M, 2.1z), Equity Style Call/put writing (+$214M), Equity Style Dividend (+$167M) and Equity Broad Based 
Multi Cap (+$148M).
•
This week, retail investors continued to buy AI datacenters and electrification (JPAMAIDE), Top 30 AI/Datacenter 
Beneficiaries, Growth, Mag 7, along with US Companies with High Direct China Revenue Exposure, see Figure 4,Retail Single Stock Activity by Themes.
•
Activity in Mag7 this week: Retail investors bought NVDA (+$556M), GOOG/L (+$283M), AMZN (+$20M), and sold 
TSLA (-$8M), META (-$15M), AAPL (-$31M), MSFT (-$79M). 
•
Outside of Mag7, retail investors favored Tech (+2.0B, 1.1z), and were net sellers of nearly all other sectors, led by 
Financials (-$354M).
•
Top five retail stocks last week: NVDA (+$556M), GOOG/L (+283M), NBIS (+$259M, 3.5z), SNDK (+$233M), TSM (+
$201M, 2.9z). Bottom 5 retail stocks: SPCX (-$219M), MRNA (-$80M, -2.2z), MSFT (-$79M), MSTR (-$60M), AES (-
$51M, -4.1z).
•
In options, retail share is off its highs, Figure 74Retail Options Trading Share. The most traded options were in the following names, echoing previous 
weeks: MU, NVDA, TSLA, META, SNDK, AMD, AMZN, AAPL, GOOG/L and SPCX. For the most bought/sold delta and 
gamma names, see Retail Activity in Options. Also see Retail Investors Options Activity by Sectors.

Futures Activity (Non Retail): Over the past week, Futures traders net bought ~$12.9B, primarily driven by net buys in ES 
(~$11.9B) and NQ (~$1.7B), and partially offset by net sales in RTY (~$0.7B).
