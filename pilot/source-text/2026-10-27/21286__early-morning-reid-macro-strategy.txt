It’s been another difficult 24 hours for markets, as investors grapple with AI growth concerns, higher oil 
prices, and ongoing fiscal stress. Admittedly, yesterday saw the S&P 500 (+0.58%) post a third consecutive 
gain, closing back within 1% of its record high. But as we go to press this morning, all those risks have 
resurfaced. First, the latest earnings after the US close failed to reassure investors, with futures on the 
NASDAQ 100 (-1.32%) pointing to fresh declines. Second, geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have 
seen oil prices bounce back this morning, with Brent crude (+1.73%) currently around a five-month high 
of $110.70/bbl. And third, France’s fiscal position has remained in the spotlight, with the country’s 10yr 
yield (+8.3bps) closing above 5% yesterday for the first time since 2002. Indeed, that risk-off move has 
also pushed the 30yr Treasury yield (-3.1bps) down to 5.38% this morning, which would be its lowest 
closing level in the last month. So the overnight backdrop has clearly turned more negative since 
yesterday’s session. 
We’ll start with the oil moves, as several geopolitical headlines came through overnight that led to mounting 
concerns about a fresh escalation in the conflict. Among others, the New York Times reported that the US 
and Israel had drawn up plans for renewed strikes against Iran after the midterm elections, with the 
report saying targets would likely include Iran’s energy infrastructure. Moreover, the strikes on Saudi 
Arabia continued, with two people killed in an attack on the Shaybah oil field in Saudi Arabia, whilst another 
was killed in a strike on the King Fahd International Airport. In the meantime, the US State Department issued 
an ordered departure for non-emergency government employees and family members in Saudi Arabia. 
All those headlines have come amidst little sign of progress towards a deal. Indeed, Iran’s foreign minister 
Abbas Araghchi wrote in an FT op-ed yesterday that “we have no trust whatsoever in the sincerity of the 
US administration’s professed desire for peace with Iran.” And he also said “there is little reason to 
expect progress now”. So that dampened hopes for a near-term resolution, and oil prices have continued to 
rise over the last 24 hours. So Brent crude is currently up +1.73% this morning at $110.70/bbl, and 
investors are also pricing a longer period of high oil prices extending well into 2027. For instance, the 12-
month Brent future (+0.75%) is also up to $88.39/bbl this morning. 

The other big development after the US close were the earnings releases from Meta and Tesla, with tech 
stocks struggling to gain traction since. For Meta, its shares rose by more than +2% in after-hours trading, 
as it beat on Q3 revenue ($64.6bn vs $63.3bn est.) and delivered Q4 guidance towards the upper end of 
expectations ($73-76bn vs $73.6bn est.). The company also slightly boosted its 2026 capex plan from a 
$130bn-145bn range to $135bn-145bn. However, Tesla fell by -3% after-hours after it cut its 2026 capex 
target from $25bn to $20bn. So that raised questions about the speed of its transition into AI and robotics, 
while current revenue and earnings were close to expectations. 
In aggregate, those results took some of the shine off the tech mood overnight, which had been a bit more 
optimistic yesterday. So this morning, futures on the S&P 500 (-0.66%) and the NASDAQ 100 (-1.32%) are 
both pointing lower. Moreover, we’ve seen that echoed across Asian equity markets this morning, with all 
the major indices falling back. That includes sharp losses for the KOSPI (-3.26%), the Hang Seng (-2.87%) 
and the Nikkei (-2.00%), whilst there have been relatively smaller declines for the CSI 300 (-1.26%) and 
the Shanghai Comp (-0.89%). Earlier in the session, the CSI 300 had even entered bear market territory, 
having fallen more than -20% since its closing peak in June, although it’s pared back those losses since 
and is now slightly above that level. 
Before those earnings, US markets had actually put in a decent session yesterday, with tech stocks leading 
the way. That was partly thanks to the earnings report from SpaceX after the previous day’s close, which 
helped to reassure investors that the tech rally was still durable, and we saw some of the most AI-
exposed names do particularly well yesterday. For instance, the Philly semiconductor index (+1.97%) 
continued to recover, whilst the NASDAQ (+1.05%) also put in a strong session. So that helped to lift US 
equities more broadly, with the S&P 500 ultimately up +0.58%, closing within 1% of its record high. And over 
in Europe, there were even bigger gains, with the STOXX 600 (+0.92%) and other indices all posting a solid 
performance, with tech leading the way there too. 
But even as equities saw growing optimism, the fiscal concerns were still present around Europe, with 
France’s 10yr yield (+8.3bps) closing at a post-2002 high of 5.01%. And with the 10yr bund yield only up 
+2.1bps to 3.42%, that pushed the Franco-German 10yr spread up to 159bps, its highest closing level 
since 2011. Now admittedly, the spread remains some way beneath its Euro crisis peak, when it closed 
at 189bps in November 2011, but the continued rise has seen other sovereigns come under pressure 
too. Among others, the Italian 10yr spread over bunds widened +3.8bps to 116bps, whilst Spain’s 
widened +3.1bps to 66bps, marking its widest level since June 2025. 
For Europe, matters weren’t helped by the latest rise in energy prices, which added to concerns on the 
inflation side. For instance, European natural gas futures (+2.78%) were back up to €87.43/MWh. And 
even though Brent crude was little changed in yesterday’s session, the 1yr Euro inflation swap (+3.5bps) 
still rose to 3.82%, its highest level since May. And with more inflation priced in, investors priced in more 
ECB rate hikes too, with the amount priced by the June 2027 meeting up +2.0bps on the day to 64bps. 
Over in the US, Treasury yields saw a smaller increase than their European counterparts, with the 10yr yield 
(+1.1bps) only rising to 5.12%. However, that’s pulled back given the risk-off tone overnight, with the 10yr 
yield (-3.5bps) falling back to 5.08%. 
Elsewhere overnight, the Bank of Korea left their policy rate at 3%, in line with expectations. But they also 
said growth this year and next would be “considerably higher” than their August projections, and 

Governor Shin said they needed to maintain a tightening policy stance. Meanwhile in New Zealand, 
inflation fell more than expected in Q3, falling to +3.7% on a year-on-year basis (vs. +3.8% expected).   
Finally, the other inflation release yesterday came from the UK CPI print, which rose more than expected to 
+3.5% in September (vs. +3.4% expected). That was the highest level for headline CPI in nearly a year, 
whilst the core CPI print also hit a 6-month high of +2.8%, although that was in line with expectations. So 
collectively, that helped to cement investor expectations for a BoE rate hike in November, particularly after 
the strong labour market data the previous day. In fact by the close yesterday, markets were pricing in a 98% 
chance of a November rate hike. 
Looking at the day ahead now, data releases include the US weekly initial jobless claims, and the European 
Commission’s preliminary consumer confidence indicator for the Euro Area in October. Otherwise from 
central banks, we’ll hear from the ECB’s Kocher, Panetta, Pereira and Escriva.
