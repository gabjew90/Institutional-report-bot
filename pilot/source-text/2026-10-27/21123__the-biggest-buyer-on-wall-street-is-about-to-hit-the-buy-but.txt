Turn those machines back on!
Forget retail, hedge funds and the Fed. One of the biggest structural buyers of US equities is about to
return. Corporate buybacks have been unusually quiet during earnings blackout, but the window is
reopening fast. With a record $1.3 trillion in authorizations, the machines are getting ready to buy
again.
Let the ramp in buying begin
Goldman estimates that just 12% of S&P 500 companies are currently in an open buyback window, with
another 22% exiting blackout by the end of the week.
The timing matters. Corporate buying has been running well below normal, leaving plenty of room for
a rebound.
GS buyback desk
The missing bid
October buyback activity has been running roughly 40% below the YTD average. As blackout
restrictions lift, expect corporate demand to start moving back toward normal levels.
And this time, corporate America has more ammunition than ever.
GS buyback desk
A record $1.3 trillion waiting in the wings
The corporate bid has never been bigger on paper. According to Goldman's Ranganath desk, 2026
YTD repurchase authorizations have reached a record $1.305 trillion, up from $1.151 trillion in 2025
and well above previous peaks.
That is an extraordinary amount of authorized firepower heading into year-end.
Importantly, this isn't just about announcements. Companies have already been putting money to
work.
Goldman
Buybacks are accelerating
S&P 500 gross buybacks grew 15% year-over-year in H1 2026, with growth accelerating to 20% in Q2.

The underlying trend was already strengthening before the latest blackout.
Meanwhile, there is relatively little new equity supply to absorb that demand.
Compustat
No issue with issuance
There has been a lot of talk about supply. Goldman expects US equity issuance to amount to less than
1% of Russell 3000 market capitalization in 2027. Not a huge problem.
Dealogic
Not in focus. Then in focus
Just five new repurchase programs totalling $2 billion were announced last week. Hardly front-page
material. Expect the news flow on buybacks to pick up significantly over the next few days as we should
get some major announcements of new authorizations.
Birinyi
The bottom line
The biggest structural buyer of US equities is coming out of blackout, armed with record authorizations
and facing limited new supply. Whatever the macro narrative, the technical bid is about to get a lot
stronger.
