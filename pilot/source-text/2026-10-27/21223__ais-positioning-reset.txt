Outside of earnings, one of the biggest incremental developments has been the move in credit, which was
particularly exacerbated by rising rate hike concerns following last week’s hotter than expected inflation
print. AI-related HY spreads widened 25bp by the middle of the week, compared with just 2bp for non-AI
issuers.
With GIR forecasting $720bn in combined project finance and direct hyperscaler issuance in 2027,
investors are beginning to question the cost of capital required to sustain the investment cycle. Flagging
the latest work from our credit strategist Amanda Lynam; we also hosted Amanda for a webinar this
morning.
Neoclouds remain a focal point in this context (space was down -25% last week). Relatedly, flagging our
CoreWeave upgrade to Buy from Gabriela Borges/GIR overnight.
With AI capex growth potentially past its peak, credit quality, funding requirements and ROIC are
becoming increasingly important to equity valuations. This leaves the upcoming earnings season as a key

test of whether strong fundamentals can arrest the deterioration in sentiment and price momentum.
The very early read from the Q3 earnings reporting season (with 10% of S&P having reported) supports
the view that the rates of earnings growth and estimate upgrades have peaked. Per GIR: thus far, 53% of
firms have beaten consensus EPS by more than a standard deviation of analyst estimates. This remains
above the 25-year average of 50% but a substantial step down from the average rate of 64% during the
past four quarters.
Source: Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research
Growth expectations are getting downgraded. Flagging a must read note from Ben Snider & our GIR US
Strategy team.
After months of intensifying pressure from interest rates, concerns about the rising cost of capital have
caused the equity market to downgrade its growth expectations. A sharp reduction in investor positioning
has amplified equity market moves. During the past two weeks our Cyclicals vs. Defensives pair has
declined by 6%, and rates and credit markets have reflected similar pressures.
Looking ahead, GS forecasts decelerating but still solid growth into 2027: S&P 500 EPS growth of 36% in
2026 slowing to 11% in 2027, or 18% after adjusting for non-recurring 2026 accounting distortions. Real
US growth should also modestly decelerate amid higher oil prices/rates. AI capex is currently contributing
about half of S&P 500 EPS growth, an impulse expected to diminish as capex growth slows and
depreciation builds.
Even with growth slowing, equities should continue to rise as long as earnings continue to grow. A
decelerating growth backdrop usually supports the outperformance of stocks with secular earnings growth

over those with cyclical growth profiles.
What to own when slowing growth meets narrow breadth? If earnings and economic growth is
decelerating, own secular growth > cyclicals…
The team recommends consumer-facing laggards for a “catch-up” and highlights a screen of defensive
stocks with quality attributes if concerned on a “catch-down.” Stock screens included in note.
