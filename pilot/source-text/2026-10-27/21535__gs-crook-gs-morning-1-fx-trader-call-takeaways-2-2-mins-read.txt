1) FX Trader Call Takeaways
Key takeaways from our weekly FX Trader Call, occurring every Wednesday @ 2:00 LDN. Reach out to
receive a recurring calendar invite.
Teresa Alves (FX Strategy): We think the AI CapEx lens is the best one through which to view the USD
performance over the past week, but also YTD. That has been raising US asset return prospects vs RoW,
whilst also putting pressure on US inflation and rate differentials. Going forward, we think this AI-led
relative performance will continue to be the key driver for the USD, and we see risks as relatively two-
sided. If CapEx growth proves to be durable and some of these worries fade, that should be supportive
for USD, whereas there are interesting trades for the scenario where AI expectations come down sharply.
JPY is uniquely placed to benefit in that scenario, and on the EM side we have been flagging ILS as a
tech hedge. In terms of funders, we think it makes sense for people to position for the downside tail in
EUR. The vulnerabilities that we've seen over the last few weeks are the types of things that could lead to
larger FX moves, and I think because of the discontinuity there, it's difficult for pricing to reflect that ex-
ante, and so we think those tail hedges look attractive there. That asymmetry is why we continue to prefer
EUR as a funder here relative to the other popular funders, like CHF or CAD, which we think still have
some merits, but where the risk reward has become a bit less certain and less attractive.
Luke Molyneux (G10 Spot Trading): Three areas across the G10 space where we are currently running
risk: (1) EURGBP downside; (2) AUD & NOK longs; (3) USDJPY shorts. On (1), EURGBP is potentially
moving into a new lower range – EUR downside risks are well known, and for GBP, since Burnham's
party conference speech, we've taken out some of both the political and fiscal risk premium whilst ruling
out an early election, and there has also been talk of closer integration with Europe. Over the Budget next
week, should you see any pullback in cash, a 1.32 handle in GBPUSD remains a really solid base to build
cash longs for a potential move higher into year-end. On (2), we continue to like the higher quality G10
currencies, AUD and NOK, in a market that is focused on fiscal, given they both have strong public
finances and decent carry profiles as well. We have a small amount of outright AUD longs on, but NOK is
the real standout with ToT back in vogue. I like the way EURNOK trades with a continued grind lower – in
terms of levels, 10.35 is our first target to the downside. On (3), we continue to hold USDJPY shorts in
decent size. The topside is going to be capped by intervention or selling interest, and there are a few
ways we can get to a lower USDJPY, via either a tech-related sell-off, which is pretty well advertised, or
more aggressive rate hikes by the BOJ, which is possibly the slightly underpriced tail.

Brian Dunne (FX Options Trading): The best implementation for JPY higher over the medium-term is 2y
USDJPY downside held in binary format. There is a bunch of supply that the vol market sees in 2y-5y
expiries, so we think these 2y binary puts in USDJPY are the most efficient way to hold a short USDJPY
delta. In CHF, positioning in options has gotten a lot cleaner. I think that it's more of a carry trade, and we
have rolled some of our positions into long USD against CHF in call spreads that expire between 6m and
1y. If you look at the carry that you get in the forwards, either relative to vol or relative to the skew that
option markets are pricing, the USD call as a vanilla looks actually quite expensive. So we like 35d vs 10d
call spreads in USDCHF.
2) ADAM CROOK – 2 Mins Read: IMF Takeaways – LINK
Major takeaways from the GS team that met policymakers + investors in Bangkok. 8 bullets below – see
the full piece here.
1/ "Uneasy Resilience”: Surprise on how well DM + EM absorbed the energy shock, but scepticism this
resilience lasts. Consensus: Inflation risks skew higher; central banks lean hawkish; investors more
hawkish on DM monetary policy than GS.
2/ Geo-Politics: Middle East re-escalation a clear, imminent concern; little pushback on GS view that
more oil is getting through Hormuz; no-one expects any break-through on Russia/Ukraine. 
3/ AI’s Impact on Macro a Big Point of Discussion: Conversation centred around: Capex-driven
inflation; productivity; labour-market politics; AI's role in driving rates + USD (especially last week's Rates-
rally / dollar-weakness. Real Money clients have cautious bias to add US duration. However, fiscal
dynamics, hyperscaler issuance + non-US yields seen as capping any rally. What could cause the Fed to
turn? "when US equities break."
4/ Fiscal: UK Looks Good; France + Poland on Watch:
-> France: Consensus that the market will force adjustment, not politics; absence of spread
fragmentation raises the bar for ECB/EU intervention.
-> UK: Positive vibes on UK, as adjustment is already underway; landing a credible Budget could extend
UK asset outperformance.
-> Poland: Consensus too many hikes priced, but worry inflation is about more than energy;
Consolidation hard given standoff between President + Government

5/ EM: Still Constructive: Anchored inflation, stable FX + credible central banks justify EM
outperformance, but spreads to the US are now tight + policy is diverging:
-> India + South Africa are turning hawkish to protect their currencies, while Mexico stays dovish
-> Long BRL (FX + rates) strongest consensus trade. 
-> Other focus areas were Colombia (IMF programme speculation) + Hungary
-> Key risk seen as more robust Fed hikes + stronger USD.
6/ Asia: Rare Pessimism on China/India, Korea the Favourite.
-> China + India: For the first time in recent memory, the room was gloomy on both. China: Expectations
have shifted from a fiscal push to spark demand to Beijing raising revenue for mild consolidation. India: AI
is seen as a headwind to growth; foreign equity positioning remains heavy.
-> Korea: KRW the top Asia long, supported by exports, fiscal revenues + repatriation. KRW rates the
only Asian rates market clients like, via front-end receivers + flatteners.
-> High-yielders: Consensus bearish on PHP, IDR + INR, and on EM Asia rates broadly, since US yields
above 5% exceed most local yields.
7/ Turkey vs Nigeria: Frontier Carry – Diverging Views:
-> Turkey: Consensus notably more cautious, TRY positioning lightened. Most agree now is not the time
to cut but expect the central bank to cut anyway, given its dovish guidance + history of easing early. 
-> Nigeria: Central bank more willing to let NGN appreciate while guiding rates lower. Reserves are on
track to be well above USD 60bn by year-end, FX backlog to foreigners is essentially cleared.
-> Other: Consensus less constructive on Egypt; Kazakhstan still constructive but less so on FX tactically.
Policymaker feedback on Lebanon and Ukraine was negative.
8/ South Africa: Rating Upgrades Ahead, January Hike in Play. No pushback on our constructive
fiscal view; we expect upgrades from S&P first, then Moody's. November hike looks unlikely if the Fed
holds in October, but January hike is plausible, and the SARB sounded balanced. On politics, ANC losses
in Johannesburg + other key areas could strain the Coalition. 
3) TMT Q3 Earnings Review
TMT SPEC SALES (JOHNSTONE) - Overview: Broadly solid. Meta, SAP, TXN (+ve analog) and IBM
(+ve CAP) beat expectations, reinforcing healthy AI-led advertising, cloud migration, analogue recovery
and enterprise software demand. While LRCX, Nokia, BESI and Pearson produced respectable numbers,
but softer guidance or already-high investor expectations limited upside; Telia and RELX were steady.
Meta to me while not raising capex did not answer the question on 2027 capex intensity and rather

pushed that out to Q4 and also did not answer on the topic that SpaceX raised that power, cooling and
electrical infrastructure, rather than chips alone, increasingly constrain AI deployment. Full note here.
EQUITY RESEARCH (SHERIDAN) - META: Meta reported Q3 total revenues of $64.60bn (vs. GSe
$63.70bn, Visible Alpha Street $63.47bn & guide of $61.0-64.0bn), an increase of +26% YoY, including
Family of Apps revenues of $64.16bn (+26% YoY) and Reality Labs revenues of $443mm ((6)% YoY). AI
spend remains a priority with an emphasis on standing up AI compute capacity in the coming quarters (&
remains an attractive area of investment/ROIC when measured against the broader opportunity set). We
maintain our positive view and conviction that current stock performance can move toward our 12-month
price target. Despite a recent positive re-rating in the stock (META +30% over the last 2 months vs. SPCX
+1%), we still see a positive risk/reward skew on current shares when measured against the core
business operating momentum and the early indications of AI scaling (products + returns). We reiterate
our Buy rating and adjust our 12-month price target from $860 to $885 as we update our operating
estimates for this earnings report (including mgmt commentary). Based on its after-hours stock price,
META currently trades at ~23x / ~19x our updated 2027/28 GAAP EPS estimates (ex-cash), respectively.
