As storm Austen hits these shores this morning, clouds kept darkening for markets yesterday, as the wobbles 
of recent weeks all collided at once. First, there was mounting stress around France, with the Franco-
German 10yr spread (+14.8bps) seeing its biggest daily widening since the initial pandemic turmoil in March 
2020. Second, AI fears continued to gather pace, with the Philly semiconductor index (-6.72%) falling to its 
lowest level since July, which dragged down equities more broadly. And third, the latest geopolitical fears 
meant oil prices kept rising as well, with Brent crude (+4.00%) hitting a 5-month high of $113.17/bbl. So 
collectively, those concerns drove a huge risk-off move, with the 10yr Treasury yield (-16.6bps to 4.95%) 
seeing its biggest daily decline since August 2024. Although Asian markets are also down, US and European 
equity futures are relatively flat as I type so the sell-off hasn't accelerated overnight. Today we see global 
flash PMIs which will as ever be interesting. Note that exactly a month ago today, the US flash numbers were 
strong and helped drive an almost 15bp increase in both 2yr and 10yr Treasury yields. This felt like a very 
outsized move relative to the importance this data usually has in the US, but it showed the mood in global 
bond markets at the time. That couldn’t have been further from the mood in the last 24 hours so an 
interesting set up for today.   
It’s difficult to know where to start given everything going on at the moment, but it was France that led the 
day’s biggest market moves yesterday. There wasn’t an immediate catalyst for the selloff, but the other two 
stress points (AI growth fears and higher oil prices) both hit risk appetite, which left France as the most 
vulnerable given it was already facing market pressure. So all that pushed the Franco-German 10yr spread up 
to 174bps, taking it to levels last seen at the peak of the sovereign crisis in November 2011. Moreover, the 
10yr spread now isn’t too far away from its single-currency-era peak that month, when it closed as high as 
189bps. 
Those French moves were part of a clear risk-off pattern rather than a global bond selloff, as the 10yr OAT 
yield (+5.8bps) hit a post-2002 high of 5.07%, whereas the 10yr bund yield (-8.7bps) fell back instead to 
3.32%. Moreover, those moves cascaded elsewhere, with the Italian 10yr spread to bunds (+8.7bps) 
widening to 124bps, the most since the Liberation Day turmoil in April 2025. And the stress was clear from 

other asset classes, as the STOXX Banks (-4.17%) hit a 4-month low, some -14% off their recent highs in mid-
September. The Euro also weakened -0.14% against the US Dollar, while defensive currencies including the 
yen (+0.87%) and the Swiss France (+0.48%) benefited. 
Given the latest stress, yesterday led to fresh questions about whether the ECB would even be able to hike 
rates again this year. Admittedly, that’s still the baseline market expectation. But with a 69% chance of 
another hike priced by December this represented a sharp pullback from 90% at the close on Wednesday. So 
doubts on the sustainability of further ECB hikes are rising. Indeed, the market moves were also creating a 
broader tightening in financial conditions, with the STOXX 600 down -1.05%, whilst European HY spreads 
widened +20bps to their highest level since April 2025 at 369bps. 
As all that was happening, yesterday also brought a fresh wave of concern around AI-driven growth, reversing 
some of the optimism from earlier in the week. So it was a rough session for several of the most affected 
names, and the Philly semiconductor index (-6.72%) had its worst day since June, whilst CoreWeave’s share 
price (-10.06%) fell to its lowest since May 2025. That carried over into the CDS space too, where 
CoreWeave’s 5yr CDS widened +66bps to 1162bps. After its results the night before, Meta (-6.98%) fell and 
its CDS widened +7.2bps to 118bps. That marked a sharp turnaround from the initially positive after-market 
reaction to its results on Wednesday night as the sentiment around the company’s rising capex plan soured. 
Put together, this all helped drive the broader indices lower, with the S&P 500 (-1.21%) posting its worst daily 
performance since July, whilst the NASDAQ (-2.60%) had its biggest decline since June. However, note that 
the main defensive sectors rallied strongly, with consumer staples (+3.11%) and utilities (+1.98%) leading the 
way as two thirds of S&P 500 constituents moved higher, helped by the fall in yields. This also marked the 
biggest outperformance of the equal-weighted S&P 500 (+0.77%) over the market cap index since July 2024.  
The other important theme yesterday was the latest jump in energy prices, driven by mounting fears of a fresh 
escalation in the Middle East. Indeed, yesterday saw Brent crude (+4.00%) close at a 5-month high of 
$113.17/bbl, which added to fears about more persistent inflation. Moreover, European natural gas futures 
(+2.99%) hit another post-2022 high of €90.04/MWh, which particularly hit the continent as growth concerns 
interacted with the fiscal stress. In part, the latest moves followed a New York Times report we mentioned 
yesterday, saying that the US and Israel had drawn up plans for renewed strikes against Iran after the 
midterm elections. So that drove speculation about some kind of escalation after the November 3 vote. And 
we also heard the Houthis claim a recent attack on Saudi Arabia’s Yanbu oil site, which follows a drone 
attack at a Saudi oil field the previous day.   
In the midst of all these market pressures, an interesting theme was the flight to US Treasuries, which 
returned to its traditional risk-off role in periods of turmoil. In fact, the 10yr yield fell -16.6bps to 4.95%. 
Moreover, that came despite some strong US data, demonstrating how the financial stress was weighing on 
sentiment rather than the fundamentals. For instance, the weekly initial jobless claims fell to a 3-month low 
of 194k in the week ending October 17 (vs. 200k expected). And in turn, that pushed the smoother 4-week 
moving average to its lowest since September 2022, back when the unemployment rate was just 3.5%. 
Asian equities are weak but the sell-off hasn't accelerated so far and most indices are well off their opening 
lows. The Nikkei (-0.90%) and KOSPI (-0.89%) are recovering from notably higher losses. In China, the CSI 300 
(-0.54%) and Shanghai Composite (-1.20%) are at 15-month lows but may recover a bit after the lunch break. 
The Hang Seng (+0.58%) has rallied as I've been writing this. S&P 500 (-0.13%), Nasdaq (-0.03%) and Stoxx (-
0.08%) are only very slightly lower, showing the sell-off has stabilised for now. 

In Japan, September inflation rose as expected overnight, with core CPI accelerating to a nine-month high of 
+2.1% y/y and moving above the BOJ’s target, supported by higher energy costs and yen weakness. Core-
core CPI increased to +2.4% from +1.9%, while headline CPI rose to +2.1% from +1.9%. Meanwhile, JGBs are 
rallying with global bonds, with 10-year yields down -9.5bps to 2.77% and 5-year yields falling -7.8bps to 
2.20%. 
Separately, Japan’s manufacturing sector expanded at a slower pace in October, while services activity 
strengthened. The flash manufacturing PMI eased to 53.3 from 54.1, while the services PMI improved to 52.0 
from 51.3. The composite PMI held steady at 52.3, signalling a 19th consecutive month of private-sector 
expansion.   
Looking at the day ahead now, data releases will include the October flash PMIs from Europe and the US, 
along with UK retail sales for September, and the University of Michigan’s final consumer sentiment index for 
the US in October. Otherwise from central banks, we’ll get the ECB’s Consumer Expectations Survey for 
September, and we’ll hear from the ECB’s Cipollone.
