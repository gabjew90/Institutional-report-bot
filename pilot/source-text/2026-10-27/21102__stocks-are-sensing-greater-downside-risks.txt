 
Stocks Are Sensing Greater Downside Risks 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, OCT 20, 2026 - 03:00 PM 
Authored by Simon White, Bloomberg Macro Strategist, 
The recent rise in the VIX has been driven by an increase in implied downside tail-risks for the 
stock market. 
The VIX is waking up. After over 10 weeks of sitting below 20, which is no mean feat with two 
ongoing wars and mounting concerns over the extent of AI capex, it finally rose above this level 
in recent days. 
 
The chart above shows the VIX, and my model for the vol index based on the difference 
between out-of-the-money put skew and call skew of the S&P. 
As we can see, it gives a very good fit. Typically, the moves in the VIX are largely explained by 
the negative of the call skew: it tends to dampen the VIX when it rises, often due to animal 
spirits as investors chase or enhance upside, and vice versa when call skew falls. 
 

 
But sometimes put skew is the dominant driver, as has been the case this week. Its rise 
underpinned the increase in the VIX more than the decline in call skew. 
 
This indicates the market mood is perhaps more fragile than the near all-time highs in the index 
would convey, given that the implied probability the market faces a correction has risen. 
Index correlation, however, is still doing a good job of keeping the VIX from climbing even more. 
Average single-stock volatility is rising again, but correlation is still near its lows (even though it 
has moved a bit higher), limiting how much single-stock-vol feeds through to the VIX. 
 

 
 
Low correlation assumes common-factor risks are muted.  
A potential slowdown in AI capex is one obvious counter to that; rising bond yields and volatility 
is another. 
There are likely to be further abrupt jumps in the VIX.
