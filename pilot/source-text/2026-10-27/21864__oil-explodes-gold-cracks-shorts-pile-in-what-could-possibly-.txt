Markets at a crossroads
Oil is accelerating toward $120, gold is fighting to hold the critical $4,000 level, and the violent
reversal in Treasury yields is opening the door to further systematic bond buying. Meanwhile, AI fears
are intensifying, hedge funds are slashing net exposure, and short positioning is becoming increasingly
aggressive. The big question: are markets approaching another leg lower, or is the increasingly
defensive positioning setting the stage for a squeeze?
Oil's momentum accelerates
Oil continues to power higher following its latest breakout, with $120 emerging as the next
psychological magnet. The $118 level marks the highest close since the oil turmoil began and remains
an important hurdle.

Make or break
Gold is trading right at the critical $4,000 level. Price remains well below the now downward-sloping
200-day moving average, and as we all know, nothing good tends to happen below the 200-day. On the
other hand, the major long-term trendline converges around current levels, while $4,000 has acted as
key support over the past year. A massive technical crossroads. Make or break.

Threats on the rise
Goldman's Privorotsky highlights the alarming pace of emerging cybersecurity threats, with critical PHP
exploits, a major Java vulnerability and a Python supply chain compromise hitting simultaneously.
Some of this may have contributed to yesterday's AI concerns. The irony? AI is dramatically lowering
the cost of discovering and exploiting vulnerabilities, while the attack surface keeps expanding. This
could drive a major acceleration in cybersecurity spending, with potentially significant knock-on
effects across software, cloud infrastructure and the broader digital economy.

The easy money is made
In our October 8 note, Everybody is betting on higher rates, that is the problem, we outlined the case
for a reversal in yields, highlighting crowded positioning, stretched technicals and even The
Economist's front cover as a potential contrarian signal. Fast-forward two weeks, and the US 10-year
has moved from channel highs all the way down to its 50-day moving average, just above channel
lows. The tactical call played out very well. From here, things get trickier.
