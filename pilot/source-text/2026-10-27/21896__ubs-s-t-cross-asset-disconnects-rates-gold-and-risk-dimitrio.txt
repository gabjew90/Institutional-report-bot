Dimitrios Laloudakis
23-Oct-2026, 07:15 ET (EDT)
*Sales desk commentary. Not a product of UBS Research. For institutional investors only.*
• United States: The rates bid yesterday appears largely driven by the continued selloff in the tech / large cap
growth space, specifically within AI, semis, and speculative growth names. The 1st figure plots the current
peak-to-trough drawdowns across all three at -21%, -29%, and -52%, respectively. The sharp selloffs can offer
some form of explanation for the massive bid in USTs. Over the past year, front-end rates traded far more
aligned with inflation pressures e.g. oil prices but have since shifted to become more correlated with equity
pricing. 2nd figure plots 21d rolling correlation of 2s against AI winners and brent crude. Given the equity stress
concentration, the VIX index remains relatively subdued and has not broken out above +2 std dev bands (3rd
figure). My expectation is that this dynamic can broadly continue given the fact that the index equity
fundamentals continue to show signs of improvement e.g. fwd EPS growth inflects higher, operating margins
reach new levels, and equity multiples are less rich than the surface may imply (SPW vs. SPX).
• Japan: Outside of overnight inflation data, the more eye-catching headline was from Nippon Life Insurance
that stated they will consider purchasing more JGBs in the 2nd half of the fiscal year and “…certainly at levels
that warrant investment.” However, Nippon still finds itself to reduce its overall domestic bond exposure as
they rotate funds into higher yielding securities. Nonetheless, JGBs (5s and 10s) rallied by ~6bps each.
• Commodities: The lack of bid in the yellow metal seems incongruent with the px action in yields (10yr USTs
more -10bps on a WTD basis). The 4th chart highlights that Dec-27 SOFR implied yields have shifted lower,
5y5y reals have come off their recent peaks, but spot XAU has nonetheless shifted lower. It seems that gold is
still trading in conjunction with risk assets as opposed to underlying macro fundamentals (4th figure plots
rolling 1y correlation to NDX).
