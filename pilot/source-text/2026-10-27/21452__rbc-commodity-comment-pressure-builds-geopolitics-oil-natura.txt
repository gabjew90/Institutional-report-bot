Geopolitics: Pressure Builds 
 
helima.croft@rbccm.com 
Like clockwork, we had another round of anonymous reporting 
in the media from Pakistani and Qatari officials on deal 
progress as Brent breached the $110/bbl mark, but from our 
vantage point, we see the direction of travel pointing to near-
term escalation. We see little incentive for Iran to let up on its 
pressure campaign with the midterms less than two weeks 
away, and Tehran’s leverage seemingly heightened with the 
Houthis continuing to attack critical infrastructure in Saudi 
Arabia. It is our understanding that attacks on tankers and 
maritime traffic have gotten worse in recent weeks despite 
elevated loadings and exports from the Gulf. There were at 
least 16 attacks on ships last week, including two sunk Emirati-
linked cargo ships, and our conversations with regional officials 
and industry leaders have indicated that an increasing number 
of attacks on vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz are 
going unreported. Meanwhile, the Houthis have continued to… 
…target Saudi Arabia, with an intercepted attack on 
Wednesday against an Aramco field in the east of the Kingdom 
resulting in two fatalities. With Riyadh Airport remaining 
effectively closed, we are also closely watching reports that 
the US is withdrawing non-essential diplomatic staff from 
posts in Saudi Arabia, underscoring the growing risk in the 
region’s second front. Hence, while flows through the Strait 
have picked up in recent weeks, we continue to view rising 
crude exports as a lagging indicator of the pressure building in 
the region. Moreover, we continue to point to the enormous 
financial and military costs associated with the US-led escort 
operation that has enabled these barrels to exit the region. 
With Democratic control potentially of one or both chambers 
of Congress, we would expect greater congressional 
questioning of the full cost of the Hormuz escort operation as 
well as the broader military campaign against Iran. 
Oil: One Step Forward, Two Steps Back? 
christopher.louney@rbccm.com 
Despite the visibility of higher Middle East loadings in recent 
weeks, prices continue to reflect a tighter, riskier oil market. 
While Dated Brent currently sits at around $119.79/bbl (up m/m 
but slightly off recent highs), in the paper market, the term 
portion of the curve has also rerated higher, reflecting a tighter-
for-longer view as geopolitical challenges persist. This has 
occurred despite several weeks of relatively elevated Middle East 
volumes —which may in fact be on the precipice of slowdown. 
We are closely watching regional transits and loadings data 
amidst initial signs of softer activity. For instance, our latest data 
suggests that 7-day moving average commodity transits in the 
SOH on Sunday were 4 vessels lower w/w (a ~15% decline), and 
this week’s transits appear set to grind even lower. While the 
most recent datapoints often face volatility of revision (due to 
dark activity and related challenges), recent strikes could be 
deterring operators or threatening the STS transit system in 
place.

Natural Gas: Winter Weakness 
christopher.louney@rbccm.com 
Natural gas prices retreated this week as production recovered 
and forecasts increasingly pointed toward a mild winter, 
reinforcing expectations for weaker heating demand. New 
near- and long-term NOAA outlooks favor above-normal
temperatures across much of the northern and eastern US, 
pushing prices to multiweek lows. Meanwhile, LNG has 
provided bullish support, with feedgas demand topping 21
bcf/d this week, a new high as maintenance season ends and 
terminals ramp towards peak production. Golden Pass Train 1 
topped 800 MMcf/d of feedgas demand, a critical milestone as 
it advances toward commercial operations in Q4. Trains 2 and 3 
should support further demand growth through 2027, 
potentially adding another 1.6-1.7 Bcf/d of feedgas demand.
This expanding LNG demand provides a growing structural 
counterweight to seasonal weather weakness, yet near-term
price direction will continue to be dependent on temperatures 
and the pace of production recovery. 
Gold: A Central Pillar 
christopher.louney@rbccm.com 
Central banks are a key part of our secular healthy view on gold 
prices, expecting net purchases on an ongoing basis. Yet, Russia 
sold ~6.2t of gold in September, the 10th month in a row of 
sales, pushing their gold holdings to their lowest since 2019. 
However, with nearly ~337t (net) of central bank gold 
purchases in 2026 through August according to IMF data (note 
that benchmark quarterly data from the World Gold Council 
typically counts much higher volumes as it accounts for 
unreported official sector activity; H1 2026 estimates were 
already at 345.4t), we note that central bank activity has been 
strong. China for example added ~23t in September, extending 
its buying streak to 23 months, and Poland and countries in 
central Asia have also featured in the list of top buyers this year. 
Our interactions over the last year or two only reinforce the 
idea that central bank interest is strong, driven by de-
dollarization, diversification, and, increasingly, geopolitics. 
 
Clean Energy: Solar Shuffle 
allison.enck@rbccm.com 
Last week, the US ITC ruled that imports from India, Indonesia,
and Laos injured US solar manufacturers, clearing the way for 
more AD/CVD duties and setting the stage for the next solar 
panel import shuffle. As tariffs squeeze Asian suppliers, African 
nations have filled the gap, now accounting for 40% of solar 
module imports in 2026, led by Ethiopia, Kenya, Nigeria, and
Djibouti. This alternative supply chain already faces scrutiny. 
Commerce's July anti-circumvention investigation into Ethiopia 
examines whether Chinese components processed there, 
including cells subsequently assembled into modules in 
Vietnam, are evading duties on Chinese solar products. Chinese 
cell shipments to Ethiopia have since collapsed, while Kenya and 
Nigeria continue absorbing supply, raising the prospect of 
broader investigations into Chinese-linked manufacturing
across Africa. A preliminary ruling for the Ethiopia case is 
expected in December, with a final decision anticipated in May.
