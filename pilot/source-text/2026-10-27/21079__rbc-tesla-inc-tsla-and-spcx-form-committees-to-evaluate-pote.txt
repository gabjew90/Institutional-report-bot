Tesla, Inc.
TSLA and SPCX Form Committees to Evaluate Potential Merger
NASDAQ: TSLA | USD 373.71 | Outperform | Price Target USD 480.00
Sentiment: Positive
What happened? On the SpaceX (covered by RBC Capital Markets analysts Ken Herbert and Brad Erickson) Q3/26 earnings call,
CEO Musk confirmed that both SpaceX and Tesla have formed special committees of independent directors to evaluate the idea
at the request of shareholders. Importantly, he noted that because he is a conflicted party, he cannot participate in the special
committee discussions directly. On the likelihood of a deal, CEO Musk was measured — he pushed back on the notion that a
merger is a certainty, while personally expressing support for the idea. He flagged that a transaction would require a 50% vote
of all Tesla shares outstanding, not just those who vote, which he described as "quite a high hurdle." Musk explicitly stated that
the special committees have not reached agreement on the terms of any transaction and that there is no guarantee they will.
On the strategic rationale, CEO Musk cited several areas of potential synergy including superintelligence software, chip design,
Terafab, robotics, and connectivity.
What do we think? As we discussed on Friday (see report), we think a deal could require SPCX to pay a meaningful premium for
TSLA shares given Musk controls only ~20% of Tesla's voting power versus ~82% at SpaceX, creating an asymmetric governance
dynamic that could require adequate compensation for Tesla shareholders. In our framework, we believe TSLA holders would
be receptive at $480/share, reflecting our SOTP intrinsic value plus a ~15% premium, which at current prices would imply an
approximately 50/50 ownership split of the combined entity.
That said, we think the deal math is more nuanced from SPCX's perspective. Given that SpaceX represents ~70% of our
combined pro forma valuation, SPCX shareholders could contribute the majority of the value while owning less than half of the
combined company at a $480/share purchase price — a dynamic that could face pushback. There are, however, meaningful
merits to a combination. Terafab is perhaps the most compelling near-term catalyst, as a joint chip manufacturing facility could
eliminate costly R&D duplication across two of the most compute-intensive companies in the world and unlock well over $1T
in long-term cost savings versus sourcing chips externally, based on our estimates. We expect further commentary on the TSLA
Q3/26 earnings call tomorrow at 5:30pm ET.

Space Exploration Technologies Corp.
Valuation
Our $225 price target is based on applying a weighted average 14.9x EV/EBITDA multiple to our 2029E EBITDA $193.4B. We value
the company on a SOTP basis, applying a 25x multiple to Space, 14x (down from 15x) to Connectivity, and 15x to AI, all at their
respective FY29 EBITDA estimates. We view the company as very well-positioned to execute against its strategy, even if ambitious.
We believe our valuation framework applies a multiple comparable to other A&D peers, but is slightly ahead from a Connectivity
and AI perspective. We view our price target as supportive of our Outperform rating.
Risks to rating and price target
• Starship Development Risk: Delays or failures in Starship's development could impair SpaceX's launch, Connectivity, and AI
growth strategies.
• Launch Failures & Operational Hazards: Mission failures, launch delays, and uninsured asset losses pose ongoing material risks
to operations and reputation.
• Regulatory & Licensing Risk: Failure to obtain or renew FAA, FCC, or international licenses could materially disrupt operations
across all segments.
• Space Environment & Collision Risk: Orbital debris, radiation, and LEO congestion expose satellites and launch vehicles to
malfunction or total loss.
• AI Segment Integration & Execution Risk: The recently acquired xAI segment remains early-stage, loss-generating, and subject
to significant integration and execution uncertainty.
• Key Person Dependency — Elon Musk: Loss or reduced involvement of Elon Musk could significantly disrupt strategy, culture,
and investor confidence.

Tesla, Inc.
Valuation
Our $480 price target is based on a sum-of-the-parts methodology that values the standalone car business at 1x '27 Sales,
Megapacks at 20x (cap-goods) 2035E EBITDA and the autonomy pieces, FSD @ 10x 2035 Revenues, robotaxis @ 20x 2050 EBITDA,
and humanoid robots @ 25x 2050 EBITDA. All forward values are discounted back to '27 year end. We also include a ~15% premium
for a potential SpaceX acquisition to our SOTP intrinsic value. Our price target supports an Outperform rating.
Risks to rating and price target
• Tesla is a growth company and quarterly results may be lumpy owing to timing of new vehicles/deliveries and a variety of
manufacturing issues, which could cause stock price volatility.
• Costs may be a bigger headwind than we (and Tesla) expect.
• Competition from both traditional OEMs and technology players.
• Regulatory risk.
• Supply-chain inefficiencies.
• Autos are cyclical and the economy could impact demand.
