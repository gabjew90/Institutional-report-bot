Privo on Risk: Confidence has clearly been shaken, yet the demand side of AI still looks
remarkably robust. Token consumption continues to grow, B200 compute pricing remains
extraordinarily firm (breaking out in spot mkt) and SpaceX is talking about sub one year
paybacks on new compute deployments. Meanwhile, we have a series of almost unfalsifiable
concerns. First, people worry the industry simply cannot deliver the projected gigawatts…
some estimates are in the high 40s for 2027, while actual delivery could be closer to the low
20s. Then the exact opposite concern, that we deliver too much capacity and inference
demand cannot absorb it. I’m not sure how we can simultaneously be worried about both, but
somehow we are. Add midterm/energy risk, rates, credit stress and sovereign spreads in
France, and there is plenty for the wall of worry bucket. I would have thought the
hyperscalers remain the critical anchor for equities. They continue to tell you demand is
extraordinarily robust, monetization is accelerating and the bridge towards more organic
revenue and free cash flow may be arriving faster than expected. But it’s a difficult risk
environment, with credit increasingly setting the tone. At a bigger picture level, we’ve moved
from a savings glut to a spending glut, with capital competing for returns across the economy.

That probably demands lower multiples and greater certainty around future cash flows.
Neither is particularly helpful for equities in the short term. Credit spreads and the availability
of financing are becoming increasingly important signals to watch.

From GIR, Neil Mehta looks at specialty contractors: Ahead of 3Q earnings, we refresh
our estimates for PWR, MTZ, and MYRG where we focus on incremental management
commentary around transmission and distribution projects as well as updates on operational
execution and backlog growth. While we are lowering our price targets to reflect market
uncertainty around outer year contributions given the caution around the power thematic, we
believe current valuations across the specialty contractors increasingly reflect these
concerns. Following the group’s recent underperformance, we see an increasingly
compelling risk/reward. Over the next several years, we believe T&D projects from utility
scale customers will be a key driver of durable earnings growth, and we view this as a
structural tailwind. We continue to view a large portion of load-growth driven by non-AI
factors, with data center awards presenting an additional source of growth over the long
term. Amid the current backdrop and following recent stock moves, we highlight our relative
preference for PWR and MTZ. We believe Quanta offers exposure to key structural tailwinds
as one of the main players within T&D and with a strong track record of execution. We also
highlight MTZ where see a compelling risk/reward following underperformance and remain

constructive on long-term fundamentals. Full note:

AVEX – company defending the move in the stock with some details and a sell side call –
Noah comments here: A Bloomberg report stated the U.S. Navy had suspended purchases of
BlackSea Technologies' GARC vessels due to performance issues and more suitable
alternatives. AVEX responded to the article, stating it has factual inaccuracies, and that it had
delivered original GARC units to contract terms, and has since been awarded additional
funding for modifications and upgrades to enhance GARC units operationalized around the
world today. AVEX stated its financial assumptions related to BlackSea do not change, and
we do not make any changes to our model. With the stock at 12X P/E and 7X EBITDA on
2027E, with substantial growth potential and already solid margins, we think valuation is very
attractive; and we are Buy rated on the stock. Full details:
