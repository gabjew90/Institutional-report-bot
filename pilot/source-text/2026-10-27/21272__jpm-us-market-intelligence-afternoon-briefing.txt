JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
SPCX's results, SMCI's positive pre-announcement and the initial read-through from Meta's 
earnings this evening appear to be easing near-term AI concerns, helping sustain the recent 

rebound across the basket. That said, during the regular trading session, our trading desk 
noted that while activity picked up, there was little evidence of meaningful re-risking into AI 
infrastructure. We will see how flow and price actions involve overnight; for now, these 
catalysts look supportive of stabilization rather than confirmation of a full reset in conviction.  
  
The strength beyond AI/tech was also notable, particularly across multiple pockets of 
Cyclicals (Industrials, Financials), alongside software's outperformance. The broader point is 
that an AI recovery and continued market broadening can coexist, supported by a resilient 
earnings backdrop. UAL's results and its 2027 double-digit pretax margin offered another 
encouraging demand signal, with strength across premium, economy and corporate travel 
despite elevated fuel costs.  
  
Overall, we would still look for overnight follow-through, but the past 24 hours have 
strengthened the near-term case for equities.

immediately after the initial earnings release] C You in January: META is currently trading up 
+3% after hours after producing a Q3 result that I would characterize as inline with investor

lives on! But the Q4 Revenue growth really is great. Focus on that.

aggressively to support both our core business and emerging AI initiatives.

focused on improving Reality Labs profitability and expect losses to narrow

than the rest given its lower rating, pointing to a reactionary move around 
broader AI-ecosystem fears rather than credit concerns specific to the 
hyperscalers themselves (JPM Daily Credit Strategy Update, 20-Oct-26).  
o   Supply is not driving the widening, with IG hyperscalers largely absent since 
the $25bn Google deal on 6-Aug. Unlike July, this is not an anticipation-of-
supply widening as in that case all hyperscalers would be widening, which 
would have shown up across all hyperscalers rather than the uneven move 
seen now.  

o   Rates added to the pressure with last week’s hot CPI print raising concerns 
that the Fed would tighten faster than anticipated. This impacts the more debt-
dependent segments first, namely HY neoclouds and by implication HG 
hyperscalers (JPM Daily Credit Strategy Update, 19-Oct-26).  
•         Trade Rationale:  
o   Long side: Hyperscaler fundamentals are robust. Hyperscaler debt is up 
$117bn YTD, yet the cohort's average rating improved modestly because the 
new debt went mostly to higher-rated borrowers. J.P. Morgan Research sees 
the widening as overdone unless earnings show fundamental weakness. If the 
selloff is contagion from HY rather than a hyperscaler-specific problem, solid 
results should let IG spreads retrace.  
o   Short side: Earnings releases set the hyperscaler wides over the summer. 
Hyperscaler issuance could also return after earnings, which would make 
supply a driver again. With HY AI at all-time wides, further contagion into IG 
would run through the weaker links, Oracle first. A short protects against 
capex guidance or jumbo deals that renew funding concerns.

US MKT INTEL VIEW – we published below in the October 19 Morning Briefing 
•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – Last week's sell-off ran against our tactical bullish call, so we 
did a reassessment on our view and risk factors. In our Oct 12 Morning Briefing, we flagged 
below as key risk factors “(i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI 
Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer 
Data”. Of the risks we flagged, the Momentum / Tech Unwind and AI Credit Fears were the 
main drivers for last week’s selloff, while the fundamental picture remained encouraging. 
ASML and TSMC delivered healthy earnings and consumer data stayed resilient. We 
therefore retain our tactical bullish stance. The underlying support from earnings and 
macro resilience remains intact, and the AI sell-off has not come with evidence of 
deteriorating earnings in the results reported so far. That said, we don't expect the rotation 
out of Tech to end abruptly, though its pace could moderate. We favor expressing our 
constructive growth and earnings view through Cyclical exposure outside Tech, while 

selectively buying Tech weakness, particularly in Semis and Memory, potentially through 
defined-risk options structures. Large-cap Banks are another area of interest. Earnings were 
generally positive, and the recent sharp pullback could offer an opportunity to participate in a 
recovery in XLF. Heading into a much busier reporting week, broadening and AI concerns 
remain the two key themes for investors, and all eyes are on META earnings and NVDA's 
GTC Berlin. 
•         RISKS (Unchanged WoW) – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) 
AI Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker 
Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – Following last week's sell-off, we revisit our three key catalysts: (i) 
Macro / Fed: We think the inflation component of this week’s PMIs will be critical. On Oct 28 
Fed (note that the Fed has entered its blackout period), a hold could provide relief by 
removing residual near-term hike risk, but the bigger focus will be any remarks on the FY27 
policy path. (ii) Rates Stabilization: Lower Treasury yields have already delivered part of 
our anticipated catalyst. From here, we would look for sustained moderation in rates vol and 
no renewed yield spike to support the broader rotation. (iii) Tech Earnings / AI 
Monetization: META's results and NVDA's GTC Berlin will be important tests of revenue 
delivery, spending plans and demand visibility. Beyond the hyperscalers, evidence that AI 
investment is broadening and generating measurable revenue across other sectors would 
strengthen the case for a more durable AI trade. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 19) – Following last week's sell-off, we favor a more 
selective approach to expressing our bullish view. We retain a preference for Tech and for 
exposure to a potential rebound through both pairs trades and derivatives. Within Cyclicals, 
we expect the rotation to continue. We remain constructive on Financials and Retailers, 
given consumer resilience and their relatively limited direct exposure to AI-related concerns. 
Internationally, we continue to favor Brazil and Japan. We are also monitoring Reopening 
opportunities that could benefit from lower energy prices and rotation away from AI. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with 
a preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 

Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your shopping 
list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and Retailers 
given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy prices. Other 
elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower energy prices. 
Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and Japan. 
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long 
but would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / 
yields, which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, there 
is likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the Tech tape. 
Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus bullish play. The 
AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we like Tech in the near-
term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we think you need to see 
the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; excluding AI-related 
plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper yield curve, and favorable 
capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real Estate, Telecom, and Utilities may 
participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most likely to lag the SPX, ex-AI plays. 
The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which would squeeze EU and RTY higher; 
plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, Cruise Lines, Discretionary, Hotels, etc) 
is another area of interest with a preference to play via options.
