Finally, as we await META's earnigs after the close, Bloomberg macro strategist, Michael Ball, 
notes that third-quarter earnings are giving the rotation into quality a fundamental underpinning: 
Demand remains resilient, but higher input and financing costs are making operating leverage 
and balance-sheet strength increasingly important. 
The 3Q earnings season has yet to produce any major red flags. JPMorgan reported debit and 
credit-card spending up 10% y/y, while Wells Fargo said something similar, with early-stage 
delinquencies historically low. GM’s results highlighted consumers have held up “remarkably 
well,” while Hasbro beat its own expectations for revenue and profit. 
The cracks are showing up in margins rather than demand. Companies can still sell, but rising 
input and financing costs are absorbing more of the revenue growth. PepsiCo lowered its 
earnings outlook because margins missed expectations despite stronger sales. Delta’s fuel bill 
exceeded guidance by more than $500 million even as demand and pricing remained strong. 
 

 
 
That helps explain why the quality factor is outperforming large cap growth. Research by RBC 
finds that higher quality earnings are being rewarded as market leadership broadens away from 
growth, tech and higher-momentum names. 
Higher financing costs reinforce the divide. Widening spreads are already becoming a warning 
sign for small caps, whose earnings and balance sheets are more sensitive to refinancing 
conditions. At the same time, AI investment remains a key support for growth and risk 
sentiment, but investors are becoming more selective as they scrutinize the returns and 
financing behind the most capital-intensive projects. 
The earnings message is nuanced: Demand is holding up but converting that resilience into 
profit is getting harder. That macro backdrop puts an even greater premium on companies with 
strong operating leverage and less balance-sheet leverage than normal.
