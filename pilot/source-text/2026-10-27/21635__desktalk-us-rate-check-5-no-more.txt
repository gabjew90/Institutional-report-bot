DeskTalk - US rate check: 5% no more
By Preston Pagli
USTs are rallying sharply, with 10y yields trading ~15bps lower and below 5.00% for the first time in nearly a month as
the tech selloff deepens and fiscal fears fade. With oil trading back near this year high's, a meaningful rise in longer-term
inflation expectations has failed to sustain, reinforcing the view that markets are repricing growth and real rates rather than
inflation.
Positioning suggests the move still has room to run. Citi's David Bieber notes structural shorts are at risk of a squeeze
above 106-00 in TY futures, with 10s currently trading above that level at 106-10. On the flow side, activity remains skewed
toward upside duration exposure with call buying in the belly and call skew flipping bullish. Traders have continued to largely
pare back not only their October FOMC bets, but December as well, with now less than 20bps priced through the
remainder of the year. 
Looking at the bigger picture, our inflation strategist Ben Wiltshire notes since Q3, higher nominal yields have been driven
largely by a repricing of the real cost of capital rather than inflation, with breakevens and inflation forwards remaining
subdued, real yields have effectively traded as leveraged nominals. However, as credit cracks emerge and AI optimism fades
at the margin, it is becoming more likely that future rates rallies are driven by lower real yields rather than lower inflation
expectations. Wiltshire notes, given uncertainty around whether duration can sustain a rally with inflation still above target, the
cleaner expression is through options on real-vs-nominal yield spreads, which offer attractive asymmetric upside.
US Rates Strategy (Oct 20): MTD Breakeven Changes: Our US Rates Strategy desk remains tactically neutral on
duration given that rates continue to flip flop between AI slowdown concerns and rising energy costs and express their long
duration bias by receiving October OIS with the expectation of a pause at the next FOMC.
