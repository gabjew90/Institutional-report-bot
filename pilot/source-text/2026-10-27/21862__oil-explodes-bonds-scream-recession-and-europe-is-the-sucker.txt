Oil's next shockwave
Oil is accelerating towards $120, volatility is finally waking up, and systematic buying could add further
fuel to the squeeze. But the real story extends well beyond crude. Oil and US yields are suddenly
pricing very different macro scenarios, while Europe's energy vulnerability is once again being
exposed. French bonds are under pressure, European equities are struggling, and even the once-
resilient banks are cracking. Is the oil squeeze becoming a broader market problem?
Oil's momentum accelerates
Oil continues to power higher following its latest breakout, with $120 emerging as the next
psychological magnet. The $118 level marks the highest close since the oil turmoil began and remains
an important hurdle.

The rates connection
Oil and the US 10-year yield moved closely together until the latest collapse in yields. The short-term
divergence has now become extreme, with the two markets suddenly pricing very different macro
scenarios. Oil is pricing persistent inflationary pressure, while bonds are increasingly pricing weaker
growth and flight-to-quality demand. Something has to give.

Not all rate connections are equal
There is obviously more to the French bond selloff than oil, but surging energy prices are hardly
helping. Unlike US Treasuries, French yields remain under upward pressure as Europe's energy
vulnerability compounds an already fragile fiscal and political backdrop.

Trust the sucker

Europe's pride
European banks used to be immune to almost everything. Lately, they seem to be catching every
negative shock. Surging oil prices and exploding French yields are now adding to the pressure. The
