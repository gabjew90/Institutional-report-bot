GS TMT SPEC SALES – Friday 23rd October 
Sentiment: strong AI demand is not enough. “I think we’re all rudderless currently,” as one PM put it,
captures the mood. Shorts are outperforming longs, despite robust AI demand highlighted by Meta, SpaceAI
and ASML. The GS AI vs AI at Risk basket {GSPUARTI Index} fell 10% yesterday, taking losses to 28%
over one month and 13% YTD. At noon EST yesterday, roughly two-thirds of hedge funds were down; TMT-
focused funds had lost 1.8% (-1.3 SD), bringing MTD losses to 8.6% (see take).  
What could turn sentiment? Earnings have done little to stabilise confidence so far (TBF we still have a
way to go), shifting the debate towards possible catalysts: the election, easing macro headwinds (the Middle
East headlines do not help) or stronger AI datapoints and renewed thematic momentum. SOX has
underperformed the S&P 500 by 13% over nine sessions—its worst such stretch in more than 20 years—and
is testing its 200-day moving average for the first time since June 2025 (thanks Pete). Even so, it still leads
the S&P 500 by 40% YTD and 85% since the start of 2025.
Credit and power remain the main constraints, with funding concerns spreading across the AI sector.
For credit Meta yesterday in focus as its reported future spending commitments rose to $825bn from roughly
$700bn in July, while negative free cash flow, a higher spending floor and an explicit equity-financing vehicle
have reinforced expectations of further funding needs. Its five-year CDS reached a record, Oracle’s
remained near its highs and Microsoft’s also widened. More press, this time the WSJ, flagging roughly $3trn
of off-balance-sheet commitments, including $1.2trn of leases, not helping and S&P Global on the
interconnected AI financing (Reuters). 
I think Brian Garrett’s Sunday email highlights a further positioning issue as he flagged that investors appear
to have cut net exposure without a similar reduction in gross, leaving crowded long/short momentum trades
vulnerable to an unwind. Brian on Sunday put gross exposure at 213.9%, the 83rd percentile over three
years—still high given the losses. Recent price action also suggests limits to appetite for the potential
issuance ahead. The key questions are whether AI-at-risk names have become crowded and what could
trigger a change in market leadership.

The counterpoint: Nebius is still securing funding. Despite the credit concerns, Nebius has secured a
$3.5bn financing facility. This shows that financing remains available, although the short-dated facility does
not resolve its longer-term funding needs.

OTHER KEY NEWS/EVENTS 
• Lots of headlines on Major U.S. Banks’ concerns over AI agents in accounts but the raise an important
distinction: access that is unauthorised by the bank is not necessarily unauthorised by the customer. The report
says major US banks are restricting third-party agents amid difficulties verifying user instructions and reports of
activity apparently beyond those instructions, although Morgan Stanley says it has not observed rogue agents
accessing its platforms. David Sacks argues that much of this activity instead involves customers willingly sharing
credentials with assistants such as Muse, Instinct and Grokbot—a potential breach of bank terms, rather than
necessarily a cyberattack—and that banks should enable controlled, user-directed access. Despite the legitimate
security concerns, his argument raises the question of how much of the problem reflects genuinely rogue
behaviour versus customer demand running ahead of banks’ access controls. The distinction matters, but

customer consent alone does not resolve the risks around transaction authority, unintended actions and liability.   
 
• AI: Microsoft’s MAI-DxO system, which coordinates AI agents like a panel of doctors, diagnosed 83% of complex
simulated medical cases correctly versus 36% for individual generalist physicians, according to a study in Science.
However, specialists and physician teams were not included in the comparison, and much of the performance
improvement came from stronger underlying models rather than Microsoft’s coordination system alone. DxO
improved accuracy and reduced estimated costs on some complex cases, but added little on emergency-
department cases. Microsoft is now planning real-world evaluations with Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center,
alongside standard patient care, and is developing a healthcare foundation model with the Mayo Clinic for release
in 2027. The ambition is to build better clinical decision-support tools as part of its push towards medical
superintelligence, although the simulated results do not yet establish real-world clinical performance.

TECH 
• SAP – getting a lot of questions for the feedback on SAP as 26% was prior to this seen as the nirvana. Most think
the quarter was good and then ask questions on the implied slowdown in current cloud backlog (CCB). For the
most part the slowdown (SAP reiterating that CCB will slow in Q4 but not giving clarity on scale mostly attributing
to multiple macro related cautiousness) post Q3 beat this is generally viewed as prudence, with year-end CCB
growth of around 25% seen as achievable by Bulls. My guess is management wanted to leave itself some room for
upside. Bears asking is this was the peak and ahead of a large Q4 amid a softer macro backdrop and the risk that
IT budgets shift towards AI. I think the bigger issue is also the positioning as this was a consensus long in and
probably seeing some profit taking from HFs – last few weeks has been LO demand…net/net its taking a breather,
after good run and print and questions now are what are YOU willing to pay for it?
 
• Meta did a few calls with Erics - feedback is positive on the core business but more cautious on the investment
outlook and where capex is going and the 14C filing did not help market sentiment - investors largely agree that
the quarter was strong and advertising materially beat expectations, with Q4 also appearing healthy, but the ads
business is increasingly being taken for granted. The debate has shifted to whether Muse can convert
encouraging usage into attractive monetisation and unit economics, and how high opex and capex ultimately need
to rise to support it. The lack of firm capex guidance has reinforced the view that the spending and free-cash-flow
trough may have been pushed out, leaving investors debating when peak investment comes, an end to negative
earnings revisions and tangible progress in Muse monetisation. Reality At roughly 25x the upper end of the
earnings range, the next leg higher likely requires both sustained ad strength and greater confidence that AI
investment will generate acceptable returns. Plus questions again on need to raise

 
• BESI’s call suggested that HBM hybrid bonding is delayed rather than cancelled: current demand can still
be met with conventional techniques, but all three major memory manufacturers continue to develop the
technology as lower power, heat and latency and higher bandwidth become increasingly important. Advanced-
packaging momentum remains strong, with hybrid-bonding orders already around twice full-year 2025 levels,
supported by leading-edge logic, co-packaged optics and data-centre demand. Flat Q4 revenue reflects shipment
timing rather than weaker demand, with deliveries spread across Q4, Q1 and Q2 and visibility extending to around
six months or longer. The opportunity is also broadening into TC Next, GPU hybrid bonding, photonics, power
devices and a potential mature-node recovery, while BESI continues to view ASML and Applied Materials as
development partners rather than immediate threats. The key investor debate remains when strong qualification
activity will convert into meaningful high-volume HBM revenue.
 
• Western Digital: beat F1Q expectations, with revenue of $4.38bn versus $4.15bn consensus and gross margins
of 58.1% versus 55.9% consensus and 55–56% guidance. F2Q guidance was also strong, with revenue of $5.1bn
versus $4.59bn consensus and gross margins of 60–61% versus 57.8%. Despite this, the shares reversed from
around 4% higher to 8% lower as rising input costs and higher capex raised concerns about free cash flow and
margin durability, compounded by a weak market. Management remains confident that demand will support further
revenue, margin and earnings growth, and sees potential competitor capacity additions—identified by Jim as likely
Toshiba—as unlikely to disrupt the supply-demand balance. However, near-term capex will remain modestly
above the long-term 4–6% range to fund heads, media and factory automation. Jim still sees constrained industry
supply and upside in WDC, but believes longer-term capacity expansion could cap HDD valuations and prefers
Buy-rated Seagate for its stronger pricing momentum.  
 
• DISCO's 2Q briefing should be viewed positively, with management pushing back on two key investor concerns.
Firstly, despite fears around HBM despecification and potential changes in advanced packaging requirements,
DISCO said customers continue to actively purchase its equipment with no change in demand trends. Secondly,
management highlighted a broadening demand base, including increasing OSAT investment activity, while also
indicating no meaningful concerns around material sourcing. Goldman Sachs believes concerns over the recent
increase in production support staff are overdone given DISCO's history of scaling shipments successfully with
less experienced personnel, and sees scope for consensus earnings upgrades ahead, underpinning its Buy rating.
 
TELCOS 
• Telia call takeaways: Management’s commentary suggests the unchanged FY26 revenue growth guidance is
conservative, as it implies a year-on-year decline in Q4 despite the CEO expecting growth to remain positive, albeit
slightly softer than Q3. Cost reductions also provide greater confidence in EBITDA and cash-flow delivery, with
underlying 2027 FCF effectively upgraded by around SKr1bn, or 10%, given guidance was maintained despite
SKr600mn of restructuring costs and SKr300–400mn of RAN-sharing costs. There also appears to be upside to
shareholder returns, although management indicated that any update is unlikely before the H1 2027 CMD.  
 
• Nokia call: Stock seemed to roll on the pull forward comments and US optical names all down HSD not
helping. The bull part was that AI and cloud demand remains very strong: Optical and IP demand is accelerating
across front-end networking, data-centre interconnect and scale-across architectures, with the constraint firmly on
supply rather than demand. Most of the roughly €5bn AI/cloud order book is expected to convert within 12 months
or during 2027. *** Margins should improve, but not immediately: New-product ramps, low initial IP volumes
and supply-chain inflation diluted Q3 margins, although management expects Q4 improvement and stronger

operating leverage as volumes scale. Portfolio pruning and accelerated restructuring should also support
profitability. *** Mobile remains structurally subdued: Management expects the broader radio market to stay flat,
with no imminent investment cycle or meaningful pre-buying. Pricing should rise to offset broad-based component
inflation. 
 
• TEF: BBVA has reportedly agreed with the Spanish government to gradually sell its Telefónica stake without
putting downward pressure on the shares, according to Vozpopuli. The bank has reduced its holding from 5% to
1.965%, taking it below the significant-shareholder reporting threshold and allowing it to sell the remainder without
further notifications. The exit is intended to ease tensions with the government following BBVA’s hostile bid for
Banco Sabadell, and was accelerated by the loss of its Telefónica board seat and the operator’s decision to halve
its dividend.
 
• Orange: France’s Competition Authority has ordered Orange to halt its fibre rollout and wholesale commercial
activity in five of Mayotte’s 17 municipalities, following complaints from Mayotte THD, its parent Réunicable and the
local Department-Region, according to Alloforfait. The regulator found that Orange’s focus on profitable, densely
populated areas threatened the viability of the €183mn public-initiative network, and also required it to give
Mayotte THD full access to its civil engineering infrastructure. Orange disputes the ruling and plans to appeal,
arguing that the rollout is necessary to rebuild infrastructure after Cyclone Chido devastated its copper network.

MACRO AND TRADING 
• US TRADING: US equities came under pressure overnight as AI financing concerns, rising oil prices and
escalating Middle East tensions outweighed earnings from Meta, Tesla, Lam Research and IBM. Investors

sought safety in bonds, pushing the US 10-year yield down around 15bps to 4.97%, while crowded trades
unwound: the GS hedge-fund positioning pair fell 4.2%, its worst day since 2011, taking its monthly decline to
10.4%. At midday, fundamental long/short, systematic and multi-strategy funds were all down more than 1%—the
first simultaneous decline of that size since Covid. The GS AI basket is now 23% below its June high, and high-
beta momentum has erased its YTD gains, although weakness was concentrated: 335 S&P 500 stocks rose, and
the index excluding AI has outperformed the broader benchmark by 6% this month. Desk flows remained sell-
heavy, led by large-cap tech, semiconductors, hardware and ETF shorting, with little appetite to buy AI exposure;
derivatives activity also pointed to de-risking and increased demand for protection. Attention now turns to next
week’s megacap earnings, with WDC initially up 2% after hours.
