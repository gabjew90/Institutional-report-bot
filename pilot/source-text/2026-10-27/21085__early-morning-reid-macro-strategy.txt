It’s been another 24 hours of uncertainty and volatility, and that’s not me talking about our household during 
a wet half term. In markets, what started as a widespread morning sell-off dominated by AI concerns, higher 
oil prices and ongoing worries over French sovereign debt turned into a more positive session as the US 
came in, ultimately driving the S&P 500 to close +0.28% higher as the index saw the most daily advancers 
since April. 
Today we see the first of the hyperscalers report, with Meta out after the bell. This comes as SpaceX 
shares surged by about +8% in after-hours trading yesterday (vs NASDAQ Futures -0.16%), as the 
company exceeded quarterly revenue and net income expectations amid stronger AI revenue growth. 
Elon Musk also disclosed that SpaceX and Tesla have formed committees to consider a potential merger. 
Those results have boosted the tech mood overnight, with Nasdaq futures up +0.75% overnight, with those 
on the S&P +0.30% higher. 
Asian bourses are also reversing yesterday’s losses this morning, which were partly triggered by a 
sharp fall in Korean data centre company Elice Inc (-32%) after its trading debut on Monday morning. 
However, they are back up +29.9% this morning highlighting the large vol in tech at the moment. Staying in 
Korea, the big Asian heavyweights Samsung (+4.62%) and SK Hynix (+3.91%) are also recovering helping 
the KOSPI (+2.61%) rebound even if it's off the highs of around +4.75% earlier in the session. Elsewhere, 
the Nikkei (+0.77%) is also trading higher on the tech rebound. In contrast, Chinese equities remain under 

pressure, with the CSI 300 (-0.65%), Hang Seng (-0.35%), and Shanghai Composite (-0.31%) all trading lower. 
The S&P/ASX 200 is flat. 
Delving more into yesterday’s main stories now. Brent dropped from around $112 to $106 in the early 
London afternoon after several unconfirmed reports indicated that Qatar and Pakistan had put 
forward a proposal that could potentially revive talks aimed at ending the conflict in the Middle East. 
That in turn sparked both an equity and bond rally, as investors looked past ongoing AI worries to focus on 
hopes for a resolution. Later, Brent crude started to climb again, as the story was discredited by a 
diplomat involved in negotiations. According to Bloomberg, the diplomat said that while talks to reach a 
deal are ongoing, reports that the negotiating sides were close to a solution were false. After the 
European close we then heard Tasnim report Iran's President Pezeshkian as saying that he believes “the US 
will attack again if we negotiate”. So this left Brent back at $108.79/bbl by the close, -0.68% on the day but 
well off the day’s lows. And Brent is trading back up +1.4% at around $110.36 this morning. European 
natural gas futures also round tripped yesterday but ultimately declined -3.38% to €85.06/MWh, after 
climbing to their highest levels since 2022 intraday. 
The relief from energy that played out late in the European session meant equities rallied as investors 
overcame some of their earlier AI jitters. Tech-heavy indices such as the Philadelphia Semiconductor 
Index (-0.29%) and Nasdaq (-0.02%) still underperformed, but this was a big improvement after chip 
stocks had been nearly -2.5% down early on. Exemplifying that intra-day swing was CoreWeave, which 
closed +1.65% higher on the day after trading as much as -6.36% lower near the open. Outside tech, stocks 
performed much better, with 403 daily advancers in the S&P 500, the most since April, while the equal-
weighted version of the index rose by +1.15%. 
In Europe, although stocks recovered from their morning lows, they still finished down, with the Stoxx 600 (-
0.14%), CAC 40 (-0.38%) and DAX (-0.06%) all lower, whilst the STOXX Banks Index (-0.35%) edged lower 
to a new 4-month low. However, the euro recovered to finish +0.21% against the dollar. 
Turning to bond markets, the initial sell-off also reversed later in the day, so by the close we saw yield 
declines led by 10yr bunds (-5.4bps), with BTPs (-4.8bps), gilts (-2.2bps) and OATs (-0.8bps) also lower. 
In addition to the pullback in energy late in the European session, this rally came as Germany’s ZEW 
investor expectations survey saw a larger-than-expected decline in October (28.1 vs 30.0 exp., 34.7 
prev.). However, French bonds continued to underperform on a relative basis, with the 10yr OAT-Bund 
spread reaching 153bps, its widest level since 2011. So the French concerns are still there, even if the 
domestic unrest seems to have calmed for now. 
In the US, 10yr Treasury yields also declined by -3.0bps to 5.11%, though 2yr yields (+0.4bps) ended the 
day little changed as oil prices reversed their decline. Earlier in the session, a decline in yields had also 
been supported by soft housing data for September. This included a decline in housing starts (-5.2% vs 
+1.9% exp.) and a sharp slowing in pending home sales, with their annual growth falling to its lowest 
since 2023 at -12.6% yoy. So signs of the recent rise in yields weighing on the US housing market. 
In yesterday’s other data, in the UK the unemployment rate for June-Aug registered a big drop (4.7% vs. 4.9% 
est). So despite the number of job vacancies also falling, it was an overall hawkish batch of data, with 
payrolls also up by +13k. The UK inflation print is out just after this hits your inbox, but the labour market 
data will lean hawkish for many on the MPC and left 2yr gilts (+3.3bps) as a major exception from 

yesterday’s global bond rally. Staying in the UK, our economists are conducting a survey of expectations 
ahead of next week’s budget from the new Burnham Government. If you have a couple minutes to spare, they 
would appreciate your responses here. 
Early morning data showed that Japan's exports increased by +16.7% in September compared to the 
same month last year, driven by robust demand for semiconductors and AI-related products that continued 
to support growth. This marked the 13th consecutive month of gains following a 9.3% rise in August, 
although it was below expectation of a +21.3% increase. Meanwhile, imports rose by +18.9% y/y, falling 
short of the forecast of a +20.4% increase. A decrease in imports contributed to reducing Japan's trade 
deficit to -536.6 billion yen in September from -1.11 trillion yen in the previous month. 
To the day ahead, there will be a lot of data from the UK this morning, including September CPI, RPI and 
public finances. Central bank speakers include the ECB’s Lagarde and Cipollene and the BoE’s Bailey. We’ll 
also get earnings from Meta, Tesla and Lam Research among the notable names. Finally, the US is set to hold 
a 20yr bond auction at $13bn.
