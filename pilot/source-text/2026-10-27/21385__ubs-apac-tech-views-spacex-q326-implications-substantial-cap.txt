SpaceX Q326 implications: Substantial capex 
upgrade to accelerate its build-out
SpaceX share price rallied 8% after hours following its stronger result, confident outlook 
across SI (super-intelligence), Space and Connectivity and its ability to scale its compute 
with a fast payback. We highlight key metrics for SpaceX and implications that are 
relevant to the Asia tech supply chain including a substantial raise to UBS SpaceX capex - 
for direct SpaceX implications see also the report from covering analyst John Hodulik:
1. Results and profitability beat expectations.  Sales grew 87% QoQ / 161% YoY 
to US$14.6bn, above the street's US$12.9bn led by Superintelligence (SI) at US
$7.56bn (+195% QoQ), connectivity at US$5.25bn (+29% QoQ) and space at US
$1.77bn (+104% QoQ). Starlink subscribers grew from 12.0mn to 13.6mn, with 
monthly consumer ARPU up 5% QoQ to US$69. Management characterized the 
quarter as an inflection point with operating income swinging from a net loss to a 
US$2.8bn gain driven largely by AI OpM improvement from -207%/-49% in 
Q325/Q226 to positive 6% as cloud services ramp.
2. Monetization of the deployed capacity is ramping with AI. SI solutions/
infrastructure ramping from US$2.2bn to US$7.0bn (US$608mn adding 
Cursor) and most of the rest from cloud ramps.  Backlog has increased to US$63bn 
and ARR was projected to grow for the company to US$100bn by year-end (US
$8.3bn monthly run rate exiting the year) and US$500bn to US$1trn exiting 2027. 
The company believes its capex for cloud service is now getting a payback under a 
year and in some cases 6 months as compute continues to stay very tight and value 
of  a GW of compute rises with rising model intelligence.  For its own service, it is 
also seeing growing adoption for its Grok Bot agentic platform since the launch in 
August,  reaching 800k paid weekly users and >1mn run rate for Grok Enterprise 
(+50% QoQ). 
3. Upgrading its plans to deploy GW of compute. SpaceX installed GW of 
compute grew from 1.4GW to 1.7GW QoQ in Q326 and revised up its year-end 
target from >2GW to 2.5-3GW exiting the year.  For 2027, it targets adding further 
10GW of capacity to exit at 12.5-13GW, up from prior 10GW.  CEO Musk cited 
competitive differentiation on turbine blades and vanes plus renewable sources to 
help scale power and cooling capacity by 10GW in 2027 with upside stretch target 
to add 20GW. 
4. Accelerating NVIDIA Blackwell and Rubin rack installations. SpaceX 
continues to deploy mainly on NVIDIA, with 300MW in Q326 implying 2k GB300 
racks and target for another 600k in Q426 (8.3k racks)  or about 1.3GW.  For 2027, 
the company expects to maintain the pace at 200k/month upgrading to NVIDIA's 
upcoming Vera platform.  A deployment of 2.4mn Vera's would be ~33k racks, 
8.2GW capacity at 227kW and 1.10 PUE and at US$8-9mn a rack would cost US
$260-300bn for the compute and US$350bn including networking and data 
center infrastructure.  
5. UBS Capex upgraded to US$310bn in 2027. Q3 capex was US$19.6bn, above 
US$18.1bn expectation, with US$16.4bn of the spend for AI, US$1.5bn 
connectivity and US$1.6bn for space. Q4 was raised to US$35-40bn, above prior 
street US$19.8bn.  UBS analyst John Hodulik revised up SpaceX capex for 2026-27 
from US$56bn/US$124bn to US$73bn/US$310bn.  
6. Operating cash flow and funding for the capex ramp. The company exiting 
Q3 with US$90bn cash and US$39bn debt and ROIC improving from -3.6% to

Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Investing in the technology sector involves a high degree of risk. Rapid technological changes, 
increasing competition and exposure to macroeconomic cycles are among the many risks 
faced by investors in technology stocks. Moreover, it is extremely difficult to project the 
financial results of tech companies since their operating models are highly volatile and 
unpredictable. Finally, valuing technology stocks can prove challenging, as neither traditional 
nor non-traditional valuation measures have provided much insight into how these stocks 
trade. Valuation methodologies in the sector include target price/book multiples based on the 
long-term average ROE and the cost of equity.
