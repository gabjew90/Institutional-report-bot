 
"Please Don't Stop The Museic": Everything You 
Need To Know Ahead Of Meta's Earnings 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, OCT 21, 2026 - 09:50 PM 
Two months ago, Facebook Meta was the Magnificent 7's designated AI loser: lots of capex, 
nothing to show for it, and a stock that bottomed at $543.67 on August 18. Then on September 
8 Zuckerberg launched Muse, followed in October by the Muse Wonder frontier model, and the 
stock ripped 43% in five weeks to a $777.59 high on September 24 (despite exploding churn 
that would make Cablevision blush). 
Tonight, a week earlier than usual, Meta reports Q3 earnings, and the bar is about as high as it 
gets this earnings season. As JPMorgan's TMT trader Mark Schilsky puts it in his latest Tech 
Sketch earnings preview, "Please Don't Stop the Museic" (available to pro subs): 
"A lot can change in two months. Two months ago, META was trading ~$550 because investors 
viewed it as a relative AI loser (with a pretty meaningful legal overhang). They thought META 
had nothing to show for all of Zuckerberg's largesse. Well, he delivered: Muse and Wonder. As 
such, the stock is up nearly +40% off the lows and investor sentiment is white hot. Can 
management keep the party going? All eyes are on 2027 – especially the Opex guide – if we 
even get it this print... But we've a LOT of goodness recently. We're going to need some more 
surprises to break out to $800+…" 
A small correction: the stock is up closer to 30% from the lows now (and falling). Meta just 
snapped a five-session losing streak, its longest since July, and has closed in the red in nine of 
the last 11 trading days. At around $708 it sits 9% below the September peak. Which raises a 
question that a lot of people on the buyside are asking quietly, and UBS asked out loud:

Expectations: 9 Out Of 10 
Schilsky grades investor expectations for every big Internet name reporting over the next two 
weeks on a 1-to-10 scale. Netflix, which reported last night, got a 3. Alphabet gets a 5. Amazon, 
Spotify and Etsy each get a 7. Meta gets a 9, the only red box on the sheet (note the asterisk on 
the 2027 guides: "If we get it"): 
 
Goldman's desk sees the same thing from the positioning side. The bank's cash desk has Meta 
positioning at 8/10, and as the Goldman US equities desk put it in its Mid-Day Color note today, 
the "stock remains an overweight for most investors following an explosive move higher 
post-Muse release." Goldman's AIS team adds that the buyside is "largely braced for a top-line 
beat, with focus on FY26 capex guide and whether Muse plus ads monetization lets 
management keep raising OPEX into 2027 without the stock punishing them for it." 
Meta has plenty of company. Even as hedge funds dumped tech at the fastest pace in a decade 
(Info Tech saw "the largest 2-week % net selling on our record," -3.3 standard deviations, per 
Goldman Prime), the one corner nobody wants to sell is the megacaps: Mag 7 net exposure on 
the Goldman Prime book just hit a new record high, now around 22% of total US net exposure: 
 

 
 
Put differently, everyone is in the room. Goldman's sales desk framed tonight as "the first live 
test of whether a pure play hyperscaler holds the line on 2026/27 capex while FCF is under 
pressure." 
How big a move is priced in? According to Goldman's derivatives desk (GS Vol Views, available 
to pro subs), the implied move of 6.57% "looks cheap vs avg realized move 9.9% past 5 prints", 
while "flat near-term put call skew (20th %-ile vs 10y) shows constructive positioning." We 
checked the math, and Goldman is right: Meta has moved at least 8% the day after each of its 
last five reports, in both directions. Notably, the last two went the wrong way, and so did the last 
two Q3 prints: 
 

 
 
Meta's earnings are big enough to move the index too: Goldman's TMT specialists flagged that 
Friday's QQQ straddle is pricing a 2% move around the print, and see "attractive risk-reward in 
owning QQQ or META gamma." (The last time anyone tried that, on October 13, META won the 
gamma squeeze lottery.) 
The Quarter: "Just" The High End, Please 
On the actual Q3 numbers, the bar is simple. Meta guided to $61-64 billion in revenue, and 
according to Schilsky, "while channel checks have reportedly been solid, investors are expecting 
'just' the high end of the $61B to $64B guide this quarter. As such, the bogey is ~$64B (+26% 
Y/Y FXN) – with a slight upward bias. The Street is at ~$63.25B." 
Here is the full Bloomberg consensus. Note that the median revenue estimate is $63.33 billion, 
while the single highestsellside estimate, $64.07 billion, is basically where the buyside bogey 
starts. The EPS range, $2.61 to $4.43, is also unusually wide for a company with this much 
coverage, probably because not everyone has penciled in the same legal charges (more on that 
below). And Q3 capex is expected at $41 billion, vs. less than $19 billion a year ago: 
 

 
 
The good news for bulls: the ad market is cooperating. Goldman's internet analyst Eric 
Sheridan, in his "US Digital Ad Q3'26 Preview" (also available to pro subs), sees "a resilient 
digital advertising environment into Q3, with solid momentum exiting the quarter," with AI 
"increasingly becoming a structural driver of ad share gains." Sheridan reiterated his Buy and 
raised his price target to $860 from $725. Deutsche Bank's Benjamin Black (Buy, $820 target, 
now at 27x 2027E earnings "to reflect the strong adoption of the Muse agent") is at $63.7 billion 
and cites agency checks showing CPM growth "accelerated ~350bps sequentially in 3Q after 
accelerating roughly 650bps in 2Q," which "compare favorably to Street expectations for 
approximately 150bps of FX-neutral deceleration." 
For Q4, the Street is at ~$73.5 billion (+24% FXN). Schilsky says investors "will tolerate no more 
than a 1-point FXN decel on the top end of the guidance range," so the expected guide is 
$72-75 billion. Deutsche hears a slightly lower top end, "roughly $74-74.5bn." 
In other words, a beat on the quarter is the price of admission. In July, it was the revenue guide 
and another capex hike that sent the stock down 8%. As we tweeted at the time:

Muse: Show Us The Numbers 
The reason the stock is where it is, of course, is Muse. And since the agent launched with only 
three weeks left in the quarter, all the data so far comes from third parties. Schilsky's ask is 
blunt: "Will we get any hard and fast Muse-related KPIs from Zuckerberg? DAUs? Downloads? 
Anything? We all know it's been a big success, but with that success comes a LOT more Capex 
and Opex. Investors need to have confidence in forward-looking user metrics in order to credibly 
underwrite Zuckerberg's investments." 
The bull data: per Sensor Tower, Muse hit 10.9 million downloads since launch, 10.2 million of 
them in the US, making it one of only a dozen apps ever to reach 10 million US downloads in 40 
days (the last one was Meta's own Threads, which, well, we all know how that ended). Deutsche 
Bank's Figure 1 shows Muse running far ahead of ChatGPT, Sora and Gemini on post-launch 
downloads, users and time spent, though still a rounding error vs. ChatGPT's 50 million US 
DAUs: 
 
The bear data: as we detailed in "Peak Muse?" earlier this month, daily net user additions have 
slowed more than 60% from the September surge, Amazon blocked Meta's agents from its store 
within two weeks of launch, and banks are now following. Bloomberg's Dave Lee adds another: 
much of Muse's success "has come from sheer brute force," with almost 39% of Meta's entire 
marketing spend this month going to pushing Muse, per Sensor Tower. Nothing says organic 
growth like buying it. 
 

 
And then there is monetization, which is still mostly a promise. Muse can make money "through 
subscriptions, a commerce take-rate model, interchange spread, and advertising on Muse's 
Feed tab," per Deutsche, and the $100-a-month "Maximum" tier we broke down in September is 
a start. But Schilsky warns that "Muse monetization may not be terribly meaningful in 2027; it's 
set to hopefully ramp in 2028." Which brings us to the number that actually matters.

So it's probably no surprise that, per Goldman's FICC desk, "entirety of credit market has their 
focus on META earnings." And that's before counting what doesn't show up on the balance 
sheet at all: as Needham's Laura Martin calculated in early October, Meta has $628 billion in 
off-balance sheet commitments vs. $112 billion of on-balance sheet debt and leases. Add them 
to enterprise value, and the stock gets 35% more expensive. Regular readers know we have 
been warning since last October that the AI buildout runs on debt; Meta is now the test of how 
much more of it the market will take before it asks for equity instead. 
And speaking of bills, there's a fresh one. Just hours before the print, a New Mexico judge 
ordered Meta to pay $25 billionafter a jury found the company misled consumers about data 
privacy in the Cambridge Analytica case. The state had asked for $35-40 billion; Meta argued 
for a cap of $3.45 billion. We flagged it as soon as it hit:

That's on top of the ~$10 billion attorneys general settlement that Schilsky says is already 
headed for the 2026 opex guide (and which "no one really cares" about). We'll see if the market 
cares about two and a half times that. Meta will probably appeal, but for a company about to 
burn free cash flow, it's one more line in the funding plan. 
Everyone Wants In, Nobody Wants The Bill 
So here is the setup into tonight: 
●​ expectations at 9/10,  
●​ positioning at 8/10,  
●​ Mag 7 exposure at a record,  
●​ an implied move that Goldman calls cheap,  
●​ a buyside bar for 2027 spending that's $50 billion above the Street,  
●​ a product whose growth may already have peaked, and... 
●​ an equity raise that the majority of the buyside expects as soon as the stock lets it. 
Bloomberg points out that at 19x forward earnings, Meta is the second-cheapest Mag 7 stock 
after Nvidia. Well, it probably should be. 
The bull case is that Zuckerberg beats, guides Q4 to $75 billion, drops a Muse DAU number 
and keeps 2027 vague, and the stock grinds to $800 and the raise. The bear case is that he 
does all that and then says the words "notably larger" about 2027 capex. Judging by Meta's last 
two Q3 prints (-4% and -11% last October), and the last two prints overall (-9% and -8%), we 
know which one the tape has favored lately: the market keeps paying Meta for products and 
charging it for the bill. 
Schilsky's own closing question sums it up: "Can management keep the party going?" Maybe. 
But with the stock already 9% off the highs before the print, and Meta reporting a week early in 
what UBS suspects is a race to the equity window, we suspect the party is closer to last call. 
And the moment the cap raise lands, the bill arrives for everyone in the room.
