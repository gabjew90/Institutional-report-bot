 
Goldman Says Silver "Still Stuck" After Billions Of 
Spec-Selling, Puts Panic-Bid 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 22, 2026 - 02:05 AM 
Two weeks ago we asked whether silver bears had gotten a little too comfortable (Oct 9). The 
answer, so far: not yet. Silver is down roughly 3% since, to $57.48 this morning, and the specs 
are still selling. 
That is the message from the latest installment of Goldman's Robert Quinn's running silver 
diary, titled, with even more economy than the last one, "Silver Futures: Still Stuck" (available to 
pro subs). His summary: 
"Commitment of Traders covering October 6th - 13th revealed ongoing bearish Managed Money 
Silver flows. Macro and micro headwinds emerged. Despite a Dollar retracement over the 
ensuing 5 sessions, softness in structural demand indicators continued to weigh on Silver. 
Shorts seemingly initiated. While this presents an opportunity for a tactical bounce, the Iran War 
and its Dollar impact loom." (emphasis ours) 
Translation: the dollar let go of silver's throat for a few days, and silver still couldn't breathe, 
because the AI and solar trades that were supposed to be its bull case are now getting carted 
out on stretchers. 
 

 
 
Below we update the four-chart framework from our last post with Quinn's five new exhibits, and 
check how our "asymmetry to the upside" call is holding up (spoiler: it's been a 
character-building fortnight). 
Three Weeks, $2.2 Billion, And Counting 
Our October 9 post ended with a question: was the late-September dump capitulation, or just 
the start? Friday's COT data answered it, and not the way we hoped. Per Quinn, in the week to 
October 13 "Managed Money sold $565mm, marking the 3rd straight week of selling for a 
cumulative $2.2bn." Again it was a mix of tired longs and fresh bears: $160mm of liquidation 
and $405mm of new shorts. December silver lost 3.4% over the period. 
Goldman's chart of Managed Money net positioning shows the result: net length of roughly 
$1.7bn, below even the February trough and the lowest level on Goldman's two-year chart (read 
off the chart, so approximate). For context, the same measure sat above $9bn in the summer of 
2025. 
 

 
 
Quick bookkeeping note: the $12bn figure we cited last time was the broader Managed Money, 
Other and Non-Reportable aggregate; this chart is Managed Money alone. Different bucket, 
same direction: down. 
The Dollar Hit 1-Year Highs... Then Let Go. Silver Didn't Notice 
During the COT window, Quinn writes, "macro and micro headwinds emerged." The macro one 
you know: "The US Dollar reached 1 year highs and gained 0.3% overall." That was the week of 
the hot core CPI print, when, as we put it at the time, hawks circled as soaring CPI lifted the 
dollar and hammered everything else (Oct 14). The dollar broke out of its 18-month range, 
rate-hike odds spiked, and silver plunged 6% in a day to a two-month low: 
rate hikes are in the room again...​

But here is the new wrinkle. Over October 13-20, "the Dollar backtracked 0.5%." Silver still fell

only thing holding silver back. This week the dollar blinked, and silver fell anyway. Which means 
the second leg of Quinn's framework just broke. 
The Bull Case Got Liquidated Too 
Recall that Quinn tracks two equity baskets as real-time barometers for silver's structural 
demand: Goldman's Data Center basket (the AI buildout) and Solar (photovoltaics, one of 
silver's biggest industrial uses). Two weeks ago both were rising and silver ignored them. Now 
they are falling, and silver is paying attention: 
"US Solar companies surrendered their recent rebound and fell 4.7%. China Solar also traded 
poorly, dropping 1.6%. And Data Centers plunged 7.9% as concerns over Memory, Neoclouds, 
and general credit worthiness afflicted AI stocks." (emphasis ours) 
And it didn't stop there: over the following five sessions "Data Centers and Solar remained soft."

There is a nasty feedback loop buried in Quinn's last bullet, too: "some of the aforementioned 
Dollar stall resulted from AI unwinds, which also read negatively for Silver." In other words, the 
one thing that finally knocked the dollar off its highs (AI panic) is the same thing that kills silver's 
industrial-demand story. Heads the dollar wins, tails AI loses. Quinn's companion note on 
Nasdaq ("Memory Meltdown (2)", Oct 20) fills in the detail: Goldman's Hyperscaler basket "lost 
3.6% as credit spread widening accelerated" over October 13-16, as the AI selloff finally 
reached the big boys. 
Shorts Are Piling In, And Paying Up For Puts 
Now for the part that matters for anyone tempted to fade the move. Quinn sees fresh bears 
arriving after the COT cutoff: 
 

 
"Shorts seemingly initiated. Silver futures aggregate open interest rose $1.6bn with most 
surfacing on the heaviest day of price action. Moreover, normalized 25 delta put-call skew 
richened to a 98% 1 year rank at one point." 
The chart makes the point: aggregate open interest jumped from roughly 107k contracts to 
around 114k in mid-October, right as the price lurched down to the $56 area on CPI day. Rising 
open interest into a falling price is the textbook signature of new shorts, not exhausted longs 
throwing in the towel. 
 
And the options market is screaming the same thing. Two weeks ago Quinn flagged skew in the 
top two-year decile; now it hit a 98% one-year rank, with Goldman's normalized 3-month 
25-delta put-call skew ripping from around -10 to near -3, the richest since the July spike 
(approximate): 
 

 
 
Put differently: specs have dumped $2.2bn in three weeks, fresh shorts are piling into futures, 
and everyone left is buying downside protection. That is a positioning setup that tends to end 
with a violent squeeze; the question is only what pulls the trigger. 
The Tactical Bounce... And The Iran Problem 
Quinn, to his credit, sees it too, then immediately hedges: 
"While this presents an opportunity for a tactical bounce, the Iran War and its Dollar impact 
loom. Absent an end to the conflict, Silver may struggle to convincingly rally unless FX energy 
betas dampen much more." (emphasis ours) 
Translation: as long as every Hormuz headline sends Brent up and the dollar with it (the "FX 
energy beta"), silver's rallies are on borrowed time. With Brent back above $110, WTI near $99, 
and Iranian attacks on shipping still escalating into the midterms, that beta isn't dampening any 
time soon. Which is also a polite way of saying that Goldman's FX team's call that dollar upside 
"could stall", which Quinn leaned on two weeks ago, has so far worked for about five sessions. 
Bottom Line 
Time for some honest bookkeeping. On October 9 we said "one more flush toward the mid-$50s 
is entirely possible if Brent keeps climbing", but that "the asymmetry is to the upside." We got 
 

 
the flush (silver hit roughly $56 on CPI day); we're still waiting on the asymmetry. Anyone who 
took the August bait on $90 silver digitals (Aug 21) now needs a roughly 57% rally to get paid; 
and with gold steady at $4,122, the gold/silver ratio has widened to about 72 by our napkin 
math, from 70 two weeks ago.

But here's what changed: two weeks ago silver was stuck because of the dollar alone. Now it is 
stuck because of the dollar and the AI unwind, and the two are feeding each other. That makes 
the bears' position stronger in the short run, and far more crowded. Managed Money net length 
is at a two-year low, new shorts are piling in, and skew just hit a 98% one-year rank. The last 
time Quinn found positioning this washed out, in late July (we flagged it on Aug 6), the 
December contract ripped 15% in six weeks. 
We stick with the contrarian side, with a shorter fuse. Silver doesn't need peace in the Gulf, it 
just needs one of two things: a Hormuz de-escalation that knocks the dollar's energy bid, or the 
AI credit rout getting bad enough that the Fed's hiking talk turns into cutting talk. Our money is 
on the second: a market that has priced three more hikes while data centers fall like dominoes 
is a market about to change its mind, and when it does, the most crowded short in metals gets a 
very bad day. Until then, silver remains what Quinn says it is: stuck, just a little lower.
