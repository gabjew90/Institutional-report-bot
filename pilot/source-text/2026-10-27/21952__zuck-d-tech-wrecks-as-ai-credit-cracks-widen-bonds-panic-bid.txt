Dated Brent - the most accurate price for physical barrels - is back on the rise again... 
 
The squeeze on oil-product flows from the Gulf, along with escalating Ukrainian attacks on 
Russian refineries, has sent refined products even higher, most notably diesel. Governments 
are tapping stockpiles to ease tightness, while US President Donald Trump is pushing measures 
to lower prices ahead of the midterm elections, including a diesel deal with Moscow. 
However, despite the fact that a tanker loaded with Russian diesel set sail for the US today as 
Moscow begins implementing the supply deal (vessel has a capacity of 40,000 metric tons, 
equivalent to only 300,000 barrels), diesel prices rose today, erasing yesterday's optimistic 
decline...

Finally, circling back to where we started, Meta’s earnings suddenly showed investors are 
increasingly demanding that revenue growth translates into earnings and free cash flow.  
As Bloomberg macro strategist, Michael Ball, notes, the latter will become a more important 
distinguisher for technology stocks as rising financing costs bring greater scrutiny to future 
CapEx spending.  
Alphabet, SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI and others AI names are competing for enormous 
amounts of capital at the same time credit investors are becoming more selective about project 
returns. The marginal AI project therefore increasingly needs to have a clearer road to returns. 
The table below shows strong free-cash-flow metrics have not yet consistently translated into 
better equity returnsacross technology stocks. 
 

 
 
That relationship bears watching as the next phase of the AI trade may be less about who can 
spend the most and more about who can keep spending without having to ask capital markets 
for permission. 
Apple’s strong free cash flow and relatively modest infrastructure spending have turned it into a 
defensive hedge as hyperscalers and chipmakers swing with the AI-capex debate. The catch is 
valuation: Apple now trades at a premium while growing more slowly than many peers. 
Renewed pressure on the AI trade today shows how fragile sentiment remains. Barclay’s BETS 
team notes the recent rebound in semiconductor stocks closely resembled normal mean 
reversion after eight straight declines. They highlight that weak participation across AI names, 
particularly neoclouds and memory stocks, reinforces that investors did not broadly re-embrace 
the trade and continue to watch credit markets closely.  
The bottom line: the market has stopped treating the AI selloff as a positioning wash and started 
treating it as a growth problem, and it is hedging accordingly... 
Which may help explain why rate-hike odds plummeted today. 
 

 
 
The tell was in the first chart all the way at the top - Treasuries bid into an oil spike: the market is 
reading higher oil as a growth threat, not an inflation threat, which is a more worrying signal for 
risk assets than the index moves themselves.
