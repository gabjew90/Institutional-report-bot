 
"Rekt": McElligott Says The Bond Short-Squeeze 
Just Blew Up Every "High Sharpe" Trend Trade 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, OCT 20, 2026 - 09:15 PM 
After three straight weeks of client marketing on three continents, Nomura's Charlie McElligott 
got back to his screens to find that the trends he left behind are no longer trending. 
In his latest note, aptly titled "DE-TRENDED" (available to pro subs), McElligott says the 
defining feature of the market right now is a sweeping loss of legacy cross-asset "Momentum," 
one that accelerated sharply last week, and that it all traces back to one place: 
...it all starts with the squeeze in Bond Shorts, where the lower Yields are waking-up potential 
"Reversals" in everything from "Anti-Dollar" FX to Small Cap / Value Equities (anything 
non-AI)...and being further boosted today by the local news reports of potential "Iran progress" 
(again), which fits my "Trump wants a deal into Midterms" -Upside risk -scenario, with Energy 
off, Bonds squeezing further and all just as the Equities market shows Leveraged Fund Nets 
back at 1y / 3y / 5y Lows OOOOF (emphasis ours) 
Translation: the most crowded trade of 2026, short duration, just got squeezed, and the unwind 
is spilling into every trend-following book that leaned on it. 
 

 
 
Below we walk through the damage across asset classes, why McElligott doesn't (yet) see a 
regime change, the growth-scare tail he's watching, and the curious case of the buy side loading 
up on calls while dumping stock. 
The Momentum Unwind Goes Cross-Asset 
Start with the scoreboard. Nomura's cross-asset momentum factor is down 4.01% over the past 
five days and 2.72% month-to-date, with US equities momentum the worst of the bunch: -5.87% 
on the week and -7.08% MTD, which takes a chunk out of its +7.29% YTD gain. Interest-rate 
swap trend strategies are bleeding in both the US and Europe: 
 

 
 
Inside equities, McElligott describes it as "reverse dispersion", with "everything else" 
outperforming the legacy AI winners. Equal-weight vs the S&P is up a 2.2-sigma move over five 
days, short-term reversal is +1.8 sigma, while the high-minus-low momentum factor is down 2.5 
sigma, or 6.8%, on the week. Regular readers will recognize this as the "catch-down" Goldman 
warned about over the weekend, when Ben Snider noted that either way breadth resolves, 
momentum loses (Oct 18): 
 

 
 
Ground Zero: The Bond Short 
McElligott is clear that this isn't (yet) the market repricing a whole new macro regime. Instead, 
it's the mechanical unwind of crowded trend trades: 
This does not (yet) seem to be about repricing the probability distribution into a full-blown 
"Macro Regime Change," and for now is moreso a function of obvious "High Sharpe" Trend 
trade -crowding then reversing sharply and rolling downhill, where basic PNL monetization spun 
itself into a larger risk management de-grossing impulse, particularly in the Leveraged 
-community...and originating primarily within the "Bond Short" ground-zero (emphasis ours) 
Nomura's QIS trend model now flags almost every major bond contract as "Reversing." The 
long end did the heavy lifting: USD 30Y futures returned 3.25% last week and USD 20Y 2.77% 
(a 1.87 z-score on a five-year lookback), with every one of those contracts still showing negative 
trailing Sharpes on every horizon from three months to five years. In other words, the bond short 
paid for years, right up until it didn't: 
 

 
 
The same flags light up across G10 FX and STIRs, from EURUSD and GBPUSD to SOFR and 
Euribor futures: 
 
And the "anti-dollar" reversal kept going this morning: on the Iran "compromise proposal" 
headlines, the Bloomberg dollar index slid 0.4% to its lowest since September 30. 
 

 
So why did it happen during a week of hot inflation data? McElligott's explanation is refreshingly 
simple: hot prints are the best time to take profits on a short. 
...the Bonds / STIRS -reversals last week were INITIALLY about PNL being taken in crowded 
Shorts around the "Hot Inflation" prints, which then simply ballooned into a larger covering 
squeeze (intuitively, "hot" prints are a reasonable time to monetize some of your short in Bonds / 
STIRS instead of having to chase on a day where hypothetical weak / light US data would send 
us into a more violent squeeze) (emphasis ours) 
That squares with what we observed in real time, when a week of hotter-than-expected CPI 
somehow ended with the 10Y at a three-week low, because, as we put it, "the pain trade was 
long AI, short bonds" (Oct 17).

sustainability, and credit and financing costs, all of which drove further liquidations. It didn't help 
that South Korean data-center firm Elice plunged more than 30% in its trading debutovernight:

The de-grossing also lifted the stocks nobody wanted. Legacy shorts and underweights in both

repricing the Energy Shortage / Inflation / Hawkish Central Bank "worst case scenario" lower yet 
again. 
Readers may recall that in his "BINARIES" note (Oct 9), McElligott laid out exactly this 
permutation: conciliatory "Iran Deal Talk" into the midterms would see energy and rate vol 
smoked, with the trades then in the "bombed-out stuff" like gold and IWM upside. Eleven days

Not A Growth Scare... Yet 
The bigger question is whether this turns into something worse. McElligott doesn't believe last 
week's reversal marked a capitulation into a "growth scare / recession" base case, which would 
send rates sharply lower and endanger a Treasury market still positioned for high rates on the 
"Run Hot" thesis of AI-capex trickle-down, global fiscal dominance and 
energy-as-national-security. But he doesn't rule it out either: 
However, we could absolutely still get to that "Growth Scare" place in short-order, IF said higher 
Rates, wider Credit Spreads and sticky Energy prices / ongoing shortages in Refined product 
could suddenly lead to sharply more restrictive FCI, in-turn creating the dreaded "Demand 
Destruction" -scenario in the US Economy (emphasis ours) 
That explains the client flows he's been flagging in low-strike receivers and curve caps, which 
pay off in a growth scare but also in an Iran-deal relief rally. And the rates-vol picture is shifting: 
while the intermediate-to-long end remains heavily skewed "payers over" thanks to legacy 
high-strike hedges and CMT accrual flows, Nomura's swaptions desk says the left-hand side 
(1y1y, 1m10y) has now likely flipped to "receivers over" in aggregate. Put differently, the front 
end is now paying up to hedge lower rates, not higher. 
For what it's worth, today's data didn't do the soft-landing crowd any favors: pending home sales 
collapsed 7.3% to a record low and housing starts plunged to 1.221 million, well below the 1.305 
million expected. 
Sell The Stock, Buy The Calls 
McElligott's final observation is perhaps the most telling. The latest OCC data through Friday 
showed a massive spike in one-week net call demand, at the 100th percentile for ETFs and the 
98th percentile for single stocks on a five-year lookback, while put demand sat at the 5th and 
19th percentiles respectively: 
 

 
 
His read: a textbook "stock replacement" move by the buy side. Investors cut their underlying 
exposure into the selloff, which lines up with prime brokerage data (hence no need for puts), but 
remain nervous about missing a year-end rally, so they bought cheap upside to keep a foot in 
the door. Fear of missing out, now available in a defined-risk wrapper. 
Bottom Line 
McElligott's diagnosis is that this is a crowding unwind, not (yet) a regime change: high-Sharpe 
trend trades got too crowded, the bond short cracked on hot data, and risk managers did the 
rest. His upside scenario, a pre-midterm Iran deal that unleashes the "Left Behind" stocks, is the 
one he's been flagging for weeks. 
We're more skeptical. Today's "deal" lasted about as long as it took Tehran to issue a denial, 
while the growth data keeps rolling over: record-low pending home sales, near-COVID housing 
starts, and a credit market that has been pricing AI financing stress for weeks. The swaptions 
desk seeing "receivers over" on the left side, and clients buying low-strike receivers and curve 
caps, tells us the smart money is quietly hedging McElligott's tail scenario, not his base case. 
With leveraged fund nets at multi-year lows and calls at the 100th percentile, the market is 
positioned for a rally it can't commit to. Our bet: the growth scare gets here before the Iran deal 
does.
