Sentiment check: SPCX commentary lifts IPPs. SPCX’s power demand 
commentary on the 3Q26 earnings call lifted the power complex on 
Wednesday. Management discussed a “blue sky” scenario involving adding 
20GW of incremental power and cooling next year, with high confidence in at 
least 10 GW. Against a weaker AI sentiment backdrop, the comments reinforce 
the speed and scale of demand. While we see power names benefitting at large, 
we continue to see IPPs as best positioned, given existing near-term supply. 
Ahead of todays session, our trading desk saw supply in CEG, with investors 
reducing exposure post contracting wins, in our view.

XE: X-energy and American Electric Power announced a Joint Development 
Agreement (JDA) to study the feasibility of a proposed 960MW, 12-unit Xe-100 small 
modular reactor (SMR) plant in Sullivan County, Indiana. Amazon plans to support 
project development through a funding agreement and subsequently purchase the power 
under a long-term contract. AEP’s now-retired coal plant, Breed Generating Station, is 
the primary location under consideration for the project. We see the announcement as an 
encouraging signal, following through on 2Q call commentary that pointed to a 1GW 
announcement by year-end. As the parties progress through feasibility studies, we 
believe incremental details around firm contracted offtake, financing structure, capex, 
and licensing timeline could build meaningful investor confidence in the long-term 
growth outlook.
•
ATRL CN:  nuclear – Indiana MoU highlights multi-billion dollar U.S. new-build 
opportunity. Our Smid Industrials team noted that  AtkinsRéalis announced it had signed 
a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with Hoosier Energy Partner and the state of 
Indiana to explore the potential development of a campus of up to eight CANDU nuclear 
reactors, providing up to 6GW of nuclear-powered baseload generation. The MoU will 
facilitate initial discussions related to deployment scenarios for ATRL’s CANDU 
technology in Indiana to provide affordable electricity, provide grid resilience and energy 
security, while simultaneously providing economic opportunities for local residents. 
Likely worth ~$12-24B, we view this announcement as another proof point of the 
significant upside potential of new-build opportunities, reinforcing our Overweight 
rating.
•
Electric Utilities & Power: sparking credit: AEP announces data center PPA with 
Google; Venture Global gets involved in AI boom; PSPS warnings in California; 
weekly briefing. Our Utilities and Power Credit colleagues noted that PJM fast-tracks 
2.1 GW of storage and gas capacity. PJM Interconnection approved three projects totaling 
approximately 2.1 GW through its Expedited Interconnection Track, including Engie 
storage projects of 860 MW in Pennsylvania and 800 MW in Ohio. An LS Power 
subsidiary also received approval for a 455 MW uprate at the Hunterstown combined-
cycle plant, with all three projects expected online by mid 2029. Venture Global to build 
gas-fired generation facilities to power the AI boom. Natural-gas exporter Venture 
Global announced today that it will be providing power to data centers by building gas-
fired generation facilities. The company has agreements with GE Vernova and Baker 
Hughes Energy Services to procure turbines and transformers for the on-site power 
plants. Venture Global already operates its own off-grid power plants, and the new 
projects set to come online in late 2028 will not be connected to the grid either. AEP adds 
fast-deploying generation options for large-load growth. AEP signed an agreement to 
purchase up to 1.6 GW of Mainspring Energy linear generators. These smaller, 
dispatchable generation units can potentially be deployed more quickly than 
conventional central-station generation. Mainspring will install the first 190 MW in 
2027, and AEP has an option to order another 200 MW a year through 2034, with the deal 
expected to represent a value of over $4 billion. AEP plans a $4 billion Google-backed 
fuel-cell project. American Electric Power plans to invest approximately $4 billion in a 
702 MW fuel-cell generation facility near Cheyenne, Wyoming, supported by a 20-year 
PPA with Google. The project, which is approximately 92% owned and controlled by 
AEP, is scheduled to begin construction in 2026 and reach full operation by year-end 
2028. The facility will use Bloom Energy technology to produce electricity from natural 
gas without combustion, with plans to integrate carbon capture technology.
