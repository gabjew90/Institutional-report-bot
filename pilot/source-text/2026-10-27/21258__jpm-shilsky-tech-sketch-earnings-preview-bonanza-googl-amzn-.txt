SCHILSKY’S Q326 EARNINGS PREVIEW BONANZA
GOOGL Q326 Earnings Preview – Street (Consensus) Fighter II: Search vs. Cloud:
Fight!
GOOGL is a well-debated stock; it’s on the Battleground for a reason. On the one hand, Google Cloud
Revenues are likely to crush this quarter, thanks to TPU sales. On the other hand, Search Revenue growth
is expected to miss the Street because the alt data (which is always right!) suggests so. Yes, I get it. It’s
tough for GOOGL’s stock to work when Search is decelerating, but GOOGL is also on a path to where
Google Cloud Revenues could be equal to Search Revenues by late 2028. That’s massive! Who thought
that was possible 1 year ago? And now we’re debating whether or not Search is going to miss by like -50
bps this quarter. Who cares?!? GOOGL is a MASSIVE AI winner that will likely launch a ‘real’

competitor to META Muse in the coming months. Focus on the Revenue & Earnings ramp in 2027 – not
near-term worries about a small Search miss.
GOOGL reports Q326 earnings on Wednesday October 28th after the close. (Later than usual!)
Q326 Earnings Expectations:
• Search: This will be the most scrutinized KPI on the print because everyone ‘knows’ that Search is likely
to miss. Everyone ‘knows’ that Search is likely to miss by about -100 bps because that’s where the alt data
is – even though the alt data is so often wrong. This is a BIG part of the reason GOOGL’s stock has
languished since Q2 earnings. All told, investor expectations are for Search Revenue to grow ~+14% Y/Y
FXN – and reported. (I’ve heard as low as +13.5% and as high as +15.0% Y/Y – no one is above the
Street’s ~+15.0% Y/Y). Given how scrutinized this KPI is…if this beats, the stock is almost assuredly
going higher T+1.
• Keep an eye on Paid Clicks growth – they grew +13% Y/Y in Q226. The comps get 3 points harder this
print. There’s no explicit investor expectation here, but I think it’s safe to say that if the 2-year stack holds
up, that would be viewed favorably by investors.
• YouTube: Honestly, no one really cares about YouTube. As long as growth doesn’t dip below +10% Y/Y,
investors will continue to ignore it. The Street is at +11% Y/Y; investor expectations are similar.
• Google Cloud: Street Revenue growth estimates (~+90% Y/Y) are too low and investors are expecting a
solid beat – largely due to TPU sales. Now, modeling quarterly TPU sales is quite difficult, especially
since we don’t truly know what GOOGL’s Revenue/GW is. I’ve heard a wide range of estimates from
$10B to $35B per GW – with the majority of people in the $20B to $30B range. As for Q326 Google
Cloud Revenue growth investor expectations, most are in the +105% to +115% Y/Y range. I’ve heard
higher, but I don’t think that’s where most investors truly are. As for implied TPU sales in the quarter,
most are in the $5B to $7B range. Any chance Anat gives us any real sense for how many TPUs GOOGL
sold in the quarter? I doubt it, but I suppose you never know. FWIW, I think the pressure on her to provide
additional clarity on this rapidly growing Revenue line item will grow TREMENDOUSLY in 2027.
• Google Cloud Margins: For me, this is the sneaky MOST IMPORTANT KPI of the print. I received a
TON of feedback to my GOOGL Bull Pitch from two weeks ago and while I think it’s highly likely that
GOOGL will generate anywhere from $5B to $10B of EBIT per GW of TPUs sold, the reality is that this
is a heavily debated point amongst investors. As such, given that this is the first quarter in which TPU
sales are likely to be truly meaningful, any beat to the Street’s ~33% EBIT Margin estimate will be
rightfully viewed by investors as modest validation that TPU margins are close to – or possibly higher
than – core Google Cloud Margins. Keep a close eye on this number this quarter. It might result in
significant EPS revisions in the out years.
• Operating Income: Investors expect a beat above the Street’s ~$43B estimate. The level of expected beat
largely hinges on how much each investor expects Search to (possibly) miss being offset by their TPU
sales estimates. All told, based on my conversations, investor expectations are for a ~+$2B beat; call the
bogey ~$45B.

2027/2028 Investor Expectations:
• 2027 Google Cloud: Universally, investors believe that Street Revenue growth estimates of +68% are too
low – largely due to the Street mis-modeling TPU sales. Most investors are >+100% Y/Y; call the
expectation +100% Y/Y to +120% Y/Y.
• Implicit in this debate is ‘What should we capitalize TPU sales at?’ Rightly or wrongly, I think what most
investors will do is they will take TPU Sales numbers, apply a margin against it, tax effect it, and calculate
a ‘TPU EPS’ figure and then apply an NVDA/AVGO NTM P/E against it, somewhere in the mid-teens.
Call it ~15x. And then apply a somewhat higher multiple, 20x?, to ‘core’ GOOGL EPS to arrive at a
blended P/E multiple in the high teens. I think this is a simple and reasonable way to account for the
impact TPU sales will have on GOOGL’s valuation in the coming years.
• 2027 Capex: While the Street did an admirable job of raising 2027 Capex estimates before and after
Q226 earnings, they still remain too low at ~$305B. Investor expectations are much higher, mostly in the
range of $330B to $350B.
• 2028 Capex: Trust me, I’ve been asking investors for weeks for this number, but the most common
answer I’ve received is a combination of ‘Higher’ and ‘If anyone tells you with any certainty what their
estimate for Google’s 2028 is, they are b*#!$#!ing you.’
• 2027 EPS: The majority of investors I have spoken with are in the $14.50 to $16.00 range although I’ve
heard higher. [It largely depends on what your TPU estimates are both in terms of GW sold and
EBIT/GW.] The Street is at ~$15.
• 2028 EPS: The majority of investors I have spoken with are in the $18.00 to $25.00 range although I’ve
heard higher. The modal outcome here is the low $20s. [Again, this largely depends on TPU estimates. It
also makes sense that the range of estimates is wider…the future is unpredictable, after all.] The Street is
at ~$18.
META Q326 Earnings Preview – Please Don’t Stop the Museic:
A lot can change in two months. Two months ago, META was trading ~$550 because investors viewed it
as a relative AI loser (with a pretty meaningful legal overhang). They thought META had nothing to show
for all of Zuckerberg’s largesse. Well, he delivered: Muse and Wonder. As such, the stock is up nearly
+40% off the lows and investor sentiment is white hot. Can management keep the party going? All eyes
are on 2027 – especially the Opex guide – if we even get it this print. Combine that with any Muse-related
KPI’s Zuckerberg might provide us with and you potentially have the recipe for the stock continuing to
power higher. But we’ve a LOT of goodness recently. We’re going to need some more surprises to break
out to $800+…
Doug recently Upgraded META to a Buy: “We believe there’s still meaningful upside potential as Meta is
in the early stages of releasing frontier models and AI-driven products beyond advertising, notably Muse
AI agent and Meta Model API access. Frontier models are at the core of Meta’s product and monetization
pipeline over a multi-year period, and its path toward superintelligence.”

• 2026 Opex: First and foremost, this guide has to be upped by +$10B on both the top and bottom ends

$165B to $169B is already quite narrow. There’s a chance Susan narrows it on a like-for-like basis to
$166B to $168B, but given that the 2027 guide looms MUCH larger in the minds of investors, this doesn’t
really matter all that much.
• 2027 Opex: This is a hot topic of debate for investors. While nearly everyone agrees that Street estimates
of ~$203B are too low, I have heard a wide range of possibilities from $210B to $230B. All told, most
investors are expecting a 2027 Opex guidance range with $220B or $225B on the high end. Thus, expect a
guide that looks like $210/$215B to $220/$225B – if we even get it this print. [Remember, META changed
its guidance philosophy last year and proclaimed that it no longer will give forward-year Opex/Capex
guides on the Q3 print. But I also suppose Susan could change her mind again if she wished. Also,
META’s history suggests that they may want to start this number out ‘heavy’ and then look to walk it

the ‘old’ ways of guiding.]
• 2027 Operating Income: The Street has 2027 OI growing about +$18B Y/Y – but the Street’s 2027 Opex
estimates are also about -$18B too light. In other words, can Susan put in the Q3 (or Q4) release the same
thing she did in the Q425 earnings release?: “Despite the meaningful step up in infrastructure investment,
in 2026 we expect to deliver operating income that is above 2025 operating income.” We’ll see next
quarter.
• 2026 Capex: Everyone is resigned to the view that META will end near the high end of the current
guidance range of $130B to $145B. As such, don’t be surprised if Susan brings up the bottom range up to
something like $135B to $145B. Again, 2027 matters far more.

• 2027 Capex: The Street’s ~$200B remains too low. Most investors I spoke with are in the range of
$225B to $250B. If Susan were to guide 2027 Capex this print, which I don’t think you should assume,
investors expect a range that looks just like $225B to $250B. As for 2028 investor expectations…’higher’.
• In the Q325 Release Susan guided, “Our current expectation is that capital expenditures dollar growth
will be notably larger in 2026 than 2025. We also anticipate total expenses will grow at a significantly
faster percentage rate in 2026 than 2025, with growth driven primarily by infrastructure costs, including
incremental cloud expenses and depreciation.” At the very least, I think it’s likely that she will issue a
similar guide regarding 2027 Capex given that we all ‘know’ that 2027 Capex dollar growth will be larger
than in 2026. As for Opex, investor expectations are for slower growth in 2027 so either she may not
‘guide’ that figure on this print or give us some verbiage that is the opposite: ‘grow at a slower rate in
2027 than 2026.’ Opex growth matters WAY MORE than Capex growth because investors care so MUCH
MORE about EPS than FCF.
• All told, please focus on the sum of 2027 Opex/Capex. Accounting for how much META will spend on
datacenters via capex (cash costs upfront) vs. opex (lease payments to operators like Crusoe) is difficult
due to do with absolute precision. As such, once we get the initial 2027 Opex and Capex guides from
Susan, focus on the sum…Buyside Consensus for 2027 Opex + Capex is in the range of $435B to $475B –
that’s a midpoint of $455B.
• Yes, a majority of investors still believe that an Equity Raise is likely post-Q3, assuming the stock is high
enough. (Certainly >$800. ~$850 would be ‘better’.) We all know that META is going to burn FCF next
year; the only question is whether the cost of debt or the cost of equity will be lower when they need to
raise capital.
• 2027/2028 EPS: There’s definitely some real concerns that the initial 2027 Opex guide (if we get it this
quarter – which I doubt – but we’ll definitely get it on the Q4 print) will be high enough that 2027 Street
EPS estimates of $34 might come down first to as low as ~$32. Part of the issue also is that Muse
monetization may not be terribly meaningful in 2027; it’s set to hopefully ramp in 2028. On the flip side,
I’ve heard 2027 EPS estimates from investors as high as $37 with the majority of investors close to the
Street’s $34. Thus, ‘real’ Bulls are focused on 2028: I’ve heard estimates anywhere from $35 to $45 with
the majority in the low $40’s vs. the Street’s ~$39.

management is struggling to fix their engagement problem. Ted said at the Bloomberg Screentime
conference: ““Overall, we’re not growing as fast as I want us to, and we’re working on making that move
faster,”…“We are, though, also doing things that create a lot of headwind to that number.”” Does that

investors. Honestly, any short-term pop in the stock post-print feels like it will be short-lived. The Q4 print

SPOT Q326 Earnings Preview – Can Management Remix the Bull Case?:
SPOT remains a frustratingly crowded long headed into Q3 earnings season. SPOT is in the same
‘frustrating long bucket’ as AMZN, CVNA and TTWO. In other words, I hear the most complains about
this quartet on any given day during which they underperform. SPOT has been uniquely frustrating
because it entered the year in the ‘AI loser bucket’, but laid out a credible plan to launch an AI remixing
product that could result in a meaningful ARPU uplift, along with a credible counterweight to Suno/Udio,
and yet the stock has been stuck in a modest trading range ($450 to $550) ever since the May Investor
Day. So what does SPOT have to do in order to break out of this trading range? First off, they need to print
a clean quarter. (Last quarter was a touch mixed, let’s be honest). And second…they need to LAUNCH
NEW PRODUCTS/TIERS. Until that happens, SPOT may continue to be ‘stuck’.
Investor expectations for the AI remixing product have slipped. No longer do a majority of investors
expect the AI remixing product to launch in Q426; now, it’s mostly viewed as a Q127 debut. First, we
need WMG and SONY to sign licensing deals. Maybe we get a surprise announcement on this earnings
call – but I doubt it. WMG should occur before SONY.
A NFLX Side Note: Part of SPOT’s underperformance this year (-9% YTD) can be attributed to NFLX.
NFLX has de-rated considerably this year with the stock down -25% YTD and in my estimation, SPOT
can only trade at so big of a premium to NFLX as they are so often compared to one another as the
predominant subscription-based Media companies in the public markets. Tacitly, if you are long SPOT,
you are also long NFLX. [This is a big part of the reason that Long SPOT, Short NFLX remains a VERY
popular pair trade…that I endorse.]
