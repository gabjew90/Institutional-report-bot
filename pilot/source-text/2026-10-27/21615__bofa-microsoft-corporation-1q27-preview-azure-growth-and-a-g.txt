Azure: buildout execution remains the key to growth 
The conversion of Azure-related RPO into revenue remains gated by Microsoft’s ability 
to deliver additional compute capacity. We remain constructive on execution as 
Microsoft is bringing new infrastructure online faster and is also increasing output from 
its existing fleet. 
• 
Faster deployment accelerates revenue conversion. Microsoft reduced GPU 
deployment times in its largest regions by nearly 50% over the past year, shortening 
the interval between infrastructure investment and revenue generation. 
• 
Greater throughput increases available capacity. Hardware and software 
optimization increased throughput for Copilot workloads by 4x since the beginning 
of the year. With demand exceeding supply, these gains were quickly monetized and 
contributed to Azure’s 4Q26 43% YoY growth, supporting our view of continued 
Azure acceleration in FY27. 
• 
Maia 300 supports efficiency and longer-term growth. Microsoft plans to 
deploy Maia 300 in CY27 as it shifts more AI inference workloads onto in-house 
silicon. The system is expected to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt than 
leading GPU systems. We believe this should help Microsoft serve more AI demand 
for the same amount of power, supporting Azure growth and improving inference 
economics. 
• 
Datacenter expansion remains on track. Microsoft added ~1GW of capacity per 
quarter over the last three quarters and remains on track to roughly double total 
capacity by CY28. Fairwater’s first Wisconsin facility came online back in April, six 
weeks ahead of schedule, enabling earlier revenue recognition, with a second facility 
scheduled for completion in CY28.  
Into the print, we will focus on whether faster deployment and efficiency gains continue 
to translate into incremental revenue, and what the timing of additional capacity implies 
for Azure’s growth outlook.

The new Copilot: from assistant to operating layer 
Microsoft introduced a redesigned Copilot that brings together chat, delegated work, 
application development and AI agents within a single user interface. Home serves as 
the starting point, combining Chat with Cowork and embedding Word, Excel and 
PowerPoint directly within Copilot. Code allows users to build applications, dashboards 
and automations using natural language, while Autopilot allows users to build AI agents 
with their own identity and memory that can monitor activity, perform recurring work 
and continue operating when the user is away. 
Supporting the new Copilot, Microsoft is connecting it to company-specific data and 
business context through Fabric IQ. This lets Copilot use the organization’s proprietary 
information to answer questions and complete multistep tasks, while keeping existing 
data permissions in place. 
We view the redesign as an important step in Microsoft’s effort to position Copilot as 
the operating layer and primary interface for enterprise AI. Rather than requiring users 
to move between individual applications and separate AI tools, the new experience 
provides a single place to ask questions, create content, delegate workflows and build 
software, with Copilot ultimately routing each request to the appropriate capability or 
underlying LLM.  
More importantly, the new design brings together several of Microsoft’s core 
advantages, including its distribution across enterprise productivity applications, access 
to organizational context, model choice and an integrated security and governance layer. 
We believe these capabilities differentiate Copilot from standalone AI assistants as the 
market shifts from chat-based AI interactions toward agents capable of completing end-
to-end work. 
A credible path to ARPU expansion 
Everyday AI tasks, including drafting, summarization and analysis, remain covered by the 
$30 fixed-price Microsoft 365 Copilot subscription, while more advanced capabilities 
such as Cowork, Code and Autopilot consume Copilot Credits through usage-based 
billing. Importantly, these capabilities generally require an underlying user subscription, 
allowing Microsoft to layer consumption revenue on top of its existing per-seat model. 
We believe the structure provides a credible path to higher ARPU while better aligning 
pricing with the complexity and potential value of the work performed.  
Copilot paid seat growth in focus 
Alongside higher ARPU, sustained paid-seat growth is critical to the Copilot story. Paid 
seats rose from 15mn in 2Q26 to 20mn in 3Q26 and 30mn in 4Q26, with net additions 
more than doubling QoQ in 4Q26. We believe continued momentum is important to 
support the stock and validate Copilot as a meaningful growth lever beyond Azure.

Updating our estimates under the new

Under the new reporting structure, Microsoft will report revenue across two segments, 
Agents & Infrastructure and Devices & Consumers, and will disclose Azure revenue in

like GitHub and Security Copilot, which we believe gives investors a cleaner view of the 
underlying business.

For 2Q27, we model total revenue growth of 17.8% YoY cc and EPS of $4.84. We model

Price objective basis & risk 
Microsoft Corporation (MSFT) 
Our price objective of $600 is based on 28x our CY27E P/E, a premium to the peer group 
of 18-25x. Sustained revenue growth and margin profile warrants the multiple above the 
high end of the peer group range, in our view. 
 
Downside risks to our price objective: 1) near term gross margin pressure as the Azure 
business grows as a percentage of overall revenue. 2) AI applications and model 
providers could innovate at a faster rate than Microsoft, potentially limiting the 
company's growth profile. 3) Enterprise application spending has proven to be highly 
cyclical, given the more discretionary nature of applications projects.
