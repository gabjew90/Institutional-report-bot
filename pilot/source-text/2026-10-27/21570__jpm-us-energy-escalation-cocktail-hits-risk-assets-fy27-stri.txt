Brendan’s Brief:
In Brief: Global markets firmly trading risk off this morning as oil volatility picks up and early mega-cap earnings disappoint. On 
the commodity front, it feels hard to argue things have not spiraled meaningfully in recent sessions. The whack-a-mole approach 
to alleviating bottlenecks, be it freight or diesel, appear to have done little to arrest the rise in prices, attacks in the gulf have risen 
both in cadence and scope and escalation by the US & Israel, increasingly appears to be the base case.
 
In a market already short freight, tankers are being damaged with increased frequency. While unconfirmed, Iran is reporting that 
seven oil tankers either caught fire or were damaged in the Strait overnight. This dilapidation in conditions in the strait comes as 
the Houthi/Saudi conflict splinters. While operations were reportedly unaffected, yesterday’s attack on Shaybah (1mbd), was the 
latest sign that energy infrastructure is increasingly in scope in a manner it has not been during the duration of this conflict. Throw 
the embassy evacuations and Israel running fighter jet trials to “rile” up Iran and you have the cocktail for a bit of towel throwing 
on “de-escalation hopes.” All told, the headlines leave the ’27 WTI strip pushing $88—a wartime high.

There are some counterbalances out there, however. The WSJ is out with an article this morning detailing the collapse in Iranian 
oil on water. Since the US has reimposed its blockade, Iranian oil on water has fallen from ~90mb to less than 1 million. 
Previously, the oil on water provided a cushion. It was outside of the reach of the US— on shores in or around Asia where it could 
be sold to China. The collapse in floating storage means that Tehran is increasingly deprived of revenues. According to Centcom, 
the blockade has denied Tehran of ~$25bn of oil revenue. Perhaps the most interesting part of this article is Iran’s loss of its once 
reliable customers:
 
“Emma Li, an analyst at energy tracker Vortexa, also reported a major drop, estimating that there were about 6 million barrels of 
Iranian oil off Singapore as of Oct. 9. She said Chinese oil refineries are increasingly turning to alternative suppliers, including 
Brazil and West Africa.”
 
This is interesting to me . It is interesting because the consensus from folks is that the IRGC does not abide by “Iranian financial 
conditions.” I would think at some point, presumably, the financing of the war machine is afflicted by the issues in the Iranian 
economy. Oil sales are 30-40% of Iranian government revenue. Of course, you assume the removal of the blockade means a lot of 
that comes back, but this loss of customers (re: alternative suppliers) would threaten that on a more structural basis.

We’re going to need those runs. Even accounting for offsets in US & ROW run increases, the API estimates that global refineries 
are still processing around 3.3mbd less than last year.

EU Natural Gas: Demand & Storage Tracker: Otar out with the latest EU gas supply & demand tracker. For the week of 
October 12-18, total natural gas demand in NWE+UK averaged 454 Mcm/day, or 13% higher WoW
 
•
The pickup in weekly demand was driven by a seasonal decline in temperatures across the region. HDDs averaged 11 HDDs, 
broadly in line with the 10-year average, but higher than the 8 HDDs recorded the week before. Despite the recent increase in 
HDDs, overall October weather remains about 0.2stdev warmer than 10-year normal. Colder forecasts have also been 
extended into November, with the monthly projected count increasing by 11 HDDs during the week, although the overall 
November outlook remains 1.1stdev warmer (compared to 1.4stdev warmer last week) (Figure 1).
•
The weather-related uplift in heating demand was concentrated in Germany. Residential demand increased by 17 Mcm/day 
(+17% WoW, -6 Mcm/day YoY), while non-residential (primary power) demand rose by a further 16 Mcm/day (+15% WoW, -
11% YoY). An increase in German HDDs from an average of 10 to 14 WoW was the primary driver of the broader cooling 
trend across the region.

FMG remains in negotiations with CMRG on price, which led to a 4-5Mt inventory build in October alone. As reported by

which accounted for 86Mt of sales in FY26 or ~43% of FMG’s total shipments. The company has now indicated a 5Mt per 
month inventory build rate (largely at China ports) is the likely outcome until prices can be agreed on. FY27 guidance has 
been maintained, but the company notes that if an agreement can’t be reached by the end of the calendar year, production may 
need to be adjusted lower. We have assumed 15Mt inventory is built this quarter, with the ~19Mt inventory built this half 
destocked over 2HFY27. This, of course, depends on a price settlement. It is essentially a timing issue (i.e. working capital 
build, then unwind @ ~$35/t including freight). However, we have also lowered our achieved price expectations from 85% 
realisation to 80% over the next 3 quarters. This drives a 15% FY27 NPAT downgrade, but leaves our valuation essentially 
unchanged at $22ps. We recognise the uncertainty of the situation may keep prospective investors on the sidelines. However, 
with the stock near a 5yr low, and trading at 0.67x NPV / 5.4x FY28 EV/EBITDA, we retain our OW rating. Key catalysts are 
marginal iron ore supply cuts that could see the market stabilize / rise (prices remain within the cost curve), and FMG settling 
prices for SSF with CMRG.
