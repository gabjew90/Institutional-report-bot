GS TMT SPEC SALES – Wednesday 21st October 
Asian tech rebounded overnight, with South Korea’s KOSPI up 2.3% and Taiwan’s Taiex gaining 1.2%
following earnings beats from SpaceX and Super Micro, which rose 8.1% and 16%, respectively, in
extended trading. This followed a volatile US session: QQQ recovered from a 1.1% decline to finish down
just 0.09%, as reports of US–Iran proposal. The US desk flagging muted volumes and continued net selling
of semis/AI on the desk suggested limited conviction. AI-financing concerns remained and the near heard of
Korean neocloud Elice bounced hard +30%. Salesforce’s agreement to buy Braze at a 58% premium. After
the close, Netflix fell 5% on softer Q4 growth guidance and a reduced full-year advertising outlook despite an
EPS beat; SpaceX beat revenue and EBITDA expectations, 
 
RESULTS: 
• SpaceX shares jumped ~8% after-hours/pre-market on the strong print and bullish call (Q1 2027 revenue
guided $33-37B vs Street ~$22B; Q4 capex $35-40B for accelerated AI buildout), with themes of SI demand
strength - ~93% of SI revenue ($7.0B+) was driven by compute sales/leasing, with Cursor adding a meaningful but
smaller piece and advertising a modest $0.5B. The SI segment as a whole is now larger than Starlink and
accounts for just over half of total SpaceX revenue (payback <1 year, Grok Bot scaling, $63B backlog ), In term of
compute demand SpaceX raised its 2027 compute capacity target from ~10 GW to ≥13 GW (many have been
sceptical of this), and the upside scenario expanded from ~15 GW to >20 GW. Current capacity stands at 1.7 GW,
with 2.5–3.0 GW targeted by year-end 2026. Starship/Starlink progress, and Musk confirming special committees
exploring a Tesla merger as a “good idea.”   
Elon Musk said Terrafab is targeting a first functional test chip, likely SRAM, at its Austin research lab in May or
June next year, with an ambition to begin volume production at the main Gibbons Creek site around Q3 2028. He
stressed that these timelines are very aggressive and thanked ASML and other suppliers for accelerating
equipment deliveries. Longer term, he expressed optimism that Terrafab could dramatically accelerate chip
production, ultimately reaching its ambition of producing a terawatt of superintelligence chips annually. At 1pm

Q3 results that beat Street and GS forecasts. Management remained confident in durable double-digit revenue

growth, supported by member gains, pricing and advertising, citing healthy engagement and churn trends and
international strength, particularly in India. The key investor debates remain US/Canada engagement and
advertising execution, with ad-product delays, third-party integrations and measurement capabilities weighing on

MEDIA AGENCIES Apple bites again – see Adam’s note with an Industry expert and Apple’s blog (see -
Apple’s iOS 27 privacy changes are negative for the open-web advertising ecosystem because WebKit now
blocks signals used by identity-resolution providers such as The Trade Desk and LiveRamp, making users
harder to identify across websites and weakening ad targeting, attribution and frequency control. This could
reduce advertiser demand and put pressure on open-web CPMs, while shifting more budget towards
platforms with large pools of logged-in first-party data—particularly Meta, Google and Amazon—as well as
digital video, commerce and in-app advertising. Adtech companies are likely to accelerate workarounds such
as probabilistic measurement and direct server-to-server data matching, so the eventual impact remains
uncertain and will depend on how effective these alternatives prove, how quickly iOS 27 adoption rises and
whether Apple extends similar restrictions to macOS. Overall, the change reinforces the structural advantage
of scaled “walled gardens” while creating further execution risk for identity-resolution platforms and open-
web publishers. Apple’s rationale and further detail.. Also worth a read Digiday

targeting life sciences research and drug discovery—a core vertical traditionally dominated by Veeva’s Vault

Workbench targets the exact same workflow orchestration and experiment planning.

TELCOS 
• Tele2: The key takeaway from the call was reassuring capex commentary. The main question had been how much
of the lower 2026 capex reflected phasing, but management indicated that only SEK50–75m of hardware spend
will slip into 2027—equivalent to just c.0.2% of sales—suggesting today’s reduction is largely structural and could
provide a low-single-digit uplift to FCF. Elsewhere, there were multiple questions on agentic AI, although the
strategic and financial implications remain unclear. The competitive environment is unchanged, while leverage of
1.5x leaves the balance sheet undergeared and may fuel speculation around Xavier Niel’s intentions. Given the
mixed investor feedback, positioning may also be somewhat short.  
 
• European satellites face a significant challenge in building a sovereign alternative to Starlink, according to
the FT. Eutelsat’s OneWeb has around 600 satellites operating at a much higher orbit, compared with Starlink’s
active fleet of more than 10,000. Europe’s planned IRIS² network has a projected €15.6bn budget—equivalent to
more than €40mn per satellite—while SpaceX’s satellite manufacturing cost is reportedly around $1.3mn each,
although these figures measure different costs. Limited European launch capacity and heavy debt at major
regional satellite operators add to the challenge. Policymakers are seeking to support regional champions by
accelerating competition approvals for defence mergers and considering looser rules on telecoms consolidation,
but Europe still faces substantial scale, funding and execution hurdles.  
 
• Nexfibre’s shareholders—Liberty Global, Telefónica and InfraVia—have declined to offer remedies to address
the Competition and Markets Authority’s concerns over the proposed £2bn takeover of Netomnia, according to
Telecompaper. The consortium called the regulator’s interim report fundamentally flawed, arguing that it relies on
speculative and unrealistic assumptions, including that CityFibre is a viable alternative buyer or that equivalent
investment would proceed without the deal. It also argued that the UK wholesale market has changed significantly,
citing BT’s acquisition of TalkTalk and concerns over CityFibre’s financial sustainability. The deal’s financial
backers warned that regulatory hurdles and unpredictable decisions risk deterring international investment in UK
digital infrastructure.

really a read PSON other than it may have gained share. See James take Stride Pearson’s key Virtual Schools
competitor, reported Q1 FY27 (calendar Q3 2026) revenue of $639mn after the close on 20 October, up 3% yoy
and 4% ahead of Visible Alpha consensus, which expected a 0.8% decline. General Education revenue fell 1.8%,
beating expectations for a 4% decline by 2%, but total enrolments fell 2.5% versus consensus of -0.6%, a 2%
miss, while General Education enrolments declined 6.2% versus -4.1% expected. Management attributed weaker-
than-anticipated enrolments partly to technology-platform issues affecting applications and new-student retention.
Q2 and FY27 revenue-growth guidance of 1% and 2% at the midpoints matched consensus, while FY27 revenue
per enrolment is expected to rise 1.5–2.5% versus flat consensus expectations. Overall, the results offer a slightly
positive read-across for Pearson: Stride’s partly company-specific challenges suggest further scope for share
gains following Pearson’s stronger relative performance in the 2025/26 academic year. Pearson reports Q3/9M26

results on 22 October; we expect Q3 group organic growth of 5.4% versus consensus of 5.5%, and Virtual Schools
growth of 9.7% versus 12.4% (see our preview).
