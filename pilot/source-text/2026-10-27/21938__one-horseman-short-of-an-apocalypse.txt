One horseman short of an apocalypse
Three horsemen are already riding through global markets. Each brings a different threat, and none is
particularly welcome at this stage of the cycle. One horseman short of an apocalypse — but three may
be more than enough.
1 - The oil horseman
The first horseman rides on crude. Brent has surged roughly $10 in a month as Middle East tensions
return to centre stage. An oil shock is the last thing a fragile global economy needs.
Macrobond
Trending up
And there is a reason crude is moving. Rumours of planned US/Israeli strikes on Iran and reports of
further ships damaged in the Strait of Hormuz are putting the geopolitical risk premium back into oil.

Bloomberg
From oil shock to energy shock
And it isn't just oil. European natural gas and electricity prices are climbing again. What begins as a
geopolitical risk premium can quickly become an inflation problem.
Bloomberg
2 - Le cheval français
The second horseman comes from Paris. French 10-year spreads versus Germany have widened some
50bp in a month. What started as a French political problem is increasingly looking like a European
bond-market problem.
Macrobond
Déjà vu, Paris?
Zoom out and the picture gets even more uncomfortable. French spreads are approaching levels
previously associated with major episodes of European financial stress. The bond market is clearly
losing patience.

Macrobond
Contagion, anyone?
France is no longer trading in isolation. Spreads across Europe's periphery are also moving wider. The
question is whether this remains a French problem — or becomes a European one.
Bloomberg
CAC crash
And the pain isn't confined to bonds. French equities have suffered their worst relative performance in
three decades, with the CAC hitting a record low versus the broader European market. The French
discount keeps getting bigger.
Morgan Stanley
3 - AI credit
The third horseman is riding straight through the world's favourite trade. Nvidia's five-year CDS has
widened roughly 35bp in a month (even gunning for the King?). The AI boom isn't dead, but credit
markets are starting to ask some uncomfortable questions.

Macrobond
Hyperventilating
And Nvidia isn't alone. CDS spreads are widening across the hyperscaler universe, with some names
now trading at levels associated with significant credit risk. Equity investors may still be debating the AI
story. Credit investors are getting nervous.
SEB X-Asset
The money-go-round
So what is credit getting nervous about? Perhaps the circular financing underpinning the AI boom.
Suppliers, hyperscalers and AI labs are increasingly funding one another. The system works beautifully
— as long as fresh money keeps coming in.
SEB X-Asset
Follow the money
The circularity problem in two charts. A large share of AI ecosystem revenues still comes from

recycled capex, while vendor-financing announcements have exploded. The critical question: when
does real end-user demand catch up?
TS Lombard
TS Lombard
Flat-lining
And here is another uncomfortable part. The AI boom is booming but hyperscaler earnings
expectations have barely budged since July. More hype but without corresponding upward revisions.
Marquee
Semis
First the hyperscalers lost momentum. Now the semiconductor stocks are feeling the pressure too. The
weakness is spreading across the AI trade. Is this just another rotation — or something more sinister?

Bloomberg
Has the damage already been done?
Before declaring the AI trade dead, consider this: semiconductors now trade at a forward P/E discount
to the S&P 500. Some of the bad news is already in the price. The question is how much.
Koyfin
Bubble territory
History suggests major market bubbles tend to peak around 40% concentration. The AI Big 10 are now
in that neighborhood. The notable exception? Railroads, which managed to reach 63%. Still room to
run — or already playing with fire.
Flow Show

Love that pattern
And for those looking for historical parallels, this one is rather uncomfortable. The SOX is tracking a
pattern eerily similar to the dot-com bubble. Back then, new supply arrived just as demand began to
fade. History doesn't have to repeat — but what if it rhymes?
SEB X-Asset
One horseman short
Oil threatens inflation. France threatens European bonds. AI threatens the market's most crowded
growth narrative. Three horsemen. Three different risks. And a market that could really do without a
fourth.
