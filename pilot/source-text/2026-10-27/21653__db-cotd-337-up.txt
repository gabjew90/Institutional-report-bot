DB CoTD: 337 UP! 
 
 
Since Bloomberg began tracking the number of S&P 500 advancers in October 1996, 2,887 of 7,827 
trading days have seen more than 300 stocks rise. As today’s CoTD shows, yesterday was one of them, 
with 337 stocks up. Yet the index fell -1.21% — the second-worst S&P 500 performance across all 2,887 
of those 300+ advancer days. 
The only worse occasion was 27 January 2025, when the index fell -1.46% despite 351 stocks rising. 
That was the day DeepSeek challenged assumptions around the need for costly AI capex. NVIDIA fell -
17%. 
Interestingly, US AI capex has been questioned repeatedly in the 21 months since. Yet from the day 
before DeepSeek arrived on the scene, NVIDIA is still up nearly +50% and the Bloomberg Global AI index 
is up a similar amount. 
However, NVIDIA is only +2.5% since 29 October 2025 — the date we highlighted in Monday’s CoTD, 
when Meta’s capex plans were seriously questioned after its results and the AI market leadership 
changed. Monday’s CoTD here is a neat snapshot of these last 12 months: AI leadership has shifted 
from those writing the cheques to those cashing them. The Global AI index is still up around +17% but 
outside Nvidia’s flat performance, other large US AI companies such as Oracle (-53.3%), Broadcom (-
15.6%), Meta (-13.4%), Microsoft (-3.0%) are all lower. 

So yesterday’s unusual combination — broad participation but a sharply lower index — brings us back to 
the question that has repeatedly confronted markets over the last two years. Is this simply another 
temporary wobble in the AI capex story, or is the market finally starting to believe that the size of the 
cheques matters more than the promise of what they will buy? Next week is a big one for this story with 
Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon all reporting. Rest well!
