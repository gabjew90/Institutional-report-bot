Big in bonds
The 10-year yield dropped approximately 15bps yesterday, its largest one-day decline since August
2025 according to GS. The AI unwind is increasingly forcing investors to reassess longer-term growth
expectations, driving demand for duration.
GS/Bloomberg
The house always wins
The irony is hard to miss. AI stocks are getting hammered, growth expectations are being repriced,
and Treasury yields are plunging. Meanwhile, Bessent is getting exactly what he wants: lower
borrowing costs. The equity market's pain is becoming the Treasury's gain. Sometimes the house really
does win.
The easy money is made
In our October 8 note, Everybody is betting on higher rates, that is the problem, we outlined the case
for a reversal in yields, highlighting crowded positioning, stretched technicals and even The
Economist's front cover as a potential contrarian signal. Fast-forward two weeks, and the US 10-year
has moved from channel highs all the way down to its 50-day moving average, just above channel
lows. The tactical call played out very well. From here, things get trickier.

AI's unintended consequence
The correlation between Nasdaq and US 10-year yields remains striking. As Goldman's Privorotsky puts
it, "the very thing creating the equity problem is helping solve the rates problem." The AI selloff is
becoming a powerful tailwind for bonds.

CTAs joining the party?
The US 10-year yield has now closed below the short-term CTA threshold, potentially triggering
additional systematic bond buying. With flight-to-quality demand picking up, trend-following flows
could add further fuel to the rates reversal.
Credit where credit is due. JPM flagged the CTA risks in early October, writing: “Trend followers have
been riding the bond selloff, but the positioning is now increasingly vulnerable to mean reversion.”
GS
AI loses, bonds win
The AI selloff is effectively pricing in lower capex and weaker growth, taking pressure off yields.
Goldman Sachs' Privorotsky highlights the emerging divergence between rates and oil. The catch? If AI
stabilizes and growth expectations recover, yields could quickly come under renewed upward
pressure. The very force supporting bonds today could become tomorrow's headwind.
