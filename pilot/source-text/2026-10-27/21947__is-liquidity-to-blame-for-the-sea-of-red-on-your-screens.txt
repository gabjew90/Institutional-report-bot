 
Is Liquidity To Blame For The Sea Of Red On Your 
Screens? 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, OCT 23, 2026 - 03:25 PM 
Authored by Simon White Bloomberg macro strategist, 
A squeeze on excess liquidity earlier this year is beginning to have an impact on global risk 
assets. 
It’s unlikely to change until either monetary policy is loosened or animal spirits are reanimated. 
In the end, it’s always about liquidity for markets.  
When there is a surplus of it, it will find its way into assets. Even if the backdrop is negative or 
the economic data uncooperative, liquidity, when there’s enough it, will boost stocks and other 
risk assets, reflexively changing the backdrop. Liquidity makes the weather. 
The same is true when liquidity is scarce. Perfect ex ante conditions for a rising stock market 
won’t lead to one if it is starved of liquidity. Those ideal conditions become, ex post, very 
negative. 
Greater cracks are starting to show in the AI trade, but they could have appeared at any point in 
the last few months. There are several proximate reasons, such as concerns about compute 
overcapacity, but the remote reason is excess liquidity (real money growth versus economic 
growth). 
It has been falling for several months, but it operates with a three-to-six month lag, thus we are 
in the period where it is likely to have a more noticeable impact. 
 

 
 
What’s driving this decline in excess liquidity? It’s not money growth, with US M1 recently 
boosting the median money growth for the G10. Instead, it’s the lagged impact of a pick-up in 
growth and, more so, inflation to blame. 
 
That is why one can’t look at money growth in isolation. Some of it is used up to support real 
economic growth, and some of it is eaten up by inflation, as capital needs to be replaced. 
 

 
Only what’s left is available to support the market. That portion is wanting, and risk assets are 
starting to pay the price. 
This framework gives a direct mechanism to explain why the rise in oil prices is ultimately bad 
news for stocks. 
It’s unlikely to change until monetary policy loosens, or there is a new strand to the AI narrative 
that reinvokes animal spirits, unlocking more savings or boosting bank credit.
