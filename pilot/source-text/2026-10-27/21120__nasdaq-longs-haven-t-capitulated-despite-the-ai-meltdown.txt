Something has to give
Nasdaq is approaching a critical crossroads. Tech is dramatically underperforming small caps, credit
stress is finally catching up with equities, and volatility has surged. Yet futures positioning remains
stubbornly elevated, bearish hedges are quickly unwound, and NDX continues to hold its broader
trading range. With earnings approaching and the critical 29k futures level in focus, the tension
between deteriorating internals and resilient positioning is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.
Something has to give.
Technical crossroads
Despite the violent rotation beneath the surface, Nasdaq remains within its broader trading range. The
index has already broken below its 100-day moving average, with the 200-day and the longer-term
rising trendline becoming the next important technical references.
The combination of relatively elevated futures positioning, rapidly reversing hedges and a
deteriorating technical picture makes the next directional move particularly interesting.
29k (futures) is the make or break level to watch.

The great tech underperformance
NDX has dramatically underperformed the Russell 2000 over the past 7–8 sessions, marking one of the
sharpest stretches of tech underperformance versus small caps in a long time. The NDX/Russell spread
captures the sheer magnitude of the rotation. This is no ordinary sector shuffle. The former market
leaders are getting hit hard while small caps show remarkable relative resilience.

The longs that won't leave
The AI meltdown has inflicted serious damage across memory, neoclouds and hyperscalers, but
Nasdaq futures positioning has barely blinked.
Goldman highlights that non-dealers actually bought $3.3bn of Nasdaq futures between October 6 and
13, despite the index falling 2.1%. Short covering of $7.3bn more than offset $4bn of long liquidation.
The result? Non-dealer positioning entered the latest selloff at the 94th percentile of its two-year
range.
The uncomfortable takeaway: the AI trade has suffered a violent correction, but Nasdaq futures
positioning has yet to experience a comparable washout.

The bears can't stay bearish
The next leg lower finally brought out the bears. As hyperscalers joined the AI selloff, Nasdaq futures
open interest increased by a net $1.4bn, with much of the activity coinciding with the October 16
decline.
Nasdaq's funding premium relative to the S&P 500 disappeared, while implied volatility and put skew
jumped as traders scrambled for downside protection.
But the bearish conviction didn't last. As Nasdaq stabilized on October 19, relative funding recovered
and both volatility and skew retreated.
The market is quick to hedge the downside, but apparently even quicker to abandon those hedges
when prices stabilize.

The volatility round trip
Nasdaq's latest selloff produced a sharp repricing of downside risk, with three-month implied volatility
and 25-delta put skew moving higher together.
Yet both reversed as the index bounced. The speed of the reversal suggests traders remain reluctant
to maintain expensive downside protection for long.
That leaves an interesting question heading into earnings: is the market correctly anticipating
stabilization, or becoming complacent before the next move?
GS
Tech volatility
VXN has spiked during the latest selloff, leaving traders in an awkward spot. Implied volatility is no
longer cheap enough to make downside protection particularly attractive, but neither is it expensive
enough to offer a compelling volatility-selling opportunity.
Unless, of course, you believe the NDX trading range will continue to hold.
The result is a tricky setup: elevated futures positioning, deteriorating technicals and volatility that
offers no obvious edge in either direction.

Credit finally matters
NDX spent much of the recent period ignoring, if not outright dismissing, widening CDS spreads. That
has changed. Nasdaq is finally responding to the stress in credit markets, and that stress remains very
much alive. The growing alignment between equities and credit is worth watching closely, particularly
as AI-related credit concerns continue to linger.

The earnings trap?
Nasdaq is entering earnings with a rather unusual combination: significant damage across AI
leadership, elevated futures positioning and downside hedges that have already started to unwind.
The bearish case is straightforward. If earnings fail to stabilize the index, crowded positioning could
become an additional source of selling pressure.
The bullish case is equally interesting. With bearish futures activity appearing short-lived, a recovery
through important technical levels could force another round of covering.
The setup is increasingly binary. Positioning hasn't capitulated, hedges aren't sticking, and earnings
are approaching. Something has to give.
