Midterms Unlikely a 'Clearing Event'
SPX 200 DMA in Play as Credit Spreads Continue to Widen
WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW: 
Since 1962, the SPX has averaged a -13.76% drawdown at some point in the June-
December window during midterm election years. Currently, the SPX has seen just a
-4.5% drawdown post June 1st, which would be the second-smallest such drawdown
since 1962. The reason stocks often perform well in November during midterms is
because SPX typically is weak INTO the elections. We have the opposite setup this year.
SPX also moved inversely to the average pattern in February/March. Therefore, while
consensus is that we see a post midterm rally, we think the opposite is actually more
likely.
■
SPX 200 DMA in Play. So far, the small-caps, mid-caps, transports, semiconductors,
as well as many sectors have tested their 200 DMAs. We suspect SPX is next, which
would bring it towards 7300 at a minimum. This is also the area of the summer lows,
so an overshoot of the 200 DMA would also not be surprising like we saw in March
of this year.
■
A Year Unlike Any Other. 33% of days in 2026 have seen price and breadth move in
opposite directions. History says it’s unlikely to continue much longer.
■
There's No Fear. Put/Call ratios remain well below levels seen in March and even
July of this year. Hard to think we have seen a meaningful low when there is a lack
of hedging.
■
Junk Spreads Continue to Blow Out. Since 1996 this is just the fifth time that B vs.
CCC spreads have reached 7.8% or more. In the four prior occurrences, the SPX was
at a minimum -11.8% from a 52wk high. Twice it was already in a -30% drawdown
by the time spreads hit where they are now. The current -2% drawdown on SPX
certainly stands out and suggests downside risk for stocks.
■
Tepid Bounce for Semis. As we discussed last Thursday, we gave semis a chance to
bounce off support, but they failed that test. While the 200 DMA is holding for SOXX
and SMH, the equal-weight (XSD) has already lost its 200 DMA. We continue to think
semis work lower over the coming weeks and months.
■
Tech Credit Continues to Deteriorate. Hyper Scaler CDS continues to widen, and IG
Tech CDS is now the widest it's been since 2022 with the S&P Tech Sector just a few
% below highs.
■
Private Credit Can't Rally. BDCs at multi-year lows, unable to sustain a rally, and
have diverged meaningfully from software.
■
Software the New Mo'. Equal-weight Software breaking out of a two-year range
with relative strength at YTD highs. If looking for momentum, this is the spot.
■
Other Vulnerable Groups. Industrials, Healthcare, Restaurants, Airlines, Casinos

Where’s the Midterm Vol? 
 
SPX Drawdowns During 
Mid-Term Election Years 
 
Since 1962, the SPX has averaged a -13.76% drawdown at some point 
in the June-December window during mid-term election years.  
Currently, the SPX has seen just a -4.5% drawdown post June 1st, which 
would be the second smallest such drawdown since 1962.  
Only 2006 was smaller at -3.6%, but there was a -7.7% drawdown from 
May through mid-June of that year. This year did have a -9% 
drawdown in March, but that clearly was not mid-term related so we 
used June 1st as the cutoff to try to isolate when market might have a 
focus on them.
