FEROLI’S PMI PREVIEW – his full note is here 

•         We [Feroli and team] look for the October flash manufacturing PMI to decline to 54.5 
from 55.9 in September, which marked the best level since the summer of 2022. The 
regional Empire and Philadelphia Fed manufacturing surveys sent mixed signals in October, 
but the average ISM-weighted composite of these surveys only inched down slightly from 
56.6 in September to a still-solid 55.8. 
•         The PMI output measure jumped from 53.1 in August to 56.2 in September, close to the 
recent May peak when the index reached 56.6. New orders also rose from 54.3 to 56.1, with 
the press release noting that some of the improvement was due to continued front-loading 
by firms over concerns of higher prices and supply challenges, coupled with rising demand 
from AI-related investments. Backlogs of work have remained steady in an elevated range 
in recent months, likely signaling continued strength in manufacturing production ahead. 
•         The September PMI report also signaled elevated price pressures, likely reflecting 
renewed increases in global energy prices. The index for input prices ticked up to 65.6 from 
65.2, following some reversal from the spike in May. However, output prices continued to 
moderate, inching down to 59.0 from 59.4. 
•         We expect the October flash services PMI to continue to point to resilient activity, 
with the headline business activity index moving down to 58.0 from 58.8 in September. 
The last report marked the best level for headline business activity in the current cycle, 
with the press release noting that growing demand is rising ahead of operating capacity. 
The new business index also reached a multi-year high at 58.5 in the final September 
report. The employment index, which has swung from a cycle trough of 47.3 in May to 
55.2 in September, stands at its highest level since mid-2022. 
•         The last report pointed to an acceleration in inflation pressures, with input prices 
jumping from 58.9 to 65.9, the fastest pace since late 2022, while output prices also 
rose following a brief improvement in August, from 55.8 to 58.2. The press release 
attributed this pressure to higher fuel, transportation, and labor costs. 

 
 
 
TRADE IDEA FROM JPM CREDIT DESK (WILLIAM JARVIS): CCC Performance…and 
thoughts headed down the 2026 Homestretch – If you would like to trade with team or 
receive the full note, please reach out the team here 
•         As we have all experienced, CCC performance has been a significant contributor to HY 
underperformance this year, which is especially notable given 1) tight mutual fund 
performance (tightest 1Q/4Q performance dispersion since 2005…Exhibit 2) and 2) the 
underperformance of CCCs…(CCC/B differential is 764bp…only 5bps away from 12m 
wides and well wide of 1y/5y/10y/15y averages…Exhibit 3).  CCC’s are notably lagging 
the pack down-4.22% YTD vs our index +0.09%.    
•         However, CCC underperformance is complicated and NOT as much as most 
people think.  Why?  1) CCCs are only a mere 8.5% of the index (Exhibit 4) and 2) the 
attribution of performance is HEAVILY concentrated (Exhibit 5).   
•         What do to from here? History tell us…you’re going to want to be in CCC and B 
risk during rate hiking cycles…just look at the data from Nelson: “When rates spike over 
3-months, BBs, Bs, and CCCs posted an average total return of -0.6%, +0.9%, and +2.3%, 
respectively.” (Exhibit 7).  
•         How do you trade it?  Take a look at Portfolio Bond Basket TRS and/or HYCDX Junior 
Mezz and Equity Tranches (Exhibit 1).  For tranches, both showcase extremely high cash 
correlation over time (Exhibit 6) and have underperformed locally.   

•         When do you add risk?  Tactically speaking, I’m waiting for signs of local 
stabilization…which I think we will get very soon.  New Issue calendars have slowed and 
dealer inventory has normalized, but vol remains elevated with earnings in full swing 
(65/500 SPY reported) and election season upon us.  
•         So…it’s Oct 21st and we’re coming down the 2026 homestretch.  The finish line is 
in sight and funds are neck-and-neck with the sound of thundering hooves all around.  How 
you’re positioned in CCCs and Single-B’s will likely determine the winner of this 
year’s Lipper’s “Run for the Roses”. 
 
 
US MKT INTEL VIEW – we published below in the October 19 Morning Briefing 
•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – Last week's sell-off ran against our tactical bullish call, so we 
did a reassessment on our view and risk factors. In our Oct 12 Morning Briefing, we flagged 
below as key risk factors “(i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI 
Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer 
Data”. Of the risks we flagged, the Momentum / Tech Unwind and AI Credit Fears were the 
main drivers for last week’s selloff, while the fundamental picture remained encouraging. 
ASML and TSMC delivered healthy earnings and consumer data stayed resilient. We 
therefore retain our tactical bullish stance. The underlying support from earnings and 
macro resilience remains intact, and the AI sell-off has not come with evidence of 
deteriorating earnings in the results reported so far. That said, we don't expect the 
rotation out of Tech to end abruptly, though its pace could moderate. We favor 

expressing our constructive growth and earnings view through Cyclical exposure outside 
Tech, while selectively buying Tech weakness, particularly in Semis and Memory, 
potentially through defined-risk options structures. Large-cap Banks are another area of 
interest. Earnings were generally positive, and the recent sharp pullback could offer an 
opportunity to participate in a recovery in XLF. Heading into a much busier reporting week, 
broadening and AI concerns remain the two key themes for investors, and all eyes are on 
META earnings and NVDA's GTC Berlin. 
•         RISKS (Unchanged WoW) – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; 
(iii) AI Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker 
Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – Following last week's sell-off, we revisit our three key catalysts: (i) 
Macro / Fed: We think the inflation component of this week’s PMIs will be critical. On Oct 
28 Fed (note that the Fed has entered its blackout period), a hold could provide relief by 
removing residual near-term hike risk, but the bigger focus will be any remarks on the FY27 
policy path. (ii) Rates Stabilization: Lower Treasury yields have already delivered part of 
our anticipated catalyst. From here, we would look for sustained moderation in rates vol and 
no renewed yield spike to support the broader rotation. (iii) Tech Earnings / AI 
Monetization: META's results and NVDA's GTC Berlin will be important tests of revenue 
delivery, spending plans and demand visibility. Beyond the hyperscalers, evidence that AI 
investment is broadening and generating measurable revenue across other sectors would 
strengthen the case for a more durable AI trade. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 19) – Following last week's sell-off, we favor a more 
selective approach to expressing our bullish view. We retain a preference for Tech and for 
exposure to a potential rebound through both pairs trades and derivatives. Within Cyclicals, 
we expect the rotation to continue. We remain constructive on Financials and Retailers, 
given consumer resilience and their relatively limited direct exposure to AI-related concerns. 
Internationally, we continue to favor Brazil and Japan. We are also monitoring Reopening 
opportunities that could benefit from lower energy prices and rotation away from AI. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with 
a preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 

Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your 
shopping list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and 
Retailers given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy 
prices. Other elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower energy 
prices. Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and Japan. 
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long 
but would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / 
yields, which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, there 
is likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the Tech tape. 
Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus bullish play. The 
AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we like Tech in the 
near-term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we think you need to 
see the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; excluding AI-related 
plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper yield curve, and 
favorable capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real Estate, Telecom, and 
Utilities may participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most likely to lag the SPX, 
ex-AI plays. The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which would squeeze EU and 
RTY higher; plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, Cruise Lines, 
Discretionary, Hotels, etc) is another area of interest with a preference to play via options.
