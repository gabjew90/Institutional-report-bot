OFS Kick Off + Refining
We hosted a call on Friday with our Natural Resources Research team - Refining, E&P, OFS, Midstream,
CleanTech, Power / Utes, Metals, Trading Views.
HAL. Quickly running through the quarter – headline revenue ahead of Street ($5.7 bn vs Street at $5.58
bn) with adj EBITDA slightly below and EPS broadly in line. On revenue - D&E revs beat with C&P
below – don't think this will be shocking to most. Commentary from the release: ‘Based on our early
outlook for 2027, I expect mid-single digit revenue growth and operating margins of about 15%, an
approximately 200 basis point improvement. This outlook assumes a flat North America market and does
not require any recovery in North America hydraulic fracturing. To us, that recovery is purely upside’…
Street at ~6% revenue growth and margins at ~15%.The bar was relatively low this quarter - and we think
positioning over the last couple of weeks has been cleaner vs last quarter / throughout conference season
(desk has positioning score of 5/10). At the end of the day - the guide on the call at 9 AM ET will likely
drive px action and read through for the group (heavy # of OFS prints over the next several days). NAM
expectations for HAL are low – and would think the prepared remarks reinforce this. We've been fielding
the question for some time now - what gets folks interested in Services again? Clearly crude price not the
force here (dated Brent at $117/b this AM) and MOU / collaboration / contract headlines haven't done
much - so we think it's about resetting the bar (again) on numbers + showing # of ways to win. WFRD the
next Services company to report EPS (post close today) – would imagine LATAM + Africa + ME
commentary from HAL will be a read through to some extent.
Refining. Will get the bellwether print this week from VLO on Thursday pre-market and it’s safe to say
expectations are extremely high for the group. Cracks have come off albeit still well above seasonal
averages and the question of ‘seasonal roll’ has been more topical on the desk as of late. WCS remains
wide (and continues to price at a steep discount) though Brent/WTI has come in a bit … while we have
said conversations on the desk highlighted more willingness to fight the move higher over the last couple
of months – we think specialists are more neutral on the group now, though are looking for signs / reasons
to fade strength. 3Q EPS will likely be extremely strong (ie VLO could see $25 EPS this quarter) – though
more concerns on leading edge consensus numbers drifting lower for coming quarters with recent crack
moves. Expect there to be less of a focus on the buybacks for several reasons (reach out to discuss) and
potential to see capital spend surprise to upside on secondary unit projects / initiatives has been more
topical. On that front – would imagine with where cracks are / views on mid cycle, the payback period on
these potential projects isn’t a huge concern. Feedback welcome.
