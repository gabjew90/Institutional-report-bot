JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Yesterday, stocks rallied and breadth improved after headlines around 10:30 a.m. pointed 
some chatter for US-Iran deal progress. While later headlines cooled that optimism (talks 
were still ongoing and pushed back on claims that a deal was close), equities held their 
gains in the afternoon session, with RTY being a notable outperformer. We don't think much 
has changed beneath the headline noise. Geopolitics remains a wild card, and there is little 
evidence of real progress. Yesterday’s price action, especially the sharp squeeze in RTY, 
looks more like short covering than growing confidence. Our desk flow also supports that 

view. Our desk told us (~11am ET yesterday) that volumes running about 32% lower, and 
clients were still hesitant to re-gross.  
  
Overnight, AI sentiment is getting a lift from two constructive headlines: SPCX’s blowout 
earnings and SMCI's upside preannouncement, but these two catalysts alone would not 
resolve investors' concerns about financing risk and the scale of AI capex. Jensen Huang's 
GTC remarks offered little that was new, so attention now turns to META's earnings this 
afternoon.

META EARNINGS BUYSIDE BAR (MARK SCHILSKY) – You may see his full earnings 
preview here 
•         A lot can change in two months. Two months ago, META was trading ~$550 because 
investors viewed it as a relative AI loser (with a pretty meaningful legal overhang). They 

thought META had nothing to show for all of Zuckerberg’s largesse. Well, he delivered: 
Muse and Wonder. As such, the stock is up nearly +40% off the lows and investor 
sentiment is white hot. Can management keep the party going? All eyes are on 2027 – 
especially the Opex guide – if we even get it this print. Combine that with any Muse-related 
KPI’s Zuckerberg might provide us with and you potentially have the recipe for the stock 
continuing to power higher. But we’ve a LOT of goodness recently. We’re going to need 
some more surprises to break out to $800+… 
•         Q326 Earnings Expectations: 
o   Revenue: While channel checks have reportedly been solid, investors are 
expecting ‘just’ the high end of the $61B to $64B guide this quarter. As such, 
the bogey is ~$64B (+26% Y/Y FXN) – with a slight upward bias. The Street 
is at ~$63.25B. 
o   Muse: Will we get any hard and fast Muse-related KPIs from Zuckerberg? 
DAUs? Downloads? Anything? We all know it’s been a big success, but with 
that success comes a LOT more Capex and Opex. Investors need to have 
confidence in forward-looking user metrics in order to credibly underwrite 
Zuckerberg’s investments. Hopefully we get something concrete. 
•         Q426/2027 Guidance Expectations: 
o   Q426 Revenue: Unsurprisingly, investors expect a beat relative to Street 
Consensus of ~$73.5B (~+24% Y/Y FXN). Based on recent conversations, it 
looks like investors will tolerate no more than a 1-point FXN decel on the top 
end of the guidance range. As such, investors anticipate a guide that looks 
like $72B to $75B (+20% to +25% Y/Y). 
o   2026 Opex: First and foremost, this guide has to be upped by +$10B on 
both the top and bottom ends due to META’s recent AG settlement. But no 
one really cares. Beyond that, the current guidance range of $165B to $169B 
is already quite narrow. There’s a chance Susan narrows it on a like-for-like 
basis to $166B to $168B, but given that the 2027 guide looms MUCH larger 
in the minds of investors, this doesn’t really matter all that much. 

o   2027 Opex: This is a hot topic of debate for investors. While nearly 
everyone agrees that Street estimates of ~$203B are too low, I have heard a 
wide range of possibilities from $210B to $230B. All told, most investors are 
expecting a 2027 Opex guidance range with $220B or $225B on the high 
end. Thus, expect a guide that looks like $210/$215B to $220/$225B – if we 
even get it this print.  [Remember, META changed its guidance philosophy 
last year and proclaimed that it no longer will give forward-year Opex/Capex 
guides on the Q3 print. But I also suppose Susan could change her mind 
again if she wished. Also, META’s history suggests that they may want to 
start this number out ‘heavy’ and then look to walk it down throughout the 
year. I know this hasn’t happened for two years, but perhaps we finally get 
back to the ‘old’ ways of guiding.] 
o   2027 Operating Income: The Street has 2027 OI growing about +$18B Y/Y 
– but the Street’s 2027 Opex estimates are also about -$18B too light. In 
other words, can Susan put in the Q3 (or Q4) release the same thing she did 
in the Q425 earnings release?: “Despite the meaningful step up in 
infrastructure investment, in 2026 we expect to deliver operating income that 
is above 2025 operating income.” We’ll see next quarter. 
o   2026 Capex: Everyone is resigned to the view that META will end near the 
high end of the current guidance range of $130B to $145B. As such, don’t be 
surprised if Susan brings up the bottom range up to something like $135B to 
$145B. Again, 2027 matters far more. 
o   2027 Capex: The Street’s ~$200B remains too low. Most investors I spoke 
with are in the range of $225B to $250B. If Susan were to guide 2027 Capex 
this print, which I don’t think you should assume, investors expect a range 
that looks just like $225B to $250B. As for 2028 investor 
expectations…’higher’. 
o   In the Q325 Release Susan guided, “Our current expectation is that capital 
expenditures dollar growth will be notably larger in 2026 than 2025. We also 
anticipate total expenses will grow at a significantly faster percentage rate in 

2026 than 2025, with growth driven primarily by infrastructure costs, including 
incremental cloud expenses and depreciation.” At the very least, I think it’s 
likely that she will issue a similar guide regarding 2027 Capex given that we 
all ‘know’ that 2027 Capex dollar growth will be larger than in 2026. As for 
Opex, investor expectations are for slower growth in 2027 so either she may 
not ‘guide’ that figure on this print or give us some verbiage that is the 
opposite: ‘grow at a slower rate in 2027 than 2026.’ Opex growth matters 
WAY MORE than Capex growth because investors care so MUCH MORE 
about EPS than FCF. 
o   All told, please focus on the sum of 2027 Opex/Capex. Accounting for how 
much META will spend on datacenters via capex (cash costs upfront) vs. 
opex (lease payments to operators like Crusoe) is difficult due to do with 
absolute precision. As such, once we get the initial 2027 Opex and Capex 
guides from Susan, focus on the sum…Buyside Consensus for 2027 Opex + 
Capex is in the range of $435B to $475B – that’s a midpoint of $455B. 
o   Yes, a majority of investors still believe that an Equity Raise is likely post-
Q3, assuming the stock is high enough. (Certainly >$800. ~$850 would be 
‘better’.) We all know that META is going to burn FCF next year; the only 
question is whether the cost of debt or the cost of equity will be lower when 
they need to raise capital. 
o   2027/2028 EPS: There’s definitely some real concerns that the initial 2027 
Opex guide (if we get it this quarter – which I doubt – but we’ll definitely get it 
on the Q4 print) will be high enough that 2027 Street EPS estimates of $34 
might come down first to as low as ~$32. Part of the issue also is that Muse 
monetization may not be terribly meaningful in 2027; it’s set to hopefully ramp 
in 2028. On the flip side, I’ve heard 2027 EPS estimates from investors as 
high as $37 with the majority of investors close to the Street’s $34. Thus, 
‘real’ Bulls are focused on 2028: I’ve heard estimates anywhere from $35 to 
$45 with the majority in the low $40’s vs. the Street’s ~$39. 
  

EARNINGS MACRO READ-THROUGH 
•         SUMMARY – Macro read-through from COF and SYF earnings suggested consumer 
resilience reaches beyond the large banks. SYF summarized it well: “The consumer 
continues to show signs of strength. I think it's been a little bit surprising, not only to us, 
but to the industry more broadly.” The strength was showing up across spending and 
credit metrics (COF domestic card charge-off rate fell to 3.98%, down 65 bp year-over-
year). That demand backdrop appears to be giving companies room to pass costs on. 3M 
said its "price execution" "fully offsets material and oil cost pressures." It raised its oil 
inflation estimate to $185 million from the $150–175 million range given in July, to be offset 
by pricing actions taken in Q2, on top of about $280 million of tariffs. UAL said it had 
recaptured 50% of higher fuel expense in Q2 and expected 80–90% in Q3 and full recovery 
by Q4. Together, this commentary suggests that firm demand is letting companies pass 
higher energy and tariff costs on to customers, which could keep inflation pressure sticky.  
•         CONSUMER HEALTH 
o   [Consumer Resilience] COF – "So the US consumer and the overall 
economy remained resilient despite the high energy prices and everything 
when you pick up the news every day, one would think the world is falling 
apart." 
o   [Consumer Resilience] SYF – "The consumer continues to show signs of 
strength. I think it's been a little bit surprising, not only to us, but to the 
industry more broadly." 
o   [K-Shaped Consumer] ALLY – "…the higher end consumers seem to 
have plenty of discretionary expenses, and I think they are fueling part of the 
consumer economy. There is a segment of customers that are probably 
involved in what I call kind of cash sorting. They are kind of prioritizing and 
triaging on a monthly basis, where they are putting their dollars." 
o   [Credit Quality] SYF – "Both our 30-plus and 90-plus delinquency rates at 
the end of the third-quarter were in line with the prior year. Our net charge-off 

rate was 4.79%, reflecting a decrease of 37 basis points from 5.16% in the 
prior year." 
o   [Consumer POS Growth] MMM – "… retailer inventory stabilize and POS 
continues to show positive momentum on the back of NPI and retailer-specific 
programs, we expect the growth trend to improve in the fourth quarter.” 
o   [Price-Insensitive Demand] UAL – "We observed minimal to no negative 
impact on demand from higher price points, a trend we see continuing."  
•         [ENERGY/INFLATION IMPACTS 
o   [Inflation Impacts / Real Wages] COF – "Now as a result of inflation, real 
wage growth turned negative in April and May on a year-over-year basis, but 
it was back in positive territory ever so slightly in June as inflation ticked back 
down." 
o    [Fuel Cost Recapture] UAL – "In the quarter, we were able to recapture 
50% of the increase in fuel expense and accounting for the sharp rise in fuel 
recently, we expect to recover 80% to 90% in the third quarter and full 
recovery by the fourth quarter." 
o   [Inflation Pass-Through] GE – "At the same time, we are facing 
inflationary headwinds as well. So broadly speaking, our pricing approach or 
on spare parts catalog for this year is going to be consistent with what we did 
last year." 
o   [Tariff + Oil Cost Shock] MMM – "But since then, we've had tariffs of 
about $280 million. We have oil now, which is $185 million. We obviously 
were able to offset all of that, plus more…"  
o   [Gas Prices & Inflation Not Biting] SYF – "I think higher gas prices, fairly 
broad-based inflation. And then you look at consumer confidence that has 
been weak all year, but it's not impacting. None of those factors are really 
impacting consumer behavior."

US MKT INTEL VIEW – we published below in the October 19 Morning Briefing 
•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – Last week's sell-off ran against our tactical bullish call, so we 
did a reassessment on our view and risk factors. In our Oct 12 Morning Briefing, we flagged 
below as key risk factors “(i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI 
Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer 
Data”. Of the risks we flagged, the Momentum / Tech Unwind and AI Credit Fears were the 
main drivers for last week’s selloff, while the fundamental picture remained encouraging. 
ASML and TSMC delivered healthy earnings and consumer data stayed resilient. We 
therefore retain our tactical bullish stance. The underlying support from earnings and 
macro resilience remains intact, and the AI sell-off has not come with evidence of 
deteriorating earnings in the results reported so far. That said, we don't expect the 
rotation out of Tech to end abruptly, though its pace could moderate. We favor 
expressing our constructive growth and earnings view through Cyclical exposure outside 
Tech, while selectively buying Tech weakness, particularly in Semis and Memory, 
potentially through defined-risk options structures. Large-cap Banks are another area of 
interest. Earnings were generally positive, and the recent sharp pullback could offer an 
opportunity to participate in a recovery in XLF. Heading into a much busier reporting week, 
broadening and AI concerns remain the two key themes for investors, and all eyes are on 
META earnings and NVDA's GTC Berlin. 
•         RISKS (Unchanged WoW) – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; 
(iii) AI Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker 
Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – Following last week's sell-off, we revisit our three key catalysts: (i) 
Macro / Fed: We think the inflation component of this week’s PMIs will be critical. On Oct 
28 Fed (note that the Fed has entered its blackout period), a hold could provide relief by 
removing residual near-term hike risk, but the bigger focus will be any remarks on the FY27 
policy path. (ii) Rates Stabilization: Lower Treasury yields have already delivered part of 
our anticipated catalyst. From here, we would look for sustained moderation in rates vol and 
no renewed yield spike to support the broader rotation. (iii) Tech Earnings / AI 

Monetization: META's results and NVDA's GTC Berlin will be important tests of revenue 
delivery, spending plans and demand visibility. Beyond the hyperscalers, evidence that AI 
investment is broadening and generating measurable revenue across other sectors would 
strengthen the case for a more durable AI trade. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 19) – Following last week's sell-off, we favor a more 
selective approach to expressing our bullish view. We retain a preference for Tech and for 
exposure to a potential rebound through both pairs trades and derivatives. Within Cyclicals, 
we expect the rotation to continue. We remain constructive on Financials and Retailers, 
given consumer resilience and their relatively limited direct exposure to AI-related concerns. 
Internationally, we continue to favor Brazil and Japan. We are also monitoring Reopening 
opportunities that could benefit from lower energy prices and rotation away from AI. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with 
a preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 
Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your 
shopping list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and 
Retailers given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy 
prices. Other elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower energy 
prices. Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and Japan. 
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long 
but would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / 
yields, which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, there 
is likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the Tech tape. 
Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus bullish play. The 
AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we like Tech in the 
near-term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we think you need to 
see the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; excluding AI-related 
plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper yield curve, and 
favorable capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real Estate, Telecom, and 
Utilities may participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most likely to lag the SPX, 
ex-AI plays. The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which would squeeze EU and 

RTY higher; plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, Cruise Lines, 
Discretionary, Hotels, etc) is another area of interest with a preference to play via options.
