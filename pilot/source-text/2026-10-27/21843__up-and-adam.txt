Amid concerns about AI financing, the PHLX Semiconductor Index posted its steepest daily decline (-6.7%) since June and has trailed 
the S&P 500 over the past 12 trading days by the second-widest margin (-17%) in 20 years. Historically, when the SOX has trailed by this

P/E multiples at a discount to the S&P 500, and solid hyperscaler earnings results expected, we remain optimistic on semis.
Friday, October 23, 2026
Amid broader affordability frustrations, rising health insurance costs are squeezing paychecks, as shown by KFF’s annual benefits 
survey. Workers with employer-sponsored family coverage are contributing an average of $7,471 toward premiums, up 9% YoY—an 
even faster increase than last year’s 7.6%. The 2026 premium hikes are more than twice the pace of wage growth. More hikes could be 
on deck in 2027, as Medicaid spending cuts take effect, which may exacerbate challenges in the private health insurance market.

Last week, big banks’ 3Q earnings calls offered a constructive view on consumer spending. This week brought more of the same, this 
time from credit card issuers with more of a sub-prime footprint. Synchrony Financial and Capital One posted YoY credit card spending

As The Pentagon Purchases 850 F-35s, It’s A Case Study Of Robust Orders For Defense Contractors 
Source: Kaiser Family Foundation
Chart of the Day: Premiums Pack Punch
The F-35 strike fighter—which combines air and ground attack capabilities—is the world’s fourth-most-numerous military aircraft, with 
nearly 1,400 delivered thus far to the US and 12 other countries. The F-35’s numbers are set to keep expanding: the Pentagon has signed 
a contract to purchase 850 over the next ten years. Long-term contractual visibility ensures that manufacturing capacity can scale up 
alongside demand. Sustained growth in defense spending by the US and its allies is a key reason we favor the Industrials sector. 
As we noted yesterday, Treasury yields have become less tethered to oil. Case in point: WTI rose 1.9% yesterday, yet the 10-year yield 
fell 17 bps to 4.96%, its first close below 5% in a month and the steepest one-day decline since August 2024. The deepening rout in AI-
related shares and renewed Middle East hostilities sparked a flight-to-safety in Treasuries, with short-covering in Treasury futures 
amplifying the move. This is an important reminder that Treasuries can provide diversification when risk assets come under pressure.
