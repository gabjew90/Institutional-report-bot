• CREDIT - Worth a listen Tony P podcast with GS chief credit strategist, Amanda Lynam:A few points
stood out to me. Amanda says in the current AI buildout, the key focus is no longer just the cost of capital,
but its availability and strategic value. Investors—especially equity investors—are now adopting a holistic
approach, blending analysis of both credit and equity markets. She highlights growing scrutiny of off-
balance-sheet exposures, including leases and purchase commitments. Across five leading hyperscalers,
these now total $2.8 trillion—an eightfold increase since 2023. Hyperscalers remain low-levered and
highly rated, but investors are increasingly focused on these commitments and inconsistent rating-agency
treatment: S&P adjusts leverage for off-balance-sheet leases, while Moody’s and Fitch do not. Potential
methodology changes are therefore worth watching. Particularly interesting was Amanda’s observation
that some companies are explicitly acknowledging the need to secure capital before markets become
saturated—a new development that highlights the distinctive nature of the AI financing cycle.

• Lee Coppersmith email worth a read its core message is that the recent sell-off is primarily a positioning
and leverage unwind rather than a collapse in the AI fundamental story. While the S&P fell less than 0.5%
last week and remains only 2.5% below its highs, hedge funds have suffered significant losses, with TMT-
focused funds down nearly 10% month-to-date and around $20bn of TMT selling over the past two weeks.
He argues that investors have become much more defensive, but gross leverage remains very elevated,
leaving markets vulnerable to further de-risking. The key near-term risk is that discretionary selling

transitions into systematic CTA selling, where positioning models imply meaningful downside supply if
markets weaken further. At the same time, volatility indicators are starting to signal genuine stress despite
limited index declines. Looking ahead, Lee believes this week's major catalysts, particularly Microsoft,
Alphabet, Amazon and the Fed, are critical for restoring confidence around AI spending and returns. His
base case is for a relief rally given the scale of the hedge fund unwind and increasingly defensive
positioning, but he holds that view cautiously given elevated leverage, negative systematic flow dynamics
and ongoing geopolitical risks, which is why he prefers defined-risk upside exposure through call spreads
rather than outright longs.
• AI SECURITY: The all In podcast this week had Nikesh Arora on who main message was the
accelerating cyber threats and creating a new category of software that needs securing—and enterprise
defences are not yet ready:
• • AI makes existing weaknesses more urgent. He argues that increasingly powerful models can uncover
vulnerabilities faster, exposing years of deferred upgrades and maintenance. More defensive compute
alone will not solve this while companies’ systems and security data remain fragmented.
• Security needs to move beyond preventing every breach. His emphasis is on bringing signals and data
together so companies can identify unusual activity and respond quickly, rather than relying solely on
continually patching vulnerabilities.
• Agents create a separate security and accountability problem. Companies need to know which agents are
running, what information they can access and what actions they are authorised to take. His banking
example distinguishes malicious access from an agent acting with a user’s credentials; he sees liability and
support costs as major concerns. His comments on who bears liability are opinions, not settled legal
conclusions.
• The controls around AI are lagging deployment. His most important point comes around 40:08–40:59:
businesses are enthusiastically deploying AI before the necessary security infrastructure is in place. His
preference for open-weight models also reflects a desire for greater control and auditability, although
access to model reasoning should not itself be treated as proof that an output is reliable.
• Amazon: CEO Jassy says AWS’s 2026 cloud capacity is sold out; most large capacity blocks for 2027 are

• AI Tokenomics: Ramp reports 2 consecutive months of virtually no growth in token spending but token
volumes continued to rise; falling effective prices and a shift toward more efficient models appear to be
absorbing much of the usage growth.Gavin Baker argued on X that falling token prices alone do not imply
weaker GPU demand; the relevant measure is revenue per GPU, or token price multiplied by token
throughput, relative to GPU rental cost. He says faster inference and a shift toward lower-margin open-
weight models can increase the amount of compute needed per dollar of end-customer revenue, supporting
demand for cloud capacity, chips, memory and networking.

• Palantir: Investor feedback following GIR’s October 8 upgrade to Buy from Neutral suggests balanced
positioning, with roughly half of conversations more sceptical about further outperformance. Three
debates dominate: growth, the durability of Palantir’s forward-deployed engineer (FDE) advantage and
customer wallet share. We see scope for significant upward estimate revisions even as revenue growth
slows—likely within the next eight quarters and potentially as early as 2H26—although the pace of
moderation will be difficult to model. Our 60x multiple implies approximately 1.3x P/E/G on 2026–2029
EPS growth, versus 2.0x next-twelve-month P/E/G for large-cap software peers; in our upside case of
approximately $6.50 in 2029 GAAP EPS, the stock currently trades at 0.50x PEG. We also view the
feedback loop between FDEs and product development as structural and difficult to replicate, particularly
for companies with historically more contained product roadmaps, while using AI FDEs to scale delivery
requires years of domain experience. Finally, we believe enterprise AI remains at an early stage, with
adoption across core operational workflows and sovereign AI use cases into 2027 potentially driving
another step-change in the addressable market and easing concerns about wallet-share ceilings.
