1) JAN HATZIUS - Downside Growth Risks
Chief Economist Jan Hatzius out with his latest ‘Global Views’. See here for the full piece.
BOTTOM LINE: Global growth in 2026 has been remarkably unremarkable, with the world and G3
tracking potential as fiscal easing and the AI boom offset drags from the Iran war and US tariffs. Our
standing forecast sees more of the same in 2027 as lower energy prices offset fading fiscal/AI tailwinds,
but risks are skewed to the downside. The most immediate concern is energy, where gasoline, diesel, jet
fuel and European/Asian gas prices are back near March/April highs on Middle East escalation fears.
Higher long-end yields (DM curves up ~100bp since early 2026) could subtract a cumulative 0.6% from
GDP via housing, durables and capex if sustained. The AI growth impulse is set to peak in late 2026/early
2027 and AI's ~0.4pp boost to US consumer spending via wealth effects will fade - gradually in our base
case, more sharply if AI stocks continue to decline.
Central Banks: We expect the Fed to hold on 28th October but deliver a 25bp hike to 4-4.25% on 9th
December as spot inflation remains too high, with upside inflation risks (mainly energy) that could prompt
another hike in March; renewed cuts in late 2027/early 2028. The ECB likely holds on 29th October and
hikes a final time to 2.75% in December, though French bond-market turbulence (likely to shave 1/4-
1/2pp off annualised growth) poses downside risks to that call. We expect the MPC to hike 25bp to 4% on
5th November, with risk of further moves though 4+ priced hikes look excessive. Hikes also expected in
Canada and New Zealand this week and Australia. Across the board, our strongest global view remains
that central banks will deliver fewer hikes than priced.
Cross-Asset: Our strongest global view is that central banks will deliver fewer hikes than currently priced.
Our rates strategists flag underweight positioning, diminished technical risks, and risk asset pressures as
reasons why the recent rally might extend. The market’s narrow breadth makes our equity strategists wary
of momentum factor exposure, but they still expect the S&P 500 to resume its climb after the midterm
election, as earnings continue to grow solidly. Our credit strategists remain cautious on IG technology
bonds but see scope for some near-term reversal in HY given the scale of the recent selloff. Our FX
strategists expect the Japanese yen to act as a hedge against further AI jitters, in part because such jitters
would shrink US-Japan interest rate differentials. Our commodity strategists recommend targeted
exposures to oil products and European natural gas to hedge near-term escalation risks alongside longer-

term exposures to copper, gold, and select power markets to benefit from structural supply/demand
imbalances.

3) Brazil Webcast Replay & Key Quotes
Key quotes from the speakers…
Alberto Ramos (Head of Latin America Economic Research): The market is looking for strong signals
regarding fiscal policy direction. A number of very expensive promises and measures were
made/undertaken during the campaign, so the fiscal baseline has visibly deteriorated. I think it's in the
interests of the administration to take measures to slow down the pace of the increase in primary spending,
which increased by close to 6% YoY in real terms over the last 4 years. Many people are of the view that
the administration should go to Congress before the new term starts in order to approve another
constitutional amendment to the fiscal measures. 4 years ago, the administration asked for a constitutional
amendment that was approved during the transition period, which increased primary spending by >160bn
BRL. The administration’s incentive now is to look a little bit more orthodox.
Carlos Loureiro (Head of Latin America Equities Local Trading): Historically, Equities have not
performed in Lula’s governments. Of course there are trades here and there, but I don't expect investors to
be long Equities on a long-term horizon in Brazil. It is just a trading market.
Thiago Marinheiro (Head of Brazil Macro Trading): The first reaction should be to adjustment your
portfolio, hedge your exposure, and then wait to see how the dust settles and find good levels to jump into
the trade again. We may see FX trade better than Rates in the long-term, because we expect to see Rates
trading higher with a restrictive money policy. The carry trade in FX will play its role, and BRL could
potentially outperform Rates for the next few years.
4) RBNZ & AUD CPI Previews
RBNZ: We now expect the RBNZ to raise the Official Cash Rate by 25bp at each of its October and
December meetings to a terminal rate of 3.25% (prior: +25bp in December to 3.00%). Beyond December,
our base case remains that the RBNZ keeps the policy rate on hold at 3.25% as existing space capacity in

the New Zealand economy reduces further pass-through of higher oil prices and a weaker currency. Our
slightly higher terminal rate forecast is now in line with the RBNZ’s long-term OCR path. Full note here.
AUD CPI: We expect headline CPI inflation to accelerate to 0.79%mom, which would take the year-over-
year rate 35bp higher to 4.32%yoy. Compositionally, this reflects a further rise in automotive fuel prices
(GSe: +9.0%mom) and a rebound in domestic (GSe: +4.1%mom) and international travel (GSe:
+5.6%mom) alongside the school holiday period. Property rates are also measured in September and
expected to rise sharply (GSe: +5.0%mom). In underlying terms, our component-level forecasts are
consistent with trimmed mean inflation of 0.29%mom, which would leave the year-over-year rate little
changed at 3.60%yoy. Full note here. Additional component-level detail can be found in GIR’s latest
Inflation Monitor.

5) Earnings This Week
See below for an overview of the earnings results to come this week (h/t Sean Johnstone, TMT Spec Sales
– full note here)
BOTTOM LINE: A big week ahead with markets already jittery after Meta raised the low end of its 2026
capex guide, turned FCF-negative and flagged multi-year equity funding needs via new Class C shares; its
CDS spreads also widened, leaving the market looking for "growth without another spending shock."
Hyperscaler results - MSFT and GOOGL Wednesday, AMZN Thursday - will be the key test of the AI
spending cycle after Meta's mixed print, with any signs of further equity issuance or balance-sheet
pressure unlikely to be well received. Cloud growth quality is in focus: growth rates, sequential trends,

backlog conversion, AI vs traditional cloud mix, and margins. Oracle hosts its Investor Day on the 28th. In
Asia, Samsung and Hynix both report.
Hyperscaler Previews: MSFT - stock up 38% since July print; GIR sees upside to 46-47% cc Azure
growth vs Street 45% (guide 44-45%); FY28 capex debate (GSe $319bn vs Street $252bn). GOOGL - key
debate on Search growth resilience vs agentic AI disruption (GIR -1% vs cons on Search in Q3); Google
Cloud offsetting bull case (GIR ~+100% yoy vs cons +96%); Capex (GIR $350bn in 2027). AMZN -
focus on AWS growth/margins (cons ~41%/37%), reports of idle NVDA racks that cannot switch on due
to lack of power, and further capex upward revision scrutiny (~$220bn for 2026). ORCL CMD - execution
of its 10GW capacity roadmap, capex intensity/timing (has said 27-28 peak), customer concentration,
upside to FY26-30 targets.
