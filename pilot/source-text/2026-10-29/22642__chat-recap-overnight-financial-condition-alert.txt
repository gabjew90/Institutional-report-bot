Brazil Confessions 
 

Long 2/3 EWZ in the high conviction portfolio - Opportunity lost, lessons learned... 
  
Brazil - We have been long-term bulls since the back-to-back Category 5 
capitulations of 2015 and 2020. Two full washouts inside five years carted out 
the old guard and left maximum negative psychology — the same setup we 
saw in uranium in 2021. That gave us confidence to build a position and go long-
term. 
  
In 2021, after a long flight, we spent an enlightening afternoon in São Paulo 
with André Esteves. Esteves is the founder and controlling shareholder of BTG 
Pactual — in our minds, the Warren Buffett of Latin America. The case stacked: 
Brazil has the highest real rates in the world, it’s a major commodity producer, and 
our thesis in our book -- "When Markets Speak" -- is that the new bull market in 
hard assets puts countries controlling those assets in the driver’s seat. High 
conviction means buy and hold the core. 

  
ALERT - That’s our standard playbook on high beta sectors — gold, silver, 
emerging markets, uranium. Our average holding period runs over three years, 
but we trade around the position (selling strength and buying different levels 
of capitulation selling) while keeping the core position. That discipline cost us 
this week. We had the trade alert written out and missed the sale. Much of the good 
news was already priced in. 
  
The first-round Bolsonaro victory was showing 80% on the betting sites. Fully 
priced. No risk in lightening, high risk in not. We should have at least sold upside 
calls or bought downside puts as protection. 
  
Lesson: buy and hold is sound philosophy, but on a high beta asset it can be 
wisdom misplaced.

What is Cheap, what is NOT? 
  
Brazil trades at roughly half the S&P’s multiple — about 12x earnings versus 26x — 
and that gap has persisted for most of the past 20 years, which is exactly why it 
stops being interesting on its own. A five-year-old can see Brazil is cheap. 

The edge isn’t the discount; it’s knowing when the discount closes. In 2008 
and 2000, when the US was in a bear market, that premium compressed toward 
zero. And in a genuine commodity bull market — the kind our hard-asset 
thesis is built on — Brazil can flip to a premium over the US, because the 
assets it controls are the ones the world is scrambling for. So the valuation isn’t the 
trade. The regime shift is.

T-Bills and AI 
  
One thing a lot of investors overlook is that the AI sector is pumping hundreds of 
billions into the T-bill market yearly with money they borrow at the long end. 
  
The chart tells you what's happening simplistically. Hyperscalers and neoclouds 
borrow for 10 years or more, spend the capex on chips and servers, etc and those 
companies put the cash into T-bills. 
  
To exemplify, hyperscalers are projected to issue $ 400 B in 2027. The top US semi 
makers and AAPL are expected to generate $512bl in free cash flow this year, and 
this will rise to $ 747 B in 2027. This money goes into cash and equivalents (i.e., T-
bills). 
  
So what can Bessent do with this? 
  
He can cut coupon sizes in the upcoming QRA (Nov 4), keep the buyback cap at 
$6bl, and fund it with T-bills. He thereby removes ~$355bl of long-duration net 
supply from the market on a yearly basis. 

  
This would go a long way in neutralizing the colossal AI borrow long/invest short 
that's happening now.
