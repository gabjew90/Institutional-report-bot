o   FEROLI ON THE PRESS CONF – Feroli added that: “Chair Warsh will 
have to explain why the committee hiked after a two-tenths core PCE print in 
September but is now holding after an expected three-tenths outturn. 
Disallowing follow-up questions won’t resolve the challenge. We believe the 
various Fed speakers cited earlier provide a reasonable blueprint for what to 
say: the Committee is biased to hike but wants to take more time to observe 
how last month’s hike has affected the economy. Conveying a bias to hike, 
something Warsh’s role model Greenspan regularly did, is a form of forward 
guidance, and so it may be difficult for the chair to get this across. However, 
one can hope there is some on-the-job learning going on. A decision to leave 
rates unchanged less than a week before the midterm elections that goes 
unexplained would only encourage thoughts that policy is being driven by 
considerations other than employment and inflation.”

offer incremental insights, we may see Fed-induced volatility extend into the

2027 path following the hawkish September inflation print; and, (ii) emerging 
concerns around AI and whether that influences the Fed’s economic 
assessment on either growth or inflation. 
•         MIDTERM UPDATES – We updated the Midterm election chart below for both traditional 
poll and prediction markets. On whether traditional polls may underestimate GOP, one 
article from The Hill (here) today pointed out that the underestimations were not significant

the average polling miss and took an average of all polls conducted in the final three weeks 
of a campaign for House, Senate and governor compared with the end result. He found that 
in the two most recent midterms, 2018 and 2022, polls did not underestimate Republicans.

Democrats by 0.3 points.".  
 
 
EARNINGS MACRO READ-THROUGH 

•         SUMMARY – Macro read-through from earnings since yesterday’s close points to 
steady demand, an AI capex cycle that is still accelerating, and cost pressures that are 
spreading across energy, transportation and skilled labor. A few key observations: (i) AI 
infrastructure remains the clearest source of corporate demand. Corning said hyperscalers 
keep pressing "to do more and more" and are helping fund new capacity through multiyear 
agreements. UPS put the data center logistics opportunity at $15 billion. (ii) Energy and 
freight are becoming a larger source of cost inflation. Freeport-McMoRan said diesel costs 
have more than doubled this year and US electricity rates rose significantly between Q2 and 
Q3. (iii) one interesting comment from Xylem (Machinery) said utility projects are slowing 
because contractors are absorbed by AI jobs, suggesting that rather than taking skilled labor 
in construction, the current buildup in AI infra could actually lead to higher costs for 
specialized labor in the short/medium term. Selected quotes below. 
•         AI INVESTMENT 
o    [Corporate Demand – AI Networking] Corning (GLW) – "What we see in 
our demand is a continued press from our customers to do more and more. 
And as a result, what you see us doing when we're doing these multiyear 
agreements is asking those customers. Our customers are primarily the major 
hyperscalers, given our long-standing relationships, help us fund and secure 
that capacity so that we can be assured of that demand and we reduce any 
amount of risk to our shareholders."  
o    [Data Center Sales +80%] Hubbell (HUBB) – "Data center sales 
increased over 80% on an organic basis...in a strong underlying market." 
o    [AI Capex Supercycle] Nucor (NUE) – "...we're in the middle of what we 
call the AI Super Bowl, the hyperscaler has committed enormous amount of 
capital to these data center build-outs..." 
o    [Data Center Logistics Boom] UPS (UPS) – "...with the explosion of data 
center growth...we estimate the logistics opportunities associated with data 
centers alone, is $15 billion." 
•         CONSUMER HEALTH / SPENDING 

o    [K-Shaped Spending] UPS (UPS) – "It's a bit of a K curve for sure. If you 
have assets you're spending, if you don't, you're stretched. But as long as the 
employment rate stays fairly stable...consumers are going to spend..." 
o    [Utility Spending Turns Cautious] Xylem (XYL) – "...utility customer 
spending decisions took longer to materialize amid a more cautious and 
deliberate spending environment." 
•         INFLATION / PRICING 
o    [Energy Impacts / Airline Demand] Boeing (BA) – "... in fact, the high fuel 
prices are driving them to really, really want these aircraft sooner. ... we 
haven't seen anybody wanting to replan their order growth... So far, the 
airlines have been, for the most part, flowing the higher fuel into ticket prices, 
think over the next coming quarters, we'll have to watch that." 
o    [Logistics Costs Elevated] Coca-Cola (KO) – "Our commodity exposure 
overall remains manageable. However, we are seeing elevated cost 
pressures...driven by increased logistics and transportation costs." 
o   [Energy Impacts] Freeport-McMoRan (FCX) – "Diesel was the biggest 
factor, but we are seeing increased electricity rates. We saw that quarter to 
quarter in the US between second quarter and third quarter, a significant 
increase in electricity rates. But the biggest part of that was diesel, and you've 
seen more than doubling of diesel costs for the year. But we consume about 
275 million gallons of diesel a year, and 100 million gallons of that is in the US, 
and then we've got 80 million gallons in Peru and the balance in Indonesia."  
•         LABOR MARKET 
o   [AI Absorbing Contractors] Xylem (XYL) – "In some cases, due to contractor 
availability, they are tied up on AI jobs [AI jobs are absorbing contractors, 
meaning utilities cannot get crews], for example, or they are re-scoping them 
due to inflation, or as I mentioned before, they are bidding them as 
construction manager at risk, which takes more time."  
•         POLICY / TARIFF 

o   [Section 232 Curbing Imports] Nucor (NUE) – "...strengthening Section 232 
measures are allowing us to realize a greater share of the strong 
demand...finished steel imports are down more than 13% year-over-year and 
down 24% compared to 2024." 
•         SUPPLY CHAIN 
o   [Supply Chain / Production Ramp] Boeing (BA) – "... We are improving, 
but we're not yet to where I can say we're stably producing wings to support 
the 47 a month rate. So we're going to want to dwell on that for a little while to 
make sure we're stable and don't end up in an unstable situation with 
production. Now that informs our next rate increase, obviously. So we didn't go 
smoothly from 42 to 47 as we did from 38 to 42..." 
  
US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the October 26th Morning Briefing 
We remain Tactically Bullish. The SPX has lost 2.4% over the last 2 weeks, including a 
shift toward defensive sectors. MTD, SPX is -36bp with 8 / 11 sectors positive and Energy, 
Staples, Utilities, HC, Financials, Materials, RE, and Discretionary all beating the index. 
What makes us think that stocks will move higher? Generally speaking, an economy growing 
at/above trend GDP and positive earnings growth produces a bull market. Flash PMIs, for 
the second consecutive month, point to an economy growing ~5% annualized, in real terms. 
So far, about a quarter of the SPX has reported earnings with no dips to the pre-earnings 
season forecast (~13% revenue growth, ~30% EPS growth, and ~15% margins … full details 
in the Additional Thoughts section). So that is the basis for the view. A refocus on macro and 
micro fundamentals, rather than AI fears, should be supportive of stocks. That said, the 
signal is less clear as we have yet to have a complete positioning washout despite sentiment 
being firmly in the Fear region.
