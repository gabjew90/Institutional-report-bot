Fed, ECB, BoJ ahead this week – How could they surprise the markets?
Brief thoughts from around the floor….
-> Fed: Warsh echoing Waller's "hikes don't need to come at consecutive meetings" plus a clear "no pre-set
pace" message -> Likely keeps hike-premium priced into 2027. Clearest misstep risk is a repeat of July,
when Warsh’s most dovish answers came when explaining why the Fed wasn't hiking; therefore, a risk he
focuses on the current decision, rather than the path, and the market reads dovish.
-> Fed: More airtime on inflation likely extends curve flattening + keep the front end selling off; more
discussion on tighter financial conditions likely re-steepen the curve; with ~75bps priced through Q3
2027, a 15bp rally more likely than a selloff; in FX, AUDUSD likely moves most on a surprise hawkish
Fed.
-> ECB: Statement not expected to mention sovereign spreads - Lagarde will likely call France a political
problem rather than a monetary one; will probably add that the ECB has tools to contain contagion + keep
policy transmission working. Bigger risk for EUR-FI/Bonds is a dismissive, or very hawkish, tone on
French spreads that would leave markets feeling an ECB backstop is far away.
-> ECB: Fewer hikes would be bearish for EUR + bullish for Bunds; focus on credit easing or
transmission tools would be bullish for EUR + the periphery.
-> BOJ: Mildly dovish pause as base-case, underlying inflation converging toward 2%. Board members
will likely cut FY26 core inflation forecast + raise FY27 on energy. In Rates, short covering largely
concluded following the recent JGB rally, we expect a gradual post-meeting sell-off, leaving the medium-
term expectation of a 2.00%+ terminal rate intact
-> BOJ: BOJ will not push back against a December hike (~80% priced), mindful of the risk of USDJPY
rising back above 160. However, with the market already priced for this outcome, and Western Core CPI
running at 4% annualized over the last 3m, the market could have a higher-bar for what constitutes a
hawkish meeting.
Thoughts welcome - Adam
Fed:
Brandon Brown (MD, US Treasury Trading): “Heading into the FOMC on Wednesday, the market prices 3
bps in October meeting and 20 more in December. A slew of dovish Fedspeak walked October pricing

down and grew market confidence in a quarterly pace ahead of CPI and PPI, which came in fairly hot.
Despite expectations of a high core PCE print on Friday, we and the market expect a skip on Wednesday.
The difficult part of the meeting will be conveying a hawkish stance despite the slower pace in the face of
higher inflation readings. With 23bps cumulatively priced thru December, it will be difficult for Warsh’s
presser to cheapen Z6. In order to prevent a rally, sufficient hawkishness such that the market maintains
hike premium well into 2027 will be required. Making clear there is no pre-set pace and that hikes could
come at any time would likely be enough.
On the flipside, there is risk the presser comes across somewhat dovish given the slower pace and the
October skip. With nearly 75bps priced thru Q3 2027, some hike premium may be priced out – we think in
a 15bps move, an extension of the rally is somewhat more likely than a selloff.”
Mike Cahill (Head of G10 FX Strategy): “The market risk going into this meeting is that Chairman
Warsh’s most dovish answers in the July meeting came on questions where he was asked to explain why
the Fed wasn’t hiking at that meeting. His tendency towards less forward guidance could also cause him to
focus more on the current decision than the trajectory. This should be easier with a hike already delivered
and the market a little more comfortable with the new style, but that still seems like the clearest potential
market misstep.
Overall we will focus on his characterization of inflation risks vs the tightening in financial conditions
where the more time he devotes to recent market movements the more dovish the message will likely be
interpreted, but the message from other Fed speakers has been that they see the September move as part of
a process.
Will Marshall (Head of US Rates Strategy): “A framing along the lines of Governor Waller’s comment
that “hikes do not need to come at consecutive meetings, but they should be in place in an acceptable
period of time” could help to justify the hold while reinforcing the forward-looking bias to hike further. To
the extent that Chair Warsh draws on some of the characterizations from Jackson Hole and the September
press conference, it would at least help to reinforce the floor under hike pricing without necessarily
knocking the market it off of the roughly quarterly profile currently reflected in market pricing.
To the extent there’s a clear set of factors that could impart a stronger tilt, it would be the airtime given to
inflation risks versus the tightening in financial conditions. Our economists expect a 34bp September core
PCE print, and energy prices are near the post-US/Iran conflict highs. Meanwhile financial conditions
have tightened, but not to an extreme degree given the hawkish Fed repricing. A relative emphasis on
inflation signals with little attention on tighter financial conditions likely reinforces the flattening
environment we’ve been in, reinforcing hike risk and keeping the front-end the main outlet to inflation
news, whereas more weight on tighter financial conditions likely sees some re-steepening of the curve.
Tom Cox (G10 Options Trading): “I am surprised the market has taken the probability of a hike back
down so low after a hot CPI where odds were briefly 50/50. Have heard from Waller but official stance is
still no forward guidance. Cheapest USD calls remain USDJPY with surprisingly high premium still for

USD puts there (1m rr 2.15 not far off highs of the year) although AUDUSD would likely be the largest
mover in the case of a surprise hike. Implied gaps in most of G10 45-55bps.”
ECB
Mike Cahill (Head of G10 FX Strategy): “The key for FX and RV FI will be how much Lagarde draws on
the differences in their tools and whether she makes that an explicit feature of the messaging. Steps
towards credit easing would have very different implications for the Euro and spreads vs Bunds vs simply
hiking less. So far, the ECB has indicated they are ready to respond to the tightening in financial
conditions and more benign underlying inflation with potentially fewer hikes (bearish EUR / bullish
Bunds), but we are not yet approaching levels where they’re actively considering the various transmission
mechanisms they have (which would be bullish EUR and bullish for the periphery).
I expect that is still going to be the message for now, so any emphasis on tighter financial conditions
without much focus on sovereign issues would still be bearish for the currency.”
Jan Scheffel (Global Co-Head of Short Term Macro Trading): “Having sent a pretty hawkish message at
the September meeting and opened up the door to sequential hikes, the fact that winter TTF prices are now
25% higher than at the time of the ECB meeting and 40% higher than at the end of September, market
participants, from the perspective of mid-September, could well have expected that another rate hike
would be on the cards at this week’s meeting.
However, the ECB already had pushed back against the hawkish interpretation of their communication and
against the close to one for one correlation between higher energy prices and rates, even before stresses
emerged in France. The widening of the Oat/Bund spread from 90bps at the time of the ECB meeting to
almost 180bps at one stage last week and the associated tightening in financial conditions, was a clear
signal from markets that there is a limit to the ability that European economies are able to withstand ever
higher rates.
We expect the ECB to keep the statement at this week’s meeting largely unchanged, reiterating the data
dependant, meeting by meeting approach, whilst acknowledging upside risks to inflation and downside
risks to economic growth. We would expect no formal mention of sovereign spreads and France in the
statement, but Lagarde will certainly be asked about it in the press conference. We would expect her to
fend off the question by pointing to the fact that this requires a political solution rather than a monetary
policy one, in line with comment by Moulin in recent weeks. We would, however, expect Lagarde to point
out that the ECB has many tools available to contain any contagion and to ensure the smooth transmission
of monetary policy.
We consider the key risk at the meeting that it is somewhat more hawkish than the market expects. Whilst
the ECB was keen to break the one for one correlation between energy prices and rates, the fact that the
markets didn’t react at all to the 40% rally in winter TTF over October might be a bit of a stretch.
Furthermore, a very hawkish attitude to the recent widening of French sovereign spreads, which leaves the
market feeling that any ECB backstop is far away, is another key risk to European bond markets and wider
risk assets.”

Will Marshall (Head of US Rates Strategy): “The balanced message our economists expect would likely
nod towards the ongoing upside risks from energy, the benign underlying inflation data thus far, and
tighter financial conditions, and reinforce a “measured approach” to policy tightening. For the market, the
relative emphasis on higher energy prices versus tighter financial conditions will inform the
hawkish/dovish tilt, which we think that should be most relevant to the very front-end (e.g. through Mar-
27 pricing). The feedback between hawkish policy risks and sovereign spread volatility likely to put a
speed limit on how much points a further out can selloff on a more hawkish signal, however. Given our
economists’ view that Largarde is likely to say little about sovereign issues, we think this keeps value
along the EUR curve in front-end vs belly flattening exposure.”
Tom Cox (G10 Options Trading): “Here focus will be on how perceived reaction function to widening
European credit spreads (particularly French) will affect decision at the next (December) meeting. As
such, favourite trade from team PE at the moment is buy 6m options funded by either 1m window ko’s or
1m strangles with the curve inverted 1m-3m.”
BOJ
Sung Mo Koo (FICC Rates Structuring Strats): “We expect the BOJ to hold the policy rate unchanged at
1.25% in a mildly dovish pause, viewing the probability of an overt surprise in either direction as minimal.
Underlying inflation converging toward the 2% target indicates the cycle is progressing in line with
projections, reinforcing the case against back-to-back rate hikes or outsized increments.
While higher energy prices will lift the FY2027 CPI forecast, the Board will likely see limited secondary
pass-through into wages and broader prices. The BOJ will not push back against a December hike (~80%
priced), mindful of the risk of USD/JPY rising back above 160; however, there is also little reason for
policymakers to add hawkish commentary to endorse it. More importantly, Governor Ueda will likely
keep options open given heightened overseas volatility. With short covering largely concluded following
the recent JGB rally, we anticipate a gradual post-meeting sell-off rather than an abrupt repricing, leaving
the medium-term expectation of a 2.00%+ terminal rate intact.”
Mike Cahill (Head of G10 FX Strategy): “Recent news reports suggest that the BoJ is likely to drop hints
at tightening again this year, and the BoJ has repeatedly said they are now focused on keeping underlying
inflation around 2%.
But with the market already priced for this outcome, and Western Core CPI running at 4% annualized over
the last 3m, I think the market will have a higher bar for what constitutes a hawkish meeting vs the media
reports that they intend to send a hawkish message. We think domestic policies are gradually turning more
positive for the Yen, but very recently the appeal of USD/JPY downside hedges has been more on the side
of shifting capex expectations while more aggressive domestic policy shifts still look further out.”
Will Marshall (Head of US Rates Strategy): “Our economists are not anticipating a particularly urgent
signal from the BoJ alongside an expected on-hold decision, with board members likely to nudge their
FY26 core inflation forecast lower and take their FY27 core inflation forecast higher. Governor Ueda’s
messaging around the exchange rate, financial conditions, underlying inflation, and the neutral rate will all

be key to shaping the market’s read, but assuming the BoJ does not indicate increased risks of inflation
overshooting its target, we think the market’s likely to remain in a position of pricing sustained, rather than
strongly front-loaded hike risk.”
Given it will take time under our economists’ forecasts for evidence to build that inflation is on track to
return to 2% (with acceleration likely first before deceleration from next Spring), a deliberate policy
approach combined with the relative policy stance versus global peers should keep the 5y part of the curve
and skewed towards underperformance. That nonetheless represents a sustained shift from the prior
regime where pressure showed up further out the curve, reflecting in part recent evidence of a more
supportive demand backdrop”
Tom Cox (G10 Options Trading): “USDJPY been range trading for a while. Trade-weighted JPY close to
the highs of the year reducing the risk of any imminent intervention but global growth concerns preventing
any fast move back higher. Wouldn’t expect too much out of this BoJ, worth noting GPIF quarterly
investment results on 6th Nov where we’ll see if they’ve moved bond allocations at all within existing
limits. Something that was very in focus a month or so ago.”
