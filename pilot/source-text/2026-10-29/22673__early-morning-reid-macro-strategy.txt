Ahead of a busy day today that includes the Fed and BoC decisions and earnings from Microsoft and 
Alphabet after the US close, markets remained resilient, with the Nasdaq (+1.18%) and S&P 500 
(+0.60%) advancing for a second day. The tech optimism has continued in Asia overnight, also 
supported by blowout results from SK Hynix, which is surging by +9.8% as I type. Yesterday’s gains were 
also helped by a pullback in oil as Brent fell -1.67% to $113.88/bbl. However, Brent is +1.48% higher again 
overnight as the WSJ reported the Trump administration warning Gulf states to be prepared for a new 
operation against Iran after the midterms. Oil markets have also been watching the outcome of 
yesterday’s election in Israel, which remains too close to call with preliminary results trickling in. 
Before we go through yesterday’s moves, let’s start with the main event of this week, which is the Fed 
decision at 6pm LDN today. As a reminder, our economists expect the Fed to leave rates unchanged, 
and futures are pricing less than a 10% chance of a hike. The interest will therefore be in Chair Warsh’s 
press conference and how the Fed balances still-elevated inflation and solid growth against the recent 
tightening in financial conditions. Our economists expect the statement itself to contain only limited 
changes, although dissents to a hold are possible. The broader message is likely to be hawkish: our 
economists think recent inflation data support the case for further tightening over time and foresee 
50bp of further hikes across December and March. They also think Warsh is likely to make clear today that 
tighter financial conditions are not a substitute for rate hikes. You can find their full preview here. 
Ahead of that Fed decision, US Treasury yields edged slightly higher yesterday, led by the 2yr yield 
(+1.9bps). The 10yr rose +1.0bps to 5.00% and is another +2.3bps higher overnight. The marginal rise 
came as investors digested a large batch of economic data, which was on balance firmer than expected. 
Most notably, the Conference Board consumer confidence index rebounded sharply in October to 91.7 
(vs 81.0), reversing the decline that it had seen since June amid the rise in energy prices. And while 
durable goods orders for September were a smidgen below expectations (+1.8% vs +1.9% expected), under 
the fold we saw some signs of strength, including new orders for computers and related products rising +3% 

mom. This also put their annual growth at +26.7% yoy, the highest since 1997. Finally, on the labour side, 
ADP payrolls showed US private job gains at a still solid average of 16.75k/week in the 4 weeks to 
October 10. 
The strong data yesterday also added to the resilience of US equities, which were already advancing 
amidst a continuing buoyant tech mood. Indeed, the Philly semiconductor index (+3.60%) posted a big 
gain, while the Nasdaq (+1.18%) and the Mag-7 (+0.94%) also marched higher. Notable tech moves came 
from CoreWeave (+7.94%), Nvidia (+0.90%), and Broadcom (+4.00%). European equities also continued this 
week’s advance, with the Stoxx 600 (+0.47%), DAX (+0.71%), and CAC 40 (+0.51%) all gaining. Only the 
FTSE 100 (-0.75%) underperformed, dragged by a fall in HSBC (-5.42%) after its Q3 earnings fell short of 
estimates. The true test of the tech-rally will be today, with Alphabet and Microsoft amongst those reporting 
their earnings. 
There were a lot of other busy developments going on yesterday, including Israel’s election. This saw the 
highest turnout since 1999 at 77.7%, with the news of historically high turnout helping the shekel rise by 
+0.83% against the dollar yesterday as this was seen as potentially reducing the chances of Prime Minister 
Netanyahu retaining power. However, two exit polls put the Netanyahu-aligned coalition and the 
opposition bloc led by Gadi Eizenkot neck and neck at around 51 seats, well short of the 61 seats 
needed for a majority, while another has given Netanyahu’s coalition a marginal lead. The full seat 
distribution, including which parties reach the 3.25% entry barrier, may take a day or two to settle. Both 
Netanyahu and Eisenkot told their supporters overnight they would be able to form ruling coalitions. 
However, as I type, prediction markets see no successful government formation as the most likely outcome, 
which could then lead to a new election. Netanyahu is seen as slightly favoured to get the first mandate to 
form a government as his bloc leads in the partial results this morning. 
From a global macro perspective, Israel’s election has been seen as potentially important in terms of 
what it means on Iran. With Netanyahu having maintained that regime change in Iran is ultimately 
necessary, the distinction between Israel’s political blocs could matter considerably if tensions re-escalate 
after the US midterms. The risks of re-escalation are in focus again as the WSJ reported last night that 
the US has told several Gulf Arab countries to be prepared for a short, intense military campaign after 
the midterm elections. 
These overnight developments have pushed Brent crude +1.50% higher at $115.59/bbl this morning. 
Prior to that, Brent had declined by -1.67% yesterday, while WTI fell -1.83% to $99.90/bbl, amid tentative 
diplomatic hopes. Reported comments by Iran’s Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei reiterated Iran’s 
conditions for ending the war, as he aimed for “ending the war and the ‘neither war nor peace’ situation”. This 
follows a regional push to avoid renewed escalation, including Monday’s news of Iran’s meeting with Saudi 
Arabia, Türkiye and Pakistan. In another positive oil market sign, we heard Bloomberg report that Saudi 
Aramco was offering spot oil cargoes to European buyers for both this month and next. 
The overnight news has also left European natural gas futures (+0.84%) on course for a new post-2022 
high this morning. They had retreated by -0.64% yesterday, amid news that Russia’s president Putin spoke 
with Finland’s president and Norway’s Prime Minister, his first call with Western European leaders in over a 
year. Additionally, a localised temporary ceasefire in Southern Ukraine took effect, brokered by Melania 
Trump and the Pope. 

European rates saw muted moves yesterday ahead of tomorrow’s ECB decision. The 10yr bund yield fell -
0.8bps, but yields on 10yr gilts (+0.2bps), BTPs (+0.8bps) and OATs (+2.2bps) edged higher. The OAT-bund 
spread widened back by +2.9bps to 156bps. That came as France’s consumer confidence fell to 80 in 
October (vs. 85 expected), the lowest reading since June 2013 and the joint lowest since the start of the 
data in the 1970s. This follows the recent protests in France, although it is worth noting that Euro Area 
consumer confidence also deteriorated in October. 
Staying in Europe, we saw a notable milestone in the FX space as the euro lost ground against the dollar (-
0.44%) for a fifth session in a row, falling to a 17-month low of 1.1125. The euro’s continued slide came 
despite lower oil prices and stronger European equities yesterday, as well as stabilisation in the OAT-Bund 
spread since last Friday. Positioning factors may be at play, though concerns over the European growth story 
may also be playing a part. Indeed, our economists’ euro area surprise indicator has recently turned 
negative for the first time since June, while yesterday’s Bank Lending Survey from the ECB showed 
credit conditions at their tightest since 2023 (see our economists’ BLS reaction here). 
Asian equity markets are trading broadly higher this morning, building on Wall Street’s overnight gains 
amid renewed enthusiasm around AI-related stocks. Across the region, South Korea’s KOSPI (+6.80%) is 
surging after chipmaker SK Hynix (+9.84%) reported blockbuster third-quarter results, with net profit 
tripling from a year earlier. This has helped push US Nasdaq futures +0.35% higher. Japan’s Nikkei 
(+1.11%) is also extending its recent rally, while gains elsewhere are more modest, with the Hang Seng 
(+0.36%), CSI 300 (+0.76%), and Shanghai Composite (+0.81%) all in positive territory. The S&P/ASX 200 (-
0.03%) is lagging regional peers, as stronger-than-expected inflation data reinforced expectations of 
another RBA rate hike next week. 
That data showed Australia’s headline CPI rise by +1.3% q/q in Q3 (vs. 1.2% expected) after +0.6% in Q2. 
The closely watched trimmed mean measure rose +1.0% q/q, lifting annual core inflation to 3.7% (vs. 3.6% 
expected and previous). And in September alone, annual headline inflation also edged up to 4.3% y/y from 
4.0% (vs. 4.2% expected). Meanwhile, RBA Governor Bullock said today that “Some of the international 
and domestic upside risks to inflation have materialized or are materializing”. Markets have increased 
expectations for further tightening in response, with a hike now over 80% priced for next week’s RBA 
meeting. 10yr Aussie yields are +5.6bps higher at 5.21%. 
In New Zealand, the Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) left its official cash rate unchanged at 2.75% 
in a narrow 4-3 decision, surprising markets that had widely expected a 25bp increase. The decision 
contrasted with market pricing and economist expectations, with a hike having been more than 90% priced 
ahead of the meeting. While policymakers cited easing headline inflation and a patchy economic recovery, 
they also signaled that additional tightening may still be required to ensure inflation returns sustainably to 
2%. The unexpected pause has weighed on the New Zealand dollar, which is trading -1.2% lower against 
the US dollar as I type. 
Finally, to the day ahead now, in addition to the Fed’s decision, we’ll also get the BoC’s decision later this 
afternoon. Data releases include US September advance goods trade balance, retail inventories, France Q3 
total jobseekers, Italy August industrial sales, September PPI. Notable earnings include Alphabet and 
Microsoft. Otherwise today the UK’s Chancellor Healey will present his first autumn budget under the new 
Labour administration.
