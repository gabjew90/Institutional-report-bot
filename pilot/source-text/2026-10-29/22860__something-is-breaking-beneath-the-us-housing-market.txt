Housing's uncomfortable reality
US homebuilders are holding a massive trading range, but the fundamentals beneath the surface are
becoming increasingly uncomfortable. Margins are shrinking, construction costs are rising, buyer
traffic has collapsed, and inventories are building even as affordability remains deeply stretched.
Meanwhile, a potentially hawkish Fed threatens to add another layer of pressure through higher rates.
TS Lombard sees the risk of further homebuilder underperformance.
The range survives... for now
XHB has been stuck in a massive trading range since 2024. The latest bounce started near the lower
end of that range, but the broader technical picture remains uninspiring. With homebuilder
fundamentals deteriorating and rates potentially moving higher again, the risk is that support
eventually gives way. For now, though, the range remains intact.

The margin squeeze
Homebuilder profitability is deteriorating fast. Operating margins have fallen below pre-Covid levels as
weaker new-home prices collide with rising construction, labor and financing costs. The problem is no
longer just weak demand. Builders are increasingly getting squeezed from both sides.

TS Lombard
Costs up, prices down
The housing margin squeeze in two charts. Construction input inflation is accelerating again, with
goods and services costs rising around 10–11% annually, while median new-home prices have been
falling. Builders are losing pricing power at precisely the moment their costs are rising. Not a
particularly attractive combination for earnings.
TS Lombard
Where have all the buyers gone?
Prospective buyer traffic has collapsed. TS Lombard highlights that the NAHB buyer traffic index has
fallen to 18, its weakest reading since the Covid recession. With affordability still severely stretched,
the demand side of US housing is showing little sign of a meaningful recovery.

TS Lombard
The affordability trap
Mortgage rates are back around 7.3%, while US housing affordability remains near historically
depressed levels. TS Lombard warns that a more hawkish Fed could add renewed upward pressure to
rates, further undermining housing demand. The uncomfortable reality: cheaper homes alone may not
be enough to bring buyers back.
TS Lombard
The inventory problem
US housing is facing another uncomfortable divergence. Inventories are rising even as housing starts
and permits weaken. Builders are already responding to softer demand by reducing construction
activity, but the growing supply of available homes could keep pressure on prices and margins.
Another reason the sector may struggle to sustain a recovery.

TS Lombard
The Fed could make things worse
TS Lombard sees renewed upside risk to US yields as economic growth remains resilient and inflation
pressures persist. With Fed communication already leaning hawkish, the risk is that interest rates stay
higher than housing investors would like. Homebuilders are particularly exposed: weak underlying
fundamentals leave little room for another rates shock.
TS Lombard
Short housing, not the market
TS Lombard is expressing its bearish housing view through a relative-value trade: long the equal-
weight S&P 500 (RSP) versus short US homebuilders (XHB), at a 1.75:1 ratio to account for volatility
differences. The logic is straightforward. The broader equity market can remain resilient even as
housing fundamentals deteriorate. Rather than betting on an outright market decline, the trade targets
continued homebuilder underperformance.
