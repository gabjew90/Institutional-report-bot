The Bullion Report: Structural Support, Tactical Caution
Clients are structurally bullish on gold but tactically cautious. As year-end approaches, participants are
increasingly focused on protecting existing P&L rather than adding risk. Hence the gold price remains caught
in a low-conviction range, supported by meaningful buying interest near the lower end, around $4,000/oz, but
lacking sufficient momentum for a sustained rally. The tightening monetary-policy backdrop continues to
constrain the upside, leaving the market treading water and providing little incentive for discretionary
participants to take significant positions.
Longer-term structural support remains intact through geopolitical risk, de-dollarization, non-dollar settlement
demand, and continued central-bank buying, but these themes have yet to provide a catalyst strong enough to
drive gold out of its current range.
Physical demand from Asia remains strong, led by kilobar demand destined for mainland China, alongside
some large-bar activity. Buying began around the start of October and has remained consistent, arriving earlier
than in recent years despite the traditionally stronger seasonal demand. Refinery conditions are also
tightening, with production orders and available refinery slots becoming more difficult to secure. Chinese
demand is contributing to front-end tightness in gold, while some market participants are showing interest in
transacting against renminbi rather than exclusively against US dollars. Read the full note here.
(Jonathan Garber, Macro Sales)

French Consumer Confidence Slumps To Lowest Since 2013 - French consumer confidence collapsed to
80 in October, down five points from 85 in September and well below the Bloomberg consensus estimate of 85
— the lowest reading since June 2013. The miss was broad-based: the personal financial situation outlook fell
sharply to -24 from -15, unemployment concerns jumped to 61 from 56, and savings capacity deteriorated.
Answers to this survey were collected between Sept. 25 and Oct. 19 2026, capturing some of the social unrest
in France. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
UK Shop-Price Inflation Hits 2½-Year High As Retailers’ Cost Buffers Erode – The British Retail
Consortium’s October shop-price index showed inflation accelerated to 2.1% y/y from 1.4%, the fastest since
February 2024, amid elevated energy, transport, and employment costs. Food inflation rose to 2.9% from
2.5%, with ambient food prices up to 3.2% from 2.2% – the highest since September 2025 – while non-food
inflation more than doubled to 1.7% from 0.8% – the highest since December 2023 – led by furniture and
home-improvement goods. The BRC warned that “the dam is starting to break” after retailers absorbed costs
for two years, signalling broader price pass-through as official CPI reached 3.5% in September. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)
UK Minimum Wage Rise Set To Outpace Inflation: Reports – UK Chancellor John Healey is likely to
announce a near-4% increase in the minimum wage on Wednesday, The Times [paywall] and The Telegraph
reported [paywall]. The National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over would rise 3.9% to about
GBP13.20 an hour from GBP12.71 in 2027, exceeding current inflation of 3.5%. The rate for 18- to
20-year-olds would increase to around GBP11.27 from GBP10.85, slowing convergence with the adult rate
amid employer concerns over youth hiring. The Times said the move would end Labour’s manifesto pledge to
align the under-21 rate with the living wage. The proposed adult rate is close to the Low Pay Commission’s
April estimate of GBP13.18. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
EU Weighs Wider China Import Curbs – The EU is considering quotas or tariffs on Chinese chemicals,
furniture and machinery as early as Q1 2027, seeking to narrow a goods deficit that reached EUR360 bn last
year while avoiding retaliation. The plan would broaden its trade-defense push following safeguards sought by
Germany and an agreement capping annual Chinese hybrid-vehicle imports at 350K. UBS Wealth
Management CIO believes efforts to protect domestic industry may add to trade friction and uncertainty for
companies exposed to Chinese supply chains or demand. Broader defenses could increase dispersion,
favouring companies with strong balance sheets, pricing power and diversified supply chains, while reinforcing
policy support for reshoring, electrification, defense and advanced manufacturing. CIO forecasts 15%
Eurozone earnings growth in both 2026 and 2027, supported by a pick-up in manufacturing, continued cost
discipline and higher investment in AI, electrification and defense. Recent market weakness has lowered
valuations, while earnings revisions have broadened across sectors. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

HSBC: Softer Q3 Under The Hood, But Volume Trends Improve - UBS Research: HSBC’s third-quarter
profit landed broadly in-line with expectations, though UBS analyst Jason Napier sees a slightly softer
underlying print as weaker income offset better credit costs. Pre-provision profit excluding notable items was
3% below consensus, with revenue 2% light as banking NII missed by 2% and fee and other income came in
1% lower. Pre-tax profit was $9.6 bn. The brighter spot was credit quality, with impairments 21% below
consensus, while loan volumes rose 1% quarter-on-quarter and deposits were broadly flat on a
constant-currency basis, Jason noted. Wealth was softer, however, with divisional profit 10% below UBS
estimates and wealth fees down 3% quarter-on-quarter. Capital also came in a touch light: CET1 was 13.9%,
20bp below consensus, although HSBC announced another up to $1 bn buyback and maintained its $0.10
interim dividend. Management also upgraded the expected Basel 3.1 benefit to 40-50bp. HSBC retained FY26
guidance and its targets through 2028. Jason expects investor focus to centre on NII momentum, the trajectory
of Wealth revenues and costs, and the scope for further capital returns. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
RBNZ Pathway In Focus – Front-end Kiwi is the topic of the week: the cash rate stands at 2.75% and the
RBNZ is all but certain to hike on Wednesday. However, the topic in focus is what comes after: will the RBNZ
hike to around the neutral rate (3-3.5%) or will it have to hike into restrictive territory? The negative output gap,
soft domestic price pressures, softening weekly activity, and no meaningful AI capex suggests terminal will be
around neutral. But if Labour wins the Nov. 7 elections (polls suggest the race is too close to call), does the
RBNZ make a quick hawkish shift in the face of possible fiscal dominance? Or does Labour and National’s
shared aim of a fiscal surplus by 2028 mean little impact at the aggregate level? Historically, 1y1y tends to
peak somewhere between nine months and three months before the last hike. For now, I expect RBNZ
terminal around neutral and like holding received 1y1y but positioning, the election/RBNZ, and trade-weighted
NZ dollar are meaningful risks. See Macro ANZ. (William Borgerhoff Mulder, Rates Derivatives Trading)

Chile: Weak Growth Keeps Easing Debate Alive – Latam Economics: The Central Bank of Chile (BCCh) is
likely to hold rates at 4.50% on Tuesday, but the balance of risks is becoming increasingly complicated. Higher
oil prices and potential El Niño effects present upside inflation risks, while softer activity and labour-market
conditions pull policy in the opposite direction. UBS Strategy sees a high hurdle for a hawkish pivot despite
higher US rates, making the tone around future easing the key market focus. Thursday’s unemployment data

weakness. A more explicit acknowledgement that easing could resume would be dovish for Chilean rates and
potentially weigh on CLP, while a continuing emphasis on inflation risks would argue for a longer 4.50% hold.

China's Industrial Profits Jump – China’s industrial profits rose 14.4% y/y in September, accelerating from
4.2% in August to the strongest pace since June, while profits increased 15.5% in the first nine months.
Computer, communication and electronic equipment manufacturing was a key contributor in
January-September, with profits surging 103.8%. Export-oriented firms face greater external uncertainty, while
subdued consumption keeps pressuring businesses reliant on domestic demand. The data covers industrial
firms with annual revenue of at least RMB20 mn from their main operations. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

broader market sentiment, while EU did not move in line with the previous Friday and showed lower beta. The
auction may have contributed, with bonds remaining mid-market offered afterwards on screen levels. Auction
take-down was relatively decent, with somewhat longer tails and only €10–20 mn allocated around mid-levels;
average overbidding remained broadly in line. EU and the broader SSA sector could see catch-up. French
agencies tightened only around 0.5–1bp versus EGBs. French covered bonds tightened with a lag, while other
names moved only minimally despite improved covered-bond sentiment across jurisdictions. OATs are
widening slightly in the London morning. (Antonin Sahm, Rates Trading)
EUR Vol Softens As Skew Rotation Continues - EUR volatility softened after some weeks of one-directional
client buying following the OAT widening. Front-end gamma traded heavy as that flow reversed. Skew rotation
remains a key theme after developing over roughly the past month, with the underlying correlation between
rates and implieds changing as OAT spreads widened and RX richened. Hence, vol has been bid on the rally
as part of the broader unstable conditions in Europe. (Jaime Turon, Rates Options Trading)
