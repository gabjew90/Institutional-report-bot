As the Federal Reserve shifts toward tightening monetary policy, investors are 
increasingly focused on how higher rates will reshape the North American equity 
landscape. Leveraging in-depth analyst insights, this report delivers a cross-sector 
thematic analysis, highlights industries, and identifies top actionable stock ideas 
best positioned to benefit—or face headwinds—from anticipated rate increases. 
Explore these perspectives and more in the following pages.
US Economics and Rates View: Our economists expect the fed funds rate to go 
from 4.00% to 4.25% in December and then stay on hold thereafter (see Report). 
The 10-year yield is hovering around the near-term expectation of ~5.00% as yields 
form new ranges at these higher levels (see Report).
Equity Strategy View: While higher-for-longer rates have broad implications for 
consumer affordability, housing, capital spending and the business cycle, we do not 
think the current rates backdrop should derail equities given the strong earnings 
anchor (see Rates Report). A shallow hiking cycle, representing a reversal of last 
year’s insurance cuts, should be manageable for equities, although a broader cycle 
of four to five hikes would likely create more significant downside and is not our 
base case. Based on roughly 80 years of analysis, the relationship between the 10-
year yield and S&P 500 multiples follows an inverted “U.” Stronger earnings 
growth allows equities to absorb higher yields before multiples begin to de-rate. If 
current earnings projections are realized, history suggests that equities should be 
able to withstand a 10-year yield closer to 5.5-6.0%, especially if AI-led 
productivity growth supports a higher-multiple environment. The durability of 
earnings growth will therefore remain the focal point for equity investors. This 
report introduces 3 new thematic baskets offering exposure to higher rates 
(JPRHIRTB <Index>), lower rates (JPRLORTB <Index>) and opportunities 
with limited sensitivity to the direction of rates (JPRAGRTB <Index>).
•
The first-order impact of higher rates on corporate interest expense 
should be gradual because debt is predominantly fixed and long-dated, while 
stronger Financials profitability and higher returns on substantial cash 
balances provide near-term offsets. Although higher rates could have negative 
second-order effects on the consumer and AI capital spending, healthy 
corporate balance sheets, elevated margins and ample household liquidity 
should limit the sensitivity, with greater pressure concentrated among lower-
income consumers, smaller companies, highly leveraged businesses and 
capital-intensive industries outside the AI buildout. 
•
Sector leadership will depend on the shape of the curve. Bear steepeners 
have historically favored Cyclicals such as Energy and Financials, while bear 
flatteners have favored Technology. Bond proxies and long-duration sectors 
outside Technology remain the most negatively exposed. Our base case of a 
shallow hiking cycle therefore keeps us in Growth and Quality Growth, with 
large-caps favored over smaller companies. A broader hiking cycle would 
increasingly support a rotation into Low Volatility. The principal risks are that

Beneficiaries of Higher Rates (JPRHIRTB <Index).Based on equity analyst input, this 
basket contains companies positioned to benefit from higher rates through (1) asset-sensitive 
lending, investment portfolios or client-fund balances that generate higher interest income; 
(2) strong balance sheets and self-funded growth that limit financing pressure; or (3) 
defensive demand and differentiated growth that support relative resilience. Banks, Specialty 
Finance and Insurance anchor the income-sensitive exposure, complemented by Payroll 
Processing, HSA Administration and selected Managed Care businesses. Cash-rich 
Restaurants and Media add financial flexibility, while selected Integrated Oils and 
Exploration and Production companies contribute balance-sheet strength and flight-to-
quality resilience. Auto-Parts Retailers and Waste Services provide defensive demand, while 
selected Semiconductors, Automotive Power Suppliers and Renewable Infrastructure 
businesses contribute mission-critical spending or market-share opportunities. 
Beneficiaries of Lower Rates (JPRLORTB <Index>). Based on equity analyst input, this 
basket contains companies positioned to benefit from lower interest rates through (1) 
variable-rate debt or refinancing needs that create scope for interest-expense relief; (2) 
exposure to financing-sensitive demand and capex; or (3) capital-intensive growth and long-
duration valuations that are pressured by elevated funding costs. Housing, Construction 
Materials, Coatings and Housing-Related Retail capture potential demand recovery, 
alongside Mortgage Lending, Credit Data and Real Estate Platforms. Autos, Boating and 
Timeshare add affordability sensitivity. REITs, leveraged Healthcare Providers and 
Commercial Mortgage REITs provide exposure to financing relief. Selected Utilities add 
relief from holding-company debt refinancing pressures, while AI Infrastructure and Clean 
Energy contribute funding and valuation exposure, and AI Infrastructure and Clean Energy 
add funding and valuation exposure. 
Agnostic Beneficiaries of Rates (JPRAGRTB <Index>). Based on equity analyst input, 
this basket contains relatively rate-insensitive companies with (1) recurring or non-
discretionary demand; (2) manageable leverage, fixed-rate debt or limited external funding 
needs; or (3) company-specific growth drivers and offsetting rate effects that reduce 
dependence on the rate path. Essential Services, Consumables Retail and Large-Cap 
Pharmaceuticals establish the defensive foundation. Subscription Entertainment, Video 
Gaming and Payments add recurring engagement or durable cash flows. Cybersecurity and 
Public-Sector Software contribute secular demand and mission-critical workflows, while 
established Data-Center Operators offer pricing power and funding access. Selected 
Industrials, Automotive Platforms and Restaurants round out the exposure through later-
cycle resilience, balanced rate effects or financing insulation.

J P M O R G A N
Over the past year, market expectations have pivoted from rate cuts to hikes, resulting in 
higher borrowing costs (see Report). In the process, long-dated Treasuries are down sharply 
-7% and forward PE has compressed by ~3x. However, equities are still up +12% YTD on 
much stronger than expected earnings growth (28% and 22% for 2026 and 2027, respective-
ly). US 30yr bond yield has risen to ~5.35%, near the highs in ~20 years. A confluence of 
factors has caused the back-end of the yield curve to sell-off, including repricing of Central 
Bank policy, sticky inflation (ongoing oil and trade shocks), rising fiscal deficits globally, 
and possible crowding out effects with signs of foreign investors shifting in favor of long-
term corporate debt over Treasuries (LTM $403B vs. ~$397B). While “high(er)-for-longer” 
rates have broad implications for consumer affordability, housing, capex spending, and the 
business cycle broadly, we discuss the impact to equity fundamentals, valuations, and sector 
/ thematic positioning. This report introduces 3 new thematic baskets offering exposure 
to higher rates (JPRHIRTB <Index>), lower rates (JPRLORTB <Index>) and opportuni-
ties with limited sensitivity to the direction of rates (JPRAGRTB <Index>).
The market is currently pricing in two hikes by April, with the Fed expected to hike once 
more this year. A shallow hiking cycle (i.e., a reversal of last year’s insurance cuts) should 
be manageable for equities. If inflation reaccelerates and the forward curve starts to price 
in a broader hiking cycle (i.e., 4-5 hikes), equities will likely face more significant downside, 
although this is not our base case. The bigger question, however, is what happens to long-
term rates and how much further the back-end could sell-off. Based on ~80 years of histori-
cal analysis, there is an inverted "U" relationship between the 10yr bond yield and 
S&P 500 multiples. The relationship is positive up until an inflection point, which is a func-
tion of the earnings growth backdrop. During periods of above-trend EPS growth (~15% 
y/y), 10yr yields can continue to rise to ~5% before the equity multiple starts to de-rate. 
Currently, forward consensus EPS growth is calling for 20%+ and the S&P 500 is trading 
at ~18x on 2027 EPS. If these strong growth projections are realized, history suggests 
that the multiple not only has room to re-rate higher but that equities should be able 
to withstand a 10yr yield level closer to ~5.5-6.0%. While our analysis indicates that the 
current rates backdrop should not derail equities given the strong earnings anchor, we 
believe the primary risk remains centered around further geopolitical escalation in the Mid-
dle East (see Equity vs. Oil Price Sensitivity).
The durability of earnings growth will remain the focal point for equity investors. First-
order effects from higher rates on fundamentals (i.e. interest expense) are expected to be 
gradual due to predominantly fixed and long-dated debt issued by corporates. In the short 
term, this is more than offset by rising profitability for Financials and a higher return on cash 
balances (still at a lofty ~$2.4T). While there could be a negative second order effect on the 
AI capex cycle and the consumer, the sensitivity should be low given corporates are starting 
from very healthy balance sheets and sit on much higher margins than historically, while 
households (excluding low-end) continue to sit on ample liquidity and record wealth. Rising 
rates could further drive a spending gap between the upper and lower income cohorts, but 
it is worth noting that an increasing percentage of consumption is driven by the upper seg-
ments.

earned on HSA cash deposits and other client-held funds placed with depository partners, 
and has exhibited outsized growth amid higher rates and increased Enhanced Rate penetra-
tion. HQY is also working to reduce rate-driven volatility through greater Enhanced Rate 
penetration and the use of forward contracts to lock in higher five-year Treasury rates. For 
context, HSA cash placements have an average duration of ~3 years for cash placed with 
FDIC partners and ~5 years for cash placed in the Enhanced Rates product.
