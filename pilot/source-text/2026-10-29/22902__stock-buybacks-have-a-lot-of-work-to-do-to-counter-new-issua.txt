 
Stock Buybacks Have A Lot Of Work To Do To 
Counter New Issuance 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, OCT 27, 2026 - 04:45 PM 
Authored by Simon White, Bloomberg macro strategist, 
Meta’s new stock offering along with Anthropic’s upcoming IPO will add to the surfeit of supply 
of equity, posing a structural headwind for the stock market. 
Net equity supply in the second quarter was the highest on record, coming in at over $150 
billion. 
 
In almost every quarter back to the late 1990s, net equity supply has been negative. Typically 
M&A and especially buybacks have more than outweighed new supply from secondary 
issuance and IPOs, but that has been flipped on its head this year. 
The hyperscalers’ perceived existential need for capital to build out compute capacity has led 
them to increasingly reach for equity financing in addition to debt issuance, especially as the 
latter starts to become more expensive. 
 

 
Furthermore, unlisted firms now desire access to funding from public markets, with Anthropic’s 
IPO set to eclipse the previous record sized initial offering from SpaceX in the summer. 
Buybacks are ongoing. If firms are still free-cash-flow positive after the capex uplift, they will 
continue to repurchase their stock.  
But in the aggregate for S&P 500 firms, FCF is set to fall given the enormous size of capex. 
That may interfere with buyback plans, in which case the headwind from positive equity supply 
would strengthen.
