TSLA/SPCX M&A Considerations - the base driver for TSLA upside
At this point we believe it is clear that SPCX (covered by Anthony Valentini) and Tesla will pursue
a merger. On the SPCX earnings call last Tuesday, CEO Elon Musk disclosed that SPCX and TSLA
had formed special committees to discuss a merger, with Musk voicing support for the merger
noting there was "a lot of merit to combining the companies."
We believe the anticipated merger is now a key part of the Tesla bull case, as it provides upside
potential to the stock (via a premium to the stock's trading price at the time of announcement),
while also limiting downside potential on the stock as investors will likely be hesitant to short
the stock ahead of a potential merger announcement. 
We discussed the potential TSLA/SPCX merger in detail in our recent note Answering the FAQs
about a TSLA/SPCX merger, and we highlight several key considerations.
• Timing: Last Wednesday it was reported that Musk sought to announce a merger by the first
half of 2027 so that it would close during the Trump administration.1  We see this target as
reasonable, as the broader investor view is that a deal would be preferable under the current
administration, given the more supportive M&A backdrop and Musk's relationship with the
administration. The exact timing is unclear - we could see a scenario where a deal is
announced in the coming months to allow Elon to advocate for a combination at Tesla's AGM
(Dec 3), or an announcement could be some time in 1H27.
• Rationale: A merger would likely be pitched as an AI vertical integration play, with Musk
stating on the SPCX call last week that there was "a lot to be gained from cooperating on
developing super intelligence software, chip design, the Terafab, obviously robotics..." While
we suspect there could be some financial synergies, the true value would likely be in vertical
integration. 
We however view Musk's desire to consolidate control as the crucial element. Musk has long
cited a desire to own ~25% of Tesla (vs current ~20%), with that ownership threshold giving
him comfort that he would be "influential" enough within the company to pursue AI/robotics.
While the 25% threshold could be reached by achieving the "Mars-shot milestones" within his
2025 comp plan, it would also be well exceeded by a SPCX acquisition of TSLA. 
Musk owns ~48% of SpaceX, more than double his Tesla ownership. Furthermore, Musk has
~82% voting power of SPCX due to his ownership of SPCX Class B shares (10x voting power of
Class A). If SPCX were to acquire Tesla, Musk would likely maintain over 50% of the voting
power of the combined company (details below). While Musk has not stated his desired
ownership percentage of SPCX, we believe that over 50% voting power of the combined
company would satisfy his stated control objective (well over the 25% TSLA target).
• Valuation: Using our TSLA PT of ~$450, it would imply a ~20% premium to the current ~$372/
share stock price. Using the current SPCX share price of ~$147/share (~$2.0tn market cap),
acquiring TSLA at ~$450/share (~$1.8tn) would require SPCX to offer ~3.1x SPCX shares per
TSLA share. 
Assuming an acquisition with valuation of $2.0tn/$1.8tn for SPCX/TSLA, Musk would have
~53% of the total voting power post combination

Core business outlook improved
Alongside the case for TSLA stock to be supported by a SPCX combination, we believe it is also
supported by favorable traction on TSLA's core efforts - both on the current business, and also

Our key takeaway from 3Q EPS was that the outlooks on the core Tesla businesses (i.e.
Automotive, Energy) were incrementally more positive, with both appearing to track positively
into '27. 
Auto volumes returning to growth
Vehicle delivery volumes have been a positive surprise thus far in '26, with Tesla putting up
significant volume beats in the past two quarters and '26 tracking to record volumes following
two consecutive years of volume declines. Volume estimates have seen slightly positive
revisions as of late, with '26 up to 1.80mn (from ~1.65mn ahead of 2Q deliveries), '27 up to
1.92mn (from ~1.82mn ahead of  2Q deliveries), and '28 up to 2.09mn (from ~1.98mn ahead of 
2Q deliveries); our estimates are modestly ahead of consensus. Tesla ended 3Q with its largest
backlog of passenger vehicles in four years, and with mgmt working to expand production
capacity (added net ~175k units of reported capacity YTD'26), we believe out-year volumes
could see continued positive revisions. Auto margins have also been stronger y/y, with our '26
estimate of 17.6% up ~230bps  y/y. 
Strength in the auto business is likely driven by both rising FSD take rates and the growth of
exports from Giga Shanghai.
• FSD: Active FSD subscriptions have grown nearly ~60% y/y in YTD'26 (vs ending 4Q25), driven
by robust take rates in NA. In 3Q FSD had over 75% penetration on NA deliveries, up ~20pts
q/q from the record high penetration in 2Q. Mgmt noted FSD continues to be a significant
demand driver in the US, with customers buying the vehicles specifically for their FSD
capabilities, a reminder that FSD is now a key value proposition of Tesla vehicles for
consumers.
We suspect FSD has bolstered NA volumes, while also benefiting margins as FSD revenue
flows through at near 100% margins.
And in Europe, with a forthcoming vote on regulatory approval of FSD (see here), we believe
this could support volume upside within Europe as well
• Shanghai Exports: Growth in China exports has been a key tailwind in '26, with Tesla
reporting record deliveries in a number of RoW markets in 3Q (i.e. Australia, Japan), and
China exports now account for ~25-30% of Tesla's global volumes. We assume China exports
are accretive to margins, as mgmt has noted regional mix tailwinds from APAC sales (which
are supplied by China exports)
Longer term, Elon Musk issued a 5mn volume target for 2030, well ahead of cons. 2.7mn units,
though our estimate of 2.6mn units is well below this lofty target.

as Elon famously stated that Tesla was considered to be taken out for $420 back in 2018 (since
then the stock has split twice). He has also embedded "420" in other parts of his business,
including his acquisition of Twitter (now X) for $54.20/share, and initially pricing Tesla Robotaxi
fares for $4.20 per ride, with other references as well.
We've also seen other values ranging to north of $500. These values naturally reflect the view
that some sort of a control premium would be needed to entice Tesla shareholders. Recently,
investor Brad Gerstner of Altimeter cited the potential for Tesla to be between $420-5502 .
We believe a value of $450 is consistent with Tesla's trading history, and would enable the claim
of a bid price near TSLA's all-time high. In the past two years, TSLA's stock price has averaged
~$365. Yet we've seen stretches of time where TSLA traded at $450 or above - in Dec'24/Jan'25
(when TSLA was boosted by a post election rally), and various parts of 4Q25 (when TSLA was
boosted by the AI trade + comp package approval + strong FSD data). TSLA's all-time high was
just under $499 on Dec 22, 2025.
Moreover, with TSLA expected to be acquired with SPCX stock, TSLA's price naturally should also
reflect a view on where SPCX will trade. The Bloomberg consensus PT of $247 for SPCX implies
67% upside to its current price, while the SPCX PT of our colleague Anthony Valentini of $300
implies over 100% upside. Of course, there are a number of factors that could impact how SPCX
trades over the coming year, but if upside materializes, this too should imply upside for TSLA
stock.
Conversely, we also appreciate that with timing of deal announcement / deal close somewhat
uncertain, and with some level of risk that a deal doesn't close (albeit which the market likely
sees as lower risk), a view of TSLA's price should reflect this dynamic. 
We upgrade Tesla to Overweight (from Equal Weight) and increase our PT to $450 (from $370),
underpinned by our 2030 probability-weighted scenario analysis. This process uses a 4%
discount rate over 4 years (unchanged) and a 95x base case EPS multiple (prior 85x) to arrive at
our PT.

Evaluating our upside and downside cases
We also assume upside and downside cases of $550 and $250 respectively.

Valuation Methodology: We value TSLA using a probability-weighted scenario analysis of 2030, reflecting the potential binary outcomes for the
stock. We forecast a model case, and then flex the results into bull/bear and uberbull/uberbear scenarios, each of which generating an independent
earnings estimate. From there we assign an EPS multiple and discount rate, deriving a share price for each scenario. Finally, we assess the probability
of each earnings / share price scenario under a base case, upside case, and downside case, generating a probability-weighted average target price
under each case. This process, using a 4% discount rate (over 4 years) and a 95x base case EPS multiple, generates our Overweight rating and $450
price target. We set our target multiples with reference to peer levels, adjusted for differing levels of profitability and growth.
