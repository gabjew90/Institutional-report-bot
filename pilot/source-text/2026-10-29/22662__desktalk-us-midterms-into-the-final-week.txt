DeskTalk - US Midterms: Into the final week
Markets
Varshitha Karamsetty
20 hours ago
US midterms are now just one week away. Navigating the election backdrop may prove challenging amid a crowded
macro calendar this week, though the consensus view remains that knee-jerk market moves will be avoided in the
immediate aftermath. Polling remains tight in Senate battlegrounds. While a Dem house is largely expected, margin of
victory is important. 
What's changed since our last update. Arguably very little. FOMC, other major central bank decisions, earnings, and
fiscal risks have largely overshadowed midterms as an event risk. Nate Silver sees a 75% chance of a Democratic Senate
win, which is more bullish than prediction markets. However, individual Senate battleground polling remains extremely
tight, which Nate also highlights. The bar remains low for the Democrats to retake the House, with the number of toss-up
races narrowing to just 17 races (~4% of all House seats) according to Cook Political Report. 
Bottom line. While prediction markets suggest a Democratic sweep is increasingly priced, we continue to view the
Senate as a genuine toss-up. Positioning largely reflects other macro risks with investors adding bearish positions to US
equities, USD remains net long though risks of outflows after last week's leveraged selling. For treasuries, in the very
near-term, markets have flipped tactically long into FOMC, with refunding taking more focus on Nov 4. 
Global Macro Strategy finds that 1d price action post midterm elections has negative follow-through the following days.
Should we see pullbacks in markets following a Dem sweep, that would offer a buy the dip opportunity, especially for
SPX and DXY. USTs are an exception with t+1 price action generally positive or flat post midterms, and the curve
flattening. 
Keep an eye on early voting. We've already seen 32m+ votes cast, with registered Democrats accounting for 45% of
early votes in states which report party registration data (vs Republicans at 39%). Most states can pre-process mail
ballots, which should support timely results on election night. However, a handful of states, including Pennsylvania,
Wisconsin and New Hampshire, may take longer to report if races are tight.
On election night, early results from North Carolina, Kansas and especially Texas could provide important clues. A
Democratic win in Texas would likely accelerate pricing of a broader Democratic sweep scenario during the Tokyo
session.
