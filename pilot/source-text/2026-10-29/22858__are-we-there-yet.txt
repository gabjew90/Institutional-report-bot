Enough puke?
The SOX has now managed two consecutive up days, and Asia is on the party. After weeks of tech pain,
the obvious question is whether the worst is behind us. Positioning has been flushed in some places,
but remains uncomfortably crowded in others. Six charts on where we stand.
Momentum massacre
This is not your ordinary momentum unwind. Goldman Prime data show fundamental long/short
momentum exposure collapsing from nearly 70% at the peak to around 15%. From crowded to
deserted in a matter of months.
GS PB
Asia net leverage
Asian fundamental long/short managers have slashed net leverage by 16.9 percentage points to 48%,
the 21st percentile over the past year. A lot of risk has already left the building.

GS Prime
Semis: The pain trade
King Callahan calls this "one of the more dynamic & painful stretches" in semis he can remember.
The paradox: earnings and capex estimates keep rising, valuations are getting cheaper, and
positioning is cleaner. Yet investors remain reluctant to step back in.
"While positioning is cleaner (though, I do not get the sense sentiment has turned outright ‘bearish’ out
there), fundamentals remain strong (capex / EPS ests continue to go up) and valuations are attractive
(SOX now trades at a ~1x discount to its 15 year avg), there has been plenty of apprehension about
‘stepping back in’ to the capex / supply chain trade – at least until the market appears ready to reward
companies for revisions (.. not just compress multiples on ‘peak’ concerns)" (Callahan)

The new haves and have-nots
The divergence is getting interesting. The S&P 500 excluding AI is retreating after testing its highs,
while the Nasdaq 100 is bouncing from the bottom of its summer range. Yesterday's winners and losers
are no longer quite so obvious.
