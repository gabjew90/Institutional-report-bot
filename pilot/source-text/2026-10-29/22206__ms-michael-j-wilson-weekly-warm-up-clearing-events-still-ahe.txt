Equity markets have been adjusting to the mid-cycle transition 
(peak rate of change in growth) since June.  This has been 
exacerbated by  slowing growth in the Fed's balance sheet, 
capital scarcity and the midterms. Several  clearing events lie 
ahead that should mark the end of the correction.  
• Mid-cycle transition well advanced at the style and factor level. Our mid-
cycle transition that began in June with the peak rate of change in growth 
and more hawkish policy has been priced at the stock level for the most part. 
This transition has been seen in the underperformance of early cycle leaders 
that tend to have higher operating and financial leverage. As a result, the 
quality factor has consistently led during this shift and is now breaking out, 
as shown in last week's note. While we expect quality to continue to perform 
well, our call is now well understood and appreciated via positioning. We 
have argued the transition typically ends with the index (S&P 500) 
converging with weaker breadth under the surface. During this period, the 
quality factor can still hold up even if the index trades down towards our 
tactical downside target (7100-7200, a slight break of the 200-day moving 
average). Breadth has started to improve across the S&P from narrow levels 
which lends itself to our initial thinking that index and breadth would "meet 
in the middle" as the mid-cycle transition is digested. Our year-end base case 
remains 8000 for the S&P 500.
• We see 3 clearing events ahead that are likely to play a role in ending this 
correction.  1) AI-related investor concerns around the supply and cost of 
capital, safety and NIMBYism.  These should all come to a head over the next 
3-4 weeks but may present a headwind for the market until resolved. 2) 
Midterm elections are typically a market headwind initially and usually then 
lead to very strong 6-12 month returns post the event. The path between 
here and there is likely a messy one and may linger a few days or weeks after 
election  day with the administration hinting at a potentially more aggressive 
military stance once the election is over.  3) Financial conditions have 
tightened significantly over the past several months with the Fed beginning 
to hike rates with limited visibility on how many hikes are likely to follow, a 
reduction in the growth of the Fed's balance sheet and higher energy prices.  
This tightening is weighing on risk assets and will likely linger into November 
and/or until market developments lead the Fed to pivot. 
• Of all the concerns, we think liquidity presents the biggest risk and 
catalyst for a final low.  Warsh came into the job with an emboldened 
commitment to get inflation back to 2%, a target he said the Fed failed to 
achieve for almost 5 years. Since then, the Fed has only hiked rates one time, 
raising questions about its intent and strategy to get inflation back to target.

Clearing Events  Still Ahead
The equity markets have been correcting since June. We identified this correction as a mid-
cycle transition that coincided with the initial peak rate of change in earnings revision 
breadth that arrived after a recessionary low—in this case, the Rolling Recession trough 
which was reached near Liberation Day. Early cycle stocks (semis, financials, consumer 
cyclicals) tend to underperform during this transition as the market moves toward higher 
quality factors. Mid-cycle transition winners include more asset-light, services-oriented 
stocks with lower operating and financial leverage—i.e., quality.
Over the past month, we have been wrestling with the wide divergence between the poor 
breadth of the market and the resilience of the S&P 500 Index ( Exhibit 1 ). This too is 
typical during mid-cycle transitions when the market rotates toward quality. After all, 
there is no higher quality large cap index in the world than the S&P 500, so it makes sense 
for investors to crowd into the S&P 500 during this transition.
Exhibit 1:
The S&P 500 and breadth measures are starting to converge by meeting in 
the middle, as we suggested a few weeks ago. This is a sign we are getting closer to the 
end of the correction. 
Source: Bloomberg, Morgan Stanley Research 
In addition to a peaking rate of change in earnings revisions, we also typically witness a 
Fed pivoting toward a more hawkish stance as growth runs too hot and inflation shows 
up. While others have focused on the war in Iran and the spike in energy prices as the 
primary reasons for inflation and higher back-end rates, we have argued it's been mostly 
about stronger-than-expected nominal GDP growth dragging both the front-end pricing 
and back-end rates higher ( Exhibit 2 ). Warsh's desire to let markets trade to fair value 
also inspired traders to embrace this theme. This was core to our "run it hot" thesis from 
the summer of 2025, which inevitably reached a point where the Fed had to react. We 
reached that point in June, and the Fed has chosen to let the market do most of the work 
via back-end rates and fed funds re-pricing. Meanwhile, they have slowed the rate of 
balance sheet expansion by about half ( Exhibit 3 ), which helps to explain why the 
rotations have been more violent than normal, in our view. This was further amplified by 
the significant crowding in the price momentum factor levered to early cycle stocks (i.e., 
semis).

We think this also helps to explain some of the market action over the past several 
months. To be more specific, financial conditions have tightened substantially, and Warsh 
seems to be ok with that, including the damage it is causing within both the stock and 
credit markets. Our quality call was fundamentally driven but has been exacerbated by 
tighter liquidity provisions at a time when the demands for liquidity have been rising.

As noted many times in our research, the damage beneath the surface of the market has

media and financials) has been on par with significant drawdowns in the past. That's 
encouraging to us, but it also appears there is still some unfinished business at the index 
level, as noted above, and breadth needs to improve to actual buying rather than just 
short covering as gross exposures are reduced.

AI-related questions. There are several investor concerns lingering over AI-related stocks 
at the moment. First, doubts about the eventual return on capital have arisen again as 
investors began to question the timing and size of the revenue opportunity. Second, there 
are new questions about the cost of capital for certain players in the space and even the 
availability of capital in credit markets to fund the spending, given all of the demands 
from governments and non-AI-related capex. Over the next month, markets may need to 
absorb even more supply. Focus on that supply is normal but will linger until it's 
completed. Credit spreads for lower quality players may stay wider during this time, 
keeping financial conditions tight. The supply is the event, and once it's cleared, this risk 
should subside. Semiconductors and other capex beneficiaries represent the epicenter of 
these funding concerns. After identifying the top for semis in June based on our revisions 
breadth analysis and mid cycle transition thesis, we now believe the group is in the final 
stages of its correction. The charts below suggest we should find support in mid-
November, about 10-15% lower. We think that low will coincide with the funding concerns 
bottoming.

The bigger overhang for the markets related to the midterms is the indication the 
administration has been floating about potentially escalating the war in Iran or perhaps 
some other action they would be willing to take once the election is over. In fact, in our 
recent conversations with clients, this particular concern has moved front and center as a 
risk for markets given its unknown character. Most clients, including ourselves, have little 
ability to predict such things. However, one dynamic we do feel more comfortable with is 
our view  that such surprises have often led to excellent buying opportunities in the short 
term and are exactly the kind of variable that could cause the final capitulation discussed 
earlier. Bottom line, the midterms should be a clearing event given the anxiety around the 
event itself and the possible post-event surprise(s). The historical performance of equity 
markets post-midterms should provide investors with the extra confidence to step in 
should a quick downdraft arise.
Financial conditions and Warsh's resolve to fight inflation. We have already discussed 
how the Fed has been modestly tightening this year with one rate hike and slower growth 
in its balance sheet since Warsh's arrival. These actions, combined with the real economy's 
thirst for capital, have led to a rather sharp tightening in financial conditions that may not 
be fully appreciated. Combined with the sharp rise in energy prices, this tightening is on 
par with what happened in 2018 and almost as severe as the Fed's very deliberate 
tightening in 2022.

whether money is too tight. On that score, gold has been weaker lately, along with crypto,

and the last Fed meeting, at which the Fed raised rates by 25 bps. Looking at Exhibit 12 , 
gold has been a good barometer of how the market thinks about Warsh as Fed chair.

past few weeks, we aren't expecting any major changes in that regard this week. To be

We think the more important development for monetary policy could come through the 
balance sheet and other sources of liquidity provided by both the Fed and Treasury. First, 
on the Fed, we think it's unlikely there will be any meaningful change in balance sheet 
growth without more stress first. In the past, we have tracked funding markets as a key 
barometer to watch, and on that score, things appear to be stable. SOFR spreads and

interbank rates are not behaving in a way that would force intervention. The most recent 
example was last year, when spread widening forced the Fed to first end QT and then 
begin buying assets in December with the Reserve Management Program. We will be 
watching this closely for early signs the Fed is about to add liquidity. This is what 
happened in 2018 as well and why we alluded to that earlier as a potential clearing event 
for this correction—i.e., liquidity conditions may need to get worse before they get better. 
To be clear, we respect the new Chair's resolve to only use the balance sheet as an 
"emergency" policy tool; however, it's hard to define what that means exactly. We think 
funding market stress has triggered action from the Fed in the past, and we think it could 
again under this new leadership. The question is how acute does it have to get before it 
gets better.
Of course, there are other liquidity measures that can be taken by the Treasury. In fact, 
buybacks have been used independently from the Fed as a means of suppressing bond 
volatility more than as a direct attempt to lower rates at the back end. These buybacks 
worked very well in March and April to reduce bond volatility as a means of increasing 
liquidity via higher collateral availability in the lending system. More recently, bond vol has 
been stickier to the upside, perhaps due to the limited size of these buybacks compared to 
March and April—i.e., Treasury may need to increase further. The good news is it has a 
sizable balance in its General Account that could be utilized, if necessary. Meanwhile, we 
expect issuance to continue to be skewed more to the front end as another way to control 
bond volatility and even rate levels at the back end. In short, the Fed and Treasury have 
tools to manage liquidity, which does appear to be tight at the moment. The question is 
what has to shift for them to act more aggressively? In that context, this may be the most 
important clearing event of all. We will be monitoring our proprietary sentiment indicator 
shown above and other indicators below as signs things are getting closer to a place 
where the Fed and Treasury will act.  Stay tuned.

Bottom line: We think the correction that began in June is now in its final stages, with 
much of the damage already done beneath the surface. However, a final convergence of 
index toward weaker, but improving, breadth is possible as several clearing events play

meantime, we continue to favor quality while remaining patient for a better opportunity 
to add risk more aggressively.
