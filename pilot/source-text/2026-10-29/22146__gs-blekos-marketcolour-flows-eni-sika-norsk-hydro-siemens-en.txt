Flow: Our floor has now seen an aggregate of ~$20bn of net TMT selling over the past 2 weeks (Info Tech
+ Comm Services) across both all client types + execution channels. As a reference point, we’re
approaching the same amount of TMT selling we saw during the last AI selling episode in July (-$23bn net
sold over 2 weeks).
HF Colour: US equities were net sold for a 3rd straight week (4 of last 5), driven mainly by long-and-short
sales in Single Stocks which collectively saw the largest de-grossing activity since late July. Industrials
was among the most net sold sectors, with HFs selling the group at the fastest pace in 6+ months. In
contrast, Real Estate and Staples were by far the most net bought sectors this week, which continues to
point to a defensive posture.
HF Performance: US L/S Gross leverage rose +2.2 pts to 216.1% (64th percentile one-year), while US L/S
Net leverage fell -0.3pts to 43.4% (0th percentile five-year). Fundamental LS managers down 1.1%
(alpha: -0.3%) this week and now down 3.8% MTD, giving back a quarter of their 2026 performance this
month. Still up 8.8% YTD (from +17.4% at end of June).
CTA: Our latest model has CTAs long $38.5bn of S&P futures, in the 91st percentile historically,
representing nearly 80% of aggregate net longs across the 21 markets tracked. The S&P is already below
its short-term trend threshold, with the more important medium-term level around ~1.3% below Friday’s
close.
This week: Busy week of both macro and micro with a full slate of central bank decisions (FOMC + BOC
Wed, ECB Thurs, BOJ Fri) and the heaviest week of Q3 earnings (44% of S&P market cap reports; MSFT,
GOOG, NOW, KLAC Wed // AMZN, INTC Thurs).

ECB: We maintain our forecast for one more hike at the December meeting. An additional hike in March
looks possible if energy prices continue to rise (especially for gas in case of a cold winter). But we see a
significant hurdle to speeding up the pace of hikes or raising the deposit rate above 3%.
Norsk Hydro (Down to N / PT NKK79 from NKK 100/ 10% Upside): We lower 2026/27 EBITDA by
-6%/-6% to reflect lower earnings in Aluminium, Metal Markets and Energy and higher costs related to
gas. Our 2028 increase by 4% on cost revisions. Our 12m price target is lowered by 21% to NOK 79/sh
which we base on a 50/50 (from 25/75) blend of 1x NAV and 5.5x EV/EBITDA (down from 6.5x).
ENI (Buy / PT EUR29 / 10% Upside): We make adjustments to our estimates, resulting in EPS changes of
+6.7%/+11.0%/+12.5% for 2026E/27E/28E, incorporating a lower tax rate as well as the higher dividend.
ENI trades at a c.20% premium to the EU Big Oils average on 5.6x 2026E EV/DACF, which we view as
justified by its low-carbon satellite strategy, strong execution and leading exploration capabilities.
Sika (CL BUY / PT CHF225 / 25% Upside): We continue to see upside from any easing in raw material
and energy costs, leaving us +4% above consensus 2028 EBITDA, rising to +7% if raw material costs
were to revert to 2025 levels. With the stock trading on c.23x P/E, still around a 20% discount to its
historical average, we reiterate our Buy rating and see c.+22% upside.
Siemens Energy (CL BUY / PT EUR212 / 80% Upside): We see the Capital Markets Day (Nov 11) as the
next step in the Siemens Energy re-rating story. In this note we detail a checklist of items for the day,
including our thoughts on 2030 targets. We forecast group EBITA to increase c.6x between FY 2025 and
FY 2030, putting our estimate 11% ahead of Bloomberg consensus by FY 2030. This leaves shares trading
on c.5x 2030E EV/EBITDA, with visibility and growth expanding.
