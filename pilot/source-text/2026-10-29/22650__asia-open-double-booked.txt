Asia Open - Double booked
By Preston Pagli
The rebound in tech entered its second day, with gains in semis helping lift headline indices ahead of pivotal earnings
and the FOMC on Wednesday. The rally was supported by lower vol, constructive options activity and continued demand
for growth despite underlying market breadth remaining weak. Outside equities, markets were largely in wait-and-see
mode. UST yields edged higher in a modest bear-flattening move after an average 5y auction while the dollar index
firmed to its strongest level in two weeks. Looking ahead, a packed Asia calendar includes AUD CPI at 00:30 GMT,
shortly after the RBNZ is expected to hike at 01:00 GMT, later on the BoT is likely to remain on hold at 07:00 GMT.

In Focus‌
Equities: US tech extended Monday's rebound, pushing headline indices higher despite still weak breadth and low
participation ahead of hyperscaler earnings this week. Lower vol supported the move thus far, with spot VIX- 5%. Flows on
the cash equity desk were skewed toward Info Tech, Financials, Consumer Disc., Healthcare and Energy. Options flows
remained constructive, with buyers of delta active for a consecutive session amid a combination of hedge unwinds and new
upside positioning. Looking ahead, current FOMC straddles have compressed, and imply moves of 0.65% for SPX and 1.11%
for QQQ. SPX has traded down on 6 straight FOMC days. From a systematic positioning perspective, key CTA levels
remain in focus. 
Rates: The UST curve bear flattened with 2s +2bps on thin volumes as markets away Wednesday's FOMC where our
economists are expecting a hold and little guidance from Warsh. In SOFR there has been a chase of richening at the front
end, with the market now extended longs whites tactically. In contrast, the market is short structurally, and further cheapening
is required before systematics are active. Citi's David Bieber notes longs remain vulnerable into FOMC with recent longs in
SFRM7 in loss below 95.33.
US Treasury Auction Recap: $70 billion of 5-year notes. The auction tailed the 1pm market by 0.4bp to yield 4.845%. The
bid/cover ratio came in at 2.33 which is at the 36th percentile of the past 12 auctions.
FX: The dollar firmed to its strongest level in two weeks ahead of Wednesday's FOMC meeting. EUR was the weakest
performer across G10, notching a fifth consecutive day of losses as France consumer confidence (80) hit its lowest level in 13
years. JPY traded relativity neutral to 157.92 with notable option strikes at 157.85 and 158.00. Cable weakened 0.25% as vol
ticked ahead of the UK budget announcement. Fiscal headroom is expected at £14-15bn from £12bn currently, anything

below £10bn would be very negative for gilts. Our trader believes the RBNZ could lean more neutral/dovish tonight and keeps
risk on the table. Overall interbank FX volumes were approximately 30% below average. Net aggregate flows during that time
frame were shifted towards buying of NZD and ZAR and selling of NOK and PLN.
Commodities: WTI crude fell nearly 2% to $100, consistent with the broader energy sector weakness in equities today. The
US and Russia are discussing a potential return by American companies to the Russian oil market as part of wider talks to
end the war in Ukraine. Precious metals gold and silver those 1% and 0.80% respectively.
Elsewhere‌
BRL normalization continued as positioning cleans up in the wake of Sunday's election. USDBRL trades 5.34 after trading as
high as 5.60 Monday. Our trader saw flows mainly one-way on the LHS today. Mostly from offshore global custodians (acting
on behalf of RM names buying local assets) and a bit more from exporters.
ILS: Exit polls from the Israeli election show the race is likely going to head toward a stalemate with neither party gaining the
support needed for a majority. ILS strengthened moderately to 3.054 on the exit poll. 
Ahead:
Client call tonight at 21:00 ET/01:00 GMT: CITI 30: US Hyperscalers' Earnings Preview - Please join our US
internet/software team as they walk through Citi’s expectations and investor feedback in the US ahead of Hyperscaler
earnings.
AUD: CPI MoM, YoY, Trimmed Mean MoM for September due at 00:30 GMT. Our economists expect headline CPI to rise
0.6% MoM, lifting Q3 inflation to 1.2% with, risks towards 1.3%. Trimmed-mean inflation should accelerate to 1% QoQ (3.6%
YoY), which would be enough to trigger a 25bp hike in the Nov RBA (house view). Bottom line, though, our economist thinks
that Hauser comments last week makes CPI almost irrelevant for the Nov RBA. Details may still determine the degree of
hawkishness. Event jump risk priced at 30bp.
NZD: RBNZ Monetary Policy Review and Official Cash Rate decision at 01:00 GMT. Our economists are now expecting the
RBNZ to hike 25bps to 3.00% despite a softer than expected Q3 CPI print. The revised call reflects three forward-looking
concerns: higher global neutral rates rendering the current OCR slightly accommodative, elevated oil prices sitting $15-20/bbl
above RBNZ forecasts (adding ~0.4pp to Q4 CPI if sustained), and a weaker TWI running below RBNZ projections. 3% will
likely mark terminal rates.
EUR: Germany GfK Consumer Confidence for November at 07:00 GMT. Bloomberg consensus expects confidence to remain
negative at -30.1.
THB: BoT Benchmark Interest Rate decision at 07:00 GMT. Our economists expect the BoT to remain on hold, with the policy
rate unchanged at 1.00%.
