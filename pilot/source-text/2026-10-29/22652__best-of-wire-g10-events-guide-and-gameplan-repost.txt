Best of Wire - G10 events guide and gameplan (repost)
By Varshi Karamsetty, Preston Pagli
72 hours. Five central bank decisions. A pivotal CPI print. A key budget update. We lay out the ultimate playbook
for the week's catalysts, including Citi views, market risks and potential trades. Four central banks (FOMC, ECB, BoC,
BoJ) are expected to stay on hold, while the RBNZ is seen hiking. BoC is the wild card, as market pricing and economist
expectations diverge. Megacap earnings and month-end flows should keep the trading environment extremely volatile.

WEDNESDAY
UK Autumn Budget (12:30 GMT/08:30 ET)
View: Citi Research expects a market-friendly budget that prioritizes fiscal credibility over major tax hikes. Fiscal headroom
should come between £14-15bn, delivered via targeted revenue measures such as closing loopholes on capital gains tax,
raising bank surcharge (from 3 to 4.5%) plus efficiency savings. Gilts will be watching the DMO remit revision, with our
strategists expecting funding to increase by £11bn, but we see +£5bn via gilts (to £251.2bn) and +£6bn met by Treasury bills. 
Risks are neutral, negative longer-term. A reduction of fiscal headroom to £14-15bn from £23.6bn (~£11.5bn due to erosion)
is priced in, but anything below £10bn would be negative. The shift shorter and cushion of Treasury bills may ease near-term
pressure, though it does little to address longer-term challenges, which should keep yields elevated. Larger spending
commitments, including defense, will be delayed, but ultimately prolongs uncertainty. 
Trades: A benign budget sets us up for a modest market response. Nov MPC will remain in price and our STIRT desk think
it's too early to receive Nov/Dec MPC. For GBP, max short positioning has eased amid HF buying, moving their net position
neutral. An in-line budget could drive further inflows and a tailwind for EURGBP downside (where French fiscal strains
persist). 0.8395/0.8410 offers initial resistance, followed by 0.8440/60, which was previous support and now acts as
resistance. Wait for the FOMC to clear before entering in GBPUSD. FTSE 100 could tactically outperform the domestic FTSE
250. ur European CETS Head likes selling UK domestics (CGDOUKDM). They have outperformed markets in the last few
weeks by 6% and will only rise modestly if the budget is "passable". See our Multi-Asset note.
 
Bank of Canada (13:45 GMT/09:45 ET)
View: OIS pricing and economist consensus diverge - while OIS pricing sees a strong probability of a hike at 20bp, our
economists expect a rate hold at 2.25% with Bloomberg economists exactly split between a rate hike/hold (17/35). The bank
has a penchant for surprise, so a 25bp hike cannot entirely be ruled out, especially if its framed as a "risk management" hike,
with officials inclined to wait to see how higher rates impact the economy, before hiking again. Updated MPR should still show
higher headline inflation, with core inflation mostly unchanged at 2.2% for 2027. 
Risks: Of course, the clear upside risk is if the BoC decides to hike versus our expectation of a hold. If they decide to hold,
guidance and communication could lean hawkish, which should keep market pricing sticky at +35bp into year-end (which
includes 20bp for Oct meeting), and a total of 107bp into Sep 2027. Acknowledgment of the softer labor market, excess
supply, and limited inflation pass-through would likely be interpreted as most dovish.

Trades: STIRT prefers tactically being paid in the front meeting, but lighter on risk further out the curve, regardless of whether
the BoC opts for back-to-back tightening. Meanwhile, USDCAD has recently broken above the 1.40 handle driven by
leveraged CAD short covering. It'll be interesting if this level continues to hold even in a BoC hike scenario. 
FOMC (18:00 GMT/14:00 ET)
View: The FOMC is widely expected to leave rates unchanged with just 2bps priced and no SEP update. As a result,
expectations for the meeting itself are relatively low. Our economists expect Warsh to reiterate that inflation is not yet
returning to target quickly enough and that the Fed has removed only a "dose" of restriction. Our house view is for one final
25bp hike in Dec and cuts resuming in mid-2027.
Risks are neutral. No hike, no guidance from Warsh means markets will have limited policy signals. The key question will be
whether Warsh acknowledges tighter financial conditions following the AI-led equity selloff and how he explains the decision
to leave rates unchanged. Conversely, any indication that the Fed is taking signals from markets or monitoring financial
conditions more closely could be interpreted as dovish. Warsh may also be reluctant to characterize financial conditions as
having tightened. 
Trades: Our rates strategists are still receiving Oct OIS (+2.2bp priced), assuming a hike in Dec. Depending on levels out of
the meeting, it's also worth considering a Dec/Jan OIS flattener. FX Strategy desk view any initial USD weakness following an
unchanged decision as an opportunity to buy USD dips into the press conference, as the broader macro backdrop remains
supportive for USD. Event jump priced at 40bp. Further, Thursday's data releases are expected to be positive overall, with our
economists expecting GDP to jump towards 3.1%. In equities, options imply the largest FOMC-related move since mid-2025
(+/- 90bps), but driven largely by megacap earnings after the close. 
THURSDAY
ECB (13:15 GMT/09:15 ET)
View: The ECB is widely expected to hold rates, attention ia instead on whether Lagarde addresses French spread weakness
and the potential use of ECB tools, at her press conference (13:45 GMT). Ultimately, support is likely to remain rhetorical
rather than actionable. A Dec hike remains our base case, but explicit guidance will likely be avoided. No new staff forecasts
expected. 
Risks lie beyond the rate hike itself, notably developments in France, but any signals from Lagarde on a possible early
resignation are also highly relevant. Citi Economics provides details thoughts, and sees veterans de Cos and Knot as the
most likely candidates to take presidency, both are also referenced in the media, so it wouldn't be a complete surprise. 
Trades: Spread volatility is reaching a tipping point which could see markets start to price a greater probability of no hikes,
despite escalating energy prices. Our rates strategists like receiving Dec26 ECB €STR in this scenario. We remain bearish
EUR across various crosses - EURAUD, EURGBP, EURCHF. EURNOK is FX Strategy's preferred vehicle which should
benefit from US/Iran re-escalation, while EURGBP is in focus thanks to the budget (more above). Belgian HICP releases pre-
meeting ahead of regional prints from Fri, where our trading desk sees most upside risks relative to consensus expectations
in Italy. Full HICP preview here.
FRIDAY
BoJ (~03:00 GMT/12:00 JST)
View: BoJ is expected to leave the policy rate unchanged at 1.25% with Takata dissenting (hike) not expected to be a
surprise. Ueda should keep Dec tightening on the table without delivering an overtly hawkish message. Our economists
continue to expect a 25bp hike in December (OIS pricing at 20bp) and maintain a broader path of quarterly hikes toward a
2.0% terminal rate by mid-2027. The characterization of underlying inflation could also advance from "approaching 2%," in the
outlook report. 
Risks are modestly hawkish, especially if the BoJ upgrades its inflation assessment more aggressively or signals growing
confidence in the sustainability of wage-price dynamics. Ueda is unlikely to ignore the recent correction in global equities and
volatility surrounding the AI trade. If he states that the BoJ will avoid a rapid pace of hikes to avoid a significant correction in
asset prices, this could be considered dovish

Trades: A non-event BoJ could see USDJPY retest the 159-160 area, though intervention risks are well known and argue for
staying short USDJPY. 156.05/156.10 marks recent lows. With an implied event move of 52bp, the BoJ is the week's biggest
risk event. Hike expectations look appropriately priced, with our rates strategists continuing to see range-bound trading as the
most likely near-term outcome.

WHAT'S DONE
Australia CPI (00:30 GMT/20:00 ET Tue)
View: Citi Economics expects headline CPI at 0.6% MoM, below consensus, but the focus will be on trimmed mean inflation.
A pickup to around 1.0% QoQ (3.6% YoY) from 0.8% prior would reinforce a Nov RBA hike. A modest downside surprise will
unlikely derail a hike given RBA Hauser's warnings last week that inflation is above target. However, inflation details could
influence the degree of hawkishness by the committee.
Risks are skewed towards higher near-term pricing. OIS markets see just 60% chance of a RBA hike next week, which
should be closer to 75-80%, according to our trader. That said, the bar for markets to price a meaningfully more hawkish path
beyond November remains relatively high. Our local desk saw reasonable two-way flows in the first two meetings ahead of
the print, and the risk reward of outright paid in Nov and Dec RBA have lost value, slightly.

Trades: There is still decent risk reward in the cheaper steps of Nov/Dec and Dec/Feb which should retain some premium if
the downside miss is modest, our traders note. Our preferred FX expression is short EURAUD given FOMC risks skewing the
USD leg. Short AUDUSD makes sense even if Nov RBA is fully priced. AI jitters persist, and depending on how equities/risk
perform in the near-term, 0.7000 is key support. On the flip side, 0.7030-50 marks topside resistance, coinciding with 55 and
100dMAs - a break would be very encouraging for further extension. Event jump risk priced at 25bp. O/n AUDNZD is around
13v, depending on RBNZ, too.
RBNZ (01:00 GMT/21:00 ET Tue)
View: 25bp rate hike expected (Citi e.), and fully priced. 3.00% will likely mark the terminal rate, with our economists warning
high unemployment, slowing GDP as well as external risks such as higher global neutral rates and oil prices should constrain
the bank's ability to hike further. A pre-emptive rate hike in anticipation of stronger Q4 CPI (~0.4pp) makes sense.
Risks are dovish. Markets are prepared off hawkish messaging in tomorrow's statement, but with 3 more hikes priced into
2027 (113bp into Sep according to Bloomberg WIRP), there is plenty of room for disappointment. Event jump risk is high at
52.5bp.
Trades: Our STIRT desk argues that given elevated oil, the scope for RBNZ to materially out-dove current market pricing of
+17.5bps in the Oct-Dec meeting step is limited. While weakening terms of trade and the upcoming New Zealand election
(Nov 7) leave risks skewed lower for NZDUSD, our trader prefers to remain sidelined for now, with 0.5730 seen as key
resistance. In AUDNZD, positioning and cross-interest have moderated, but we remain nimble around current levels, focusing
on the established 1.23-1.25 range. FX Strategy writes that investors may be inclined to by the dip in AUDNZD should AU CPI
disappoint given that the RBA is still expected to hike, with the 1.2275-1.23 area holding as support.
