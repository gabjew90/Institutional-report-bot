Europe Open - Full house of catalysts
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
RBNZ delivered a surprise hold vs market consensus of a 25bp hike, but the 4-3 vote implies that the rate hike has likely
been pushed to Dec. This is our new house view, with OIS pricing still indicating a hefty tightening cycle (+92bp into mid-
2027) but NZDUSD plunged -1.2% overnight. In-line AU CPI solidifies a rate hike next week, Nov OIS pricing rises to
21bp, more modest moves in spot FX. Treasury yields and USD head higher into the FOMC tonight, the former likely
thanks to higher oil prices. WSJ reports that the US told several Gulf countries to prepare for a short, intense military
campaign post midterms. Israel general election counting so far indicates a deadlock scenario, driving USDILS higher. 
A heavy slate of catalysts expected ahead with the UK Autumn budget to be presented from 12:30 GMT. Fiscal
headroom is expected between £14-£15bn with major taxes avoided (capital gains tax, bank surcharge and efficiency
savings likely). This will result in a benign outcome for gilts, eyes on DMO remit revision as well. BoC meeting is a
wildcard with market pricing tilted to a hike but economist consensus (including Citi) expect a pause. FOMC is expected
to hold with Warsh maintaining neutral guidance. Tech megacap earnings after the close are the larger driver for US
equities than FOMC. Strong Q3 results from SK Hynix has driven Kospi higher by 6%, with positive stock sentiment
across Asia. As we go to print, BoT left rates on hold.

DeskTalk - US Midterms: Into the final week
In Focus‌
RBNZ delivered a surprise hold, leaving the OCR unchanged at 2.75% in a split 4-3 vote. All MPC members agreed that
tighter policy is likely required, and therefore the split decision reflects timing rather than direction. Market participants price in
+21bp for Dec, our economists push their 25bp rate hike call to this meeting. Beyond Dec, each meeting still sees 17-18bp of
hikes (92bp cumulative into Jun) which has lots of room to reprice. NZ bonds are unsurprisingly higher while NZDUSD
plunged -1.2% with volumes 4.5x higher than recent averages. 0.5600 is holding as decent support as we go to print. 
AUD Q3 trimmed mean CPI rose by 1% QoQ, in line with Citi forecasts and validates our call for a 25bp hike next week. Our
trimmed mean estimate for Q4 is retained at 0.8%, which RBA officials should highlight and why we see upside risks to the

cash rate. Nov OIS hike pricing moves from +16bp pre-data to +21bp at print, but AUDUSD not getting a boost here. 0.7050
remains strong technical resistance into FOMC tonight, and we warned previously that the bar for RBA to outhawk markets is
high, hence the muted spot reaction. Our trader Pat Hu says going long AUD should be left for another day, potentially add if
we see a move towards 0.6930-50 in AUDUSD and 1.5940-50 in EURAUD. Dual events has driven AUDNZD to new highs,
now trading above 1.25 at print. Our options desk note that RBNZ surprise has impacted expectations on RBA (which is likely
why +25bp isn't fully priced), but markets remain long AUD local short date options and hence AUD front end vols pricing
remain at discount
Rates: Antipodeans mostly took spotlight with slight bearish winds seen in treasuries, US 10s trading at 5.02% amid higher oil
prices too. Our desk saw better selling in the intermediates alongside some interest in 5s/5s. Volumes peaked at 1.2x of
recent averages before tapering to 0.6x as we enter the early London trading session. It's a busy session ahead with FOMC,
BoC and UK budget. Our rates strategists provide three takeaways in their Morning Call - #1 While Chair Warsh is unlikely to
give any guidance, we see a case for more global 2s5s flattening momentum going forward. #2 Ahead of today’s UK Budget,
the gilt market appears primed for a reduction in headroom, higher future borrowing via PSNFL flexibility, a small increase in
the gilt remit albeit with fewer Longs. #3 Today’s auction: €5.5bn of 10yr Bund - see RV within
Equities: Kospi surges 6% overnight, after SK Hynix reports robust Q3 earnings. Rising memory ASPs and increased sales
of high-value products drives profits higher to KRW 83.3tn, with the company also announcing a KRW ~100trn more to
shareholders, including ~KRW 14trn in Q3 dividends and an additional 20tn buyback. India's Nifty lags peers but Jio platform
shares rallied 21% in its trading debut. Nasdaq futures lifting higher by 0.5% at print. Megacap tech including Microsoft and
Alphabet report after the US cash equity close with tonight's straddles at SPX 0.65%, QQQ 1.11%, IWM 1.15%. Event
premium for FOMC has compressed, with SPX implied now inline with prior 8-meeting average (0.67% implied / 0.66%
realized). Note that SPX has traded down on 6 FOMC days in a row.
FX: Interbank volumes have overall been running approximately 13% higher than 30d averages in the Asia session, with
NZDUSD and USDILS volumes significantly higher. Our platform also sees leveraged names buying GBP, AUD (into budget,
post CPI) while selling USD, JPY and EUR overnight. That said, USD saw a steady rally overnight with EURUSD now
attempting to break the 1.11 handle. We stay bearish, but prefer crosses such as EURGBP, EURCHF and EURAUD. 
Commodities: Oil prices lift higher but mixed headlines overnight. WSJ reports the US has told several Gulf countries to be
prepared for a short, intense military campaign after the US midterms, people briefed on the conversations said, but a final
decision by President Trump has not been made. Meanwhile, Trump also said that a meeting would soon be arranged
involving Russian President Putin and Ukrainian President Zelenskyy to end the war between their countries.
 
Stacked deck ahead:
UK Autumn Budget at 12:30 GMT: Citi Research expects fiscal headroom to come between £14-15bn, delivered via targeted
revenue measures such as closing loopholes on capital gains tax, raising bank surcharge (from 3 to 4.5%) plus efficiency
savings. Gilts will be watching the DMO remit revision, with our strategists expecting funding to increase by £11bn, but we see
+£5bn via gilts (to £251.2bn) and +£6bn met by Treasury bills. 
Overall, expect a market-friendly budget barring any surprises (headroom <£10bn would be a negative surprise). This should
be enough to invigorate the GBP bull trade, but we need FOMC to clear before entering into the cable trade. We see
EURGBP downside, and also expect FTSE 100 to tactically outperform the domestic FTSE 250. Our European CETS Head
likes selling UK domestics (CGDOUKDM). They have outperformed markets in the last few weeks by 6% and will only rise
modestly if the budget is "passable". See also our Multi-Asset note.
BoC at 13:45 GMT: OIS pricing and economist consensus diverge - while OIS pricing sees a strong probability of a hike at
20bp, our economists expect a rate hold at 2.25% with Bloomberg economists exactly split between a rate hike/hold (17/35).
The bank has a penchant for surprise, so a 25bp hike cannot entirely be ruled out, but if they hold, guidance and
communication should remain hawkish. STIRT prefers tactically being paid in the front meeting, but are lighter further out
given that market pricing will be entirely dictated by energy prices (+107bp priced into Dec).Meanwhile, USDCAD has recently
broken above the 1.40 handle driven by leveraged CAD short covering. It'll be interesting if this level continues to hold even in
a BoC hike scenario.
FOMC at 18:00 GMT: Wide consensus for a hold with just 2bp priced and no SEP update. No hike, no guidance from Warsh
will leave markets with limited policy signals, the key question is whether he acknowledges tighter financial conditions

following the AI-led equity selloff. Our house view remains for a final 25bp rate hike in Dec, we are received the Oct meeting.
A supportive macro backdrop means that any USD weakness should be faded. Event jump risk priced at 40bp. Equity options
markets imply the argest FOMC-related move since mid-2025 (+/- 90bps for SPX), but largely influenced by megacap
earnings after the close.
Elsewhere‌
ILS: General elections have resulted in deadlock with Israel PM Netanyahu's bloc and opposition led by Eiisenkot are neck
and neck, both on track to secure 51 parliamentary seats. Two Israeli Arab parties are expected to win 18 seats, a surprise
outcome thanks to larger voter turnout at 78%, according to our local desk. Counting continues, but so far with neither major
bloc close to the 61 seats needed for majority, the key question for the opposition will be whether they join forces with the
Israeli Arab parties to begin legislating against Netanyahu, and create a short-term minority government.
Overall, our local desk summarizes these results are market negative, but they do not give Netanyahu enough power to
create a new government and will likely keep us guessing over the next few weeks. USDILS has jumped to 3.07 (+0.80%). It
is very likely that the AI and US equity sentiment would continue to be a leading factor in determining the level of the shekel,
our options trader writes. 
THB: BoT held rates at 1%, in line with expectations.
