USA: FOMC Leaves Fed Funds Rate Unchanged; Cook
and Hammack Dissent Preferring a 25bp Hike
28 October 2026 | 2:35PM EDT | Research | Economics
BOTTOM LINE: The FOMC left the target range for the fed funds rate unchanged at 3.75-
4.00% at its October meeting. Governor Cook and President Hammack dissented from the
Committee’s decision, preferring a 25bp increase in the target range for the fed funds rate.
The post-meeting statement noted that “the accumulation of more information will help the
Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the
Committee’s 2 percent goal,” reiterated that inflation “remains elevated,” and upgraded its
characterization of economic activity to say it is expanding at a “strong” pace (vs. “solid”
previously).
MAIN POINTS:
1. The FOMC left the target range for the fed funds rate unchanged at 3.75-4.00% at its
October meeting, following a 25bp hike at the September meeting. Governor Lisa Cook and
Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, who had also dissented in favor of a 25bp hike at
the July meeting, dissented from the Committee’s decision, preferring a 25bp increase in the
target range for the fed funds rate.
2. The post-meeting statement reiterated that inflation “remains elevated” and noted that “the
accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance
appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal.” The
FOMC also upgraded its characterization of economic activity to say it is “expanding at a
strong pace” (vs. “solid” previously). The rest of the statement was unchanged relative to the
September meeting.
