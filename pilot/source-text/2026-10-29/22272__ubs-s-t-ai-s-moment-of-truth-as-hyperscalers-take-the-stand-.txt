AI's Moment Of Truth As Hyperscalers Take
The Stand
Michael Romano
25-Oct-2026, 20:20 ET (EDT)
AI De-Risking. Unlike the July AI unwind which was fueled by forced selling on the back of ubiquitous
leverage, October’s AI selloff reads more structurally in nature as investors are increasingly questioning
whether monetization/AI demand can keep pace with the size and cost of the infrastructure build given the
explosive repricing in funding costs. This month’s repricing across the AI complex and the rotations have been
equally as violent (and painful) as in July; in many ways it’s been worse given structural concerns around
funding. Meta’s lack of clarity around the path of AI capital intensity did little to ease the market’s growing
concerns around the scale and returns of the infrastructure build, pushing risk & positioning to extremes. To
put the moves in context: 
• Rotations within AI. Over the past three weeks, the move higher in funding costs drove a 17% retracement
in UBS’s AI Winner basket; the funding sensitive neocloud complex was down 2x that (-34%) as investors
rotated up the quality ladder within AI (Hyperscalers -7% over the same period), and shorts pressed low quality
vulnerabilities. 
• Sector/Factor Rotations. UBS’s L/S Momentum Factor underperformed the L/S Quality Factor by ~24%
over the past three weeks as investors rotated into safety within and away from AI. Momo’s +66% peak
outperformance vs. quality has completely unwound. UBS’s AI Winner basket underperformed XLP by 24%
over the same three-week period in October.
• Key Support Levels. Key indices are all trading at key support levels – expect risk to pivot hard this week.
SMH (200d MA, 215), SPY (100d MA, 758), and QQQ (3m low, 700).
• UBS PB Data: Through October, Long exposure was cut from 90%-tile to 15%-tile (5Y) while shorts rose
from the 15%-tile to the 80%-tile. Nets are now at the 25%-tile with grossed above the 90%-tile.
Hyperscaler EPS a Circuit Breaker For The AI Trade: Expect the market to focus on the relationship
between cloud growth and capex.
• If Azure / AWS / Google Cloud remain strong, management continues to describe AI demand as capacity
constrained, and forward capex plans remain intact, that would suggest the underlying AI demand story has
not really changed and the recent move has been more about positioning, funding and concerns around the
return on all this spending.
• The more concerning outcome would be slowing cloud growth alongside another leg higher in capex, which
would start to validate the emerging bear case that infrastructure spending is getting ahead of monetization.
Peak Vulnerability. For Nvidia / Micron and the rest of the AI infrastructure complex, this week is really about
whether the hyperscalers can show that the revenue is arriving fast enough to justify the build. Price action
over the next seven days will likely dictate price action for the next seven weeks. The extreme increase in
shorts leaves the market vulnerable to a squeeze, while breaks of technical supports will shift the market to
broad-based selling vs. rotation.
