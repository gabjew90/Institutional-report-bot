AI's Moment Of Truth As Hyperscalers Take The Stand
Unlike the July AI unwind which was fueled by forced selling on the back of ubiquitous leverage, October’s AI
selloff reads more structurally in nature as investors are increasingly questioning whether monetization/AI
demand can keep pace with the size and cost of the infrastructure build given the explosive repricing in
funding costs.
If Azure / AWS / Google Cloud remain strong, management continues to describe AI demand as capacity
constrained, and forward capex plans remain intact, that would suggest the underlying AI demand story has
not really changed and the recent move has been more about positioning, funding and concerns around the
return on all this spending. The more concerning outcome would be slowing cloud growth alongside another
leg higher in capex, which would start to validate the emerging bear case that infrastructure spending is getting
ahead of monetization.
For Nvidia / Micron and the rest of the AI infrastructure complex, this week is really about whether the
hyperscalers can show that the revenue is arriving fast enough to justify the build. Price action over the next
seven days will likely dictate price action for the next seven weeks. The extreme increase in shorts leaves the
market vulnerable to a squeeze, while breaks of technical supports will shift the market to broad-based selling
vs. rotation. Full note here.
(Michael Romano, Head of Hedge Fund Derivative Sales, Americas)

expected and widespread social protests “could constrain fiscal consolidation efforts”. Moody’s said the Aa3
rating (with a negative outlook) “assumes that a deficit-reducing budget for 2027 will be adopted, while
financing costs and social tensions will abate somewhat in the next few months”. (Matthew Cowley, Macro

US Equities: Long-Duration Capital Vs. Short-Duration Rebalancing - The medium-term bullish view
remains intact despite recent hedge fund and systematic unwinds. Over the past 15 years, long-duration
capital from retail investors, retirement assets, and foreign investors has gained influence over the US equity
trend, while faster systematic, quantitative, and hedge fund strategies increasingly drive short-term volatility.
This barbell market structure can produce sharper, more frequent corrections, but persistent buy-the-dip
demand also supports faster, often V-shaped recoveries. Retirement migration toward more equity-oriented
products and continued foreign allocations provide structural inflows, whereas leveraged and model-driven
strategies amplify the market’s path. Unless labor-market weakness, prolonged inflation, geopolitical shocks,
or fading US exceptionalism materially erode capital persistence, systematic deleveraging is more likely to
create temporary dislocations than a durable trend reversal. Short-duration capital increasingly determines the
path; long-duration capital increasingly determines the destination. (Rebecca Cheong, Derivatives Strategist)
