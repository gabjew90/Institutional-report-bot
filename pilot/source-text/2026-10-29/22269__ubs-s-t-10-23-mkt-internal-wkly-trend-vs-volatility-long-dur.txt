Trend vs Volatility: Long-Duration Capital vs. Short-Duration Rebalancing
Publication Date: October 23, 2026
Overview
My medium-term bullish view remains intact despite the recent Hedge Fund and Systematic unwinds. While
investors can easily become focused on headline news and the short-term impact of deleveraging, I believe
the more important story is the continued evolution of U.S. equity market structure over the past 15 years.
The defining structural change has been the growing dominance of long-duration capital, including
Retail Investors, Retirement Assets, and Foreign Investors, alongside the rapid growth of
short-duration trading capital, including Systematic, Quantitative, and Hedge Fund strategies. The
former increasingly drives market trends, while the latter increasingly drives market volatility. As a result,
corrections have become more frequent and sharper, but recoveries have also become faster and more
persistent.
Consequently, short-term volatility and long-term market direction should be viewed as separate
forces, with the former increasingly driven by fast-money positioning and the latter increasingly
supported by persistent sources of capital. Unless the underlying drivers of capital persistence deteriorate
meaningfully, episodes of systematic deleveraging are more likely to create temporary dislocations than
durable changes to the long-term trend.
Key Investor Trend Observations:
• Retail ownership (direct and retirement-related) has steadily gained market share
• Foreign ownership of U.S. equities has increased consistently over the past 15 years
• Systematic and Quantitative strategies have experienced significant growth and operate with increasingly
shorter trading horizons
• Traditional Pension ownership has declined and has gradually been replaced by Retail-Directed Retirement
Assets
• Hedge Funds and Systematic Strategies remain a relatively small portion of aggregate ownership. While they
have limited influence on long-term market direction, their high turnover, leverage, and model-driven trading
behavior increasingly drives short-term market volatility

2
Key Market Implications:
• Extended and accelerated equity rallies driven by leverage, product innovation, and structural inflows 
• A higher frequency of V-shaped recoveries as systematic deleveraging is increasingly met by persistent
buy-the-dip demand 
• A growing separation between trend and volatility, with long-duration capital driving the former and
short-duration capital driving the latter
• Rising structural equity allocations as retirement assets migrate toward more equity-oriented investment
vehicles 
• More persistent buy-the-dip behavior from Retail Investors and Foreign Investors, supported by recurring
retirement contributions, long investment horizons, and ongoing global asset allocation toward U.S. equities
