Markets: Some geopolitical relief this morning. Feared weekend escalation against Iran didn’t materialize.
Diplomatic discussions continue (slim hope but watching). French protests were more contained than
previous episodes (downgrade avoided). Asia semis largely catching down to where the US closed. Big
surprise is Brazil, where Lula managed to overturn a first round deficit to win the election, albeit with
legal challenges looming. Expect sizable pressure across Brazilian assets and second order exposures
given how far this was from prediction market expectations.
Gross Struggled: Russell +2%/RSP +1.1%, Nasdaq -70bps/SOX -3.2%, VXN elevated as tech implied
reflect left tail fears. Bonds catching a bid on growth concerns as AI capex runs into capital constraints.
Client backdrop remains incredibly tough. US equities sold for a third consecutive week, largest single
stock de-grossing since July. TMT ~$20bn sold over two weeks. Fundamental nets at YTD lows, but gross
still 63rd percentile. Global tech selling -3.3σ over two weeks, aggregate long sales outpacing short covers
3.4:1. Single stocks sold for six consecutive weeks. Performance equally ugly across discretionary L/S,
multi-strats and quants. Nets are low, but gross is what hurt last week (GSPESHRT - most vs least short in
Europe +3sd on 60d lookback).
Hyperscalers: Pace of innovation, applications and use cases keeps me optimistic on demand. But the cost
of capital has objectively repriced, and there isn’t an obvious solve. Hyperscaler and data center credit
spreads have widened across the capital structure, compounded by execution and refinancing risks.
Google, Microsoft and Amazon critical this week. Not simply whether capex intentions hold, but how
backlog converts into cloud revenue and, critically, how the next leg gets financed. Worst case: capex
accelerates, funded increasingly by expensive debt. Arguably worse: capex gets cut because financing isn’t
available. Best case: capex rises alongside accelerating organic cash flow, with a credible path to self
funding through 2027/28. Middle ground: capex flat/up, demand robust, but more equity financing. Bad
for hyperscaler equity, potentially good for credit.
Macro/Central Banks: Busy week. UK Budget risks, particularly potential bank taxes (growing client
interest in shorting UK domestic consumer basket). China policy meetings, US 2yr/5yr/7yr supply, ECB,
BOJ and FOMC. Market expects the Fed to skip. Question is whether Warsh focuses on recent hot
inflation or acknowledges how aggressively financial conditions have tightened. For everyone exposed to
AI/in the nominal growth camp, we’re hoping for the latter.
Risk: Hard to have high conviction and I think most I speak to are playing defense. Gross feels tricky,
performance bleeding across strategies and political events next week make taking a big swing difficult.
Energy remains critical. Still confused by a policy that effectively telegraphed escalation and allowed
markets to pre-price an energy shock. Perhaps the strategy is maximum military pressure ahead of a last

minute grand bargain (talks ongoing today). Positioning, sentiment and performance all severely
constrained…. shorts squeezing, nothing working. Usually darkest before the dawn and arguably it
probably wouldn’t take much to stabilize risk, but we’re lacking a catalyst. Hyperscaler guidance and the
Fed are obvious opportunities, although risks appear two sided on both. Watching credit.
