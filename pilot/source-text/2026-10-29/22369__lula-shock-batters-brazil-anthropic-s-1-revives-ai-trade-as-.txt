Tl;dr: After last week's below-the-surface shitshow in the US, this week started off calmly above 
and below with META (raising cash thru equity, down on day though), ANT (issuing its S-1), & 
SPCX (big debt issue went-off) providing some support for all of AI (Neoclouds surged from an 
ugly open), lifting NDX to outperform (DOW lagged but all majors green) but flows showed sell 
skew with supply in tech. Credit markets rebounded modestly (even hyperscaler CDS and 
data-center debt). Brazil stole (pun intended) the headlines though with stocks, bonds, and BRL 
all cratering on Lula's surprise (delayed) win. US Treasuries were mixed (short-end weak), the 
dollar whipsawed to flat, gold managed small gains as bitcoin drifted lower from Friday. 
 
Last week it was bonds that decoupled; today it is stocks... for now...

Energy 
Oil slowed its recent surge today, but continues to hover near six-month highs as Iran risk really 
hasn’t gone anywhere. 
Oil prices dipped overnight as Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials travel to Iran for more 'talks'. 
Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said his country is ready to back “any understanding” that 
leads to regional unity and isn’t seeking nuclear weapons, according to another state-run media 
outlet. 
That said, it wasn't all rainbows and unicorns as the Saudi coalition said it destroyed three 
drones in Riyadh. Hezbollah is preparing for a new regional war after receiving funds from Iran, 
according to a Reuters report that cited Lebanese security personnel. That followed weekend 
clashes between Yemen-based Iranian-backed Houthis based and Saudi forces, ratcheting up 
risks to energy flows. 
Additionally, Trump said it's "getting to be decision time" on whether he makes a deal with 
Tehran or resumes attacks. 
 

 
“The market is pricing in some risk of escalation after the US midterm elections, when 
Washington may feel less constrained by the immediate political cost of higher oil prices,” said 
Jorge Leon, head of geopolitical analysis at Rystad Energy AS. 
“That risk could keep prices elevated even if flows improve.”

Finally, despite fragility in the AI trade, Bloomberg macro strategist, Michael Ball notes that 
renewed call buying in some of its hardest-hit names offers the Nasdaq a path to stabilize if 
earnings are in line and the Fed strikes the right tone. 
Positive AI earnings guidance now requires many variables to go right simultaneously: compute 
pricing, utilization, equipment lives and financing costs -- all of which face constant technological 
changes. Anthropic’s IPO filing Monday renews scrutiny on all those factors of the AI trade. 
Cheaper tokens ultimately favor users more than the increasingly leveraged infrastructure 
 

 
builders. Said another way, being right about AI adoption no longer guarantees being right about 
who captures the economics. 
Yet recent options flows suggest dip buyers are starting to return to the traditional AI capex 
names. The semi-index has fallen 21% in a month from intraday peak to trough, while Nebius 
Group and Coreweave are down 39% and 43% on the same basis, respectively. Despite that 
damage, traders have been building longer-dated call positions across these names and other 
chip providers. To be sure, these look more like defined-risk bets on a rebound after severe 
drawdowns, helping pull NDX put/call skew off recent highs rather than signaling a broader 
regime change. 
 
That shift is worth watching because a rebound in the weakest AI names would improve the 
index-level backdrop. MenthorQ shows SPX net gamma slightly negative, while Nasdaq 
positioning is considerably more so. While the impact depends on where spot trades, dealer 
hedging will generally amplify moves while the indexes remain skewed toward negative gamma, 
giving volatility sellers less incentive to step in. 
 

 
Getting through the Fed and mega-cap earnings cleanly would lower implied volatility, draw 
volatility sellers back in and pull dealer gamma toward a more stabilizing regime. Add call-driven 
rebounds in AI names and vanna becomes an additional broader tailwind to the Nasdaq 100 
and S&P 500. 
The key area to watch is the 7,690-7,700 zone for the SPX. 
●​ A sustained move above there would materially improve the gamma backdrop. 
●​ Below it, any AI rebounds at the single-stock level still face a mechanically unstable 
index. 
The real tests start Wednesday: the Fed, then the hyperscalers have to justify capex that Meta 
is now funding with equity. And for those who are positioned short gamma, the long-awaited 
squeeze may just have arrived.
