The volatility paradox
Volatility markets are sending mixed signals. Mag 7 options look unusually cheap relative to the
broader tech complex, even as Nasdaq volatility remains historically elevated versus the S&P 500. The
recent compression in tech vol has been extraordinary, but beneath the surface, skew remains well bid
and vol-of-vol is swinging violently. Meanwhile, Goldman sees opportunities to add upside exposure
across AMZN, SPY and Korea. A market where volatility is resetting, but conviction remains far from
settled.
Mag 7 vol looks cheap
Mag 7 implied volatility is trading at a substantial discount to the broader tech complex. The relative
vol ratio sits at just the 15th percentile over the past four years, suggesting Mag 7 options are
unusually cheap versus the rest of tech.
GS
The upside chase is back
Nomura's McElligott highlights aggressive upside call buying across Mag 7 ahead of earnings. AAPL
saw 70k January $380 calls bought, representing $570mn in delta exposure, while GOOGL attracted
$430mn and MSFT $630mn. That's roughly $1.6bn in combined delta exposure across three names.
The upside chase is returning just as Nasdaq volatility is being crushed.
The Mag 7 positioning divide
McElligott highlights an extraordinary positioning divergence in Mag 7. Long/short hedge funds have
pushed their rolling Mag 7 beta toward the 97th percentile historically, while mutual fund beta remains
near the 11th percentile. Hedge funds are heavily exposed to the AI giants, while traditional long-only
managers remain structurally underexposed. Two very different positioning realities beneath the same
market.
Nomura
Down sharply, but still elevated
The NDX/SPX implied vol spread has compressed sharply following last week's blowout. The three-day
compression was the largest in four years, yet the 1-month ATM spread still sits at the 94th percentile.
Tech vol has come down hard, but remains historically expensive relative to the broader market.

AMZN technicals
AMZN has pulled back from all-time highs, but the big-picture uptrend remains firmly intact. The 200-

longer-term bullish structure remains in place.

Two macro upside trades
Goldman likes two upside expressions. In the US, GS favors SPY November 6 $780 calls, positioning for
potential clearing events that could drive equities toward new highs, with volatility expected to remain
supported on a breakout. In Korea, GS likes EWY December 18 $180/$210 call spreads, arguing that
renewed AI momentum could trigger a positioning squeeze after heavy hedge fund selling and sharp
reductions in exposure. This ties directly into our Korea: From panic to FOMO note from this morning.
EWY has also reclaimed its 200-day moving average for the first time since the breakdown, adding
technical confirmation to the positioning squeeze.
