TECH
• Cadence beat and raise - with management raising 2026 guidance and reinforcing the view that AI is
becoming an incremental growth driver rather than a disruption risk. The key positive was evidence that

AI adoption is accelerating usage of core EDA tools, supporting roughly 18% year-on-year organic
recurring revenue growth, while new products such as Maestro AI provide an additional monetisation
opportunity over time. Combined with ongoing strength in IP, hardware emulation and emerging “Physical
AI” opportunities, the results suggest Cadence is extending its position as one of the highest-quality and
most durable growth stories in software, with improving visibility into AI-driven revenue expansion and
limited evidence that open-source AI is impacting demand.
• TSMC (2330 ) — CoWoS price hikes could reach 20% amid severe capacity shortages. Advanced
packaging lead times have reportedly extended from 3–6 months to 6–8 months, with customers
prioritizing guaranteed deliveries over pricing. Industry estimates suggest CoWoS monthly capacity could
exceed 130k wafers in 2026 and target 200k in 2027, alongside continued CoPoS development. (CT)
• TI / PMIC — Analog-chip recovery accelerates, supporting Taiwan PMIC suppliers. Texas Instruments
sees resilient AI data-center demand alongside recovering industrial and automotive orders, with power
efficiency and thermal management driving higher analog content. (CT)
• NVIDIA — Major capacity expansion involving three Taiwan suppliers. NVIDIA identified Unimicron
(3037 TT), KYEC (2449 TT) and ASE(3711 TT) as partners in its advanced-substrate, testing and CPO
expansion. Substrate capacity is targeted to reach 2.5 times its current level over three years, while CPO
optical-engine capacity could expand 30-fold by end-2028. (ED)
• ABF substrates — NVIDIA, Intel and AMDreportedly make substantial capacity prepayments. Industry
reports suggest the three companies committed more than NT$100bn to secure future high-end substrate
supply. (CT)
• NVIDIA (NVDA US) — New Optics-on-Interposer (OOI) technology reshapes advanced packaging.
NVIDIA is developing an architecture that integrates optical engines directly onto the silicon interposer
rather than conventional organic substrates, reducing electrical transmission distance, power consumption
and latency while improving bandwidth density. (ED)
• Apple F4Q26 Preview - We expect in-line results, with iPhone revenue +15% yoy, Services +10% and
gross margins of 47.8%, including a 1pp tariff-refund benefit. Higher iPhone prices and the Duo launch
should offset modest volume declines linked to pricing and the delayed iPhone 18 launch. Double-digit
growth in iCloud+, AppleCare+, subscriptions and advertising should offset slower App Store growth.
Memory/component inflation and supply constraints remain headwinds, but iPhone, Mac and iPad price
increases should increasingly support revenue and partly offset costs into December. FY2027 growth
drivers include full-year pricing benefits, Duo-led replacements, spring 2027 launches of the iPhone 18,
18e and Air 2, and easier comparisons after F2H26 supply constraints; premium positioning, carrier
promotions and Apple Upgrade should support demand. Key debates: whether price/mix can offset unit
and margin pressure, and whether double-digit Services growth can withstand slower App Store trends and
regulatory pressure.
TELCOS

• US Telcos (DT surprised its not down more) Verizon’s CEO’s tone on satellite on the Q3 call became
more defensive between Q2 and Q3. At Q2, the CEO largely dismissed it as a niche rural threat; by Q3,
Verizon was treating SpaceX as a credible potential competitor it needed to prepare for. Its defence has
shifted from physics to cost and time: Verizon argues that a competitive SpaceX terrestrial network would
require 65–80k macro sites, substantial spectrum, radio access infrastructure and backhaul, 4–6 years to
build and an estimated $300–500bn of investment. Per the CEO this would ultimately leave SpaceX with
broadly similar network economics. Verizon has also hardened its stance on offering satellite players an
MVNO agreement: Q2’s “no reason” became “absolutely no benefit whatsoever” in Q3, alongside a
dismissal of the prisoner’s-dilemma argument. US Towers are up again today, while last week’s moves in
US telcos have raised questions about DT’s relative resilience: it is down just 3% over the past week,
versus a 9% decline for TMUS. Plus today Verizon beat on net-add expectations, but with little boost to
service revenue and TMUS had specifically singled out Verizon as a nuisance earlier this quarter….
• Satellites: Europe will maintain plans to reserve at least 70% of its upcoming 2 GHz satellite spectrum
tender for European firms despite US pressure, according to Les Echos. European Commissioner Henna
Virkkunen said the EU would not change its position (finally pushing back). The spectrum is critical for
direct-to-device satellite services, which connect standard mobile phones directly to satellites. The policy
creates a barrier to Starlink and Amazon’s Project Kuiper as they pursue global expansion, escalating
transatlantic technology tensions as Brussels seeks greater strategic autonomy and less reliance on foreign
space infrastructure.
• UK telecoms: Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy is seeking stronger government powers to manage failures
in the highly leveraged telecoms sector, according to the FT and TU. The review follows emergency
approval of BT’s £400m rescue takeover of TalkTalk out of administration to protect broadband services
for 2.5 million customers. Because TalkTalk’s network supports schools, hospitals and emergency
services, disruption was considered a genuine risk to life and public-service continuity. Proposed reforms
include a supplier-of-last-resort framework to protect customers when providers collapse.
