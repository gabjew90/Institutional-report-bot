As we hit the last week of October, markets are continuing to grapple with three major themes: France, Iran 
and AI. Although less relevant for DM investors, Brazil's surprise election result overnight, which could 
lead to a -25% currency adjusted decline in Brazilian equities today if overnight derivative contracts are 
a guide, will also be one to watch. So lots going on and also a busy week ahead around the globe. So for 
those who saw their clocks go back this weekend, I hope you used it for an extra hour of sleep! 
Although several hundred thousand people marched and protested in France over the weekend, the more 
extreme scenes of the previous weekend were absent, suggesting the authorities have regained some control 
of the situation. France also got a boost on Friday night as Moody’s defied market expectations and 
maintained their Aa3 rating (negative outlook) on the sovereign. The expected one-notch cut would only 
have taken it in line with S&P’s A+, so shouldn’t have been too destabilising. The Euro spiked a couple of 
tenths very late on Friday immediately after the news and is +0.13% higher this morning, with French bond 
futures rising nearly three-quarters of a point.   
Next the Middle East. This is likely to remain one of the main market focuses this week, with the pause in 
direct US-Iran fighting looking increasingly fragile. Reports from various media suggest Washington and 
Israel are considering renewed strikes after the US midterms, which are only 8 days away now, against 
a backdrop of a larger US military presence in the region, reduced diplomatic staffing and increasingly 
forceful Iranian warnings about retaliation. Trump said to Fox yesterday that it's "getting to be decision time" 
on whether he will resume attacks or make a deal. At least the deal part is still part of the conversation.   
Meanwhile, disruption around the Strait of Hormuz remains a key market risk, particularly after last week’s 
renewed rise in oil prices. Before the US vote comes Israel’s election tomorrow, the sixth since 2019, 
with the final polls still pointing to deadlock: neither Netanyahu’s bloc nor the opposition appears 
close to the 61 seats needed for a majority, while Eisenkot’s opposition has a modest lead in the latest 
polling averages. Protracted coalition talks or fresh elections are reasonable risks. Netanyahu has said he 
does not favour a pre-emptive strike on Iran before the election, but has left the post-election position 

open. A result that weakens Netanyahu could lower the immediate odds of Israeli-led escalation, 
whereas another stalemate — or a Netanyahu-led coalition — could leave the incentive for renewed 
military action intact. 
The third theme is AI, and this week should provide an unusually important test of the recent concerns 
around the sector. Alphabet and Microsoft report on Wednesday, with Amazon following on Thursday, so 
the market’s reaction to their results and, especially, the latest capex plans could rival Wednesday’s 
FOMC for top billing. Indeed, given the recent AI-related market wobbles, the earnings may ultimately 
matter more for markets than the Fed, the ECB meeting on Thursday or the BoJ on Friday. September's 
important PCE comes the day after the FOMC, so will have a bit less impact than normal but will count 
heavily towards the December FOMC. Other US releases include Thursday’s US Q3 GDP. Elsewhere, 
Wednesday also brings the UK Autumn Budget, where those of us in this country will keep a careful eye 
on what the Chancellor has in store for our taxes! Friday is particularly busy in Europe, with flash October 
inflation and preliminary Q3 GDP releases. 
Focusing in on the US week ahead now, our economists expect the Fed to leave rates unchanged on 
Wednesday. Futures are pricing in less than a 10% chance of a hike after briefly spiking above 50% less than 
a couple of weeks ago after the CPI print. The interest will therefore be in Chair Warsh’s press conference 
and how the Fed balances still-elevated inflation and solid growth against the recent tightening in 
financial conditions. Our economists expect the statement itself to contain only limited changes, 
although dissents to a hold are possible. The broader message is still likely to be hawkish: our economists 
think recent inflation data support the case for further tightening over time, even if the Fed chooses to pause 
this week. They also expect Warsh to make clear that tighter financial conditions are not a substitute for 
higher policy rates. 
The US data should matter for that debate too. Tomorrow brings September durable goods orders and 
October Conference Board consumer confidence, with our economists expecting durable goods to remain 
firm, helped by aircraft orders and AI-related capex. The biggest data day is Thursday, when September 
personal income and spending, PCE inflation and the advance Q3 GDP estimate are all released. Our 
economists are tracking September core PCE at +0.36% MoM, equivalent to 3.14% YoY absent 
revisions, although revisions to some August PPI components could keep the annual rate from 
rounding up to 3.2%. Our economists expect Q3 real GDP growth of +3.3% annualised, up from +2.2% in Q2. 
On Friday, the Q3 employment cost index will provide an important read on labour-cost pressures; a 
strong upside surprise would make it harder to maintain the view that the labour market is not adding to 
inflation pressure. 
Elsewhere in the US, today brings the September Chicago Fed national activity index and October Dallas Fed 
manufacturing survey. Wednesday also sees the September advance goods trade balance and retail 
inventories, while Friday brings October MNI Chicago PMI. Other large US reporters during the week outside 
of Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon include Visa, Coca-Cola, Boeing, Mastercard, Intel, Caterpillar, Eli Lilly, 
ExxonMobil and Chevron. 
Globally, central banks dominate. Our economists expect the ECB to keep policy rates unchanged on 
Thursday (see here), following tomorrow’s bank lending survey. Our Chief Japan economist expects the BoJ 
to hold on Friday (see here). The market is pricing less than a 3% probability of either hiking. However, 

it's an 85% and 88% probability of the Bank of Canada and Reserve Bank of New Zealand of hiking on 
Wednesday. 
Europe has a particularly busy end to the week but before that Germany’s October Ifo survey is out today and 
November GfK consumer confidence on Wednesday. On Friday, flash October inflation is released across 
the region: our economists expect headline CPI of 2.92% YoY in Germany, 3.58% in France and 4.95% in 
Italy. Preliminary Q3 GDP figures for Germany, France, Italy and the Eurozone are also due on Friday. In the 
UK, the main event is Wednesday’s Autumn Budget. 
In Asia, China’s Central Committee plenum runs through to Thursday, while September industrial 
profits are released tomorrow. Japan has a heavy Friday calendar, including October Tokyo CPI, September 
unemployment, the job-to-applicant ratio, retail sales, industrial production and housing starts, alongside 
the BoJ decision. Australia’s September CPI is due on Wednesday. 
Politics adds another layer. The market will react to Brazil's shock election result today that we mentioned 
above and go into detail on below, Israel holds legislative elections tomorrow, the EU-Canada summit takes 
place on Thursday and Friday, and the G20 foreign ministers meet in Atlanta on Friday and Saturday. 
Finally, it is a major global earnings week beyond US megacap technology. SK hynix (Wednesday) and 
Samsung Electronics (Thursday) earning will have global ramifications from the tech sector, while healthcare 
earnings includes Novartis, Eli Lilly, AbbVie, AstraZeneca and Sanofi; energy includes Shell, TotalEnergies, 
ExxonMobil, Chevron and BP; and autos include Porsche, Ford, Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen. 
In Asia, the KOSPI (-0.90%) is leading declines as weakness in technology shares weighs on sentiment. 
Elsewhere, the Nikkei (-0.34%), CSI 300 (-0.47%), and Shanghai Composite (-0.09%) are also trading 
moderately lower. In contrast, the Hang Seng (+0.51%) and S&P/ASX 200 (+0.17%) are outperforming and 
bucking the broader regional trend. S&P 500 (+0.20%) and Nasdaq (+0.24%) futures are higher.   
In Brazil, President Lula pulled off a remarkable and genuinely market-moving comeback overnight, winning a 
record fourth term by 51.8% to 48.2% against Flávio Bolsonaro, despite having lost the first round 45.1%-
47.0% three weeks ago. It is the first time since Brazil returned to direct presidential elections in 1989 that 
the first-round runner-up has gone on to win the runoff. The result wrong-footed a market that had 
increasingly priced a Bolsonaro victory. A Tokyo-listed ETF tracking Brazilian equities fell around -15% in 
Asian trading, with the real also tracking a -10% decline according to derivatives. Bolsonaro had been viewed 
as the more market-friendly candidate and, after right- and centre-right parties increased their Senate share 
from 47% to 63% in the first round, investors had been anticipating a better chance of fiscal consolidation. 
Lula, by contrast, has promised an adjustment without yet specifying how it will be delivered. The important 
medium-term point, however, is that the rightward shift in Congress remains even after Lula's victory, which 
should constrain the administration's ability to pursue a materially looser fiscal agenda. So once the 
immediate election premium and Bolsonaro's challenge to the result are digested, the bigger question for 
Brazilian assets will be whether Lula can use that congressional constraint to establish a credible fiscal path. 
That matters, especially with debt concerns already keeping rates high. 
Recapping last week now, markets grappled with several risks including the French fiscal situation, AI growth 
fears, and a fresh jump in energy prices. We’ll dive into each, but in terms of the size of the market moves, the 
French situation saw the biggest reaction. In fact, the weekly widening in the Franco-German 10yr spread 

was still +29.5bps, even as it retreated by -6.8bps on Friday. That came as France’s 10yr OAT yield 
climbed +21.5bps last week to 5.01%, whilst the 10yr German bund yield fell -8.0bps to 3.34%. So that 
left the spread at 167bps, not far off from its closing peak during the Euro Crisis, when it moved as high as 
189bps. Other assets struggled as well, with both the CAC 40 (-2.19%) and the STOXX Banks (-5.10%) 
falling to their lowest level since March and June, respectively, although the STOXX 600 saw a 
comparatively smaller decline of -1.39% (-0.31% Friday). 
As that was happening, several AI names remained under pressure last week, with the Philly semiconductor 
index losing ground for a third consecutive week, falling -3.21% (+0.44% Friday). And, in turn, losses for chip 
stocks dragged down US equities more broadly, with the NASDAQ down -0.71% (-0.21% Friday), whilst 
the S&P 500 fell -0.47% (-0.52% Friday). The individual decliners included CoreWeave, which fell -11.65% (-
2.46% Friday), and whose 5yr CDS rose another +19bps last week to 1146bps. Meanwhile, Meta’s share 
price fell -9.40% last week (-0.96% Friday) following its earnings, and its 5yr CDS rose +22bps to 127bps.   
Elsewhere, Brent crude oil prices rose for a 3rd consecutive week, up +6.24% (+3.14% Friday) to 
$116.72/bbl, closing in on its $118.35/bbl post-2022 peak reached in March. Moreover, European natural 
gas futures also hit a post-2022 high of €93.85/MWh, having risen +7.20% last week (+4.23% Friday). That 
came as fears mounted about a wider escalation in the Middle East, including a New York Times report which 
said that the US and Israel had drawn up plans for renewed strikes against Iran after the midterm elections. 
Amidst all that, US Treasuries put in a strong performance, with the 10yr yield down -7.9bps last week 
(+4.1bps Friday) to 4.99%. Otherwise, the Euro weakened somewhat against the Dollar, falling -0.33% 
last week to $1.1202. And in credit, spreads widened across the board, with US HY (+6bps), EUR HY 
(+40bps), US IG (+5bps), and EUR IG (+15bps) all widening. Indeed, for US HY, it was the 6th consecutive 
weekly widening, which is the first time that’s happened since 2022. The headline moves understated the 
decompression down the rating spectrum: Euro BBs widened +26bps and Bs +46bps, while CCCs were 
+105bps wider. US BBs widened just +3bps and Bs +8bps compared with +59bps for CCCs. So one of the 
clearer messages from the week was that the pressure remains concentrated in the weakest credits 
rather than representing indiscriminate HY selling, especially in the US.
