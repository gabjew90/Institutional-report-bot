The risk-off tone has turned the tide for global duration in the lead-up to the late 
October and early November central bank decisions. The relative emphasis on 
inflation risks versus the recent tightening in financial conditions is likely to 
determine the strength of the floor under very front-end rates. Although the recent 
rally has compressed the gap between longer-term US yields and fair value, the other 
duration-friendly factors—including underweight positioning, diminished technical 
risks, and risk asset pressures in response to higher yields—are largely intact. We 
continue to think the belly of the curve is the right place to anchor a long bias amid 
the push and pull of energy risks and AI-related volatility. The feedback between 
hawkish ECB risks and sovereign credit volatility is likely to keep a speed limit on how 
much ERZ7 can sell off even if the ECB offers a more relaxed message about 
sovereign stress.  Flatteners (e.g., 2s5s) remain our preferred tilt in core Euro area 
rates heading into the winter. For the UK, we expect the news from next week’s 
Autumn Budget to be more about the maturity mix than the magnitude of issuance, 
which should bode well for swap spread longs. Stability at the long-end of the JGB 
curve seems to be on firmer ground amid a more supportive demand shift of late; we 
continue to expect bearish pressure to come from the 5y part of the curve, with 
multiple routes to further flattening. Our economists now expect each of the BoC, 
RBA, and RBNZ to hike at their upcoming meetings; cumulative hike pricing through 
the next year seems excessive in both Canada and New Zealand given the slack in 
both economies, but we see clearer value in front-end flatteners in Australia.

Hedge value on full display. The recent duration rally has compressed the 
n
valuation gap that we highlighted earlier in the month as one of the reasons for a 
more constructive tilt on duration. The other supporting factors behind that 
argument—including underweight duration positioning, less asymmetry to 
higher yields from technical risks, reduced bearish pressure from abroad, and 
volatility in credit and equities in part related to higher yields—remain in place to 
varying degrees, however. The clearer demonstration of duration’s usefulness as 
a risk asset hedge when AI worries are in the driver’s seat does not preclude 
some reversal of the rate rally if the recent risk-off tone moderates and the rise in 
energy prices persists. But the more apparent portfolio value is at least 
consistent with some durability to the compression in term premium. An on-hold 
decision is likely at next week’s FOMC, which comes against the backdrop of still 
present inflation pressures but a clearer dose of financial conditions tightening.
