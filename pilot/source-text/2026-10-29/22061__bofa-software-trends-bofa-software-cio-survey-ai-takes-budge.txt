AI is the highest priority, software budgets up ~5% in ‘27 
Our 2H26 survey of ~300 CIOs/technology budget decision makers reinforces that AI 
now sits at the forefront of IT spending plans. AI deployment is the highest 2027 priority 
for ~40% of respondents, with ~36% expecting AI to exceed 10% of software budgets, 
up from ~29% in 2026. Total software budgets are expected to increase by 5.3% in 
2027, with AI applications, including agents/assistants for front- and back-office 
workflows, are expected to see the largest spending increase, alongside continued 
investment in data, cloud, and security. At the bottom of the list, the areas with the 
lowest spending priorities are CRM, ERP and HCM/Workforce, trends that are well 
reflected in the revenue trends of the underlying sector leaders.

Microsoft and large AI labs are leading the field for now 
Our survey suggests that Microsoft and the AI labs are leaders across nearly every AI 
capability category. Microsoft ranked first in cloud infrastructure at ~88% and 
infrastructure software at ~83%, best positioned to monetize through applications, 
cloud, and security. On Infrastructure software, AI labs were in the range of 70%, likely

move 25% of AI pilots into production. About 54% and 47% of respondents noted that 
Cybersecurity risks and pricing represent the most notable inhibitors for AI deployment. 
AI security concerns drive incremental security spending, with AI security and identity 
leading the spending priorities, and 22% of CIOs noting it plans to allocate more than 
10% of the cybersecurity budgets to protecting AI deployments and workloads.

 BofA CIO Survey: Software budgets up, AI 
strategies are the focus 
We conducted our 2H26 CIO spending survey with responses from more than 300 
technology budget decision makers across various verticals and company sizes. 
Respondents represent ~20% SMBs (<1,000 employees) with a weighted average IT 
budget of $57mn, ~40% mid-sized organizations (1,001-5,000 employees) with IT 
budgets of $134mn, and ~40% enterprise organizations (5,000+ employees) with IT 
budgets of $422mn. We believe the survey results are a good directional indicator, as 
opposed to an absolute indicator of spending intentions. 
Key takeaways 
• 
Software budgets are expected to grow 5.3% in 2027, with cloud and 
cybersecurity spending growing faster. 
• 
AI is taking a larger share of software budgets, funded through both 
incremental budgets and reallocation of existing budgets. 
• 
Three top priorities for spending (in order): AI, Cloud and on-prem 
infrastructure and Cybersecurity 
• 
Cybersecurity budgets are expected to grow 6.9%, with AI security and identity 
leading incremental spending priorities. 
• 
Bottom three priorities (lowest priority first): HCM/workforce, ERP and CRM. 
• 
Reasons to deploy AI - Productivity and cost savings, but only 17% report 
measurable enterprise-wide returns from AI. 
• 
At the enterprise level, AI adoption remains early, with most organizations 
moving only a small portion of AI pilots into production. Cybersecurity risks and 
pricing remain key barriers to broader AI deployment. 
• 
Back office leads enterprise software AI investment interest, with 
ITSM/ITOM ranking above CRM, ERP and HCM in overall spending priorities 
• 
Public cloud budgets are expected to grow 8.4% in 2027, with hybrid cloud 
remaining the most common deployment strategy. 
Vendor specific highlights 
Microsoft winning across the stack 
• 
MSFT stands out for breadth across the entire IT stack, from infrastructure and 
cloud to security, applications and AI. It leads cloud-infrastructure AI consideration 
at 88.4%, while Copilot reaches 76.7% current or planned production use. The 
breadth of offerings supports monetization across multiple channels, with Azure 
capturing the core infrastructure consumption behind enterprise AI deployments and 
Copilot supporting safe AI deployment at scale through governance, business 
context, security and workflow logic. 
Enterprise software: AI interest yet to translate into paid adoption 
• 
ServiceNow: Registered 54.5% back-office AI usage likelihood, while ITSM/ITOM 
ranks above ERP and HCM in overall back-office AI budget priorities. These results 
support our view that NOW can capture incremental spending through workflow 
automation, measurable productivity gains, and cost savings for customers. 
• 
Salesforce: Leads traditional front-office application vendors at 60% AI usage 
likelihood. In our view, this supports AI monetization opportunities across data 
preparation, workflows and agentic use cases, but incremental spending will depend 
on competitive pricing and measurable sales and service improvements.

Methodology and calculations 
• 
Exhibit 2: We estimated software budget growth by assigning the midpoint to 
each response range: 3% for ±1–5%, 8% for ±6–10%, and 0% for no change. 
We assumed ±15% for the open-ended increase or decrease of more than 10%, 
multiplied each assigned value by the response count, and divided the sum by 
all 309 responses. This produces a midpoint-weighted estimate of 
approximately 5.3% software budget growth in 2027. 
• 
Exhibit 3: We estimated IT staffing and hiring budget growth by assigning the 
midpoint to each response range: 3% for ±1–5%, 8% for ±6–10%, and 0% for 
no change. We assumed ±15% for the open-ended increase or decrease of 
more than 10%, multiplied each assigned value by the response count, and 
divided the sum by all 309 responses. This produces a midpoint-weighted 
estimate of approximately 3.4% hiring growth in 2027. 
• 
Exhibit 5: We estimated the 2026 and 2027 allocations by assigning 
representative values to each response category: 0% to the 0% category, 3% 
(midpoint) to 1-5%. 8% (midpoint) to 6-10%, and 15% to the open-ended “over 
10%” category. For each year, we multiplied these assigned values by the 
number of responses in each category, summed the results, and divided by the 
number of respondents providing a known answer, excluding “don’t know” 
responses. This yields midpoint-weighted estimates of 8.3% in 2026 and 9.4% 
in 2027, up 14.2% YoY. 
• 
Exhibit 22: We assigned 5.5% (midpoint) to the 1-10% increase category, 15% 
(midpoint) to the 10-20% increase category, 25% to the increases above 20%, 
0% to flat spending, and -5% to declines. Each assigned value was multiplied by 
its response count, with the sum divided by all 309 respondents. This yields a 
midpoint-weighted average estimate of ~8.4% public cloud budget growth in 
2027. 
• 
Exhibit 22 / Exhibit 23: We grouped respondents by organization size: SMB 
(<1,000 employees), mid-sized (1,001-5,000 employees), and enterprise (5,000+ 
employees). Within each group, we divided the number selecting each public-
cloud spending or deployment option by the total respondents in that size 
group: 62 SMBs, 123 mid-sized organizations, and 124 enterprises. Spending 
growth includes all three increases categories, while growth above 10% 
combines the “10-20%” and “over 20%” responses. Cloud-strategy percentages 
were calculated using the same group totals, allowing direct comparison of 
hybrid, multi-cloud, and other deployment approaches by organization size. 
• 
Exhibit 25: We assigned 3% (midpoint) to the 1-5% increase category, 10.5% 
(midpoint) to 6-15%, 20% to increases above 15%, 0% to no change, and -5% 
to decreases. Each value was multiplied by its response count, with the sum 
divided by all 309 respondents. This yields a midpoint-weighted average of 
~6.9% cybersecurity budget growth in 2027.
