Corporate Earnings In Focus With Central Banks expected To Lean Hawkish
This week is dominated by corporate earnings and monetary policy, with the former most likely to deliver
surprises. In the US, 44.5% of S&P and 38% of Nasdaq market cap will report. After the AI selloff, Alphabet,
Microsoft and Amazon need to show that cloud revenue, cash generation and infrastructure returns can keep
pace with accelerating capex. Oracle’s AI World, which runs through Wednesday is also key, particularly for
any update on cloud demand, AI infrastructure commitments and the financing of its buildout. The AI financing
premium can fade if this week’s hyperscaler results deliver, particularly after substantial de-grossing and
systematic selling. But this is no longer a market where a generic demand beat is sufficient. Investors want
proof that revenue arrives before financing costs and depreciation overwhelm the return. 
On central banks, the Fed, BoC, RBNZ, ECB and BoJ all meet within a 72-hour window and the theme is
expected to be hawkishness. Chief US economist Jonathan Pingle expects the FOMC to remain on hold on
Wednesday but with a hawkish tilt; the pause reflects a desire to assess trends rather than any shift toward
neutral. Similarly with the BoJ and ECB: both are expected to pause but deliver hawkish signals. In Japan, it’s
a question of timing, with more hikes expected as part of a cycle while in Europe, policymakers are on
tenterhooks watching for any second-round effects from higher energy prices to emerge. In Canada, the team
expects a 25bp hike to 2.50% given energy-driven rising headline inflation and in New Zealand, a 25bp hike to
3.00% is expected despite a dip in inflation. Where there are dissents, they are likely to be towards doing
more, rather than less.

Germany’s ifo Index Up On Better Business Expectations – The German ifo index rose 0.3 points in
October to 90.2 (consensus 90), the highest level since May 2023. This was driven in particular by a rise in
business expectations (+1.2 points to 91.6; consensus 90). By contrast, the assessment of current conditions
declined 0.7 points to 88.8 (consensus 89.9). The sectoral details will be published at 10.30 CET at
www.ifo.de. The October value of the ifo index is standing well above the Q3 average of 88.5. The
Bundesbank expects GDP growth to pick up in Q4 after having likely stagnated in Q3 (Q3 GDP to be released
30 October; UBSe: 0.1% q/q for Q3; 0.3% q/q for Q4). (Felix Hüfner, Research - Economics & Strategy -
European Economist)

Brazil's Lula Wins Fourth Term; Markets Set for Significant Repricing – President Luiz Inácio Lula da
Silva was re-elected for a record fourth term on Sunday, taking 51.76% of the vote to Flavio Bolsonaro’s
48.24% in a surprise outcome, according to the electoral court. The runoff was not without controversy, with
allegations of voter suppression and the Supreme Court ordering polling stations in several key states to
remain open beyond regular voting hours. Senator Flavio called for an annulment of the runoff, accusing the
Supreme Court of changing the rules in the middle of the voting day. Flavio’s annulment request is unlikely to
hold water given his campaign also asked for additional time in specific states that were impacted by adverse
weather. Focus now shifts to Monday’s market reaction and fiscal outlook. Brazil’s fiscal outlook again takes
center stage, as debt/GDP trajectory resumes its ominous path. The desk expects a significant re-pricing of
Brazilian assets on Monday, as the market adjusts to another four years of Lula, with USDBRL potentially at
5.3 Monday. Note that FX didn’t react that much in the first round; it was more about rates and equities. EM
strategist Roque Montero thinks USDBRL could drift to 5.5-5.6 by end-2026 given December negative
seasonality. In rates, Roque expects a 100-150bp move higher. Equities could correct by 10-15% based on
latest EWZ reaction. (Justin Wensek, Head of Latin America Sales)
France Assets May Get Reprieve As Moody's Chooses Not To Act On Rating – French assets may be in
for some relief on Monday after a weekend in which renewed protests didn’t lead to serious incidents, and after
Moody’s Ratings opted to maintain its rating on French sovereign debt unchanged at Aa3 – one notch above
Fitch and S&P. OATs were indicated around 5.5bp tighter, while EU bonds were opening around 0.5bp to 1bp
tighter in asset-swap terms. Moody’s said “slower growth and a higher deficit have pushed France's debt
burden somewhat higher than previously expected”. Government bond yields have risen more rapidly than
Moody’s had expected and widespread social protests “could constrain fiscal consolidation efforts”. Moody’s
said the Aa3 rating (with a negative outlook) “assumes that a deficit-reducing budget for 2027 will be adopted,
while financing costs and social tensions will abate somewhat in the next few months”. (Matthew Cowley,
Macro Sales)
RBA’s Hunter Warns Further Rate Rise May Be Needed - RBA Chief Economist Sarah Hunter said rates
may need to rise again, with inflation remaining elevated even after the cash rate reached a 15-year high of
4.6% last month. “Unfortunately, not quite yet,” she said when asked whether policy had done enough. Hunter
cited rising fuel costs, supply-chain pressures and domestic demand exceeding sustainable supply, noting
government spending was “part of demand.” While not pre-announcing a hike, she warned the RBA must
ensure inflation does not become embedded. UBS Economics sees the RBA hiking another 25bp, most likely
in November. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Japanese Lifers Remain Selective On Foreign Bond Exposure - Japanese life insurers have recently
published second-half fiscal-year investment plans, with discussions focused primarily on JGBs rather than
foreign bonds. Activity in JGBs has centred on switching from lower-yielding bonds into higher-yielding
securities, while most accounts remain patient on adding outright duration. More substantial buying could
emerge if yields rise further or if confidence increases that yields have peaked. Year-end expectations for 10y
JGB yields are concentrated around 3.0%-3.3%, with some views extending to 3.6%. Expectations for 30y
JGB yields are 4.0%-4.4%, with upper-end estimates around 4.6%. USDJPY year-end assumptions are
generally clustered between 155 and 158. Among life insurers, appetite for non-JPY investments with FX risk
remains limited. Foreign bond demand is selective, with interest concentrated in US investment-grade credit
and private credit. Accounts with existing OAT exposure have generally maintained positions while waiting for
improved entry points rather than reducing risk. Some accounts that were underweight OATs have used
spread widening to move closer to neutral allocations, while some long-term investors are evaluating
purchases on further weakness and continue to monitor FX-hedged relative value versus JGBs. (Kimiko
Nakayama, Rates Sales)

outlook, deeming near term ECB intervention as unlikely. Some are beginning to identify attractive

US Equities: Long-Duration Capital Vs. Short-Duration Rebalancing - The medium-term bullish view
remains intact despite recent hedge fund and systematic unwinds. Over the past 15 years, long-duration
capital from retail investors, retirement assets, and foreign investors has gained influence over the US equity
trend, while faster systematic, quantitative, and hedge fund strategies increasingly drive short-term volatility.
This barbell market structure can produce sharper, more frequent corrections, but persistent buy-the-dip
demand also supports faster, often V-shaped recoveries. Retirement migration toward more equity-oriented
products and continued foreign allocations provide structural inflows, whereas leveraged and model-driven
strategies amplify the market’s path. Unless labor-market weakness, prolonged inflation, geopolitical shocks,
or fading US exceptionalism materially erode capital persistence, systematic deleveraging is more likely to
create temporary dislocations than a durable trend reversal. Short-duration capital increasingly determines the
path; long-duration capital increasingly determines the destination. (Rebecca Cheong, Derivatives Strategist)

AI’s Credit Cracks Look Contained, But Hyperscalers Face $1 trn EBIT Test - Equity Strategist Sunil
Tirumalai says recent technology-market weakness reflects concentrated credit stress rather than a broad
fracture in the AI investment story. Hyperscaler bond yields have risen, but higher US Treasury yields explain
nearly all the increase, except at Oracle, while spreads remain broadly stable. Stress is focused on more
leveraged issuers and those with large contingent liabilities. Neoclouds such as CoreWeave account for only
5–7% of expected AI datacentre capex over the next four to five years. The larger test is returns: UBS
Research forecasts six hyperscalers will invest $7.7 trn over 2026–30, with a 15% return on invested capital by
2031 requiring $1 trn of incremental EBIT and about $3.4 trn of revenue at current cloud margins. That
depends on AI delivering productivity gains and the ecosystem monetising them. Meanwhile, EM and Asian
earnings upgrades remain intact, with the selloff appearing more driven by flows and sentiment than by
fundamentals. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
