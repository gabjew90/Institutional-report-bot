After a night of freedom while my family headed off to Centre Parcs yesterday, my brief spell of peace and 
reflection is nearly over. After work today, I’m driving to join them for a couple of days. So while you’re all 
focused on major tech earnings and the FOMC, against my will I’ll be zip-wiring, aerial roping, kayaking, 
swimming, trying not to sink in the rapids, and, of course, paying the bills. Spare a thought for me.   
Markets have started the week in a broadly optimistic mood, with the S&P 500 (+0.70%), Nasdaq Composite 
(+0.82%) and STOXX 600 (+0.77%) all advancing yesterday, whilst longer-dated bonds mostly rallied. The 
OAT-Bund spread tightened -13.8bps to 153bps, which is the biggest daily narrowing since March 2020. 
But politics remained firmly on the radar, with Brazilian assets selling off sharply after President Lula’s 
surprise election victory over Flávio Bolsonaro, leaving the Bovespa down -13.88% and the real -8.06%, 
the worst USD adjusted day for domestic equities in nearly 35 years but not as bad as this time 
yesterday. And today brings another potentially much more consequential election for global markets in 
Israel, not least because of what the result could mean for the next phase of the confrontation with Iran. Oil 
markets remain volatile, with both Brent (-0.78% to $115.81/bbl) and WTI (-1.46% to $101.76/bbl) 
eventually settling slightly lower amid news of talks between Iran and the Mecca Pact countries. 
Starting with equities, the positive tone saw the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rise +2.82% after 
falling -3.21% last week, whilst the Mag 7 gained +0.76%. Some of the recent AI-related 
underperformers also rebounded, including CoreWeave (+1.76%), despite initially falling in pre-market 
trading after Elon Musk wrote on X on Sunday that “neoclouds” would see “guppy-level death and 
consolidation”, arguing that debt markets would be reluctant to finance companies overwhelmingly reliant 
on AI data centres for their revenues. Meta (-1.88%) dipped after announcing plans to raise as much as 
$95bn through a stock sale. Meanwhile, Microsoft (+1.54%) gained after announcing that it will offer 
buyouts to about 60,000 employees as part of an effort to trim costs. Europe joined the rally too, with the 
DAX (+0.91%), CAC 40 (+0.83%) and FTSE 100 (+0.72%) all advancing. 

There was also some more encouraging news from the Middle East yesterday, although the overall daily 
move was muted as investors remain wary of extrapolating too much from the latest diplomatic efforts. 
Senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan – which form the Mecca Defence Alliance – met 
Iranian representatives in Tehran in the hope of promoting greater regional stability, with state-run IRIB 
reporting that Iran would support “any understanding” that strengthened regional unity. That comes 
after reports last week that the US and Israeli militaries had begun drawing up plans for renewed strikes on 
Iran. 
The positive read across from those diplomatic developments was partially offset by Reuters reporting that 
Hezbollah was preparing for a possible resumption of hostilities with Israel, with Brent reaching $117.44/bbl 
intra-day after the report. But the more upbeat news dominated in the end, with Brent settling -0.78% 
lower at $115.81 and trading another -1.04% lower this morning. By contrast, European natural gas 
futures climbed +3.01% to a new post-2022 high of €96.68/MWh amid a supply outage in Norway, even 
as US natural gas prices (-1.44%) fell to a 6-month low amid the warm weather. 
In Europe, France was the biggest story. 10yr OAT yields fell -11.6bps after Moody’s surprised the market 
on Friday by leaving France’s credit rating unchanged. The weekend protests were also controlled in a way 
the previous weekend’s weren’t. That all helped the OAT-Bund spread tighten -13.8bps to 153bps, its 
largest one-day decline since March 2020, with the move amplified by 10yr Bund yields rising +2.2bps. 
Elsewhere, 10yr BTP yields fell -8.2bps and gilt yields were down -1.2bps. At the front end, 2yr Bunds rose 
+4.9bps with French (-4.5bps) and Italian (-2.5bps) lower. There was likely some unwinding of flight to 
quality here too, while bund yields were also boosted by the energy component as well as a stronger-than-
expected IFO survey reading that saw the expectations component rise to its highest since 2022 at 91.6 
(vs. 90.0 expected). 
In the US, 2yr Treasury yields (+3.5bps) reversed some of last week’s decline amid the risk-on tone, 
although longer maturities rallied amid the pullback in US energy prices, leaving 10yr Treasury yields -
0.7bps at 4.99%. 
Asian equity markets are mostly trading higher this morning but US and European futures are slightly 
lower. Within the region, the KOSPI (+3.27%) is outperforming, led by strong advances in heavyweight 
chipmakers Samsung Electronics (+4.23%) and SK Hynix (+6.12%). Sentiment has also been boosted by 
stronger-than-expected third-quarter GDP data from South Korea (details below). Elsewhere, the Nikkei 
(+1.55%) is posting solid gains, while Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 (+0.49%) is also higher. In contrast, 
Chinese equities are underperforming the broader regional rally, with the CSI 300 (-0.39%), Hang Seng (-
0.64%), and Shanghai Composite (-0.45%) all trading lower. S&P 500 (-0.09%), Nasdaq (-0.38%) and Stoxx (-
0.41%) are all lower with 10-year US Treasury yields -2.1bps lower at 4.968% as we go to print. 
Earlier, Chinese industrial profits accelerated in September, driven primarily by strength in AI-related 
industries, while consumer-facing sectors continued to face headwinds from weak domestic demand. 
Industrial profits rose 14.4% y/y in September, marking the fastest pace of growth since June. For the 
first nine months of 2026, profits at large industrial firms increased 15.5%, marginally below the 15.7% 
growth recorded over January-August. 
Turning to South Korea, the economy expanded faster than expected in the third quarter of 2026 as 
robust global demand for advanced AI chips continued to support growth. GDP rose +3.4% y/y in the 

three months to September, exceeding expectations of +3.0%, although moderating from 3.7% growth in the 
previous quarter. On a quarterly basis, GDP increased +1.0%, ahead of consensus forecasts for a +0.8% 
rise. 
Another election worth watching today is in Israel, and potentially a much more important one for 
global markets. Neither Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc nor the opposition led by former military chief Gadi 
Eisenkot currently appears to have a clear route to the 61 Knesset seats needed for a majority, with the 
smaller parties, the 3.25% electoral threshold, and potentially the Arab parties likely to prove crucial to the 
eventual coalition arithmetic. Our economists think that leaves everything from another Netanyahu-led 
government to an opposition or unity government, political deadlock and potentially another election 
on the table. 
But the bigger global question is what the result means for Iran. There is broad agreement across the Israeli 
political mainstream on preventing Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon, so an opposition victory shouldn’t 
necessarily be interpreted as a dovish outcome. However, there is an important difference over the 
endgame. Eisenkot has said Israel should be prepared to intervene militarily if necessary to prevent an 
Iranian nuclear weapon, whilst stopping short of pursuing regime change. Netanyahu, by contrast, has 
maintained that regime change is ultimately necessary to remove the Iranian threat. That distinction 
could matter considerably if tensions re-escalate after the US midterms. So today’s vote won’t remove the 
prospect of another confrontation with Iran, but it could have an important bearing on what Israel ultimately 
wants any confrontation to achieve. So the aftermath and coalition arithmetic will be very relevant well 
beyond Israel. 
To the day ahead now, US data includes the October Conference Board consumer confidence index,

also be a busy day for earnings, including Visa, HSBC, NXP Semiconductors, Teradyne and Boeing. Finally, 
the US Treasury will auction $70bn of 5yr notes.
