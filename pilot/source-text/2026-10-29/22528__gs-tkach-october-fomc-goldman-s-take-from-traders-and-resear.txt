TLDR: The Fed is expected to deliver a hold this week. A hike is effectively off the table given dovish
Fed-speak since September, the midterms and growing AI-capex downside risk, so the focus is on how
Warsh squares a hold with his messaging after a hot CPI, with a December hike still largely priced. On the
trading side, in rates, we like buying dips positioning for belly outperformance in a rangebound market. In
FX, skip rich ATM vol in favor of cheap USD skew via GBPUSD risk reversals, favor selective USD
strength vs. EUR, and sell USDJPY and USDCAD into strength. In equities, we like put spreads for a
selloff given steep skew, and outright calls for a rally. In credit, favor CDX HY over IG locally (tactical
compression).
Views from GS US Economics Research Team
• Comments from FOMC participants since their last meeting point to a compromise to raise the funds rate
every other meeting. This means that the FOMC is set to skip raising rates at its October meeting but is
likely to deliver a second hike in December. We think the FOMC might modify their statement to adapt
language from Vice Chair Jefferson’s recent speech to say, “Any further adjustments to the target range for
the federal funds rate to support a timelier return to the Committee’s 2 percent goal will depend on trends
in the data, the evolving outlook, and the balance of risks.” We do not expect significant new information
from the press conference.
• The Fed’s hawkish pivot has contributed to a large increase in interest rates that has driven a moderate
tightening since September in broad financial conditions. The tightening is only a bit more than is usually
associated with one rate hike and much less than is usually associated with the four hikes the market has
now priced, meaning that the FOMC is unlikely to see it as excessive or a replacement for hikes.
• If the FOMC sticks to an every-other-meeting pace in 2027, it could consider hiking in March, by which
point we will have five more months of inflation data. We do not expect a hike in March because we
expect the inflation trend to improve as the effects of tariffs, the energy shock, and AI price pressures
diminish. We also suspect that some FOMC participants share our views that the economy is not
overheated and that the inflation overshoot caused by past supply shocks will largely have to be waited
out, and they might prefer to limit the total size of the hiking cycle. If concerns about growth or
employment emerged, that would likely bring hikes to an end too. The more extreme and long-lasting the
energy shock becomes, the more FOMC participants will worry that inflationary pressures could broaden
and the greater the risk of a larger “January effect” in the inflation numbers as companies take the
opportunity provided by start-of-the-year price increases to pass along their higher costs. Our baseline and

probability-weighted Fed forecasts are considerably less hawkish, both because we are skeptical that the
FOMC will hike four times and because we expect cuts down the road.

Views from GS Trading and Strategy Desks
All of the below are written as of 26Oct26 market close and are subject to market movements
Rates:
• We view current pricing of 3bp hike premia as purely a function of the price action into July FOMC as
opposed to any credible chance of a hike. The combination of a/ uniformly dovish communication since
September meeting, b/ proximity of the meeting to the midterm elections, and c/ growing balance of
downside risks as AI capex is called into question makes the probability of a hike at this point nothing
short of unfathomable. From a bottoms-up perspective, the September dot plot forecast remains
likely/perfectly intact, and we have only remarked one voting dot (Williams, lower) over the past month.
Warsh opened his September’s press conference by emphasizing the role of hikes in delivering “a timelier
return to the Committee’s 2% goal.” How he will thread the needle between the policy decision and this
mantra remains a mystery, particularly in light of the hot CPI report earlier this month. Although we
expect a hawkish hold, December meeting at 23bps cumulatively priced leaves little room for error given
the lack of weight we put on the tail of a 50bp hike. We estimate consensus positioning as short 2x z6 vs
long 1x z7 on the well-held view that Trump will re-escalate tensions in the Middle East as soon as the
midterms pass, and believe the most painful quadrant for the vast majority of market participants is the
SVB-esque torque steepener led by the front contract should the AI drawdown worsen dramatically.
Similar micro-flattener expressions ie paid Dec/Jan vs rec Mar/Apr from 9bps in each jump offer cheap

convexity to the modal scenario of a tapering hiking cycle as the inflation trajectory normalizes into q2 of
next year. (Thank You Brian Bingham– Short Macro Trading)
• On whether the rally continues from here, we still see this as a rangebound market. The Fed is very likely
to deliver in December, and the bar for a March hike is low absent better news on the inflation front
between now and then, so it is unsurprising that some additional premium remains in the market. The type
of catalyst that could drive a more meaningful rally is a resolution of the Middle East conflict. From here,
we like buying on dips and putting on steepeners into flattening moves, where we see better value on that
side of the range. We expect 10-year Treasuries to trade in a 4.80–5.30 range, and think it will be
challenging for yields to rally beyond that. On positioning, CTAs remain short, while hedge funds are
currently positioned lightly, having unwound sizeable long-duration positions — mostly in Europe and to
a lesser degree in the US — during the earlier sell-off, which was painful for them. Real money has moved
from underweight duration to less underweight, which has likely contributed to a portion of the rally, with
real money buying a touch more post-CPI. (Thank You Mike Mitchell – Government Trading)
• An on-hold decision is likely at this week’s FOMC, which comes against the backdrop of still present
inflation pressures but a clearer dose of financial conditions tightening. With market pricing through mid-
2027 roughly aligned with an every-other-meeting pace, the Committee's relative emphasis between those
two factors (inflation and FCI) is likely to inform the degree of stickiness in very front-end pricing. Belly
longs typically perform relatively better through rate hikes and the immediate aftermath, and while the
valuation asymmetry has moderated, we continue to think positioning for belly outperformance is a
sensible bullish-leaning expression. The midterm elections are a potential source of post-FOMC market
volatility; given limited focus on the potential implication of different outcomes for fiscal policy, we think
the initial rates market response largely depends on perceived medium-term implications for AI regulation
and any developments in the Iran conflict around the event. Recent evidence suggests a shift in risks
around the trajectory for AI investment would likely dominate as a driver of duration. (Thank You GS
Rates Strategy)
FX:
• The options market is pricing in a 45bp gap, which looks rich relative to historical FOMC meetings.
Given how much is already embedded, owning ATM vol here is difficult to justify unless we get the
hawkish communication slip-up from Warsh. Instead, USD skew remains relatively cheap. Front-end
GBPUSD risk reversals look like attractive protection for a hawkish Fed outcome. In spot space, the
combination of central bank meetings, month end flows, and the marginally better risk backdrop favor
selective USD strength here. We think EUR will remain heavy, with scope to retest previous lows of
1.1113. Elsewhere, BOJ risk is a more dovish message than the market expects. We would however use
any following rally in USDJPY as an opportunity to sell USDs. Finally, we think the commodity
currencies can continue to outperform and would look to sell USDCAD ahead of a BOC that we think can
sound hawkish. (Carlie Ladda FX Delta One and Sarah Stone FX Options)
Equities:

• This month, equities have seen a good amount of stress on a large TMT pullback culminating in a
meaningful VIX vol event. We’ve seen vol start to perform on some of the larger moves to the downside
and tails have stayed extremely firm as hedging demand remains strong. The narrative around AI growth
has grown more bearish post credit concerns surrounding neocloud funding (specifically in reaction to the
CRWV selloff), which makes this week’s big tech earnings particularly important to investors. On top of
that, the street is still paying close attention to geopolitical headlines – Iran war developments are top of
mind heading into US midterm elections next week. Despite swaps pricing in a low likelihood of a rate
hike during this week’s FOMC meeting, there’s no shortage of upcoming catalysts that are keeping index
vols and skew at historically elevated levels on a YTD lookback. After the recent pullback in equities,
SPX spot is now chopping around the 50-day moving average and the bulk of dealer long gamma sits to
the topside. Going into the next set of catalysts (Election, Tech Earnings and FOMC, the desk thinks that
options continue to be the best way to play these events). At this point, SPX put spreads look like the best
way to play for a selloff with skew still quite steep (SPX 6Nov 7200 7550 put spread costs 29.5 dollars ref
7720f). For a rally, the desk thinks that calls are cheap enough to own outright (SPX 6Nov 7750 call costs
50.3 ref 7720f). (Thank You Cindy Lu and Robert Blank – Index Derivs Trading)
• Our and the market’s expectations for Wednesday’s FOMC meeting is that the committee will keep the
funds rate unchanged, but that the Fed will hike in December. The press conference is likely to remain
focused on the potential need for ongoing tightening, but with a hike by December almost fully priced and
nearly two more hikes after that, the bar for Chairman Warsh to sound hawkish relative to market pricing
is also quite high. This week’s calendar is crowded with big tech earnings and PCE alongside the Fed. The
setup is challenging with several crosscurrents (namely AI and energy risks) and the midterms coming up,
but it may be that clearing the FOMC meeting and big tech earnings sets up a locally friendlier patch for
risk assets, as clearing the FOMC has for the last two meetings. We would want that equity upside in
protected form because there are risks to that view from oil escalation, the midterms, and shifting AI
sentiment. USD shorts in options form, given the lower implied volatilities in FX, might offer exposure to
the Fed meeting if it is perceived as more dovish. (Thank You Vickie Chang – Macro Research)
Credit:
• The market is pricing no hike, and the rates rally that followed looks like it is holding. Against this
backdrop, we think it is time to favor CDX HY over CDX IG again locally (tactical compression). Lower
yields take pressure off the weakest balance sheets, so HY has the most room to retrace if the relief holds.
They also give the AI-capex names in IG another shot at coming to the primary market. In our view, CDX
IG was insulated on the way wider by gamma into this week's expiry. With most of that rolling off, it is
now exposed to continued volatility in IG AI CDS, as well as continued European weakness on the
Main/IG pair. A benign FOMC (no hike, as the market is pricing) and continued stabilization in rates and
vol would, on the other hand, help CDX HY stabilize and find continued strong footing from US core long
real money demand. (Thank You Eric Wu – Index Derivs Trading)
Commods:

• Oil rallied sharply to six-month highs as the market repriced odds of a more violent new wave of
American and Israeli attacks against Iran. Military assets movements and rhetoric from the administration
signaled a higher likelihood of large-scale hostilities potentially before the mid-terms (sooner than was
previously expected). Iran countered with threats to retaliate against energy and economic assets
throughout the Gulf. The risk of longer-term disruption to production and exports led the back end of the
crude curve to rally, outperforming the front. While the physical markets were balanced in October thanks
to large volumes of AG exports through STS, spreads struggled to keep up with the rally in back end flat
price. Front vol and call skew finally found a bid, after months of selling off to below pre-war levels, as
traders rushed for upside convexity on those threats. The best performer in the vol space has been more
deferred vols in cal27, supported by spec demand for both calls and puts (the renormalization trade is still
the dominant one, further and further down the curve), and producer put hedging demand. (Thank You
Edouard Mifsud – Crude & Crude Dervis Trading)
