GS TMT SPEC SALES – Wednesday 28th October
Overnight earnings helped steady the AI trade: Celestica rose 12%, while SK Hynix gained 10.5% after a
Q3 beat and a $45bn shareholder return plan, lifting Samsung 8% ahead of its October 29 results. Seagate
fell 5% after hours despite an in-line quarter and stronger guidance; but expectations were raised post
Western Digital, SanDisk reports tomorrow. Nasdaq 100 up% and momentum gain 2.5% following a
roughly 20% drawdown, with high-beta momentum up around 8% over three sessions. Thirty-year
hyperscaler bonds led the risk-on move in credit, while data centres outperformed in equities. Attention
now turns to Alphabet, Microsoft(tonight) and Amazon tomorrow.
Better earnings, oversold conditions and easing selling pressure are supporting the bounce, and the TMT
desk sees tentative renewed buying across AI. However, after roughly three months of performance
struggles, investors may want steadier prices and fewer outstanding risks before adding materially; choppy
trading remains the base case.

KEY RESULTS

• ADYEN: - Topline is decent but it’s the margin messaging that is going to be the debate today. The
positive is it delivered on the topline at 20% organic (200bps was inorganic) and reiterated the revenue
guide for the FY (Net revenue growth between +21-23% y/y on a constant currency basis) – so tick the
box as implies Q4 accelerates. The issue is the margin messaging that is new it now expecting 50%
margins for 2026 vs. street at 52% so a 4% EBITDA downgrade for FY26– this to relate to scaling its
recent acquisitions. It is withdrawing its medium-term margin guide (previously was above 55% by FY28,
while for FY 26 it was broadly inline with FY25 so 53% margins and including the recent acquisitions
street was moving down towards 52%). On the analyst call back the positive points adyen are making is
that the 2026 cohort is strongest ever. On the margins it is saying the margin reset is 1) an investment
mostly into people…senior folks in SF in general (TBF this makes sense as senior staff retention has been
a debate amongst investors) and 2) related to acquisitions that it’s looking to scale more aggressively
(should help topline going fwd). Saying it removed guide as the investments that need to be done to
capture to growth opportunity, they wouldn’t have been able to make it to 55pct by 2028. Suggesting that
flattish margins is the right ballpark. While on the medium-term topline growth, saying that the building
blocks mentioned at cmd is very much intact, this investment to likely add on top and help durability of
this level of growth for a longer period of time….question is will street upgrade the topline and trim
margins or just trim margins and wait to see on the topline? See GIR take
• DSY: The headlines look better than the underlying picture: the Q3 beat was driven by licences, while
the upgraded full-year guidance largely reflects the inclusion of ArisGlobal. The stock has rallied strongly,
and it is unclear whether a closer look at the results will leave investors more bullish. The next catalyst is
the CMD on 17 November. Q3 revenue beat expectations by 2%, largely driven by licences, which grew
14% versus consensus of 2.5% and beat revenue expectations by 14%. Recurring revenue missed, with
organic growth of 3% versus consensus of 4.2%. ARR has yet to accelerate, so we will need to wait
another quarter for evidence of improvement. The most encouraging development was Life Sciences,
which appears to have stabilised: growth was 1%, including some M&A, versus consensus of −1%.
Medidata was flat and has a new CEO. Core grew in the mid-single digits, broadly in line with consensus.
Operating profit beat expectations by 4$, benefiting from the licence revenue beat. Full-year guidance was
raised, but excluding ArisGlobal, the outlook is largely unchanged. Revenue is now forecast at €6.40–
6.46bn, or 4–5% growth, versus €6.30–6.42bn and 3–5% previously. Consensus is €6.37bn but excludes
the acquisition. Operating margin guidance rose to 32.5–32.7% from 32.2–32.6%, again helped by the
licence beat. For Q4 Street is 5.6% organic growth vs. guide is 5 -8% not clear what street has in for M&A
– so at best inline. See GIR take
• KPN In line. Group services revenues 0.4% ahead, Adj EBITDAaL 0.9% ahead. Good cost savings in
Q3 and on track to meet the FY guidance. In the mix reassurance on B2B LCE will be received well.On
the consumer side they lost 9k consumers in broadband – this is a large decline relative to -1k last Q but is
due to a one off change in terms and conditions. FCF looks like a beat but this seems due to phasing and
first 9M FCF flat yoy. Expect stock to trade flat. See GIR takev
• TELENOR: – Q3 mixed and FY guide reiterated. Q3 Nordics service revenue -0.6% vs cons, while
group EBITDA 2% beat related to opex savings in Q3 (but deferrals to Q4 as in its pulled from Q4).

However Nordics EBITDA in line. On the trends, there will be questions around the ongoing subdued
growth in Norway and Finland. WC was +NOK675mn and looks like a big beat, but it is supported by a

expectations were low and stock was a relative short, expect stock to trade flat today.
• Visa: reported a roughly in line quarter and guided relatively in-line with expectations for FY2027.
Will/GIR expectsv this quarter to be somewhat of a clearing event as Visa set the stage for growth to
remain solidly double digits, and put a floor under VAS expectations in the low to mid 20s, which was
roughly in-line with the midpoint of the 2027 net revenue sensitivity analysis we published in our earnings
preview (despite expecting to renew 25% of their volume, from 20% in FY26).
• ASM – Beat and raise. Q3 revs 5% beat, GP 6% beat. For Q4 2026, expect revenue of €1.23mn +/-5% at
cc (+3% vs cons at midpoint). For 2027, guides to revs >€5.7 billion at cc (5% ahead of cons, and up from
top end of €3.7-€4.6bn range given at CMD). Positive commentary on Advanced logic/foundry that is

strong momentum in 1.4nm; ASM can now confirm a solid double-digit increase in served available
market at 1.4nm compared with 2nm, together with higher market share. ASM also expect strong growth
in memory; based on current visibility. ADRs looking up 8%, Call at 2pm today. Overall a solid print and
the first beat in this space to be rewarded so far – representative of the mood of the market today. (See
GIR take )

• Logitech fell 7% after hours despite a beat and better guide (margins could have been better). Q2 revenue
in-line, gross margin 3.6% ahead of expectations and EBIT 15% ahead. Growth in gaming, video
collaboration, pointing devices, keyboards and combos was partly offset by weakness in tablet accessories
and webcams. The disrupted semiconductor supplier reopened in September and is ramping production,

issue fully resolved by Q4 FY27. The Q3 revenue headwind is now $70–80m, versus $200m previously
expected. Q3 guidance is $1.36–1.43bn revenue (−4% to +1% year on year in USD; −5% to flat in
constant currency), 41–42% non-GAAP gross margin versus 43.5% consensus, and $220–255m operating
income. At the midpoints, revenue guidance is 6% above consensus, but EBIT is 1% below. See GIR take

• Temenos again reported weaker revenue but a cost-driven profit beat. Q3 revenue of $292.8m missed
consensus by 1%, with subscriptions 6% below expectations and maintenance 2% ahead; EBIT of

delayed large African deal, without which growth would have exceeded the guidance/consensus
benchmark of more than 30%. Guidance remains unchanged for ARR growth at c.12%, subscription and
SaaS growth at c.9% in constant currency (consensus 7%; GIR 10%). To hit the Q4 subscription and SaaS
guidance requires growth of 16%. On call mgt saying the required sequential increase is similar to Q4
2025, with the delayed deal helping the comparison and a similar share of large deals still assumed.
Management is investing to improve deal execution but did not quantify how much Q4 business is
secured; a growing reliance on large deals since Q2 2025 adds quarterly volatility. On ARR the recent
North American wins initially contribute to ARR rather than revenue, with more revenue expected in Q4
and a larger impact from 2027. FY26 EBIT growth guidance raised to c.12% in constant currency from
c.9% (consensus c.10%), while reported EPS growth guidance increased to c.10% from c.7%. and
reported free cash flow growth at c.16%—See GIR take

TELCOS
• Orange: A solid call—the message was that French trading is resilient, consolidation is on track and
Spain is gradually improving. Management views satellite and agentic AI as opportunities to integrate into
its offering, rather than immediate threats to pricing. Net/net street estimates need to go up….
** French consolidation: The timetable is unchanged, with signing targeted by end-2026 and closing by
end-2027 (Bouygues recently suggested things could slip a little). Employee consultations and antitrust
work are progressing in parallel, and management does not currently expect employee legal action to
delay signing. 6 December is the long-stop date. On the 4pm reiterating this. Prenotification already
started.
** French ARPU: There is some investor debate about convergent ARPU, which is trending down: it was
stable to slightly higher quarter on quarter, but slightly lower year on year, mainly because of the mix of
new customers rather than changes in the existing base. On the 4pm Orange saying the pressure is more at
low end of market and back to school was not aggressive this Q.
** Spain: Management expects further improvement in Q4, prioritising customer retention and value over
aggressive customer acquisition.
** Satellite: Orange has signed a letter of intent with DT, TEF and Vodafone to explore a bid for MSS
spectrum in H2 2027. Implementation is envisaged in 2029, with wholesale access for other European

operators. The aim is to complement terrestrial coverage and enable seamless switching between terrestrial
and satellite networks. Management was clear that Orange is not planning to launch its own satellites.
** Agentic AI: Management does not see automatic pressure on pricing. European prices are already
transparent, and cheaper alternatives are widely available, yet Orange continues to differentiate its offering
and reduce churn. Its focus is on integrating its apps and services into the agent ecosystem.
• AMT (watch US TELCOS again) – on the call the only question anyone cares about was question 1 on
Starlink and while mgt would not comment directly on the talks the message was that Starlink is a
potential scale new customer and they are encouraged by its public statements and steps to build the
spectrum portfolio needed to compete. AMT would not disclose specific discussions with Starlink but
expects any large-scale network builder to consider it a leading tower partner, citing its site locations,
quality and ability to bring sites on air faster than competitors. Starlink will provide details on its network
plans and rollout timing. "We have an unmatched portfolio in the U.S. in terms of where our sites are
located, the quality of those sites. We can get them on air faster than anybody else. We should be the first
call that everybody makes. You should assume that that is kind of what is happening there. We are excited
about the prospect, and in terms of timing and things like that, we will let them speak to what their
network plans and their deployment cadence will be."
Another interesting question on the call concerned DISH’s equipment, particularly given the Verizon
CEO’s view that Starlink is at least 4–6 years away from becoming a real competitor as it takes time to
build a network. According to reporting on DISH’s bankruptcy and lease disputes, it had deployed more
than 144,000 radios across over 24,000 US tower sites when it stopped paying tower companies. This
raises a key question: could buying that equipment allow Starlink to bring those sites online more quickly?
