Global Equities: A Return To Defensives
And Selective Broadening
Zain Nizami
28-Oct-2026, 06:56 ET (EDT)
Fast moving recovery from extremes. The oversold bounce in Tech has likely happened and all eyes
remain on oil.
From the extreme setup on Thursday, which looked overdone at the time, there has been a significant
recovery in prices. Semiconductor stocks have significantly outperformed software, volatility has fallen, and
cyclicals have outperformed defensives. The oversold bounce has probably already happened.
Investor sentiment remains fragile, and I would not confuse the tactical price recovery I thought was possible
from Thursday’s conditions with a resumption of risk addition. This came on the back of increased risk
management led net shorts (to hedge) which created the oversold conditions to start with, and then the
subsequent exhaustive recovery. The increase in US nets is in the chart below; Europe remains high. This
makes the market susceptible to squeezes, some of which have been observed over the last day or so, and I
would say Europe is a coiled spring if energy prices fall. While oil is also lower, that gauge is probably one of
the more important data points going forward. Most roads to broadening do rely on oil falling so geopolitical
developments are crucial.
My bias would be to fade long delta and remain cautious about re-risking or chasing Tech. There has
been a significant pickup in Tech flows and strong crossing rates, but the willingness to add incremental risk in
Tech is still not apparent as I think about the incremental dollar invested in a fragile tape and for the time of
year. I would be fading these moves which are likely an unwind of the increased hedging nets.
For portfolio construction, a return to defensives alongside selective broadening looks more logical than
chasing the rebound, with the broadening leg importantly dependent on oil moving lower. If Oil moved lower,
that would present significantly different investment conditions for broader (non Tech) cyclicality and RoW
indices. That remains the most exciting investment proposition, but we need to see Oil move lower in a
meaningful and persistent way.

The initial oversold conditions were partly created by increased risk-management-led net shorts and hedging.
The subsequent recovery has therefore also had a squeeze component. US nets have increased, while
Europe remains high, leaving the market susceptible to further squeezes.
OAT/Bund stabilisation is helpful from extreme levels but the lack of worsening weekend datapoints helping
here is a function of just how negative the pricing was, rather than material solutions to the main problems.
UBS Research’s CTA work points in the same direction. Momentum has deteriorated across US tech-heavy
indices, Nasdaq positioning has roughly halved over the past two weeks, and a further $1.2 bn of selling is
projected. S&P longs have also declined, while improving Dow momentum could attract $1.5bn of buying.
European signals remain mixed. In other words, we have already seen a meaningful defensive rotation from
CTAs, but it does not appear complete. View the CTA note
There are several catalysts to navigate over the next week or so, with earnings, geopolitics and the FOMC
particularly important. Fragility in sentiment, and focus on performance, is likely to lead to over-sensitivity over
these data points. There is a consensus view that the pent-up cash levels will be deployed as we clear these
hurdles and post mid terms, but for now it’s about navigating the fragility of Tech investing.
Expectations for UK budget surprises on Wednesday remain low. A benign outcome would likely be what
investors want given the gradual improvement in confidence, although the potential for an election call cannot
be ruled out. That would create a near-term hurdle for risk addition, even if it ultimately became a clearing
event in the fullness of time.
