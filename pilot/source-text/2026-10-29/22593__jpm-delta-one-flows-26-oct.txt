In this publication, we analyze flow and positioning metrics for global futures and 
US-listed ETFs across asset classes. 
Weekly Highlights
ETF investors aggressively bought the Semis dip again as Semiconductor ETFs 
logged record inflows. Regionally, Canada stood out with strong inflows, while Brazil 
was quiet in the final week ahead of the runoff election. Non-tech sectors were weak, 
led by Utilities. In Fixed Income, Munis stayed firmly bid and intermediate Treasuries 
saw renewed demand, while Money Market ETFs flipped to outflows. Cross‑asset 
hedges were pared back: gold was sold and managed-futures ETFs saw material 
outflows. In futures, equities and rates both saw ~1z net buying, while leveraged funds 
covered RTY shorts but added to Ultra Bond shorts. CTA signals point to de‑risking 
in US and Japan equities as volatility picked up, and to additional pressure on Brazil 
assets in this morning’s sell‑off.
ETFs
•
Asset classes: Equities were near-average ($23.4Bn, -0.2z), Fixed Income was 
strong ($15.2Bn, 0.6z), Commodities saw outflows (-$1.6Bn, -0.9z), and 
Currency/Multi-asset was roughly flat.
•
Regions: US inflows were near average ($17Bn, -0.1z). International DM was 
below-average ($5.4Bn, -0.5z), including moderate outflows from Europe (-
0.5z) but strong inflows to Canada (1.4z). Brazil flows were near-flat after two 
consecutive weeks of strong inflows into the final election round.
•
Sectors: Semis led for a second straight week, with a pivot toward unlevered 
products. Semiconductor ETFs took in a record ~$12.7Bn (4.3z; SOXX +
$6.5Bn, SOXL +$2.8Bn, SMH +$2.6Bn), surpassing the prior $9.3Bn record 
from late-July. Outside semis, flows were broadly weak again, led by Utilities 
(-2.4z), Materials (-1.4z), Financials (-1.1z), and Staples (-1z).
•
Styles: Defined outcome ($0.9Bn, 1.3z) drew strong inflows, while Multi-
factor (-1.4z), Volatility (-0.8z) and Thematic (-0.7z) ETFs saw outflows.
•
Levered ETFs: Levered products saw another strong week (~$4.3Bn, 1.7z), 
led by SOXL (+$2.8Bn, its 2nd highest ever, but well below last week’s +
$7.2Bn), TQQQ (+$1.3Bn), and SOXS (+$0.8Bn).
•
Fixed Income: Munis ($4.4Bn, 1.8z) posted a 4th straight week of strong 
(though decelerating) inflows. Medium-term gov’t bonds ($2.8Bn, 2.1z) and 
Corporates (0.7z) also drew strong demand, while Money Market (-$0.9Bn, 
-1.1z) and Loans (-1.2z) recorded outflows.
•
Commodities: Precious Metals (-$1.6Bn, -0.9z) saw strong outflows, 
concentrated in GLD (-$2.3Bn). Agriculture also saw notable outflows (-1.3z).
•
Currency/Multi-Asset: Liquid Alts posted their first weekly outflows in ~3 
months (-1.5z), mainly from managed futures products.

Futures
•
Flows: Last week we saw large net buying (>1.5z) in S&P 500, FTSE 100, FTSE Taiwan, 
TAIEX, UST Long Bond, UK 10y and Copper, and large net selling (<-1.5z) in Dow 
Jones, SMI, China A50, NIFTY and CAD futures.
•
CTAs: CTAs likely sold US equities as short-term signals turned negative on SPX/NDX 
and volatility rose. They also likely sold Japan equities and are near neutral across 
APAC overall (while still short China), and sold Bovespa/BRL on this morning’s sell-
off. In rates, they likely trimmed shorts as short-term momentum turned positive in the 
US and Europe. They also flipped short US Natgas, remain short metals (ex-Copper), and 
are long USD vs DM FX (ex-JPY).
•
CFTC Positioning: Asset Managers added to EAFE (1.5z) and cut VIX shorts (2.1z) 
w/w, likely following the Oct 16 intraday VIX spike. They also extended duration, 
rotating out of UST 2y-10y into Ultra Bond futures. Leveraged funds significantly cut 
RTY shorts (+3z), sold VIX (-1.9z), and increased Ultra Bond shorts (-2.9z). Managed 
Money sold Copper (-1.9z), US Natgas, and Ags.

*Unless otherwise specified, all Z-scores are calculated over a 1Y period. Z-score = (Current Value - Average)/Standard

Flows and Positioning Definitions
1. Net Flows: The net flow traded on a futures contract, calculated across the futures chain. Volumes are classified via an 
attribution of trade-by-trade flows, based on whether the trade price was on the bid side (identified as a sell flow) or offer side 
(identified as a buy flow) of the order book.
2. AM + HF: Asset managers’ and Hedge Funds’ positioning in the relevant futures contract, as reported in the latest CFTC 
Traders in Financial Futures report. Expressed as a Z-score.
3. Non-Commercial: Non-commercial entities’ positioning in the relevant futures contract, as reported in the latest CFTC 
Commitment of Traders report. Expressed as a Z-score.
4. OI Change: The weekly change in the notional value of contracts outstanding for the relevant futures contracts.
5. OI Correlation w/Spot: The 3M correlation between changes in the number of contracts outstanding and the price of the 
future’s underlying spot index. A positive (negative) correlation indicates likely increased (decreased) positioning as open interest 
is growing at the same time as the market is rallying (selling off), indicating the incremental contracts opened are most likely 
initiated by investors looking to go directionally long (short), respectively.
6. CTA Signals: Whether CTAs are likely to be short, neutral, or long a certain futures contract based on key momentum signals – 
see more below.
CTA Signals Definitions
1. CTA Signal: Whether CTAs are likely to be short, neutral, or long in a certain futures contract based on key momentum 
signals.  Our metric looks at seven signals, assigning a score of -1, 0, or 1 depending on whether the index is below/at/above the 
relevant signal. The net score determines our CTA metric. The seven signals we look at are 1M, 3M, 6M, and 12M momentum 
and 50d, 100d, and 200d moving averages. In each cell, the top number is the price level of the signal that indicates where CTAs 
are likely to buy or sell the contract, and the bottom number is the distance from the signal to the current futures price. Cells 
shaded in green are currently long momentum signals and those in red are short signals.
2. 1w Chg: Indicates the change in CTA signals in one week’s time if futures levels are unchanged. “↑” indicates CTAs are likely 
to become longer/less short, “↓” indicates they will become shorter/less long, and “-” indicates no change.
Liquidity Definitions
1. Bid/Ask Spread: The average bid/ask spread on the futures contract, expressed as a percentage of notional, in bps. 
2. Market Depth: The average number of contracts at the top of the order book intraday (averaged across the bid and offer side).
3. ADV: The average daily notional value traded on a particular futures contract.
