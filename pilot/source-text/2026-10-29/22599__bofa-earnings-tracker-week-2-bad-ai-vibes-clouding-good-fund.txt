30% done: healthy beat rate, 3Q still tracking 27% YoY 
After Week 2, results from 123 S&P 500 companies (30% of index earnings) are now in: 
78% beat EPS expectations, just shy of the 80% beat rate after Week 2 last quarter and 
well above the historical post-Week 2 average (65%). Reporters beat EPS by a slim 2% 
in aggregate but a stronger +6% excluding Meta’s one-time litigation charge. With the 
bulk of the index still to report, consensus 3Q EPS growth is still tracking 27% YoY (see 
Exhibit 2).  
Corporates remain optimistic despite more cost pressure  
Our NLP analysis of earnings transcripts suggests corporate sentiment cooled from 2Q’s 
record high but remains elevated, with mentions of “weak demand” still well below 
average. Although mentions of “inflation” and “cost pressures” are up 40% YoY, 
guidance has remained net positive: the ratio of above- to below-consensus EPS guides 
is 1.6x MTD, down from 2.6x in July but above the October average of 1.0x. Analysts’ 4Q 
and 2027 estimates have also edged higher MTD, but the gains have been driven 
entirely by Tech and Energy (see Exhibit 17-Exhibit 18). 
Tech fundamentals strong, cycle fears stronger  
Despite incremental evidence of AI monetization (ad engagement, Muse metrics), Meta 
(recap) fell 7% after outlining plans to “continue investing aggressively,” potentially 
raising “hundreds of billions” to fund investments over the next few years. Meanwhile, 
LRCX (recap) fell 12% as supply constraints capped 4Q guidance roughly in line with 
consensus. The breadth of positive EPS revisions in Info Tech remains the strongest of 
any sector (nearly 5x more upward than downward revisions to 2027 EPS MTD), but 
fears that today’s AI boom is paving the way for tomorrow’s bust have put the sector on 
pace for its worst month vs. the S&P 500 since DeepSeek in January 2025 (-5ppt MTD). 
Macro trumps micro: 3Q prints not driving dispersion 
Companies that beat EPS expectations outperformed by an average of 0.7ppt the next 
day, below the historical average of 1.0ppt and, surprisingly, in line with companies that 
missed EPS expectations (misses historically underperform by 2ppt the next day). One-
day moves after reporting have also been smaller than usual, averaging 3.7% vs. 5.3% in 
2Q. Instead, AI anxiety, rate relief and higher oil have driven a rotation out of Momentum 
and into Value and Cash Deployment (see Exhibit 28). Amid fears of overinvestment, 
investors are rewarding near-term cash generation: High FCF/EV leads all factors we 
track MTD, up 11.4% and more than 15ppt ahead of the average Momentum factor. 
Up next: busiest week, AI still in focus 
180 S&P 500 companies that account for over a third of index earnings are scheduled to 
report this week, including Google (10/28), Microsoft (10/28), and Amazon (10/29). All 
eyes will be on AI monetization vs. spending, with hyperscalers’ 2027 capex estimates 
already revised up by nearly 90% YTD.

 3Q26 Earnings: Week 2 
Earnings commentary 
TMT 
• 
Meta (Interactive Media) – Earnings call: “We anticipate 2026 capital expenditures, including principal payments on finance 
leases, to be in the range of $135 billion to $145 billion, narrowed from our prior outlook of $130 billion to $145 
billion…Looking beyond 2026…we expect to continue investing aggressively to support both our core business and 
emerging AI initiatives…Our AI investments are already delivering substantial incremental advertising revenue through 
stronger engagement, better recommendations, and more efficient monetization” 14-C: “[M]anagement had previously 
presented three forecast cases to the Board during a regularly scheduled Board meeting in May, and that, in each case, these 
long-term forecasts showed the Company’s need to raise hundreds of billions of dollars over a period of several years 
beginning as early as fall 2026.” 
• 
SpaceX (Telecom) – “There are many customers asking for compute, and we're actively building the supply to meet both 
internal and external demand. We expect to have additional new customers in future deals and will continuously evaluate the 
highest margin usage for our compute assets across both new and existing cloud service agreements and serving inference for 
our own SI products… At current pricing, new compute deployments are generating a payback of less than a year.” 
• 
Texas Instruments (Semis) – “So, look, we are seeing, as I said, the broadening of the demand coming across the three 
main markets, that we focus on, for the long-term, Industrial, Automotive, and Data Center. But in general, the 
demand was strong across the board, across markets, sectors and regions.” 
• 
Lam Research (Semi Equipment) – “As we move into calendar year 2027, we see cleanroom space availability and supply-
chain constraints for certain components continuing to limit chipmakers and equipment companies' ability to fully 
meet end demand.” 
Industrials 
• 
United Rentals (Equipment Rental) – “By vertical, the trends of the first half of the year carried into the third quarter. 
Construction continues to grow across non-residential and infrastructure. And on the industrial side, power once again 
posted double-digit growth, as did metals and minerals. Now, as I mentioned earlier, a variety of end markets drive our 
growth…With this in mind, we saw projects kick off in the third quarter across numerous end markets, including energy, 
manufacturing, infrastructure, and data centers.” 
Consumer 
• 
Capital One Financial (Consumer Finance) – “So pulling way up on credit, we think credit is in a solid – in a very good place. 
We'll note that delinquencies have been kind of moving with seasonality for a few months now as opposed to beating it. 
But all told, we see it in a very good place.” 
• 
American Airlines (Airlines) – “Premium unit revenue increased 18.5% year-over-year, outperforming Main Cabin unit 
revenue by more than 4 points… Corporate demand remains strong and American is capturing meaningful share as our 
managed corporate revenue increased 28% year-over-year, our sixth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth.” 
• 
Tractor Supply (Retail Farm Store) – “We're also encouraged by spending trends among younger customers and the 
performance of our rural stores. Our younger demographic led comparable sales growth across age groups in the third 
quarter, with strength among Gen Z and millennials. And across our store base, rural locations return to more positive 
comp growth and, again, outperformed the other population density groups.”

Still early, but guidance has remained constructive so far 
The three-month guidance ratio (the # of above- vs. below-consensus quarterly or annual 
EPS guides) was 2.3x as of September, the strongest reading since 2021. By sector, 
Tech and Financials saw the most favorable guidance trends, while Utilities and 
consumer sectors saw the weakest.  
So far in October, there have been 21 above-consensus EPS guides, 13 below-consensus 
guides and 22 neutral, bringing the ratio of above-consensus to below-consensus guides 
to 1.6x, above the October average of 1.0x although off peak levels we saw this summer 
(2.6x in July).

BofA spending data suggest consumer spending remains healthy  
Bank of America aggregated credit and debit card spending per household increased 
6.2% YoY in September and 5.1% excluding gasoline, both the third strongest growth 
rates over the past four years. The strength was broad, with 71% of retail categories 
posting positive transaction growth YoY. By income, the top 5% saw the strongest 
spending growth, followed by the lower-income cohort, which has been supported by 
stronger wage growth (see BofA Institute’s latest Consumer Checkpoint for more). 
 
While the consumer has remained resilient so far, history suggests it can take multiple 
quarters for higher oil to dent consumer spending, posing downside risk to 4Q if 
geopolitical conflicts remain unresolved (see Oil FAQs).

Beats are now the norm; 2027 risks are macro vs. micro 
Following big positive surprises in the last two quarters, healthy beats are the status quo 
and may not be enough to drive stocks higher. Investors will be listening for reassurance 
that growth can be sustained (consensus 2027E EPS +16% vs. 2026E >30%) despite 
rates and commodity pressure. 2027 risks are skewed to the downside in our view (we 
forecast +12% YoY, see Outlook). Ultimately, index EPS remains highly dependent on the 
durability of the AI capex cycle and successful monetization, but the interplay between 
geopolitics, rates and Tech may be underestimated – a higher cost of capital and 
geopolitical bottlenecks could gum up what has so far been a mostly smooth story.

Appendix 
Guidance Ratios 
Earnings guidance: We track the number of instances of above- vs. below-consensus 
management guidance for earnings over the last three months for S&P 500 companies. 
If a company issues changes to its outlook more than once in a one-month period, we 
incorporate all instances of guidance into our aggregate number. The ratio also includes 
all instances of above- or below-consensus earnings guidance issued by a company (for 
example, if they issue both quarterly and annual guidance). The one-month and three-
month revision ratios are calculated as they are for estimate revision ratios. The data 
source is Bloomberg. For companies that provide both GAAP and Operating guidance, or 
for REITs that provide both EPS and FFO guidance, we remove one data point if both 
data points provide the same guidance direction, otherwise both data points are used.  
Capex guidance: We track the number of instances of above- vs. below-consensus 
management guidance for planned capex over the last three months for S&P 500 
companies, calculated the same way as above; data source is FactSet. 
 
Earnings Revision ratios 
Beginning in April 1986 (and January 2006 for Real Estate), we calculate the ratio 
between the number of companies in the S&P 500 for which Thomson Financial 
consensus earnings estimates have been raised versus those that have been lowered. 
We apply the same calculation to the S&P 500 GICS Sectors (where we use FFO for 
REITs within Real Estate). One-month revision ratios are computed as the number of 
companies for which estimates this month are higher than they were last month, divided 
by the number of companies for which estimates this month are lower than they were 
last month. For example, within a particular subgroup of the market, if consensus 
estimates have increased for 5 companies and have decreased for 10, the estimate 
revision ratio will be 0.5 (5 divided by 10). 
The three-month revision ratio is similarly defined as the total number of earnings 
estimate increases divided by total number of earnings estimate decreases during the 
last three months. For example, in February 2002, the three-month revision ratio is 
computed as the total number of companies with increased monthly estimates, divided 
by the total number of companies with decreased estimates throughout December 2001, 
January 2002, and February 2002. 
We similarly calculate the S&P 600 (small cap) earnings revision ratios (one-month and 
three-month) by the same methodology, with data beginning in February 2001. 
For earnings/sales estimates, we look at the change in the FY1 estimate (current 
unreported year) if there's more than 120 days between the current date and the 
company's fiscal year-end date, otherwise we look at the change in the FY2 estimate 
(next unreported year). 
Methodology: Earnings Calls Sentiment 
With the help of BofA's Predictive Analytics team, we parsed through earnings calls 
transcripts to calculate sentiment for the S&P500 universe of companies. We use the 
Loughran McDonald's financial dictionary to calculate sentiment scores as per the 
definition below. 
Sentiment score = No. of Unique positive words - No. of unique negative and uncertainty 
words 
The sentiment score is computed with three different filters: the full transcript, 
management discussion and answers of CEO/CFO from Q/A section. Calculated scores 
were then averaged. Loughran-McDonald Sentiment and Uncertainty: 
1. 
Loughran-McDonald Sentiment: Examples of positive words include accomplish, 
achieve, outperform, stabilize, strength and negative words such as abandon, 
abnormal, downturn, evade, failing, stagnate. In total, the lexicon has 2,355 
negative words and 354 positive words.

ambiguity, hidden, fluctuate, doubts, unclear. In total, the lexicon has 297
