 
"The Tail Is Wagging The Dog": 0DTE Traders Now 
Suppress S&P Volatility More Than Dealer Gamma 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, OCT 26, 2026 - 04:00 AM 
By Christian Daas, Bloomberg Markets live strategist and reporter 
While some investors position for AI earnings, geopolitics and interest rates to send stocks 
either to new highs or into a tailspin, zero-day option traders have increasingly bet that the S&P 
500 Index will remain calm. 
An analysis of 0DTE options flow from UBS derivatives strategist Kieran Diamond shows a 
marked shift toward tactical traders buying calls and selling puts when the market declines, and 
doing the opposite during rallies. 
The net effect of what they term a “counter-trend delta bias” is the equivalent of buying low and 
selling high, which further suppresses swings on the S&P 500.  
Zero-day to expiry options have taken on an outsized importance in the daily moves of the 
equities gauge, with around 66% of the overall volume expiring within a day, according to 
Garrett DeSimone at OptionMetrics. That’s increased the focus on short-term positioning as 
both retail and institutional traders seek insight on how options flows are influencing the $66 
trillion US benchmark index.  
“This counter-trend trading pattern seems to be a response to very rangebound markets,” said 
Diamond. “The magnitude of these options flows means that this can start to look self-fulfilling, 
with options investors helping to limit price moves in either direction.” 
 

 
 
This intraday tactical shift since 2024 is notable, with larger volumes trading on smaller moves, 
helping limit S&P 500 realized volatility. The pattern resembles the stabilizing effect of positive 
gamma, with traders buying into dips and selling into rallies, much like option dealers who are 
long gamma and similarly buy declines and sell rallies to stay delta-neutral. 
The counter-trend trading bias is one factor that has helped contain moves. Another is the long 
option-dealer gamma regime often seen in the S&P 500, much of which is driven by the 
prevalence of a big institutional trader selling one-day iron condors — a put spread and a call 
spread. 
The growth of the 0DTE counter-trend trading flow is now so great that the net effect outweighs 
the impact from the option dealer re-hedging, according to UBS.  
 

 
 
To be sure, the pattern could change if the market has an extended selloff, beyond the 
AI-focused declines that pushed the S&P 500 down 1.2% on Thursday. 
“While the counter-trend flow has so far been very consistent, there’s nothing to stop this 
dynamic reversing in a period of higher volatility,” Diamond said. “If 0DTE investors decide to 
instead chase a move lower by buying puts, then their flows could exacerbate the move, and 
also leave market makers managing short gamma risks.” 
While the impact on the S&P 500 has been to suppress moves, some of the short-term trading 
in the technology sector, for instance, has had the opposite effect. That’s given rise to 
successful intraday momentum strategies with flows tending more to exacerbate rallies and 
declines. 
“Dealer hedging associated with 0DTE options can reinforce intraday momentum or contribute 
to mean reversion, depending on the prevailing gamma environment,” said Adrien Geliot, chief 
executive officer of Premialab. “We saw strong performance from semiconductor-focused 
intraday momentum strategies during the April–June rally, while the current interest in SPX 
reversal signals highlights how these dynamics can differ across market conditions.” 
 

 
He added that the number of live strategies for that segment in Premialab’s QIS database has 
increased 50% since the end of March 2025.  
“The key challenge is translating these signals into strategies that can capture these effects 
consistently, while accounting for execution costs,” Geliot said. 
In general, the demand for option positioning insight is growing, with more retail platforms 
entering the space and providing market color. Each has its own methodology to map intraday 
option Greeks — the gamma, vanna and charm metrics that signal how traders and dealers 
may need to act — with the aim of giving an edge to market speculators. 
The boom in 0DTE trading has only increased the relevance of positioning as an important 
technical driver of intraday price action. The delta-adjusted notional value in the S&P 500 option 
complex now easily surpasses the average daily volume in the E-Mini futures — a sign that the 
options “tail” is at times wagging the stocks “dog.” 
 
The greater access to data and client demand for insight has driven some institutional providers 
to refine their estimates of net dealer positioning. While early frameworks generally assumed 
that all calls were sold by end clients and all puts were bought, newer models are more 
sophisticated.  
 

 
JPMorgan Chase & Co. derivatives strategists recently wrote that they have moved away from a 
proxy for S&P 500 gamma positioning based on open interest to a more flow-based approach 
using the Cboe data that provide a granular breakdown of whether each option transaction was 
bought or sold by clients.
