DB nailed the tech unwind. Now what?
Credit where it's due. On October 9, Deutsche Bank's Binky Chadha and team tactically downgraded
tech from overweight to neutral, warning of an unfavorable risk/reward setup and a potential sharp
reversal.
The timing was excellent. Mega-cap growth and tech have since fallen 7%, while the rest of the S&P
500 has gained 5%. That's 12 percentage points of underperformance in just two weeks.
The interesting question now is whether the unwind has gone far enough.
DB
Not quite there yet
Despite the violent rotation, DB estimates tech has another roughly 5% of relative downside before
reaching the bottom of its long-term trend channel. That lower boundary has provided support on
several previous occasions. The unwind has been brutal, but the technical reset may not be quite
complete.
DB
The great positioning reset
From crowded to neutral in just two weeks. DB estimates mega-cap growth and tech positioning has
collapsed from the 85th percentile in early October to the 33rd percentile, the lowest in six months and
approaching the lows seen during the Iran-war selloff. Whatever happens next, this is no longer the
same positioning setup that existed when DB sounded the alarm.

DB
The $30 billion paradox
Here's the twist. Despite the tech selloff, investors have poured $30 billion into tech funds over the past
two weeks, including a staggering $12 billion into leveraged ETFs. Meanwhile, the rest of the market
saw $4.5 billion in outflows. Thursday's $1.5 billion tech outflow suggests some of that dip-buying may
already be reversing. The risk is that yesterday's aggressive buyers become tomorrow's forced sellers.
DB
The discretionary/systematic divide
The broader equity market has undergone a major positioning reset, but the divide between investor
groups remains striking. According to DB, discretionary positioning has fallen to the 18th percentile,
while systematic strategies remain at the 79th percentile. Human investors have already turned
cautious, but the machines are still relatively heavily exposed. That leaves an important source of
potential selling pressure should volatility accelerate.
DB
The earnings wildcard
Positioning has collapsed, but the earnings picture tells a very different story. According to DB, current
tech positioning is consistent with earnings growth of just 10%, versus roughly 50% expected for Q3.
The last two major reversals higher in tech coincided with strong earnings releases. With another wave
of results due, the gap between positioning and fundamentals is becoming increasingly interesting.
The next V-turn may be closer than the bears think.
The setup is becoming increasingly asymmetric: further positioning-driven downside is possible, but
strong earnings could force investors to rebuild tech exposure into a market that has already
aggressively derisked.
