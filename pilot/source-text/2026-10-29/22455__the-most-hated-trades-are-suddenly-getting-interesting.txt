The great market shakeout
Markets are bouncing, but the real story is what sits beneath the surface. Semis are holding critical
support as fundamentals remain resilient, systematic selling has intensified, and seasonal tailwinds are
starting to kick in. Meanwhile, France is trading at a record relative discount, gold shorts are piling up,
silver is experiencing a historic washout, and Brazil is facing an increasingly uncomfortable fiscal
reality. Plenty of extremes, and potentially some interesting turning points.
The range survives
SOX bounced perfectly off its 200-day moving average and the lower end of the range. The trading
range in place since May remains intact. Let's see how this develops, but one thing is clear: chasing
breakouts in either direction has been an expensive game. More here.

The real "SOX" economy isn't cracking
Goldman highlights that Korea's electronics sector accounted for roughly 60% of the country's annual
GDP growth in Q3. Tech exports remain strong, machinery investment accelerated, and semiconductor
equipment imports continued to rise. While semiconductor equities have suffered a sharp valuation
reset, the underlying activity data continue to tell a very different story. Another reason to question
whether the recent SOX washout has gone too far.

The semiconductor upside trade
BofA highlights SMH as offering one of the most attractive upside call-spread payouts among liquid
ETFs that have sold off since the October 6 Nasdaq peak. December at-the-money call spreads
targeting a one-standard-deviation rebound offered roughly 2.8x payout potential as of October 26.
With earnings, the Fed and midterms approaching, the bank sees the possibility of a post-catalyst risk-
on reversal. An interesting defined-risk expression of the semiconductor rebound thesis.

CTAs dump Nasdaq

The Santa rally starts here?
According to Deutsche Bank, October 27 has historically marked the final seasonal low for the S&P
500, with equities typically rallying into year-end. The setup is particularly interesting in midterm years,
with the S&P 500 rising over the three months following early October in 21 of the past 23 midterm
years.
With the midterms just one week away, election uncertainty is about to fade just as year-end seasonal
tailwinds kick in. Seasonality is no guarantee, but history suggests the window for a year-end rally is
opening right now.

Nobody wants France
The French equity discount has reached historic proportions. After more than three years of
underperformance, the CAC 40 is trading at its cheapest relative valuation to the Eurozone on record,
according to JPM. Investors have been abandoning French exposure, and sentiment is deeply negative.
The contrarian temptation is obvious. The question is whether cheap is actually cheap enough. More
here.

Silver: Covid-level dislocation
Silver is now trading 25% below its 200-day moving average, the widest discount since the March 2020
liquidation. Before Covid, you have to go back to 2013 to find a comparable dislocation. One of the
most extreme technical washouts in silver's history. The question is whether this is exhaustion selling or
a market repricing a fundamentally weaker outlook. More here.

Brazil's fiscal time bomb
Lula has won another term, but the fiscal arithmetic is getting increasingly uncomfortable. TS Lombard
notes that Brazil's debt-to-GDP ratio has climbed to 81.9%, up from 71.7% when Lula took office in
2023. Under a business-as-usual scenario, debt could reach almost 118% of GDP by 2036. The election
may be over, but the real battle is only beginning: convincing investors that Brazil can stabilize its debt
trajectory.
TS Lombard
Brazil's fiscal cliff
TS Lombard warns that without meaningful fiscal reform, Brazil could face a partial government
shutdown in 2028. Mandatory spending is progressively crowding out discretionary expenditure,
potentially pushing available funding below the minimum needed to operate the government. The
uncomfortable conclusion: Brazil's next fiscal adjustment may be forced by arithmetic rather than
political choice. More on Brazil tomorrow.
