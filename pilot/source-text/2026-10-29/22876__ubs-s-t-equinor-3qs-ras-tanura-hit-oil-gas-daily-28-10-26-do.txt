Spec sales comment
A claimed Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia’s largest refinery, the 550kb/d Ras Tanura facility, has driven oil
prices up this morning with Brent touching $116/b. Saudi Aramco has not yet confirmed the attack, but the
news underscores the challenge of progress towards a ceasefire even as regional leaders engage with Iran to
discourage further attacks. Iranian state media reported on comments from Supreme Leader Khamenei
reiterating Iran’s conditions for ending the war, which seem not to have changed materially since the failure of
the MoU earlier this year. Yesterday’s parliamentary election in Israel and the upcoming US midterms are the
most important imminent catalysts for oil, with recent reports suggesting the US and Israel may be preparing
for a short but intense military escalation following the US midterms designed to pressure Iran into a more
flexible negotiating stance.

#FOMC : The UBS base case for this evening FOMC is rates are held unchanged likely by a 10–2 vote, with
Warsh maintaining a hawkish message that there is still “more work to do” on inflation. The pause reflects both
the proximity of the mid-term elections and the fact that the problem is sticky inflation around 3%, rather than
accelerating inflation or an overheating economy, reducing the urgency to hike immediately. The expectation is
then for 25bp hikes in both December and January, with Warsh emphasizing the inflation trend and
commitment to price stability while avoiding strong forward guidance. Markets broadly align with this near-term
view, pricing only around a 10% chance of an October hike versus 92% for a 25bp December hike and 33bps
cumulatively priced by Jan. Link here
#UK_Budget : UBS Economics - The key message is that the Autumn Budget is likely to be cautious rather
than transformational, with a much tighter fiscal position forcing the government to balance targeted support
and investment against fiscal discipline. Fiscal headroom may have fallen from £23.6bn in March to around
£10bn, with the Chancellor expected to rebuild only a modest buffer of roughly £15bn through smaller tax
increases while deferring tougher welfare and departmental spending decisions to 2027. Near-term priorities
are expected to include cost-of-living measures, housing support and increased public investment. For
markets, maintaining the fiscal rules should be supportive for gilts, GBP is expected to remain relatively
resilient into 2027, and equities offer selective opportunities in housebuilders, property, lower-value consumer
names and public-investment beneficiaries, rather than a broad-based UK equity rerating. A windfall tax
consideration for Banks has been socialised in press in recent weeks - verbal consensus for UK banks
indicates the bank levy moves towards 8% from the current 3%. UBS has modelled 3% -> 6% which translates
to an implied 3-4% cut to NWG/LLOY numbers and 1-2% cut for BARC/HSBC numbers. Link here
