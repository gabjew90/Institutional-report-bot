 
"Peak Frustration": Gross Exposure Finally Cracks 
Into The Most Consequential Earnings Week Of The 
Year 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, OCT 26, 2026 - 03:35 PM 
The S&P 500 fell all of 50bps last week. Two-thirds of its members actually finished green. 
And yet, according to Goldman derivatives guru Brian Garrett, whose latest Weekend Prep note 
(available to pro subs) landed Sunday afternoon, it was the week the market's plumbing finally 
gave way. Here is how he opened, straight from the desk: 
"from the trenches, this week just felt different … conversations on the desk are moving to peak 
frustration as ytd p&l rachets lower with fewer calendar sessions remaining to make it back … 
net exposure flinched about a month ago (remains in 0th pctl) ... gross exposure flinched this 
week (largest weekly de-gross week since july) … all while breadth actually improved (2/3's of 
spx names were green on the week) and the index itself only dropped 50bps wire to wire" 
In other words, the index barely moved while the people who own it ran for the exits. 
Regular readers will recall that just two weeks ago Garrett argued that "Underweight Is 
Overcrowded," with nets so low that the next premium dollar would chase calls, not puts. What 
changed? The one thing that was still holding up: gross. As he puts it, "the damage underneath 
has been anything but benign." 
 

 
 
Below we walk through all 13 of Garrett's charts (they are, as usual, some of the best on the 
Street), and why the one market still pretending nothing happened is the one most people 
watch: the S&P. 
Nets Flinched A Month Ago... Now Gross Has Too 
Start with Goldman's Prime book. US equities have now been net sold for the third straight 
week, and four of the last five. More importantly, Garrett writes, "two weeks ago we flagged that 
net exposure had decreased significantly but gross remained high, that changed this week as 
single stocks collectively saw the largest de-gross activity since late july." 
And it was all longs heading for the door: "the global book net selling was driven entirely by long 
selling … this is risk off flow into one of the most consequential earnings weeks of the year (44% 
of spx prints eps this week)." 
The cash desk saw the same thing. Long-onlies and hedge funds were both better sellers, led 
by TMT, and the desk reports 
23 billion. We

The P&L damage, per Garrett: 
 

 
●​ Fundamental long/short: -1.1% on the week, -3.8% month-to-date, still +8.8% YTD... 
after peaking at +17.4% in June. Put differently, roughly half the year's gains are gone, 
with two months left to make them back. 
●​ Systematic long/short: -2%, its worst week since 2024 (-1.1% MTD, still +18.7% YTD). 
The single ugliest day came Thursday. Garrett's chart of the daily change in Goldman's hedge 
fund VIP basket versus the most-shorted names puts last Thursday's move at roughly -3.4%, in 
the same tail as Covid in March 2020 and DeepSeek Monday. That squares with the VIPs' worst 
day since 2011, which came as two-thirds of the S&P rallied... and the index tumbled. 
 
Recall that hedge fund leverage had already hit "Liberation Day" lows at the start of the month. 
With nets in the 0th percentile and gross now coming down too, the obvious question is how 
much is left to sell. We discussed the buy-the-dip case in Saturday's "The AI Hangover"; 
Garrett's charts suggest the hangover has a few more mornings in it. 
More 5% Days Than The Dot-Com Bust... With A VIX Of 18 
Why is everyone so tired? Garrett's answer is the best chart in the pack: 
"one of the best illustrations on trader exhaustion is the count of >5% daily moves in the gs 
momentum factor basket (ticker, gsprhimo) … this index has seen +/- 5% realized move on 30 
 

 
occasions in 2026 … this is more than any other year in our dataset (2000, 2009, 2020 
inclusive) and we still have 2 months left … i'm not sure we can handle much more flipping of 
the thematic script(s)" 
 
The numbers: 23 such days in the 2000 tech bubble (average VIX 23), 16 in the financial crisis 
(average VIX 31), 21 during Covid (average VIX 29). And 2026? Thirty, with an average VIX of 
just 18.1. In other words, crisis-grade factor violence, priced like a normal year. Diversification at 
its finest. 
The drawdown matches the frequency. Goldman's momentum pair is back near roughly -43% 
from its peak, within a whisker of the August low. 
 

 
 
Goldman isn't the only one counting. As we tweeted this morning, the crash in TMT momentum 
is now bigger than in 2000: Morgan Stanley puts the tech momentum trade down 62% from its 
highs, while the S&P sits just 2.5% below its record. 
Ex-AI Is The New AI 
The rotation underneath is just as extreme. Goldman's desk notes that the AI winners vs 
AI-at-risk pair is now down 13% on the year, and the S&P ex-AI (SPXXAI) has overtaken the 
S&P itself, outperforming by 7.4% in just three weeks. Garrett's chart shows that three-week 
spread at the widest in data going back to 2006. 
 

 
 
The desk's verdict: "we are in the middle of a de-gross episode, where short term stops 
outweigh medium term fundamentals." 
It shows up in the Nasdaq's internals too. For most of the summer, single stocks swung far more 
than the index, the dispersion regime Garrett described as the S&P being "correlated with 
almost nothing but itself" earlier this month. That is now unwinding fast: NDX 3-month implied 
correlation has jumped to roughly 0.33 from the high-0.20s, while the ratio of average stock vol 
to index vol has slid from about 2 to roughly 1.75. Translation: the dispersion trade is being 
forced shut, which is exactly what a de-gross looks like. 
 

 
 
And then there is Garrett's "fun chart": Apple is negatively correlated to the Nasdaq 100 for the 
first time in history, on a 3-month realized basis, in a series that goes back to the early 1980s. 
One of the index's mega-cap anchors now trades as a hedge against the index. Nothing says 
"healthy market" like that. 
 

 
 
"Can You Send Me The Bond CTA Sheet?" 
Here is where it gets interesting for the rates crowd. Garrett, deadpan: 
"'bg can you send me the bond cta sheet?' – has been said zero times in 10 year prior to this 
week … the cohort has been massively short fixed income for months (great trade) … the global 
retrace (lower) in bond yields has created a large technical demand tailwind (5.02% in us10y 
was key) … short $300bn total at current (short $145mm dv01) … 1w flat tape = cover $12bn 
dv01 (+25bn in up tape) … 1m flat tape = cover 42bn dv01 (+165bn in up tape)" 
Put differently, CTAs are sitting on a $300 billion short in a market that just broke their trigger, 
and Goldman's model has them buying in almost any scenario that does not involve yields going 
back up. 
Why are yields falling? Because AI is. Garrett's chart of 10-day returns shows the long bond 
(TLT) ripping roughly 5% while Goldman's AI basket slid roughly 15% over the same span. This 
is the reflexive loop Goldman's Delta One desk spelled out midweek, and which we flagged in 
real time: 
 

 
 
The punchline: bonds are now more volatile than stocks. TLT's 30-day realized vol is back 
around 12, above the S&P's roughly 10.5. Having spent much of the past year warning that AI is 
now a debt bubble too (as we have since last October), we'd note the irony: the AI selloff is now 
the best thing that has happened to the Treasury market in months. 
 

 
 
The VIX Is Panicking. The S&P Isn't. (Yet.) 
On the derivatives side, vol compressed a little after the previous Friday's panic bid (when 
delta-adjusted VIX hit roughly one-year highs), "but scar tissue remains." The tell is in the 
Nasdaq: the NDX 1-week straddle went out at 3%, near one-year highs, while the SPX 1-week 
straddle priced 1.8%, around the 60th percentile. For the first time in a long time, Garrett says, 
the indices "appear to reflect" how loud the macro and micro tape will be this week. 
 

 
 
Meanwhile, Garrett says, "the vix market is (rightly) more nervous than the spx market over the 
next month … both elevated but one is showing extreme sign of panic while the other is middle 
percentile." His chart of VIX 1-month 25-delta call vol against SPX 1-month 25-delta put vol 
shows the two lines that normally travel together pulling sharply apart. 
 
 

 
Fatigue shows up in volumes as well: cash and derivative volumes dried up into the weekend, 
which Garrett says "speaks to lower gross as well." 
Which brings us to the trend followers. Equity CTAs are long $48 billion of global stocks, almost 
all of it in the US (they are already short most global indices), and "we are sitting on top of the 
medium term thresholds in both spx and ndx – below which additional sell pressure unlocks." 
The NDX has already poked through its medium-term trigger, while the SPX sits only about 2% 
above its own and is once again leaning on its 100-day moving average. 
 
 

 
 
Brazil Gets The Asymmetry, The Midterms Get Nothing 
One more thing: Sunday's Brazilian runoff, which Garrett calls "one of the most asymmetric 
macro events in recent memory," with the vol surface implying a -22% / +13% two-way move for 
the outcome. EWZ's 1-month put/call skew has exploded to around 1.66, the highest in data 
back to 2006. 
 

 
 
Contrast that with the US, where "the excess variance in spx and qqq priced for the US 
midterms" is, per Garrett, "(checks notes) exactly zero." Presumably the market has decided 
that, compared with Brazil (and its post-election diesel cliff), US politics is a known quantity. 
Bold. 
All of that is now utterly destroyed after Lula's shock victory over Bolsonaro. 
Second Inning Or Eighth? 
Garrett ends with the only question that matters into a week when 44% of the S&P reports: 
"feels like we're either in the 2nd inning of a repricing of the entire AI ecosystem, in which case 
the index has some material catch down … or we're in the 8th inning of a significant de-gross 
episode (leaning in this camp right now) … we need bond volatility to subside, we need 
midterms out of the way, and we need some proof of AI capex payback in earnings (oil < 90 
would also help) for the 8th inning metaphor to come to fruition … i'm hopeful" 
He backs the hope with a trade: the desk likes upside call spreads on Goldman's AI basket 
(gstmtaip), with Nov 105/120% costing 2.75%. 
We'd love to share the optimism. But count the conditions: bond vol is rising (TLT is 
out-swinging the S&P), the midterms are priced at zero, oil is nowhere near 90, and the capex 
payback proof is due this very week from the same companies the market just spent two weeks 
 

 
dumping. Four for four, the checklist is unchecked. So while Garrett leans eighth inning, we lean 
toward the second, with the index doing the "material catch down" the rest of the market already 
did. Then again, an S&P 50bps from flat after the worst momentum year on record has never 
looked more like a game that has barely started.
