WEEKLY MARKET COMMENTARY 
Tech Risk Premium Rising Ahead of Key Earnings  
 
Cross-Asset Volatility: Implied volatilities rose across asset 
classes last week as oil prices gained on rising geopolitical 
tensions and investors pulled back from Tech & AI stocks 
ahead of key earnings. Oil volatility jumped the most, with 
the OVX Index up over 9 pts wk/wk to 60% (70th percentile 
high over the past year) as Brent oil prices ended the week 
near a 1-year high. Credit volatility continued its upward 
climb, with the VIXIG Index up another 3 pts to a 6-month 
high of 35 (90th percentile), more than double the realized 
volatility for IG credit, as AI debt concerns escalate. Interest 
rate volatility gained modestly ahead of this week’s FOMC 
meeting. Though the Fed is widely expected to remain on 
hold at this meeting (~90% implied probability per the OIS 
market), focus will be on any hawkish dissents and press 
conference signals on the path of rates for Dec and 2027. 
SPX® options are implying a one-day move of ~1.3% for 
Wednesday’s Fed meeting, the same as for next week’s US 
mid-term elections. FX is currently the only asset class 
where implied volatility is still trading significantly below 
average (see Exhibit 1).  
Equity Volatility: While the VIX® Index ended last week 
mostly unch’d at 19.5%, it masked significant sector rotation 
underneath the index surface (e.g. Comm Svc down 6.2% 
vs. Energy up 1.6%). Implied stock dispersion, as measured 
by the DSPXSM Index, increased to 37% (63rd percentile 
high) as we enter a key week of mega-cap Tech earnings. 
Concerns over the outlook of the AI trade can be seen in the 
rising volatility premium for Tech stocks, with the QQQ-SPX 
1M implied vol spread widening to ~10%, over 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average and trading just 
below the July extremes. See Exhibit 2. Investors now have 
more opportunity to trade Tech earnings risk with expanded 
trading hours for 24 of the most actively traded single stock 
options going live last week. So far, over 180k contracts have 
traded in the first week, with the early 7:30-9:25am morning 
session making up nearly 93% of the flow (vs. the 4-4:15pm 
afternoon session). As more brokers enable extended 
trading hours, we expect liquidity in these sessions to grow. 
Skew: Sentiment in SPX options swung sharply last week as 
the initial market rebound saw investors chasing with upside 
calls, before the sell-off at the end of the week led to renewed 
hedging demand. SPX 1M skew (25-delta ratio) ended the 
week in the 69th percentile high (vs. 90th percentile a week 
ago). Demand for tail risk protection, as measured by SPX 
downside convexity (10D vs. 25D ratio), remained in the 99th 
percentile high, though off the extremes from a week ago. 
See Exhibit 3. Retail positioning in the Tech stocks has also 
turned more cautious ahead of earnings this week, with call 
demand in the mega-cap Tech names falling to a 6-month 
low while put demand jumped to a 1-year high.

How to read this chart:
The chart shows how various asset class implied 
volatilities are trading relative to their own history, using 
a 10-year lookback. 
The volatilities are ranked by z-scores, which compares 
the current volatility level for an asset versus its own 10-
year history. A z-score of +2, for example, would mean 
that the current volatility level is trading 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average (i.e. rich) while a 
z-score of -2 would mean it’s trading 2 standard 
deviations below average (i.e. cheap)
Because each asset class volatility trades at different 
levels and often defined differently (e.g. lognormal vs. 
normal vol, price vs. bps vol), by standardizing using z-
scores, we’re able to compare different asset class 
volatilities on the same scale - and see at a glance if 
there are any divergences or dislocations in the cross-
asset volatility markets
