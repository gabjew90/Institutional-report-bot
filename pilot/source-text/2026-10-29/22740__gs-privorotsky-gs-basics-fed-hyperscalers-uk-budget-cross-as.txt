Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
28 Oct 2026
Markets/Flows: Big beat from SK Hynix overnight...+10%. Revenue +32% sequentially, operating profit
+38%, margins at 80%. More than half of 2027/28 capacity already under long term agreements. Semi
tape bid. We’ve now had a series of positive developments this week, as some of the financing concerns
beginning to abate. The demand side of the equation never really changed. It was the debate around
supply, financing and a risk reduction exercise across the Street. End result… some of the lowest nets all
year..among the largest two week institutional tech liquidations on record and now solid numbers. Early
signs of offense yesterday. People picking up parts of the semi complex, some demand for upside vol. But
it’s early. People have barely begun to re-risk. What we’ve really seen so far is the abatement of supply,
rather than meaningful fresh demand.
Fed: No projections, so focus shifts to the vote, statement and Warsh. Hold fully priced, with two dissents
probably the central expectation. Zero would be a dovish surprise, three would reinforce December as the
next hike. Watch for any acknowledgment of tighter FCI or softer employment in the statement. The real
debate is how much the aggressive tightening in financial conditions offsets stubborn inflation. Rates have
repriced materially, payroll growth is weak and housing is cooling… there is room for a more balanced
message. My guess is they tee up December without committing, but the hard yards in pricing are what
happens thereafter… just how far does this hiking cycle extend? Watch the long end if the message sounds
too soft on forward inflation. Not much event risk priced in equities, and I wouldn’t expect fireworks.
Microsoft/Google: These really matter. Eye watering expectations… buyside probably closer to Azure
growth around high 40s%, Google Cloud around mid 90s%. The question isn’t whether those are good
numbers, but whether they can surpass already high expectations. Hyperscalers monetize the underlying
traffic, compute and infrastructure, and those volumes remain incredibly robust. That should translate into
accelerating cloud growth, backlogs converting to revenue faster and ultimately stronger cash generation.
Azure probably needs a guidance raise to be properly supportive. The nuance is capacity… if they miss
elevated expectations simply because they cannot bring enough compute online. That’s disappointing for
near term revenue but potentially even more bullish for the scarcity premium and future spending. Watch
customer concentration, how broad the demand really is and, crucially, the relationship between revenue
growth, capex and margins. The market wants evidence that the incremental economics work.
UK Budget: Today. Limited fiscal headroom and a government that needs new revenue sources. Potential
bank taxes, gambling levies, property related measures and some support for first time buyers. Banks
particularly sensitive given the debate around additional taxation. The question is whether the package
ends up worse than feared or whether Healey delivers something more moderate. As ever, the OBR
forecasts and gilt reaction probably matter as much as the headline measures.

Middle East: Oil back on the bid. Monster rally across Gulf equities yday as markets got excited about the
collective bargaining emerging among regional states. Perhaps some optimism that the region could
resolve its own problems without further US involvement and, ideally, without another bombing
campaign. Region opens lower and giving back some of those gains now as the WSJ reports that
Washington has warned Gulf allies to prepare for a potentially short, aggressive campaign against Iran
after the midterms. No final decision, but hardly reassuring. Oil bouncing again. Meanwhile, Israel’s
election looks deadlocked. Netanyahu’s Likud appears to have outperformed some expectations, but
neither bloc has a clear majority. Potentially weeks of coalition uncertainty ahead.
Risk: Always darkest before the dawn? On the surface it would be logical to temper your enthusiasm for
the broader market. Energy remains elevated, geopolitical risks unresolved and we still have the midterms
next week… an obvious source of known unknowns. But the aggregate market got extraordinarily
pessimistic on AI, took nets too low and is now getting hit with a succession of positive developments and
remarkably robust numbers. The hyperscalers are absolutely critical today. The Fed could also offer a
more two sided interpretation of the economic backdrop. Yes, inflation is hot, but rates have repriced
materially, payroll growth is anemic and housing is softening. There is room for a less threatening
message. Confidence remains low, positioning is light and the micro in AI is getting better. Credit traded
much better yday and that is still my focal point. Feels like gross can start working again, particularly in
the right pockets of tech. Still think we need to be selective given the macro, but the set up into year end is
now cleaner once we get past the macro headwinds.
