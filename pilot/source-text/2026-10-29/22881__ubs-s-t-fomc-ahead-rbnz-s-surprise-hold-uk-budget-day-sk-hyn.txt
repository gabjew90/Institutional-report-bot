The FOMC meeting on Wednesday could shift the focus back toward macro fundamentals and policy
expectations after macro markets have mainly taken cues from the equity risk-off spillover over the past week.
The meeting also comes at a time when equity sentiment appears to be stabilizing ahead of Alphabet,
Microsoft and Amazon earnings, while neocloud stocks and their credit spreads have moderated from recent
extremes.
With a pause expected, the key uncertainty is Chair Warsh’s messaging: how he reconciles insufficient
progress on inflation with holding rates unchanged, how conditional he makes subsequent tightening sound,
and how much weight he places on financial conditions and uneven growth. Given that the recent bond rally
has been driven in part by risk-off dynamics, rates could be particularly sensitive to a hawkish surprise. US
Economist Jonathan Pingle expects Chair Warsh to deliver a hawkish hold, emphasizing that inflation remains
elevated and that the job is not done, with rate hikes in December and January still the base case.
Together, the FOMC and hyperscaler earnings will test whether the tentative improvement in sentiment can
broaden into a more durable equity rally. Strong cloud growth and cash generation, alongside easing rates
volatility and less-hawkish Fed communication, could support that outcome. Conversely, weaker AI signals or
renewed rates pressure could undermine the nascent recovery.
(Jason Cheng, Macro Sales)

RBNZ Surprisingly Holds; NZD Falls Sharply - RBNZ holds rates at 2.75%, against expectations for a 25bp
hike. The RBNZ voted 4-3 to hold the OCR, with Governor Breman among those voting to keep rates on hold.
The RBNZ said core inflation is broadly in line with expectations and medium-term inflation expectations
remain well anchored, while hold voters said they need time to assess the impact of recent OCR hikes.
NZDUSD falls sharply by 1.5% on the surprise hold. AUDNZD squeezed higher to 1.2600 before retracing a
tad. The trading desk noted that offshore accounts are unlikely to like the RBNZ's rate hold decision, and
therefore favour buying dips in AUDNZD, ideally around 1.2475/85, where the pair initially broke higher. NZ
Economist Stephen Wu expects the RBNZ to hike 25bp each in December and February to a terminal rate of
3.25%, well below market expectations. The OIS market is pricing in a cumulative 89bp of hikes for June 2027.
(Jolie Ho, Macro Sales; Marcus Mills, FX Trading)

households. The Guardian says the support package could exceed GBP1 bn. The paper says reversing earlier
bank-surcharge cuts could raise upwards of GBP2 bn annually. The Times pointed to a planned GBP1 bn

SK Hynix’s KRW100 Trn Payout A Key Positive Surprise; Shares Jump - SK Hynix’s third-quarter results
were slightly ahead of consensus. Revenue beat UBS analyst Nicolas Gaudois’s latest estimate, while
operating profit was in-line. The main positive was a KRW100 trn shareholder-return programme for 2026,
comprising at least KRW30 trn in cash dividends and KRW60 trn in share buybacks and cancellations,
including the KRW40 trn repurchase already completed. The company is expanding its cash dividend to
KRW20,000 per share, split equally between earnings and gains on its Kioxia investment. The upside surprise
in shareholder returns should support the shares after their recent correction. SK Hynix rose 3.5% at the open,
with the scale of the payout helping offset lingering concerns over AI spending, HBM pricing and higher capex.
(Kevin Cheng, Equity Sales)
CBC Smoothing Supports A Stronger TWD Bias - Taiwan's central bank governor attributed the latest

unity arrangement. Arab parties outperform initial exit-poll expectations by four seats to reach 18 MKs after
historically high turnout. That takes the opposition bloc, including the Arab parties, to 70 seats, comfortably

BoC Should Hike By 25bp Wednesday - Canada Economics expects the BoC to raise its policy rate by

Markets Into The Midterms - US Rates / Vol - US yields have retraced 20–25bp over the past month, a
meaningful reversal from the recent highs. Iran de-escalation hopes plus the AI credit-spread blowout drove
risk-off, FTQ and a renewed bid for duration. Oct. 22 was the clearest print: SPX down 1.21%, 10s richer by
17bp on the day. Equity/bond correlation flipped back negative, at least briefly, putting Treasurys back in their
more traditional risk-off hedge role. That matters - in a 5.25–5.50% 10y world, inflation risk can overwhelm the
hedge and leave bonds trading with equities. At today's yield levels, that dynamic appears to be flipping. And
it’s not just equities. Oil beta has faded materially. Brent continues to whip around on Iran headlines, while
Treasury yields have largely stopped following over the past two weeks. Across the Atlantic, OAT/Bund
widening has flushed out carry, with French political and fiscal risk in focus. A Democrat sweep does not derail
banking deregulations. If anything, it could incentivize the administration to accelerate Q4/lame-duck priorities
which keeps clients positive on ASW spreads. Read the full note here. (Swati Raghupathy, Rates Sales)

Chile Holds, As Expected - Chile’s central bank unanimously held its benchmark interest rate at 4.5% for a
seventh consecutive meeting, as widely anticipated, while warning that external shocks have substantially
increased inflation risks. Policymakers highlighted escalating Middle East tensions and the impact of a strong
El Niño on global food prices and warned of “higher-than-usual uncertainty” surrounding the outlook. Officials
reiterated that future interest rate decisions will continue to be made on a meeting-by-meeting basis as they
assess evolving inflation pressures. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

Tuesday as early supply flipped modestly better to buy, with hedge funds and long-only investors adding longs

renewed fundamental demand is being amplified by short covering across higher-beta and high-short-interest
exposures. Negative S&P 500 breadth and equal-weight weakness, however, suggest index strength remains

exposure to the artificial intelligence complex. Earnings are driving greater dispersion, with clean beats
rewarded and misses punished asymmetrically, while softer crude on Tuesday left Energy as the clearest
macro laggard. Activity remained subdued ahead of the FOMC and hyperscaler earnings, after which attention
should increasingly shift from macro and factor beta toward earnings execution, capital expenditure and
