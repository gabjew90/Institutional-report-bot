1) Monster Beat by SK Hinyx
SK Hynix (000660 KS) beat on Q3 profit by a wide margin, reaffirmed HBM shortages through at least
2030. +10%. Revenue +32% sequentially, operating profit +38%, margins at 80%. More than half of
2027/28 capacity already under long term agreements... We’ve now had a series of positive developments
this week, as some of the financing concerns beginning to abate. The demand side of the equation never
really changed. It was the debate around supply, financing and a risk reduction exercise across the Street.
End result… some of the lowest nets all year... among the largest two week institutional tech liquidations
on record and now solid numbers. Early signs of offense yesterday. People picking up parts of the semi
complex, some demand for upside vol. But it’s early. People have barely begun to re-risk. What we’ve
really seen so far is the abatement of supply, rather than meaningful fresh demand.
Definitely reassuring to the AI trade to see a big beat being rewarded.
2) All eyes on Microsoft/Google
These really matter. Eye watering expectations… buyside probably closer to Azure growth around high
40s%, Google Cloud around mid 90s%. The question isn’t whether those are good numbers, but whether
they can surpass already high expectations. Hyperscalers monetize the underlying traffic, compute and
infrastructure, and those volumes remain incredibly robust. That should translate into accelerating cloud
growth, backlogs converting to revenue faster and ultimately stronger cash generation. Azure probably
needs a guidance raise to be properly supportive. The nuance is capacity… if they miss elevated
expectations simply because they cannot bring enough compute online. That’s disappointing for near term
revenue but potentially even more bullish for the scarcity premium and future spending. Watch customer
concentration, how broad the demand really is and, crucially, the relationship between revenue growth,
capex and margins. The market wants evidence that the incremental economics work.

3) Macro Assets in Fed Hiking Cycles
Historically, looking at asset performances after the first Fed hike across nine tightening cycles since the
early 1980s, 1) equities have tended to struggle initially after the first hike but recover quickly, 2) yields
have generally risen into the first hike, with further increases after the hike more pronounced in larger
hiking cycles, 3) the yield curve has tended to continue flattening, especially in larger cycles and 4) the
real trade-weighted USD has tended to strengthen into the hike, with mixed performance afterwards.

Our baseline of a modest number of hikes, growth around potential, and gradual inflation relief points to a
modestly positive path for equities. History highlights three potential risk scenarios to that view: an earlier
end to tightening on better inflation news, which would be the most positive for risk assets; an interruption
driven by growth or financial stress concerns, which would at least initially be less friendly; or a more
extended tightening cycle if growth remains resilient, which could raise the risk of a larger growth
downgrade down the road.
