The great volatility disconnect
Something unusual is happening beneath the surface of equities. Defensive sectors are showing
bubble-like behavior, US earnings moves keep exceeding what options price in, and Europe is paying
up for volatility that rarely materializes. Meanwhile, the semiconductor washout is creating interesting
upside opportunities, while AI weakness is increasingly trading like a growth shock. BofA's latest work
reveals a market where the biggest risks aren't necessarily where investors are paying to hedge them.
The defensive bubble
The AI trade is wobbling, but the bubble-like price action hasn't disappeared. It has simply moved
elsewhere. BofA's Bubble Risk Indicator shows healthcare and staples approaching extreme levels
following the historic rotation into defensives. Interestingly, similar dynamics appeared during the
dotcom bubble and post-Covid melt-up. Even the supposedly safe corners of the market are starting to
look frothy.

The earnings volatility paradox
BofA highlights a striking divergence between US and European earnings volatility. In the US, more
than two-thirds of the S&P 500's top 50 stocks by market cap have recently delivered earnings moves
larger than options implied, a 98th-percentile reading. Europe is the opposite, with only around one-
third of stocks exceeding their implied moves despite an unusually elevated earnings premium. US
earnings options have been underpricing the action, while European investors have been paying up for
moves that haven't materialized.

Megacap moves keep surprising
The options market has been struggling to keep up with US earnings volatility. BofA finds that realized
earnings moves have been outpacing implied expectations, particularly among the largest index
constituents. Despite rising option premiums, the biggest stocks continue to deliver outsized surprises.
The message heading into megacap earnings: expensive-looking options haven't necessarily been
expensive enough.

The semiconductor upside trade
BofA highlights SMH as offering one of the most attractive upside call-spread payouts among liquid
ETFs that have sold off since the October 6 Nasdaq peak. December at-the-money call spreads
targeting a one-standard-deviation rebound offered roughly 2.8x payout potential as of October 26.
With earnings, the Fed and midterms approaching, the bank sees the possibility of a post-catalyst risk-
on reversal. An interesting defined-risk expression of the semiconductor rebound thesis.

When AI breaks, bonds rally

BofA highlights an important change in the market's reaction function. During the October 22 Nasdaq
selloff of nearly 3%, US 10-year yields fell more than 16bps. AI weakness is increasingly trading like a
growth shock rather than an inflation or rates shock. Meanwhile, the volatility gap between Nasdaq and
S&P 500 continues to widen. The implication: the next serious AI unwind could produce a very different
cross-asset response than investors experienced during the inflation-driven selloffs.
