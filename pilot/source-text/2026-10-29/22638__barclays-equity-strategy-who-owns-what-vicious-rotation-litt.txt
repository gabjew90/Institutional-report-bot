Equity Strategy - Who Owns What
Vicious rotation, little retreat
Aggregate equity positioning hasn’t budged, but risk-off
rotation is fierce underneath: dollar, bonds & defensives bid;
EU equities (France), Banks & Cyclicals shunned. Unwind risk
remains if AI/earnings/credit crack, but fundamentals still
hold & a narrower winners vs. losers divide is healthy.

Aggregate equity positioning holds up. Despite recent equity weakness, there is little evidence
of broad de-risking. Long-only inflows remained strong at ~$148bn in October, the third-best
month on record, taking YTD inflows above $1trn for the first time (as per EPFR). Retail remains
cautious and macro HFs and CTAs have cut exposure, but risk-control positioning is still near the
highs as volatility stays subdued, leaving it the key source of unwind risk if volatility spikes. Net-
net, aggregate equity exposure remains modestly above average, albeit below the summer
highs.
Underneath the surface, though, the risk-off rotation is significant. Equities still dominate
cross-asset flows, but their lead over bonds and cash is narrowing. Treasuries have regained a
haven bid and their role as portfolio hedges, while global bonds have outperformed equities for
the first time since March, challenging TINA. Tighter financial conditions, peak-growth concerns
and the closing of CTA Treasury shorts reinforce the case for duration. Yet resilient activity, solid
Q3 earnings and limited credit outflows argue against a wholesale risk-off move. Higher oil and
scrutiny of AI capex versus RoI remain headwinds, but year-end seasonality, returning
buybacks and the midterms could offer catalysts for risk appetite, especially if
accompanied by any easing in the energy shock.
Regionally, Europe is the clear loser. US flows have rebounded from September as dollar
sentiment improved, while RoW inflows strengthened across EM and Japan. Europe remains
the clear laggard and a call on oil direction. US investor selling has accelerated, LO positioning
is at its lowest since early 2025, and HFs and CTAs have cut EU longs. France continues to suffer
the heaviest outflows as fiscal concerns bite, while UK appetite remains weak ahead of today's

Sector rotation has been brutal, but lacks fundamental confirmation. October’s rotation has
broadened the market at Tech’s expense. LOs continue to buy Tech, but CTAs have trimmed
Tech/SOX exposure, while Europe has seen a modest defensive turn after two years of strong
Cyclical inflows: the extreme positioning gap between AI winners and laggards has narrowed,
Banks have seen sharp outflows, Energy demand has strengthened, and crowding has risen in
low-vol and low-debt baskets. This remains a pain trade for Europe’s Cyclical/Capex-heavy
funds, particularly as positioning is yet to fully adjust. But the fundamental case for a
wholesale defensive rotation isn't clear to us, as the sharpest MTD Cyclical
underperformance since the GFC sits uneasily with still-resilient data and earnings.
