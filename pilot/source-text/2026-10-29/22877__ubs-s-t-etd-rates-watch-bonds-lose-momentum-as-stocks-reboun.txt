ETD Rates Watch: Bonds Lose Momentum
As Stocks Rebound
Daniel Noorian
28-Oct-2026, 05:28 ET (EDT)
The RBNZ kicked off the first of eight meetings in the next seven days by surprising markets and leaving rates
unchanged at 2.75%. The committee cited global uncertainty weighing on domestic demand. The bank did
warn that further tightening is likely needed, but this remains conditional on the economic outlook. In Australia,
the latest inflation data showed a pick-up from the prior quarter, with the trimmed mean q/q reading rising from
0.8% to 1.0%, while the y/y print also picked up from 3.6% to 3.7%.
RBA Governor Bullock also testified to the Senate Committee and warned: “some of the international and
domestic upside risks to inflation have materialised or are materialising. Inflation will therefore likely be higher
than was our base case in August.” Markets are now pricing an 86% chance of a 25bp hike next week, up from
60% on Tuesday. 3y futures sold off to a low of 95.05 on the inflation release but found some support, rallying
back to 95.08 at the end of the session. The IR strip ended the day down as much as seven, led by the whites
in a flattening move. IRM7/M8 dropped four ticks to -22.
US fixed income was also weaker in the Asia session, with TY trading down to a low of 105-26 while the SOFR
strip is down as much as 3.5 ticks. There were a number of factors driving the weakness. First, the
aforementioned Australian inflation data weighed on fixed income. Secondly, there was a report from the Wall
Street Journal [paywall] that the US had told several Gulf Arab countries to be prepared for a short, intense
military campaign after the midterms. Speculation was further boosted by reports the US had sent an
additional Patriot system to Israel.
Finally, the better tone in equity markets also added to the weakness in fixed income. Over the last few weeks,
weakness, especially in tech, has supported fixed income. However, buyers have returned, with the Kospi up
6.63% on Wednesday, boosted by earnings from SK Hynix where profits were stronger than expected. The
company also announced plans to return more money to shareholders. They also warned that component
shortages vital to Nvidia will persist until 2030, keeping prices and operating margins elevated.
The Fed meeting on Wednesday didn’t act as a brake on activity, with volumes in-line with recent averages,
while activity in fixed income was skewed to the downside. There was a 2s20s block steepener, while option
activity was also skewed to the downside, with the TYZ6 105/103.50 put spread bought for 16 in 3k while the
Week 5 October TY 105.25 puts were bought for 9 in 5k. Those expire tomorrow. SOFR activity remains light
in the overnight session, as it was during the pit session.
In these low-volume sessions it is really hard to pick out many themes from activity. The most obvious one is
the lack of any notable upside interest in USTs now equities have found support. Recent SOFR activity is a
good example of the lack of any clear theme. Accounts have been focusing on low-premium plays looking to
cover the possibility of the Fed actually refraining from hiking in December. The one flow I thought was
interesting was the buyer of 3.5k SFRF7 95.5625/95.6875/96.125 call ladder for a 0.25 credit. There hasn’t
been much interest recently in fading call skew.
One of the more popular questions in recent days has been about CTA positioning. That is because they have
been a major driver of markets recently, as they were caught out by moves in equities and fixed income. The
latest update from Elena Amoruso shows that CTAs remain heavily short duration, although short covering

picked up in Japan and Australia. For USTs, a move towards 4.8% to 4.85% could lead to more meaningful
short covering. The interesting change has come in credit, where long liquidations have now turned into
outright short positions across most credit markets. US IG remains the exception, with a marginally positive
signal. 
UBS economics expect no change from the Fed tonight but expect two dissenters (Dallas Fed President
Logan and Cleveland Fed President Hammack), although they caveat that it could be only one, or even three.
They expect Chair Warsh to say there is more work to do, with inflation still elevated. We expect he notes that
between meetings, the FOMC can watch and evaluate financial markets and the economic data, and fall back
on his now typical ‘it’s the trend not the data point’ abstraction from the high-frequency ebb and flow of data,
markets and other events. They also expect him to keep the press conference brief and limit follow-up
questions.
The US 5y auction on Tuesday tailed by 0.3bp, marking the 12th straight five-year auction tail. The
bid-to-cover was 2.33, in line with the last six average of 2.31. The 2y auction was about the direct bid, but it
was the rebound in the indirect bid that stood out in the 5y auction. Indirects took 60%, up from 54.3%
previously and in line with prior averages. That left directs with 29.4% and dealers with 10.6%.
As EU budget haggling continues, Politico reports that the European Commission is looking into whether to
use revenue from a global tax agreement to finance the next seven-year budget. A corporate tax known as
CORE, which charges a lump sum on companies’ revenues over EUR 100 mn, has emerged as the most
unpopular option, especially in Germany. As a result, President Macron suggested using part of the tax on
multinationals to fund the EU budget.
