As widely expected, the Fed held rates at 3.75–4.00%, with two members favouring another hike. The
majority’s willingness to wait, despite resilient activity and elevated inflation, signals less urgency to
tighten. Markets reacted with slightly steeper Treasury curve and weaker USD.
The Fed paused following September’s increase, with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favour of a 25bp
hike. The statement describes strong growth, resilient spending and robust investment. Warsh is talking about
several indicators pointing towards a strong labour market and repeats his take on inflation being above
target and that Fed is committed to the inflation target. Nevertheless, the majority wants more information
before adjusting policy again. Markets seems to want a “little less conversation, a little more action please“
Fed statement
We interpret this as a pause in tightening rather than a change of direction. December remains in play, but
choosing to wait despite a ﬁrm economic backdrop suggests that the majority sees limited need to move
quickly.
The willingness to wait eases near-term rate expectations somewhat, but the commitment to inflation ﬁghting
is again being questioned and longer yields increase. The slightly weaker USD supports this interpretation.
We would not be surprised to see curves steepen more, alternatives to the USD do not look particularly
tempting these days so USD-weakness could prove short lived.
