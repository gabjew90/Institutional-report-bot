GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Supportive Micro. Deposit
Competition. Downside Growth Risk 27 Oct 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
27 Oct 2026
Markets: Overnight rebound in the US following through into the broader tech tape. Hynix +5%, Samsung
+4%, KOSPI +3%. Question is whether this is an oversold bounce in QQQ’s or the beginning of
something more sustainable. Giving back some of yday's gains with oil reversing higher. Generally, the
week ahead of major political events, with this much risk premium already embedded, tends to be
constructive. The market struggles to go down, even if people remain reluctant to add risk.
Energy: Big focus on diplomacy in the Gulf. Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani delegations in Tehran, Qatar
also mediating, with plenty of last minute overtures to prevent another war. Nothing particularly
substantive as far as I can tell. Look, it’s not crazy that something could still happen, but the odds of a
breakthrough remain low. Oil has come off the boil from Friday’s escalatory highs. Imagine if there’s no
tangible progress by Thursday morning, we start climbing the escalatory ladder into the weekend again.
That said, the market will remain skeptical that much happens immediately ahead of political events next
week.
Banks: HSBC down -5.6% in Hong Kong, setting a difficult tone for the sector. What stood out was the
increased deposit competition in both the UK and Hong Kong contributing to NII compression. Something
to watch out for in Europe more broadly with increased competition for deposits and the lingering
background threat of agentic capital allocation.
AI: Neoclouds under pressure yesterday following Elon’s comments on financing and consolidation. Some
relief in AI credit, with spreads coming off Friday’s wides, but this remains the most important thing to
watch. At this point in the cycle, it’s increasingly about the market’s willingness to supply capital and at
what price. There’s a lot of theoretical debate about compute oversupply into 2027/28. Perhaps we’re
being too flippant about the sheer scale of spending and the possibility that it eventually drives excess
capacity, structurally lower returns and lower multiples across the various pathways. Unclear. What is
clear is that the cost of capital has risen materially, and that’s changing the economics of the entire
complex. Any sustained relief in credit should be a powerful catalyst. Meanwhile, the micro remains
incredibly robust. Celestica surged ~17% aftermarket, beating comfortably and guiding to $40bn of
revenue in 2027, up 90% YoY, with 2028 demand indications even stronger. Positive read through for
networking/optical.. Cadence also beat and raised, with $8.2bn of backlog and strong AI driven design
demand. Rambus beat as well (still slipped post mkt). Demand is clearly not the problem today. The
debate is how much we should discount those future cash flows given the uncertainty around supply,
competition and financing.
Risk: We’ve stabilized in front end rates, objectively good for risk. Energy remains too high for comfort,
but that’s more likely to produce chop than a sustained selloff absent another escalation. Credit is the one

to worry about. Still flashing yellow to red relative to equities. We need credit to calm down, and if it does,
I think that could be a meaningful catalyst for a broader rally. Hyperscalers + Fed are the main event this
week. As outlined yesterday, focus on four things. Capex: flat/up is fine, down would be disastrous for
picks and shovels. Backlog: how quickly is it converting into revenue? FCF: how much of that revenue is
actually translating into cash generation in the out quarters? Financing: how do they fund the next leg, and
do they increasingly choose equity over debt given the strength of their underlying businesses? These are
the questions that matter far more than another round of headline beats. Flows were a little more
supportive yesterday. Having been net sellers of tech for much of the past few weeks, we finally saw some
client demand engaging on the other side (at least in Europe). Feels like the modal view remains that we
chop around until we clear next week’s political events. Optimism in pockets, but vigilance still warranted.
