IDEAS & INSIGHTS – IN BRIEF 


[Page 15]
 
 
•         ASIA EQUITY STRATEGY: Our aggregate measure of investor positioning in Asia 
(measuring across LO, HF, Deriv, retail, systematic, cross-asset investors) dropped 
from the 75th percentile to the 32nd percentile from mid-June to late-July. The first two 
positioning risks we discussed in early June (leveraged products in Korea, Momentum 
crowding in Tech ex-TSMC in Taiwan) have now largely played out, while risks from 
equity issuance and related liquidity tightness in China are ongoing, in our view. In 
terms of LO positioning, by far the biggest change in 2Q was the substantial 
positioning cuts in Tech – both Korea and Taiwan (Figure 2). Elsewhere, we note that 
EM asset class inflows have resumed since July (with $19bn of inflows) and a major 
new buyer has emerged in Korea in the form of corporate buybacks. Team is cautious 
over next few weeks but in the medium term, we believe that the trajectory for Asian 
equities remains upwards, driven by a combination of AI capex and adoption, 
security/resilience spending, and a global macro environment that looks medium-term 
balanced. 
•         GLOBAL EQUITY STRATEGY: Team remains bullish into year end, supported by 
positive earnings revisions in all regions. Regionally, the EA stands out with three 
consecutive months of PMI improvement. In terms of Tech/AI trade, team remains of 
the view that it will struggle to dominate in 2H, unlike what transpired in 2025. 
thematically, positive on Basic Resources and Defense. 
•         EA INFLATION: Headline inflation increased to 3.3%oya due to fuel prices; core 
inflation inched down to 2.4%oya. Core decline driven by services, while core goods 
inflation increased further. We raise our Brent and TTF price assumption; headline 
inflation to increase to 3.4%oya 
•         JAPAN FX: Since the intervention at end-July, expectations for BOJ rate hikes 
have increased, and we currently expect rate hikes roughly once per quarter for the 
time being. On the other hand, if there is no major change in market expectations for 
the Fed monetary policy, we think it is likely that USD/JPY will remain in a 155–165 
range. The most realistic intervention option appears to be an allocation shift by the 
GPIF that does not involve changes to its basic portfolio. That said, the flow impact

EMERGING MARKETS MONETIZATION – Ellen Wang / Victoria Campos 
The global momentum/tech rebound drove EM 7% higher since the July-end low, 
despite choppy price action. Broadening and AI continue to dominate the narrative in 
EM, with the recent global macro backdrop net supportive. We are bullish on EM 
given (i) improving macro; (ii) weaker USD; (iii) AI CapEx tailwinds, but continue to 
expect divergence in EM performance at the country/sector level and view country-
relative trades as more relevant for the monetization strategy in this environment.  


[Page 18]
 
 
•         EM MONETIZATION MENU – Key themes and trades include: (i) TACTICALLY 
LONG China H-shares over A-shares: the pair has been trading well in July given 
the momentum rotation. Despite this outperformance, H-shares still lag A-shares YTD 
and we see further room into China Tech earnings next week. (ii) We also express 
this view via a TACTICALLY LONG in China Internet (KWEB). (iii) TACTICALLY 
LONG Taiwan following the Global Tech selloff in July. Taiwan offers a less volatile 
exposure to AI and Asia Tech relative to Korea. 
• HEDGES – (i) We like long LatAm (ILF) as a diversifying play 
against weakness in the EM AI theme and for relative resilience 
against geopolitical risks. (ii) India (INDA) equity strength has been 
driven in part by a rotation during the AI de-risking phase, but foreign 
investor flows have also turned more supportive again, supported by 
improving macro. You may consider a L/S pair with LONG India and 
SHORT Southeast Asia (ASEA).

Report: Weekly Wrap | Weekly Wrap | Making Sense of the Chop & Re-Thinking 
Momentum 
  
Summary:  While we’re technically in the tail end of Summer, it hasn’t felt like it this 
week, due to the momentum and equity reversal.  Still, volumes and HF turnover 
remain light—they were about -1z this past week (even on Wed).  With performance 
still up MTD despite taking a hit this week, and gross flows in the process of 


[Page 19]
 
 
bottoming, alpha could improve over the next few months, but the path remains 
tricky. 
Key Points: 
1.    How bad is HF performance?  Our estimates are almost exactly the 
same as they were 2 weeks ago…All Strategy returns are back to +0.8% 
MTD (they were >2% early this week).  Crowding in the US has suffered due to 
shorts rallying sharply, but there are signs the worst could be behind us. 
2.    Will we see re-grossing? Rolling 20d gross flows in the US have bottomed 
and are getting less negative…and every year since 2018 we’ve seen 20d 
gross flows turn positive by mid-Sep. 
3.    What’s the view on momentum from here?  Momentum reversed lower 
this week and HF positioning continues to decline.  Ultimately, the deep 
drawdown seen recently has historically been followed by much more 
mixed future returns (vs. consistent rebounds post shallow declines).  Hence 
the view is unclear, though the two deep drawdowns that occurred around mid-
year (i.e. in line with seasonal de-grossing) were followed by rebounds over the 
next 20d/60d, which could offer a glimmer of hope. 
4.    Are investors turning more cautious?  Sort of…HF net flows in the US 
remain negative and overall positioning changes have been very neutral in the 
past 1wk/4wks.  Our US TPM is ~40th %-tile (i.e. slightly below average), but 
both retail and institutional sentiment continues to trend higher, which 
could be positive for the market on a medium term basis. 
5.    What sectors/themes look interesting?  AI Vulnerable vs. Core continue 
to see a rotation / unwind even if Semis vs. Software rotation in flows are 
slowing.  HC in the US could continue to do well (as HF positioning hasn’t 
rebounded that much and ETF flows remain positive), though EU HC could 
also do better through the end of Q3 and we’re seeing HFs turn more bullish.  
Retail stocks have been hit negatively and we’ve seen HFs short pockets of 
these again (e.g. Global Brands), while Metals & Mining positioning does not

look stretched despite the rally in the past month.  EU Banks have been sold 
sharply by HFs which is interesting.
