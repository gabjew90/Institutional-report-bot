USTs extended Friday’s sell-off yesterday, but this time it was the long end that took the lead. The 10Y 
UST yield broke 4.75%, 5Y yields reached 4.50% and 2s10s retraced part of Friday’s flattening. September is now 
close to 70% priced for a hike, with around 60bp total priced through mid-2027. As our US econ Pelle puts it, since
Warsh’s appearance in Jackson Hole, the burden of proof has shifted from “why hike?” to “why not hike?”, meaning 
soft data now needs to stop the Fed rather than firm data needing to push it. Oil is making the case for a hold 
harder. US forces struck Iranian launchers before Iran retaliated against regional US bases. Trump has left the 
door open to further limited strikes while playing down a broader war. Brent is holding above $90pb, keeping the 
inflation channel alive even without a full escalation through Hormuz. For rates, we note that geopolitical risk is still 
being expressed through higher yields and term premium rather than just front-end repricing. US labour market 
will be the key focus this week. Today’s JOLTS and ISM manufacturing are the first prints to watch. Our 
US economist sees risk for an upside surprise in both (Mizuho: 7.62mn for openings and 55.8 for ISM). Those 
numbers would reinforce the view that aggregate demand remains too strong for the Fed to sit comfortably on 
hold. Friday’s NFP is the key event this week, where an improvement vs July is expected but where our US econ 
sees room for a downside surprise (30k) vs the Street consensus (58k).  
Bunds sold off on Monday despite softer German inflation, with the 10Y moving firmly above 3.30% as oil, 
gas and the global duration sell-off dominated. The market is no longer prepared to reward modest downside 
inflation surprises while the energy impulse is moving in the opposite direction and September supply is about to 
increase. Today’s EZ aggregate inflation is therefore less about the headline print (given the energy price 
increase) and more about whether core and services can confirm that second-round effects remain under 
control. A soft number should offer the front end some relief given the almost 3% terminal priced if you look at 
EUR 1y1y OIS, but probably not enough to reverse the broader move unless energy also stabilises, which depends 
on the geopolitical narratives play. Rehn’s warning that the Iran war could become an inflation problem highlights
the problem the ECB is currently facing; one where domestic data argues for patience, while rising energy prices 
raise the cost of waiting. 
Gilts have some catching up to do. Gilts reopen into a meaningfully worse global rates backdrop. While the UK 
was closed, 10Y USTs broke 4.75%, Bunds moved above 3.30% and oil extended its rally. The initial move should 
therefore be a mechanical bearish catch-up, with the long end most exposed after yesterday’s global steepening. 
The question is whether the adjustment extends from there. Locally, UK shop-price inflation accelerated to a two-
year high, led by food, reinforcing the risk that the energy shock broadens into the consumer basket. That may 
leave the front end vulnerable (70bp priced through July 2027 – also looking too hawkish to us given the weaker 
UK econ fundamentals), although this domestic data is unlikely to settle the BoE debate.  
The risk-off tone continued today in Asian equities, in line with the sluggish price action in US S&P futures 
seen overnight. JGB yields rose ~4.5-5.5bp across the curve, a move that was likely a combination of the global 
rates selloff, solid Japanese data (incl. Q2 company sales, profits and capital spending) and a weak 10y JGB 
auction. Meanwhile, USDJPY spot marched higher overnight after having retraced part of the move higher seen 
post Jackson Hole, with the pair now trading close to 160.
