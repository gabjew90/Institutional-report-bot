Goldman Upgrades Humanoid Forecast By 4 Times 
As Warehouses Become Ground Zero For 
Deployments 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, SEP 01, 2026 - 01:40 AM 
Beyond Unitree's blockbuster IPO, already sputtering as investors fret over a humanoid robotics 
bubble, Goldman has released an 80-page Physical AI note that dramatically upgrades robot 
deliveries this year, at the end of the decade, and in 2035.  
 


[Page 2]
 
Eric Sheridan, managing director and internet/e-commerce equity analyst at Goldman, raised 
his base case to 75,000 humanoid shipments in 2026, 890,000 in 2030, and 6.5 million in 2035, 
versus a previous estimate of 51,000, 256,000, and 1.4 million. 
 
"We are raising our humanoid robot market forecast. The global market is now projected to 
reach approximately 890,000 units by 2030 and 6.5 million units by 2035 (representing a $138 
billion market opportunity)," Sheridan told clients on Sunday. 


[Page 3]
 
 
He said the report lays out a series of key conclusions supporting the upgrade: 
1. Logistics and warehouse operations serve as the primary early adopters forn 
general-purpose humanoids. eCommerce/Retail companies (like Amazon and Walmart) are 
leading this transition. We frame ~5.6% of leverage in Amazon's total cost to serve via 
automation by 2030, which is estimated to yield a ~$72 billion cost savings and a ~240 bps 
EBIT margin tailwind in our upside analysis from a wider deployment of robotics. 
2. While automotive body welding is highly automated, final assembly and partsn sorting remain 
manual. Automakers are actively exploring humanoids to mitigate labor challenges. In a 


[Page 4]
 
scenario analysis where companies adopt humanoids (priced between $20,000 and $60,000) at 
a 10% to 50% rate, we estimate it could drive a 1% to 6% operating margin expansion on a 
gross basis (although the net effect could be less if some savings are passed on in the form of 
lower vehicle prices). 
3. The mobility of humanoids is accelerating the shift from hardware-boundn Programmable 
Logic Controllers (PLCs) to software-defined virtual PLCs (vPLCs). The vPLC market is 
projected to grow 20% to 30% annually, which would call into question the legacy 
hardware-software lock-in of dominant incumbents like Siemens, Rockwell Automation, and 
Schneider Electric. 
4. Humanoid proliferation represents a massive structural growth driver for then semiconductor 
supply chain. The semiconductor addressable market (SAM) is estimated at $3,000 to $6,000+ 
per humanoid, spanning high-performance compute modules ($1,500–$4,000+), 
analog/mixed-signal chips ($750–$1,050+), and edge memory ($600–$800+). 
5. We recently launched Executive Perspectives powered by Goldman Sachs, a new globaln 
initiative that looks to capture insights directly from companies across our global coverage. We 
detail full results from the first survey, which related to robotics and physical AI, and highlight 
that ~40% of respondents expect at least 10% of workflows to be automated in 3-5 years with 
general purpose robotics. 
Sheridan pointed out that deployment of humanoid robotics will primarily be in the logistics 
space first. Amazon already has more than 1 million robots across 300-plus sites. Walmart has 
automated freight into 3,100 U.S. stores and runs over half of e-commerce volume through 
automated facilities.  
After logistics, humanoids are expected to be widely adopted across the auto industry as large 
manufacturers are "actively exploring humanoids to mitigate labor challenges."
