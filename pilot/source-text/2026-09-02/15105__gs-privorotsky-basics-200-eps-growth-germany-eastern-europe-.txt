Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Equity Sales / Basics
Wed 2 Sep 2026, 3:09am ET
Markets: Tricky day for markets yesterday. Geopolitical escalation is now feeding directly into the macro
tape… crude, gas and distillates higher, rates under pressure, equities weaker and correlations picking up
across the board.
Iran/Hormuz: the US escalated again, targeting Iranian air defense, radar and communications
infrastructure around the Strait. Iran retaliated across the region and threatened a larger response. So far
this still feels relatively contained versus what could have happened (Iran retaliated attacking military
asset not energy)… but the focus is entirely on Hormuz. The US claims Iran’s ability to disrupt shipping
has been materially degraded and Chris Wright said 17m barrels transited Monday. That number is heavily
debated, but if flows really are normalizing anywhere near that level, oil could probably find its way
lower. The bigger question is US reflexivity. Until now, every time energy hit the top of the range we saw
intervention, diplomacy or some attempt to cool prices. Yesterday was different… the US escalated while
oil was already rising and during market hours. Either there is a view Iran is weak enough to press the
advantage, or it sees a limited window. Historical precedent has been acute periods of escalation leading to
aggressive de-escalate (window until Labor Day?). But crude is just one component of the problem as
distillate/gasoil/diesel and critically European natural gas have all broken out.
Hardware: Remains strong. Michael Dell: “If you keep growing EPS 200%+ y/y something good will
happen.” Dell smashed numbers… AI server demand remains through the roof and CPU demand is also
accelerating as inference broadens. The message is increasingly simple: token demand/compute demand
continues to beat already bullish expectations.. Key to watch reaction function from DELL today.
Broadcom tonight should reinforce. This remains the fundamental counterweight to the cycle/rates
concerns.
AI/Cyber Security: Lots of early discussion around OpenAI’s Astra. Astra can perform increasingly
complex autonomous tasks using less compute, but the broader issue is monitorability… as models get
more capable and reasoning more optimized, it may become progressively harder to understand why they
do what they do. By compressing intermediate reasoning into abstract representations rather than readable
traces, models effectively blind Chain of Thought monitoring tools. There is a real emerging trade off
between capability and alignment/oversight. Reinforcing this dynamic, Palo Alto Networks raised forward
guidance across all key metrics following Q4 results. CEO Nikesh Arora highlighted that "the latest
advancements in AI are elevating cybersecurity to the top of the CIO priority list, and will serve as durable
tailwinds as we progress towards our $20 billion FY30 NGS ARR target."
Rates: Overnight decent moves at front end in Japan (Ueda spoke)/SK/Australia while RBNZ back to back
hike has resulted in a bull steepener. The reaction function is shifting. This is no longer just a long

end/term premium story… higher commodity inflation is now pulling the front end with it as the market
prices a greater chance the Fed actually responds. That matters. You potentially have a supply shock
hurting growth at the same time as the Fed is pushed toward tightening. Not a great backdrop for
multiples.
Europe: Looks particularly exposed. Nat gas prices are back sharply higher… hard to see Europe
sustainably outperforming while that persists. Germany has also blamed Russia for an attempted sabotage
attack involving a Ukrainian cargo aircraft, as well as a potential attack at a German Power plant. This
adds to the broader pattern of sub NATO threshold attacks across Europe. Ukraine is meanwhile warning
that Russian airspace is becoming increasingly unsafe for airlines. I think this is becoming a meaningful
tail risk… the conflict increasingly spilling into European infrastructure, aviation and security policy.
Risk: Markets were soggy into Jackson Hole and have remained soggy since. The setup is increasingly
simple. Energy is rising, the Fed looks less willing to look through it, and multiples are under pressure.
Yesterday’s softer ISM/JOLTS data helped at the margin, but now it is over to NFP. So for me, oil sits at
the center of everything. If the administration can get crude, gas and product prices lower, yields can
stabilize and risk has a path to recover… provided NFP/CPI cooperate. If energy keeps rising, the Fed
reaction function gets dragged with it and sustained upside becomes much harder. Beyond that, the
underlying AI fundamentals remain exceptionally strong… Nvidia/Dell (watching Broadcom tonight )are
still telling the same story: demand is not slowing and inference demand continues to grow.
side-note: Trading as if they were the same asset. if we want relief on rates we first need relief on energy.
