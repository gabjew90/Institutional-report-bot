Oil Is Back To Trading Iran Strikes But Without The 
Risk Premium 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, SEP 01, 2026 - 03:55 PM 
Authored by Frank Monkam via Bloomberg, 
Options markets expect upside in oil prices to stay contained even as the US resumed strikes in 
Iran after about a one-month hiatus and on Friday announced a takeover of Venezuela oil 
reserves. 
Front-month volatility skew is showing no bid in upside tails for oil as a result of the move... 
 
On the contrary, we’re seeing call premium fade and low-delta puts trading higher than their 
levels on Thursday last week before the Venezuela deal was announced. 
Moreover, riskies are underperforming the bounce in flat price, with 25-delta 1-month risk 
reversal - which measures the premium that call options command over puts of similar tenor and 
moneyness - still near the bottom of the recent range.

Data show Strait of Hormuz crossings have been contracting of late, with reports suggesting 
anywhere from 2 million to 4 million barrels/day getting through versus the US administration’s 
claim of 8m-10m b/d. 
Even so, markets appear hesitant to lean earnestly one way or the other. 
This has resulted in quite a tame reaction function to the renewed strikes. 
We had highlighted a U-turn decay dynamic taking place in oil markets, whereby oil-related 
headlines are generating increasingly limited market reaction over time as the US and Iran 
constantly shift course. 
While a return to kinetic warfare is certainly a sign of escalation, markets seem to chalk it up to 
what could be another round of posturing from both parties rather than a meaningful 
intensification, particularly as it comes to a potential worsening in physical market disruptions. 
As oil risk gets transferred from cracks back to flat price, and systematic upside-price levels 
potentially get hit, this complacency could fade fairly quickly as we move past the futures roll 
window in Brent this week.
