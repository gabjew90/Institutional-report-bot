Amid Macro Uncertainty & Sloppy Seasonals, 
Goldman's Flow Gurus See Positioning Set For 
Markets To Move Lower 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, SEP 01, 2026 - 05:30 PM 
Investors have taken down US equity exposure across segments and sectors, according to 
Goldman Sachs flows gurus latest note, while volatility has repriced lower on the index and 
single stock level. 
Gail Hafif, Brian Garrett, and Lee Coppersmith note that with macro uncertainty hanging in the 
air, positioning indicates investors are angling for an event that moves markets lower...  
This is in sharp contrast to the equity vol market, which is currently assigning a very low 
probability of risk-off and is charging ~ytd lows for convexity.  
Here's what they are watching... 
1. Sentiment/Positioning  
Despite trading less than 115bps from ATHs going into today's session, the market is exhibiting 
signs of nervousness across myriad of indicators. The latest AAII bulls versus bears investor 
sentiment survey had over 44% of investors leaning bearish toward the market.  AAIIBEAR bear 
has averaged >40 for the last four weeks, which historically has coincided with ~7-10% 
drawdowns. 


[Page 2]
 
 
This attitude toward risk is not just theoretical, investors are quite literally putting their money 
where their mouth is in terms of portfolio risk allocations.  
Our latest GS Sentiment Indicator reading, a measure of investor positioning, was +0.27 which 
is down from +1.1 last week. This puts positioning at a neutral level reflecting the risk appetite at 
this point in time.

2. Hedge Funds 
We saw fast money continue to take down risk last week as US L/S Gross leverage rose +1.9 
pts to 207% (19th percentile one-year) – while US L/S Net leverage fell -0.7 pts to 47.6% (1st 
percentile one-year). US Fundamental long/short ratio (MV) also decreased -1.2% to 1.597 (1st 
percentile one-year). 
This is by far the lowest levels of exposure we have seen in over a year – with lower levels only 
seen during liberation day in the ChatGPT era (post 2023). With several key equity market 
hurdles cleared, we think this provides a cleaner set-up for a delayed summer chop higher 
through Labor Day.  
 
Goldman Sachs FICC and Equities and Prime Services data as of 28-Aug-26. 
 3. Prime Book - Tides Turning  
While we saw single stocks be net sold for a second straight week (-0.7 SDs 1-year), driven by 
short sales outpacing long buys (3.3 to 1), we interestingly saw all defensive sectors (Health 
Care, Utilities, Staples) be net sold on the week.  
This defensive selling together was the largest $ net selling since Apr ’25.  
Though not necessarily a “greenlight” risk-on signal, it is indicative of the cautious rotation back 
into more cyclical pockets of the market with consumer discretionary and TMT catching a bid.

4. Thematic Shifts  
With pivotal earnings prints from both prominent software and semis names, the almost 
canonical bifurcation has shown signs of convergence.  
With “at risk” companies noting material benefits from AI development, the winners and losers of 
the AI revolution are looking to be defined less by the sub-sectors they represent, and more by 
the manner in which AI can be used as a tool to retain and enhance legacy moats. 
To the effect, the 1m correlation between our Broad AI (GSTMTAIP) and Software (GSTMTSFT) 
baskets jumped +0.7 over the past month to reach +0.15 after reaching a low of -0.56 at the end 
of July.

5. Fund flows  
On the topline, net flows into global equity funds remained positive in the week ending August 
26 (+$9bn vs +$40bn in the previous week). 
However, within DM, US funds drove net outflows while EM funds saw net inflows, driven by 
Global EM benchmark funds (Korea funds saw net outflows). 
At the sector level, technology funds saw the largest net inflows while financial funds saw the 
largest net outflows. 


[Page 6]
 
 
Source: EPFR, Haver Analytics, Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research 
The retail bid abated temporarily last week as US equity funds saw $4.39bn of outflows – a net 
change of -$33.3bn from last week. Meanwhile, money market funds saw $9.2bn in inflows last 
week which is a net change of over +$10bn from last week. Overall, the bid was strong enough 
in previous weeks to plausibly explain this week as a healthy breather. However, if macro risks 
and uncertainties persist going into a seasonally difficult period in the second half of September, 
this can continue. 
The structural bid, though, has remained intact and will likely not be deterred in the longer term 
especially with the market close to ATHs.  
6. Buybacks 
Our buyback desk volumes finished the week at 1.2x vs 2025 YTD ADTV and 1.1x vs 2024 YTD 
ADTV, with flow heaviest in Tech, Financials, and Health Care sectors. 
We anticipate volumes will remain at this pace over the next week.

With liquidity trending lower into Labor Day,  this moderated corporate bid still provides a 
notable layer of support to the market. 
Looking ahead, we estimate the Q3 blackout window will begin ~9/15, when roughly ~40% of 
the S&P 500 is estimated to enter their quiet period ahead of earnings - also corresponding to 
the worst two-week period of the year for the S&P.

While the pace of the post-July rally felt dramatic in nature, these cohorts’ pace of re-risking was 
generally more moderate on a historical basis at the 64th percentile on a 5y lookback.

8. Panic Index…or lack thereof  
Our GS Panic Index is an indicator of the percentile rank of the weighted average of 1-month 
S&P implied volatility, VIX volatility, S&P 1m put-call skew, and the S&P term structure slope 
(1m vs. 3m) on a rolling 2y lookback. 
The latest reading was 0.73/10 which normally indicates complacency in the market.  
Now, we think investors are positioned and waiting for an event to bring markets lower amid all 
the “question marks” sitting between here and retaking the highs - defining the complacency as 
perhaps too tilted to an anticipated leg lower.

9. Cost of Risk Premia  
In that vein, if we haven’t mentioned it enough, we’ll say it again – the cost of equity vol is low. 
SPX 1m IV is in the 3rd percentile on a 1y lookback and the 1m average S&P single stock IV is 
in the 17th percentile on the same lookback.  
While realized vol has moved concurrently lower, we continue to remain in the camp of “hedge 
when you can” as monetary policy, geopolitical, and domestically political catalysts line the road 
ahead, and take advantage of cheaper short-dated gamma to play for a belated grind higher.

10. Liquidity  
S&P top of book liquidity has held in remarkably well this summer as the dog days came in just 
in time for summer last licks. 
Friday was the first time in almost three weeks that top of book fell below the 1y rolling average. 
At $11.12mm as of last week’s close, the ability to transfer risk remains robust though we expect 
a moderate weakening through the official end of summer as OOO emails hit their peak.

Bonus. Seasonality 
It is well-broadcasted that September is the worst month seasonally for the S&P with the 2H 
being the worst two-week period of the year.  
 
This is a difficult dynamic to fight and may play more of a role come mid-month. 
Interestingly, one index's trash is another factor's treasure. 
Nearly to the day, the trajectory of momentum and SPX diverge with returns averaging +6% vs 
-1.25% respectively between September 15th and the end of the month.
