 
Goldman's Favorite Trade If Democrats Sweep The 
Midterms 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SUNDAY, NOV 01, 2026 - 03:30 PM 
Prediction markets have spent the past month pricing a Democratic sweep. The stocks that 
should benefit most from one have spent the past three weeks going the other way. 
That disconnect is the lead trade in this week's "Global Reflections" (available to pro subs) from 
Goldman's custom baskets team, led by Louis Miller, who sets the scene in appropriately 
seasonal fashion: 
In true Halloween spirit, the market got spooked and the AI pain trade continued. Hyperscaler 
earnings did little but leave the market hyper-focused on the AI funding and returns debate. 
Meanwhile, appetite to add risk in ex-AI pockets is also anemic as we await the midterms and a 
decision on Iran. 
Put differently, the market only trades two things right now, AI and oil, and Miller says "demand 
for AI- and oil-uncorrelated trades has never been higher." His answer, mostly, is Healthcare. 
Below we go through his five ideas for the week after Halloween, and the one problem with the 
headline trade that Goldman only half-mentions. 
Broadening Worked "Perfectly"... Maybe Too Perfectly 
First, the backdrop. Goldman's S&P ex-AI index (SPXXAI) "worked perfectly in October," Miller 
writes, and the chart shows just how perfectly: on Thursday it posted its third-best day of 
outperformance vs. the S&P ever (+2.2pp, bottom panel), and it now sits about 5 points ahead 
of the index over the past year (chart source GS FICC & Equities, Bloomberg): 
 

 
 
As we noted on Friday, ex-AI stocks were heading for their best month since 2022. But Miller 
adds a caveat that the broadening crowd may want to hear: "a potentially weaker growth outlook 
would warrant special caution on pockets that have primarily benefited from a short squeeze." 
Translation: not everything ex-AI went up because it's good; some of it went up because it was 
short. 
The Sweep Trade: Obamacare 
On to the midterms, where Polymarket and Kalshi have Democrats at better than 90% to take 
the House and betting markets price a full sweep at around 70%. In that scenario, Goldman's 
favorite long is its Affordable Care Act Beneficiaries basket (GSHLCACA): 
Democrats have heavily pushed to revive the enhanced ACA premium tax credits that expired 
at end-2025. Reauthorizing these subsidies should improve the earnings outlook for hospitals, 
outpatient providers and healthcare insurance companies that currently face rising bad debt and 
uncompensated care costs. 
And here's why Goldman says the entry point is "attractive." The blue line below is Kalshi's odds 
of a Democratic sweep, now 68%. The black line is the ACA basket relative to the S&P ex-AI, 
which has dropped roughly 7% from its early-October peak even as the sweep odds kept rising: 
 

 
 
Goldman's other midterm calls: 
●​ AI Power (GSENEPOW) gets a tailwind if more power supply is sold as the cure for 
electricity inflation. 
●​ AI hardware ex-Power (GSTMTAIH) could suffer if policymakers converge on making 
datacenters bear more of the grid cost, which "could drive weaker datacenter project 
economics." 
●​ Corporate tax (GSPUTAXP) is "worth putting on your radar." Because the president can 
veto, the midterms are unlikely to change tax law, so 2027-28 earnings are "largely 
insulated", but a higher rate further out is a risk "beyond 2029." 
Which is the problem. The same veto that protects corporate tax rates also stands between 
Democrats and an ACA subsidy bill. Trump has already promised to "veto everything" a 
Democratic Congress sends him, and he let those same credits expire in the first place. The 
only realistic route runs through a shutdown or debt ceiling standoff, where (as Citi noted this 
week) the price of Democratic votes may be delaying spending cuts rather than adding new 
subsidies. Maybe the basket's 7% slide is the market reading the veto pen, not missing the 
polls.

The kicker, per Miller: "Healthcare AI Beneficiaries exhibit low correlation to AI, rates, and oil, 
providing genuine diversification value in otherwise AI and/or energy correlated portfolios." AI 
exposure that doesn't trade like AI; diversification at its finest, at least on paper. Goldman's 
credit team reached a similar conclusion from the other side of the capital structure on Friday, 
with chief credit strategist Amanda Lynam naming Healthcare/Pharma among the IG sectors 
with the lowest correlation to AI spreads, i.e. the new "defensives". 
 

 
For those who'd rather not pick sectors at all, Goldman's systematic team pitches its Growth 
Value Rotator, up 27% YTD long-only (GSRHBUBG) and 18% long/short (GSRHBUBT), as a 
way to trade growth/value swings "without calling their timing or direction." 
Underpriced Disruption: Software... And Your Insurer 
Next, the trade that has been quietly crushing it. Goldman's Software vs. Semis pair 
(GSPUSOSE) is up 88% from its June lows, which Miller attributes partly to "momentum's 
recent reversal of fortunes" (the pair has delivered a 3x beta to the momo unwind, black line 
inverted) and partly to the AI-disruption narrative for software becoming "a lot more nuanced 
than a year ago": 
 
The catch: Software's forward P/E has gone from 17x to 23x in four months, so earnings now 
have to separate the companies with "structural earnings pressure" from those with "sufficient 
competitive moats." Given that momentum has been "unplayable" for weeks, a 3x beta to the 
unwind cuts both ways. 
The most interesting idea in the note, though, comes in Miller's "food for thought": the Binance 
hack, the biggest heist in crypto history at some $6.3 billion, points to another pocket of 
"underpriced AI disruption": insurance. The sector "benefited from a defensive rally in October 
but could face significant earnings headwinds from underappreciated underwriting risk" (he 
 

 
points to a Munich Re white paper on the topic), particularly if risk relief rotates Financials flows 
back into banks. We had our own take on what the hack did to the macro narrative: 
Longer term, Goldman expects the cybersecurity TAM to keep growing as AI use cases spread, 
and notes rising client interest in non-US ways to play it. 
Greek Banks, Japanese Banks, And A Trick-Or-Treat Defense 
Rally 
Finally, Europe and Japan. European banks just posted their worst month since March 2023. 
Goldman doesn't expect "significant OAT-Bund tightening near-term" (nor do we, France being 
France), but thinks the selloff "looks overdone" given limited spillover risk and solid 
fundamentals, with ROTE seen expanding 200bp through 2028. Its preferred way in is Greek 
banks (GSXEGRBK), which de-rated roughly 10% in sympathy with the sector but are now 
separating on the back of solid Q3 NII beats, an Alpha Bank capital markets day on Nov 5 and 
MSCI's developed-market upgrade in May 2027: 
 
In Japan, bank stocks have "disconnected from solid underlying earnings," and Goldman thinks 
BoJ signaling for a December hike (Ueda left the door open on Friday, with one board member 
already voting to hike) sets them up for a year-end relief rally. The risk, Miller concedes, is that 
"an 'escalate to de-escalate' strategy does not materialize" with Iran, "driving incremental 
inflation and rates pain." 
 

 
And then there's European Defense (GSSBDEFE), up 13% in three weeks. Part of that, 
Goldman admits, is simply non-AI exposure plus "high short crowding." But with GS research 
expecting European defense spending to grow at an 8% CAGR over FY25-30, while valuations 
imply declining medium-term earnings, the desk sees potential for a roughly 30% re-rating back 
to September 2025 levels if Q3 results show production ramps and cash generation are on 
track. Trick or treat, indeed. 
The Vote Is Priced. The Veto Isn't. 
Miller's note is a useful map for a market that, as Pasquariello and Wilson both described this 
week (more on that here), has stopped trading as one index and started trading as two factors. 
On the diversifiers we're with him: Healthcare AI's low correlation to AI, rates and oil is real, 
Goldman's credit team sees the same pattern in spreads, and insurers look complacent about a 
cyber risk that just cost one exchange $6.3 billion in a single heist. 
The headline sweep trade is another matter. A Democratic House looks like a near-lock, but the 
ACA subsidies Goldman is betting on still need a signature from a president who has promised 
to veto everything and who let them lapse in the first place. Add a Fed that is still hiking, Brent 
above $110 and Washington reportedly preparing Gulf allies for operations against Iran after the 
vote, and gridlock is the most likely outcome, not an Obamacare revival. The basket's 7% slide 
may be the market getting that right, early.
