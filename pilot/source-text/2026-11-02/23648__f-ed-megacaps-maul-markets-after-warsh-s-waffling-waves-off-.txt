The Fed & Rate-Hike Odds 
The statement showed a Pause (as expected), 2 dissents (less hawkish than expected but Lisa 
Cook joined the gang), a commitment to "accumulate more information" was the excuse 
The presser saw Warsh double down on his rejection of signaling policy decisions: "I don't 
believe in it. I don’t favor it. I also don’t believe in prejudging meetings. A lot of things can 
happen." 
Warsh was bullish on the economy and hawkish on inflation: 
"On the employment side of the Fed’s dual mandate, conditions are quite positive, and yet 
Americans, of course, are still dealing with inflation that has been running above the 
committee’s objective for close to six years. It’s our responsibility to get inflation down." 
Real activity is clearly "pointing in the right direction," Warsh says. 
Then he hinted that markets are doing some of the Fed’s work for it: 
"While overall financial conditions are tighter, I’m unwilling and unable to call them tight. But 
broadly, these developments go in the direction of achieving a longer, more durable period of 
economic growth and price stability." 
Warsh confirmed a change to policy meetings timing: 
In 2027, one of the Fed’s meetings will focus on broad monetary policy strategy but without a 
vote on policy itself. So next year there will be seven rate decisions scheduled, not eight. And in 
2028 there will be only six. 
 

 
"Politics played no role in the decision," Warsh says. 
The bottom line, as far as UBS is concerned, is that the new wording about accumulating more 
data appears to nod to Warsh's view that he bases his decisions on trends rather than single 
data points. If trends continue as they are, then December is on... and so we saw hike odds for 
Dec remain high but Jan odds drop during the presser... 
 
Overall, rate-hike expectations dipped a little with 2027 the most volatile during Warsh's 
presser... 
 
Combine that with the hawkish tone in the presser (pointing to stronger economic growth and 
still-elevated inflation as robust capital spending underscores the economy's resilience) and 
 

 
Warsh sounding confident on the outlook for inflation expectations (which he suggests remain 
anchored), the equity market's jump during the press conference was a little bewildering (as it 
always is).

Energy 
A lot of moving parts as always in the energy space... 
Oil prices surged back up near multi-month highs today as traders weighed the prospect of 
resumed US military strikes on Iran after the Midterms. The US has reportedly (WSJ citing 
unidentified people) told several Persian Gulf nations to be prepared for a short, intense 
campaign. Trump was leaning toward a large post-election attack but hadn't decided, the paper 
said. 
That threat arrived right as diplomacy hopes were dashed with Iran rejecting a proposal from 
Qatar to end the US-led war on the country, calling it "unacceptable" (with the big sticking point, 
once again, being the nuclear issue). But then, later in the day, this HL dropped: 
*ARAGHCHI:IRAN CONTINUES TO GIVE DIPLOMACY CHANCE TO SUCCEED - kocking 
crude off its highs 
This came alongside the Houthis claiming that they struck Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery 
(which has a processing capacity of 550,000 barrels a day and is located at one of the 
kingdom's main crude-export hubs) in the Eastern Province with a ballistic missile. A Saudi-led 
military coalition, which includes Bahrain and Egypt, separately said that its air defenses 
intercepted and destroyed three ballistic missiles launched toward the Eastern Province and 
four drones targeting the kingdom's southern region. Saudi Arabia's Civil Defense also reported 
damage following another intercepted Houthi attack. 
Additionally, prices were supported by US commercial crude stocks falling more than expected 
(a fourth consecutive weekly decline), and crude exports jumped to nearly 5 million barrels a 
day, the highest level on record.
