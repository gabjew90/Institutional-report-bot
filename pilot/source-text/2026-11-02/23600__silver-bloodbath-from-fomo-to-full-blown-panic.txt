The great silver puke
Silver is imploding. The metal is testing $50, volatility is catching aggressive bids and CTAs are
pressing shorts, with potentially more selling to come. Even more striking, silver has decoupled from
the dollar, suggesting something bigger than a conventional macro move. And the implications may
extend well beyond precious metals. Silver has tracked KOSPI remarkably closely, raising an
uncomfortable question about the psychology of manias and whether the AI unwind has further to run.
Oversold? Absolutely. But is the liquidation over?
Can't catch a bid
Silver continues to implode, testing the $50 level as we write and trading well below its 200-day
moving average. The next meaningful support sits at the major long-term trendline in the $46–48 area.
A market struggling to find a bid.

Silver fear
Silver volatility typically trades with an upside skew: silver rallies, volatility rises. The latest sell-off has
flipped that relationship, with vols catching aggressive bids as prices plunge. The message? Investors
are scrambling for downside protection in a rather panicky fashion. A notable shift in a market where
volatility has traditionally been driven by upside speculation.

Decoupling from the dollar
Silver and the dollar have traded in a remarkably tight inverse relationship this year. The latest silver
puke has broken that relationship, with the metal sharply underperforming the inverted DXY. Silver is
no longer simply trading the dollar.

The psychology of manias
Silver and KOSPI have traded remarkably closely, with Korean equities often following silver's moves
with a lag. While silver is also an input into the AI ecosystem, the connection may have more to do with
the psychology of manias than fundamentals. Momentum, crowded positioning and FOMO have fueled
both the silver and AI trades. When the narrative cracks, the same forces can work violently in reverse.
Silver has already suffered a brutal liquidation. The question is whether the AI mania still has
considerably more room to the downside.

CTAs pressing the downside
CTAs are already running substantial silver shorts, but there is still room to add as downside
momentum builds. Positioning is increasingly bearish, yet systematic selling pressure may not be
exhausted. Further weakness could trigger another wave of CTA selling, turning an already brutal
liquidation into a self-reinforcing momentum unwind.
