Flipping
Just three weeks ago, Deutsche Bank's Binky Chadha downgraded Tech to neutral, ahead of a brutal
rotation. Fifteen trading days later, he's flipping right back to overweight.
The logic is simple: Tech has gone from crowded to unloved, while earnings remain strong. Binky sees
the risk/reward flipping again and history suggests he may be onto something.
LSEG Workspace
From the top to the bottom in 3 weeks
Mega-cap growth and Tech have underperformed the rest of the S&P 500 by more than 16 percentage
points in just three weeks, taking relative performance to the bottom of its decade-long trend channel.
Deutsche Bank notes that the sector has staged five V-shaped recoveries from similar levels in the past
2½ years.
Deutsche Bank
History says buy the rotation
We have seen 5 rotations away from Tech in the last 2 1/2 years. This one is somewhat shallower, but
Binky thinks it fits the bill. If he is right, we are in for a ride. The last five rotations into Tech saw the
sector gain an average 38%.
Deutsche Bank
Not crowded anymore
Tech positioning has collapsed from elevated levels to just the 40th percentile. Three weeks ago,

everyone was positioned for perfection. Now investors seem to be bracing for an earnings recession.
Wall Street sentiment apparently moves faster than the fundamentals.
Deutsche
Pricing in an earnings collapse?
Current positioning implies Tech earnings growth of roughly 10%, versus 47% in Q3 and consensus
expectations of 36% by the end of next year.
In other words, investors are positioned for a dramatic slowdown that hasn't actually shown up in the
earnings numbers. Yet.

Who got growth?
Tech is a natural stand-out in terms of revenue growth in Q3. Top line growth estimates have actually
also been revised up over the past month.
FactSet
So much for the one-trick AI pony
For those still insisting this is nothing but an AI-driven earnings boom, the data suggest otherwise. The
median S&P 500 company is growing earnings by 14.4%, near two-decade highs outside recession
rebounds. Strip out Tech and Energy entirely, and earnings are still growing at 12%.
Apparently, corporate America didn't get the memo that everything outside AI is supposed to be dead.
