Morning from a cabin in a forest in the middle of nowhere. As the world debates the arrival of AGI and 
superintelligence, I'm moving around my lodge trying to eke out a WiFi or 4G signal. It's as painful as the 
harness I was strapped into yesterday to do a big zip wire over a lake! Talking of technology, as expected big 
tech earnings have been more influential this week than central banks. Alphabet (-12.64%) saw it worst day 
on record yesterday after results which weighed down the S&P (-0.30%) even though 406 stocks in the 
index advanced. After the bell disappointing results from Amazon and Intel added to the souring tech 
mood, and Nasdaq futures are currently -1.86% lower after the cash index closed -1.38% last night. The 
tech selloff has supported Treasuries, with 10yr yields (-8.0bps) falling to their lowest level in 6 weeks at 
4.94% and edging to 4.923% this morning. By contrast, in Europe the DAX (+1.31%), CAC 40 (+1.05%) and 
Stoxx 600 (+0.77%) all advanced, supported by upbeat earnings and a slightly dovish ECB on-hold 
decision. A retreat in energy prices also supported European bonds yesterday, but oil prices have moved 
higher again overnight on fresh concerns about US-Iran escalation. 
So a lot going on. Let's start with those after-hours earnings. Amazon’s Q3 net sales fell just short of 
analyst expectations ($201.94bn vs $202.26bn est.) while spending on property and equipment soared 
in the quarter ($77bn vs $59bn est.). So while cloud revenue growth was a touch above expectations, this 
was insufficient to ease concerns about the rising capex spend, and Amazon’s shares slumped by -7% in 
extended trading. If that move is replicated in today’s regular session, it would leave the stock marginally 
down year-to-date. Meanwhile, Intel plunged by -23% after-hours after its Q4 revenue guidance missed 
estimates ($15-16bn vs $17.05n est.). In addition to sending Nasdaq (-1.86%) and S&P 500 (-0.89%) 
futures lower, these earnings have exacerbated the sell-off in Asia this morning, with the KOSPI (-
3.99%) and Nikkei (-2.22%) sliding. 
This follows a weaker US session as Alphabet (-12.64%) posted its biggest drop on record after 
underwhelming results the night before, which left its share price -9.32% YTD. The S&P 500 (-0.30%) was 
underpinned by 406 stocks advancing and the equal weight index (+1.21%) rose to its highest level since 
August. So it was a concentrated tech led sell-off with the Nasdaq (-1.38%), Magnificent 7 (-1.83%) and 

Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (-1.42%) all substantially lower. Tech underperformers also included 
CoreWeave (-4.99%) and Oracle (-3.45%). In other tech news, Microsoft completed a $25bn bond offering, 
becoming the last of the US hyperscalers to turn to debt markets this year. The sale marked Microsoft’s 
first major bond issuance since 2017. 
The subdued US performance stood in contrast to Europe, where equities rallied after a slate of upbeat 
earnings, including those from Adidas (+5.76%) and Shell (+2.16%). That in turn pushed several indices 
higher, including the DAX (+131%), CAC 40 (+1.05%) and Stoxx 600 (+0.77%). 
Turning to rates, the correction in the AI trade was a boon for Treasuries, with both 2yr (-9.8bps to 4.60%) 
and 10yr (-8.0bps to 4.94%) yields falling to their lowest levels since mid-September. The decline in 
Treasury yields came despite a strong batch of US data yesterday. Among them was the advance Q3 GDP 
print, which showed the economy expanding at a +3.4% q/q annualised rate (+3.2% exp.), up from +2.2%q/q 
in Q2. And the labour market remained resilient too, as weekly initial jobless claims edged down to 194k (vs. 
198k exp.). Remarkably, that took the 4-week average in initial claims down to their lowest level since 
October 1969, at 196.75k. So the data bolstered Warsh’s bullish growth assessment from Wednesday during 
the FOMC. Meanwhile, the September PCE report showed headline PCE inflation +0.5%m/m (+0.3% m/m 
prior) and +3.6% y/y (+3.4%y/y prior). Core PCE came in at +0.3%m/m (+0.2%m/m prior) and +3.1%y/y 
(+3.0%y/y prior), all in line with expectations. 
Over in Europe, the focus was on the ECB meeting where ECB President Christine Lagarde announced 
plans to step down as President by Feb 7. The news that she might step down early had long been 
expected, even if its timing was not previously known. She'll now only have the December and early 
February meetings left. Importantly, this does mean French President Macron will be involved in the 
decision on the next ECB leader, as opposed to the next incoming French President. It also opens the 
door for her to play some role in the French election campaign, with Lagarde saying she was leaving early to 
have the freedom to speak on matters away from monetary policy. Our economists expect that the 
leadership change should not have much short-term impact on the direction of ECB policy.    
In terms of the ECB decision itself, the ECB kept rates on hold at 2.50%, as widely expected, with 
President Lagarde striking a balanced tone but signalling that “the direction of travel” is towards 
another hike in December. The decision press release tilted a bit dovish, noting that “underlying inflation 
has remained broadly stable so far” and that tighter financial conditions were “likely to weigh on inflation and 
growth”. That said, the near-term dovish read across was largely offset by President Lagarde in the press 
conference. She suggested that yesterday was a “no hiking decision” as the ECB could afford to wait for 
more information, while implying that a December hike is the clear baseline absent a surprise over the 
next six weeks. Overall, our economists see yesterday’s decision as reducing the risk of rates going to 3% or 
beyond, in line with their view of 2.75% being the likely terminal rate. See their reaction note here. 
So while pricing of a December rate hike was little change at 84% yesterday, money markets dialed back 
prospects of 2027 ECB hikes, with 64bps of hikes now priced by next September (-10bps on the day). 
That meant front-end bonds rallied with 2yr OAT (-7.1bps), BTP (-7.9bps) and bund (-5.3bps) yields falling. 
Bonds were also helped by decline in energy costs, with European natural gas futures down -2.12% to EUR 
98.59/MWh after reaching their highest level since 2022 on Wednesday. Yields on 10yr bunds (-1.5bps), 
BTPs (-2.0bps) and gilts (-2.7bps) also all declined, though OATs (-0.1bps) underperformed, with the 10yr 
OAT-Bund spread rising back to 156bps. 

Staying on the energy front, oil prices have had a volatile 24 hours, with Brent crude trading +1.07% higher 
at around $116.11/bbl as I type, after almost reaching $118 overnight. Brent had fallen as low as $113 
yesterday after Semafor reported that the White House was involved in ongoing negotiations centered 
on a temporary peace deal with Iran. However, we then saw contrasting news later in the US session, with 
the WSJ reporting that a top US admiral has briefed Trump on military options against Iran. Meanwhile, Iran’s 
Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf said that Iran would not compromise on its position for ending the 
conflict, which comes after the WSJ reported the previous day that US Vice President Vance had reached 
out to Iran’s top security official to revive talks. 
Now to the rest of Asia, and the BOJ left rates unchanged at 1.25%, in line with expectations, with eight of 
the nine board members voting in favour of the decision. Board member Hajime Takata was the lone 
dissenter, advocating for a 25bp rate increase. The central bank also noted that underlying consumer 
inflation has now reached its 2% target and is expected to remain around that level. In a surprise 
announcement, the BOJ said it would nearly triple the pace of sales of its ETF holdings from January, 
increasing annual disposals to an estimated ¥860bn ($5.5bn). At this pace, the process would take 
roughly 43 years to complete, compared with more than 110 years under the previous schedule. 
Probably beyond the natural life of the EMR regardless of the pace! 
Focus now turns to Governor Ueda’s press conference which will start after we go to press, where 
markets will be looking for further guidance on the timing and pace of future rate hikes. Following the interest 
rate decision, the Japanese yen (+0.22%) is strengthening higher for the third straight session trading at 
157.38 against the dollar as we go to print. Staying with Japan, early morning data showed that Tokyo CPI 
rose +2.6% y/y, below September's +2.7% increase and in line with market expectations while core-core 
CPI rose +2.6% annually, after a +2.9% increase the previous month. 
As we mentioned at the top, tech sensitive Asian indices are notably lower but other markets are more mixed 
with the CSI 300 (-0.27%) and Shanghai Composite (-0.19%) edging lower, while the Hang Seng (+1.75%) is 
outperforming seemingly on a short covering rally on those stocks that had funded the AI rotation trade.   
In other overnight news, crypto assets have come under pressure after Binance, the world’s largest 
crypto exchange, suspended deposits and withdrawals following a suspected hacking incident. Bitcoin is 
down -0.40% this morning, following a -3.03% slide yesterday. 
Finally, today we will get October flash inflation releases for France, Spain, Germany and Italy, ahead of the 
Euro Area aggregate next Wednesday. Our European economists expect inflation to rise in Italy and 
France, while easing slightly in Germany and Spain. Based on that, they expect the Euro Area aggregate 
to see headline HICP rising to 3.90% YoY in October (3.77% prev),  while core HICP is likely to remain little 
changed at 2.5%, as marginal upside pressures from core goods are expected to be offset by a slight easing 
in services. You can read their full preview here. Over the pond we’ll also see the US Q3 employment cost 
index. Earnings today include ExxonMobil and Chevron.
