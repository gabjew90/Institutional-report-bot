 
Partying Like It's 1999... In Coca-Cola: BofA Says 
Defensive Froth Is A Feature Of Bubble Markets 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, OCT 28, 2026 - 11:55 AM 
Last week, BofA's derivatives team made the counterintuitive case that the flight-to-safety has 
become a bubble of its own, with the anti-AI rotation pushing healthcare and staples to the top 
of its Bubble Risk Indicator. One week (and another nearly 3% Nasdaq puke) later, the 
defensives are still there, and BofA has gone back 30 years to show that this has happened 
before... right at the peak of the dotcom bubble. 
From the latest Global Equity Volatility Insights note, "The age of idiosyncratic risk" (available to 
pro subs): 
As the AI trade wobbles, defensives like healthcare and staples have seen a historic rotation 
that has paradoxically pushed their BRIs towards bubble-risk levels. It turns out this is not 
unprecedented as we saw similar behaviour in the late 90s Dotcom era and COVID melt-ups. 
These dynamics provide further evidence that markets continue to move towards a more 
bubble-like state. 
Healthcare: Frothier Than Bitcoin 
A quick refresher: BofA's BRI, which flagged the Nasdaq crossing its "bubble threshold" back in 
June, is a purely price-based gauge that distils an asset's returns, vol, momentum and "fragility" 
into a single 0-to-1 bubble reading, with anything above 0.8 flagged as elevated bubble risk. As 
of Oct 23, US healthcare sits at roughly 0.8, the highest of any asset BofA tracks, with staples 
(about 0.74) and energy (about 0.7) right behind. For comparison, Tech is around 0.5, the 
Nasdaq about 0.3 and the Mag7 a modest 0.25: 
 

 
 
Put differently, the "safe" corner of the market is now showing more bubble-like price action than 
the AI trade it was supposed to hedge, not to mention Bitcoin, Ethereum and Kospi. 
Diversification at its finest. 
1999 Called: It Wants Its Defensive Bubble Back 
The new piece of evidence this week is BofA's 30-year heatmap of BRI readings across S&P 
sectors. During the late-90s dotcom mania, and again in the post-COVID melt-up, the bubble 
readings lit up nearly everywhere, defensives included: 
 
BofA's takeaway is that "structurally bubble-like markets can create frothy dynamics in sectors 
where fundamentals may not." In other words, when the whole market goes reflexive, even 
toothpaste and pharma start trading like momentum stocks. And for BofA, the emergence of 
froth in healthcare and staples is just more evidence for its broader call that markets are moving 
towards an "increasingly reflexive and boom-bust prone regime." 
 

 
Nothing says "healthy market" like a dotcom analogue for the sectors that are supposed to 
protect you from the dotcom analogue. 
Still Nowhere Near 2000... Yet 
Before anyone dusts off the Pets.com sock puppet, there is a meaningful difference. The froth 
today is still highly localized. Only about 15 S&P 500 stocks currently have a BRI above 0.8, 
versus 50 to 100 at the frothiest points of the dotcom bubble: 
 
Measured by index weight the gap is even starker: those 15 names make up just 2.2% of the 
S&P's market cap, compared with 20% to 40% at the dotcom peaks: 
 

 
 
And who are those 15? A list that would confuse any 1999 day trader: Moderna (MRNA), Zebra 
(ZBRA), CrowdStrike (CRWD), IFF and Revvity (RVTY) top the chart, with Coca-Cola (KO), 
Amgen (AMGN), Cboe (CBOE) and Palo Alto (PANW) not far behind. 
 
 

 
Where Else The Froth Is Building 
BofA has now made the BRI a weekly feature, and has expanded it to popular equity themes. 
Cloud compute shows the highest (if not yet extreme) bubble reading among themes, followed 
by oil & gas and high dividend: 
 
The biggest one-week jump, however, came in high dividend stocks, followed by low vol and 
biotech. Yes, the bubble is now in the stuff that pays you to wait: 
 

 
 
Drill down to BofA's custom baskets and the hotspots get even more eclectic. The highest 
readings belong to the Brazil Election basket (0.94) and the US Cyber Security basket (0.92), 
followed by US Software (0.76), Oil & Gas ex-Renewables (0.73), Pharma Hyperscalers (0.70) 
and, fittingly, Japan Defensives (0.70). At the bottom: China Internet, Macau Gaming and the 
China Anti-Involution basket, which register essentially zero froth. 
Meanwhile, the broader stress backdrop is getting less friendly. BofA's Global Financial Stress 
Index rose from +0.09 to +0.20 last week, its highest level since April, as stress rose across 
asset classes and regions. As in April, commodities and equities are the most stressed asset 
classes, crude implied vol stress hit its highest since July 31 (when the Iran conflict was more 
active), and European stress is now above its long-run median. The bank's GFSI Risk Allocator 
flipped to a 4.9% underweight from neutral the week before. 
 

 
When Even Coca-Cola Is A Momentum Trade 
BofA is careful to stress that a high BRI is a "watch out" signal, not a "get out" one: historically, 
readings above 0.8 have come with more near-term downside risk but broadly similar upside, 
which is why the bank prefers adding asymmetric protection to outright selling. Its trade of 
choice from last week was a put-vs-put switch: buying XLP or XLV November puts while selling 
equal notional XLK November puts, for a small credit (0.23% and 0.20%, respectively, at the Oct 
19 open). 
We'd frame it more simply. In a market where everyone hides from AI risk in the same handful of 
"safe" names, those names stop being safe. The dotcom heatmap is a reminder that when 
liquidity floods everything, the bubble eventually shows up everywhere, including the corners 
that are supposed to be immune. And with stress now at April highs, we suspect the next leg of 
this rotation will be less about which bubble to own and more about which one deflates first. 
Our money is on the one that's priced like a Treasury but trades like a meme stock.
