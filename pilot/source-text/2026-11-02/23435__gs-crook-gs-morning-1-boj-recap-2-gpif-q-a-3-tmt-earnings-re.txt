1) BoJ Recap
JAPAN ECON (OTA): The Bank of Japan (BOJ), as was widely expected, decided to maintain the policy
rate at 1.25% (by a majority vote). Board member Hajime Takata dissented, proposing a rate hike to
1.50%. In the October Outlook Report, the BOJ growth forecast was broadly unchanged. Regarding the
price outlook, while the BOJ slightly lowered its inflation forecasts for FY2026, FY2027 forecasts were
raised slightly to reflect higher crude oil prices since the summer. The closely watched wording on the
underlying inflation rate was changed from achieving 2 percent target "between the second half of fiscal
2026 and fiscal 2027" to "underlying CPI inflation is at a level that is generally consistent with the price
stability target of 2 percent". Regarding the risk balance, the expression that the risks to economic activity
are generally balanced was maintained. The BOJ continued to judge that price risks are skewed to the
upside, keeping its assessment unchanged that "there is a risk of underlying CPI inflation deviating
upward to a level above the price stability target of 2 percent." However, we highlight that the outlook
report added a note implying caution on the risk of yen depreciation due to rate hikes by many foreign
central banks. There was no major change in the future monetary policy stance from September,
maintaining the stance of raising the policy rate toward the neutral rate. Full piece here.

2) Q&A on a Potential GPIF Allocation Shift
Karen Fishman, Senior FX Strategist, addresses the common questions around a potential allocation shift
by Japan’s Government Pension Investment Fund (GPIF), including the possible changes, the signs to
watch, the feasible timeline, and the likely implications for the Yen. Summary of the key questions &
answers below. Full piece here.
What is the Most Likely Timeline if GPIF Were to Change its Asset Allocation?
The earliest possible timeline for implementation seems to be Spring 2027. In the current medium-term
plan announced in March 2025, GPIF introduced an “annual verification” to affirm or change the current
asset allocation by the end of every fiscal year.
Does GPIF Need to Make a Change to its Allocation Targets for a Shift to Occur?
No. Current allocations stand at the midpoint of the allowable bands, so GPIF could simply start by
shifting its holdings towards the outer bounds. It could also just allow more passive adjustment in an
environment of rising global yields.
If GPIF Were to Maintain its Current Strategy but Raise its Domestic Asset Allocations to the Top of the
Allowable Range, How Much Would that be Worth in $bn?
Based on holdings at the end of June 2026, roughly $90bn of purchases of domestic bonds and roughly
$125bn of purchases of domestic equities would bring the allocation shares to the top of the allowable
bands on the domestic side, all else equal. Such flows in each asset would be roughly comparable in size

to Japan's JPY intervention in July-August, though spread out over more than a few days. In any case, we
continue to believe that the signal in any shift by GPIF should matter more than the actual flow, especially
as it should lead other investors to follow.
What are the Implications of a Potential Shift for JPY?
We have argued since September that the higher probability of a shift in flows relative to earlier this year,
combined with a faster pace of tightening by the BoJ (which has relieved some of the downward pressure
on JPY), means that valuation can play a bigger role in JPY performance as the policies that have justified
the Yen’s undervaluation for years appear to be shifting in a more constructive direction. That leaves short
USDJPY a much more attractive portfolio hedge now than it has been in recent years, especially with the
likelihood of greater AI-related volatility that could push both equities and yields lower together—a
market regime that typically benefits the Yen most.

3) TMT Earnings Review
Latest views from Rich Privorotsky, Head of EMEA Flow Intermediation and Head of European One
Delta Trading, following another challenging session overnight. Full piece here.
Amazon: AWS grew 39.3% to $46bn, ahead of consensus, although perhaps a little light of whispers given
the bar set by others this week. Capex remains enormous at $220bn this year and “meaningfully more” in
2027. Jassy guiding to AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028. He has “even more conviction…
that the investment we’re making in AWS will yield a much larger revenue and free cash flow generating
business.” On inference: “Enterprises are still very early in using inference at scale, and yet inference
already accounts for well over half of AWS’s AI revenue and growing.” The problem is everything else.
Advertising grew just 12% to $19.6bn versus 18% expected, the first miss in seven quarters. Consumer
growth was softer, Q4 sales guidance a touch light and trailing FCF negative $42bn.
Intel: Down 20%+ overnight, and having an even bigger impact on the broader semiconductor tape. Q4
revenue guided to $15-16bn versus ~$17bn expected (operational issue at Israeli fab). The divergence
within the business is extraordinary. Data Centre and AI revenue grew 78%, with server CPU demand so
strong Intel simply cannot meet it. Meanwhile, PC demand is contracting as memory inflation drives
hardware costs higher. The challenge is an increasingly pronounced mismatch between capacity and
demand. Manufacturing and packaging constraints have limited Intel’s ability to capitalize on the server
CPU boom, while the consumer business faces a very different environment. Clearly some questions
around capacity allocation and how quickly supply can respond. While the read-across for PC exposed
suppliers is negative, some might view Intel's packaging and fab bottlenecks as company specific rather
than a secular breakdown in enterprise compute demand.
Themes: Core businesses financing this extraordinary investment cycle are coming under greater scrutiny.
Is advertising slowing, is the consumer slowing, or both? Amazon’s advertising miss follows another miss
in search from a peer earlier in the week. Prime Day timing explains some of Amazon’s weakness, with
management guiding advertising back to high teens growth in Q4, so probably premature to extrapolate.
Reddit adds another wrinkle, observing that “AI mode does not drive traffic.” Could AI eventually
cannibalize some of the advertising economics financing the investment cycle itself? Too early to
conclude, particularly as AI may also stimulate commercial search activity, but worth watching. The
bigger issue is increasingly funding and returns.
