How high can yields go before equities
break?
Everyone fears higher Treasury yields, but JPM argues the S&P 500 may be more resilient than
investors think. With earnings growth running at extraordinary levels, equities could potentially
withstand 10-year yields approaching 6%. Corporate balance sheets provide another cushion, with
most debt locked in at fixed rates. But beneath the index, the picture is less comfortable. Smaller
companies, rate-sensitive sectors and debt-funded AI infrastructure face a very different reality. The
next big rates trade may be less about shorting the market and more about identifying where the pain
will show up.
The 6% yield paradox
JPM's historical work challenges the conventional wisdom that rising yields automatically crush equity
multiples. When earnings growth is strong, stocks can absorb substantially higher rates before
valuations come under pressure. With consensus EPS growth above 20%, JPM sees the S&P 500
potentially tolerating 10-year yields closer to 5.5–6%. The critical variable isn't just rates. It's whether
earnings can keep delivering.

Earnings are doing the heavy lifting
The productivity backdrop matters enormously. JPM's analysis shows that stronger productivity growth
can support higher equity multiples even as bond yields rise. With AI potentially lifting corporate
efficiency, the market may be able to withstand a higher-rate regime than historical averages suggest.
The catch? Those productivity gains need to show up in actual earnings, not just expectations.

The corporate rate shock that isn't

happening... yet
Here's why higher rates haven't crushed corporate earnings. JPM estimates that roughly 91% of S&P
500 nonfinancial debt is fixed-rate, insulating much of corporate America from immediate refinancing
pressure. Companies locked in cheap funding during the previous cycle, leaving the impact of higher
yields to filter through gradually. The problem is concentrated among businesses that need fresh
capital rather than those already sitting on long-dated debt.
JPM
Not all sectors are created equal
The shape of the yield curve may matter as much as the level of rates. JPM's historical analysis shows
bear steepeners typically favor cyclicals such as Energy and Financials, while bear flatteners have
been more supportive of Technology. Bond proxies and long-duration sectors outside tech remain
particularly vulnerable. The important point: higher yields don't produce one uniform equity outcome.
They create very different winners and losers.
JPM
The AI funding divide
The AI boom has an increasingly important financing divide. JPM estimates that CoreWeave's debt
carries an 8–9% cost, while Oracle's FY27 capex plans of $90–95bn imply around $70bn in net cash
spending after funding offsets. Higher rates increase the cost of building new capacity and put greater
pressure on project returns. AI demand can remain exceptionally strong, but the economics of
financing that growth are becoming increasingly important.
The real trade is beneath the index
The divergence is increasingly visible. SPY remains relatively resilient despite 10-year yields around
5%, while IWM has struggled considerably more. JPM favors large-cap Quality Growth over smaller,
more financing-sensitive businesses, arguing that stronger balance sheets and longer-dated funding
provide protection. A shallow hiking cycle may be manageable for the index, but a broader tightening
cycle could increasingly favor defensive low volatility exposure.
