Takeaways from our F1Q27 Callback with Management
We caught up with Microsoft investor relations following the F1Q27 earnings call. We discussed assumptions around the 2Q27 
and FY27 outlook as well as the key drivers of 1Q performance.
Azure Performance & Outlook
• Ranked drivers of Azure upside:
1.         GPU efficiencies. Copilot token throughput improved 50% QoQ. That freed capacity, which went to 
Foundry customers.
2.       Revenue per token. Premium model launches, including OpenAI, carried higher prices. This could be cyclical 
and does not expect it in Q2.
3.       CPU efficiencies. Underutilized CPUs were identified and monetized for other workloads.
• Foundry was the #1 driver for two reasons: freed GPUs went to customers building AI apps, and customers accessed 
model APIs through it.
• Demand remains broad-based across enterprises and SMBs, with most large customers increasing both GPU and 
CPU consumption.
• The 2Q27 Azure guide of 49-50% assumes continued efficiency improvements and does not assume a repeat of the 
favorable revenue-per-token dynamics seen in 1Q.
• 2H Azure framework, include key swing factors including supply availability, first-party demand prioritization, 
efficiency gains, capacity deployment timing, and tougher compares.
Foundry & AI Platform Momentum
• Foundry revenue more than tripled y/y and continues to emerge as a meaningful AI platform within Microsoft's 
broader AI stack.
• Strong adoption across multimodal models, developer tooling, security offerings, and AI application development.
• Capacity unlocked through efficiency gains is increasingly being directed toward Foundry customers.
M365 Commercial Cloud
• ARPU is the lead driver. Copilot is now the largest contributor, followed by E5, then E7 (now at millions of seats).
• Consumption is mostly GitHub Copilot. Turning on usage-based billing monetized agentic workflows that already 
existed.
• New M365 Copilot agentic features (CoWork, Autopilots) launch at GA with consumption pricing and will ramp with 
adoption.
• Seat growth is moderating. Most of it comes from SMB and frontline workers, though the enterprise base is still 
growing.
• Execution issue in the core E3/standalone motion. IR compared it to the Azure AI vs. non-AI balancing about two 
years ago.
o   Fixes are in place but will take time in a large sales org. The guide assumes only “some improvement.”

Microsoft Corp. 
Company description 
Microsoft is the world's largest software company, with more than $280 billion in annual revenue spanning enterprise and 
consumer markets. The company is best known for its Windows operating system, Microsoft 365 productivity suite, and broad 
portfolio of business applications. Microsoft also operates a leading gaming ecosystem through Xbox. Growth is increasingly 
driven by Azure, one of the world's largest cloud platforms, alongside a rapidly expanding portfolio of AI offerings, including 
Microsoft 365 Copilot, GitHub, AI and Security Suite. Key growth drivers include cloud migration, AI infrastructure demand, 
and the adoption of agentic AI solutions across Microsoft's vast installed base of enterprise and consumer users. 
Investment strategy 
We maintain our Buy rating on Microsoft, supported by our view that cloud and AI-related revenue streams can sustain 
double-digit growth while supporting operating leverage. In Azure, we see AI as an incremental demand driver, with the 
emergence of AI agents likely to increase compute consumption and expand enterprise adoption of Microsoft's cloud platform. 
We expect Azure AI Foundry, Copilot Studio, and related AI services to strengthen Microsoft's competitive position and 
contribute to higher cloud workloads over time. In Productivity and Business Processes, Microsoft 365 Copilot and other AI-
enabled features should support greater customer engagement and improve the value proposition of the Office ecosystem. We 
also expect continued Microsoft 365 ARPU expansion through further migration to higher-value E5 and E7 licenses, increased 
Copilot penetration, and the benefit of recent Microsoft 365 pricing changes. Overall, we believe Microsoft's ability to monetize 
AI across both infrastructure and applications supports a favorable growth outlook. 
Valuation 
Our $680 one-year target price for Microsoft is based on ~23x PE multiple to our FY29 GAAP EPS of $32.10, which we discount 
back to a year forward using ~12% cost of equity. We see the EPS multiple as a better valuation metric due to the more stable 
earnings growth for a mature company like MSFT and the ramp in near-term CapEx that result in a more volatile FCF. 
Risks 
We see potential downside risks to achieving our target price to be:
• The aggressive ramp in CAPEX investment in midst of the ongoing build out of AI capabilities could hurt longer term trajectory 
if AI adoptions are slower than expected.
• Competition in the hyperscale cloud market, requiring MSFT to aggressively invest here vs. AMZN’s AWS and GOOGL’s GCP 
and potentially fast deceleration in key Azure trajectory. 
• A larger acquisition that drives earnings dilution in the nearer term.
