## Marketcolour + HF Update, Midterms / Europe Conviction List /
Alstom / Ryanair / LVMH / Nicotine ##
Ioannis Blekos
2 Nov 2026
Good Morning,
US equities finished the week lower: SPX -182 bps to 7580 (6/11 sectors +ve, worst session in 4m, Energy
(+1.91%), Consumer Staples (+1.38%), Tech -4.24%), NDX -346 bps to 28384 (~7% move in October), and RTY
-166 bps to 2811. Nasdaq posted its worst session since June and second-worst YTD, with the Tech sector
-4.24% as a whole. The AI trade took the biggest hit: the GS AI Semis basket (GSCBSMHX) finished the week down
-8.25%, and the GS Data Center basket (GSTMTDAT) finished -7.35%. In Europe, EU High Beta Momentum
(GSPEMOMO -2.8%) well in the red alongside EU Beta (GSP3BETA -2.5%). Our EU Stagflation pair (GSPESTAG
+2%) was amongst the best performing basket.
 
Interesting: NDX vs. Equal Weight S&P suffered its worst month since '02, the SOX broke below its 200dma for the
first time since June '25, and Software vs. Semis logged its second-best month in 10 years. Meanwhile, AI vs. 'AI at
Risk' saw its second-worst month since the launch of ChatGPT, while S&P ex-AI posted its strongest month (+7.64%)
over the same period.
 
HF Performance: Fundamental LS managers up 0.3% (alpha: +0.1%). Gains from Monday through Thursday were
partly erased by Friday's losses. Positive alpha came from stock selection and Health Care, partly offset by Brazil
exposure. Communication Services and Info Tech were the most net bought sectors, with Info Tech seeing net buying
for the first time in 5 weeks. North America was the only net bought region October: Fundamental LS managers down
3.5% (alpha: -1.8%). YTD: Fundamental LS managers up 9.1% (alpha: +6.6%).
 
Flow: Asset Mgrs finished net sellers (reversing early week tech demand + some) while HF flows finished balanced.
No surprise, most supply was seen at the epicenter of tech, though the velocity was very orderly vs what we saw the
last ~2 weeks. The magnitude of the moves felt systematic / top-down driven with ETFs ending the week at ~31% of
tape (highest full session level since June). Month-end dynamics also at work.
 
Baskets: In a Democratic sweep scenario, our favorite long is Affordable Care Act Beneficiaries (GSHLCACA).
Why? Democrats have heavily pushed to revive the enhanced ACA premium tax credits that expired at end-2025.
Reauthorizing these subsidies should improve the earnings outlook for hospitals, outpatient providers and healthcare
insurance companies that currently face rising bad debt and uncompensated care costs.
 
US Midterms: prediction markets imply >90% that Democrats win the house and >65% they win the senate; fiscal
deadlines would be the main focus for legislative action under a Democratic majority (e.g. spending authority will need
to be extended and the debt limit will need to be raised); we do not expect major fiscal policy changes over the
next 2yrs but spending could increase. Link
 
Europe Conviction List: We add Deutsche Bank and Carlsberg to the Conviction List while removing HSBC, Haleon,
and CTP. Link
 
US Strategy: Q3 2026 earnings results have underscored the fundamental challenge facing equity investors: Earnings
growth is strong, but it is no longer accelerating. Alongside strong earnings results, analysts have continued to lift

forward earnings estimates this season, but the rate of upward revisions has slowed. Similarly, Q3 results support our
view that AI investment spending will continue to grow but that the growth rate of AI capex is peaking. Link
 
ALSTOM / 2026 guidance downgrades: L-MSD growth (vs 5% previously). Adj EBIT margin 1% (vs 6.5%
previously). FCF -1.5bn (vs positive previously). The warning is “primarily” due to milestones on Coradia Max slipping
into later years, but also due to “prolonged uncertainties in the Middle East”. The company say the Coradia Max issues
were first identified in Q1.
 
Ryanair – H1’17 – initial take looks ok, Q2 PAT in line, Q3 fares trending modestly higher yoy, importantly new
€800m SBB announced from Feb shows confidence that they will weather the storm and come out stronger – altho
still only 25% hedged on FY28 fuel (up from 15%) is clearly a headwind in to next yr.
 
EUROPE NICOTINE - BAT up to Buy, Imperial remains a Buy. We upgrade BAT to Buy (22% upside) and retain a
Buy on Imperial (22% upside). Both offer double-digit total returns, supported by robust earnings growth, rising capital
returns and c.6% dividend yields. Into FY27, we believe nicotine's defensive qualities and best-in-class margins should
prove attractive amid consumer and input cost uncertainty Link
 
LVMH (BUY / PT EUR495 / 25% Upside): We argue the transition from a commercial, fashion-driven brand to a more
institutional, handbag and accessories-focused strategy centered on the "art of travel" has already weighed quite a bit
on sales over the last twelve months and is likely to be significantly less of a headwind in 2027. + Second, on Dior, we
see evidence of a gradual improvement in sales. Link
 
UNILEVER - 10 investor debates into Unilever CMD. Link
 
Positioning in Mag7 has been extremely resilient despite a volatile TMT tape. It quickly went from under owned in
June to near the YTD highs in exposure levels now. This group should continue to act as a source of funds limiting any
near-term dip buying demand.

 
 
HF Pain: Our HF VIP vs VIP Short just had it’s 3rd worst month in the past 15 years. Pnl protection > pnl generation
right now will limit offense being played in the near term (buyers live higher).
