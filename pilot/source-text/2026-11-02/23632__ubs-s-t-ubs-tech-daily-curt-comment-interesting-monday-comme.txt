Tim on Intel , PC Hollows Out 1H:27 Numbers, But Longer-Term Story The Same

2
INTC guided well below even the most bearish investor expectations owing to a much worse than expected
PC segment - Revenue was $8.1B (-9% Q/Q and -5% Y/Y), below expectations as higher memory prices,
limited availability, and constrained IT budgets weakened PC sell-through and drove a sharper late-quarter
OEM inventory correction
The path to recovery in PC looks challenging given our view that there is no relief in sight for memory
pricing/supply - especially given how much server demand is growing for agentic applications.
Having said that, INTC's actions around capex (we think up at least 50% in C2027, if not more) and adding
more substrate capacity for the server business should not really dampen enthusiasm for the growth parts of
the story - e.g., foundry (AAPL CPU first big engagement, we think), advanced packaging, server, and ASIC.
When we net it all out, our C2027 estimates of course come down, but we still think ~$3 EPS is doable for
C2028 and >$4 for C2029 and the Terafab process technology sharing/relationship (for which we think INTC
has to ultimately get a license) adds some optionality - albeit longer-term. Also, over the longer-term,
sustained weakness in the PC business may allow INTC to accelerate its server supply and push its
roadmap forward more aggressively, but time will tell on this. In the meantime, mid to high 20x P/E does not
leave much room for multiple expansion so the market probably needs to roll its basis forward to C2029 for the
stock to really start to work again. Net, our PT comes down to $80, and we remain Neutral

Francois on STM

Q4 gross-margin guidance surprised positively at 39%, despite 40bps of unused-capacity charges and 30bps
of manufacturing-efficiency headwinds. Management expects Q1 margins at least in line with 38% consensus
(UBSe 38.6%). The manufacturing programme should add c.400bps by end-2027/early-2028, while utilisation
and mix provide additional upside. We continue to expect >45% gross margin by 2028E versus 43%
consensus.
We stress-test an extreme scenario where AI revs halve by 28E on significant hyperscaler cuts/share loss,
reducing AI EPS to c.$0.5/share versus $1.0 in our estimates. Even then, STM would generate c.$3.5 of EPS,
including $3.0 from non-AI, still c.30% below the 2023 peak. Applying 10x to ex-AI earnings, versus 14.9x
historically 2Y fwd, embeds a further c.20-30% downside to non-AI EPS and implies c.$30-35/share, or
c.15-20% downside. We believe materially greater downside would require increasingly aggressive
assumptions
David on Apple
UBS Evidence Lab data (> Access Dataset) that tracks iPhone availability across 30+ geographies indicates
that wait times for the iPhone Duo are trending lower (-7 days WoW) but are still materially elevated at ~89
days on average. The Duo's WoW delta is in-line with our expectations as Apple looks to satisfy global
demand from early adopters and premium feature-focused consumers. While reports (link) of display issues
are a concern in our view, we do not believe that they are the primary driver of the Duo's declining wait times.
In addition, wait time trends for the Pro (-3 days WoW) and Pro Max (unchanged WoW) are similar to last
year's models at this point in time. If the data continues to track similarly, we expect wait times for the Pro and
Pro Max to trend lower next week
