The AI dollar is here 
 
Over the last two weeks we have seen episodes of sharp sell-offs in tech stocks and the dollar at the same 
time. We have been arguing for a while that the risk-off properties of the dollar cannot be taken for 
granted in the event of an AI-driven correction in the market. Below we offer a few more observations on this 
dynamic. 
  
First, the American reliance on foreign equity inflows is historically unprecedented. These are currently 
financing the entire US current account deficit, a rare occurrence coinciding with very high concentration in 
the equity market (figure 1). There has of course been an unprecedented US equity inflow but what 
tends to be under-appreciated is the impact of valuation effects. We estimate that roughly 90% of the $20+ 
trillion value of US equities owned by foreigners is attributable to price appreciation rather than underlying 
buying. Put simply, for every $1 of US equities that has been bought there are roughly $10 equities now 
owned (figure 2). 
  
Second, beware equity hedging dynamics. For the past ten years, the correlation between US stocks and the 
dollar has been negative, creating huge incentives for the underlying US equity exposure to be held 
unhedged. There is however strong empirical evidence that when the source of an equity shock is US-
centric the dollar-equity correlation flips to positive. In this world, the USD becomes additive to equity losses 
and incentivizes hedging shifts to the stock of US assets akin to the price action we saw around Liberation 
Day. 
  
Finally, validation of the USD down, equity down dynamic should be sought after in US real yields going down 
as well. We have been arguing throughout the year that the single most important driver of the 
combined rates higher, equities higher move has been a huge ramp up in capex expectations. A move lower 
in both US real yields and equities would be consistent with these expectations being downgraded. 
  
We are not expressing an opinion here on the outlook of the AI ecosystem. What we do however hope to 
emphasize is that the risks to the dollar are a lot more two-sided than the market has been pricing over the 
last few weeks.
