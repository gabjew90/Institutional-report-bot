The positioning trap
The great market purge is underway. Hedge funds are puking AI longs, systematic strategies are
getting slaughtered and Goldman's risk indicator has collapsed. But don't mistake pain for capitulation.
The machines may have more selling to do. Meanwhile, volatility is finally waking up, the crowd is
chasing expensive protection and the market's downside tail is getting uglier. Is this the mother of all
positioning resets or the beginning of something much nastier?
The positioning reset
According to Goldman's Privorotsky, hedge funds are aggressively unwinding AI longs, while
systematic strategies are taking a beating. Goldman's Equity Systematic L/S estimate fell 2.17% last
week, its worst weekly drawdown since December 2023. Fundamental L/S net exposure is back at the
7th percentile, largely thanks to index short covering. Meanwhile, single stocks have been sold for
seven consecutive weeks, and retail sentiment has turned deeply bearish. The positioning reset is real.
Whether it is finished is another question.
The risk indicator
Goldman’s risk indicator suffered one of its largest monthly declines in October, highlighting the extent
of the risk-off shift. Still, we are not quite at contrarian buy levels, which kick in below -1.

Anything but straightforward
History offers little comfort. Following the 11 largest declines in Goldman’s RAI since 2005, the S&P 500
has delivered returns ranging from -17% to +7% over one month and -32% to +18% over six months.
In other words, the positioning reset is no guarantee of a bottom. Plenty of tail risk in both directions.

Sell low, buy high
The crowd keeps getting volatility wrong. They entered the latest panic heavily short vol, only to get
squeezed and forced to chase protection at much higher prices. Sell low, buy high. Rinse and repeat.

Index vol finally gets the memo
Single-stock volatility and credit markets have been pricing serious stress for weeks. Index volatility?
Not so much. According to Garrett, that disconnect is finally starting to close as macro equity volatility
catches up with the panic underneath. The question is whether this is just the beginning.

The great volatility disconnect
According to Goldman's Brian Garrett, the gap between single-stock chaos and relatively subdued
index volatility remains extraordinary. Looking ahead, SPX 12-month implied correlation is trading
near 20%, among the lowest readings in history. The options market is pricing another year of extreme
dispersion in 2027.
The danger? A sudden correlation spike could turn today's stock-level chaos into tomorrow's index-
level volatility shock.

The right tail is disappearing
One of the defining features of 2026 was extreme upside volatility in some of the largest stocks. That is

changing. According to Garrett, downside volatility is now taking over, with short-dated skew already
repricing the new reality. The upside panic is turning into a downside panic.

Back from the dead
The left tail is back with a vengeance. After months of upside volatility dominating the tape, downside
risk is back in charge. Investors spent much of 2026 paying up for upside participation. Now the
insurance they neglected is becoming increasingly expensive.

The midterm tailwind
According to JPMorgan's Market Intelligence team, the S&P 500 has posted positive returns 6, 9 and 12
months after every midterm election since 1990, averaging gains of 11.4%, 12.5% and 14.7%,
respectively. Meanwhile, JPMorgan warns the recent de-risking is not yet capitulatory, with quant selling
and elevated tech positioning still posing risks. But with momentum exposure down to just 30% of its
peak and widespread hedge fund deleveraging, the ingredients for a powerful alpha rebound are
falling into place.
