 
Squeeze Or Trapdoor? Why Goldman Wants Clients 
To Rent The AI Rebound Instead Of Owning It 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, OCT 28, 2026 - 11:55 PM 
Here is a number that tells you everything about this market: Goldman's momentum factor 
basket has moved 5% or more in a single session 30 times in 2026. That is more than in 2000 
(23 times), the financial crisis (16) and Covid (21)... with two months still to go! 
Which perhaps explains why, a few hours before Microsoft and Alphabet report and Warsh's 
Fed delivers its rate decision, Goldman's derivatives desk has a very specific recommendation 
for the AI rebound: rent it. From this morning's GS Vol Views (available to pro subs): 
AI concerns, Middle East tensions and midterm elections could sustain near-term volatility -- yet 
earnings support & lighter positioning (nets 5yr lows per GS PB) could help equities into 
year-end. More constructive flows have materialized of late -- note call volumes ~57% of the 
tape this week, above ytd avg. We favor renting upside via limited loss structures and pairing 
that with SPX lookback puts as our preferred hedge. 
In other words, the desk wants exposure to a squeeze without having to believe in it. And 
looking at the vol surface, it's easy to see why: the options market is pricing megacap tech as if 
the scary part is over, while pricing neoclouds as if it has barely started. 
The Cheapest Seat In The House Is... Mag7 
Start with the surprise. After two weeks in which hedge funds and long-onlies dumped tech 
(Goldman derivatives guru Brian Garrett's cash desk counted $20 billion of TMT selling, 
approaching the $23 billion July AI unwind, in his Sunday Weekend Prep (available to pro subs), 
you might expect megacap vol to be bid to the moon. It isn't. According to Goldman, the Mag 7 
are being offered at cheap implied vol relative to the broader tech complex, with the ratio sitting 
in just the 15th percentile of the past four years. 
 

 
 
The desk's trade expressions are telling. For Amazon, which reports Thursday, Goldman likes 
the Dec 280/310 call spread for $4.50 (stock ref $253.60), noting the stock is "~10% off YTD 
highs and holding support levels" and that "recent tech de-leveraging mirrors July's setup, when 
the stock jumped +15% on earnings." 
Then there is Microsoft, which reports tonight: up 50% off its summer lows, an 8 out of 10 GS 
positioning score, and an implied move of just 4.7%... versus a 15.5% move last quarter. Either 
the options market knows something, or it has already forgotten July. 
For the index, the desk likes the SPY Nov 6 780 call for $3.45 (ref $771.40), as it "expects vol to 
float on a break towards ATHs." Translation: if stocks rip, vol rises with them, which is not what 
happens in a healthy market. 
The Fastest Vol Crush In Four Years 
The cheapness is recent. Last week, as Meta's capex guide and record CDS blowout dragged 
the AI complex lower, the spread between Nasdaq and S&P implied vol exploded to roughly 
12.5 vol points, the widest in Goldman's four-year chart (roughly, read off the chart). Garrett 
flagged on Sunday that the NDX 1-week straddle went out at 3% (~1-year highs) vs 1.8% for 
SPX. Since then, Nasdaq vol has been dumped: 
After the NDX vs SPX implied vol spread blew out last week, we have since seen NDX vols 
underperform meaningfully. Though the 1m ATM spread still ranks in the 94th %-ile (vs last 4y), 
the 3d compression episode was the largest seen over that time frame. 
 

 
 
Put differently, the spread is now back at 7.5 vol points, still well above its 5.2 four-year average 
but a long way off the 12.6 high. That's a lot of vol sold into the single most important earnings 
week of the quarter, with the most crowded Nasdaq futures long on record sitting on top of it, 
which is something we discussed earlier in "Record Nasdaq Longs Walk Into Megacap Earnings 
Week." 
The Index Is Pricing Correlation, The Stocks Are Not 
There is a reason the index got expensive while the generals got cheap: correlation. As every AI 
name traded as one block on the way down, index vol was bid harder than single-stock vol. 
Goldman's structuring team (Adrien Simonet's note "Monetizing Elevated NDX Implied 
Correlation", also available to pro subs) shows NDX 12-month implied correlation climbing from 
the mid-20s over the summer to 32.6%, while the average stock-to-index vol ratio slid from 
above 2.0 to 1.8. 
 

 
 
The desk's answer is a dispersion trade: short index vol, long single-stock vol, in a "cross 
corridor" variance swap through December 2027 on 19 NDX names from Apple and Nvidia to 
Strategy and Sandisk. The basket's weighted stock strike is 46.7 vol against an index strike of 
23.6. As Simonet puts it, the structure works by "harvesting the index volatility risk premium to 
fund long single-stock volatility," and the long stock leg "has historically realized above index 
volatility in previous drawdowns." 
Regular readers know we have been fixated on this split since July, when record-low correlation 
was masking the AI leverage boom, followed by Goldman's own "dispersion bomb" warning in 
August and the subsequent dispersion collapse. Three months later, the same trade is back on 
the menu at a better entry. Garrett's own read of the same data on Sunday: "i'm not sure we can 
handle much more flipping of the thematic script(s)." 
Neoclouds: Down 28%, And The Converts Are Even Cheaper 
Than The Stock 
If megacap vol is on sale, neocloud vol is on fire. Goldman's neocloud basket (GSXUNEOC) 
"remains down over 28% MTD" even after Tuesday's 7% bounce. For investors who want to 
play "further reversion," the desk's preferred vehicle is CoreWeave's (CRWV) convertible bonds, 
which have dropped 1-2 points delta-neutral this month while the stock fell 30%: 
The converts offer ~300bps pickup to straights and currently imply 46-57 vol vs listed options 
80+ and allow for hedging via CDS and straights bonds. 
 

 
 
JPMorgan is looking at the same paper from the credit side. In Tuesday's international Market 
Intel briefing (available to pro subs), relaying color from the bank's FICC and credit research 
teams, JPM notes that AI-related CDS has widened on "technical selling pressure and hedging 
demand" as lenders buy protection against their own data center loans, pushing CDS above 
cash-bond spreads. CRWV is "interesting b/c also has converts outstanding, with CDS trading 
at a slightly positive basis," while JPM credit analyst Tom Egan argues CoreWeave's is "a 
financing challenge, not a solvency one," projecting LTM EBITDA rising from ~$4.4 billion in 
June to nearly 
130 billion of contracted revenue.

Maybe. But the same JPM note concedes that for neoclouds "bankruptcy risk exists," with 
hyperscalers likely to buy the wreckage "for pennies on the dollar." Which is a remarkably 
candid way of describing what a convert holder's recovery might look like. Since the neocloud 
nightmare spread to Nscale ten days ago, the list of candidates has only grown. 
Korea: The Chase Nobody Is Positioned For 
The desk's highest-beta idea is in Seoul, where "a reinvigorated AI trade could spark a chase in 
Korea," because Goldman's Prime book "has seen heavy net selling MTD with net & gross 
allocations falling sharply." The trade: EWY Dec 180/210 call spread for $3.70 (ref $157.65). 
 

 
 
Naturally, we were right on top of this one. We warned in early October that the only buyer had 
left the building as Kospi lost its $40 billion buyback crutch, followed by "The AI Canary" on Oct 
20. Goldman's strategists caught up three weeks later: 
So now that the target is cut and the hedge funds are out, the derivs desk thinks it's time for the 
chase. Nothing says "contrarian" like a sell-side target cut followed by a call spread. 
Exotic Upside, Discount Prices (Terms And Conditions Apply) 
For clients who want AI upside but only a little of it, Goldman's "light exotics" menu offers 
3-month 105%/130% continuous knock-out calls on AI hardware names, a structure built for 
investors "expecting a cyclical 10% to 25% bounce rather than an unconstrained run." You get 
1-for-1 upside above 105%, unless the stock touches 130% intraday at any point, in which case 
you get nothing. In exchange, the price is a fraction of a vanilla call: 
 

 
 
Roughly 65% to 81% off a vanilla call, with Micron at 2.03% of notional and KLA at 1.84%. 
That's a bargain, until you remember that in a year when the momentum basket has moved 5% 
in a single day thirty times, a 30% run in a chip stock over three months is hardly a tail scenario. 
And that is precisely the scenario where this structure pays zero. 
Then there is the hedge Goldman actually prefers. An SPX March 2027 90% lookback put, with 
a daily lookback (i.e., the strike ratchets up with the market) until December 31, costs 1.82%, 
which the desk says lets clients "eliminate the need to perfectly time the market & call the top for 
just a ~10bp premium" over the average vanilla put of the past five years. It has "resonated with 
clients who want downside protection without ruling out the chance for a Santa rally." 
Translation: the desk's clients want to be long into year-end, and they want to be told exactly 
where the top was after the fact. 
Second Inning Or Eighth? 
The bulls have a case. Nets are at five-year lows, calls dominate the tape, JPM's International 
desk notes the SOX vs SPX drawdown now "rivals 2008" with "bottoming signals" coming from 
its positioning team, and Goldman's vol desk can find cheap upside almost everywhere it looks. 
 

 
 
(chart source JPMorgan, Bloomberg; terminal colors inverted) 
Even Garrett leans that way, framing the debate as either "the 2nd inning of a repricing of the 
entire AI ecosystem, in which case the index has some material catch down" or "the 8th inning 
of a significant de-gross episode (leaning in this camp right now)." But read his conditions for 
the 8th inning: bond vol must subside, the midterms must be out of the way, and AI capex must 
show payback in earnings (oil below $90 "would also help"). That's three things that have to go 
right, against a variance market that, per Garrett, is pricing the midterms at "(checks notes) 
exactly zero." 
And that's the tell. When even a constructive desk prefers to rent upside through capped, 
knock-out and spread structures, and pairs it with a put that remembers the high, it is pricing a 
bounce, not a new bull market. We think the lookback is the trade, the knock-outs are the 
marketing, and the record Nasdaq long is the risk. The upside requires Microsoft and Alphabet 
to deliver perfection tonight; the downside requires one more Meta... or one more neocloud. As 
we said on Monday, the trapdoor under this market is systematic, and the funds that would 
normally buy the dip have already been stopped out of it. 
Then again, thirty 5% momentum shocks in ten months is its own kind of trend. We'll know 
which inning it is by Friday's close.
