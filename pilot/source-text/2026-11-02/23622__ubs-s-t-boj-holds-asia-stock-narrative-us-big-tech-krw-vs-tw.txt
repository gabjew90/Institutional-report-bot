BoJ Holds; Statement Modestly Hawkish
BoJ held rates at 1.25%, as widely expected, with an 8-1 vote split. The statement reads modestly hawkish
and supports the base case for a December hike. The main additions are firmer inflation projections beyond
energy, explicit exclusion of the proposed food-tax cut, and a documented acceleration in ETF disposal
(JPY330-860 bn annually, starting January 2027). The inflation outlook is modestly firmer than UBS Research
expectations: FY2027 core CPI at 2.6% vs. 2.5%. The BoJ also identified wage-price interaction and
inflation-expectation risks. Its growth forecast for FY2027 at 0.7% is a touch soft but doesn't invalidate the
normalization path. Altogether, the tone so far is not enough to drive JPY, which continues to trade largely with
risk-on/off swings. Front-end BoJ pricing is a touch lower, but still has three more hikes priced in over the next
12 months.
(Jason Cheng, Macro Sales)

Amazon AWS Miss Meets Bold 2027-28 Growth Signal - Amazon’s Q3 print is weaker than hoped, led by
39% AWS growth and softer AWS margins, but the more striking signal is management effectively pointing to
more than 40% AWS growth in both 2027 and 2028. That unusually specific longer-term commentary
complicates an otherwise disappointing release and puts the upcoming call squarely on the credibility and
drivers of that acceleration. AWS grew 39% y/y versus buyside bogeys around 44-45%, while margins of
38.7% also fell short of roughly 40% expectations. The surprise is Amazon’s willingness to be unusually
specific further out. Management called out high-teens y/y advertising growth in Q4 2026 and effectively
indicated AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028. The latter is notable, although buyside expectations
for those years were already above 40%, limiting the scope for a meaningful reset higher. The call therefore
needs to bridge the gap between the Q3 AWS miss and that longer-term confidence. (Christina Dwyer,
Specialist Sales)

Intel Sinks On Weak Guide - Intel [INTC] is down 16% versus roughly 8.7% implied volatility after a
third-quarter beat was overshadowed by a materially weaker fourth-quarter outlook, with supply constraints
limiting the company’s ability to meet robust server demand as personal computer demand softens. The speed
and magnitude of the supply impact are the key negatives and risk reviving execution concerns just as
sentiment around the story had begun to improve. Third-quarter revenue and earnings per share came in at
$16.5 bn and $0.52 versus consensus of $16.58 bn and $0.41. Intel Foundry was the standout at $6.7 bn
versus $6.05 bn expected, while Client Computing came in below consensus at $7.3 bn versus $8.95 bn.
Gross margin was 44% versus 42% expected, while operating margin was 19.6% versus 16.1%. The focus,
however, is firmly on fourth-quarter guidance of $15-16 bn in revenue and $0.25 in earnings per share versus
consensus of $17.14 bn and $0.44. Management said its immediate priority is unlocking additional supply to
meet strong Xeon server processor demand, while also pointing to a personal computer slowdown continuing
at least through the first half of next year. (Robert Ruple, Specialist Sales)
Alphabet Feedback Feels Most Negative Since Early 2025 - Alphabet feedback is bad. Honestly, it hasn’t
felt this negative since early 2025. Search was obviously the most debated metric this quarter. Market
participants spent a lot of time nitpicking whether a slight miss versus the Street was priced in, but the reality is
no one was this low. The company flagged a host of one-time headwinds on the call, but even with “the
highest search query growth in five years,” that wasn’t enough to offset faster growth in “lower monetization” AI
Mode queries. And unlike the 1xers, that looks like a much more structural headwind. The miss inevitably
raises the bigger question: what does the growth trajectory look like once query mix normalizes? That concern
only gets louder as Meta, Muse, and other agents gain traction. At some point, Alpahbet likely has to confront
the classic innovator’s dilemma and launch an agent of its own, which, per Wednesday’s headlines, appears to
be underway. The problem is obvious: at least initially, that product may compete directly with Google’s own
economics. Not impossible to navigate, but hardly an easy needle to thread. (Christina Dwyer, Specialist
Sales)

Lagarde To Leave ECB Early, Saying She Can’t Remain Neutral - ECB President Christine Lagarde will
depart on Feb. 7, 2027, ahead of her term’s October expiry, seeking freedom to speak beyond monetary
policy: “I don’t think that I can remain neutral.” Her departure gives EU leaders time to choose a successor
before France’s presidential election. Lagarde did not answer whether the timing was deliberate or identify the
issues she wished to address. French President Emmanuel Macron called for an orderly succession, while
representatives of Marine Le Pen’s National Rally criticised the move. Financial markets showed little reaction
to the news. Former Dutch and Spanish central bank governors Klaas Knot and Pablo Hernandez de Cos are
widely seen as frontrunners to replace Lagarde, according to media reports. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
ECB Maintains Measured Inflation Response, Moulin Says - The ECB continues to take a measured
approach to inflation as most prices remain stable and pressures are largely imported, Governing Council
member Emmanuel Moulin said. He said the ECB will monitor the inflation outlook "very closely" and act at
future meetings based on incoming data. Moulin also reiterated his opposition to canceling or freezing
sovereign debt held by the Bank of France, arguing that such a move would create a hole in its balance sheet
and could cause difficulties across the euro area. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
Tokyo Inflation Eases But Stays Above BoJ Target - Tokyo’s October core CPI rose 2.6% y/y, matching
forecasts but easing from September’s 2.7%. The measure excludes fresh food but includes fuel; excluding
both, inflation slowed to 2.6% from 2.9%. Both remained above the BoJ’s 2% target. These leading indicators
of national inflation keep further hikes in view. Producer price increases remain a potential source of consumer
price pressure, rather than a demonstrated explanation for October data; neither measure showed
accelerating annual inflation. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Aussie Producer Price Growth Eases, But Beats Expectations - Australia’s final demand PPI, excluding
exports, rose 1.1% q/q in Q3, above the 1.0% consensus forecast but below Q2’s 1.3%; annual inflation held
at 3.6%. Construction and services led increases, reflecting higher wages, skilled labour shortages and freight
costs for imported materials while lower petroleum and coal manufacturing prices partly offset them. The
figures suggest persistent producer cost pressures ahead of the RBA’s November meeting, despite slower
quarterly growth. September headline CPI inflation had accelerated to 4.3% y/y from 4.0% in August. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Egypt's Central Bank Raises Rates For First Time Since 2024 - The Central Bank of Egypt has raised its
benchmark rate by 100bp to 20%, its first increase since 2024. The move comes after a difficult month for the
pound, which is down more than 7% as regional conflict and elevated energy prices weigh on the current
account. USDEGP NDFs moved lower after the announcement, with the desk quickly seeing selling around
the 6m tenor. The sharper reaction came at the front end, where the 1m implied yield fell from around 24% to
17%, suggesting the market is taking some comfort that the depreciation path may stall for now. An additional
100bp of carry, however, seems unlikely to materially alter the near-term outlook. The rate hike may therefore
have limited bearing on spot direction, with the pound likely to remain largely at the mercy of external factors.
(Jack Johnson, FX Trading)
Market Movers
US Tech Weakness Drives Rotation, Not Risk-Off - Tech and AI weakness is still producing rotation rather
than risk-off in US equities, with constructive breadth and flows broadening into Value, Small Caps, Consumer
and rate-sensitive cyclicals. The key driver is positioning: long onlys continue to trim crowded winners into
strength, while hedge funds moved from early selling to net buying as covering added fuel to previously
under-owned exposures. AI is being repriced against demanding earnings expectations rather than
abandoned. Some de-risking in hyperscalers ahead of AMZN results puts the focus on the next test for
crowded positioning, while Consumer is seeing better buying across investor types after an extended period of
underweight and short exposure. Lower yields and crude are helping the rotation, but earnings, positioning
and single-stock dispersion remain more important than macro beta. Vol confirms the absence of broader
stress. S&P vol continues to compress, with SPX call-spread demand and VIX put-spread buying pointing
toward upside participation and vol fading rather than a rush for protection. Even GOOGL’s roughly 12%
decline versus about 5% implied has failed to generate aggressive downside demand, with longer-dated put
selling instead dominating flows. Tech protection is also being unwound through MAGS hedges, while MSFT
overwrite supply persists. (Victoria Kalamaras, Equity Derivative Sales)
