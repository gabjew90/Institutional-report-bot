Optical M&A: Bloomberg and Reuters report that Marvell is in advanced talks to acquire Lumentum,
which has a market capitalisation of roughly $83bn, with a deal potentially coming as soon as next week.
The combination would pair Marvell’s DSPs, custom silicon and Celestial AI optical fabric with Lumentum’s
high-power external lasers (UHP/CW sources), used in Nvidia’s co-packaged optics (CPO) designs. Nvidia
has already invested roughly $2bn in each company, highlighting the scarcity of reliable light sources as AI
clusters begin adopting co-packaged and near-packaged optics. Lumentum rose 9–11% after hours, while
Marvell fell more than 8% on financing and integration concerns around a stock-heavy deal. The read-
across is positive for the broader optics/CPO supply chain, particularly Coherent, a direct peer with its
own $2bn Nvidia deal. In Europe, Nokia looks the clearest beneficiary, with positive implications for
Aixtron too.  
TELCO M&A unconfirmed press on Bloomberg that Deutsche Telecom plans to combine with its TMUS have

its works council.

AWAY FROM GS: 
 
TECH 
• Adyen: more spending, stronger growth? The debate is whether investment lifts growth above roughly 20% or
merely sustains it. Management expects operating leverage over time; bears question whether growth now costs
more. The previous hiring push hit margins while growth held rather than accelerated. The positive: A record 2026
customer cohort, specialised US hiring, equity-based pay and acquisitions could extend growth. Management still
backs roughly 20% as a guardrail—not guidance—and expects acquisitions to become significant but has not
quantified the revenue upside. The concern: Dropping the FY28 margin target of at least 55% within a year,
without a new medium-term revenue framework, adds uncertainty after previous resets. Management says 55% is

achievable but would sacrifice longer-term growth, given the opportunities identified through acquisitions and
customer feedback.  
What matters next: Do analysts raise revenue forecasts or cut earnings and wait? With acquisition upside and
margin recovery unquantified, I fear the latter; US feedback is a useful check. Capital priorities remain organic
growth, selective M&A consistent with the single platform, then surplus cash returns—with no timing, amount or
trigger specified. The next catalyst the February’s 2027 outlook for revenue guidance, EBITDA and CapEx
commentary, and acquisition updates—not separate Orb or Talon.One forecasts—to test whether spending
delivers growth, leverage and cash returns. Still think stock down 12% is overdone but as one PM said “You
cannot sleep well at night owning adyen and its going to remain in the penalty box until the communication
becomes more consistent and more reliable”
 
• DSY: The headlines look better than the underlying picture with the Q3 beat driven by licences, while the
upgraded full-year guidance largely reflects the inclusion of ArisGlobal. What did not help the stock was the CFO
getting somewhat irritated with an analyst questioning the quality of the upgrade. On call back saying that Q3
EMEA was helped by deal timing as in some deals from Q4 landing and Q4 in EMEA not likely as strong. Incoming
also questioning the Q4 guide – the message from mgt is that Q4 was derisked (it does imply recurring slows).
One analyst asked directly on whether Q4 was actually a downgrade on prior Q4 expects(Q3 beat) and the guide
now includes ArisGlobal the CFO quite heatedly over it…but this a repeated question today. Post quarter despite
the beat street estimate are unlikely to change much and stock is very expensive given the growth – next up the
CMD on the 17th 
 
• ASM –the bull pt off the call mgt see 1.4nm is bigger than 2nm next year…”CEO it will be actually pretty, pretty
big.” Lots of focus on the 2027 guide now €5.7bn, implying c.35% yoy growth. Management has much better
visibility than in prior years, hence the early guidance, and says demand is real. On whether the guide is
conservative, management said it essentially mirrors the expected WFE uplift rather than embedding a cushion.
1.4nm will be the biggest contributor in 2027, more than 2nm! On 2028 AND BEYOND, management expects
2028 to also be a growth year. Again bullish 1.4nm saying it has more players than 2nm, so they expect continued
strong 1.4nm sales into 2028 and beyond, with DRAM and photonics as further growth drivers. Consensus already
models c.20% growth in 2028. On MARGINS, GMs currently 52% and Management sees no reason for it to fall
and expects a positive trajectory, as better pricing and efficiencies offset component cost inflation. On BACKLOG,
conversion typically takes 3 to 6 months, and the tone from customers is much more committed. On CHINA,
demand is very strong, particularly in mature foundry. That segment is coming down but is fully compensated by
growth in memory and power/analog. Management cautioned that visibility in China is always difficult. (See GIR
take )

envelope. Mitigations include advance purchasing, refurbishment, reuse and more efficient device specifications.

transparent comparison tools. Its defence rests on competitive pricing across KPN and Youfone, alongside
network quality—not on preventing customers from switching. Flagging AI is also an operating-cost opportunity.
Internal deployment is intended to improve customer engagement, change working practices and support lower

• Amazon / Globalstar: The FCC has approved Amazon Leo’s $11.5bn acquisition of Globalstar, giving Amazon
25 MHz of internationally harmonised mobile-satellite spectrum, around two dozen active satellites, approval for
the 48-satellite C-3 constellation and ground stations in Texas, Florida and Alaska, among other locations. Amazon
has committed to maintaining Emergency SOS and Find My services for current and future Apple devices, which
use around 85% of Globalstar’s global capacity. It has also applied to launch 5,105 additional direct-to-device
satellites from 2028, alongside its existing target of deploying 3,232 satellites by July 2029. (Telecoms)

year company veteran takes over shortly after Apax Partners bought out Warburg Pincus to gain full ownership in
a deal valuing Odido at around €6.5bn. (Telecom Paper)
