ETD Rates Watch: Tech Troubles Trigger A
Bond Bid
Daniel Noorian
30-Oct-2026, 04:59 ET (EDT)
Tech stocks and the Nasdaq were the drivers of fixed income late Thursday and beyond. As at 0700 GMT,
Nasdaq E-mini futures are down 2% after earnings from Amazon and Intel raised concerns over capex. Intel
shares plunged 22% in after-hours trading, while Amazon dropped as much as 7%. The last 24 hours have
seen some huge moves in popular names, with Alphabet and Caterpillar among the largest decliners on
Thursday. The market is becoming much more selective about who it backs.
This damaged sentiment in the Asia session, with the Nikkei down 2.12% while the Kospi is down 3.48%.
USTs went bid as a result, with TY futures tracking Nasdaq futures tick for tick in the overnight session.
Volumes were huge. Admittedly, there could have been some month-end impact, but TY traded 331k
overnight, double this week’s average. There was block buying in the belly, while the SOFR strip was up as
much as 8.5 ticks, with SFRZ6 up two. Markets are now pricing 18bp of hikes in December, down from 21bp
on Wednesday. SOFR option activity picked up, with paper buying narrow put spreads such as the SFRZ6
95.8125/95.75, which has traded at 2.25.
How long this bid hangs around remains to be seen. Early flows have seen accounts selling into the rally, with
OATs the big underperformer. The OAT future has been under pressure since 0700 GMT, falling 42 ticks in
the first 30 minutes of trading. Liquidity and volume are obviously challenging early on. French inflation data
also came out ahead of expectations, while GDP was weaker than expected. The data showed the economy
actually contracted in Q3.
OATs are supposed to benefit from Friday’s index extension, with the Euro Treasury index set to extend by
0.14 years. Staying on OATs, there were two large undisclosed OAT blocks on Thursday, with both looking
like sells: one outright and one possibly against Bunds. 
European inflation data are the main highlight on the calendar on Friday, but we will also get a chance to hear
from Fed and ECB members, with the blackouts over. A number of ECB members have already spoken, with a
familiar theme emerging. Bank of France’s Moulin said the ECB wasn’t seeing second-round effects spilling
into wages, while Simkus also said they weren’t seeing second-round effects yet. Kassik offered similar
remarks, saying inflation wasn’t broad-based yet.
UBS still expects the ECB to hike in December and deliver one more hike in March. Reinhard Cluse and team
said the press conference was not dovish, despite the statement focusing on growth concerns. President
Lagarde said the Governing Council had discussed whether to hike rates, while post-meeting source reports
referred to the views of “three ECB policymakers”, also pointing to a December hike. 
The moves in tech names diverted attention away from the BoJ, which left rates on hold as expected. In his
press conference, Governor Ueda sounded hawkish, warning markets that every meeting was live and noting:
“at every policy meeting, including the next one, we will assess the likelihood of our outlook materialising, as
well as the risks, and make our decisions accordingly.”
DP World CEO Yuvraj Narayan warned of further price pressures to come due to the disruption in the Strait of
Hormuz. He said the group’s trucking network was now moving over 3,000 containers a day. Previously, the
terminals at Jebel Ali port would handle around 40,000 per day. He said trucking was proving four or five times

This would lead to “the greater likelihood that higher costs feed through into prices, at a time when households

Last night’s US 7y auction came in line, with a bid-to-cover of 2.46 versus a six-auction average of 2.49.
End-user demand was solid, with indirects taking 57.8% while directs took 32.8%. That is another solid
showing from directs, given their recent average in the 7y auction was 26%. Dealers were left with 9.4%, their
lowest allocation since December.
