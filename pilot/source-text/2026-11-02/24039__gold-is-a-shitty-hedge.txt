The great gold liquidation
Gold is crashing, and the flows explain why. According to Quinn at Goldman Sachs, speculative
investors have sold $19.5bn of gold over five consecutive weeks, with selling accelerating into late
October. Even falling real yields have failed to support prices. Worse, positioning suggests the
liquidation may not be over.
Gold loses the big one
Gold broke below the massive $4,000 support on Friday and is now trading further below its 200-day
moving average than it has in a very long time. The $3,800 area is the first support to watch,
coinciding with the longer-term trend line. But remember, nothing good happens below the 200-day.

$19.5bn and counting
According to Quinn at GS, speculative gold selling accelerated during October 20–27, with $8bn in net
selling, marking the fifth consecutive week of outflows. Since September 22, cumulative selling has
reached $19.5bn. Of the latest weekly selling, $5.2bn came from long liquidation and $2.8bn from
fresh shorts.

The gold paradox
Gold is ignoring one of its traditional bullish drivers. Quinn notes that gold fell 1.6% during October 20–
27 despite an 11bp decline in US 10-year real yields. This is a positioning-driven market.

More to liquidate?
The selling has been aggressive, but positioning is hardly washed out. According to GS, Other gross
longs remain above their two-year median despite five consecutive weeks of liquidation. The risk?
Further technical deterioration triggers another wave of selling, irrespective of falling real yields.
Flows, not fundamentals, could drive the next leg lower.

Gold's fear trade
Gold lost another 4.3% between October 27 and 30, and options traders are increasingly paying for
downside protection. GS notes that three-month implied volatility and put skew have risen, with
normalized 25-delta put-call skew reaching the 85th percentile of its one-year range. The gold
correction is increasingly becoming a volatility story.
GS
Shitty fear hedge
People love calling gold the ultimate hedge against global risk. The recent price action tells a different
story. Gold has been crashing even as equity volatility surges. The lesson? Gold is not the reliable fear
hedge everyone makes it out to be. Never confuse the safe-haven narrative with actual protection.
