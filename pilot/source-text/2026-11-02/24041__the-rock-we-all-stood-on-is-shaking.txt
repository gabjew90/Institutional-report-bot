From great to just good
Every earnings season, Wall Street tells us how fantastic corporate America is doing. Another quarter,
another round of beats, another reason to buy stocks.
Except Q3 is looking a little less fantastic. Growth is slowing, beats are shrinking, guidance is getting
weaker, and investors are no longer applauding everything.
Here are the charts showing where the earnings bull case is starting to crack.
The acceleration era is over
Goldman's David Kostin puts his finger on the problem: earnings growth remains strong, but it is no
longer accelerating.
"Q3 2026 earnings results have underscored the fundamental challenge facing equity investors:
Earnings growth is strong, but it is no longer accelerating... it would also mark an end to the pattern of
acceleration that has characterized the last few quarters."
Slowing where it matters
The median S&P 500 company saw revenue growth slow from 7.4% in Q1 to 7.2% in Q2 and 7.0% in
Q3. Consensus expects another slowdown to 6.4% in Q4.
Not exactly a collapse. But the direction is becoming increasingly difficult to spin.
FactSet
The shrinking beat
Companies are still beating estimates, just not by as much. Q3's cumulative EPS surprise is running
well below the extraordinary levels seen in Q1 and Q2.
The earnings beat machine is still working. It is simply producing smaller treats.

GIR
The guidance problem
And here is where things get more interesting. Jefferies finds that revenue guidance revisions for 2026
have become considerably less positive, even as EPS revisions remain strong.
"We found that revenue guidance revisions for C26 have gotten much less positive on balance."
In other words, the bottom line is holding up better than the top line. That is reassuring for now, but
hardly the kind of revenue momentum bulls want to see.
Jefferies
Beats get a shrug. Misses get a firing squad
The asymmetry is becoming brutal. Earnings beats are generating little excitement, while misses are
being punished aggressively — particularly in large-cap tech.
When good news barely moves stocks and bad news gets hammered, the market is sending a
message.
Jefferies
The future isn't what it used to be
Jefferies argues that the real drama is in the outlooks, not the reported numbers. Current
fundamentals remain reasonably solid, but increasingly cautious guidance is weighing on investor
sentiment.
The market, apparently, has decided to start worrying about tomorrow rather than celebrating
yesterday. The SPX is back down to just 18.4x NTM EPS.

Jefferies
Tony P on the big D
Goldman's Tony Pasquariello argues that Q3 will ultimately be remembered as a disappointment.
Despite extraordinary growth in parts of cloud computing, several major players missed expectations
in their core businesses.
"....despite a handful of cloud businesses that are compounding huge growth rates on huge
denominators, Q3 results will be remembered as a disappointment. Alongside the never-ending
debates on returns, capital and free cash flow, to my eye this was the central issue, for the moment
anyway: at a time when hyperscaler capex intentions are clearly NOT slowing down, a few of the big
guns missed somewhere in the core of their business" (Tony P)
