conference 
o   The long end sold off following Chair Warsh’s reveal that there will not be a

the policy statement, was dismissive when asked about household inflation

conditions, resurrecting the narrative that long-term rates might have done 
the Fed’s work for them, obviating the need for further rate hikes 
o   Given these developments, we continue to find value in 10s/30s 
steepeners, which will also benefit should Treasury stay the course and keep 
auction sizes steady next week 
o   A dovish press conference, including a victory lap on market-based 
inflation expectations, undermined their stability. We find the rise in inflation 
pricing to be persistent and in-line with historical magnitudes. Maintain 1Yx1Y 
zero-coupon inflation swap longs 
•         FED DAY VIEWS, FX (PATRICK LOCKE) – Dollar lower, seemingly linked to the 
announcement that the Fed will decide policy settings at a lower frequency in the future. 
There's not a direct obvious policy pass-through to the dollar here, so the knee-jerk 
weakness must essentially reflect some perception of unconventional decisions 
reintroducing some institutional risk premium for USD. Above and beyond that, introductory 
comments restating commitments to get inflation down seem largely recycled, and as we 
alluded to in our prior post, fall short of a more vociferous hawkish bias that is needed to 
see the dollar make session highs. Alongside all this, short end US rates following suit 
lower, and leading to some steepening of the curve. 
o   There is arguably a benign (dollar-negative) reading of his inflation 
comments. In the context of last month's 33bp core CPI print, Warsh noted 
inflation compensation is going the right way, inflation expectations are 
sending the right signals, and that there's an "overwhelming consensus" that 
the "medicine" (one hike, and presumably priced hikes in the forwards) is 
kicking in. Again in the context of dollar longs requiring some more hawkish 
rhetoric, these initial comments didn't seem to meet that bar. 

o   On the whole, the dollar was net-sold between the change in the meeting 
schedule as well as comments on inflation that did not screen as hawkish as 
they possibly could have. In this context, 2s30s has twist steepened through 
the meeting. As we’ve discussed ad nauseum, that tends to be a very telling 
signal for the dollar, and in the context of the schedule change / inflation 
commentary, the USD has correspondingly sold off. That remains a useful 
signal for the FX space. All that said, the magnitude of dollar weakness is 
actually fairly modest, perhaps reflecting a lack of general participation. This 
outcome probably favors the various pockets of G10 that have some real-
asset linkages (NOK, AUD, CHF).

EARNINGS MACRO READ-THROUGH 
•         SUMMARY – Today's earnings gave a lot of good color on the labor market: (i) ADP 
reported fiscal Q1 pays per control growth of 2%, ahead of expectations. It also raised its 
full-year fiscal 2027 outlook to around 1%, up from 0–1%. (ii) Skilled labor is still a recurring 
theme. (iii) Meanwhile, two sub-50bn companies (ODFL and CHRW) plan to cut headcount 
even as their core businesses grow. That suggests automation / efficiency gains may 
weaken the usual links between business growth and labor demand. (iv) Wage inflation 
remains a concern, especially for sub-50bn companies: rising benefits and healthcare costs 

have also pushed total employment expenses higher. Outside the labor market, comments 
from GEV and VRT reiterated the acceleration in data center both in the US and 
internationally (both companies highlighted the growing demand in Europe). Selected 
quotes below. 
•         CONSUMER HEALTH / SPENDING 
o    [Leisure Soft; Healthcare Strong] ADP (ADP) – "we've seen strength in 
areas like trade and transportation, but also continued strength in healthcare 
and education...financial services continues to show some resilience. We've 
seen a little bit of softening in places like leisure and hospitality." 
o    [Consumer Trade-Down] Generac (GNRC) – "I think consumers are a bit 
under stress...there are consumers who they still have backup power needs, 
but maybe home standby is just not attainable in terms of where their budget 
is today." 
o    [Backlog Near $200bn] GE Vernova (GEV) – "almost $30 billion in new 
orders and an expansion of our backlog from $176 billion to approximately 
$200 billion in 3Q" 
o    [Rates Weigh on Housing] Lennox (LII) – "underlying demand remains 
constrained by homeowner affordability. Elevated mortgage rates and 
broader consumer uncertainty." 
•         LABOR MARKET 
o    [Employment Outlook Up] ADP (ADP) – "As a result, we now forecast 
pay-per-control growth of around 1% for fiscal 2027, which is an improvement 
on our prior outlook of 0% to 1% growth. Labor markets continue to 
demonstrate resiliency and we have seen our clients continuing to hire at a 
steady pace." 
o    [Headcount Down; Volume Up] C.H. Robinson (CHRW) – "Our average 
headcount was down 10.4% year-over-year in Q3 and was down 2% 

sequentially...decouple headcount growth from volume growth while growing 
volume." 
o    [$30m Wage Inflation] C.H. Robinson (CHRW) – "we have been 
absorbing approximately $30 million of annual wage inflation" 
o    [Factory Hiring Ramps] Generac (GNRC) – "We expect to achieve 
significantly higher levels of output from our existing facilities through added 
production lines and an increase in manufacturing staffing levels." 
o    [Headcount Cuts Outpace Shipments] Old Dominion Freight Line 
(ODFL) – "our average headcount in the third quarter was down 4%, 
comparing favorably to the 2% decrease in shipments per day." 
o    [Wages +3%–3.5%] Old Dominion Freight Line (ODFL) – "we gave a 
wage increase that was between 3%-3.5%, and then just our people are 
working more hours." 
o    [Layoffs Near Lows] ADP (ADP) – "it's pretty broad-based across 
industry verticals...the layoff numbers are...near multi-year lows and the hiring 
volumes on a gross basis continue to come in at a steady pace" 
o    [Turnover at Record Low] Clean Harbors (CLH) – "All-time low in 
voluntary turnover rates have been great." 
o    [Labor Paces Data Centers] Vertiv (VRT) – "power availability, 
permitting, general sentiment and construction site skilled labor availability 
are pacing items, but not slowing down growth." 
o    [Automation Offsets Labor Shortage] Waste Management (WM) – "our 
collection business is very labor intensive...We're absolutely looking at how 
can we bring technologies to the forefront of our operations to reduce labor 
dependence given that there's going to be a labor shortage. Long-term."

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the October 26th Morning Briefing 
We remain Tactically Bullish. The SPX has lost 2.4% over the last 2 weeks, including a 
shift toward defensive sectors. MTD, SPX is -36bp with 8 / 11 sectors positive and Energy, 
Staples, Utilities, HC, Financials, Materials, RE, and Discretionary all beating the index. 
What makes us think that stocks will move higher? Generally speaking, an economy 
growing at/above trend GDP and positive earnings growth produces a bull market. Flash 
PMIs, for the second consecutive month, point to an economy growing ~5% annualized, in 
real terms. So far, about a quarter of the SPX has reported earnings with no dips to the pre-
earnings season forecast (~13% revenue growth, ~30% EPS growth, and ~15% margins … 
full details in the Additional Thoughts section). So that is the basis for the view. A refocus on 
macro and micro fundamentals, rather than AI fears, should be supportive of stocks. That 
said, the signal is less clear as we have yet to have a complete positioning washout despite 
sentiment being firmly in the Fear region.
