Taiwan: The next tech domino?
Taiwan has been the ultimate AI winner, but cracks are starting to emerge beneath the surface. Korea
is getting hit, semiconductors are struggling, and foreign investors are pulling billions from Taiwanese
equities. Yet Taiwan continues to outperform, leaving some increasingly stretched divergences. The
fundamentals remain extraordinary, but so are the expectations. With positioning crowded, technical
supports approaching and the semiconductor cycle showing signs of stress, the risk of a violent catch-
down is growing. We look at the cracks, the key levels and a cheap way to position for a reversal.
Taiwan technicals
Taiwan continues to defy the carnage in KOSPI and the broader tech complex, although the index has
pulled back from recent ATHs. The steep short-term trendline sits just below current levels, alongside
the 100-day moving average. A break below this support zone could accelerate downside momentum.
The 200-day remains much lower, leaving plenty of room for a more meaningful correction.

The crowded Taiwan trade
Some selling has emerged, but zoom out and Taiwan allocations remain at the 91st percentile over the
past five years, according to Goldman. Positioning has barely normalized despite the recent volatility.
Plenty of potential supply if investors decide to reduce exposure more aggressively.

The gap
The gap between KOSPI and Taiwan continues to widen. There are obvious fundamental differences
between the two, but relative performance has become increasingly stretched. Divergences like these
can persist, but when they start closing, the adjustment can be violent.

The SOX connection
The Taiwan vs SOX divergence started widening this summer and has become increasingly
pronounced. Taiwan continues to price extraordinary AI resilience while the broader semiconductor
complex is sending a more cautious signal. Few are paying attention to this disconnect, but history
suggests these gaps deserve respect.

The AI concentration problem
Taiwan is in a league of its own when it comes to AI exposure. BofA's chart shows Taiwan standing far
above the rest of Asia in AI trade exposure relative to GDP, while remaining a net energy importer. The
AI boom has delivered extraordinary growth, but concentration works both ways. Any moderation in
global AI spending could hit Taiwan disproportionately, especially with energy costs remaining
elevated.
BofA
When semis sneeze, Taiwan catches a cold
Taiwan's export machine remains heavily dependent on the global semiconductor cycle. ANZ
highlights the historical relationship between Taiwan's exports and the SOX, with semiconductor
performance potentially providing an early signal for export momentum. With semis already
experiencing significant volatility, the risk is that equity weakness eventually spills into the real
economy. Taiwan's extraordinary macro strength could become more vulnerable if the chip cycle loses
momentum.

ANZ
Taiwan's AI currency
The AI trade is no longer just an equity story. BofA finds that TWD has the strongest correlation to AI
equity returns among Asian currencies. Meanwhile, foreign investors have pulled $26 billion from
Taiwanese equities in October alone, according to the bank. Taiwan's strong external balances provide
a cushion, but another leg lower in AI equities could put renewed pressure on the currency and
broader risk appetite.
BofA
The growth miracle has a catch
Taiwan's nominal GDP surged 19% in H1, powered by extraordinary semiconductor demand and rising
export income. ANZ finds that changes in income terms of trade explain around 62% of nominal GDP
growth over the past two decades. The AI boom has only reinforced that relationship. Great when chip
demand and pricing are accelerating. Potentially less great when the cycle turns. Taiwan's economic
strength is increasingly tied to one very powerful global investment theme.
ANZ
The chipflation cushion
Taiwan has managed to offset surging energy import costs through stronger semiconductor pricing.
However, the balance is becoming less comfortable. ANZ notes that Taiwan's net terms of trade
deteriorated 4.6% in Q3 as import prices outpaced export prices. The risk is straightforward: if chip
pricing momentum fades while energy costs remain elevated, one of Taiwan's most important macro
cushions starts to disappear.

The semiconductor déjà vu
ANZ compares the current semiconductor cycle with the 1999–2000 episode. The historical overlay is
a reminder of how quickly semiconductor momentum can reverse after an extraordinary advance.
This is not a prediction of another dot-com collapse, but the asymmetry is worth watching. When
growth expectations are exceptionally high, even a moderation in the cycle can generate a
disproportionate market reaction.

The reversal risk
Nobody is questioning Taiwan's extraordinary AI-driven fundamentals. The question is how much of
that exceptional growth is already priced in. With Korea under pressure, foreign investors pulling
billions from Taiwanese equities, and the Taiwan–KOSPI divergence increasingly stretched, the setup
deserves close attention. Watch the key technical supports. A break could quickly turn relative
resilience into an aggressive catch-down trade.
We like low-delta options in setups like these. The chart shows the EWT December 95 put. We wouldn't
overcomplicate things with put spreads. Keep it simple, treat it as a lottery ticket, and let the convexity
do the work if Taiwan starts catching down.
