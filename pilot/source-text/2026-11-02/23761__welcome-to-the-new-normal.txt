Stranger things
The S&P 500 just did something it hasn't done in nearly 40 years: it fell despite more than 80% of its
stocks rising. Meanwhile, hedge funds are de-risking at a historic pace, the Magnificent Seven are
losing their magic, and Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson thinks the correction may be approaching its
final act.
Here are our favorite charts from Nick Savone, Managing Director at Morgan Stanley's equities desk,
this week.
Welcome to the new normal
Single-stock volatility relative to the index is approaching extraordinary levels, while correlations
between S&P 500 stocks have collapsed. In other words, the index tells you almost nothing about the
chaos underneath. Welcome to the new normal.

Dispersion gone wild
The sector breakdown tells an interesting story. Materials, Industrials and Communication Services are
seeing the biggest dispersion, while Staples and Utilities remain relatively calm. Meanwhile, single-
stock volatility is elevated across almost every sector. A stock picker's paradise — or nightmare,
depending on your positions.
MS QDS

The Magnificent Seven aren't so magnificent
Equal-weight equities have started outperforming as the Magnificent Seven stumble. With mutual fund
year-end approaching, the question is whether portfolio managers continue defending yesterday's
winners or finally rotate into the rest of the market. The latter would be a rather interesting change of
character.
MS TIS
Hedge funds have had enough
All this volatility has taken its toll. Hedge-fund de-grossing since June has reached 8%, the largest
sustained reduction on record. Net leverage is back at 51%, but recent buying has been driven largely
by short covering. Not exactly conviction buying.
MS Prime Brokerage
A rotational bear market hiding in plain sight
Europe's STOXX 600 is only around 3% below its highs. Underneath, however, cyclicals have
underperformed defensives by 13%, while price momentum has unwound 14% since June. Morgan
Stanley sees an increasingly attractive setup for a reversal, particularly in Semiconductors, Hardware
and Banks.
Morgan Stanley
One last flush?
After all that damage, are we finally getting close? Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson believes the
correction is entering its final stages, although Semiconductors could still fall another 10–15% before
finding support around mid-November. His year-end S&P 500 target remains 8,000.

The midterms: A Democratic sweep?
Prediction markets are assigning a 66% probability to a Democratic sweep, according to Morgan
Stanley's election dashboard. The bank highlights NC-03, FL-07, FL-13 and VA-05 as races worth
watching for signs of a broader Democratic wave. Politics is about to become another market catalyst.

Luxury has a new address
Luxury hotels represent just 2% of global hotel properties but generate 15% of room revenue. Morgan
Stanley expects the five largest global hoteliers to deliver approximately 20% annual EPS growth
through 2028, versus 10% for luxury goods companies. Apparently, the rich would rather book the
suite than buy another handbag.
