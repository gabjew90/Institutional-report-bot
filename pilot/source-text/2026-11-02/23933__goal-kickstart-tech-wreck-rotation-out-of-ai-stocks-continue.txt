Tech wreck - rotation out of AI stocks continues into midterm elections
Last week, global AI-linked stocks sold off sharply driven by 
concerns around AI capex, monetisation and disruption, 
with Nasdaq down 9% from its October peak 
(underperforming S&P equal-weight by 12%). The US 
earnings season has generally been strong, with S&P 500 EPS 
growth tracking 33% year-on-year in Q3 2026 but 
momentum is slowing. This week, the focus will be on the US 
midterm elections, US labour market data, and European 
central bank meetings, inflation and PMIs. 
Risk appetite declined in October amid several risk-off 
rotations across and within assets, with our Risk Appetite 
Indicator (RAI) falling to -0.5 on Friday. The rotation from 
AI-related stocks into “not AI” areas has been particularly 
sharp: the S&P 500 low vol index outperformed by 17% in 
October, one of its strongest relative monthly returns since 
the Tech bubble (Exhibit 1). As we have discussed before, 
Tech dominance in equity benchmarks increases the benefits 
of style diversification and exposures which are less 
correlated with AI stocks. Indeed, the performance of 
Nasdaq vs. low vol has followed that of AI vs. ex-AI stocks so 
far this year (Exhibit 2) - since the summer the S&P 500 low 
vol index has become negatively correlated with Nasdaq
