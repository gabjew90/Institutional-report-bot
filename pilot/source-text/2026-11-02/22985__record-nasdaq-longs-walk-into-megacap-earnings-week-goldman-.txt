 
Record Nasdaq Longs Walk Into Megacap Earnings 
Week: Goldman Warns "Meaningful Futures Selling 
Could Ensue" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, OCT 28, 2026 - 02:00 PM 
Last Tuesday, we asked whether Nasdaq was at a crossroads, with futures longs stubbornly 
refusing to capitulate despite an AI meltdown under the surface. One week, one Meta faceplant, 
and one $95 billion equity raise later, we have the answer: they still haven't. 
In fact, they bought more. According to the latest CFTC data, Nasdaq non-dealer net positioning 
hit a new nominal high in the week through October 20, as non-dealers bought $9.5 billion of 
futures while the index slid 3.5%. That was the third straight week of buying, for a cumulative 
$21.8 billion since September 29. 
 

 
 
That is the main finding of a note published this morning by Goldman futures trader Robert 
Quinn (available to pro subs), and his conclusion is about as blunt as desk notes get: length 
remains elevated heading into more megacap tech results, and if Amazon, Microsoft, and 
Alphabet "fail to impress, meaningful futures selling could ensue." 
In other words, the most crowded position in the market is about to meet the most important 
earnings week of the quarter, with MSFT and GOOGL reporting Wednesday after the close, and 
AMZN on Thursday. Oh, and the FOMC is on Wednesday too. What could go wrong? 
The Day Meta Brought The Shorts Back 
Regular readers will recall that for about an hour after its Q3 print, Meta was the savior of the AI 
trade. Then the market read the fine print, CDS blew out to a record, and the stock tumbled. 
Quinn's numbers show just how violent the October 22 session was. Meta fell 7.0% and 
dragged the entire AI complex with it: Hyperscalers -4.1%, Memory -8.6%, Semis -6.5%. The 
company's forecast of a significant spending increase rattled credit markets which, as Quinn 
notes, were already worried about the sustainability of the debt-fueled AI infrastructure boom 
 

 
(something we have been pounding the table on since last October, when AI quietly overtook 
the banks as the largest sector in the IG market). 
Nasdaq fell 2.9% that day, and here is the punchline: futures aggregate open interest jumped 
$10.6 billion, the largest one-day increase of 2026 excluding roll periods. Combine rising OI with 
a falling price and underperforming funding, and the conclusion is straightforward: sizeable new 
shorts were initiated. 
 
Note that blue spike on the right. Open interest went from roughly 272K contracts to over 290K 
in a single session (roughly, read off the chart), as fast money finally pressed the short side 
against a mountain of real-money length.

But Quinn makes a subtle point here: funding cheapened on both an outright and a relative 
basis, which means shorts weren't the only ones heading for the exit. Longs were cutting too. 
 
Nasdaq 3-month funding is still elevated at roughly 85, well above the ~50 lows of August, but it 
has rolled over from the mid-October highs near 88. Relative to the S&P, the move is clearer 
still: the Nasdaq-vs-S&P funding spread spiked to around 7.5 last week, matching the highs of 
the past year, before sliding back toward 4.5. 
 

 
 
Translation: the premium investors are paying to be long Nasdaq via futures is finally starting to 
deflate. Which is what you'd expect if the marginal holder has started taking chips off the table. 
The Systematic Seller Has Already Turned 
So who was selling? According to Quinn, CTAs were likely a key driver, with Goldman's Futures 
Strategists framework flagging a momentum break. The chart below shows why: Nasdaq 
short-term momentum has collapsed to roughly -3.5, medium-term momentum is sitting right on 
zero, and even the long-term signal has slid from around 16 at the start of October to about 8. 
 

 
 
And it's not just Goldman's futures desk seeing this. JPMorgan's positioning team notes that 
CTA US equity exposure has fallen every single day since October 13, led by Nasdaq. 
Meanwhile, Goldman's own CTA model (per the desk's Monday positioning recap) has trend 
followers selling roughly $23.5 billion of global equities this week in a flat tape, rising to $60.7 
billion in a down tape, and buying a measly $3.6 billion if stocks rally. That roughly 17-to-1 
asymmetry is the same kind of trapdoor Goldman flagged in yesterday's hedge fund rout piece. 
Which leads to the strangest part of the current setup: 
Hedge funds have already bailed. Goldman Prime shows US long/short net leverage at 43.4%, 
the 0th percentile on a five-year lookback, and fundamental L/S net leverage at a one-year low. 
Hedge funds were busy capitulating last weekwhile non-dealers (read: asset managers and 
other real money) were busy buying $9.5 billion of Nasdaq futures. 
Put differently, the "smart" money is flat, the trend followers are selling, and the biggest Nasdaq 
futures long on record (in nominal terms) sits with the cohort that tends to buy dips by default. 
Nothing says "healthy positioning" like the pros hiding under the desk while the index buyers set 
new records. 
 

 
The Bull Case: Nobody Wants To Be A Hero (In Either Direction) 
To be fair, not everyone thinks the overhang is a disaster waiting to happen. 
JPMorgan's Market Intel desk remains Tactically Bullish, arguing that Monday's session went a 
long way toward re-establishing the AI theme and that a well-received batch of hyperscaler 
prints could build momentum into the post-midterm seasonal window. Their delta-one team 
flagged a record ~$12.7 billion of semiconductor ETF inflows last week, smashing the prior 
record from late July. Then again, JPM concedes that we still haven't had a complete positioning 
washout, even with sentiment firmly in Fear. Over in London, JPM's international desk summed 
up the mood perfectly: nobody has much incentive to be a hero ahead of this week, though it 
notes the SOX-vs-SPX drawdown now rivals 2008, which could set up a tactical AI-infra bounce 
before year-end. 
Goldman's own US sales trading desk sees two-way risk too. It notes that recent tech bounces 
have been driven mostly by an absence of heavy supply rather than real buying, and the derivs 
desk is pitching short-dated QQQ call spreads to play a potential positioning squeeze through 
earnings. In other words, if the hyperscalers deliver, there is plenty of short (and underinvested 
hedge fund) fuel for a melt-up. 
And for now the bulls have the upper hand: Nasdaq rose more than 1% again on Tuesday as 
the AI rebound extended on the back of Anthropic's IPO filing (so length is probably even bigger 
now). 
What Has To Go Right On Wednesday And Thursday 
The bar, however, is far from trivial. Goldman's EMEA head of 1D trading, Rich Privorotsky, laid 
out the four things that matter far more than another round of headline beats: 
●​ Capex: flat or higher is fine; a cut would be disastrous for picks-and-shovels names. 
●​ Backlog: how fast it converts into revenue. 
●​ FCF: how much of that revenue actually turns into cash in the out quarters. 
●​ Financing: how the next leg gets funded, and whether the hyperscalers follow Meta and 
lean on equity instead of debt. 
He also warned that the cost of capital for the AI complex has risen materially and that credit 
remains the one to worry about, still flashing yellow to red relative to equities.

A Record Long With No One Left To Sell To 
Quinn's math is simple. Non-dealers are carrying a record long, hedge funds are already flat, 
CTAs are flipping to sellers, and the funding premium is fading. If Amazon, Microsoft and 
Alphabet fail to impress, the only cohort with real ammunition to sell is the one sitting on the 
biggest position, and it has nobody left on the other side except the systematic sellers already 
heading for the exit. 
Could the hyperscalers deliver a squeeze instead? Sure, and Goldman's derivs desk is 
positioned for it. But with capex guides that now dwarf free cash flow, Meta's equity raise setting 
a precedent, and AI credit still cracking at the edges, we think the asymmetry runs the other 
way: the upside requires perfection; the downside just requires one more Meta. And with the 
critical 29K level in Nasdaq futures we flagged last week having held (barely, by roughly a 
hundred points) on October 23, there isn't much of a cushion. 
Then again, record longs have never been a problem... until the moment they are.
