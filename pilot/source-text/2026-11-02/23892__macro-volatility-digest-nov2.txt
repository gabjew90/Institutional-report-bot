WEEKLY MARKET COMMENTARY 
Tech Rout, Muted Vol: Bullish Positioning Unwinds  
 
Cross-Asset Volatility: While equities sold off last week on 
disappointing Tech earnings, implied volatilities were fairly 
muted across asset classes. Equity vol ended the week 
unchanged, with the VIX® Index hovering around 19.5% (75th 
percentile high over the past year). Interest rate volatility 
declined modestly following the Fed meeting, with the OIS 
market now pricing in ~75% probability of a hike at the next 
meeting. Positioning in TLT options has flipped notably in 
recent weeks, with skew falling from near a 1-year high to 
near a 1-year low on stronger demand for upside calls. The 
fear of higher rates has dissipated, with more investors now 
positioning for long-end yields to fall on the back of a more 
uncertain macro outlook. The correlation between stocks 
and bonds has also flipped. The 1M rolling correlation 
between the SPX® Index and the 10Y Treasury yield has 
jumped from -70% to +40% as investor focus shifts from 
inflation to growth risk. This is despite ongoing geopolitical 
turmoil in the Middle East which has kept oil prices elevated, 
though oil volatility did moderate slightly last week ahead of 
the US midterm elections, with the OVX Index down 1.5 vol 
pts to 58% (64th percentile). Gold was the only asset class to 
see an increase in implied volatility last week, with GLD 1M 
implied vol up 1.6 pts to 23% (49th percentile) as gold prices 
fell to a 1-year low. 
Equity Volatility: Despite the large sell-off on Friday, with 
the SPX Index down 1.8% (and QQQ down 3.5%), there was 
very little panic in the options market, with SPX 1M fixed 
strike vols increasing by just 0.6 pt. Moreover, SPX put skew 
and put convexity both fell, as investors monetized existing 
hedges. As we’ve highlighted over the past few weeks, 
hedging demand has gone up notably in SPX options, which 
helped to keep a lid on the VIX move last week. For more, 
see our VIX Index decomposition here. The equity sell-off 
was almost entirely driven by disappointing Tech earnings, 
with 9 of the 11 S&P sectors up on the week (led by 
Healthcare +3% vs. Comm Services down 8.5%). The 
extreme sector rotation is another reason why index volatility 
has been so muted. Average single stock vol, as measured 
by the VIXEQSM Index, gained 0.4 pt wk/wk despite earnings 
now more than halfway complete.  
Skew: Skew remains steep in SPX options, with 1M skew 
(25-delta ratio) trading in the 80th percentile high. Positioning 
in single stock options has also shifted notably, with call 
demand dropping sharply and hedging demand increasing. 
The percent of stocks in the S&P top 100 trading with 
inverted call skew (a sign of extreme bullish sentiment) has 
dropped from a high of 44% in early October to just 10% now. 
Looking at retail flow on Cboe’s exchanges, retail put buying 
in the mega-cap Tech stocks hit a YTD high last week, 
accounting for 34% of all opening trades (vs. avg of 24%).

How to read this chart:
The chart shows how various asset class implied 
volatilities are trading relative to their own history, using 
a 10-year lookback. 
The volatilities are ranked by z-scores, which compares 
the current volatility level for an asset versus its own 10-
year history. A z-score of +2, for example, would mean 
that the current volatility level is trading 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average (i.e. rich) while a 
z-score of -2 would mean it’s trading 2 standard 
deviations below average (i.e. cheap)
Because each asset class volatility trades at different 
levels and often defined differently (e.g. lognormal vs. 
normal vol, price vs. bps vol), by standardizing using z-
scores, we’re able to compare different asset class 
volatilities on the same scale - and see at a glance if 
there are any divergences or dislocations in the cross-
asset volatility markets
