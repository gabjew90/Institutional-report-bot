Fed's hawkish hold overshadowed by dampening equity risk sentiment. The FOMC left rates unchanged
at 4.0% in a 10–2 vote, broadly matching expectations against the backdrop of approaching midterm elections,
mixed inflation data and recent Fed commentary emphasizing growth and AI-driven capex. The statement
upgraded the pace of economic activity from “solid” to “strong,” while the Fed also outlined plans for
strategy-focused meetings in 2027 and 2028. During the press conference, the December stub rallied 3bp and
the USD weakened broadly across G10. Hedge funds were significant USD sellers ahead of the decision, with
10-day cumulative net flows reaching -2.1σ. Meanwhile, weakness in large-cap growth, technology and
AI-related stocks coincided with a sharp rally in rates, highlighting shifting cross-asset correlations. Looking
ahead, UBS Economics expects 25bp hikes in December and January, taking the federal funds rate to 4.50%,
followed by a prolonged hold.
The tech selloff deepened, but positioning points to squeeze risk. Tech endured a volatile week as key
Mag 7 earnings and the FOMC erased earlier gains, leaving the sector roughly in line with the S&P 500 before

2
Friday’s sharper selloff. Idiosyncratic moves in Alphabet, Amazon and Intel drove weakness, while software,
recently viewed as more defensive, also came under pressure following disappointing results from
ServiceNow, Fortinet and Twilio. Even Microsoft’s beat-and-raise, evidence of AI monetization and supportive
Oracle commentary failed to restore confidence in the AI trade. The central question is whether resilient
hyperscaler AI capex can offset mixed earnings and help Tech find a floor after a roughly 6% October decline.
Near term, earnings and midterms remain key catalysts, while favorable November seasonality provides
support. Positioning looks increasingly constructive for a tactical rebound: hyperscaler de-grossing has
resumed, short exposure remains above the 80th percentile over five years, and SOX is down 14% MTD.
Tech could remain rangebound, but cleaner positioning, elevated shorts and resilient AI fundamentals
increasingly create scope for a short-covering rally.
November seasonality and midterms could fuel year-end equity chase. With November historically the
S&P 500’s strongest month and the midterms approaching, the seasonal backdrop for equities is increasingly
supportive. The index has gained 1.9% on average in November since 1950, with only one negative
November in the past decade. This coincides with a backdrop of elevated short-squeeze risk, as low net
exposure and multi-year-high gross exposure create scope for a year-end performance chase. Midterms
provide another potential tailwind, with the S&P higher six months after every midterm since 1950. Weaker oil,
lower VIX and MOVE, and downward pressure on 10y yields provide additional support into year-end. Markets
are pricing limited election risk. The SPX one-day implied move for Wednesday has fallen to 70bp from 90bp
last week, roughly in line with nonfarm payrolls and CPI pricing. EURUSD event premium has also declined,
while Nov. 4 TLT options show no obvious kink. With Nov. 6 SPX ATM volatility near 12, downside protection
through next week remains relatively cheap, particularly given the risk of concurrent Iran escalation.
The ECB provided a glimmer of hope to French markets. French and Eurozone financial conditions will
have to deteriorate considerably before the ECB takes any action. On Thursday, ECB president Lagarde
provided just enough of a signal to provide some comfort for investors. During the post policy meeting press
conference, Lagarde noted the ECB has tools to ensure the smooth transmission of monetary policy. There
was no more than that, but it contrasts favourably with the alternative line she could have delivered: the French
know what budget action they must take. Lagarde also observed that financial conditions had tightened as a
consequence of high yields and wider spreads, which, if sustained, would weigh on growth and inflation. There
was nothing to shift the markets away from a December rate hike (forecast by UBS and 21bp priced by the
market), but again those remarks provided a slightly softer edge to proceedings. Lagarde announced she will
leave the ECB on Feb. 7, eight months early, to give her more freedom to comment on French fiscal matters.
Lula win sparks sharp repricing in Brazil. President Lula became the first candidate to overturn a first-round
defeat and win reelection in the runoff, capturing 74% of available second-round votes and defying polls,
prediction markets and the UBS aggregator. The result caught markets positioned the wrong way, driving a
sharp repricing across Brazilian equities, FX and rates. BOVA11, which tracks the Ibovespa, fell 14.5%, while
the SMAL11 small-cap ETF dropped 28.1%, with prior rally leaders hit hardest and exporters and USD-linked
stocks outperforming as the BRL weakened. USDBRL briefly traded above 5.60 before retreating toward 5.40,
while rates moved sharply from pricing cuts to hikes. Implied volatility has since declined, but positioning
remains unsettled. With volatility lower and skew flatter, buying puts to hedge upside exposure looks attractive,
including the December 93.5% IBOV put at a 0.75% premium. Selective opportunities are emerging in
oversold domestic cyclicals, while defensive sectors remain attractive. Fiscal uncertainty remains the key
overhang, with the finance minister appointment, fiscal framework and BCB reaction function the main
directional catalysts.
TOPIX rebalance marks another milestone in Japan’s governance and market reform. An estimated $63
bn traded at Thursday’s close as the first leg of TOPIX’s major rebalancing took effect, marking Japan’s
largest index rebalance. Membership will increasingly reflect liquidity, investability and free-float-adjusted
market capitalization rather than listing history. Beyond the immediate liquidity impact, the changes represent
another step in Japan’s long-running governance reform, building on the Stewardship and Corporate
Governance Codes, reduced cross-shareholdings and greater emphasis on cost of capital, capital returns and

valuations. While the index changes do not guarantee better governance, they make membership less of a
legacy entitlement and reinforce the broader reform agenda. Near term, markets remain focused on AI
spending, supporting technology and capital goods, and higher rates and yields, benefiting banks. Although
the BoJ held rates today, a December hike is expected. Longer term, improving governance and market
liquidity remain important structural tailwinds, with the TOPIX rebalance marking another milestone in Japan’s
market reform.
