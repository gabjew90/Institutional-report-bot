What we have learnt from the hyperscalers is that AI demand is not the problem, capacity and the cost of
delivering it are: cloud growth accelerated (AWS reported $46.0bn, up 39%, Azure reported $33.2bn, and

whether demand exists to how efficiently hyperscalers can build capacity and ultimately generate returns
on the investment.

TELCOS

• US Telcos: The FCC is proposing to make it easier for ordinary smartphones to connect directly to
satellites outside mobile network coverage. Its 2024 Supplemental Coverage from Space (SCS) rules
required satellite operators to lease mobile spectrum across large, nationwide territories, forcing
companies such as Starlink and AST SpaceMobile to seek repeated waivers. Proposal FCC 26-71 would
expand eligible spectrum from five to 13 bands, adding 482MHz, and let providers serve specific local
areas or use spectrum licences they already own rather than secure nationwide leases. It would also replace
complex leasing arrangements with standard agreements to coordinate with neighbouring spectrum users.

• Elisa: Following meetings with the CEO, CFO and Head of IR, GIR sees a slower Finnish service-
revenue recovery despite easing competition. Q4 contract repricing should help, but renewed mobile
competition and corporate pressure put even the low end of FY26’s 0–2% telecom growth guidance at
risk, with modest downside to FY26/27 consensus growth of 0.3%/1.7%. Finland remains structurally
attractive, but stronger macro support is unlikely before mid-2027, limiting confidence in a recovery
before H2 2027 and underpinning GIR’s Neutral rating, lowered from Buy in September. ISS efficiencies
should support growth from 2027, although the margin benefit is unquantified, with DC benefits from
2028. Deeper customer relationships should help mitigate agentic-AI disruption, while AI offers cost
savings. Elisa remains dividend-focused, with a conservative, self-imposed 1.5–2.0x leverage target; fibre
JVs could release capital, while towers are a lower priority given their strategic role in autonomous
networks
• Telefónica: Rivals have urged the CNMC to reject its proposed five-year voluntary framework replacing
cost-based duct and pole regulation, warning of a cumulative 116% wholesale price increase and €1.50
more per month for retail fibre customers (Cinco Dias). Telefónica’s infrastructure represents 99.2% of
regulated wholesale physical infrastructure and supports over 80% of alternative network deployments.
Telefónica puts the monthly impact at just €0.08–0.16 per line, arguing that rates remain 39% below 2011
levels and well below those in markets such as France. The CNMC is reviewing submissions and expects
to notify the European Commission between late 2026 and January 2027 on whether cost-based regulation
should remain.
• UK: VMO2 CEO Lutz Schueler is calling for a full investigation into BT’s TalkTalk acquisition, warning
that bypassing normal competition scrutiny would undermine regulation and investor confidence (Telecom
Paper). He criticised the CMA’s two-week review deadline, the government’s lack of transparency after
receiving its confidential report, and proposed public-interest changes to the Enterprise Act that would
facilitate the deal. He also argued that enabling BT to buy a major rival and wholesale customer while the
CMA challenges Nexfibre’s Netomnia acquisition creates a regulatory double standard that could weaken
alternative networks’ ability to compete with Openreach.
