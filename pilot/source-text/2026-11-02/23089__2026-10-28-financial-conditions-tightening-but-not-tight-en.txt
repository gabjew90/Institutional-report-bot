US Watch - Quick Insights
 
28 Oct 2026
FINANCIAL CONDITIONS TIGHTENING BUT
NOT TIGHT
Davide Oneglia / Freya Beamish
The FOMC kept rates unchanged, 10-2. The statement upgraded the expansion from solid to strong. But also
continues to point to job gains running in parallel with supply, though our own assessment suggests wage growth
may already be bottoming. Similarly, Warsh's presser highlighted that financial conditions are tightening for some
businesses but not tight overall. In our view, the increase in yields in over H2 is exacting some discipline in the AI
space but there is not evidence in the real or financial economy that financial conditions are tight.
It wasn’t long ago that the overwhelming consensus on the Street was that the Fed couldn’t hike: the US economy
was “brittle”, and a White House-loyal Warsh wouldn’t disappoint his boss before the midterms. Our view was the
opposite – domestic inflation and bond market pressures would force the Fed to act and investors to reprice the
terminal rate. But now that most of our expected Fed quarterly-hike trajectory is priced in and that hotter core
inflation and stable-to-reaccelerating job markets support a hawkish view, the same pundits that said Warsh
couldn’t hike complain about him not hiking today. This is unsurprising – the sell-side has a habit of
overcompensating for forecasting mistakes by doubling down in the opposite direction. They didn’t see the
September hike coming, so it must be the Fed now hikes at every meeting and/or throws in a 50bps hike along the
way! 
The reality is that the Fed is likely OK with current rate pricing and wants to keep the market from extrapolating a
more aggressive path with a hike at every meeting. While an increasing number of FOMC members are coming
around to the idea that the Fed needs to do more to tame inflation, the debate about how much tightening is
required is ongoing and depends on the data, especially in job markets, showing sustained reacceleration. Hiking
today would have reduced Fed’s optionality.
September was always the politically contentious FOMC meeting. Greater importance of quarter-end meetings with
SEP, strong bond market pressures and safer distance from the midterms compared to October made that a test
for Fed independence. With a hike then, market urgency has faded.
Make no mistakes: a gradual Fed can be a more hawkish Fed. Broken consensus after September re-ossified
around the view that even if the Fed does raise rates, it won’t go far. As we explained yesterday, there are good
reasons why the consensus might be wrong again. One of them is that the fast pace of policy tightening
(accompanied by ugly language about “sacrifice ratios” and other niceties) was a major factor behind the 2022-23
monetary squeeze, not just the level of rates. A more gradual hiking cycle this time would have a smaller impact on
sentiment and leave the economy with more time to adjust to higher rates without major front-loaded impact on
output. This might well imply a higher terminal rate than consensus pencils in, if the AI burst doesn’t materialise.
