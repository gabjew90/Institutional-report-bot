The buying power nobody is talking about
Equity positioning has been slashed, but the buyers are starting to return. Retail is piling into leveraged
ETFs, institutional appetite is showing signs of life, and QQQ shorts are sitting at multi-year highs.
Meanwhile, call buying has yet to fully recover and corporate buybacks remain resilient. The
interesting setup: several sources of potential buying power are lining up just as the market attempts
to turn the recent bounce into something bigger.
The positioning reset
JPM's Panigirtzoglou notes that broad equity positioning has fallen sharply from its mid-September
peak. Institutional investors, including hedge funds, have materially reduced exposure during the
recent shakeout. The important takeaway: the market may have more room for institutional buying
than the price recovery alone suggests.
JPM
Dipping the toes
Vanda highlights early signs of a revival in institutional risk appetite. After last week's sharp slowdown,
daily net call buying has rebounded above its one-month moving average. An encouraging shift
following weeks of weakening institutional demand, particularly across AI themes.
Vanda
AI buying is back, but selectively
Vanda highlights early signs of renewed institutional appetite for AI after weeks of de-risking.
Hyperscaler call-put ratios surged above 2.0 this week, with roughly $720mn in net call buying. Broad
software is attracting fresh demand, while semis are stabilizing. AI software, however, continues to lag.
The shift has yet to register in one-month positioning indicators, suggesting rotation within AI rather
than a broad return of conviction.

Vanda
Retail is back
Retail investors have been aggressively buying the dip. JPM reports that October inflows into leveraged
equity ETFs have reached their highest monthly level on record, helping stabilize AI-related stocks. The
speculative crowd isn't running away from the correction. It's buying it.
JPM
Missing call buyers
Despite record leveraged ETF inflows, retail call buying has yet to stage a meaningful comeback
following the June/July deleveraging. JPM sees scope for this impulse to return into year-end. The
interesting setup: retail is already buying equities, but the options crowd hasn't fully reengaged.
Another potential source of upside demand.
JPM

The QQQ short squeeze setup
QQQ short interest has surged to its highest level since 2023, according to JPM. While some of these
shorts reflect hedging rather than outright bearish bets, the elevated short base adds another potential
source of buying pressure. Combine that with reduced institutional equity exposure, and the setup
becomes interesting. If tech continues to recover, short covering and investors rebuilding exposure
could add fuel to the rally.
JPM
The corporate bid survives
AI capex is exploding, but tech companies haven't abandoned share buybacks. Repurchases have
remained surprisingly resilient. Corporate demand remains an important cushion for equities,
although tighter financing conditions could eventually threaten that support.
JPM
The buyback bid
November is historically one of the strongest months for corporate repurchases. Goldman notes that
the month accounts for roughly 10.1% of annual buyback spending, second only to August. Meanwhile,
YTD repurchase authorizations have reached $1.34 trillion, up 14% year-on-year. Another potential
source of demand as equities head into year-end.
