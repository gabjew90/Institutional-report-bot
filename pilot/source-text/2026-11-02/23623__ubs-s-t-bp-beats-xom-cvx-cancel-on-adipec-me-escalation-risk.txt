Spec sales comment
Brent touched a 6 month high this morning, passing through $117/b, on news of fresh attacks on vessels in the
Strait of Hormuz and Saudi energy assets and the absence of progress from recent attempts to engage
diplomatically with Iran. US VP Vance’s letter to Iranian officials has yet to elicit a response, but Iran’s
messaging has been consistent and clear – they are willing to engage only when the US commits to lift the
blockade on Iranian oil and to unfreeze Iranian funds. The WSJ reported yesterday that President Trump
continues to take briefings on military options for Iran, and has signalled that attacks may restart after the US
midterms next Tuesday. In another sign of growing concern about the security situation in the Middle East,
senior executives at Exxon and Chevron have pulled out of the ADIPEC conference due to take place in Abu
Dhabi next week. I continue to believe that midterms are not likely to be a catalyst for renewed ME diplomacy
by the US, whatever the outcome. While spot prices and the front end of the forward curve seem to be pricing
in a higher probability of escalation and disruption to energy production and exports, I continue to believe the
market is not realistically pricing in the medium or longer-term impact of the ME conflict, and that prices could
spike higher unless there is more substantive evidence of diplomatic progress than has thus far been the case.
BP shares are outperforming this morning on a solid 3Q beat (10% ahead of consensus at operating level,
15% ahead on adjusted NI and 11% on net CF ex WC), which was delivered despite the sharp move up in
consensus expectations following the trading update earlier this month. With the results release, new CEO
Meg O’Neil articulated 4 strategic priorities, including the goal of using excess cash to accelerate the settling of
remaining Macondo liabilities (~$5bn). The one thing that BP did not deliver this morning was a surprise on

2
cash returns to shareholders, and while this was absolutely not expected (with the December 15th CMD
coming up) it is still a minor near-term negative after what we’ve seen elsewhere in the sector over 3Qs.
Specialists still see BP as the integrated energy name which has delivered the most positive fundamental
change this year, and while I expect positioning and performance to improve relative to Shell and TTE ahead
of the coming CMD, generalist investors who rotated out of BP following the departure of previous Chair Albert

financial framework, but while specialists are mostly on board it may take some time before the message filters
through to the wider market. Take a look at the 3Q note from Josh and let me know if you’d like to catch up
with the team on BP or 3Q results for the sector as a whole.
