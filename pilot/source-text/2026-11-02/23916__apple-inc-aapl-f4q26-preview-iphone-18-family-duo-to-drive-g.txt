F4Q26 earnings preview 
 
 
We forecast AAPL F4Q26 EPS of $2.01 (v. $2.03 prior), above FactSet consensus of 
$1.99, with revenue $113.6 bn (v. consensus $113.4 bn). Revenue of $113.6 bn 
represents +11% yoy growth, at the high-end of AAPL’s guidance for revenue to grow 
between 9-11% year-over-year. First, we forecast iPhone revenue of $56.6 bn (+15% 
yoy), in-line with consensus of $56.6 bn (+15% yoy), reflecting (a) -4% yoy unit decline, 
in-line with IDC estimates; and (b) ASP growth of +20% yoy on premium mix shift and 
price increases. Second, we forecast Services revenue to grow +10% yoy, in-line with 
consensus +10% yoy and the company guidance for F4Q26 Services growth of 9.5% yoy 
(similar to June quarter growth of +12% with a 250bps incremental qoq forex 
headwind). Services growth should be driven by momentum across iCloud+, AppleCare+, 
and other subscriptions, partially offset by slower App Store revenue growth. Third, we 
forecast gross margins of 47.8% (v. consensus 47.5%), near the midpoint of AAPL’s 
guidance for 47-48% gross margins (46-47% ex-tariff refund).  
 
For the December F1Q27E quarter, we forecast EPS of $2.88 with revenue of $151.2 bn 
(v. FactSet consensus estimates for EPS of $2.92 and revenue of $152.2 bn), gross 
margins of 46.8% (v. consensus 47.1%), and opex of $20.3 bn (v. consensus $20.6 bn). 
We expect AAPL to guide to F1Q27E revenue growth of LSD%-MSD% yoy (v. GSe +5% 
yoy). We are encouraged by resilient premium iPhone demand despite the broader 
smartphone market downturn, easier yoy comparisons for Mac, and recent price 
increases across the iPhone, Mac and iPad portfolios, with AAPL expected to realize the 
first full-quarter benefit from iPhone price increases in F1Q27E. We also expect 
continued momentum across Services, supported by recent price increases for Apple 
Music and Apple TV+, alongside continued strength in iCloud+ and AppleCare+.

iPhone 18 Pro lead times modestly softer; Pro Max trends remain resilient 
 
 
We believe iPhone 18 Pro series unit demand is potentially tracking modestly below 
the iPhone 17 Pro series based on lead-time trends (not surprising given price 
increases), although iPhone 18 Pro Max trends and other early demand indicators 
remain encouraging. Specifically, our daily Apple lead-time tracker suggests that global 
iPhone 18 Pro-series lead times are modestly shorter versus the comparable iPhone 17 
Pro launch period, and are normalizing more quickly despite an industry backdrop 
marked by component constraints. That said, iPhone 18 Pro Max lead times have held up 
relatively better, while early sell-through data points to continued resilience in premium 
iPhone demand. We also believe potential demand deferral ahead of the iPhone Duo 
launch could be contributing to relatively softer lead times. While Duo wait times began 
at ~100 days on launch-day pre-orders, early lead-time data is often inflated by 
launch-week hype and should normalize over time.  
First, our daily Apple lead-time tracker suggests global iPhone 18 Pro series lead 
times are modestly below those of the iPhone 17 Pro series. (Exhibit 2). During the 
first 42 days after orders opened, average global iPhone 18 Pro series lead times, 
weighted by regional and Pro/Pro Max model mix, measured 24 days vs. 25 days for the 
iPhone 17 Pro series over the comparable period. More specific trends by model and 
region include: 
Global lead times for the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max models are slightly lower than 
n
the prior iPhone 17 and 16 Pro models (Exhibit 2, Exhibit 3). The lead times are 
similar across the four colors. Moreover, lead times have normalized more quickly, 
with iPhone 18 Pro lead times declining from 22 days to 13 days after ~20 days of 
availability and Pro Max lead times declining from 30 days to 24 days after ~27 days, 
compared with ~39 days for the iPhone 17 Pro Max. Additionally, the iPhone Duo 
global lead time averaged ~100 days on the first week of pre-order. 
By region, iPhone 18 Pro series lead times across the US, UK, India and Japan have

the prior-generation Pro series (Exhibit 5, Exhibit 6, Exhibit 7, Exhibit 8, Exhibit 9,

demand from Mainland China given a wider price differential this year: the iPhone 18 
Pro 256GB is ~10% cheaper in Hong Kong, while the gap on the iPhone 18 Pro Max 
2TB widens to ~12.5%. Conversely, Mainland China has seen shorter lead times, 
which we believe could partly reflect demand deferral ahead of the iPhone Duo, 
given Mainland Chinese consumers’ greater familiarity with foldable smartphones. 
Additionally, the iPhone Duo lead time is consistent across regions. 
Second, early demand indicators remain encouraging. First, iPhone 18 Pro Max lead

additional support to premium iPhone demand as Apple moves through the holiday

elevated smartphone pricing weighing on broader end-market demand, partially offset

resilience of premium demand. We expect ~20% yoy iPhone ASP growth, supported by 
(a) demand being concentrated on the premium iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max models 
given the absence of the base iPhone 18, (b) $100 price increases on the iPhone 18 Pro 
and Pro Max, and (c) $100 price increases across Apple’s existing US iPhone portfolio,

#1: App Store: Growth moderates amid evolving commission structures 
 
 
We estimate F4Q26 Apple App Store revenue of $7.48 bn (+2% yoy), decelerating from +3% yoy in 
F3Q26. Per Sensor Tower, C3Q26 App Store spending growth slowed to +2% yoy (v. +3% in C2Q26), 
reflecting weaker trends in July (+1%) and August (flat), before improving to +5% in September. By 
category, spending trends moderated across Games (~40% of App Store revenue) and Entertainment 
(~15%), while Photo & Video (~9%) saw improving growth through the quarter. Geographic trends were 
mixed, with the US (~33% of revenue) declining 7% yoy (v. -6% in C2Q26) and China (~20%) slowing to +8% 
(v. +10%), while Japan (~10%) improved modestly to -2% (v. -3%). Beyond softer underlying spending 
trends, we believe App Store growth potentially faced incremental pressure from greater availability of 
off-app payment options in the US and commission changes in China, Japan and Brazil, with the 
China/Japan changes previously estimated to represent an aggregate ~5pp headwind to App Store revenue 
growth. Longer term, while broader adoption of off-app payment options remains a risk, we continue to 
believe the convenience and seamless user experience of in-app purchases should support the App Store’s 
contribution to overall Services growth. Key potential catalysts for a reacceleration include (1) continued 
growth in third-party AI app monetization, supported by the recent launch of Muse AI, and (2) greater 
monetization of alternative marketplace transactions for iOS apps.

Thesis summary, rating, price target, key risks 
 
 
We are Buy rated on Apple with a new 12-month target price of $390 (v. $360 prior) 
reflecting 40X (v. 37X prior) our NTM+1Y EPS. The increase in our target multiple reflects 
our increased confidence in the iPhone replacement cycle following the launch of the 
iPhone Duo.  
Key risks include: 
Weakening consumer demand for products and services. Apple’s products and 
services are typically sold to consumers and any weakness in the macroeconomic 
environment could reduce demand for Apple products and services. Apple generated 
~50% of its revenue from iPhones (F2025), which is highly dependent on purchases 
driven by upgrades. Lengthening replacement cycles due to macroeconomic headwinds, 
improved product durability, or lackluster product innovation could all negatively impact 
upgrade demand.  
Supply chain disruption. Although Apple’s suppliers have a global footprint, the 
majority of final assembly occurs in China. Increased geopolitical tension may result in 
disruptions to global trade including through tariffs. While Apple has a robust supply 
chain network, it may rely on one or a few key suppliers for unique or 
hard-to-manufacture at scale parts.  
Intensifying competition. Apple competes across personal devices (e.g., handsets, 
tablets, PCs, headphones) and a variety of services (e.g., video streaming, app 
distribution, advertising, music streaming, online fitness, cloud storage, product 
warranties). Although Apple is the largest company and the most well resourced among 
its competitors, it is not the market leader in every single business line. For instance, in 
video streaming, it faces several key competitors that invest more heavily than Apple in

Regulatory risks. Apple is subject to intense regulatory scrutiny in all the major markets 
that it operates in. Regulatory intervention could result in weakening Apple’s 
competitive advantages if it is forced to make its proprietary products or services 
available for competitors to use.  
Capital allocation execution. Apple has a history of M&A, which has no guarantee of

ROI or come under deeper regulatory scrutiny.  
Thesis summary 
Apple designs, manufactures, and markets personal technology devices and sells a 
variety of related services. Its long history and track record of (1) designing 
category-defining and innovative products (e.g., Mac, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, 
AirPods, iPod); (2) protecting digital privacy; and (3) delivering premium services & 
experiences have contributed to an unmatched brand strength. Apple’s brand loyalty 
has resulted in a growing installed base of users that provide Apple with visibility into 
revenue growth by reducing customer churn, lowering customer acquisition costs for 
new product and services launches (e.g., Apple TV+, Apple Fitness, Apple Watch), and 
encouraging repeat purchases, such as phone upgrades. 
We are Buy-rated on AAPL as we believe that the market’s focus on slower product 
revenue growth masks the strength of the Apple ecosystem and associated revenue 
durability & visibility. Apple’s installed base growth, secular growth in services, and new 
product innovation should more than offset cyclical headwinds to product revenue, such 
as a reduced iPhone unit demand due to a lengthening replacement cycle and reduced 
consumer demand for the PC & tablet category. Valuation is attractive relative to AAPL’s 
historical multiple - both on an absolute & relative basis - and compared to key tech 
peers. The majority of gross profit growth over the next 5-years should be driven by 
Services, which should mark an inflection point in the Services investment narrative and 
support AAPL’s premium multiple. The durability of Apple’s installed base and the 
resulting revenue growth visibility from attaching more Services and Products is what 
underpins the recurring revenue - or Apple-as-a-Service - opportunity.  
Key risks to our view include weakening consumer demand, supply chain disruption, 
intensifying competition, regulatory risks, and capital allocation execution.  
Our F26/27/28 EPS estimates are largely unchanged on average.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
