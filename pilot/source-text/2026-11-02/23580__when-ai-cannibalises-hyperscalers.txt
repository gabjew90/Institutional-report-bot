• When AI cannibalises hyperscalers. Is the Wile E. Coyote moment getting closer? AI capex keeps sprinting ahead while the revenue 
road threatens to disappear beneath it. It appeared first as a US AI lab-related concern: steep token price falls and AI spending 
peaking since August. Anthropic’s pre-IPO S1 did little to dissipate revenue slowdown concerns. This week’s 3Q earnings added a more 
uncomfortable twist: AI can not only hurt software companies but it can also cannibalise hyperscalers’ core advertising business - 
the main cash engine funding the AI build-out. The first signal came from Alphabet’s Google’s 3Q core advertising franchise (revenue 
growth fell from +20% y/y to +12%, $63.3bn). Amazon’s advertising business also fell short of expectations, +11% y/y (c$20bn), 
its lowest growth rate since it first broke out figures in 2020. They deny any connection, but Amazon’s decision to block Muse (and 
OpenAI’s Dots) adds another layer. AI agents making purchases (and taking decisions) on behalf of users does not just threaten 
advertising economics. They can disintermediate the customer relationship/ownership itself. Muse hit US financials first - customer 
inertia underpinning low-cost deposits was put under review. It appears it could also hit hyperscalers. And yet, capex keeps on 
sprinting.
• Another DeepSeek moment. Back then, on 27 January 2025, NVIDIA fell c17%, wiping out approximately $593bn of market value, the 
largest one-day market cap loss in Wall Street history. Yesterday, Alphabet marked the second worst, -13% (-c$500bn). Intel is adding 
to tech anxiety today, down c30% today and c50% from its June peak. And speaking of AI labs, seeing Open AI investor Softbank down 
c50% since peaking in June brings little comfort. Hyperscalers will find new ways to monetize AI traffic, no doubt. And lower token 
prices will likely carry the technology deeper into personal and working life. But market anxiety is rising as the cash burn accelerates. 
The swift widening of spreads already raised RoI questions, well before 3Q earnings landed. But there is some hope. While stronger 
credits remain outsiders (Microsoft’s jumbo deal yesterday was a good example), weaker credits are starting to take note of credit 
market signals: Oracle’s CMD.
 

 
• Credit investors: the only adults in the room. Hyperscalers are starting to take note of those painful bond and CDS signals, particularly 
as credit concerns begin to spill over and dent share-price performance. And not only did Meta surprise the market by turning to equity. 
This week, Oracle also delivered a distinctly bond-friendly investor day: plans to return to positive FCF in 2H28, bring debt down to 
3.5x EBITDA in 2029 and cap next year’s gross debt issuance at $10bn, alongside another $10bn of equity. That follows the 2025 
$25bn bond offering and $20bn equity raise earlier this year.
• The haves: MSFT “safer than USTs” – Mariya Entina, DoubleLine. With a $115bn book, and a b/c peaking at 4x, demand for quality tech 
paper appears to remain strong. True, Microsoft had not tapped the US IG market since 2017 ($17bn) and, along with J&J, it remains 
the only Aaa/AAA-rated US corporate in town, sitting one notch above S&P’s US sovereign rating. Pricing was tightened aggressively 
and attrition was significant, but final demand remained robust at c2.5-3x (see below).

• US rates and credit rally. And the primary market offers juicy NIPs: flows return to credit markets this week. And to less AI-
exposed €HY. Along with juicy NIPs (see LH below), credit flows have improved this week. Not for €IG funds, which have endured four 
weeks of outflows (albeit at a slower pace this week). Meanwhile, USD funds have seen their largest inflow since early July, fueled by 
strong yields on US credit. Although €IG funds have recorded their fourth straight week of outflows, this week’s outflow of $178mn is 
considerably smaller than last week’s -$929mn, and has also been offset by a $506mn inflow into €HY funds. €IG outflows this week 
have once again been concentrated in the 1–4Y bucket (-$193mn), bringing the October balance for this bucket to -$1.5bn. In stark 
contrast, US funds have recorded a significant inflow of $4.6bn (+$2.8bn in $IG and +$1.8bn in $HY), in line with returns of +0.7% for 
$IG credit and +0.8% for $HY. Lastly, equity funds have mirrored the poor relative performance by European credit, with outflows from 
European equities (-$930mn, making it three straight weeks of outflows) compared with inflows into US equities (+$12bn, making 
it three weeks of inflows).
