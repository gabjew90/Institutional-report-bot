Q3 GDP underscores Warsh's go-slow approach, we will 
see in Dec how serious Warsh really is. 
Doing one’s sums, the net drivers of Q3 GDP growth were discretionary spending – by 
consumers and the Federal government. The upside to consumption for the things we want 
to buy looks increasingly limited by the higher cost of the things we must buy. As far as 
government is concerned, there are no upside limits. A “blue wave”, if one occurs, would 
choose to swap defense allocations into transfers, not slow the overall pace of spending. 
As for the AI revolution, the surge in equipment spending is being offset by imports and 
construction spending has yet to be impactful for overall GDP – as I wrote earlier this week 
(“AI is everywhere but in the business cycle”). Q3 real GDP increased by $200bn in Q3, the 
combination of inventories, imports of nonpetroleum goods, and business spending on 
equipment and intellectual property fell by $5bn in Q3. Discretionary real consumption 
increased by $86bn and defense spending increased by $25bn (54% of the increase in real 
GDP, and an average of 58% in 2026YTD).  
  
This allows Warsh to envision the current upward trajectory for the funds rate as a mini 
version of 2022-23 – getting policy rates to where inflation looks likely to settle as tariffs roll 
off and the war with Iran ends, rather than where it is right now. Add to this sanguine view 
on inflation the supply side argument from AI that perhaps includes income destruction 
amongst the higher-educated higher-skilled workers -- those that drive spending from the 
top branch of the “K”. A funds rate set to the current pace of inflation would be 100BP 
higher, assuming a neutral rate of 1%. Warsh choses a different trajectory, the same one 
chosen by those who put him in this seat -- another hike and then rate cutting in 2027. As 
he told us yesterday, demand destruction from monetary policy is from a policy mistake 
not a policy option, and higher rates are already slowing the flow of capital to AI borrowers 
(not really, but okay, it is his microphone). 
  
The trajectory to a higher rate profile is, instead, likely to be chosen for him. Nominal 
growth in final sales of domestic product is averaging around 6% with non-financial CP 
A2/P2 at 4.3% and the 10Y UST at 5%. The pace of bank lending (12W %Ch of a 4W moving 
average) has been reaccelerating since mid-July and is increasing at a 7.4% SAAR as of 
mid-October. Financial conditions are far from tight and there is money to be lent. That the 

AI sector is going through a revaluation of its equity prices and borrowing spreads is hardly 
doom and destruction, merely markets functioning. Commodity prices staying higher for 
longer will increasingly translate into higher goods prices (excluding food and energy) and 
then, with a lag, service prices. Then there is the unrelenting growth in the Federal deficit 
mitigating the impact of tightening monetary policy (Inverting Policy is Warsh’s Challenge). 
Hard to see anything less than a 100BP increase in the funds rate over the next 12 months, 
or sooner if Warsh is as serious as he says. 
  
There are always outs to this outlook, too numerous for this quick letter, but to my thinking, 
the creation of higher inflation is like making soup. Toss in the ingredients and add heat. 
How the different bits interact, at what temperature, is beside the point. In the end, its 
soup.  
  
For the December meeting, market probability of a 50BP hike is zero. The market is 
effectively begging you to take the other side, I think it’s a reasonable bet. To my view, and 
the view of TSL, Warsh will find himself hiking a lot more than he expects and, in turn, more 
than what the market expects. For the moment, there is some relief in markets as they 
think about pressured consumers. In response, the10Y is rallying, probably to a 5.70%-
5.75% yield. If the run of data in the coming weeks show sustained inflation, employment, 
and resumed spending (Sep drop offset the Aug surge, it is monthly data), we will see in 
Dec just how serious Warsh is about his “street cred”. 
  
All the best,
