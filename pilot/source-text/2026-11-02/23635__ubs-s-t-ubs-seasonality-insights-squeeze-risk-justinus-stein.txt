UBS Seasonality Insights: Squeeze Risk
Justinus Steinhorst
30-Oct-2026, 06:12 ET (EDT)
UBS Seasonality Insights explores recurring yearly, monthly, weekly, and daily patterns in financial markets.
Beyond equities, we delve into seasonal patterns for a variety of indices and macro assets from currencies to
commodities. Just before the start of each month, we update and distribute both the attached chartpack and
the highlights email below.
• 5 Seasonality Highlights for November -
1) Mind the Year-End Squeeze Risk
• For the S&P 500, it doesn’t get better than November. The index has gained 1.9% on average since 1950,
the best month of the year for equities (Chart 1). Over the last 10 years, there has been only one instance of
a negative November (2021). Halloween marks the beginning of the strong six-month “Buy in November”
stretch, the counterpart to the famous “Sell in May” adage (Chart 2).
• The positive seasonal collides with an acute short squeeze risk. Net exposure remains very low whilst
gross is at multi-year highs as shorts have risen sharply. Below we outline four (seasonal) catalysts that could
drive the squeeze and trigger a broader year-end performance chase.

2) Midterms See Uncertainty Subside
• A Blue Sweep has become the base case, but the magnitude of Democratic gains as well as the outcome of
key Senate and gubernatorial races (e.g. Texas) remain important unknowns into next week. Unwinding
uncertainty out of election day has historically seen strong forward equity returns at impressive hit rates
across different time horizons (Charts 3-4). Notably, at six months post election day, the S&P has been higher
for every single midterm since 1950.

3) Oil Has Its Worst Month
• Falling oil prices could provide another tailwind: November marks the weakest month for the commodity,
down 2.9% on average (Chart 5). Metals, on the other hand, see notable strength. Copper has its best month
of the year (+1.7%). Near-term tails around Iran remain fat with scope for both significant escalation and
de-escalation. Should oil decline, however, swapping Energy equity exposure into Metals & Mining
{UBXXMETL} looks compelling.

4) Volatility Declines for Equities and Bonds
• The VIX Index sees its biggest decline of the year in November (Chart 6). More importantly, given recently
elevated rates volatility, the MOVE Index sees its second weakest month of the year (Chart 7). A further
decline should be a big tailwind for equities.

5) Yields See Downward Pressure
• Another standout: November is the weakest month for US 10y yields (Chart 8). The ~30bps retracement to
~5% has already buoyed pockets like Housing {UBXXHOME} and Bond Proxies {UBXXBOND}. Why yields
are falling matters though: lower yields on energy disinflation are bullish for the broad index; lower yields on
weaker AI growth assumptions are not.
