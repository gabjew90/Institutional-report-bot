Market Commentary - the power of 2 
 
 
October was a challenging market for many investors as a host of stock ideas that were 
working stopped working, leaving room for others to fill the void.  As we exit October, 
the broad market S&P 500 is down 0.9% for the month while the Tech-laden NASDAQ 
Composite Index is down almost 7% — illustrating the bifurcation taking place between 
these two indices.  And within the S&P 500, we see a similar bifurcation, with the S&P 
500 equal weighted index actually trading UP around 5% for October as investors have 
rotated out of Mega-cap Tech and into a slew of ‘old economy’ HALO stocks among 
others. 
Notably in October the AI trade has taken a pause as investors question the durability of 
the pace of infrastructure investment amidst uncertainties around the public credit 
markets needed to fund so many projects (see charts of the month below).  CRWV in 
particular became a focal point of concern, but Gabriela Borges upgraded CRWV to Buy 
on Oct-19 in “CRWV: Upgrade to Buy: NTM datapoints on contract economics are likely 
to be more positive than the market expects” (see also “CoreWeave: How big is the 
funding gap?”). 
And in addition to the funding concerns, the investor focus for the hyperscalers — MSFT, 
AMZN (on the CL), and GOOGL all reported results last week — is showing signs of 
shifting from how much these companies will spend on AI infrastructure development 
(i.e., data center construction) to how much these companies will earn on all the 
investment they have planned and our planning.  Shares of both GOOGL and AMZN sold 
off post results following marginal misses from their traditional businesses — search for 
GOOGL and eCommerce for AMZN.  Both of these businesses —- search and 
eCommerce — may be susceptible to AI disintermediation from Agentic AI alternatives.  
But Eric Sheridan cautions that the Agentic AI era is still very much in its infancy as he 
discusses in our two postview notes: “GOOGL Q3’26 Review” and “AMZN Q3’26 Review.” 
And any shortfall in cloud revenue continues to be tied to supply chain bottlenecks, not 
demand falling short. 
Finally, with 2 months to go in 2026, we can’t help but think of all the things that are 
coming in pairs as we head towards year-end, including: 
HALO vs AI. At the core of the rotation trade is a trade that has popped up from 
n
time to time throughout much of the AI infrastructure build-out: the HALO trade 
(heavy assets, low obsolescence). Guillaume Jaisson and Peter Oppenheimer first 
referenced this trade back in February with the global strategy note, “The HALO 
effect: Heavy Assets, Low Obsolescence in the AI era.” The idea is that HALO 
companies — manufacturers, bricks & mortar, etc. — are more insulated from AI 
disintermediation, providing a safer alternative to an AI trade that may seem 
crowded and susceptible to a negative inflection in fundamentals from time to time.  
The issue with the sustainability of the HALO trade, however, is that the AI 
technology era is likely to be real (at least in some form).  So shifting away from 
stocks that have the structural growth winds at their back into a group of cyclical 
stocks catering to an ‘old economy’ can often feel wrong way.

n
course, but they are selecting from 2 candidates in most elections and the aggregate

Conviction List Explained - refrain 
 
 
On June 1, 2023 we introduced this new investment list which highlights a selection of 
fundamental Buy-rated US stocks across the Goldman Sachs Americas Global 
Investment Research department. These ideas are sourced from our US research 
analysts, but chosen by members of our Investment Review Committee. This new 
‘Conviction List - Directors’ Cut’ is designed to provide investors with a curated and 
active list of 20-25 of what we believe to be our most differentiated fundamental Buy 
ideas across our US stock coverage. 
The subcommittee, which includes the Americas Director of Research and the other 
senior research managers listed as authors of this report, will collaborate with each 
sector analyst to identify top ideas that offer a combination of conviction, a 
differentiated view and high risk-adjusted returns. The subcommittee will then choose 
what they view as the top 20-25 ideas across the department for the list. We intend to 
refresh and publish the list monthly to allow the list to become part of the investment 
process of Portfolio Managers. 
Importantly, we do not express thematic or factor views with this list. Rather, this list 
focuses on bottom-up fundamental analysis from our analysts. While our analysts will 
incorporate their view of macro impacts on the fundamentals of their stocks, we will not 
intentionally express factor, rates, inflation or other macro views in the construction of 
this list. Our analysts appropriately incorporate the impact of these factors in their 
estimates which are a critical component to our selection process. 
Finally, inclusion on this list is not a stock rating and addition to or removal from this list 
does not necessarily represent a change in the analyst’s investment rating for such stock.

Critical data center component provider with proven track record of through-the-cycle growth 
APH is a best-in-class supplier of connectors and electronic components which are sold 
into datacenters (~45-50% of revenue), as well as industrial (high teens % of revenue), 
autos (10%), Aerospace & Defense (11%), and mobile devices (5%), including the iPhone. 
In addition to its existing product lines, APH is an M&A compounder with strong FCF 
conversion, enabling it to supplement organic growth with acquired growth and achieve 
reliable above-industry expansion rates.  Concerns around APH’s dominance in copper 
applications when fiber optic cables are poised to grow faster long-term in data centers 
are likely overdone, as Amphenol has both product sets and because copper has several 
alternative use cases. 
The right product, at the right time, right now. APH employs an entrepreneurially 
n
driven general management team structure to engage with customers across a full 
set of solutions and technology needs — a structure tailor made for the rapidly 
evolving needs of datacenter customers. At the same time, the company’s diverse 
end market exposure provides it with long- and short-cycle opportunities that 
should translate into a more predictable revenue stream. The company’s nimble 
operating structure is also allowing it to better navigate the supply chain constraints 
that have plagued many companies in the broader AI ecosystem. 
Not just a copper company. With the acquisition of CommScope in January, APH 
n
significantly increased its fiber optic footprint, setting the company up to mirror the 
technological changes that its customers are undertaking.  APH raised its fiber optic 
revenue targets post 2Q results and again post 3Q on Oct-28 highlighting the 
traction that the company continues to make in this critical higher-tech offering. 
Overall, the combination of copper and fiber momentum positions the IT Datacom 
segment to grow over 60% again in 2027.   
Investing for growth. The company continues to invest for growth, both through 
n
opex and capex. Capital efficiency is a key part of APH’s business model. Look for a 
~30% increase in revenue in 2027 on the back of only a ~5-10% increase in capex. 
For the CommScope business in particular, management has noted that Amphenol’s 
strong balance sheet allows it to support higher levels of investment in the business 
(e.g., new product development, capex) compared to CommScope’s previous owner 
(which operated with very high financial leverage). APH’s experience in past difficult 
cyclical periods as well as its nimble operating structure should also allow the 
company to adjust its operations and investment levels if datacenter headwinds 
become too stiff. 
Where we are different. 
Delaney’s $3.90 2027 EPS estimate is the Street high and sits 9% above the consensus 
mean, reflecting a more positive view on the company’s broader AI opportunity 
stemming from copper and fiber related products, along with solid growth in markets 
including A&D and industrial. Additionally, EBIT are at a record high today, but Delaney 
forecasts even further improvement in 2027 driven by price-cost and operating 
leverage.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
