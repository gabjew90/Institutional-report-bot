Welcome to the first business day of November. It feels like only yesterday we were sweltering through the 
hottest days on record. Now the bobble hat is back for the next 4-5 months and, rather more importantly and 
depressingly, my golf ball has stopped running merrily down the fairways. The week ahead is a big one in the 
US and with it for global markets, with tomorrow’s midterm elections and Friday’s October jobs report the 
obvious highlights, alongside the ISM surveys and Wednesday’s Treasury quarterly refunding announcement. 
Elsewhere, central-bank decisions are due in Australia tomorrow, Sweden on Wednesday, and the UK and 
Norway on Thursday, while China’s private PMIs, German activity data and Japanese wages will also be in 
focus. Earnings remain busy too, with Apple and Palantir today, AMD tomorrow, and ARM and a broader slate 
later in the week. 
Even all those events above may be trumped by the next move in tech after a tumultuous end to last week. 
Over the weekend our US equity strategists upgraded US tech from neutral to positive, reversing a well-
publicised move they made three weeks ago. They see the sector having underperformed the market by 
16% over those three weeks, with it being down -11% against the rest of the index being up +6%. It's now at 
the bottom of a channel versus the index, levels its bounced aggressively off 5 times in the last 2.5 years. See 
their piece here.   
The recent turmoil in tech was a fitting end to a tough October which Peter Sidorov has just reviewed in our 
monthly performance review. It was a month that investors grappled with mounting doubts around the AI 

trade, French sovereign stress, and higher energy prices. Brent crude (+13.6%) extended its rise amid 
concerns over renewed US-Iran escalation, while European natural gas rose above €100/MWh for the 
first time since 2022. Treasury yields reached post-2002 highs early in the month but ended up posting their 
best monthly return of 2026 (+2.3%), as sovereign bonds rebounded amid deteriorating tech sentiment and 
dovish-leaning decisions from the Fed, ECB and BoJ. Equities were mixed, with the STOXX 600 (+2.0%) and 
the equal-weighted S&P 500 (+5.0%) gaining, but the Nasdaq (-5.3%) suffering its worst month since 
March 2025 as concerns around AI capex intensified. European credit and European banks also struggled 
as OAT-Bund spreads neared their 2011 peak. See the full review of a complicated month here.    
Looking at more depth at the week ahead, tomorrow’s midterms will determine control of Congress for the 
final two years of President Trump’s term. Polling suggests Democrats are favoured to gain the three 
seats needed for a House majority, while the Senate remains a much closer contest and the outcome in 
either chamber could take several days to become clear if the margins are tight. Polymarket currently put 
the probability of the Democrats taking the Senate at 72.0%. Well renowned pollster Nate Silver currently 
suggests a 75% probability. 
From a near-term fiscal perspective, the lame-duck session after the election could prove at least as 
consequential as the composition of the new Congress according to our economists, with government 
funding expiring on December 11 and Republicans likely to seek a reconciliation bill that could include 
additional defence spending and potentially address the debt limit. Markets will also look to see what 
happens to the Iranian conflict in the days after midterms. The concern is clearly that energy prices become 
less of a pressing political issue once the vote is out the way, allowing for more aggressive action in and 
around Iran. For this morning though, Brent is -3.47% lower after Axios reported that Trump favours giving 
diplomacy longer to work, rather than a policy of imminent strikes. This was apparently relayed in a call to 
Israeli PM Netanyahu who was said to be concerned by this news. S&P 500 (+0.49%) and NASDAQ 100 
(+1.01%) futures have snapped back up this morning.   
Friday’s October employment report is the main US data release. Our economists expect headline payrolls 
to rise by +55k, up from +29k in September, with private payrolls also increasing by +55k after +46k. 
They expect the unemployment rate to remain at 4.2%, hours worked to stay at 34.4 and average hourly 
earnings growth to rebound to +0.3% MoM from +0.1%. On those forecasts, their payroll-based proxy for 
nominal compensation growth would ease a tenth to 4.1% YoY, which would still be consistent with solid 
consumer spending growth. 
There are several labour-market indicators ahead of Friday. Tomorrow’s September JOLTS report will provide 
the latest read on hiring, quits and layoffs, which have been relatively stable over the past year. On 
Wednesday, our economists expect ADP private employment growth of +75k, down from +90k 
previously. Given the correlation between ADP and nonfarm payroll growth, that release could have an 
important bearing on expectations heading into Friday. 
The business surveys will be another focus. Our economists expect today’s October manufacturing ISM 
to rise to 54.8 from 54.5, followed by an increase in the services ISM to 55.1 from 54.9 on Wednesday. 
Watch out for the prices paid component as well. These surveys should also offer a useful comparison with 
the much weaker picture from consumers: our economists expect Friday’s preliminary University of Michigan 
sentiment index to fall to 45.7 from 48.0 in October. Thursday also brings Q3 nonfarm productivity and unit 
labour costs, while the Fed’s Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey is due today. 

The data will be viewed against the backdrop of last week’s FOMC meeting, where the Fed left rates 
unchanged but Chair Warsh reiterated that further tightening is likely to be needed. Barring meaningful 
downside surprises this week, our economists think the labour-market data should reinforce expectations of 
another rate hike at the December 9 meeting. There is also plenty of Fedspeak this week, with New York 
Fed President Williams’ moderated discussion on Friday among the more notable appearances. On the 
fiscal side, Wednesday’s Treasury quarterly refunding announcement will be another important event for 
rates markets. See our strategists' preview here.   
Moving to Europe, the main events are central-bank decisions. The Riksbank announces on Wednesday, 
followed by the BoE and Norges Bank on Thursday. Market probabilities for a hike are 70.4%, 95.6% and 
4.4% respectively. See our UK economists' preview of the BoE's likely first hike since 2023 here. 
Germany has a run of September activity data, beginning with retail sales today, followed by factory orders on 
Thursday and industrial production on Friday. Inflation releases include Switzerland tomorrow, the Eurozone 
on Wednesday and Sweden on Thursday. 
In Asia, China’s October private services PMI on Wednesday follows manufacturing earlier today and the 
official ones over the weekend (more below). Japan’s September labour cash earnings are out on Thursday, 
while the BoJ publishes the minutes from its September meeting on Wednesday. In Australia, the RBA 
decision is tomorrow and our economists expect rates to remain on hold even though the market is 
pricing an 82% probability of a hike. See why they disagree with consensus here.   
Finally, earnings remain busy. Apple and Palantir report today, followed by AMD and Arista Networks 
tomorrow, and ARM, Qualcomm, McDonald’s, Novo Nordisk and Uber on Wednesday. Thursday’s slate 
includes Toyota, Sony, Deutsche Telekom, Rheinmetall and Nintendo, while Moderna and Mitsubishi Heavy 
Industries are among those reporting on Friday. 
Over the weekend, China’s official PMIs delivered a noticeably weaker-than-expected start to Q4. The 
manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3 in October from 50.1 in September, below the 50.0 consensus. The details 
were also soft, with the production index falling to 49.8 from 51.7 and new orders to 49.4 from 50.5, although 
high-tech manufacturing remained relatively resilient at 51.3. Meanwhile, the official non-manufacturing 
PMI slipped to 49.7 from 50.2, taking it below 50 as well, which was where the consensus was. 
Construction was particularly weak while services were flat at 50.0. So after September’s better numbers, 
the official October surveys suggest momentum weakened again at the start of Q4, with softer domestic and 
external demand still weighing on the broader economy. In slightly better news this morning’s private 
sector manufacturing PMI matched expectations at 52.0 in October, only slightly below September's 
52.1. 
Asian equity markets are snapping back this morning with the KOSPI (+2.77%) leading gains buoyed by 
index heavyweights SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. Elsewhere, Chinese stocks are also trading 
decently higher with the CSI 300 (+0.89%) and the Shanghai Composite (+0.97%) outperforming the Hang 
Seng (+0.72%). Meanwhile, the Nikkei (-0.01%) is back to flat with Kioxia down over -15% after a Q3 
forecast miss. Kioxia was the largest company in the index in late June trading at over 36,000. It's now 
back to just over 11,000 showing the recent dispersion in the AI trade. Elsewhere the S&P/ASX 200 (-0.57%) is 
lower  as the RBA starts its two-day policy gathering.   

Separately, South Korea’s manufacturing sector expanded at its slowest rate in eight months during 
October as output and demand weakened. The S&P Global PMI fell to 51.3 in October from 53.9 in 
September, marking the lowest reading since February. 
Recapping last week now and the biggest market moves came with the volatility in the AI trade, with a strong 
recovery early in the week giving way to a renewed sell-off that sharpened on Friday. This was exemplified by 
the swing in the Philly semiconductor index, which had been more than +8% higher on the week as of 
Wednesday, but ended up posting a fourth consecutive weekly decline (-0.84%) following a -7.20% 
slump on Friday. The Nasdaq saw an even larger weekly decline (-2.00%) after posting its worst day since 
June on Friday (-2.86%). And the Mag-7 fell -2.24% (-1.72% Friday), with Alphabet (-12.61%) and Amazon (-
3.08%) losing ground after underwhelming results. The S&P 500 also ended the week lower (-0.57%, -
1.82% Friday), though the equal-weighted version of the index still gained on the week (+0.80%, -0.79% 
Friday) amid a rotation trade. 
Outside the US, equities mostly held on to their weekly gains despite Friday’s losses. In Europe, the rebound 
of the Stoxx 600 (+1.73%, -0.36% Friday) was led by the DAX (+2.53%, -0.85% Friday) and the CAC 
(+2.40%, -0.01% Friday). In Asia, both the Kospi (+3.25%, -3.48% Friday) and Nikkei (+0.95%, -2.12% 
Friday) gained, though Chinese equities struggled with the CSI 300 down -2.92% (-1.39% Friday). 
European credit also outperformed, with IG (-4bps) and HY (-12bps) credit spreads tightening after the earlier 
sell-off. 
Sovereign bonds gained amid the risk-off tone and on-hold decisions by the Fed, ECB and BoJ. Treasuries 
outperformed, with both 10yr (-6.6bps, -0.9bps Friday) and 2yr (-9.4bps, -1.0bps Friday) yields falling to 
their lowest levels in seven weeks, at 4.93% and 4.59% respectively. In Europe, 10yr bunds (-2.2bps, -
3.2bps Friday) and gilts (-7.2bps, -1.0bps Friday) advanced, with OATs (-4.7bps, +1.6bps Friday) also 
gaining despite a renewed widening in OAT-Bund spread on Friday.   
Across other asset classes, oil prices saw a modest increase, with Brent crude (+0.79%, +2.40% Friday) 
ending the week at $117.64/bbl, within touching distance of its 2026 high, amid persisting concerns over 
possible US-Iran re-escalation after this week’s midterm elections. Meanwhile, gold (-3.04% on the week) 
and silver (-8.89%) fell to their lowest levels since last autumn. Finally, in the FX space, the Brazilian real (-
8.44%) and the Israeli shekel (-1.12%) were two of the worst performing currencies last week after their 
elections.
