Halloween horror
Tech has come under renewed pressure, with key technical levels breaking, hedge funds reducing
exposure and AI credit risk rising. Here are the charts that matter most.
The tech breakdown
The Nasdaq has broken below its trading range and slipped beneath the 200-day moving average for
the first time since the April panic. The last breakdown reversed abruptly. Bulls will be hoping for a
repeat.
LSEG Workspace
Semis lose their footing
The SOX looks even uglier. Semiconductors are breaking below range support and the 200-day moving
average for the first time since the bull market began in May last year. The former market leader is
now leading on the downside.
LSEG Workspace
Half a trillion dollars of Halloween horror
Alphabet's roughly $500 billion one-day market-cap wipeout ranks as the second-largest on record.
When the biggest companies in the world start losing hundreds of billions in a session, the market has
a problem bigger than a few disappointing earnings reports.

Dow Jones
The Magnificent Seven lose their magic
The Mag 7 are no longer doing all the heavy lifting. Equal-weight equities have started outperforming
as the former leaders stumble. With mutual fund year-end approaching, will managers keep defending
yesterday's winners or finally rotate into the rest of the market?
MS TIS
The Korea AI trade goes into reverse
The great Korea AI pile-in is unwinding. Goldman's desk reports heavy net selling this month, with gross
allocations falling from roughly 7% to 4.9% and net allocations from around 8% to 3.5%. The fast
money that chased the AI trade into Seoul is now heading for the exits.
Goldman

Buying the dip?
US tech was the most net-bought sector this week, but the bigger picture remains ugly. According to
Goldman, this week's hedge fund buying recovered only around 40% of the selling over the previous
three weeks. The Mag 7 were net sold in four of the past five sessions. Hardly a stampede back into
risk.
GS Prime
The Mag 7 exposure nobody escaped
Despite the selling, hedge fund sensitivity to the Magnificent Seven remains unusually elevated.
Nomura puts long-short beta to the group at roughly 0.32, the 97th percentile of its history. Managers
may be cutting positions, but their portfolios are still very much hostage to the same seven stocks.
Nomura
The only AI high
Costs to protect the debt of numerous US technology heavyweights against default hit fresh record
highs Friday.
ICE
When tech stocks and tech credit disagree

Another warning from beneath the AI hood: the relationship between tech equities and tech credit has
weakened sharply. Both markets have faced pressure, but they are increasingly telling different stories
about the same companies. That divergence deserves attention.
Anu Ganti - S&P Global
The disappearing buyback put
The AI spending boom comes with an opportunity cost. BofA estimates hyperscaler buybacks at just $7
billion year-to-date in 2026, compared with a peak near $46 billion in 2021–22. Cash that once
supported share prices is increasingly going toward GPUs and data centers. Nvidia gets paid.
Shareholders get fewer buybacks.
BofA
When tech becomes the entire market
Goldman's Privorotsky highlights just how dependent overall risk appetite has become on technology.
The latest correlation between its Risk Appetite Indicator and the Nasdaq 100 has spiked toward 0.93.
The market's mood ring is increasingly just the Nasdaq in disguise.
Goldman
The crowded trade gets uncrowded

Now for the other side of the story. US momentum positioning has collapsed to its lowest level since
early 2023, according to JPMorgan. The momentum unwind has already been substantial. The question
is how much more selling remains.
JPM PI
From crowded to neutral
Deutsche Bank's positioning indicators tell a similar story. Mega-cap growth and large-cap tech
exposure have fallen sharply, with positioning now around neutral and at approximately the 40th
percentile historically. This is no longer the universally crowded long it once was.
Deutsche Bank
