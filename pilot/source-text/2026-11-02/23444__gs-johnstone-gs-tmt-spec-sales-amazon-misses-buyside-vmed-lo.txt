GS TMT SPEC SALES – Friday 30th October …..Happy Halloween quite apt in this market
US Tech down overnight after Amazon missed heightened buyside expectations despite promising that
AWS growing 39% to $46bn in the Q (that is a massive number) and mgt saying it will continue to grow at
over 40% for the next 2 years. Intel guided Q4 below as a PC slowdown is expected through at least H1
2027, driven by memory price/availability and OEM inventory discipline results missed. Crypto also faced
disruption as Binance suspended withdrawals and pledged to cover losses after an attack affected an
estimated $6.3bn of funds, while Polymarket halted trading on its international exchange after a third-party
software provider was compromised.
 
What we have learnt from the hyperscalers is that AI demand is not the problem, capacity and the
cost of delivering it are: cloud growth accelerated (AWS reported $46.0bn, up 39%, Azure reported
$33.2bn, and Google Cloud reported $30.7bn, up 103%), backlogs remain substantial and management
teams continue to signal strong demand, but record capex, chip/power/data-centre constraints - we saw 2
hyperscalers flagged third-party AI chip/capacity delays that pushed some capacity into 1H27 and Q3 Capex
came in light even as full-year guides stayed elevated. FCF pressure remains. The key debate shifting from
whether demand exists to how efficiently hyperscalers can build capacity and ultimately generate returns on
the investment.
 
 BT up 5% as the  FT is reporting that Virgin Media O2 is finalizing a wholesale agreement to utilize BT's
Openreach network (not confirmed) for customer expansion outside its footprint while freezing its own cable-
to-fibre upgrades. This makes sense given its leverage and recent debate over if VMO2 will go into a Liability
Management arrangement, as the FT flags credit stress on VMO2's £22 billion debt pile, which triggered a
sharp bond sell-off. Things have not been helped by regulatory opposition signaling a block of the £2 billion
Nexfibre transaction eliminated a planned £1.1 billion cash injection, rendering full proprietary fibre rollout
economically unviable.

• Amazon -11% AH -the main issue was heightened expectations as buyside forecasts had risen into results,
and delivery broadly met or beat sellside expectations on key operating metrics but fell short of the higher buyside
bar. AWS grew 39.3% to $46.0bn, well ahead of sellside’s 37.7% but below buyside expectations of around
43%, while its 38.7% operating margin missed the roughly 40% investors wanted. Group operating income of
$28.1bn beat consensus of $26.2bn, but revenue and advertising missed (at $19.6B, vs. Street $20.9B) Pushing
back on Agentic shopping impact Amazon adds. Q4 guidance also disappointed: the top ends of $247bn revenue
and $33.5bn operating income were below buyside expectations of $247–248bn and $34–35bn, respectively, with
both midpoints below Street. Cash capex of $75.9bn pushed trailing-12-month free cash flow to −$42.4bn, that will
add to the funding debate. The key positive was Jassy’s projection of AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and
2028 (and on call saying see at $1trillion $ run rate business) , but that was not enough to offset the near-term
disappointment.  
interesting comments on Trainium and workloads - Amazon sees its vertically integrated AI stack as a
structural cost and margin advantage for AWS, with most Bedrock workloads already running on Trainium, which
offers attractive price-performance and economics, while owned or long-leased data centres avoid the ~35ppt
operating-margin disadvantage Jassy associates with neocloud infrastructure. Jassy believes Bedrock could
ultimately become as large as EC2 as customers adopt multiple AI models, with 6–8 models expected to be good
enough for >95% of workloads within 2–3 years. Lower inference costs should also unlock significant new AI
demand, with security emerging as an important use case and potentially accounting for ~20% of tokens within 1–
2 years as models identify and help patch vulnerabilities missed by humans.

TELCOS 
• US Telcos: The FCC is proposing to make it easier for ordinary smartphones to connect directly to satellites
outside mobile network coverage. Its 2024 Supplemental Coverage from Space (SCS) rules required satellite
operators to lease mobile spectrum across large, nationwide territories, forcing companies such as Starlink and
AST SpaceMobile to seek repeated waivers. Proposal FCC 26-71 would expand eligible spectrum from five to 13
bands, adding 482MHz, and let providers serve specific local areas or use spectrum licences they already own
rather than secure nationwide leases. It would also replace complex leasing arrangements with standard
agreements to coordinate with neighbouring spectrum users. Source:
https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-426297A1.pdf  
 

• Elisa: Following meetings with the CEO, CFO and Head of IR, GIR sees a slower Finnish service-revenue
recovery despite easing competition. Q4 contract repricing should help, but renewed mobile competition and
corporate pressure put even the low end of FY26’s 0–2% telecom growth guidance at risk, with modest downside
to FY26/27 consensus growth of 0.3%/1.7%. Finland remains structurally attractive, but stronger macro support is
unlikely before mid-2027, limiting confidence in a recovery before H2 2027 and underpinning GIR’s Neutral rating,
lowered from Buy in September. ISS efficiencies should support growth from 2027, although the margin benefit is
unquantified, with DC benefits from 2028. Deeper customer relationships should help mitigate agentic-AI
disruption, while AI offers cost savings. Elisa remains dividend-focused, with a conservative, self-imposed 1.5–
2.0x leverage target; fibre JVs could release capital, while towers are a lower priority given their strategic role in
autonomous networks  
 
• Telefónica: Rivals have urged the CNMC to reject its proposed five-year voluntary framework replacing cost-
based duct and pole regulation, warning of a cumulative 116% wholesale price increase and €1.50 more per
month for retail fibre customers (Cinco Dias). Telefónica’s infrastructure represents 99.2% of regulated wholesale
physical infrastructure and supports over 80% of alternative network deployments. Telefónica puts the monthly
impact at just €0.08–0.16 per line, arguing that rates remain 39% below 2011 levels and well below those in
markets such as France. The CNMC is reviewing submissions and expects to notify the European Commission
between late 2026 and January 2027 on whether cost-based regulation should remain.  
   
• UK: VMO2 CEO Lutz Schueler is calling for a full investigation into BT’s TalkTalk acquisition, warning that
bypassing normal competition scrutiny would undermine regulation and investor confidence (Telecom Paper). He
criticised the CMA’s two-week review deadline, the government’s lack of transparency after receiving its
confidential report, and proposed public-interest changes to the Enterprise Act that would facilitate the deal. He
also argued that enabling BT to buy a major rival and wholesale customer while the CMA challenges Nexfibre’s
Netomnia acquisition creates a regulatory double standard that could weaken alternative networks’ ability to
compete with Openreach.

• US TRADING: Tough sledding for broader tech following a generally underwhelming slate of overnight Tech
earnings + AMZN down ~10% AH (after -4% today) on weaker than expected AWS growth in qtr (39% vs cons >
40%) though mgmt is guiding in the press release to >40% AWS growth rates in 2027 and 2028. Elsewhere, clear
out-performance in S&P Equal Weight and cyclical/rate sensitive expressions of the market (also had benign PCE
today). Consumer caught a larger bid today which felt part ongoing rotation (out of Tech w/NDX -2.5) but also
driven by some covering in under owned names (NCLH, CROX, BOOT). There are also pockets of better
fundamentals like in restaurants this week including CMG +8% today and EAT, WING yesterday, and SBUX
tonight. Our floor was a 5 on a 1-10 scale in terms of overall activity levels with Asset Mgrs finishing slight net
sellers (supply in comm services, discretionary vs demand in macro) while HF flows were ~flat. We did not see a
material uptick in single stock activity despite the magnitude of the moves. Felt very top-down with SPX volumes
explosive at +35% vs 20d avg.
DERIVS: Another test for the AI trade today as disappointing Google earnings stifled budding optimism in
the market. The main theme of the day was the vol crush for most of the session as QQQ front month

fixed strike vols were offered down a vol at the lows, with SPY down more than 80 bps of vol. The
magnitude of the crush was observable in VIX futures moving nominally lower. This is reflective of vol
premium being taken out of the market post-several prominent tech prints. Unsurprisingly, skew was also
bid on the move lower - particularly in SPX gamma tenors. Earlier in the session, we saw a cust away
buy 15k SPX 31Mar 5500 puts as a crash hedge. In the singles/thematics space, we saw clients come in
to hedge single-name tech exposure and more sparsely add convexity in higher conviction positions. This
market continued to trade thematically with moderate SPX moves failing to fully capture the AI complex
today (GSTMTAIP -1.5%) being used as a source of funds for everything else (SPXXAI +1.5%). The S&P
implied move through tomorrow is 0.70%. (TY Gail Hafif)
