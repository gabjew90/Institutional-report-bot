Soggy tech, Russell revived
The market is starting to look very different beneath the surface. Nasdaq remains trapped in its trading
range, tech volatility continues to command a substantial premium, and CDS stress refuses to
disappear. Meanwhile, Russell has staged an impressive comeback, reclaiming its 200-day moving
average after a brutal washout. Extreme short positioning, flagged by GS near the lows, has helped
create the conditions for a powerful squeeze. The question now is whether this is simply another
positioning-driven rebound or the beginning of a more meaningful shift in market leadership.
Still stuck
NDX remains stuck in the same trading range that has been in place since May. The line in the sand
remains at the lower end of the range, around 29k in futures, with the 200-day moving average sitting
another 500 points lower. Until we break out of this range, in either direction, there is little reason to
get excited.

Russell revived
Russell puked below its 200-day moving average earlier this month but has managed to claw its way

Small cap vs NDX
Credit where credit is due. In our early October note on the logic behind small-cap outperformance, we
highlighted GS's take on extreme Russell positioning. Systematic exposure was down at the 7th
percentile, while leveraged funds were running record shorts in Russell 2000 futures. GS flagged the
potential for a powerful squeeze if yields reversed lower. Fast-forward to today, and that call looks
remarkably well timed.

The rotation trade
Tech remains stuck in a range, volatility premiums are elevated and credit stress has yet to normalize.
Meanwhile, Russell has reclaimed key technical support, helped by the unwind of extreme bearish
positioning. How much of that squeeze remains is unclear, but the relative performance shift is hard to
ignore. The interesting trade may not be about the direction of the overall market, but about who leads
from here. As long as tech struggles to regain momentum, small-cap relative outperformance
deserves attention.
