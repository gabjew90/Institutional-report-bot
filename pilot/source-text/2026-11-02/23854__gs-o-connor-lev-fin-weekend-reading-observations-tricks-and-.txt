As expected, the FOMC left the target range for the fed funds rate unchanged at 3.75-4.00% at its October
meeting. However, a selloff in AI stocks snowballed into Friday’s close, leaving the Nasdaq with its worst
session since June and its largest monthly loss in more than a year.
With 60% of S&P 500 companies now reporting earnings, EPS growth is on track to register 33% YoY
growth in Q3’26, matching Q2 as the strongest quarter in recent decades, outside of post-recession
recoveries. Yet, every member of the Magnificent Seven closed down on Friday and with the cohort
expected to report slower earnings growth for Q3 than the remaining members of the S&P 500, the
resiliency of the equity market remains in question – particularly coming into this week’s midterm
elections.
Credit, conversely, appeared to have a sleepily constructive week as returns snapped back into the black
amidst nominal moves in spreads (HY was only 3bps wider while loans were 1bp tighter) and a light
primary calendar ($4.44bn priced in HY across 4 deals and just 3 benchmark-sized loans launched on the
week). Yet, as much as the index-level stability painted a positive picture, volatility under the surface told
a different story as we enter a pivotal stretch for risk assets.
HY TRIPLE-Cs: STILL SPOOKY
Last Tuesday felt different. Following a broad risk-on rally and a newfound bid for some previously
eschewed lower-quality names, the Bloomberg US HY triple-C index’s OAS tightened 30bps to 1088bps
on Tuesday – the first 30bps-or-better daily tightening since February – before giving back 28bps by
Friday’s close: a rare, if brief, glimmer of hope for lower-quality issuers.
However, the constructive tone wasn’t universal. 25 HY index bonds still fell 5pts+ on the week: 12 of
which were rated triple-C or worse.
To put this figure into perspective:
• 1.2% of the triple-C-or-lower bucket falls 5+ points on a median week. Last week, it was 5.31%;
• The 5+ point metric has been above 5% for four weeks running, bringing the 6-week count of 5+ point
decliners to 147 – the second highest frequency over the last 18 months only to the May-June 2025 selloff;
• Unlike in May 2025 where 84 bonds fell 5+ points (when the whole market was dropping uniformly),
last week the median HY bond rose 0.28pts and the rest of the triple-C-or-lower bucket (outside of blow-
ups) averaged a 1.06pt gain

So, while HY triple-Cs logged their first weekly gain in over two months (and their best performance since
April), picking the right names matters more in this market than overall triple-C beta.
LOANS: ALSO A BIT HAUNTED
US leveraged loans have been the proverbial best house on a challenged block all year. The floating-rate
asset class has outperformed its fixed-rate credit brethren, rising 3.04% YTD vs. returns of +0.60% and
-1.17% for HY and IG, respectively.
However, last week 53 leveraged loans moved more than 3pts, demonstrating a similar volatility to HY…
so could the tide finally be turning for the worse?
Consider:
• Loan funds posted their first weekly net outflow since the week ended June 24th;
• The current distressed cohort of loans is weaker than at earlier peaks, with 62% of the borrowers carrying
CCC+ ratings or lower;

• Single-Bs accounted for 24 of the 53 movers – more than the 22 rated CCC+ or lower – and fell nearly as
much on average (-1.01 vs. -1.04pts), showing that dispersion has not just been confined to the deepest
distressed tier in loans
Loans do have a few positive tailwinds, though:
• Only $2.04bn of new deals launched last week – the lowest total since the pre-Labor Day slowdown –
providing a positive boost in an already technically-friendly asset class; and
• When 20% or less of the market is trading at $100+ (currently: 7.4%), median returns have been higher
on both a 6-month and 12-month basis for the asset class, going back to 2007
TAKEAWAY
Last week's lower-quality rally looked convincing at the index level, but under the surface, selection is still
King. In HY, the share of triple-Cs falling 5+ points is running at more than four times its median-week
rate even as the median bond rallies. In loans, the issue is structural: 53% of distressed loans mature within
three years – the highest near-term wall of any recent distressed peak – and the exits are narrow: none of
2026's refis were for triple-C issuers, just 31% were B-minus, and only 4 of the 17 BSL-to-private-credit
takeouts were triple-C, against a distressed cohort that is 62% CCC+ or lower. Add software's 32% share
of distressed volumes versus a 12% index weight, and the cost of being wrong is as high as it has been in
recent memory – a phenomenon unlikely to reverse through the rest of earnings season and into year-end
