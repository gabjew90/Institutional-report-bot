Tl;dr: After yesterday's Warsh waffling and GOOGL's gouging (half a trillion in market cap 
eviscerated), today's reaction was best summarized by one veteran trader who called markets 
'dazed and confused'. Consensus long MSFT saved the day but overall NDX was ugly, Small 
Caps were squeezed higher as a metric-ton of mixed macro data and lower oil prices left bond 
yields lower and gave the rate-watching doves a boost (also helped early by lower oil prices 
which ended unch on late spike). Credit spreads widened. The dollar whipsawed to end unch, 
Gold tested down towards $4000 again as bitcoin ripped higher off $80k ahead of AMZN EPS. 
 

 
 
Bonds and oil traded together in general today until the last couple of hours while stocks were 
Alphabattered... 
 
Let's start on the most decoupled... 
Stocks & Credit 
Alphabet (and Fortinet, ServiceNow, and others) drove the bust today in stocks, obviously 
weighing down Tech/AI the most, ending three big days of outperformance (Broad AI -2%, Ex-AI 
+1%)... 
 

 
 
All the AI Leader cohorts were weaker today with NeoClouds taking it on the chin, down 5% 
after 3 strong recovery days... 
 
This was GOOGL's worst day ever (worse than Lehman's lashing and COVID's crash), pulling 
the stock back to its lowest since March 2026 lows and triggering a death cross... 
 

 
 
And while MSFT surged after hours last night with a strong beat, the major consensus long 
ended the day in the red (as a 'source of funds' according to Goldman's traders)... 
 
Overall, only Small Caps managed gains on the day with DOW also impacted by CAT's ugly day 
(CAT+GOOGL = -625pts). NDX ended down around 1.5% with the last hour seeing some 
buying pressure return into AMZN... 
 

 
 
As Value massively outperformed Growth on the day (after 3 days of relative pain)... 
 
With stocks down, credit market's optimistic week was ruined with IG spreads blowing back out 
towards recent wides(and Hyperscaler credit back in scary mode)... 
 

 
 
And data-center CDS widened... 
 
With META's Beignet SPV bonds blowing out today... 
 

 
 
However, UBS traders noted that the equity tape was healthier than the headline indices 
suggest, with capital rotating out of crowded AI and Growth exposure into Value, small caps, 
Consumer and other rate-sensitive cyclicals rather than leaving equities. 
Flows reportedly reinforced the rotation with limited outright shorting and more covering point to 
genuine sponsorship for under owned exposures. 
Goldman confirmed that activity had picked up (to a 5 out of 10) with an overall skew for sale: 
●​ LOs are -20% better for sale (-$1.5bn net sold). In TMT, they are better to buy in Info 
Tech vs selling Comm Services. Elsewhere, Buying Fins vs Selling HC.  
●​ HFs are evenly skewed on very small notionals. TMT flows are the exact opposite of 
LOs – buying Comm services vs Selling Info tech.  
Lower yields and weaker crude are accelerating, rather than driving, the broadening. Yields are 
3–6bp lower, helping housing, Consumer, Financials, Utilities and REITs, while Energy remains 
offered as crude retreats.  
Energy 
VP Vance's reported outreach to Iranian hardliners overnight pulled oil prices notably lower 
(briefly wiping everyone's memory about the potential for re-escalation after the Midterms). 
Additionally on the bearish crude side, a dozen European countries pledged this morning to 
jointly release 42.6 million barrels of oil by the end of January, in line with a G7 deal struck this 
month to ease global energy supply concerns. 
But then, around late morning, Iranian Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf repeated the 
same-old-same-old denial of progress(that Iran’s conditions have been repeatedly and clearly 
 

 
stated to the mediators; the carrot-and-stick approach has no effect on Iran; progress can only 
be achieved once Iran’s seven conditions have been agreed upon), and that pulled oil prices 
back higher. 
Additionally, Iran claimed at least three oil tankers struck naval mines in the strait over the past 
24 hours, with one vessel catching fire east of the strait and a dhow sinking near Oman's 
Khasab after a security incident. 
WTI's bounce was assisted by the technicals as the $100 Maginot Line stalled the selloff with oil 
ending only marginally lower (also bear in mind its Brent's contract roll which always makes the 
settlement messy)... 
 
Shell CEO Wael Sawan didn't help, saying today that elevated prices "no longer look like a 
temporary spike" given how rapidly global fuel inventories have been drawn down, 
characterizing the current environment as one of "structural stability in higher oil prices." 
However, despite his comments, diesel prices fell as Putin affirmed his commitment to ship fuel 
under a quota system ​to meet its new export commitment to the US... 
 

 
 
But, EU NatGas remains at its highest level since 2022... 
 
The bottom line is that the key $100 level is being sustained by structurally low inventories and 
persistent Hormuz shipping risk, while today's intraday softness reflects tentative diplomatic 
outreach between the US and Iran. The market is in a tug-of-war between geopolitical risk 
premium and any signals of de-escalation... but for today, lower oil/diesel meant helped sustain 
lower rates... 
Rates 
The aftermath of a confusing Fed meeting is a repricing in the rate outlook, with about 10bps 
shaved off from terminal 2027 pricing from the highs seen earlier this week. Still, the sell-side 
consensus continues to lean toward more hikes, including as soon as December, as economic 
 

 
data does little to justify the Fed's hold (hot PCE, strong growth, solid jobs data, but weak 
spending/low saving). 
The bulk of the repricing was in 2027, now pricing in less than 2 hikes next year... 
 
With Dec hike odds back at 80% and Jan hike odds falling to around 35%... 
 
That dovish shift in the short-end was helped by lower oil prices to pull the long-end lower too, 
but the whole curve was bid today (and notably bid into the bell even as oil kept pushing 
higher... 
 

 
 
...with 10Y yields battling around 5.00%... 
 
...as the curve continued yesterday's steepening... 
 

 
 
Will this all change after tonight's AMZN earnings? 
Everything Else 
The dollar ended unchanged after whipsawing up and down around the ECB decision to hold 
rates flat but pretend to be hawkish... 
 
Gold drifted lower all day, testing down towards $4000... 
 

 
 
Bitcoin trading was wild as it couldn't decide if it was a high-beta tech stock or an fiat 
alternative... 
 
Finally, as Bloomberg's Simon White noted earlier, hedge funds are increasingly offsetting their 
long books of AI stocks by shorting at the index level. Up until this year, funds were shorting 
mainly single names, but in the recent tech rout, it looks as if they are adding to index shorts, 
allowing them to maintain their core AI longs. 
 

 
 
This shift is changing the nature of the market, supplying a tailwind for correlation. With 
underlying risks rising as firms leverage up for AI capex, the VIX is more greatly exposed to 
shocks higher, and stock prices to deeper declines.
