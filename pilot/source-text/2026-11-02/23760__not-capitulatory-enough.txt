Not capitulatory
The momentum unwind has been brutal. Tech positioning has collapsed, and some momentum
exposures are back at multi-year lows. Surely, that qualifies as capitulation?
Not according to JPMorgan:
"We wouldn’t classify the de-risking we’ve seen as capitulatory per se and there are some lingering
risks, such as potential for Quants to de-gross and US Tech flows still elevated."
The charts below explain why.
Just neutral
From crowded to merely neutral. Tech positioning has collapsed, but at the 40th percentile, this is
hardly an outright short.
Deutsche Bank
Why not more serious?
The broader market tells a similar story. Goldman's sentiment indicator is still slightly positive. Plenty of
room before we get to genuine pessimism.
GIR
Far from lows
Momentum has been taken to the woodshed. Hedge fund leverage? Not so much. Global gross
leverage remains at the 89th percentile over three years.

JPM PI
Quants
And here is the multi-trillion-dollar elephant in the room. Quants have barely de-grossed, with gross
leverage still at the 98th percentile over one year and 95th over five years. That's a lot of potential
selling power.
JPM PI
Not negative enough yet
Thursday saw aggressive hedge fund selling. But zoom out to four-week flows and the picture looks
considerably less dramatic. One ugly day does not make a capitulation.
JPM PI
Overall tech flow: still crowded
Tech may have taken a beating, but the money hasn't exactly left the building. Combined 60-day flows
from hedge funds, retail and ETFs remain near peak levels. Still crowded.

JPM PI
Still no proper panic
The de-risking continues, but hardly at panic speed. JPMorgan's US Tactical Positioning Monitor has
slipped to the 33rd percentile. Lower, yes. Capitulation, no.
