MARKETING TRIP:  CLIENT COLOR – Andrew Tyler & Federico Manicardi 
• GENERAL SENTIMENT – The view from the APAC client base is notably 
more bearish than in the US but perhaps less so than in EU. APAC clients, on 
average, have outperformed their global peers and are still up double digits on 
the year so many lack incentive to re-engage markets until a clear trend 
emerges.  
• THOUGHTS ON MACRO – On the US, we started with the US Consumer 
which remains robust with less evidence for the K-shaped economy and little 
evidence for consumer cracks than is suggested by the media. First, we 
begin with the Consumer Cash Pile with the top 4 income quintiles having 6% 
- 27% more cash in their Checking + Savings + HY Savings / MMF accounts 
in inflation-adjusted terms relative to YE2019. In nominal terms this is nearly 
$8T. Second, spending remains robust with the Retail Sales monthly 
spending growth numbers almost double the growth rate witnesses pre-
COVID, e.g., 2014 = $420bn / month, 2019 = $505bn / month, YTD2026 = 
$723bn / month. Higher energy prices are not crowding out discretionary 
spending. JPM Equity Analyst Rick Shane maintains a weekly note using 
Chase spending data (here). We view credit card delinquency and charge=off 
data as proxies for consumer stress and we are seeing a normalization to pre-
COVID trends and not a deterioration from there. Recall, late-2021 and early-
2022 were the strongest period of consumer health in US history, proxied by 
average credit score and debt/income ratio. Third, the corporate sector 
remains healthy. SPX are near ATHs when excluding periods immediately 
following recessions with robust growth and record margins. If we use HY as a 
proxy for non-public business, which employ nearly half the US workforce, 
default rates at 2.7% vs. 3.2% long-term average; and this is after ~20% of HY 
market-cap has been upgraded to IG in the post-COVID era. 
o The client reaction was one of surprise given media headlines and the 
lack of a positive response in the Consumer Equities. On the former, I 
cannot speak to media motivations, but the data does not support the 

hypothesis that a consumer crack is imminent or that a consumer-led 
recession is likely over the next few quarters. On the latter, it may be 
more helpful to breakdown the Consumer into sub-sectors: (i) Airlines / 
Cruise Lines / Hotels – demand remains robust despite higher prices 
which was confirmed by recent earnings releases / mgmt commentary. 
It may be the case that post-COVID experiences mean more than 
goods/products; (ii) Autos / Homebuilders – these are the rates-
sensitive sectors and where affordability issues continue to shift 
consumer spend preferences. With average ages of cars more than 10 
years and of homes ~45 years, playing these sectors via parts / 
suppliers may make more sense; (iii) Retailers – one of the biggest 
headwinds is the cost structure where some clients flag that 
freight/transportation costs are 30-40% of total cost and those rates 
have more than tripled YTD. There are some idiosyncratic issues, e.g. 
LULU / NKE that may also weigh on the sector; (iv) Restaurants – 
there was not conviction in the discussion on drivers of these group but 
a trade down the quality curve within quick service is viewed as 
negative to the consumer strength hypothesis; (v) Staples – this group 
is view as the most at-risk to margin compression and with higher rates 
/ inflation is a group that is likely to remain a source of funds. 
• CREDIT CONCERNS – Globally, the credit environment is troubling 
especially but the consensus, now, seems to be that hyperscalers are fine and 
that the big players will continue to dominate but that ROI remains positive for 
smaller players, including neoclouds. In our absence, our FICC colleagues 
hosted a call on financing (Oct 19) and am sharing some color from TMT 
Sector Spec Josh Meyers. The summary is that Credit markets are becoming 
more discriminating on AI Infra, but there’s nothing to suggest a broader 
funding crisis. Hyperscalers are fundamentally well-positioned, while 
leveraged GPU and data center developers face increasing financing 
constraints. The biggest concerns are growing debt issuance, interconnected 

finance arrangements, higher borrowing cost and the ability to deliver 
compute capacity on-schedule.  
o BIG-PICTURE FINANCING – Capital remains available, and 
performance has been resilient: The HY compute index was down on 
the week but is +372bps YTD, outperforming broader HY; but terms are 
tightening. AI infrastructure has absorbed more than $500 billion of 
debt year-to-date across investment grade (>$350b), leverage (>$60b), 
structured, and private credit markets ($100b+ best guess). Despite 
rising borrowing cost, the financing ecosystem remains functional (all-in 
funding costs for hyperscalers maybe have increased ~120bps), with 
much of 2026’s issuance supported by 2027 commitments. Banks 
retain substantial lending capacity, but have become more selective 
about underwriting terms (it’s not just about chip value, but also the 
economy; and things have swung a bit in their favor on price). Equity % 
will also need to move up as volumes get up to 20GW+. Tarek sees 
power availability, rather than financing, as the primary constraint on AI 
infrastructure expansion. He also argues that circular financing and 
Nvidia credit support may be necessary to finance depreciating GPUs 
through investment-grade markets, although these arrangements 
introduced meaningful concentration and correlated downside risks. 
o CDS –  AI credit spreads seem increasingly disconnected from 
fundamentals. AI-related CDS spreads have widened significantly, 
driven more by technical selling pressure and hedging demand than by 
deteriorating fundamentals. Banks are actively purchasing CDS 
protection to hedge their growing data center lending exposure, 
creating persistent pressure on spreads even where credit 
fundamentals remain strong; this has pushed CDS spreads above 
comparable cash-bond spreads, creating attractive opportunities for 
bond investors willing to sell protection (only need to post an initial 
margin). Names trading positive basis like this are very attractive. 

CRWV interesting b/c also has converts outstanding, with CDS trading 
at a slightly positive basis 
o US IG TMT CREDIT RESEARCH – Strong fundamentals don’t 
guarantee tight spreads. MSFT, GOOGL & AMZN remain 
fundamentally resilient, supported by substantial cash generation and 
diversified businesses. Their credit spreads have held up relatively 
well, while more externally-financed companies (particularly Oracle) 
have underperformed; ORCL and SpaceX widened significantly on 
reports that each is seeking off-balance-sheet financing. Erica Spear 
expects the leading hyperscalers to continue investing despite higher 
borrowing cost, but believes growing debt supply may pressure credit 
spreads into 2027. Leveraged GPU and data center financing is more 
vulnerable to rising rates, potentially requiring larger equity 
contributions, additional guarantees or revised deal structures. Nvidia 
represents a particularly important transmission channel for financial 
stress because of its interconnected relationships with GPU customers, 
including CRWV. The fundamental outlook remains constructive, but 
near-term credit spreads have been a bit bumpy.  
o US HY TMT CREDIT RESEARCH – CRWV’s is a financing challenge, 
not a solvency one. The high-yield market is challenging for unsecured 
CRWV financing right now, but investors seem to underestimate the 
companies’ future cash generation (it gets funding, builds compute and 
then generates EBITDA… so there’s a delay). JPM Credit Analyst Tom 
Egan projects CRWV last 12mo EBITDA increasing from ~$4.4b in 
June 2026 to $7.3b by YE and nearly $20b by YE27. The company has 
substantial liquidity, ~$130b of contracted revenue commitments and 
potential access to additional equity and customer-supported financing. 
Tom believes hyperscaler customers and vendors have strong 
incentive to support financing arrangements that ensure CRWV we can 
deliver contracted capacity. While future growth will require additional 

capital, there’s also flexibility to finance projects through SPV's and 
credit backstops rather than traditional high-yield issuance. 
• FRANCE / EUROPE CONTAGION – There are concerns that France is a 
leading indicator of EU-wide distress. In our view, these concerns are 
exaggerated. Admittedly, France faces headwinds (a ~3% primary deficit, 
debt/GDP of c.120% and below-potential growth). However, history suggests 
that a debt/GDP ratio above 180% typically marks the point where an 
economy crosses the Rubicon. For France, that is another 10–15 years away, 
even on unchanged policy. The fiscal profiles of Greece, Italy and Spain are 
much stronger, given primary surpluses and growing economies (driven by a 
mix of domestic and global dynamics). Fo example, Italy could stabilize debt 
trajectory with ~50bp of government spending cuts (vs. >300bp for France). 
Finally, the ECB backstop (TPI, OMT, PEPP reinvestment flexibility) limits the 
risk of disorderly spread widening, although activation is conditional.  We keep 
viewing the situation as more of FR idiosyncratic story and a Sov Crisis 2.0.  
o FRENCH EQUITIES – The complex splits into Exporters (e.g., Luxury, 
Consumer, Industrials) and Domestics (e.g., Banks, Infrastructure, 
Utilities). Both are shorted, which makes France a funder at the country 
level. The drivers differ, however: geopolitics/tariffs for Exporters and 
fiscal concerns for Domestics. 
o FRENCH BONDS – The move was clearly made worse by positioning 
(HF crowding in carry proxies) and by the ownership profile of French 
debt (~60% held by non-residents, roughly half of them outside 
Europe). OATs now discount debt/GDP above 150% (vs. c.120% 
actual) and an implied deficit above 8% (vs c. 5% debated). That deficit 
would be mathematically hard to reach even if fundamentals 
deteriorate. We don't call for a material reversal until elections in 2Q27, 
but we see room for relief into year-end around the budget discussion. 
o EU BANKS – The tape has echoed the 2011–12 sovereign crisis: EGB 
spreads wider, EUR and EU Banks lower. In our view, the earnings 
trend for EU Banks is unchanged, and material dips (>15% vs. the 

index) should be bought. The backdrop is already attractive for call 
ratio spreads and call spreads. 
• MIDTERMS – (i) the concerns were focused on AI regulation and a federal 
law being pass. This is unlikely to happen as Trump has said that he would 
veto any law and it is impossible for Dems to achieve the 2/3 majority in both 
chambers of Congress via these midterms necessary to override a 
Presidential veto. This is a state-level problem as well as permitting but those 
state-level headwinds are not uniform across the US meaning that a data 
center not built in one state could be moved to another. (ii) If Dems take the 
House, they could defund military efforts in the Middle East, forcing an off-
ramp for Trump. (iii) Tariff policy is unlikely to be changed given President’s 
broad latitude to conduct foreign policy. (iv) Longer-dated bonds could see a 
bid as Dems halt Trump’s deficit spending. (v) Single sector views are not 
affected given the inability to pass new laws / regulations and that even a Blue 
Wave pointing to a Dem winning the 2028 election is too far away to alter 
current views. 
• TREASURIES – Before the rally on Oct 22, the view was that 5.50% was 
more likely than 5.00% for the 10-year yield. The market is pricing a hike in 
Dec 2026 and then 2x in FY27; clients see upside risk to that forecast based 
on the strength of household and corporate balance sheets, accelerating 
growth, and inflation. The downside risk (fewer hikes than expected) comes in 
the form of a near-term end to the US / Iran Conflict and then quick 
normalization of oil prices, refined product prices, petrochemical prices, and 
shipping / insurance rates. The downside view is low conviction with many 
thinking the Fed DOTS (no hikes in FY27, cuts in FY28 & FY29, and reaching 
2% target in FY29) are not tied to high probability outcomes. For now, the 
market pricing remains the baseline for Equity investors. 
• TRADE IDEAS & FEEDBACK – Given the hesitancy to re-engage markets 
there were not a ton of high conviction ideas discussed. Most discussions can 
be categorized as AI vs. non-AI, both at the stock / sector-level and at the 
country-level.  

o AI / TECH – Generally, the fundamentals remain positive but the 
pushback on the bull hypothesis is (i) Semis – revenue driven by 
constrained supply chain-induced price spike which are unsustainable 
in a DCF forecast period. (ii) Neoclouds – near-term turmoil may 
expand to the entire group, and bankruptcy risk exists; but, 
hyperscalers likely buy for pennies on the dollar in event of bankruptcy. 
(iii) Software – unlikely to be used as a source of funds going forward 
given the need to use software to scale AI adoption and that large 
companies continue to see earnings estimate increases. Security and 
Infra Software are consensus long plays. (iv) When to Buy – need a 
combination of a positioning puke, hyperscaler capex confirmation, 
Semis earnings reports, credit to tightening, and positive seasonality. 
All points to early November should it come to fruition. 
o BROADENING – since the July 28 local lows, the SPX is +4.8% 
(through Oct 22) with 6 of 11 sector positive and 4 outperforming the 
SPX though more than half of the index has produced a negative return 
over that time and 69% of stocks lagging the SPX. This points to the 
difficulty of picking a single stock and hedging with a sector ETF. There 
was no consensus on what shifts this price action but there was no 
pushback to the hypothesis that many businesses were built for a lower 
rates environment. The 10Y yield averaged 2.39% for the 2010s after 
averaging 4.44% in the 2000s and 6.65% in the 1990s. In this decade, 
the 10Y has averaged 3.14% through Oct 22. 
▪ CAN AI AND EX-AI WORK SIMULTANEOUSLY? aka can we 
see an ‘Everything Rally’? On a short-term basis, Yes. On a 
longer-term basis, No. The revenue growth and earnings 
differential point to Tech domination once the market stabilizes. 
Higher rates point to Quality plays within sectors. AI adoption / 
monetization means that management assessment add to 
dispersion, e.g., while ~65% of SPX companies mentioned AI 
improvement in operations 26Q2 earnings less than 5% were 

able to quantify the impact. Is AI Quantification another metric 
when assessing management quality? Do those companies 
begin to see a discount relative to peers who cannot quantify the 
impact? Our guess is Yes but think this is a better conversation 
with single stock / single credit analysts and specialists. 
o CYCLICALS – Excluding (i) Energy, clients focused on (ii) Financials 
esp. GSIBs and credit cards. (iii) Materials have some interest, but the 
SPX sector is tilted towards gold which had some interest but was met 
with more indifference than a directional view. (iv) Industrials are 
viewed primarily as an AI play without much differentiation within the 
sector except for (v) Aero/Def which had a negative outlook in the eyes 
of the clients given the potential for a Blue Wave to halt a military 
funding spike though a lame duck Congressional bill to fund the military 
could prove to be an upside surprise for the group. (vi) the Macro 
environment remains supportive of Cyclicals and the near-term bullish 
shift in the yield curve is additive in the context of strong PMIs. 
o FUNDING SHORTS – With Software increasingly becoming a long 
play, this may be as simple as +AI vs. -Broadening. The trick is the size 
of Tech requires multiple sectors and/or geographies to offset. Many 
agreed that a combination of Consumer sector, Healthcare sector, 
Smidcaps, and Europe could be used to offset Tech exposure.  
o OTHER ASSET CLASSES – (i) FX – most thought the Dollar would 
remain range-bound but that it had likely reached a local high given 
market turmoil is likely to subside post-Midterms; (ii) Gold – given the 
decline it is interesting but it was viewed as continuing to trade 
inversely to the Dollar despite CB demand; (iii) Credit – none viewed 
Credit as superior to Equities; (iv) Copper – agreed with the JPM bull 
case / ties to China growth reboot. Other base metals were not 
mentioned. (v) Silver – viewed as part of the AI trade with Equities 
providing more leverage to the AI theme.

EMERGING MARKETS MONETIZATION – Ellen Wang / Victoria Campos

remains off YTD highs with choppy price action in recent months. We are bullish on 
EM given (i) continued global macro strength; (ii) AI CapEx tailwinds; (iii) diversifying 
trade given Broadening exposure via commodities; greater scope for policy easing; 
political catalysts. We continue to expect divergence in EM performance at the 
country/sector level and view country-relative trades as more relevant for the 
monetization strategy in this environment.

consumers. (ii) We express the AI winners vs losers view via TACTICALLY LONG

POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE 
POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE IS NOT A PRODUCT OF RESEARCH 
  
Report: Prime Time / Monthly Wrap | Many Signs of De-Risking as HF 
Performance is Hit Hard 
  
Brief Summary: October is a month that most HFs will probably want to forget.  
There’s been seemingly nowhere to hide from a strategy perspective as there have 
been challenges across the board.  In many ways, most of this can be traced back to 
AI / Tech as credit concerns and lackluster earnings have not been able to stem the 
tide thus far.  What’s perhaps surprising is that the broader indices in the US and 
Europe were basically flat to up this past month, which makes the HF pain stand out 
even more.  In our view, there have been a lot of different signs of de-risking over the 
past month, which—taken together—could suggest an improvement is due soon.  
However, the lack of capitulation (at least prior to today), concerns about escalation 
in the Middle East post US midterm elections, and lingering AI concerns do make it 
hard to have strong conviction on the path forward. 
  
Key Points: 
  
1. Performance deteriorates sharply in October: Global HF net returns were 
down ~4% on a rolling 20d basis as of last Friday, with the MTD estimate at -
2.6% through Thurs, Oct 29 (likely close to -3.5% after Friday). The global 

long-short spread was -2.2% MTD, with declines across all regions: -1.5% 
Asia, -1.9% N. Am., -2.4% EMEA. 
2. Alpha rebound potential, but risks remain: Prior -3z events (in 20d L-S 
performance spread) saw positive forward 20d spreads in 6 of 7 cases (avg 
+1%). De-grossing has been prevalent (Multi-Strats especially), and 
Momentum exposure is at >12m lows, though risks include potential Quant 
de-grossing and still-elevated US Tech flows/futures positioning. 
3. US positioning drifts lower but not sharply: The US Tactical Positioning 
Monitor fell to the 33rd %-tile as of Oct 29, though 4wk change was neutral. 
HFs cut net leverage earlier in Oct, before adding slightly this week.  Retail 
stayed sidelined (sentiment at 6th %-tile), ETF flows stayed positive (+1z), 
and CTA positioning fell MoM but is only 44th %-tile LT in US equities. 
4. US Midterms and seasonal Bank trades in focus: The D1 team’s Split 
Congress Pair (JPMSC26P) has rallied to 2yr highs, with HFs more bullish on 
Split Congress longs via short covering (mainly HC), though shorts (Cap 
Markets stocks) also saw buying/covering. Separately, US Banks have a 
strong Nov seasonal track record, Financials ETFs saw the strongest inflows 
last week, and HFs remain net long Banks. 
5. TMT vs. Defensives sentiment gap echoes prior lows: Social Media 
sentiment dropped to the 6th %-tile, coinciding with a wide TMT-Defensives 
spread also at the 6th %-tile — similar to several prior episodes where Tech-
led weakness was followed by Tech-led rebounds (prior episodes: late 
Mar ’26, Mar/Apr ’25, early Aug/Sep ’24, late Apr ’24, and end of Dec ’22). 
6. US Health Care and Utilities diverge: HFs flipped to buying Biotech and 
Med Tech after months of selling, with HC flows the strongest of any sector 
last month. Conversely, Utilities rallied but HFs kept selling, leaving the group 
with the lowest net positioning of any major industry. 
7. Europe positioning rebounds off lows: EU Positioning hit a low of the 
5th %-tile before rebounding to the 14th %-tile.  The recent dip and rebound 
suggests potential for further gains. EU Momentum positioning has declined 

with very negative flows over the past 3m, but HFs are still long Banks and 
bearish on Autos, Lux Goods, and Software. 
8. China/HK positioning remains depressed: China/HK aggregate positioning 
is the most bearish of any major region at just the 5th %-tile.
