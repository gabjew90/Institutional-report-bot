Good morning, all eyes on Midterm Elections on Tuesday. Public opinion polling suggests national
sentiment has swung by around 10-11pp toward Democrats, and prediction markets imply high odds
Democrats will win the House majority with a wider margin of control (around 236 seats) than either party
has had in several years. See Alec Phillips note for a preview. While data centers have emerged as a theme
in some campaigns this year, we expect the focus to fade somewhat after the election. The issue ranks low
among voter priorities, and state governors are likely to shift attention once election season is over.
Congress might limit data center tax incentives, but a national moratorium looks unlikely in any election
outcome. Instead, we expect the congressional focus to shift to AI regulation, where a compromise that
codifies basic federal oversight and self-testing is possible but restrictive regulation looks unlikely.

Oil. GS is Raising Price Forecast on High Supply Disruption Risks. Although Mideast output continues to
recover as expected, we are raising our crude oil price forecasts because substantial downside supply risks
are likely to sustain a large near-term risk premium, while our new global supply disruption allowance
captures high disruption risk over the medium term. We raise our Dec26 forecasts to $105/bbl for Brent
(from $90) and $94 for WTI, and our 2027 average forecasts to $88 for Brent (from $80) and $78 for WTI
(from $73).
US earnings – mid season. Strategist Ben Snider shares US 3Q 2026 midseason earnings update. Results
have underscored the fundamental challenge facing equity investors: Earnings growth is strong, but it is no
longer accelerating. Similarly, Q3 results support our view that AI investment spending will continue to
grow but that the growth rate of AI capex is peaking. One area of ongoing acceleration is hyperscaler
cloud revenues, which continued this quarter to show increasing signs of return on AI investments. Cloud
revenue growth for AMZN, GOOGL, and MSFT grew by 54% year/year in Q3. However, elevated
investor expectations and crowded positioning coming into the quarter set a high bar for share prices.
Alongside accelerating AI revenues, the 3Q results also increased investor focus on risk of AI disruption,
particularly among TMT stocks.

China Banks. 3Q26 First Take: Strong PPOP and Accelerated Risk Disposal. Speak to Shuo Yang?
We highlight that the five large Chinese banks delivered stronger-than-expected 3Q26 results, with
average PPOP and net profit growing 16% and 7% year-over-year, respectively. The outperformance was
primarily driven by stabilizing net interest margins and solid bond investment gains, which provided
capacity for accelerated risk disposal and a 47% average year-over-year increase in provision charges. We
maintain our Buy ratings on CCB and BOC, noting CCB delivered a clean beat with robust earnings
quality, and we believe preserving reasonable earnings growth alongside contained balance-sheet risks will
continue to support sector valuations.
