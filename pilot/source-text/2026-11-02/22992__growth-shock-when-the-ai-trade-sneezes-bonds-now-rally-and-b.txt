 
"Growth Shock": When The AI Trade Sneezes, 
Bonds Now Rally, And BofA Has A Hedge For It 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, OCT 28, 2026 - 01:40 AM 
For much of the year, every AI scare came with a side order of higher yields. Not anymore. 
Two weeks ago, BofA's derivatives team argued that the AI bubble will burst with stocks AND 
yields tumbling, based on the market's reaction to the OpenAI revenue scare. Last Thursday 
provided an even cleaner test case. Per the team's latest Global Equity Volatility Insights note, 
"The age of idiosyncratic risk" (available to pro subs), 10-year yields dropped more than 16bps 
on Oct 22 as the Nasdaq declined nearly 3%: 
AI-driven weakness in recent weeks has traded as a growth shock... Price action on 22-Oct was 
a striking example of this reaction function, with 10y yields dropping more than 16bps as the 
Nasdaq declined nearly 3%. 
 

 
 
In other words, the bond market has decided that an AI unwind means a weaker economy, and 
is pricing it accordingly. Which, given how much of US GDP growth (and capex) now runs 
through data centers, is hard to argue with. 
It also loops back to something we discussed earlier this month, when BofA warned that the AI 
put is now the Treasury market's last line of defense. Last Thursday, that line held. 
The Nasdaq Is The AI Trade's Shock Absorber 
The second tell is in vol. The spread between Nasdaq and S&P vol, both implied (VXN minus 
VIX) and realised, has widened again in recent weeks, with the implied spread back near 9-10 
vol points: 
 

 
 
For BofA, this highlights "the Nasdaq's asymmetric reactivity to the AI trade relative to the S&P," 
and supports its ongoing preference for owning Nasdaq over S&P in both delta and vol as the AI 
bubble keeps building. Translation: when AI moves, it moves the Nasdaq first and most, in both 
directions. 
The Bear Hedge: Nasdaq Lookback Puts... But Only If Yields Fall 
If AI weakness keeps trading as a growth shock, BofA's hedge of choice is a hybrid that only 
pays out in that scenario: an NDX September 2027 90% max lookback put, contingent on 
10-year SOFR swap rates falling at least 25bps. When first pitched on Oct 12, it cost 3.62% 
indicatively, a 46% discount to the plain max lookback put and 23bps cheaper than a vanilla put. 
The lookback feature resets the strike to 90% of the highest NDX close observed (daily), so 
investors don't need to time the top. 
Since then, rates have drifted lower and growing interest in the trade has pushed the correlation 
entry point higher, so it's no longer quite the bargain it was. BofA still likes it, for two reasons: 
●​ Rates are unlikely to have fallen as far as they would in a more prolonged AI unwind 
 

 
●​ The outright cost of the hybrid remains attractive relative to other longer-dated hedges 
For those looking to spend less, NDX expanding put spreads contingent on lower rates offer 
capped protection for a lower premium. 
The Bull Trade: Call Spreads For A "Clearing Event" 
Now for the other side of the barbell. Over the next few weeks, the market has to get through 
megacap earnings, the October FOMC and the midterm elections. BofA thinks that once those 
are out of the way, the passing of so many catalysts could act as a "clearing event" for risk into 
year-end and spark a bullish shift in sentiment. 
For investors who have cut risk in recent weeks, BofA recommends call spreads, which cap the 
upside but also cap what you can lose. Screening options-liquid ETFs that have sold off since 
the Oct 6 Nasdaq peak, SMH (semis) and EEM (emerging markets) offer the best payout ratios 
on December ATM / +1-sigma call spreads, roughly 2.8x, with SPY at the bottom of the list at 
about 2x: 
 

 
 
Among Nasdaq 100 single names that have sold off since the peak, T-Mobile (TMUS) and 
Western Digital (WDC) top the list with payouts of close to 4x, followed by SPCX, Lumentum 
(LITE), Constellation (CEG) and Lam Research (LRCX): 
 

 
 
BofA concedes that the absolute payout ratios "may appear modest," but notes that moving the 
lower strike further out-of-the-money would boost the potential payoff. 
Growth Shock Or Clearing Event? 
BofA is effectively offering both sides of the same coin: a cheap way to protect against a long AI 
unwind that drags yields down with it, and a cheap way to participate if the catalyst gauntlet 
clears the air. 
If we had to pick, we'd lean toward the growth shock. Every recent AI wobble, from the OpenAI 
revenue miss to CoreWeave's implosion to last Thursday's puke, has come with bonds rallying, 
and that reaction function is unlikely to reverse just because a few megacaps beat estimates. 
The midterms and the Fed may well clear the calendar, but they won't clear the question of who 
is paying for the AI buildout, and at what return. 
 

 
Then again, the market has been bailed out by the "AI put" every time so far. That's precisely 
why BofA wants you to own both.
