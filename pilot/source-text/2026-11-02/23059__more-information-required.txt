FOMC Post-Meeting Comment October 2026 
 
 
Summary 
 The FOMC remained on hold today, but Hammack and Cook dissented in favor of a hike. 
 According to the formal statement, the accumulation of more information will help the 
Committee determine the policy stance (for December). 
 At the press conference, Warsh stuck to his principle of not providing forward guidance. 
However, he did prepare the markets for another rate hike in December. 
 After they have gathered more information, we expect the FOMC to hike at the next meeting 
in December. 
 Starting next fall, the FOMC will dedicate one policy meeting to a discussion on monetary 
policy strategy without making a rate decision. Warsh expects that there will be two such 
meetings in 2028. The FOMC is sticking to 8 meetings in total per year.  
 Our baseline forecast for 2027 is that the Fed remains on hold, but the risk is to the upside as 
we expect inflation to rise further in the coming months. 
The statement 
As expected, the FOMC kept the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged at 3.75-4.00% 
today. The decision was not unanimous, with Hammack (Cleveland Fed) and Cook (Board of 
Governors) dissenting because they preferred to raise the target rate by ¼ percentage point at 
this meeting. We had expected three dissents, so the voting members of the FOMC were a little 
less hawkish than we had expected. Governor Cook’s dissent is noteworthy given that President 
Trump is still trying to remove her from the Fed. In the formal statement, the FOMC said that the 
economy was expanding at a strong pace (an upgrade from the ‘solid’ pace in the September 
statement). According to the statement, the accumulation of more information will help the 
Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 
2% goal. There was no update of the economic projections, the next will be in December. 
The press conference 
In his prepared speech, Warsh said that economic growth has strengthened, unemployment is 
lower and nonfarm payroll growth higher than last year. At the same time, inflation has been 
above target for close to 6 years, so the discussion in the FOMC was mostly about the inflation 
side of the dual mandate. He said that financial conditions have tightened, but are not restrictive. 
He noted that energy prices have risen again and that it’s the Fed’s job to prevent that relative 
price changes broaden out. Warsh also stressed that the Fed has shown the willingness to act (in 
September). During the Q&A, Warsh explained that the FOMC will be better positioned to make a 
decision at the next meeting in December because this was going to be an information-rich 
interval. Without providing forward guidance, he essentially made the case for another rate hike in 
December.

However, he was perhaps a little more subtle than at Jackson Hole when he said that underlying 
trends in inflation have not meaningfully improved. What’s more, there were fewer dissents (only 
2) than at the last meeting with a hold in July (3 dissents). In fact, we had expected the same three 
dissenters from July. Consequently, the yield curve steepened with the 10 year US treasury yield 
rising by about 3 bps and the 2 year yield losing about 4 bps between the release of the 
statement and the end of the press conference, as Warsh and the FOMC did not appear as 
hawkish as might have been expected.  
When asked how much the AI boom is contributing to inflation, Warsh said that the Committee 
thinks that the economy is strong but not too strong, so this does not tell him that AI is pushing 
inflation higher. This suggests that the FOMC is still focusing on the supply shock from the Middle 
East, rather than a possible demand shock from AI investment. When asked about the impact of 
interest rates on AI, Warsh stressed that he thinks that interest rates do affect AI investment. We 
could therefore conclude that if the FOMC were to become concerned about the contribution of 
AI investment to inflation, they might consider additional hikes to address this. 
Warsh announced that starting next fall, the FOMC will dedicate one policy meeting to a 
discussion on monetary policy strategy without making a rate decision. Warsh expects that there 
will be two such meetings in 2028. The FOMC is sticking to 8 meetings in total per year. 
Regarding politics, Warsh dodged a question about President Trump’s negative opinion of the 
FOMC participants that he did not appoint. Of course, he denied that the midterm elections next 
week played a role in today’s decision to remain on hold. The final question was whether the Fed 
was going to be involved in Governor Cook’s hearing and Warsh answered “not to my 
knowledge.”  
Conclusion 
Without giving forward guidance, Warsh prepared the markets for another rate hike in December. 
Keep in mind that in the September dot plot, the median FOMC participant expected one more 
hike in 2026, followed by a pause in 2027. However, as the conflict with Iran continues, the 
median FOMC participant may start to consider an additional hike in 2027, instead of remaining 
on hold. Our baseline scenario is also that the Fed remains on hold in 2027, but the risks are 
clearly to the upside.
