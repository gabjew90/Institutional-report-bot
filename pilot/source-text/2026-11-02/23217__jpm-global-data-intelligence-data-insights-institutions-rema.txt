Data Intelligence Sentiment Deep Dive: Institutions Remain Cautious on Stocks, but Underlying Signals Turn More Supportive 
 
Brief Summary: Institutional investors remain cautious on global equities but the underlying picture is becoming more constructive. 
KOSPI’s October sell-off has coincided with institutional sentiment and interest both falling below average, a contrarian setup historically 
associated with a constructive, albeit choppy near-term backdrop. In the US, institutional engagement is improving, and sector breadth 
remains supportive, despite softer institutional sentiment. Meanwhile, retail sentiment is now close to levels that have historically 
coincided with market rebounds. In Europe, institutional interest in OATs is easing from multi-year highs, with yields little changed.

Key Points 
 
1) KOSPI: constructive setup, but expect a choppy path. Institutional sentiment (i.e. relative bullish vs. bearish views) and 
institutional interest (i.e. absolute level of engagement), both have fallen below average alongside KOSPI’s October sell-off. 
Historically, this setup has been associated with positive returns over the next 20 days, but elevated near-term volatility and a still-
cautious technical backdrop suggest the AI/semis trade could remain choppy. 
2) SPX: cautious sentiment, but the underlying picture is improving. Institutional sentiment remains below average, while 
institutional interest has picked up into a catalyst-heavy week. Sector breadth also looks constructive, and any recovery in XLK could 
provide further support to SPX. 
3) Retail sentiment has continued to weaken across sectors and is now close to YTD lows. We are watching for signs of 
stabilization, as previous troughs in retail sentiment have often coincided with market rebounds. 
4) Cross-Asset View: Institutional mentions of French government bonds have started to ease from multi-year highs, while 
yields are little changed, suggesting fiscal and political concerns may be more embedded in market expectations.  
Thematic Charts & Additional Detail 
KOSPI: The c.19% (local currency terms) decline in KOSPI throughout the month (1st-27th Oct) has coincided with a deterioration in 
institutional sentiment and interest, with both falling below their long term averages as concerns over AI infra spending and elevated 
valuations resurfaced. Historically, this combination of subdued sentiment and interest levels has been associated with positive returns 
over the next 20 days (1.8% vs. 1.5 % on average, local currency terms), alongside a higher probability of positive performance (61% 
vs. 56% on average). That said, this setup appears less constructive in the immediate terms, with 10-day volatility historically running 
above average (6% vs. 5%). At the same time, our JPM Strategists remain cautious until KOSPI shows signs of deceleration in key 
support zones (Feb-Oct pattern lows; see here). And despite this week’s earnings driven rebound (+c. 7% on 28th Oct), these conditions 
are yet to emerge, suggesting volatility across the AI/semis trade may persist in the near term. 

SPX: As highlighted in our previous note, institutional investors have remained cautious towards SPX going into the Q3 reporting 
season, despite a supportive earnings backdrop (here). Institutional sentiment has dropped below average, although interest has been 
picking up as investors’ engagement builds up going into a catalyst heavy week, with approx. 44% of S&P 500 companies reporting this 
week, alongside the Fed and key US macro releases. While this sentiment/interest setup has historically been associated with broadly 
average 20-day forward returns, sector breadth points to a more constructive signal: 8 of 11 sectors have positive month-to-date returns, 
outperforming SPX, while XLK US remains c.3% lower, leaving room for a potential recovery in a key driver of SPX performance. 
SPX Sector Performance (as of 28 Oct): 8 of 11 sectors have positive month-to-date returns, outperforming SPX, while XLK US remains 
c.3% lower.

US Retail: With retail sentiment now close to year-to-date lows, we will be watching for signs of stabilization, as troughs in retail sentiment 
have often coincided with market rebounds. There’s been broad-based weakness across defensives, cyclicals and tech.  

Cross-Asset View – Institutional interest in French government bonds (OATs) lower but yields little changed.  Institutional 
mentions of French government bonds (OATs) have started to ease from multi-year highs, while the 10Y OAT yield is little changed 
following Moody’s decision to affirm France’s sovereign credit (4.9%, c. -10bps since announcement). The divergence between easing 
investor attention and the relatively limited moves in 10Y yields could suggest that markets are becoming more used to the current fiscal and 
political backdrop; we will continue to monitor for a sustained easing in attention as a potential signal of stabilisation.

Signal from the Noise 
Signal from the Noise reflects market timing signals for the US equity market (S&P 500) based on fundamental and (externally derived) positioning 
data. For the framework to be bullish on the S&P500, either the Positioning or Fundamental signals need to be flashing ‘Stron
g Buy’ (and likewise for a 
low conviction bullish view). The ‘Strong Buy’ strategy demonstrated an 8.6% annual return and 1.7x Sharpe ratio (69% per trade hit ratio) from 2010 
to 2021. 
Tactical Positioning Monitor 
Tactical Positioning Monitor (TPM) reflects the level of broader positioning data. For the framework to be bullish on the S&P500, short
-term 
positioning data points either need to be very light (-1.5z), or rising (+1.5z) but with markets below peaks. The strategy demonstrated an 8.9% annual 
return and 1.0x Sharpe ratio (69% per trade hit ratio) from 2015 to 2021. 

Through the Retail Lens 
Through the Retail Lens focuses on US retail trading activity and social media volumes. The product combines proprietary and high frequency retail 
flow data from our QDS Research team, with analysis of social media volumes at a single stock, thematic, sector and market le
vel. 
Strategic Indices: Fundamental Toolkit (SIFT) 
Strategic Indices: Fundamental Toolkit (SIFT) is a proprietary toolkit of 21,000 aggregate global equity indices with an extensive cross section of 
46 different metrics across each index from the 1960s – created by our colleagues in the J.P. Morgan Equities Structuring team. With its comprehensive 
metrics and long history, the database can be used for a variety of investment analyses for the market, sectors & factors.
