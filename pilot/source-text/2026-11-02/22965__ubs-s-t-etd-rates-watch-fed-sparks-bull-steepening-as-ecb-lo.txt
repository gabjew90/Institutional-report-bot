ETD Rates Watch: Fed Sparks Bull
Steepening As ECB Looms And Gilts
Absorb Budget
Daniel Noorian
29-Oct-2026, 04:51 ET (EDT)
Fixed income markets are becoming harder to call. On Wednesday, I was starting to think the rally in rates was
over, with the only catalyst for buying a meaningful sell-off in equities. After the Fed I’m not so sure.
I thought the initial statement was hawkish, especially with Governor Cook’s dissent, but the change to
meetings in 2027 and 2028 was seen as dovish by the market. The obvious inference: fewer live meetings,
fewer hikes. I think that is the wrong take, but can see why the market ran with it. SFRZ7 rallying over 10 ticks
from Wednesday’s low of 95.34 suggests the market agrees.
Further comments in the press conference from Chair Warsh that the economy wasn’t running too hot and that
financial conditions were tighter also gave the market something to think about. Markets are now pricing
20.6bp of hikes in December, with some Street commentators suggesting December could be the last hike in
the cycle. UBS US chief economist Jonathan Pingle called it a “kind of hawkish hold” in the latest US
Economics Perspective. He also said that his call for a January hike could be off, with inflation “not getting out
of control” but not moving down. 
The long end may beg to differ with that interpretation. The UST curve steepened post-Fed, with 2s30s 12.7bp
steeper and 5s30s 8.6bp steeper. It was a bull-steepening move. However, you could argue the moves
reflected a market questioning the Fed’s credibility and its stomach for the inflation fight. So far, the move
hasn’t continued in overnight trading.
With the Fed meeting out of the way, TY 30-day implied vol is now back below 6%, trading at 5.98%. It started
the week at around 6.4%. Call skew has also picked up, despite flows on Wednesday afternoon being heavily
tilted towards downside structures. I thought it was noteworthy that the two largest options trades just ahead of
and after the Fed were actually in calls. Paper paid 19 for 18.7k FVZ6 104.50 calls and 27 for 30k TYZ6
106.50 calls (v 105-31+). Open interest is showing the TY calls are new risk.
As for SOFR, the largest flow on the day was a buyer of 30k SFRZ6 95.6875/96.5625 put spreads for 1. That
is actual new risk, not a position adjustment. Someone was looking for a hawkish outcome, or possibly a hike.
As it is now, I suspect accounts will lean into the short vol trade in the front end and there have already been
some signs of that in flows this week. Early flows on Thursday have seen accounts selling put spreads to buy
call spreads.
No moves are expected from the ECB on Thursday and UBS economics expects the press conference to
focus on three things. First, President Lagarde will need to address OAT spread widening and potential ECB
contingencies. Second, her succession plan will likely feature among journalists’ questions. Questions around
a potential increase in the Minimum Reserve Requirement are also likely to feature.
It is fair to say that Wednesday’s UK Budget wasn’t especially well received, although, to be fair, Chancellor
Healey managed to avoid upsetting the Gilt market. Difficult decisions were simply kicked down the road.
Suggestions of an earlier election continue, or even another fiscal event in the spring.

Speaking to the BBC on Thursday, when asked about welfare spending, which the OBR said would rise by
around £16 bn every year until 2031/32, Healey said: “the system is unsustainable……there will be a reduction
in the benefits bill, we’ll do that in a Labour way.” He also warned “hard decisions” lay ahead.
There will be plenty of examples of the law of unintended consequences from decisions taken. Landlords and
rents are one example. On page 80 of the OBR’s Economic and Fiscal Outlook report was a section on the
long-run economic impact of measures. Section 3.46 looked at landlords and warned that changes to property
income taxes risk “a steady long-term rise in rents if demand outstrips supply – which we think is more likely
than not.” They argue fewer potential landlords are likely to enter the market, while others will pass on costs as
pricing power remains with those who stay.
None of this really helps the Bank of England. Markets are still pricing just over 100bp of hikes by July 2027.
Despite comments from Chief Economist Huw Pill about market pricing, it has remained consistent. The Bank
may not want to deliver four hikes, but the government isn’t really doing much to help the situation. Prime
Minister Andy Burnham has consistently denied he plans to call an early election (don’t believe something until
it’s been officially denied), but that might be something to consider when looking at the expected rate path.
Admittedly, that is speculative.
Against the backdrop of central bank meetings and earnings, there has been an increase in newsflow around
the Gulf. One day it is all about renewed attacks, the next it is about ongoing negotiations. The latest update
from the Wall Street Journal [paywall] claims that US Vice President JD Vance has been engaging with
Mohsen Rezaei, the secretary of the Supreme National Security Council. This is viewed as an attempt to find
common ground to persuade President Trump not to launch fresh attacks.
