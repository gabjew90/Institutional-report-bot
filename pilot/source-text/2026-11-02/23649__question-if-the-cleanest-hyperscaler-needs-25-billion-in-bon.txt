overnight note, "GS Basics - Tech Rotation. FCF Inflection. Labor/AI" (available to pro subs): 
"We're transitioning from a market rewarding capex growth to one demanding evidence of 
returns." 
In other words, cash is now the scoreboard. One hyperscaler paid for its AI out of operating

Alphabet: The Funding Short Was A Short For A Reason 
We thought a funding short with "a Search miss already in the price" and a TPU-fueled cloud 
beat was "the kind of setup that squeezes." It was the kind of setup that missed everywhere 
except Cloud. As we detailed last night, Search grew 12% vs. the ~14% buyside bogey, 
YouTube ads grew 9% (below the 10% line JPM's Schilsky said investors would tolerate), and 
operating income of $40.6bn was 6% light. Goldman's US sales trader Ariana Contessa 
summed up the floor reaction: 
"GOOGL -7%... Solid Cloud Growth, however Search growth +12% vs. expects +14% will likely 
feed share-loss narrative." 
Privorotsky was more blunt: one megacap platform "shed ~10% after hours following consumer 
ad misses even as Cloud growth remained robust."

Which makes this morning's bond deal more interesting, not less. Microsoft ended September

about to spend $50bn+ in Q2 and accelerate from there. As we put it back on Oct 13, "debt

And for the record, the desks saw it coming. JPMorgan's credit desk warned before the prints 
that "hyperscaler issuance could also return after earnings, which would make supply a driver 
again," while Goldman's index derivs trader Eric Wu noted this week that lower yields "give the

noticed too: Microsoft, up more than 4% in early premarket trading, has since shrunk that gain 
to around 1%. 
Fed: "A Fairly Volatility-Suppressing Event" 
The Fed was a sideshow. The FOMC held at 3.75-4.00%, upgraded growth to "strong" from 
"solid," and added the line that "the accumulation of more information will help the Committee 
determine the policy stance" (close to the Jefferson-style language Goldman's economists 
expected). Privorotsky's take: 
"Thought Warsh was balanced. Direct references to tightening FCI early in the press 
conference, suggesting awareness of how much work markets are already doing. Two hawkish 
dissents, Cook and Hammack, but Logan staying put was a positive surprise. Growth 
assessment upgraded, inflation still elevated, so hardly dovish. December still looks like a hike, 
but little reason for material repricing... A fairly volatility-suppressing event." 
The market agreed: per Goldman's floor, the S&P closed +28bps at 7,744, the VIX fell 5.65% to 
16.53 and the 10Y was unchanged at 5.02%. Goldman's economists heard Warsh say rate 
hikes are already working and that financial conditions "have tightened but are not tight," and 
they still see a second and final hike in December, with March possible if inflation runs hot. 
Goldman's Will Marshall said the tell would be airtime on financial conditions. Warsh led with 
them. 
We still think the Cook dissent smells political, and Warsh's praise for inflation compensation 
mostly measures TIPS illiquidity. But the bigger point is that with September core PCE expected 
at 3.1% and $100 oil, the Fed has handed the tightening job to the bond market and the AI 
capex cycle. As of this morning, both are on duty. 
 

 
Oracle, Credit And The Pain Trade 
Privorotsky's most interesting call concerns Oracle, which rallied 10% yesterday on its investor 
day: 
"Arguably the most important development for credit. Stock rallied double digits as management 
outlined a path to positive FCF in H2 FY28 and $30bn+ in FY29, with capex stepping down in 
FY29. Incremental capital raises capped at $20bn through FY30, excluding refinancing... Not a 
capex cut… an attempt to demonstrate that the economics work and the funding cycle has an 
end. Potentially very significant for levered AI credit, although still need to see spreads 
respond."

On risk, Privorotsky sees the pain trade as hyperscalers down while de-risked picks and shovels

Show Me The Cash... Or The Order Book 
Privorotsky ends on a constructive note: 
"Credit remains the most important thing to watch. Stability improving, with Oracle potentially 
more consequential for the levered infrastructure complex than either hyperscaler print. But 
we're transitioning from a market rewarding capex growth to one demanding evidence of 
returns. For now, micro remains supportive and I'd expect dips to get bought." 
We agree that credit is the tell, which is precisely why we are less sanguine. Yesterday's 
scorecard split the hyperscalers into payers and burners, but this morning showed that even the 
payer is borrowing, at a time when Bloomberg reports investors already own nearly all the AI 
bonds they want and 10Y yields sit at 5%. As we have been warning since last October, when 
we first noted that "AI Is Now A Debt Bubble Too," the AI buildout runs on debt, and the pit is 
bottomless. Microsoft just confirmed it with a 10-part exclamation point. 
 

 
Dips may well get bought in the stocks. In the bonds, the new question is who buys the next $25 
billion, and the $25 billion after that. Amazon reports after the close tonight; we'll be checking 
the bond calendar first thing tomorrow.
