Q3 2026 earnings results have underscored the fundamental challenge 
n
facing equity investors: Earnings growth is strong, but it is no longer 
accelerating. Despite a volatile macro backdrop and falling valuation multiples, 
an improving earnings outlook has put the S&P 500 on pace for its fourth 
consecutive year of double-digit returns. With 60% of S&P 500 companies 
having reported, S&P 500 EPS growth is on track to register 33% year/year in Q3 
2026. This would match Q2 as the strongest quarter in recent decades outside of 
post-recession recoveries. However, it would also mark an end to the pattern of 
acceleration that has characterized the last few quarters. Excluding 
non-recurring items, we expect S&P 500 EPS growth of nearly 30% in 2026 will 
slow to a still-impressive 18% in 2027. 
Alongside strong earnings results, analysts have continued to lift forward 
n
earnings estimates this season, but the rate of upward revisions has slowed. 
Consensus estimates for 2027 S&P 500 EPS have risen by 2% since the start of 
the earnings season. While companies with estimate upgrades continue to 
outnumber those with downgrades, earnings revision breadth has moderated 
relative to the last several months. In particular, analysts have trimmed profit 
margin estimates for many companies alongside rising energy prices.  
Similarly, Q3 results support our view that AI investment spending will 
n
continue to grow but that the growth rate of AI capex is peaking. The 
hyperscalers are on track to spend roughly $800 billion in capex this year (+96% 
year/year) and analyst estimates suggest that spending will grow to $1.2 trillion 
in 2027 (+44%). Those estimates remained fairly stable this reporting season, in 
contrast with the pattern of upward revisions in recent quarters. Reflecting the 
likely deceleration in AI capex growth, analyst estimates show earnings growth 
for S&P 500 AI infrastructure stocks peaking at 80% this quarter and slowing to 
roughly 40% in 2027. 
One area of ongoing acceleration is hyperscaler cloud revenues, which 
n
continued this quarter to show increasing signs of return on AI investments. 
Cloud revenue growth for AMZN, GOOGL, and MSFT grew by 54% year/year in 
Q3. However, elevated investor expectations and crowded positioning coming 
into the quarter set a high bar for share prices. Alongside accelerating AI 
revenues, Q3 results also increased investor focus on risk of AI disruption, 
particularly among TMT stocks.
