G10 central banks: mostly holding before further hikes 
The Fed remained on hold at today’s meeting, as widely expected, and we viewed 
Chairman Warsh’s comments as balanced and broadly consistent with both a slower 
every-other-meeting rate hiking pace and a limited total number of hikes. Consistent 
with this, we continue to expect a second and final hike in December. But we think a 
third hike in March 2027 is possible if inflation comes in higher than we expect, even 
as we see downside risks to growth. Warsh also made two scheduling 
announcements regarding the frequency of policy statement and rate decision 
meetings starting next year as well as the task forces, which we will continue to keep 
a close eye on. 
Like the Fed, we expect the ECB to remain on hold at tomorrow’s meeting before 
delivering a final 25bp hike in December. That said, we continue to think that the 
recent tightening in Euro area financial conditions owing to turbulence in the French 
bond market could substitute for policy tightening, posing downside risk to our 
forecast, though a further rise in energy prices—particularly gas prices—could make 
a March 2027 hike possible.   
We similarly expect the BoJ to remain on hold at this week’s meeting and expect no 
substantial changes to the economic and price assessments in the October Outlook 
report. Looking ahead, we continue to expect the BoJ to next hike rates in January 
2027, though the upcoming post-meeting press conference will be important to 
watch for clues on future policy actions.  
By contrast, the BoC delivered a 25bp rate hike at today’s meeting, and we continue 
to expect the BoC to hold in December given likely Q4 growth weakness before 
delivering a final 25bp hike in January 2027.

Elections: some surprises, US midterms remain key to watch  
President Lula da Silva’s surprising victory in Brazil’s presidential elections catalyzed a 
significant repricing across Brazilian assets, and we think personnel and policy 
announcements—particularly regarding who will lead the Ministry of Finance and the 
fiscal consolidation strategy—will be key for asset performance ahead. We expect 
measures to contain primary spending growth and further tax increases under a fourth 
Lula term, though with such measures likely to be modest at best and the tax burden 
already high, we continue to believe that a fiscal regime shift will be difficult to achieve.  
Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud Party also performed better than expected 
in Israel’s national elections, but the results currently show that no party has achieved a 
majority, which will likely trigger coalition-building efforts. We believe a significant shift 
in Israel’s security and foreign policy stance is unlikely regardless of the ultimate result, 
as the main coalition blocs broadly agree on these issues.  
Despite these electoral surprises, next week’s US midterm elections remain the key 
focus, with prediction markets continuing to assign high odds to a Democratic sweep, 
though we do not view the risks of increased AI regulation or an escalation in the Iran 
conflict as particularly sensitive to the outcome. That said, if both risks materialize post 
the midterms, we think it could weigh on equity prices as well as Treasury yields on 
expectations for AI regulation, and have mixed implications for the broad Dollar.  
AI: uneven Q3 earnings, concerns remain  
AI jitters have ebbed and flowed as key companies in the AI ecosystem have delivered 
uneven results amid a Q3 earnings season that we find has broadly tracked substantially 
stronger than expected. Indeed, we estimate that over half of reporting S&P 500 
companies have beaten consensus EPS expectations and upgrades to analyst earnings 
estimates continue to outnumber downgrades. But we continue to expect S&P 500 
earnings growth to decelerate in the coming years as the tailwind from the AI investment 
boom gradually fades. As such, we think the AI trade will remain volatile as questions 
about the long-term sustainability and returns of the AI investment cycle persist. And 
with recent labor market data showing some signs of AI-related hiring headwinds, we 
think questions about the extent and timing of AI labor displacement will also grow.
