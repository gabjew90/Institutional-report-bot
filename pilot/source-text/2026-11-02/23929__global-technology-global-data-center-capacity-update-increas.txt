We recognize rising execution risk of building and delivering data centers from 
power supply issues, regulatory uncertainty, and high leverage. That said, we still 
see potential for sustained, outsized growth in capacity through high visibility 
projects. A key area of investor push-back to our data center capacity model has been 
that our estimates are conservative which we attribute to a higher barrier to entry for a 
project’s inclusion in 451 Research’s pipeline. Accordingly, we expect the increased 
execution risk to disproportionally impact projects that were not originally considered in 
our forecasts.  
Power supply constraints. Recently, Microsoft exited a lease commitment due to a 
n
delayed RFS date as a result of power supply issues (see WSJ article from 
10/19/2026) and Oracle’s New Mexico project has faced challenges over power 
supply permitting (see WSJ article from 9/25/2026). The ability to have visibility 
into secured power is becoming more important as a result. 
Community concerns. We have received a rising volume of investor questions 
n
regarding potential delays related to the data center directives by governors in Texas 
and Pennsylvania, both of which effectively paused their respective fast-track 
approval processes. We view the primary burden falling on speculative builds, 
although we flag ongoing headline risk and the potential for slower project 
development. The GS Sustain team recently authored a report detailing the key 
areas of community push back to data center development, along with potential 
mitigating solutions. The team noted that the solutions may not fully allay 
community concerns, where perception of the societal and/or economic impacts of 
AI, transparency of the individual data center development process and broader 
NIMBY push back could supersede innovation/solutions. 
High leverage. HPC data center construction is typically highly levered (80-90% 
n
loan-to-cost) which means returns are highly sensitive to compression in the debt 
yield spread. We continue to monitor further pressure on hyperscaler bond yields, 
and on industry yields from increased headline delays. We foresee increased focus 
on lease duration and credit-quality of the tenant with data center asset as a 
response to rising cost of credit, with asset-backed debt from high quality data 
center projects providing access to 15-30 year contracted cash flows from 
investment-grade tenants. In addition, once projects mature, capital recycling 
offers developers a path to monetize existing projects to finance new 
development at a lower cost of capital.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
