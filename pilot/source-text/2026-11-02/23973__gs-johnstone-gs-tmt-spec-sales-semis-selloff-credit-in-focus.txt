GS TMT SPEC SALES – Monday 2nd November
Semis sold off sharply on Friday following an earlier period of stabilisation, despite volumes running
around the 20-day average and limited supply on our desk. The positive this morning we are seeing Asian
semi names bounce. Last week’s results suggest AI demand remains robust, with power availability,
delivery costs and funding the main pressure points. Elevated gross exposure and subdued risk appetite
amid macro uncertainty are amplifying the sell-off, with EU semis retesting local lows.
Credit remains the key risk gauge: Negative FCF at Google, Amazon and Meta, alongside more than $3tn
of forward commitments, is shifting attention from growth to the duration of funding needs. Microsoft
remains self-funding but has just issued $25bn in debt. Record hyperscaler CDS levels, Oracle’s CDS at
around 288bp and S&P’s negative outlook on Meta (still maintains the AA- rating) getting headlines.
Amazon’s $80bn secured hardware facility and the growing use of leases illustrate a shift towards
structures that protect liquidity and ratings. While power limits near-term deployment, credit stress could
ultimately force a pause in capex. OpenAI’s $10bn reduction in its raise and soft close also weaken the
offtake narrative supporting Oracle. (CNBC) – Also see Gabriela note on software where the repeated
question she gets is “What is the quantum of financing that still needs to be raised to support the
datacenter build out, and is the credit market deep enough to support it?”

• Americas Tech: Software – What we learned this week: AI Supply/Demand, Infrastructure & Financing -
In the last two weeks, our conversations in the AI ecosystem have focused on two big picture questions: 1)
What is the next leg of AI demand in the enterprise post coding tools, and how do those tokens get
distributed between frontier, frontier n-1, and open source/open weights? 2) What is the quantum of
financing that still needs to be raised to support the datacenter build out, and is the credit market deep
enough to support it? The volatility reminds us of the Software volatility in late Jan / early February: there
are bear cases that are hard to disprove, and it takes time for investors to do the work to separate out
nuances in specific stocks and businesses. We view the last 4 trading days as a good starting point for new

information to help the market refine its assumptions, as our work over the last few weeks suggests that: 1)
the amount of financing needed may be less than what investors expect 2) the market is still deep enough
to support new issuance from IG-issuers 3) enterprise token demand may take another step up in 2027.
• Capgemini’s 3Q26 results delivered a third consecutive quarter of mid-single-digit organic growth,
outpacing global peers despite tougher comparatives. Strong pipeline momentum improved Q4 visibility
and confidence heading into FY27, while CEO Aiman Ezzat highlighted increasingly diversified growth
and the medium-term AI transformation opportunity. FY26 organic growth guidance rose for a second
time to 4–4.5% from 3.5–4%, implying c.2–3% in Q4 and appearing broadly de-risked, with upside
potential; we raise our forecast to 4.4% from 4.1% and expect gross margin stabilisation next year as
restructuring progresses. Despite the recent rally, the shares trade at a c.40% discount to Accenture, which
we view as increasingly unwarranted given Capgemini’s growth outperformance. Agentic AI remains a
long-term IT services risk, but our thesis is supported by Capgemini’s position at the upper end of the
value chain; a re-rating requires sustained organic growth, WNS revenue synergies and margin resilience.

• ASML will link senior leaders’ long-term incentive pay, including CEO Christophe Fouquet’s, to a new
Workplace Experience Index, according to Fortune, citing a staff memo first reported by Business Insider.
The move follows a fall in employee engagement to 73.5% from 78% in 2025, with more than 38,000 of
its roughly 44,000 employees responding, amid restructuring plans to cut around 1,700 roles, mainly in
technology and IT management. A subsequent union agreement rules out compulsory layoffs until 1 May
2027 and prioritises internal redeployment. The new index uses six survey questions covering trust in
leadership, confidence in speaking up and day-to-day working conditions, although its weighting in
executive pay and start date remain unspecified. The policy highlights the importance of retaining ASML’s
highly specialised workforce as the sole supplier of EUV lithography equipment.

• Telefónica/Indra: The ECB has selected Telefónica and Indra subsidiary Minsait to develop and operate
secure communications infrastructure for the planned digital euro, according to Vozpopuli. Telefónica
Global Solutions and Telefónica Tech will manage the private network, routing between sites and security
services. Minsait will handle platform and API integration, identity verification and trust certificates
through IndraMind Cybersecurity, supported by a 24/7 security operations centre. The project aims to
connect central banks, commercial financial institutions and payment intermediaries across the eurozone
through sovereign, distributed infrastructure designed to support offline transactions.
• NOKIA – A bug in Nokia’s software caused an outage lasting up to nine hours on Orange’s mobile
network in France, affecting both its core network and backup equipment. Orange is seeking
compensation from Nokia. The incident raises questions about product reliability and could damage
Nokia’s reputation, prompting operators to scrutinise its software quality and network resilience more
closely.

• France: The National Assembly narrowly approved an amendment to the 2027 budget bill proposing a
5.5-cent-per-GB tax on internet traffic, drawing strong criticism from Free founder Xavier Niel, according
to Universfreebox. Introduced by MP Laurent Lhardit, the levy would target large content and streaming
platforms such as Netflix, YouTube, Disney+ and TF1, aiming to raise an estimated €5bn. Network
operators, including Free, Orange, SFR and Bouygues Telecom, would have to measure and report traffic
volumes, but content distributors would be legally responsible for paying the tax. Critics warn that
providers could pass the cost on to consumers through higher subscription prices and additional charges
for large video-game downloads.
