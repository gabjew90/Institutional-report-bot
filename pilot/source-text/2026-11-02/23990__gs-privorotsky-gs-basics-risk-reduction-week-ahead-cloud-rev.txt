GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Risk Reduction. Week Ahead. Cloud
Revenue Growth. 2 Nov 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
2 Nov 2026
Markets: Rebounding from a challenging end to last week. KOSPI +2%, Tokyo Electron +5% after
beating numbers and guiding to 87% operating profit growth, putting a bid back into the tech tape. Oil
down 3% as diplomatic channels remain open. Some welcome relief after Friday’s volatility, although
Kioxia down another 15% reminds us this isn’t an indiscriminate rebound. The big debate remains
whether we are seeing fundamentally deteriorating AI economics or forced liquidation of crowded
positions.
Flows/Positioning: Market de-grossing AI longs, with reports of substantial risk reduction. Quant
struggled as "The GS Equity Systematic L/S Performance Estimate fell -2.17% during the same period –
the worst weekly drawdown since Dec ’23". Our US data is a little more nuanced. Fundamental L/S nets
recovered to only the seventh percentile, largely through index short covering. "Single Stocks were net
sold for a 7th straight week, driven by long sales, while Macro Products were net bought for a 2nd straight
week, driven by long buys and to a lesser extent short covers." (PB) AAII Bull-Bear back negative, CNN
fear/greed into the 20s...sentiment/positioning has been reset.
AI/Hyperscalers: Supportive news flow early in week … then Intel down -29% on Friday. In contrast,
Amazon (a well owned position), went from down 7% pre-market to closing just 1.4% lower despite all
the FCF concerns. Stepping back Hyperscalers cloud growth delivered: Azure +48%, AWS +39%, Google
Cloud +103%… extraordinary demand (see below). That said, negative FCF, equity capital raises and
growing leverage requirements have raised legitimate questions about returns on capital. Imo Intel’s
warning, was more about consumer PCs, margins and manufacturing than AI demand itself. AMD
tomorrow important. The bigger AI debate is getting a bit circular. Half the market argues we cannot
possibly build enough power and data centers to support 2027 spending, so growth for the picks and
shovels must roll over and multiples compress. The other half argues we are about to massively
overproduce compute, destroying rental economics and again demanding lower multiples. I think the
bigger issue is that nobody really knows what to worry about. Strong demand is undeniable, but the return
on the next trillion dollars of investment is much less certain. When positioning is crowded, every new
concern becomes another reason to de-risk. We can debate magnitude but certainly less crowded this week
then the week before.
Midterms: Big political event tomorrow. Historically, the period following US midterms has been positive
for equities, with the removal of uncertainty often more important than the outcome itself. A lot of worry
is already reflected in positioning. Don’t want to speculate on the result… just think getting the event
behind us could be constructive, particularly after last week’s washout.

Oil/Iran: Oil down 3% is clearly welcome. Diplomatic overtures through Qatar continued over the
weekend, and the fact that negotiations remain alive is a positive. Still no agreement, and the range of
outcomes remains enormous. Unclear whether the diplomatic window survives the elections or whether
the US returns to escalation afterwards. I would much rather see diplomacy than another military
confrontation, but difficult to have particularly strong conviction here.
Treasury/BOE: QRA Wednesday feels potentially underappreciated. The big question is whether Treasury
offers some duration relief through smaller long end auctions, increased buybacks or a greater shift
towards bills. Interestingly, long end cuts weren’t explicitly raised in the dealer questionnaire, making an
immediate reduction less certain. A constructive surprise could be meaningful for both duration and
growth equities, while unchanged issuance might disappoint on the margin. BoE: Thursday’s 25bp hike to
4% is almost fully priced. The bigger question is whether Bailey validates the three to four additional
hikes priced over the next year. Feels a bit aggressive given weakening growth, although energy inflation
remains the obvious risk. Would lean towards the market overpricing the full tightening cycle.
Risk: Busy week. ISM manufacturing + Apple tonight is important as a relatively defensive tech name,
particularly what it says about iPhone demand and early Duo orders. AMD tomorrow is critical after Intel,
with Arista another useful read on AI networking. NFP friday Payroll consensus around +65k,
unemployment 4.2%, wages +0.3%. Feels almost impossible to navigate. Pretty much every client I speak
to is struggling. Last week felt like wholesale de-grossing… momentum unwinding, most shorted
squeezing, leverage coming out of the system. Defensives and quality through the roof while AI multiples
compress. The market is effectively pricing an abrupt growth slowdown, stalling AI capex, lower nominal
growth and rates, and a premium for quality balance sheets. The question is how much is signal versus risk
reduction, and whether multiples now discount enough bad news to create upside asymmetry. Hard to
argue with price, but nothing in last week’s hyperscaler results suggested spending intentions were
weakening… if anything, the opposite. Meanwhile, AI monetization continues to improve. With the cost
of capital repricing, major political events looming and investors protecting strong YTD gains, nobody
wants to add risk. My gut says we’re setting up for a painful year end rally… under owned, poorly
participated in and impossible to chase. Friday certainly felt like capitulation but think need to clear events
for risk to be added.
