#Markets : EStoxx -45bps to start as the AI unwind picks up again after the close. The US cash session rallied
into the bell, with the S&P finishing at the day's highs (-30bps) while Nasdaq lagged on disappointing
hyperscaler earnings and capex guidance. Post-close prints accelerated the move: Nasdaq futures are -1.9%
after AMZN (-7%) missed on AWS growth and margins, and INTC (-23%) beat on the quarter but guided light
on supply constraints. A SNDK beat offers little offset. It's stating the obvious, but the implied move for futures
is ~80bps on index weights alone, so contagion risk for the broader AI complex is evident – AI value chain
lower aft-hrs NVDA (-1.6%), Neoclouds all lower. That likely drives further rotation today, building on
yesterday's session where Value outperformed Growth by 2.7% and Consumer Staples (+1.6%) beat the SOX
(-1.4%), worth noting MDLZ +7.6% aft-mkt on results. Asia trading down in pockets in sympathy with the US
move. Kospi -54%, Taiex -2.5% and Japan sharply lower, leaving the Hang Seng (+1.4%) as the clear regional
outlier. The BOJ's hawkish hold, flagging that the inflation trend has reached its 2% target, has the yen firmer
near 157.60/USD. That sits within a broad dollar sell-off, with CHF topping the G10 board on haven demand.
The 10yr extends Thursday's rally to 4.92% (-2bps). WTI briefly broke $105 before settling back near $103
after Iran signalled no shift in its peace deal terms on receipt of Vance's letter, so the energy risk premium
stays firmly embedded into the weekend, ultimately +/- a few Dollars on Crude having muted impact on equity
markets for the time being. Lower yields providing some support, but the question into the weekend is whether
the AI complex sees an orderly rotation into defensives and value or a broader de-gross across momentum
and equity markets in general.
#BoJ : The BoJ was modestly hawkish, keeping a December hike firmly in play. The expected 8-1 hold came
with firmer inflation guidance, with FY2027 core CPI raised to 2.6% versus UBS’s 2.5% forecast and greater
emphasis on wage-price dynamics and inflation-expectation risks. The BoJ excluded the proposed food-tax
cut from its forecasts and accelerated ETF disposals from January 2027. Despite slightly soft FY2027 growth
at 0.7%, the overall message remains consistent with continued policy normalisation.
#Flow: Flows on Thursday finished balanced (50/50), with turnover remaining elevated as investors navigated
a busy earnings season. Banks were the busiest sector (17% of turnover) and finished better to buy, driven by
strong demand for Spanish names. Technology was also active (16% of turnover), with supply in semis, while
Healthcare (15% of turnover) saw demand as the sector ground higher. Industrials (12% of turnover) finished
balanced, with supply in electricals, demand in logistics and two-way flow in A&D. Elsewhere, Basic
Resources and Energy saw supply, while Food & Bev, Div Fins and Telecoms saw demand.
