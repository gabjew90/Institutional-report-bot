A more cautious tech mood is the main theme in markets as we go to print, as underwhelming results from Alphabet 
and Samsung have renewed doubts about the returns on AI spending. That comes after a decent session yesterday, 
which saw the S&P 500 (+0.28%) close within 1% of its record high following the Fed’s on-hold decision, which also 
led to a steepening in the Treasury curve. In Europe, the 1yr inflation swap reached its joint highest level since 
2023 ahead of today’s ECB decision as natural gas prices breached the EUR 100/MWh level. That said, oil prices are 
reversing much of yesterday’s rise overnight amid reports of renewed US-Iran outreach. 
Starting with the tech moves, Alphabet’s shares sank by almost -10% in after-hours trading, after its Q3 sales fell 
just shy of analyst expectations due to disappointing Google Services revenue ($96.3bn vs $98.4bn est.). With ad 
revenue growing at its slowest pace in five quarters, the results raised questions about whether the payoffs will 
come fast enough to justify the company’s soaring capex spend. Meanwhile, Samsung Electronics is trading about -
5% lower in Asia this morning after the operating profit in its chip unit came in below analysts’ optimistic 
expectations and the company delayed its new shareholder return plan. 
Microsoft did gain about +3% in extended trading after delivering a beat in cloud computing revenue (+48% yoy 
vs +44% est.) but this was insufficient to brighten the overall gloomy tech mood this morning. Nasdaq futures are 
trading -1.20% lower, with those on the S&P 500 down -0.35%. This is a notable change from yesterday’s session, 

which saw an ongoing rebound in the Philly semiconductor index (+1.75%). That was led by a surge for Oracle 
(+10.73%) as it projected a return to positive free cash flow as well as strong gains for CoreWeave (+7.32%), though 
the broader Nasdaq (+0.27%) saw only a modest gain. 
A more downbeat mood is also visible in most Asian markets this morning, with KOSPI (-1.20%) down after 
Samsung’s earnings, though it has recovered from about -3% down earlier on. Elsewhere, the CSI 300 (-1.20%) and 
the Shanghai Composite (-0.93%) are also trading lower. In contrast, the Nikkei (+0.66%) and the S&P/ASX 200 
(+0.45%) are bucking the broader regional weakness, while the Hang Seng (+0.06%) has fluctuated between gains 
and losses. Tech earnings will remain in focus today with Amazon and Intel reporting. 
Prior to the overnight moves, yesterday’s main event was the FOMC decision, which kept the fed funds rate at 3.75-
4.00% as widely expected. The decision saw two dissents, with Governor Cook and Cleveland Fed President 
Hammack voting in favour of a 25bp hike. The Committee upgraded its view on economic activity from “solid” to 
“strong” and noted still elevated inflation, but an on-hold decision was framed as allowing for “the accumulation of 
more information”. Chair Warsh repeatedly returned to the theme of strong growth in the press conference and 
noted that “while overall financial conditions are tighter, I am unable and unwilling to call them tight”. While 
Warsh continued to eschew forward guidance, his comments reinforced the September SEP signal that saw the vast 
majority of the FOMC expect another rate hike by year-end. Our economists continue to see the Fed raising rates by 
25bps in December and March. You can see their full Fed reaction here 
Away from policy, Warsh laid out a planned change in the FOMC meeting schedule by next fall, saying that in 2027, 
one of the eight FOMC meetings will be designated for a strategy discussion with the other seven being policy 
decision meetings. He signaled that in 2028 this would then shift to six policy meetings and two strategy meetings.  
Markets pared back Fed hike expectations following Warsh’s press conference, having gone into yesterday’s 
meeting pricing fully three 25bp hikes over the next year. By the close, pricing of a December hike eased from 91% 
to 84%, with 71bps of tightening priced by next September (-5.2 bps on the day). The Treasury curve saw a notable 
steepening in response. The 2yr yield ended the day -4.0bps lower at 4.70%, down from around 4.77% just before 
Warsh spoke. The 10yr yield closed +2.0bps higher at 5.00%, but is a couple of basis point lower again this morning. 
The curve steepening weighed on the dollar, with the euro yesterday (+0.26%) recovering from its 17-month low 
against the greenback. 
Central banks will again be in focus today, with the latest ECB decision, which is also widely expected to keep rates 
on hold after the September hike. Virtually nothing is priced for today’s meeting though a hike is 89% priced by 
December. Our economists’ baseline is a final hike to 2.75% in December, with some risk of a further hike to 3% in 
early 2027. For today, they see the ECB focus as being on communications. With energy prices rising above the 
September “adverse” scenario, some hawkishness is justified, but higher yields and tighter financial conditions 
leave room for a balanced overall tone. See their full ECB preview here. 
Ahead of the ECB meeting, European markets saw modest moves. Yields on 10yr bunds were +1.2bps higher, while 
those on OATs (-0.9bps) and BTPs (-0.3bps) edged lower. The bund sell-off was stronger at the front-end with the 
2yr yield rising +4.1bps amid a resurgence in energy costs. Front-month European natural gas rose above the EUR 
100 level for the first time since the 2022 energy crisis, settling +4.85% higher at EUR 100.72/MWh. The latest rise 
comes amid historically low European gas storage for this time of the year. However, the gas refilling outlook hasn’t 
really worsened of late so the rise of over +35% in natural gas prices so far this month appears to represent a rising 

risk premium heading into the winter and may well be overdone. The rise in energy put pressure on inflation pricing 
once again, with the 1yr euro inflation swap up +5.3bps to its joint-highest level since 2023 at 3.88%. 
Rising energy prices also meant European equities struggled to gain traction. The Stoxx 600 (+0.07%), FTSE 100 
(+0.07%), and CAC 40 (-0.00%) closed relatively unchanged, while Spain’s IBEX (-1.45%) registered a steeper fall. 
However, there were more positive moves in European credit, with IG (-4bps) and HY (-16bp) spreads tightening 
after their recent sell-off. 
While European gas prices reached new highs, oil had a more mixed 24 hours. Brent settled +1.26% higher at 
$115.32/bbl yesterday but is trading -1.15% lower this morning after the WSJ reported that US Vice President 
Vance is engaging with Mohsen Rezaei, the secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council and a key hard-
liner in Iran’s regime, to revive peace negotiations. Trump sought to downplay the report last night, saying that 
Vance’s outreach focused on offering medical supplies to Iran. The moves come amid concerns on possible re-
escalation between the US and Iran, with AP reporting yesterday evening that US officials have told allies to be 
prepared for renewed military action against Iran after next week’s midterm elections. 
In other Middle East news, the Israeli shekel (-2.06%) was the worst performing major currency yesterday, as Prime 
Minister Netanyahu’s bloc achieved a slight edge over the opposition bloc in Tuesday’s election. However, there 
is no clear path to a governing majority, as potential outcomes include complex coalition building, a broader unity 
government or fresh elections. See our EM strategists’ take on the election outcome and the path ahead here. 
Over in the UK, yesterday’s main focus was the first budget from Chancellor Healey. The key announcements 
included a 2pp additional tax on property and savings income from April, which comes on top of a 2pp rise last year 
and takes the basic, higher, and additional rates for property and savings income to 24%, 44%, and 49% respectively. 
Meanwhile, the bank tax increase was smaller than expected, from 3% to 6% for 15 months from January. Some 
energy reforms, like the removal of standing charges, were also proposed. Finally, Healey announced that he would 
lower the UK’s fiscal headroom to £15bn in 2029-30, before it rises back to £22bn in 2030-31. Our economists see 
the net impact of the budget as marginal, with Healey successfully buying himself more time as big questions 
remain regarding the UK’s long-term fiscal position. You can see our UK economists’ budget recap here. Gilts 
outperformed in response to the budget, with 10yr yields down -2.5bps to 5.32%. 
Finally, the Bank of Canada raised its policy rate by 25bps to 2.50% as expected. The statement said that “there 
could be a need to increase the policy rate further” but that the Governing Council agreed to proceed carefully, 
noting that there was still some excess supply in the economy. In the press conference, Governor Macklem said that 
the hike was “needed to reduce the risk that elevated energy prices could spill over and become generalized 
inflation”. Canada’s 2yr yield rose by +4.0bps in response, with money markets pricing another BoC hike by January. 
Looking to the day ahead, markets will continue to navigate an event-packed calendar that includes the ECB’s rate 
decision, US Q3 GDP, September PCE, and Eurozone October economic confidence data. Amazon, Intel, and Blue 
Owl are among those reporting earnings. The US will also hold an auction for $44bn of 7yr Notes. Finally, today 
marks the start of the EU-Canada summit.
