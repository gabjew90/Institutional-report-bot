 
Encased In Carbon-ite: Alphabet Post-Mortem As AI 
Mode Eats Search Pricing, Cash Burn Deepens, 
And Buybacks Vanish 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 29, 2026 - 06:15 PM 
Well, that was ugly: a few hours after we laid out everything the buyside wanted from Alphabet 
(GOOGL), the company delivered almost none of it, and the stock plunged after an ugly top and 
bottom line miss. This morning it is down roughly 11% premarket, around $290, dragging 
Nasdaq futures down 1% while the Russell is up. The only number that beat was Cloud, and 
even there the buyside was hoping for more. 
Having slept on it, the sellside verdict is in, and it's not much kinder than the after-hours tape. 
Below we go through what JPMorgan's trading desk and Goldman (via its trader Thales Arruda 
and internet analyst Eric Sheridan) make of the wreckage, plus a few things in the filing that the 
headlines missed... like the fact that a company that bought back $39.8 billion of stock in the 
first nine months of 2025 has bought back zero in 2026, and is now selling it instead. 
"A Straight Up Miss" 
We start with JPMorgan's Mark Schilsky, who last week gave Alphabet a middling 5 out of 10 on 
his expectations gauge and summed up the pre-print debate as "Street (Consensus) Fighter II: 
Search vs. Cloud." Overnight, writing in the bank's Market Intel morning briefing (available to pro 
subs), he was less playful: 
The Stock is Now Encased in Carbon-ite: GOOGL is trading down -7% after hours after printing 
a quarter than I can only characterize as a straight up miss. Search missed Buyside estimates 
by -2%! Despite query growth being the highest it's been in 5 years…pricing is declining way 
worse than investors were thinking. YouTube missed! (And no one cares about YouTube!).

Best Query Growth In Five Years... Slowest Search Growth Of 
The Year 
This is the part that should worry bulls the most. In the earnings release, Pichai boasted that 
"our year-over-year query growth rate is the highest it's been in the last five years." And yet 
Search & Other revenue grew just 12% to $63.35 billion, down from 16.8% in Q2 and 19.1% in 
Q1, and well short of the ~14% buyside bar. 
Put differently, Google is answering more questions than ever and getting paid less for each 
one. 
 

 
Management offered several culprits. Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler called FX "the 
single largest driver of search just relative to Q2," and Goldman's TMT desk (available to pro 
subs) adds two more "vertical specific dynamics": i) some APAC retailers hit by the EU 
eliminating the de minimis exemption, and ii) media and entertainment spend normalizing after 
the FIFA World Cup. Bernstein threw in the pull-forward of Prime Day ad spend into Q2. 
But the one that matters is AI Mode. Schindler conceded "the lower monetization we were 
seeing in AI mode," while insisting that "the majority of AI mode queries are incremental, 
creating more monetization opportunities over time." Over time is doing a lot of work in that 
sentence. Barclays' Ross Sandler zeroed in on management "prioritizing user experience first" 
and monetization later, noting that blue-link queries are not growing as fast as total queries, and 
that more aggressive agentic flows in Search next year may make that worse. His verdict: an 
investor "code yellow," though not an "AI roadkill" scenario. 
Morgan Stanley, meanwhile, points out that the gap between Alphabet's search growth and 
Meta's advertising growth has hit a new recent peak of roughly 13 points. Recall that Meta was 
the one that got crushed after earnings last week. 
And YouTube? It grew 9.2%, dipping below the +10% line that Schilsky said last week would let 
investors "continue to ignore it." Well, consider it noticed: 
 
It won't get easier from here. CFO Anat Ashkenazi warned that "we would expect a slightly 
larger FX headwinds to our consolidated revenues in Q4 relative to the prior quarter," and 
Search is still lapping the acceleration that began in Q3 2025. In other words, the comps get 
harder and the currency gets worse, just as the agentic products that are supposed to fix 
monetization are still being rolled out.

So who is paying for all this? Well, increasingly, not Alphabet's own cash flow. Buried in the 
release is a financing section that would have been unthinkable a year ago. In the third quarter, 
Alphabet: 
●​ bought back $0 of stock, vs. $11.5 billion in Q3 2025 (and $0 for the entire first nine 
months of 2026, vs. $39.8 billion a year earlier); 
●​ sold 12 million Class A shares for $4.3 billion and 24 million Class C shares for $8.1 
billion through its at-the-market program, $12.4 billion of new stock, with $27.6 billion still 
left on the ATM; 
●​ issued $28.7 billion of senior unsecured notes, pushing quarterly interest expense to 
$967 million from $143 million a year ago. 
Some napkin math: the ATM sales imply average prices of roughly $358 for the A shares and 
$338 for the C shares. With the stock at ~$290 premarket, Alphabet sold stock 15-20% above 
where it trades today, which may be the best trade it made all quarter. 
Add it all up and the swing is staggering. In the first nine months of 2025, Alphabet returned 
$44.4 billion to investors through its financing line. In the first nine months of 2026, it raised a 
net $115.8 billion, including $42.9 billion of common stock (with $10 billion from Berkshire), 
$19.1 billion of mandatory convertible preferred and $76.3 billion of net new debt: 
 

 
 
Regular readers will hardly be surprised: we flagged the shift the day Alphabet announced its 
$80 billion equity raise back on June 1, and asked whether it marked a sea-change in one of the 
market's most powerful drivers, the megacap buyback bid. And as we have been warning since 
last October, when we showed that AI had quietly become a debt bubble too, the AI buildout 
runs on external capital, whether it comes from bondholders or, now, from shareholders being 
diluted: 
Management, for its part, says it expects "to generate an attractive ROIC that remains 
meaningfully above our cost of capital." Maybe. But the cost of capital just went up: the stock 
Alphabet is selling to fund the build is now 11% cheaper. 
Broadening, Take Two 
The market's reaction this morning is a familiar one. Here is JPMorgan's Market Intel desk, in its 
morning briefing (available to pro subs): 
GOOG earnings are a bit of a setback for the nascent re-ignition of the AI / Tech trade and 
today's pre-mkt price action point to a resumption of the broadening trend we have seen over 
the last few weeks. Given the economic strength and robust earnings cycle, we may see an 
extension of this move similar to what we saw in earlier in the year. The Dovish press 
conference was a surprise but keep an eye on upcoming Fedspeak, which kicks off on Friday, 
for additional cues. Middle East escalation remains another key risk as we await the completion

The desk notes that premarket "Mag7 dragged lower by GOOG with Semis and Memory 
underperforming the broader Tech tape and Software is down 3%," while Discretionary and

That broadening was already well underway before last night. Through Tuesday's close, 
Communication Services, home of Alphabet and Meta, was the worst S&P sector in October, 
down 8.2%, with Tech the only other sector in the red. Energy, Staples, Utilities and Health Care

Price Targets Follow Price 
And then came the inevitable parade of price-target cuts, with most brokers trimming their 
targets while keeping their ratings. Bernstein went to $325 from $385 (Market Perform), BofA to 
$380 from $430, JPMorgan to $375 from $425, Morgan Stanley to $375 from $400, Barclays to 
$405 from $425 and Susquehanna to $400 from $460. Citizens was the lone downgrade, to 
Market Perform, while Canaccord held its Buy and $450 target: 
 
Even after the cuts, the median target sits about 30% above the premarket price, which is either 
a screaming buy signal or a reminder that price targets tend to follow price, not the other way 
around. BofA notes that at ~$291, the stock trades at about 19x its 2027 earnings and 16x 
2028, with core search valued at just 10x. Cheap, if you believe 2028 earnings are real and that 
nobody else claims them first. 
Here is the thing, though: the bull case still rests on the same three-step sequence it did 
yesterday. Search keeps funding the build, Cloud and TPUs turn backlog into profit, and agentic 
AI eventually monetizes all those "incremental" AI Mode queries. Last night, step one wobbled, 
step two was fine, and step three remains a promise for 2027. Meanwhile, the funding is shifting 
from Search cash flow to the ATM and the bond market. 
Goldman's Arruda is right that we're moving "from a market rewarding capex growth to one 
demanding evidence of returns." The dip buyers may well show up, as both Goldman and JPM 
 

 
expect. But for a company that was the poster child of self-funded AI just a year ago, zero 
buybacks, $12 billion of stock sold, $29 billion of bonds issued and a $350 billion capex bill 
coming in 2027 is not the evidence the market was asking for. If anything, it's the opposite. Then 
again, with query growth at a five-year high, at least Google will be able to search for the 
answer.
