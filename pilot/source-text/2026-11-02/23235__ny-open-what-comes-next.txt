NY Open - What comes next
By Varshi Karamsetty
Active session overnight post FOMC with UST rally holding and extending, supported by RM paying flows. USD stalls,
while FOMC was largely a non-event for stocks. Instead, weaker tech earnings weighed on Kospi/Asia tech, while
Nasdaq futures are down -0.9% at print. Oil markets only slightly weaker despite further headlines that US is involved
with ongoing negotiations on a temporary peace deal with Iran. ECB President Lagarde confirmed she is stepping down
on Feb 7, confirming months of speculation and ahead of her term expiry in Oct 2027. EUR FI were rallying overnight,
with EURUSD steady at 1.115.
US data dump at 08:30 ET with an expected acceleration in Q3 GDP, core PCE should be realized at 0.31%. Personal
income/consumption data also release with weekly jobless claims. ECB is expected to hold rates at 09:15 ET but more
focus on Lagarde's presser on any signals on France and further comments on her departure. Final Israel general
election results are due tonight, with the majority of votes counted, a deadlock scenario is likely with Israeli-Arab parties
important for coalition formation. US 7y auction due, Amazon and Intel report after the close. President Trump is in South
Carolina today.

In Focus‌
Rates: FOMC held rates in line with expectations in a 10-2 vote, but Warsh sounded dovish as he said he didn't think the
economy is running hot. Coupled with fewer "live" meetings, limiting the scope of rate hikes, USTs rallied as much as 10bp.
Momentum stalls overnight but gains are held, US 10s steady at the 5% handle. Our desk saw better outright paying of RM
across the curve, especially in the 10y segment and some sellers of long end spreads. We also saw two-way flow in Dec
FOMC, 0-2yr SOFR saw better receiving, with HFs being active in the <1yr and banks receiving the 1.5y-2y sector. Our
economists continue to expect a 25bp rate hike delivered. 
Prepare for a US data dump at 08:30 ET including core PCE MoM, Advance Q3 GDP, Personal
Consumption/Spending/Income and weekly jobless claims. Our economists expect core PCE at 0.31% MoM post CPI/PPI
data, with supercore rising to 0.35%. Uptick in GDP would be considered hawkish, but details will matter. Continued AI-related
capital expenditures should support activity higher at 3.1%, but residential investment will likely remain a drag. Markets will
likely be more sensitive to downside surprises in growth than details of PCE.

ECB follows at 09:15 ET, with President Lagarde's press conference at 09:45. After months of rumors, Lagarde confirmed she
is stepping down as president from Feb 7, before her term officially expires Oct 31. Our economists had previewed their
thoughts here, expecting de Cos and Knot as current contenders for the role. Back to the decision, expect a hold but more
attention on whether Lagarde addresses French spread weakness and the potential use of ECB tools. Expect careful
wording, but ultimately explicit guidance should be avoided. OAT volatility may soon not just limit further hikes but prevent
them: our strategists are received Dec26 ECB €STR. Kocher speaks at 12:00 GMT. Just out - Belgian HICP comes stronger
than expected at 5.26% YoY vs 4.69% prior. More regional data release tomorrow - Full HICP preview here.
Equities: Mixed tech earnings (Alphabet and Samsung disappointed, Microsoft beat) on net drove tech stocks weaker, with
Nasdaq futures down 1% at print. Kospi traded as low as -3% before recovering -1.2% into the close, Japan stocks held
higher. Generally positive sentiment in Europe, with our strategist flagging CGDOSOHO (Iran deal squeeze risk) higher after
headlines of mediation efforts from the US. FOMC was a non-event for US stocks, CETS continues to like the combination of
large cap growth and strong balance sheet stocks through QQQ, hyper-scalers (CGUSHYPR). Intel, Amazon are the tech
heavyweights reporting after the cash close today. 
FX: Continue to sell rallies in EURUSD towards 1.1310, we also remain bearish EUR across various crosses - EURAUD,
EURGBP, EURCHF. EURNOK is FX Strategy's preferred vehicle which should benefit from US/Iran re-escalation. Our London
trader thinks EURJPY works into BoJ Fri. Interesting from our SG trader - MoF data released overnight revealed net sale of
foreign bonds for a 6th consecutive week by Japanese investors and is corresponding with recent long-dated JGB
performance to some degree. It could be starting to signal a gradual yet increasingly evident of capital turning around. Along
with BoJ entering a ‘new phase’ of policy, it is building into a more compelling case of stronger JPY from here and he am
inclined to think that a top may already be in place. Build shorts in USDJPY into 158.00-50, with stop above 160 the play
worth exploring. 
Elsewhere, AUDNZD sharp move higher post RBNZ seems outsized. RBNZ Breman was clear overnight stating a high
likelihood of a Dec hike. Nov 7 elections are being treated as a potential negative event but may not be. Our trader has
bought AUDNZD mid-Dec put spreads and is bearish on the cross. 
Commodities: Constructive headlines overnight follows WSJ reports that US VP Vance sent a letter to Iran on the peace
framework, at direction of President Trump. According to Semafor, the US administration is involved in ongoing negotiations
around a temporary peace deal, though roadblocks remain before it can advance. Muted response in oil with markets likely
wanting more substantial development to retrace longs. ICYMI, Citi Research warned that Israeli political gridlock draws a
more binary outlook for oil, but a deal by year-end remains our base case. Dig deeper. Gold is down -1%, but $4000
psychological handle continues to hold. 
Elsewhere‌
SEK: Sep/Q3 GDP printed at 2.1% YoY/0.4% QoQ vs 3.5%/1.4% respectively prior. Despite the downtick, this remains the
fastest growth since 2019 (except post lockdown period). Separately, our economist flags that the Economic Tendency survey
reported the highest level of businesses optimism since mid-2022, confirming the recovery in GDP is real. The case for
Riksbank hikes is building, but ultimately depends on inflation trends. We retain our base case of a Dec hike.
JPY: Tokyo CPI YoY for October tonight 19:30 ET. Bloomberg consensus expects headline unchanged at 2.7% YoY with core
(Ex fresh food, energy) easing to 2.7% YoY vs 3% prior. Inflation running above 2% supports expected hawkish messaging
from the Oct BoJ meeting. Also on the radar - jobless rate (23:30 GMT), retail sales and IP for September at 23:50 GMT. 
CHF: SNB's Tschudin speaks at Money Market Event in Geneva at 13:00 ET so will unlikely be policy relevant.
CAD: BoC Deputy Gov. Vincent opens John Kuszczak Memorial Lecture. Remarks will not be published on the BoC website
though live audio/video webcast will be available.
ILS: Final general election results are expected tonight after absentee ballots are counted but unlikely sway the current result.
PM Netanyahu's coalition is projected to win 53 seats versus the opposition Eisenkot's bloc, below expectations at 48,
resulting in a deadlock scenario. Two Israeli Arab parties doubled their seats to 19, making them the potential kingmakers
during negotiations to secure a majority. Recall 61 seats are required for a majority, Netanyahu will remain in power as
coalition negotiations begin. 

Desk flows continue to see large election-related ILS long unwinds. The daily technicals favor buying pullbacks, with room
before momentum becomes stretched: RSI(14) is 59.6. The 20D/55D/100D/200D EMAs are 3.0850/3.0555/3.0415/3.07, all
rising, our local desk trader flags. For the next 24 hours, the test is whether coalition headlines can produce a credible route
to government. That could remove risk premium quickly; prolonged deadlock leaves dips better supported.
BRL closed flat on Wed after panic flows post elections started to ease. While the potential upside in BRL (due to
expectations of reforms and all) has disappeared for now, the good old framework remains valid: carry vs fiscal risks.
USDBRL should remain between the 5.30-5.40 until we get some fiscal clarity, but medium-term, our trader remains long
BRL. As we go to print, FGV inflation comes at 1.47% MoM/5.24% YoY, a slight miss to consensus. 
UAH: Key Rate Decision at 08:00 ET. Our economists expect NBU to raise rates by 50bps to 16.50%, thus delivering third
hike in a row. The decision would be warranted by inflation developments, where headline CPI increased already to 10% in
September.
EGP: Deposit Rate Decision. Market consensus is for a rate hold at 19%.
