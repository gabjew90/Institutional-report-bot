 
Flash Crash: Kioxia Warns NAND Price Surge 
Ending, Sending Sandisk Plunging 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, OCT 30, 2026 - 03:10 PM 
In memory, the only number that matters is the second derivative. This morning it turned. 
Sandisk did just about everything right last night: a revenue and EPS beat, 85% gross margins, 
a guide above the Street, another $5 billion of buybacks and an 8-for-1 stock split for good 
measure. And the stock went... nowhere after hours. Then, at 5:54am ET, Bloomberg reported 
that Sandisk's Japanese fab partner Kioxia had guided its profit below estimates while warning 
that the AI-fueled surge in flash memory prices was easing, and that was that: Sandisk tumbled 
as much as 8% premarket to a session low of $1,197.57, down 16% from Wednesday's high of 
$1,419, with Micron (MU) sliding in sympathy. 
 
Goldman's TMT desk summed it up in one line this morning in its TMT Today note (available to 
pro subs): "SNDK -7% despite beat (MU lower in sympathy)... Kioxia guidance missed (further 
negative datapoint in Memory/Semis)." JPMorgan's Market Intel desk had the memory ETF 
(DRAM) down 5.6% premarket as "renewed fears over AI Monetization" (and a 25% crash in 
 

 
Intel, which blamed memory prices for a "surprisingly severe PC correction") hit the entire semi 
complex. 
Quite a turnaround from Wednesday, when futures were rising on "Hynix-fueled memory 
mania". 
From 70% To "Mid-20s" 
Here is the paragraph that did the damage. Kioxia said it expects operating profit of ¥2 trillion 
($12.7 billion) in the current quarter, well short of estimates of ¥2.35 trillion, and as Bloomberg's 
Takashi Mochizuki writes: 
That's after the average selling price of NAND flash memory rose a mid-20% in the three 
months to September, compared with 70% in the prior quarter. Kioxia's outlook suggests the 
unprecedented price spikes of the past year or so are starting to level off. 
The September quarter itself was fine, even spectacular: operating profit rose 22-fold from a 
year ago to ¥1.99 trillion, in line with estimates, on revenue of roughly ¥2.48 trillion (up 40% 
QoQ), and Kioxia declared an interim dividend of ¥90 per share, the first in its history. The 
problem is what comes next. Do the napkin math and the ¥2 trillion guide implies operating 
profit growth of... roughly zero in the December quarter, after a 57% sequential jump in the 
September quarter (and the consensus ¥2.35 trillion had baked in another ~18%). 
 
In other words, prices are still going up, just at roughly a third of the pace they were three 
months ago, and for stocks priced on the assumption that the curve only goes vertical, that is a 
problem. Bloomberg also notes Kioxia's executives "have warned against excessive price rises 
for fear of alienating customers," which is a polite way of saying the customers have started to 
push back. Ask Intel, which this morning guided to a ~12% drop in PC units this year and 
 

 
another ~10% in 2027, citing "memory constraints" among the culprits. In memory (as in 
everything else), nothing cures record prices like record prices. 
Kioxia's own stock, by the way, had already fallen around 60% from its June peak before the 
print (Goldman's Jay Narain notes it has still returned 2,400% since its December 2024 IPO), 
and since the results came out after the Tokyo close, Kioxia holders get the whole weekend to 
think about it. 
Spot Already Told You 
None of this should surprise regular readers. Back on June 25, the day the memory trade 
peaked, we showed that NAND spot prices had stopped going up back in March, and with a few 
exceptions, prices are now the lowest they have been in 7 months. 
 
A month later we followed up with "NAND Price Drop Is Canary In AI Stock Trade Coalmine" 
(Jul 27), at which point Kioxia had already retraced 40% from its peak. Contract prices, which 
reset with a lag, kept climbing for two more quarters, and that lag is what is now closing. Spot 
rolled over in March; contract growth just went from 70% to the mid-20s. It doesn't take a 
semiconductor PhD to guess which way the next contract reset leans. 
Meanwhile, the supply response we have been flagging all year keeps coming. Kioxia itself 
plans to spend more than ¥5 trillion with Sandisk on Japanese capacity over the next six years, 
funded in part by a US ADR listing next year that could raise at least $10 billion. As we put it 
when the first leg of that buildout was announced: 
Add Micron's $24 billion Singapore NAND fab, China's YMTC grabbing 13% of the global NAND 
market (tied with Sandisk), and DRAM champion CXMT entering the "booming" flash market in 
 

 
September, and you get the picture. Even Samsung disappointed on NAND overnight: 
Goldman's Giuni Lee said its NAND profit was "slightly lower than our expectations (slightly 
lower bit shipment and ASP)". 
"Beat But Not Enough," The Sequel 
Which brings us back to Sandisk, whose quarter was, objectively, solid. Goldman's TMT desk 
recap: 
Quarterly results above the Street: SanDisk reported revenue of $11.03 bn, in line with GS at 
$11.22 bn and above Street (Visible Alpha) at $10.74 bn, while gross margin of 85.1% was in 
line with GS at 85.7% and the Street at 84.9%. Non-GAAP EPS of $49.51 was in line with GS at 
$49.80 but above the Street at $46.73.​
​
Datacenter and KV cache commentary: Management struck a bullish tone on its datacenter 
business, noting that it now accounts for roughly half of industry bit demand (up from ~20% in 
2020) and that it expects revenue mix to shift further toward this end market (up from 43.5% 
FY1Q27). The company attributed this growth to KV cache... SanDisk expects KV cache to 
account for ~35% of the AI datacenter market by 2030, with datacenter expected to represent 
over 55% of the NAND market in CY27.​
​
4Q guidance is above the Street on revenue and EPS: Revenue was guided to $12.4 bn at the 
midpoint, which is below GS at $13.55 bn but above the Street at $12.08 bn... Non-GAAP EPS 
guidance of $55.00 - $57.00 (midpoint of $56.00) is below GS at $59.71 but above the Street at 
$52.94. 
Note the detail: the guide beat the Street but fell well short of Goldman's own numbers ($12.4 
billion vs $13.55 billion in revenue), which is probably a decent proxy for where the buyside 
whisper was sitting. Management also said multiple customers "had previously underestimated 
their NAND requirements" and want more bits, which Sandisk will happily sell "at newly 
negotiated, higher prices." Kioxia, which shares fabs with Sandisk, just told us how much higher: 
a lot less than before. 
If this feels familiar, it is because we have seen this movie before. Back in August, Sandisk also 
beat on every line and the stock tumbled anyway, prompting this from the same Goldman desk: 
Fast forward three months and Goldman's Jim Schneider reports in his earnings review 
(available to pro subs) that the long-term agreements "now cover ~50% of planned FY27 and 
 

 
65% of FY28 bit capacity." That's the same coverage as in August, even though Sandisk closed 
two more deals (10 agreements with 8 unique customers to date). The floor pricing is a real 
cushion, but half of next year's bits are still exposed to whatever the contract market does, and 
Kioxia just told everyone what the contract market is doing. 
To be fair, Schneider remains a buyer, arguing the NAND market "is seeing very limited supply 
additions for the foreseeable future," lifting CY26-CY28 EPS estimates ~7% and pointing to 
$10.5 billion left on the buyback authorization. His opening line last night was also quite 
confident: "We expect the stock to trade modestly higher following a strong quarter." It was, until 
5:54am. 
And yes, the 8-for-1 split takes effect November 20. At least the losses will look smaller. 
The Fed Is Watching Too 
There is one more party that won't mind seeing memory prices cool: the Fed. As we noted on 
Wednesday, when Warsh blamed rising rates for hammering AI: 
Memory price inflation has been leaking into everything from PCs to phones to servers, and 
Goldman's Rich Privorotsky noted this morning that Intel's PC business is "contracting as 
memory inflation drives hardware costs higher." Slower NAND price hikes are good news for the 
CPI and the PC makers. For the memory stocks, which have been priced for the opposite, not 
so much. 
Peak Pricing, Or Just A Breather? 
The bulls will say that NAND prices rising "only" mid-20% a quarter is still an extraordinary 
market, that data center demand (now half of all NAND bits, per Sandisk) is structural, and that 
Kioxia's management has been deliberately conservative to avoid "alienating customers." All fair 
points. Goldman will host its Korea & Japan memory analysts on Sunday evening to make that 
case, and the bank's China desk said it was still a buyer of memory overnight. 
But memory is a cyclical business that spent the past year convincing itself it was a structural 
one, and the tell has always been the rate of change. Spot NAND peaked in March. Contract 
price growth just went from 70% to the mid-20s. Kioxia is guiding flat profits while spending ¥5 
trillion on new capacity, China is adding supply on every front, and the biggest buyers (PC 
makers, smartphone OEMs, Intel's customers) are cutting units because they can't afford the 
chips. We'd bet the mid-20s is the next stop on the way down, not the bottom, and with the 
Bloomberg piece warning that Big Tech's $750 billion AI spending spree is colliding with slowing 
 

 
core growth, the hyperscalers that are supposed to absorb all those new bits may soon start 
negotiating harder too. 
Or, as Goldman's desk put it back in August: fundamentals still supportive, but expectations very 
high. Three months later, the fundamentals are a bit less supportive, and the expectations 
haven't moved.
