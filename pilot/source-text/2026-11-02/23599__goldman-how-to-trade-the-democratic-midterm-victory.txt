Sweep-stakes
Tuesday's midterms are approaching, and betting markets now put the probability of a Democratic
sweep at 67%.
Goldman has its trading playbook ready. Long healthcare and renewables. Short Big Pharma, defense,
crypto and potentially the AI complex.
The twist? It is less about which laws Democrats can pass and more about which sectors suddenly find
themselves in Washington's crosshairs. Here are the trades.
Running hard to stand still
Before the trades, the bigger question: can Tuesday finally get Nasdaq out of its multi-month trading
range?
As King Callahan puts it, NDX is "running very hard to stand very still."
Bloomberg
The AI Power trade gets political
The AI trade has a new political problem: the electricity bill.
Democrats want to protect households from soaring power costs, potentially forcing data centers to
shoulder more of the infrastructure burden.
The question is no longer whether America needs more AI infrastructure. It is who gets stuck paying for
it.
Goldman: Long renewables and grid infrastructure. Buy protection on hyperscalers and data-center
equipment.

Long healthcare, short Big Pharma
Democrats want to revive ACA subsidies, potentially boosting patient volumes and reducing
uncompensated care for healthcare providers.
The other side of the trade? More aggressive drug-price negotiations.
Goldman: Long ACA beneficiaries. Short drugmakers.

Marquee
Long Government, short defense
Democrats have traditionally pushed for domestic spending to rise alongside defense budgets.
Goldman sees potential upside for government contractors, research institutions and healthcare-
related spending beneficiaries, while defense-exposed equities could face relative pressure.
Goldman: Long non-defense government spending. Short defense beneficiaries.

Wall Street, Meet Washington
A Democratic victory could mean a few less friendly faces in Washington.
Short Big Tech: Antitrust is back on the menu.
Short investment banks and crypto: More regulators, fewer friends.
Short tariff-sensitive stocks: And don't expect Democrats to rescue you from tariffs. Goldman
actually sees more upside than downside in tariffs into early 2027.
Long: homebuilders
Housing affordability remains a political priority. Expanded housing tax credits and first-time buyer
incentives could support the sector. The chart shows homebuilder index vs equal-weight SPX.
Goldman: Long homebuilders.

Death & Taxes
Here Goldman is less alarmist. With Trump retaining veto power and the 21% corporate tax rate
already permanent, Goldman sees little immediate earnings risk for 2027–28.
Further out, however, high-tax companies could face a less friendly valuation backdrop.
The tax bill can wait. The market doesn't have to.
