It was a very choppy US session with plenty of information to digest, albeit mixed. The FOMC left rates
unchanged, as anticipated, in a 10-2 vote, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Hammack
dissenting in favour of a 25bp hike. Chair Kevin Warsh gave no clear signal regarding a December hike,
though he noted that the Committee has “made clear our readiness to act as circumstances require, and we
have shown our willingness to act,” adding that “it’s our responsibility to get inflation down.”
The messaging feels somewhat strange, particularly Warsh’s reference to seeing more data given that the
market has had plenty of it since the September FOMC that would, at the very minimum, support a hawkish
stance and tone toward the December FOMC. This is even more notable given Warsh acknowledged that the
“FOMC’s discussion over the last couple of days focused mainly on the price stability side of our remit”. The
statement additionally pointed to economic activity expanding at a strong – rather than solid – pace in
September.
Adding to the ambiguity, Warsh announced that in 2027, one meeting will focus exclusively on broad monetary
policy strategy, with neither a vote on the federal funds rate nor a policy statement, with that becoming two
meetings in 2028. Perhaps one shouldn’t be too surprised given his goal is likely flatter distribution curves with
fatter tails.
USTs ended mixed, with the curve twist-steepening amid front-end outperformance, while the long end lagged,
driven by both a lack of clarity around a December rate hike and the inherent ambiguity that comes with
reducing the number of meetings in the future at which the FOMC will vote on changes to the Fed funds rate.

Alphabet Search Miss Revives AI Cannibalization Risk, Weighs On Nasdaq – Alphabet’s (GOOGL) Q3
Search growth of 11.8% vs. 14-14.5% buyside and 15% Street expectations, puts AI-driven Search
cannibalization risk firmly back in focus, particularly against Meta’s advertising strength and the lingering threat
from Muse and agent-based products. YouTube also fell short at 11% growth versus a 12% bogey, while total
operating income of $41 bn missed expectations of $44-45 bn. The weakness therefore flowed through
beyond the top-line Search miss. The AI and cloud read-through offered limited offset. Feedback from the
earnings call was notably negative, with Search emerging as the key pressure point and the debate quickly
shifting from whether the miss was priced into what it says about the growth trajectory from here. The broader
concern is how Search economics evolve as Meta Muse and other agents gain traction. GOOGL increasingly
looks likely to face the classic innovator’s dilemma: launch an agent that can compete in the emerging
category, but potentially pressure its own economics in the process. Nasdaq futures fell as much as 1% after
the close as Alphabet sold off 9% in after-market trading on a Q3 EPS miss and lower-than-anticipated capex,

Samsung Electronics Q3 Results Miss Estimates; Memory Outlook Remains Firm – Samsung
Electronics’ Q3 results fell short of expectations despite record memory earnings. Revenue of KRW195.3 trn
was 7% below UBS Research’s latest estimate and 3% below consensus, while operating profit of KRW107.4
trn missed UBS by 9% but was broadly in line with consensus. Net income missed consensus by a wider 14%,
partly due to KRW9.5 trn in net finance expenses. Memory remained the bright spot as HBM4 sales ramped
and server SSD demand strengthened, keeping the core AI-memory thesis intact. Shareholder returns was
more positive, with Samsung indicating KRW90-110 trn for 2026, including KRW55-75 trn still to be distributed,
above investor expectations ranging from no further return to KRW50 trn in Q4. A “substantial portion” is
expected through share buybacks and cancellations, while the policy from 2027 onwards will be announced
after the late-January board meeting. However, the earnings shortfall, alongside planned 2026 capex of
KRW84.4 trn, weighed on sentiment, with the shares falling 2.5% immediately after the release. (Kevin Cheng,
Equity Sales)

Fed’s Warsh Says Task Forces Have Year-End Deadline, But Some Will Miss That – While commenting
on the continued work of the five task forces, Chair Warsh said some may need more time than the year-end
deadline to submit their findings. The task force on productivity has an important upcoming session on Nov. 18
for a “field hearing”, listening to commentary from experts. Warsh said the balance sheet task force will likely
offer its findings early in Q1 next year. However, there is likely to be a long period of consideration into any
conclusions before the Fed decided whether to take subsequent action or not. Warsh said, “I think it is unlikely
to have any material effect on this policy conjuncture.” (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)

Korean Business Optimism Hits Four-Year High – The BoK’s October composite business sentiment index
rose 5.1 points m/m to 104.2, the highest since August 2022, driven by improved confidence in both
manufacturing and nonmanufacturing. Manufacturing sentiment advanced 6.1 points to 105.6 while
nonmanufacturing rose 4.3 to 103.2. The BoK cited increased investment and production in semiconductors,
automobiles and shipbuilding, supporting upstream industries, while a stronger won helped ease imported
raw-material costs. November’s outlook improved 2.2 points to 101.8, above the optimism threshold of 100.

corporate sentiment alone. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Looking Beneath BoT’s Hold – UBS Economics: The Bank of Thailand maintained its policy rate at 1% in
October, signalling a slightly more hawkish tone than August due to stronger growth forecasts and heightened
inflation risks linked to oil prices and El Niño. It raised its GDP growth projections to 2.4% for 2026 and to 2.1%
for 2027, driven by robust exports and investment in technology and AI. However, economist Grace Lim notes
that the recovery remains uneven, constrained by high household debt and weak consumption. Inflation risks
have also intensified, with headline inflation now forecast at 2.1% in 2026, though the BoT views these
pressures as temporary. While the BoT has not closed the door to further rate cuts, Grace thinks the October
meeting suggests that the hurdle for further easing has risen. She anticipates the next move to be a 25bp rate
hike in Q4 2027, contingent on broader domestic growth. A significant downturn could reopen discussions on
easing. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Market Movers
FOMC Schedule Change Drives Sharp Swap Curve Steepening – The FOMC voted 10-2 to hold the target
range steady, with Hammack and Cook dissenting. While the hold was not a surprise, Cook’s dissent and the
absence of dissents from Logan and Kashkari were. The biggest reveal from Chair Warsh’s presser was the
new meeting schedule starting in fall 2027. While the FOMC will maintain eight meetings, one will focus only
on monetary policy discussion, with no rate decision or policy statement, and the committee envisions the split
becoming six-two in 2028. The market read the schedule change as one less opportunity to hike, and the swap
curve strongly twist-steepened during and after the presser, with 5s30s steepening 6bp from the lows.
Spreads were also massively bid during the presser, with 10y spreads reaching -37.8 before the move faded
and they closed close to pre-FOMC levels. (Emma Cai, Rates Trading)
