All references to “we/us/our” refer to the views and observations of the desk.
Industrials were up 210bps on the week bringing the month to date above 300bps with materials up
similarly on the week but less on the month. Both outperforming broader markets down 50bps weighed
down by tech. Hit peak earnings with half of the coverage reporting. Will continue to digest all the
nuances over the weekend – reach out if you would like to discuss takeaways. By subsector defense,
aerospace and autos led with transports, data center and airlines lower. At the top of the board from a
single stock perspective included better earnings across many under owned names including HAYW
+27% with the pool end market showing modest signs of improvement. BAH up 14% more a function of
short squeeze across defense and Fed IT with more mixed earnings, TXT +12% with no charge taken with
Q3 earnings report that was expected, IP up 9% with signs of the company specific turnaround in place
and FTV up similar with low expectations and better Q3/guide update. On the flip side we saw worse
earnings from OI down 22%, DAN off 15%, several transports – TFII LSTR CHRW down 12-14% and
BA off 9% after lowering expectations for timing of free cash flow goals. Next week we’ll get the US
midterms, a fresh set of data points including the ISM, class 8 truck orders, auto sales and the last heavy
week of Q3 earnings before heading into the final two months of 2026.
AI/data center and valuation has been very topical in the last few days and a noticeable change from prior
periods of momentum / AI sell offs. Lots of comparisons between AI industrials vs. other names across the
space – debate whether NVT should trade cheaper than OTIS for example looking out a bit. Most assume
the answer is no. Will try to frame both sides of the debate here. On the positive side clearly AI/data center
related industrials are growing much faster than most everything else across the sector and are exposed to
an area that is deemed to be more secular growth. But is that growth peaking with NVT orders below
organic growth for example in Q3. On the other hand the growth came out of nowhere starting a few years
ago with debates around durability of that growth and whether there is a cliff event to come for those
without some type of recurring service revenue tail providing longer term visibility and sustainability.
Basically, is the strong revenue and earnings stream today a bubble at some point tomorrow. We think the
case study of NVDA’s multiple is the reason names in industrials have gotten hit harder in recent months.
NVDA derated starting really in 2022 while names across power, electrical equipment and E&Cs saw their
multiples reach all time highs along with earnings estimates just months ago. We’re getting closer to
forming a bottom with the recent sell off. If NVDA is the right comparison there is still a bit to go. Look
closely at the chart below.
