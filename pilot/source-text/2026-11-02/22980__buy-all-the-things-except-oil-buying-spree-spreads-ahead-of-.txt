And finally, Bloomberg macro strategist, Frank Monkam, is less enthusiastic, warning: first, the 
rotation; then the drop... 
Looking beyond Tuesday’s broad based re-risking in equities, the next leg lower is poised to 
unfold in three phases: rotation, contagion and ultimately liquidation. As credit stress builds 
around the AI investment complex, the initial adjustment in equity positioning would conceal a 
broader deterioration in risk appetite, with defensive rotation temporarily cushioning headline 
benchmarks before increasingly correlated selling takes hold. 
The first phase involves positioning migrating away from tech and AI-linked exposure toward 
defensive and lower beta sectors. Such rotation can initially sustain headline indexes, creating 
the appearance of resilience even as market leadership deteriorates. 
 

 
 
The second phase emerges as worsening financing conditions spread beyond the AI complex, 
challenging the market’s ability to absorb sector-specific stress. Rising credit volatility and a less 
hospitable funding backdrop increasingly undermines EPS forecasts in a broad sense. What 
begins as an orderly rotation then would evolve into a more generalized repricing of the equity 
risk premium, with credit increasingly dictating the direction of risk appetite. 
 
 

 
The final phase materializes when defensive rotation can no longer contain selling pressure. 
The dispersion, correlation gap is closing in rapidly since September, a dynamic that’s critical to 
monitor as credit continues to reprice. As correlations rise, systematic deleveraging and portfolio 
de-risking will amplify index level declines. 
 
The critical inflection point is therefore not around the current rotation, but when credit stress 
ultimately impacts market breadth. Then what starts as an orderly shift from one sector to 
another risks turning into outright liquidation.
