Big Tech stumbles as the market questions AI returns
The tech sell-off is deepening as disappointing results from Amazon, Intel and Alphabet collide with a market
scrutinising AI-related capex plans at all levels. Last week, Meta’s was too large while Tesla’s was too small.
This week, the pressure points varied among the reporting technology leaders. Intel beat Q3 expectations but
materially weaker Q4 guidance sent shares more than 20% lower, with PC demand softening even as supply
constraints limited its ability to meet robust server demand. Alphabet’s disappointment centred on Search,
where faster growth in “lower monetisation” AI Mode queries added to questions around the economics of its
core business. Amazon was different again: Q3 AWS growth fell short of elevated expectations, but
management simultaneously offered unusually strong visibility on AWS growth in the coming years.
The common denominator is therefore less a deterioration in AI demand itself and more that disappointments
elsewhere are landing in a market already highly sensitive to the economics and returns of AI investment.
UBS’s US trading team stressed that beneath Thursday’s weak headline index, the tape looked healthier than
the headline suggested, with capital rotating out of crowded AI/growth leadership and into under-owned
cyclicals, value, rate-sensitive exposures, including consumer, and defensives. UBS’s Rate Cut Winners
basket was up 3.2% on the day.
Market internal dynamics showed Microsoft and Google earnings triggered selling in AI names on Thursday,
with AI Software down 3.0%, AI Semis down 2.9% and AI Risk down 2.6%. Once again, retail investors used
this knee-jerk earnings-triggered weakness to buy the dip, with UBS RMM flow at the 72nd percentile vs. 3y,
marking the sixth buy-the-dip this month. Systematic selling was a big driver of excess sell flow Thursday. For
Friday, ES1 has the most sell triggers at 5 within -2.5%, while RTY1 has the most buy triggers at 3 within
+2.5%.

Eurozone Q3 GDP Up 0.4% q/q, Stronger Than Expected - Eurozone GDP grew 0.4% q/q (1.3% y/y) in
Q3-26, above UBSe and consensus of 0.2% q/q. However, similar to H1-26, we flag the outsized contribution
of Ireland, where GDP grew 5.5% q/q in Q3, contributing around 0.2pp to EZ Q3 GDP. Excluding Ireland,
Eurozone Q3 GDP growth would be at 0.2% q/q, following 0.3% q/q Eurozone ex. Ireland growth in Q1 and
Q2-26. Today’s number is based on estimates from 12 Eurozone countries with growth rates varying from -1%
q/q in Lithuania and -0.1% q/q in France to +5.5% q/q in Ireland. Among the large Eurozone countries, Spain
once again showed the strongest growth rate, followed by Germany, while Italy and France underperformed
with either stagnant growth or a mild contraction. (Anna Titareva, Research - Economics & Strategy - Pan
European Economist)

Eurozone Inflation: Weaker Germany, Stronger France & Spain - Germany surprised to the downside, with
both national CPI and HICP tracking below expctations, largely reflecting softer food prices and, to a lesser
extent, services. France moved the other way, with HICP rising more than expected as services and energy
inflation surprised higher, while Spanish inflation eased slightly, broadly in line with consensus. Based on the
country releases available so far, UBS research is tracking Eurozone HICP around 3.8-3.9% y/y ahead of the
4 November release. (Anna Titareva, Research - Economics & Strategy - Pan European Economist)
BoJ Holds; Statement Modestly Hawkish - BoJ held rates at 1.25%, as widely expected, with an 8-1 vote
split. The statement reads modestly hawkish and supports the base case for a December hike. The main
additions are firmer inflation projections beyond energy, explicit exclusion of the proposed food-tax cut, and a
documented acceleration in ETF disposal (JPY330-860 bn annually, starting January 2027). The inflation
outlook is modestly firmer than UBS Research expectations: FY2027 core CPI at 2.6% vs. 2.5%. The BoJ also
identified wage-price interaction and inflation-expectation risks. Its growth forecast for FY2027 at 0.7% is a
touch soft but doesn't invalidate the normalization path. Altogether, the tone so far is not enough to drive JPY,
which continues to trade largely with risk-on/off swings. Front-end BoJ pricing is a touch lower, but still has
three more hikes priced in over the next 12 months. (Jason Cheng, Macro Sales)
Tokyo Inflation Eases But Stays Above BoJ Target - Tokyo’s October core CPI rose 2.6% y/y, matching
forecasts but easing from September’s 2.7%. The measure excludes fresh food but includes fuel; excluding
both, inflation slowed to 2.6% from 2.9%. Both remained above the BoJ’s 2% target. These leading indicators
of national inflation keep further hikes in view. Producer price increases remain a potential source of consumer
price pressure, rather than a demonstrated explanation for October data; neither measure showed
accelerating annual inflation. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
ECB’s Rehn Says Energy Shock Isn’t Over, Moulin and Nagel on Inflation – In a blog post on Thursday’s
the ECB’s hold, Finland’s Rehn said higher energy prices are expected to keep inflation well above target in
the near term, but noted that core inflation remains relatively stable and there are “no signs of accelerating
wage growth.” Crucially, “the energy shock is not over,” and any transmission of renewed energy-price
increases into other prices and wages must be “monitored with vigilance, as it can creep in unnoticed.”
France’s Moulin struck a similar tone, arguing that most prices remain stable and inflation pressures are
largely imported. He said the ECB will monitor the inflation outlook “very closely” and respond at future
meetings based on incoming data. Moulin also reiterated his opposition to cancelling or freezing sovereign
debt held by the Bank of France. Germany’s Nagel said policymakers are prepared to raise rates again if
needed to return inflation to the 2% target. (Matthew Cowley and Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
Italy Q3 GDP Flat - As Expected - GDP growth in Italy stagnated in Q3, in line with UBSe and below
consensus (0.1% q/q), and after +0.2% q/q in Q2. The ISTAT press release with more details has not yet been
published. (Felix Hüfner, Research - Economics & Strategy - European Economist)
BoE Survey Shows Rising Inflation Expectations As Wage Growth Cools - The latest BoE Decision
Maker Panel survey shows firms’ expectations for year-ahead CPI rose to 3.3% in the three months to
October, from 3.1% in the three months to September. On wages, firms expect year-ahead growth of 3.4% in
the three months to October. Combined with the prior data, that implies firms expect wage growth to fall by
0.5% over the next 12 months. (Daniel Noorian, ETD Sales Trading)
Aussie Producer Price Growth Eases, But Beats Expectations - Australia’s final demand PPI, excluding
exports, rose 1.1% q/q in Q3, above the 1.0% consensus forecast but below Q2’s 1.3%; annual inflation held
at 3.6%. Construction and services led increases, reflecting higher wages, skilled labour shortages and freight
costs for imported materials while lower petroleum and coal manufacturing prices partly offset them. The
figures suggest persistent producer cost pressures ahead of the RBA’s November meeting, despite slower
quarterly growth. September headline CPI inflation had accelerated to 4.3% y/y from 4.0% in August. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Yen Finds Support In Hawkish BoJ - The Bank of Japan’s policy meeting on Thursday reinforced
expectations of further rate hikes, with potentially important implications for both the yen and Japanese
equities. While the BOJ kept its policy rate unchanged at 1.25%, it maintained its focus on upside inflation
risks, raised its fiscal 2027 inflation forecast from 2.4% to 2.6%, and described underlying inflation as broadly
consistent with its 2% price stability target. It also surprised markets by accelerating ETF sales from roughly
JPY330 bn to JPY860 bn annually from January, shortening the estimated disposal period from 112 years to
43 years. For the yen, the shift is supportive: previous BOJ meetings frequently resulted in yen selling as
investors questioned the central bank’s willingness to tighten policy, but more frequent rate hikes and a clearer
normalization path appear to be changing that perception.Read the full note here. (Sara Onozato, Rates
Sales)
