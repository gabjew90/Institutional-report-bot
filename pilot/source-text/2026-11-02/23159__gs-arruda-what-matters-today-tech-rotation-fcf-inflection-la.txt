1) Tech Rotation. FCF Inflection. Labor/AI
Hyperscalers: The hyperscaler tape was messy on headline execution, but the underlying compute
trajectory remains robust: one mega cap platform shed ~10% after hours following consumer ad misses
even as Cloud growth remained robust. Second was cleaner...core cloud beat whispers, positive quarterly
FCF with total contracted obligations expanding. While an optically "light" capex outlay reflected third
party facility delivery sequencing rather than reduced appetite, with next quarter guided back up.
Oracle: Arguably the most important development for credit. Stock rallied double digits as management
outlined a path to positive FCF in H2 FY28 and $30bn+ in FY29, with capex stepping down in FY29.
Incremental capital raises capped at $20bn through FY30, excluding refinancing. Oracle officially guided
to non-GAAP operating margins of 40%+ by FY30 on $225 billion+ in revenue (with high-20s project
returns on fully ramped gigawatt, with ~$16bn annualized revenue per fully ramped gigawatt. Impressive
economics on paper, although dependent on execution, utilization and financing assumptions. Not a capex
cut… an attempt to demonstrate that the economics work and the funding cycle has an end. Potentially
very significant for levered AI credit, although still need to see spreads respond.
Risk: Pain trade is hyperscalers u/p while de-risked picks and shovels start working. Hyperscalers might
weigh on sentiment, but the broader AI demand and spending message remains supportive. Advantest’s
upgrade reinforces the micro, while supportive overnight developments n the optical space helps the
broader group. Credit remains the most important thing to watch. Stability improving, with Oracle
potentially more consequential for the levered infrastructure complex than either hyperscaler print. But
we’re transitioning from a market rewarding capex growth to one demanding evidence of returns. For
now, micro remains supportive and I’d expect dips to get bought. Geopolitical lottery ticket remains a pre
midterm US/Iran meeting… an enormous positive surprise if it happens. (TY Rich Privo)
2) Alphabet - Search Business Slows, Compute Continue to Require Investments
Key themes: 1) Search & Other revenue disappointed relative to GS/Street estimates, with management
pointing to FX headwinds as the primary driver in addition to tougher revenue comparatives as the
company laps in acceleration in Search revenue growth; 2) emphasized their frontier model leadership
position (further demonstrated by the release of Genie 4 yesterday and Gemini C4 Carbon today); 3)
Cloud revenue growth (+103% YoY) and revenue backlog (now at $561bn, +256% YoY & with a
comment that ~55% will convert into revenue in next 24 months) all reflected strong operating momentum
in the Cloud segment, with additional availability of compute standing as a gating factor to even faster

growth; 4) the focus on agentic AI is the next area of platform and product evolution – with mgmt
highlighting Universal Cart, Gemini Agent into Search and Workspace & plans to extend agentic
experiences deeper across Chrome and Android – in addition, mgmt reflected that agentic is additive to
Search by solving high value user questions as opposed to automating or disrupting discovery; & 5)
reiterated their capex guidance for 2026 ($195-205bn, our updated estimate is ~$203bn) with a further
material increase expected in 2027 (not yet guided, our updated estimate is ~$350bn), and mgmt citing
that they expect ROIC to remain meaningfully above their cost of capital while acknowledging the
continued disconnect (reflected in free cash flow) in terms of investing deeply to meet demand and close
current compute constraints.
We reiterate our Buy rating & adjust our 12-month PT from $435 to $385 as we update our forward
operating estimates to reflect this earnings report & management commentary.
we continue to view Alphabet as well-positioned against the rising secular growth theme anchored around
wider adoption rates of AI across consumer and enterprise compute including the evolution of its
consumer facing products/applications
3) Microsoft - 1QFY: At the center of 2027 enterprise deployments
1QFY revenue of $92.3bn (+19% cc yoy) was 2% above the Street and non-GAAP EPS of $4.89 (+27%
yoy) was 3% above the Street. In the last two weeks, we believe investor focus across the AI ecosystem
has shifted to the sustainability of token demand (and a more nuanced discussion around token economics
and mix), and the depth of the financing markets. Against this backdrop, Microsoft illustrated several
proofpoints that suggest its strategy on enabling AI in the enterprise is working into 2027, with respect to
both apps and its platform/infrastructure business:
Coding agents have been the primary use case for AI thus far; the next phase of adoption hinges on
broader AI transformation in Enterprise. We are seeing evidence of Microsoft being at the center of this
transition, with Copilot revenue growth accelerating qoq, daily users +~50% qoq and customers actively
consuming Copilot credits through usage based billing +75.
On model strategy: MAI alongside a model-agnostic harness is resonating. Microsoft views the enterprise
AI framework as three-pronged: 1) Customers can use the latest frontier model, 2) dynamically route
across models based on quality, speed + cost, or 3) train proprietary models on customer traces, including
with MAI.
Azure growth reflects both broad-based demand and Microsoft’s ability to extract efficiencies from the
stack. Azure grew 48% in 1Q and, despite a delay in 3P capacity shifting into 2H, Microsoft guided to
49%-50% cc growth in 2Q, with demand continuing to exceed available supply and growth broad based
across enterprises and SMBs, core cloud and AI infrastructure.
4) AI Health Care - Broadening well beyond Drug Discovery (GSHLCAIP)
AI's impact on drug development appears broad rather than concentrated in one step (discovery), with
reported gains in molecule design, lab optimization, manufacturing documentation, regulatory drafting,

and trial planning. While nearly all of the evidence is preclinical or operational, and there is no human
clinical data yet, AI developers have expressed there is "no single problem to solve" in health care,
because bottlenecks run from target identification through molecule design, manufacturing, regulatory
filings, and trial design and execution. For an investor, this implies benefits may go to healthcare
companies that apply AI across both R&D and operations, not just to specialists in AI-driven molecule
design.

5) FOMC Recap: Sticking to an Every-Other-Meeting Pace
Comments from Fed officials in recent weeks have pointed to a compromise to hike every other meeting.
Consistent with that, the FOMC left the funds rate unchanged today at its October meeting. The meeting
provided little new information and little reason to think that the FOMC will deviate from that plan
anytime soon. We continue to expect a second and final hike in December. A third hike in March is also
possible if inflation comes in higher than we expect.
We saw Chairman Warsh’s comments as balanced and broadly consistent with both a slower every-other-
meeting pace and a limited total number of rate hikes. Warsh emphasized the strength of the economy and
the labor market but said that neither he nor most other FOMC participants thought the economy was
running too hot. He noted that inflation compensation and inflation expectations had moved in the right
direction. And he said that raising interest rates is already having an effect and that financial conditions
have tightened but are not tight.
Warsh also made two scheduling announcements. First, he said that he has given the task forces a deadline
of the end of this year and expects most of them to submit their reports by then. Second, he said that the
FOMC will continue to have eight meetings per year but that, starting next fall, it will devote one meeting
in 2027 and two meetings in 2028 to broad monetary policy strategy, and those meetings will not have a
policy statement or a vote on the federal funds rate.
