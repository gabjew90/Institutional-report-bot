Last week, the scoreboard continued to tell only a fraction of the overall story as a violent undercurrent –
fed by AI agita and vicious de-grossing – put both investors and performance on edge. While the S&P 500
finished just 0.5% lower for the week, Thursday saw the largest wave of equity long selling in more than a
year. This punctuated an eight-session stretch during which hedge funds de-grossed over seven of those
days, sending the GS Risk Appetite Indicator to one of its largest daily drops on record.
Credit’s movements were similarly deceptive: overall spreads widened slightly (HY +6bps, IG +5bps),
leaving their corresponding valuations still relatively tight on a post-2007 lookback basis (HY 23rd
percentile, IG 13th). And yet, massive moves in IG hyperscalers (our 30y hyperscaler basket widened
17bps WoW – its worst week on record outside of the similar moves in July) and triple-Cs (finished the
week with an average OAS of +1127 – a new four-year wide) continued to put investors on edge. This
apprehension builds heading into a consequential week, with 44% of the S&P 500’s market cap reporting
and central bank decisions looming.
“TRIFURCATION” IS STILL A THING
As mentioned coming out of the summer, the Lev Fin market has developed into three distinct segments:
1) the generics; 2) the AI universe; and 3) lower-quality credits. Those three pillars are still intact today.
Generics
HY has logged seven weekly losses over the last eight weeks with mutual fund flows in the red for six of
the last seven weeks. Yet, trading flows have remained constructive in the non-triple-C segment of the
market…for now. 40.1% of junk bonds still trade inside an OAS of +200bps – a metric that has remained
unchanged over the last three weeks – depicting ongoing demand for well-known, preferably up-in-quality
risk.
This dynamic has been well illustrated in the muted new-issue market, where 13 of the 14 tranches which
have priced in October remain above their respective OIDs. These tranches have risen an average of
1.6pts, reflecting meaningful outperformance compared to the broader market tone.
AI Universe
While non-AI-related spreads (excluding triple-Cs) are 22bps wider over the last two weeks, USD HY AI-
related spreads have widened by 78bps to a new high of 400bps over Treasuries (h/t GIR’s Amanda
Lynam).
The roughly 1pt move lower last week in our HY AI datacenter basket (GSHYAIDC), coupled with the
fact that there has not been a new HY AI deal MTD, shows that investors are still trying to formulate a

game plan for adding risk in this space. This caution is especially notable ahead of what is expected to be
an active 2027 issuance year for AI across multiple currencies and asset classes.

Lower-Quality Credits
Approximately 90% of the HY market is represented in the double-B and single-B tiers, leaving the other
10% in a world of its own. Consider the following stats (with thanks to GIR’s Shamshad Ali):
• 88.2% of triple-Cs by notional are lower over the past 60 days
• 70.6% are down 7-12pts
• 22.5% are down 10-30pts
• Triple-C daily moves are running 3.3x those of double-Bs, leaving spreads for triple-Cs 5.5x as wide as
their double-B counterparts
• The 20 worst-performing triple-C bonds are down an average of 14.3pts in October, accounting for 49%
of the rating bucket’s total points lost. The other 185 triple-C bonds are down an average of just 1.6pts,
highlighting the significant dispersion, even within the triple-C tier

TAKEAWAY
Earnings season is coming. 59% of the HY market is set to report numbers by the end of November,
offering a timely test of earnings durability for levered issuers.
While much of the market’s focus has centered on weakness in the lower-quality tiers, it is notable that
two of the five largest issuer decliners this month have bonds rated single-B by S&P (JBLU’s B-rated
bond is down 34pts MTD; FMC’s B+-rated bond is down 25pts).
As GIR noted in their piece last week, aggregate HY fundamentals were solid through the second quarter;
however, metrics for triple-C-rated issuers remained weak. Furthermore, the recent rise in yields suggests
that the fundamental improvement among performing issuers is likely to stall.
Fortunately, it feels like investors still have cash to deploy. However, this liquidity now faces a multitude
of competing options. Capital could rotate toward the upcoming vintage of IG AI debt, flow into well-
known, low-vol credits, or even selectively target severely beaten-down triple-C bonds.
In this “adult swim” environment, all ratings segments of the market – not just the triple-C tier – have
effectively been put on notice with still minimal room for error
