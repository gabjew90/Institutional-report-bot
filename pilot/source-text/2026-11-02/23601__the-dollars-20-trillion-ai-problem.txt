The dollar's $20 trillion AI problem
The AI unwind may be turning into something much bigger than a tech correction. Foreign investors
own more than $20 trillion in US equities, while the dollar's traditional safe-haven properties are
coming into question. Deutsche Bank warns that an AI-driven sell-off could trigger a dangerous
feedback loop between US equities, currency hedging and the dollar. The real risk? Stocks and the
dollar falling together, with declining real yields confirming a broader repricing of the AI boom. The
next tech correction could become a dollar shock.
The $20 trillion exposure
DB estimates that roughly 90% of the value of foreign-owned US equities reflects accumulated price
appreciation rather than net purchases. This has created an enormous exposure to US market
valuations. With foreign equity inflows now financing the entire US current account deficit, the dollar
has become increasingly dependent on the equity cycle.
DB
When the hedge becomes the risk
For much of the past decade, dollar strength helped cushion US equity losses for foreign investors. But
a US-centric shock can flip that relationship. StoneX shows the five-session correlation between AI
sentiment and the dollar reaching an extraordinary +0.97, versus +0.31 over 20 sessions. If stocks and
the dollar decline together, unhedged foreign investors suffer losses on both sides. Even without
selling equities, increasing currency hedge ratios could create additional dollar selling pressure.
StoneX
The AI capex confirmation
The next signal comes from US real yields. DB argues that soaring AI capex expectations have helped
push both equities and real yields higher. A synchronized decline in tech stocks, the dollar and real
yields would suggest investors are starting to discount a weaker AI investment cycle. That would turn a
technical equity correction into a broader macro repricing.

The trade implication
The obvious AI correction trade is to short tech. The less obvious one may be to short the dollar. DB's
argument is that a US-centric equity shock could reverse the traditional stock-dollar relationship,
forcing foreign investors to rethink currency hedges on an enormous stock of US assets. Watch the
Nasdaq, DXY and US real yields together. If all three decline, the market may be pricing something
much bigger than a positioning unwind. The next AI sell-off could become a dollar sell-off.
