Weekly Market Guide
Highly Rotational Beneath the Surface: The S&P 500's sideways, choppy trading pattern continues to mask significant
turbulence and rotation beneath the surface. While market leadership has shifted rapidly across sectors, styles, and factors, the
index has remained above key support levels, suggesting that capital is rotating within equities rather than exiting the asset
class. This dynamic is creating a more favorable backdrop for active management and stock selection. More recently, leadership
has rotated away from high-beta, AI-driven areas of the market as investor scrutiny around AI funding, capital spending, and
return expectations has increased. The result has been elevated volatility beneath the surface even as the broader index remains
largely range-bound.
Rate Relief Supports Out-of-Favor Areas as Breadth Improves: Participation has broadened meaningfully recently as lower
interest rates have provided relief for the "average stock." More than 50% of S&P 500 constituents recently reached new four-
week highs, signaling improving market internals and healthier participation beneath the surface. Rate-sensitive and defensive
sectors have also responded favorably to the decline in yields. While the rebound has been encouraging, many of these areas
remain near important technical resistance levels, suggesting additional confirmation is needed before declaring a durable
change towards a risk-off posture.
Credit Markets Warrant Closer Monitoring: Questions surrounding the debt and equity financing required to support ongoing
AI-related investment have coincided with widening high-yield credit spreads. While investment-grade credit markets have
remained relatively stable, the divergence bears watching as broader credit conditions often provide an early signal of
rising risk appetite or stress.
Fed Holds Rates Steady: The Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged, as expected, citing continued economic strength
alongside still-elevated inflation. While policymakers chose to pause, the committee maintained a hawkish tone, with two
dissenting votes favoring further tightening. As a result, the Fed continues to leave the door open to additional action should
incoming economic and inflation data warrant it.
Bottom Line: The current environment reinforces several key investment principles: diversification remains critical,
improving breadth provides a favorable backdrop for active management, and patience and discipline remain essential
as markets work through this period of evolving leadership. While the major indexes may continue to trade sideways in
the near term, the underlying backdrop is improving. Broader participation, strengthening market breadth, resilient economic
fundamentals, and continued earnings growth are encouraging developments beneath the surface. Although rotations are
creating short-term volatility, they are also expanding opportunities beyond the narrow group of stocks that has led the market.

The rapid shifts in market leadership serve as a reminder that diversification still matters. Although the S&P 500 remains highly 
concentrated in Technology, capital is largely rotating within equities rather than exiting the asset class. Historically, elevated 
concentration has often preceded periods of stronger performance from the "average stock." As market breadth continues to broaden, 
we believe a diversified portfolio remains well positioned to benefit from a more favorable environment for active stock selection.

Relief Rally in Defensive Areas
Lower rates have fueled a sharp rebound in the interest-rate-sensitive and defensive sectors. However, despite the recent strength, 
Utilities and Consumer Staples remain in relative downtrends and are nearing important resistance levels. A breakout would support the 
case for a more sustainable advance, but failure at resistance would suggest the move is simply a relief rally within an ongoing period of 
underperformance.

Index Definitions
The S&P 500 is an unmanaged index of 500 widely held stocks that is generally considered representative of the U.S. stock market.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA) is a price-weighted average of 30 significant stocks traded on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and the NASDAQ.
The NASDAQ Composite is a stock market index of the common stocks and similar securities listed on the NASDAQ stock market.
The MSCI World All Cap Index captures large, mid, small and micro-cap representation across 23 Developed Markets (DM) countries. With 11,732 constituents, the index is
comprehensive, covering approximately 99% of the free float-adjusted market capitalization in each country.
The MSCI EAFE (Europe, Australasia, and Far East) is a free float-adjusted market capitalization index that is designed to measure developed market equity performance,
excluding the United States & Canada. The EAFE consists of the country indices of 21 developed nations.
The MSCI Emerging Markets Index is designed to measure equity market performance in 23 emerging market countries. The index's three largest industries are materials,
energy, and banks.
The Russell 2000 index is an index measuring the performance of approximately 2,000 smallest-cap American companies in the Russell 3000 Index, which is made up of 3,000
of the largest U.S. stocks.
The NYSE Alerian MLP is the leading gauge of energy infrastructure Master Limited Partnerships (MLPs). The capped, float-adjusted, capitalization-weighted index, whose
constituents earn the majority of their cash flow from midstream activities involving energy commodities, is disseminated real-time on a price-return basis (AMZ) and on a
total-return basis (AMZX).
The Barclays Intermediate Government/Credit Bond index measures the performance of U.S. Dollar denominated U.S. Treasuries, government-related and investment grade
U.S. corporate securities that have a remaining maturity of greater than one year and less than ten years.

The Euro Stoxx 50 Index is a market capitalization weighted stock index of 50 large, blue-chip European companies operating within Eurozone nations. Components are
selected from the Euro STOXX Index which includes large-, mid- and small-cap stocks in the Eurozone.
The China CSI 300 is a capitalization-weighted stock market index designed to replicate the performance of top 300 stocks traded in the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock
exchanges. It had a sub-indexes CSI 100 Index and CSI 200 Index.
The S&P 500 Futures is a capitalization-weighted index of 500 stocks. The index is designed to measure performance of the broad domestic economy through changes in the
aggregate market value of 500 stocks representing all major industries.
The DJIA Futures is a stock market index futures contract traded on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange`s Globex electronic trading platform. Dow Futures is based off the Dow
30 stock index.
The Nasdaq 100 Futures is a modified capitalization-weighted index of the 100 largest and most active non-financial domestic and international companies listed on the
NASDAQ.
Europe: DAX (Deutscher Aktienindex (German stock index)) is a blue chip stock market index consisting of the 40 major German companies trading on the Frankfurt Stock
Exchange.
Asia: Nikkei is short for Japan's Nikkei 225 Stock Average, the leading and most-respected index of Japanese stocks. It is a price-weighted index composed of Japan's top 225
blue-chip companies traded on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.
