Tech Valuation Risks And Treasury Refunding Set This Week’s DirectionTech remains the central risk to
equities after last week’s 3.5% fall in the stocks, and a 4.5% decline in hyperscalers, with concern centred on
whether roughly $10 trn of investment through 2032 can generate adequate returns. The US midterms matter
particularly for tech, with the Texas governor race worth watching given the debate over how much restriction
to impose on data centres is one of the main issues.
 In US rates, Wednesday’s quarterly refunding is expected to maintain the $125 bn package of $58 bn 3y, $42
bn 10y and $25 bn 30y for November-January, although some have argued there may be a reduction in
long-end issuance. More likely, it’s the guidance for future refunding announcements that will be more
important than the headline sizes.  
The benign outcome would be a signal that it may reduce longer term auction sizes from May 2027 and
consider dropping the 20y issuance. A more aggressive alternative discussed in the market would see the 20y
scrapped and reduced 30y issuance, all replaced by bills, with potentially substantial curve implications. The
US Rates preview is here.
Monday’s macro focus is US ISM, particularly prices paid. The RBA is expected to hike 25bp to 4.85% on
Tuesday. Swiss CPI is expected at 1.2% y/y, versus 1.0%, although no SNB action is expected. Norges Bank
is expected to hold at 4.5% Thursday, while the BoE is expected to hike 25bp to 4.0%, alongside updated
forecasts, minutes and the press conference. Friday brings Japanese wage data, Fed speakers and US
payrolls, with consensus at 65,000 jobs and 4.2% unemployment, while 4.1% remains possible on rounding.
(Simon Penn, Macro Sales)

Germany's September Retail Sales Unexpectedly Fall - Germany's September retail sales came in sharply
below expectations. Sales fell 2.0% m/m against a consensus estimate of a 0.4% rise, and dropped 2.3% y/y
versus an estimate of -0.2% — a significant miss on both measures. The result marks a stark reversal from the
prior month's 1.3% m/m gain (itself revised down slightly to 1.2%). The weak print adds to a broader picture of
economic strain in Germany: the Bundesbank flagged that GDP likely stagnated in Q3, PMIs showed business
activity slowed more than expected in October (though still in expansionary territory), while inflation did ease to
2.8% y/y in October, below the 3.0% estimate. Taken together, Monday's retail sales miss reinforces concerns
that Germany's domestic demand recovery remains elusive heading into year-end. (Nana Antiedu, Macro
Sales)

that the markets may be underestimating the work that needs to be done to get inflation back under control.

Oil Eases As Iran Diplomacy Cools Prices, But Supply Buffers Are Wearing Thin - Oil prices eased on
Monday, with Brent down 2.8% at about $110/bbl and WTI down 3.3% at $100.67/bbl, as hopes for diplomatic
progress tempered the geopolitical premium embedded in crude following October’s rally. Iranian and Qatari
officials have held three consecutive days of talks aimed at ending the conflict with US and Israel, as US
President Donald Trump tells Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu he prefers diplomacy as reported by
Bloomberg [paywall]. The pullback comes against an increasingly fragile supply backdrop. ADNOC CEO
Sultan Al Jaber said nearly 1 billion barrels have been drained from global oil inventories since the conflict
disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reports [paywall]. Diesel inventories in some markets
are at 35-year seasonal lows, while shipping and logistics costs have risen from roughly 5% to 30% of the cost
of a barrel. Meanwhile, OPEC+ kept its December production targets unchanged on Sunday, as several Gulf
producers remain below target despite previous increases. Attention now turns to the group’s Nov. 29 meeting,
when members are expected to address 2027 production quotas following a capacity review. (Nana Antiedu,
Macro Sales)
Trump Says To Become ‘Mr. Veto’ If Democrats Win Congress - US President Donald Trump said he
would veto legislation if Republicans lose control of both the House and Senate, before potentially negotiating
deals with Democrats. Speaking to the New York Post’s Miranda Devine for a “Pod Force One” interview
scheduled to air Monday, Trump said: “I guess I’ll become Mr. Veto. I’m not going to let them through.” His
remarks came as Decision Desk HQ forecasts cited by The Hill gave Democrats an 83% chance of winning
the House and a 63% chance of taking the Senate. Republicans controlled both chambers during the first two
years of Trump’s first term, before Democrats took control of the House in January 2019. Trump’s stated
approach points to a risk of legislative delays if Democrats win both chambers, though he also signalled scope
for eventual compromise. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
US Backs Russian Gas Transit Revival Via Ukraine, Faces European Resistance – WSJ - The US is
promoting a resumption of Russian gas sales to Europe through Ukraine as part of efforts to secure an energy
ceasefire before winter, The Wall Street Journal reported [paywall], citing unnamed US, European and
Ukrainian officials. Envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff reportedly proposed sales through a US
intermediary to circumvent European restrictions during September visits to Moscow and Kyiv. Transit stopped
after the Russia–Ukraine agreement expired at the end of 2024. The proposal could restore supply, but its
implementation remains uncertain amid European opposition and the EU’s Russian-gas phaseout plans. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)
China Official PMIs Fall Back Into Contraction; RatingDog Manufacturing Still Expands - China PMIs,
both manufacturing and non-manufacturing, fell back into contraction in October. Manufacturing PMI slid to
49.3 from 50.1 and vs. 49.9 expected amid weaker production and new orders, while non-manufacturing PMI
dropped to 49.7 from 50.2 vs. 50 expected, weighed down by a sharp decline in financial sector activity.
Composite PMI fell to 49.7 from 50.7, according to the National Bureau of Statistics. Despite this, China
economist Yu Song is not too concerned, noting that PMIs have been a poor gauge of real growth this year
and have been influenced more by inflation trends. Lower input and output price indices signal easing
commodity costs, which could benefit broader economic stability. Separately, China's RatingDog
manufacturing PMI came in line at 52 after 52.1 prior, with output rising at the fastest pace since April amid a
solid rise in new orders. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

BoE, Riksbank, RBA To Hike; Brazil To Cut - UBS Economics team expect the BoE to hike 25bp to 4% on
Thursday. The hawkish surprise would be an 8–1 vote rather than 7–2. Activity and labour data have held up
since September, inflation has surprised to the upside, and energy prices point to near-term inflation above the
BoE's adverse scenario. The team also expects the Riksbank (hike 25bp to 2%) and RBA (hike 25bp to

pause they expect already starts this week given the selloff in markets and the time it will take to get clarity on
government appointments and fiscal policy. Finally, Banxico’s meeting is also a close call. A 25bp cut looks
possible post the dovish minutes last month, but recent MXN depreciation muddies the waters. Rates strategy
are received 1y1y rates amid excessive tightening expectations but a cut this week could hurt Banxico's
credibility and reduce their conviction in this trade after a strong run. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)

Deep Speak: Warsh Hits Hawkish High; Lagarde Less Hawkish Than In September - Rates Strategist
Elena Amoruso’s LLM-based tracker of central bank sentiment finds that the October Fed and ECB press
conferences told increasingly different stories: Fed chair Warsh’s hawkish signal strengthened further, while
ECB President Lagarde’s tone seems to oscillate depending on whether there is a rate hike decision to
defend. The contrast was particularly clear on growth: a meaningful source of hawkishness at the Fed, but
much less so at the ECB. Kevin Warsh delivered his most hawkish press conference since taking office and
this time the shift was led by growth rather than inflation alone. After unconventional early appearances, the
Chair’s delivery also appears to be settling into a more established pattern, with policy-relevant content holding
up and the narrative increasingly centred on the policy outlook. At the ECB, Elena’s model reads Lagarde as
less hawkish than in September, but her tone has shown relatively large swings across recent press
conferences. Hawkish signalling became less explicit, with a lack of hawkish forward guidance weighing on the
overall reading. Inflation remained the main source of hawkishness, but even their concerns were broadly
stable rather than intensifying. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
November Seasonality, Midterms Could Fuel Year-End US Equity Chase - Network Thoughts: With
November historically the S&P 500’s strongest month and the midterms approaching, the seasonal backdrop
for equities is increasingly supportive. The index has gained 1.9% on average in November since 1950, with
only one negative November in the past decade. This coincides with a backdrop of elevated short-squeeze
risk, as low net exposure and multi-year-high gross exposure create scope for a year-end performance chase.
Midterms provide another potential tailwind, with the S&P higher six months after every midterm since 1950.
Weaker oil, lower VIX and MOVE, and downward pressure on 10y yields provide additional support into
year-end. Markets are pricing limited election risk. The SPX one-day implied move for Wednesday has fallen
to 70bp from 90bp last week, roughly in line with nonfarm payrolls and CPI pricing. EURUSD event premium
has also declined, while Nov. 4 TLT options show no obvious kink. With Nov. 6 SPX ATM volatility near 12,
downside protection through the week remains relatively cheap, particularly given the risk of concurrent Iran
escalation. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)

Copper Resilient In The Face Of Risk Aversion/AI Sell-Off - UBS Research: Daniel Major thinks that
AI/data centres are one of a number of secular electrification demand drivers that underpin the bullish copper
demand narrative. But AI is not the dominant driver of the positive fundamental outlook for copper, which is
based on supply constraints versus resilient (not transformational) demand growth. However, the copper price
is driven by narratives and risk appetite/aversion driving speculative investment flows as well as the physical
supply and demand. Considering the sharp sell-off in the AI power/hyperscaler capex theme, it has been
surprising/encouraging the resilience of the copper price holding close to record highs versus UBS AI power
basket/copper equities (COPX) down around 30%. Feedback on copper from LME Week was cautious
reflecting macro/AI unwind concerns, but a material reduction in net long positioning in copper has been offset
by sustained tightness in the physical market. Near-term, Daniel is cautious of further deterioration in
traditional demand from Middle East escalation and higher oil/rates; US tariff unwind. But in his view, recent
price action demonstrates that physical tightness rather than speculative narratives are supporting copper.
(Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
