30 Oct 2026
Markets: Another challenging session overnight session.  Asia looks like a potential outright gross unwind. KOSPI
down around -3.5%/TWSE -2.5%. AI winners getting hit 3-5%, while consumer and other AI laggards catch a bid. 
 
Amazon:  AWS grew 39.3% to $46bn, ahead of consensus, although perhaps a little light of whispers given the
bar set by others this week. Capex remains enormous at $220bn this year and “meaningfully more” in 2027.
Jassy guiding to AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028. He has “even more conviction… that the
investment we’re making in AWS will yield a much larger revenue and free cash flow generating business.”
On inference: “Enterprises are still very early in using inference at scale, and yet inference already accounts for well
over half of AWS’s AI revenue and growing.”   The problem is everything else. Advertising grew just 12% to
$19.6bn versus 18% expected, the first miss in seven quarters. Consumer growth was softer, Q4 sales
guidance a touch light and trailing FCF negative $42bn. 
 
Themes:  Core businesses financing this extraordinary investment cycle are coming under greater scrutiny. Is
advertising slowing, is the consumer slowing, or both? Amazon’s advertising miss follows another miss in
search from a peer earlier in the week.  Prime Day timing explains some of Amazon’s weakness, with management
guiding advertising back to high teens growth in Q4, so probably premature to extrapolate. Reddit adds another
wrinkle, observing that “AI mode does not drive traffic.” Could AI eventually cannibalize some of the advertising
economics financing the investment cycle itself? Too early to conclude, particularly as AI may also stimulate
commercial search activity, but worth watching. The bigger issue is increasingly funding and returns.
 
Intel: Down 20%+ overnight, and having an even bigger impact on the broader semiconductor tape. Q4 revenue
guided to $15-16bn versus ~$17bn expected (operational issue at Israeli fab). The divergence within the business is
extraordinary. Data Centre and AI revenue grew 78%, with server CPU demand so strong Intel simply cannot
meet it. Meanwhile, PC demand is contracting as memory inflation drives hardware costs higher. The challenge is
an increasingly pronounced mismatch between capacity and demand. Manufacturing and packaging constraints
have limited Intel’s ability to capitalize on the server CPU boom, while the consumer business faces a very
different environment. Clearly some questions around capacity allocation and how quickly supply can respond.
 While the read-across for PC exposed suppliers is negative, some might view Intel's packaging and fab
bottlenecks as company specific rather than a secular breakdown in enterprise compute demand. 
 
Oil/Iran: Tehran appears to have rejected the latest mediated overtures, reiterating its seven conditions for peace.
Trump has cancelled some weekend campaign appearances, while Senator Chris Murphy has been making
some fairly alarming warnings about potential escalation. Other headlines conversely, says the parties are “very
close” to an agreement. The hopeful interpretation is that this is all part of an escalate to de escalate strategy. But I
took note yesterday that even with oil trading lower, Middle Eastern equities were under pressure again. Despite the
bounce earlier in the week Saudi and the broader Gulf struggled, suggesting regional investors remain genuinely
concerned about escalation.  Very difficult to carry much conviction around this one… the range of outcomes
remains enormous but think plenty of risk premia priced.
 
Risk:  Some genuinely difficult earnings reactions this week, but the distinction matters. We knew there was a lot
of length parked in hyperscalers and that expectations were demanding.  What reported results demonstrate is

that enterprise cloud consumption and contracted backlogs remain resilient, even as the market intensifies its
scrutiny over balance sheet financing and multi year ROIC. The market is increasingly questioning who ultimately
earns the returns and how the investment cycle gets financed. Those are legitimate concerns, particularly with
cash flow under pressure and financing needs rising. The risk is that financing constraints eventually force
capex discipline… we’re just not seeing that in spending intentions yet. With the midterms next week, Iran
unresolved and the index heavyweights under pressure, it’s understandably difficult to add risk. Near-term price
action remains challenging across tech as the tape digests elevated capex commitments, even as forward
contracted backlogs remain substantial.. The point at which hyperscalers shift from needing more capacity at almost
any cost to optimizing returns on what they’ve already built is the one I’d really worry about. For now we chop with
light positioning and bearish sentiment, post political events will be chance to climb wall of worry.
