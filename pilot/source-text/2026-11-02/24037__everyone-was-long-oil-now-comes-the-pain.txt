The great oil unwind
Oil is getting crushed, and the setup is looking increasingly dangerous. Trend followers are sitting on
sizeable longs, speculative accounts have piled into upside calls and volatility is waking up. Meanwhile,
Goldman estimates that a massive $27 geopolitical risk premium has been supporting Brent. With
prices testing key trend support, is this just a violent shakeout, or the beginning of a much bigger
positioning unwind?
The oil puke
Oil is printing its biggest downside candle in a long time, taking prices straight back to the trend line in
place since the July lows. The 50-day moving average sits around $103.50. Do the bulls defend the
trend, or is a bigger breakdown coming?

The oil long trap
Trend followers entered today's selloff heavily long oil, leaving the market vulnerable to a potentially
violent unwind. With key technical levels under pressure, systematic strategies could turn into
aggressive sellers. CTA downside convexity is serious, raising the risk of a self-reinforcing liquidation.

Oil's volatility trap
According to Goldman's Tallulah Adams, speculative and macro accounts returned as sizeable buyers
of crude calls, while implied volatility surged and one-year volatility reached three-year highs. The
options market has been pricing escalating upside risks, leaving a potentially nasty mismatch between
bullish positioning and today's price action.

GS
Goldman raises oil forecasts
Goldman's Struyven raised his December Brent forecast from $90 to $105 yesterday, citing elevated
supply disruption risks. The interesting part? Goldman estimates that roughly $27 of Brent's price was
geopolitical risk premium as of Friday, accounting for most of October's rally. Meanwhile, fundamental
fair value remained around $90, with global inventories broadly flat. The key question now is how
much of that premium the market is willing to unwind.
GS
The oil illusion
Goldman's estimates show the global oil market was broadly balanced in September and October,
despite the geopolitical chaos. Persian Gulf production disruptions have been offset by higher non-Gulf
output, weaker demand and supply adjustments. In other words, the rally was increasingly driven by
geopolitical fear rather than actual inventory draws.
GS
Oil vol
Oil volatility is waking up, but we're nowhere near panic territory. OVX is pricing roughly 3.7% daily
moves, leaving volatility neither particularly cheap nor particularly expensive. Despite today's violent
price action, the volatility market is not yet signalling outright capitulation.

The bottom line
The setup is increasingly fragile. Oil is testing key technical support just as crowded positioning meets
a potential unwind of the geopolitical risk premium. A successful defence of the trend could trigger a
sharp reversal, but a decisive break risks unleashing another wave of systematic selling. The next
move could be violent in either direction.
