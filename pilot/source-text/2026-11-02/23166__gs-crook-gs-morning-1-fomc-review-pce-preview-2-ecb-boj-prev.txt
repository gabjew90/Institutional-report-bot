1) US ECONOMICS (MERICLE) – FOMC Review & PCE Preview
October FOMC: The FOMC left the target range for the fed funds rate unchanged at 3.75-4.00% at its
October meeting. Governor Cook and President Hammack dissented from the Committee’s decision,
preferring a 25bp increase in the target range for the fed funds rate. The post-meeting statement noted that
“the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to
ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee’s 2 percent goal,” reiterated that inflation “remains
elevated,” and upgraded its characterization of economic activity to say it is expanding at a “strong” pace
(vs. “solid” previously). We continue to expect a second and final hike in December. A third hike in March
is also possible if inflation comes in higher than we expect. We saw Chairman Warsh’s comments as
balanced and broadly consistent with both a slower every-other-meeting pace and a limited total number
of rate hikes. Warsh emphasized the strength of the economy and the labor market but said that neither he
nor most other FOMC participants thought the economy was running too hot. He noted that inflation
compensation and inflation expectations had moved in the right direction. And he said that raising interest
rates is already having an effect and that financial conditions have tightened but are not tight. Full notes
here & here.
PCE: We estimate that the core PCE price index rose 0.34% in September, corresponding to a year-over-
year rate of +3.11%. Additionally, we expect that the headline PCE price index rose 0.51% in September,
and increased 3.64% from a year earlier. Full note here.
2) ECB & BOJ Previews
EUROPE ECON (STEHN) – ECB: The incoming data since the September Governing Council have
shown resilient growth, steady underlying inflation, higher energy prices and tighter financial conditions.
ECB commentary has emphasised that a “measured approach” to policy tightening remains appropriate.
The Governing Council is therefore widely expected to stay on hold. We look for no change to the formal
policy language or the inflation risk assessment. President Lagarde is likely to strike a balanced message
between ongoing upside risks from energy prices, benign underlying inflation data and tighter financial
conditions. We maintain our forecast for one more hike at the December meeting. An additional hike in
March looks possible if energy prices continue to rise (especially for gas in case of a cold winter). But we

see a significant hurdle to speeding up the pace of hikes or raising the deposit rate above 3%. Full note
here.
JAPAN ECON (OTA) – BOJ: We think the BOJ has no need to conduct consecutive rate hikes, and we
maintain our expectation that it will keep the policy rate at 1.25%. We expect no substantial changes to the
economic and price assessments in the BOJ's October Outlook Report versus the previous July
assessment. However, we see the board members' core CPI forecast being adjusted downward for FY2026
accounting for the current low inflation rate, and upward for FY2027 on re-surging crude oil price. While
the Composite Index of Inflation Expectations, which the BOJ focuses on as a gauge of underlying
inflation, is expected to have turned downward slightly in July-September quarter, we expect the BOJ's
language regarding upside risk to underlying inflation exceeding 2%, to be maintained exactly as in the
July report. Full note here.

3) FX Trader Call Takeaways
Key takeaways from our weekly FX Trader Call, occurring every Wednesday @ 2:00 LDN. Reach out to
receive a recurring calendar invite.
Alberto Ramos (Head of Latam Economic Research): The market consensus was not positioned for this
come-from-behind win, and the signal from the polls was very misleading. There are two things that the
new administration needs to do: (1) appointments (the cabinet, particularly the Minister of Finance), and
(2) measures that point in the direction of fiscal consolidation. The Minister of Finance is an important
seat, but perhaps not as much as the market thinks, as it is still the President that calls the shots – Lula
signs off on every package of measures that the Minister of Finance puts forward. There is also no date to
make these appointments – there is still an existing government with a sitting Minister of Finance. In
terms of the fiscal outlook, there is a trade-off between implementing measures that appease the market

versus the expensive promises made during the campaign. I don't expect a major package of fiscal reforms
before the end of the year. It seems that they will aim for a primary surplus of 0.5% of GDP, which is the
existing target for 2027.
Praneet Shah (Global Head of FX Options Trading): Last week, USDJPY was the go-to instrument for FX
and cross-asset clients who had exposure to AI Credit or Equities and needed structural hedges, with
demand for 3m-6m downside binaries. FX will typically outperform on a cross-asset basis when you get
higher recession risk, and JPY has a high beta within FX to an AI slowdown. Japan also owns $1.3trn of
US Equities, which would be a second-order impact supporting this trade. More generally, currencies that
have more to come out in terms of rate hike premium are going to do worse relative to those that don't
have much in already – EURAUD works on this front, and it has also crossed the multi-year technical
break at 1.60.
