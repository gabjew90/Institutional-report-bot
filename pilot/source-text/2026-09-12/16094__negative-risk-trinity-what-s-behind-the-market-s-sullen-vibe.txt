"Negative Risk Trinity": What's Behind The Market's 
Sullen Vibe, And What Comes Next 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, SEP 10, 2026 - 04:01 PM 
Despite stocks trading just shy of all time highs, traders are increasingly listless and cautious, 
prompting questions among strategists what is the reason for this lack of excitement, and 
prompting speculation how this episode plays out and what happens next.  
Let's start with the latest note (available to pro subs) from JPM's Positioning Intel head John 
Schlegel, who wrote that "not a lot had changed at the aggregate positioning level over the past 
week, despite market gyrations" adding that modest hedge fund re-grossing picked up last week 
- as hedge funds added gross exposure on 4 of the past 5 days for a 1.3-sigma increase in the 
past week, the highest since late June.  
More concerning is that while net changes were muted (global HF nets are 40-50th %-tile on a 
5yr basis) - as HF flows were mixed day-to-day, and they ended with a small sell skew -  JPM 
finds that "sentiment is turning less constructive" as both retail and institutional sentiment 
appear to be reversing lower/pausing, after prior increases (more in the full JPM note here). 
It wasn't just JPMorgan: in its latest note, Goldman's Prime Brokerage (available here to pro 
subs) found an almost identical market dynamics and writes that "US equities were slightly net 
bought on the week (+0.2 SDs 1-year), driven by long buys in Single Stocks partially offset by 
short sales in Single Stocks and Macro Products."  
Specifically, macro products (Index and ETF combined) were modestly net sold (-0.3 SDs 
1-year), driven almost entirely by short sales. US-listed ETF shorts increased +1.5% (-4.4% 
month/month), led by shorting in Credit and Small Cap Equity ETFs partially offset by covering 
in Large Cap Equity ETFs. 
On the other hand, Single Stocks were net bought for the first time in three weeks (+0.5 SDs 
1-year), driven by risk-on flows with long buys outpacing short sales (1.6 to 1).

Info Tech, Comm Svcs, Financials, and Utilities were the most $ net bought US sectors, while 
Real Estate, Energy, Industrials, and Staples were the most $ net sold. 
 
Curiously, despite the recent solid market performance, US L/S Gross leverage rose +1.5 pts to 
208.5% (27th percentile one-year), while US L/S Net leverage increased +0.9 pts to 48.4% (4th 
percentile one-year). Similarly, US Fundamental long/short ratio (MV) increased +0.5% to 1.605 
(4th percentile one-year), both effectively the lowest they have been since Liberation day. 
 
A more in depth analysis of the above was also provided at the start of the week by Goldman's

Another possible reason for the lack of enthusiasm may come not from stocks at all, but rather 
the aggressive unwind of the carry trade taking place in FX. As Goldman Delta-One head, Rich 
Privorotsky, wrote today (note available to pro subs), Bessent was unusually explicit overnight: “I 
am the house now … and you can bet against me if you want.” He argued that Treasury has an 
informational advantage because of its visibility into Japanese policymakers and the BOJ. As a 
result of this rhetoric, and the likely imminent rate hike by the BOJ, the yen objectively continues 
to appreciate as the market leans into BOJ tightening/repatriation. To Privo, the secondary 
implication matters more for equities: what happens as yen funded carry trades unwind back 
into Japanese bonds/equities? And then he goes on to make the same observations, namely 
that "The S&P and mega cap complex have felt strangely heavy without a great fundamental 
reason. Worth keeping in the back of the mind that some leverage/carry may simply be diffusing 
out of the system." 
Earlier today Nomura's Charlie McElligott also addressed this topic in his latest note (available 
here) writing that on an Equities vibe-check - the generic narrative which was being used as 
justification behind the recently low-enthusiasm, low-positioning - dynamic (referring to what the 
GS Prime Berokage highlighted, namely that at the end of last week, Equity HF Net Exposure 
was just at a 2 %ile on 1Y lookback) had been around concerns he calls call the “Negative Risk 
Trinity”:  
1.​ Historically demonstrated bearish September seasonality / “Midterm Election Drawdown” 
-analogs; 
2.​ Rates risk continuing on over-supply / issuance dynamics = “Crowding-Out” of UST 
Duration (which we analyzed extensively here)  
3.​ “Hawkish” central bank - impulse being seen globally off the back of ongoing 
Commodities price surges and latent disaster-in-waiting with Energy and Petrochems 
and the Iran / SoH -mess 
So between the “bearish” risk narrative and under-positioning around it, to McElligott it wasn’t 
surprisingly then that “The Market Traded Like It’s Short” into the handful of Stock rallies which 
we experienced last week, with glimpses of more of the same “Positive Spot : Vol Correlation” 
(in this case, “Spot Up, Vol Up” yet-again). Hence, it looked like Nets bottomed into Thursday 
before the +1.1% SPX return on the session, and forced some add-back “Chase” to an extent. 
Taking a step back, to the Nomura strategist, the theme of the past week has been “Back to the 
High Sharpe & Trend -Trades,” as we are seeing a fresh reacceleration in many legacy 2026 
leadership themes which had recently been sputtering, but are now getting their legs again:

But while McElligott, as so often happens, leaves his readers with a potential market trapdoor 
cliffhanger, another Goldman trader had a more upbeat take.  
In a late Wednesday note from Goldman's John Flood, the head of the bank's Americas Equities 
Sales trading, writes (full note available here) that he just got back from a week in Asia and - 
echoing all the above - was "taken aback by how cautious / skeptical a lot of this community 
currently is on the AI trade (aka the stock market)." To be sure, plenty of scar tissue still exists, 
with a lot of Asia based hedge funds dropping 20+% of their ytd performance in July. Flood's 
main takeaway from the trip: investors in the Americas are likely underestimating China’s AI 
(open-weight) adoption rates while investors in Asia are simultaneously likely overestimating 
them. "This probably means it eventually shakes out somewhere in-between." 
In any event, after July’s historic momentum crash (thanks Leopold) and investors' lackluster 
performance in August, Flood is currently "witnessing a lack of willingness to play any 
meaningful offense" as positioning and sentiment remain muted with concerns over geopolitics, 
rates, and inflation surfacing in every client conversation, similar to McElligott's negative risk 
trinity above.  
However, unlike the Nomura trader who leaves on a cautious note, to Flood the upcoming IPO 
pipeline has the potential to be the spark that motivates investors (both institutional and retail) to 
start playing offense again.  
It's not just IPOs, however, and below, the Goldman trader highlights several macro signals that 
suggest investor sentiment and positioning might be too cautious at the moment (in front of 
some potentially high profile deals and post a truly stellar Q2 earnings season). To that point, he 
lays out his bullish thesis in charts, which capture the following: S&P 500 EPS growth in Q2 
2026 was ~30% year / year excluding the "other income" related to some private investment 
stakes. Earnings for the hyperscalers and the AI infrastructure companies benefiting from their 
capex spending increased by 54% year / year in Q2, accounting for about 50% of S&P 500 EPS 
growth during the quarter. However, earnings growth for the rest of the market has also been 
strong and accelerating. Excluding the Energy sector profits that were boosted by higher oil 
prices, the rest of the S&P 500 posted year/year EPS growth of 14%.
