2) Oracle and Microsoft Bullish Datapoints
Oracle (+7.5% premkt) — the desk read this as about as bullish a compute datapoint as could be asked for:
OCI +121 , GPU utilization 97.9% renewals/resales clearing at ~20% premiums, and RPO at $664bn;
compute remains scarce and pricing strong, supportive of the hardware trade. [1] Cloud infrastructure
revenue jumped 121% to $7.4bn (vs. Street $7.19bn), Q1 sales rose 30% to $19.3bn, and >$30bn of AI
cloud contracts were booked; capex ran $28.5bn in the quarter with FY27 guidance affirmed at $70bn
(plus $20-25bn of customer prepayments). [2]
Microsoft (+20bps premkt) — plans to more than triple data center capacity to >38GW by 2032 from
~12GW today, per Bloomberg, framed as a road map for company-owned and leased facilities excluding
neocloud rentals. [2] GS Research Equity team's take from Communacopia this week was that Microsoft's
near term focus is accelerating monetization of already-deployed capacity ("revenue ready"), with dock-
to-live times down >50% over the past year; the 12-month price target is $640 (28x P/E on SNTM
adjusted net income). [4]

4) Chance of Bab el-Maneb Strait Closing?
Yesterday's important development was arguably Yemen, not Hormuz. The Houthis seized Mocha and
advanced to the Hanish Islands, giving them considerably more leverage around Bab el-Mandeb just as
Saudi Arabia relies more heavily on the Red Sea/Yanbu route (polymkt now saying 25% chance Bab el-
Maneb closed by Dec). There were also unconfirmed reports of attacks on the East-West pipeline. This
increasingly looks like a two front problem around the Gulf and Red Sea exit routes. That makes
Monday's reported GCC/Iran meeting in Salalah an important anchor. I'm not particularly optimistic, but it
gives the market a visible diplomatic event and a glimmer of an offramp. More generally optimistic that
there is a lot now in the price and all the incentives are to find off ramps. (TY Rich Privo)
