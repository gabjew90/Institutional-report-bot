Gold at the neckline
Gold’s longer-term trend support sits lower, but the 50-day MA is now less than $100 below spot. More
immediately, a smaller H&S formation is threatening to trigger.
CPI should decide whether the first support holds or the neckline gives way.

The $250 disconnect
Gold has separated from its usual relationship with the inverted dollar. If that gap closes through gold
rather than the dollar, spot has roughly $250 of catch-up potential.
LSEG Workspace
Specs still loaded
The August spike pulled shorter-term punters into the trade. Some longs have been trimmed since, but
positioning remains elevated when you zoom out.
That leaves gold vulnerable if the neckline breaks, but also shows that the recent sell-off has not
forced a broader capitulation.

GS buying the upside
Against that mixed setup, the GS vol trading desk is adding upside convexity ahead of CPI by selling 1-
month 1x4 call spreads and buying hedged outright calls.
The desk believes much of the bad news is already priced ahead of CPI and the FOMC, with $4,330
holding as post-Jackson Hole support. Gold also remains underowned across its client base.
GS sees roughly $100/oz of downside from a Fed hike. Conversely, soft CPI followed by no hike could
produce a $400/oz rally over two weeks, sending upside vol sharply higher.
LSEG Workspace
The convexity tell
Investors remain reluctant to pay more than 25% implied vol when gold has realized around 17% over
much of the past decade. That has left upside convexity underowned into a potentially large event.
Gold vol has risen since late August despite the decline in spot. A more extreme version of the same
setup appeared in mid-June, when downside hedging pushed vol higher just as gold was forming a
local low. The squeeze followed a few weeks later.
Gold vol is not dirt cheap, but once spot catches a bid, upside skew can reprice quickly.
