Widening Gap Between Macro Stress & Risk 
Premium Calls For Repricing 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, SEP 10, 2026 - 08:40 PM 
Authored by Frank Monkam, Bloomberg macro strategist, 
The disconnect between macro stress and broader risk premium has rarely been wider, raising 
the risk of a repricing that either pulls oil and yields lower, or leads to weaker equities. 
An equally weighted z-score of Brent and 10-year yields sits at a +1.8 sigma, while the Citi 
Macro Risk Index -- a widely-used macro risk barometer -- is at a -0.7 Z-score. 
That has pushed the gap between the two to about 2.5 z-score points, the widest in over three 
years, as the chart below shows. 
 
Oil and rates have emerged as the two most consequential macro pressure points for markets 
during the current cycle. 
Brent above $100 keeps inflation risks elevated, while Treasury yields near cyclical highs tighten 
financial conditions. 
Yet this combination of shocks has yet to be translated into a commensurate repricing of 
broader macro risk. 


[Page 2]
 
That creates an asymmetric setup: Either oil and yields retreat, closing the gap from the macro 
side, or broader risk pricing eventually catches up.  
The catalyst for the latter would not necessarily require yet another shock or further 
deterioration in the current macro landscape; simply a set of catalysts that would enable the 
transmission channel to open. 
Those include: 
●​ A persistent, rather than temporary oil shock that could force a more hawkish Fed 
reaction function. 
●​ A macro driver that forces stocks to trade the same factors as oil, inflation and rates that 
could drive re-correlation and lift volatility, given realized stock correlations are near 
extreme lows. 
●​ A compression in swap spreads that could signal a disorderly yield repricing is morphing 
into Treasury micro-structure stress and a potential repricing to higher rates volatility.
