Those yields might not hurt yet, but diesel 
     
   
 
 
Scores on the Doors: oil 80.9%, global stocks 14.7%, US stocks 10.9%, cash 2.5%, HY 
bonds 2.0%, US$ 0.7%, gold -0.4%, IG -1.2%, govt bonds -1.9%, bitcoin -12.4% YTD. 
The Biggest Picture: markets stop panicking when policymakers start panicking…but no 
panic anywhere despite the highest 30yr yield since Jun’07 and spiking commodities; oil 
at $100/bbl takes headlines but diesel is the key real-economy pressure point: shipping, 
trucking, agriculture, construction, mining; note crude is well below Ukraine peaks vs. 
diesel at a $6 record (Chart 2); blasé markets + bravado policy = recipe for volatility. 
Tale of the Tape: markets usually test policy resolve: July 31st US-Japan intervention 
saw yen test 160 before success <154; 30yr Treasury yield jumped +16bps the day after 
QE3 news (2012), +51bps in days after QE4 (2020); in truth a big surge in yields is too 
harmful to both Main St & Wall St…the $1tn Treasury General Account is waiting. 
The Price is Right I: “peak yield” trades are working ahead of central bank hikes to 
restore credibility; but note the long-duration rally in XBI, KRE, REITs, small caps is 
unmatched by flows (Charts 3-6); not too late to hedge AI-soaked stock indexes. 
The Price is Right II: but watch transportation stocks (IYT), which are now flirting with 
the 200-day moving average @ 80… a break below confirms summer “as good as it 
gets” macro derisking becomes an autumn stagflation event.

On asset allocation: the diversification benefit of holding bonds is fading fast, so 
yields may stay high even if Treasury buying caps the surge; in the 2000-2019 “secular 
stagnation” era of rapid globalization & record-low inflation, bonds & stocks did not 
move together…a 2-3% yield was almost a bonus; today, returns are correlated again 
(Chart 8), and allocators may require higher yields before moving wholesale into bonds. 
Flight to quality: stocks that can generate big free cash flow without excessive debt 
were the best-performing factor of the last 30 years, better than momentum, growth, 
dividends (Chart 9); today S&P 500 FCF yield is at record lows, high-FCF stocks (VFLO) 
are up 37% YTD; in a stagflation year like ‘22, the index rose 9% vs. SPY -18%. 
On AI: despite $1.5tn spent in past 3 years, scant evidence yet of economy-wide 
productivity gains; in fact, total factor productivity is falling below trend (Chart 10), a 
measure highly correlated the past 50 years with consumer confidence; sometimes Main 
St knows what Wall St doesn’t.
