Trump needs lower oil
Since oil spiked in August, the probability of Democrats regaining the Senate has risen from 41% to
above 50%. With Brent nearing $110/bbl last night and just seven and a half weeks until the midterms,
markets must consider how long Republicans will tolerate elevated prices.
Trump said on Tuesday that oil would remain high until after the midterms before falling sharply. That
may have reinforced expectations of a prolonged conflict, but the administration could still feel
compelled to ease the pressure, particularly with the 10-year yield approaching 5%.
De-escalation looks difficult, but so would the administration’s final two years if Democrats regained
both the House and Senate. Oil remaining comfortably above $100 through the midterms is therefore
far from certain.

A JPY special
The yen supertanker is turning
For two years, USD/JPY largely ignored collapsing rate differentials. That regime may finally be
breaking.
Faster BoJ tightening, political tolerance for a stronger yen and the reversal of Japanese capital
outflows are now aligning. Intervention may have started the move, but TS Lombard argues that
repatriation is what could sustain it.
A break below 150 may therefore be only the beginning. Its fair-value models point towards 130–140.

The great divergence closes
The US–Japan 10-year yield spread has collapsed over the past two years, while USD/JPY remained
close to its highs. The yen is finally beginning to reconnect with rates.
If that convergence continues, the pressure remains towards a lower USD/JPY.
TS Lombard
Carry meets vol
The carry trade works until volatility overwhelms the yield pickup. As JPY vol rises, the risk-adjusted
return from remaining short yen deteriorates rapidly, forcing leveraged positions to shrink.
Intervention was the catalyst. Volatility is what can turn it into a broader unwind.

The global yield problem
A GPIF rotation into JGBs would matter far beyond Tokyo. Japanese investors have historically been
major buyers of foreign debt; becoming more price-sensitive, or actively repatriating, removes an
important source of demand just as global government issuance is accelerating.
The yen turn could therefore place additional upward pressure on US and European yields.

Natixis
JPY can wake up VIX
A stronger yen does not merely reflect global risk-off, it can create it. A disorderly break lower in
USD/JPY could force carry-trade deleveraging across global risk assets, turning the yen rally into a
broader volatility event.
With VIX recently reset, equity volatility offers an attractive hedge against that tail. If the yen
supertanker accelerates, global vol may not stay asleep.
