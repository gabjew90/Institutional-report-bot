The yen supertanker is turning
For two years, USD/JPY largely ignored collapsing rate differentials. That regime may finally be
breaking.
Faster BoJ tightening, political tolerance for a stronger yen and the reversal of Japanese capital
outflows are now aligning. Intervention may have started the move, but TS Lombard argues that
repatriation is what could sustain it.
A break below 150 may therefore be only the beginning. Its fair-value models point towards 130–140.
The great divergence closes
The US–Japan 10-year yield spread has collapsed over the past two years, while USD/JPY remained
close to its highs. The yen is finally beginning to reconnect with rates.
If that convergence continues, the pressure remains towards a lower USD/JPY.
TS Lombard
Carry meets vol
The carry trade works until volatility overwhelms the yield pickup. As JPY vol rises, the risk-adjusted
return from remaining short yen deteriorates rapidly, forcing leveraged positions to shrink.
Intervention was the catalyst. Volatility is what can turn it into a broader unwind.

The BoJ is playing catch-up
Japanese financial conditions remain unusually easy. Real rates are still negative across much of the
curve, while inflation and credit growth suggest policy is well behind the economy.
TS Lombard expects quarterly hikes from January 2027 and sees the terminal rate reaching 2% by Q4
2027.

The wage spiral is here
Japan’s inflation story is no longer only about imported costs. Wage growth and services inflation are
now moving higher together, giving the BoJ greater confidence that domestic inflation can survive
tighter policy.
That makes sustained normalization, and a stronger yen, much more credible according to TS
Lombard.
TS Lombard
It was always about flows
Japan’s enormous current-account surplus was overwhelmed by portfolio and carry outflows. For much
of the past two years, Japanese investors continued buying foreign assets despite increasingly
attractive domestic yields.
That behavior is starting to reverse. Capital is moving back towards Japan, turning repatriation, not
intervention, into the structural driver of JPY strength.
TS Lombard
The GPIF whale wakes up
GPIF normally keeps domestic bonds close to its 25% benchmark weight. That allocation had already
risen to 27% by the end of March, and the fund is permitted to move as much as 6ppt above
benchmark.

A further shift towards JGBs would represent a major repatriation flow. The world’s largest pension fund
may only be starting to turn.
TS Lombard
The global yield problem
A GPIF rotation into JGBs would matter far beyond Tokyo. Japanese investors have historically been
major buyers of foreign debt; becoming more price-sensitive, or actively repatriating, removes an
important source of demand just as global government issuance is accelerating.
The yen turn could therefore place additional upward pressure on US and European yields.

Positioning still has fuel
The market is not positioned for a full yen revaluation. Speculative positioning remains short, leaving
plenty of fuel if USD/JPY breaks decisively below 150.
The first phase was intervention. The next could be forced covering.

150 is not fair value
TS Lombard sees USD/JPY breaking 150, but its relative-price and rate models point towards fair value
between 130 and 140.
That is the real risk for yen shorts: 150 may look like major support on the screen, but fundamentally it
could be only the gateway to the next regime.
The huge trend comes in around these levels, so watch the JPY very carefully here.

JPY can wake up VIX
A stronger yen does not merely reflect global risk-off; it can create it. A disorderly break lower in
USD/JPY could force carry-trade deleveraging across global risk assets, turning the yen rally into a
broader volatility event.
With VIX recently reset, long equity volatility offers an attractive hedge against that tail. If the yen
supertanker accelerates, global vol may not stay asleep.
