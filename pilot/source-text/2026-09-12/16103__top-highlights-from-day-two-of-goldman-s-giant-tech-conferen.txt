Top Highlights From Day Two Of Goldman's Giant 
Tech Conference 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, SEP 10, 2026 - 08:35 PM 
The closely watched Goldman TMT Communacopia + Technology Conference is taking place in 
San Fran this week, and yesterday it was day 2 with presentations from Microsoft, IBM and Dell 
among others (for the recap of Day 1, see here). Below we summarize some of the key views 
from Goldman's Sales and Trading  team, but as one can imagine (from what is basically a giant 
attempt to boost investor excitement), the overall message from the second day of the 
conference presentations was, just like after day 1, "decidedly bullish." 
During the second day, companies discussed how agentic AI is driving significant demand 
tailwinds for both services and equipment, all the way from systems to components. Companies 
highlighted higher structural demand driven by AI, which are driving capacity additions given 
extended demand visibility from customers. Companies struck a bullish tone on a broad-based 
recovery across end markets - including industrial and automotive - driven by lean inventory 
levels, content growth, and steadily improving demand. IBM expects to see accelerating 
revenue growth in its Consulting business in 2H26, which it believes is idiosyncratic in nature 
due to its larger exposure to Gen AI service areas relative to peers.

●​ Resilient Consumer Spending: Multiple companies (BKNG, TOST, Block, ETSY)

organizational structures to maintain output while reducing fixed costs, leading to

Main highlights:

partnering with third-party LLMs for agentic commerce. Booking Holdings is testing

Takeaways for US Semiconductors:  
Key Takeaway #1 - AI Adoption Trends and EDA Software: Companies discussed how agentic 
AI is driving significant demand tailwinds for both services and equipment, all the way from 
systems to components. Importantly, a number of companies cited concrete ways in which they 
are implementing AI in their internal business processes to drive incremental revenue growth via

new product development and faster innovation cycles in services. Companies also cited their 
use of internal AI tools to drive cost efficiencies, in line with past commentary. 
●​ Cadence: Cadence continues to see design momentum with traditional and 
non-traditional chip and system designers. The company noted chip complexity is 
expected to grow by 48X over the next five years, which is a key driver for its agentic AI 
solutions - enabling accelerated recurring revenue growth. The company also shared 
optimism around physical AI, and expects it to be a large market opportunity in the 
medium-term. 
●​ IBM: IBM highlighted the benefits of its cost-effective, open-source AI tools such its 
smaller LLMs, RHEL AI, WatsonX code generation tools, and WatsonX Orchestrate for 
scaling agentic AI. Management also highlighted the growing importance of customer's 
data in the context of AI, and sees GenAI as a key new source of value in its Consulting 
business. 
●​ Applied Materials: Applied noted shorter time to market, and yield and output innovation 
driven by AI as key ROI provider in the industry to Applied and to its customers. AI is 
increasingly being applied to new product development that translates to incremental 
revenue growth and market share gains for the company over time. Applied also cited 
improved value proposition in services using AI as an additional driver of ROI in the 
industry. 
Key Takeaway #2 - Semiconductor Capital Equipment, Foundry and Memory: Companies 
highlighted higher structural demand driven by AI, which are driving capacity additions given 
extended demand visibility from customers. Supply/demand remains tight across the supply 
chain, as evidenced by comments from TSMC (relative to advanced packaging and wafer 
capacity), Amkor (relative to advanced packaging) and Teradyne (relative to testing). Specific to 
memory, SanDisk noted that NAND remains under tight supply/demand conditions - and this is 
expected to last through 2027 and potentially 2028. 
●​ TSMC: TSMC continues to view AI as a multi-year megatrend that remains at a very 
early stage, with lower compute costs expected to accelerate AI adoption and ultimately 
drive greater demand for advanced silicon. TSMC noted that execution in Arizona 
continues to progress well, with Phase 1 achieving yields comparable to Taiwan and 
already generating profits, while TSMC expects approximately 30% of its N2-and-beyond 
capacity to be located outside Taiwan in five years, primarily in Arizona. Despite dilution 
from the N2 ramp and overseas expansion, management remains comfortable achieving 
its long-term gross margin target of 56% and above, supported by disciplined capacity 


[Page 5]
 
planning, high utilization, productivity improvements, cost reductions, and pricing that 
reflects TSMC’s value. 
●​ Amkor: Management discussed phase 2 of its Arizona campus, driven by strong demand 
for advanced packaging. Amkor expects compute revenue to continue to grow at 30% 
YoY in 2027. Amkor reiterated its $11 bn 2030 revenue target, and expects 2028 CapEx 
levels to be elevated and sustain in 2029, before moderating in 2030 and 2031. 
●​ SanDisk: The company remains confident in its long-term financial targets underpinned 
by its long-term agreements (NBMs), which themselves have been enabled in part by 
strong and growing datacenter demand. Management also highlighted its low capital 
intensity, and reiterated it sees share repurchases as central to its capital return policy. 
●​ Teradyne: Teradyne sees increasing opportunities with hyperscale customers, whose 
diverse requirements across chip, system, and datacenter testing create reinforcing 
demand across all three businesses. Management also expects the test equipment 
market to at least grow in line with overall semiconductor CapEx. Teradyne expects 
AI-driven growth to accelerate through 2027, supported by rising share gains at a major 
merchant compute customer, expanding engagements with hyperscale custom silicon 
customers, and strong momentum in ASIC testing. 
●​ Applied Materials: Applied Materials expects leading-edge logic, advanced packaging, 
and memory to account for ~80% of WFE in 2026 and similar mix going ahead, and also 
reiterated its outlook for ~40% revenue growth in 2026. Management sees strong growth 
continuing into 2027 and 2028 given increasing orders and backlog with longer-dated 
visibility. The company also expressed confidence in its Process Control business, which 
is expected to grow more than 50% in 2026, driven by increasing demand for yield 
improvement, performance optimization, and materials engineering at advanced nodes. 
Key Takeaway #3 - Analog: Companies struck a bullish tone on a broad-based recovery across 
end markets - including industrial and automotive - driven by lean inventory levels, content 
growth, and steadily improving demand. Demand growth remains outsized in idiosyncratic end 
markets such as datacenter and aerospace/defense - and this is expected to continue in these 
areas for the foreseeable future. 
●​ NXP: Management highlighted improving demand trends across end markets, including 
in automotive, and improving revenue visibility through longer lead times despite a lack 
of OEM inventory restocking. NXP also discussed its strategic pivot to physical AI, its 
hybrid manufacturing strategy, and its gradual price increases and their impact on 
revenue and margins.

Key Takeaways from TSMC presentation: Presenters: Wendell Huang, SVP & CFO 
●​ AI remains in the early stages of a multi-year megatrend: TSMC continues to see 
increasing adoption of AI. Management also highlighted the shift from generative AI 
toward agentic AI, which should broaden compute demand beyond accelerators to 
CPUs. TSMC remains confident in the durability of the AI cycle based on discussions not 
only with its direct customers but also with its customers’ customers. Recent visits of US 
CSPs further reinforced management’s confidence, as cloud service providers appeared 
well prepared in areas including power and broader infrastructure deployment. 
●​ Lower compute costs should drive greater consumption and silicon demand: 
Management believes open-source models and continued improvements in computing 


[Page 18]
 
efficiency will reduce token costs, leading to greater AI consumption and broader 
adoption, supporting incremental demand for advanced silicon. TSMC has already 
incorporated this dynamic into its capacity planning and continues to expand 
aggressively to support what it views as structural, multi-year demand growth. 
●​ Disciplined demand validation remains critical amid aggressive expansion: TSMC uses a 
combination of top-down and bottom-up analysis to validate demand. Its top-down 
assessment considers macroeconomic conditions, semiconductor demand, 
fabless/system companies, the foundry industry, etc, while its bottom-up approach 
evaluates demand across different segments and incorporates feedback from customers 
and customers’ customers. Management acknowledged that semiconductors will remain 
cyclical, but believes correctly identifying the underlying megatrend is more important 
than attempting to forecast every short-term fluctuation. 
●​ Arizona execution is demonstrating that TSMC’s manufacturing model can travel: TSMC 
reiterated that overseas expansion is based on customer demand for geographic 
flexibility and an appropriate level of government support. Arizona Phase 1, using N4 
technology, is in operation, with yields comparable to Taiwan, satisfactory quality and 
reliability, and positive profitability. Management views this as an important milestone 
because it demonstrates internally and to customers that TSMC can produce wafers in 
the US with the same quality as in Taiwan. Equipment move-in for Phase 2, which will 
use N3 technology, is expected to begin soon; construction of Phase 3 has started; and 
preparation work for Phase 4 and the first US advanced-packaging facility is underway. 
●​ Arizona has substantial room for further expansion: In addition to its original Arizona 
development, TSMC has acquired another parcel of land capable of accommodating an 
additional five to six fabs, supporting its recently announced incremental US$100bn 
investment. Based on its current plan, TSMC expects approximately 30% of its 
N2-and-beyond capacity to be located outside Taiwan in five years, primarily in Arizona. 
However, the most advanced technologies will continue to ramp first in Taiwan because 
the early-stage technology transfer requires close collaboration and frequent movement 
of engineers among R&D and manufacturing sites. Overseas deployment will generally 
follow after the technology has stabilized in Taiwan. 
●​ Customers increasingly value and are willing to pay for geographic flexibility: 
Management noted that customers initially requested only a modest amount of 
geographically diversified supply, but their requirements have increased as TSMC has 
demonstrated successful execution in Arizona. TSMC believes this geographic flexibility 


[Page 19]
 
represents incremental value and intends to reflect that value in its pricing, with 
customers increasingly willing to recognize the associated premium. 
●​ Technology, manufacturing excellence, capacity, and trust underpin TSMC’s competitive 
moat: Management believes customers select a foundry partner based on access to the 
best technology, the ability to ramp rapidly into high-volume manufacturing with attractive 
economics, sufficient capacity, and confidence that the foundry will not compete with its 
customers. TSMC is confident in maintaining this lead by continuing to advance its 
roadmap. Management also highlighted its record of rapidly ramping new technologies, 
the scale of its wafer and advanced-packaging investments, and its consistent delivery 
against customer commitments as key elements of customer trust that cannot be 
replicated through CapEx or engineering recruitment alone. 
●​ Pricing remains strategic rather than opportunistic: TSMC does not intend to raise prices 
simply because near-term utilization is high, nor reduce prices simply because utilization 
temporarily weakens. Instead, the company’s approach is to price sustainably and 
consistently based on the value it provides, while ensuring that customers can remain 
successful over the long term. Management noted that TSMC’s structural profitability has 
continued to improve, with its long-term gross margin target increasing from 50% five 
years ago to 53% and now 56% and above. 
●​ Leading-edge wafer and advanced-packaging capacity remain tight: Management 
continues to build capacity wherever possible to meet customer demand. N3 demand 
remains particularly strong: unlike prior technology cycles, when demand for a node 
typically began declining two to three years after its introduction, TSMC is adding further 
N3 capacity and still does not expect supply to be sufficient. The company is also 
expanding advanced-packaging capacity to address strong accelerator demand. Outside 
TSMC, management identified power and memory as potential bottlenecks cited by 
other parts of the AI supply chain, while emphasizing that foundry capacity itself remains 
difficult to expand because fabs require two to three years to build and another one to 
two years to ramp. 
●​ TSMC’s disclosed AI revenue understates its broader exposure: Management reiterated 
that its disclosed AI revenue definition is relatively narrow and primarily captures GPUs, 
custom AI accelerators and memory base die. It excludes CPUs and networking 
products because TSMC cannot reliably determine whether each chip is ultimately 
deployed in an AI system. As agentic AI drives incremental demand across x86, Arm, 
and RISC-V CPUs, as well as networking and other supporting silicon, TSMC’s total 


[Page 20]
 
AI-related exposure is therefore broader than its reported AI-accelerator revenue. The 
company intends to retain its existing definition until it can more accurately distinguish 
the end use of these products. 
●​ No visible barrier to continued transistor scaling: TSMC is currently ramping N2, with 
A14 expected approximately two years later, while development of the subsequent node 
is already underway. Management does not currently see a technical issue preventing 
delivery of the next node or continued advancement beyond the existing roadmap. 
TSMC views close collaboration with customers as a key foundry advantage, allowing 
the company to develop each technology against specific customer performance, power, 
density, and economic requirements. 
●​ Cross-node capacity optimization is an underappreciated advantage: TSMC highlighted 
its ability to generate additional output through productivity improvements and to convert 
tools and capacity across technology nodes. For example, the company can use 
portions of its N5 capacity to support N3 demand and can also optimize capacity across 
less-adjacent nodes where technically feasible. Management views this cross-node 
flexibility as a distinctive element of TSMC’s manufacturing excellence, allowing the 
company to respond more quickly to demand changes while reducing the risk of capacity 
obsolescence. 
●​ Advanced packaging is strategic primarily because it enables wafer growth: CoWoS 
capacity remains tight, and TSMC already started outsourcing selected packaging 
processes to OSAT partners to support additional growth. Management emphasized that 
advanced packaging is intended primarily to ensure and enable wafer demand rather 
than serve as a standalone profit pool. TSMC is therefore developing multiple packaging 
architectures, including panel-level packaging, etc, while remaining open to customers 
using alternative packaging providers, as either approach ultimately supports demand for 
TSMC wafers. 
●​ Advanced-packaging execution remains a key differentiator: TSMC noted that its current 
advanced-packaging capability supports approximately 5.5x reticle size, with a roadmap 
to reach approximately 14x reticle sizes within the next several years. While competing 
roadmaps may also appear attractive, management believes execution and the ability to 
deliver in high-volume manufacturing will ultimately determine competitive outcomes. 
TSMC does not believe competitors can use advanced-packaging wins to meaningfully 
displace its leading-edge wafer business, as foundry and packaging remain 
fundamentally different businesses. 


[Page 21]
 
●​ A unified TSMC culture supports overseas manufacturing execution: TSMC’s objective is 
not to create separate Taiwan and US cultures, but to establish a consistent TSMC 
culture centered on integrity, commitment, innovation, and customer trust (ICIC). Locally 
hired Arizona employees spend six to eighteen months training and working in Taiwan, 
while experienced Taiwan-based employees are assigned to Arizona to work alongside 
local teams. Over time, the number of Taiwan assignees should decline as locally trained 
employees assume greater responsibility. TSMC is applying this approach across its 
overseas manufacturing sites. 
●​ Management remains comfortable with its 56%-and-above long-term gross margin 
target: TSMC reiterated its 6 major drivers of profitability: new-technology ramp, pricing, 
cost, product mix, utilization, and FX. The N2 ramp is expected to dilute 2026 GM by 
approximately 2-3ppts, but its profitability should improve over time toward the corporate 
average; management also noted that N2’s structural profitability is better than N3 at a 
comparable stage. Overseas expansion is expected to dilute GM by 2-3ppts during the 
early portion of the current five-year period, then widening to 3-ppts later as more 
overseas fabs begin production. Each new fab will initially create dilution before 
improving along its learning curve. Taking these puts and takes together, management 
remains very comfortable that disciplined capacity planning, healthy utilization, cost 
reduction, productivity gains, and pricing that reflects TSMC’s value will enable the 
company to deliver for shareholders and achieve its long-term gross margin target 'of 
56% and higher' with focus on the 'higher' side.
