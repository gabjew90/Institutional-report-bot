 
"The Longer Fragile Market Conditions Continue 
Without A Healthy Reset, The More Violent The 
Moves Will Be..." 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 23, 2026 - 01:20 PM 
By Christian Daas and David Marino, Bloomberg Markets Live reporters and strategists 
The stomach-churning selloff that rocked equities has options traders girding for more volatility 
ahead. 
Stocks have been increasingly turbulent in recent weeks, roiled by concerns ranging from higher 
interest rates to a Democratic sweep in US midterm elections that may change the regulatory 
landscape for artificial intelligence-focused firms. To derivatives strategists, the pattern pointed 
to an increase in so-called fragility — a condition where long periods of calm are broken up by 
oversized market swings.  
Now a roughly 8% tumble in the Nasdaq 100 Index over Thursday and Friday and the S&P 500 
Index’s more than 10% slide from its peak has taken measures of equity volatility to their highest 
levels in months — a sign that the turbulence that lurked beneath the market’s surface is 
breaking through. Some options mavens say there’s a growing risk that sudden selloffs will 
become more frequent, driving overall volatility higher.  
“Index volatility has been relatively subdued despite the turmoil under the surface, given 
multi-decade low stock correlation,” said Ben Bowler, head of global equity derivatives at Bank 
of America Global Research. “Idiosyncratic risk has dominated and markets have chosen to 
rotate, rather than rupture, under stress. Nonetheless, a broad-based shock that drives a 
meaningful pickup in stock correlation, and hence index volatility, remains a risk.” 
 

 
 
The sudden jump in volatility can be seen in the realized swings of the Cboe Volatility Index, or 
VIX, which spiked to the highest level since the April 2025 tariff turmoil. That market fragility has 
been on the rise, especially in the technology sector that’s been leading the market to record 
highs.  
“Single-stock fragility has remained rampant in 2026, with the number of fragility events in tech 
stocks on track to be the second-highest in over 30 years,” topped just by 2025, said Bowler.  
For most of the year, volatility in broader indexes was comparatively low — largely because the 
big moves notched by individual stocks tended to counter each other. Equities marched higher 
and gauges such as the VIX, remained largely unperturbed in the face of risks including wars in 
Ukraine and Iran, rising Treasury yields and a Federal Reserve preparing to hike interest rates.   
Much of that was due to several factors, including a record-low correlation between stocks as 
well as a growing flow of zero-day to expiry option selling that provided dealers with futures to 
buy on market declines and to sell on rallies. More broadly, excitement over the economic 
potential of artificial intelligence gave investors little reason to stay on the sidelines.  
That calm has been eviscerated in recent days, as doubts over the durability of the AI trade 
accelerate a day after Anthropic PBC’s blockbuster IPO. The VIX on Friday spiked to its highest 
level since the tariff-related market shocks of April 2025, signaling a sharp rise in the cost of 
downside protection. At the same time, correlation rose to the highest since March and the flows 
in 0DTE options are shifting. 
 

 
“Markets can stay calm while risks build beneath the surface, as volatility-suppressing forces 
help steady conditions,” said Bram Kaplan, JPMorgan’s head of Americas equity derivatives 
strategy. “In a shock, that support can fade quickly, leading to a sharp repricing as investors 
de-lever.” 
 
It was only in mid-October that the S&P 500 ended the longest period without a 1% drop since 
the Covid-19 pandemic in 2020. But Friday’s big moves may signal something broader. 
“The longer that the fragile conditions continue, without a healthy reset/shakeout of positioning, 
the more violent the moves — in both spot and vol — can be when they finally arrive,” said 
Jeremy Wien, managing partner at hedge fund Moo Point Capital.  
While correlation among the S&P 500’s 50 largest stocks has risen, individual tech stocks have 
continued to see sharp divergences. BofA strategists noted a spike in one-day dispersion during 
the Nov. 5 selloff, with a similar, albeit smaller, episode on Friday as names such as Micron and 
Nvidia made extreme moves. 
 

 
 
That signals the market upset in early November was more of a positional pain rotation than a 
complete “sell everything” situation. The question now is whether the selling will become even 
more widespread across asset classes and markets, which would keep volatility higher for 
longer.  
Whether the VIX reverts back to the 20s or spikes further “will likely depend on whether the 
current move is a repricing of elevated valuations/expectations or is the early stages of 
something more sinister, like a credit event that can lead to knock-on effects that shock the 
broader system,” said Wien.  
“Whichever it ends up being, the market has been awakened from the complacency that many 
of us felt was prevalent for the last several months.”
