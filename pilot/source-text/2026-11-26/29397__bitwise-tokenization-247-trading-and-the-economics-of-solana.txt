Solana has never struggled to define its raison d’être. The vision of building an onchain Nasdaq dates 
back to its 2018 founding. It aimed to be the largest trading market and a price-discovery engine. Over 
time, the vision broadened and grew more ambitious. Internet Capital Markets promises to go beyond 
trading existing financial assets and to make it possible for anyone to create, issue, trade, and raise 
capital around assets and ideas on a globally accessible network.
More than six years after its genesis block, what Solana has achieved on its path toward that goal is 
extraordinary. It processes nearly 1,800 transactions per second, far more than other major general-
purpose blockchains combined. So far, it has been a primary venue for speculative trading, which has 
helped it build resilience and stress-test its infrastructure under heavy load. Now, it is starting to onboard 
the largest share of tokenized equities volume across all blockchains, bringing it a step closer to realizing 
its ambitious Internet Capital Markets vision. 
But this is only the beginning, and Solana still has a long way to go. 
We think Solana presents a distinct opportunity, and investors underestimate its growth potential as 
more financial activity moves onchain. 
Solana wants to become the infrastructure for global financial markets. Its vision is a world where 
financial activity operates at the speed and scale of the internet. If it succeeds, the opportunity extends 
far beyond the crypto market Solana serves today.
In this report, we explain how Solana's infrastructure was designed to support high transaction volumes 
and how it manages to offer some of the cheapest fees for everyday transactions across all chains. We 
look at the businesses that build on Solana today and explain why many more are likely to follow. We 
discuss tokenization, which is expected to drive a significant share of onchain activity and revenue. 
Solana is entering its next phase from a position of strength, but success is not inevitable. It must now 
defend and extend its lead while delivering increasingly ambitious upgrades. We are optimistic, but the 
opportunity comes with execution risk. With this in mind, we explore Solana's future valuation.

Understanding Solana
A blockchain built for financial markets 
Solana was built to be a fast blockchain that could support financial markets at scale.1 Its early pitch 
described it as a “blockchain at Nasdaq speed” that would enable people to trade on a high-performance 
public network while retaining control of their assets. 
That idea has since evolved into the concept of “Internet Capital Markets”: a financial system where assets 
can be issued, traded, transferred, and used as collateral on the same network. The goal is to make moving 
money and assets as easy as moving information online.
For its vision to work, Solana must handle the scale of global financial markets, with the capacity to process 
billions of orders and settle transactions quickly and cheaply. 
How Solana handles transactions 
Virtually all early blockchains used sequential execution, processing transactions one after another. 
This quickly led to congestion. Imagine a restaurant chef who must finish each order before starting 
the next. As more customers arrive, the queue grows endlessly. Early blockchains were in a similar 
position, with demand exceeding capacity, processing times growing ever longer, and fees rising as 
users competed for priority.
Solana was the first blockchain to tackle this issue. To do so, it introduced parallel execution, which 
processes transactions at the same time as long as they don't conflict. If they do, one must wait for the 
other to finish. The key is identifying which transactions can run together, which Solana does thanks to 
its account system.

Understanding Solana
Solana runs on a network of computers called validators. Their role is to process transactions and verify 
that they follow the rules. When someone makes a payment or a trade, that information needs to reach 
the other validators so they can check it and update their records. The challenge is sharing these updates 
quickly across the network as new transactions keep arriving.
The validator that executes the next block is known as the leader. It divides the block into small pieces and 
sends them to other validators. Those validators copy the pieces and distribute them across the network. 
The pieces travel simultaneously, spreading the work without requiring the leader to send the full block 
to everyone. Once validators have enough pieces to rebuild the block, they check it and vote to help the 
network agree on which block to accept. Incredibly, for a batch of transactions, this process takes a fraction 
of a second.
Parallel execution and efficient block sharing help Solana handle more activity and reduce congestion. The 
network can charge a low, fixed base fee, while users can add an optional fee to prioritize their transactions.

The main strengths of Solana applications are their accessibility and composability. Users only need an 
internet connection and a smartphone or computer to access Solana’s trading, lending, and payment 
apps. They can then buy an asset through a trading app and use it as collateral in a lending app. Everything 
happens in a few clicks, possibly in a single transaction.
Who keeps the system going 
Validators run Solana, processing transactions and maintaining the shared network record. Holders can 
opt to stake their SOL (the Solana network's native token), which means locking their tokens with a 
validator, who then uses it to build more blocks and increase its voting power. In return for locking their 
SOL, holders earn yield. 
SOL is also used to pay for transaction fees. Half of the base fee is burned (permanently removed from 
circulation), which reduces the total supply of SOL. The rest, along with other fees, is split between validators 
and delegators. For the most part, the yield comes from new SOL issuance. But, with growing network 
activity and falling inflation (thanks to recent upgrades to SOL's tokenomics), the fees are expected to make 
up a larger share of the stakers’ earnings.

Solana does not guarantee the execution sequencing natively, but services built on top of it do. Traders 
can submit transactions as a bundle that executes in exact order and atomically, meaning all transactions 
succeed or none do. For a long time, traders paid for this mostly through tips denominated in SOL. New 
services also accept priority fees, meaning both mechanisms are increasingly used to pay for ordering.
This is where the Google analogy becomes clear. Google earns nothing from the search itself, but a lot 
from businesses that want to appear in the right place at the right moment. On Solana, regular transactions 
are cheap, but traders who value speed or position will pay for them.
We call this ordering value: the economic advantage of when a transaction executes relative to others. The 
network captures much of this value through priority fees and tips. As more trading moves onchain, this 
revenue should grow, and capturing it is crucial to Solana's success.

Solana's Platform Advantage
The platform opportunity
Many critics argue that blockchains like Solana won’t accrue value in the long term. Useful technology 
rails get commoditized, so their fees converge toward operating costs. Stock exchanges are the classic 
example. NYSE and Nasdaq list some of the world’s biggest companies and handle hundreds of billions 
of dollars in trades every day, yet their parent companies are worth only a small fraction of the value that 
trades through them. If blockchains are just infrastructure for moving and trading assets, they should be 
valued the same way.
But this logic overlooks something important.
Nasdaq is not a platform, and you cannot build a business on top of it. You can trade and market make, but 
nobody can launch an exchange, game, payment service, or marketplace on Nasdaq. On Solana, anyone 
can, without even asking for permission. So, there’s an argument that Solana isn't just an unnecessarily 
complicated stock exchange, but more like Apple’s App Store.
The iPhone’s value comes from the millions of apps built for it. Apple says its App Store facilitated about 
$1.3 trillion in sales in 2024,2 and Apple took no commission on more than 90% of it. That’s how the flywheel 
works: The more developers earn on the platform, the more motivated they are to build, the more people 
need an iPhone to use the apps they build, and the more valuable the platform—whether Apple's iOS or 
the Solana network—becomes.
Why Solana attracts developers
Solana has built a vibrant ecosystem, and it keeps growing. The number of apps earning more than 
$100,000 a year has doubled since January 2025, from 54 to 107, even as total app revenue has fallen 
from its early-2025 peak. In our view, that’s a sign that businesses are putting down roots on Solana and 
are here for the long haul.

The app-network symbiosis
Like the App Store, Solana retains a minority of the value created on its network. In early 2025, network 
fees made up nearly half of combined chain and app revenue. Over the next 18 months, even after the 
bull market peaked, Solana applications continued to gain traction, and their fees held up better than the 
network’s. By September 2026, Solana’s share of total fees had fallen to 15%, with apps earning the rest. 
Thanks to low transaction costs, more value now stays with the businesses building there, a trait of every 
successful platform.
Solana applications retain most of the revenue
Weekly share of reported network and application revenue
As apps grow, so do the network’s fees, because every app action is a transaction that pays the network. 
As app revenue nearly doubled from July to September 2026, what users paid to Solana rose from $17 
million to $27 million per month. Apps also create demand for SOL because every transaction is paid in 
SOL and much of Solana’s trading is priced in SOL.
We believe this is what sets Solana apart from traditional exchanges. An exchange that does one thing can 
be commoditized. However, Solana is a platform where a growing number of businesses earn real revenue, 
making it much harder to replace.

Internet Capital Markets
The tokenization opportunity
Brokers and issuers are moving conventional securities onchain, while crypto platforms now list stocks, 
ETFs, and Treasury funds. This trend will drive blockchain adoption over the next decade, and we see 
Solana as one of the few networks already built to support this activity at scale.
Bringing those assets onchain is getting easier, and we’ve seen progress happening on a few fronts:
Issuers: Since September 2025, approved investors have been able to hold Galaxy’s Class A shares on 
Solana. These are the same shares that trade on Nasdaq and have the same voting and dividend rights.
Brokers: In June 2026, Backpack launched Backpack Securities, giving eligible customers access to U.S. 
stocks and ETFs through traditional brokerage infrastructure. Clients can convert supported holdings into 
Solana tokens backed by securities held through a special-purpose vehicle (SPV), then convert them back 
into brokerage holdings through Backpack, subject to its terms.4
Regulators: In 2026, the SEC approved a Nasdaq rule allowing eligible stocks and ETFs to settle as tokens 
and granted qualifying venues a five-year exemption for permissioned trading of tokenized U.S. stocks on 
blockchains, subject to conditions including the preservation of shareholder rights.5
Exchanges: Nasdaq plans to connect its own stock tokens with Kraken’s xStocks, allowing investors to 
move between the two. Nasdaq expects its tokens to launch in the second quarter of 2027.6
Yet tokenization is still in the very early stages. On September 30, 2026, RWA.xyz tracked $3.3 billion 
of tokenized stocks and ETFs, compared to roughly $152 trillion of listed-company value tracked by 
CompaniesMarketCap, or about $1 in every $47,000.7
The starting point is small, but it is growing quickly. Tokenized stocks rose from about $0.7 billion at the 
start of 2026 to $3.3 billion by the end of September, a jump of nearly 5x. With issuers, regulators, and 
exchanges moving in the same direction, we expect that growth to accelerate.

What sets Solana apart is that it remains inexpensive at scale. In Q3 2026, it processed about 14.2

Risks and Considerations
Risks and Considerations
Our thesis depends on several things going right at the same time, but none of these is guaranteed. Below 
are the areas we believe pose risk to our thesis:
Loss of capital. SOL is volatile, and investors could lose all their invested capital. The risks outlined below 
could each affect the value of SOL, sometimes sharply and at short notice.
Regulation. A friendly regulatory environment cannot be taken for granted. A future administration or court 
ruling may change today’s largely open stance toward tokenization. If traditional companies, financial or 
otherwise, feel threatened by tokenization, they will likely fight it, including through regulatory channels. 
Rules outside the U.S. may also limit who can trade tokenized stocks, which matters because much of 
today’s volume comes from outside U.S. market hours. 
Network resilience and governance. Solana’s usefulness depends on the network remaining available 
and trusted. Past outages show that disruption can happen. Further outages, difficult upgrades, validator 
concentration, or governance disputes could weaken confidence, even if demand for tokenization 
continues to grow.
Adoption. Our thesis assumes that issuers, institutions and users will move a meaningful share of activity 
onchain. That shift may take longer than expected or remain limited to particular use cases, even where 
the regulatory environment is supportive and Solana continues to compete effectively for the activity that 
does move onchain.
Competition. Even if the regulatory environment is welcoming and tokenization happens on public 
blockchains, Solana still needs to win the market. The opportunity is so large that many competitors will try 
to capture it. Today, Ethereum and L2s hold most tokenized assets by value, led by Treasuries and funds. 
Robinhood and Base have a distribution advantage because their platforms onboard tens of millions of 
users. While we believe Solana is uniquely positioned to capture the flow of tokenized assets, it's not the 
only game in town.
Fee capture. We assume the network will continue capturing fees from ordering value, which is where 
most of the Solana REV comes from. However, this value isn't guaranteed to remain with the network. 
Applications and validators can strike offchain deals to sell ordering value themselves, circumventing Jito 
tips and priority fees, which are the main channels for capturing it today. Our model assumes that fees per 
dollar of value traded remain close to today’s levels. If they fell, that would impact the network’s growth.
Scaling. To achieve the high transaction volumes needed for our thesis to play out, Solana needs to 
process 12 times today’s transaction load and about 2.5 times the block compute for tokenized stocks 
alone. This number rises to 5x in the bull case. The throughput upgrade needs to happen while keeping 
everyday transaction costs low and the network resilient. An outage during heavy trading would erode the 
trust institutions need. While Solana has a strong record of solving difficult engineering challenges, a future 
bottleneck is not out of the question.
Decentralization. The cost of running validator software rises as the network scales. If stake concentrates 
among fewer operators, or if governance disputes over fees or inflation become divisive, institutions and 
regulators may question the network’s resilience, even amid growing demand for tokenization.
Security. Exploits and wallet compromises remain common, and AI tools can make the vulnerabilities 
easier to find. A hack of key tokenization infrastructure would be a major setback for the network. Over the 
longer term, the threat of quantum computing breaking today’s cryptographic primitives looms. Migrating 
wallets to post-quantum signatures will require significant coordination.
Model risk. Our valuation rests on assumptions that are difficult to assess today. We don’t know the trade 
size when institutions arrive, how tokenized trading volume will grow relative to regular stocks, or what 
investors will be willing to pay for network revenue in five years. Today, Solana trades at 230x revenue, while 
our base case uses a 40x multiple. The price outlooks are sensitive to changes in these inputs.

Looking Ahead
Institutional interest in crypto is still young, but its growth shows how quickly the asset class is maturing. 
Solana, a major trading platform leading the way, is one reason why. 
Solana is developed enough to attract crypto-native companies and investors, yet it still has ample room 
to grow. And its ambitions are boundless. It wants to become the place to trade and issue any asset, 
raise capital, and serve as a payment network for agents and humans alike. Executing this vision is 
unprecedented in difficulty, but so was bringing the network to where it is today.
And today, Solana processes almost twice as many transactions as the 17 other major chains combined. 
Most transactions cost a fraction of a cent. Yet users still paid $69 million in Q3 for valuable transactions.
It is also a place where businesses make money. Together, Solana apps have earned $3.6 billion since the 
start of 2025, more than all apps on Ethereum and Hyperliquid L1 combined.
Its technology has been tested under tough conditions, proving it can handle higher trading volume.
A taste of what comes next is already here. Tokenized assets, especially stocks, are in an early, rapid growth 
phase, with trading volume exceeding $8.3 billion in Q3. As AI agents start paying for services, Solana 
offers the kind of foundational layer they need. Monthly stablecoin payments on the network grew almost 
fivefold this year to $3.3 billion, and Visa, Stripe, and Western Union are already building on it.
All in all, we think Solana starts from a position no other chain has, with the speed, liquidity and apps to lead 
onchain markets. We are excited about the journey ahead.
