Life after the squeeze
The monster squeeze has changed prices, but not necessarily the bigger picture.
Equities are running into major resistance, the AI trade is becoming increasingly selective, and
positioning suggests there could still be fuel left in the squeeze.
Meanwhile, some of the more interesting contrarian setups are emerging elsewhere, with gold trading
at unusually depressed levels and King Dollar finally showing signs of fatigue. The "easy" part of the
bounce may be behind us. The next phase is about picking the right trades.
Back into the wall
SPX has gone from extreme oversold territory to major resistance in a single session. Not something
you see very often. The 7350–7400 area is big resistance, with the 200-day moving average coming in
there. The longer-term trend comes in around 7200 (all cash levels).
The big question is whether the "easy" part of the bounce is already behind us.

Big rallies, broken trends
According to BTIG, there have been 42 previous instances since 1985 of NDX rallying 6% or more while
closing below its 200-day moving average. The median return over the following 30 days was -5.35%,
with the market higher only 39% of the time.
Despite the monster bounce, NDX futures remain well below the 200-day moving average, with the
short-term descending trendline still intact. Perhaps most telling is that even a rally of this magnitude
hasn't managed to get NDX back above its 21-day moving average. That tells you just how beaten up
tech is.

The squeeze that didn't clear the shorts?
S&P E-mini futures open interest surged $36.6bn between November 17 and 20 as the index plunged
5.9%. Friday alone saw a $26.6bn increase, the biggest single-day change of 2026 outside roll periods.
Interestingly, Monday's monster squeeze saw open interest decline by just $7.9bn. Either substantial
fresh longs entered the market or a meaningful portion of the bears is still hanging around. Potentially
both.

Jensen can't fix everything
The return of Jensen has provided spectacular relief, but the bigger AI debate remains unresolved.
As UBS points out, investor focus has shifted from the sheer scale of AI spending to the returns that
spending can generate. Expect increasing differentiation between companies that can fund AI
investment internally and those dependent on debt and speculative growth.
The next phase of the AI trade could be much more about dispersion than beta.
The great AI derating
Bernstein's analysis of 66 stocks across nine sectors shows that data center exposure explains roughly
45% of the variation in valuation compression.
Interestingly, memory and server OEMs have suffered some of the biggest valuation dislocations, while
networking names have held up considerably better.
The market is becoming increasingly selective about which AI exposure it wants to own.

SNDK – priced for disaster
SNDK was trading at just 2.5x forward earnings as of Friday's close, compared with its three-year
average of 10.4x.
The 76% multiple compression is almost twice what Bernstein's data center exposure model would
imply, although AI-driven pricing potentially makes the company's true exposure considerably higher.
The market clearly doesn't believe these earnings are sustainable.
Technically, SNDK remains deeply oversold, but as we often point out, oversold can stay oversold for
much longer than most think possible. The stock has been oversold since October and has more than
halved since then. SNDK is trading well below its 200-day moving average, with the 50-day about to
cross below it.
Things look so bad that the contrarian in us is starting to pay attention.

Less crowded
Months of AI-led volatility have forced investors to de-gross aggressively across Asia. Momentum
exposure has come down substantially. There is considerably less positioning excess to worry about
should the rebound gather momentum.

Playing the right tail
The NT ratio (Nikkei relative to TOPIX) has taken a hit during the AI liquidation. GS likes positioning for
a potential recovery toward October levels via Nikkei outperformance call spreads. An interesting way
to play an AI comeback in Japan without having to get the outright market direction right.

New money, old gold
Tether held an estimated 146.5 tonnes of gold as of June, putting it alongside Libya and ahead of the
Philippines in terms of gold reserves. If classified as a gold ETF, Tether would rank as the seventh
largest globally.
Perhaps more interestingly, SocGen estimates that around 65% of inflows into Tether's gold products
could represent new money entering the gold market rather than a reallocation of existing exposure.
The gold investor base is expanding beyond traditional players.

King dollar
The dollar has come a long way. DXY is putting in its biggest down candle in a while, just as it
approaches the major descending trendline that has been in place since mid-2023. The dollar is also
trading rather stretched versus its 200-day moving average.
Bullish on the dollar or not, we could be setting up for a dollar pause from here.
