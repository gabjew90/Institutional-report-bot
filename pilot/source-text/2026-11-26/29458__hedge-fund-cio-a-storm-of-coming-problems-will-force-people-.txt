 
Hedge Fund CIO: "A Storm Of Coming Problems 
Will Force People To Argue Over Whether To Shut 
AI Off, If That's Even Possible" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 23, 2026 - 03:55 AM 
By Eric Peters, CIO of One River Asset Management 
“Almost all participants judged that, under their baseline outlooks for inflation and the labor 
market, further action to raise the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be 
appropriate, in order to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 2% objective,” read the Oct 
27-28 FOMC meeting minutes, released Wednesday. Friday’s S&P 500 close was 10.8% below 
where the index stood on Oct 28. The Nasdaq 100 was 16% lower. Markets are the fiercest 
machines ever devised to humiliate humans. Our desire to be right exceeds our willingness to 
accept a reality that contradicts it. So, most of us go about life living within our lies. Markets 
force us to confront them. “I’m very concerned about inflation. It’s heading in the wrong direction 
and it is spreading out,” Fed Governor Waller said on Wednesday. “If you really want to get it 
down you’ve got to start taking aggressive actions. You might need to shock people,” he added, 
pressing for 50bps in December. Which, in purely economic terms, is roughly equivalent to one 
Jensen affair with a Chinese spy. “We’ve now had multiple months in a row of borderline 
disturbingly high inflation rates with little progress in the sectors where we would expect to be 
seeing some progress,” The Fed’s Goolsbee said Thursday, markets tanking. Investors tend to 
limit their humiliation by accepting losses in small doses, because the alternative is insolvency. 
Central bankers face no such discipline, so theirs arrives all at once, at scale. “Inflation has 
been above target for more than five and a half years,” said Fed Governor Cook on Friday, 
neglecting to take responsibility for a chronic policy failure that spans nearly her entire time on 
the Board. “Waiting longer to implement rate increases risks getting behind the curve. If we fall 
behind, additional and larger rate increases may be required later to catch up.” 
Walls:  
 

 
“We’re bad at thinking through existential issues, individually and collectively,” said Sparks, 
investor, entrepreneur, iconoclast. He’s been one step ahead of the AI trade for the past 5yrs. 
“Only one technology ever forced us to face existential risk, nuclear weapons, and that’s the 
only evidence we can handle it at all. But we’re 80yrs into the nuclear age, a tiny flash in human 
history. And proliferation turned out to be easy to manage. You need enriched uranium.” Anyone 
can access AI. “Humans are great at storytelling and denial. That’s how we’ve coped with every 
technology so far, and how we’re dealing with this one. But this is the first technology with 
existential risks that we can’t contain.” 
“Imagine a neural implant that let you read every thought in another person’s head,” said 
Sparks. “If that arrived tomorrow it would destroy the world. You’re allowed to sit at dinner 
without unloading everything you’ve thought onto the people around you. If you couldn’t, nobody 
would go to dinner.” We get along because nobody can see inside us. “Now think about the 
systems we’ve built. Legal, financial, software, all of it. They’re just as flawed as we are, and 
they work for the same reason. Nothing could ever look inside and find every weakness at 
once.” 
“We’ve now built the machine that can,” said Sparks. A system that can look inside every other 
system and find every weakness, the way the implant reveals every thought. “What’s your 
defense against someone who knows your secrets? There is none. Talk to the cybersecurity 
people. They can’t fix every flaw, because when you fix one you create another. There’s 
probably no such thing as a defensible system.” A world without walls can’t function. “If you 
create a godlike thing that can jump every wall, how does the world exist? We can’t survive with 
gods among us.” 
“Humans can’t build walls that can’t be penetrated. If we could, we would have already,” said 
Sparks. Who rebuilds them? “The only way is to let the AI do it. You unleash it to improve itself, 
which is the thing everyone is now so worried about.” The machine rewriting the machine, 
recursive self-improvement. “We’d be asking it to improve to a point where we’ll never 
understand it. And a thing you can’t understand, you can’t turn off.” What then? “Then you hope 
at the end of that process, it chooses to help us - please, God, be kind to me - but why would 
it?” 
“That genie is now out of the bottle. As a species, we’ve already made the decision. There’s no 
turning back at this stage,” said Sparks. So, what happens? “Short term is the easy prediction. 
The next 1-3yrs don’t work out well. Old, clunky systems hit by a godlike one. Hysteria, chaos, 
disruption.” And then? “We won’t just accept our fate. We’re survivors, adapters, we’ll fight. The 
 

 
question is what our response looks like, and nobody in AI can tell you.” Meanwhile? “There’s 
an argument to not get on the plane. Everything is networked now.” 
“Now the market,” said Sparks. “Stocks are wildly overpriced. And earnings are 30% above the 
trend line over the past 50yrs. We’ve built a highly leveraged, circular money system, everyone 
funding everyone, and it has pushed earnings into a bubble.” I asked whether this time 
above-trend earnings are justified. “There’s always a justification, until there isn’t.” Okay, slash 
earnings back to trend. “Stocks are 40-50% too high on their own merits. And the Fed is finally 
tightening.” I nodded. “Few people share my view. That makes me very comfortable with it.” 
“So the market should be down 40-50% before any of this,” said Sparks. “Now imagine the 
debate that’s coming. The entire market is levered to the AI trade. And a storm of problems will 
force people to argue over whether to shut the thing off, if that’s even possible.” The data center 
moratorium fights? “Minor compared to what’s coming. Imagine the bear market when society is 
fighting over whether to turn AI off. That’s where this ends.” What should you own now? 
“Treasuries, threes to fives. It’s not sexy.” What’s the right mental model? “Think like a German 
Jewish family, circa 1934. Channel that. Because that mindset helps you survive what’s 
coming.” 
Anecdote:  
Trade for long enough, and you discover that the market has a godlike ability to peer into your 
soul, learn your character flaws, your private fears, weaknesses, pain thresholds. And the 
market uses this knowledge to tease you, sometimes letting you get away with recklessness, 
rewarding your greed, luring you to commit more brazen sins. At other times it creates false 
hope that you’ll escape whatever predicament you’ve gotten yourself into, allowing you to 
believe your own lies, letting you imagine you’ll slip its grasp this one time. Just like it did to 
leveraged Nvidia longs this Tuesday evening, when a blowout earnings report lifted the stock 
sharply higher after hours. By Friday's close it had lost 23% on the week. The process wears 
you down, so that at your weakest moment you puke positions at the absolute low, knowing it’s 
almost certainly a mistake, but grateful to end the torture. And breathe again.  
When enough people hold the same view and pair it with arrogance, the market rounds up the 
herd and culls it in a violent flush. One man’s stop loss pushes price lower to the next man’s 
point of maximum pain, a runaway wildfire, a scorched earth cleanse. Sodom. Gomorrah. Like in 
February 2018, when the sellers of equity volatility watched their hallowed inverse VIX products 
lose 96% of their value in a ferocious afternoon. There was no specific catalyst. No real reason. 
Just the market gods at work. And it’s the very personal struggle with this omniscient 
superintelligence that makes those of us who have survived more introspective and humbler 
 

 
with time. It keeps us on the lookout for those moments when the herd has been rounded up 
and is being led to slaughter. Doing everything in our power to avoid that fate, and to capitalize 
on the chaos.
