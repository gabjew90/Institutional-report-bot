Key excerpt from Jan Hatzius on the downside risks to our base case of steady global growth in 2027…
First, a renewed escalation in Iran, which would hit real income. The positive wealth effect in 2026 has
supported spending at the higher end of the income spectrum, partially offsetting the impact on
consumption from higher energy prices. If markets were to remain weaker in 2027, alongside a significant
energy shock, that could have a bigger impact on spending.
Second, bond market turbulence, especially in the Euro Area and in France and in the run up to the
presidential election. France runs a very large budget deficit (~ 5.5% of GDP), quite far away from where
they need to be for a sustainable budget situation.

Third, a sharper slowdown in AI investment than is currently expected (either because of pushback against
data centres, indigestion in fixed income markets, memory shortages, or saturation), or a larger-than-
expected impact on the labour market (a downside risk, at least from a monetary policy perspective)

4) COMMODITIES RESEARCH (DART) – Europe Winter Tracker: A Cold-Driven Rally

Colder European Weather Forecasts Drive TTF to 90 EUR: Exceptionally colder-than-average two-week
temperature forecasts for NW Europe drove TTF to 90 EUR/MWh, up 6% WoW. TTF outperformed oil
prices, which moved lower yesterday, consistent with headlines a potentially delayed timeline for
geopolitical escalation in the Middle East. To be sure, even near-term weather forecasts can be very
volatile, and not all weather models point to this level of cold. However, if this potential cold shock were
to realize without any offsets, we estimate NW Europe would end winter with gas inventories only 14%
full (well below our 20% base case), the lowest since the 2018 record-low 9% fill.
There’s Further Upside Risk to TTF if the Cold Holds: The colder-than-average weather in NW Europe
realized thus far this month and forecast for the next two weeks is nearly 4 standard deviations colder than
average. It is cold enough that, even if the remainder of winter realized in line with 10-year average
temperatures, winter as a whole (Nov 1st to March 31st) would average near 1 standard-deviation colder
than average. In such a scenario, and assuming ME LNG supply continues to rise through winter, we
estimate TTF would likely have to rally to 106 EUR/MWh to attract enough incremental LNG supply (via
discouraging LNG demand outside of Europe) to help manage its end-winter gas inventories back to more
comfortable levels near 20% full, well above our 65 EUR/MWh 1Q27 TTF base case under average
winter weather.
ME LNG Supply Risk Not Over: While Persian Gulf LNG exports have continued to average above our
base case, the sequential decline in export flows from over 60% of pre-war levels in early Nov to 25%
over the past week suggest Mideast LNG supply outlook remains uncertain, posing further upside risk to
TTF this winter.
