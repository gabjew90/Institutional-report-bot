GS Basics - Jensen Back. Commodities/China. Bear Mkt for
Inflation.
Rich Privorotsky
24 Nov 2026
NVDA: Jensen back as CEO before the US open Monday, Nvidia +31.5%. Any doubt the Trump put is back? 
Massive squeeze after shorts added into end of week. First order, I think Jensen’s return takes some of the doubt
out of Nvidia’s willingness to keep underwriting the AI buildout. Given how aligned he is with the administration, he
may lean in harder. CoreWeave +22% tells you where that mattered. Taiwan +9%, TSMC and Foxconn both
limit up. Kospi +6.5% with Samsung and SK Hynix leading, Nikkei up 4% coming back from holiday. 
 
China: China barely participated… CSI 300 +0.14%, Hang Seng −0.73% in early trade. The administration
tightened the rules on remote access to advanced chips last week. The first tell is whether Commerce starts
granting licenses for Chinese customers to access those chips through offshore data centers… who gets
approved, how quickly and with what limits. Then the bigger question is whether Congress can turn bipartisan anger
into veto proof support for tougher chip restrictions. Jensen testifies Dec. 1.  Note Chinese 10 yr rates = 1.586%
at/near the lows year/5yr/decade...some structurally unencouraging signals on China growth/dis-inflation. 
 
Rates: The most impressive thing yesterday was how little the back end moved with Nasdaq up 6.5%. The front end
gave back some of Friday’s rally, and the curve flattened. The move I’m watching is 2y inflation swaps, which
look to have slipped through the bottom of the range (see below). 
 
Energy : Energy was the other story. Oil just kept slipping. Brent below $90, differentials offered, US refining
cracks well off the highs. Look at Dubai Dec–Jan… backwardation nearly gone, from $3.67/bbl in October to
$0.16/bbl now. The prompt spread near contango. More barrels are finding their way out of the Gulf, and unless we
get another serious escalation, I think Iran’s grip on the marginal barrel is weakening. The market is moving
back toward balance, maybe even surplus. 
 
Fed:  Williams sounded more balanced than Waller or Logan. He left successive  hikes on the table if the data
warrant it. Growth is resilient, labor is stable and inflation is still too high. What I found interesting was the mix… the
tariff impulse is fading, energy and AI bottlenecks are still pushing up some prices, and so far he sees little
spillover into broader inflation. He sees the AI risk as two sided and still has inflation going from around 3.5% this
year to just above 2% next year. December is effectively priced. If commodities keep going this way and the
questions around AI spend stick around, I think the market starts trading the last hike of the cycle. 
 
Risk: Energy is supportive. Rate impulses are supportive. Credit didn't quite have the beta of equities yday. If I'm
honest the price action is bear market (ie too violent for anyone to hold). The best thing the market could do now is
sit still for a couple of days. Let the excess vol premium bleed out and give systematics room to re-lever. Add
decent year end seasonals and even a temporary reprieve on oil or AI, and that’s a lot of potential demand. Given
where people ended up Friday and how underwater those shorts are now, I doubt they’ve already flipped long.  
Watch IFO,  Dell, Workday,  5yr auction, Consumer confidence number (first print after market sell off),
dollar/commds.
