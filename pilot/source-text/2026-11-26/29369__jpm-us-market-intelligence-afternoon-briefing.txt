•         TARIFFS – A WSJ article reported that the US is preparing to announce a new set of 
tariff measures, following the excess manufacturing capacity investigation in 16 
economies under Section 301 of the 1973 Trade Act. The tariff rate and the scope of 
products remains unknown. The announcement itself was widely expected but still added 
another layer of macro uncertainties into the year-end. 
o    POST-IEEPA TARIFF – JPM Strategist Jahangir Aziz (here) expects 
new tariffs to restore the average effective rate toward its pre-IEEPA-ruling 
level (a static rate of 13.8%) vs. 11.2% in November 2026. He added that 
the realized duties/imports ratio is a different measure: 11.1% in January 
vs. 7.1% in September (which includes the forced labor Section 301). He 
expects “the first round Section 301 tariffs to mimic the IEEPA trade deals, 
they may be reset, and likely higher, for some such as ASEAN and the EU.” 
o   WHAT COMES NEXT – In addition to Section 301 (“forced labor” and 
“excess capacity”), there are also a slew of Section 232 actions pending. 
Note that Commerce needs to report findings/recommendation to the 
President within 270 days of initiation. Some notable existing/pending 
actions below.  
▪  Semiconductor: Effective January 15, 2026 
▪  Pharmaceuticals and medical products: Effective July 31 and 
September 29, 2026. 
▪  Drones: Effective September 3, 2026 

▪  Polysilicon derivatives – 15% tariff plus minimum import-price 
rules described as scheduled. Effective December 4, 2026. 
▪  Upholstered furniture – 25% currently, scheduled to rise to 30%. 
Effective January 1, 2027. 
▪  Kitchen cabinets and vanities – 25% currently, scheduled to rise 
to 50%. Effective January 1, 2027. 
▪  Canadian autos, trucks, parts and steel – 50% tariff starting 
January 1 2027 if no trade deal is reached. 
▪  Steel, aluminum and copper derivatives – BIS proposed adding 
14 derivative categories to the tariff schedule. 
▪  Wind turbines, Robotics, industrial machinery – Currently in 
the negotiation window: October 2026 – April 20, 2027). White 
House 
o   USMCA – Jahangir (here) expects renewal in 2028 or 2029; bilateral 
frameworks could arrive earlier, especially for Mexico. The next key catalyst 
will be the public-consultation release in mid-January 2027. 
o   LEGAL CHALLENGES – We have already seen court challenges for the 
“forced labor” Section 301 tariffs (CNBC) and the USCIT is actively 
weighing a ruling on Section 301 before YE26. From Jahangir: “Section 
301 excess-capacity tariffs may also face lawsuits on similar grounds to 
those challenging the forced-labor duties. In recent cases, courts have 
ruled based on a textual reading of the law: the power to levy tariffs belongs 
to Congress, and any presidential authority to impose them must be 
explicitly delegated through legislation. If the courts continue to apply a 
similar reading of delegated tariff authority, either set of tariffs could be 
struck down, but resolution may extend well into 2027.” On whether the 
new Dem-majority Congress could impact the tariff rulings, so far the 
impacts may be limited. Feroli (here) sees overriding the President’s ability 
to impose some tariffs would still require a material number of Republicans 
to cooperate, and the best prospects for that are tariffs on Canada, the UK, 
and Japan.

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the November 23rd Morning Briefing 
•         Last week, we offered our view on the Bull vs. Bear cases, thinking that the Bull 
Case was more likely; however, the Bear Case played out in remarkable fashion pushing 
sentiment toward excessive fears and markets to oversold conditions. 
o   BULL CASE – (i) NVDA Earnings; (ii) Capital Markets; (iii) AI / Tech 
Trade Returns; (iv) Macro Data.  
o   BEAR CASE –  (i) AI / Tech Take Another Leg Lower; (ii) A Decline in 
the Incremental Buyer ; (iii) Hawkish Fed Narrative; (iv) Positioning Purge. 
•         We are moving back to a Tactically Cautious view. In hindsight, it seems obvious 
that we should have been on the sidelines but we, similar to the market, had viewed 
NVDA and capital markets activity as the necessary support for Tech to form a bottom 
and thus whiffed on the tactical bullish call. Now, the uncertainty surrounding NVDA and 
peers and opacity over 2028 and beyond makes Tech longs highly uncertain in the very 
near-term. Given the aggressive nature of the selloff, a tactical bounce seems likely, but 
we are not yet of the view that a bottom forms in the very near-term given the recently 
introduced uncertainty in Tech fundamentals. 
•         Thus, the comparison to 2018 appears to be the best historical comp where the late-
year tactical bounce was faded. We do anticipate a further rotation away from Tech, 
trades which are getting increasingly crowded creating another layer of potential pain 

should a tactical Tech bounce come to fruition. If Tech continues to falter, it is unclear if 
the balance of the SPX can rally enough to boost the index materially higher. 
•         It is worth mentioning that the underlying economy remains strong, AI capex has less 
of a direct impact on the economy, and the AI risks are more tied to financing and the 
consumption wealth effect. The earnings outlook remains positive irrespective of the AI 
outlook. SPX median earnings for 26Q3 was ~12%, which is in line with the 10-year 
average EPS growth of 12.6%, per FactSet. Further, Mislav tells us that median profit 
margins made fresh ATHs. 
•         We do think a bounce is coming but we are not yet convinced that any bounce 
can turn into a sustainable rally given headwinds from AI / Tech, uncertainty over the 
nascent hiking cycles, and the subsequent impact on the earnings outlook for FY27 and 
FY28. 
•         As for what would make us flip back to being bullish? We do not have a high 
conviction answer for that, but it seems that (i) positioning reset / reversion would be the 
start; but, we likely also need to (ii) see support for the US economic growth story proxied 
by Cyclicals outperforming Defensives or a selloff in our Recession / Stagflation baskets. 
Plus, (iii) a reduction in vol. It may be as simple as (iv) investors / market needs to travel 
through time to digest the news and going through another earnings season, which kicks 
off in mid-Jan. A positive tail-risk would be (v) if the Fed pauses in December to go in Jan 
/ Mar.  
•         MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 23) – We think a market-neutral approach is prudent. 
Within Tech, Mag7 names and Security Software appear to be the safest areas though 
there is now squeeze risk for Memory / Semis and would suggest using options for those

temr we think +SPW vs. -SPX makes sense or +SPW vs. -NDX for those looking for more 
gearing. Regionally, UK / EU are likely to outperform especially if we see the Dollar see 
profit-taking. From a factor perspective, Value will continue to outperform both Beta and 
Momentum.  

•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 16) –  The Anti-AI theme / ex-Tech rotation has 
been dominant in 26H2, even more so over the last month as Energy gains have flipped, 
the recession narrative has emerged, and investors have positioned more defensively. 
We think moving back toward Tech makes sense and like the cautious approach detailed 
in the previous Monetization Menu with primary exposure to Mag7 and then using options 
to  play a rebound in the AI themes. It seems most likely that the market will be patient 
with seeing how the catalysts play out rather than getting ahead of those events. Look for 
earnings, capital markets, and the JPM APAC Tech Conf to be a positive catalysts. In 
terms of ex-Tech plays, we prefer Cyclicals over Defensives as the growth narrative 
reboots and markets digest an incrementally more hawkish Fed. Recall, SPX earnings 
are more tied to nominal GDP growth than to real GDP growth meaning increases in 
inflation are not a bad thing for earnings and that profit margins near record levels 
suggest that pass-through is not a problem in the near-term. We continue to like Europe 
for some Cyclical / Value exposure given relative positioning and valuation, though 
another surge in the USD may temper tactical outperformance.
