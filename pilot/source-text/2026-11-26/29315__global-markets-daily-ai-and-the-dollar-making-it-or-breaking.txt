The risk is that as the market cycle matures, the conditions under which AI selloffs drive 
Dollar weakness become less likely; instead of a rotation within a still-supported broader 
risk backdrop, AI drawdowns may be increasingly associated with a bigger aggregate 
shock and higher equity volatility as was the case last week. We find that the share of 
days when the S&P 500 rises amid an AI-selloff has declined, while the share when the 
S&P 500 also falls has increased (Exhibit 5). This is consistent with our cross-asset 
strategists’ finding that broader cross-asset sentiment has become increasingly sensitive 
to AI (Exhibit 6). 
Policymakers have also increasingly focused on this risk. In the FOMC minutes’ discussion 
of financial stability, almost all participants commented on risks associated with 
disappointment around AI investment. AI was also the primary concern among survey 
respondents in the Financial Stability Report. All else equal, greater spillovers from AI 
weakness into broader risk sentiment increase the likelihood that the Dollar’s safe-haven 
characteristics dominate on AI selloff days. 
 
Higher equity volatility reinforces this dynamic. We find that as equity volatility rises, the 
Dollar becomes less sensitive to relative equity returns (Exhibit 7). This helps to explain 
why the Dollar’s performance has appeared less dependent on relative equity 
performance recently (Exhibit 8). Together, these developments suggest that the 
near-term Dollar-negative impulse from relative US equity underperformance is less 
likely as safe-haven dynamics dominate on the day.

That said, we still think a more negative view on AI would be Dollar-negative over the

AI price pressures would erode the support they provided to the Dollar for much of 
2026. Using the Dollar’s sensitivity to both long-end and front-end yields, we estimate 
AI’s contribution to DXY appreciation so far this year. Our rates strategists recently noted 
that both the increase in short-term rates this year, in part driven by AI, and AI-driven 
upward revisions to US growth expectations have put upward pressure on long-end 
yields. Assuming AI accounts for the majority of the growth-expectations impulse and 
roughly half of the policy-rate impulse, we estimate that AI has contributed roughly 
one-third of cumulative DXY appreciation through October of this year (Exhibit 9). This 
illustrates the meaningful contribution to Dollar support that could unwind should these 
AI impulses see a reversal.
