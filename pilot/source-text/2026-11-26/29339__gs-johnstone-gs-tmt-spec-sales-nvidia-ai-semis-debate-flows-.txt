GS TMT SPEC SALES – Tuesday 24th November 
Jensen is back—but better sentiment is not the same as a clearing event for semis and investors
yesterday had more questions than anything else. We saw covering in semis. . His return removes the
most acute recent investor leadership concern, restores continuity and should help confidence in Nvidia’s
ability to coordinate and support the broader AI ecosystem (CRWV/NBIS+20% led semis yesterday). That is
clearly positive for NVDA and the supply chain. For investors the Nvidia leadership uncertainty had
compounded the pressures already driving semiconductor multiples lower; reversing it does not resolve all of
investors’ concerns.  
The scale of the moves in semis names making it harder to build conviction. SMH is up 15%, yet that only
takes it back to where it was on Thursday. Investors are struggling to distinguish a durable turn from a sharp
reversal after Friday’s extreme sell-off, and sentiment remains mixed. With positioning so bearish, the
removal of the leadership overhang can fuel short covering and a painful move higher for underweight
investors—but extreme volatility is adding to the uncertainty, not resolving it.
The fundamental questions remain. 1) Jensen’s return may ease some of the additional financing
anxiety created by his departure (positive neoclouds), but it does not by itself solve the sector’s cost-of-
capital problem. 2) Datacentre restrictions fears post the Dem sweep. 3) China driven regulatory
concerns across the rest of semis/memory stocks esp. post the new BIS regulation 4) Concerns about
oversupply and inference efficiency gains reducing the compute needed for a given workload. 5) Provide
investors evidence that AI revenue can grow enough to justify the scale of capex. 
I would separate the tactical bounce from a fundamental recovery. Near term, Jensen’s return is a
credible relief catalyst after Friday’s sell-off. For a sustained re-rating, the focus shifts back to rates, credit
and financing conditions, the ability to deploy datacentre capacity, and Q4 results—particularly evidence of
revenue growth at the foundation-model companies. The remaining investigation (California AG Bonta said
the move changes nothing for his probe which Bloomberg reported Friday covers potential criminal
violations), and regulatory questions at Nvidia (the impact of the BIS was introduced on 19th Nov post
Jensen and caused much debate. Plus the commerce dept added Megaspeed to the entity list yesterday
afternoon).   
 See Pete Callahan take on investor feedback: admittedly more questions than strong opinions out there
today (.. most people in the “idk” camp?..) : BULLS think we should keep rallying on a “nobody left to sell”
positioning tailwind and the argument that this was mostly a “sentiment” bear market in AI/Semis, not a
fundamental one … vs … BEARS think we still have long-term questions (China, shell/permitting, ROIs,
Token trajectory, etc), and, in turn, it will be hard to rally much above pre-Election levels (let alone, getting
back to the 200-dma)
The historical comparison offers some encouragement, but not an all-clear. In the eight instances
when the Nasdaq closed up 7% or more in a single day over the past 20 years, subsequent returns were

choppy in the short term but generally stronger over the following month. The table below summarises those
outcomes. With only eight observations, this is context rather than a reliable signal that the current correction
is over.

RESULTS
• HPE: beat and raise, driven by stronger Cloud & AI margins and improving networking demand and
supply. F4Q benefited from scale, pricing and cost discipline, with storage revenue up 34% YoY and record AI
systems orders of $4.5bn, supported by AMD Helios momentum. Enterprise and sovereign customers represent
57% of cumulative AI orders. Networking orders grew more than 50%, while higher supply commitments should
help convert backlog. FY27 guidance rises to 27–35% revenue growth (from 13–17%), 27–35% EPS growth
(from 16–20%) and at least $5.5bn FCF (from $5.0bn). Cloud & AI revenue growth is now expected at 30–40%
(from 14–18%), with a 14% margin (from 13%); Networking growth and margin guidance are unchanged.
Management expects commodity-driven server pricing benefits to moderate, with more growth coming from units.
On their call: “after nearly 2 decades of workloads migrating to the public cloud, AI has created new economic and
operational reasons to expand on-premises and private cloud infrastructure, driving significant changes in the
server and storage infrastructure.” See GIR takev
 

TECH
• INTU was down 13% on 11/20 post 1QFY27 results, with a small (1.1%) revenue beat, and 2Q/FY27 guided
3%/1% lower. The stock reaction likely reflects weaker Credit Karma trends, with a 1.6% miss; Intuit now expects
3.0-7.0% growth vs. 10.5-12.6% prior. We continue to see risk to Intuit's 3-year and medium-term target revenue
CAGRs, given headwinds in TurboTax, CK, and potentially in QuickBooks as well. Given how early we are in AI
competition and the risk of unevenness when executing through changes to the business model, we prefer to be
conservative with our expectations for FY28, and maintain a Sell rating. See GIR take
 
• CrowdStrike announced its plans to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction, valuing Zscaler at
~$43.9bn. The primary playbook for scaled platforms in security over the last several years has been tuck in
acquisitions as a vector for outsourcing R&D: companies have acquired best-in-class private next-gen assets that
have already proven product-market-fit, and then have scaled these technologies into their installed base (e.g. see
CrowdStrike-Fiddler details here and our Revisiting Moats III on cyber moats here). CrowdStrike's comments
today, along with Palo Alto's at the time of the CyberArk deal, are illustrative of an increased importance of: a)
scale, given the need to execute strategic agreements with the C-suite and invest in R&D and go to market; b)
combining network, identity and endpoint telemetry holistically to get closer to the Holy Grail of Security: more
perfect defender visibility that ultimately inverts the likelihood of attacks in favor of the defenders. See GIR takev
 
• Amazon closes in on $7.5bn deal to buy AI optimization start-up Decart. The deal covers the Decart Optimization
Stack, software that helps AI models run faster and more cheaply across different chips. FT

TELCOS 
• Satellites: Something to watch - proposed US rules create a potential risk for SES and Eutelsat (it is
probably quite hard to kick SES out of govt functions and cable head ends but lets see and sees like a
negotiating tactic). The FCC wants to apply the same restrictions to foreign satellite operators in the US that their
home countries impose on American providers. As FCC Chairman Brendan Carr explained on X, those could
include extra fees, approval delays, operating limits or spectrum restrictions. Satellite systems licensed in WTO
member countries have long benefited from a presumption in favour of US market access under the WTO Basic
Telecommunications Agreement. The proposal could mean new conditions or delays for foreign operators,
which account for roughly one-quarter of satellite systems approved to serve the US, with European and Canadian
firms among those exposed. It could also strengthen Starlink’s position abroad by pressuring foreign governments
to ease restrictions. SES and Eutelsat have separately opposed FCC efforts to relax US satellite power limits.

 MACRO AND TRADING 
• US TRADING: US equities saw greens shoots across the board today following reports of Jensen's reinstatement
as president and CEO, effective immediately. Our desk saw an early chase with the name up > 30% and signs of
macro hedges being taken off. Our floor was a 5 on a 1-10 scale in terms of overall activity levels. HFs were much
busier out of the gates managing short books (demand in NVDA, select names in tech, and macro expressions)
relative to LOs who were quiet. Desk flows not as busy as one would expect with NDX constituent volumes up
~60%. After two weeks of relentless supply in NVDA (primarily LO), supply abatement felt like a big component of
move magnitude today.   
   
 Overall volumes EXPLOSIVE with ETFs accounting for ~37% of tape is the highest reading since April (vs 5yr avg
of 25%). For every $4 of ETF volume today, ~$1 is attributable to levered ETFs which is a record share in our
dataset. Reminder per our PB data: US ETF short flows increased by another +8.5% on Friday and were up +19%
last week (Mon-Fri) – the 3rd largest weekly increase in the past decade. Liquidity still anemic. S&P 500 top of
book (how much notional you can trade at price shown on the screens) hovered around ~$2mm...this is essentially
an intraday ytd low...for context 5 yr avg is $11mm, exacerbating price action.   
DERIVS: Vols were already under heavy pressure pre-open, with the crush accelerating following the NVDA
headlines. While desk flows were two-way, activity skewed toward vol buying, particularly in spread format. That
positioning makes sense to us, especially on the topside, where a steep vol hurdle makes a "spot up, vol up"
dynamic difficult to envision until the market reclaims the 200dma. Given the scar tissue from the past two weeks,
clients were largely reluctant to chase the spot rally. However, if the squeeze continues, we expect forced re-
risking as the street remains broadly offsides. Heading into a shortened, liquidity-impaired week, the distribution of
outcomes remains unusually wide, leaving year-end SPY call spreads as our preferred expression for upside
delta. The straddle for the rest of the week went out at 2.15%-- TY Shayna Peart
