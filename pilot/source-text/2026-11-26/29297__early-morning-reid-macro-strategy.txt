Yesterday, after heated debate, rows, tantrums and tiaras, all Deutsche Bank's economists and strategists came 
together and published our latest World Outlook for 2027. It's called “Great Expectations, but Risky Business”. Given the 
current market swings, it feels like an appropriate title. It runs through all our economic and market forecasts for the year 
ahead. Among others, our equity strategists see the S&P 500 reaching 9,000 by end-2027 on extremely supportive 
earnings, whilst oil should provide some baseline relief as Brent falls towards $70/bbl in H2. Otherwise, our rates 
strategists are broadly neutral duration, with the 10yr Treasury yield at 5.05% at end-2027, whilst our FX strategists 
see EUR/USD falling to 1.08 in H1, before recovering to 1.15 by end-2027. Meanwhile, we see the global economic 
resilience continuing, with global growth at 3.5% in 2027, and 3.4% in 2028. See the full report here. 
Imagine a scenario where you're perceived to be so important to a company that your stock increases nearly a trillion 
dollars on a day that you're reinstated as CEO. That sums up the last 24 hours. Indeed, as we published the World 
Outlook our equity strategist had an S&P 500 target +25% above current levels. A few hours later and nearly a fifth of 
that has been achieved after markets staged an astonishing recovery, with the S&P 500 (+4.73%) surging on the news 
that Jensen Huang had been reinstated as the CEO of Nvidia. In fact, it was the S&P’s best daily performance since 
April 2025, back when the 90-day tariff postponement after Liberation Day led to a huge unwind of the selloff. And in 
many respects, it felt like a similar day, with the most AI-exposed assets rapidly paring back last week’s selloff. For 
instance, Nvidia (+31.48%) saw its biggest share price jump since 2003, adding more than a trillion dollars of market 
cap at the peak of the day and $952bn by the close, which is more than double the largest ever previous daily 
market cap gain ($450bn for Microsoft in July 2026). The Philly semiconductor index (+14.03%) had its best day since 
the 90-day tariff extension. Moreover, other bullish catalysts boosted sentiment, with the November flash PMIs showing 
continued resilience across the global economy, whilst optimism on the oil supply outlook pushed Brent crude oil (-
3.46%) to its lowest close since August, at $89.74/bbl. So it was a remarkable shift from where we ended last week, 
with a huge turnaround in market sentiment. 
Asia tech is certainly catching up with the turnaround this morning with Taiwan’s TAIEX (+9.02%) surging, driven by 
sharp gains in heavyweight semiconductor stocks, although the index still remains just under 20% below its recent 
highs. The KOSPI (+7.27%) is also posting strong gains, while the Nikkei (+4.03%) is advancing after returning from a 
market holiday and the S&P/ASX 200 (+1.82%) is also firmly higher. Chinese equities are lagging the broader regional 

rally, with the CSI 300 (+0.35%) and Shanghai Composite (+0.34%) posting only modest gains, while the Hang Seng (-
0.40%) is lower. S&P 500 (+0.29%) and Nasdaq 100 futures (+0.31%) are a lot calmer but are still edging higher.  
Looking in more detail now at the Bobby Ewing style comeback of the decade. News of Jensen Huang’s reinstatement 
came out shortly before the US open. The statement from the board said that they had "received input from senior 
levels of the United States government”, and a post from President Trump said that “The United States Government 
has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security.”  
Up until then, US equity futures were already pointing to a clear bounceback from last week, with the S&P 500 on course 
to end a run of 7 consecutive declines. However, the recovery was then turbocharged by those Nvidia headlines, 
with an immediate jump in S&P 500 futures. Before the news of Huang’s return, S&P 500 futures were “only” up 
+2.16%, but the actual index ultimately ended the session +4.73% higher, with even bigger gains across the AI 
space. Indeed, it means the S&P 500 is now back within 8% of its record high, a big contrast to last Friday when it 
briefly entered technical correction territory (more than 10% below its peak). 
Whilst the Nvidia news caused the biggest moves yesterday, it’s worth remembering the note we highlighted yesterday 
from our US equity strategists (link here). They pointed out that last week’s selloff had already left equity 
positioning at a very low point, close to the bottom of its typical band (1 standard deviation either side). Then on top 
of the technical factors, the latest flash PMIs yesterday offered fresh reassurance that the global economy was still 
holding up. So unlike a lot of previous risk-off moves, there certainly hadn’t been a meaningful deterioration in the data, 
with Germany even seeing a notable acceleration. For instance, the German composite PMI was up to 55.0 (vs. 53.2 
expected), the highest since 2022, whilst the Euro Area composite PMI remained broadly steady at 51.7 (vs. 52.0 
expected). And over in the US, the composite PMI was still at 54.6 (vs. 55.6 expected), so clearly in expansionary territory 
still even if a little below expectations. To be honest, the only obvious point of weakness was France, where the 
composite PMI fell to a 6-month low of 46.2 (vs. 47.9 expected). 
That rebound for US equities cascaded across global markets, with huge moves across sectors and regions. That said, 
the equity move was pretty concentrated, and even though the S&P 500’s rise was the biggest by far of 2026 (no other 
day has been above 3%), the number of companies rising yesterday (419) wasn’t the highest for the year, as we hit 
423 a couple of weeks ago. So this was very much driven by the chip stocks. Indeed in Europe, the rally was a lot more 
moderate, with the STOXX 600 (+1.81%) and the DAX (+2.15%) seeing much smaller moves relative to the US. 
With fears of an AI slowdown receding, it also led markets to price back in a more hawkish path for the Federal Reserve 
over the months ahead. So for the next meeting in December, markets are once again fully pricing in a 25bp hike, 
with 26.1bps priced in for that decision (+3.1bps on the day). Then looking further out, 70bps of hikes are now priced 
in by the September 2027 meeting, up +6.6bps relative to Friday. Those moves also came as NY Fed President 
Williams said the Fed “must take actions over time” to bring inflation back to the 2% goal and that it has “work to 
do to get that done”. All that led to a decent flattening in the US Treasury curve, with the 2yr yield (+4.6bps) rising to 
4.69%, whereas the 10yr yield was unchanged at 4.95%. 
Over in Europe, yields saw a sharper decline, with extra support from the latest decline in oil prices. In part, that followed 
comments from President Trump to Fox News over the weekend, warning about new strikes against Iran after the New 
Year. So although that left the door open to some kind of future escalation, it also pointed away from an imminent attack, 
and Brent crude (-3.46%) fell to $89.37/bbl in response. There were also signs of ongoing diplomacy, with Axios 
reporting that US Vice President Vance met the prime minister of Qatar, which has acted as a mediator, to discuss 
Iran negotiations. In turn, that offered a boost to European sovereign bonds, with yields falling on 10yr bunds (-1.6bps), 
OATs (-9.2bps) and BTPs (-6.3bps). The main exception to that came from European natural gas (+1.45%), which 
edged up after the latest weather forecasts suggested the current colder spell would persist over the next couple 
of weeks. 

Early morning data in Asia showed that Japan’s latest PMI data pointed to a mixed start to November, highlighting an 
uneven recovery across the economy. Manufacturing activity strengthened, with the au Jibun Bank flash 
Manufacturing PMI rising to 53.7 from 53.3 in October and coming in above the consensus forecast of 53.5. 
However, momentum in the services sector softened, with the Services PMI falling to 50.5 from 51.7. As a result, the 
Composite PMI eased to 51.5 from 52.1.  
Looking at the day ahead now, and US data releases include the Conference Board’s consumer confidence print for 
November, the FHFA house price index for September, and the Richmond Fed’s manufacturing index for November. 
Meanwhile in Germany, there’s the Ifo business climate indicator for November. Otherwise from central banks, we’ll 
hear from the ECB’s Kaasik, Vujcic, Kocher, Sleijpen, the BoE’s Greene, and Bank of Canada Governor Macklem.
