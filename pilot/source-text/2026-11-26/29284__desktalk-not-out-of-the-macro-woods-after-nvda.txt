DeskTalk - Not out of the macro woods after NVDA
By Rui Ding
The "Trump put" was well and truly triggered yesterday, giving a much-needed boost to the AI trade. And yet, risk appetite is
already drying up with some markets completely reversing yesterday's move. Recent volatility and price action was never
about just one (very large) company's leadership - FOMC, dollar strength, AI concerns remain. 
FOMC pricing has come down on the recent equity correction, but OIS markets are back to pricing over 25bp for Dec. With
inflation staying elevated and a more hawkish reaction function on display, US front end sees limited relief. Williams confirmed
last night that he would be open to back to back rate hikes "if that was what was needed." Duration in the intermediate part of
the curve has been well bid, our sales desk says. Core PCE on Wed is the main event for a holiday shortened week (Citi
forecasts 0.27%MoM), more event risk in payrolls next Fri. 
USD strength is largely intact, and importantly this has been the case even when US equities were correcting lower. EURUSD
saw "relentless selling" yesterday, our trader says, confirming it was a flow driven move. USDJPY rally saw the usual Tokyo
supply overnight but 160 remains the line in the sand for now - our options desk prefers crossJPY for any risk expression
rather than USDJPY. USD strength has resumed against EM FX, with ZAR and MXN already shedding yesterday's gains.
Gold spot already struggling to sustain gains. 
AI concerns remain, as evidenced by copper prices retracing yesterday's move. While a comeback for NVDA's CEO may put
a bottom in the recent correction, the fact of the matter is that the new Congress come January is not going to be as data
center friendly. The "sell the news" reaction to ANT's IPO also suggests that investors may be more cautious about lofty
valuations in the space. My colleague Varshi noted yesterday there are few obvious catalysts for a Santa rally. 
Geopolitics has fall off the radar. Immediate escalation risk seems capped - Trump recently indicated that Iran strikes are
more likely in the new year, and indirect negotiations are ongoing. Brent crude prices have fallen below $90 but a negotiated
settlement may prove elusive. Don't forget the historical trilateral meeting last week.
