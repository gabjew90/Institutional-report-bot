IDEAS & INSIGHTS – IN BRIEF 
-       OIL OUTLOOK: The lessons of 2022 and 2026 show that geopolitical shocks 
reroute oil rather than remove it from the market. Our outlook assumes both conflicts 
continue, yet oil keeps moving and the global market remains in surplus. Oil 
consumption could theoretically recover by 4.5 mbd in 2027, but meeting that 
demand would require global refinery runs to increase by 4.1 mbd relative to 2026, 
against an expected gain of just 3.3 mbd. Refining constraints will keep product 
cracks and prices elevated, limiting demand growth to 3.5 mbd, just 0.6 mbd above 
pre-war levels. We project global oil surpluses of 1.6 mbd in 2027 and 1.4 mbd in 
2028, even after accounting for substantial inventory accumulation in China and 
OECD countries. Persistent surpluses point to a Brent in the high $70s in the first half 
of 2027, falling to the high $60s in the second half of the year and mid-$60s in 2028. 
-       GLOBAL FINANCIALS: We hosted our third Financials conference last week, with 
a total of 73 Corporates, hosting >500 investor meetings and >2,000 meeting 
touchpoints. Across banks, insurers and diversified financials, the tone was more 
measured than last year. Three common themes stood out: 1) pressure on pricing 

and margins is building; 2) clear structural tailwind from demographics and 
pension reform; 3) management teams across the sector stressed what they can 
control: structural cost-base improvements, including through AI, and disciplined 
capital deployment, with little appetite for large-scale M&A. Cyber risk was a 
thematic continuously discussed. European Banks were noticeably less positive 
compared to last year despite the favourable economic environment of rising rates 
with limited asset quality concerns. 
-       TAKEAWAYS FROM CENTRE FOR EUROPEAN REFORM 2026 CONFERENCE: The 
backdrop for the EU, which has traditionally anchored its economic policy in global 
rules and collaboration, was stark. Conference discussions portrayed the EU as 
being on the wrong side of three defining forces this year: on the receiving end of the 
China supply shock 2.0, on the sidelines of the AI boom, and particularly exposed to 
rising global bond yields. Key takeaways: Europe needs to establish a united 
European front on China trade; Currency adjustment can help rebalance trade but 
cannot alone address the challenges posed by China overcapacity; Fiscal deficits are 
structural and raise funding costs, while France’s fiscal position and greater hedge 
fund ownership of sovereign debt are sources of vulnerability; Central banks cannot 
offset energy supply shocks through tighter monetary policy alone; Europe’s AI edge 
will lie in applications which do not all require frontier models.

markets closed for a holiday; UN Security Council holds a debate on emerging 
technologies and international security

Report: Weekly Wrap | Extreme HF selling...watching for broader capitulation 

  
Summary: Abandon all hope, ye who trade tech (and risk assets more 
generally)…that would seem to be the mood after another bruising week for 
the markets and HF performance.  In fact, based on today’s index returns, we’d 
estimate HFs were down 1.5% today and could be down >3% over the past 5d, 
which would be the worst since Apr 7, 2025.  The silver lining is that, unlike what we 
were seeing in Oct and early Nov, the losses continue to be driven by beta rather 
than alpha, which makes the past week also similar to what we saw in early Apr 
2025.   
  
The clear answer to last week’s question of whether the Semis sell-off is over is “no.”  
Increasingly, the 2018 analog appears to be in focus given the 4Q selling and 
reversal in performance to basically flat YTD (we did see S&P briefly bounce in 
late Nov ’18 before ultimately selling off more in Dec).  More generally, we continue 
to see HFs reduce net risk at a magnitude that has historically been associated with 
a bounce, but we’re still waiting to see whether there’s a broader capitulation.  
Perhaps that happened today and we’ll keep an eye on how positioning continues to 
evolve from here. 
  
Key Points: 
  
1. HF performance was hit again, while gross flows continued to recover. 
Based on today’s index performance, HFs are estimated to have fallen 
another 1.5%, putting MTD returns at -4%. 5d and 63d returns are now 
below -3z, though the negative overall returns and more neutral HF alpha 
(partly due to prior de-risking by HFs) makes this quite similar to Apr ’25 
and early Aug ’24. Gross flows have rebounded from prior lows and are back 

in line with average US seasonality, with additions across most regions this 
past week. 
  
2. HF net selling and the decline in net leverage are approaching extreme 
levels. Global HF net selling is around -2z over 20 days and exceeds 2z in 
North America. Historically, periods of large HF net selling that coincided 
with a markets sell-off, have been followed by a rebound over the next 
month, but so far that’s not been the case.  The decline in net leverage from 
12-month highs is comparable to some of the most extreme episodes since 
2018 and 8wk change is around -2z. Net leverage is at the 26th percentile 
over the past five years for All Strategies. 
  
3. Broader capitulation is not yet confirmed by US TPM (prior to Fri at 
least). Despite low positioning, the US TPM’s 4wk change was only around 
-1z as of Thursday, above the -1.5z trigger.  The positioning level is at the 13th 
percentile since 2015, near year-to-date lows and closer to April 2025 levels, 
but still above more severe washout levels. CTA risk has been reduced but 
not extremely, while asset managers appear to be maintaining exposure in 
futures. 
  
4. Global positioning is low across regions, but the drawdown remains 
relatively shallow. Positioning has fallen below the 20th percentile across 
regions with the global level around the 10th %-tile and -1.5z (in line with lows 
post 2022). Japan continued to be sold and moved lower, while China/Hong 
Kong remains near its lows. Europe is also relatively low. 
  

5. US flows show a defensive rotation, while Semis remain under pressure. 
Semis continued to be sold, though US selling slowed to less than -1z this 
past week.  They remain the most net-sold US group over both the past 2 
months. US Semis positioning is back near its long-term average (63rd %-
tile since 2018 and 0z) and at its lowest since May 2025. This week, US net 
buying favored Utilities, Telecom, Pharma & Biotech, FBT, and Real Estate. 
positioning in Consumer Staples Distribution & Retail is at historical 
highs, and Healthcare has also benefited recently.
