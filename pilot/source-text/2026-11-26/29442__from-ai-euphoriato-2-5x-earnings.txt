Left behind by the squeeze
Yesterday's monster squeeze did little to resolve some of the deeper problems across the AI complex.
Several former market darlings are back under intense selling pressure, while valuation compression
across parts of the AI value chain has reached extraordinary levels.
But there is a big difference between selling scarce hardware and financing expensive infrastructure.
Not all falling knives are equal.

The great AI derating
Bernstein's analysis of 66 stocks across nine sectors shows that data center exposure explains roughly
45% of the variation in valuation compression.
Interestingly, memory and server OEMs have suffered some of the biggest valuation dislocations, while
networking names have held up considerably better.
The market is becoming increasingly selective about which AI exposure it wants to own.
Bernstein
SNDK – priced for disaster
SNDK was trading at just 2.5x forward earnings as of Friday's close, compared with its three-year
average of 10.4x.
The 76% multiple compression is almost twice what Bernstein's data center exposure model would
imply, although AI-driven pricing potentially makes the company's true exposure considerably higher.
The market clearly doesn't believe these earnings are sustainable.
Technically, SNDK remains deeply oversold, but as we often point out, oversold can stay oversold for
much longer than most think possible. The stock has been oversold since October and has more than
halved since then. SNDK is trading well below its 200-day moving average, with the 50-day about to
cross below it.
Things look so bad that you almost want to start exploring the contrarian trade.

The memory market's blind spot
The market is increasingly worried about additional NAND capacity hitting in 2028–29. However,
SNDK's new customer agreements cover roughly 50% of FY27 bit volumes and two-thirds of FY28
volumes, incorporating volume commitments, pricing mechanisms and financial protections.
Meanwhile, Bernstein estimates that if high-bandwidth flash (HBF) eventually accounts for just 5% of
NAND bit demand, the industry could require roughly 28% more wafers.
Perhaps the market is too focused on future supply and not enough on the changing nature of
demand.
Storage over memory?
STX and WDC were trading at 9.2x and 8.3x forward earnings, respectively, as of Friday's close.
Bernstein argues that the HDD pricing upcycle is still in its early stages, supported by constrained
capacity, disciplined supply additions and growing enterprise AI demand. The more consolidated
supplier base also makes HDD historically less cyclical than memory.
Bernstein prefers STX given its technological lead in HAMR.

Cheap for a reason?
Despite the massive valuation reset, CRWV still screens as under-punished in Bernstein's analysis.
The problem is not necessarily AI demand, but the ability to turn contracted demand into profitable
operating capacity after depreciation and financing costs. There is a big difference between having an
order book and having the capital to deliver it.

No relief for Nebius
Yesterday's monster squeeze appears to have done little to change the bigger picture in NBIS. The
stock is back under intense selling pressure today. The inability to sustain a bounce despite such a
powerful reversal across the broader AI complex is a worrying sign.
The $80 area is huge support, although there is a massive unfilled gap below it.

Broadcom over Nvidia?
AVGO has become Bernstein's top US semiconductor pick, trading at roughly 15x and 9x estimated
2027 and 2028 earnings, respectively.
The combination of a resilient infrastructure software business and AI exposure concentrated among
some of the best-capitalized customers in the industry makes AVGO a somewhat different AI bet. Not
all AI customers carry the same financing risk.
The stock is trading right back at major support, coinciding with the longer-term trendline. Also worth
noting is that the stock hasn't traded this far below its 200-day moving average in a long time.

When the bear thesis turns bullish
One of the big bear arguments for AI infrastructure is the growing shortage of power and land.
Ironically, those very constraints could benefit CIEN. Building smaller, geographically dispersed data
centers increases the need to connect them through scale-across networking.
Bernstein highlights CIEN as particularly exposed to this opportunity. The very problem driving the AI
infrastructure sell-off could become a growth driver for parts of the networking complex.
