S&P 500 Futures: Into and (Temporarily?) Out of the Correction
Robert Quinn  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
Tue 24 Nov 2026, 7:32am ET
Commitment of Traders for November 10th - 17th surprisingly displayed continued Non-Dealer net
buying in S&P 500 futures. Non-Dealers bought $19.1bn, marking the 6th straight week of net demand for
a cumulative $71.3bn since October 6th. New longs ($24.6bn) overpowered shorts ($5.5bn). Recall S&P
lost 2.4% in the observation window.
That said, categorical changes were less constructive. First, Hedge Fund dominated. Hedge Fund
purchased $25.5bn, 71% of which were gross long additions. Some of this conceivably represented offsets
to single stock shorts and/or put positions. Consider, S&P 3 month implied volatility and normalized 25
delta put-call skew both richened.
Secondly, Asset Manager shorts surged, demonstrating apparent sensitivity to the AI and growth
slowdown. Asset Manager short selling ($9.4bn) was the largest since July of 2025. Given coincident falls
in AI Semiconductors (4.8%) and High Beta Cyclicals (5.2%), short-term correlations between Asset
Manager shorts and these barometers became more negative.
With markets further deteriorating into the November 20th close, S&P short selling broadened. From
November 17th - 20th, S&P plunged 5.9% and funding cheapened. S&P E-Mini futures aggregate open
interest jumped $36.6bn. Most of the OI gain occurred on November 20th: $26.6bn, enough for the
biggest 1 day change in 2026 excluding roll periods.
Interestingly, the price rebound on November 23rd, sparked by Jensen Huang's reinstatement, resulted in a
modest OI retracement ($7.9bn). Thus either significant long buying ensued or some bears held.
