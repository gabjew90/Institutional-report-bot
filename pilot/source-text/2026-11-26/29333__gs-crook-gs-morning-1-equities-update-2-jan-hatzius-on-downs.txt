Key excerpt from Jan Hatzius on the downside risks to our base case of steady global growth in 2027…
First, a renewed escalation in Iran, which would hit real income. The positive wealth effect in 2026 has
supported spending at the higher end of the income spectrum, partially offsetting the impact on
consumption from higher energy prices. If markets were to remain weaker in 2027, alongside a significant
energy shock, that could have a bigger impact on spending.

Second, bond market turbulence, especially in the Euro Area and in France and in the run up to the
presidential election. France runs a very large budget deficit (~ 5.5% of GDP), quite far away from where
they need to be for a sustainable budget situation.
Third, a sharper slowdown in AI investment than is currently expected (either because of pushback
against data centres, indigestion in fixed income markets, memory shortages, or saturation), or a larger-
than-expected impact on the labour market (a downside risk, at least from a monetary policy perspective)

Sun Lu (Asia Macro Strats): Recovery in AI sentiment, month-end importer hedging and potential RBI
smoothing can provide a good entry point to put leveraged USDINR carry trade, as LO’s interest to
diversify away from AI and cover India UW is likely to continue. If AI sentiment continue to stabilize, we
think there remains room for rates to bear-flatten (refocus on high inflation and Fed hawkishness), and
have some spillover to Asia AI-linked low-yielder rates market (KRW, SGD, MYR, TWD).
Rishi Radia (Asia Macro Strats) on INR Equity Inflows: Equity inflows into India have reached nearly
US$12bn in November, and GIR estimates long-only funds remained ~175bp underweight India as of
end-September - a move back to neutral would imply roughly US$24bn of inflows, with broader flow
potential likely larger. India has also been the only regional market to see both FX and equity gains since
the start of November. The flow math is supportive for INR unless the RBI absorbs the inflows: India runs
a ~US$30bn monthly trade deficit, partly offset by a US$17bn services surplus, ~US$7bn
primary/secondary income and ~US$2bn/month FDI, leaving the underlying BOP at ~-US$4bn/month
before portfolio flows. The RBI allowing oil companies to source USD directly from the central bank could
remove ~US$10bn/month of USD demand, and importer hedging ratios have fallen since the 20% FERR
in early October. We expect RBI dollar buying to become more meaningful around 92 once the ~US$13bn
NDF short has been substantially covered. Full note here.

4) COMMODITIES RESEARCH (DART) – Europe Winter Tracker: A Cold-Driven Rally – LINK
Colder European Weather Forecasts Drive TTF to 90 EUR: Exceptionally colder-than-average two-
week temperature forecasts for NW Europe drove TTF to 90 EUR/MWh, up 6% WoW. TTF outperformed

oil prices, which moved lower yesterday, consistent with headlines of a potentially delayed timeline for
geopolitical escalation in the Middle East. To be sure, even near-term weather forecasts can be very
volatile, and not all weather models point to this level of cold. However, if this potential cold shock were to
realize without any offsets, we estimate NW Europe would end winter with gas inventories only 14% full
(well below our 20% base case), the lowest since the 2018 record-low 9% fill.
There’s Further Upside Risk to TTF if the Cold Holds: The colder-than-average weather in NW Europe
realized thus far this month and forecast for the next two weeks is nearly 4 standard deviations colder
than average. It is cold enough that, even if the remainder of winter realized in line with 10-year average
temperatures, winter as a whole (Nov 1st to March 31st) would average near 1 standard-deviation colder
than average. In such a scenario, and assuming ME LNG supply continues to rise through winter, we
estimate TTF would likely have to rally to 106 EUR/MWh to attract enough incremental LNG supply (via
discouraging LNG demand outside of Europe) to help manage its end-winter gas inventories back to more
comfortable levels near 20% full, well above our 65 EUR/MWh 1Q27 TTF base case under average
winter weather.
ME LNG Supply Risk Not Over: While Persian Gulf LNG exports have continued to average above our
base case, the sequential decline in export flows from over 60% of pre-war levels in early Nov to 25%
over the past week suggest Mideast LNG supply outlook remains uncertain, posing further upside risk to
TTF this winter.
