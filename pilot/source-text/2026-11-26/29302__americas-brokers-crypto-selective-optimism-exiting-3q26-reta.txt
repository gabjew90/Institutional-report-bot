Return to positive retail trading growth, with potential upside from structural growth and ECM activity: Our 
1.
industry data suggest an inflection in retail trading in 4QTD after muted 3Q26 volumes, leaving us cautiously optimistic 
around further growth from here, with potential upside to activity from recent large-cap IPOs. Our proxy for retail 
industry equities trading volumes rose 4% sequentially in October, and another 14% MoM MTD in November, the first 
positive MoM growth since June (Exhibit 2). Despite the recent improvement, there remains potential room for further 
volume growth, given: 1) favorable seasonality, as retail trading volumes has historically improved through the end of the 
year, although 4QTD volumes remain lower YoY and have underperformed historical seasonality (Exhibit 3, Exhibit 4, 
Exhibit 5, Exhibit 4); 2) continued margin balance and account growth, both of which have historically supported further 
volume growth (Exhibit 6, Exhibit 7, Exhibit 8, Exhibit 9); and 3) upside optionality from a reacceleration in large-cap IPO 
activity, with November US IPO volumes 540% higher YoY MTD, which have historically driven retail trading volumes, as 
well as underlying account and asset growth (Exhibit 10, Exhibit 11, Exhibit 12, Exhibit 13). In addition, several retail 
brokers offer customers access to IPO allocations, providing incremental monetization through underwriting/selling fees 
or spreads on allocated shares (Exhibit 14). 
Crypto volumes remain modestly higher QoQ, although falling vs. September, and with a potentially negative 
2.
inflection ahead, given falling crypto market cap: 4Q26 TD crypto volumes remain stronger on a QoQ basis, but have 
fallen MoM in October and remain flat in November MTD, and crypto market cap falling to the lowest level since 2024 
could presage further volume/stablecoin market cap declines. Crypto trading activity has risen 7% QoQ in 4Q26TD, after 
a meaningful acceleration in crypto volumes toward the end of 3Q26, driven by several positive regulatory developments 
(see our report here). However, there has been a meaningful decline from the September peak, as volumes fell 13% MoM 
in October and are flat in November MTD. Further, total crypto market cap has declined 27% in 4Q26 TD to the lowest 
level since 2024. Given crypto market cap has historically correlated with spot trading volumes (59% R-squared - Exhibit 
20), this could presage further volume declines later in the quarter. In addition, stablecoin market cap has remained 
muted, now down -6% YoY (Exhibit 25). While stablecoin adoption continues to diversify beyond crypto trading into 
emerging use cases (e.g., payments and agentic commerce), stablecoin market cap has remained correlated with the 
broader crypto market (50% R-squared). As such, continued pressure on crypto market cap could weigh further on 
stablecoin market cap as well. Given the uncertain backdrop for spot crypto trading and stablecoin growth, we modestly 
reduce 2026E/27E/28E crypto trading by 1%/2%/2%, and stablecoin market cap estimates by 1%/8%/9%. Following 
several false starts, both crypto trading volumes and market caps have now remained near trough levels for 13 months 
(above a median of 4 month over previous cycles), and remain 52%/65% below their 4Q25 peaks (Exhibit 15, Exhibit 16, 
Exhibit 17).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted 
are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized 
ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary 
depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. 
Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based 
on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a 
stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future 
compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the 
potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of 
the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be 
discussed in research.
