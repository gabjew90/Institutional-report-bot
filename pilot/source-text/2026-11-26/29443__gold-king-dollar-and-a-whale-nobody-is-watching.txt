From euphoria to apathy
Gold is trading unusually far below its 200-day moving average, speculative positioning has been
cleaned up, and CTAs are running sizeable shorts.
Meanwhile, the dollar is looking increasingly stretched, systematic flows could turn supportive, and a
relatively new player is emerging on the demand side of the gold equation.
Could gold be setting up for a bounce sooner rather than later?
Rare in gold
Gold rarely trades this far below its 200-day moving average.

Abandon ship
Non-commercials have aggressively reduced their net long exposure to gold over the past two months.
Recall that everybody and their mother was extremely bullish on gold during the late-August squeeze.
We haven't reached the opposite extreme yet, but the apathy surrounding gold is getting rather loud.

The new gold whale
According to SocGen, Tether has been the world's fifth-largest net buyer of gold since 2023,
accumulating 87.5 tonnes through June 2026. Only three central banks and one ETF accumulated
more over the period.
Since early 2023, Tether's gold holdings have grown by 31% annually, compared with 13.6% for gold
ETFs and just 1.8% for central banks. There is a relatively new and rapidly growing player on the
demand side of the gold equation.
Soc Gen
New money, old gold
Tether held an estimated 146.5 tonnes of gold as of June, putting it alongside Libya and ahead of the
Philippines in terms of gold reserves. If classified as a gold ETF, Tether would rank as the seventh
largest globally.
Perhaps more interestingly, SocGen estimates that around 65% of inflows into Tether's gold products
could represent new money entering the gold market rather than a reallocation of existing exposure.
The gold investor base is expanding beyond traditional players.
