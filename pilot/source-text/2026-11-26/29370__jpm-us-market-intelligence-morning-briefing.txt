I don’t know what to make of today. There’s an element of “I should have seen that coming,” 
but, in reality, I shouldn’t have. None of us should have. Yet here we are. This business has 
a way of surprising and humbling you. Today was one of those days. 
o   After the P&L destruction over the past week, it’s easy to appreciate 
today’s move in isolation. But it’s worth asking whether presidential 
intervention in corporate governance is something the market wants. 
Removing the Jensen Huang uncertainty around NVDA is clearly positive. 
However, the broader uncertainty around the rules of corporate governance 
and political involvement just got A LOT worse (recall it was only ~a year ago 
where Trump called for Lip-Bu Tan’s resignation, only to renege on that a few 
weeks later). Over time, this should weigh on equity multiples, particularly 
with a Democratic Congress and the prospect of a Democratic president in 
2028. 
o   The counterfactual is that this could support multiples for companies with 
strategic importance and close government ties. The administration has made 

clear how much AI matters to it, and that’s definitely positive. and while this 
rally might have some legs given positioning, I don’t think this is getting us 
back to peak euphoria anytime soon.

2027: SELECTIVE RISK > INDISCRIMINATE RISK-ON 
• 
Quality is late innings and the broad low-quality squeeze is maturing. 
• 
What comes next is bifurcation within low quality: own leveraged 
companies that can service their debt; short those dependent on lower rates 
and refinancing. 
• 
Credit is already pricing the divide. Equities are not. 
• 
TRADE IDEA: LONG Interest Coverage Pair (JPUPINTC) 
  
  
The Great Leverage Divide: Own Survivors, Short Dependents 
From Risk at Any Price to Selective Risk 
2026 rewarded chasing risk with limited regard for fundamentals, leverage, or macro 
sensitivity. We believe that regime is shifting. As short covering fades, fundamentals 
should reassert and capital should become increasingly selective. 
The next opportunity is not High Quality versus Low Quality, but within Low Quality. 
Leverage alone matters less than the ability to service it, favoring companies with 
financeable debt over those dependent on lower rates, refinancing, or external capital. 
Quality Has Had Its Move. Dispersion Is Next. 

The defensive quality rally is entering late innings, with JPM Pure Quality Factor (JPQPURE) 
already fading its recent advance. Meanwhile, short covering has fueled the broad low-
quality rebound. As the short-covering impulse fades, fundamentals should take over, 
with debt-service capacity the key differentiator. 
TRADE IDEA: LONG Interest Coverage Pair (JPUPINTC) 
 
WHY NOW? 
Macro: The Sweet Spot for Dispersion 
Restrictive rates + positive nominal growth + low recession risk create pressure without 
triggering a wholesale flight from leveraged equities. 
• 
Restrictive rates expose weaker capital structures as interest expense rises and 
maturities approach. 
• 
Positive nominal growth supports earnings and companies with manageable debt 
burdens. 
• 
Contained recession risk preserves operating leverage without prompting 
indiscriminate risk-off. 
• 
Available but selective credit favors financeable leverage over refinancing 
dependency. 
The result is selective financial pressure: stronger leveraged companies retain upside 
participation, while vulnerable borrowers face a growing drag from interest expense and 
refinancing needs. 
Positioning: The Marginal Buyer Must Change 
• 
Short covering drove the first leg: Consensus shorts rallied >1σ over the past 
month, contributing meaningfully to the low-quality rebound. 

• 
The marginal buyer must change: As forced covering fades, incremental long 
demand must sustain the next move. 
• 
Re-grossing should be more selective: If de-grossing stabilizes or reverses, 
investors should rebuild exposure with greater focus on fundamentals. 
Positioning can still support low quality, but fundamentals should increasingly determine 
where incremental capital goes. 
Cross-Asset Signal: Credit Has Moved. Equities Are Next. 
The Great Leverage Divide is already visible in credit, where US HY CCC/BB dispersion has 
widened sharply. Equity dispersion remains muted by the low-quality squeeze. 
As short covering fades, equities should follow credit in separating financeable from 
refinancing-dependent leverage. The opportunity: position before equity dispersion 
catches up. 
 
WHY INTEREST COVERAGE? 
Leverage alone does not determine whether a company’s debt burden is sustainable. What 
matters is the earnings and cash flow available to service that debt. 
The Interest Coverage Pair (JPUPINTC) isolates that distinction: 
• 
LONG High Interest Coverage (JPUXHINT): US stocks excluding Financials and 
Real Estate with above-industry-average net debt-to-equity and top-quartile interest 
coverage relative to sector peers. 
• 
SHORT Low Interest Coverage (JPUXLINT): US stocks excluding Financials and 
Real Estate with above-industry-average net debt-to-equity and bottom-quartile 
interest coverage relative to sector peers. 
• 
The pair is Sector-neutral, with a 25% sector cap, 5% single-name cap, and 
quarterly rebalancing. 

The pair targets the growing divide between companies with financeable leverage and those 
increasingly dependent on lower rates, refinancing, or external capital. 
WHAT BREAKS THE TRADE? 
The sweet spot is selective pressure, not systemic stress. 
• 
Rapid dovish pivot: Lower rates extend the rally in the most distressed and 
refinancing-dependent companies. 
• 
Renewed short squeeze: Technical flows temporarily overwhelm fundamental 
differentiation. 
• 
Deep recession: A sharp earnings contraction pressures leveraged companies 
across both legs. 
• 
Disorderly credit event: Systemic tightening triggers a wholesale flight from 
leverage. 
BOTTOM LINE 
Higher-for-longer rates + positive nominal growth + no recession = the Great 
Leverage Divide. As the low-quality squeeze fades, fundamentals should reassert and 
separate financeable leverage from rate-dependent balance sheets. 
Position now for 2027 selectivity: LONG Interest Coverage Pair (JPUPINTC). 
  
US MKT INTEL VIEW 
•         Last week, we offered our view on the Bull vs. Bear cases, thinking that the Bull Case 
was more likely; however, the Bear Case played out in remarkable fashion pushing 
sentiment toward excessive fears and markets to oversold conditions. 
o   BULL CASE – (i) NVDA Earnings; (ii) Capital Markets; (iii) AI / Tech Trade 
Returns; (iv) Macro Data.  
o   BEAR CASE –  (i) AI / Tech Take Another Leg Lower; (ii) A Decline in the 
Incremental Buyer ; (iii) Hawkish Fed Narrative; (iv) Positioning Purge. 
•         We are moving back to a Tactically Cautious view. In hindsight, it seems obvious that 
we should have been on the sidelines but we, similar to the market, had viewed NVDA and 
capital markets activity as the necessary support for Tech to form a bottom and thus whiffed 

on the tactical bullish call. Now, the uncertainty surrounding NVDA and peers and opacity 
over 2028 and beyond makes Tech longs highly uncertain in the very near-term. Given the 
aggressive nature of the selloff, a tactical bounce seems likely, but we are not yet of the 
view that a bottom forms in the very near-term given the recently introduced uncertainty in 
Tech fundamentals. 
•         Thus, the comparison to 2018 appears to be the best historical comp where the late-
year tactical bounce was faded. We do anticipate a further rotation away from Tech, trades 
which are getting increasingly crowded creating another layer of potential pain should a 
tactical Tech bounce come to fruition. If Tech continues to falter, it is unclear if the balance 
of the SPX can rally enough to boost the index materially higher. 
•         It is worth mentioning that the underlying economy remains strong, AI capex has less of 
a direct impact on the economy, and the AI risks are more tied to financing and the 
consumption wealth effect. The earnings outlook remains positive irrespective of the AI 
outlook. SPX median earnings for 26Q3 was ~12%, which is in line with the 10-year 
average EPS growth of 12.6%, per FactSet. Further, Mislav tells us that median profit 
margins made fresh ATHs. 
•         We do think a bounce is coming but we are not yet convinced that any bounce 
can turn into a sustainable rally given headwinds from AI / Tech, uncertainty over the 
nascent hiking cycles, and the subsequent impact on the earnings outlook for FY27 and 
FY28. 
•         As for what would make us flip back to being bullish? We do not have a high 
conviction answer for that, but it seems that (i) positioning reset / reversion would be the 
start; but, we likely also need to (ii) see support for the US economic growth story proxied 
by Cyclicals outperforming Defensives or a selloff in our Recession / Stagflation baskets. 
Plus, (iii) a reduction in vol. It may be as simple as (iv) investors / market needs to travel 
through time to digest the news and going through another earnings season, which kicks off 
in mid-Jan. A positive tail-risk would be (v) if the Fed pauses in December to go in Jan / 
Mar.  

•         MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 23) – We think a market-neutral approach is prudent. 
Within Tech, Mag7 names and Security Software appear to be the safest areas though 
there is now squeeze risk for Memory / Semis and would suggest using options for those 
looking to play a rebound. Assuming we see an aggressive near-term bounce, longer-temr 
we think +SPW vs. -SPX makes sense or +SPW vs. -NDX for those looking for more 
gearing. Regionally, UK / EU are likely to outperform especially if we see the Dollar see 
profit-taking. From a factor perspective, Value will continue to outperform both Beta and 
Momentum.  
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 16) –  The Anti-AI theme / ex-Tech rotation has 
been dominant in 26H2, even more so over the last month as Energy gains have flipped, 
the recession narrative has emerged, and investors have positioned more defensively. We 
think moving back toward Tech makes sense and like the cautious approach detailed in the 
previous Monetization Menu with primary exposure to Mag7 and then using options to  play 
a rebound in the AI themes. It seems most likely that the market will be patient with seeing 
how the catalysts play out rather than getting ahead of those events. Look for earnings, 
capital markets, and the JPM APAC Tech Conf to be a positive catalysts. In terms of ex-
Tech plays, we prefer Cyclicals over Defensives as the growth narrative reboots and 
markets digest an incrementally more hawkish Fed. Recall, SPX earnings are more tied to 
nominal GDP growth than to real GDP growth meaning increases in inflation are not a bad 
thing for earnings and that profit margins near record levels suggest that pass-through is not 
a problem in the near-term. We continue to like Europe for some Cyclical / Value exposure 
given relative positioning and valuation, though another surge in the USD may temper 
tactical outperformance.
