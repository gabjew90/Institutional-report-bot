What just happened?
Some quick thoughts on where we stand post Friday/Monday volatility.
Friday looks like a classic positioning flush. Whether it proves to have been the clearing event is still an
open question.
The equity move is obvious: the S&P closed Friday at its lowest since April on heavy volume and awful
breadth, then rallied 4.7% on Monday, led by Nvidia’s 31.5%. But the more interesting signals are
elsewhere:
Credit stopped deteriorating, but lagged the equity reversal. On Friday, CDX IG and HY had reached their
widest closes since late March. Equities recovered roughly half last week’s fall on Monday; CDX
recovered only around a quarter to a third of its widening. HY at 376bp is still only 9bp inside Friday’s
385bp. Credit catching up is the next confirmation I want.
Rates behaved better. The original pressure partly came from higher real yields, so their subsequent fall
removed some of the shock. Monday’s equity rally barely disturbed 10y nominal or real yields, although
real yields remain above early-Nov levels. The original rates shock has eased, not fully reversed.
The dollar is the awkward signal. DXY closed at 104.34, its highest since March 2025. Some of that may
reflect specifically European pressure, with colder weather and higher gas prices adding to EUR
headwinds, alongside wider US-European rate differentials. So the dollar alone isn’t decisive. A stronger
dollar alongside renewed credit widening and broader EMFX weakness would be much harder to dismiss
as a global stress signal.
EM remains mixed. Last week local rates split by beta, with PLN and KRW rallying while BRL and ZAR
sold off, and eight of nine major EM currencies weakened. Monday brought a broad rally in local curves
but only a split recovery in EMFX. The next test is whether differentiation within similar-beta markets re-
emerges.
Importantly, though, a clearing doesn’t require the fundamentals to be resolved. That clearly isn't the case
today. The AI capex and free-cash-flow debate is still with us, and lower-quality credit remains stressed. It
does, however, require the forced selling to have largely run its course.
So we don’t need another spectacular equity rally, we need the absence of renewed stress. That means:
Friday’s S&P close holds, breadth remains reasonable when Nvidia isn’t up 30%, credit tightens further,
real yields stay below their Nov peak and the dollar stops making highs.

My clearest line in the sand is credit: CDX HY closing back above Friday’s 385bp would make me
materially more cautious. Otherwise, I’d want to see at least two of the following: an S&P close below
Friday’s 6,910, 10-year real yields above the November peak around 2.91%, or DXY closing above 104.34
alongside renewed EMFX weakness.
If none of these trigger by the end of next week, with a full post-Thanksgiving week behind us, I’d be
more comfortable treating Friday as the clearing.
The obvious signal was Monday’s equity rally. The more useful (and durable) signal will be whether the
stress continues to fade around it.
