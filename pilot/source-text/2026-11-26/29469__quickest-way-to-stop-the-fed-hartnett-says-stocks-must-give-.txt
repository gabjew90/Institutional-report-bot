Stocks Must Give Up All Of 2026's Gains... And The 
S&P Is 80 Points Away 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 23, 2026 - 03:00 AM 
Give Michael Hartnett this much: when he says risk-off, he means it. 
Last Saturday, with the S&P 500 at 7,562 after a week in which everything went wrong except 
stocks, BofA's chief investment strategist told us he was staying risk-off. His bear case began 
below 7,000, and its ingredients were a dollar that was turning into a wrecking ball and central 
banks hiking into a slowdown. He also mentioned, almost in passing, that the fastest cure for an 
inflationary, K-shaped AI boom is lower stocks and wider credit spreads. 
It took five trading days. 
The S&P closed Friday at 6,925, down 8.4% on the week, its worst since Liberation Day, after a 
seventh straight loss took it through the 200-day average and into correction. The Nasdaq 100 
finished 19.8% below its October record, within spitting distance of a bear market, the VIX 
spiked above 39, and the biggest IPO of all time came within a dime of breaking its offer price a 
day after it opened with the company loading up on retail momentum flippers, and leaving 
institutions out in the cold. We closed last week's post by saying there were plenty of bears by 
temperament and almost none by position, a comfortable state of affairs... until the temperament 
gets a margin call. Well, the margin call came. 
So yes, he called it. And since Hartnett has now been risk-off for six straight Flow Shows, here 
is our running tally of how the call has evolved, updated for this week: 
 

 
 
Anyone expecting him to ring the bell after a 10% drop will be disappointed. 
In his latest Flow Show, titled "From Johnny Lovedrop to Billy Big Boots" (available to pro subs 
in the usual place), which went out on Thursday night, before Friday's 3.5% puke, and is the last 
one until December 3, Hartnett says only two questions matter heading into 2027: when do the 
rate hikes end, and do they end before a recession? The title, as best we can tell, is the Fed's 
career arc in six words: the central bank that spent years as Wall Street's sweetheart has put on 
the big boots. Hartnett's own chart puts it more politely. In short, it's a soft landing if the Fed is 
still the Street's best friend, and a hard one if it has decided to fight inflation for real.

"Quickest Way To Stop The Fed": 80 Points To Go 
Which brings us to the first of this week's two Zeitgeist quotes, and the most important sentence 
in the note: 
Zeitgeist: "Quickest way to stop the Fed is stocks giving up all their YTD gains." 
The S&P finished 2025 at 6,845. That is Hartnett's number, and here is how he frames it in his 
Tale of the Tape: 
Tale of the Tape: “when will rate hikes end?”; if Fed still Wall St’s BFF then credit & stock slump 
(IG CDX >80bps, SPX <6845) will short-circuit hike cycle; but if Fed now inflation tough guy 
post-midterms, hard landing risks grow, FOMC March 18th/April 28th earliest markets can 
position for “peak rates”; top in US$ “wrecking ball”, lows in XBT/gold/KOSPI (1st casualties of 
hikes) best tells to signal peak hike concerns. 
 

 
Translation: there are two Feds on offer. If Warsh's Fed is still Wall Street's friend and still thinks 
the stock market is too big to fail, a slump in stocks and credit short-circuits the hiking cycle, and 
the two levels that do it are the S&P below 6,845 and IG CDX above 80bps. If instead the Fed 
has taken the midterms as an order to fix "affordability" (and after missing its inflation target for 
years it has some catching up to do), then the earliest anyone can price the end of the cycle is 
the March 18 or April 28 FOMC, and the odds of a hard landing go up with every meeting in 
between. 
On the first of those two levels, the market didn't waste any time. From Friday's close the S&P 
needs to lose another 80 points, or 1.2%, which these days is a slow morning (Friday alone was 
3.5%). IG CDX is blowing out... 
 
... cash IG spreads widened about 10bps on the week to the widest since May 2025. 
 
 

 
Is it working? A little. By Friday afternoon the 50bps hike that Governor Waller wanted to 
"shock" people with was off the table, January hike odds were below 60%, and then this hit: 
Hartnett is hardly alone here. On Tuesday we laid out Goldman's version of the same argument 
in "The Reflexive Warsh Put: Goldman Says A Stock Slide Is The Only Thing That Stops The 
Hikes", and on Friday Goldman's Tony Pasquariello asked if the Fed is about to "get properly 
rough". So the sell-side agrees on the mechanism: the market has to do the Fed's job badly 
enough that the Fed stops doing it. 
The problem is that nobody has told the Fed. On Friday, with the S&P down 3.5%, the VIX at 39 
and the Nasdaq a rounding error from a bear market, Governor Cook said financial conditions 
are too accommodative and that waiting risks bigger hikes later. Philadelphia's Paulson is open 
to back-to-back hikes. Wednesday's minutes showed almost all officials see more tightening. 
And Warsh? On Tuesday we learned that the Fed is modeling a crash. Modeling one and 
stopping one are different jobs. Billy still has his boots on.

Go through the dates. Last October's dovish cut marked the top in bitcoin. Trump's nomination

So the tells that hike fear has peaked are the same things in reverse: a top in the dollar, and 
lows in bitcoin, gold and the KOSPI, the first casualties of the hikes. Where do they stand? The 
dollar just had its strongest weekly close since April 2025, bitcoin is down seven weeks in a row, 
gold is back where it was in September 2025, and The Market Ear is asking how much more 
pain the KOSPI has left. That's zero for four, although Friday did bring the first hint of a change, 
with the haven bid rotating from the dollar into the yen.

writing about Wall Street still waiting for one, with JPMorgan's desk pointing out that a clearing

case for him: it's hard to have a clearing event when nobody has cleared anything. Here is his

No Release Valve: Six Straight Losing Years For The Long Bond 
Why did stocks get no help from bonds for most of this selloff? Here is The Price is Right: 
The Price is Right: 30-year US Treasury on course for 6th year of negative price return, longest 
streak since 1963-69 Vietnam War; GT30 yield just -25bps from recent high as Nasdaq -15%, 
banks -16%, oil -22%...why no Q4 release valve for risk assets; greatest risk remains that yields 
rise, not fall in next recession because debt levels too high. 
The 30Y Treasury is on course for a sixth consecutive year of negative price returns, the longest 
losing streak since 1963-69, when the bill for Vietnam and the Great Society was coming due. 
As of Hartnett's writing its yield was just 25bps off the recent high even though the Nasdaq was 
down 15%, banks 16% and oil 22%. That, he says, is why risk assets had no release valve in 
Q4. And then comes the line that should worry everyone who is counting on duration to save 
them: the greatest risk is that yields rise, not fall, in the next recession, because there is simply 
too much debt. 
Since Thursday the valve has opened a crack. The 30Y closed Friday at 5.31%, down 18bps on 
the week, and as we showed in "Bonds Haven't Noticed Yet", Citadel Securities sees a record 
 

 
3.5-sigma gap between yields and growth. Maybe bonds are finally noticing. But there is a 
tension in Hartnett's note, the same one we flagged last week, and it has only grown. On page 
one the biggest risk is that yields go up in a recession. On page three, as we'll see, his 
hard-landing playbook starts with allocators buying bonds. And last week he told us nobody 
wants to touch Treasuries until they yield 5.5% to 6.5%. All three can't be right at once, and in a 
country running record October deficits at full employment, we know which one we'd keep. 
Soft Landing: Buy Below 7,000 (We're Below 7,000) 
On to the second question, whether the hikes end before a recession begins. Hartnett gives 
both answers in The Biggest Picture: 
The Biggest Picture: “do rate hikes end before recession?”; if soft landing SPX <7k a buy, 
breadth bull resumes spring (SPW on 16x, MID 15x, ACWI ex.US 12x not scary), just wait for 
narrative flip from hikes to cuts, then buy cyclicals Cs...crypto, chips, copper, consumer, condos;

Start with the happy ending. If lower inflation, stable employment and higher productivity are 
visible by the spring, earnings growth holds above 10%, and in that world the S&P below 7,000 
is a buy. It closed Friday 75 points below that. The "breadth bull" resumes, and the parts of the 
market that never joined the AI party are no longer expensive: the equal-weight S&P trades at 
16x forward earnings, mid-caps at 15x against a 30-year average of 16x, and stocks outside the 
US at 12x against an average of 13x. 
 

 
 
 

 
The sequencing matters to him, though. Preserve capital now, wait for fear to become visible, 
and once the narrative flips from hikes to cuts, buy the cyclical "Cs": crypto, chips, copper, 
consumer and condos. In other words, everything that got destroyed this month.

Hartnett adds two details. The dollar was very strong in both years. And the Nasdaq managed

We'd add a third, and it speaks directly to his two questions. The Fed's last hike of that cycle 
was in May 2000, and it spent all of 2001 cutting, by 475bps. The answer to "when do the hikes 
end" turned out to be "soon." It made no difference, because the answer to "do they end before 
recession" was no. So the two analogs doing the rounds point in opposite directions. In the 2018 
version, Powell hikes in December, the low is in by Christmas Eve and he folds in the first week 
of January. In the 2000 version, the Fed stops early and the bear market runs for two more 
years anyway, because by then the problem is earnings. The gap between them is the gap 
between a positioning accident and a capex bust, and Chart 11 above tells you which one 
Hartnett thinks this is.

2027, According To People With 3.6% Cash 
Hartnett also summarizes how the fund managers in Tuesday's survey are set up for next year, 
and it is useful mostly as a list of what happens if he's right. They expect a soft landing, lower 
inflation and slower AI capex growth, and they go into 2027 overweight stocks, commodities, 
emerging markets, healthcare and industrials, and underweight cash, bonds, REITs, consumer 
and energy. Their picks for best asset class are international stocks (36%) and US stocks 
(24%), for best index the Nasdaq and MSCI EM (21% each), and for best currency the yen 
(42%). 
Their year-end 2027 targets: a 10Y yield of 4.9%, EUR/USD at 1.12, the S&P at 7,926 and gold 
at $4,341. The first of those was hit on Friday, thirteen months early. The S&P target is 14% 
above the market and the gold target 24% above it, so there is at least plenty of room to be right 
(gold tested $3,500 on Friday).

Does The Fed Still Love You? We're 80 Points From Finding Out 
Hartnett has said for over a decade that markets stop panicking when policymakers start 
panicking. This week's note is that maxim with a price tag: 6,845 on the S&P and 80bps on IG 
CDX. The first is a bad Monday away. And the market has already done some of the Fed's 
thinking for it, taking out the 50bps hike, un-pricing December and pulling the 10Y back below 
5%. 
We agree with him on the mechanism. Where we'd push back is on the idea that 6,845 is where 
it ends. A Fed that has missed its target for 67 months, has three hot CPI prints in a row on its 
 

 
desk and just watched voters punish the party in power over prices is going to need more than a 
flat year for the S&P before it stands down. Cook told us as much on Friday. In 2018 it took a 
20% drop, and that Fed didn't have an inflation problem. Our guess is that Billy takes the boots 
off only when Hartnett's hard-landing tells start flashing, claims at 250K and IG at 100bps, and 
by then it is the left-hand column of his matrix that matters. 
Which is why our number of the week is $310 billion: the most that has ever gone into equity 
funds in four weeks, from investors holding 3.6% cash, of whom just 3% expect a hard landing. 
Everybody on Wall Street wants a clearing event. $310 billion says nobody has cleared. 
So enjoy the turkey, and the bounce if the Breadth Rule delivers one. Hartnett is back on 
December 3, and by then we'll know if the Fed still loves Wall Street, or if it has found someone 
else.
