Market Observations: 11.16.2026–11.20.2026 
The S&P 500 finished the week sharply lower and extended its losing streak to seven consecutive trading sessions. 
The broad-based selling has pushed the S&P into a technical correction—defined by a 10 percent pullback from its 
all-time high. Weighing on the market was investor trepidation around the AI trade as strong quarterly results and 
forward guidance from Nvidia and the successful public offering from Anthropic did little to renew optimism 
around the sector. The selloff was global, with almost every major market benchmark finishing the week lower. 
Adding to the negative tone was increased risks of U.S. strikes on Iran, and the snowballing risk of hacks and 
system outages. 
Fedspeak Leans Hawkish (Again): Federal Reserve (Fed) Governor Waller told CNBC that he is very concerned that 
inflation is heading in the wrong direction and broadening, suggesting that if the Fed is not going to signal a more 
aggressive pace in the months ahead then it should raise by 50 basis points at the upcoming December meeting. 
His concern stems from the worry that people are starting to think 3 percent inflation may be the new norm. Fed 
Vice Chair Jefferson said the ongoing AI buildout is putting upward pressure on inflation, though productivity

consider an outsized rate hike. Lastly, St. Louis Fed President Musalem signaled support for multiple interest rate 
increases in the coming months to bring inflation under control. 
FOMC Meeting Minutes:  According to the meeting minutes from the Oct. 27–28 Federal Open Market Committee 
(FOMC) gathering, nearly all Fed officials agreed they would likely have to raise interest rates in the months ahead 
to return inflation to their 2 percent target. The minutes also showed two policymakers said an increase of more 
than 25 basis points could be warranted in December if incoming inflation data proved unfavorable. Among 
officials who backed the decision to hold rates last month, the minutes showed it was a close call for a few. Others 
in the majority said market expectations for rate increases in December and in 2027 were broadly consistent with 
their assessment of appropriate policy.  
Q3 Earnings:  Through Nov. 20, 468 companies in the S&P 500 have released third quarter results, with over 84 
percent beating expectations. Aggregate earnings for this group are up just over 32 percent from a year ago, and 
solidly ahead of the roughly 25 percent projected growth rate at the end of September. On the sector level, nine of 
the 11 S&P sector groups have posted better than expected results, with healthcare and utilities posting the 
biggest upside surprises. On a year-over-year basis, all 11 sectors posted gains with the energy sector delivering 
the strongest growth followed by technology and industrials. Full-year expectations call for S&P 500 earnings to 
grow by 30.4 percent this year, followed by 20.4 percent in 2027, and 16.4 percent in 2028. 
Economic Roundup: Initial jobless claims increased 1,000 to 203,000 during the week ended Nov. 14, above the 
consensus estimate of 198,000 but still well below the 222,000 reading a year earlier. On the consumer front, retail 
sales growth moderated in October as a pullback in big ticket items, like cars, masked resilient demand for other 
goods. The value of retail purchases increased 0.2 percent after a gasoline-fueled 0.8 percent advance in 
September, according to Commerce Department data.  
The Week Ahead: The focal point of this week’s data calendar will be the October personal consumption 
expenditures inflation, along with personal income and consumption, on Wednesday. October durable goods 
orders and new home sales will also be released on the same day. Due to the holiday, Fed speeches will be 
minimal, although the central bank is scheduled to release the Beige Book report on Wednesday. The earnings 
calendar will be light with just a dozen members of the S&P 500 scheduled to release results. Amongst this group 
will be Dell Technologies, Analog Devices, Workday, and Deere & Co. The stock market will be closed on Thursday 
in observance of the Thanksgiving holiday and will close at 1 p.m. ET on Friday.

Definitions 
The Dow Jones Industrial Average is a price-weighted average of 30 blue-chip stocks that are generally defined as 
the leaders in their industry. It has been a widely followed indicator of the stock market since Oct. 1, 1928. 
Standard and Poor’s 500 Index is a capitalization-weighted index of 500 stocks. The index is designed to measure 
performance of the broad domestic economy through changes in the aggregate market value of 500 stocks 
representing all major industries.  
The Nasdaq Composite Index is a broad-based capitalization-weighted index of stocks in all three Nasdaq tiers: 
Global Select, Global Market and Capital Market. The index was developed with a base level of 100 as of Feb. 5, 1971.
