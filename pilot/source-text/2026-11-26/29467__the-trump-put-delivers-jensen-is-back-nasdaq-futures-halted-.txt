 
The Trump Put Delivers: Jensen Is Back, Nasdaq 
Futures Halted... And Goldman's Privorotsky Asks 
Whether To Sell The Squeeze 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 23, 2026 - 06:00 PM 
Update (1130ET): Goldman's equity analysts have opined on Jensen Huang's reinstatement 
and see the announcement as a positive for the stock and broader AI ecosystem across several 
dimensions: 
●​ Strategic and ecosystem: As we had written previously, we believe Mr. Huang's vision, 
reach, and external convening power has been important to both Nvidia and the broader 
AI ecosystem. However, it remains to be seen whether the company will continue to 
negotiate new multi-party investments among ecosystem partners at the same scale as 
it had previously done. 
●​ Leadership and culture: As we had written previously, we believe Mr. Huang has been 
integral to Nvidia's corporate culture and ethos - and we believe his return will largely 
reinforce the company's existing culture. We continue to expect the company to execute 
on its previously announced product roadmap. 
●​ Financial and regulatory: As previously reported in the press, the US Department of 
Justice has been conducting an investigation into whether some of Nvidia's customers 
did engage in export control violations following shipments of products to jurisdictions 
including Singapore. We continue to believe Nvidia's operations in certain jurisdictions 
could face more scrutiny going forward. In addition, press reports have also suggested 
that an SEC investigation into the company's past disclosures is underway. We believe 
these investigations are likely to serve as an overhang on the stock until they are 
resolved, and/or the company provides more disclosure on these items. 
We retain our Buy rating given the positive risk/reward we see for shares based on our current 
view of Nvidia's fundamentals, and in light of Mr. Huang's reinstatement as CEO.  
 

 
We expect the stock's multiple to re-rate following this announcement, but note that we expect it 
to remain impacted pending the investigations noted above. We will continue to re-examine our 
view on the stock as additional information becomes available from Nvidia's key customers, as 
well as the results of any ongoing internal or external investigations.

At 3:07am ET on Monday, Goldman's head of EMEA Delta One trading Rich Privorotsky asked 
the question in his morning note "Nvidia. Positioning. Fed Hawkishness" (available to pro subs): 
would Trump's public push to reinstate Jensen Huang "leave some to wonder if the Trump 
market put appears to be back?" 
Six hours later, he had his answer. 
Less than a week after chairman Harvey Jones said the board "does not intend to reinstate 
Jensen as CEO," and some 11 hours after Trump's Truth Social plea, Nvidia brought Huang 
back "effective immediately," with the board saying it "received input from senior levels of the 
United States government" and is now "confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no 
national security risk." Trump followed with his own post: the Attorney General has confirmed 
the government "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct." 
Deep state denied, again. 
 
The market reaction was everything Privorotsky's positioning data said it would be: NVDA up as 
much as 30% premarket, S&P futures up 4.6% at session highs and Nasdaq futures up so 
much they were halted.  
 

 
 
So much for "sticking plasters." 
The Trump Put Delivered (In Under 12 Hours) 
Credit where due. Privorotsky had dismissed the weekend's Asian support measures (China 
lowering margin requirements, Korea easing buyback curbs, Taiwan tightening short-selling 
rules) as "the sort of sticking plasters you don't really want to see marking the bottom." The 
White House comments on Nvidia, he argued, were "more meaningful": 
"Trump's public support for reinstatement feels entirely purposeful and will leave some to 
wonder if the Trump market put appears to be back? The structural issues remain, but the acute 
concerns driving last week's selloff were Nvidia export restrictions, remote compute access, 
TSMC licensing uncertainty and an already troublesome AI backdrop compounded by growth 
deceleration and accounting questions. For now, this is an Nvidia trade, and a more business 
friendly administration helps." 
"Entirely purposeful" turned out to be an understatement: the board's own release credits 
"senior levels of the United States government." Bessent, who a week ago told Nvidia to look 
outside the company for a CEO, had already called the spy angle "kind of a canard" on Sunday, 
and Rene Haas took himself out of the running by extending at Arm through 2033. The board 
was out of options, and probably out of patience with a stock that had lost roughly $2.7 trillion 
from its October peak. 
 

 
Still, Privorotsky's caveat from this morning is worth rereading now that the champagne is out: 
"let's not confuse a rhetorical pivot with regulatory relief." Bessent still defends the remote 
access restrictions and the investigations remain active. Trump's own statement tells Nvidia to 
"continue its thorough internal investigation of the Company's compliance with U.S. export 
controls," and the board just created a National Security and Export Control Committee and a 
new Chief Compliance Officer post. The real test, per Privorotsky, is still "whether licenses are 
granted for offshore data centers, particularly existing facilities." Rehiring a CEO grants zero 
licenses. As he laid out in Friday's note, the BIS regime squeezes China on three fronts (chips, 
remote compute, overseas foundries), and none of that changed this morning. 
Shorts: "Arguably The Missing Ingredient" 
Why did a CEO headline move the whole index 4%? Because of who was positioned for it. Here 
is the Goldman prime brokerage data Privorotsky flagged: 
"Fundamental L/S Gross leverage -3.6 pts to 204.8% (13th percentile 1-year) and Net leverage 
-3.1 pts to 42.3% (1st percentile 1-year). ..Beta Adjusted Fundamental L/S Gross leverage -8.3 
pts to 214.2% (1-year low) and Beta Adjusted Net leverage -8.8 pts to 48.2% (5-year low)...This 
week's shorting activity was the largest since April 2025." 
And his conclusion, written hours before the board caved: 
"The difference now is that investors aren't simply defensively positioned… they've now got 
meaningful shorts in the book. Arguably the missing ingredient. The setup for a bear market 
rally is glaringly obvious. The question is whether you're meant to sell into it or let it breathe." 
We flagged the biggest single-stock short wave since 2022 on Sunday, while Morgan Stanley's 
Savone was channeling Clubber Lang as hedge fund nets closed in on COVID lows, and the 
most crowded longs just had their worst stretch in 20 years. Combine 1st-percentile nets, fresh 
shorts and a 30% move in the index's biggest weight, and you get this: 
Last Thursday Privorotsky smelled a squeeze on Anthropic day and it lasted about an hour. This 
one had a catalyst with the President's name on it. 
"The VIX Kissed 40" 
The sentiment backdrop was about as washed out as it gets. Per Privorotsky, "Vol reached 
extreme levels into the weekend, with the VIX kissed 40 and genuine left tail stress emerging," 
and normalization alone should be a tailwind. The checklist: 
●​ CNN Fear & Greed around 10; 
 

 
●​ put/call ratios near the 95th percentile; 
●​ CFTC leveraged funds reportedly net short Nasdaq futures (ouch); 
●​ the VIX curve at its steepest inversion since April 2025. 
That came on top of Goldman's Panic Index hitting a record 9.8 after the fifth-biggest VIX spike 
on record. "Historically, VIX closes above 35 have often preceded positive equity reversals over 
the following weeks," Privorotsky wrote. "Small samples, not a forecast, but the asymmetry 
looks compelling." Monday's open delivered the asymmetry in one session. Whether it lasts 
"over the following weeks" is the part still to be earned. 
The Fed Hasn't Sounded This Hawkish Since 2023 
Because here is the thing a Truth Social post can't fix. Privorotsky's other big theme this 
morning was the Fed: 
"I will continue my overtures about the mispricing of the Fed. The hawkish rhetoric is clear, but 
the market is pricing a growth shock and an AI capex slowdown, commodities have collapsed, 
inflation expectations are near their lows for the year and real rates look far too high for this 
environment. Financial conditions have tightened materially." 
His side-note shows just how hawkish: Bloomberg Economics' Fedspeak sentiment index 
(higher = more hawkish) closed at 14.1, roughly the highest since early 2023 and up from 
around -5 at the start of the year (chart source Bloomberg, via GS; levels read off the chart, so 
approximate): 
 
 

 
For context, the index peaked at 19.2 in June 2022, when the Fed was hiking 75bps a meeting. 
Hence Privorotsky's hope: 
"My hope rests with Kevin Warsh, who has historically been attentive to financial conditions. My 
prediction is that the first FOMC member to acknowledge the tightening will provoke an outsized 
reaction. Interestingly, Bessent has already raised the risk of a Fed policy mistake. The question 
is when the Fed itself gets there." 
Bessent, as we noted on Sunday, has warned the Fed risks overtightening, and the 
administration has just shown how fast it moves when it wants a market outcome. The Fed is a 
different animal: Waller is still pitching 50bp hikes and the minutes said "almost all" members 
want more. One put arrived this morning. The one that matters for multiples hasn't. We have 
asked how much runway is left before Fed over-tightening bites, and a squeeze driven by short 
covering doesn't lengthen it. 
Hello Again, 2018: "Credit Is The Tell" 
The analog Privorotsky keeps coming back to is the same one Goldman's top derivs trader 
dusted off last week: November/December 2018. "Financial conditions have tightened faster this 
cycle, although the equity drawdown was deeper before bottoming in 2018." We'll just remind 
readers how that one ended: 
2018 had its share of violent bear market rallies before Christmas Eve too. Which is why the 
most important line in Privorotsky's note is the one that tells you how to judge today's rip: 
"The distinction now is between a positioning squeeze and a genuine recovery in risk appetite. 
Credit is the tell. If Nvidia rallies but neocloud spreads remain under pressure, we're probably 
just covering shorts. The broader concern is that AI infrastructure has become increasingly 
dependent on external capital precisely as the cost of that capital rises. The weekend's 
administrative pivot helps sentiment, but it doesn't solve financing or oversupply." 
Well, Nvidia is rallying. Now watch the neoclouds. We have been pounding the table on AI's 
debt problem since last October, when we warned that AI had quietly become a debt bubble too; 
junk bonds just suffered their biggest blowout of 2026, and Gartner thinks over 80% of 
neoclouds will fold. Jensen's return doesn't refinance a single GPU-backed loan. 
And the week doesn't get easier: 
●​ flash PMIs today, then Dell earnings Tuesday; 
●​ the Beige Book and core PCE Wednesday, "the marquee inflation release ahead of 
payrolls, the December FOMC and November CPI"; 
 

 
●​ Thanksgiving thinning out liquidity, "potentially amplifying the squeeze" (as if it needed 
amplifying). 
The Put Was Real. The Bottom Is Another Matter 
Privorotsky's verdict this morning was already pointing the right way: 
"It's adult swim out there. We've moved beyond a routine correction into something resembling a 
bear market in parts of AI. The structural questions won't disappear overnight, but the 
combination of extreme positioning, elevated vol, a potential Nvidia policy pivot and a more 
visible Trump put creates a compelling tactical setup." 
Strike "potential." The pivot happened, the put is visible from space, and the tactical setup paid 
out before the cash open. Goldman's Tony Pasquariello was mapping the signposts for a tactical 
bottom this very morning, and today checks off a few of them. 
But note the word Privorotsky keeps using: tactical. A rehired CEO doesn't grant offshore 
licenses, doesn't lower real rates and doesn't fix the funding problem under the AI buildout. With 
the Fed talking its toughest game since 2023 into a 100bp-plus financial conditions shock, we 
suspect his open question ("sell into it or let it breathe") gets answered the skeptical way: let it 
breathe through Thanksgiving, and sell it if neocloud spreads don't follow Nvidia. The Trump put 
clearly works. The Fed put, the one that ended 2018, is still nowhere to be seen.
