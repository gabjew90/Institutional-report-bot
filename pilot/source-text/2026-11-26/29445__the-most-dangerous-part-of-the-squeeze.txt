The morning after the squeeze
Buying panic and chasing the subsequent squeeze are two very different trades.
Equities are back at major resistance, historical parallels are uncomfortable, and cross-asset signals
remain far from convincing. Yet positioning suggests there could still be fuel left in the squeeze.
The risk/reward is getting increasingly complicated.
Back into the wall
SPX has gone from extreme oversold territory to major resistance in a single session. Not something
you see very often. The 7350–7400 area is big resistance, with the 200-day moving average coming in
there. The longer-term trend comes in around 7200 (all cash levels).
The big question is whether the "easy" part of the bounce is already behind us.

Big rallies, broken trends
According to BTIG, there have been 42 previous instances since 1985 of NDX rallying 6% or more while
closing below its 200-day moving average. The median return over the following 30 days was -5.35%,
with the market higher only 39% of the time.
Despite the monster bounce, NDX futures remain well below the 200-day moving average, with the
short-term descending trendline still intact. Perhaps most telling is that even a rally of this magnitude
hasn't managed to get NDX back above its 21-day moving average. That tells you just how beaten up
tech is.

The morning after
Since 1985, there have been only twelve previous instances of NDX falling 5% or more one day and
rallying 5% or more the next. Only two saw positive returns the following day, while the average five-
day return was -4.9%.
Even more striking is the average maximum drawdown of 16.4% over the following 30 trading days,
according to BTIG.

BTIG/TME
Down first, up later?
The historical downside hasn't necessarily precluded substantial subsequent upside. Following the
twelve previous violent NDX reversals, the average maximum draw-up over the next 30 trading days
was almost 10%. However, most of the biggest upside moves occurred after the first 15 trading days.

The squeeze that didn't clear the shorts?
S&P E-mini futures open interest surged $36.6bn between November 17 and 20 as the index plunged
5.9%. Friday alone saw a $26.6bn increase, the biggest single-day change of 2026 outside roll periods.
Interestingly, Monday's monster squeeze saw open interest decline by just $7.9bn. Either substantial
fresh longs entered the market or a meaningful portion of the bears is still hanging around. Potentially
both.

Jensen's Liberation Day
Ron Adler (JPM) points out that while removing the Jensen uncertainty is clearly positive for NVDA,
presidential intervention in corporate governance introduces a much bigger risk that could weigh on
equity multiples over time.
The exception could be strategically important companies with close government ties, particularly in
AI. Adler sees room for the rally to run given positioning, but doubts we are getting back to peak
euphoria anytime soon.
Jensen can't fix everything
The return of Jensen has provided spectacular relief, but the bigger AI debate remains unresolved.
As UBS points out, investor focus has shifted from the sheer scale of AI spending to the returns that
spending can generate. Expect increasing differentiation between companies that can fund AI
investment internally and those dependent on debt and speculative growth.
The next phase of the AI trade could be much more about dispersion than beta.
Selling strength, not weakness
UBS remains neutral on US and global equities and sees the rebound as an opportunity to reduce
excessive megacap tech concentration.
The bank favors broadening exposure toward equal-weight equities, European equities, industrials and
health care.
The AI story doesn't have to be over for the concentration trade to be past its prime.

Not normal
There is nothing normal about this market. VIX is back trading close to 30 following yesterday's vol
puke, despite SPX futures being down less than 1% as of writing. This market remains under severe
stress.
Comparing trending and mean-reverting assets isn't ideal, but VIX isn't far from the peak panic levels
we saw in March, when SPX was trading some 700 points lower.
Extreme markets continue.
