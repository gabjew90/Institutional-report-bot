The next rates rally has an equity leg
The bond short is crowded. So is the utility short.
A turn in yields could put both trades under pressure. And with CTA positioning showing significant
upside convexity in bonds, the next rates rally could have an equity leg attached to it.
The bond short
Goldman Sachs S&T's CTA model puts US 10-year Treasury positioning at roughly 99% of its maximum
short.
Meanwhile, Goldman's broader funds positioning indicator shows an extreme duration underweight, a
setup that has historically preceded lower 10-year yields over the following three months.
GS
The convexity
The positioning matters because the flow profile is highly asymmetric.
CTAs are sitting on extreme upside convexity in bonds. A move higher in Treasuries could trigger
additional systematic buying, adding fuel to the rally.
GS
The other crowded short
It isn't just bonds.
Utilities were the most heavily shorted US sector last week, with Goldman seeing the largest short
selling in the sector in almost six years.


[Page 2]
JPMorgan's prime-brokerage series tells a similar story: hedge-fund net exposure to utilities has fallen
to its lowest since 2018, around 2.5 standard deviations below normal.
GS
The equity leg
Higher yields have hurt utilities.
From September 4 to 25, the 10-year yield rose 39bp, while XLU fell around 8.4% relative to SPY. The
inverse pattern has persisted through the spring and summer.
If yields finally reverse, utilities are where the equity squeeze could show up.

The watch
BofA estimates its Treasury trend positions would likely hold unless the 10-year yield falls roughly 18bp
in a week.
Watch 10-year yields and XLU/SPY together.
Falling yields without relative utility strength would weaken the thesis. But if yields break lower and
utilities start outperforming, two crowded shorts could start unwinding together.
That is when the rates rally gets its equity leg.
