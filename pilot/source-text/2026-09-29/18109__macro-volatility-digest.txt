WEEKLY MARKET COMMENTARY 
Equities Unfazed by Spike in Rates Volatility 
 
Cross-Asset Volatility: US bond yields climbed to a two-
decade high last week, yet reaction in the cross-asset vol 
market was mixed. Not surprisingly, rates vol jumped higher, 
with the MOVE Index surging over 15nms to near a 1-year 
high of 96 bps vol. In contrast, equity vol ended the week 
almost unch’d, with the VIX® Index hovering near a 1-year 
low of 14.9%. This contrast is even more surprising 
considering the increase in yields came almost entirely from 
higher real yields (i.e. tighter financial conditions) rather than 
higher inflation expectations, yet risk assets remain largely 
unfazed with stocks trading near record highs. Credit 
volatility increased as spreads widened notably, with the 
VIXIG Index gaining ~5 pts to 25 bps vol (52nd percentile high 
over the past year). More duration-sensitive corporate bonds 
saw even bigger increases in volatility, with LQD 1M implied 
volatility jumping to the 96th percentile high. While oil prices 
fell last week, oil volatility gained, with the OVX Index up 5 
pts to 55% on the back of elevated geopolitical risk  
Equity Volatility: While SPX® index volatility was mostly 
unch’d last week, single stock volatility increased further, 
with the VIXEQSM Index up another 2 pts to 38.5%. As we 
get closer to Q3 earnings, we see scope for single stock 
volatility to rise even more relative to index vol, especially 
against a backdrop of higher rates which have historically 
been a catalyst for more stock dispersion. The spread 
between VIXEQ and VIX Index has already widened by 5 pts 
over the past two weeks to 23.5% though it remains well off 
the record high of 34% seen in July. While SPX vol remained 
unchanged, both RTY and QQQ 1M implied volatilities 
gained by 1.0+ pt last week, with the RTY 1M implied-
realized volatility spread screening as particularly rich (76th 
percentile high, see Exhibit 2). Amongst global equity 
indices, Brazil stands out as having the richest implied 
volatility ahead of its presidential election on Oct 4th. EWZ 
1M implied volatility has almost doubled over the past month 
to near a 4-year high of 46% even as realized volatility 
hovers at just 24%. The spread between the implied vs. 
realized volatility, at 22.5%, is also trading at an 8-year high. 
The bid for EWZ vol is not surprising as elections have 
historically been some of the biggest catalysts for Brazilian 
equities. EWZ 1M realized vol has exceeded 50% in each of 
the last 3 presidential elections.  
Skew: Sentiment in the equity market remains remarkably 
constructive despite sharply rising bond yields. Not only is 
the market back to a record high, demand for protection has 
also fallen to near a 1-year low. SPX 1M skew is trading in 
the 5th percentile low currently as investors rotate out of 
hedges and into upside calls (Exhibit 3). And it’s not just at 
the index level – about 40% of the top 100 stocks are trading 
with inverted call skew (a sign of extreme bullishness).

How to read this chart:
The chart shows how various asset class implied 
volatilities are trading relative to their own history, using 
a 10-year lookback. 
The volatilities are ranked by z-scores, which compares 
the current volatility level for an asset versus its own 10-
year history. A z-score of +2, for example, would mean 
that the current volatility level is trading 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average (i.e. rich) while a 
z-score of -2 would mean it’s trading 2 standard 
deviations below average (i.e. cheap)
Because each asset class volatility trades at different 
levels and often defined differently (e.g. lognormal vs. 
normal vol, price vs. bps vol), by standardizing using z-
scores, we’re able to compare different asset class 
volatilities on the same scale - and see at a glance if 
there are any divergences or dislocations in the cross-
asset volatility markets
