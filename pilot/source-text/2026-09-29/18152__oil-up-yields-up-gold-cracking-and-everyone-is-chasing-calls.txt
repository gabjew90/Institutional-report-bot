Opportunity cost
As GS’s Privorotsky points out, front-end real rates have moved sharply higher over the past few weeks,
with the 2-year back around its highest levels in more than two years. Inflation expectations have not
risen nearly enough to offset the move.
That is a tough setup for a zero-yielding asset. Gold can hedge inflation, fiscal stress and geopolitical
risk, but when cash suddenly offers a large positive real return, the opportunity cost starts to bite.

China is cutting length
Shanghai open interest fell by 11k contracts, or 2.6%, during the day session as longs were liquidated
into the open.
The headline positioning probably overstates the true speculative long, as a large +SGE/-SHFE
onshore EFP position inflates SHFE open interest. But Chinese specs are still long, according to
Goldman's gold desk.
Now add the calendar. SHFE is closed 1–7 October for National Week, and with Iran and rates both
moving, there is little incentive to carry large speculative length through a week-long shutdown.
Expect more liquidation into the holiday.

Oil owns the bond market
The correlation between WTI and the US 10-year yield is the tightest in 35 years.
Even more striking: the relationship between the average US stock and the 10-year yield is now the
most negative since 1960.
Oil → yields → equities. That is increasingly the macro chain that matters.

Bond convexity
The positioning matters because the flow profile is highly asymmetric. CTAs are sitting on extreme
upside convexity in bonds. Any meaningful reversal in yields could therefore bring systematic buying
with it.
GS
Longs trimmed
GS notes that institutional equity length has been trimmed meaningfully. US index positioning has
fallen from a record 48% of open interest in May to 36%, while CTA/global systematic equity exposure
has dropped from $145bn in August to ~$70bn.
After selling nearly $75bn over the past month, GS now estimates CTAs could buy ~$25bn over the
next month in its baseline scenario.
The positioning overhang has become considerably cleaner.

Merde
France's bond market is already under pressure, with the 10-year OAT-Bund spread around 111bp.
Importantly, that widening predates any potential US diesel ban.
But this is where things get interesting. If gasoil moves materially higher from here, the question is
whether French spreads follow.
The transmission isn't automatic. It requires higher diesel prices to translate into more fiscal support,
budget concessions or weaker activity. But that is precisely the vulnerability. More here.
