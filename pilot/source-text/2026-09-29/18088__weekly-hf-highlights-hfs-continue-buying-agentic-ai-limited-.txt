This Week’s Highlights (as of Thursday, September 24th)
HFs ended net buyers of global equities via long additions as the pace of re-grossing accelerated primarily across US equities post 
FOMC. Tech led much of the net buying across US equities amidst a narrower backdrop as the NDX gained +1.6% over the 4 sessions vs. the 
S&P +70bps. HFs continued to buy into the Agentic AI theme (MSXXCLAW) as they added to longs, while they also pressed shorts in disrupted 
agentic names (MSXXARSK), particularly within the Consumer Discretionary space. HFs were also buyers of Semis broadly, alongside Global 
Memory (MSXXGMEM) where the net buying was extremely small in size. Positioning wise, HF net exposure has continued to increase 
meaningfully across Agentic AI names and Hyperscalers as the cohort has continued to lean into less-crowded pockets of the AI trade, while net 
positioning in Memory remains well off peak levels at ~6% of global net exposure (vs. ~12% at peaks). Industrials also saw demand driven by 
short covers in Airlines (MSXXFLY) and Capital Goods (MSXXCAPG), followed by Staples and Utilities amidst a bid for defensives. Health Care 
also continued to see large buying flow driven by continued outsized covering in High SI Biotech names (MSXXBIOS) driven by HC-dedicated 
HFs. Notably, the group has unwound a meaningful amount of the short exposure they had added since the start of July. Note that despite the 
active re-grossing flow across US equities, US-based L/S fund gross leverage across their global portfolios has remained near lows vs the 
last 12m which continues to leave room for HFs to add gross. While net leverage has increased from shorter term lows of 50% ahead of last 
Thursday’s rally, current levels at 54% still sit at moderate levels – coupled with lighter gross dynamics, HFs have room to add on both the long or 
short side. 
European equities ended net sold over the week as HFs largely pressed shorts – though, the selling was led entirely by Industrials as HFs were 
sellers of EU Aerospace & Defense (MSESAER) and EU Industrial Transports (MSESITR). Flows were relatively quiet across the broader region, 
with smaller buying seen in EU Financials, Materials and Health Care. Asia ex-Japan also ended net sold driven by supply in China, though it’s 
worth noting HFs were net buyers of Taiwan and South Korea as Asia-based HFs added back to longs in Semis and Hardware. Japan ended 
net sold as HFs were sellers of all areas in the region outside of Tech, as they rotated capital back into JP Electronic Equipment names. While 
small signs have emerged of HFs buying back into Asia AI trades, this remains in its early stages as gross flow across equities outside of the US 
represents a fraction of what was unwound through the prior two months. 
PnL continued to be challenged through this week with the average global L/S fund ending the week flat (vs. MSCI +44bps), while the average 
US L/S fund posted small losses of ~5bps, lagging the S&P +71bps. On a MTD basis, the average global L/S fund is down -1% (vs. MSCI -
48bps), while the average US-based L/S fund is down -1.2% (vs. S&P +33bps). Performance across EU-based L/S funds has similarly been 
challenged given the group continues to run elevated exposure across US equities – the group has posted losses of -1.2% through 
September, mitigating losses seen in the SXXP -2.2%. The average Asia-based L/S fund has also given back ~66bps MTD (vs. MSCI Asia -
12bps). On a YTD basis, the average global L/S fund is still up +7.6%, capturing roughly half of the upside in the MSCI (+14.1%). The average US-
based L/S fund is up +8.8% YTD, while the average EU-based L/S and Asia-based L/S are up +3.8% and +7.9%, respectively.
