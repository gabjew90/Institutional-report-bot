A peek at the peaks; new highs in PJM & ERCOT
Record-high demand in both PJM and ERCOT... prices only reacted in PJM. PJM
saw its all-time record peak load in early-July during a heat wave across the entire
Northeast and Midwest, topping out at over 168/162 GW gross/net of demand
response (DR). This beat its prior 166 GW record set way back in 2006 and was well
above its 154 GW forecast. Prices followed suit, with realtime prices spiking over
$2,000/MWh on both July 2/3. PJM saw another smaller peak just a few weeks later
in mid-July at 159 GW; prices similarly peaked over $1,000/MWh. Load/prices were
fairly tame the rest of the summer, but forwards remained strong.
In ERCOT, we saw demand top out at 91 GW in late-July, also setting a new all-time
record. This came well above last year's 84 GW peak and this year's forecast of 88
GW (net of DR). Renewables/storage performed well, though, so real-time pricing
peaked in the low-hundreds and never came close to the $5,000/MWh cap. Forwards
have been similarly weak through 2026, particularly in recent weeks following Gov
Abbott's letter calling for a pause in the Batch Zero review process. This weighed
more on 2026/2027 than the outer years, and the ERCOT curve is now in sharp
contango.
Elsewhere, we saw MISO demand spiked up to 125 GW (up 5 GW from last year's
peak, but in-line with forecast). New England, New York and California demand were
all down some from last year. New England was basically in-line with forecast, New
York was ~500 MW below the forecast, and California missed its forecast by a wide
margin of 3.5 GW.
Gas Turbine Orders Remain at Highs. Nigel Coe's Industrials team published the
latest from McCoy (link) – global gas turbine orders in Q2 were 38 GW, up +71%
YoY and the all-time record order level. This implies annualized orders of 135 GW,
based on historical Q1/Q2 seasonality (+34% over 2025's 100 GW orders). Orders
from utility/IPP customers in 2Q were 26 GW, up +58% YoY. Orders from industrial
customers for aeroderivatives/bridge solutions were 10 GW, up +78% YoY.
Sentiment meter. We hold our sentiment meter flat at a "2" following our trip last
week to PJM/CEG (see our takes). The notable update from PJM was that their intent
is for all load with existing PPAs (ie: TLN/AWS and VST/META) will be subject to RBP
cost allocation and IRAS curtailments. This was bothersome, but ultimately remains
subject to FERC weigh-in. Our understanding is that the cost/curtailment risk would
fall on the customer not the IPP, and we expect the deals will remain intact. CEG's
tone, on the other hand, has only continued to improve after bottoming out on their
3/31 business update call. ERCOT forwards were sharply down again last week
(2-5% across the curve), likely pricing in concerns of further delays in new load coming
online from the Batch process / 765 kV buildout. Oncor's two lines being conditionally
approved Friday helped calm some fears, but we're monitoring continued political
pushback.

Gas Turbine Orders Remain at Highs
Our Industrials team, led by Nigel Coe, published the latest from McCoy (link), showing 38 GW of global turbine orders
in 2Q26, up +71% YoY and the highest order level for a single quarter in history. Assuming historical seasonality, with
1H as ~49-50% of the full year total, this points to annualized 2026 orders of ~135 GW, +34% YoY vs. 2025.
2Q orders for utility/IPP customers came in at 26 GW, up +58% YoY. Industrial orders came in at 10 GW in 2Q, up
+78% YoY. This implies annualized orders as up +18% for utility/IPPs and up +75% for industrial. This strong growth
in industrials orders tracks with the rise of bridge power solutions. Utilities/IPPs have historically accounted for a large
majority of the turbine market (71% in 2025), but only accounted for 63% YTD.

Events Calendar / Regulatory Watch 
9/29, 10/12 – FERC order on PJM’s RBP and IRAS/ C&M filings (ER26-3380, ER26-3515). This originated 
with a PJM Board Chair letter (link) in mid-August that implemented a critical issue fast-path process for large 
load additions (link). The goal was to address the development of reliability-focused solutions to ensure 
resource adequacy while connecting large loads. The original proposal looked at adjustments to the load 
utilized in capacity auctions, if such load is not capacity backed. We covered our initial thoughts in the 
Fleishman Daily on 8/12/25 and then further in our PJM/CEG visit takeaways note (link). PJM’s non-capacity 
backed load proposal (NCBL) was subsequently revised (slides) to be voluntary and similar to demand 
response, rather than mandatory. PJM is also focused on improving the accuracy of load forecasting and 
setting up an expedited interconnection process for sponsored generation. Stakeholder feedback was largely 
oppositional, and alternative stakeholder proposals were filed in October, with additional meetings held in 
early November. PJM then hosted a vote on 11/19, with all proposals failing. PJM was planning to review 
stakeholder proposals, comments, and the vote results to develop their own proposal, originally planned by 
YE25. PJM pushed this to January to be after the 2027/2028 auction results on 12/17. As part of their co-
location order on 12/18, however, FERC required PJM to file within 30-days (by 1/19) with a summary of 
proposals made in the CIFP process.  
On 1/15/26, Bloomberg reported that the White House and PJM state governors were going to urge PJM to 
run an emergency procurement auction, with the hyperscalers bidding on 15-year contracts for new 
generation (see our take – report link). We got more details the next day, with a Fact Sheet and a Statement of 
Principles from the White House. The White House / Governors’ proposal is one-time reliability backstop 
auction (RBA) for new build and includes an extension of the collar mechanism at current prices for two more 
auctions. Later in the day, PJM submitted their own proposal (letter), which appears very similar to what 
seemed to be the most agreed upon items: 1) improving load forecasting; 2) voluntary bring your own 
generation (BYOG); 3) curtailment if not BYOG; 4) holistic PJM market review; 5) consideration of the collar 
extension; 6) immediate initiation of an RBA. PJM began the discussion on the proposal for a RBA at their 
Members Committee meeting on 1/22, then held a formal stakeholder process on the implementation and 
submitted a supplemental filing on 2/23 – we discussed our in the Fleishman Daily on 2/24.  
PJM took a 5-week pause from the stakeholder process to develop their own proposal, which they shared on 
4/10 (see our takes –  report). The initial proposal was for a 6-month period of bilateral contracting, followed 
by a central procurement targeting a total 15 GW of new capacity (though highly subject to changes by the 
EDCs). PJM then ran a brief stakeholder process in May featuring initial presentations from 17 stakeholders 
ahead of a planned MC vote on 5/27. On 5/19, however, PJM issued a letter that pushed out their timeline of 
proposal, officially combining the reliability backstop procurement (RBP) critical issue fast path with the 
pending Connect and Manage (C&M) process and moving up the central procurement to September 2026. 
Rather than the previously scheduled MC vote on 5/27, PJM held a stakeholder meeting, presenting their 
updated proposal (see our takes in the May 25 Power Trip – link). Ahead of their Members Committee vote on 
6/30, PJM issued a new proposal that effectively backed off C&M (see our takes in the Fleishman Daily on 
6/22). As expected, though, all C&M proposals subsequently failed to pass the Members Committee vote on 
6/30. For the RBP, only the Data Center Coalition/Joint EDC proposal passed (PJM’s own proposal failed),

12/10 – ERCOT to file Batch Zero eligibility report. ERCOT kicked off a stakeholder engagement process on 
reviewing large loads in batches back in early-2026. They presented their draft framework back on 2/3 (link), 
proposing that large loads be reviewed in batches every six months, rather than the current process of 
individualized review by transmission service providers. We discussed some of our initial views in the 
Fleishman Daily on 2/4. ERCOT presented an update to the PUCT at their 2/20 Open meeting, followed by a 
series of workshops finalizing their proposal. ERCOT Board unanimously approved the proposal for Batch 
Zero on 6/2. The PUCT approved ERCOT’s Batch Zero proposal at their 6/18 Open Meeting. ERCOT was 
planning to notify large loads of their classification (“base load” or “studied load”) by 8/7, but this timeline 
shifted with TX Governor Abbott’s letter on 8/4 calling for a data center verification process. ERCOT 
subsequently paused the Batch Zero process in response. We got a firmer timeline from ERCOT at PUCT’s 
meeting on 8/20. We now expect ERCOT to communicate conditional load classifications (subcategories 
within base and studied load in Batch Zero) to TSPs by 8/31, which is required to begin the verification 
process. ERCOT will then begin to send out Request for Informations to large loads for the data center 
verification. We should get the final Batch Zero eligibility verification report from ERCOT by December 10 
ahead of the PUCT meeting on December 17. The final timeline to conclude the Batch Zero transmission 
studies remain to be seen (expect more information in ~a month). 
Recall that ERCOT had presented 205 GW total eligible for inclusion in Batch Zero (65 GW base, 25 as either 
base/allocated (TBD) and 114 GW as allocated/studied load). Under the original timeline, large loads would 
have had until 8/31 to make any ERCOT-required modifications ahead of ERCOT putting the numbers out on 
9/2. ERCOT was planning to put out their final report including the MW allocation for studied load by 4/9/27. 
FERC Commissioners. The Trump administration named confirmed Commissioner Swett (R) to be Chair on 
10/24. Swett, along with David LaCerte, was confirmed by the full Senate earlier in October 2025. Swett 
previously was counsel at law firm Vinson & Elkins and previously worked at FERC under former Chairman 
McIntyre. FERC now has a 3-2 Republican majority. FERC’s first open meeting with Swett as Chair occurred 
11/20, as she laid out her focus on ensuring that the U.S. can connect and power data centers as quickly as 
possible. Her second meeting on 12/18 was the co-location order. At her third meeting on 1/22, she 
commended PJM on its filing of the capacity auction quadrennial review and encouraged continued consensus 
building in the large load additions CIFP as well. She noted FERC is ready to act quickly. At her fourth meeting 
on 2/19 she continued to keep a “personal tally” of PJM’s multiple market reform timelines and is planning to 
hold all stakeholders accountable. At the sixth meeting on 4/16, Chair Swett noted that she was “perplexed” 
by PJM's proposal, as the White House/PJM Governors' Statement of Principles called for a procurement by 
September 2026, while PJM's proposal targets a central procurement in March 2027. Swett then held a FERC 
Technical Conference on PJM governance on 7/23.

interconnection proposal (RM26-4, EL26-67 – EL26-72). On 10/23, Energy Secretary Chris Wright wrote a 
letter (link) to FERC, urging them to initiate rulemaking procedures on a proposal regarding the 
interconnection of large loads pursuant to the Secretary’s authority under Sector 403 of the DoE Organization

connections. Wright gave FERC a deadline of 4/30/26 for final action on the proposal, but in mid-April, FERC 
issued an intent to act by 6/30. On the surface, accelerating review would only further accelerate the power 
demand part of the equation. We’re watching for more actions on the supply part. As part of this process, 
FERC requested comments on the DoE’s proposed Advanced Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANOPR) to 
expedite large load interconnections. Initial comments were due to FERC by 11/21/25 (there was a 7-day 
extension from the original deadline of 11/14), with response comments due two weeks later on 12/5. We 
covered our thoughts in the Fleishman Daily on 11/24/25. During FERC’s Open Meeting on 6/18, FERC issued 
show cause orders to each of the six RTOs (PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO), directing them to justify 
or reform the tariff provisions that govern large interconnections. The RTOs all requested and received up to 
90-day abeyances ahead of the 8/17 deadline to either justify current tariffs or file tariff changes (by 11/15). 
11/16 – PJM and Transmission Owners compliance filings on FERC order in co-location paper hearing 
(EL25-49, ER26-1088). The original FERC order on this was back in December, which allowed for more 
optionality for co-location. See our note – link. FERC found PJM’s existing tariff unjust/unreasonable on co-
located load arrangements and laid out 3 new options for PJM’s revised tariff to be submitted within 60 days. 
Initial briefs as part of a paper hearing were submitted 2/23/26 and response briefs were due 3/25/26. We 
included our thoughts on response briefs in the Fleishman Daily on 3/27. Both VST and CEG filed significant 
complaints on PJM’s paper hearing, arguing that PJM’s proposal would delay the start of new transmission 
services until 6/1/2029 and effectively create a 3-5 year moratorium on co-located load. PJM submitted its 
reply brief on 4/24, reiterating the proposed in-start date of 6/1/2029. We are now waiting for a FERC action 
in the paper hearing. As part of the December order, PJM also needed to submit several compliance filings 
with proposed tariff edits. PJM submitted its initial tariff reviews on 1/20 (link). On 4/16, FERC issued an order 
on PJM’s first compliance filing. FERC accepted part of the filing but sided with a complaint submitted by CEG 
and rejected PJM’s proposal to replace “point of interconnection” with “point of change in ownership” in the 
definition of co-located load. PJM submitted an updated tariff with an updated definition on 5/18. FERC issued 
an order on 6/18, which accepted in part and rejected in part PJM’s compliance order. In the ongoing paper 
hearing, FERC also directed PJM and Transmission Owners to submit further compliance filings within 60 days 
(by 8/17). VST and CEG submitted requests for clarification, and PJM TOs and EXC/FE both submitted 
requests for rehearings on FERC’s 6/18 Order. PJM and the TOs have filed for 90-day extensions for the filings 
on Contract Demand Services to align the timeline with the filings in the show-cause order, which FERC 
approved on 8/14 (deadline now 11/16). PJM filed their updated proposal for Interim NITS on the original 8/17 
timeline, which moved up the Interim NITS start date to 6/1/2028 (from 6/1/2029).

must have an ELCC >65% and lower emissions than a coal or oil resource. Applications for energy storage

generation), AlphaGen offered a 35 MW uprate at its Keys CCGT, and there was one other confidential bid.

generation. We discussed in the Fleishman Daily on 12/31/25. All-in-all, Maryland well came short of its 3 
GW goal. Maryland has been higher on our list of states that could ultimately look at regulated generation, as 
the state is a big net energy importer, but historically has been adverse to new gas plants/pipelines. Currently, 
the state is also dependent on TLN’s Brandon/Wagner coal/oil plants that are under RMR.
