SEPTEMBER 8, 2026 
  
20 SECONDS:  Tech broadening/Global narrowing. Per aspera ad ASTRA. USDJPY 
& new France probability tree. Global Equity Strategy & thoughts on EU GDP.  
  
•         Asian equities and US/EU futures are lower this morning. USDJPY briefly fell 
below 153, weighing on Japanese exporters (-3%; Autos -5%). Crude extended gains 
as we await details of the Iran-Oman deal. Energy and Rates appeared to matter more 
overnight. 
  
•         Tech broadening/Global narrowing try to play out. 
•         Last Monday, we moved to neutral/tactically cautious on equities as several 
risks linger: terminal G4 central-bank rates, energy prices and crack spreads, 
politics, and July/August’s forced rotation out of Tech. On energy, the odds of a US-
Iran deal continue to fall, while commodity inventories are approaching ‘code 
red’. JPM oil traders see higher prices as the only long-term solution to curb 
demand. 
•         Cheap EU equity index hedges make sense, in vanilla or rates-hybrid 
formats. Let me know if you want to see the EDG desk’s pricing. 


[Page 2]
 
 
 
 
•         Will global-equity dips be bought? Most likely. None of these risks looks large 
enough to end the cycle, while revisions remain solid, GDP growth is accelerating, 
and positioning is light. Kian, for example, sees an ideal backdrop for EU banks: i) 
a 2–3% ECB rate sweet spot, ii) stronger lending, iii) robust capital-markets activity, 
iv) cost discipline, and v) no material deterioration in asset quality. 
  
•         We see scope for AI infrastructure and bottlenecks to rebound, though Tech 
leadership should remain broader and more dispersed than in 1H26. 
•         OpenAI Astra triggered the AI-infrastructure rerating. Its strong cost-per-token 
efficiency on agentic tasks improves the economics, encouraging adoption and 
supporting infrastructure spending. 
•         That said, AGI is being invoked too freely to justify the market reaction. Re-
grossing and its impact on alpha spreads—which we flagged last week—clearly also 
matter.  Per aspera ad Astra aptly captures the P&L dynamic since late June. 
  
•            BoJ risk is showing up more in FX/rates than in Equities. About half of 
the unwind in USDJPY longs seem to have taken place already and technicals 
imply next major resistance at 152. Meanwhile, the upcoming budget might be 
a reminder of the structural issues for yen. 
•            For Equities, the setup is not comparable to 2024 and Rie sees Japanese 
equities absorbing higher rates. Our Derivative Strategists in HK like low 
premium TPX call spreads or TPX-USDJPY hybrids to position for a post-BoJ 
grind higher.

IDEAS & INSIGHTS – IN BRIEF 
•         GLOBAL EQUITY STRATEGY:  For the overall equity market call, we believe that 
robust activity backdrop will prevail, with continued upward earnings momentum – 
continue to call for buying the dip. Team stays with the view that non-US equities 
have a good chance to outperform the US for the second year in a row.  Regional 
earnings differential is narrowing, and another driver of better RoW performance could 
be market broadening 
•         EU PRIVATE CONSUMPTION: The detailed GDP breakdown reinforces the view 
that private spending remained remarkably resilient through the first half of the year. 
Private consumption rose at an average annualised pace of 0.9% in 1H26, with a 
particularly strong 1.6%ar increase in 2Q, well above our expectations. Two factors 
explain this: 1) the hit to household incomes in 1H26 appears to have been much less 
severe than we had anticipated; 2) households appear to have supported spending by 
saving less. 
•         EA GDP: Revised up from 1.8% ar to 2.6%. This revision was entirely driven by 
Ireland, now up 47.4% ar. Details  show a 1.0%-pt lift coming from domestic final 
sales, with household consumption up 1.6% ar, government consumption up 0.6% ar 
and fixed investment flat. Households' purchasing power was hit by higher energy 
prices in 2Q, leaving headline HICP prices running at a near 4.5% ar pace. This hit 
has been smoothed through a lower saving rate.  
•         CATALYSTS TODAY: China Trade. Japan GDP. Canada’s retaliatory tariffs 
scheduled to take effect.

EMERGING MARKETS MONETIZATION – Ellen Wang / Victoria Campos 
The global momentum/tech rebound drove EM 7% higher since the July-end low, 
despite choppy price action. Broadening and AI continue to dominate the narrative in 
EM, with the recent global macro backdrop net supportive. We are bullish on EM 
given (i) improving macro; (ii) weaker USD; (iii) AI CapEx tailwinds, but continue to 
expect divergence in EM performance at the country/sector level and view country-
relative trades as more relevant for the monetization strategy in this environment.  
•         EM MONETIZATION MENU – Key themes and trades include: (i) TACTICALLY 
LONG China H-shares over A-shares: the pair has been trading well in July given 
the momentum rotation. Despite this outperformance, H-shares still lag A-shares YTD 
and we see further room into China Tech earnings next week. (ii) We also express 
this view via a TACTICALLY LONG in China Internet (KWEB). (iii) TACTICALLY 
LONG Taiwan following the Global Tech selloff in July. Taiwan offers a less volatile 
exposure to AI and Asia Tech relative to Korea. 
• HEDGES – (i) We like long LatAm (ILF) as a diversifying play 
against weakness in the EM AI theme and for relative resilience 
against geopolitical risks. (ii) India (INDA) equity strength has been 
driven in part by a rotation during the AI de-risking phase, but foreign
