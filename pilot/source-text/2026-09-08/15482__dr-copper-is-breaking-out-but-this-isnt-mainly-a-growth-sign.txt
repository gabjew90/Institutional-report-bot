Copper enters uncharted territory
Copper is trading into uncharted territory. Record prices, record US inventories and depleted ex-US
stocks have created a classic squeeze, with tariff distortions and short-covering, not industrial demand,
doing much of the near term lifting. Still, it fits Privorotsky’s broader view: in a spending-heavy, late-
cycle economy, commodities can keep working until energy or rates finally break demand. The real
question is what stops the spending, not simply whether the Fed hikes.
LSEG Workspace
Upside - the floor keeps rising
The tariff report is ~2 months overdue. Every week it sits, the front-load bid stays alive. July alone
pulled a record 225k MT into US warehouses; LME warrants are 51% cancelled, 121kt earmarked to
leave London. If the levy is confirmed, the $430/t backwardation seen in mid-August could become the
new normal, hardening the CMX/LME wedge.
Underneath, this is a genuine regional physical squeeze. TC/RC in scarcity pricing (−$200 to −$270/t),
China smelters sub-75%, Chile/Indonesia outages still bleeding supply. Smelters can't just ramp back
on — feedstock's gone. That caps how fast LME restocks.
The less obvious leg is power infrastructure.
The first AI trade was chips, HBM and servers. The next is getting enough power to the data centres.
Hyperscaler capex is heading towards $1.2tn by 2027, while data centres could consume around 9% of
US electricity by 2035. That requires grids, transformers and cabling, all copper-intensive.
UBS identifies grid constraints as the biggest bottleneck for new data-centre capacity. Unlike the tariff
squeeze, this is a multi-year, capex-funded source of demand. Korea’s $1bn Eximbank facility for
Glencore points in the same direction: copper is increasingly being treated as a strategic resource.

Another upside risk
Goldman’s James McGeoch highlights another upside risk. GS already expects mine supply to fall 0.7%
this year, with a modest 260kt refined surplus increasingly dependent on scrap use rising nearly 5%.
That makes any EU restriction on copper scrap exports a potential right-tail event. Europe exports
more than 500kt of copper scrap, mainly to Asia, while US exports exceed 1mt. With mine supply
already shrinking, tighter scrap flows could quickly erase the expected surplus.

Light positioning, tight market
GS puts options positioning at just 3/10 and futures length at 4/10, while managed money has barely
reacted to copper printing fresh highs. Tight scrap supply, contracting Chinese secondary production
and strong US imports leave room for positioning to chase the move.
GS
What breaks the copper trade?
This is still a regional dislocation, not a global shortage. Inventories are adequate but concentrated in
the US, and the marginal bid is driven by tariff expectations. Any delay or softening of Section 232
could unwind the front-loading quickly and return metal to the LME.
China is the bigger fundamental risk. Weak construction, continued property stress or renewed US-
China friction could turn the largest consumer from support into a drag. A stronger dollar, higher real
yields or recovering smelter output would add further pressure.
Net: The structural power-demand story remains intact, but further upside increasingly depends on
policy. Treat Section 232 as the catalyst for either the next leg higher or a sharp unwind.
