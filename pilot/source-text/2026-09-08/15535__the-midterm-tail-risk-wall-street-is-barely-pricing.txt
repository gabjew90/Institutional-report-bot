The midterm setup
We are front-running the midterms, but the market setup is already taking shape. Most outcomes leave
the broader fiscal regime intact, while options are pricing very little election risk.
The conventional gridlock outcome looks modestly risk-on. The real tail is a Democratic sweep, and
the sharper rotation that could follow.
The post-midterm tailwind
Post-midterm seasonality has historically been supportive for equities.

Midterms won't change the fiscal regime
According to BofA's Michael Hartnett, this isn't a Thatcher/Reagan 1980 or Trump/Brexit 2016 regime-
change election. Whoever wins, the bigger 2020s trend remains political populism and fiscal excess.
Right or left, populists spend to stay popular. US nominal GDP has surged 63% in six years, from $20tn
to $32tn. The structural trade remains his Anything But Bonds logic.

Gridlock = Goldilocks
In Hartnett’s framework, a GOP sweep would be the full risk-on outcome: another green light for AI and
a return of US exceptionalism.
But he sees the more conventional GOP Senate + DEM House outcome as modestly risk-on. Less policy
firepower, fewer major legislative surprises and the fiscal regime largely intact.
Gridlock = Goldilocks.

The risk-off election
BofA sees a DEM sweep as the real market tail: higher taxes and regulation would mark a shift from
populist capitalism toward populist socialism, an EPS-negative combination.
Hartnett’s playbook: stocks down more than 10%, lower US yields and a weaker dollar, with
international equities outperforming the US. Preferred hedge: short financials and short USD.
BofA
Election vol? What election vol?
With the midterms two months away and Senate control looking like a coin flip, SPX options are pricing
just a 0.82% post-election move. The VIX curve is showing little event premium either.
History says that may be fair. Midterms rarely generate big index moves, and with leverage coming
down and correlations low, this election could be much more about rotation than outright vol.
Bottom line: don’t overpay for index protection. BofA prefers a VIX calendar call-spread collar, selling
the October 16 put and buying the November 22–30 call spread, to benefit from potential pre-election
vol support. Beneath the index, election vol looks relatively cheap in small caps and regional banks.
