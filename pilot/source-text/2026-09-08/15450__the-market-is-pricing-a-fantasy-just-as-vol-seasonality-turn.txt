Something has to give
The current equilibrium won’t hold, argues Deutsche Bank’s Henry Allen.
Rates markets are still underpricing the inflation pressure ahead, with only modest tightening cycles
priced across central banks. Either inflation rolls over, or rates need to move higher. If it is the latter,
risk assets will eventually have to adjust.
Something has to give, and several asset classes remain poorly prepared for a more aggressive
tightening cycle.
DB
Still underpricing the Fed
Markets are pricing a shallow hiking cycle despite underestimating the Fed’s hawkishness in four of the
past five years.
The ISM services prices-paid index hit a four-year high in August and, based on its relationship with the
last inflation wave, points to CPI above 5%. Meanwhile, Warsh called the 2% target “firm and fixed,”
and strong payrolls give the Fed room to focus on inflation.
Historically, inflation around 4–5% has been consistent with more than 200bp of first-year tightening.
One-and-done hiking cycles are also extremely rare.
The market is pricing a modest adjustment. History points to a cycle.
DB
The ECB disconnect
ECB pricing has barely moved since late July, with around 72bp of hikes priced by June 2027 versus
74bp at the peak. Yet European gas is up more than 18%, growth has surprised to the upside and
inflation expectations have risen, as we discussed recently here.
According to DB, the ECB has historically had a more hawkish reaction function than the Fed, reflecting
its sole focus on inflation. Yet euro volatility also remains relatively subdued despite the renewed surge
in gas.
Gas has repriced the risk. Neither the ECB curve nor euro vol has fully responded.

The curve is still betting on peace
Brent trades at $96, but the six-month contract sits near $83 as the curve continues to price a
reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and lower oil prices ahead.
That expectation has repeatedly been pushed back. If the blockage persists, the back end will
eventually have to adjust higher, removing an important source of support for equities and credit.
The curve is pricing relief. The physical market keeps refusing to deliver it.
DB
Risk assets without a safety net
Risk assets have shrugged off a 70bp rise in US 10-year real yields, supported by resilient global
growth. But the next inflation impulse increasingly looks like a negative supply shock: higher prices
alongside weaker growth.
That is a much tougher backdrop for equities and credit, and the usual policy cushions are limited.
Inflation restricts rate cuts, while high yields and elevated debt constrain fiscal stimulus.
Risk assets remain resilient, but the policy put is much further away.
DB
The vol paradox
Cross-asset pricing remains full of contradictions. Equities have barely moved, volatility has collapsed,
yet rates and inflation risks continue to build.
With autumn getting underway, the seasonal volatility window is beginning to open. The low point in
VIX seasonality is now only days away.
Complacency is high just as the calendar turns less friendly.
