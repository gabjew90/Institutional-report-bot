Let them eat cake (and AI’s Price is Right moment)
Please bear with us as we eventually wind this all back to the arithmetic of power
demand and our broader coverage list.
And let it be said plainly: we believe AI will rewrite the human story. It is a stunning
leap forward.
But awe is no substitute for accounting.
We continue to see imbalances here - financial and societal - sharp edges that demand
a steady hand and a clear eye. Beneath the architecture of the new machine age lie
fractures, maybe froth, and profound structural strains. The system is blinking yellow to
us, perhaps red, with oddities we cannot simply wave away in our enthusiasm.
We are capitalists. We champion technology, and that timeless human hunger to outdo
ourselves, to fashion the new. But we are analysts first, who can be right and can be
wrong. Our perspective on these topics is no different; we are acutely aware of the
aphorism that being early is indistinguishable from being wrong. When the music plays,
we love the dance - but when the floor begins to buckle, our duty is to name the risks,
no matter how bright the lights and no matter the hour.
And - as far as we see it, we are living through a strange era - a peculiar theater of the
absurd that future generations will likely look back on with a laugh. Or a sob. Or maybe
both.
We're the TAM... Let start by "admiring" recent reports that a frontier lab is reportedly
staking out a $30T TAM (Total Addressable Market).
It is a number so formidable - in our opinion - that it instantly evokes Dr. Evil standing
in front of the “United Nations Secret Meeting Room,” pinky pressed to his lip,
demanding $100B.
If these whispers hold true, this company has managed to out-hype even that spacy
company's gravity-defying $26.5T number. Truly, a magnificent Price is Right moment
for the ages. You have to hand it to the architects at the AI “lab” - managing to
seamlessly weave both Dr. Evil and Bob Barker into a single pitch deck.
For those keeping score at home, let us ground this "TAM": U.S. nominal GDP hovers
right around $32T, while the entire economic output of planet Earth - yes, every factory,
power plant, farm, McDonald’s, and Tim Horton’s - sits at roughly $126T.
So, how could the team at this frontier AI "lab" possibly justify that sort of TAM? Well,
we haven’t seen the deck - but here’s a wild guess: you. And me. And all of us. All of
human labor.
Pundits claim we're all going to be replaced. Daily displacement estimates feel like a
twisted auction block - 30%! What about 50%? Do I hear 60%?!

...but we're not so sure if that's right, especially around pace.... But there is a
glaring paradox at the heart of the tech boom: they know code can automate code -
that’s their backyard, we get it. What they drastically underestimate - in our opinion
- is the stubborn friction of the physical; the real world: how rapidly a force capable
of upending the digital realm will inevitably destabilize everything else. When trillions
of dollars hang in the balance, real commitments around data centers and power, we
believe that velocity of spillover is everything. Everything.
Let's say it again, that velocity, that pace is important. Critically. To understand why,
we must look closely at Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s warnings on a podcast from
earlier this year. “I could buy $1 trillion of compute that starts at the end of 2027. If
my revenue is not $1 trillion dollars, if it’s even $800 billion, there’s no force on earth,
there’s no hedge on earth that could stop me from going bankrupt if I buy that much
compute.”
The warning, we surmise, was meant for the ecosystem and to likely highlight
Anthropic's relative conservatism, but the takeaway is unmistakable, at least to us:
at a trillion-dollar scale, timing is existential. If AI revenue lags behind infrastructure
commitments, companies are expected to drown in the capital costs of idle compute.
OpenAI CEO Sam Altman’s recent remarks on a podcast suggest a growing realization
that the pace of AI adoption may be falling short of expectations and running into the
shoreline of human inertia: “…the economy just has so much inertia. People keep doing
the same things they're doing. They keep buying from the same... company. They
keep....wanting to use their tools in the same way. I think it's actually a positive in
many ways. And it's going to make this big transition in front of us go smoother and
slower. And I'm grateful for it. But I think it means we've all been too ambitious on
timelines, even with this incredible technology. I think AI is one of the most incredible
technologies humanity has ever invented. Society and the economy will adapt more
slowly…. My own example of this was after Netflix came out and started shipping DVD's
even before they started streaming, it was amazing to me that people still went to
Blockbuster. It was incredible to me… That is an example that's stuck in my head of...
Force of habit and the way people do things and changing behavior. It's just much
harder than... the tech nerds realize.”
See, AI eats many digital abstractions for breakfast, but it likely hits a brick wall
against atoms, not to mention the messy, institutional complexities of the rest of the
economy.
Yes, certain roles - maybe call center work - are acutely vulnerable due to their
repetitive, digitized, and closed-loop nature. But, rather than overnight replacement,
automation will likely arrive as an "autonomy slider": AIs will likely handle 80% of
routine volume, while human workers transition to supervisors overseeing fleets of AI
agents, handling only the remaining 20% of edge cases.
However, predictions that computer vision and AI would eliminate radiology proved
unfounded to-date. Despite advanced AI image recognition, the profession is thriving
because the role extends far beyond pattern matching - it involves complex patient
care, messy real-world contexts, and multifaceted clinical responsibilities.
...and physical AI needs more than LLMs. Well, what about robots? AI and LLMs
can power robots to replace and augment work. Robots are poised to transform
physical labor: yes, we agree. In many good ways, actually. But, automation and
adoption will likely unfold selectively and at a measured pace - and, again, pace
matters. And while LLMs have provided a powerful catalyst for physical AI, they remain
far from a silver bullet.
Scaling embodied intelligence requires moving well beyond the confines of internet-
scraped text to generate entirely new classes of real-world data. As roboticist Rodney
Brooks highlighted in his blog last year, progress ultimately hinges on capturing novel
sensory modalities - such as high-fidelity tactile feedback - that largely exist completely
outside the domain of LLMs.

In the physical world, also, AI alone isn't enough, in our opinion. Despite many upstart
autonomous trucking vendors - robots on wheels - claiming otherwise, pure AI "end-to-
end" (E2E) neural-net architectures are no panacea. They rely on what Aurora leaders
dubbed the "train and pray" approach - hoping massive statistical regressions will fix
black-box unpredictability.
Aurora chose a smarter path, in our opinion: a hybrid system fusing AI with software
and fundamental engineering. The Aurora Driver combines advanced software, lidar,
radar, cameras, and vehicle-agnostic computing. While machine learning captures
human-like nuance, engineered rules help guarantee safety and control.
Don't just take Aurora's word for it; here's Waymo in a recent blog post that is well
worth the full read.
• "You can’t build trust with a black box. AI is powerful, but it isn't magic. Pure
end-to-end (E2E) neural architectures, where a model takes in raw pixels and
directly outputs steering commands, run the risk of black box failures. That means,
it's hard to understand how decision making happens in full E2E systems. While
Waymo’s system transforms sensors into driving decisions in real time, we’ve added
an independent onboard validation layer. This architectural choice is non-negotiable
for safely scaling at L4. This is a separate, AI-based safety system that monitors
every trajectory proposed by the Waymo Driver. It checks these plans against
hard physics-based constraints and traffic laws by incorporating techniques like
Reinforcement Learning and reasoning inspired by Generative AI. If the AI proposes
a path that violates a limit or risks a collision, the validation layer acts as a hard
backstop."
So, yeah, we think the frontier "lab's" TAM estimate might be..."just a bit
outside" (another Major League pop culture reference for those ancient enough to
remember). Humans still matter.
If they believe it, that's sobering. To be fair, they may actually believe their own
projections. And if they do, that is a sobering thought.
Why? Because as "they" algorithmically plot the displacement of human labor for ends
unknown - our air and our world are worse for the wear. And with Sustainability as our
mandate, we are compelled to call it out.
• Consider the Trump administration's EPA. A proposed rule would dismantle Clean Air
Act public disclosure requirements for data center pollution, allowing nitrogen oxide,
carbon monoxide, and heavy metal emissions to be approved behind closed doors.
The agency calls it "administrative relief" to speed up energy dominance and put
local states in the driver's seat. Really?
• This follows the shadow of Colossus. When Elon Musk’s supercomputer in Memphis
outgrew the electrical grid, xAI simply bypassed it. Just across the state line in
Southaven, Mississippi, they built their own massive, off-grid power plant with
trailer-mounted gas turbines. We believe that framing these trailer-mounted gas
turbines as "temporary" may have served to streamline the regulatory process.
Securing a standard Clean Air Act permit would have invited intense public scrutiny -
and stalled the project for years. "Mississippi environmental regulators and xAI have
argued in court filings that the turbines are exempt because they are “mobile” and
intended to operate onsite for less than a year," according to Reuters.
• In response, the EPA updated its Clean Air Act rules in January 2026, explicitly
closing this loophole and classifying clustered mobile turbines as stationary,
permit-requiring polluters.
• Nevertheless, in March 2026, "the Mississippi Department of Environmental
Quality (MDEQ) has approved the construction of 41 gas turbines at xAI's data
center in Southaven, Mississippi." According to Reuters, 57 off-grid turbines are
now installed.
• The physical footprint is minimal, but not the potential atmospheric toll.
According to Earthjustice, "xAI’s power plant in Southaven has the potential
to emit more than 1,700 tons of nitrogen oxides (NOx) each year. The illegal
turbines also have the potential to release up to 180 tons of fine particulate
matter, 500 tons of carbon monoxide, and 19 tons of formaldehyde — a toxic,
cancer-causing chemical — each year."

So this is the message...: Whether by design or default, the message broadcast by the
AI ecosystem is chilling. And that chill is increasingly being reflected in public polling
around AI: We may dismantle your livelihoods and strain your environment, and you
have no recourse, because this technology is inevitable. Even worse, their massive data
center buildouts are "crowding out demand for Treasuries," driving up interest rates,

effective.
...and this is democracy: Against this messaging fail, a powerful wave of authentic
grassroots resistance is rising. Driven by transparently hollow (or worse) rhetoric, this
organic democratic pushback isn't just a local flashpoint - it is a formidable roadblock
slowing data center deployment nationwide and positioning itself as a defining issue in
the US midterm elections. Again, that pace of deployment is paramount. Axios recently
wrote that "the Senate GOP campaign arm, in a private memo to top AI companies,
warns that toxic views of U.S. data centers are killing the party's chances of holding a
vital seat in Ohio."
Hurray for humanity.
Things are starting to wobble. And hurray for markets. They are starting to sniff this
out.
For more than 2 years, our solitary refrain has been simple: Something’s got to give.
The frantic, unsustainable (in our opinion) sprint of the AI buildout should collide with
unyielding brick walls: power that simply doesn’t exist, moratoria acting as actual
brakes, and a dizzying, circular labyrinth of high-stakes (in our opinion) financing
that appears stacked like a house of cards. Not to mention that the AI “lab” growth
trajectory appears to be wobbling (see our note) with a bullwhip effect looming large.
The skepticism is no longer just a whisper - it’s registering in the credit default swaps
of tech giants - and, obviously, the rates at which they can finance their debt.
• In a recent article titled, "This Is How the A.I. Debt Binge Sinks the Economy", the
New York Times noted: "Already there are a few hints that bond market investors —
who are far more adept at sniffing out potential trouble than the more blithe equity
investors — are getting antsy. Investors are starting to buy insurance to protect
them if the hyperscalers go bankrupt. Based on the current cost of this insurance,
investors still think these A.I. companies are good for the money, but the mere
existence of such insurance for the likes of Alphabet or Microsoft would have been
virtually unthinkable just two years ago."
• In another recent article titled "The multiplying risks of financing data centers", The

• Ok, it's not circular, it's...spherical.
• Whoa, wait? What is this? Last night, after Nvidia's earnings report, the Wall

- a 2-month-old financing initiative offering credit support to cloud providers in

• That's an interesting wrinkle...
So...Why dwell on Nvidia and the markers we’ve laid out? Because they are
foundational. They rewrite the rules of everything we cover, and with trillions in data
center commitments hanging in the balance, the speed of this buildout is everything.
We also watch, and we wonder: Can LLMs alone truly solve the physical AI puzzle?
Nope, in our opinion.
We have worried, we do worry, and we will keep worrying. We look at an AI ecosystem
that is murky, over-levered, power-starved, politically radioactive, with top of funnel
demand fraying at the edges (again, see the traction Chinese models have gained and
our previous note). That has been our path. And today, we believe the reckoning is
closer than it has been at any point in the last few years.
The cracks are showing; dams can break. Gravity always wins in the end. And so do
bullwhips. Something's got to give.
In the near term, for many of our stocks, the headlines may well induce vertigo,
demanding the fortitude to look past periodic turbulence. Yet a panoramic, long-term
view reveals an unyielding reality, in our opinion: the American surge in electricity
demand and the global push for sovereign energy independence are inexorable
forces stretching far beyond the AI ecosystem. Following decades of flatlining below
a 1% CAGR, U.S. power demand is accelerating through a historic inflection driven
by electrification, reindustrialization, and geopolitics - independent of data center
expansion. And our power mix is changing - with nuclear likely destined to play a
significantly more prominent role. This powerful, underlying growth story remains
intact.
Buckle up, buckaroos.
