Rebecca’s On-the-Road Observations + $77 Excess Buy Flow Today 
Greetings from California. Rebecca’s travel = volatility again? Hopefully not for long this time...  Overall, today’s flow Highlights below suggested that we are close to the 
end of the anticipated weakness ahead of the Thursday IPO, unless more cyber attacks and outages keep happening that trigger further Retail Anxiety and Unwind. 
 
One data point captures the current investor mood particularly well: 120+ client DL requests in just the past two weeks, compared with a normal run rate of 50–80 
clients per month. The surge speaks to the unusually high level of investor anxiety and uncertainty in this market.  This came through clearly in my first day of client 
meetings. There were a number of good, recurring questions, so I wanted to share my answers below as a way to put the recent market moves into perspective. 
 
For additional context, I would also point you to two of my recent nots: “11/13 Market Internal Weekly: Special Q&A Edition; Amid News-Triggered Weakness, 
Real-Money Support Strengthened Further” and “10/23 Market Internal Weekly: Trend vs Volatility: Long-Duration Capital vs. Short-Duration Rebalancing” 
 
1) IPO Historical Analog (Charts 1 & 2): In the 11/13 Weekly, I suggested that market could be weighed by IPO this week as SPX fell -5.8% on average during the 
three weeks ahead of Major IPO, partially due to cash raised for purchase. Post-IPO, SPX rallied +9.5% on average the next 3 months, likely because excess cash that 
wasn’t allocated was deployed back to the market subsequently. So far this time, SPX -5.2% over the last three weeks, in-line with history 
2) “Trend vs Volatility” Reminder as explained in the 10/13 Weekly: 
o 
Major Trends have been set by the two largest holders of US Equities – Retail Investors (42%) via both direct trading and self-directed retirement funds and 
Foreign Investors (18%) as they own 60% of US Equities together 
o 
Volatility (around the Trendline) has been dictated by downside feedback loop following headline shocks. This feedback loop was mainly driven by fast 
money accounts including Hedge Funds, Systematic Funds (Risk Control, CTA, Risk Parity), Leveraged ETPs, Short Covering and to some extent, 0dte options 
3) “Volatility” - Washed-out Positioning suggested Bottoming of Current Local Mini-Trend: In yesterday’s Market Internal Insight published by Megan, we 
highlighted the even more washed-out fast money positionings especially the close to 5-year low UBS PB L/S Net Exposure.  Risk Control, CTA and Leveraged ETP 
exposures are also very low @ 2%, 5% and 11%-tile vs 1Y, respectively. This implies even if there is another external shock, an intraday -2% drawdown doesn’t have 
the same quant feedback loop to turn into a -4% drawdown on the same day anymore 
4) “Trend” - Resilient Real Money Flow suggests Upside Trend bias: Prior to today, Retail bought-the-dip in 3 of 3 down days and 7 of last 8 down days, and 
Foreigners bought at over +4x standard deviation.  These two trends suggest uptrend once volatility subsides. However, Retail sold-the-dip modestly today (See 
Highlights Below).  As long as Retail sell-the-dip behavior doesn’t last for more than 2 days, their Upside Implication remains.  
5) “What Trigger Retail Investors to Sell-the-dip?” is the most common question I got today. Based on my analysis of UBS RMM Intraday Flow and Stock Flow Bias 
since 2013, they bought-the-dip about 65-70% of time. Below is a checklist of what I have observed their general behaviors so far: 
o 
NO – Bad Earnings. Good stocks sold off on bad earnings created a discount for their purchases 
o 
NO – Monthly/Quarterly Economic Releases create short-term noises most of the time unless it is a meaningful shift 
o 
MAYBE – FOMC 
o 
YES – Generally, beginning of a new phenomenon that creates Social Anxiety  
o 
YES – Beginning of a New War (not an Update of an Existing One) 
o 
YES – Beginning of Tariff Announcement (not when it is implemented) 
o 
YES – Beginning of Covid when people started working from home without knowing when they could return 
o 
YES – When Bitcoin dropped massively that triggered Margin Call and they need to Sell Equities to fund margin accounts
