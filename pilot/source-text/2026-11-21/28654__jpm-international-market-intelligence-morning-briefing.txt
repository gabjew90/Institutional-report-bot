20 SECONDS: ‘Everything selloff’ as short-lived tech relief drags global equities. 
Spillovers from capex-peak narrative. Souring macro narrative and implications for 
dispersion. Outlook season has arrived. Catalysts. 
  
•         Short-lived Tech relief, with the hoped-for clearing event turning into 
another confidence test. Sea of red this morning, following on from 
yesterday’s sharp reversal in NDX.  
•         Everything selloff – not even defensives spared (India holding on as the 
exception). Client feedback points to continued risk unwind, with limited 
appetite to add exposure here. VIX pop also unhelpful, spiking to levels seen 
in March. 
  
•         AI and Tech have been at the epicentre of the selloff. Expanding China-
export control concerns and growing cyber threats on reports of customer 
data leaks continue to sour sentiment. 

•         Korea and Taiwan worst hit in APAC. Taiwan likely has further to go as 
retail de-leverage process is still underway and it is better held by 
foreigners vs Korea (where de-risking occurred earlier). 
•         US Electricals research sees negative implications from China Tech 
earnings for AI capex-related names after Tencent called for peak $ capex 
in 2027 and Alibaba cash flow should turn positive in 2H28 as spending 
moderates. 
  
•         But the selloff has started to broaden beyond Tech, weighed by the 
macro narrative as risks of US strikes on Iran grow and Fed speak leaning 
increasingly hawkish. 
•         Dispersion has been a key feat of this market, with Tech driving 
everything. In Q3, over 40% of SPX constituents had a negative 3m rolling 
beta to the index. But this is now clearly normalizing (chart below), with our 
tracker breaking below 20% as risk-off extends across the index.  
  
•         Outlook season has arrived! US Econ writes on two very key points – 
capex and wealth effect – both of which are set to moderate next year. 
  
•         Turning to next week, key catalysts incl. Global S&P PMIs; Taiwan IP; 
US PCE; Fed Beige Book; ECB Oct minutes; BOK rate decision.

-       JAPAN LIFERS’ FOREIGN BOND INVESTMENT: Japanese lifers’ FX-denominated 
asset portfolios consist mainly of corporate bonds (48% as of end-March 202W6) 
and “other foreign securities” (27%), while government bonds account for just 16%. 
From an FX perspective, the key question is whether large-scale repatriation would 
involve selling FX-unhedged foreign bonds. We see high hurdles for this; we believe 
that the relative appeal of yen bonds has improved, but this alone is unlikely to 
prompt a major shift from foreign securities into JGBs, and lifers can respond to yen 
appreciation risk by raising hedge ratios rather than selling foreign bonds outright.

INT MKT INTEL MONETIZATION: We are tactically bullish on Global Equities 
• Broadening & International (ex-US) have been leading over the past 
month, benefitting from the ex-AI rotation/diversification needs and 
implications from US midterms (flatter geopolitical curve in 2027 on improved 
prospects for ME and RU/UA & more bearish data center narrative further 
weighing on AI).  
• EU outperformance further supported by strong earnings (~13% median 
YoY EPS growth in Q3, same level as the US) and positioning (relative vs 
US reached extreme lows). 
• Geopolitical resolution does not appear in the cards near term; EU vs US 
positioning now back to neutral levels; earnings catalyst has passed; 
and Tech selloff screening extreme on several metrics (incl. speed of 
unwind, 6 consecutive weeks of HFs selling US Semis; QQQ short interest 
levels, very bearish retail sentiment). 
• The above supports a tactical Tech bounce, led by US/APAC Semis & AI 
thematics. Solid Fundamentals (AI unit economics, scaling laws and token 
growth); Valuations already implying imminent EPS cuts; and absence of 
classic cycle-peak signals, such as inventory builds or shortening lead times 
offer further support,  
• But this bounce is more technical/short-term, with the Tech recovery 
less V-shaped vs H1 as buyside capex expectations showing signs of 
peaking, and the rotation out of AI likely to extend on funding pressures; 
questions over AI ARR trends; and policy uncertainty. Outside of AI, we think 
there are a few themes that may be prove durable.

Report: Weekly Wrap | Is the Semis Sell-Off Over? 
  
Summary: This past week was still choppy, but ultimately felt like a reprieve as 
performance rebounded a bit and de-grossing slowed.  Semis remain choppy 
and HFs were still de-risking the group early this week, but if this were to shift more 
bullishly, then it’s possible that they could rebound in the near-term, given the large 
reduction in positioning (e.g. HF net exposures down 67% from peak globally) and 
sentiment very depressed.  Europe and Japan positioning appear to be going in 
opposite directions, but ultimately this just puts them both closer to the global 
average…i.e., regional skews were reduced further. 
  
Key Points: 
-       Semis saw another heavy week of HF selling. HF net flows in US Semis 
were below -3z for the second week in a row, and HF selling extended to a 
sixth consecutive week—the longest streak on record, with unusually 
large cumulative net selling. Flows varied by region: Japan also saw selling, 
while AxJ was roughly balanced and EMEA Semis were bought. Retail flows 
suggested dip-buying and Tech ETFs saw inflows. 
  

-       Semis positioning has reset materially, though a rebound may require 
stabilization and renewed HF buying. Global HF net exposure has fallen 
67% from its year-to-date high to its current level—lowest since 
September 2025.  The global long/short ratio declined from 4.4x to 2.1x as 
well.  Retail sentiment towards Semis has also weakened to a very low level.  
Looking ahead, with the combined 6m positioning + price z-score near -3z, we 
could see a bounce similar to what happened post the same type of reduction 
in Momentum by late July. However, global Semis exposure remains 
around the 80th percentile versus data since 2018, so positioning is not 
especially low on a long-term basis.  Furthermore, as the 6m z-score of HF 
positioning remains lower than the 6m z-score relative returns, history 
suggests the rebound might not be that robust unless this starts to 
change (i.e. HFs get more bullish). 
  
-       Gross flows and HF performance showed signs of stabilization. After 
near -2z de-grossing on Monday, flows calmed over Tuesday–Thursday, with 
Thursday the most positive global re-gross day in about a month. Multi-
Strat gross flows also appeared to rebound from recent extremes. HF 
performance gained about 1.5% over five days and is now roughly flat 
month to date globally (-0.3% across All Strategies). Europe is up 1.7% 
MTD, while Asia is down 2%. 
  
-       Gross leverage dipped; US positioning remains low while Europe has 
rebounded. HF gross leverage fell 4% week over week, while net leverage 
was broadly unchanged. The US Tactical Positioning Monitor was little 
changed at the 20th percentile since 2015. Europe rebounded more quickly 
from depressed levels alongside outperformance…European flows have 

increased by +1.5z relative to US flows over the past 1–3 months.  This brings 
relative EU positioning (vs. US) back to neutral. 
  
-       Japan was broadly sold, with positioning now below average on a 
combined basis. HF flows were -1.4z WoW and -2.3z MoM.  HFs sold nearly 
all sectors during the week and all 11 sectors were sold over the month. HF 
positioning remains above average on a long-term basis, but combined 
Japan positioning (e.g. HFs, CTAs, margin balances, etc.) has fallen to the 
24th percentile since 2015, near year-to-date lows, with a roughly -2z 4wk 
change.
