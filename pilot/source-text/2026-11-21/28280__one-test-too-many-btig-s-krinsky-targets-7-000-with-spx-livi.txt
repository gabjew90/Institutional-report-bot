 
One Test Too Many: BTIG's Krinsky Targets 7,000 
With SPX "Livin' On A Prayer" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, NOV 18, 2026 - 04:15 AM 
The S&P 500 is now roughly 500 points below its October high. BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky thinks 
it has another 300-400 to go. 
At 10:24am this morning, when BTIG's chief technical strategist pulled his S&P chart, the index 
was still clinging to 7,388, about 40 points above its 200-day moving average. Twenty minutes 
later, it wasn't: 
By the time Krinsky's latest note, "Livin' on a Prayer" (available to pro subs), hit inboxes at 
11:04am, the S&P was around 7,320 and the subtitle had written itself: 
"The SPX made a high of 7844 in early October, and is currently ~7320. So it's off ~400 points, 
and we think there is risk to 7k, or another ~400 points. In other words, 'Whoa, We're Half Way 
There'... it's hard for us to come away with any other conclusion than SPX is also headed for a 
break below its 200 DMA, and at a minimum down to the 7k-7100 zone." 
(By our math, 7,844 to 7,320 is closer to 525 points, which makes Krinsky's "half way" generous 
to the bulls... but we'll let the Bon Jovi reference slide.) 
Put differently, a 7,000 print would leave the S&P roughly 10.8% below its October high, i.e., in 
official correction territory, while the top of his 7,000-7,100 zone sits about 9.5% below the peak. 
Either way, the 200DMA is not the floor. 
Five Tests And You're Out 
Regular readers will recall that last Thursday, with the S&P back above 7,600 on a ~4% bounce, 
Krinsky said it was a gift to sell. As we put it in "Well, Here We Are", his "Overhead Supply" note 
(available here for pro subs) argued that getting above the downtrend line near 7,700 "would be 
an impressive statement by the bulls, but we suspect it stalls below that and turns lower again." 
 

 
It did exactly that: from roughly 7,632 when that chart was pulled to ~7,320 today is a ~4% 
round trip in three sessions. Which brings us to the problem Krinsky flags this morning: the S&P 
is now testing the 7,250-7,350 support zone for the fifth time since early June. 
"Last Thursday we said, 'we suspect it (SPX) stalls below that and turns lower again.' It has 
done that, and now with SPX once again testing the top of 7250-7350 support zone, we suspect 
its one too many tests and we see a break below the 200 DMA towards the 7k-7100 zone." 
The logic is as old as charting itself: "the more times you test a given level, whether support or 
resistance, the more likely that level is to break." Every bounce off the same shelf uses up a 
little more of the buyers who defended it, and by the fifth visit there aren't many left. You can see 
the two dashed lines in Krinsky's chart: the 7,350 shelf the S&P has leaned on since June, and 
the 7,000 line below it where the index topped out last winter before the April breakout. 
 
Everybody Else Is Already Below The Line 
Perhaps the bigger tell is that the S&P was the last one to the party. As Krinsky lists it, small 
caps, mid caps, the Nasdaq, semis, industrials and transports (among others) were already 
below their 200DMAs before the index followed them this morning. Breadth - or lack thereof - is 
where it shows most clearly. 
Start with the Russell 2000 (IWM), which closed at $271.51 on BTIG's chart, about 2.9% below 
its 200DMA, on its third test of support since October: 
 

 
"Third test of support for IWM. This isn't what a bottom looks like having just spent several 
weeks churning around its 200 DMA." 
 
Which is awkward for whoever was buying. As we noted on Monday in "Bounce Or Bull?", 
Goldman's futures desk spotted Russell 2000 funding richen by 13bps on Nov 11 (more than 
twice the typical daily move), a sign that retail and the "small fry" were levering up in small caps 
into the worst front-end selloff since 2024. The big institutions, whose Russell longs have 
tracked SOFR pricing all year, were going the other way. So far, the institutions are winning. 
Mid caps (MDY) have held up a little better, sitting about 1.2% below their 200DMA and still 
above the May lows near $650, but Krinsky isn't fooled: 
"Mid-Caps (MDY) have held up a bit better, but same story this doesn't look like a bottom." 
 

 
 
Nasdaq And Software: The Bounce That Wasn't 
The Nasdaq Composite is in worse shape. At 24,407 on BTIG's chart it was nearly 3% below its 
200DMA, and back at the same July lows that held twice before: 
"Nasdaq (CCMP) now third test of support which held in July and again last week, but firmly 
below its 200 DMA." 
 
Three tests of the same level for the Nasdaq, three for the Russell, five for the S&P. Spot the 
pattern. 
 

 
Then there's software, which for a few weeks this fall looked like the one corner of tech that 
could live without the AI trade. Not anymore. The iShares software ETF (IGV) just lost both the 
100 line it reclaimed in August and the rising trendline off its April lows, falling 3.4% on the day 
to $95.60, now barely 1.4% above its 200DMA: 
"Software finally had some momentum going in the fall, but that too has failed and IGV now 
back near its 200 DMA." 
 
Apple: The Last Safe Haven Standing? 
Which leaves Apple, the stock that has been tech's flight-to-safety trade all year. In Sunday's 
"Boring is Beautiful" note (available here for pro subs), which we covered in "Boring Is Beautiful: 
BTIG Warns The Last Time This Happened Was 2000", Krinsky flagged that AAPL hit a 
52-week high on back-to-back days and closed more than 1% below that high on each, a 
classic sign of upside exhaustion. The history isn't pretty: 20-day returns were negative 89% of 
the time, with an average loss of 12.57% and a median of 8.97%. 
Today he doubles down: 
"At the end of the day, Apple (AAPL, Not Rated) is still a cyclical tech company. Yes it's not as 
exposed to the AI theme, and it's more defensive than semis, but the signal we discussed on 
Sunday shows a high likelihood AAPL trades materially lower over the coming weeks." 
 

 
 
Some napkin math: AAPL was at $351.64 on BTIG's chart, about 16% above its 200DMA at 
~$302. An average 12.57% drop from there lands at roughly $307, almost exactly on the 
200DMA. In other words, the signal doesn't need anything dramatic, just Apple catching down to 
everything else. 
For a counterpoint, look no further than Morgan Stanley, which just went max overweight stocks 
with an 8,700 S&P target for 2027 on the logic that "risk assets having cheapened, we embrace 
risk." Then again, the bank's bear case of 6,000 is a full 1,000 points below Krinsky's target... 
which tells you how wide the range of outcomes has become. 
Oversold Is A State Of Mind 
The part of the note that should sting the "buy the dip" crowd most is Krinsky's dismissal of the 
oversold argument: 
"We continue to see too many views that we need a bounce or we are oversold. That might 
have been the case on November 5th, but the market used up its oversold conditions and is 
now in a much weaker spot." 
Recall that on Nov 5, when the semis index puked 12% and every S&P sector closed red, 
Krinsky called it "early signs of capitulation, but far from a full washout". The bounce that 
followed burned off the oversold readings without repairing a single chart. Now the index is back 
at the lows with no cushion, the dollar at 18-month highs, the 2-year pricing a Fed openly 
debating 50bps on Dec 9, Nvidia's first earnings without Jensen days away, and a growing list of 
 

 
critical systems going dark (Microsoft 365, the FAA, banks and, as of this morning, the NSA's 
classified network). Diversification at its finest. 
Could the fifth test hold like the first four? Maybe. But with the S&P already through the 
200DMA, the generals finally joining the soldiers below the line, and the last "safe" tech name 
flashing an exhaustion signal, we side with Krinsky: 7,000 is the more likely destination, and the 
bulls who waited for a better entry may get one - just not from the level they hoped.
