DeskTalk - Markets drop, chop and stabilize
By Varshi Karamsetty
Pretty sharp intraday moves we are seeing at the NY mid morning - IPO and single-stock noise weighed on tech, while
weakness across EMFX and gold points to a broader capitulation and de-risking. We do see limited conviction, and, as
always, there are tentative signs of stabilization into the European close.
The sudden turn in US equities suggested IPO jitters, with our D1 desk highlighting their IPO Funding basket (CGUSAFND
Index) outperforming Nasdaq, up 2% vs 0.3% respectively. Investors are selling AI winners to fund new issuance, with AMD
headlines adding to the noise. Both AMD and Nasdaq are stabilizing at print, with latter finding support towards 27,000. Semis
are unsurprisingly the most active sector according to our cash desk, while our Cybersecurity basket, CGTSCYBR Index, up
5% intraday.
No rest for gold with XAU down -1.2%, which in turn is weighing on the commodity complex and ZAR, once again among the
worst performers. Our sales desk noted that recent gold selloffs have seen a sticky ownership base, suggesting that investors
may have been willing to tolerate larger growth drawdowns. However, should broader risk assets come under broader
pressure, ETF holdings could become a meaningful source of supply. They like expressing this risk via knock-out structures.
As for broader USD, our trader agrees that there is no obvious reason behind the retracement but does remark that it is a
very low conviction market. Our eTrading desk note elevated FX volumes, highlighting USDCNH, where interbank volumes
were ~500% higher than historical averages. USDBRL is back up 0.6% after brief knee-jerk post BCB intervention. Liquidity is
thin heading into local holiday tomorrow, but our trader does think that intervention should stabilize price action for now. 
Bearish winds in rates markets, unable to rally despite risk off sentiment. Oil prices remain ~1% higher as we stay tuned to
more headlines. Axios reported that President Trump is still considering a joint attack with Israel, but strikes are not imminent.
Meanwhile Iran's IRGC said that there would be no nuclear talks until Hormuz is settled.
