 
The Stock Market Is The Economy Now: UBS Finds 
Equity Wealth Effect Running At Twice Dot-Com 
Peak 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, NOV 19, 2026 - 05:00 PM 
For decades, economists forecast the economy first and let the equity strategists figure out what 
stocks would do about it. 
Well, at UBS at least, that order has now been reversed. In his latest Global Macro Chart of the 
Day (available to pro subs), UBS chief economist Arend Kapteyn explains how the sequencing 
has flipped: 
The US equity market is increasingly a driver of our macroeconomic forecasts, not just an 
outcome of them. We used to model the economy first, hand the scenario to the equity 
strategists, then feed the market impact back into the outlook and repeat. Now we often start by 
asking how equities would react to a shock such as an oil spike, tariffs or AI disappointment, 
because that reaction alone can swing consumption. US holdings of equities and mutual funds 
are close to 200% of GDP, up from 108% just before the dot- com bust (excluding another 110% 
of GDP in pension entitlements). Every 10% move in the market therefore shifts household 
wealth by 20% of GDP, so even a small propensity to consume out of wealth adds up. 
In other words, the tail is now wagging the dog, something that regular readers will hardly find 
shocking: back in September we noted that the wealth effect is the only consumer left. 
200% Of GDP In Stocks 
The reason is simple arithmetic. US household holdings of equities and mutual funds are now 
close to 200% of GDP, up from 108% just before the dot-com bust (and that's before another 
110% of GDP sitting in pension entitlements). Which means that every 10% move in the market 
shifts household wealth by roughly 20% of GDP. 
 

 
Apply even a modest propensity to spend out of that wealth and it adds up fast. Using the Fed's 
Distributional Financial Accounts, UBS assumes households spend around 6 cents of every 
extra dollar of housing wealth (the literature's range is 2-9 cents) and about 3 cents of every 
extra dollar of equity wealth (a classic survey puts it at 2-5 cents, and a recent American 
Economic Review study at 3.2 cents). 
The usual caveats apply: lower-income households spend far more of their gains (and own far 
fewer stocks), and the shift into 401(k)s means more of that wealth is locked away until 
retirement. But the direction is unmistakable. 
From Housing To Equities 
Run those propensities through four-quarter changes in household wealth and, excluding the 
pandemic whiplash, the US wealth effect is now the largest since before the financial crisis. 
The difference is where it comes from. Back then, housing wealth climbed by nearly 70% of 
GDP over nine years. Equity and mutual fund holdings have done the same since Q1 2023 
alone. 
 
The result: at 0.9pp of GDP over the past four quarters, the equity wealth effect is almost 3x its 
10-year pre-pandemic average and twice its level on the eve of the dot-com bust. Nothing says 
 

 
"healthy, diversified growth" like an economy whose biggest tailwind is benchmarked against 
March 2000. 
What Goes Up... 
And here's the catch, which UBS leaves for the reader. The same math works in reverse, and 
with household equity exposure at 200% of GDP the math is roughly twice as brutal as it was in 
2000 during a downturn. 
Some napkin math (approximate, and assuming UBS's 3% propensity holds on the way down): 
a 10% drawdown erases roughly 20% of GDP in household wealth, or around 0.6% of GDP in 
potential consumption. A garden-variety 20% bear market makes that about 1.2%, which is not 
far off the entire wealth effect UBS calculates today, and enough to tip a slowing economy into a 
recession. Considering how much of that equity wealth is tied up in a handful of AI names, an 
"AI disappointment" (one of the shocks UBS now runs through the equity market first) would 
probably do the job just fine. 
Which, maybe, is the real reason the Fed (and the White House) keeps such a close eye on the 
S&P: when the stock market (which as we know is now just a handful of AI stocks) is the 
economy, every correction doubles as a recession scare, and every crash in Nvidia or any failed 
Anthropic IPO, is a national emergency. Literally.
