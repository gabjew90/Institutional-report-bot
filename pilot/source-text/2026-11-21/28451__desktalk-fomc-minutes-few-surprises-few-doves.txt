DeskTalk - FOMC minutes: Few surprises, few doves
By Preston Pagli
The October FOMC meeting minutes delivered few surprises, though they did reveal that a "couple" of participants
believed an increase of more than 25bp in December could be warranted if incoming inflation data proved unfavorable.
This comes as Fed Governor Waller, ahead of the hotter than expected October CPI print (Nov 10) said he would favor a
50bp hike in December, and reiterated that view again today. In contrast, NY Fed President Williams pushed back on the
idea, arguing the current situation does not warrant such a move, with Cleveland Fed's Hammack also preferring tightening in
"smaller increments". 
Markets interpreted the minutes as modestly hawkish. Front-end UST yields rose roughly 2bps, while the DXY pushed higher,
to 103.84. Equities also faded with SPX paring minor gains to trade -0.2%. Traders have slightly increased their bets of a Jan
hike with OIS jumping from 70% to 73%. 
Our US economists continue to expect the Fed to remain highly responsive to inflation data. However, with no additional CPI
release before the December 9 FOMC decision, Fed commentary is likely to remain under scrutiny until the blackout (Nov
28). At the same time, a sustained equity market selloff could eventually temper the Committee's willingness to extend the
hiking cycle beyond December. Our economists are currently tracking Core PCE at +0.27%, which is due Nov 25.
