The rally everybody expects
Nomura's McElligott sees the ingredients for a mechanical relief rally as this week's major risk events
clear. A volatility reset could force dealer buying, while systematic strategies may add exposure as
markets recover. The problem is that everybody seems to be expecting the same squeeze.
Meanwhile, SPX is breaking key support levels and volatility is surging. If equities cannot rally despite
these potentially supportive flows, that would be a much more troubling signal.

The next systematic squeeze
Nomura sees meaningful CTA buying triggers clustered around 2% and 4% above Wednesday's closing
levels.
A 2% rally across global equities would trigger an estimated $7.9bn of buying, rising to $21.1bn in the
4% upside scenario.
There is plenty of potential mechanical buying if markets can get moving higher. The problem for now,
though, is that the market has made a massive reversal from where futures traded earlier in the day.
Nomura
The volatility reset trade
Nomura highlights an important dealer dynamic into next week. Much of the gamma expiring this week
is actually long gamma, leaving dealers net short gamma as longer-dated customer puts remain in
place.
If volatility and skew retreat after this week's event risk clears, dealers could be forced to buy back
short futures hedges.
A volatility reset could therefore generate mechanical equity buying, even without any improvement in
the fundamental picture. For now, the VIX is continuing to move aggressively higher.

Tech correlation at extremes
Nomura's implied correlation measure for XLK has surged to 46.4%, putting it in the 99th percentile of
its historical range.
Tech stocks are increasingly trading together as the sector unwinds. McElligott sees potential for a
return to volatility dispersion if index volatility retreats and individual stocks start moving more
independently.
We would add that when implied correlations reach these extremes, something is usually wrong
beneath the surface.
