The breakdown is here
SPX is breaking key support as volatility surges. The market is looking increasingly ugly, just as
everybody seems to be waiting for the same squeeze.
Meanwhile, earnings risks are building, inflation remains sticky, and some extraordinary divergences
are emerging. From Apple's extreme outperformance to the brutal unwind in emerging markets and
hedge fund positioning, there is plenty to unpack.

The rally everybody expects
Nomura's McElligott sees the ingredients for a mechanical relief rally as this week's major risk events
clear. A volatility reset could force dealer buying, while systematic strategies may add exposure as
markets recover. The problem is that everybody seems to be expecting the same squeeze.
Meanwhile, SPX is breaking key support levels and volatility is surging. If equities cannot rally despite
these potentially supportive flows, that would be a much more troubling signal.

Extremely rare divergence
BTIG highlights just how extreme Apple's outperformance has become. On November 17, AAPL closed
just 1.2% below its 52-week high while SPX was trading below its 200-day moving average.
Over the past 20 years, this combination has occurred on only 14 trading days, clustered in three
periods: 2007, 2010 and 2011. More on Apple here.

BTIG/Bloomberg
Apple: Expensive or not?
According to GS's early-November snapshot, Apple traded at around 35x forward earnings versus 19x
for NDX, a historically extreme premium.
The FCF comparison tells a different story. Apple's FCF yield was slightly above NDX's, with the relative
spread broadly in line with historical trends.

The puke
Fundamental long/short funds continue cutting exposure. Gross leverage has fallen 4.4 points so far in
November to 206.3%, putting it at the 23rd percentile over the past year. Net leverage has dropped an
even sharper 7.6 points to 41.6%, the lowest level in five years.
GS
Tech liquidation at extremes
Hedge funds continue to aggressively dump global tech. On a trailing six-week basis, selling has been
by far the most aggressive in a decade, with a staggering -4.4 z-score. The global tech long/short ratio
has collapsed from a YTD peak of 2.9 to just 1.79, approaching 10-year lows.
