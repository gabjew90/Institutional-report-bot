Taiwan trouble
A month ago (here), we flagged the growing risks in Taiwan, highlighting crowded AI positioning,
accelerating foreign outflows and stretched divergences versus Korea and semiconductors.
Fundamentals remained strong, but expectations were extreme, leaving Taiwan vulnerable to a sharp
catch-down.
Fast forward, and EWT has crashed 34% from its all-time highs, trading well below the 200-day moving
average. The technical damage is massive, foreign selling continues, and positioning has been
slashed. We aren't ready to catch falling knives just yet, but some of these extremes are becoming
hard to ignore.

The 10X Put

The $99 billion exodus
Foreign investors continue dumping Taiwan. Another $6bn left the market last week, taking cumulative
foreign net selling to a massive $99bn YTD, according to Goldman. Of that, an extraordinary $75bn has
come from TSMC alone.
The selling continues
Hedge funds have been aggressively reducing Taiwan exposure MTD, driven by both long liquidation
and fresh shorts. Net allocation has dropped to 4.4%, the 24th percentile over the past year, but still
the 85th percentile over five years. Gross allocation has also declined to 3.8%, remaining at the 89th
percentile over five years.

Hated
Taiwan is now the biggest underweight among both Asian and global EM active funds. According to
Goldman, Asian funds are 530bps underweight Taiwan, while EM funds are 425bps underweight. Both
groups reduced exposure further in October. Taiwan has become the market nobody wants to own.

The seasonal trade isn't working
Taiwan has historically enjoyed a strong seasonal run through Q4 and into Chinese New Year. Not this
time. The market is diverging sharply from its usual seasonal pattern as the AI unwind continues.
