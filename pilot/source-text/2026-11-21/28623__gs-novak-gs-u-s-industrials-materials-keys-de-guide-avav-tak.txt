trough in fiscal 2026, the 25%+ rebound embedded in consensus appears realistic at best and aggressive at
worst.

From GIR – Noah out with takeaways from AVAV dinner – full details here:
Backlog. Post F1Q, AVAV stated it had visibility into 86% of its full-year revenue guidance. Since then,
the company has announced 1) a ~$315mn award in the Autonomous Systems segment under a $940mn

IDIQ contract, with the $315mn funded portion expected to convert to revenue within 12-18 months; and
2) a $405mn order from a Middle East customer for LOCUST X3, expected to convert to revenue within
12-24 months. Those monthly dollar run rates would imply the year is now close to 100% sold on the prior
86% plus those two larger new contracts, and AVAV will win more new business in the year. F2Q book-to-
bill should also be strong as a result.
Budget Environment. The company sees a continued strong funding environment for its product verticals.
Management is not particularly concerned with a short continuing resolution given the amount of backlog
in hand and few new starts relied upon; and it is not expecting additional large reconciliation dollars in the
final FY2027 budget. The final FY27 base will likely be higher than FY26, which had grown substantially
itself. AVAV sees multiple years of authorization growth still to be converted to outlays, that includes
substantial increases in funding in its product areas. Unmanned, counter-drone, and space remain high on
the pentagon priority list; while also fielding substantial international demand.
Margins. At its 2026 Investor Day, AVAV highlighted a financial target EBITDA margin expansion from
14.5% (FY26) to 18-20% (FY30). AVAV expects margin expansion to come in part from a higher mix of
international sales and fixed price contracts. The company also sees additional opportunities to reduce
costs through in-sourcing. Capacity expansions will allow AVAV to bring certain manufacturing
components back in-house, lowering costs and improving margins.
