Asia Open - Trendless
By Preston Pagli
Risk aversion intensified as investors continued to unwind crowded positioning and reassess the broader macro
backdrop following Anthropic's record shattering IPO. SPX (-2.02%) declined for a sixth consecutive session while vol
climbed sharply with seemingly nowhere to hide with 11/11 sectors weaker. USTs caught a flight-to-quality bid while the
dollar remained well-supported at EM's expense. President Trump finished up his "great and consequential" trip to China,
with a follow-up news conference scheduled for Friday at 17:00 GMT. In commodities, gold (-3%) weakened to a 15-
month low against the hawkish backdrop while crude firmed amid continued Iranian attacks on VLCC's in the SoH.

In Focus‌
Equities: SPX fell for a sixth consecutive losing session, its longest streak since April 2024. Nvidia's blowout earnings failed
to become the clearing event markets had hoped for while sentiment deteriorated further after another potential "clearing
event" in the Anthropic IPO, debuting 34% above issue price before reversing sharply. All 11 sectors fell while spot VIX surged
past 28. Flows on the cash desk were skewed toward buying of Healthcare, Industrials and Energy and selling of Info Tech,
Financials, Consumer Discretionary, Comm Services, and Consumer Staples. SPX again closed under the 200dma but also
below the key support trend line from Liberation day low at $7,245 (ref $7,175). Our equity trading strategy desk outlined in
their most recent note, as earnings season comes to a close, the next leg of idiosyncratic catalysts will feature a number of
year-end investor days, with Q4 typically a big quarter for these events.
Rates: USTs bull steepened in a flight-to-quality bid amid the broader equity sell-off a day after the Oct Fed meeting minutes
suggested growing concerns around shifting AI sentiment. Our economists wrote yesterday "We do not think officials will
pursue aggressive rate hikes if sentiment materially worsens and financial conditions tighten substantially". Jan OIS pulled
back to 67% from 71% on the AI unwind.
US Treasury Auction Recap: $19 billion of 10-year TIPS. The auction stopped on the screws to yield 2.903%. The bid/cover
ratio came in at 2.43 which is at the 73rd percentile of the past 12 auctions.
FX: The dollar gained against a hawkish backdrop with DXY trading 103.94. The most notable weakness was within EM and
specifically LatAm, coming under stress against mounting headwinds with BRL, MXN, and the COP the clear laggards. EUR
proved resilient comparatively with the desk seeing buying and GBP selling as cable weakness continued following
Wednesday's services inflation miss. USDJPY traded just shy of the closely watched 160 handle. Volumes on our eFX trading

desk were approximately 10% above average in the period from 12:00 GMT to 19:00 GMT. Net aggregate flows during that
time frame were shifted towards buying of CNH and HUF and selling of MXN and ZAR.
Commodities: Crude (+0.70%) crept up against an increasingly complex macro backdrop with Iran's attacks on tankers in
the SoH multiplying and President Trump saying he is no rush on escalation, and likely to defer any additional action until the
new year. Gold weakened for a fourth consecutive session, hitting its lowest level in over a year as a hawkish Fed continues
to weigh on the bullion.
Elsewhere‌
BRL: The BCB stepped into the market via a spot USD auction after USDBRL climbed to 5.927, with the central bank selling
$1bn at 5.86 across five dealers. USBRL initially retreated to 5.858 before a broader risk-off move saw the pair retrace
entirely. While the BCB's intervention has partially stabilized price action for now, our trader maintains an offsides position
until Q1 2027, when seasonal weaknesses subdues.

Client Call at 02:00 GMT: Citi 30: What's New from Citi's 2026 China Conference - China Technology
GBP: At 07:00 GMT public finances data and retail sales prints simultaneously. Bloomberg consensus expects a pickup in
sales with headline printing at 1.7% YoY vs 0.8% prior, core sales at 1.8% YoY vs 0.8% prior.
AUD: RBA MPB's Fry-McKibbin-Appearance (00:15 GMT).
EUR: ECB Cipollone speaks in Singapore (03:10 GMT) while President Lagarde and Nagel feature at the Euro Finance Week
at 08:15 and 13:00 GMT respectively. Also keep an eye out for Eurozone negotiated wages for Q3 (10:00 GMT), where an
upward print could add fuel to the Dec debate, our economists expect a slight uptick to 2.5%. Eurozone Consumer
Confidence at 15:00 GMT for November, with our economists expecting -17.5.
CNY: 1y/5y LPR decisions at 01:00 GMT. Citi and consensus expect unchanged rates at 3.00% and the 5-year at 3.50%. Our
economists view an LPR cut in November as not plausible after the PBoC discussed shifting loan pricing benchmarks away
from the LPR. We expect the PBoC to stay on hold for the rest of 2026 followed by two 10bp cuts in 2027E.
LKR: CBSL Overnight Policy Rate Decision (02:00 GMT). Our economists expect the CBSL to keep the overnight policy rate
on hold at 8.75%. Citi sees a higher-than-expected hurdle for further hikes after the pre-emptive 100bps May increase, with
material FX pressure and a spike in credit growth the two metrics that could prompt tightening, but the LKR has been stable,
Brent prices have fallen and credit growth is easing.
MYR: Trade Balance MYR for October (04:00 GMT. Our economists forecast a trade balance of MYR 35.67bn versus a prior
MYR 33.72bn, with export growth at 44.6% YoY (prior 54.50%) and import growth at 40.0% (prior 52.70%).
