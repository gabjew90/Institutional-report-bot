JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE
We are seeing a healthy rebound this morning, led by Mag 7 and broader tech. In addition to the capital market
headlines, PANW’s strong earnings are adding to the momentum, with results pointing to resilient
cybersecurity demand. Fed pricing was little changed from yesterday following Waller’s reiteration of the 50bp
point, though the recent repricing for December 2026 and the Q1 2027 meetings has been hawkish. Today we
will have another busy Fed calendar (Hammack, Goolsbee, Barr, Barkin and Paulson), which should help

clarify the path from here. We still see a 50bp move in December as unlikely. For the equity bull case, the
bigger question is how the market prices the Fed path through 2027.

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the November 16th Morning Briefing
•  BULL CASE – (i) NVDA Earnings – reiterating capex and support for existing financing deals is critical,
as well as clarity on future leadership given a movement to return Huang to the company. Given the speed
with which Huang was ousted leads some to believe there are more disclosures coming, which that
company would need to address. Our Equity Derivatives team notes NVDA flows have shifted from
downside protection to playing for earnings surprises, with little fear of another downdraft (ii) Capital
Markets – this week’s events need to trade well to improve conviction on both broader equity markets and
on the potential wave of ECM / DCM / M&A deals which are expected to accelerate before the next
Congress is seated on Jan 3. (iii) AI / Tech Trade Returns – our JPM APAC Tech Conference kicks off
in HK with ~250 firms presenting including Samsung, SK Hynix, and TSMC. The stabilization / move
higher is likely to be led by HFs who have been sellers for 6 consecutive weeks, a Positioning Intel
record. (iv) Macro Data – the major release this week is Retail Sales where another slowdown is expected
with the risk that a downside print fuels the Recession narrative. Our recession basket (JPRECES Index) is
+15.8% QTD vs SPX -1.2%. An inline or stronger print would help shift flows back towards Beta /
Cyclicals supporting markets.
•  BEAR CASE – (i) AI / Tech Take Another Leg Lower – this could be a combination of NVDA failing to
reassure on AI ecosystem financing, a delay to expected capital markets events, or perhaps negative pre-
announcements are our APAC Tech Conf. Irrespective of the genesis, sentiment remains fragile and
returns on precarious footing. (ii) A Decline in the Incremental Buyer – we have seen the Retail investor
step in to buy dips and buybacks tend to accelerate in up-markets rather than down-markets, should the
first domino be another downdraft surrounding AI / Tech, then we may see those buyers save their dry
powder for 2027. (iii) Hawkish Fed Narrative – while last week showed Equity resilience from higher
yields / bond vol, one should not expect that to be the norm going forward. A hotter Retail Sales print plus
continued hawkish Fedspeak, perhaps seeing multiple members discussing the potential for 50bp; there are
17x Fedspeakers this week. Should the market believe that 50bp is necessary then it seems unlikely that
hiking cycle is as shallow as the current pricing of 4x hikes (Dec inclusive). (iv) Positioning Purge –
aggregate US positioning is ~20%-ile in the Tactical Positioning Monitor, dating to 2015; and global
Semis exposure is 80%-ile, so there is room for another downdraft. Despite the recent price action, there
has not be the definitive positioning purge that has triggered a consensus buy-signal.
•  US MKT INTEL – We are Tactically Bullish. Adding to the bullish bullets from above, we think
positioning is cleaner and thus entry points are more attractive. While the AI trade has given the market
more to think about, demand is insatiable but the pace of the build out may be slower, which is not
necessarily a bad thing as the commercial adoption rate and productivity gains increase. Assuming this
week’s events unfold in bullish fashion, we look at inflation as being the primary risk but view that as
more of a problem for early 2027 as we think it is highly unlikely that the Fed moves to hike by 50bp in
Dec, which would create a tactical headwind to the Equity bull case.

•  MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 16) – The Anti-AI theme / ex-Tech rotation has been dominant in
26H2, even more so over the last month as Energy gains have flipped, the recession narrative has emerged,
and investors have positioned more defensively. We think moving back toward Tech makes sense and like
the cautious approach detailed in the previous Monetization Menu with primary exposure to Mag7 and
then using options to play a rebound in the AI themes. It seems most likely that the market will be patient
with seeing how the catalysts play out rather than getting ahead of those events. Look for earnings, capital
markets, and the JPM APAC Tech Conf to be a positive catalysts. In terms of ex-Tech plays, we prefer
Cyclicals over Defensives as the growth narrative reboots and markets digest an incrementally more
hawkish Fed. Recall, SPX earnings are more tied to nominal GDP growth than to real GDP growth
meaning increases in inflation are not a bad thing for earnings and that profit margins near record levels
suggest that pass-through is not a problem in the near-term. We continue to like Europe for some Cyclical /
Value exposure given relative positioning and valuation, though another surge in the USD may temper
tactical outperformance.
•  OLD MONETIZATION MENU (NOV 11) – We agree with Dubravko’s view: The Tech washout
appears to be at/near its completion though there remains some risk of another Semis decline, we like
owning broad-based Tech plays and would consider US Memory and Semis plays via options. CRWV,
OpenAI Q3 revenue, capital markets reopening are all supportive of a return to the AI theme; but the
moves may remain choppy as markets consider another shift higher in bond yields. The barbell trade (Tech
+ Cyclicals) makes sense but think you can get more upside from playing Cyclicals via European
names/sectors over their US counterparts due to relatively lower positioning and valuation.

•  TRADE IDEAS – (i) Long 12M Momentum vs. Short 3M Momentum – which is essentially Tech vs.
Energy / Defensives; (ii) Low Vol is less compelling – after moving form 0%ile to 25%ile, the trade is
less attractive as the factor will need to see an increase in recession risk to maintain its outperformance. If
you want to stick with Low Vol, he sees the best opportunity in Healthcare followed by Utilities, and then
staples; (iii) AI Upstream is attractive – the positioning flush has created an attractive entry point. While
hyperscalers have less upside in the near-term, their medium-term prospects are attractive with room to re-
rate higher. The Blue Wave will limited the capex curve, but tighter compute capacity should benefit
pricing power, margins, and operating cash flow for hyperscalers; (iv) Consumer Cyclicals remain
challenged – affordability remains the headwind tied to oil prices, borrowing costs, and housing
shortages. That said, he sees longer-term value in Apparel, Restaurants, and Retail vs. Energy. Regarding
Housing, mortgage rates need to approach 6.0% to see inflows into the group.
