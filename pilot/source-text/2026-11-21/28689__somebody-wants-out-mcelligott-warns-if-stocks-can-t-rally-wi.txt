 
"Somebody Wants Out!": McElligott Warns If Stocks 
Can't Rally With Every Machine Primed To Buy, It's 
Not A Positioning Problem 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, NOV 19, 2026 - 09:35 PM 
The squeeze everyone had circled on their calendars lasted about an hour. 
Nvidia blew out and raised guidance on Tuesday, Anthropic priced its record IPO at $83, above 
the range, and was indicated to open at $110, a third above the offer and a market cap north of 
$2 trillion, while VIX expiration came and went without incident. And yet, as Nasdaq futures' 
early 2% gain evaporated, the S&P slid back below its 200-day moving average by 
mid-morning. 
Which is precisely the scenario Nomura's Charlie McElligott was wrestling with in his Thursday 
note, titled The "Mechanical Relief Rally" Makes Too Much Sense--And That's A Problem 
(available to pro subs). His opening bullet sums up the dilemma: 
Mechanical / Vol-Reset -driven "Relief Rally" possible kick-off around the turn from this week's 
Quad-fecta of risk-events — but the general acceptance of this view is it's very largest 
headwind, and again creates further "Sellers are LOWER" -risk if Spot continues breaking... 
In other words, the bullish setup is so obvious that everyone already owns the trade, and that is 
the problem. 
The Quad-fecta Clears 
Regular readers will recall that on Tuesday, in "Dealers Most Short Gamma Since 2019," 
McElligott flagged a "Risk Quad-fecta" of NVDA earnings, VIX expiry, the Anthropic IPO and 
Friday's OpEx as a potential vol inflection point: 
Three of the four have now cleared, and his scorecard is about as clean as it gets: NVDA 
"extremely strong and raising guide," ANT "foaming at mouth, impossibly low allocations," and a 
 

 
VIX expiry with "no VIX Gamma to squeeze, super clean." On the allocations point, the IPO 
desk confirmed it before the open: 
Here is where it gets interesting. Surprisingly for some, McElligott says much of the gamma 
rolling off at Friday's expiry is dealer long gamma, meaning the market actually loses some of its 
stabilizing flow. A "significant 'Short Gamma' -market remains into next week," which can keep 
feeding "large TWO-WAY moves" in whichever direction the market is already heading. 
But the customer puts that remain (year-end book hedges plus tactical ones not yet monetized) 
are also the fuel: 
I believe this is where the "Rally fuel" sits—in that potential "Vanna" -impact, as currently "high" 
Vol / stressed vVol / steep Skew can reset incrementally lower again next week... If we do 
give-back some Vols in the days ahead after this "clearing" and-or hedge unwind, it will then 
"mechanically" generate real Futures $buying from Dealers, as their "Short Puts" (to Customers, 
who are Long) will then lose Delta, and force Dealers to buy back Short hedges in Futures 
unemotionally. 
Translation: if vol simply stops going up, dealers have to buy futures whether they like it or not. 
The Machines Are Loaded 
The dealers aren't the only forced buyers waiting in the wings. With "Vol of Vol" stuck high (and 
60-day realized vol of VVIX at a 2-year high), McElligott says there is "energy to get big Upside 
moves in Spot too," and Nomura's CTA model shows clustered buy triggers lining up as 
short-term trend windows flip from short to long. Roughly $7.9 billion of global equity futures 
buying kicks in on a 2% rally, and $21.1 billion on a 4% move, including $9.3 billion in the S&P 
alone: 
 
 

 
The reason is that trend followers have already been flushed. The S&P, Russell, Nasdaq, 
Nikkei, TOPIX and FTSE signals all sit at -8, versus +66 a month ago (+90 for the FTSE), with 
Hang Seng at a maximum short -100: 
 
Then come the vol-targeters. "You guys and gals all know my schtick," McElligott writes: a 
sustained vol reset lower "can then, in time, lead to the Target Vol / Vol Scaler -cohort need to 
add-back Equities Exposure in the weeks ahead too." The setup is there: 1-month realized vol 
of 23.2 sits in the 99.8th percentile versus 15.4 for 3-month realized, and some of the biggest 
daily moves are set to drop out of the window, including the -4.4% from Nov 5 (chart source 
Nomura): 
 
Per Nomura's grid, vol control funds would add roughly $62.9 billion over the next month if daily 
moves average close to zero, but the same cohort dumps $23.2 billion on a single 5% down 
day. A single bad day resets the clock: 
 

 
 
The Pros Have Already Puked 
The other half of the bull case is positioning, and here Goldman's latest monthly Prime Insights 
& Analytics Chart Pack (also available to pro subs) does the talking. Its title: "Fundamental L/S 
Net Exposure Below Liberation Day Lows. Value Rotation." 
Last month, the same chart pack had pros dumping tech at a record pace as short leverage 
neared a 5-year high. Four weeks later, the de-risking has gone further. Fundamental L/S net 
leverage fell another 7.6 points MTD to 41.6%, a 5-year low, while gross leverage dropped 4.4 
points to 206.3%. We put out this chart last night: 
 

 
 
Hedge funds have heavily net sold global equities so far in November, with long sales outpacing 
short sales roughly 3 to 1. According to Goldman, the trailing 4-week de-grossing in global 
equities is the largest since Feb '23 and one of the largest in the past decade: 
 
Single stocks were net sold in 7 of the last 8 weeks, with the pace accelerating this month, while 
ETF short flows have become more volatile, "suggesting investors are actively using Macro 
Products to hedge and manage exposures": 
 

 
 
The epicenter, unsurprisingly, is tech. On a trailing 6-week basis, the pace of selling in global 
Info Tech since early October is "by far the fastest in the past 10 years" at -4.4 standard 
deviations, and the sector's long/short ratio has collapsed to 1.79 from a YTD peak of 2.9, 
approaching 10-year lows: 
 
Within US tech, the shorting has concentrated in chips, so much so that net exposure to Semis 
& Semi Equip has dropped below Software for the first time since the start of 2026. Remember 
when semis were the only thing anyone wanted to own? 
 

 
 
The factor picture tells the same story: exposure to medium-term Momentum is on track to fall 
for a 6th straight month to the lowest since Dec '22, while Value exposure has jumped to 5-year 
highs "amid increased stagflation concerns": 
 
As for the scoreboard, Global Fundamental L/S funds are down 0.2% in November (+8.9% 
YTD), driven by beta losses partly offset by short-side alpha. TMT funds are down another 4.5% 
MTD, leaving them up just 0.6% YTD. So much for the AI trade being a hedge fund hotel. 
 

 
 
Goldman draws the obvious conclusion: after "persistent de-grossing and heavy net selling," the 
market "could be setting up for a tactical rally in the near term, as seasonality and technicals are 
set to become friendlier." But the bank adds an important caveat that most of the Street seems 
to be skipping over: 
That said, it's worth noting that other positioning measures including GIR's Sentiment Indicator 
and Risk Appetite Indicator do not point to capitulatory behavior by a broader set of market 
participants. 
Put differently, the pros have capitulated; everyone else hasn't. Keep that in mind, we'll come 
back to it. 
"It Makes Too Much Sense" 
So with dealers, CTAs, vol control and an 11-point hedge fund underweight in tech (per 
Goldman Prime, as we noted this morning) all pointing the same way, why is McElligott 
nervous? Because everybody else has done the same math: 
But the thing that I'm struggling with is that this rationale behind "US Equities event-risk 
clearing" then leading to a "Pain Trade Rally" -set-up... is increasingly HYPER socialized, 
because it "Makes too much sense" (As "Nobody has it on"... hence Customers are 
high-conviction that "Rally" becomes "Path of Least Resistance" in peak perversion, after the 
fugazi year of performance has left them FRAGILE) 
 

 
And it is already trading. Index call skews have started to "perk-up a touch again" for the first 
time in weeks, and Nomura's desk has seen traders setting up "VIX Downside" plays over the 
past few sessions:

Early Thursday also showed "Signs of Life," with what looked like a "RE-GROSS back into the

"Somebody Wants OUT" 
Which brings us to the real point of the note. The macro tape has been flashing growth scare for 
weeks: the Bloomberg Commodity Index is down 8.5% MTD, energy down 13.4% and industrial 
metals down 8% (with "Druck short Copper"), Treasuries are rallying, defensives are bid and 
 

 
financial conditions are "tightening rapidly off jacked Real Rates, wider Credit Spreads and a 
Realized Vol shock in Equities." 
McElligott concedes some of this may simply be the mechanical de-grossing of crowded longs 
and shorts rather than "a true growth signal." But he also flags something bigger: the market's 
view of Nvidia has flipped, with the "Central Bank of AI" that "was previously viewed as a LONG 
Put for the ecosystem" now "completely turned upside down and being viewed as de facto 
SHORT Put for the market." A blowout quarter apparently doesn't change that. 
And then there is the wealth effect. With stocks now the single largest component of household 
wealth (a point UBS hammered home today in "The Stock Market Is The Economy Now"), 
McElligott worries the "Set it and forget it" dip-buying crowd could start rebalancing into real 
yields, "with Bonds back to 'Working as your Risk-Asset Hedge' again." Hence his test for next 
week: 
IF by early next week we find the Equities tape STILL unable to stabilize and move higher 
despite these aforementioned mechanical pieces being "in-place" to create Vol-tied and 
Systematic -buying flows that could reverse this drawdown... it's telling me there are larger 
forces at play, that somebody wants OUT, that the "Growth Scare" is a real thing, that 
Leveraged Funds hands are still "VaR -tied" into this YE window for performance / capital / 
existential -reasons... 
...And perhaps most crucially, that the 401k class and Real Money "Asset Allocators" are 
reallocating in Fixed-Income further and downshifting in Risk, in a break from the 
hyper-conditioned "Buy the Spot Dip / Sell the Vol Rip" Pavlovian behavior of the past ~15 yrs. 
Buyers Higher... Or Sellers Lower? 
This is where the two notes fit together. Goldman shows the professional community has 
already sold, and sold hard: net leverage at a 5-year low, record tech selling, momentum 
exposure gutted. If the market can't rally on that alone, the marginal seller is probably sitting in 
the much larger pool Goldman says has not capitulated: the same retail and 401(k) crowd that, 
per the EPFR data McElligott cited on Tuesday, was still pouring money into global equities at a 
99.9th percentile pace last week. 
Does the relief rally happen? Maybe, and probably violently if it does: $21 billion of CTA buying 
on a 4% move, dealer vanna and an underweight Street are not nothing, and Thanksgiving 
week is historically kind to anyone who can just sit tight. But we lean toward McElligott's darker 
scenario. The cleanest possible quad-fecta (a blowout NVDA print and a record IPO indicated a 
 

 
third above the offer) couldn't keep the S&P above its 200-day much past the first hour of 
trading. When the good news is out, the machines are primed, the pros are flat and the index 
still can't hold, the problem is something much bigger than positioning. 
Then again, if "everyone" knows the squeeze is coming, the most painful trade is probably no 
squeeze at all. We'll check back early next week, which by McElligott's own clock is when we 
find out who wants out.
