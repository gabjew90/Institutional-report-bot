Energy 
Today's energy sector price action was just as volatile as stocks, swinging from losses to gains 
as WTI hit $85 and rebounded on the back of renewed Middle East shipping incidents - Al 
Jazeera reported five vessels struck in and around the Strait of Hormuz, and the UK Maritime 
Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported a cargo vessel approached by a small skiff with weapons 
and ladders.  
The overnight weakness came from improving Gulf export flows and VP Vance's comments that 
the US would not allow the Iran conflict to become an extended commitment: "we're not going to 
stay there forever," he said; but those bearish drivers were trumped by multiple images of 
tankers on fire (Black Sea/Bulgaria and Bahrain/Hormuz)... 
Additionally, the US struck several locations on Iran’s Qeshm Island in Hormuz, the country’s 
semi-official Mehr News Agency reported. 
But, late in the day, selling resumed, pulling crude prices back to unchanged... 
 

 
 
“Crude oil prices should see some risk premium priced back into the market as tensions grow, 
while the latest flow data also points to a notable reduction in volumes in recent weeks,” said 
Ryan McKay, senior commodity strategist at TD Securities. 
And the fact that the number of empty tankers sailing to collect cargoes of Middle East crude is 
sliding (according to Tankertrackers.com, a firm that monitors vessel movements) didn't help, 
potentially jeopardizing a recent revival in regional oil flows that has weighed on futures (with 
the prompt spread back at zero)... 
 
 

 
Some traders appear to be “bottom fishing,” seeing an opportunity to buy oil at a bargain after 
Monday’s selloff, which has added to the volatility, said Arne Lohmann Rasmussen, chief 
analyst at Global Risk Management. “But in general, a very nervous market.” 
Diesel prices on both sides of the pond were basically flat today (near 3-mopnth lows)... 
 
And lastly within the energy space, US NatGas prices jumped (notably decoupling from EU) as 
midday weather forecasts shifted colder, boosting heating demand expectations. Production has 
also dipped in recent days, with Bakken output falling an estimated 2.4% day-on-day, helping 
tighten the domestic supply picture. 
 
Nevertheless, the higher energy prices did not hurt bonds... 
 

 
Rates 
Mixed macro (strong retail sales, weak industrial production (weather), and weak home sales 
sentiment) along with more FedSpeak today and the CNN scare pushed Treasury yields lower 
(shrugging off the oil moves)... 
 
St. Louis Fed President Musalem signaled support for "several" rate hikes before spring 2027, 
which initially helped push the dollar to a fresh 19-month high early on. 
But then, Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said the quiet part out loud, discussing The 
Fed's need to intervene in the markets from time to time. 
"When stress hits, there may be very little time to decide what principles to operate under or 
how to communicate them," Hammack said Tuesday in remarks prepared for an event in 
Washington. She said that the central bank should set a high bar for Treasury market 
interventions, stepping in only when there’s a risk that market dysfunction “will spill into the real 
economy,” and not when changes in liquidity and volatility reflect fundamentals.  
Treasury purchases should also be considered when other options, such as providing liquidity 
through the market for repurchase agreements, or repo, falls short. Hammack said she supports 
"enhancing" the Fed’s standing repo facility by expanding its hours of operation. 
Her comments this morning come after yesterday saying that she prefers for the Fed to raise 
interest rates in small and steady increments to rein in elevated inflation. 
And finally, NY Fed President John Williams said the central bank still has work to do to bring 
inflation back to its 2% goal, but dismissed the suggestion the Fed needs to consider an 
outsized rate hike. “I don’t think the situation calls for dramatic action,” Williams said Tuesday. 
 

 
“In my view we still have our work to do to make sure we bring inflation back down to 2% in a 
timely way and in a sustained manner,” he said.  
However, that relative hawkishness was trumped by equity weakness (remember Goldman 
noted the reflexivity between stocks and rate-hike-odds this morning), leaving Jan 2027 
rate-hike odds lower on the day... 
 
And the rest of the bond curve was well bid (choppy though as the chart shows) with all yields 
down around 3-4bps (with 10Y Yield hovering around 5.10%)...
