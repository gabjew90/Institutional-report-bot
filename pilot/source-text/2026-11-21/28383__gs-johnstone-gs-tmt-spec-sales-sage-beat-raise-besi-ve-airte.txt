• BANKS PUSH BACK ON AI AGENTS – On the consumer side, banks are drawing clear boundaries
around responsibility for third-party AI tools. Citi, JPMorgan, Bank of America PNC and Citizens warn
that customers are responsible for activity by AI agents they allow to access their accounts, according to
TechCrunch These tools can analyse spending, offer advice and make payments, but banks say they will
not take responsibility for transactions, whether intentional or accidental. Citi’s terms also leave customers
responsible if an agent leaks their credentials or is hacked, although Citi-provided agents do not carry the
same additional liability. This creates a trust barrier for AI companies entering personal finance. Banks
also face a competitive threat from agents that could help customers avoid fees and low-interest deposits.
As in ride-hailing and food delivery the broader question is how much control service providers will give
third-party agents over the customer relationship.

• ASML – Following an investor tour of the Wilton facility Alex reiterates his Buy highlighting progress in
High NA, a strong competitive moat and opportunities to increase output and services revenue. High NA
fleet availability is 87%, with targets of 90% by year-end and 93% in 2028, and ASML expects 13 tools to
be operating by year-end. The technology can increase customer capacity and simplify production by
reducing multiple-pass patterning, while a longer-term move to 12-inch masks could boost productivity by
40%. ASML’s expertise and ability to integrate complex technologies across suppliers remain difficult to
replicate: one reticle-related project alone required around ten years of collaboration with MIT. Alongside
its reiterated plans to increase EUV and immersion capacity by 30% in 2027 and 2028, GS sees further
output gains from better factory-space use, adjusting the High NA/Low NA production mix, shorter
production cycles and AI-assisted tool testing. Services should benefit from a growing EUV installed base,
while upgrades offer additional growth, including higher-margin precision software upgrades requiring no
tool downtime. GS also views Match Act concerns as overdone: additional restrictions on DUV/immersion
shipments to China would depend on implementation beyond the US, which remains uncertain, with
meaningful effects unlikely before 2028. Given high utilisation of lithography capacity in China, any
resulting production shift overseas could also support additional lithography spending elsewhere.

• Broadcom Earnings Preview – We expect investors to focus on (1) management's confidence on FY27
guidance; (2) datacenter and customer readiness for FY28 deployments; (3) incremental competition from
MediaTek and others. We believe expectations are muted heading into the print as investors reassess the

• Dish Wireless has agreed a $525m settlement with American Tower over its abandoned 5G network and
is negotiating similar deals with other creditors, including Charter Communications. The payment will
come from a $2.4bn creditor trust established by the FCC using proceeds from AT&T’s purchase of
EchoStar’s spectrum, but requires FCC approval to release the funds by year-end. Crown Castle, which
has the largest creditor claim at around $3.5bn, said at Wednesday’s court hearing that Dish had not
approached it about a settlement. The disputes follow EchoStar’s decision in 2025 to wind down the
network and sell spectrum to AT&T and SpaceX after the FCC criticised the slow rollout. Those sales
secured tens of billions of dollars in cash and stock, making EchoStar one of SpaceX’s largest
shareholders, despite the failure of its nationwide 5G ambitions. Dish Wireless filed for bankruptcy in June
alongside fellow EchoStar subsidiary Dish DBS, which used Chapter 11 to restructure with its financial
creditors. Dish has since resolved disputes with bondholders who alleged that EchoStar had diverted value
away from them, and is now seeking to settle claims from infrastructure providers left owed money when
the network closed.
