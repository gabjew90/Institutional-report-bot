The great market puke
What a week. SPX has broken key technical levels, hedge funds are aggressively cutting exposure, and
the Asian AI unwind is turning into a full-blown liquidation.
Meanwhile, gold and silver are experiencing extraordinary selling pressure, with momentum collapsing
and volatility exploding.
From the massive disconnect between hedge fund positioning and earnings expectations to the brutal
washout in precious metals, some remarkable extremes are emerging across markets.
The puke
SPX broke below the 200-day moving average early in the week, took out the longer-term trendline
midweek, and is now trading below the massive 7,000 level (futures). RSI has reached its most oversold
levels since the Liberation Day panic. Extreme markets continue.

Nets
According to MS PB, US L/S hedge fund net exposure has dropped to 42%, the lowest in a year and just
the 8th percentile since 2010. Hedge fund positioning typically tracks earnings expectations, with
stronger growth driving more exposure and weaker growth leading to de-risking.
Historically, net exposure at these levels has coincided with median expected SPX earnings growth of
just 5%, with the highest reading around 10%. Current consensus expects 16% earnings growth next
year. Quite a disconnect between positioning and earnings expectations. More here.

Dumping
GS prime brokerage data confirms the liquidation. Hedge funds continue dumping Asian AI/tech
exposure, with long sales outpacing short sales by 2.4 to 1. Korea and Taiwan have dominated the
selling, with funds both unwinding longs and adding fresh shorts. Net allocation to Korea is now back to
levels last seen in early January.

Just get me out
Despite the latest puke in KOSPI, volatility has remained surprisingly calm. KOSPI VIX is up from its early
October lows, but the reaction looks relatively muted given the scale of the sell-off.
One possible explanation is that investors are simply dumping their longs rather than chasing
downside protection. Stop-loss selling without the usual scramble for puts. At this point, you just want
out.

The other AI winner
DB makes an interesting argument: India could become one of the biggest beneficiaries of AI without
winning the race to build the technology.
While the US and China spend hundreds of billions developing models, chips and infrastructure, India
can focus on adopting increasingly cheap AI across its enormous domestic economy.
The country has nearly 1.5 billion potential users, an established digital infrastructure and a massive
technology workforce. DB argues that AI adoption, rather than AI invention, could ultimately be India's
biggest advantage. More here.
