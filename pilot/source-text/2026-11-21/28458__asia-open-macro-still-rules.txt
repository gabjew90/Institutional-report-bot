Asia Open - Macro still rules
By Preston Pagli
Nvidia's blowout earnings failed to become the clearing event markets had hoped for with SPX (-23bps) notching a fifth
straight session in the red and closing below the 200dma. Oct FOMC meeting minutes revealed a "couple" of
participants believed an increase of more than 25bp in December could be warranted. Meanwhile, fresh Fed
hawkishness boosted the dollar and pressured EM currencies, while softer UK services inflation fueled a rally in Gilts. In
commodities, gold (-3.2%) reached its lowest level in over a year while copper slumped 5% on the perceived data center
slowdown. WTI fell more than 3% as inventories rose for a second consecutive week, topping expectations with a 3.38m
barrel build.

In Focus‌
Equities struggled to find conviction after stronger than expected Nvidia (-3%) earnings of $2.68 on $115.8bn, with our TMT
specialist Jon Zauderer noting despite the results, the name could trade range-bound until there is clarity on the succession
plan, with a report suggesting Arm CEO Rene Haas has been approached. SPX (-0.23%) traded below it's 200dma at $7,346
while NDX snapped its 4 session losing streak to trade +0.42%. Overall market breadth improved following Tuesday's historic
weakness with advances/decliners roughly split in SPX. Flows on the desk included buying of REITs and Utilities and selling
of Info Tech, Financials, Consumer Discretionary, Industrials, Healthcare, Comm Services, Energy, Consumer Staples and
Materials. Our derivatives desk notes Nvidia de-risked some of the downside tail but did not shape up to be the clearing event
some had expected, leaving Jan ATM: SPX -0.3v, QQQ -0.6v, IWM -0.6v. Clients continued to manage hedges actively with
Thursday's Trump-Xi meeting vols implying SPX: 1.33%, QQQ: 2.12%.
FX: The dollar rebounded on fresh Fed hawkishness following Governor Waller reiterating his call for a 50bp hike in
December, driving a sharp selloff in EM. Cable weakened nearly 50bps after a services inflation miss, trading 1.31, a near
one-year low. MXN (-2.7%) was the worst performer despite constructive headlines out of APEC with Sheinbaum saying
"we're well advanced toward reaching a deal with the US", in reference to trade. Volumes on our eFX trading desk were
elevated across EURNOK, EURSEK, USDZAR, and USDTHB. Net inflows were skewed toward CZK, ILS, and SEK while
selling was seen through JPY (Banks), NZD (Banks) and PLN. 

Rates: USTs gained on the session despite afternoon weakness upon the release of the Fed's October meeting minutes
which revealed a "couple" of participants believed an increase of more than 25bp in December could be warranted if incoming
inflation data proved unfavorable. Duration richened with 30y yields falling 4bps to 5.40% following a decent 20y auction.
US Treasury Auction Recap: $16 billion of 20-year bonds. The auction stopped through the 1pm market by -0.1bp to yield
5.459%. The bid/cover ratio came in at 2.63 which is at the 36th percentile of the past 12 auctions.
Gilts rallied following a miss in services inflation and weaker BoE projections. The rally resulted in a 7bp decline in yields
across all tenors with our trader seeing better buying in 5y-10y area.
Commodities: WTI crude dropped over 3% to $83 today after US inventory data showed crude and gasoline stockpiles
swelled. Crude crept up into the afternoon after the IDF announced it would be conducting small operations in Hamas
controlled territory in Gaza. Hamas called the Israeli actions “a blatant violation of the cease-fire agreement”.
Copper plunged 5% with our commodity sales desk citing the key issue for longs as lingering data center demand, while the
firmer dollar adds to the downside pressure. Remember, our Commodities Strategy team revisited Copper on Nov 6, warning
of modest downside with prices likely to grind lower to $13.3k (0-3m).
Elsewhere‌
ZAR (-2%) was one of the weakest performers, pressured by a 2.7% decline in gold and a largely in-line October CPI print
ahead of tomorrow's SARB decision, where the market and our economist expect a hawkish hold. Our trader notes while
the SARB is likely to highlight the firming in underlying prices, today's print is unlikely to materially alter its outlook given
decisions are typically made before the CPI release and forecasts are not re-calibrated ahead of the decision.
