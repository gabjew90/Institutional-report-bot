Markets are still struggling to find a floor under the selloff, as the S&P 500 (-0.23%) posted a 5th 
consecutive decline yesterday, whilst most indices in Asia have also fallen overnight. Moreover, there 
are still plenty of jitters under the surface, as other sectors beyond tech have come under pressure as well. 
So it remains a difficult environment, with the risk-off tone increasingly spreading to other asset classes. For 
instance, yesterday saw fresh questions about how much the Fed could keep hiking rates, which saw the 
10yr Treasury yield (-3.8bps) fall to 5.07%. Meanwhile, the concerns about economic demand also drove 
fresh losses across commodities, with Brent crude oil (-1.28%) falling to $91.95/bbl, whilst gold (-3.23%) 
hit a 14-month low of $3,725/oz. So it was another rough day, with little respite wherever you looked. 
Once again, there wasn’t a single driver behind the selloff. Admittedly, fears of an AI-related slowdown 
remained in focus, with Nvidia (-2.89%) sliding again after its results on Tuesday night, extending the 
decline from its early October peak to -37%. However, across the broader tech space, the NASDAQ 
(+0.42%) and the Mag-7 (+0.50%) actually recovered after four consecutive declines. So it wasn’t just a 
tech story, and the equity losses have become increasingly broad-based over the last couple of sessions. In 
fact, the S&P 500 is now down -3.15% so far this week, which if sustained would be the biggest weekly 
decline since the Liberation Day tariff announcements in April 2025, when the index fell more than -9%. 
And in level terms, the S&P 500 now stands at a 5-month low, and it’s also down -6.33% from its October 
peak, making this the biggest peak-to-trough decline since March. 
That trend has continued in Asia overnight, with most of the major indices falling back this morning. The 
Hang Seng (-1.83%) is leading the way, with the index currently on course for its lowest level since June. But 
that’s been echoed elsewhere, with the CSI 300 (-0.24%) on course for its lowest level since July 2025, 
whilst the Shanghai Comp (-0.29%) would hit its lowest since August 2025 as it stands. Meanwhile in 

South Korea, the KOSPI (-0.32%) has also lost ground, although Japan’s Nikkei (+0.48%) has seen a 
relative outperformance this morning. In Australia, however, the S&P/ASX 200 is down -1.04%, with 
matters not helped by an unexpectedly weak jobs report, which showed unemployment rising to a 5-year 
high of 4.9% in October (vs. 4.7% expected). Looking forward, S&P 500 futures (+0.60%) have recovered 
this morning, but a consistent theme of recent days has been that an initial recovery in US equity futures 
is followed by fresh losses around the cash open. 
With the equity selloff showing few signs of relenting, the risk-off tone was also clear for sovereign bonds, 
with yields falling on both sides of the Atlantic as a result. Admittedly, things looked quite different earlier in 
the session, as Fed Governor Waller made another set of hawkish comments on CNBC. He said that “We 
have to be a little more aggressive in our rate hikes to try to get inflation down”, and that if the Fed 
didn’t signal that another hike was coming in January, “then you have to go 50 in December to put it in 
place and get it done.” But ultimately, the risk-off mood dominated, with 10yr yields (-3.8bps) ending the 
session at 5.07%, and this morning they’re down another -1.6bps to 5.05%. 
Against that backdrop, we also got the minutes of the Fed’s October decision, when they kept rates on hold 
after the October hike. There were some hawkish takeaways, as it said “almost all participants” had a 
baseline that included higher rates. Moreover, it said “a couple of participants” thought a rate hike larger 
than 25bps “could warrant consideration at the next meeting if incoming inflation data were unfavorable.” 
But given that future rate hikes are already priced in, the release didn’t lead to much of a market 
reaction. 
As all that was happening, there was some mild relief on the inflation side, as commodities continued to 
decline across the board. For instance, Brent crude oil (-1.28%) fell to $91.95/bbl, whilst WTI oil (-3.20%) 
came down to $83.26/bbl. Overnight, oil prices have stabilised after the New York Times reported that 
President Trump was considering plans for further strikes on Iran, although he was yet to make a 
decision. But there hasn’t been too much of a market reaction to that, with Brent crude only up +0.21% this 
morning to $92.14/bbl. Meanwhile in the metals space, prices (-5.30%) saw their biggest daily decline 
yesterday since July 2025 amid doubts over the AI investment boom, while agricultural commodities 
including corn (-2.34%) and wheat (-3.97%) also fell back sharply. So that took some of the inflationary 
pressures off, with the 1yr Euro inflation swap (-9.2bps) down to 3.00%, whilst the 1yr US inflation swap (-
5.0bps) fell to 2.02%. 
Whilst the market mood is clearly downbeat at the moment, this pattern fits into something I noted earlier 
this week (link here) on how some of the bearish risks are working to offset each other right now. For 
instance, as fears of an AI slowdown have gathered pace and equities have seen bigger declines, we’ve 
simultaneously seen sovereign bond yields come down (easing the short-term fiscal stress), and 
inflation pressures also decline (easing concern about rate hikes). So whilst there are plenty of prominent 
risks on the horizon, it’s important to remember these aren’t just additive, and it’s actually quite difficult for 
all the most bearish outcomes to happen at once. 
Earlier in Europe, markets also struggled, with another risk-off move across the continent. That was clearest 
for equities, with the STOXX 600 (-1.03%) falling to a three-week low. Indeed, it marked the first time the 
index had back-to-back declines of more than 1% since March. And there was fresh pressure on the 
Euro itself too, which weakened another -0.21% against the US Dollar yesterday to $1.0975, marking its 

lowest close since April 2025. Then for sovereign bonds, we also saw a modest pullback in yields, with 
those on 10yr bunds (-0.7bps), OATs (-1.5bps) and BTPs (-0.4bps) all falling back. 
Here in the UK, gilts saw a relative outperformance after the latest CPI print for October, with the 10yr yield (-
8.2bps) falling to 5.29% by the close. The release showed headline CPI at a 12-month high of +3.6%, in 
line with consensus. But gilts rallied because the BoE staff projection was for a larger increase to +3.7%, so 
investors adjusted their expectations in a slightly dovish direction in response. For instance, the amount of 
rate hikes priced by September 2027 fell -6.8bps on the day to 86bps by the close. In addition, the dovish 
repricing also contributed to sterling (-0.45%) falling to its weakest level against the dollar in almost a 
year at $1.3149.
