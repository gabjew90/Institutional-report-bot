It’s been another hectic and negative week and one where even positive news is struggling to help the market 
stabilise. The only bright spot for me was hearing about one of my twins scoring 6 tries in the C team rugby. 
He hates rugby as he’s relatively small and doesn’t like to get hurt so I can only imagine he’s learnt to run as 
fast as he can to avoid the bigger boys. From small acorns! 
Back to the negatives and the S&P 500 (-2.02%) has been colliding with tackles all week and yesterday 
saw the most number of decliners within the index in 2026 so far. It also fell for a 6th consecutive 
session, which also marked its longest losing streak since April 2024. And if we get a 7th fall today, that 
would make it the longest run of declines since the pandemic slump began in February 2020. So the pressure 
is becoming increasingly widespread, and that’s been felt across other asset classes too. In fact, the 10yr 
Treasury yield (-2.3bps) fell back to 5.05% given the risk-off tone and is trading around 5.01% this 
morning. However, European sovereign bonds struggled by comparison, with the 10yr bund yield (+3.5bps) 
rising to 3.40%, amidst a fresh rise in energy prices. The continued risk off in Asia will likely reverse some 
of that at this morning’s open.   
When it came to the equity move, the selloff has become increasingly broad, with 464 decliners in the S&P 
500, the most since December 31 last year. The equal weight index was down -1.77% as all of 24 of the 
S&P sector groups fell on the day. This included sizeable declines for traditionally defensive sectors such 
as Consumer Staples (-1.48%) and Health Care (-2.07%). Tech slightly underperformed, with the Nasdaq 
(-2.25%), Magnificent 7 (-2.67%) and Philly semiconductor index (-2.41%) all posting similar declines. So 
there was little respite all round, and the S&P 500 is now down -5.10% so far this week, leaving the index on 
track for its worst performance since the Liberation Day tariff announcements in April 2025. Meanwhile, the 
VIX volatility index (+3.88pts) rose to its highest since March at 28.44. Over in Europe it was a similar 
picture, with fresh losses for the STOXX 600 (-0.88%), FTSE 100 (-0.93%), DAX (-1.06%) and the CAC 40 (-
0.81%). 

Asian equities are extending the global risk-off sentiment this morning with the tech-heavy markets 
bearing the brunt of the declines, with Taiwan’s TAIEX (-6.23%) and the KOSPI (-5.44%) leading regional 
losses. The KOSPI has fallen to a nine-month low, while the TAIEX has slipped to a seven-month low 
amid broad-based selling in chipmakers. Japan’s Nikkei (-4.92%) is also sharply lower as persistent 
weakness across the semiconductor complex continues to weigh on investor sentiment. Elsewhere, the 
Hang Seng (-2.33%) is approaching bear-market territory, and nearly down -20% from its January peak, while 
the CSI 300 (-1.85%), Shanghai Composite (-2.10%), and Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 (-1.58%) are also posting 
notable declines. The downward momentum is continuing in futures with contracts on the S&P 500 (-
0.50%), Nasdaq 100 (-0.98%) and Stoxx (-1.70%) all notably lower with Europe catching down. 
The risk-off tone has also weighed on a wide variety of other asset classes, with gold (-2.68%) sliding to a 14-
month low of $3,625/oz yesterday and down another -2.2% as I type this morning. Bitcoin (-1.25%) has 
fallen to its lowest level since early July, and US HY credit spreads (+6bps) reaching their widest level 
since May at 344bps.   
Matters haven’t been helped by the latest geopolitical headlines, which helped push energy prices higher and 
added to fears about inflationary pressures. Among others, Axios reported that the White House had asked 
the Pentagon and intelligence experts for their view on how Iran would respond to the different strike 
scenarios prepared by the US military. So that echoed the previous evening’s report in the New York Times 
that President Trump was considering plans for further strikes on Iran. While oil prices settled little changed 
on the day given the demand concerns, European natural gas futures (+4.46%) moved up to €84.46/MWh. 
Meanwhile in Europe, the geopolitical headlines were also challenging after Russian President Putin 
promised to retaliate forcefully after Ukraine damaged Russia’s bridge to Crimea. So that dispelled 
hopes for some kind of de-escalation, given Putin said that the bridge strike ended talk of a partial ceasefire.   
That backdrop helped to drive a selloff across fixed income, particularly in Europe. So yields on 10yr bunds 
(+3.5bps), OATs (+6.8bps) and gilts (+2.6bps) all moved higher. That came as near-term inflation 
expectations moved higher, with the 1yr Euro inflation swap up +1.1bps to 3.01%, while markets also 
priced a greater likelihood of further ECB tightening. For instance, the number of rate hikes priced by 
September 2027 was up +3.1bps on the day to 69bps. Meanwhile, Bundesbank President Nagel said that 
“We may need to take further action and tighten the interest-rate screw.” However, he did say that unlike 
2022, there was a much lower risk of second-round effects, and said that he didn’t see a wage-price spiral 
either.   
Over in the US, Treasuries fared relatively better, as the equity selloff put mounting pressure on fixed income. 
Indeed, the 10yr yield (-2.3bps) ended the session at 5.05%, while 2yr yields fell -4.2bps to 4.76%. Yields 
are 3-5bps lower again this morning across the curve. In addition, we also saw an unexpected rise in the 
weekly initial jobless claims, which rose to 203k in the week ending November 14 (vs. 198k expected). 
So that saw the 4-week moving average rise another +2k to 197.75k. However, in the greater scheme of 
things these numbers are just off the lowest since man first walked on the moon. So hard to get too 
concerned.   
The pullback in US yields came despite continued hawkish Fed commentary. For instance, Chicago Fed 
President Goolsbee said that “we’ve now had multiple months in a row of borderline disturbingly high 
inflation” and suggested that he will consider all options at the December meeting. Meanwhile, 

Governor Barr saw further rate adjustments ahead, noting that “financial conditions are quite 
accommodative”.   
Overnight in Japan, Core CPI, which excludes fresh food and energy, rose 2.2% YoY in October, slightly 
below the 2.3% consensus estimate but above the Bank of Japan's 2% target. Core-core inflation also 
eased to 2.2% from 2.4% previously, while headline CPI accelerated to 2.3%, its highest level since 
November 2025 but again a couple of tenths below consensus. The slightly better data than expected is 
unlikely to materially alter the BOJ's cautious normalisation path and a December hike.   
Finally, Presidents Trump, Xi and Putin announced plans to boost supplies of refined petroleum 
products following talks on the sidelines of the APEC summit in China yesterday. The implications for 
global supply remain uncertain, however, given the constraints facing each country. For instance, a rise in 
Russian exports of refined products appears unlikely for the foreseeable future given the recent re-escalation 
in Ukrainian strikes against Russian refineries. And oil prices still edged higher yesterday given the latest 
developments in the geopolitical sphere. Separately, President Trump also announced he’d be holding a 
news conference at the White House today following his trip.   
To the day ahead now, and the first thing out is German PPI and UK retail sales for October at 7am London 
time just after this hits inboxes. Otherwise in the US, we’ll get the University of Michigan’s final consumer 
sentiment index for November. Finally, central bank speakers today include the Fed’s Waller and Cook, and 
the ECB’s Lagarde, Cipollone, and Nagel.
