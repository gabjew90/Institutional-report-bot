GS Privorotsky - GS Basics China Restrictions. 2018 Playbook.
FCI Tight. 20 Nov 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
20 Nov 2026
Markets: Sharply lower from the US open yesterday. A lot of confusion about what exactly went wrong.
For many, myself included, the catalysts were meant to be supportive… instead we got a pretty violent
liquidation. I think objectively there are a few things, both macro and micro, that are challenging the
market.
AI/China: The US has moved towards a far more aggressive chip export regime. The new BIS rules,
effective November 19 and directed by Treasury and the NSC, represent a pretty sizeable intervention. The
rules tighten restrictions not just on direct chip sales to China, but also on specified advanced compute
accessed remotely by Chinese entities through overseas data centers. Effectively an attempt to close the
offshore compute loophole, with substantial overlap with the proposed RASA legislation. China now faces
pressure on three fronts: direct access to advanced chips, remote access to overseas compute and its ability
to manufacture leading edge technology, with tighter restrictions on overseas foundries. Think the most
immediate issue isn’t the permanent loss of Chinese demand, but the uncertainty over who can access
what, and under which licensing regime. That’s potentially enough to freeze incremental investment
decisions. I think this partially explains why the Asian data center trade has been so acutely weak,
alongside broader China weakness and the Shanghai market testing its lows. Potential knock on effects
into copper and gold, although broader liquidity and positioning are clearly also at work. Think this has
been the most acute/recent concerns but they only compound the issues that have driven multiples down:
a.) cost of capital (further challenged by change of leadership at NVDA) b.) DC restrictions c.)
oversupply/efficiency unlocks for inference d.) lack of revenue to support capex.
Technicals: Broke just about every level possible. Bearish high/low close, SPX reversing 3.3% intraday
and taking out virtually every reasonable area of support. Breadth was bad, with only 38 S&P constituents
advancing and 464 declining. Negative gamma into expiry likely amplified the move. Perhaps more
disturbing was the failure of the defensive bid… utilities and staples sold off alongside everything else,
despite some relief in Treasury yields. They still outperformed relatively, but the protection in absolute
terms disappeared. What had been a rolling rotation increasingly looks like outright equity liquidation.
Macro/Rates: I’ll post the chart below, but financial conditions have tightened extraordinarily quickly.
Waller’s 50bp comments certainly helped shock the market. We’ve had a litany of Fed speakers this week
(forward guidance back?), mostly hawkish. In hindsight, the November/December 2018 parallels feel
increasingly close. The Fed risks looking in the rear view mirror at inflation and the AI trade, effectively
overtightening into a market already showing considerable stress. 2yr inflation breakevens have collapsed
towards their lows, leaving 2yr real yields effectively near the relative highs. That’s an extraordinary
tightening in real policy conditions at precisely the point the equity market is signaling deteriorating

growth and liquidity. Interestingly, the long end may already be starting to price a policy mistake… yields
falling and breakevens compressing even as equities and gold liquidate. Increasingly looks like a transition
from an inflation problem to a growth/liquidity problem.
Risk: Most are in some version of risk reduction mode… about as uninspiring a price action as you can
have. Perhaps there was a technical component yesterday, some forced funding or liquidation that won’t
repeat, but you can’t break this many charts, across this many assets, and pretend we’re still trading a bull
market tape. Increasingly think the market pivots when central banks pivot. Receiving rates and owning
duration remains the one trade that feels misaligned with what equity internals have been telling us… and
I still think that’s relatively attractive. Gold I think is an important coincidental indicator to watch
alongside equities...clear liquidation/reaction to real rates/FCI. From a contrarian positioning perspective,
the sheer depth of current pessimism and one sided de-grossing suggests the market may be approaching a
tactical local low. However, we’re a bit devoid of catalysts now. PCE next Wed, NFP and Dec FOMC.
Think November CPI is next major event to anchor towards but far enough away that it probably won’t
register just yet… potentially the one that forces a recalibration from the Fed.
