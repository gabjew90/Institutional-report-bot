GS TMT SPEC SALES – Friday 18th September
US equities finished higher overnight, with the S&P delivering one of its strongest September sessions and broad
participation across the index. AI and Mag7 were the main drivers, helped by a pause in rates and continued
digestion of this week’s Fed hike. Momentum and AI-linked baskets also outperformed, with MegaCap Tech
+168bps, Momentum +304bps and AI Semis +348bps. The market’s AI safety debate also appears to have shifted
quickly: rather than slowing compute demand, the emerging view is that safer AI may require even more compute
for testing, alignment and deployment.
Key stories remain centred on AI infrastructure and semis. Apple is reportedly considering a return to the server
market using Nvidia NVLink Fusion with its own M8 Ultra chip, potentially benefiting Hon Hai, Quanta, Wiwynn
and Inventec as ASIC demand grows across CSPs. Nvidia and Google are also partnering on an AI power-
management alliance to support more flexible, grid-enhancing data centres, with Delta and Lite-On seen as
potential beneficiaries. Separately, Jensen Huang said Nvidia’s chip sales volume could double next year on strong
demand across GPUs, CPUs, networking and robotics, reinforcing the message that supply—not demand—
remains the key constraint.

While US telcos traded down (TMUS -5.5%; AT&T -2% & VZ -3%) on concerns on X chatter around Meta’s
Muse and rival AI agent Instinct has focused on their new ability to make outbound calls to U.S. businesses,
including negotiating phone and cable bills on behalf of users. This could become a risk for telcos because AI
agents can wait on hold, escalate with support teams, switch users to cheaper plans, remove add-ons and claim
credits at scale—potentially increasing pressure on ARPU, retention economics and customer-service costs. The
same risk applies to European names as these AI assistant role out. But its NOT just telcos as other industries
could also face pressure -industries most at risk are those with recurring bills, negotiable pricing, confusing add-
ons, high churn incentives and customer-service friction—including cable/broadband, insurance, utilities,
banks/credit cards, subscriptions, travel, healthcare billing, retail returns, auto leasing and gyms. If AI agents can
persistently call, wait on hold, compare offers, cancel services, claim credits and renegotiate terms on behalf of
customers, companies that rely on inertia or under-claimed discounts could face higher retention discounts, lower
add-on revenue, more support volume and weaker pricing power.

TECH
| • | Semicap component shortage worsening: Korean media reports that lead times for critical components used in
semiconductor manufacturing equipment have more than doubled. A deposition equipment maker (Company A)
said deposition part lead times have stretched from ~4 months to as long as 10 months, while a laser processing
equipment maker (Company B) now waits up to 40 months for critical components from Japan — with suppliers
unwilling to shorten times due to capacity limits and no alternative sources available. Even a packaging equipment
maker (Company C) with a domestic supply chain is seeing delivery times ~50% longer than usual (four months
now six-plus). The industry's reluctance to use Chinese components amid US-China trade tensions is
compounding the problem, as excluding competitively-priced Chinese suppliers leaves few alternatives. Net:
reinforces the tight supply / strong demand backdrop for semicaps, though it also flags a potential constraint on
equipment makers' ability to fulfil orders. Link | | • | Agentic AI & Adyen – getting a few asked Mastercard plans
to roll out an agentic payment option for everyday purchases through a partnership with the startup Alchemy. This
should not be a surprise that we see more Agentic commerce given what going on with Agentic AI that we are
seeing new infrastructure like Mastercard's Agent Pay for Machines (AP4M) and Alchemy's AgentPay, which
facilitate secure, high-frequency, and micro-value machine-to-machine (M2M) payments across both traditional
card networks and Web3 stablecoin rails. For Adyen, this evolution and should be a growth catalyst rather than a
disruption. It’s a key launch partner for Mastercard's AP4M, Adyen launched its own dedicated solution, Adyen
Agentic, in June 2026. By allowing enterprise merchants to connect once and accept payments across competing
standards—such as Google's AP2, OpenAI/Stripe's ACP, and Shopify's UCP—Adyen secures its position as the
essential processing layer for the emerging machine-driven economy. |
While on Adyen – I hear 2nd hand that Cleveland are out on Ayden today and while still mixed are more positive. I
have a had US hedge funds pushing concerns that Stripe Link and agentic personal-shopping agents could
disintermediate Adyen over time by reducing wallet share at the merchant level. That bear case feels overstated:
consumers can still transact normally with Adyen merchants without using Stripe Link, and even if Link becomes
more important, Adyen could potentially orchestrate Link, Shop Pay and other future agent-led payment flows
within its broader payments platform. Stripe Link is Stripe’s digital wallet and one-click checkout product,
allowing consumers to save and reuse payment credentials across Link-enabled merchants.
| • | AI Capex (Credit) - GS refresh the gross hyperscaler issuance implied by our forecasts. We now expect $420
billion of gross debt issuance in 2027, which reflects some modest upward revisions in CapEx forecasts. This
forecast would represent more than a 60% increase vs. our full-year 2026 estimate of $250 billion of direct global
hyperscaler IG debt issuance. So far this year, $229 billion has been issued across a range of currencies. We

continue to expect 65% to 75% of the 2027 hyperscaler gross debt issuance to price in the USD IG bond market,
subject to market conditions and investor receptivity in light of issuer concentration considerations. So far this
year, we have tracked $578 billion of global, gross AI-related debt issuance, which includes a broad universe of
firms. Hyperscalers continue to represent ‘just’ 40% of overall AI-related debt issuance, underscoring the wide
range of AI-related exposure in the global credit markets. The share of AI-related notional across the IG and HY
corporate credit indices has been increasing. The largest exposure is in the USD IG market, at 12% of total
notional, followed by USD HY at 6%. In the EUR market, the figures are more contained at 3% for IG and 1% for
HY. We see meaningful scope for this to grow as the multi-year issuance cycle extends. | | • | OpenAI: The
Information with an article on OpenAI’s reported talks for a new private round at a potential $1.2tn+ valuation
highlight why staying private still makes sense: it buys more time to scale revenue, invest in compute and avoid
public scrutiny of cash burn, pricing discounts and margins. The opportunity remains huge, especially with strong
demand for newer models such as GPT-6 Astra, but the funding burden is also rising, with OpenAI already having
raised or secured substantial capital while key backers such as SoftBank, Amazon and Nvidia face their own capex
or financing constraints. The key investor debate is whether OpenAI can convert model momentum into durable
revenue growth fast enough to justify another mega-round, or whether the combination of heavy server needs,
safety concerns and aggressive competition from Anthropic makes the risk/reward more stretched. |
| • | CCL: Industry sources expect the transition from copper to optical I/O to be gradual rather than a direct
replacement of CCL . CPO is unlikely to materially reduce high-end CCL demand in the near term, as current
architectures still require M8+ materials, larger boards and more PCB layers; industry checks suggest CPO scale-
out remains early, with 2026–27 shipments still only at sub-2mn / ~5mn units. Rubin Ultra may adopt NPO, but
high-speed links inside AI servers still require low-loss PCB/CCL, while longer-term SerDes migration toward
224G/448G should continue to raise material requirements.(ED, CT) | | • | Apple server: Apple is working on an
enterprise AI server built around in-house M8 Ultra chips, targeting a 2029 launch, for ASIC development in AI-
oriented usage and applications. Local media names several potential beneficiaries, including Hon Hai Precision
(2317), Quanta (2382), Wiwynn (6669) and Inventec (2356).(CT) |
TELCOS
| • | TEF/Zegona: Vozpopuli reporting renewed Telefónica interest in Vodafone Spain has added c.€400m to
Zegona’s market cap in four days, with the shares up more than 10% on hopes of a potential takeout in H1 2027.
Spain M&A clearly remains a credible option and the market is reacting positively, but TEF management sounded
more cautious at GS this week, talking down a Spanish deal despite the FT/Vozpopuli reports and seeming more
focused on Germany, where consolidation arguably looks more needed after the 1&1 loss. Per the press the key
tension is valuation: Telefónica is reportedly closer to €8-10bn for Vodafone Spain versus Zegona’s late-2025
valuation of €14.7bn, so investors are split on whether TEF is genuinely stepping back or simply trying to keep a
lid on expectations while hedge funds add exposure to the takeout optionality. |
| • | Starlink V3: SpaceX is set to launch its first fully operational Starlink V3 broadband satellites on Starship
Flight 14, planned for 22 September 2026 pending regulatory approval. The mission would deploy 26 larger,
higher-capacity V3 satellites, with each capable of up to 1Tbps downlink and the new generation expected to
deliver roughly 10x the broadband capacity and data density of prior satellites, potentially supporting gigabit
consumer speeds. Starlink already serves around 13m broadband customers and 22m mobile direct-to-cell
customers globally across c.11,100 LEO satellites, making the launch an important step in scaling satellite
broadband capacity. (ISPReview) |

MEDIA & INTERNET
| • | Meta fake-ads ruling: A German court ruled that Meta (META.O) is liable for fake investment ads posted by
third parties on Instagram and Facebook, after a German financial portal and its founder said their trademarked
logo and image were used without consent. The court said Meta could not rely on the Digital Services Act’s lack-
of-knowledge defence because its algorithms and advertising systems give it control over content, ordered Meta to
remove the ads, pay damages and disclose details of the ads and revenue generated, though Meta disagrees and
may appeal. Reuters |
