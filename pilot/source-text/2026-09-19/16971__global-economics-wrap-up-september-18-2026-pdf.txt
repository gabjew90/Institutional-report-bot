Is poor economic sentiment really about the economy? 
n
Consumer sentiment remains very weak across major economies despite 
o
healthy labor markets, a booming stock market, and solid consumer 
spending and GDP growth. 
Cross-country relationships confirm that high price levels 
§
post-pandemic are lowering sentiment, while amplified political 
biases have also led to sharp shifts after recent global elections. 
One explanation is that people have generally become more downbeat on 
o
the state of the world for reasons unrelated to the economy, and overall 
happiness levels in the University of Chicago’s General Social Survey have 
yet to recover after deteriorating sharply in 2020. 
We find that this lower happiness is strongly correlated with lower 
§
confidence in societal and government institutions post-pandemic. 
This suggests that low reported economic sentiment likely reflects a more 
o
fundamental, downbeat assessment of the state of the world rather than 
the economy.

Our financial conditions index is little changed this year despite the large rise in 
n
interest rates, implying little net impact on GDP growth. 
Interest rates have risen substantially since the start of the year, but their 
o
impact on overall financial conditions has been roughly offset by tighter credit 
spreads, higher equity prices, and a weaker dollar. 
As a result, the impulse from changes in financial conditions to GDP growth is 
o

in rates alone.
