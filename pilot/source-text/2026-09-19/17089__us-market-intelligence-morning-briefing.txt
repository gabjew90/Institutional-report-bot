JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
The biggest macro updates since yesterday’s close was BOJ’s rate decision as the 7-2 vote 
was interpretated as “less hawkish than feared” by the market. We saw JPY depreciation 
post event with USDJPY adding 1.3% to 157.98 this morning. For US equities, this busy 
Central Bank week continues to add pressure on global bond yields. For the Fed 
particularly, the focus will shift to next two week’s Fedspeak to assess rate hike path for Q4 
and 1H 2027, starting from Fed’ Bowman today at 9.30am ET; ultimately, we remain the 
view that Warsh’s speech did not provide clear information around how close we are to an 
inflation level / trend where the Fed holds. On the positive note, the slew of diplomatic 
events next week seem to provide a buffer for the geopolitical situation, with WTI holding 
around $101 this morning.

POST-FED MKT THOUGHTS – Below was published in the September 17th Morning 
Briefing 
The market reacted to a hawkish Warsh press conference, but it reminds me of Jackson 
Hole, a speech that he referenced multiple times. Post-Jackson Hole, we wrote, “ I do not 
think we know anything more about what he is thinking in terms of rates today than we did 
yesterday. If inflation is too high, he has had 2x chances to attempt to hike and failed to do 
so. If inflation is trending lower, which contradicts much of his speech, then the Fed holds.” 
After yesterday, our view is similar. We know very little about how close we are to an 
inflation level / trend where the Fed holds; we know very little about when Warsh expects AI 
to move from inflationary to disinflation/deflationary; we know very little about his own 
forecast relative to his peers. The Dots reflect a Fed that sees 1x more hike between now 
and YE2027 with inflation reaching its target in 2029. Is the implication that either forecasts 
are based on immaculate disinflation, or the Fed is implicitly moving away from a 2.0% Core 
PCE target? In any case, we are likely to learn more from other Fed members than from 
Warsh. 
  


[Page 5]
Fundamentally, this is an economy that is likely to remain above trend level growth, near full 
employment. Any acceleration in nominal growth will boost SPX earnings; given that 
financial conditions are unlikely to materially tighten the economy remains supported by 
both monetary and fiscal policy. The key for stocks remains energy prices / SoH. If we see 
US / Iran move toward a deal or another variables push oil lower (RU/UKR; China) then that 
would buttress the bull run but we may chop sideways / lower over the next few sessions 
until we gain clarity on inflation and the Fed. For now, we maintain our Tactically Cautious 
/ Neutral view. What would flip our view to Bullish? Lower oil / bond yields. The bond 
market may coalesce around Warshspeak and that the Fed’s commitment is enough to 
contain inflation expectations and bond yields. US / Iran may return to the negotiating table 
lowering oil price, or otherwise action a move lower in crude. What would flip our view 
Bearish? We would need to see signs that the Consumer and economy are breaking or 
that the AI theme has ended, which would likely be initiated by a capex cut. We think the 
most likely outcome is that we get what we need from Fedspeak and the Middle East and 
move higher; however, timing remains uncertain and positioning suggests more downside 
can come if uncertainty prevails. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (SEP 14) – We are still bullish on Tech and like the AI Theme, 
Semis, and Ma7 as the way to express this view. Given today’s price drop on the ‘AI 
Pullback’ story, we would be dip buyers as we think it is unlikely to materialize into revenue 
declines / margin compression. Our views on Cyclicals and RTY are tied to the outcome of 
the Fed Meeting / Press Conference. Generally, a steeper yield curve is supportive of 
Cyclicals and if we see yields move lower, then we would anticipate SMid-caps to see 
tactical outperformance. We maintain our preference for MegaCaps / Large-Caps, though 
would exercise caution on being short RTY into the Fed print. Further, should we see yields 
move materially lower, some of the better upside risk/reward plays appear to be among the 
‘Reopening Trade’ plays from 2021, e.g., Airlines, Autos, Consumer Goods esp. 
Footwear/Sportswear, Homebuilders, and Restaurants as these groups have lagged due to 
concerns around the consumer, gasoline / oil, inflation, and rates putting current positioning 
in low single-digits. From a factor perspective, Momentum and Quality Growth are among 
our favored plays. For EM ex-AI, look to Latam for exposure.

US MARKET INTEL VIEW 
We maintain our Tactically Cautious / Neutral view into Wednesday’s Fed Meeting. As a 
reminder, this is not a fundamentally based view, as we see both the macro (above trend 
GDP growth) and micro / earnings environment (>20% EPS growth for 26Q3 and 26Q4) as 
supportive of stocks. We see our view supported by a 10Y approaching / breaching a new 
cycle high (4.99%), credit spreads likely to widen given elevated issuance, and heightened 
volatility especially if Warsh maintains opaque communications. Further, positioning is not 
yet giving a clear directional signal. 
  
Historically, when the 10Y yield makes a new cycle high, it takes up to 8 weeks for Equities 
to fully digest. Our colleague, Craig Cohen flags that over the last 60 years, when the 10Y 
yield has a 2 standard deviation move higher, then Equities have negative monthly returns. 
Currently, a 2z move is ~50-55bp and MTD the 10Y yield is +21.7bp, with more than 50% of 
the move coming after Thursday’s PPI print. We view Craig’s data of supportive of our view 
that bond vol is a bigger headwind than bond yield levels. Yields are resetting higher in 
response to stronger, persistent growth. In the 1990s, the 10Y yield averaged ~6.65% for 


[Page 8]
the decade as SPX gained 316%, NDX 1556%, and RTY 200%. For comparison, since the 
COVID low on Mar 23, 2020 through Friday’s close, the SPX is +242%, NDX +319%, and 
RTY +190% with the 10Y yield averaging 3.16%. 
  
Longer-term, inflation remains a risk with US / Iran pressuring commodity prices and 
shipping rates, exacerbated by the El Niño weather phenomenon. In realized, and it filters 
into core inflation, then 2x rate hike priced into markets may prove to be too low which could 
create a headwind to stocks. That said, with the AI theme intact and neutral / light 
positioning this is a market where buying the dip is likely to remain profitable. Clarity around 
the Fed’s reaction function, inflation outlook, and stabilizing commodity prices are enough to 
reignite the bull run into year-end.
