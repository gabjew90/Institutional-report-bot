Gold is stuck
Gold has bounced over the past two sessions, but it remains a rather dull asset. The shiny metal is
trading at exactly the same levels as in late March. We have seen a few attempted breakouts in both
directions, but there is simply no medium-term trend. Also worth noting: gold remains below its 200-
day MA.
LSEG Workspace
The dollar connection
Trading gold has basically meant trading the dollar this year. The dollar “overshoot” (inverted) has now
been reset following the FOMC.
LSEG Workspace
Hikes slow, rather than derail
The dollar is not gold’s only near-term problem. Higher rates also weigh on rate-sensitive ETF demand.
Goldman argues that the latest Fed tightening should slow the appreciation path rather than alter the
longer-term destination.
Importantly, much of the expected tightening already appears reflected in ETF holdings. GS still sees
gold grinding toward $4,650 by year-end, versus around $4,350 currently.

The buyer that does not care
ETF investors may care about rates and the dollar. Central banks apparently do not.
Goldman’s nowcast has central-bank purchases running at around 91 tonnes per month on a three-
month seasonally adjusted basis, compared with a pre-2022 average of just 17 tonnes. GS has
consequently raised its assumed buying pace for 2026–27.
This is the structural bid beneath the tactical noise.
GS
Convexity is still there
Despite the dull price action, demand for gold calls remains around three times historical averages.
This matters because elevated call positioning increases the sensitivity of prices to any fresh demand.
Should the dollar roll over and gold finally break higher, dealer hedging could turn an initially modest
move into something much more aggressive.
GS
Stuck, not broken
The tactical setup remains uninspiring. Gold is below the 200-day MA, the dollar is acting as a
headwind and no convincing medium-term trend has emerged.
The structural setup is much stronger. Central-bank demand continues to raise the floor, while
elevated call positioning leaves considerable upside convexity if the dollar turns lower.
Gold is not a chase while technically stuck, but the eventual breakout could be anything but dull.
