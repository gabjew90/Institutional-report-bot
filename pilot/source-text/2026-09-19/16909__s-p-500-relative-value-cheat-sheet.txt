Some positive signs for equities, TMT out of “penalty box” 
We nudged up our year-end target to 7400 this week and launched a 12m target of 
7800, modest returns from here. Multiples have compressed for a good reason – higher 
earnings. But current multiples imply much lower inflation than we are likely to achieve 
in the near term, and we see elevated sell-off risk into year end. We are encouraged by 
the Fed’s focus on inflation, as higher inflation/long rates are anathema to longer 
duration growth stocks which make up a decent chunk of the S&P 500. We here upgrade 
Communication Services, dominated by two hyperscalers, on better macro and micro. 
Tech thesis played out: AI capex takers beat spenders 
We see balanced risk/reward between Info Tech (market-weight) and Comm Services 
today as investors now worry about cuts or stalled AI capex. This is not our view, but our 
core thesis on Comm Svcs has largely played out – we downgraded it to uw and raised 
Info Tech to mw in mid-2025 on risks around increasing capital intensity, leverage and 
opacity of the hyperscalers v. sales growth potential in chips and hardware. Info Tech 
has since outperformed Comm Services, where derating on asset intensity and financial 
leverage have pushed Comm Svcs to near-historical lows (4th percentile) in relative PE 
and removed crowding risks. It has moved up in our momentum/value model (Exhibit 4) 
but still has a low price momentum rank given recent underperformance.  
Cloud sales > AI spend in 2H27, productivity surge 
Cloud revenue growth is forecast to outpace AI spend by 2H 2027, barring another large 
upward revision to AI capex in 2027. Communication Services has seen stronger 
improvement in productivity than either IT or the S&P 500. Long-term growth 
expectations (a contrary signal) are at all-time highs for Info Tech but are in-line with 
market expectations for Comm Services, suggesting less risk of disappointment.  
Debt / rate risk manageable for now 
Fed hikes have hurt Telecom Services more than any other sector historically, but this 
group is a small fraction of today’s Comm. Svcs sector. Leverage has increased for the 
two biggest companies in the sector, but is termed out longer than S&P 500’s average 
debt and at lower interest rates due to hyperscalers’ opportunistic capital raises amid 
the 2025/26 window of tight credit spreads, lower rates and strong appetite for 
growth/AI. Financing needed over the next few years will increase this burden and raise 
capital costs, but our IG strategists see debt capital needs as manageable. 
Risks: supply, off balance sheet risk, transparency… 
Supply coming online via private company IPOs has been delayed but is another 
overhang from a competition perspective – our analysis of holdings changes around the 
SpaceX IPO provides evidence that investors sell old stuff to buy shiny new things 
(note). Off-balance sheet commitments for hyperscalers give us pause: financial leverage 
has risen gradually, but off-balance sheet future commitments have taken off. Evidence 
of less earnings transparency, perhaps from one-time items/investment gains is starkly 
evident in the divergence between free cash and earnings for Hyperscalers (see Clouds 
forming note).

Raise Comm Svcs to marketweight 
We raise Communication Services to marketweight from underweight as valuations have 
become more attractive, alongside productivity gains and improving visibility into AI 
monetization (Google & Meta are 78% of the sector weight). Communication Services 
has de-rated to near its cheapest relative forward P/E (Exhibit 4), while inflation-adjusted 
sales per employee are higher and growing faster than both the S&P 500 and Info Tech 
(Exhibit 5). Hyperscaler multiples have also compressed with stronger earnings growth 
and remain inexpensive relative to Info Tech (Exhibit 6–Exhibit 7). 
The AI investment cycle remains the key risk, but the gap between Hyperscaler capex 
growth and cloud revenue growth is expected to narrow. Cloud revenue growth is 
forecast to outpace capex growth in 2027, showing monetization is not too far from 
catching up with the spend (Exhibit 8).

Closing out our 2025 thesis  
In June 2025, we lowered Communication Services to underweight (see 2025 sector swap 
note) calling out peak crowding, margin cyclicality and EPS volatility. Today, crowding has 
come down from its 2025 peak, EBITDA margins are elevated but still reasonable (Exhibit 
19), and earnings volatility has moderated (Exhibit 20). The sector is up 4% YTD (well 
below the S&P 500’s +12%) but better valuation and the normalization in crowding and 
earnings risk suggests the upside/downside risk is more balanced.  
Crowding has neutralized 
Long only (LO) funds continue to be O/W Comm. Svcs but their relative weight is down to 
1.26 from the 2025 peak of 1.37. We see positioning as mostly neutral (not necessarily 
bullish) as it has retreated below its average of 1.29 since 2019. LOs’ relative exposure to 
Hyperscalers is at 1.22, down from 1.34 in early 2025, while their Semis exposure is back 
to where it began the year at 1.15. Relative to their O/W in Semis, LOs’ O/W in 
Hyperscalers is at its lowest level since May 2023.

historical excess over Technology (Exhibit 20).

Opportunities, Value Traps and Momentum Breakdowns 
We classify industries based on their framework ranks as follows: 
• 
Opportunities are industries with above average (6 or higher) ranks in all three 
ranks. 
• 
Value Traps are industries that have above average ranks in valuation (6 or higher) 
but are in the bottom tertile in price momentum and earnings momentum (ranks 1-
3). 
• 
Momentum Breakdowns are industries with below average ranks in valuation (4 or 
lower), lower price momentum ranks than in the previous month, and below average 
earnings momentum ranks (4 or lower).

Traps, and Momentum Breakdowns as defined above.

Methodology 
Definitions of valuation metrics: 
• 
Price/Book: month-end market cap divided by latest total common equity.  
• 
Price/Operating Cash Flow: Month-end market cap divided by the last twelve 
months cash flow from operations as of the most recent fiscal quarter. 
Note: All industries’ multiples are relative to the S&P 500 ex. Fins. for P/OCF. 
• 
Forward Price/Earnings: Month-end market cap divided by I/B/E/S consensus next 
twelve months earnings. 
Implied upside is based on comparing the current relative multiple vs the historical 
average relative multiple. Industries with <10 yrs of data history are excluded. Boxes 
highlight sectors/industries with the highest greatest upside and implied downside. 
Momentum & Value Model 
Our tactical “momentum and value” framework was backtested from January 1997-
December 2004, and has been running live since January 2005. We calculate price 
momentum, earnings momentum and valuation for the S&P 500 GICS sectors and 
industries relative to the index beginning in January 1997. We exclude industries that 
have only one company or have less than five years of historical price data. For our 
backtest, we have used as a market benchmark the portfolio of equal-weighted S&P 500 
industries that were eligible for the framework at a given point in time.  
Price momentum 
The price momentum of an industry is the change in the industry’s relative price in the 
current month vs three months ago, with relative price defined as the month-end industry 
price dividend by the month-end index level of the S&P 500. Industries are assigned a 
price momentum rank from 1 to 10, with 10 being the strongest price momentum and 1 
being the weakest. Sectors are assigned a price momentum rank from 1 to 11, with 11 
being the strongest price momentum and 1 being the weakest.  
Earnings momentum 
The earnings momentum of an industry is the change in relative forecast EPS between 
the current month and three months ago. Relative EPS is the ratio of the 12-month 
rolling forward I/B/E/S consensus earnings forecast of a given industry divided by that of 
the S&P 500. Industries are assigned an earnings revision rank of 1 to 10, with 10 being 
the strongest earnings momentum and 1 being the weakest. Sectors are assigned an 
earnings revision rank of 1 to 11, with 11 being the strongest earnings momentum and 1 
being the weakest. 
Valuation 
We calculate the earnings yield (E/P) of an industry as the ratio of current relative forward 
(consensus) EPS divided by current relative price. We then calculate the earnings yield 
relative to its historical average by dividing the current E/P by the average E/P for the 
industry since the framework’s inception in January 1997. For industries introduced after 
the inception date, at least one year of data is required before an industry is eligible for a 
rank. Industries are assigned a valuation rank from 1 to 10, with 10 being the highest 
relative earnings yield (most inexpensive) and 1 being the lowest relative earnings yield 
(most expensive). Sectors are assigned a valuation rank from 1 to 11, with 11 being the 
highest relative earnings yield (most inexpensive) and 1 being the lowest relative earnings 
yield (most expensive). Current data is compared to this average using the following 
formula: (Current E/P – Historical Avg. E/P) / Abs (Historical Avg. E/P). We calculate both 
the current and historical average relative to the market because of P/E multiple 
expansion in the market, and use earnings yield (inverse of P/E) to accommodate 
industries which have negative aggregate earnings.

Combined rank 
The combined rank of an industry is the sum of the three ranks from the price 
momentum, earnings momentum, and valuation factors above, with 30 ranking the best 
and 3 ranking the worst. For sectors, 33 ranks the best and 3 ranks the worst.  
 
Bear market signposts  
Our bear market signposts highlight 10 signals that have generally preceded market 
peaks. See descriptions below and FAQs: How do bull markets end? for more. 
• 
Conference Board Consumer Confidence: Consumer Confidence hits 110 within 
the last six months. 
• 
Conference Board Stock Price Expectations: % of respondents expecting higher 
stock prices over the next 12 months minus the % of respondents expecting lower 
stock prices over the next 12 months hits >20ppt within the last six months.  
• 
Sell Side Indicator: Sell Side Indicator (BofA’s proprietary sentiment metric 
measuring Wall Street’s average equity allocation) is in “Sell” territory within the 
last six months. 
• 
Long-term Growth Expectations (LTG): The S&P 500’s LTG expectation (based 
on a bottom-up aggregation of sell side analyst estimates) is >1 standard deviation 
above its five-year average. 
• 
M&A: The number of M&A deals (3m trailing sum) is >1 standard deviation above 
the 10-year average within the last six months.  
• 
P/E + CPI: The sum of the S&P 500’s trailing PE and YoY CPI is at least one 
standard deviation above the 10yr average.  
• 
Cheap vs. expensive stocks: Low Fwd PE stocks (bottom decile in S&P 500) 
underperforms High Fwd PE (top decile in S&P 500) by at least 2.5ppt over the prior 
6 months. 
• 
Yield curve: Inverted yield curve (2s10s) in the last six months.  
• 
Credit stress Indicator: BofA’s Credit Stress Indicator (CSI) drops below 0.25 
within the last six months, indicating excessively loose credit conditions.  
• 
SLOOS: Banks tightening lending standards based on the Senior Loan Officer 
Opinion Survey’s data on the percentage of banks tightening commercial and 
industrial loans to large firms.
