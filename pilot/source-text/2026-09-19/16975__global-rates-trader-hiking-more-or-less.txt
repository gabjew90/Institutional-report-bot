The Fed’s inflation-focused message should reinforce a flattening environment over 
the near-term. The strong investment and labor market backdrop is likely to keep 
longer-term yields in a higher range, but it will be difficult to sustain a return to 
steepening until after the market gains clarity on the extent of rate hikes—which 
typically only occurs around the time of the last hike. Rallies are likely to be the lower 
vol outcome for the time being, and we tactically prefer selling vol on longer tenors 
over duration exposure. The hawkish ECB reaction function to energy price moves 
offers a degree of protection to belly forwards. We think this flattening dynamic 
continues in the near-term, with energy still the key uncertainty—we recommend 
long EUR 1y1y vs CHF. The BoE’s Gilt-friendly QT plans helped bull-flatten the UK 
curve, but the incremental policy signals point to a November hike. This should keep 
flattening front-end gaps like M7M8. The message that accompanied the BoJ’s 25bp 
rate hike fell short of the market’s hawkish expectations; given front-end pricing and 
the relative richness of 5s on the curve, we favor paying 5y JPY on the 2s5s10s fly. 
United States and Canada 
A sense of stability, if not relief. The Fed accompanied its unanimous decision 
n
to hike with a set of more hawkish signals—the number of projections indicating 
at least one more rate hike, the higher median dot further out the forecast 
horizon, and Chair Warsh’s repeated indication that the starting point was not 
restrictive. Our economists now expect an additional rate hike in October. The 
clearer, inflation-focused Fed message solidifies a flattening backdrop in the 
near-term, but was not so hawkish as to clearly argue for starting to price growth 
downside into the forwards. Instead, we think that some of the risks surrounding 
the policy approach have diminished, but the broader backdrop of strong 
investment spending and a stable labor market remain in place, entrenching 
longer term yields in a higher range. In the near-term we think rallies are likely to 
be associated with lower volatility. Lower yields are likely to come more from the 
belly of the curve as the asymmetry to additional hikes is a limit on very 
front-end rallies and a headwind to generalized steepening absent a shift in the 
growth picture, which leaves risks around our UST yield forecasts tilted higher 
and flatter. With oil likely to maintain its influence over day-to-day moves 
(Exhibit 1), we think short vol expressions further out the curve (below) are 
preferable to outright duration exposure for the time being.

The high-water mark around hikes. Fed pricing over the next year implies a path 
n
that sits not only above our economists’ baseline, but above the hawkish side of the 
range of survey estimates and the Fed dots, implying roughly three more hikes by the 
middle of 2027. While we think market pricing is towards the hawkish end of the 
likely distribution of outcomes, history shows it is unusual for front-end forwards to 
peak meaningfully ahead of the end of rate hikes, with the 2015 cycle the notable 
exception for forwards more broadly and the 2022 cycle the exception for the very 
front-end (due to the regional banking shock). In 1997 and 1999—hiking cycles that 
also began from higher starting points—the peak in rates during the window 
between the first hike and three months after the last move came about a month 
after the lone hike in 1997, and was more or less coincident with the last hike of the 
series that began in 1999. Also notable is that in the three months following the peak 
in front-end forwards, rallies tend to be relatively parallel in nature, with the belly 
modestly outperforming on average (Exhibit 2). While we think that the longer-term 
case for lower yields depends on a shift in the policy path and leans towards a 
steeper curve, history suggests the case for steepening is weak while markets 
grapple with the eventual endpoint of hikes, and positioning for dovish relief is 
ultimately best done on an outright basis.

Mixed dynamics behind an easier funding backdrop. Funding conditions have 
n
remained benign through the September seasonal TGA increase, supported by 
negative bill supply locally and subdued demand for funding from levered funds, 
with an ongoing reduction in levered investor short futures positions. Against that, 
money fund AUM growth has been sluggish, while WAM has been stable after 
declining through the summer, the mix of which has seen the 1m/3m bills-OIS curve 
move towards the steeper end of its range (i.e. 3m bills trading relatively cheaper). 
Shorter money fund WAM likely reflects hike pricing and near-term policy 
uncertainty, consistent with the typical pattern where more slope (either positive or 
negative) at the front-end of the curve tends to bias WAM lower (Exhibit 6). That 
said, currently WAM remains well above the lows of the previous hiking cycle, when a 
much steeper curve corresponded to more defensive money fund positioning. If 
markets refrain from adding additional near-term hikes—or even take out some hike 
pricing beyond October—a flatter bills curve should see modest WAM lengthening. 
AUM growth, in contrast, could remain subdued if risk asset returns remain firm, 
though a flatter overall yield curve bodes more favorably. Overall, we expect benign 
funding conditions to persist against this backdrop, even after accounting for the 
marginal impact to bill supply from expanded buybacks and current absence of Fed 
reserve management purchases.

BoE announces Gilt-Friendly QT Plan. Yesterday, the BoE proposed continuing to 
n
reduce its APF Gilt holdings, while ending active sales to the private sector, subject to 
a final decision by April 2027. APF holdings would fall from £488bn to £120bn by 
2034, with £222bn of shorter bonds running off passively, £146bn of 8-22y Gilts 
sold to the government at £20bn per year, and the longest-dated £120bn retained 
to back banknote issuance. Although the £46bn annual reduction is faster than 
surveyed expectations (Exhibit 10), we think this will be outweighed by the removal 
of private-sector duration supply, particularly at the long end. The QT process will 
continue to supply bonds to the market, but now via the DMO’s regular issuance plan 
rather than APF auctions. The initial rally and swap-spread widening appear 
justified: we estimate that pausing private-sector sales and the potential maturity 
transformation through DMO issuance are together worth around 4bp of Gilt 
richening on swap, with the cessation of long-end sales potentially offering further 
upside, although balanced by the faster-than-expected QT pace and longer QT 
horizon. The plan should compress Gilt-specific risk premium and reinforce our bias 
for a flatter curve and lower long-end yields.

Japan 
Falling short of the mark. The BoJ meeting fell short of the high bar markets had 
n
priced for the policy path. Though the statement leaned hawkish, market attention 
centered on the two dissents favoring a hold. While the BoJ clearly intends to raise 
rates further and signaled a shift in the case for hikes towards controlling price 
pressures rather than normalization, the absence of a clear signal toward faster hikes 
disappointed expectations. Markets also appeared to interpret the dissents as a 
challenge to further tightening given the two dissents came PM Takaichi appointees, 
drawing attention to the two board seats whose terms expire in July 2027 (one of 
which is Board Member Takata, who had recently suggested larger or sequential 
moves could be necessary). Even so, markets continue to price a hawkish BoJ 
trajectory, and JGB curves have generally maintained the shift flatter as cheapening 
has continued its migration into the belly. With front-end policy pricing still on the 
hawkish side of what we think is a likely near-term path, we recommend paying 5s on 
the 2s5s10s fly (entry = -8.6bps, target = +2bps, stop = -13bps) to position for 
behavior more typical of global curves where 5s bear the brunt of 
underperformance.
