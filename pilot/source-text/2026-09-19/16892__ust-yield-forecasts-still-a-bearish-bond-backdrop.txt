UST yield forecasts – Still a bearish bond backdrop  
 
• The recent Fed hike may help long-end UST yields to stabilise, but only in the near term 
• Short end is likely overpricing further tightening, but may push back (rather than price out) 2027 hikes  
• We raise our 2Y yield forecasts to 4.8% from 4.1% for end-2026 and 4.6% from 4.0% for end-Q3-2027 
• Long-end UST negatives such as AI-related supply, fiscal concerns, Japanese repatriation risks persist 
• We raise our end-2026 10Y yield forecast to 5.1% (from 4.6%), see it reaching 5.5% in next 12 months 
 
A 10Y yield of 5% may be a staging post, not a destination 
We have been bearish on the long end of the UST curve all year, but in recent weeks 
the benchmark 10Y yield has run ahead of the uptrend we had forecast – largely 
because of a more hawkish shift in the Fed outlook than we expected. Following the 
25bps hike at the 16 September FOMC meeting, we raised our terminal Fed rate 
forecast by 50bps, with another hike expected in December (see FOMC – Moderate 
statement, hawkish presser). In line with this, and given our view that several long-end 
negative factors are still in play, we revise our yield forecasts higher across all time 
horizons (Figure 1).   
Going forward, the short end of the curve may be relatively protected by the overall 
tightening already priced in – around 50bps more than we or the median FOMC dots 
project. But until the market is more confident that inflation will return to target, we 
expect rate-hike pricing to be pushed back rather than materially reduced. As a result, 
the 2Y yield may peak only after the expected December rate hike. We raise our 2Y 
yield forecasts to 4.8% from 4.1% for end-2026 and 4.6% from 4.0% for end-Q3-2027.
The recent curve flattening may remain largely in place through the December hike, 
but we think long-end yields will lead the curve steeper again in 2027. Key factors 
behind the bearish long-end bond backdrop – including AI-related investment and 
issuance, US fiscal concerns, and Japanese repatriation risks – remain either active 
or potential challenges. Even if the impact of oil prices on yields eventually fades, 
additional risk premium for inflation uncertainty may be required at the long end. 
Accordingly, we raise our 10Y UST yield forecasts to 5.1% (from 4.6%) for end-Q4-
2026, and we see scope for the yield to reach 5.5% over the next 12 months.
