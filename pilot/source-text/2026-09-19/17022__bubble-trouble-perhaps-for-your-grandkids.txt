One day they will matter
Q3 earnings season is almost here, and we all know the script: beat, raise, hallelujah, rally. Never bet
against Corporate America, bears go to die, and by week two you are wondering why you ever shorted
a stock in the first place. Fair enough. But before the earnings machine gets going again, let us indulge
in a little bubble trouble. None of the charts below says the party ends tomorrow. In fact, it doesn't. But
earnings are stretched versus history, AI is doing an extraordinary amount of the heavy lifting and
margins are sitting in increasingly rarefied air. These are the charts that don't matter — until one day
they suddenly do.
The overshoot
S&P 500 EPS has surged to 14% above its 90+ year trend channel after consecutive quarters of
exceptionally strong earnings growth.
DB
Not just one measure
Same message, shorter timeframe. Real S&P 500 earnings are running well above their 30-year trend.
FactSet
Earnings > economy
The divergence isn't just versus history. Actual S&P 500 EPS growth is also running well ahead of what
real GDP would imply.

Super-cycle margins
Then there are semis. Strong demand and constrained supply have sent industry gross margins to
multi-decade highs, with memory margins around 80% — more than double their historical average.
Consensus expects the good times to continue.

Perfection isn't just tech
And zooming out doesn't exactly make things look cheap in earnings terms: profit margins for the

None of this says earnings are about to collapse. It says the bar is exceptionally high: huge AI
spending, extraordinary semiconductor economics and margins near records.
The bears' last hope? When perfection becomes the baseline, you don't need disaster to create
trouble.
