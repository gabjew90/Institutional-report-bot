Narrowing breadth: warning signal or healthy rotation?
Factory Daily letter · 18 Sep 2026 · 12 min read
The bull market is not yet broken, but tighter policy, narrow leadership and deteriorating participation leave the market increasingly
vulnerable to technical setbacks.
Written by
Trading Strategy, Pictet Trading & Sales

US equity resilience masks breadth deterioration under the surface
The Federal Reserve’s (Fed) September rate increase leaves US equities confronting a less forgiving policy environment at a time when the
S&P 500’s internal foundations are already weakening. The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) raised the federal funds target range by
25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00% yesterday, citing elevated inflation alongside solid economic activity, resilient domestic spending and robust
capital investment. Updated policy projections also leave scope for further tightening before year-end.
The immediate equity-market issue is not that the primary bull trend has already broken. The S&P 500 remains above its rising 200-day
moving average and close to record territory. Rather, the post-FOMC backdrop increases the significance of a deterioration that was already
visible beneath the headline index. Fewer stocks are participating in the advance, approximately one-third of constituents are at least 20%
below their respective peaks, and weakness is concentrated in several sectors that previously drove market leadership.


[Page 2]
This divergence has left the market increasingly dependent on a narrow group of heavily weighted companies. Such leadership can persist,
particularly while earnings growth remains robust. However, a higher policy rate and the prospect of additional tightening reduce the
tolerance for elevated valuations and increase the importance of earnings delivery, balance-sheet quality and cash-flow durability. The risk is
therefore not simply that weaker constituents continue to fall; the more consequential threat is that the correction spreads to the dominant
stocks still supporting the capitalisation-weighted benchmark.
Importantly, however, and as we will discuss today, deteriorating breadth does not necessarily imply that the broader bull market has run its
course. As long as earnings growth remains robust, a narrowing of leadership may prove more indicative of rotation than outright
liquidation. The key question is therefore whether weakness spreads across the market indiscriminately, or whether leadership evolves as
investors place greater emphasis on earnings quality, balance-sheet strength and valuation discipline. 
The bull market remains intact, but participation is weakening
The S&P 500 remains supported by its long-term trend, yet a growing share of constituents is already undergoing a substantial correction.
Historical evidence argues for short-term caution without implying that a major bear market is inevitable. The index remains close to record
territory, but the strength of the headline benchmark masks a material deterioration beneath the surface. Fewer stocks are participating in
the advance, a rising share of constituents has entered bear-market territory and weakness is concentrated in several former leadership
groups.
This divergence does not, by itself, signal the end of the bull market. Importantly, breadth deterioration does not necessarily imply that the
broader uptrend has run its course. As long as earnings growth remains resilient, narrowing participation may prove more indicative of
rotation beneath the surface than outright liquidation. The capitalisation-weighted index remains above its rising 200-day moving average
and conditions remain far removed from the indiscriminate selling typically associated with systemic capitulation. Nevertheless, the internal
evidence currently points to a less resilient market increasingly dependent on a narrow group of large-cap leaders. The key question is
whether participation can stabilise and broaden before weakness spreads to the stocks still supporting the benchmark.
The first participation warning comes from the divergence between the S&P 500 and its advance-decline line
While the index continues to trade near 7,586, not far from its recent high, the advance-decline line has fallen sharply from its July peak to
approximately 1,462. The breadth measure has broken well below its previous high and the former reference area near 2,020, suggesting that
an increasing number of constituents are declining even as the headline index remains relatively resilient.
Such divergences have historically served as warnings rather than automatic reversal signals. Narrow leadership can persist, particularly
when earnings remain supportive. However, a market supported by fewer stocks becomes increasingly vulnerable to disappointment.
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 17/09/2026.
Almost one-third of the S&P 500 has slipped into bear-market territory, even as the headline index remains close to record highs


[Page 3]
The share of index members trading at least 20% below their respective peaks has risen to around 32%, signalling that the correction beneath
the surface is no longer confined to a handful of names. This represents a meaningful deterioration in market participation, although it
remains below the levels reached during previous episodes of severe market stress, including 52.8% in February 2016, 56.0% in December
2018, 92.9% in March 2020, 72.0% in October 2022 and 53.7% in April 2025. The distinction matters: a reading near 32% points to broadening
weakness, but remains some distance from the levels typically associated with capitulation.
For now, the market occupies an uneasy middle ground. Internal damage is too widespread to dismiss, yet not severe enough to indicate a
broad washout. A sustained move above 40%, and particularly beyond 50%, would suggest a more advanced deterioration in breadth and
accordingly would materially raise the risk of a deeper market correction.
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 17/09/2026.
Sector-level stress has moderated slightly but remains elevated
The proportion of Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS) sectors with more than 30% of their constituents in bear-market territory
has fallen to approximately 27%, after briefly rising above 50% during the latest bout of market weakness. This suggests that selling pressure
is no longer broadening at the same pace, although the indicator remains above the low-stress readings typically associated with strong,
broad-based participation.
The current configuration is consistent with uneven market leadership rather than systemic capitulation. A sustained decline below the
recent 10%-20% range would point to improving participation across sectors. Conversely, a renewed rise above 35%, and especially beyond
50%, would suggest that internal weakness is broadening once again and reinforce the negative message from other breadth indicators.
The easing in sector stress does not contradict the deterioration in aggregate breadth. Rather, it suggests weakness has become more
concentrated. Fewer sectors now meet the broad-stress criterion, but those that do still contain a high proportion of stocks in bear-market
territory. This remains particularly relevant given the significant benchmark weights of the Technology, Consumer Discretionary and
Communication Services sectors.

Bear-market participation is highest in Technology, Consumer Discretionary and Industrials
The headline breadth data conceal significant sector dispersion. Information Technology shows the greatest internal damage, with 56.2% of
constituents in bear-market territory. Consumer Discretionary follows at 51.1%, while Industrials and Communication Services stand at
43.4% and 37.5%, respectively.
All four readings exceed the S&P 500 average of 32.2%. This concentration matters because Technology, Consumer Discretionary and
Communication Services contain several of the market's largest and most influential companies. Persistent weakness in these sectors could
therefore become increasingly visible in the capitalisation-weighted benchmark.By contrast, Real Estate, Energy, Financials and Utilities
remain considerably more resilient, with only 13.3%-16.1% of constituents in bear-market territory. Consumer Staples, Healthcare and
Materials occupy an intermediate position, with readings between 23.5% and 28.0%.
The dispersion suggests the market is undergoing a rotation rather than an indiscriminate liquidation. Defensive, value-oriented and
selected real-asset sectors continue to display greater resilience than the former growth leaders. While this development may ultimately
prove constructive, it has not yet generated sufficient participation to offset the deterioration in the larger growth sectors.

The tech sector remains the principal source of market fragility
The breadth deterioration within Information Technology remains especially important. Approximately 56.2% of sector constituents are
trading at least 20% below their respective highs. While this has eased from a recent peak of 65.8%, the reading remains historically elevated.
It also exceeds the 54.5% peak recorded in 2018 and remains only moderately below the 62.7% level reached in 2015. Although still well below
the capitulation extremes of 94.7% in 2020 and more than 90% in 2022, the comparison confirms that the sector correction is already
extensive at the constituent level. The recent decline from 65.8% to 56.2% therefore represents an initial stabilisation rather than a restoration
of healthy leadership.
A sustained move below 40% would provide stronger evidence of internal repair, while a decline into the 20%-30% range would indicate that
a broader group of technology stocks is recovering. Conversely, a renewed rise above 65.8% would suggest that selling pressure is intensifying
again. A move beyond the 71% level reached during the 2024 correction would point to a more advanced deterioration in breadth and
increase the risk that weakness among second-tier technology companies spreads to the sector's largest constituents.
This creates an important asymmetry for the broader market: the S&P 500 can remain resilient while a large number of smaller technology
stocks decline, provided the largest index weights continue to hold up. If those dominant names begin to participate in the correction,
however, the headline benchmark could adjust much more rapidly.

Our own indicator has also entered warning territory
The PTS Market Environment Indicator (MEI) has fallen below its 5-point warning threshold, with only three of its eight bullish conditions
currently satisfied. The S&P 500 remains above a rising 200-day moving average and correction breadth is still positive, but MA200 breadth,
both McClellan signals, net new highs and the higher-high/higher-low structure have weakened.
Historically, periods in which fewer than five of the eight bullish conditions were satisfied have been associated with lower absolute returns
than a buy-and-hold approach, but materially lower risk. Annualised volatility declined from 18.1% to 9.9%, while maximum drawdown
improved from -56.8% to -21.3%.The current reading should therefore be viewed as a risk-management warning rather than any standalone
signal to exit equities. A recovery above the 5-point threshold, driven by improving breadth and momentum, would indicate a more
supportive market environment. Conversely, a further deterioration in the number of satisfied conditions would strengthen the risk-off
message.

The technical configuration reflects a fragile bull market, with 7,492 defining the immediate risk
The US equity market remains structurally constructive but increasingly dependent on narrow leadership. The S&P 500 continues to trade
above its rising 200-day SMA, preserving the primary bull trend, while the longer-term Elliott Wave structure remains consistent with an
extension towards 8,218 and potentially 8,747.
Beneath the surface, however, participation has deteriorated materially. The advance-decline line is diverging negatively, approximately 32%
of constituents are in bear-market territory, and weakness remains concentrated in Technology, Consumer Discretionary, Industrials and
Communication Services. The PTS Market Environment Indicator's move below its warning threshold reinforces the view that the near-term
environment has become less supportive.
The key technical battleground is now 7,492. Holding this level would preserve the possibility of another advance towards 7,938-8,022 and
potentially 8,218. A decisive break below 7,492 would expose 7,314 and the rising 200-day SMA near 7,175.
The market should therefore be viewed as structurally bullish, tactically corrective and internally fragile. As long as earnings remain
supportive, the current backdrop remains more consistent with rotation and narrowing participation than a wholesale breakdown in the
broader uptrend.
The current pullback remains broadly consistent with our previously published scenario, which envisaged a corrective phase before
the bull market resumes into November
A successful defence of 7,492, supported by a reversal in RSI trendline support, would preserve this roadmap and support a recovery back
above the 50-day SMA near 7,612.
A subsequent break through 7,688-7,744 would reopen the path towards 7,938-8,022 and the extended objective near 8,218. Conversely, a
decisive break below 7,492 would confirm that the correction is extending, exposing 7,314 and potentially the rising 200-day SMA near 7,175.
While such an outcome could delay the expected recovery, it would not in itself invalidate the primary bull trend.

Higher yields may test both AI leadership and rate-sensitive sectors
As we discussed yesterday (again here), the AI investment cycle and stronger earnings growth have raised the threshold at which higher yields
begin to exert more meaningful pressure on equity valuations. With the US 10-year yield now around 5% and global yields remaining
elevated, that threshold may increasingly be tested, while the most immediate vulnerability remains the market's dependence on a narrow
group of AI-related leaders: any moderation in capital spending, earnings expectations or productivity optimism could have an outsized
impact on the capitalisation-weighted benchmark. More broadly, a deterioration in breadth among rate-sensitive sectors, including small
caps, real estate, homebuilders and more leveraged companies, would suggest that higher borrowing costs are beginning to affect the wider
earnings and financing environment rather than valuations alone.
Technically, 7,492 remains the pivotal level for the S&P 500. Holding this support would preserve the correction-then-recovery scenario
outlined above, while a break below it would increase the risk of a deeper pullback towards 7,314 and potentially the rising 200-day SMA near
7,175.
For now, the evidence points to a market that is becoming more selective rather than one that has entered a broader bear phase. As long as
earnings remain supportive, narrowing breadth may prove more indicative of rotation than outright liquidation. Nevertheless, further
deterioration in participation, particularly within rate-sensitive sectors or among the largest technology names, would be an important
warning that the market's tolerance for higher yields is approaching its limits.

The Exposure Optimisation Model
The PTS Exposure Optimisation Model (EOM) is a proprietary model
designed to give tactical signals on the prevailing short-term regional
equity market regime. The model is applied to each market on a mutually-
exclusive basis, with output expressed by way of exposure scores (US;
Europe; Japan and EM); in each case an average of underlying factors scores
with dynamic weighting applied according to the prevailing structural
market volatility regime. Exposure score ratings are: very bullish (80-100%);
bullish (60-80%); neutral (40-60%); bearish (20-40%); and very bearish (20-
0%). Factors are as follows:
Regime: A combination of technical indicators, moving averages and a
Constant Proportion Portfolio Insurance (CPPI) strategy, the regime score is
designed to reflect short-term market momentum in the region;
Valuation: Built around the forward price-to-sales ratio, the dynamic valuation
score is based on a calculation that is re-set on bull/bear market regime
changes and calibrated to facilitate regional comparison;
Economics: The economics score draws on real economy activity data (i.e.
heavy truck sales and shipping traffic), economic signals from financial markets
(i.e. yield-curves) and medium-term economic indicators (i.e. PMIs and
unemployment data); 
Flows: The flows score draws upon both active and passive weekly EPFR fund
flows data giving us an indication of risk appetite going into the week ahead; 
Market Sentiment: The market sentiment score is built around classic regional
market risk indices (i.e. VIX; ARMS) as well as soft survey data (i.e. U.of
Michigan Consumer Sentiment). The scoring system is applied in a similar
fashion at both regional and market level, while at sector level we refer only to
the Regime, Flows and Valuation factors.

Technical Analysis
The technical analysis used in this presentation combines traditional
technical tools: graphical analysis (trend lines, support lines, continuation
and reversal patterns) which determines the tendency, mathematical
indicators (moving averages, RSI, MACD) used as numeric filters and Elliot
wave theory which allows us to build a scenario with target levels and
invalidation points. We also occasionally refer to Ichimoku Cloud Technical
Analysis. 
Elliott Wave Theory: According to Elliott Wave Theory, markets move in
impulse waves – with five sub-waves (numbered 1-5 or I-V) following the
direction of the main trend, followed by three corrective sub-waves (A-B-C)
(example below).  These waves follow a set of specific rules and are linked to
each other by target and retracement ratios based on the Fibonacci sequence,
and the characteristics of each wave form an integral part of the reflection of
the mass psychology it embodies.
Relative Strength Index (RSI): A momentum oscillator that measures the
speed and change of price movements. It ranges from 0 to 100 and is typically
used to identify overbought or oversold conditions in a market. An RSI above
70 suggests that a stock may be overbought, while an RSI below 30 indicates
that it may be oversold.
The Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD): A trend-following
momentum indicator that shows the relationship between two moving
averages. It is calculated by subtracting the 26-period Exponential Moving
Average (EMA) from the 12- period EMA. The result is the MACD line. A nine-
day EMA of the MACD, called the signal line, is then plotted on top of the
MACD line, which can function as a trigger for buy or sell signals. When the
MACD crosses above the signal line, it is typically considered a bullish signal,
and when it crosses below, it is considered bearish.
Ichimoku Cloud Technical Analysis: Ichimoku Cloud is a popular technical
analysis tool used to identify potential trends and support/resistance levels in
financial markets. It consists of several components, including the Kumo
(cloud), the Tenkan-sen (conversion line), the Kijun-sen (base line), and Senkou
Span (leading span).
