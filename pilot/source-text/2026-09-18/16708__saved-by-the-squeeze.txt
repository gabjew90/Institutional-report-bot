Saved by the squeeze
Yesterday’s post-Fed move put the systematic sell threat firmly back on the radar, with the S&P closing
less than 1.1% below Goldman’s key short-term CTA threshold. Then came today’s squeeze. With SPX
rallying roughly that amount, the positioning picture suddenly looks a lot less threatening. In other
words: yesterday’s looming CTA problem just got considerably smaller.
Not that long
Coming into today, Goldman estimated CTAs were long $37.5bn of US equities — just the 22nd
percentile on a one-year lookback.

Less scary already
Yesterday’s CTA estimates looked ugly: $5.4bn of SPX selling on a flat tape and a massive $54bn in a
down tape, versus $17bn of buying in an up tape. Today’s squeeze changes that math.
GS: “We anticipate today’s move will moderate some of these flows.”

Low leverage
Fundamental hedge funds are far from maxed out. Goldman Prime puts US fundamental L/S gross
leverage at the 31st percentile over the past year — and net leverage at just the 5th percentile.

Macro mavens sold aggressively
Finally, remember what happened before today’s squeeze: hedge funds aggressively dumped macro
products last week, with Goldman recording the largest dollar net selling since Liberation Day.
So the setup after today’s rip is rather different from where we started: systematic supply is becoming
less threatening, hedge-fund leverage remains low, sentiment is washed out — and plenty of risk has
already been taken off.
