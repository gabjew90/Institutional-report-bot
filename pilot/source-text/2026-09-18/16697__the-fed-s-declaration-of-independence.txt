The Fed's Declaration of Independence 
FOMC Post-Meeting Comment September 
2026 
 
 
Summary 
 Today the FOMC unanimously decided to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 
25 bps. 
 The Committee’s projections showed that they anticipate another hike before the end of the 
year, before remaining on hold through 2027. The first cut is expected in 2028. 
 The new set of economic projections essentially showed a new reaction function with a much 
higher policy rate trajectory needed for a similar inflation outcome. This was underlined by an 
upward revision of the neutral rate. 
 Warsh did not submit any projections himself and during the press conference he gave no 
forward guidance. 
 During the press conference Warsh admitted that the hike cannot address the supply shock 
that caused inflation, but he stressed that the Fed can prevent second-order effects. 
 In our view, the materialization of the second hike depends on the developments in the 
Middle East and the resilience of the US economy. Since this is a stagflationary shock, we still 
think it is more likely that this turns out to be a “one and done” hike, although we also still do 
not rule out that second hike. 
 Overall, the main message from today’s FOMC decision, projections and press conference is 
that the Committee is committed to defend its monetary policy independence.  
 In fact, the projections suggest that by the time that President Trump leaves office, the Fed’s 
policy rate will be higher than when Chair Warsh started. 
Introduction 
Today, Kevin Warsh passed his credibility test by hiking the Fed’s policy rate against the wishes of 
the White House. As seemed inevitable after last Friday’s disappointing CPI report, the FOMC 
raised the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 bps to 3.75-4.00%. In fact, the FOMC did 
so unanimously. After being all talk and no action at the previous meeting in July, they now 
delivered. According to the FOMC statement “today’s policy action will support a timelier return 
to the Committee’s 2 percent goal.”  
The projections reveal a new reaction function 
In fact, we got more than we bargained for. The new set of economic projections essentially 
showed a new reaction function with a much higher policy rate trajectory needed for a 
similar inflation outcome. This was underlined by an upward revision of the neutral rate. 
The Committee still expects headline and PCE inflation to end up in the final quarter of 2027 at 
the same place as in the June projections, but they think it now requires two rate hikes in 2026 
(including today’s) and remaining on hold through 2027. Only three months ago, the FOMC 
thought that they would hike once in 2026 and then cut once in 2027. The first expected cut has 
now been delayed to 2028. What’s more, they expect to remain above neutral in 2029 as well.

Finally, even the neutral rate was revised upward to 3.2% from 3.1%. Overall, this shows that the 
Fed now thinks that stronger and sustained monetary tightening is needed to get inflation back to 
target.  
With PCE projections for 2027 identical to the June projections, but the federal funds rate 50 bps 
higher, we could even say that the FOMC has changed its reaction function. Or should all change 
be attributed to the lower unemployment rate projections? Probably not, listening to Warsh’s 
press conference this was clearly about the sticky inflation process itself. In fact, he said that the 
unemployment rate (4.1% in August) is basically consistent with full employment. We would add 
that in any linear Taylor this means that policy rate changes can only be attributed to inflation or 
the neutral rate, given that the FOMC expects the unemployment to remain unchanged in the 
coming years.

A short press conference reflecting Jackson Hole 
The press conference lasted only 29 minutes, about 15 minutes shorter than usual. Warsh referred 
to his Jackson Hole speech several times. His standard that “we must be confident that underlying 
inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed” has not been satisfied he said 
and therefore they raised rates. The Committee agreed with Warsh’s assessment that he would be 
hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive. 
Warsh stressed that the economy has been resilient given the geopolitical circumstances and that 
they play a major role in the inflation outlook. Of course, he did not mention that US foreign 
policy plays a non-negligible role in this. Warsh did not give any forward guidance and declined 
to comment on his talks with President Trump. Asked whether the markets pressured him into 
hiking, he said that the Fed observes what markets have to say, but “today was our decision”, 
based on the economy.

Conclusion 
The new reaction function implied by the FOMC projections also means we have to 
substantially adjust our forecasts for the federal funds rate.  
The FOMC projections are in line with our view that a new hiking cycle of 3-4 rate cuts as priced in 
by the markets is not appropriate given the supply shock origins of the current spell of inflation. 
In fact, we fear that the stagflationary impact could ultimately also affect the real economy, and 
not just inflation. Therefore, we still have our doubts about the second hike and we think that the 
Fed may be forced to cut earlier (next year) than they now expect.  
At the same time, we suspect that the upside revision of the neutral rate will lead to a higher 
terminal rate than we previously assumed.  
Therefore, we now expect only two rate cuts after today’s hike. Moreover, we now expect only 
one cut in 2027 and one in 2028. We put the first cut in the final quarter of 2027, which is in line 
with our new energy price forecasts indicating that CPI inflation will fall to 2.2% by then. We now 
assume that the terminal target range for the federal funds rate will be 3.25-3.50% instead 
of 3.00-3.25%.
