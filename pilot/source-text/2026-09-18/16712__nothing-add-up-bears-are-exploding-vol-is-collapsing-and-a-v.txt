Chase convexity
Markets are pricing contradictions everywhere. AAII bears are surging as the VIX collapses, oil
headlines scream panic while volatility remains calm, and AI credit risk is repricing while equity vol
barely moves.
When the signals diverge this sharply, the opportunity lies in cheap convexity, not chasing spot.

Complacent
Oil volatility has remained remarkably calm throughout the latest surge. A genuine upside panic
should have sent vols sharply higher. Instead, we have seen almost the opposite.
We view vol as the market’s ultimate truth serum. So far, it is not confirming the panic in spot.

returns, while another disruption offers convex participation in a renewed squeeze.

Selling bonds to buy GPUs
JPMorgan estimates that US tech net bond issuance will reach roughly $335bn in 2026, up from $140bn
last year.
European tech issuance is also accelerating. Gross issuance is tracking at more than €90bn
annualized, compared with approximately €35bn in 2025.
Combined, JPM estimates that the increase in US and European tech net issuance will be approximately
$260bn this year.
This is happening within a broader surge in US investment-grade supply. Net HG issuance is expected
to rise from around $615bn in 2025 to nearly $1tr in 2026, with longer-dated hyperscaler issuance
contributing materially to the increase.
The AI capex cycle is increasingly being financed through the bond market, and it is adding real
duration at a time when sovereign supply is already elevated.
JPM
Vol is the laggard
The next question is whether equity volatility has adequately responded to the signal coming from
credit.
If CDS continues widening while equities remain near their highs and implied volatility stays
compressed, downside protection looks cheap relative to the deterioration being priced by
bondholders.
The JPM numbers suggest AI-related issuance alone is unlikely to produce a disorderly global bond
selloff. But widening CDS alongside resilient equities and subdued vol creates a cleaner asymmetry.
The market does not need the AI thesis to collapse. It only needs investors to question the timing of the
returns, the size of the financing requirement or the ability of every participant to earn an adequate
return on the capital being deployed.
The chart compares CDS across selected AI-linked issuers with VXN. It is not a like-for-like issuer
comparison, but the cross-asset message is clear: credit risk has repriced more aggressively than
volatility in the equity complex these companies dominate.

The inflation gap
BofA estimates that today’s equity valuation is consistent with inflation of roughly 1.7%. Its economists
expect inflation of 3.2% in 2026 and 2.4% in 2027.
That gap matters because investors do not own earnings in isolation. Future profits have to be
discounted back to today.
If inflation remains above what equity valuations appear to assume, bond yields and real rates can
remain elevated even while profits continue to rise. The market can therefore get the earnings story
right and still get the index return wrong.
BofA’s equity valuation framework implies a materially more benign inflation backdrop than its own
economic forecasts. The 1.7% figure is not a market inflation breakeven.
The bull case is becoming a harder equation. The S&P no longer simply needs companies to deliver
strong profits. It increasingly needs strong profits without strong inflation.
And underneath those headline earnings numbers sits another vulnerability.
BofA
The hidden concentration
The five largest companies now account for a record 27% of next 12-month S&P 500 earnings.
More importantly, semiconductors and semiconductor equipment are expected to contribute 9.6
percentage points of the 15.4% consensus S&P 500 EPS growth expected in 2027.
That means 62% of the entire index’s expected EPS growth comes from one industry.
This does not mean the semiconductor forecasts are wrong. It means the margin for error is shrinking:
the companies carrying earnings growth must keep delivering while elevated inflation prevents the
discount rate from providing relief.
BofA also expects hyperscaler capex growth to slow from more than 90% in 2026 to around 35% in
2027. That is still huge growth, but the acceleration phase is fading just as the index becomes
unusually dependent on semiconductor earnings.
