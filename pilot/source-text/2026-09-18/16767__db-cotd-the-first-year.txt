DB CoTD: The first year 
 
 
In this week’s CoTDs we’ve focused on the start of what’s only the 15th Fed hiking cycle in 70 years. On 
Monday (link here) we showed that 10yr Treasury yields rose in the first year of 11 of the last 12 hiking cycles 
where daily data is available. Yesterday (link here) we looked at all 15 hiking cycles and showed that, if 
market pricing proves correct, this would be the shallowest hiking cycle in history. 
Following on from that, today’s chart comes from Henry Allen and a similar version is also included in my 
The Home Straight pack. It shows the level of US CPI at the time of the first hike in each of the previous 14 
hiking cycles and compares it with the amount the Fed tightened over the first 12 months of the cycle. 
Many other factors clearly matter, but there’s a pretty tight correlation between the two. This makes sense on 
paper, as the higher inflation is, the more the Fed need to do to get it under control again. 
The six shallowest first-year hiking cycles all began with CPI around or below 2%. Indeed, the cycles that saw 
less than 100bps of tightening in the first year started with CPI either just above or just below 1%—in other 
words, very low inflation at the onset. 
Using the best fit line, if you take DB’s forecasts for US CPI this month (at 3.55%), that correlates with just 
over 200bps of Fed hikes in the first year of this cycle. Clearly, nobody expects anything close to that

tendency to underestimate the scale of Fed hiking cycles at the start.
