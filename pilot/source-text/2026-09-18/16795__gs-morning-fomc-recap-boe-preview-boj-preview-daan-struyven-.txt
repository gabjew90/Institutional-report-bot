1) US ECONOMICS (MERICLE) – September FOMC Recap: Adding an October Hike - LINK
The FOMC raised the funds rate by 25bp to 3.75-4%. While the hike was widely expected, the meeting
was more hawkish than we expected in a few ways.
1/ A 16-2 majority projected at least one more hike this year, and there were no dissenting votes against
today’s hike.
2/ The median funds rate projection remained quite elevated through 2029, and the median neutral rate dot
rose from 3.06% to 3.25%.
3/ Chairman Warsh described the hike as having “removed a dose of accommodation” three times
We now expect the FOMC to deliver a second 25bp hike in October, a change from our previous
expectation that September would be the only hike. We think October is the most likely time for the next
move because it is most natural to deliver hikes that the FOMC presented today as supporting “a timelier
return” to the 2% target at consecutive meetings. We have kept our forecast for the terminal rate
unchanged at 3.25-3.5% by adding to the September and December 2027 rate cuts we already expected a
third 25bp cut in March 2028. Additional hikes are possible but not our base case.

2) UK ECONOMICS & STRATEGY (MOBERLY/COLE) - BOE Preview
UK ECONOMICS (MOBERLY): We think a hold is the clear base case at this meeting. Nothing in the
recent committee commentary — including Bailey's TSC testimony last week — pointed to imminent
tightening, and we think the MPC's incentive is to wait and see whether the recent run-up in energy prices
sticks before acting. We look for a 6-3 vote, with Pill, Greene and Mann dissenting again; Lombardelli is
the wildcard, so a 5-4 is a realistic risk but not our call. On communication, we expect the committee to

acknowledge that risks of material second-round effects have intensified, but to leave guidance unchanged
and reiterate that a restrictive stance remains necessary — i.e. no strong steer on upcoming meetings, with
some risk of a more hawkish tilt signalling tightening if energy pressures don't ease. On QT, we expect a
£50bn pace, keeping active sales broadly steady and letting lower redemptions do the work. Looking
further out, we have added a November hike: plugging the latest energy prices into our projections takes
headline inflation close to 4% (3.9% in Q1), a level the Bank's own framework flags as the danger zone
for second-round effects, and stronger recent growth data reduces the downside risk to activity…”
RATES STRATEGY (COLE): BoE today. The market doesn't think they're hiking. If there is anything to
the logic that earlier hikes have an efficient impact on financial conditions, then maybe we shouldn't fully
dismiss the idea that it is live. More importantly, back in July the majority (6-3) made a claim that
financial conditions were sufficiently restrictive to get inflation down to target. There's a risk that the vote
split today is 5-4, a slightly more hawkish split, but if it's 6-3 and the majority still makes this claim
regarding financial conditions, I think we keep pricing November and don't re-price substantially.
However, the most dangerous thing here for the market is if the BoE says that financial conditions are not
tight enough, in which case we may be pricing 50bps for November.

3) BoJ Preview
JAPAN ECON (OTA): We maintain our forecast that the next BOJ policy rate hike will be on September
18, by 25bp to 1.25%. The yen, which we consider as one factor influencing the timing of rate hikes, has
recently been strengthening. However, given that this yen appreciation is likely at least in part the result of
the market pricing in further rate hikes, holding off on a rate hike could lead to renewed yen depreciation.
Therefore, we think this yen appreciation will not deter the Bank of Japan (BOJ) from hiking. Financial
markets are beginning to price in the possibility of an acceleration in hiking, such as consecutive rate hikes
in September and October. However, we think this is unlikely unless inflation overshoots (i.e., the BOJ
falls behind the curve) and it becomes necessary to quickly raise the policy rate to a level well above the
neutral rate. Currently, various statistics do not show any signals indicating overshooting inflation. We
therefore still expect a subsequent rate hike in January 2027, with another (to 1.75%) in July 2027. Full
piece here.
RATES STRATEGY (COLE): The BOJ is expected to hike this week, continuing the normalization
process and effectively shifting toward a quarterly hiking cadence. However, the hurdle for moving to
consecutive-meeting hikes remains high. Such a shift would require evidence that inflation risks are
materially overshooting the BOJ's 2% objective. JPY weakness alone is sufficient to justify this week's
move but not a sustained acceleration in tightening. Under-delivering relative to current pricing would risk
renewed currency weakness, making USDJPY a key market variable around the meeting. The preferred
view remains that the next BOJ hike occurs in December or January, rather than through consecutive-

meeting tightening. Japan differs from the US and Europe in that policy is still not close to restrictive
territory. The bar for Governor Ueda to signal a transition toward genuinely restrictive policy is extremely
high. Unless inflation begins materially overshooting BOJ forecasts, further hawkish acceleration is
unlikely.
IRP SALES (RANGWANI): With 3 x 25bps priced by April, including tomorrow's 25bps, and some risk
of a back-to-back move (Nov at 7bps), it’s worth thinking about how BoJ could meet, exceed or miss
expectations. Our bias is that with 1y1y rates at 2.2% pricing is quite full, perhaps reflecting recent
Bessent commentary rather than BOJ shifts in thinking or domestic fundamentals. On hawkish side:
dissents from either Takata or Tamura for 50bps would be meaningful. We have no outlook report but if
Ueda shifted to talking about risks of underlying inflation overshooting 2% (in contrast to last outlook
report that showed reaching in later half of projection period). On dovish side: looking out for any dissents
from the newly appointed Takaichi governors (Kurita and Shizume). Or commentary from Ueda that need
time to assess how the hike just delivered will impact the inflation outlook.
STIR TRADING (MAWER-DAVIES): We have seen broadly better paying of the front-end today. After
the hawkish Fed, people think the BOJ will need to do even more. Positioning wise, we think it is slightly
paid in BOJ dates and received 1y1y and 2y1y as a lot of people have sympathy with the view that
terminal pricing is stretched, but don't have the appetite to fade.
G10 VOL TRADING (ALEXIS): All focus here on the BoJ tomorrow where we have 90bps of an implied
gap in USDJPY. After a hawkish FOMC we feel risks here are very much skewed to the topside with 2
BoJ hikes priced into year end and JPY rates pricing the fastest rate of hikes across G10 curves. This
asymmetry has been reflected in the flow we've seen with fast money looking to own topside gamma
which looks cheap vs the ATM vol, as the market still holds significant intervention premium in the RR.

4) Weekly Trader Call – Daan Struyven Views
See below views from Daan Struyven (Co-Head of Global Commodities Research, Head of Oil Research)
on our weekly FX Trader Call.
Saudi East-West pipeline flows vs total Gulf flows…
The capacity of the pipeline is six to seven million barrels per day. Over the last few weeks before the
closure, we were seeing roughly 2 million barrels per day of crude exports via the pipeline from the Yanbu
port and roughly 1.5 million barrels per day of crude going to Western refiners in Saudi Arabia. So for a
total of roughly 3.5 million barrels per day of total oil volumes at risk. While there is a lot of uncertainty
around the damage and the time it would take to fix it, our conversations with people in the region suggest
that it's not unreasonable to assume a base case where some flows pick up, perhaps in a week or so, but
that it would take several weeks in the base case for the flows to fully come back as the damage is more
severe than in April when flows were back within a few days.


[Page 4]
It's important to not only focus on Yanbu but keep the broader perspective on exports from the Middle
East. And there the perhaps surprising news, is that estimated oil exports from Persian Gulf countries that
you can see here in blue have actually edged up slightly in September to roughly 17 million barrels per
day, that is 70% of the pre-war level of 23 million barrels per day. So what's happening is a reduction in
flows via the Red Sea on the west but a pickup in flows on the east via the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf
of Oman. This chart, to be clear, incorporates our estimation of dark flows using the pattern of past upward
revisions to data.
To be clear, that is still low, the market is still likely in a deficit of a couple million barrels per day, and
there are clearly downside risks to our base case where Mideast exports recover further, because from an
Iranian perspective, you look at this picture, you conclude that you may need to escalate to reduce exports,
increase oil prices and increase the pain in the US.
Can we see materially higher oil prices from here?
Three factors have dampened the upside, SPR, China imports weakness and adaptation in the Middle East,
including dark flows. In terms of dark flows, they are continuing and ramping up, so at least for now, until
Iran were to escalate further by damaging infrastructure, that is still helping to dampen the upside. On SPR
releases, the answer is the opposite. We are already seeing a significant slowing in OECD SPR releases,
which have accounted for 40% of the global visible oil draw since the start of the war. So a given size of
the oil market deficit in coming months should lead to bigger draws in OECD commercial excluding
SPRs, and therefore create a more positive price effect. For Chinese crude imports, while they have edged
up slightly from the lows in the spring, we think that Chinese imports will remain low. They're still down a
staggering 4 million barrels per day year-over-year. While they might edge up somewhat because they are
low and because refiners in China may want to export refined product at a slightly higher level to gain
profit margins, the price sensitivity of Chinese end-use demand of gasoline and diesel, we think is
structural. That has been the biggest driver of the collapse in imports, and so we think that Chinese crude
imports will remain weak.
If we actually lost the 3.5mb/d from East-West pipeline (with the key assumption that loss in West
flows/Red Seas is not compensated by higher East flows), and that persisted for 1-2 months, what should
the price reaction be?
We think that for a year that is worth $30-40 dollars (if it happened for entire year). For 2 months, that
would be just over $5 in terms of fair value. But would note we think that actual prices would rise a lot
more, given the risk premium, and the market putting weight on the outage lasting longer – but we think
those would be your bounds.
