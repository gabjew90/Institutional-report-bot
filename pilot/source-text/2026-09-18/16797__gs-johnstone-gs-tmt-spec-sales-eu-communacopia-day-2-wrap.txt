GS TMT SPEC SALES – Wednesday 17th September
US markets finished lower overnight, extending the week’s losing streak despite an initial rally ahead of the
FOMC. The Fed delivered the expected 25bp hike to 3.75–4.00%, but surprised hawkishly with dots showing one
further hike in 2026, no cuts next year and higher inflation projections. Tech was highly bifurcated: AI
infrastructure and optical names led, with Optical +4.8%, Neoclouds/Miners +3.3% and Network Equipment
+2.4%, helped by LITE +10%, CRDO +7%, COHR +7% and ALAB +7%, while Software -0.9% and Internet
-1.4% lagged. Momentum was the clear factor winner, while Bitcoin-related and high-short-interest names
underperformed. Within AI, the market favoured profitable infrastructure exposure, including data centres and AI
semis, while software-at-risk and AI-productivity names sold off. Oil fell 3%, reversing much of the prior day’s
gains on positive East-West pipeline headlines, though energy remains a focus given limited clarity on the Iran
war.
EU Communacopia Day 2
Day 2 of EU Communacopia was broadly stable-to-constructive across telcos and media. In telcos, Spain remains
the clearest positive, with TEF, Orange and Zegona pointing to better pricing, rational competition and M&A
optionality, while Germany remains the main area of caution and BT, KPN and CityFibre reinforce the fibre,
consolidation and efficiency themes. In media, RELX, Scout, Prosus, JCD, Publicis, Auto Trader and Informa all
sounded broadly constructive, supported by AI/data monetisation, stronger marketplace positions, disciplined
capital allocation and improving event/advertising trends, although macro softness and stock-specific execution
debates remain. See more details below
AI infrastructure and compute
Nebius / NeoCloud: Nebius is reportedly raising on-demand cloud GPU prices from 1 October, highlighting
continued strong AI compute demand and tight supply. The increases apply across major Nvidia GPUs, with H100
rising from $3.85 to $4.50 (+17%), H200 from $4.50 to $5.40 (+20%), B200 from $7.15 to $8.50 (+19%) and
B300 from $7.85 to $9.50 (+21%). This follows a previous round of price hikes in May and appears to come from
a customer pricing notice shared by users on Reddit and X, with no formal public press release yet visible on
Nebius’s website.
Intel / Meta AI chips: Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan indicated that only around half of CPU demand can be supplied,
driven by strong AI agent growth. He also noted that 18A is entering volume production and that 14A could move
into production earlier, potentially in Q1 next year. Separately, Meta is reportedly targeting 1GW of internally
developed AI-chip compute within 12 months and accelerating its self-developed AI chip roadmap, including

MTIA 4500 Arke and next-generation MTIA 500 Astrid, with design completion expected within a month and
datacentre use targeted for 2027 via TSMC tape-out.
Company updates
Allegro: 2Q26 was in line with the July pre-close, but 3Q26 trends show clear acceleration, with Polish GMV up
14–15% YoY, International GMV up c.100% and Group GMV up c.17–18% in the first ten weeks, all ahead of
GSe/consensus. FY26 guidance was upgraded across Poland, International and Group, while advertising, Allegro
Pay and managed delivery remain strong; the release should be viewed positively. See GIR take
AI safety and product news
AI security: OpenAI disclosed several previously undisclosed model-misalignment incidents and introduced a new
reporting framework to track and triage future issues. Examples included models fabricating data, concealing
information, trying to bypass network restrictions and agents sharing files they should have kept private, although
OpenAI said none of the new cases involved a third-party hack or breach. The key takeaway is that AI security
concerns are moving from theory to live operating risk, with alignment and monitoring still not solved well
enough to scale without more transparent evidence and oversight.
Anthropic: Anthropic is folding Claude’s chat and cowork features into one interface and launching new document
tools. Reuters
Manus: Manus is reportedly targeting a $4bn valuation in its first funding round since the Meta breakup.

TELCOS
| • | TEF: My impression from TEF management was that Germany is more of a priority for M&A than Spain,
where the market structure remains tougher after the 1&1 loss and the potential benefits from consolidation look
greater. Spain sounds good, consistent with the tone from Orange and Zegona, with B2C performing well, churn
stable despite price rises, bundling helping, B2B driving growth and wholesale pressure expected to stabilise by
2028. Brazil is tracking as expected, the UK fixed market remains more challenging than hoped, and the CFO is
focused on faster deleveraging, balancing investment for growth with balance-sheet repair, while watching France
for signals on wider consolidation support. See Andrew’s take |
| • | KPN: Bounced as CEO more bullish yesterday than IR at a conference last week. The domestic market sounds
pretty solid despite VODZiggo being more aggressive ahead of its IPO/spin, with management seeing no material
deterioration in competition and resilient B2C/mobile trends. Security upsell is working well, SME/B2B
momentum remains strong into 2027, KPN is taking action where enterprise growth has been weaker, and
management remains confident on cost efficiencies, reiterating €100m of savings by 2030 with scope for a further
update next year. Satellite looks like only a limited threat in the Dutch market given fibre coverage and mobile
network quality, while AI is being pushed more actively in B2C to support agents and improve service efficiency. |
| • | DT – investor debate still remains around whether DT will try and merge with TMUS – views here now more
mixed. DT making no comments on this. Bears of the view that TMUS faces tougher US competition as At&T &
Verizon cross sell on fibre gains traction Plus questions on the US up and coming auctions. DT playing down
satellite risk as are most Telco operators |

| • | CityFibre: Like VMEDO2 UK, CityFibre still needs to address its balance sheet and leverage. The more
interesting point from the meeting was the upcoming CMA review of the Netomnia deal, due by 15 December, and
whether the regulator imposes remedies, blocks it or clears it (if blocked Cityfibre would still be interested). If
approved, the deal looks positive for BT and VMEDO2, as altnets increasingly appear focused on consolidation
and balance-sheet restructuring rather than further fibre overbuild; CityFibre should also benefit from having
signed Sky as a wholesale partner and selling higher speed products (BT weas saying its wholesalers are also
pushing higher speeds). |
MEDIA
| • | RELX – more I listen to RELX more I think they are in a decent position to monetise AI despite some of the
AI risk concerns. On stage CFO Nick Luff discussed: (i) expectations for continued strong top-line performance,
with STM and Legal accelerating; (ii) difficulty in replicating RELX's legal data, and the value it provides to
workflows that require integration with legal content; (iii) the opportunity around fraud prevention in Risk; (iv)
how they manage token costs; and (v) Middle East events in the Exhibition segment. See GIR’s take |
| • | Scout: My impression is that Spain is going well and management is becoming more confident. Adam’s key
question this morning is whether there is upside risk to consensus private-subscriber forecasts — which feels
possible — as new products may help reduce churn in the private subscription business. |
| • | Prosus: Tencent still drives the stock, and with US frontier-model momentum becoming more contested,
Chinese AI models look increasingly interesting. The key debate is whether mgt’s operating turnaround and
buyback maths are enough to make Prosus worth more than “Tencent at a discount”; with the NAV discount
already around 40%, it is hard to see it widening much further. At the conference, management discussed Brazil
competition, where 99Food and DiDi have tripled investment to $150m per month in 2026, improving trends at
Just Eat, OLX potentially reaching $500m of EBITDA and $400m of FCF in FY27, the long-term PayU
opportunity and the broader capital-management strategy. Separately iFood’s press release on plans to invest
~$4.7bn in Brazil this year does not change existing FY27 guidance of $100–150m. The announcement was timed
ahead of a major partner meeting and is intended to highlight iFood’s broader economic impact in Brazil; the
figure is not incremental spend, but reflects total commerce across the platform, including revenue earned by
restaurants, drivers and advertisers. |
| • | JCD: Investor feedback was positive on this one and on the broader outdoor theme. Management sounded
confident on Q3 organic growth, supported by recent contract wins, a favourable tender pipeline and structural
share gains for OOH, with programmatic trading offering long-term upside. H2 margins still have moving parts,
but capital allocation looks disciplined and management remains comfortable with current leverage to preserve
flexibility. |

TELCOS
| • | Digi Spain: Digi has reached 3m fibre customers, making it Spain’s third-largest broadband operator behind
Orange and Telefónica but ahead of Vodafone, after adding 417k fibre customers in 2026 versus much smaller
gains at the incumbents. Its fibre base has grown eightfold over five years, its own SMART network now reaches
14.84m homes and the company invested €205m in H1 2026 to keep expanding fibre and mobile capacity.
(Expansion) |
| • | Zegona/VOD Spain has won a three-year contract to manage Amazon’s mobile connectivity in Spain, taking
over from Telefónica and adding more than 4,000 corporate mobile lines with specific roaming terms for mobile
staff. The deal builds on Vodafone’s existing fixed-service relationship with Amazon across offices, logistics
centres and warehouses, and reinforces Vodafone Business’s momentum in logistics, IoT and private 5G, where it

already manages over 17m IoT devices and supports use cases such as GPS tracking, temperature sensors, AGVs,
drones and digital twins. (Cinco Dias) |
| • | Scaleway/Iliad: Scaleway, Iliad’s cloud subsidiary, has partnered with French startup ALATYR to develop
space-based data centres that can support cloud, AI, data storage and critical infrastructure use cases. The 18-
month project will focus on architecture, security and integration with existing cloud services, using ALATYR’s
modular “CITADEL” design and robotic “BYLD” system for in-orbit assembly and maintenance. The idea is to
use continuous solar power to improve resilience and avoid some terrestrial constraints, with a first in-orbit demo
planned for 2028 and a commercial European “orbital cloud” targeted for 2030. (Universfreebox) |
