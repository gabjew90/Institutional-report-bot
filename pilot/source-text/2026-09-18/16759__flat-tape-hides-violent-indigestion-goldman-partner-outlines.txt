Flat Tape Hides Violent Indigestion: Goldman 
Partner Outlines 4 Key Factors Driving Markets 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, SEP 16, 2026 - 05:45 PM 
Halfway through September the tape still has "indigestion", and the index is doing a convincing 
job of hiding it, according to Goldman Sachs partner, Bobby Molavi's latest (must read) 
'Ruminations' (available here in full for pro subs). 
 
The S&P is back where it started June, call it 7600, after three months in which factor 
leadership, regional winners, and theme spreads have whipped around hard enough to feel like 
liquidation rather than rotation. 
With Taiwan and Korea already showing what an exuberance reset looks like and pairs like 
software versus semis or three-month versus twelve-month momentum moving as if the 
underlying market and the headline average are barely related. 
Under that flat tape sits a simple and ugly stack: the 10Y through 5.00% for the first time since 
2007, oil through $100 and leaning toward $110, a Fed that is odds-on to hike, a Nasdaq that 


[Page 2]
 
has lost its 50- and 100-DMAs, momentum that is choking, and AI winners still choking down a 
weekend essay, while the FOMC decision sits dead ahead... and then the Midterms... 
 
Molavi’s four-factor market is less a framework than a diagnosis. AI, oil, yields, and the rate 
decision are all live at the same time, and they are all pointing the wrong way... 
1. Slow the pace. Amodei’s essay calling for a slowing of frontier model improvement was swiftly 
followed by the likes of Altman, Musk and Hassabis lending support to the call to arms. A 
combination of agent swarm hacks , cyber threats, bio weapon concern, ‘whistleblower’ end of 
days warnings and the recent Hugging face attack prompting increased calls for regulation or at 
least independent evaluators. It feels like you can judge this through two lenses – one that is 
corporate and one that is more political. On the political side…this proclamation seems to have 
brought together the Ai leaders and the 'left' at a time when the politics of Data centres is getting 
more complicated (perhaps just a short term dynamic into Midterms), we have likes of Bernie 
Sanders making Ai his latest bug bear…and parts of the left globally calling for more 
restrictions, supervision and/or regulation. On the other side you have POTUS arguing that Ai is 
a critical imperative for national strength and security and development and innovation should 
not be restricted. On the corporate side, it seems there are two camps again. Those who argue 
this is self serving regulatory capture…and attempt by the winners to create a regulatory moat 
that anoints the winners and makes it hard for others (arguably Open weight in particular) to 
compete. There is also more debate about make a distinction between slowing and stopping 


[Page 3]
 
and slowing not impacting spending....if that make sense.  Yesterdays moves for the AI ‘winner’ 
complex globally were to be expected…..Chips/compute all coming under pressure....todays px 
action shows the anxieties live on.  
2. Molecules. Oil now sits comfortably above $100 and with real risks of grinding higher. East 
west routes affected after Saudia Arabia's east west petroline that runs from Abqaiq to Yanbu hit 
last week. This line was built to move up to 7m barrels a day…it is also the only major export 
route that skips the strait of Hormuz. Reserves are precariously low, the flows through the 
straight and via the east/west Saudi pipeline are slow or stopped and the selling back into the 
market by those that had surplus (eg China) will start to slow. Tanker rates, war risk premia, 
insurance costs, oil price all move wrong way. All of this as we head into winter season and , for 
the consumer, as they navigate a multitude of other cost of living and inflationary pressures.  
3. Yields. The global march higher for yields seem to relentless. A combination of inflation, 
refinancing, strong economy, evolving end buyer, deficit and outside pressures (relentless IG 
and AI related issuance) seemingly too hot to handle. Bessent's attempts to ‘bully’ the market 
into submission thus far not working in spite of some fairly heavy buyback activity. The result of 
this….a US 10 year that is now at levels not seen since 2007 and above the psychologically 
important 5%. Oh……$7 trillion worth of T bills with $6.1 trillion maturing in the next year. 
Another $4 trillion in 2027….and another $3.5 trillion in 2028. 
 
4. Rates. It feels like central banks are stuck between a rock and a hard place. Europe and the 
UK leaning to Hike when their economies need stimulating not restricting….also at a time when 


[Page 4]
 
the national debt/deficit adjusted for growth is problematic and each hike result in a need for 
more cash to repay liabilities in a worryingly self perpetuating cycle. On the US front…. US PPI 
at +5.4%, +162k Jobs added in August as well as upward revisions for June and July, and US 
CPI at +3.4%. Inflation is running at 140bps above the feds target and that is before you price 
the recent Oil rally.  
Then beneath the surface we have – positioning, narrowness, crowding, factor and theme…. 
●​ Positioning. Materially cleaner than it was. Gross, Net, Long short ratios, leverage, 
crowding, concentration – on any metric we are seeing a healthier backdrop. Some of 
the leverage across retail and hedge fund community has abated with 2x and 3x levered 
etfs losing AUM and investors choosing to take gross down until they have more 
conviction.  
●​ Momentum. The factor that has ruled them all in recent years going through a period of 
chop and churn. The interplay between 12 month and 3 month momentum and how the 
make up of momentum is evolving . Yday we saw the 3m/12m momentum pair have an 
11% relative swing and saw long term momo u/p by the most amount in 5 years. The 
positioning that felt clean at the end of July has gotten ‘cleaner’ with positioning now 
sitting in the 41st %-ile on a 1 year look back. Long term winers (Ai) selling off 
hard….long term loser (Software) outperforming. Short term momo shifting towards 
energy, quality and defensives….and perhaps….for the first time in a while Momentum 
starting to decouple from Ai theme.  
 
●​ Theme. Ai remains the dominant theme with two cross currents and a big question mark. 
The capex and spend seems locked and has surprised to the upside. The scarcity and 


[Page 5]
 
bottleneck trade is normalising. Cyber remains bid for and software has mounted a 
remarkable round trip from being AI roadkill to being an Ai beneficiary. The focus now 
shifting to AI and proof of impact and revenue. At the GS communicopia conf we saw 
lots of examples of impact – 80% reduction in product and design build times 
(Visa)….automation of roles (Servicenow)…..20% productivity gains (SAP). So the 
impacts are starting to come through in a real way. A shift from Ai capex and Ai 
infrastructure to Ai benefits and Ai monetisation.  
 
...and to finish…. 
●​ Power law. VC backed exits were around $300bn in 2019 and 2020….and rose to 
$865bn in 21 before collapsing to between $115 and $155bn between 2022 and 
2024….so far in 2026……VC backed exits have accounted for $2.18 trillion…more than 
the previous 5 years combined! The scale of value creation in the private markets hitting 
new highs with $3 to 4 trillion of venture backed companies in private markets. To put 
some context on that….here are JUST the Ai startups with a $10bn valuation and $100m 
revenue run rate as things stand…. Mercor, Eleven labs, Baseten, Harvey, Lovable, 
Openevidence, Mistral, Nscale, Fireworks, Sierra, Moonshot, Perplexity, Cognition, 
Scale, Deepseek, Waymo, Xai, Openai, Anthropic. The bottom to top being $10bn 
valuation to $965bn valuation (at previous raise)…and a total market cap at previous 
raise of……circa $3 trillion. Most of which created in the last 2 years.  


[Page 6]
 
 
●​ The power of words. S&P has evolved from a savings tool and a concurrent diversified 
hedge to living costs and inflation towards a very narrow bet on tech and Ai hardware 
and capex. As the market has shifted in make up (more tech, more ai, more ai 
dependent) and shifted towards future value (colonising mars, robots, driverless taxis, 
agents, bots) then it has become more dependent on stories….and belief. A bet on 
founders and missions….and TAM rather than cash flows and valuations. Or put another 
way….much more ‘story’ and future orientated than anything we’ve seen before. With 
that shift it is unsurprising how stories and the power of words are having more 
impact…to take two examples….Citrini research Ai report earlier this year…or Dario 
Amodei essay over the weekend. Somewhat calls into question the theory that ‘sticks 
and stones might break my bones but words will never hurt me.’ 
●​ Reallocation vs repatriation. Japan yields and asset price reflation are at levels where 
lack of domestic exposure is costing you in risk assets (See Nikkei ytd as a case and 
point ) and the need to go abroad to find ‘yield’ is no longer as compelling as it once 
was. What this means for traditional flows of money (Japanese buying of US treasuries) 
remains to be seen…but given the quantum of foreign buying the Japanese have done 
across end asset classes (bonds and equities) and via different asset owners (insurance, 
hold co, banks, saving and investment trusts)..whether what we see is growing allocation 
towards domestic or an actual repatriation from abroad back home…the structural 
tailwinds for Japan remain strong.

Molavi’s point is that the index is lying: the S&P is still around the June 7600 level, but the 
market underneath it is being yanked around by four things at once - AI, oil, yields, and the next 
rate decision.  
AI winners are digesting the weekend “slow the frontier” pile-on even though CapEx looks 
locked and the trade is starting to shift from chips and bottlenecks toward proof of revenue; oil is 
through $100 and can grind higher after the Saudi east-west Petroline hit left Hormuz-skirting 
supply impaired into winter; the 10-year is above 5% for the first time since 2007, Bessent’s 
buybacks are not winning, and a huge T-bill wall is still rolling; and the Fed is odds-on to hike 
with inflation already 140 basis points over target before that oil rally is fully in the models. 
Positioning is cleaner than it was, which only makes the factor violence more obvious - 
momentum just flipped hard, long-term AI is for sale, software and defensives are catching a bid 
- so the tape looks unchanged while the things actually driving it are all pointing the wrong way.
