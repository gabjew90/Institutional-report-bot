'The Barrel Is The Bond Market': Goldman, Nomura 
Agree That 'Energy Is Running The Tape, Not AI' 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, SEP 16, 2026 - 02:58 AM 
The tape is not an AI story this morning (and was not yesterday), it is an energy-into-rates story.  
 
Goldman’s one-delta desk head Rich Privorotsky and top Nomura strategist Charlie McElligott 
arrived at the same place from different angles: crude is setting the bond market, the bond 
market is setting risk, and until the barrel stops breaking higher, valuation arguments - AI 
included - are downstream. 
Privorotsky put it bluntly... 
“Rates + Energy: This is still the big one.”  
Prices keep going higher on more Saudi/Houthi escalation overnight, and that is pushing yields, 
prompting "a toxic combination for risk as stagflation concerns rise". 
He flagged a couple of small potential release valves. 
Trump asked Zelensky to stop hitting Russian refining infrastructure, while Ukraine has signaled 
openness to a reciprocal energy ceasefire if Russia is serious.  


[Page 2]
 
Trump also said Iran “wants to make a deal quickly and desperately.” Iran pushed back, 
unsurprisingly, but after weeks with basically no diplomatic signal, at least the tone has shifted 
slightly. 
Then the Goldman BSD dropped the most important line: 
"I don’t think risk can properly work until energy stabilizes. 
Oil is still breaking out, alternative routes are insufficient and inventories are too tight.  
The marginal barrel sets the price. With diesel/product this elevated, the inflation problem 
broadens through freight, food and PPI.  
Yields then have very little reason to sustainably fall. Unless the military strategy suddenly 
works much better, the credible off ramp is diplomacy. We got small hints in that direction yday 
but so far no convincing signs.  
Think Xi/Trump meeting next week could be important.” 
That is the whole transmission: The barrel is the bond market, and the bond market is the gate. 
McElligott, in this morning’s Nomura cross-asset note - THE STRAW THAT STIRS THE DRINK 
- said the same thing in his own inimitable manner. 
“Quick note on the one 'Macro Doomsday'-story that isn't tied to AI...”  
He pointed back to his Sept. 10 note: rates, Europe especially, were “Waking Up” to “Energy / 
Petrochem Shock 2.0,” realizing the shortages are increasingly “terminal.”  
The difference versus the first Iran War / Strait of Hormuz pass is China.  
Beijing is no longer emptying emergency reserves into the shortage.  
 
It is rebuilding crude inventories, “more than likely in coordinated conjunction with Iran to ‘turn 
the screws’ on the U.S.” 


[Page 3]
 
Goldman's estimate of the hit to oil flows from the Persian Gulf now stands at 6.7m b/d... 
 
Overnight, bonds, stocks, gold, credit, and crypto were all slowly bleeding out, but, as McElligott 
notes, the dollar (“Energy Independence / Sovereignty”), energy equities (“The Barbell”), 
commodity carry, rates trend and long vol were the favored uncorrelated hiding places. 
"...yet again, Crude Oil / Energy is 'The Straw that Stirs the Drink' of the current global macro 
risk-regime.” 
The physical setup is the same one Privorotsky is staring at. 
“The World is Short Energy, Refineries, VLCCs and all the things.” 


[Page 4]
 
 
China is back in the market for oil and lifting offers to back-fill what it emptied earlier in the war... 
 
McElligott’s read is that they know exactly what they are doing with the “Achilles Heel” of the 
Trump Administration, and with an increasingly prickly Europe as well (German push to lobby 
the EU for new China tariffs): taking crude to new war highs, crunching cross-asset markets, 
leaning on already fragile equities sentiment and jacking up interest-rate risk. 


[Page 5]
 
“End of the day, ‘All Assets are Short Rate Vol’ — and the Energy panic is finally beginning to 
kick-in on shortages (Trump finally tweeting about Diesel, hullooooo), leaving Central Banks the 
useless ‘Demand-Side’-tool of ‘Policy Rate Hikes’ to try and sort out a ‘Supply Shock.’” 
That is Goldman’s inflation-through-freight-food-PPI point in Nomura language, and diesel is the 
patient zero...  
 
McElligott flagged European diesel crack spreads dragging EUR swaption vol back toward early 
Iran War highs... 
 


[Page 6]
 
Negative convexity is sitting in the background: the huge tilt to intermediate high-strike payers, 
CMT range accruals feeding the bid to skew and vol-of-vol, plus a recent impulse of large VIX 
call buying - 125k Nov 31s at $0.94 and 126k Nov 34s at $0.89 on Sept. 4, 128k Oct 28s at 
$0.92 on Aug. 28 - creating a potential “Energy Pocket” if risks suddenly accelerate, with dealer 
puckers in both swaptions and VIX options, in a capital-markets environment where banks and 
dealers have low loss tolerance. 
 
Privorotsky’s snapshot of the tape is the same market McElligott is mapping mechanically. 
“We’ve blown through 5%, long end yields are taking out the highs, oil is still breaking higher 
and macro buyers are absent. At the risk of being reductionist… rates need to stabilize before 
any of these valuation arguments matter (and that needs oil to stabilize).” 
McElligott’s version of "it has to get worse first" is the condition under which that stabilization 
doesn’t happen. 
To break the "Macro Malaise" of muddling through - low ATM implied vols, rates moving inside 
the daily straddle, crude chopping on headlines, equities still not far from ATHs - energy has to 
break to new extremes first.  
An export ban “WOULD CERTAINLY DO THAT,” then knock into the rate-vol component, which 
drags asset beta with it.  


[Page 7]
 
With Trump "backed into a corner of his own making", what could come next is a left-tail bomb 
on the global economy, particularly Euro and Asian countries lacking energy independence: a 
hypothetical U.S. energy export curb or ban, which “will increasingly pick-up Delta the longer 
this mess continues… and both Iran and China are incentivized to see it continue.” 
That is why both desks are dismissive of AI as the now catalyst even while they keep talking 
about it. McElligott: 
...without the energy/rates story as a much more credible shock / left-tail risk, the “AI Slowdown” 
story “remains a ways-away from anything tangible, and I’m somewhat dismissive of this being a 
‘now’ pullback catalyst.” 
The Dario / Sam / Elon / POTUS conversation over the weekend into yesterday highlights 
frontier labs feeling their gap over open-source players shrinking - in his view an attempt at 
regulatory capture and “too big to fail” status, already across the Rubicon and headed toward 
quasi-nationalization under natsec in the “AI Cold War” vs China.  
Take it from Trump yesterday: 
“We’re leading China in AI. We’re the most sophisticated country in the world, and frankly I want 
to keep it that way because whoever wins AI wins.” 
Whoever wins AI wins... later. Right now the marginal barrel is still setting the price, diesel is still 
broadening the inflation problem, 5% is already in the rear-view, and two desks that do not 
usually rhyme this cleanly are saying the same sentence: risk does not work until energy 
stabilizes, and energy does not stabilize until someone produces a diplomatic off-ramp that is 
more than a tone shift. 
Xi/Trump next week is on Goldman’s calendar. An export ban is on Nomura’s. 
Until one of those, or something that looks like one of those, McElligott's straw that stirs the 
drink is still crude.+ 
Tl;dr: Solve energy to solve rates... Risk doesn’t work until then... the Band-Aid hike is theater... 
The barrel is the bond market...
