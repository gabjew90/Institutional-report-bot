US Week In Review 
The October minutes showed a Fed pausing to gather more information, with economic activity 
described as expanding strongly supported by resilient spending and robust business investment, 
while participants generally viewed the labor market as stable. Inflation was noted as remaining 
elevated and broad based in the view of most participants. A majority also noted that financial 
conditions had tightened since September, while some flagged risks from AI-related debt and 
complex financing. Despite the pause at the meeting, almost all participants judged that further 
rate increases would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlooks, while a couple said a 
hike larger than 25 bp could be considered if inflation data were unfavorable. It is important to 
note however that the minutes predate the October CPI reading that came in slightly above 
expectations, with core inflation at 0.3% for a third consecutive month. 
This week, Fed communication reflected a wider range of views on the policy outlook. Hawkish 
comments came from non-voting member of the Fed, St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem 
who emphasized that “the economy will likely require several interest rate increases before the 
spring of 2027” to achieve price stability. His remarks echoed those of Dallas Fed President Lorie 
Logan and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, who last week supported raising rates in 
small increments.  
Governor Christopher Waller kept his hawkish stance indicating he was which he said was “very 
concerned about inflation,” which was “headed in the wrong direction” and “spreading out.” He 
argued that more aggressive action is needed and continued to advocate for a 50 bps hike in 
December. Waller also said the Fed should reverse last year’s three insurance cuts, meaning that 
it may need to raise rates further to bring inflation down. By contrast, New York Fed President 
John Williams said he did not “think the situation calls for dramatic action.” 
Retail sales rose 0.2% m/m in October, slowing from the previous month’s 0.8% gain but coming 
in above expectations. Excluding autos, sales rose 0.6% (exp. 0.4%), while excluding autos and 
gas, sales rose 0.5% (exp. 0.3%). The control group, which feeds into the GDP, rose 0.6% (exp. 
0.3%), compared with 0.5% in September, suggesting continued resilience in underlying demand. 
Seven of 13 categories registered gains, with the largest increase including miscellaneous store 
retailers (4.7% m/m) and online retailers (1.2% m/m), suggesting consumer spending remained 
strong overall. However, there were some weaknesses in the report, as spending in autos and 
parts, and furniture and home furnishings stores declined 1.6% and 1.3%, respectively. Spending 
at gasoline stations and health and personal care stores rose 1.5% m/m each. 
Online, gas, and miscellaneous spending were top contributors to October sales

What to Watch for Next Week 
Next week’s economic calendar features the Conference Board’s Consumer Confidence survey 
on Tuesday, followed by the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) report and the Fed’s 
Beige Book on Wednesday. Following last week’s slightly higher than expected CPI and in-line 
PPI readings, consensus expectations are for annual PCE inflation to edge higher in October. 
Headline PCE is expected to rise 0.3% m/m, down from 0.5% in September, while core PCE is 
expected to round to 0.3% m/m for a consecutive month. On the year, headline PCE inflation is 
expected to increase to 3.8%, from 3.6% in September, while core PCE inflation is forecast to 
rise to 3.2%, from 3.1%.

US Rates Strategy 
Treasuries for the most part this week rallied with a slight but inconsistent flattening bias, with 
ongoing concerns in risk asset markets, and a nuance underneath that if the Fed continues to 
tighten into weaker risk markets a “recession-esque” flattening is appropriate.  A “recession-
esque” flattening is actually our view as we look to next year, combined with less AI pressure on 
the rates market earlier in the year (but more later in the year). That said, given the move in 
equities, we would think the bullish priced action is still slightly lackluster, though as we write the 
move is accelerating somewhat into the end of the week. 
This is why for the medium-term view we favor buying dips in the long end, but we also view entry 
level as more critical than ever, because as we wrote in our analysis of AI impact on the bond 
market (here), there may be ~$155mm/01 of unhedged liabilities in the 5y15y part of the curve 
that is exposed and may need to be hedged, which could make any rally limited if not supported 
by actual signs of economic weakening.  Lower inflation would also do the trick, but Natixis 
Economics sees a steady decline over the course of the year but nothing near-term on the inflation 
front that will shift the Fed’s hawkish stance. So even for the medium term we’d rather wait for 
5.20%-5.25% before enacting any longs. 
This is especially as in the near term, we had seen little catalysts beyond weaker equities for a 
durable rates rally. Two weeks ago we had suggested potentially scaling in from 5.10%-5.20% in 
10s (5.17% was the high) but last week we were hesitant as the only spark we could see for a 
rally was equity weakness and that was not something we could predict ... but here we are.  Our 
thinking was also that while we think 50bps is not on the table in December, as indicated even by 
many of the hawkish members of the Fed, we doubt even a soft payroll repot will take December 
off the table and assuming a number similar to recent months, the December meeting is likely to 
be quite hawkish and keep January well priced (as it is now).

LatAm Highlights 
  Brazil – Fiscal shocks and the BRL weakness 
The BRL is depreciating significantly more than other emerging-market currencies, as fiscal risks 
have come to the forefront. In our note, Brazil – Fiscal shocks and the BRL weakness, we explain 
the linkages between fiscal concerns and BRL weakness, focusing on how a lack of confidence 
in debt sustainability can trigger a rapid build-up of risk premia across Brazilian assets. We also 
propose a methodology to gauge whether these concerns have been fully priced in. Looking at 
past episodes of Brazil-specific fiscal stress, we find that the BRL may have room to depreciate 
further unless authorities announce a stronger commitment to fiscal responsibility, while global 
factors could exacerbate the adjustment. 
The BRL has depreciated significantly more than other EM currencies 
Source: Natixis, Bloomberg 
  Chile – The growth numbers are really bad 
GDP growth for Q3 collapsed by 1.8% q/q sa, worse than expectations (-1.4%). This pushed 
annual growth down to -1.1% y/y, from -0.2% in Q2. The breakdown by demand components is 
even worse. Every single major subcomponent contributed to the contraction, which is unusual: 
private consumption was down 0.31% q/q sa, government consumption fell 0.26%, and gross 
fixed capital formation plunged 3.65%. Exports were down 2.14% and imports were up 0.75%.  
We now forecast 2026 GDP to contract by between 0.75% and 0.25% y/y for the full year. 
However, we believe the economy will rebound to around 2.0% in 2027 as mining activity 
normalizes. Despite the massive slowdown, inflation is on track to be around 4.8–5.0% y/y by 
year-end, significantly above the 3.0% target. Inflation expectations (23m forward) remain 
anchored at the target. 
We don’t see a clear path to rate cuts despite the recession-like GDP numbers, given current 
inflation pressures and continued pressure on the CLP. Therefore we continue to forecast the 
policy rate to remain unchanged at 4.50% through the first half of 2027.
