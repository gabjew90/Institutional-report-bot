record high. The steepest declines have hit industries tied to the AI buildout: Electrical Equipment (-25.6%), Construction & Engineering

Friday, November 20, 2026
For the first time since 2022, Americans preparing Thanksgiving dinner will face higher costs than a year ago. According to the Farm 
Bureau, a typical meal for 10 will cost ~$59 in 2026, up 6% YoY. Turkey prices saw the largest increase (+10%), followed by milk, rolls, 
and cranberries. Rising food prices reflect higher costs for key agricultural inputs, particularly diesel and fertilizer. The silver lining? 
Thanksgiving dinner still costs ~9% less than its all-time high in 2022.

As The S&P 500 Nears Correction Territory, Fundamentals Paint A Brighter Picture
Chart of the Day: Wallets Get Carved
The S&P 500 is on track for its steepest weekly decline since April 2025 (-5.1%) and sits 8.2% below its Oct. 6 record high. While the 
pullback is unsettling, perspective matters: the average annual max drawdown is ~13%. Encouragingly, fundamentals remain solid, 
with consensus estimates pointing to 16% EPS growth in 2027. Meanwhile, the NTM P/E ratio has fallen to its lowest since 2023, creating 
what we view as an attractive entry point. With the 14-day RSI at 31, nearing oversold territory, weakness may be technically overdone.
With more than $190b raised MTD, November marks the year's fifth monthly issuance record. YTD issuance has surpassed $2t, another 
all-time high, partly fueled by hyperscaler borrowing. Despite this supply surge, IG spreads have held steady at 86–92 bps this month. 
IG total returns are down 0.6% MTD but up 1.6% QTD, outperforming high yield (0.0% QTD) and the S&P 500 (-6.2% QTD). Issuance 
should ease into year-end, supporting spreads. With index yields near 6%, we continue to favor higher-quality bonds.
