Overview
 The setup heading into 2027 is markedly less clean than the one we faced a year ago. Individual market technicals, and the 
forecasts they produce, are in meaningful tension when viewed side by side: medium-term breakdowns across several leading 
markets flag real risks to the macro-outlook, yet the weakness is not yet broad enough to argue for an outright negative call on 
the first half of the year. Our base case is a continuation of the mid- to late-cycle regime through the early quarters of 2027, 
with a close watch on the cracks that opened in the second half of 2026 — and, critically, on whether that anxiety leads to 
breakdowns across a wider set of cyclically sensitive markets. 
2026 in review- setting the stage for the 2027 year-ahead outlook
When we wrote our 2026 year-ahead outlook, markets seemed more convincingly poised at an inflection point, a setup that lent 
itself to a well-formulated and cohesive view across asset classes.  There, base metals’ emerging breakouts from multi-year ranges, 
which historically have been very good leading indicators for a pro-cyclical rotation across other asset classes and an eventual lift 
in the global manufacturing data, provided a cornerstone to extrapolate the throughput across other markets. This included the 
view that DM bond yields would rise into the early months of 2026, TIPS breakevens would bullishly reverse from key support 
and eventually challenge the upper end of the longer-term trading range, and the curve would initially steepen. We also thought 
that the curve could eventually inflect flatter by the middle of the year, as the things associated with the lift in the manufacturing 
data could eventually lead the market to rethink the Fed path. We thought equities would carry a bid through the first half of the 
year, led initially by cyclicals. The outlook favored the S&P 500 tracking the underside of the longer-term channel resistance that 
spanned from the March 2009 post-GFC low into the middle of the year. In the foreign exchange markets, we felt confident pairs 
like AUD/USD would advance as it is typically correlated with other pro-cyclical markets. However, we also thought that the US 
dollar’s technical setup could see it stay relatively bid during the pro-cyclical rotation in the first half of 2026. These dynamics 
would have marked an outlier, but not something that was completely unprecedented. Lastly, we had a forecast that base metals 
would stage a material rally on the back of their multi-year base breakouts and for precious metals to consolidate in the first half 
of the year, on the backs of their impressive multi-quarter rally legs.  While the initiation of a war in the Middle East created 
sizable gyrations across markets and most importantly broke Crude out of its anticipated trading range, our outlook views largely 
played out through the first half of 2026. 
At mid-year, we highlighted two key risks to monitor through the summer, which we felt could lead to increased volatility in the 
fall.  The similarities between the equity market dynamics surrounding the datacenter buildout and the 1999-2000 dynamics 
associated with the internet infrastructure buildout marked one of those risks. The other risk was the top pattern formations on 
base metals charts, which had developed near our 2026 longer-term rally target objectives (Figure 1). Those concerns proved 
warranted, as the AI hardware group corrected heavily through the summer and into the fall, and base metals did as well. We also 
saw the prospects that weakness across those two market segments could spread to a broader risk-off through other cyclically 
sensitive markets. For the first of those risks, the unwind of such a heavily concentrated market position carries its own technical 
risks as it relates to leverage and market sentiment. Furthermore, market participants could also extrapolate the market 
performance to shifts in the underlying fundamentals and the pass through to the broader economic cycle, both directly and via 
the wealth effect channel. The cyclical risks surrounding the base metals weakness are more straightforward. As noted above, 
base metals’ price performance has a long and consistent track record of leading broader pro- and anti-cyclical market rotations, 
as well as the global manufacturing data. Since the global manufacturing PMI data most closely tracks the 12-month metals’ price 
momentum, we not only pay close attention to recent price pattern developments and the implications for future market trends, 
but we also keep a close eye on year-ago base effect. The base metals’ potential downside reversal from major resistance and 
increasingly difficult year-ago base effect into the 1Q27 presents a low hurdle for the regression-based PMI forecast to turn 
meaningfully lower in early-2027. Even without any further damage on the metals’ charts, the 2H26 weakness already points to a 
meaningful setback in the manufacturing data, assuming metals’ prices stay near current levels into the end of the year(Figure 2.This patern, the recent weak performance, and their leading relationship with the Global manufacturing PM
I point to a meaningful risk heading into 2027. ).

Contradictory Technical Setups and our 1H27 Outlook
Despite the concerns cited above, US and European long-term equity index rally structures remain intact, albeit under 
considerable threat following the Nov declines. DM yield charts are bearishly trading cheaper than their 2H26 pattern 
breakdowns, and while curves have bear flattened in the fourth quarter, only select forward curves have marginally inverted. The 
S&P 500 rally has decelerated for a multi-month period below longer-term channel resistance, but the index has not broken 
longer-term trend or pattern support to suggest an impending reversal into a protracted correction period and mean reversion from 
longer-term channel resistance (Figure 3). Within the equity market, many groups associated with the AI theme have weakened 
considerably from their mid-year peaks and have broken down through critical longer-term pattern support. Naturally, 
breakdowns across leadership groups could act as leading indicators for the broader market indexes, however, we have not seen 
other cyclically sensitive groups break down from their medium-term ranges.While the risks have increased in recent weeks, the 
setup still seems most consistent with a mid- to late-cycle regime. As a base-case view, we suspect the S&P 500 Index will 
continue to trade above its key medium-term support in the 6800-7000 area. The persistent price action above that key support 
allows for the index to remain in the upper end of its longer-term trend channel. While the market has already decelerated 
meaningfully below this channel resistance in 2H26 and has seen rapid changes in leadership in recent months, which could 
signal the terminal phase for the longer-term advance, the yield curve is not inverted. Past bull cycles have typically formed their 
distribution patterns in the context of an inverted yield curve, which signaled tight monetary policy. The 2000 cycle peak was an 
exception, and there are other similarities between the current dynamics and that period, but we will leave these concerns in the 
risk scenario for now and until a wider array of markets have broken key pattern and trend support levels. Outside the US, 
European headline indexes have intact bull trends and lack low-frequency price patterns that would suggest the indexes are in the 
terminal phases of their bull markets. Japanese and Korean markets, however, have both broken down from broad price patterns 
that could be viewed as distribution patterns, like US semiconductor indexes. Setups across both country indexes favor a more 
cautious stance until clear bottom patterns are present or the markets are able to sustain back above their respective break down 
levels. Additionally, Chinese equity indexes have bearishly trended through the summer in correlation with many base metals.

factors have impacted the technical setup for the market in several ways. The most prominent of these is how position
