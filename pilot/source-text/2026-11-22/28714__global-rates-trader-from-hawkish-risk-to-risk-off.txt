Risk-off has again taken hold of the rates market, undoing much of the previous 
US-led hawkish repricing across global front-ends. Our broader baseline of steady 
growth and disinflation does not argue for sharply lower yields, but rather points to 
relief over time as inflation progress shifts the distribution of policy risks and 
reinforces bonds’ hedge value. But deeper challenges to the AI trade would change 
that calculus given the importance of the AI buildout to broader optimism and, in 
turn, markets pricing durably higher policy rates. In the US, we think the firmer macro 
data, recent Fedspeak, and the typical pattern of yields following financial conditions 
tightening bode favorably for 5y outperformance. Europe’s early-November 
front-end repricing reflected spillovers from abroad more than domestic factors. 
Softer Euro area growth prospects and the overhang of sovereign spread risks point 
towards more constrained selloffs in European duration and in turn favorable 
risk-reward to Bund longs. We think the appeal of longs sits further in on the UK 
curve given excessively hawkish very near-term pricing and a front-end that has 
significantly underperformed its beta to energy prices amid recent relief. In Japan, 
our baseline for a more hawkish-than-priced policy path argues for a flatter 2s10s 
JGB curve.
