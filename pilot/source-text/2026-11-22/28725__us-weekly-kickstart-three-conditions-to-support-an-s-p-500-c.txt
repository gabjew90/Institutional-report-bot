The S&P 500 correction and equity investor positioning 
 
 
The S&P 500 has plunged by 12% from its October 6 record high. The Nasdaq-100 
has declined by 20% and is now down 1% on the year. These sell-offs now exceed the 
indices’ respective median calendar year peak-to-trough declines since 1980. Following 
22 S&P 500 corrections since 1980, the index has returned an average of 4% during the 
subsequent 3 months, rising in 17 (73%) of those episodes. 
 
The S&P 500 forward P/E multiple has declined by 25% YTD and now registers 16x, 
close to the historical average, but valuations based on trend earnings remain 
elevated versus history. Normally, a sharp decline in valuation multiples creates an 
attractive entry point. However, while S&P 500 companies reported year/year EPS 
growth exceeding 30% in Q3, our recent conversations with investors have focused on 
the uncertainty surrounding the medium-term earnings outlook. The AI investment 
boom has lifted S&P 500 earnings about 25% above their 30-year trend, creating 
downside risk as capex tailwinds fade in coming years. The cyclically-adjusted P/E based 
on trend earnings has declined by 10% YTD and now ranks in the 83rd percentile since 
1990.
