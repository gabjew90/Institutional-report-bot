Thought of the day
What happened?
US equities fell again on Thursday as concerns about the AI trade combined 
with a more hawkish interest rate backdrop. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq 
Composite closed down 2% and 2.3%, respectively, and the sell-off 
continued in Asia on Friday. The Nikkei 225 slid over 5%, while Taiwan's 
benchmark index was 6.2% lower.
The market weakness was relatively broad. All major S&P 500 sectors 
traded lower, while declining stocks substantially outnumbered advancing 
stocks on both the NYSE and Nasdaq. Recent selling has followed 
persistent pressure on chipmakers and cloud companies as investors 
question the scale of investment required to build AI infrastructure.
Monetary policy uncertainty has added to fragile market sentiment. 
Minutes from the Federal Reserve's October meeting indicated that nearly 
all policymakers expected further tightening to be necessary if the US 
economy evolves as anticipated. Markets were pricing a near certainty of 
a 25-basis-point increase in December, with persistent inflation pressures 
also supporting the US dollar. US two-year and 10-year Treasury yields fell 
4bps and 2bps, respectively, while the US dollar held recent gains. Higher 
rate expectations and dollar strength weighed on gold, which fell to a 14-
month low of around USD 3,575 an ounce at the time of writing.

What do we think?
As outlined in our recently published Year Ahead publication, we do not 
think that the economic expansion, corporate earnings cycle, or structural 
AI investment story has broken. Our base case for 2027 remains for 
economic growth close to trend, rising corporate profits, further growth in 
AI investment, and moderating inflation later in the year. We still expect this 
combination to support additional equity gains and positive fixed income 
returns.
But the risk-reward in equities has become more balanced. We recently 
downgraded global equities from Attractive to Neutral, reflecting elevated 
expectations, high index concentration, and a wider range of potential 
outcomes around AI. In particular, the AI debate is shifting from whether 
investment will continue to whether it can generate sufficient revenues and 
shareholder returns. Today's price action supports this shift in focus.
Cumulative US technology investment in AI infrastructure could approach 
USD 5tr by the end of 2028. But we estimate that AI model and application 
revenues will probably have to reach at least USD 1.5tr by 2030 for the 
industry to earn an adequate return. While this is possible, it is unlikely 
that every company will reap the rewards from this: Competition, price 
cuts, costly monetization, and open-weight models may threaten returns. 
Financing, regulatory, and company-specific risks also add to uncertainty.
Tighter monetary policy expectations add another source of volatility. While 
we foresee further near-term rate increases (namely 25bps hikes in the US 
in December 2026 and March 2027), we do not anticipate a prolonged 
tightening cycle. The cumulative effects of higher rates look set to weigh 
on consumer demand, while we believe base effects will contribute to a 
moderation in underlying inflation.
How do we invest?
In our base case, we expect upside for equity markets in 2027. But the 
range of potential outcomes is broader. The challenge for investors in 
2027 is constructing portfolios that can prove resilient across a variety of 
scenarios.
Refocus equity exposure. In our base case, we expect corporate earnings 
growth to support equities, with around 15% S&P 500 earnings growth 
in 2027. But with the top 10 companies accounting for 39% of the index, 
relatively small changes in expectations about a handful of AI-linked 
businesses can have an outsized effect on returns.
The Eurozone is our preferred equity region, with 15% earnings growth 
forecast for 2027, a lower valuation than the US, and earnings drivers 
spanning manufacturing, defense, infrastructure, and electrification. At 
a sector level, we favor industrials and health care, while equal-weight 
strategies in the US can be a convenient way to help reduce technology 
concentration.

Within US technology, we favor financially strong hyperscalers with 
visible AI monetization, alongside select leaders in compute, foundry, 
semiconductor equipment, and infrastructure software. We remain cautious 
on leveraged capacity providers, lower-quality suppliers, and companies 
whose valuations depend on persistent bottlenecks or distant cash flows.
Rebalance into fixed income. Elevated yields offer an attractive 
combination of income and potential portfolio resilience, while providing 
a return cushion even if rates do not decline materially. We favor 
intermediate duration, broadly two to 10 years. We see attractive return 
opportunities in quality bonds, and within fixed income, we see the best 
return opportunity in emerging market and high yield bonds. EM sovereign 
and corporate bonds currently yield roughly 7.1% and 7.0%, respectively, 
while US high yield offers around 8.3%.
Use volatility, rather than fear it. Investors should prepare for volatility 
instead of trying to predict every setback. Structured strategies can 
help investors build exposure in more tailored ways, potentially limiting 
losses, generating additional income, or allowing more measured market 
participation. Higher implied volatility, all else equal, may raise the appeal of 
such approaches. However, investors must understand the potential trade-
offs, including capital loss, issuer, liquidity, and opportunity-cost risks, so we 
favor diversification and careful sizing of structures.
To read our views in more detail, please see Year Ahead: Sustaining 
resilience and Monthly Letter: Sustaining resilience.
Caught our attention
Gold comes under pressure. Gold extended its losses on Friday, falling 
1.3% to around USD 3,575/oz at the time of writing, the lowest level since 
September 2025. With a week-to-date drop of nearly 8%, bullion is on 
track to record its biggest weekly decline since March this year. Gold’s 
recent fall comes on the back of stronger US labor market data and above-
consensus-forecast inflation, which reinforced expectations of further Fed 
rate hikes. Hawkish comments from Fed officials about the discussion of 
outsized rate increases also added to the repricing, fueling the negative 
price momentum in gold.
Our view: We think the market may be overestimating both the level and 
duration of higher interest rates. We expect US inflation to level off and US 
economic activity to moderate slightly in 2027, allowing rate expectations 
to shift from further hikes to a pause, followed by cuts later in the year. 
We also expect central bank demand for reserve diversification to remain 
robust, and jewelry demand should recover in response to lower gold 
prices. While these two factors are expected to help stabilize prices rather 
than pushing prices higher, structural headwinds for the USD such as the 
US fiscal deficit trajectory should support gold over the long term. With 
the yellow metal now in technically oversold territory, we caution against 
extrapolating the recent downtrend too far. We see an attractive risk-
reward profile for a long position in gold, or investors may consider selling 
volatility to benefit from our positive price outlook. We maintain our view 
that the gold price should gravitate back toward USD 5,000/oz in late 
2027.

Our view: The results provide further evidence that Chinese demand for 
AI computing remains robust, and that cloud monetization is improving, 
even as the investment required to build capacity weighs on near-term 
cash flow. In our view, rising capital intensity and uneven returns reinforce 
the need for selectivity. Within China tech, we favor areas where earnings 
visibility, policy support, and demand fundamentals align, particularly 
onshore semiconductor equipment vendors, foundries, and other hardware 
companies benefiting from AI chip localization and technology self-
sufficiency. The latest spending commitments from large internet platforms 
should sustain demand across this domestic supply chain.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
In line: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
