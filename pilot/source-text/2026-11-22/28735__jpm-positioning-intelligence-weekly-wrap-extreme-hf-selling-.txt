Summary: Abandon all hope, ye who trade tech (and risk assets more generally)…that would 
seem to be the mood after another bruising week for the markets and HF performance.  In fact, 
based on today’s index returns, we’d estimate HFs were down 1.5% today and could be down >3% over 
the past 5d, which would be the worst since Apr 7, 2025.  The silver lining is that, unlike what we were 
seeing in Oct and early Nov, the losses continue to be driven by beta rather than alpha, which makes 
the past week also similar to what we saw in early Apr 2025.   
 
The clear answer to last week’s question of whether the Semis sell-off is over is “no.”  Increasingly, the 
2018 analog appears to be in focus given the 4Q selling and reversal in performance to basically 
flat YTD (we did see S&P briefly bounce in late Nov ’18 before ultimately selling off more in Dec).  More 
generally, we continue to see HFs reduce net risk at a magnitude that has historically been associated 
with a bounce, but we’re still waiting to see whether there’s a broader capitulation.  Perhaps that 
happened today and we’ll keep an eye on how positioning continues to evolve from here. 
 
Key Points: 
 
1. HF performance was hit again, while gross flows continued to recover. Based on today’s 
index performance, HFs are estimated to have fallen another 1.5%, putting MTD returns at -4%. 
5d and 63d returns are now below -3z, though the negative overall returns and more 
neutral HF alpha (partly due to prior de-risking by HFs) makes this quite similar to Apr ’25 
and early Aug ’24. Gross flows have rebounded from prior lows and are back in line with 
average US seasonality, with additions across most regions this past week. 
 
2. HF net selling and the decline in net leverage are approaching extreme levels. Global HF 
net selling is around -2z over 20 days and exceeds 2z in North America. Historically, periods 
of large HF net selling that coincided with a markets sell-off, have been followed by a 
rebound over the next month, but so far that’s not been the case.  The decline in net leverage 
from 12-month highs is comparable to some of the most extreme episodes since 2018 and 8wk 
change is around -2z. Net leverage is at the 26th percentile over the past five years for All 
Strategies. 
 
3. Broader capitulation is not yet confirmed by US TPM (prior to Fri at least). Despite low 
positioning, the US TPM’s 4wk change was only around -1z as of Thursday, above the -1.5z 
trigger.  The positioning level is at the 13th percentile since 2015, near year-to-date lows and 
closer to April 2025 levels, but still above more severe washout levels. CTA risk has been 
reduced but not extremely, while asset managers appear to be maintaining exposure in futures.
