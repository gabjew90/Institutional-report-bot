•         MACRO READ-THROUGH FROM BANK EARNINGS – Day 1 of bank earnings told 
a consistent story: consumer spending and credit are still strong, and the labor market is 
what's holding both up. Card spending grew 10% year over year at JPM and 13% at C. 
Delinquencies and net credit losses are falling year over year at C and WFC. Corporate 
credit looks just as healthy, with C reporting low corporate non-accruals and net credit 
losses. WFC noted that higher prices are weighing on sentiment and that lower-income 
households are more sensitive to financing costs, inflation and fuel. Even so, that group is 
described as stable, not getting worse. Overall, the read-through supports a resilient US 
consumer view, and WFC summed up the bullish case best: “as long as the economy 
continues to grow and employment stays strong, that should continue. And we're just not 
seeing signs of that changing at this point."  
•         Given today’s macro read-through, we would like top reflagging our thoughts 
on Consumer (published in the October 12th Morning Briefing) – The most recent Chase 
Spending data (here) suggested that total spending growth in September was up 6.6% 
YoY, an acceleration compared to August’s 5.3%. Spending growth was strong across all 
income cohorts. On a YoY basis (MTD in September vs. the same period in 2025), 
upper-, middle-, and lower-income spending were up 7.8%, 8.2%, and 6.6%, respectively. 
Consumer bank data (here) suggested that strong lower-income wage growth was 
supportive of this spending trend; lower-, middle-, and higher-income household wage 
growth was 4.7%, 3.8%, and 3.7% in August. Retail spending growth is being evenly 
distributed across discretionary and non-discretionary categories.  
o   Mkt Intel remains bullish on the US consumer, with the hypothesis 
supported by historically strong cash levels and subsequent spending, and 
we see the upcoming Q3 earnings release as a positive catalyst for the 
sector. The JPM Retail basket (JP2RTL) was down 8.7% from the August 
peak, as affordability and rising yields became headwinds for the sector. 
Positioning Intel tells us that the L/S Ratio %-ile since Jan 2018 for Retail 

(JP2RTL) and Consumer Discretionary (JP2CND) is at 37%-ile and 38%-
ile, respectively (data as of October 8, 2025).

EARNINGS MACRO READTHROUGH 
•         SUMMARY – Day 1 of bank earnings told a consistent story: consumer spending and 
credit are still strong, and the labor market is what's holding both up. Card spending grew 
10% year over year at JPM and 13% at C. Delinquencies and net credit losses are falling 
year over year at C and WFC. Corporate credit looks just as healthy, with C reporting low 
corporate non-accruals and net credit losses. WFC noted that higher prices are weighing 
on sentiment and that lower-income households are more sensitive to financing costs, 
inflation and fuel. Even so, that group is described as stable, not getting worse. Overall, 
the read-through supports a resilient US consumer view, and WFC summed up the bullish 

case best: “as long as the economy continues to grow and employment stays strong, that 
should continue. And we're just not seeing signs of that changing at this point."  
•         CONSUMER HEALTH & RESILIENCE 
o   [Consumer Health] C – "In the US, whilst much has been made of the AI 
boom, the consumer is still driving much of the growth, the labor market is 
stable, and companies remain active." 
o   Consumer Resilience] JPM – "… the consumer has been quite resilient 
in the face of a number of headwinds that under different circumstances 
you might have expected would present a challenge." 
o   [Consumer Resilience] WFC – "The consumer remains one of the 
economy's most important sources of resilience though higher prices 
continue to weigh on consumer sentiment." 
o   [Income Cohort Divergence] WFC – "High income households continue 
to benefit from strong asset prices, substantial home equity and stable 
labor income. And while lower income households remain more sensitive to 
financing costs, inflation and higher living and fuel expenses, they appear 
to be stable." 
o   [Macro Outlook] WFC – "And as long as the economy continues to grow 
and employment stays strong, that should continue. And we're just not 
seeing signs of that changing at this point, either from the way our -- the 
sentiment across our commercial client base or just in the actual metrics 
that we're seeing on the consumer side." 
•         CONSUMER SPENDING 
o   [Consumer Spending] C – "Spend volume up 13% and average loans up 
9%, both partially offset by declines in private label cards." 
o   [Consumer Spending] JPM – "A few points to highlight debit and credit 
spend grew 10% year-on-year with continued momentum across lending 
categories, including gas and travel supported by consumer demand." 
•         CREDIT QUALITY 

o   [Consumer Credit Quality] C – "… And we continue to see strong 
consumer credit performance as both net credit loss and delinquency rates 
in our overall cards portfolio declined year over year." 
o   [Consumer Credit Quality] WFC – "At the same time, consumer credit 
remains strong with our charge-off ratio down from both the second quarter 
and a year ago, and early-stage delinquencies at historically low levels." 
o   [Net Charge-Off Expectation] JPM – "Finally, we continue to expect the 
card net charge-off rate to be approximately 3.2%." 
o   [Corporate Credit Quality] C – "And in the quarter, corporate non-accrual 
loans as well as corporate net credit losses remained low."

financial benefits from AI and automation significantly outweigh the 
investments we have made year-to-date…"

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the October 12 Morning Briefing 
•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – we reiterate our view as we head into earnings. Last 
week, we had a test of the bullish hypothesis with last week’s OpenAI news where an 
accounting misinterpretation led to a 3.4% decline on Thursday. Though the story was 
updated across multiple media sources, Semis ended the week on a 3-day losing streak. 
Now, does the market regain its confidence in Semis ahead of earning, post-earnings, or 
not at all? The SOX is ~20% off its July lows and ~15% below its June ATHs, which 
points to an inflection point given recent price action. We think Semis can climb this latest 
wall of worry as recent earnings updates from Hynix / Samsung point to on-going 
thematic strength. Separately, breadth continues to deteriorate which we think can 
reverse into / out of earnings season which will buttress the rally. 
•         RISKS – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI Credit Fears / 
Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – (i) Macro Data – CPI on Oct 14, Retail Sales & PPI on Oct 15, Flash 
PMIs on Oct 23, and Fed Day on Oct 28; (ii) Lower Bond Yields – while growth 
continues to drive yields higher, dovish inflation prints could remove further hike 
expectations out of the market which is currently pricing a Dec hike and then 2x hikes in 
FY27; (iii) Banks Earnings – kicking off earnings season with stronger earnings and a 
favorable consumer and lending outlook would be helpful in restarting the Cyclicals trade 
and broadening the bull market. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with a 
preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 

Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your 
shopping list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and 
Retailers given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy 
prices. Other elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower 
energy prices. Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and 
Japan. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long 
but would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / 
yields, which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, 
there is likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the 
Tech tape. Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus 
bullish play. The AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we 
like Tech in the near-term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we 
think you need to see the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; 
excluding AI-related plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper 
yield curve, and favorable capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real 
Estate, Telecom, and Utilities may participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most 
likely to lag the SPX, ex-AI plays. The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which 
would squeeze EU and RTY higher; plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, 
Cruise Lines, Discretionary, Hotels, etc) is another area of interest with a preference to 
play via options.  
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (SEP 21) – We maintain our core view of +Tech vs. 
-RTY and that Tech view is a mix of Mag7, Memory, and Semis, and we would consider 
keeping the pair $-neutral or market neutral. Early in hiking cycles, we tend to see USD 
bid higher and the yield curve flattening; together, this is supportive of a move towards 
Secular Growth / MegaCap Tech. Given the likelihood of higher yields and a stronger 
USD, we think the US can tactically outperform DM. That said, the fear / uncertainty 
around AI may push the market into non-AI plays, which puts Latam in focus as a long 
play; we like Brazil / Latam irrespective of your views on AI.
