This week's game of snake will involve navigating the latest complex geopolitical news and the US inflation 
data, with September CPI on Wednesday and PPI on Thursday, the final readings before the Fed’s 
October 28 meeting, alongside a broader US activity pulse from retail sales (Thursday) and industrial 
production (Friday). After Columbus Day today where US bond markets will be closed but equities open, Q3 
US earnings season also gets properly under way tomorrow, led by the big US banks, while across the 
globe ASML (Wednesday) and TSMC (Thursday) should provide another useful read on the AI capex 
cycle. Elsewhere, China has September inflation and trade data on Wednesday, UK monthly GDP is due 
Thursday, and the IMF/World Bank meetings run through the week, with the IMF’s latest World Economic 
Outlook out tomorrow and a long list of central-bank speakers.   
The big geopolitical/energy story over the weekend is the continued complexity of the Middle East and 
Russia/Ukraine conflicts. Trump announced yesterday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an 
immediate “energy ceasefire”, but overnight developments underline how fragile that claim is. 
Zelenskyy said Ukraine supports a ceasefire if Russia does too and is awaiting details from Washington, while 
Moscow had still not publicly confirmed it. Zelenskyy also said Russia subsequently struck a thermal power 
plant in Kharkiv and other Ukrainian energy infrastructure. So for now this looks more like an untested US 
proposal than a functioning ceasefire, particularly after a similar arrangement last month failed to 
endure. 
The proposed ceasefire is closely linked to the extraordinary US pivot on Russian diesel. Ukrainian refinery 
strikes had forced Moscow to restrict fuel exports, tightening an already constrained products market. 

Trump’s deal with Putin would allow around 13.5 million barrels of Russian diesel back into global 
markets this month via a temporary US sanctions waiver, potentially including the first US imports since 
March 2022. Zelenskyy has criticised the move, while Reuters reported that Washington threatened to 
cut intelligence sharing and weapons procurement support unless Kyiv halted refinery strikes. Russian 
barrels returning would be bearish for diesel cracks, but the overnight strikes make the ceasefire increasingly 
uncertain and highlight strains between Washington and Kyiv. 
Meanwhile, Middle East energy risk has moved sharply in the other direction. Riyadh’s King Khalid 
International Airport was hit again on Sunday after repeated attacks over the past week. Saudi Arabia 
said the latest strike killed one person and injured 11, after Saturday’s attack killed 12 and wounded more 
than 300. The escalation comes as Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces counter recent Houthi 
gains near Bab el-Mandeb, the crucial Red Sea shipping chokepoint. Repeated strikes disrupting flights in 
Riyadh also underline how difficult the Houthi threat may be for Saudi Arabia to suppress. 
That leaves a meaningful risk of wider regional escalation. Saudi Arabia has intensified air strikes in 
Yemen, while Trump said the US “may” join strikes against the Houthis after an American was among 
those killed on Saturday. Overnight, Iran denied directing the Houthis and its foreign minister urged 
diplomacy with Saudi Arabia, but there is little sign yet of de-escalation. Washington has so far limited 
support to intelligence sharing while Riyadh seeks more direct US help and air-defence support. So even if a 
Russia/Ukraine ceasefire and Russian diesel waiver ease pressure on refined products, attacks on 
Riyadh and fighting around Bab el-Mandeb leave an upside tail risk for oil and shipping costs, alongside 
continuing Strait of Hormuz disruption. 
With all of that newsflow, this morning Brent is -1.32% at $102.34/bbl with other markets quiet given that 
Japan’s cash equity markets are closed and US bond markets are going to be shut for the holiday. The

Going onto more detail on the week ahead, the main event is US CPI on Wednesday. Our economists expect 
headline CPI to rise +0.57% MoM in September, up from +0.40% in August, largely because seasonally 
adjusted motor-fuel prices are expected to jump 9.6%. That would lift headline inflation to around 3.64% 
YoY. The core picture should be much softer: our economists expect +0.16% MoM, down from +0.29%, 
taking the annual rate down a couple of basis points to 2.39%. Within core, they expect goods prices to be 
unchanged, with lower used-car and medical-care commodity prices offset by firmer recreation and IT 
goods. On services, last month’s jump in wireless prices should roll off, although higher jet-fuel costs 
should support airfares. The contrast between a fuel-driven headline acceleration and a softer underlying 
core print will clearly be important for the Fed, particularly given recent messaging that further tightening may 
be needed but is not urgent. 
The inflation story does not end there. PPI follows on Thursday and import prices on Friday, both important 
for mapping the CPI details into core PCE. These are the last inflation data Fed officials will see before the 
October FOMC meeting. Our economists’ ex-ante estimate is for core PCE (released on October 29th, 
the day after the FOMC) to rise around +0.21% MoM, leaving the annual rate roughly steady at 3.0%. If 

they are close to our economists’ forecasts, the team thinks the Fed can remain on hold on October 28 and 
wait for more information before December. Chair Warsh’s fireside chat with IMF Managing Director 
Georgieva on Thursday evening will therefore be particularly interesting, as it is the final Fed 
appearance before the communications blackout. On activity, our economists expect total September 
retail sales to be unchanged (Thursday) and industrial production to rise +0.2% MoM (Friday). The Fed’s Beige 
Book is also out Wednesday. Today is Columbus Day, so US bond markets are closed although equities 
remain open. 
Earnings season is another important part of the week. JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo and 
Citigroup report tomorrow, followed by Bank of America, Morgan Stanley and BlackRock on 
Wednesday. ASML reports Wednesday and TSMC on Thursday. Our US equity strategists expect S&P 500 
earnings growth to remain at a record 34% YoY in Q3, versus consensus at 27%. Their view is that 
consensus is again embedding too much of a slowdown, leaving scope for another quarter of above-average 
beats. See their piece here. In Europe, our equity strategists expect earnings growth to reach 25% in Q3, 5 
pp above consensus (their full preview is here). So it should be a very strong earnings season on both sides 
of the Atlantic and indeed across the globe.   
Recapping last week now and markets seemed to be on high alert throughout, but ultimately put in a decent 
performance as the financial stress around the Eurozone began to moderate, albeit not in a straight line. For 
instance, the Franco-German 2yr spread came down -14.6bps to 52bps. Moreover, the 10yr spread also 
tightened -3.7bps over the week to 137bps. That went alongside stabilisation for global bonds more broadly 
after the relentless selloff of recent weeks. For instance, the 10yr Treasury yield fell -3.3bps on the week to 
5.24% (+1.2bps Friday), although it did hit its highest intraday level since 2002 on Wednesday, at 5.36%. 
However, the 10yr bund yield still edged +1.9bps higher on the week to 3.48%.   
That backdrop helped global equities recover in aggregate, though the performance was varied on the two 
sides of the Atlantic. In the US, we even saw the S&P 500 hit another record on Wednesday, with a weekly 
gain of +1.15% (+0.59% Friday). Over in Europe by contrast, the STOXX 600 was little changed (+0.03%) on 
the week despite a +0.97% recovery on Friday. And there were some continued points of stress, with 
France’s CAC 40 down 1.19% (+0.95% Friday), marking its 8th weekly decline in the last 9. Meanwhile, 
European banks also fell back, with the STOXX Banks index down -2.46% (+0.51% Friday). 
As all that was happening, the lack of any clear progress between the US and Iran kept oil prices elevated. So 
Brent crude ended the week up +2.42% (+0.42% Friday) at $104.72/bbl, whilst European natural gas rose 
by +8.93% to €81.40/MWh, its highest weekly close since 2022. That went alongside gains for other 
commodities, including gold, which rose +1.28% last week, and copper, which rose +2.18%.
