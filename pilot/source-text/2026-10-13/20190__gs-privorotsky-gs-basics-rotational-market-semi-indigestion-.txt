GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Rotational Market. Semi Indigestion.
Bond Vol Compression. 13 Oct 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
13 Oct 2026
Asia: Fiercely rotational market overnight. KOSPI -5-6%, Nikkei -1.5-2%, Taiwan -2%, with the CXMT
news reverberating through memory (Musk tweet didn't help). Samsung/Hynix down 7–10%, while
Kioxia, Advantest, Tokyo Electron and SoftBank are all under pressure. But the moves beneath the surface
are fascinating. In Japan, five stocks effectively account for the entire Nikkei decline....TOPIX is actually
trading up slightly. Not indiscriminate selling… but more unwind of crowded positions.
AI: Four stories to unpack. First, CXMT is a legitimate lt concern for memory economics. Second,
Microsoft’s Maia 300 custom chips, moving to TSMC’s 2nm process. Incrementally negative for merchant
GPU suppliers (NVDA), positive for the custom silicon supply chain and aggregate compute consumption.
Third, Hock Tan’s comments comparing AI investors to gamblers have been taken wildly out of context.
Both Broadcom and Intel reaffirmed exceptionally strong demand for another 2–3 years, potentially
through 2030. Tan sees compute demand exceeding supply and explicitly acknowledged that frontier labs
generating substantial revenue can afford expensive financing. LT ROI is a legitimate question, but hardly
a revelation. Fourth, Meta’s Muse Wonder and Wonder Full look genuinely competitive at the frontier,
potentially adding another serious player alongside OpenAI, Anthropic and Google. Some questions
around benchmarking, but impressive nonetheless. Prediction markets also anticipating another major
Google release imminently. What do I make of all this? Competition fuels competition, and competition
requires more compute. The frontier keeps advancing, driven by Western closed source labs trying to
outdo one another. Hardly bearish for aggregate compute demand, even if the economics shift around the
supply chain. Think the legitimate concerns are around the ability to complete capacity/deliver power
relative to expectations and that is fueling further de-rating in the capex vertical.
Rotation: Look, the market got itself incredibly long one corner and effectively short everything else.
Never healthy. SPX barely moved yesterday while semis unperformed and the broader market
strengthened. Dispersion has flipped on its head. Bonds stabilizing, bond vol compressing, gold finding a
bid, equal weight and Russell outperforming. Another series of pain trades for concentrated books. Last
week was difficult for alpha, and at this point in the year, people who’ve had a great run are increasingly
incentivized to protect it. Less appetite to intermediate risk, crowded positioning make for violent
rotations. I acknowledge the structural questions around AI, particularly capital intensity and returns, but
outside the competitive memory concerns I’m not sure we’ve learned much that’s new.
Energy: So much for the Russia/Ukraine energy ceasefire… couldn’t survive a news cycle. Arguably there
was never a ceasefire to begin with. Trump announced a deal that surprised Kyiv and was never confirmed
by Moscow, only for Russia to strike Ukrainian power infrastructure within hours. The Middle East isn’t
much better. Houthi strikes on Riyadh airport exposed Saudi air defence vulnerabilities, yet Brent is lower

and production remains intact. The market is distinguishing between geopolitical escalation and barrels
actually lost… reasonable, but a fragile equilibrium. Unclear where to from here, not much time until
political deadlines. Seems like everything has been tried outside of agreeing a deal on Hormuz and so far
has not yielded much to bring prices down.
Risk: Three things matter. First, bonds are objectively stabilizing. Yields have seemingly found a level,
implied vol is compressing/giving back nearly half its move off of undisturbed. Second, oil remains
unresolved. Perhaps a narrow diplomatic pathway ahead of the midterms, but the clock is running out. If
energy takes out the highs with velocity, markets will struggle in a much more correlated fashion. Third,
locally AI positioning is problematic, but aggregate equity exposure remains incredibly low. Nets are
conservative. This is rotation rather than wholesale liquidation. So where does that leave us? Probably a
choppy market while positioning clears itself out. Russell, equal weight and Europe should outperform
while AI hardware digests its excesses. But I don’t think the hyperscalers are going down much. They own
the scarce compute, and accelerating inference demand makes that installed capacity increasingly
valuable. They should provide a meaningful cushion to the index. It’s the hardware leg that struggles,
caught between crowded positioning, Chinese competition and capex concerns but I think a dip that
ultimately gets bought. Financials could reinforce the rotation, with the bar relatively low given de-rating
ahead of numbers. Watch: Waller and Barkin speaking, bank numbers and SEMICON West headlines on
capex, packaging and HBM. ASML tomorrow, TSMC Thursday to bring stability to semi trade.
