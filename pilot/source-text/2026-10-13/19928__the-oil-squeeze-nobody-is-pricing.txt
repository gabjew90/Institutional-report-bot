The Oil Squeeze Nobody Is Pricing
Oil is quietly setting up for a potentially explosive move.
Prices are approaching key breakout levels, yet implied volatility remains remarkably depressed and
the market appears far more concerned about downside risks.
Meanwhile, rising oil is already creating pressure beneath the surface, from soaring transportation
costs to vulnerable equity markets in India and Korea.
The question is whether the rest of the market is prepared for another leg higher in crude.
The oil squeeze
Oil continues to find support along the rising trend line in place since the July lows.
The setup is getting increasingly interesting. A close above the mid-September closing high around
$108.5 would open the door to a potentially violent upside squeeze.

Oil volatility: Pricing the wrong risk?
Despite oil marching towards recent highs, implied volatility remains remarkably depressed.
As we highlighted recently, the crowd remains obsessed with downside risks, while pricing in very little
upside volatility potential.
We find this disconnect increasingly intriguing, particularly with oil threatening to break higher.
Cheap upside optionality looks like an interesting way to position for a potentially violent squeeze.

The hidden oil squeeze
There is another interesting development beneath the surface of the oil market.
JPMorgan points out that the cost of shipping crude from the Middle East to China has exploded to
approximately $35 per barrel, compared with just $3 earlier this year.
The result? Delivered Middle Eastern crude is now costing Chinese buyers around $143 per barrel.
Unlike April, when the problem was a physical shortage of oil, today's squeeze is increasingly about the
cost of moving it.

The implications go well beyond the oil market. Energy importers are facing a much bigger cost shock
than benchmark crude prices alone suggest, while European refining margins are already coming
under severe pressure.
Oil doesn't necessarily need another supply shock to create problems for the global economy.

The rates connection
The tight relationship between oil and Treasury yields has loosened recently, but the underlying risk
hasn't disappeared. Another leg higher in crude could quickly revive inflation concerns and put
renewed upward pressure on yields.
With rates already elevated, this is a cross-asset relationship worth watching closely.

Oil and breakevens
US 10-year breakevens have moved higher recently, but zooming out reveals a much calmer picture.
Despite oil pushing towards recent highs, inflation expectations remain remarkably contained. For
now, the bond market is showing little concern about oil-driven inflation risks.

Oil up, India down
India remains one of the equity markets most vulnerable to rising oil prices, given its heavy
dependence on imported crude.
The relationship between oil and Indian equities is striking. Higher oil feeds directly into the import bill,
inflation expectations, currency pressure and ultimately equity valuations.
With oil threatening to break higher, this is a relationship worth watching closely.

Another headache for the AI trade?
Korea sits at an increasingly uncomfortable intersection of two major market themes.
The country is heavily dependent on imported energy, making its equity market particularly sensitive
to rising oil prices. At the same time, KOSPI has become one of the most concentrated expressions of
the global AI trade.
With Korean semiconductors already under pressure, another leg higher in crude could add to the
squeeze through higher costs, inflation pressure and tighter financial conditions.
The bigger question is whether further weakness in Korea remains a local story, or starts spilling over
into the broader AI complex.
