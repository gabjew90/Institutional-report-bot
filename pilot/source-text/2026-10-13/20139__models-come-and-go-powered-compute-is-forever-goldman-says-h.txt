 
Models Come And Go, Powered Compute Is 
Forever: Goldman Says Hyperscalers Deserve Their 
Premium 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, OCT 12, 2026 - 06:05 PM 
Who has the best AI model? Ask again next quarter. 
That, in a nutshell, is the message from Goldman's Rich Privorotsky (full note available here for 
pro subs), who after a week in which hedge funds dumped tech on an OpenAI revenue headline 
and Google reportedly started testing a model that could top Anthropic's flagship, steps back 
and asks which part of the AI stack actually has a moat. His answer: not the models. 
"Bigger picture unchanged. Compute is scarce, and the owners of scarce compute are the 
biggest beneficiaries. Part of the reason they are staying scarce is because the market is 
beginning to doubt the ability/capacity of the street to bring power/data center capacity online to 
meet demand. Hence the de-rating in the data center supply chain plays.  
That said, Hyperscalers have invested most aggressively in precisely this scarce commodity 
and can command premium economics. The market is right to reward them. Model leadership 
can change quarterly… ownership of powered compute is a much more durable advantage." 
(emphasis ours) 
Translation: the frontier model race is a beauty pageant with a quarterly reset, while the 
gigawatts already plugged in are the closest thing this cycle has to a toll road. And the very 
bottleneck that is crushing the data center supply chain names is what keeps the toll high for 
those who already own the road. 

 
 
Below we look at the model horse race, the OpenAI ARR wobble, the power wall, and how the 
hedge fund crowd is (badly) positioned into TSMC and ASML earnings this week. 
The Horse Race: Google Back In The Saddle 
Privorotsky's first observation is that the leaderboard is anything but settled: "Google reportedly 
testing Carbon, potentially competitive with Opus 5.5. Polymarket pricing Google as a serious 
contender for best model... while OpenAI and Anthropic have new models they have yet to 
reveal." 
He's not exaggerating. Goldman's note included a Polymarket snapshot showing Anthropic's 
long-running dominance in the "best model at end-2026" market collapsing in a matter of days in 
late September, with Google surging to the top. 

 
 
When we checked the market live this morning (Oct 12, 3:40am PT), Google was priced at 43% 
to own the top-ranked model on the LMArena leaderboard on Dec 31, edging out Anthropic at 
42%, with OpenAI at 9%, Meta at 3% and xAI at 2%, on $2.4MM of total volume. Which, for a 
company whose latest flagship we described as acing the SAT while struggling with the actual 
job (Sep 30), is quite the comeback. 
 

 
But that's precisely Privorotsky's point: a betting market that can swing from one lab to another 
in a week is pricing a crown that keeps changing heads. If you need a prediction market to tell 
you who's winning, nobody has a moat.

circulating, sending the Nasdaq tumbling after the FT reported disappointing OpenAI revenues

Privorotsky is relaxed about the substance: "The volatility around OpenAI's ARR last week 
appears largely a question of accounting definitions." Indeed, the gap reportedly came down to 
whether partner/cloud sales are booked gross (as Anthropic does) or net (as OpenAI does). 
What bothers him more is what the reaction revealed: "Don't want to dwell on specifics, but the 
reaction suggests more length and increasing sensitivity in these pockets." 
Put differently, the market isn't worried about who books revenue how; it's worried that everyone 
owns the same thing. Which brings us to the question we (and Goldman) have been asking for 
weeks: whether token demand can outrun collapsing token prices, the bar Goldman set for 
continued hyperscaler spending back in September.

The Power Wall: Why Compute Stays Scarce 
The reason compute stays scarce has less to do with chips than with electrons. As Privorotsky 
puts it, the market "is beginning to doubt the ability/capacity of the street to bring power/data 
center capacity online to meet demand." 

 
Regular readers know this story well. Back in April we reported that half of US data centers 
scheduled to start in 2026 will be canceled or delayed (Apr 13), with only about 5GW of the 
~16GW planned actually under construction. In August, Wood Mackenzie found that more than 
two-thirds of the power sought for US data centers will never materialize (Aug 14), after ERCOT 
interconnection requests hit 474GW against peak Texas demand of 91GW. And last month 
Goldman's own commodity team concluded that the industry would increasingly bring its own 
power plant, with behind-the-meter generation powering 25% of all data centers by 2030 (Sep 
28), up from "effectively 0%" in 2025. 
Here is the paradox: the bottleneck that is de-rating the data center supply chain is the same 
bottleneck that makes existing powered capacity more valuable. If the street can't build it, 
whoever already built it wins. Goldman's TMT specialist Peter Callahan echoed the point in his 
weekend "10 things", citing the "still durable bull case of compute demand outstripping compute 
supply (in turn, driving confidence in 2027+ capex)", while Dell management told Goldman's 
Katherine Murphy that "supply remains the primary gating factor to AI infrastructure growth." 
And, as we noted in April, with hundreds of billions in hyperscaler capex already spoken for, the 
companies writing the biggest checks are also the ones locking up the scarcest asset. Premium 
economics, in Privorotsky's words; a toll road, in ours. (More for pro subs) 
Positioning: Everyone Sold Tech On A Headline 
None of this stopped the hedge fund crowd from bailing. Per Goldman Prime data cited by 
Privorotsky, US hedge funds "rotated into Industrials and out of Info Tech... a notable reversal of 
the September trend... with the Tech selling most pronounced Thursday on the OpenAI revenue 
headline." 
The numbers: after being the most-bought US sector in 4 of the prior 5 weeks, Info Tech was by 
far the most net sold sector this week at -1.0 standard deviations, while fundamental L/S net 
leverage sits at just 52.9%, the 6th percentile on a 1-year lookback. As Privorotsky notes, "More 
AI as a percentage of the book, but the book itself has shrunk. Still a ton of capacity." (GS 
positioning data for pro subs) 
That capacity gets tested quickly. ASML reports Wednesday and TSMC on Thursday, and 
Privorotsky flags that ASML is reportedly raising spare parts prices 10%, hardly what a company 
facing collapsing demand does. Callahan adds that TSMC just had its biggest 4-day pullback 
since late July (down ~7% Tue-Fri), with investors focused on the 2027+ capex outlook, where 
Goldman's analysts sit at 
98BN for 2027E/2028E.

Bottom Line 
We side with Privorotsky. The market spent last week fretting over whose ARR was grossed up 
and whose model tops a leaderboard, while ignoring the one thing in AI that can't be replicated 
in a quarter: powered, operational compute, in a world where half of planned capacity slips and 
two-thirds of requested power never shows up. Models will keep leapfrogging each other; 
Google may wear the crown at Christmas and lose it by Easter. The gigawatts aren't going 
anywhere. 
Bet on the jockey if you like; Goldman says own the racetrack. The first real test comes 
Wednesday with ASML, followed by TSMC's capex guide before the open on Thursday.
