INTERESTING - Andrej Karpathy is highlighting a rapidly widening “funnel” of shared understanding
about LLM capabilities. Roughly 6 billion people have barely touched these systems, 1–2 billion treat
free-tier chatbots as a slightly better search or writing tool, only about 20 million professionals (especially
in math and code) are seeing complex projects that once took weeks collapse into agent-driven work, and
a tiny group of ~5,000 people with internal frontier access are watching swarms of agents tackle multi-
week software megaprojects, zero-days, new science, and frontier mathematics at speeds that already
outpace human review. The gap is driven by problem ambition, the systems’ jagged strengths in
digital/verifiable domains, and unequal access; the result is that most of the world still sees a “derpy
chatbot” while a microscopic elite is experiencing the curve going vertical—all at the same time.
Karpathy is one of the most credible voices on this trajectory: a founding member of OpenAI, former
Director of AI at Tesla (where he led Autopilot’s neural networks), Stanford PhD under Fei-Fei Li, and
long-time public educator on deep learning. His technical depth, track record of shipping real systems, and
unusually clear writing make his observations on capability gaps and the jagged frontier worth close
attention.

Memory - We view emerging high-performance NAND-based solutions - including High-Bandwidth Flash
(HBF) - as promising tools to address a worsening memory constraint in AI inference. We see a potential
TAM of ~$40 - $150B at a GPU adoption rate of 15%-50% by 2030 given the rapid proliferation of read-
heavy and memory-intensive AI inference workloads - despite potential challenges to adoption. We
believe that HBF memory can meaningfully reduce the overall cost of inference throughput by increasing
GPU utilization, resulting a ~30% lower overall cost of inference throughput - translating to as much as
several hundreds of billions of hyperscaler CapEx savings. We continue to favour SanDisk (SNDK, Buy)

and SK Hynix (000660.KS) given their technology leadership in HBF, and strong core business with
improving product mix. We also see Samsung Electronics (005930.KS, Buy) as well positioned given its
strong NAND portfolio; we see Micron (MU, Neutral) as less of a direct beneficiary given its primary
focus on DRAM and lower exposure to NAND. Although we recognize challenges to widespread HBF
adoption, we see promise in the technology which could drive meaningfully higher cost/performance at
the system level, particularly in memory-intensive inference workloads. | | • | Americas Payment Tech Q3
