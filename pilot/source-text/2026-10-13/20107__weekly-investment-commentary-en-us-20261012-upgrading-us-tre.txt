Upgrading U.S. Treasuries to lock in income
•
We upgrade U.S. Treasuries, preferring short-term bonds, as higher yields 
improve potential returns, partly funded by downgrading U.S. equities to neutral. 
•
Global bonds swung sharply last week, with 10- and 30-year U.S. Treasury yields 
hitting 24-year highs before retreating after strong bond auctions.
•
U.S. CPI and PPI offer the first look at September inflation this week. We watch 
for signs that could shift Fed expectations and Treasury yields.
Higher yields and potentially excessive Fed tightening expectations create room to 
unwind our U.S. Treasury underweight – a mainstay for most of the last six years. 
We upgrade Treasuries, favoring short-term bonds. In turn, we take profits: 
downgrading U.S. equities to neutral to manage potential volatility around the AI 
buildout and trimming our Japanese government bond (JGB) underweight. We 
retain our conviction in AI fundamentals and preference for AI within U.S. equities.

The 10-year Treasury yield has risen 110 basis points this year to around 5.25%, 
driven mainly by higher real interest rates rather than inflation expectations or 
term premium. Two forces are behind the rise in real rates: intensifying 
competition for capital is pushing up the neutral rate while Middle East tensions 
and higher oil prices have driven a sharp reversal in Fed rate expectations. Since 
January, markets have priced in 160 additional basis points of Fed tightening 
over the next 12 months. We think that is overdone. With term premium 
averaging around 75 basis points since September, higher yields offer a more 
attractive entry point. At current yields, our analysis suggests a 75-basis-point 
cushion against further yield increases over 12 months before total returns turn 
negative. That supports adding U.S. Treasuries, especially at the short end, where 
we see the greatest scope for Fed tightening expectations to unwind.

We are adding Treasuries from across the curve. For most of the last six years, we’ve been underweight Treasuries, turning 
neutral in spots. Now, yields have reset toward our long-held higher-rates view, and we want to lock in some attractive 
income. Ten-year Treasury yields sat near 0.9% back in October 2020 when we first went underweight and have since surged 
to 24-year highs above 5.3%. We are upgrading shorter-term Treasuries to overweight, where we see scope for excessive Fed 
tightening expectations to unwind, and long-term Treasuries to neutral as higher yields now provide better compensation for 
interest-rate risk. We also trim our underweight in JGBs, held since July 2023 when 10-year yields were at 0.4%-0.5%, 
compared with above 3% now. We fund the Treasury upgrades partly from U.S. agency mortgage-backed securities that have 
been a stable source of income in portfolios and we are now neutral.
We also fund the Treasury upgrades by closing our long-held U.S. equity overweight to neutral. This is a short-term risk-
management move, reflecting our belief that investors must be more dynamic in today’s volatile macro and market regime. 
We are also monitoring growing focus on AI policy and the AI buildout in jurisdictions across the country. 
Our fundamental convictions on AI transformation remain intact. Higher rates are increasingly differentiating companies by 
their ability to generate earnings and returns above the cost of capital. We still favor AI-related companies and listed 
infrastructure, where growing demand and scarcity in critical infrastructure support attractive returns. Existing infrastructure 
could grow even more valuable amid this scarcity. Despite narrative swings, we believe fundamentals will ultimately prevail. 
We also maintain our emerging market equity overweight. FactSet data shows third-quarter earnings for S&P 500 companies 
are expected to grow nearly 30% year-on-year.
Bottom line: We are managing near-term risk, not changing our core convictions. Higher yields offer an opportunity to add 
Treasuries, funded partly by taking profits on U.S. equities. We remain constructive on AI and stand ready to add risk again as 
visibility improves and attractive entry points emerge.

Market backdrop 
Global government bonds saw another volatile week, with 10- and 30-year U.S. Treasury yields hitting their highest levels 
since 2002 before retreating after strong bond auctions. European bonds also sold off, led by France amid fiscal concerns. 
Oil prices rose as Middle East tensions persisted, adding to inflation worries. We see the bond selloff as reinforcing our 
competition-for-capital thesis, while higher yields improve the case for owning Treasuries.
