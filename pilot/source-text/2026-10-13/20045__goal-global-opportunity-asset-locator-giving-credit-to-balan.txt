8.  MSCI AC World 12-month forward P/E ratios have fallen nearly 20% year to date – 
an unusually large late-cycle de-rating (Exhibit 15). Typically, the strongest earnings 
growth occurs in the recovery from a profit recession as markets enter a mid cycle 
backdrop. This time, however, the AI capex boom has accelerated earnings growth from 
a later cycle starting point, marked by already low unemployment, positive output gaps 
and elevated profit margins. The earnings boom has been closely linked to the 
releveraging around the AI capex – private sector leverage was lower than previous 
late-cycle episodes. Stronger earnings have boosted prospective equity carry and 
helped cushion global equities against higher yields. More rate-sensitive areas without 
the same earnings support have come under greater pressure: infrastructure, real estate 
and bond-proxy sectors have suffered outright drawdowns since the summer (Exhibit 
16). 
 
The largest valuation de-ratings have been driven by strong earnings in technology, 
particularly hardware, although some segments have also re-rated (Exhibit 17). As 
our US strategy team has highlighted, concerns about durability of the earnings for AI 
infrastructure stocks have weighed on valuations – at current valuations earnings would 
have to decline materially to return to long-run average levels. Hyperscalers also trade at 
relatively low earnings multiples owing to concerns on long-term profitability. However, 
following Meta’s launch of Muse and the growing focus on AI applications and adoption, 
valuations have expanded for companies benefiting from AI-related revenues or 
productivity gains.

10.  Selective option hedges continue to look attractive, with anchored implied vols 
across most assets. Overall, cross-asset implied volatility remains low, with the VIX at 
15 and FX volatility similarly anchored. Credit implied volatility has picked up but 
remains low relative to history. Within equities, unusually high dispersion has kept 
index-level volatility contained, with implied correlations close to historical lows across 
all regions. This reflects not only low correlations among stocks within the same sectors, 
but also very low correlations across sectors in the index (Exhibit 19). As a result, equity 
upside hedges into year-end are inexpensive, particularly in rate-sensitive areas of the 
market. By contrast, rates volatility has risen sharply alongside the bond sell-off over the 
summer, pushing the MOVE-to-VIX ratio to its highest level since 2024 (Exhibit 20). As 
discussed above, further sharp increases in rates are likely to weigh more on equities; 
tactically, we would sell payers on back-end rates to fund equity puts.

Buying Options: Investors who buy call (put) options risk loss of the entire premium paid if the underlying security finishes below (above) the strike 
price at expiration. Investors who buy call or put spreads also risk a maximum loss of the premium paid.  The maximum gain on a long call or put spread

Selling Options: Investors who sell calls on securities they do not own risk unlimited loss of the security price less the strike price. Investors who sell 
covered calls (sell calls while owning the underlying security) risk having to deliver the underlying security or pay the difference between the security 
price and the strike price, depending on whether the option is settled by physical delivery or cash-settled. Investors who sell puts risk loss of the strike 
price less the premium received for selling the put. Investors who sell put or call spreads risk a maximum loss of the difference between the strikes less 
the premium received, while their maximum gain is the premium received.  
For options settled by physical delivery, the above risks assume the options buyer or seller, buys or sells the resulting securities at the settlement 
price on expiry.
