JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
All three major indices closed lower yesterday, but breadth was supportive (over 60% of 
S&P 500 stocks finished higher). Similarly, we see this rotation/broadening continues into 
this pre-market sessions. Declines were concentrated in AI-linked baskets yesterday, 
especially semis (SOXX -2.6%) and memory (DRAM -4.3%). Comments from JPM Tech 
specialist Josh Meyers summarized the drivers pretty well below. On macro, oil reversed 
yesterday’s gains this morning, triggering a decline in global yields, but the situation over 
the weekend showed clear escalation (Houthi attacks on Saudi airports; Ukraine said 
Russia hit energy targets despite Trump’s ceasefire announcement, per RTRS) and we have 
seen energy markets trading range-boundedly recently. 

•         MACRO UPDATES YDAY – Below came from our Afternoon Briefing yesterday: “Oil 
extended gains as escalation on two fronts added to the supply-risk premium. In Saudi 
Arabia, a deadly Houthi attack on Riyadh's airport led to further escalation in the region. In 
Ukraine, Kyiv said Russia kept hitting energy sites after the announced "energy ceasefire." 
Fresh strikes in both Saudi Arabia and Ukraine weakened the de-escalation story behind 
Trump's Russia diesel deal and added upside risk to crude.” 
•         COMMENTS ON SEMIS/HW WEAKNESS (JOSHUA MEYERS) –   
o   The most proximate reason seems to be Hock Tan's participation in a 
panel at a DBS conference in Singapore, where he warned financing could 
become a bigger bottleneck than component shortages (in addition to 
questioning frontier lab business models, and comparing "many investors" to 
casino gamblers).  
o   Additionally, a sell-side research not over the weekend re-ignited power 
concerns that have worried investors for weeks (suggesting shortages of 
powered shell of as much as 19GW next year and more in '28). These 
worries are hitting on a low-liquidity holiday day, and news out of the middle 
east isn't helping...  
o   As investors sit on their hands ahead of an expected private frontier lab 
investor meeting on Wednesday.  
o   While the SOX struggles at its 100MA, we're seeing the sort of rotation into 
Mag 7 and Internet names that we might expect if an open bond market were 
sending rates higher...  
o   Key questions this morning have centered around why optical is particularly 
weak LITE COHR CIEN, what's driving memory lower (especially SNDK), 
why SMCI is down so much *again*, and why TTMI is being so punished for 
its capex announcement. Outside of TTMI, I don't have any company-specific 
reasons for weakness. Always appreciate your feedback/pushback on any of 
these thoughts...  

o   A few folks immediately came back on memory with the CXMT 4F2 news I 
commented on this morning in Chips for Breakfast. I agree, but personally I 
also think the worries on memory are more layered than just that one bit of 
quite speculative news...  
•         FACTOR UPDATES – Manish sees some opportunity in a Quality reversal around CPI 
release: “We flagged tightening macro conditions, and the Quality (JPQPURE) factor is 
increasingly reflecting that environment. AI optimism continues to support the broad market 
index, but conditions remain punitive for credit laggards (JP9CREDL). Beta (JPBPURE) and 
Momentum (JPMPURE) look vulnerable, as do Semis (JP2SEM). Our indicator suggests 
semiconductor sentiment has reverted to its May level and is now above the 99th percentile. 
The last comparable vertical jump in the Quality (JPQPURE) factor was during the 2023 
regional bank crisis and again during the October rate backup. In both cases, Quality 
subsequently reversed following Fed intervention—liquidity support during the crisis and a 
dovish shift at the October 2023 meeting. Looking ahead, there is meaningful risk of a 
Quality reversal around the CPI release and the Fed meeting later this month if the Fed 
turns dovish.” 
  
26Q3 EARNINGS PREVIEW – We published below in the October 12 Morning Briefing 
•         EXPECTATIONS (FACTSET) – 26Q3 expectations are for 12.4% revenue growth, 
29.6% earnings growth, with 15.0% margins. For reference, 26Q2 was 15.6% revenue 
growth, 52.3% earnings growth and 17% margins. 26Q4 is expected to print 12.4% revenue 
growth and 28.0% earnings growth.  FY26 is expected to print 12.5% rev and 32.7% EPS 
growth. FY27 is expected to print 9.4% rev and 15.8% EPS growth. 
o   11 of 11 sectors are expected to see positive Revenue growth. 11 of 11 
sectors are expected to see positive Earnings growth. This would be the 
second time in history that this occurs, 21Q2 being the other time. 
•         MKT INTEL’S THINGS TO WATCH IN EARNINGS SEASON – (i) Increased Breadth 
– Bloomberg flags that 57% of the SPX is in a bear market (down 20%+ from ATHs) and 
that this weak performance is now coinciding with declining dispersion (BBG Terminal). If 

Tech falters, then we may see a correction. (ii) Whether Beats are Rewarded – In 4 of the 
last 6 reporting periods, we have seen the 1-day reaction to earnings beats come in below 
historical median levels, with misses punished to a greater magnitude relative to historical 
performance. Earnings expectations for Tech and the SPX are high but potentially less for 
the remaining sectors. (iii) Capex – the consensus on how to end the AI-induced stock 
market boom is a cut to capex from one or more hyperscalers. That feels highly unlikely to 
occur in 2026 earnings, but the market will search for clues including a reduction in 
shareholder distributions. (iv) Inflation – here were are thinking about this through the lens 
of the Consumer sector which appears to be trading as though the Consumer is in a 
recession rather than via the Fed reaction function / bond yield lens. Already, we have seen 
DAL cut its profit guidance on energy prices and an unwillingness to pass on costs to the 
consumer. How price sensitive are consumers and is that sensitivity uniform across sectors 
are questions that we seek to have answered.

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the October 12 Morning Briefing 

•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – we reiterate our view as we head into earnings. Last week, 
we had a test of the bullish hypothesis with last week’s OpenAI news where an accounting 
misinterpretation led to a 3.4% decline on Thursday. Though the story was updated across 
multiple media sources, Semis ended the week on a 3-day losing streak. Now, does the 
market regain its confidence in Semis ahead of earning, post-earnings, or not at all? The 
SOX is ~20% off its July lows and ~15% below its June ATHs, which points to an inflection 
point given recent price action. We think Semis can climb this latest wall of worry as recent 
earnings updates from Hynix / Samsung point to on-going thematic strength. Separately, 
breadth continues to deteriorate which we think can reverse into / out of earnings season 
which will buttress the rally. 
•         RISKS – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI Credit Fears / 
Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – (i) Macro Data – CPI on Oct 14, Retail Sales & PPI on Oct 15, Flash 
PMIs on Oct 23, and Fed Day on Oct 28; (ii) Lower Bond Yields – while growth continues 
to drive yields higher, dovish inflation prints could remove further hike expectations out of 
the market which is currently pricing a Dec hike and then 2x hikes in FY27; (iii) Banks 
Earnings – kicking off earnings season with stronger earnings and a favorable consumer 
and lending outlook would be helpful in restarting the Cyclicals trade and broadening the 
bull market. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with a 
preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 
Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your 
shopping list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and 
Retailers given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy 
prices. Other elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower energy 
prices. Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and Japan. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long but 
would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / yields, 
which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, there is 
likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the Tech tape. 

Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus bullish play. The 
AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we like Tech in the 
near-term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we think you need to 
see the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; excluding AI-related 
plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper yield curve, and 
favorable capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real Estate, Telecom, and 
Utilities may participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most likely to lag the SPX, 
ex-AI plays. The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which would squeeze EU and 
RTY higher; plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, Cruise Lines, 
Discretionary, Hotels, etc) is another area of interest with a preference to play via options.  
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (SEP 21) – We maintain our core view of +Tech vs. -
RTY and that Tech view is a mix of Mag7, Memory, and Semis, and we would consider 
keeping the pair $-neutral or market neutral. Early in hiking cycles, we tend to see USD bid 
higher and the yield curve flattening; together, this is supportive of a move towards Secular 
Growth / MegaCap Tech. Given the likelihood of higher yields and a stronger USD, we think 
the US can tactically outperform DM. That said, the fear / uncertainty around AI may push 
the market into non-AI plays, which puts Latam in focus as a long play; we like Brazil / 
Latam irrespective of your views on AI.
