A&D – Noah out with his preview – full note here: Our 3Q26 A&D earnings preview provides our current
thoughts on each sub-market and single stock ideas into the results period. We like the set up for aerospace
aftermarket as concerns are priced into stocks, but we expect strong results in 3Q and positive commentary
regarding 2027, while many of the companies have specific bottom up positive drivers. Aero OE has

visible growth, but it also keeps facing risk of extension in the pace. Business jet is solid, but growth is
slower than other parts of aerospace. In defense we expect debate to continue around medium-term growth
pace and margins and for the market to determine there are risks. Defense tech has exposure to growth
priorities, and some valuations have become very attractive, though it is hard to take a view on the bottom
in the current de-rate. Government IT valuations have moved lower, though we think the demand pace will
prove still slow in this results and outlook period. Into the earnings season we like HII, WWD, TDG,
ARXS, AVEX. We are cautious on LMT, BAH, MRCY.
Aerospace OE: Boeing has averaged ~41/month deliveries of 737 MAX this summer (41 in August, 39 in
July, 42 in June) with the plan to stabilize at 47/month taking longer than anticipated. 787 production has
stabilized at 8/month, but the engine delivery performance needed to go to 10/month has not been
achieved. The OEMs continue to march output higher, supply chain is improving, demand is quite strong,
but it is not easy business.
Aerospace aftermarket: We expect aftermarket to remain strong through the medium-term. Airlines are not
making major changes to the fleet despite the fuel price as they have been able to price and they are short
new aircraft. There is also substantial pent up demand in aircraft maintenance. Pricing is currently stronger
than its historical average which itself is consistently above inflation. Many of the companies in the
vertical have unique bottom up drivers. The set up for the vertical is somewhat easier into this results
period because the stocks price in more negativity than normal.
Business jet: Market fundamentals remain solid and supply-demand remains tight. The industry has
recently proven relatively insulated from broader demand headwinds, such as disruptions in the Middle
East and higher fuel prices. Business jet operations have been increasing in the summer months yoy and
deliveries have improved August YTD versus last year.
Defense hardware: Defense stocks have been confusing to investors as they de-rate while revenues grow
and the geopolitics are complex. We think defense large-cap diversified defense stocks are wrestling with
the potential for being at peak DoD budget authorization, in large part simply due to difficult baseline
compares, and their valuations are historically highly correlated to authorization because it is the most
leading indicator. Traditional defense companies are also facing more margin questions today than they
have in the past, as the Pentagon looks to better balance risk sharing, and that has made its way into
contract terms. We still see pockets of growth and margin expansion within that backdrop, like Navy.
Chemicals – Duffy out with adjustments – full note here: 3Q26, in general, looks to be shaping up lower
across the board versus consensus expectations coming into the quarter with more risk of missing
estimates than beating. We are updating our estimates to reflect mark-to-market commodity pricing and
trends across the quarter driving fundamentals. Notables include:
1) Middle East conflict impact - In general, the impact of the Middle East conflict was not as bullish for
basic chemicals as originally believed, despite its persistence. While significant Middle East supply for
many chemical chains (e.g., PE, methanol, nitrogen, etc.) was/is curtailed and the lack of Middle East
feedstock forced some chemical producers into force majeure, alternate supply sources (e.g., China) and
demand destruction have seemingly tempered deficits created by the Middle East conflict.

2) Polyethylene (PE) pricing - With consensus expectations of a roll, July PE pricing settled down 10 c/lb,
following a 15 c/lb reduction in June. August PE pricing settled flat, but CMA implemented a net market
adjustment (down 5 c/lb) in net transaction prices for all PE grades to reflect industry discounts since the
last adjustment in January 2026, as shown in Exhibit 1. September PE pricing has not settled as of October
9, 2026, but market participants are optimistic about a 3-5 c/lb increase (see Exhibit 2).
3) Chlorovinyl pricing - After increasing 18% sequentially in 2Q, PVC prices decreased ~2% sequentially
during 3Q. We expect a decline of 2-4% during 4Q on seasonality with a 3-5% rebound in 2027.
4) Methanol/MTBE trends - During 3Q26, US contract pricing for methanol followed a downward
trajectory as the contract net transaction price remained flat in July and August before dropping in
September due to flat seasonal demand and ample regional supply. Concurrently, the US MTBE market
experienced stable domestic production and healthy export volumes earlier in the quarter, with Mexico
remaining the primary consumer of US product. Exiting the quarter in September, regional MTBE supply
tightened due to a major plant outage on the US Gulf Coast, though blending demand remained supported
by rising gasoline prices heading into October.
5) Unplanned outages - During 3Q26, unplanned production outages among US chemical producers were
largely contained to sporadic mechanical bottlenecks and brief weather curtailments along the Gulf Coast,
intermittently disrupting ethylene and propylene crackers as well as chlor-alkali and polyvinyl chloride
(PVC) units operated by majors such as DOW, LYB, and WLK. While these localized disruptions may
have temporarily tightened spot availability across intermediate and polymer lines—including vinyl
chloride monomer (VCM), polyethylene, and styrene derivatives—overall production remained
predominantly governed by disciplined economic run-rate cuts amid persistent margin compression rather
than widespread physical downtime.
Housing – Sue out with Q3 preview – full note here: Operating Conditions Point to Sequential Moderation
in 3Q Results: In contrast to a better than expected 2Q—led by share gains, product innovation, cost
actions, and tariff refunds—we look for more muted outcomes this quarter. Our view reflects a moderation
in demand across much of our coverage as the 30-year fixed mortgage rate is up 74bps since August,
hitting the low-to-mid-7% range, in addition to weak consumer confidence. Rising inflation, especially in
energy, transportation, and key commodities, is also leading to greater price/cost pressures, putting future
margins at risk. Given the set-up and limited visibility, we believe management teams will likely maintain
a cautious tone. We look for a continued focus on company-specific initiatives and capital allocation to
drive industry relative outperformance while maintaining profitability and supporting future EPS.
Although we see more limited upside to results this quarter, we expect 4Q/2026 guides will likely be
modestly narrowed at or slightly below the lower-end of the previous range as much of the current
headwinds were already incorporated.
Seasonality Suggests Upside to Stocks into Year-End While We Highlight Non-Res & Idiosyncratic
Stories for 3Q: In our view, this outlook is largely priced in with 52% of our coverage trading within 5%
of its 52-week lows. The setup suggests more limited upside to EPS across our coverage, though we
believe based on conversations buyside expectations have likely embedded the impact of higher rates and

macro headwinds. Should any of these factors turn positive in the coming weeks or months, pushing rates
and commodity prices incrementally lower, we believe the next move for the group could be higher,
exacerbated by negative sentiment and positioning. We also note seasonality implies upside into year-end.
In the near-term, we highlight the following Buy-rated building product names with idiosyncratic stories
that we believe can drive peer-relative outperformance for 3Q: AWI, CSL (on-CL), FBIN, and IBP.
Downwardly Revising 2026 Housing Starts Forecast while Inflation Drives our R&R Outlook Higher: In
line with the macro backdrop, we now expect 2026 single-family starts to decline 4.6% YOY to 900k from
920k, which implies an ~875k run rate in the last four months of the year. We are also reducing our
estimate for multi-family starts to 420k from 432k, implying 1% growth YOY, bringing total starts to
1.319mn, a decrease of 2.9%. In contrast, we are raising our 2026 and 2027 R&R forecast to +6.7% and
+2.0%, from +3.1% and +0.9%, respectively. The upward revisions are led by recently announced pricing
as companies look to offset rising inflation. More specifically, we look for price to be up 5.9% in 2026
while volumes rise 0.8%.
