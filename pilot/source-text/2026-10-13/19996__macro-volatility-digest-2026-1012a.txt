WEEKLY MARKET COMMENTARY 
Volatilities Fall on Oil/Yield Reprieve as the S&P Rallies Into Earnings Season 
 
Cross-Asset Volatility: Implied volatilities among the 
major asset classes declined across the board as the 
primary drivers of cross-asset volatility contagion, rates 
and oil, both eased.  Interest-rate volatilities declined with 
the MOVE Index retreating -9pts from its 2nd highest level 
YTD to 98 (94th percentile high) as the 10-year yield 
retraced from Monday’s 24-year peak of 5.35% following 
the strong 10-year auction with a bid-to-cover of 2.77, its 
highest level since 2016.  (Note that of the 4 tenors that 
comprise the MOVE Index, the 10-year tenor has the 
highest weighting at 40% and thus the strength of last 
week’s auction on the 10-year note had a heavier effect 
towards stabilizing rates vol as measured by the MOVE 
Index.) 
Oil volatilities also notched lower with the OVX falling from 
the 50th to 43rd percentile.  Note, however, that most of the 
pullback in front-month vols had already been priced into 
the market before Trump’s announcement in ruling out a 
strike on Iran before the midterm elections.  We believe 
the minimal reaction from the volatility markets and the 
stickiness of the longer-dated portion of the oil volatility 
term structure to the declaration is due to the fact that the 
pledge simply defers the supply disruption risk rather than 
removing it.  Dec year-end oil skew became increasingly 
convex w/w as ATM vols dropped while DOTM puts and 
calls remained pinned to week-ago levels. 
Equity Volatility/ Skew: The VIX® Index declined 0.5 pts 
w/w to 14.8 but underperformed skew by another 0.5 pts due 
to opportunistic positioning for downside convexity.  (Per the 
pre-established skew, volatility traders had expected last 
week’s S&P-500 Index® advance of +1.2% to trigger a 1pt 
VIX Index decline to 14.3). The marginal demand for 
downside protection steepened 1M S&P-500 Index 30-delta 
skew slightly from the 9th to 15th percentile.    
 
Volatility of Volatility: Vol of vol has edged lower over the 
last week with the VVIX falling from 87 to 84 (4th percentile). 
 
Term Structure: With 3 weeks to go before midterm 
elections,  expectations via the volatility term structure remain 
muted with forward vols currently implying a one-day post-
elections move of 1.2, roughly 1.3x a typical day in the current 
vol regime. 
 
Dispersion/ Correlation: Dispersion trended higher w/w 
and 1M implied correlation (COR1M) declined to 6.93% (10th 
percentile lows) on OpenAI’s revenue scare.  Although the 
revenue gap vs expectations stemmed from an accounting 
definition rather than lost sales, the outsized reaction and 
uneven recovery among chip and semi names show 
increased stock differentiation heading into Q3 earnings.

How to read this chart:
The chart shows how various asset class implied 
volatilities are trading relative to their own history, using 
a 10-year lookback. 
The volatilities are ranked by z-scores, which compares 
the current volatility level for an asset versus its own 10-
year history. A z-score of +2, for example, would mean 
that the current volatility level is trading 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average (i.e. rich) while a 
z-score of -2 would mean it’s trading 2 standard 
deviations below average (i.e. cheap)
Because each asset class volatility trades at different 
levels and often defined differently (e.g. lognormal vs. 
normal vol, price vs. bps vol), by standardizing using z-
scores, we’re able to compare different asset class 
volatilities on the same scale - and see at a glance if 
there are any divergences or dislocations in the cross-
asset volatility markets
