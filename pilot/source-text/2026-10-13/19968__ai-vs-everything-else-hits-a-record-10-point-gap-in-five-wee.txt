 
AI Vs Everything Else Hits A Record 10-Point Gap In 
Five Weeks; Goldman Says Start Buying The Losers 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SUNDAY, OCT 11, 2026 - 05:30 PM 
It's not the level, it's the speed. 
That was the message Goldman's Louis Miller brought back after a week on the road with US 
Thematics' Faris Mourad, meeting macro clients who are rattled less by where yields are than 
by how fast they got there. The ~68bp rise in rates since the end of August is "concerning", and 
"had this rally taken six months instead, it would have been perceived differently." Making 
matters worse, oil and rates "were moving in line with one another but stopped and this has 
been unsettling for many" - which is a polite way of saying the macro playbook broke at the 
worst possible moment, just as ZH flagged in "The Crazy Continues: Stocks Up, Breadth Down; 
Yields Up, Oil Down" (Oct 5). 
Add a Hormuz de-escalation scenario that is, in Miller's words, "impossible to predict", and a 
midterm election that clients now view as "a clearing event for AI Power", and you get a market 
that has split in two. 
"The equity market excluding AI is indeed representing these concerns and this chart represents 
this dynamic best: the AI vs ex-AI spread widened to a 10pp record in 5 weeks until this week. 
We like adding some non-AI exposure here despite the lack of clarity on geopolitics" (emphasis 
ours) 

 
 
Translation: everything that isn't a GPU, a data center or a power plant has already eaten the 
rates shock - and Goldman thinks the gap is now stretched enough to start buying the losers. 
 
Below we lay out why the speed of the move matters, Goldman's three favorite ways to play the 
catch-up, and why we're (cautiously) on the side of the losers. 

 
Crowded Out... Or Just Oversold? 
Regular readers will recall that just yesterday we laid out Goldman's Rich Privorotsky's warning 
that AI's bottomless capital hunger plus giant sovereign borrowing is crowding everyone else out 
- pushing up real yields, cracking CCC spreads and pinning cyclicals near their lows. 
Wednesday's futures slide as oil jumped on Hormuz tanker attacks and the 30Y hit a 2002 high 
(Oct 7) was the textbook case, and as we noted in "Not Spectacular Enough" (Oct 7), the split is 
visible in credit too, where IG AI trades a record 50bps wide of IG ex-AI. 
Now a different Goldman desk is making the other half of the argument: the crowding-out has 
happened so fast that it's now in the price. The chart above says it all: since the start of the year 
the S&P 500 is up 14.1% while Goldman's S&P ex-AI index (SPXXAI) is up just 7.2%, having 
peaked at 112.42 in August and slid into the end of September, before bouncing 2.3% off the 
low. 
And it's not like the AI side is short of support. Goldman's Jack Singer notes that consensus 
sees Q3 S&P EPS growth of 27% y/y, with AI infra spending accounting for 50% of that growth. 
In other words, half the market's earnings growth is coming from one theme - which is precisely 
why the other half looks cheap. 
Over the trailing 30 days, the only S&P sectors in the green are TMT and Energy - i.e. the two 
"AI Constraints." Everything else got hit: Healthcare -3.5%, Consumer Discretionary -3.6%, 
Utilities -4.6%, Industrials -5.8%, Financials -7.7%, Materials -7.9% and REITs -9.8%. And…

Implementation #1: Cheap Upside On The S&P Ex-AI 
The simplest way to play it is the index itself. SPXXAI Nov 103% calls cost just 0.75% (~13 vol, 
25 delta), per Miller. 
For those who don't speak options: that's a cheap, defined-risk bet that the non-AI market rallies 
3% into November - a move that would merely claw back about half of the gap that opened up 
in five weeks. 
The catalyst? Goldman's IBD team notes that markets have historically sold off into midterms 
(median -3.5% in the month before since 1974) and rallied after (median +6.5% in the four 
months following). If the midterms really are the "clearing event", November calls are not a bad 
place to sit. 

 
Implementation #2: People Still Want To Go To Concerts 
Consumer Discretionary may be the weakest sector of 2026, but Goldman sees a "differentiated 
opportunity" in its Consumer Experiences basket (GSXUCEXP). Spending growth on 
experiences accelerated from 1% in Q1 2025 to 6% in Q1 2026, versus just 2% for broader 
services, while demand commentary across live entertainment, travel, hotels, cruises, theme 
parks and sports "remains constructive". The basket details are available to pro subs. 
"The basket has underperformed the AI benchmark by 14%, but relative valuations are low, 
earnings have continued to improve, ownership remains below prior peaks, and revisions could 
inflect if upcoming earnings confirm resilient demand. Its concentration in higher-income 
consumer cohorts also offers relative insulation from broader affordability pressure and fuel-cost 
volatility" (emphasis ours) 
Even against the ex-AI index the slide was brutal: Consumer Experiences fell ~16pp relative to 
SPXXAI from its June 29 high, crashing from the top of its five-year trend channel to the bottom 
in three months. 
 
The K-shaped economy, in other words, has a trade attached: the people buying cruise suites 
don't much care what gas costs.

Goldman also likes health care themes, which "continue to be the least correlated group to AI, 
oil, and rates - the correlation is closer to zero rather than negative, while showing strong 
positive earnings revisions, especially within pockets of commercial biotech (GSHLCOMB)." 
The numbers: Commercial Biotech is projecting 111% next-12-month EPS growth and is up 
25% YTD, yet its daily returns correlate just 0.21 with AI, -0.18 with oil and -0.10 with the 10Y. 
Neurology (47% EPS growth), Autoimmune/Inflammation (27%, +25% YTD), Bioprocessing and 
Diversified Health Care tell the same story.

Side Notes: Power, Batteries, Brazil... And Selling Chaos Paid 
Goldman hasn't given up on AI entirely - it just prefers the bottleneck. Power Up America 
(GSENEPOW) is its preferred AI-related expression, with US data-center power demand seen 
reaching 108GW by 2030 (a 23% CAGR). The theme "has bottomed and is trending upwards as 
expected", and the next catalyst is the October 12 FERC decision on PJM's large-load 
framework. Favored trade: sell the Jan 85% put to fund the Jan 107.5/122.5% call spread, net 
2.3% (details available to pro subs [MARKETDESK LINK]). 
 

 
In Asia, Goldman's Battery Backup Units basket (GSXABBUS) targets a global BBU market 
seen growing from $2.2BN in 2025 to $50.2BN by 2030 - a 23x increase and an 87% CAGR, 
with Panasonic claiming 70-80% global share (more for pro subs). And in Brazil, the first-round 
election result has the rate-sensitive basket (GSBZRATE) "quickly pricing in a lower rates 
environment" - EWZ printed over $8BN of volume on Monday, out-trading IWM and TLT (pro 
subs). 
 
Finally, a bonus that "shouldn't come as a surprise": in Goldman's systematic universe, the 
winners of 2026 were the strategies selling chaos. US Equity Daily Skew harvesting 
(GSVIDSK1) is up 8.35% YTD with a 4.34 Sharpe, and Copper Volatility Carry (GSVLLP20) is 
up 30.85%. A year of record macro rotations turned out to be a gift for those paid to sell 
insurance against them. 
Bottom Line 
A record gap opened in five weeks is the sort of thing that usually mean-reverts, and Miller is 
right that a lot of bad news is in the price of the ex-AI market. If you want to play it, his first 
suggestion is the best one: a 0.75% call option has a known cost, which is more than can be 
said for owning rate-sensitive cyclicals outright while the long bond is still looking for a buyer. 

 
 
But a gap can close from either side. The clients' own diagnosis is that the ex-AI market is being 
crushed by the speed of the rate rise, and nothing in the note suggests rates are done. The AI 
side, meanwhile, has been immune to a two-sigma bond selloff because its returns are 
assumed to dwarf its cost of capital, an assumption that gets tested every time someone reports 
a revenue number, as Thursday's OpenAI scare showed. In 2000, the last time leadership was 
this narrow, the gap closed mostly because the leaders fell, not because the laggards rallied. 
And if it ends the same way this time, the consumer buying the experiences goes with it. 
So yes, the gap closes. We would just rather own the option than have an opinion on which line 
does the moving.
