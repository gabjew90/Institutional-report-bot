The AI boom is starting to crack in
unexpected places
Korea, one of the biggest winners of the AI mania, is suddenly getting crushed.
Memory stocks are breaking down, volatility is waking up, and a major source of buying support is
disappearing.
Yet Taiwan and US semis continue to hold up remarkably well.
The divergence is becoming extreme.
Is Korea simply pricing in peak memory earnings, or is it sending an early warning to the rest of the
global AI trade?
Ugly in Korea
KOSPI down 3–4%, Samsung and Hynix off around 4%, while Taiwan is broadly flat.
As Privorotsky points out, memory remains the epicenter of the weakness. CXMT unveiled a new 4F²
DRAM architecture over the weekend, another reminder that China, already accounting for roughly
10% of global DRAM revenues, is aggressively expanding capacity.
The irony is that near-term fundamentals remain extraordinary. But markets don't trade yesterday's
earnings. They trade tomorrow's revisions, and investors are increasingly questioning whether peak
memory profitability is approaching. Samsung's results failed to deliver the massive upside surprise
investors had positioned for, adding fuel to the unwind.
KOSPI cracks
KOSPI broke below the bigger trend line late last week. The overnight puke has now taken the index all
the way down to its 200-day moving average, a level tested just once since the entire KOSPI mania
began. A decisive close below it, and things risk getting even messier.

Vol in KOSPI
The KOSPI selloff has become so aggressive that even volatility is waking up. KOSPI VIX has been in
massive vol-reset mode since the market topped out, making the overnight spike particularly
noteworthy. For much of the correction, volatility barely reacted. That is now changing, suggesting the
market is finally starting to price in some genuine downside risk.

No help
Buybacks in KOSPI are going into hibernation. This has been an important source of support
throughout the rally, and that bid is now fading just as technical damage is accelerating. Not exactly
ideal timing.
ZH
Breaking down
SK Hynix, the poster child of the Asian AI mania, has broken below a dynamic triangle-like formation.
The stock is now trading below its 100-day moving average, although still comfortably above the 200-
day. With the massive buyback support we have seen fading, this looks like dead money at best in our
view.

Samsung
The other Korean tech giant, Samsung, has also lost its mojo. The stock is showing a similar pattern to
SK Hynix, although with less downside momentum. We have just reversed at the 100-day moving
average and are now threatening to break below the triangle formation. The 200-day moving average
remains much lower, leaving plenty of room for further downside.

That SOX connection
The KOSPI vs. SOX divergence continues to widen. Korea is increasingly pricing in peak memory
earnings, while US semis continue to trade the structural AI growth story. These two narratives can
coexist for a while, but we doubt such an extreme divergence can persist indefinitely. After all, AI is a
global ecosystem, and cracks in one corner have a habit of spreading.

Same AI boom. Two very different stories
The gap between KOSPI and Taiwan has become extreme. Korea is essentially a leveraged bet on the
memory cycle, while Taiwan is dominated by TSMC and the advanced semiconductor manufacturing
ecosystem.
The market is increasingly questioning the sustainability of memory profits, while continuing to reward
the structural AI infrastructure story.
The bigger question is whether Korea is simply experiencing a memory-specific earnings
reset, or whether it is the first market to recognize that the economics of the AI boom are
becoming more complicated.
Two markets. One AI boom. Very different messages.
