It's a big day for the Deutsche Bank Research Institute as we hold an in-person event today in our London 
office at noon on what superintelligence will look like and when will we get there? We have a special guest in 
renowned AI author and commentator Azeem Azhar, founder of Exponential View. This is based around a 
major new report that Adrian Cox has just published with Azeem on Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). You 
can find it here at the Deutsche Bank Research Institute. We are fully subscribed for today’s event but if you 
would like to come, please reach out and we’ll try to find a way to squeeze you in.  
Superintelligence may yet wipe out the Reid family as in celebrating my wife's birthday yesterday I discovered 
via the card he delivered that one of my 9-year-old twins spells mummy with one "m". Meanwhile, despite 
being on the planet for 52 years, I still can't wrap presents. Every present I give looks as if I don't care at all 
about the recipient because of the chaotic state of the wrapping. There is nothing I can do about it. It might 
explain why it took until my late 30s to get married though.  
After a quiet start to the week with US bond markets closed yesterday, this morning we've seen a major 
Asian tech sell-off and later today we'll see the start of the US Q3 earnings season barrage. JPMorgan, 
Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo and Citigroup all report today, followed by Bank of America, Morgan 
Stanley and BlackRock tomorrow. Financials are actually expected to be one of the sectors with the 
weakest YoY growth so be aware of that as you digest today's results. Overall, our US equity strategists 
expect S&P 500 earnings growth to remain at a record 34% YoY in Q3, versus consensus at 27%. Their 
view is that consensus is again embedding too much of a slowdown, leaving scope for another quarter 
of above-average beats. See their piece here for more. In Europe, the season really kicks off next week 
and our equity strategists expect earnings growth to reach 25% in Q3, 5 pp above consensus (their full 
preview is here). So it should be a very strong earnings season on both sides of the Atlantic and indeed 
across the globe.  
Ahead of the onslaught there has been a big sell-off in tech overnight, seemingly on concerns over 
Chinese AI competition. The sell-off, which began this time yesterday has continued to weigh on the 

KOSPI (-6.30%), leaving the tech-heavy index with its largest two-day drop (-9.79%) since July, back 
when a reassessment of the AI trade was the key theme in equities. The Nikkei 225 has also come back 
from a long weekend in Japan with a -2.21% loss.  
The catalyst for the latest AI-driven sell-off came after the South China Morning Post reported yesterday 
morning that China’s CXMT memory chipmaker had made good progress on new DRAM architecture 
that would boost memory density. So that led to a fall in several memory makers, including the likes of 
Micron (-3.88%) yesterday, and South Korea’s Samsung (-7.98%) and SK Hynix (-8.72%) this morning. In 
fact, the Philly semiconductor index fell for a 4th consecutive session yesterday, which is the first time 
that’s happened since July. Nasdaq (-0.79%) futures are lower this morning with both the S&P and Stoxx 
equivalents down a more measured -0.19%. 10yr US yields are down -4.6bps after being closed 
yesterday. 
Turning to the rest of yesterday’s developments now, it was a relatively different picture, with most 
equities advancing despite a rise in oil and gas prices. That came as markets digested the weekend 
news that the Houthi militants had bombed Riyadh’s airport, along with lingering uncertainties around 
some kind of energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine. Yet despite all that, the S&P 500 (-0.06%) 
ended the session only a little below flat, whilst the STOXX 600 (+0.65%) saw bigger gains. US equity 
gains were ultimately reversed by the continued selloff in chip stocks, with the Philly semiconductor index 
down -2.67%. That said, two-thirds of the index were higher with the Mag-7 closing up +0.47%. 
In terms of that rise in oil prices, Brent crude (+1.84%) closed up to $106/bbl, whilst European natural gas 
futures (+3.58%) hit a post-2022 high of €84.24/MWh. The risk-off in Asia has helped Brent fall back -
1.12% this morning. 
While US bond markets were closed for a holiday, the latest rise in energy prices led to a repricing across 
European rates yesterday. Indeed, higher inflation expectations were clear, as the Euro 1yr inflation 
swaps rose +4.7bps to 3.52%, which pushed front-end yields higher across Europe, as it revived fears 
about more persistent inflation over the months ahead. Indeed, the sense was that things were still 
deteriorating, particularly with gas prices hitting a new high. All that came as doubt was cast on the energy 
ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine, with Ukrainian President Zelenskyy saying that Ukraine would 
respond if Russia didn’t agree to the ceasefire. So that added to energy supply concerns, particularly with 
investors already nervous about a wider Middle East escalation after the attacks on Saudi Arabia. 
So that meant front-end yields moved up across Europe, with the Franco-German 10yr spread (+5.1bps) 
closing at a post-2012 high of 142bps, albeit with less fanfare and market stress than when 2012 levels 
were previously breached 10 days ago. The 2yr yield moved higher in Germany (+2.4bps), France 
(+1.5bps) and Italy (+2.3bps). So it was interesting that French bonds were not the worst performing at the 
front end, perhaps suggesting that much of the sizeable summer front end carry positions had now been 
squared.  
Before the late US equity dip and the overnight Asia sell-off, all the major European indices ended higher on 
the day even with gas prices notably higher. That included the Stoxx 600 (+0.65%) and the DAX (+0.76%), 
whilst France’s CAC 40 (+0.38%) still saw a rise, even though it underperformed. 

To the day ahead now, and as discussed near the top, earnings releases include JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, 
Goldman Sachs and Citigroup. Data releases from the US include the NFIB’s small business optimism index 
for September, along with existing home sales. Finally, the Fed’s Waller, Barkin and Collins will speak today.
