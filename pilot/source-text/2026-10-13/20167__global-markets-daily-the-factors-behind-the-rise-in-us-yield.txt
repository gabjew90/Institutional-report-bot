The Factors Behind the Rise in US Yields 
 
 
10y Treasury yields have risen by over 100bp since the start of the year and 130bp since 
the year-to-date lows set on the eve of the US-Iran conflict. Front-end and nearer-term 
forwards have driven a larger portion of the point-to-point move since the end of 
February, but forwards further out the curve were responsible for the most recent phase 
of the selloff (Exhibit 1). Despite the ongoing focus on inflation risks, breakevens have 
played a limited role through the broader reset, with higher longer-term yields 
disproportionately driven by the real component. 
From a global standpoint, the selloff since late August has come in two phases. The first, 
which spanned Jackson Hole and the September central bank decisions, featured 
bearish impulses from all major markets. The second, which covers the last three weeks, 
has been almost exclusively US-driven within core rates markets, with Germany in 
particular turning into an exporter of bullish shocks onto global duration amid greater 
stress in sovereign spreads (Exhibit 2). 
 
Higher policy rate expectations since late February account for ~70% of the increase in 
longer-term yields 
5y5y UST yields are 5.5-5.6% currently, which is 40-to-50bp above our G10 framework’s 
estimate of fair value, close to one standard deviation cheap (Exhibit 3). UST forwards 
have spent much of the year relatively aligned with levels implied by our model. It was 
only the break into the mid-5% range that opened up a more pronounced valuation gap, 
although it has not been unusual for US forwards to trade near the upper end of the +/-1 
standard deviation range in recent years. 
Our estimates attribute most of the year-to-date increase in longer-term yields to the 
increase in policy rate expectations (Exhibit 4). Since the start of the year, consensus

Consensus expectations for hyperscaler capex (and in turn borrowing needs) have 
moved steadily higher. We think it is reasonable to put weight on AI-related investment 
spending as a factor supporting longer-term growth optimism and cyclical resilience—
elements that have supported a higher fair value for longer-term yields directly (via 
long-term growth expectations) and indirectly (by contributing to higher policy rate 
expectations). 
We have shown that higher investment relative to savings tends to boost longer-term 
yields’ sensitivity to debt supply and that particularly elevated IG supply has a local 
impact on longer-term yields on the day of pricing. Over longer windows, however, the 
impact of corporate issuance on UST yields is significantly weaker, challenging the 
argument that that absorption of greater debt supply has been a significant source of 
accumulated upward pressure on yields. Further, aggregate measures of longer-term US 
debt outstanding relative to GDP have been stable this year.  
Overall, about 10-to-15bp of the year-to-date yield move is unexplained by 
fundamental factors. Repeating this exercise on 10y yields, the policy path contribution 
is larger (worth 80-85bp), and the contribution from other factors smaller, but the 
residual is comparable to the 10-15bp we find for 5y5y rates.

We see a range of reasons why yields are higher and have risen by slightly more than 
fundamental drivers imply. At the very front end, the market is pricing a significantly 
more hawkish Fed path than survey-based policy expectations. We recently noted that 
two factors—higher vol and the shift in hedge value, which we proxy with the correlation 
of front-end rates and equity prices—can explain about 25-to-30bp of the 40-to-50bp 
market-vs-survey gap at the front end (Exhibit 5). Uncertain macro environments and 
diminished portfolio value are both factors that should boost bond risk premium as well. 
These dynamics (higher vol, diminished diversification value) have been evident for 
longer maturities as well amid continued supply-driven swings in energy prices (Exhibit 6 
and Exhibit 7). 
 
In addition to these macro drivers, investor behavior and technical factors have likely 
supported, and amplified, yields’ rise. Our estimate of bond funds’ duration position 
relative to the Bloomberg Agg has shown a steady and significant shift underweight 
throughout the selloff (Exhibit 8), reaching levels that have historically seen a skew 
towards lower yields over the subsequent 3-to-6 months. Asset managers’ aversion to 
duration risk likely reflects aspects of the fundamental considerations pushing yields 
up—namely, limited growth risks and the elevated correlation of yields with energy 
prices.

Higher yields have also brought negative convexity in MBS and Treasury futures to the 
fore. Decreased mortgage prepayment risk and changes to long-end futures’ 
cheapest-to-deliver (CTD) bond have boosted duration exposure for long investors since 
the start of September. To the extent that those negative convexity risks are actively 
hedged, investors would have had to sell duration as yields moved up to maintain a 
steady duration position. 
Our estimate of the share of MBS holders that actively hedge would imply roughly $60bn 
in 10y equivalent paying needs through the selloff to maintain steady duration exposure 
since the start of September (though this is likely on the high side of realized flows). Over 
the same window, asset managers (who are long UST futures) would have had to sell 
$10-to-15bn 10y equivalents to offset the duration extension of their portfolio from 
CTD changes (in reality they sold significantly more, though there may be some overlap 
with mortgage hedging). Even for unhedged holders, the passive accumulation of 
duration risk as yields rise might weigh on appetite to actively “buy the dip.” Looking 
ahead, however, MBS index-level convexity is about zero (Exhibit 9), and CTD-switch risk 
is no longer clearly asymmetric to higher yields.

Despite the absence of duration risk appetite, measures of market stress have looked 
fairly orderly, and there has been little evidence of concentrated UST selling by global 
investors. The observation that dollar strength has corresponded to some foreign official 
sales is consistent with historical patterns. Swap spreads at the front-end of the curve 
have tightened since late August, but the move has been modest and the broader 
behavior of USTs versus alternative forms of near-risk free duration suggests little 
evidence of a shift in the UST supply/demand balance during the selloff. 
Time to establish a foothold 
There are upside risks to our 4.75% year-end forecast for 10y UST yields if energy risks 
remain unresolved and growth risks stay low, but a range of considerations have put US 
duration on better footing and make longs more attractive. Longer-term yields are 
elevated versus macro drivers. There are reasons for this, as we have outlined, and 
valuation-based arguments for lower yields have been challenged of late. It is not that 
common, however, for duration to be cheap and estimates of real money duration 
exposure to be firmly underweight (Exhibit 10). Negative convexity dynamics should also 
be a much smaller factor in further selloffs after playing an amplifying role recently. 
Energy remains an important source of day-to-day rates vol, but recent credit spread 
widening has introduced another correction mechanism, or at least a brake, for higher 
yields. And while our economists expect the US economy to grow close to their estimate 
of potential next year, maintaining current yield levels would boost the drag on growth 
from financial conditions from 0.2pp to more than 0.5pp. 
Under our fundamental view that underlying inflation pressures are contained amid 
limited evidence of overheating, front-end pricing is too hawkish and there is too much 
risk premium through the US curve. A credible reduction in energy risks, weaker growth 
news, or diminished confidence in the AI trade would likely take yields sharply lower. 
Absent those it may take sufficient accumulation of benign underlying inflation news to 
build the case for longs, which could take a few months, but we think there is value to 
taking a more bullish stance by fading the more pronounced pockets of recent 
cheapening (for example receiving 10s on the 5s10s30s fly). Elevated rate vol also makes
