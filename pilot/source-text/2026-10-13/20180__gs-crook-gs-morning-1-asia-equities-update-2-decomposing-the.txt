1) EQUITY TRADING (PRIVOROTSKY) – Semiconductor Indigestion – LINK
Two points from Rich Privorotsky (Head of EMEA Flow Intermediation and Head of European One Delta
Trading) from his morning note, on the semiconductor selloff overnight.
Asia: Fiercely rotational market overnight. KOSPI -5-6%, Nikkei -1.5-2%, Taiwan -2%, with the CXMT
news reverberating through memory (Musk tweet didn't help). Samsung/Hynix down 7–10%, while
Kioxia, Advantest, Tokyo Electron and SoftBank are all under pressure. But the moves beneath the surface
are fascinating. In Japan, five stocks effectively account for the entire Nikkei decline....TOPIX is actually
trading up slightly. Not indiscriminate selling… but more unwind of crowded positions.
AI: Four stories to unpack…
1/ CXMT is a legitimate lt concern for memory economics.
2/ Microsoft’s Maia 300 custom chips, moving to TSMC’s 2nm process. Incrementally negative for
merchant GPU suppliers (NVDA), positive for the custom silicon supply chain and aggregate compute
consumption.
3/ Hock Tan’s comments comparing AI investors to gamblers have been taken wildly out of context. Both
Broadcom and Intel reaffirmed exceptionally strong demand for another 2–3 years, potentially through
2030. Tan sees compute demand exceeding supply and explicitly acknowledged that frontier labs
generating substantial revenue can afford expensive financing. LT ROI is a legitimate question, but hardly
a revelation.
4/ Meta’s Muse Wonder and Wonder Full look genuinely competitive at the frontier, potentially adding
another serious player alongside OpenAI, Anthropic and Google. Some questions around benchmarking,
but impressive nonetheless. Prediction markets also anticipating another major Google release imminently.
In Summary: Competition fuels competition, and competition requires more compute. The frontier keeps
advancing, driven by Western closed source labs trying to outdo one another. Hardly bearish for aggregate
compute demand, even if the economics shift around the supply chain. Think the legitimate concerns are
around the ability to complete capacity/deliver power relative to expectations and that is fueling further
de-rating in the capex vertical.

2) INTEREST RATE RESEARCH (MARSHALL) – The Factors Behind the Rise in US Yields – LINK
BOTTOM LINE: US Treasury yields have risen across the curve, with front-end pricing hawkish relative
to our economists' Fed baseline and longer-term forwards 40-to-50bp above fair value. The reassessment
of the expected policy path from cuts to hikes, amid increased inflation risks and diminished labor market
concerns since the eve of the US-Iran conflict, can explain the bulk of the move higher in longer-term
UST yields. A more optimistic longer-run growth outlook and bearish spillovers from abroad have also
contributed, but 10-to-15bp of this year's longer-term yield move is unexplained by our macro framework.
AI investment spending is likely also contributing via its impact on cyclical and longer-term growth

optimism, though we find little evidence that absorption of related debt supply is having an outsized or
durable impact. We estimate bond funds have become underweight duration. A range of considerations –
yields elevated vs fundamentals, underweight positioning, and diminished technical risks - argue for a
more bullish stance on US duration.
Decomposing the Move: 5y5y UST yields at 5.5-5.6% are close to one standard deviation cheap vs our
G10 framework's fair value. Consensus 1y-ahead short-rate forecasts have risen ~125bp YTD, worth about
55bp to longer-term forwards (~70% of the cumulative move). Higher longer-term growth expectations
and spillovers from abroad have added another 10-to-15bp combined. Higher vol and rates' diminished
hedge value explain about 25-to-30bp of the 40-to-50bp market-vs-survey gap at the front end. MBS and
Treasury futures negative convexity amplified the recent selloff (we estimate roughly $60bn in 10y
equivalent MBS hedging flows since early September), but index-level MBS convexity is now about zero
and CTD-switch risk is no longer clearly asymmetric to higher yields

3) CEEMEA ECONOMICS (DALY) – Hungary's Medium-Term Fiscal Plan: A Key Step on the Path to
Euro Convergence – LINK
BOTTOM LINE: Hungary’s Tisza government aims to meet the Maastricht convergence criteria by 2030
and is committed to Euro adoption. The MNB’s reduction of its inflation target from 3.0% to 2.5% marked
the first concrete step towards meeting the Maastricht criteria on price stability and market convergence.
We expect the next major milestone in the convergence process around 15-20 October, when the
government is due to release its medium-term budget plan, setting out how and when it intends to reduce
the deficit below the Maastricht threshold of 3.0% of GDP.
Expectations: A key uncertainty is whether the medium-term plan will explicitly commit to a Euro
adoption date, widely expected to be either 1 January 2031 or 1 January 2032. We do not expect the
government to announce an official entry date at this stage. Instead, we think it will publish medium-term
forecasts showing the deficit below 3.0% of GDP by 2029—signalling an intention to adopt the Euro in
2031 without formally committing to it. As a credible first step, we expect the government to forecast a
decline in the deficit from an estimated 7.5% of GDP this year to around 5.0% next year.
Interest Rate Convergence: We expect the medium-term fiscal plan to provide an important fresh catalyst
for Euro-convergence inflows in Hungary. Hungarian 5y/5y swap spreads to Euro rates have roughly
halved since the April elections, but in our view there remains significant scope for further interest rate
convergence.
