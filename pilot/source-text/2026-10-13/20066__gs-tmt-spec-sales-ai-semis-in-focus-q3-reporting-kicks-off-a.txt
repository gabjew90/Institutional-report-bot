GS TMT SPEC SALES – Monday 12th October
The week is centred on AI/semis, with SEMICON West and the OCP Global Summit providing updates on
advanced-packaging bottlenecks, power, cooling and networking, alongside earnings from ASML, TSMC,
Publicis and Havas and CMDs from Accenture and AMAT. ASML enters results with solid demand, better
positioning and potential pricing upside from a reported 10% increase in Korean spare-parts prices, though
expectations remain high; TSMC should deliver a strong Q3and may raise Q4 guidance, but the key
debate is whether management offers any visibility on 2027 capex.
US Section 337 Investigation (IFX on list) - The USITC has opened a preliminary patent investigation into
Vicor’s vertical power-delivery technology, naming Infineon and 19 other companies. Process should take
12–18 months; the main near-term risks are legal costs, customer uncertainty and potential licensing fees,
with a broader settlement the most likely outcome.
Adam cutting Prosus estimates on higher investment and financing costs. INWIT’s shareholders are
exploring a take-private, and Nvidia is reportedly considering a deal with Reflection AI that could move it
closer to frontier-model development adding to competition.
Google reportedly testing Carbon, potentially competitive with Opus 5.5. Polymarket pricing Google as a
serious contender for best model by year end, while OpenAI and Anthropic have new models they have
yet to reveal.

ASML is reportedly(unconfirmed) raising prices for all EUV and DUV spare parts in Korea by 10%, with
Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix accepting the increase for deliveries from January 2027. The blanket
rise covers items ranging from consumables to major optics, light sources and drives, highlighting ASML’s
strong pricing power and its ability to capture more of customers’ profit pools. This is positive for ASML
ahead of Wednesday’s results and should support growth and margins in its services, parts and upgrades
business, which represented around 25% of 2025 revenue and is expected to grow by more than 30% in
2026. It creates a modest cost headwind for the two Korean memory makers (both down today)

Also impacting the memory names growing competition from China after CXMT unveiled progress on a
new 4F² DRAM design that could increase storage density. CXMT, which already has around 10% of
global DRAM revenue and is rapidly adding capacity, aims to launch a server-memory product using the
technology by the end of 2026. Samsung and SK Hynix are also pursuing 4F² designs but remain at the
planning stage (SCMP).
Hardware pricing and production: Taiwan’s OSAT companies could raise prices by up to 30% as AI
demand and capacity constraints benefit ASE, KYEC, Powertech and Sigurd (ED). AMD has reportedly
lifted GPU prices by around 10%, while Nvidia RTX 50-series prices in Germany have risen by up to
66%, versus 46% for AMD (CT). Higher memory costs may also force Samsung to cut fourth-quarter
smartphone output by 20–30%, potentially reducing 2026 production from 270m to around 200m units, as
DRAM prices continue to climb (ED, Yahoo Tech).
Apple reportedly cut iPhone 18 Pro component orders by up to 15% because of higher memory costs, but
raised Pro Max orders by 10–15%. This leaves overall Pro-series volumes broadly unchanged, with strong
demand for the more expensive Pro Max driving a favourable shift in the sales mix. (CT)
INWIT’s main shareholders are reportedly arranging financing for a possible take-private (Reuters):
Ardian and KKR-backed Oak Holdings 1, which own 32% and 39% respectively, are seeking to acquire
the remaining 29% public float, with Mubadala in talks to join the consortium. The potential deal comes as
INWIT faces lease disputes with key tenants TIM and Fastweb, which are seeking lower rates,
contributing to a roughly 27% fall in the shares over the past year and a market value of about €6.3bn.

| • | NVIDIA according to the weekend FT is in early talks to acquire open-weight AI startup Reflection AI
—last valued at around $25 billion—or to significantly expand its existing ~$800 million stake, potentially
via an acqui-hire or licensing structure designed to ease regulatory scrutiny. The discussions appear driven
by frustration within NVIDIA leadership, particularly CEO Jensen Huang, over hyperscalers and frontier
labs such as OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Meta developing custom ASICs that reduce reliance on
NVIDIA GPUs and CUDA, while also aiming to bolster U.S. open-weight alternatives to low-cost
Chinese models like DeepSeek following Reflection’s recent launch of its efficient Beam coding model; if
completed, the deal would mark a notable step toward NVIDIA competing more directly in frontier model

development rather than remaining solely a chip supplier. | | • | Amazon is reportedly in talks to acquire
Israeli AI start-up Decart AI for around $7bn, with a deal potentially close if negotiations hold, after
Anthropic previously considered a roughly $6bn purchase but decided not to proceed. Separately, Nvidia’s
proposed deal with Reflection AI could involve hiring staff and licensing its technology rather than a full
acquisition, helping to reduce regulatory scrutiny (FT). |

TECH
| • | IFX: US Section 337 Investigation (IFX on list)—The USITC has opened a preliminary patent
investigation into vertical power-delivery systems following a complaint by Vicor, naming 20 companies
including Infineon, Hon Hai, Delta and Quanta. The case could ultimately lead to import restrictions or
cease-and-desist orders (unlikely), but no infringement has been established, operations and shipments
remain unaffected, and the process is expected to take around 12–18 months. Near-term risks for Infineon
include higher legal costs, possible qualification hesitancy from hyperscaler customers and pressure to
negotiate a licence with Vicor, although an industry-wide settlement or licensing framework appears the
most likely long-term outcome given the importance of the companies involved to the AI server supply
chain. | | • | Worldline Q3 preview -Worldline ahead of 3Q26 results, scheduled for October 27th, 2026. We
slightly lower our FY26 growth assumptions for FS, taking a more prudent view on the segment (FS
organic growth in FY26 at -6.6% vs -6% previously), with no changes to MS growth, resulting in broadly
unchanged total organic revenue growth for FY26E at c.0%. We keep our OMDA margin and FCF
estimates broadly unchanged. |

TELCOS
| • | TELCOS - I know sentiment counts but do European Telcos REALLY matter to Elon ?…. I get the DT
short given the TMUS exposure ….. Investor feedback broadly agrees that the US should be SpaceX’s
priority: it is a large, three-player oligopoly with blended ARPU of roughly €40–44 per month and
postpaid ARPU of $54–58, versus an EU average of only €14–15. This gives SpaceX one high-value
market with consistent economics and regulation, whereas Europe is fragmented across countries,
operators, spectrum regimes and competitive structures. However, investors also caution that a global
player is unlikely to ignore Europe forever: the UK could be a relatively cheap entry point through
Vodafone’s MVNO, Germany may offer national-roaming options, and smaller operators such as
Hutchison could provide spectrum or acquisition opportunities.The main caveats are timing and scale:
Switzerland has high ARPU, but it is a small and expensive market with strong existing mobile networks,
extensive fibre and restrictive tower planning, limiting its strategic appeal. More broadly, SpaceX would
first need access to S-band spectrum, making an imminent European move unlikely. Even so, the overhang
may be difficult to dismiss because SpaceX previously downplayed plans to buy spectrum or work with
terrestrial towers before doing both, meaning many are reluctant to take its current messaging at face
value. GIR full note. |
