Privo on oil: So much for the Russia/Ukraine energy ceasefire… couldn’t survive a news cycle. Arguably
there was never a ceasefire to begin with. Trump announced a deal that surprised Kyiv and was never
confirmed by Moscow, only for Russia to strike Ukrainian power infrastructure within hours. The Middle
East isn’t much better. Houthi strikes on Riyadh airport exposed Saudi air defence vulnerabilities, yet
Brent is lower and production remains intact. The market is distinguishing between geopolitical escalation
and barrels actually lost… reasonable, but a fragile equilibrium. Unclear where to from here, not much
time until political deadlines. Seems like everything has been tried outside of agreeing a deal on Hormuz
and so far has not yielded much to bring prices down.
CPI GS preview: We expect a 0.24% increase in September core CPI (vs. +0.2% consensus),
corresponding to a year-over-year rate of +2.46% (vs. +2.5% consensus). We expect a 0.59% increase in
headline CPI (vs. +0.6% consensus), reflecting higher energy prices. Our forecast is consistent with a
slightly smaller 0.20% increase in core PCE in September. We highlight three key component-level trends
we expect to see in the report. First, we expect a pickup in autos inflation, reflecting a 0.5% increase in
used car prices, a 0.2% increase in new car prices, and a 0.2% increase in the auto insurance category.
Second, we forecast benign readings for the shelter categories—a 0.20% increase in the OER category and
a 0.17% increase in the rent category—reflecting the continued slowdown in their underlying trends.
Third, we expect a 3.0% increase in airfares, reflecting the impact of the further increase in jet fuel prices.

Looking beyond September, we expect a monthly core CPI increase of around 0.2% in October—
reflecting upward pressure from distortions to the seasonal factors caused by missing data from last year’s
government shutdown—and increases of around 0.1% in November and December—reflecting the
continued slowdown in the shelter categories and shrinking contributions from tariff-related price
increases. Full note:
