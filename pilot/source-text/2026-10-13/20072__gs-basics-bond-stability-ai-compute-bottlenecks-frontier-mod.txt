GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Bond Stability. AI Compute
Bottlenecks. Frontier Model Race 12 Oct 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
12 Oct 2026
Markets: Mostly shifting higher, with rates finally finding their feet after a tumultuous week. Treasury
auctions relatively well absorbed, bonds stabilizing, gold trying to find local low higher and oil backing
off the highs this morning. First period of relative stability in rates for some time (longer dated bond vols
retracing ~30% of their recent move). Asia sending mixed signals.
Asia: Pretty ugly in Korea. Kospi -3-4%, Samsung/Hynix -4%, Taiwan broadly flat. Memory remains the
epicenter. CXMT announced a new 4F² DRAM architecture over the weekend. China already has ~10%
DRAM revenue share and is aggressively adding capacity. Adds to concerns around peak memory
earnings despite extraordinary near term fundamentals. Also perhaps a bit of travel and arrive post
Samsung numbers which failed to massively surpass expectations. Taiwan holding up much better ahead
of TSMC. Nikkei closed for a holiday.
Energy: Riyadh airport struck by Houthi missiles, another Gulf escalation but no confirmed material
production impact. Trump announced a Russia/Ukraine energy ceasefire, seemingly surprising both sides,
although attacks have continued. Also bringing Russian diesel back into the market. Neither development
looks particularly durable or material, but both help sentiment. Brent ~$103. The underlying problem
remains unresolved (seems largely the cost of shipping and product market), but perhaps measured
escalation around $100 oil is something markets can live with. Would have thought a more durable off
ramp by now...
Sentiment: Vol flat, fear and greed back to neutral, GS risk appetite still low at -1.3. PB data tells the same
story. People have rotated heavily into tech, but aggregate nets remain severely diminished. More AI as a
percentage of the book, but the book itself has shrunk. Still a ton of capacity. An immensely frustrating
week for alpha, yet we’re within an eyelash of S&P highs and the market hardly feels well owned. in the
US HFs rotated into Industrials and out of Info Tech...a notable reversal of the September trend ..."with the
Tech selling most pronounced Thursday on the OpenAI revenue headline." After being the most-bought
US sector in 4 of the prior 5 weeks, Info Tech was by far the most net sold this week at -1.0 SDs.
Fundamental L/S net at just 52.9% (6th pct 1y)
AI Bottlenecks/Owners Of Compute: Google reportedly testing Carbon, potentially competitive with Opus
5.5. Polymarket pricing Google as a serious contender for best model (see below), while OpenAI and
Anthropic have new models they have yet to reveal. The volatility around OpenAI’s ARR last week
appears largely a question of accounting definitions. Don’t want to dwell on specifics, but the reaction
suggests more length and increasing sensitivity in these pockets. Bigger picture unchanged. Compute is
scarce, and the owners of scarce compute are the biggest beneficiaries. Part of the reason they are staying
scarce is because the market is beginning to doubt the ability/capacity of the street to bring power/data

center capacity online to meet demand. Hence the de-rating in the data center supply chain plays. That
said, Hyperscalers have invested most aggressively in precisely this scarce commodity and can command
premium economics. The market is right to reward them. Model leadership can change quarterly…
ownership of powered compute is a much more durable advantage.
Consumer: Michigan current conditions at an all time low. Affordability is becoming a massive issue, with
higher rates, oil and soft real wages biting simultaneously. Consumption slowing, not collapsing, but
sentiment/activity divergence remains extraordinary.
Europe: EU/China hybrid agreement targets a ~50% reduction in projected Chinese hybrid imports.
Potentially a substantial positive for European autos and another catalyst for a momentum unwind. Add
stability in OATs and even perceived Russia/Ukraine progress (unclear), and Europe suddenly looks under
owned.
Week ahead: CPI Wednesday the main event, consensus 0.6% headline, 0.2% core. A 10bp downside
surprise on core would be meaningful for rates. Banks start Tuesday, ASML Wednesday, TSMC Thursday,
plus PPI, retail sales and then Warsh Friday. Plenty of opportunities for the micro to reassert itself…
provided bonds behave.
Risk: Risk that bond vol compresses, yields remain high but stabilize, and the negative impulse
suppressing the rest of the economy relaxes. Nominal growth remains incredibly strong, fiscal spending
off the charts. Until bonds force a stop, we’re generating enormous nominal demand. My concerns around
capital intensity and crowding out will play out over time, but don’t necessarily matter today. Positioning
adds fuel… speculators heavily short bonds and Russell, long Nasdaq. We know where the pain trade lies.
Could easily see headline indices tread water while under owned Europe/Russell outperform. Hard to get
too optimistic with energy unresolved, but stability may be enough. And earnings could be hugely
supportive… AI profits compounding, ASML reportedly raising spare parts prices 10%, TSMC and banks
already materially de-rated. Feels more like a chop at the highs/consolidation.
