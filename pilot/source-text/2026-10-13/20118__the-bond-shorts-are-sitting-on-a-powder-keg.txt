The bond reversal is getting interesting
Treasury yields have reversed almost perfectly from major technical resistance, with both the 10-year
and 30-year turning lower.
But the real story could be positioning.
Systematic investors remain heavily short bonds, creating the potential for a powerful short-covering
rally if yields continue to fall.
Meanwhile, bond volatility is finally starting to ease, removing another potential obstacle to lower
yields.
The technicals are turning. Positioning is vulnerable. Volatility is cooling.
Could the next big squeeze be in bonds?
The 10 year
The US 10-year yield reversed almost perfectly at the upper end of its rising trend channel. The 21-day
moving average, currently around 5.15%, is the first downside level to watch.
Below that, the lower end of the trend channel and the 50-day moving average sit considerably lower,
with the latter currently around 4.9%.

The 30 year
The 30-year yield has also reversed almost perfectly from the upper end of its rising trend channel.
The 21-day moving average, currently around 5.5%, is the first downside level to watch. Below that, the
50-day moving average and the lower end of the channel come into play around 5.35–5.4%.

CTA convexity
Systematic investors remain heavily short bonds, leaving significant upside convexity if the market
continues to rally.

CTAs have played the bearish momentum remarkably well, but a sustained reversal could force short
covering and turn an orderly bond rally into something much more aggressive.

Bond volatility
Bond volatility remains elevated, but the MOVE index closed below 100 for the first time since late
September.
A further reset in volatility could help pave the way for another leg lower in yields.
