 
Three Strikes: Quants Suffer Worst Day Since 
August As Hedge Funds Notch Third "Never Since 
COVID" Day Of 2026 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, OCT 16, 2026 - 05:45 PM 
Selling the AI stocks was the easy part. Then came the bill. 
Earlier today we showed that amid the ongoing AI rout driven by sudden credit fears, hedge 
funds had dumped AI stocks at the second-fastest pace in a year, round-tripping every dollar of 
net AI buying since January 2025 in just five sessions. That covered the flows through 
Wednesday. On Thursday, Goldman Prime's Marco Laicini sent two follow-up notes (here and 
here) tracking what all that de-grossing did to the P&L in real time, and the short version is in 
the first line of his close update: 
Momentum further down, crowding hurting even more. Pain across quants. Small recover 
across Fundamentals. 
And yet the most striking line came hours earlier, in Laicini's 10:30am "Prime HFs Intraday 
Update - AI Sell Off" (also available to pro subs): 
From COVID through end-2025, all three broad strategies never fell more than 80bps on the 
same day. In 2026 it has now happened three times: 4-Feb, 28-Jul and today. 
In other words, a five-year event has now happened three times in ten months. Which, in the 
world of hedge fund risk management, tends to mean the models are wrong about what counts 
as a five-year event, and also means that the entire VaR methodology probably has to be 
reworked from scratch. 
Momentum Down 3.4 Sigma, Shorts Up 1.2 Sigma 
Start with what drove it. By 10:30am, Goldman was tracking Momentum down 3.4 standard 
deviations, Crowded Longs down 1.8 SD and Crowded Shorts squeezing +1.2 SD, which Laicini 
read as a sign of "broader de-grossing." 

 
That combination is the tell. When the stuff everyone owns falls and the stuff everyone is short 
rips at the same time, nobody is making a directional call: funds are cutting both sides of the 
book at once, and the factor that pays for it is whatever is most crowded. Today that was 
momentum, which per our market wrap suffered its worst day in three months as momo shorts 
saw a huge squeeze. Goldman's midday desk wrap, which we tweeted, put a number on the 
damage under the hood: the bank's HF VIP Long/Short pair (the most popular hedge fund longs 
against the most shorted names) was down 115bps on the day and -10% since the start of the 
drawdown on Oct 8. 
Here is how the 10:30am estimates looked: 
●​ Global Fundamental L/S down 0.9% (-1.2 SD), down every day this week, taking the 
weekly drawdown to -2.9% and the YTD gain to +9.6%. 
●​ Asia-focused funds down 2.6% (-2 SD), now down 6.8% MTD. Still up 17% YTD, "but 
23% off the late-June peak of +40%." 
●​ TMT-focused funds down 2.0% (-1.4 SD), down 4.7% in the past two sessions, and still 
up 11.1% YTD (it was 13.1% at Wednesday's close). 
●​ Global Systematic L/S also down 0.9% (-2.6 SD), "primarily coming from European and 
Asian exposure. Momentum and crowding among the key factor drivers." 
●​ Multi-Strats (equity portfolio, 4.5x leverage) down 1.2% (-2.2 SD), with momentum and 
crowding the top factor losses. 
And here are the three days in question. For full disclosure, by the close Fundamental L/S 
managers had clawed back to -0.5%, so purists can put an asterisk on strike three; the quants, 
as we show next, more than made up for it. 

 
 
Regular readers will remember the first two. In February, Goldman's Prime desk flagged that 
quants and multi-strat funds were crushed in their worst day since the COVID selloff, with TMT 
managers down 319bps, on what was a 4-sigma day for momentum: 
Today was a 4-sigma event for momentum. In theory. it is supposed to occur once ever 126 
years yet the last 4-sigma took place in 2022. 
— zerohedge (@zerohedge) February 5, 2026 
Then came July 28, the worst day for hedge funds since the COVID crash, when all three 
strategies were tracking down more than 1% for the first time since March 2020, courtesy of 
Leopold Aschenbrenner's "wrong-way" momentum book and a Korean memory-stock 
liquidation. Today rhymes with both: momentum, crowding and, once again, Asia (it remains to 
be seen of Leo blew up... again). 
Pain Across Quants 
Here is where the session got worse instead of better. As the market clawed back after Jensen 
Huang talked up CoreWeave, the stock pickers recovered some ground. The machines did not. 
At the close, per Laicini: 
●​ Global Fundamental L/S down 0.5% (-0.8 SD), now up 10% YTD. 
●​ Global Systematic L/S down 1.4% (-3.7 SD), the worst day since Aug 19th. Still up 20% 
YTD. 

 
●​ Multi-Strats (equity portfolio, 4.5x leverage) down 1.2% (-2.2 SD). 
A -3.7 sigma day for systematic L/S is not a rounding error (just ask August 2007). Goldman's 
chart of daily systematic returns in Z-score terms shows only one worse print in the past year, 
the August 19 plunge that we described at the time as the worst day for systematic funds since 
2023: 
 
Which is ironic: just yesterday, quants were the only winners in the Prime universe, up 0.5% on 
the day and leading the pack with a 21.7% YTD gain, which prompted us to quip that "the 
machines did not need to own AI to make money from it." One day later, they gave all of it back 
and then some, as the momentum factor they ride flipped hard against them. Systematic funds 
are short the losers, and today the losers were the winners. 
Goldman's top tech specialist Peter Callahan noted in his end-of-day color that this episode is 
different from the summer one: 
Unlike the momentum unwind in July, which felt mostly 'technical' in nature after the huge run in 
April-June, the current episode has created some more debates on which 'wall of worry' is most 
'acute' for investors to navigate into year end -- earnings, elections, rates/credit, ROIC, inflation, 
et al? 
Translation: July was a positioning flush; this one comes with fundamental questions attached, 
which is a much harder thing for a risk manager to wait out. 

 
As for multi-strats, a -1.2% day for the 4.5x levered equity book is precisely the number they 
posted on the second day of the February rout, which is when we warned that the pod shops' 
pain could leak into the most levered trade of them all: 
Update: MultiStrats down 1.2% volatility, crowded shorts and profitability among negative 
drivers: GS Multistrats are down so much, their degrossing will hit basis trades next. Then all 
bets are off. 
— zerohedge (@zerohedge) February 5, 2026 
With the 30Y yield down 11bps on the day and Treasuries soaring (for now) as the bond market 
prices the deflationary side of an AI bust, nobody is worried about the basis trade yet. That 
tends to be when one should be. 
Asia: 23 Points Off The Top 
The real carnage, once again, was in Asia. In a separate Prime Insights note published after the 
close, "Prime Hedge Fund updates: Asia Managers sustain -6.8% MTD drawdown; selling in AI 
exposure accelerates", Goldman's Himanshu Deshmukh confirmed that Asia Fundamental L/S 
managers lost 2.6% on Thursday, taking their MTD drawdown to -6.8% and their YTD return 
down to 16.7%, which is "back to July lows." That is roughly 23 points below the 40% high they 
reached at the end of June. Put differently, Asia's blockbuster hedge fund year has been cut by 
more than half in under four months, and Deshmukh pins the October drawdown mainly on beta 
and asset-selection alpha, "followed by impacts from Volatility, Info Tech and Korea tilts." 
The selling was concentrated exactly where you would expect. Asian equities were net sold on 
the Prime book (-1.0 SD), led by Korea and Taiwan, with long sales exceeding short covers 3 to 
1, and "selling in Korea and Taiwan was dominated by long unwinds in the AI space." 
Goldman's chart of 2026 net flows by market shows Korea in a league of its own: cumulative net 
selling is now around 13% of Asia net MV, while Japan, China and Taiwan hover near flat. 

 
 
Which is how you get a Kospi crashing into a bear market, down exactly 20% from its high 
barely a week after we noted that the only buyer had left the building. 
The positioning numbers explain why Korea is getting hit hardest. Korea net allocations have 
declined 75bps MTD to 3.64%, now just the 32nd percentile on a one-year lookback, while 
gross allocation sits at 2.59% (41st percentile 1-year). But on a five-year view both are still in 
the 86th and 88th percentiles respectively, still several times where they were in 2024 (note the 
chart runs through Oct 13, so it misses the last two days of puking): 

 
 
Taiwan is the bigger worry, because it has barely been touched (and then there is the whole 
currency thing). Allocations there were "relatively less impacted due to net buying/risk on activity 
observed in the first week of October," and gross allocation is close to all-time highs, in the 94th 
percentile on a one-year view and the 99th percentile over five years. 
 

 
In other words, the Korean leg of the AI trade has been half-liquidated, while the Taiwanese leg 
is still sitting at near-record size. Considering that foreign investors yanked a record ~$9.3 billion 
out of Taiwan in a single session this week, that is a lot of gross waiting for the exit should 
sentiment not improve. Zooming out, Asia Info Tech is still about 9% of global gross and around 
18% of global net exposure in the Prime book, roughly three times its share of net at the start of 
2025 even after the July air pocket: 
 
"Pockets Of Overreaction"... Says The Trading Desk 
Naturally, not everyone at Goldman sees a crisis. In a cross-desk email titled "AI Derisk - what 
we are seeing" (available to pro subs), the bank's London sales team summed up the house 
view: credit is "forcing a rethink of how quickly the AI capex cycle can be financed and 
delivered, with the positioning unwind amplifying the move. But with AI exposure materially 
reduced, discretionary nets low and rates rallying, trading sees pockets of overreaction, even as 
the financing constraints remain real." 
The case for the bounce, per the bank's own desks: 
●​ Rich Privorotsky, whose line that "demand for compute can grow exponentially, but the 
capital available to finance it cannot" we ran earlier, also wrote that "a lot of bad news is 
now getting priced," with neoclouds down 20% in five sessions while quality has rallied in 

 
16 of 18 sessions with an RSI of 83. His conclusion: "My gut is we've overreacted in 
pockets." 
●​ The midday desk noted that Goldman's sentiment indicator has been negative for six 
straight weeks, with its latest reading the lowest at -1.3, and that the AI trade "is still 
long, but now a 6 out 10 in terms of length and much cleaner than it was in Jun/July." 
The desk has net sold $10.5 billion of TMT in the past six sessions. 
●​ Tech specialist Callahan pointed out that the NDX forward P/E is back to ~21x, "the lows 
since the AI cycle kicked off in 2023." 
All fair points, and all of them arguments that were also made in early August, right before the 
August 19 quant quake. A 6 out of 10 on length is cleaner, but still more long than not. And as 
we noted this morning, even after the AI round trip, net exposure to the group is still in the 83rd 
percentile on a three-year view while gross leverage on the Prime book is in the 94th percentile 
of the past five years. 
Bottom Line 
The pattern of the past ten months is now hard to ignore: hedge funds crowd into the same 
momentum/AI trade, the trade cracks, everyone de-grosses at once, and every strategy (stock 
pickers, quants and the levered pods) bleeds on the same day. It happened in February. It 
happened in July. It happened today. Laicini's own summary of the intraday tape says it best: 
"Market/AI sell-off continues. Momentum down 3.4 SD, Crowded Longs down 1.8 SD, Crowded 
shorts squeezing +1.2SD indicating broader de-grossing." 
The desks say pockets of overreaction; we would take the under. A broad de-gross ends when 
gross exposure comes down, and with Prime book gross still at 310% (and diverging from net to 
an unprecedented degree) and Taiwan allocations at five-year highs, it hasn't. Throw in 
tomorrow's OpEx, which removes some of the positive-gamma cushion that has kept the S&P 
calm (the index bounced perfectly off its 50DMA today), and the next leg of this unwind may 
finally show up in the index instead of just in hedge fund P&Ls. Of course, if the deflationary 
AI-bust trade keeps sending yields lower, the Fed may get a very convenient excuse to stop 
hiking (just mind the market meltdown as the AI bust trade is, well, very deflationary). 
Then again, hedge funds managed to suffer three "once since COVID" days in under ten 
months while the S&P posted modest gains for the second day in a row. If nothing else, that 
tells you exactly who has been holding this market up.
