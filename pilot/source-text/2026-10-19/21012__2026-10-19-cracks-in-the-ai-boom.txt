CRACKS IN THE AI BOOM?
Dario Perkins
19 Oct 2026
This week I will publish a Macro Picture on what happens if the AI boom ends in a bust. “It’s a
scenario, not a forecast.” OK, it’s a forecast. I will even include an AI-generated summary in the style
of those viral social media posts that have been so popular this year. (The Perkini Report.) Look out
for it. The note is NOT going to address the question of whether AI is a bubble, or when it might
end. It focuses on the fallout. But to set the scene, here are the vulnerabilities that bother me:
1/6
AI bulls with tell you that revenues across the ecosystem are rising
rapidly and compute demand is off the scale. Fine – that is not up for
debate. The question is whether those revenues are coming from
businesses and end-users outside the ecosystem – presumably to pay
for productivity-enhancing tools – or from simple capex recycling.
Updating my analysis from earlier in the year, the story hasn’t changed
– it is still mostly capex recycling. That is not a mark of genuine
sustainability.

2/6
The more hyperscalers pump into the system, the more revenues rise. It
aint rocket science. And even as revenue rise, they are falling well short
of a decent return on capital. Since we last wrote about this issue in
June, there have been a flurry of studies showing that the hurdle rate on
AI revenue is $2.5 - 3.5 trillion pa by 2030. At $200bn in enterprise
revenue currently, that requires a massive leap. It means consumers
need to spend as much on AI services as they do on food. Bit unlikely?

3/6
Then there are issues around AI financing, where we see rapid increases
in leverage. Of course, much of this is backed by hyperscalers’ “pristine”
balance sheets. They wont default, although they might not renew all
those rolling datacenters leases. But it’s the debt in the other parts of
the ecosystem that look dodgy – from unprofitable neoclouds, Oracle,
“standalone model developers” (as Claude likes to describe them) and
equipment/power providers. By end-27, this debt could hit $2 trillion.
4/6
While AI debt is not systemic issue, it could still be a problem –
particularly if earnings disappoint, and those misses cascade through
the ecosystem as cancelled orders. Let’s not forget we are talking about
tech hardware and chips, notoriously cyclical, and prone to bullwhip
effects. There are reports of companies double- and triple-ordering
chips in anticipation of expected shortages. If demand slows, shortages
could turn into a glut. And don't get me started on NVIDIA's vendor
financing.

5/6
Some analysts also warn of “teaser rate” effects, like what happened
with subprime. While the comparison is stretched, it is true several
accounting practices are flattering AI-related balance sheets. One
example is the “depreciation cliff”, which appears only after data centers
are completed. Data center leases are another. During the teaser period
everyone wins. The seller reports backlogs while the buyer – a frontier
lab burning cash – books no expense because the capacity doesn't
exist yet.

6/6
While these are all vulnerabilities, it is important to remember that the
trigger doesn’t have to be endogenous to the boom. It could be external.
Rate hikes are one candidate, but politics is another. AI boosters on
social media live in a bubble (in at least two senses). They don’t realize
that ordinary people don’t like AI. They don’t find it useful, they REALLY
don’t want to live near datacenters, and they are worried about their
jobs. And they vote. One high-profile accident turns AI toxic.
