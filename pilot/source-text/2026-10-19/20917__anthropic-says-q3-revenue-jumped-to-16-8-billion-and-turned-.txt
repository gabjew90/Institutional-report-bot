 
Anthropic Says Q3 Revenue Jumped To $16.8 
Billion And Turned "Adjusted" Profitable... But 
Run-Rate Math Shows Growth Hitting The Brakes

THURSDAY, OCT 15, 2026 - 04:35 AM 
We got the number. 
As we previewed this morning, the entire AI complex (if not the entire market) spent Wednesday 
waiting with bated breath for one data point out of Anthropic's closed-door pre-IPO investor day 
in San Francisco. Shortly after the close, the FT delivered: Anthropic told investors its 
third-quarter revenue had jumped to $16.8 billion, "almost 50 per cent" above the $11.5 billion 
recorded in the second quarter, and that the company was "profitable on an adjusted basis" and 
cash flow positive in the quarter.

$65 billion run rate Anthropic told investors it had hit at the end of July. But Anthropic's run rate 
annualizes the most recent month, so the real question is where the company exited 
September, and that takes a little arithmetic.

The more generous reading treats $65 billion as the pace on July 31 rather than July's average. 
In that case July revenue was lower (closer to $5.2 billion, since it was still ramping), more of the 
quarter lands in August and September, and the implied growth rate rises to ~6.6% a month. 
September revenue × 12 still comes to only ~$71 billion, but the exit pace on Sep 30 works out 
to about $74 billion. 
 

 
Either way, Anthropic exited the third quarter at somewhere between $69 billion and $74 billion, 
which is what TMT Breakout also calculated shortly after the FT headline. Now project it forward 
from the generous end of the range. At the same ~6.6% a month, the run rate reaches ~$89 
billion by December. Hitting the $100 billion year-end bogey requires about 10.6% a month in 
Q4 from $74 billion, or 12.6% from $70 billion. That's the same conclusion Shanu Mathew 
reached: growth has decelerated, and the $100 billion target now needs Q4 to reaccelerate. 
Incidentally, that is what the latest token data from Ramp AI shows: while AI usage is rapidly 
rising (per Jevon's Paradox), AI spend is actually flat at best, if not shrinking... 
 
... as a result of tumbling token prices thanks to growing market share by open-source models; 
in fact as we showed earlier, token prices at Anthropic just hit an all time low suggesting that the 
price is dropping faster than the the company can convert higher volumes into revenue.  
 

 
 
Put it all on one chart and the problem is plain to see: the line that went near-vertical through 
July on the initial burst of agentic euphoria and Tokenmaxxing has flattened, and the $100 billion 
IPO bogey sits about $10 billion above where the Aug-Sep pace gets you by December.

To be clear, 46% sequential growth on a $46 billion base (Q2 annualized) is extraordinary by the 
standards of any company that has ever existed. The issue is the bar, which Anthropic itself set: 
going into the meeting, much of the Street had the NYT's "more than $100 billion" year-end 
figure penciled in. On the trend that the third quarter implies, they get closer to $89 billion. 
Here, one can argue here that the only way the previous growth rate can be recovered, is if 
Anthropic's token prices rebound strongly to pre-September levels. But for that to happen, the 
US government would need to "regulate" away Chinese open-source competition. 
And now you know why both Anthropic and OpenAi are "passively" scaremongering each and 
every day in hopes of getting the Trump admin to block competition (because only China could 
be so irresponsible as to unleash an agent swarm hell bent on crushing everything in its path). 
Actually, make that "active": as we reported earlier, OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar is done beating 
around the bush, and is now on a crusade to eliminate cheaper competitors, warning 
businesses on Wednesday that adopting low-cost Chinese artificial intelligence models carries 
material cybersecurity risks, and telling Bloomberg that firms should prioritize Western models 
"on the cyber front."  
Western models like... OpenAI course.  
"Adjusted" Profitable 
Then there is the profit claim, which will get most of the headlines and deserves the most 
asterisks. The FT doesn't say what was "adjusted," and the full S-1 with GAAP numbers isn't 
due publicly until around Oct 25. What we do know comes from the confidential prospectus 
reported by Reuters: a $42 billion net loss in 2025 (about $34 billion of it an accounting charge 
tied to financing), an operating loss of more than $8 billion, and at least $518 billion of compute 
and infrastructure commitments over the next decade, about 80% of them non-cancelable. 
Also recall also the gross-vs-net debate that blew up OpenAI's numbers last week. As 
Goldman's TMT desk explained, "Anthropic reports closer to gross revenue (full customer spend 
through cloud partners, partner cut booked as a cost)." That flatters the top line relative to 
OpenAI's net reporting, and it makes any "adjusted" margin a function of what gets booked 
where. One quarter of positive adjusted profit and cash flow, ahead of a ~$2 trillion IPO and with 
the biggest compute bills still ahead (the Microsoft commitment doesn't even start until 
November), is a data point (and one that does not take into account subscriber churn, which is 
certainly a key issue among frontier labs where a new and improved model is released between 
the 4-5 leaders every other week like hot potatoes). It is certainly a long way from a track record.

resurfaced last week, has the top three labs needing to go from a combined ~$100 billion run 
rate in July to $180-200 billion by year-end "to keep the AI trade intact." One West Coast PM in 
yesterday's Bear Traps chat set the bar even higher: "you need to see OAI + ANTH combined 
ARR going to $200 - $300bn by mid 2027 for AI trade to work," adding that "Anthropic ARR 
number tomorrow better be huge---Everyone bracing for a bad number after the OpenAI 
debacle last week."

On valuation, the picture improves. At $2 trillion, Anthropic trades at about 27x its implied

Bottom Line 
This morning we asked whether Anthropic is going public from a position of strength or need. 
Tonight's answer is both: a $16.8 billion quarter, adjusted profits and positive cash flow are 
strength by any normal standard, and good enough to keep the November IPO on track. But the 
growth rate implied by that quarter fell from about a third per month to mid-single digits, and the 
$100 billion year-end number the market has been underwriting now requires Q4 growth to 
roughly double from the Aug-Sep pace, during a quarter when token prices are falling and Meta, 
Google and the Chinese open-weight labs are all crowding the frontier with their own deeply 
discounted offerings. 
Which is why we think the November window will be set less by today's revenue print than by 
the credit market that is supposed to fund the next $518 billion. On a day when Oracle CDS 
blew out 31bps and CoreWeave's bonds traded at deeply distressed yields, a strong quarter 
from the most important tenant in the AI ecosystem is necessary. It may not be sufficient. 
Futures will tell us tomorrow whether one number can reattach the AI trade's right tail; the public 
S-1, due around Oct 25, will tell us what "adjusted" means.
