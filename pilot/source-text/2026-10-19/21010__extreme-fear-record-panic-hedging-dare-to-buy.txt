The shock
Friday's explosion in VIX and VVIX was nothing short of extreme. The price action had all the hallmarks
of forced deleveraging, with risk managers hitting the brakes and traders scrambling for tail protection
at virtually any price. This was panic hedging, not ordinary volatility buying.

Panic in the tail
The real panic was in the tails. VIX exploded higher, but the moves in SDEX and TDEX were even more
extreme. Demand for protection against a major downside shock went through the roof, with traders
seemingly willing to pay almost any price.

The hedge panic
Friday marked the first real signs of panic in index hedging according to Lee Coppersmith, following
weeks of heavy damage beneath the surface and across client portfolios.
The rush for protection was extraordinary. VIX futures volume surged to 3.6x its 20-day average, Cboe
index put volume hit an all-time high, and total put volume reached its fourth-highest level since 2015.
This was hedging activity on a scale rarely seen before.

VIX call love
VIX call volume surged to its second-highest level in five years, surpassed only by August 2, 2024, just
before VIX exploded above 60 the following Monday. Quite a shift in investor behavior.

Outlier
Friday's VIX explosion was extraordinary relative to the actual decline in SPX. The VIX is typically
around 5x as volatile as the SPX, but Friday's move was a major outlier. Since 1990, only nine other
sessions have seen a larger VIX spike alongside a smaller SPX decline. That's an extreme amount of
fear for a relatively modest index selloff.

Credit stress
Friday's VIX explosion was massive, but let's not forget that equity volatility had been remarkably
complacent, largely ignoring the stress building in credit markets. Friday finally changed that. We're
not suggesting VIX needs to fully catch up with CDX IG, but the divergence remains wide and worth
keeping an eye on.

Gamma gone
Dealer gamma reportedly dropped by $5.9bn on Friday alone, leaving aggregate positioning close to
neutral. Monthly expiration likely played a role, but the implications could be significant.
For much of this year, massive moves in individual stocks and sectors have barely registered at the
index level, partly thanks to positive dealer gamma absorbing volatility. With that cushion now fading
and investors scrambling for index protection, the risk is that volatility finally starts spreading from
individual stocks to the broader index.

Extreme fear
We're back in extreme fear territory, although still not at the panic levels seen in April. Buying extreme
fear has historically been a profitable strategy. The question is, do you dare this time?
