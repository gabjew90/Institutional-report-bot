 
Bank Earnings Day 2 Wrap From Goldman's Fins 
Desk 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 15, 2026 - 04:45 AM 
It was day 2 of bank earnings, and as Goldman bank specialist Christian DeGrasse writes in his 
end of day note, "the tape continues to be choppy & heavily influenced by shifting concerns 
relating to macro & data centers. .. One of those markets where the bulls just can’t catch a 
break." 
He's right: it's been ugly for bank investors who after suffering a dire September when banks 
plunged, October has seen more of the same despite earnings that were mostly in line (except 
for Bank of America). As Degrasse describes it "when AI goes higher, Fins often trade lower at a 
inverse correlation, and when the market gets worried on the AI capex buildout (and what the 
funding picture looks like from here), the fundamental connection between Financials & capital 
markets & AI gets reestablished."  
Away from stocks, rates round tripped this mornings CPI print and while financials closed off the 
lows "it doesn’t *feel* great and the tape is really moving against those levered to economic 
growth... There’s a clear tilt towards defensive exposure in the market with GS’ Long cyclicals vs 
defensives basket -6% off early October levels … Is the rest of the markets’ cyclical exposure 
catching up to what Financials (& capital markets) have been saying for a month now?" 
 

 
 
Turning to the specific bank results, DeGrasse says that the sentiment on these earnings 
reactions has been sharply negative, and people are latching on to negative datapoints far more 
intently than positive datapoints, adding that "this even seems to be ‘narrative piling on to price 
action’ with us struggling to even find reasons behind the underperformance in Citigroup 
yesterday." 
All of that said, the Goldman trader notes that at this point it's important to emphasize price 
action is questioning the level of earnings growth we’re going to see, not credit issues (see Bank 
P/TBV multiples); in fact, accroding to DeGrassed, credit quality is close to the bottom of the list 
on what people are debating about now (pipelines & cap market backlogs, capital, operating 
leverage, margin levers). That will, of course, change the moment we see the billions in debt 
propping up the first data center vaporize.  
Here is client feedback on today’s earnings reports from BAC, PGR, STT, MS, and  BLK in order 
of feedback volume, courtesy of Goldman's trading desk.  
BAC -5% (LINK to Ramsden) 
●​ Initial feedback tilted negative, with feedback on the quarter summed up as ‘it’s what 
they guided to mid quarter in weaker trading and lack of expense offset, but even worse 
than what was indicated’ … Stock was indicated -8% in the pre and we got a better 2027 
NII guide (NII story is key to the bulls here), with the stock failing to rally for a bit which 
prompted plenty of feedback (with a decent cohort of longer duration investors) 
 

 
wondering what gives? How can the stock continue to trade -8% lower when NII 
numbers need to go higher? 
●​ At the current point, the bulls are emphasizing that the NII-upside story is still in tact and 
that’s their primary focus – on the other side, bears are pointing to capital, they are at 
10.9% and we have GSIB pressure coming at start of new year so there’s not much 
excess capital at the moment … they kept a strong pace with the buyback in 3Q but 
going forward may have to slow or pull back on balance sheet growth (which could be a 
problem in a world client demand remains strong) – add on to this piece on ‘capital 
inflexibility’ and bears also argue that recent regulatory focus may limit levers on the 
expense side if we move more into a slower revenue environment 
PGR -6% (LINK to Cox) 
●​ Slower growth and weaker margin in Personal Auto in focus .. Plenty of negative 
feedback coming in from the insurance crowd on 1) slowdown in Direct (questions 
around whether agents already playing a part .. also some pushback to those views 
arguing any potential agents impact is far too soon or agents should help PGR on 
growth given it’s a lower cost option with any potential implications instead likely on 
margin) & 2) worsening trends in pricing (agency/direct annualized premium per policy 
-5.0%/-3.6%) … Feedback quite loud from the insurance specialist crowd – with 
pushback calling the move an overaction to what was simply a worse than expected 
monthly. 
STT  -6% (LINK to Blostein) 
●​ Top line miss coming from Asset Servicing & other fees while NII & NIM was slightly

MS -3% (LINK to Ramsden)

feared – this market is forward looking and discounting the pace of earnings growth 
moving forward
