 
Aftershock: Anthropic IPO Investors Mark Valuation 
Down To $1.5 Trillion As Growth Slowdown Sinks In 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, OCT 16, 2026 - 03:30 PM 
That didn't take long. Less than 24 hours after we did the math on Anthropic's investor-day 
numbers and concluded that the "run rate shows growth hitting the brakes," the people who 
actually have to do math (and write the checks) for a living, came to the same conclusion. 
According to a Reuters report published after Thursday's close, some current Anthropic 
investors and prospective buyers in its November IPO are now weighing a lower valuation after 
the company's latest disclosures, "amid broader concerns over the ballooning cost of financing 
AI." 
How much lower? Some of the investors who attended Wednesday's investor day in San 
Francisco discussed a valuation of around $1.5 trillion, three of Reuters' sources said, "a 
significant step down from the roughly $2 trillion valuation that investors had previously 
expected for the November share sale."

In other words, a quarter of the valuation, roughly $500 billion, vanished in a single investor 
presentation, a meeting that was supposed to settle the "strength or weakness" debate. It may 
have done just that, only not in the direction Anthropic had in mind, and just because a bunch of 
mostly arbitrary numbers (gross, net, annualized, pro forma, recasted, etc) didn't go up as much 
as expected. 
Strong Numbers, Wrong Slope 
To be fair to Anthropic, the numbers themselves were good... just not good enough. The 
company told prospective IPO investors that Q3 revenue jumped 45% sequentially to $16.8 

 
billion and that it was "profitable on an adjusted basis" in the quarter. Per Reuters, those figures 
"assuaged some concerns about the massive costs of developing AI systems." 
The problem was the slope. As Reuters' sources put it, "some investors were disappointed by 
the slowing sequential growth rate," since revenue had more than doubled from the first quarter 
to $11.6 billion in the second. Put differently, Q2 growth was well over 100%, Q3 growth was 
45%... and IPO buyers price a company like Anthropic on the second derivative. 
Which is precisely what we laid out last night. Back out the months from the $16.8 billion quarter 
and the September exit run rate lands somewhere between $69 billion and $74 billion, which 
means monthly growth slowed from roughly 33% in January-July to just 3.4%-6.6% in 
August-September. To hit the "more than $100 billion" year-end number the Street had penciled 
in, Anthropic needs Q4 growth to roughly double from the Aug-Sep pace. At the current pace, it 
ends the year closer to $89 billion: 
 
Our verdict at the time: "Today's number was good, but not huge." One day later that is close to 
verbatim how the buy side priced it. 
Goldman: "A Big Miss" 
The sell side wasn't much kinder. Goldman's Asia desk (available to pro subs) went with the 
least diplomatic framing on the Street, pinning Thursday's carnage across Asian tech squarely 
on Anthropic: 

 
Anthropic Q3 revenue was a big miss - $16.8bn for the quarter vs $5.4bn implied monthly 
run-rate for July (which was reported in Aug), implying close to 0 growth in August and 
September. This has weighed heavily on sentiment, as a key pillar of the AI investment thesis is 
the ARR growth trajectory of frontier models.The funding stress facing Coreweave and the 
neocloud ecosystem are further exacerbating concerns. 
That's a harsher read than ours (we get 3.4% a month even on the most conservative reading of 
the July mark, not zero), but the conclusion is the same, and the market agreed: Korea's Kospi 
crashed nearly 8% into a bear market and tripped circuit breakers, Japan's Nikkei sank almost 
5%, and Taiwan suffered a record ~$9.3 billion in foreign outflows, as we detailed in today's 
market wrap. 
Goldman's APAC custom baskets desk (also available to pro subs) drew the obvious parallel 
with the OpenAI fiasco from last week: 
Not a full week since OpenAI's ARR flip flop, this time we have Anthropic missing street

What $1.5 Trillion Buys You 
So is $1.5 trillion cheap? Only relative to $2 trillion. 

 
At $2 trillion, Anthropic was going to trade at about 30x its annualized Q3 revenue and 27x the 
implied September exit run rate. At $1.5 trillion, those drop to 22x and 20x. Even on the $89 
billion December run rate the Aug-Sep pace implies, the "discounted" price is still ~17x revenue, 
and that's on a gross revenue basis that flatters the top line relative to OpenAI's net reporting: 
 
For context, the $1.5 trillion figure is still about 55% above the $965 billion post-money of the 
May round, so early backers will do just fine. It's the November buyers who were being asked to 
underwrite a reacceleration that now has to happen in a quarter when token prices are at record 
lows, Google and Meta are crowding the frontier, and 83% of revenue comes from metered 
consumption that customers "can cut quickly if budgets tighten." 
Prediction markets are already there. On Polymarket, the odds that Anthropic lists at $2 trillion 
or more collapsed from 46% on Monday to 25% by Thursday evening, while the odds of a 
valuation below $1.75 trillion nearly tripled to 23%: 

 
 
The timing odds have slipped too: the market on a listing by Nov 30 is down to ~57% from 
~69% a week ago (it held steady on Thursday morning, as we pointed out, before the Reuters 
story hit). The Bloomberg report that crossed alongside Wednesday's numbers had Anthropic 
set to start formally marketing the deal as soon as the week of Nov. 9, which leaves very little 
time for Q4 to show the reacceleration the $100 billion bogey requires. 
The crypto crowd, which can actually trade Anthropic before the IPO or at least some tokenized 
shadow of Anthropic (without any actual claims to underlying assets), didn't wait for the S-1 
either. Minutes after the Reuters story hit, Liquid flagged that the report was "sending its 
Pre-IPO perpetual sharply lower":

The Bellwether Problem 
Why does any of this matter beyond Anthropic's cap table? Because, as Reuters notes, the AI 
market's "near-term prospects investors see as hinging largely on the share sale's success, 
followed by OpenAI's potential listing next year." A cooler reception for the biggest IPO in history 
is a cooler reception for the entire AI trade (as CoreWeave brutally experienced in the past 
week). 
And the IPO window was already showing cracks: data center operator SB Energy delayed its 
listing last month and nuclear developer Holtec as well as Australian data center developer, 
Firmus, pulled their plans altogether. Meanwhile, CoreWeave's shares and bonds tumbled again 
Thursday, with its 2032 bonds below 78 cents on the dollar and CDS pricing in a 
better-than-even chance of default, as investors digested Gartner's prediction that over 80% of 
neoclouds will fold and Deutsche's map of where AI credit is cracking. It took none other than 
Jensen Huang (the 2nd biggest CoreWeave investor) personally reassuring the market to pull 
the Nasdaq off its lows. No conflict of interest there.  
Meanwhile, recall that Anthropic still has at least $518 billion in compute commitments over the 
next decade, about 80% of them non-cancelable, and a $60 billion Broadcom chip-financing 

 
package syndicating at the same time. When we tweeted on Oct. 2 that Anthropic was "in a far 
bigger rush to reload capital" than OpenAI, the point was that the IPO is as much a funding 
event as a liquidity event. Every $100 billion knocked off the valuation is that much more dilution 
for the same amount of cash. 
Bottom Line 
Yesterday we asked whether Anthropic is going public from a position of strength or need, and 
answered "both." Thursday's repricing shows which one the market is pricing. While "the 
strength" (a $16.8 billion quarter, adjusted profits, 85% gross margins if the headline is right) got 
Anthropic to the table, the "need" ($518 billion of future bills and a growth rate that went from 
~33% a month to single digits) is now setting the price. 
Anthropic will probably push back, and with a public S-1 due around Oct 25 and a roadshow in 
early November, there's still time for a number that "reattaches the right tail." But having 
pounded the table on AI's financing problem since last October, we suspect $1.5 trillion is closer 
to the ceiling than the floor, especially if OpenAI's next update or another CoreWeave headline 
lands during the roadshow. After all, the buyers who walked out of Wednesday's meeting talking 
about a 25% haircut were the company's most prominent investors.
