 
Goldman: "Demand For Compute Can Grow 
Exponentially, But The Capital To Finance It Cannot" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 15, 2026 - 04:55 PM 
For two years the AI trade had one answer to every question: demand. Is the capex too big? 
Demand will absorb it. Are valuations stretched? Demand will grow into them. Is the debt piling 
up? Demand will service it. 
On Wednesday the market asked a different question: who is paying? Goldman's trading desks 
don't like the answer, and neither, as we detailed last night, does Gartner. 
Goldman's EMEA delta-one head Rich Privorotsky opened his overnight "GS Basics – Bonds 
Up/AI Down. Neoclouds. Computer Inflation" note (available to pro subs) with the line that 
should be on every AI bull's monitor this morning: 
"I think the market is discovering that demand for compute can grow exponentially, but the 
capital available to finance it cannot. I've learned to respect the credit market, and the widening 
across the AI vertical is a warning." (emphasis ours) 
Translation: the AI bottleneck is no longer GPUs, power or tokens. It's the balance sheet. 
Below we walk through the CPI trigger, how credit led the selloff, Gartner's math on why the 
neoclouds go first, Goldman's "R**" warning, the contagion from Seoul to the Russell 2000, and 
why Goldman's own desks say the flush hasn't happened yet. 
The Pain Trade, Unwound From Both Ends 
The trigger was Wednesday's hot CPI. Core rose 0.33% against a 0.2% consensus, and 
Goldman's economists (Hatzius, Mericle et al.) promptly lifted their September core PCE 
estimate to 0.32%, or 3.09% year-over-year, conceding that the report "raise[s] the probability of 
an October hike." By the close the market priced 35% odds of an October hike and 87% for 
December, per Goldman's US desk, which also notes that CPI has now been above the Fed's 
target for 67 consecutive months. A rounding error, surely. 
 

 
The S&P fell 1.11% to 7,718.94 and the Nasdaq 100 lost 2.02%, the first -1% SPX / -2% NDX 
day since late July. Bonds, meanwhile, rallied. That shouldn't happen when the Fed is being 
pushed toward hikes... unless the market thinks the hikes will break something. 
 
Privorotsky: 
"In hindsight, the pain trade was long AI/ short bonds. Now we're watching both sides unwind. 
Credit concerns have infiltrated the AI spending equilibrium, and the market increasingly seems 
to be interpreting this as a growth shock." 
The bond market agreed overnight: as we noted in this morning's wrap, the hot CPI scare has 
morphed into a growth scare, with 10Y yields tumbling 10bps to 5.08% and October hike odds 
sliding back to around 30% from a post-CPI intraday peak of 58%.

Credit Moved First 
What every Goldman desk stressed is that credit, not equities, led this selloff. Goldman's global 
neocloud basket is down 20% in five sessions, which Privorotsky calls "a roughly 1st pctl move 
over ten years, with selling accelerating." 
 

 
 
Hyperscaler IG spreads widened "another 7.4bp last week." And then the money line: 
"Perhaps this was always how crowding out would manifest itself… through the marginal 
borrower." 
The marginal borrower here is the neocloud: the leveraged middleman that borrows to buy 
GPUs and leases them to whoever will sign a contract. When debt-financed capacity gets more 
expensive, it's first to feel it. Privorotsky spells out the chain: if the appetite to finance the 
buildout is constrained, "the consequence is incrementally slower chip consumption, less capex 
and ultimately weaker growth."

Gartner Does The Math 
Which brings us to the report that "did not help." In a release published Wednesday, Gartner 
forecast that by 2030 "cost pressure and a lack of a differentiating business model will force 
over 80% of neocloud providers to cease independent operations." As we wrote in "Over 80% 
Of Neoclouds Will Fold" (Oct 14), the number came with some brutal math attached: 
"Through 2030, AI data center owners would need to generate a cumulative $8.5 trillion in 
revenue to cover announced capital spending, financing costs, and a 12% return on invested 
capital... Total enterprise and consumer AI revenue that can be attributed to AI data centers 
during this period is forecast to reach no more than $3.6 trillion." 
— Will Sommer, Sr Director Analyst, Gartner 
That leaves a hole of roughly $4.9 trillion (our arithmetic). Gartner sees more than $3.5 trillion of 
AI infrastructure spending exposed to impairment by end-2030, rising to more than $4 trillion 
through 2032, and predicts that "less than half of AI revenue will be earned by providers that 
own significant AI infrastructure." The companies writing the biggest checks get the smallest 
slice.

None of this is news to readers who have been following along. We ran the neocloud rent math 
(Oct 11) and found spot rents running four times the hurdle; Goldman itself calculated how much 
revenue is needed (Sep 27) to justify the hyperscalers' $1.7 trillion in capex; and we've mapped 
where the credit party ends (Oct 6). Two firms with no reason to coordinate, using 12-15% 
hurdles, reached the same conclusion. Hyperscalers can wait for demand to catch up. A 
company whose bonds yield 16% cannot. 
And that is precisely Privorotsky's point, even in his more constructive passages. Demand isn't 
the problem, he argues: "Aggregate token consumption continues to surprise to the upside." But 
 

 
the equilibrium "increasingly favours those with powered capacity and strong balance sheets 
over the leveraged middlemen." Gartner simply put a body count on it. 
R**: The Rate Level Where AI Breaks 
Why is a hot CPI so toxic for AI debt specifically? Goldman credit strategist Amanda Lynam 
notes that spreads now make up less than 15% of the all-in yield on the USD IG index, versus 
45% in 2019-21, which has "effectively turned segments of the corporate credit market into a 
quasi-rates product." 
 
In other words, the AI debt stack now trades on the Fed's path. 
Goldman's global head of FX vol trading, Praneet Shah, takes it a step further this morning, 
dusting off a concept from 2022: R**, "the level of rates above which financial stability/credit 
concerns start to bite." 
"Given the scale of leverage now embedded in the AI capex ecosystem, we may be 
approaching a level of yields (R**) where that ecosystem becomes materially more 
rate-sensitive than it was even six months ago. Today's bull flattening is consistent with that 
threshold being tested." (emphasis ours) 
Shah sees the stress across three channels at once: credit (wider spreads across the AI stack), 
equities (Nasdaq for AI capex demand, KOSPI for AI capex supply, "selling off together"), and 
rates (a bull flattener "consistent with the market pricing growth/credit concerns rather than 
 

 
persistent inflation"). Even FX is in on it: "The currencies most exposed to AI -- USD KRW TWD 
are all weaker whilst funding currencies EUR CHF JPY all stronger." His conclusion: the 
"equities lower / yields lower" tail "is under-hedged by clients," and his pick is USDJPY 
downside via digital puts. 
Which, for those keeping score, is exactly the shape BofA's derivatives team said the AI unwind 
would take (Oct 15): stocks and yields tumbling. Two banks, one tape.

day. SK Hynix is now 57% and Samsung 40% off their peaks. Goldman's Korea desk reports 
foreigners sold $2,748mn on the day (86% of the last 8 days' selling in Tech) while Korean retail 
bought $1,009mn. Not for the first time, institutions are selling and retail is buying. And yes, we

Back home, the contagion has hit small caps. Goldman futures strategist Robert Quinn notes 
that CDX 5Y HY spreads widened to a 6-month high "with CoreWeave contagion serving as a 
major catalyst," GS's HY Debt Sensitive basket plunged 4.9% and the Russell 2000 lost 1.9%.

Curiously, Russell 2k futures open interest jumped $1.8 billion, the largest one-day move of

was buying too. Quinn: "The identity of the contrarians - and whether they provide ongoing 
support - remains a mystery." We have a guess, and it rhymes with "Korean retail." 
 
 

 
The Complacency Tell 
On the morning of the crash, Goldman's derivatives desk (Adrien Simonet) was pitching long 
vol: the average 1-year implied vol percentile across its AI basket was 6%, with NVDA at the 1st 
percentile and TSM at the 0th, thanks to "vega supply coming from structured retail products." 
By the close the indices had realized more than 2x the implied straddle, vol-of-vol "exploded," 
and dealers are now shorter gamma. 
Retail sold the insurance that institutions are now trying to buy. 
"Not Panicky (Yet)" 
To be fair, Goldman offers some reassurance. Privorotsky thinks "we've overreacted in pockets," 
notes that "discretionary nets are low, and rates are finally rallying," and argues that "if rates 
stabilize lower they improve the equation." The demand data backs him up: TSMC guided Q4 
revenue to $52.3-54.3bn vs a $50.35bn Street, and VAT just posted organic orders up 188% 
with a 1.9x book-to-bill, the highest ever. 
But look at how that argument works. The demand case doesn't depend on rates. The financing 
case does, and Shah thinks rates may already be at the level where the AI ecosystem breaks. 
Meanwhile, TSMC kept 2026 capex unchanged at $60-64bn, which, as Johnstone notes, 
disappointed bulls who wanted a raise. And Goldman's regional desks are telling clients the 
flush hasn't happened: 
"Despite the magnitude of the sell-off this week, our pad did not see concentrated degrossing 
nor capitulation in memory." — GS Korea desk 
"Didn't seem panicky (yet), though overall trend seemed to be more like degrossing, not 
defensive rotation." — GS Japan desk 
"It feels much more like the market is cutting the right tail off of the AI trade as opposed to any 
degree of panic on the downside." — Shayna Peart, GS US derivatives 
Bottom Line 
Privorotsky's last word is a balanced one: 
"There are legitimate financing constraints, but they don't invalidate the underlying demand 
story, and if rates stabilize lower they improve the equation. We should respect what credit is 
telling us, but equally recognize that positioning, valuations and rates are all moving in a more 
supportive direction." 
 

 
Our take is less balanced.  
Tally up what Goldman's own desks reported in 24 hours: neoclouds down 20% in five days (a 
1st-percentile move), hyperscaler spreads wider, CoreWeave bonds at distressed levels and 
CDS pricing better-than-coin-flip default odds, HY at 6-month wides, Asian credit priced off 
"coreweave," a Korean circuit breaker, a Japanese CTA liquidation, record-low vol the morning it 
all started, and no capitulation anywhere. Add Gartner's $4.9 trillion revenue gap and Shah's R** 
warning, and the bounce case boils down to "rates need to fall," in a market where the Fed is 
still pricing an 87% chance of a December hike. 
We've been warning since AI quietly became the largest sector in the IG market and through the 
chip-debt SPV stampede(Oct 8) that the buildout runs on borrowed money and borrowed time. 
Demand for compute may well grow exponentially. 
Goldman has now said in writing that the money to pay for it cannot, and the bond market is the 
one doing the math.
