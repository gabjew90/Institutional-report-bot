US EQUITIES - heightened demand for protection
Friday was the first real sign of panic in index protection, following weeks of significant damage in individual
stocks and client portfolios.
The level of activity in the equity volatility space was nearly unprecedented: VIX futures traded 3.6x the 20-day
average, Cboe index put volume was the highest daily volume on record, while total put volume was the fourth-
highest back to 2015.
VIX call option volume was the second-highest in five years, behind only August 2, 2024, which preceded the
VIX spike above 60 the following Monday. Quite a change in investor behavior, and a clear indication of just how
much concern has built around broader index downside.

Another development worth flagging is the shift in dealer gamma positioning. One estimate suggests dealer
gamma fell by $5.9bn on Friday alone, leaving aggregate positioning close to neutral. While monthly expiration
likely contributed to the move, the reduction in positive dealer gamma is worth paying attention to. We’ve spent
much of this year watching enormous moves across individual stocks and sectors, with the index largely insulated
from the volatility taking place underneath. That dynamic feels more vulnerable, particularly with investors now
aggressively reaching for index protection.
The hedge fund backdrop is just as interesting. Our PB data showed US L/S net leverage fell 3.2pts last week, the
largest weekly decline since Liberation Day and fourth-largest over the past five years. At 43.7%, net leverage
now sits at a five-year low. The performance environment has been extremely challenging, with fundamental L/S
managers losing 1.68% and systematic managers 1.20% between October 9-15, despite a broadly flat global
equity market.

Global Tech saw its largest percentage net selling on record since 2016, with semis accounting for 57% of US
technology net selling. A pretty remarkable unwind taking place across some of the most popular areas of the
market.
What’s notable about the reduction in hedge fund net exposure is that essentially all of it since the end of July has
come from an increase in shorts (not a reduction in longs) – h/t GS’ Vincent Lin for the stats. Short exposure is
now near five-year highs, while long exposure remains in the 53rd/72nd percentiles over the past three/five
years… Said another way, managers have aggressively reduced their directional exposure, but haven’t
meaningfully reduced their long books. If the performance pressure continues, there’s certainly scope for further
de-grossing through long sales and short covering, but it’s also worth recognizing just how much more defensive a
lot of portfolios now look.

Meanwhile, the AI correction has increasingly become a debate around financing the buildout. AI-related HY
spreads widened 25bp by the middle of the week, compared with just 2bp for non-AI issuers. With GS Research
forecasting $720bn in combined project finance and direct hyperscaler issuance in 2027, investors are beginning
to question the cost of capital required to sustain the investment cycle.
Stepping back, I think it’s important to distinguish between a market pricing slower growth and one pricing an
outright deterioration in the earnings outlook. Our GIR team still expects S&P earnings to grow 11% in 2027, or
18% adjusting for distortions. The last two weeks have also seen a meaningful change in the cross-asset backdrop,
with 10yr yields falling more than 20bp even as HY spreads have widened. Investors are clearly becoming more
concerned about growth and the cost of financing, but that doesn’t necessarily change the broader earnings
trajectory.
My takeaway is that the market is moving into a different volatility environment but not necessarily a more
bearish one. The pressure on hedge fund portfolios has been building for weeks, with investors adding shorts and
now increasingly reaching for convexity through options. There is certainly room for further de-grossing,
particularly given the still-elevated long exposure across the hedge fund community. But with net exposure at
five-year lows, shorts near five-year highs and demand for protection accelerating, a considerable amount of
caution is already reflected in positioning.
