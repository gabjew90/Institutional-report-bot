Rates 
Hot inflation - PPI soaring along with CPI, and broadening out - and strong growth/consumer 
(retail sales solid and jobless claims at the best levels in decades)... and so of course, rate-hike 
odds and Treasury yields both fell today! 
“Markets seem to be becoming more comfortable with duration, with concerns over AI from 
overnight helping to push rates lower,” said Molly Brooks, rates strategist at TD Securities. 
We can give STIRs a pass maybe... PPI gave us no new inflation over and above the fact that 
inflation is hot (and spreading) but October's FOMC is very close to the Midterms. 

 
 
On the bond side, it appears fears over the deflationary impact of an AI bubble collapse are 
outweighing any recent growth/inflation/fiscal largesse fears. 
On the day, yields were all down bigly, led by the long-end (30Y -11bps, 2Y -6bps), erasing all of 
yesterday's spike and then some. 30Y yields are down 14bps from before CPI (and yields over 
all are down 20bps from their spike highs).. 
 

 
Here's the chart that really matters... Treasury yields against AI stocks... 
 
Bonds (at least for the last two days) are now ignoring the energy markets, and are focused on 
AI funding fears... an AI debt supply slowdown eases that pressure on the long-end. 
“The risk-off trade and AI concerns are dominating price action,” said Angelo Manolatos, a rates 
strategist at Wells Fargo. 
Bond investors appear to be looking through the economic data at the possibility that declines in 
US equity benchmarks from record highs are a harbinger of economic stress, said Steven Zeng, 
US rates strategist at Deutsche Bank. 
“We got hot inflation data yesterday, strong PPI and retail sales today, but the market is 
reluctant to price in the Fed hiking more quickly,” he said. 
“Growth concern seems to outweigh inflation, especially if bad news around AI snowballs from 
here.” 
While long-term yields reflect waning expectations for growth and inflation, they also have 
retreated over the past week on speculation that US government officials will try to lower them 
further by curtailing supply in that part of the market. 
Energy 
Oil prices soared today... although you wouldn't know it. 

 
Container ships sunk, tankers attacked, and more missiles lobbed between Saudi and Houthis... 
Add to that a big draw in crude stocks (and drop in production - storm-related) and WTI surged 
above $98 (near one-month highs)... 
 
“A combination of production outages in the Gulf of Mexico due to Hurricane Isaias, strong 
crude exports and higher refining activity on the Gulf Coast led to a strong draw in PADD 3, 
which overwhelmingly accounted for the lion’s share of the total inventory draw,” said Matt 
Smith, Americas lead oil analyst at market intelligence firm Kpler. 
On the bright side, diesel prices dipped today... as The 3-2-1 crack, which approximates turning 
crude into gasoline and diesel, fell 20c to +$65.10 

 
 
Brent prices eased a little today as news of a fresh release from emergency reserves helped 
offset increasing supply risks stretching from the Strait of Hormuz to the Black Sea. 
 
WTI and Brent have recently fallen out of lockstep as it has become cheaper to ship American 
barrels, buoying demand. 

 
 
The cost of sending a typical cargo of US crude from the Gulf Coast to Rotterdam has shrunk 
about 12% over the past week, according to data from the Baltic Exchange in London. 
But, as commodity legend, Jeff Currie also noted, Brent has become little more than a "vibe 
contract". 
“Everything associated with energy is going up but Brent,” Currie said Thursday in an interview 
on Bloomberg Television.  
“It is not connected to the real world anymore.”  
Brent’s roughly $106-a-barrel trade belies the scale of supply threats spanning the Strait of 
Hormuz to the Black Sea, according to Currie, founder and chief executive officer of investment 
firm Real Macro. Threats have escalated recently, with tanker attacks mounting around Hormuz 
and Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia intensifying. Freight markets have surged alongside crude. 
As the global backdrop deteriorates, Brent’s disconnect from physical markets is creating 
“unpriceable risk,” Currie said, deterring hedgers from using the contract to protect themselves 
at a juncture when they need it most. 
“Those futures markets are absolutely critical to the function of an industry in modern society,” 
Currie said.  
“You take away that pricing, you don’t get adequate investment, you don’t get adequate 
behavioral changes.” 
The disconnect between paper and physical markets has frustrated traders since the Middle 
East conflict began, when crude struggled to top $100 despite historic supply shortages. In 

 
contrast, Dated Brent, the most important physical oil benchmark, has surged to near $130 a 
barrel. That dissonance has contributed to traders fleeing the market, with Brent open interest 
earlier this month hitting its lowest since March 2025 even as oil dominates headlines. 
A hit to Brent’s integrity undermines the market’s ability to anticipate supply shocks with 
“profound consequences for the market and the rest of the world,” Currie said.  
“Market integrity is sacrosanct,” Currie said. “You don’t mess with it.”

Finally, if you thought today was an opportunity to rotate away from tech into defensives, don't 
forget that consumer staples are now trading at richer fwd multiples than tech... 

 
 
As Bloomberg's Tatiana Darie noted, this is not how the market has traded for much of the past 
decade. The last time that consumer stocks traded at a premium to tech shares was during the 
bear market of 2022. Back then, tech was contending with a profit recession as growth rates 
normalized in the wake of the post-pandemic surge. 
Today, tech commands some of the higher earnings growth rates in the index while staples are 
heavily lagging. Not only is the consumer-exposed sector projected to grow profits by low-single 
digits, but operating margins are also falling. That signals that the market’s defensive tilt is 
overdone and tech valuations already reflect some pretty grim assumptions about the earnings 
outlook. 
Simply put, higher rates collided with an equity market that’s already losing AI leadership as 
questions about financing the data center buildout grow. This earnings season has yet to 
provide a convincing reason for a change in leadership either, with financials drawing a mixed 
reaction over the past two days. 
Nevertheless, we are left feeling very 'Clint Eastwood' about BTFDing on the back of Jensen's 
assurances: "do you feel lucky, punk?" 
Maybe there is NO safe-haven in stocks after all... and that's why bonds are suddenly bid.
