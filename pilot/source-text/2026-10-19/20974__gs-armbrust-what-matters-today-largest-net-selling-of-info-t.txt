1) Largest Net Selling of Info Tech on Record
According to GS PB data, last week Info Tech was by far the most net sold global sector this week as the
sector saw the largest % de-grossing in 2 months and the largest % net selling on our record (since 2016),
driven by long and to a lesser extent short sales (4.7 to 1). Fundamental L/S managers down 2.7% (alpha:
-1.1%) last week, their worst week since July.
The Prime book is now under-weight Info Tech vs. the MSCI World Index by -8.4%, which ranks in the
23rd percentile vs. the past year a
Meanwhile, HFs net bought US Health Care at the fastest pace in 7 months as they unwound risk in the
sector for a 5th straight week, driven by short covers outpacing long buys. The Prime book is now over-
weight Health Care vs. the S&P 500 Index by +10.9%, which ranks in the 30th percentile vs. the past year
and in the 86th percentile vs. the past five years.

2) What to own when slowing growth meets narrow breadth
After months of intensifying pressure from interest rates, concerns about the rising cost of capital have
caused the equity market to downgrade its growth expectations. Exacerbating these rotations, this week
GS Prime Services noted a sharp reduction in hedge fund net leverage alongside a positioning pullback in
AI-related stocks. We expect S&P 500 EPS growth of 29% in 2026 and 18% in 2027. Positive but
decelerating growth has historically been associated with positive but moderating S&P 500 returns.

A decelerating growth backdrop usually supports the outperformance of stocks with secular earnings
growth over those with cyclical growth profiles. Among equity factors, stocks with price momentum,
positive earnings revisions, and low volatility have typically outperformed in decelerating growth
environments.
In contrast with the historical playbook, extremely narrow market breadth today cautions investors to be
wary of Momentum factor exposure. Narrow breadth can resolve with either a "catch down" by recent
outperformers or a "catch up" by recent laggards, but either outcome results in a reversal of Momentum.
The 8% decline of our Momentum factor this week has continued this pattern. Our base-case macro
outlook argues for a further "catch up" among some consumer-facing stocks that have recently lagged.
Investors concerned about further downside to the growth outlook and a continued "catch down" should
focus on stocks in defensive industries with "quality" attributes.
3) Large Drop in S&P Gamma
Dealers moved from nearly +$13bn entering last week to essentially flat/short by Friday. The five-day
decline was the largest in four years (Squeezemetrics), leaving the market more vulnerable to near-term
volatility until systematic overwriters replenish gamma. (TY Cullen Morgan)

Tail Hedges: 1.85m VIX calls exchanged hands on Friday, one of the highest call volume sessions since
2018 and on record.

Skew: SPX 1-Month Put-Call Skew had its largest one-day move higher since Feb of 2024.
