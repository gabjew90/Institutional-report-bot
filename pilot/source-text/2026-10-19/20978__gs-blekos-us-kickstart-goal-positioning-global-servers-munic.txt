US WEEKLY KICKSTART - What to own when slowing growth meets narrow breadth
• After months of intensifying pressure from interest rates, concerns about the rising cost of capital have
caused the equity market to downgrade its growth expectations. A sharp reduction in investor positioning
has amplified equity market moves.
• Our baseline forecasts embed slowing — but still solid — growth in 2027. Adjusting for accounting
distortions, we expect S&P 500 EPS growth of 29% in 2026 and 18% in 2027.
• We recommend consumer-facing laggards for a "catch-up" and highlight a screen of defensive stocks
with quality attributes for investors concerned about a further "catch-down." Our base case macro outlook
points to real income growth acceleration in 2027, particularly for the lowest income consumers
GOAL POSITIONING - Cross-asset positioning turning more defensive
• Our sentiment and positioning indicator turned more negative and fell to the 46th-ile, reaching its lowest
level since April, decoupled from continued strong Global PMIs.
• Stronger fixed income fund flows (Exhibit 12), the sharp decline below zero in our Risk Appetite
Indicator, and increased positioning in traditional safe havens (Gold, JPY) have been the largest drags.

Munich Re (MUVGn.DE): 3Q'26 earnings preview
• Munich Re reports its 3Q'26 results on November 12. Overall, our Net Result estimate is broadly in-line
(-1%) with company collected consensus but with a slightly different mix as a c.3% lower technical result
is broadly offset by a higher investment result.
• Our 79.2% P&C Re combined ratio estimate is c.1.8ppts less favourable than consensus primarily driven
by a higher nat cat estimate.

• We expect the normalised COR development to be in focus as our 82.5% COR is broadly in-line with
consensus (82.4%) and consistent with management commentary suggesting further upward pressure on
the c.82% reported at 2Q'26.
Swiss Re (SRENH.S): 3Q'26 earnings preview
• Swiss Re reports its 3Q'26 earnings on November 5th. Overall, our net income estimate is 18% below
company-collected consensus primarily driven by: 1) a higher nat cat estimate, and 2) an assumption that
management will continue to build resilience (through reserve additions and realised investment losses) as
the 2026 earnings trajectory remains on course to exceed its $4.5bn target.
SES SA (SESFd.PA) - Asymmetric upside potential from C-band and structural defence tailwinds;
reinstate at Buy (€7.0 PT)
• We reinstate our rating on SES at Buy with a €7.0 price target. Shares have doubled then halved within a
year. Following disappointment around C-band timing and weak 2Q results, we see an attractive entry
point, with significant asymmetric upside from incoming C-band cash and structurally higher space-
defence spend.
• Our PT conservatively discounts both opportunities but still offers 79% upside, while a blue-sky scenario
where concerns around the scale and timing of benefits ease implies >180% upside.
Europe Aerospace & Defense: What’s driving the defence sector rebound and where do we see value?
• Within our coverage, we see Buy-rated Rheinmetall (on CL) as the most compelling opportunity to
capture structurally higher European defence spending, given the dislocation between its valuation and the
growth opportunity ahead.
• Its significant exposure to Germany, where comparatively greater fiscal headroom supports spending
commitments, reinforces this view. While order timing, execution and cash conversion remain central to
the near-term debate, we believe the current valuation gives insufficient credit to the underlying demand
trajectory. See our Q3 preview for our expectations into the results and the key catalysts and risks we are
monitoring.
