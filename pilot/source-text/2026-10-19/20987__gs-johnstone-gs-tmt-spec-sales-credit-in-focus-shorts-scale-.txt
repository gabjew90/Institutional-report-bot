US equities had a difficult week as investors absorbed a combination of hotter inflation, higher oil prices
and mounting concerns around the AI trade. Investor incoming suggests a lot of pain at the moment and
increasing pessimism last week hedges not working in the TMT space (and sound like in Industrial and
consumer). Semis fell for an eighth consecutive session, memory weakened sharply, and crowded
momentum trades continued to unwind.(Global Tech saw its largest percentage net selling on record since
2016, with semis accounting for 57% of US technology net selling. What’s notable about the reduction in
hedge fund net exposure is that essentially all of it since the end of July has come from an increase in
shorts (not a reduction in longs). Hedge fund positioning reflected the risk-off move, with fundamental
long/short managers down 2.7% for the week and global books seeing their largest net selling since the
end of July, led by record selling in Information Technology. Unlike previous pullbacks that were viewed
largely as technical, the debate has broadened to rates, AI capex returns, China competition and whether
the market has overestimated the durability of AI infrastructure bottlenecks and less about earnings at Q3.

Over the weekend and this morning
• Nvidia is pushing Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron to use TSMC’s 3nm process for NVHBM base dies, a
meaningful supply-chain shift that embeds TSMC more deeply in the HBM stack, reduces differentiation
between memory vendors on the logic die and gives Nvidia greater sourcing leverage. The near-term P&L
impact on the memory names should be limited, as they retain turnkey production for standard HBM, but
strategically the move is positive for TSMC and shifts more control over the HBM architecture toward
Nvidia.
• European satellites: GIR are reinstating coverage with a Buy on SES (€7.0 PT) and Neutral on Eutelsat
(€1.6 PT). For SES, GIR see an attractive entry point after recent weakness, with meaningful upside from
incoming C-band cash and structurally higher space-defence spending, offering 79% upside to our base-
case PT and >180% in a blue-sky scenario. For Eutelsat, while defence spending provides longer-term
upside, near-term execution risk, a highly capital-intensive model, intensifying LEO competition and
limited cash generation until post-2030 constrain the rerating potential, leaving us Neutral despite the
broader defence tailwind.
• Accenture looks to sell Chinese operations for up to $1.5 billion, FT reports, but a sale has not been
confirmed. The potential process could attract private equity groups and strategic buyers, while Accenture
may retain a significant minority stake. Accenture’s Greater China operations include mainland China,
Hong Kong and Taiwan, and the company forecasts 3% to 6% revenue growth for financial year 2027.
• Interesting thought: most of the actual contractual commitments by OpenAI to various hyperscalers and
neoclouds are now well below the current spot price for compute; this is why Sarah Friar (OpenAI CFO)
noted that she could sell all of their contracted compute commitments up 3-5x from their contract value; in
a world where OpenAI disappeared, their commitments would be quickly assumed at higher prices by a
variety of players; I think the hyperscalers deeply regret every contract they signed in 2024, 2025 and
perhaps even the first half of this year. As these contracts roll-off and their compute is repriced to market,
their revenue growth should steadily accelerate which will help with the funding concerns for AI
infrastructure. Result is that I think we may be approaching the peak of external financing as a % of the AI
infrastructure buildout; Gavin Baker on X. A view echoed on the AllIn podcast over the weekend

TELCOS

• US operators: Verizon and AT&T are expected to report improving postpaid trends, with Verizon
returning to positive account growth and AT&T maintaining momentum from its fibre-led convergence
strategy. Verizon is forecast to deliver Q3 revenue of $35.0bn and EBITDA of $13.8bn, supported by
lower churn, cost savings and reduced upgrades, while 2026 free cash flow should rise 10.5% to $22.2bn.
AT&T is expected to report revenue of $31.8bn and EBITDA of $12.5bn, with 416k postpaid phone
additions and strong fibre growth supporting 5.4% Advanced Connectivity revenue growth. The key
debate for both operators will be pricing, convergence economics and the competitive impact of Starlink’s
spectrum acquisition. See Michael preview
• Nordic telecoms lost some of their shine last quarter after another Telenor guidance cut, a disappointing
Elisa update and softer Swedish growth from Tele2. Into Q3, Tele2 offers the most interesting setup, while
investor scepticism remains highest around Telenor and Elisa.
• Tele2: Comparatives are tougher, but competition has not materially deteriorated since Q2. Sweden
remains competitive in no-frills and fixed, while B2B is performing well. Cost control, lower opex and
capex near the bottom of the 10–11% range are supportive. Management’s tone appears deliberately
conservative, possibly leaving room to increase ownership.
• Telia: Swedish trends remain solid and broadly in line with Q2, with competition easing slightly at the
high end. The Halebop switch-off has not disrupted the market, pricing should offset cost inflation, and Q3
appears on track. Investor feedback is mixed, but the overall message is broadly unchanged.
• Elisa: Q3 appears broadly in line with Q2, and management still expects growth to reaccelerate in H2,
particularly Q4. The CEO was constructive on a return to higher-quality growth from 2027, supported by
improving competition, macro recovery, Finnish data centres and AI-led savings. However, International
Software Services remains exposed to geopolitical and project-delay risk, and the shares remain a
consensus short.
• Telenor: (reports week after next) The outlook remains challenging, particularly in Norway, where
competition from Telia appears to be biting. Higher energy costs in Norway and Finland and continued
weakness in Bangladesh add risk. Execution has been poor, short interest has risen and the stock
increasingly looks like a consensus short.

• Blueprints AI, founded by architect Al Dram and data scientist Coco Cao, uses AI to turn a project
description, 2D sketch or PDF into code-compliant, permit-ready construction blueprints in minutes.
Trained on eight years of approved drawings from Dram’s architecture and construction firm, its upgraded
software can produce and coordinate architectural, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing and interior
plans, automatically updating the entire project when a design changes and checking it against local
building codes. The subscription service costs $3,200 to $8,500 per month and targets design firms,
developers and property owners, but it is intended to automate repetitive drafting rather than replace
architects, as licensed professionals must still review and approve the final documents. techcrunch
TELCOS
• VMO2: Virgin Media O2 is considering reviving the defunct Virgin Mobile and O2 Broadband brands as
a dedicated low-cost tier to reduce churn and rebuild investor confidence (ISPReview). The move comes
as the group carries around £22bn of debt, faces pressure to deliver roughly £600m of cost savings through
potential job cuts and lower capex, and navigates possible CMA opposition to its Netomnia merger under
Consumer CEO Lyssa McGowan; investors are also asking whether Liberty Global and Telefónica could
pursue a liability-management exercise, using flexibility in the existing financing documents to
restructure, modify or reduce the debt outside of court.
• CityFibre: CityFibre has asked its banks to write down a significant portion of its £2bn loan in exchange
for equity and allow interest on the remaining debt to be capitalised rather than paid in cash (Bloomberg).
Lender approval is a precondition for existing shareholders to inject £900m of fresh equity through a
shareholder loan, creating a more sustainable capital structure that could support consolidation of UK
alternative networks, with Netomnia the primary target; the restructuring follows a widening pre-tax loss
of £545m, including nearly £400m of annual finance costs.
• TIM: TIM has appointed Mirko Mischiatti as Chief Enterprise and Innovative Solutions Officer and
Claudio Ongaro as Chief Technology Officer under a new organisational structure effective 19 October
2026 (press release). The changes follow the planned departures of Elio Schiavo and Leonardo de
Carvalho Capdeville at year-end, while CEO Pietro Labriola will take interim responsibility for the
Strategy, Business Development and Wholebuy function previously led by Ongaro; none of the incoming
or outgoing executives currently holds shares in TIM.
