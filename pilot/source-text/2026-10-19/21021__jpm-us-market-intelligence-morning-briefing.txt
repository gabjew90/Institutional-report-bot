JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Last week, SPX fell 2.0% and NDX dropped 5.5% as an AI-led sell-off spread across 
equities and bonds. Beneath the headline declines, the move looked more like rotation than 
broad-based liquidation: in SPX 7 of the 11 sectors advanced, led by Staples and Energy 
(+5.0% each) and Health Care (+3.4%), while 4 declined. The selling began Monday after 
China's ChangXin Memory said it would launch a denser server memory chip. Incremental 
headlines throughout the week (including concerns around the IPO markets and selloff in 
Asian markets) pressured AI trades, despite strong earnings from ASML and TSMC. Initial 
bank earnings triggered sharp selling, but the group recovered from its lows over the next 
two sessions, leaving XLF up about 1.4% on the week.  

  
In rates, despite hawkish CPI and PPI readings that should not be dismissed, Treasuries 
rallied and the curve bull-flattened, with 2-, 5-, 10- and 30-year yields down 11bp, 17bp, 
19bp and 20bp, respectively. That move was consistent with a flight to quality as concerns 
over AI equity valuations spilled into bonds. The 10Y posted its biggest weekly decline since 
August 2025. In commodities, oil was modestly higher on the week.  
 
 
  
US MKT INTEL VIEW 
•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – Last week's sell-off ran against our tactical bullish call, so we 
did a reassessment on our view and risk factors. In our Oct 12 Morning Briefing, we flagged 
below as key risk factors “(i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI 
Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer 
Data”. Of the risks we flagged, the Momentum / Tech Unwind and AI Credit Fears were the 
main drivers for last week’s selloff, while the fundamental picture remained encouraging. 
ASML and TSMC delivered healthy earnings and consumer data stayed resilient. We 
therefore retain our tactical bullish stance. The underlying support from earnings and 
macro resilience remains intact, and the AI sell-off has not come with evidence of 
deteriorating earnings in the results reported so far. That said, we don't expect the 
rotation out of Tech to end abruptly, though its pace could moderate. We favor 
expressing our constructive growth and earnings view through Cyclical exposure outside 
Tech, while selectively buying Tech weakness, particularly in Semis and Memory, 
potentially through defined-risk options structures. Large-cap Banks are another area of 

interest. Earnings were generally positive, and the recent sharp pullback could offer an 
opportunity to participate in a recovery in XLF. Heading into a much busier reporting week, 
broadening and AI concerns remain the two key themes for investors, and all eyes are on 
META earnings and NVDA's GTC Berlin. 
•         RISKS (Unchanged WoW) – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; 
(iii) AI Credit Fears / Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker 
Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – Following last week's sell-off, we revisit our three key catalysts: (i) 
Macro / Fed: We think the inflation component of this week’s PMIs will be critical. On Oct 
28 Fed (note that the Fed has entered its blackout period), a hold could provide relief by 
removing residual near-term hike risk, but the bigger focus will be any remarks on the FY27 
policy path. (ii) Rates Stabilization: Lower Treasury yields have already delivered part of 
our anticipated catalyst. From here, we would look for sustained moderation in rates vol and 
no renewed yield spike to support the broader rotation. (iii) Tech Earnings / AI 
Monetization: META's results and NVDA's GTC Berlin will be important tests of revenue 
delivery, spending plans and demand visibility. Beyond the hyperscalers, evidence that AI 
investment is broadening and generating measurable revenue across other sectors would 
strengthen the case for a more durable AI trade. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 19) – Following last week's sell-off, we favor a more 
selective approach to expressing our bullish view. We retain a preference for Tech and for 
exposure to a potential rebound through both pairs trades and derivatives. Within Cyclicals, 
we expect the rotation to continue. We remain constructive on Financials and Retailers, 
given consumer resilience and their relatively limited direct exposure to AI-related concerns. 
Internationally, we continue to favor Brazil and Japan. We are also monitoring Reopening 
opportunities that could benefit from lower energy prices and rotation away from AI. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with 
a preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 
Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your 
shopping list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and 
Retailers given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy 

prices. Other elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower energy 
prices. Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and Japan. 
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long 
but would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / 
yields, which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, there 
is likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the Tech tape. 
Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus bullish play. The 
AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we like Tech in the 
near-term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we think you need to 
see the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; excluding AI-related 
plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper yield curve, and 
favorable capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real Estate, Telecom, and 
Utilities may participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most likely to lag the SPX, 
ex-AI plays. The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which would squeeze EU and 
RTY higher; plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, Cruise Lines, 
Discretionary, Hotels, etc) is another area of interest with a preference to play via options.  
  
ADDITIONAL THOUGHTS 
•         AI CREDIT MARKET CONCERNS – Rising AI financing costs and credit-market 
concerns are a key risk to our constructive equity view. JPM Strategist Nathaniel 
Rosenbaum (here) highlights that hyperscalers widened 12bp last week to 148bp (widest 
since July 2026); JULI sector-weighted dispersion rose to 21.2bp (highest since May 2025). 
He reads this as investors demanding more compensation for lower-rated and capital-
intensive issuers rather than indiscriminately cutting HG exposure. He told us that ratings 
have not deteriorated. Hyperscaler debt is up $117bn YTD, yet average ratings improved 
modestly as issuance was concentrated among higher-rated borrowers. Unless upcoming 
earnings reveal fundamental weakness, he sees the widening as overdone (here). 

 
o   COMMENTS FROM CREDIT TRADING DESK (CHRISTIAN CROSBY) – 
“Why is the market only waking up to funding costs now? It did sound a 
bit comical to keep blaming borrowing costs in my conversations last week 
after months of upward pressure on USTs which have marginally subsided 
over the last two weeks. However, the problem now is that not only are base 
rates high, but the investible AI data set has gotten so large that any and all 
new issues have to come at wide levels. The concern here was not dissimilar 
from what we saw in July, although much of that was led by Hyperscale 
widening as IG investors worried about new issue damage and an inability to 
cut $300bn+ new issue checks in 2026 and 2027 rather than fundamental 
concerns about the hypers. The difference here is that the funding costs are 
now more about levered borrowers, specifically neoclouds, data center 
project finance, and GPU deals – all of which have an increasing skew to 
much more levered credits and offtakers. This has a compounding and 
reflexive impact – as levered credits which lack the operating cash flow 
profiles that hypers can fall back on, every point higher in borrowing costs is 

worth multiple points on the equity, and in turn weaker equities reduce the 
ability to fall back on convertible and straight equity markets for growth 
capital. We’ve already seen a growing list of pulled/delayed IPOs in the last 
few months despite record highs in US equity indices, and the idea that 
secondary rounds and converts might dry up is even more alarming for 
neocloud borrowers who have plenty of needs to fill. 
•         RATES – Jay Barry (here) points to three possible drivers for last week’s move in UST: 
(i) concerns on AI equity valuations, (ii) cleaner positioning (Treasury Client Survey longs 
fell to their lowest since August), (iii) the market may view that Treasury is “leaving the door 
open” for future long-end issuance cuts after Treasury's dealer questionnaire. He 
recommends tactical 10s/30s steepeners as a low-beta way to get long the front end. 
•         BROADENING – Last week’s tech underperformance reinforced the rotation theme, 
although the underlying drivers warrant a closer look: while an unwind of +QQQ/-IWM 
positions may benefit RTY, elevated yields remain a headwind. We therefore favor SPW 
over RTY as an expression of broadening, supported by stronger aggregate earnings power 
and less exposure to refinancing pressure. 
o   ONE CONSIDERATION FOR HEDGING – The Dispersion Index has 
increased from ~30 at its September low to ~37, though it remains below its 
July peak. If dispersion continues to rise and sector performance is 
increasingly driven by the largest constituents rather than the average stock, 
passive sector ETFs may become less effective hedges for single-stock 
exposure. This could favor D1 baskets, which can more closely match the 
underlying exposures and reduce the basis risk created by increasingly 
concentrated sector performance.

POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE – Weekly Wrap | Echoes of 2023 (and 2018)... but 
Aggregate YTD Returns Still Quite Positive Despite MTD Drawdown 
  

Summary: There’s been a lot to digest this week with Tech selling off, yields lower despite 
the hot inflation prints, and overall positioning still around neutral in the US.  Overall the 
moves we’ve seen MTD are very much in line with the rotation potential that has 
lingered since the large build up of momentum / AI longs vs. rate sensitive / consumer 
shorts.  In many ways the recent moves look quite similar to this same time in 2023 
(which saw a dip, followed by a rally into YE).  We still think an eventual stabilization and 4Q 
rally are more likely than not, due to a much stronger performance cushion than another 
similar year (2018), though there are certainly multiple risks that could change the outcome. 
  
Key Points: 
  
1. What happened this week? Poor performance, de-grossing, and rotations: HF 
performance drawdown accelerated to -2.1% MTD globally (from -0.8% last week).  
Eq L/S -2.3% MTD, Quants -1% to -2%, Multi-Strats -2%. APAC lagged the most 
(down to -3.8% MTD) with alpha deteriorating (APAC L/S spread +0.2% → -1.3% 
MTD), and both EMEA and US L/S spreads also worsened.  Multi-Strat de-
grossing was close to the worst we’ve seen in the past 2 years, though net 
flows weren’t that negative and US positioning was still around 40th %-tile (though 
11th and 8th for Europe and China/HK). 
  
2. Why did this happen even though positioning was low/neutral in aggregate? 
Crowded skews + high gross: Even with neutral aggregate net positioning (US 
TPM ~40th %-tile, 4wk +0.5z), the backdrop was fragile: gross still high LT, 3m 
combined flows into Tech had been near multi-year highs, and NDX long vs RTY 
short futures skews were extreme again. Even with the recent selling of Semis/AI, 
some extremes are still present: AI Core Revenue positioning remains 94th %-tile 
(12m) / 99th (LT), Semis vs Software skew persists (Semis L/S still ~3.0x vs 
Software L/S ~1x), and Utilities remains heavily under-owned (HF positioning near 
10yr lows). 

  
3. Echoes of 2023 (more than 2018) — rate/oil/quality setup, but YTD cushion 
argues for resilience: Macro and factor returns are similar to 3Q23 (US 10yr 
+82bps in 3Q26 vs +73bps in 3Q23, Oil >+40% vs ~+30%, Quality outperforming 
Beta, Tech > Small Caps), while positioning is still light/neutral in the US but more 
bearish in Europe and China/HK. A key similarity to 2023 (and different from 2018) 
is that there’s a stronger YTD performance cushion: HFs are still about +6% YTD 
(through Oct 15 est.), and equities are up ~+13% MXWD / +14% SPX YTD—
historically a setup that more often supports positive 4Q outcomes unless 
fundamentals (earnings/data) deteriorate.
