narratives hoping to 'look through' any energy-related moves as the rise in prices was 
broad-based. October odds spiked above 50% (and Dec odds above 95%) before pulling back a

equity weakness (but still prices in two more hikes next year)...

CPI print, led by the short-end, but as stocks weakness accelerated a bid returned, easing some 
of that pain... 
 
 

 
“The Fed has its work cut out for them — and one hike, while giving the Fed temporary bona 
fides as inflation fighters, will not be enough to satiate this unruly bond market,” said John 
Kerschner, a portfolio manager at Janus Henderson.

Bloomberg macro strategist, Michael Ball, noted that SPX's recent unusually calm trading 
regime is at risk of breakingafter today's decline moves the index closer to the bottom of a range 
where it’s supported by a positive-gamma cushion.  
Heavier positive dealer gamma holdings have created a self-reinforcing, low-vol feedback loop 
in recent weeks. Delta-hedging flows are dampening moves at the index level and pulling the 
SPX back toward large option strikes, which suppresses volatility further. That mechanical 
support helps explain why the index has remained so resilient, despite an increasingly chaotic 
macro backdrop. 
The S&P 500 has now moved through its first meaningful pressure points, between 
7,775–7,750. SpotGamma’s model puts the gauge’s volatility trigger at 7,775, where dealer 
hedging behavior and market volatility dynamics begin to shift. The index also sits just below 
7,750, where substantial positive gamma offers support. 
 

 
 
A sustained break below that area would leave the SPX in an are with weaker dealer support 
and accelerate the move lower, with 7,700 (7690) acting as the key level before positive gamma 
thins sharply, breaking down to its 50DMA...

Energy 
Adding to the pain today was yet another rise in crude oil prices with WTI back above $96 - its 
highest in three weeks... 
 

 
 
The rise in crude came as a cargo ship sank in the Strait of Hormuz after a strike that sparked a 
fire and the evacuation of crew members. Another freighter was struck Wednesday, suffering 
hull damage, according to the statement. 
“Developments over the past week are yet another reminder that recovering flows are not the 
same as secure flows,” said Emily Ashford, head of energy research at Standard Chartered 
Bank. 
“With both infrastructure and shipping increasingly vulnerable, the risk premium is proving 
difficult to unwind.” 
Meanwhile, the market for real-world barrels remains tight. With freight costs soaring, the Dated 
Brent benchmark in Europe was near $130 a barrel on Tuesday as European refiners compete 
for local supplies. Shipping oil on the benchmark Middle East-to-China route is holding close to 
its highest level ever, with prices near $1.39 million a day. 
As the Midterms loom, Iranian attacks on shipping through the vital waterway have surged in 
recent weeks and that is showing up not just in futures curves (sustained problems) but also in 
prompt markets as the WTI Cash Roll's surge scream physical tightness... 
 

 
 
Beyond the Middle East, Russia and Ukraine traded tit-for-tat strikes on each other’s energy 
infrastructure over the past day. 
Prices for European diesel, of which Russia has historically been a major supplier, have already 
more than doubled this year and continues to show no signs of blinking at Trump's so-called 
'deal' with Putin (US diesel also soared... historically responsible in large part for today's hot CPI 
print so this does not bode well for the next CPI either)... 
 

 
 
And US retail diesel prices remain very near record highs... 
 
And, circling back to where we started, obviously higher oil (diesel) prices did not help the 
situation in the short-end of the curve, just adding to inflation anxiety. 
As the Russia-Ukraine war continues to hobble refinery capacity, the International Energy 
Agency warned that diesel and other fuels will remain under pressure next year as a result of 
 

 
conflicts, despite a weaker outlook for oil consumption. The agency has urged its members to 
complete pledges to release emergency stockpiles. 
And finally, that brings us to note that all of this pain has pushed US financial conditions notably

wrong way)... 
 
Yields have been the big driver of that tightening, but even if we see that reverse a little, any 
reversal will likely be driven by further pain in equity and credit risk premia...
