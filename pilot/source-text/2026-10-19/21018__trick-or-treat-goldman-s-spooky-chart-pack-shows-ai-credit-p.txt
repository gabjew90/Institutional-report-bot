 
"Trick Or Treat": Goldman's Spooky Chart Pack 
Shows AI Credit Pricing In A 15% Drop For 
Hyperscaler Stocks 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SUNDAY, OCT 18, 2026 - 01:15 AM 
Halloween came early on Goldman's custom baskets desk. 
After a week in which CoreWeave imploded as stocks finally noticed what credit had been 
screaming (Oct 14) and the S&P posted its first "-1% SPX / -2% NDX" day since July, 
Goldman's Louis Miller sent clients his Friday evening "Global Reflections" note (available to pro 
subs) with a chart pack he describes as "in the spirit of mid October". The opening line does 
most of the work: 
"AI Credit Spreads (GSUCHS30) and hyperscalers (GSXUHYPR) specifically are at the widest 
levels of the year. The last time spreads were nearly this high, the underlying equities were 15% 
lower." (emphasis ours) 
Translation: either the hyperscaler bond market is wrong, or hyperscaler stocks have another 
15% to fall. 
Below we walk through Goldman's scariest charts, from credit to capex to market internals, and 
then the five trades Miller thinks still work in this market (spoiler: most of them don't involve 
buying AI). 
Credit Is Ahead Of Equities, Again 
Start with the chart that sets the tone for the whole pack. Goldman's AI hyperscaler credit 
spread index (inverted, in blue) has blown out to its widest of the year, while the hyperscaler 
equity basket (black) has only just begun to roll over. The last time credit got this wide, in July, 
the stocks were roughly 15% lower than they are now. 

 
 
Regular readers know which side of that gap we have been on. Hyperscaler CDS just hit a fresh 
record, as we noted in our weekly wrap (Oct 16), and the damage further down the capital 
structure is worse: seven of the 20 junk-rated AI data center bonds (Oct 15) depend on a single 
tenant, CoreWeave, whose own bonds are trading at distressed levels. Or, as we put it on 
Friday: 
There are distressed junk bonds (some $14BN) backing data centers where the only tenant is 
CoreWeave which is itself funded by distressed junk bonds.​

Meanwhile, Goldman's broad AI beneficiaries basket is now down about 25% from its June high 
relative to the S&P ex-AI. 

 
 
And Miller notes that, with the exception of AI productivity names (which are insulated from 
tech), no corner of the AI complex was spared this week. Goldman's AI theme dashboard shows 
NeoClouds down 24.8% over five days, Optical Networks down 21.4% and Memory down 
16.6%. The only theme with a meaningful gain? AI-at-Risk, up 3.5%. Diversification at its finest. 
 

 
"AI Capex Has Likely Peaked" 
Why is this happening now? Miller's answer is that the debate has moved on from "how much 
will they spend" to "how will they pay for it": 
"The widening across the complex has forced investors to confront what happens as AI 
spending growth slows. AI Capex has likely peaked and financing, sustainability, and returns on 
capital have become the debate rather than the next near term earnings revision." (emphasis 
ours) 
The math is fairly brutal. Hyperscaler capex growth peaked at 116% y/y in Q3 2026, according 
to Goldman Investment Research, and consensus has it slowing every quarter through the end 
of 2027, to roughly 20%. 
 
As spending growth slows, so does its contribution to S&P 500 earnings. Goldman estimates 
that hyperscaler capex (net of depreciation) added about 11 percentage points to S&P EPS 
growth in 2026. That falls to 7pp in 2027 and turns negative, at -1pp, in 2028, when depreciation 
from all those data centers starts to bite. 

 
 
In other words, the same spending that juiced earnings on the way up becomes a drag once it 
slows, which is all you need to know about how much of the "AI earnings boom" was AI, and 
how much was simply capex. 
What The Internals Are Saying 
Miller's spooky charts don't stop at AI. 
The broader market internals are flashing the same message: 
●​ Fear is back. Friday saw "the second highest amount of puts traded (right behind June of 
this year)." 
●​ High beta is breaking. Goldman's sector-, factor- and industry-neutral Beta pair 
(GSP1BETA) is down 15% from its recent high, and High Beta Cyclicals have broken out 
of their uptrend channel. 

 
 
●​ Growth fears are overshooting. Cyclicals vs Defensives ex-commodities (GSP1CYDE) is 
now "implying only 2% growth, falling from highs of 6%", which is more pessimistic than 
the Atlanta Fed's GDPNow (around 3.6%). 
 
●​ Hedge funds are long more beta than leverage suggests. Beta-adjusted nets sit at the 
67th percentile over five years, while net leverage is at the 17th percentile. 

 
 
The one bright spot here, at least according to Miller, is valuation: "we wouldn't confuse 
skepticism and uncertainty with overvaluation. The NDX still trades at just 21x earnings." But he 
also warns that financial conditions "shifted from being a positive driver of growth to becoming a 
negative one". Higher rates and wider spreads are now hitting debt-dependent stocks directly.

Five Trades For A Scary Market 
Despite all that, Miller is not calling for a crash. "Despite rising correlations and increasingly 
crowded positioning, the market continues to absorb the stress through rotation rather than 
index-level weakness," he writes. His five trades: 
1. Buy the broadening. The S&P 500 ex-AI (SPXXAI) has outperformed the S&P by 586bps 
since October 6, and the correlation between AI and the rest of the market has turned negative. 

 
 
Miller points to concerns about AI debt financing, and "China's AI capacity ramp challenges the 
US AI bottleneck thesis". Nominal growth and employment are holding up, he says, and the 
rates headwind has already compressed valuations, which "support[s] the case for continued 
rotation rather than a broad equity meltdown." His cheap way to play it is a hybrid: 18Dec26 
SPXXAI >105%, 10y SOFR < ATMF CMS-0.30% @ 9.5% (vs 28%/27% for the individual legs). 
In Europe, the desk's ex-Secular Themes basket (GSXESXXX) has rallied ~4.5% since October 
1 versus a flat STOXX 600. 
2. Quality, with a caveat. Goldman's Quality pair (GSP1QUAL) is up 20% in three months to a 
12-month high on ~13% realized vol, driven mostly by a selloff in the short leg as "higher rates 
have hurt long duration, highly levered, and unprofitable companies". Miller still likes it, but 
warns that "stretched technicals (RSI >80) imply scope for a sharp reversal, were inflation/rates 
concerns to ease." European Quality (GSPEQUAL) is catching up and is up 7.5% in a month. 

 
 
3. A midterm healthcare trade. Defensive buying lifted healthcare this week, and Miller's 
preferred play is long ACA-Exposed Beneficiaries (GSHLCACA), on the bet that Democrats 
revive the enhanced ACA premium tax credits that expired at the end of 2025. That would return 
patient volumes to providers currently drowning in bad debt and "self pay" patients. 
 

 
4. Brazil: the uncorrelated trade. Valuations are still around mid-cycle, foreign participation is 
below prior election-cycle peaks and positioning in rate-sensitive domestic stocks (GSBZRATE) 
is light. Miller sees upside into October 25 (Brazil's runoff), with room for P/E multiples to re-rate 
"from mid teens to high teens." 
 
5. Hedge Taiwan. This is where the AI fatigue really shows: 
"TSMC's -2.3% move on a FY guidance raise and accelerated capacity buildout tells you all you 
need to know about the current appetite to buy AI on a robust demand narrative." (emphasis 
ours) 
Hedge fund positioning in Taiwan is at record levels just as earnings "are failing to impress 
extremely elevated expectations", so Goldman's Asia desk is pushing shorts on Taiwan Semis 
(GSXATWSM) and Taiwan Tech ex-TSMC (GSXATTXT). Recall that foreigners just pulled a 
record ~$9.3 billion from Taiwanese stocks in a single day, as we noted in our weekly wrap (Oct 
16). The crowd may already be heading for the exit. 

 
 
Bottom Line 
Miller's conclusion is that "the broadening trade still has room to run" and that, as growth gets 
questioned, "the quality of growth that you are receiving increasingly matters." 
We agree with the second half. On the first, every one of his trades is effectively a bet that the 
AI damage stays contained to AI. That's a reasonable bet as long as credit stabilizes. But 
Goldman's own chart says credit is pricing a hyperscaler move that equities haven't made yet, 
and the capex math says the earnings tailwind turns into a headwind by 2028. When the bond 
market and the stock market disagree about the same companies, it is usually not the bond 
market that blinks.
