Heightened demand for protection .. Last Friday was the first real sign of panic in index protection,
following weeks of significant damage in individual stocks and client portfolios. The level of activity in
the equity volatility space was nearly unprecedented: VIX futures traded 3.6x the 20-day average, Cboe
index put volume was the highest daily volume on record, while total put volume was the fourth-highest
back to 2015. VIX call option volume was the second-highest in five years, behind only August 2, 2024,
which preceded the VIX spike above 60 the following Monday. Quite a change in investor behavior, and a
clear indication of just how much concern has built around broader index downside. (Ty Coppersmith Lee
..)

Global equities were net sold (-2.0 SDs 1-year) as gross trading activity fell at the fastest pace in nearly
three months, driven by long sales outpacing short sales (2.6 to 1). All major regions were net sold, led in
$ terms by EM Asia and North America.
… Said another way, managers have aggressively reduced their directional exposure, but haven’t
meaningfully reduced their long books. If the performance pressure continues, there’s certainly scope for
further de-grossing through long sales and short covering, but it’s also worth recognizing just how much
more defensive a lot of portfolios now look.

ETF side… In the midst of all these headwinds.. we are seeing inflows into levered ETFs
Nearly $10 billion poured into levered ETFs last week, which is the largest weekly inflow in our dataset.
The largest driver of inflows was SOXL (+3x semis), which gathered +$7 billion (a record), contributing
74% of weekly net inflows. TQQQ (+3x NDX) was the second-largest contributor, adding +$1.3 billion.

The strength over the past week pushed cumulative YTD flows for levered ETFs into near net-positive
territory. Put differently, the number of “invested units” (i.e., flows) has significantly increased... we are
watching this dynamic because if market conditions do improve, this net increase can act as a multiplier
on gross levered ETF exposure. Levered ETFs currently represent $152 billion in AUM and $386 billion
in gross exposure, the second-lowest mark since July.
Looking forward..

Our Chief US Strategist Ben Snider writes on what to own when slowing growth meets narrow breadth.
Our baseline forecasts embed slowing — but still solid — growth in 2027. Adjusting for accounting
distortions, we expect S&P 500 EPS growth of 29% in 2026 and 18% in 2027. Equity prices should
continue to rise as long as earnings grow. In contrast with the historical playbook, extremely narrow
market breadth today cautions investors to be wary of Momentum factor exposure. We recommend
consumer-facing laggards for a "catch-up" and highlight a screen of defensive stocks with quality
attributes for investors concerned about a further "catch-down."
Sector performance in various growth environments
Among equity factors, stocks with price momentum, positive earnings revisions, and low volatility have
typically outperformed in decelerating growth environments. Heading into 2027, we expect investors will
assign a scarcity premium to companies with accelerating EPS growth and positive EPS revisions.
