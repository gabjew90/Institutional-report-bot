39 years ago today we woke up to the origins of what would forever be known as Black Monday, where the 
Dow fell -22.6% on the day. It’s rare the anniversary falls on a Monday so it’s a more memorable one today. 
The late US trading session on Friday almost felt people were worried we'd get a repeat today as the S&P fell 
from flat just before the European close to -1.09% by the US one. However, this morning we are seeing a 
bounce with S&P (+0.79%) and Nasdaq (+1.29%) futures making up most of their late Friday losses.   
Clearly the main nervousness last week was around AI-related names, with credit markets starting to show 
some distress in that sector. The Philly semiconductor index was down -11.41% last week (-3.83% 
Friday), whilst the NASDAQ was down -4.48% (-2.14% Friday). At the heart of the sell-off was CoreWeave, 
which was down -25.21% last week (-11.91% Friday), whilst their 5yr CDS was up +225bps on the week 
to 1128bps with their cash bonds trading at around a 15% yield. Meanwhile, Oracle’s share price was 
down -9.31% (-4.64% Friday), and their 5yr CDS was up +44bps on the week to 300bps.   
Asia has started the week relatively calm with the KOSPI (+0.55%) and S&P/ASX 200 (+0.32%) both 
trading higher, while the CSI 300 (+0.09%) and Shanghai Composite (+0.16%) are just above flat. The 
Nikkei (-0.73%) is the region’s weakest performer, extending losses after falling -5.4% last week as 
technology stocks came under heavy pressure. Elsewhere, Indonesia’s benchmark stock index is down 
more than 5% to its lowest level in six years, as concerns over the potential MSCI downgrade we 
discussed on Friday continues to heighten fears of further foreign investor outflows. The index is down over -
16% in these past two sessions. 
Early morning data showed that China’s economic expansion accelerated marginally in the third quarter 
of 2026, with real GDP growing by +4.5% y/y. This print slightly surpassed the consensus expectation of 
+4.4% and marked a modest step up from the +4.3% expansion recorded in the second quarter. On a 
sequential basis, GDP grew by +1.2% q/q outperforming the +1.1% forecast and accelerating from +0.9% 
in the prior period. The quarterly performance was primarily underpinned by resilient export volumes and 
robust demand within the artificial intelligence infrastructure supply chain.   

Separately, China's September activity indicators were on the decent side. Industrial production rose by 
+5.5% y/y, beating expectations of +5.4% and accelerating from +5.2% in August. On the demand side, 
consumer spending showed signs of firming as retail sales expanded by +1.1% y/y - more than double the 
consensus forecast of +0.5% and a notable improvement from the +0.4% growth recorded in August. 
Elsewhere, France’s political and fiscal problems gained another complication over the weekend as 
nationwide cost-of-living protests descended into some of the country’s worst unrest in years. Around 
550,000 people demonstrated on Saturday according to the Interior Ministry, although the CGT put the figure 
at 800,000, and the violence continued into Sunday with fresh clashes in Paris and Marseille. The latest 
official figures put arrests at 2,400 and police injuries at nearly 1,000. This won't make it easy for the 
international community to be totally comfortable buying OATs, so the modest recovery in French spreads 
over the last week or so will be tested today. In a televised address last night, President Macron appealed for 
calm, saying France felt “anger and shame” at the violence. He stopped short of declaring a state of 
emergency, but said prefects could ban protests where necessary to protect public buildings and 
businesses, while distinguishing peaceful demonstrators from ultra-left groups and criminals blamed by the 
government for the rioting. With France already facing a difficult fiscal backdrop and the 2027 election 
approaching, the weekend adds another layer of political risk for markets. OAT futures are down around a 
third of a percent in price terms this morning as other DM bond futures are flat to slightly higher so there has 
been some small impact so far. 
The week ahead doesn’t really have a big focal point but instead a broad global range of releases, with the 
flash October PMIs on Friday providing the first coordinated read on activity across the US, UK, Japan 
and the major euro-area economies. Before then, the UK has a busy run of labour-market data tomorrow 
and inflation on Wednesday. Japan’s national CPI arrives on Friday, while Canada also has inflation data 
today. Central-bank action is quieter, with the Bank of Korea deciding on Thursday and the Fed already 
in its communications blackout ahead of next week’s FOMC. Earnings season also broadens out, with 
Netflix tomorrow and Meta and Tesla on Wednesday among the highlights. We have 76 S&P 500 and 69 
Stoxx 600 companies releasing with next week being even larger. 
In the US, the calendar itself is relatively light, which should leave markets continuing to digest last week’s 
much stronger-than-expected inflation numbers. Headline CPI rose +0.65% in September, taking the 
annual rate from 3.4% to 3.7%, while core CPI increased +0.33%, with its annual rate up two-tenths to 
2.6%. The important point was that the upside surprise was broad-based across core goods, shelter and 
services rather than being driven by one idiosyncratic component. PPI was also stronger than expected on 
Thursday, and our economists’ current tracking for September core PCE is +0.36% month-on-month, or 
about 3.14% year-on-year absent revisions. So there is plenty for the Fed to consider even though officials 
are now in blackout. 
As for this week’s US releases, the housing data come first. Our economists expect the NAHB housing-
market index to slip to 31 from 32 today. Tomorrow, our economists expect housing starts to rise to an 
annualised 1.300mn from 1.275mn, while building permits are expected at 1.400mn versus 1.403mn 
previously. Large surprises could still shift estimates of Q3 activity, with our economists continuing to 
expect annualised real GDP growth of 3.3%. More broadly, they expect inflation-adjusted output to 
expand 2.5% on a Q4/Q4 basis in 2026 and 2.2% in 2027. 

Later in the week, our economists expect initial jobless claims to rise to 206k from 199k on Thursday. The 
main US releases then come with the flash S&P Global PMIs on Friday, where our economists expect 
manufacturing to ease to 55.5 from 55.9 and services to 57.5 from 58.8. These numbers have recently 
mattered for markets: the sharp rise in the September PMIs helped drive an almost 15bp increase in both 
2yr and 10yr Treasury yields on September 23. Our economists still expect the Fed to remain on hold at 
next week’s October meeting, allowing policymakers more time to assess the underlying inflation trend and 
the tightening in financial conditions from the recent rise in global yields.   
Recapping last week now, and as mentioned at the top, the biggest story was the selloff in AI-related assets, 
with chip stocks taking a particular hit. For instance, the Philly semiconductor index was down -11.41% 
last week (-3.83% Friday), whilst the NASDAQ was down -4.48% (-2.14% Friday). Moreover, there were 
even larger declines for some of the most affected names, with CoreWeave down -25.21% last week (-
11.91% Friday), whilst CoreWeave’s 5yr CDS was up +225bps on the week to 1128bps. Meanwhile, 
Oracle’s share price was down -9.31% (-4.64% Friday), and their 5yr CDS was up +44bps to 300bps. So 
given the weighting of tech in US equities more broadly, that meant the S&P 500 took a hit as well, with the 
index down -1.94% last week (-1.09% Friday). However, that selloff in the AI space didn’t carry over to 
other sectors in general, with the equal-weighted S&P 500 posting a weekly gain of +0.50% (-0.40% 
Friday). Moreover, European equities actually posted solid gains, with the STOXX 600 up +2.13% (+0.72% 
Friday), marking its best weekly performance since July. That said, some indices more exposed to chip 
stocks struggled, and Japan’s Nikkei fell -5.37% (+1.39% Friday), marking its worst week since July.   
As all that was happening, AI-related growth concerns helped to drive a decent rally for sovereign bonds, with 
the US 10yr Treasury yield down -16.5bps last week (+1.5bps Friday) to 5.07%. Indeed, it was the biggest 
weekly decline for the 10yr yield since August 2025, whilst the 30yr Treasury yield fell -17.5bps (+1.5bps 
Friday) in its biggest weekly decline since April 2025. Interestingly, that came despite some very hawkish 
data last week, as the US core CPI print was at +0.3% in September, which briefly saw futures price in a 
greater-than-50% chance of a hike at the Fed’s October meeting. However, the AI selloff then meant that 
quickly unwound, and Fed Chair Warsh’s comments at the end of the week saw market pricing for an 
October Fed hike at 17% by the close on Friday. 
Staying on rates, last week saw the stress around French bonds continue to stabilise. Indeed, the 2yr 
Franco-German spread came down another -4.5bps to 47bps, whilst the 10yr spread was broadly 
steady, up just +0.5bps at 137bps. We’ll see what the weekend riots do to sentiment this morning. That 
came alongside a wider decline in sovereign bond yields across the continent, with 10yr bund yields down -
5.5bps (+1.3bps Friday) to 3.42%. Nevertheless, there was more stress in HY credit, with Euro HY spreads 
up +12bps last week, and US HY up +24bps as well. However, IG spreads were more contained, with 
Euro IG down -2bps, and US IG up +3bps.   
Finally, the risk-off tone wasn’t helped by the continued rise in oil prices, with Brent crude up +4.91% last 
week (+3.72% Friday) to $109.86/bbl. That marks a second consecutive weekly increase, and it also came 
alongside fresh gains for European natural gas futures, which moved up +7.51% last week to their highest 
level since 2022,  at €87.55/MWh. So that exacerbated inflation concerns in Europe and saw the 1yr Euro 
inflation swap rise another +21bps last week to +3.69%. And similarly in the US, the 1yr inflation swap 
was up +17bps last week to +2.74%.
