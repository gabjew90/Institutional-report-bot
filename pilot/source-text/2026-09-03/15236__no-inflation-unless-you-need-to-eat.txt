The inflation trade is waking up
Commodities are starting to send a message. BCOM has exploded higher, grains are accelerating and
a convergence of geopolitical, weather and supply shocks is pushing agricultural prices sharply higher.
So far, rates have only partly followed. But with commodities and breakevens historically moving
closely together, another leg higher could make the inflation narrative much harder to ignore.
Positioning is getting crowded, so expect reversals along the way, but the bigger macro signal is
increasingly clear: the commodity move is starting to matter.
Unstoppable?
BCOM has exploded higher since testing the longer-term trend line and the 200-day MA earlier this
summer. The index has basically dislocated from the 200-day again. Note the May reversal occurred
right around these levels.
We don't like trading against momentum this strong, but watch this space closely. A further breakout
from here could start adding real fuel to the inflation narrative.

Accelerating
Agricultural commodities are accelerating higher as several supply shocks converge. Hormuz
disruptions have hit fertiliser and key feedstock flows, while renewed Russia–Ukraine escalation is
disrupting Black Sea grain exports just as weather risks are building and global grain balances tighten.
The scale matters: the Gulf accounts for roughly 43% of global urea exports and 44% of sulphur
exports, while Russia and Ukraine remain critical to global wheat supply. Black Sea disruptions have
already pushed wheat sharply higher, with buyers increasingly scrambling for alternative supply. Add
intensifying El Niño/heat risks and tighter grain balances, and the food inflation setup is becoming
increasingly difficult to ignore.
The policy shock
When drought hits, governments worry about domestic food inflation and restrict exports. When the
world's biggest suppliers shut the door, global prices can move far more violently than the underlying
crop losses suggest.
The setup is already there. India's sugar inventories are at 30-year lows and export restrictions have
begun. Rice around $500/tonne sits between the 2025 trough of roughly $370–400 and the ~$670
peak reached during the 2023–24 export-ban squeeze.
And El Niño has never been a clean global grain-shortage trade. Strong events have cut South African
corn output by roughly 40% and Australian wheat by 22% while leaving global production essentially
unchanged.
The interesting part isn't the crop loss. It's what the crop loss forces governments to do. More here.

Positioning is getting crowded again
Managed-money net longs in corn have jumped to 317k contracts, the largest since early May, while
speculative longs have also rebuilt sharply across wheat and soybeans.
The speed matters. BofA had spec longs collapsing 88% in just three weeks during the June washout.
That leverage has now been rebuilt into the rally, leaving the complex increasingly vulnerable to
profit-taking. We got a taste of that this week: wheat hit fresh multi-year highs before reversing, with
winter wheat fading again on Wednesday as traders took profits.
