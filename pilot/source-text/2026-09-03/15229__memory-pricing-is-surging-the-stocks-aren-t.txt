Memory pressure cooker
Memory remains one of the strongest links in the AI complex, with hyperscalers absorbing higher
prices rather than cutting demand. Yet the stocks have spent the past few weeks consolidating, leaving
DRAM and its major components increasingly compressed between key trend lines.
Micron is already showing what resilience could look like, while Samsung and Hynix remain pinned
near major support. With KOSPI vol crushed and Korean speculation having cooled dramatically, the
asymmetry is getting interesting: a break higher in memory could turn this consolidation into a rather
aggressive catch-up trade.
DRAM brewing
DRAM has consolidated over the past few weeks. The ETF is now squeezed between the positive trend
line in place since launch and the negative trend line from the ATHs. The 21-day MA is sloping higher
again, with price back above it. A break higher from this consolidation could get aggressive quickly.

Memory keeps getting paid
Memory remains one of the tightest links in the AI supply chain. Amazon explicitly attributed part of its
latest capex increase to higher memory costs, yet hyperscalers continue absorbing those prices to
secure HBM, DRAM and enterprise SSD capacity.
That's a powerful signal. If customers are raising capex partly because memory costs more rather than
cutting demand, memory suppliers still have serious pricing power.

KOSPI vol
One way to play a possible catch-up in the above-mentioned theme is via KOSPI upside convexity.
Volatility has been absolutely crushed, and although vol levels remain elevated longer term, a possible
squeeze could reignite some of that old Korean upside chasing. After all, the Korean retail mania has
cooled off massively, but nothing a possible squeeze couldn't fix.
