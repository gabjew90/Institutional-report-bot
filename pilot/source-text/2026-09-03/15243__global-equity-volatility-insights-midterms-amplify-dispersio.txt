Global Equity Volatility Insights
Midterms amplify dispersion more than vol
 
US midterms more likely to amplify dispersion than vol 
With the US midterm elections ~2 months away, prediction markets (source: Kalshi) 
indicate a close battle for Senate control. Despite the uncertainty, however, S&P options 
are pricing in just a 0.82% post-election day move, and the VIX curve shows little event 
premium. This may be reasonable given midterm elections have rarely elicited large 
broad market moves historically. Moreover, today’s easing equity market leverage and 
low correlation backdrop suggest the election may prove more rotation- than vol-
inducing. We think the key for investors is to avoid overpaying for election protection, 
especially at the index level, favouring low-cost hedges such as S&P PDOs (50% cheaper 
than put spreads) and VIX calendar call spread collars (leveraging likely pre-election vol 
support). For more targeted protection, event vol in regional banks and small caps 
appears underpriced vs 2024 Presidential election moves, making hedges like IWM Nov 
puts worth considering, in our view.  
Replace SX7E divs with SX5E divs or EZ Bank equities 
SX7E dividend futures have delivered exceptional returns in recent years, but we see 
relatively better opportunities in SX5E dividends currently. SX7E dividend futures now 
appear priced for perfection, with contracts trading broadly in line with analyst 
expectations despite substantial upgrades already. By contrast, SX5E dividends offer 
better value relative to bottom-up estimates, even once risk-adjusted and after 
incorporating anticipated index-composition changes and ASML's cap-factor adjustment. 
SX5E's exposure to ASML-related index-capping effects, which provide an indirect 
gearing to the global AI theme further reinforce the relative attractiveness vs SX7E 
dividends. We therefore favour rotating long dividend exposure from SX7E into SX5E, 
while investors seeking continued upside in European banks may find a cleaner 
expression through the underlying equities. 
From long Kospi gamma to long upside 
The Kospi is finally out of bubble-risk territory. With the weakest and most leveraged 
hands shaken out during the summer, realised and implied vols have roughly halved, 
bringing BofA's Bubble Risk Indicator down from persistent >0.8 readings since October 
2025 to 0.52 and creating an opportunity to rotate from long gamma into low-cost 
upside structures.

BofA GFSITM X-Asset Risk Landscape 
Equities drive third GFSI decline in four weeks 
Prior to the risk-off tone Tuesday 1-Sep, the GFSI fell from -0.16 on 21-Aug-26 to -0.21 
on 28-Aug-26, marking its third weekly decline in the past four weeks. As of Friday, 
cross-asset stress now sits in the 22nd percentile of its historical distribution since 2000. 
The decrease in stress last week was led by equities with six of the top seven stress-
decliners coming from the asset class (Exhibit 3 & Exhibit 4). Nikkei implied vol recorded 
the largest decline in stress as it experienced a 95th percentile drop compared to its own 
history (Exhibit 6). Nikkei skew was also among the top stress-decliners helping Japan 
post the largest decrease in regional stress, though it remains the most stressed by a 
wide margin (Exhibit 5). In fact, Nikkei skew is now the least stressed equity 
subcomponent hovering on the edge of bullish territory with stress almost at half a 
standard deviation below median levels (Exhibit 2). Other notable equity stress decliners 
included the remaining equity skew subcomponents, EM equity fund flow, and volume 
flow, which measures bullish versus bearish U.S. stock trading volume (Exhibit 3). 
Across other asset classes, stress declined in commodities and credit, was unchanged in 
FX, and edged higher in rates (Exhibit 4). Notably, crude implied volatility was the only 
non-equity subcomponent among the seven largest stress decliners, coinciding with a 
decline in oil futures prices last week (Exhibit 3). 
• 
The top three stress-gainers were rates subcomponents. Govt-OIS USD 
recorded the largest rise in stress last week, but Tibor-OIS remains the 
GFSI’s most stressed subcomponent (Exhibit 2 & Exhibit 3). 
• 
All cross-asset vols and spreads saw stress decline last week (Exhibit 
7). FX vol is the least stressed by this metric while commodity vol is the most 
stressed.

BofA Bubble Risk Indicator Landscape 
BRIs sees little froth outside healthcare & cybersecurity 
The BofA Bubble Risk Indicator (BRI) is a price-based measure designed to 
detect bubble-like asset dynamics. Inspired by the way the first four moments 
describe a statistical distribution, the BRI distils an asset’s returns, volatility, 
momentum, and fragility into a single bubble-risk reading on a 0 to 1 scale; 1 
represents extreme bubble-like price action while 0 represents none. Historic 
asset bubbles have exhibited high BRI levels as they formed and peaked (see our 
2026 Year Ahead for more details).

Hedging at the index level 
With (i) post-midterm moves at the broad market level having rarely been large, (ii) 
leverage in the US equity ecosystem having moderated alongside the pullback in S&P 
and Nasdaq BRIs (see BRI section), and (iii) a low correlation, high dispersion backdrop, 
we favour low-cost, grind lower structures for investors looking to hedge an adverse 
market outcome following the midterm elections. For instance, structures like SPX 
down-and-out puts (PDOs) offer historically attractive entry points given the near-record 
steep tail put skew 3m and out (Exhibit 18), providing protection in 5%-20% drawdowns 
from current levels while being 50% cheaper than vanilla put spreads.  
Alternatively, for an index vol-based hedge, we like the VIX call spread collar that (i) sells 
the 16 put in Oct to leverage the likely vol support provided by the midterm election & 
Oct FOMC meeting, and (ii) buys the 25-40 call spread in Nov to hedge an adverse 
market reaction to the election outcome or the broader risk of a high correlation shock. 
Hedging underpriced Nov-2024 movers 
If the 2024 presidential election is a guide to which assets may be the most sensitive to 
this November’s midterms, options on regional banks (KRE) and small caps (RTY) appear 
most underpriced today relative to their 2024 election-week moves (Exhibit 19). Hence, 
we like structures like IWM puts as a more targeted midterm election hedge in an 
unwind of the biggest winners from the 2024 Republican sweep, especially as small 
caps’ rates sensitivity should also hedge FOMC risk in the coming weeks.

Replace SX7E divs with SX5E divs or EZ Bank equities, as upside is well priced 
What: Replace long exposure in SX7E dividends with SX5E dividends, which offer 
better risk-adjusted dividend upside potential and potential gearing to the global 
AI trade. Alternatively, consider replacing long SX7E div positions with banks’ 
equities exposure, thus retaining upside to earnings growth, buybacks and M&A 
catalysts. On the SX5E trade, risks are that SX7E divs outperform SX5E divs or 
that SX7E underperforms SX7E divs. 
European dividend markets have seen strong returns in recent years, particularly for 
European banks (SX7E), with 2y SX7E constant tenor dividends up 60% in the last 2 
years. Following our earlier work on the Sep'26 SX5E index review (see GEVI, 25-Aug-
26), where we highlight admittedly limited upside potential in SX5E dividends, we extend 
this analysis to SX7E too. In our view, SX7E div futures appear priced for perfection 
while SX5E divs offer more attractive upside for mid-to-longer maturities (e.g. Dec29). 
Div risk premium compression more extreme in SX7E vs SX5E: Once bottom-up 
estimates are adjusted for our expectations of the new SX5E index composition 
(Scenario 1: -WKL, -VOW3, +ENGI, +NOKIA) and ASML's cap-factor adjustment, SX5E div 
futures continue to trade below estimates for Dec28 and Dec29 tenors (Exhibit 20). 
SX7E dividends, by contrast, present a different picture. SX7E div futures are trading 
close to analyst estimates, leaving little to no upside potential and room for downside 
surprises (Exhibit 21). As a result, remaining upside potential is limited to SX5E divs, 
with DEDZ9 screening relatively attractive (Exhibit 22). Encouragingly, this is not merely 
compensation for taking on more risk. When compared against recent realised volatility, 
SX5E dividends still offer more favourable risk/reward than the equivalent SX7E 
contracts, in our view (Exhibit 23). 
Analyst estimates remain bullish despite already strong SX7E div growth: SX7E 
analyst estimates have seen strong upgrades in recent years, more so for dividends than 
for earnings. This has resulted in higher expected payout ratios. In our view, market 
participants have learned to be comfortable with owning SX7E div futures at tight 
discounts to estimates in anticipation of further analyst upgrades. Looking ahead, 
however, analysts are now forecasting similar growth in both SX5E and SX7E divs, 
despite stronger EPS and DPS growth that banks have experienced in recent years and 
lofty payout ratio forecasts (Exhibit 24 and Exhibit 25).  
Positioning highlights early buying pressure for SX7E divs: Eurex net ‘Agent’ longs 
in SX7E dividend futures appear to have been building up earlier than in previous cycles, 
suggesting investors have been looking to harvest the dividend risk premium at a faster 
pace, resulting in the value compression we’ve seen (Exhibit 26). 
Instead, SX5E divs offer AI gearing: A structural distinction favours SX5E divs over 
SX7E divs, in our view. Unlike SX7E, the SX5E carries the potential for ASML's regular 
capping (if the stock continues to rally, repeatedly reaching SX5E’s 10% concentration 
threshold1) to positively impact its dividend bottom-up estimate, an effect that 
establishes gearing to the global AI theme for SX5E dividend futures (Exhibit 27), 
something that SX7E divs do not possess.  
In summary, these factors suggest that those with long exposure to SX7E dividends may 
prefer to rotate into SX5E dividends for better risk-adjusted dividend upside potential 
and gearing to the global AI trade. Alternatively, investors may express optimism on 
European banks through the underlying equities given strong earnings momentum, 
buybacks and M&A catalysts.

Frequently asked questions about the BofA Bubble Risk Indicator (BRI) 
In our 2026 Year Ahead report, we introduced the BofA Bubble Risk Indicator (BRI), a 
price-based measure designed to detect bubble-like instability across financial assets. 
Given strong client interest, we are now adding a weekly one-page BRI update to the 
GEVI (see earlier pages). This update will track BRI levels across major markets and asset 
classes, highlight pockets of equities exhibiting the most froth, and call out recent 
increases in bubble-like price action. Below, we address some of the most frequently 
asked questions about the BRI. 
The BofA Bubble Risk Indicator is intended to be an indicative metric only and may not be 
used for reference purposes or as a measure of performance for any financial instrument or 
contract, or otherwise relied upon by third parties for any other purpose, without the prior 
written consent of BofA Global Research. The BRI was not created to act as a benchmark. 
1. How does the BRI quantify ‘bubble risk’? 
A defining feature of historical asset bubbles is that volatility tends to rise alongside 
prices. Building on this observation and drawing inspiration from how the four statistical 
moments characterize a distribution, the BRI quantifies bubble risk using the first four 
moments of an asset’s price action: (i) returns, (ii) realized volatility, (iii) price 
momentum2, and (iv) fragility/convexity.3 
These four moments typically become elevated during the frothiest phases of asset 
bubbles, reflecting the influence of exuberant sentiment, one-sided positioning and 
imbalanced supply-demand dynamics on price action. The late 90s dotcom bubble in the 
Nasdaq provides a clear and pertinent illustration of these dynamics (Exhibit 34 & 
Exhibit 35).

different speeds.

Each sub-indicator aims to capture how stretched an asset’s four moments (returns, vol,

average of each of these four moments’ percentile ranks vs history.4 However, given 
detection of bubble-like behaviour is the primary aim of the indicator, we condition this 
average percentile rank on periods when the asset is rallying by scaling it up or down 
depending on how positive or negative returns are.5

3. What is a ‘high’ BRI reading and what are its implications? 
Since the BRI is defined on a 0 to 1 scale, assets with readings approaching 1 are 
exhibiting increasingly bubble-like price action. How close to 1? While it is difficult to 
specify a universal threshold given the plethora of asset-specific dynamics that can 
present in markets, we find that BRI levels above 0.8 (i.e., top quintile) provide a useful 
rule of thumb beyond which bubble risks are likely elevated. 
Tactically, high BRI readings tend to signal asymmetric downside risk in the near-term 
(higher risk of the bubble ‘popping’), which we demonstrate empirically later in this 
section. However, rather than treating the BRI as an explicit sell signal (“get out”), which 
can risk major underperformance in a bubble, we find the BRI best positioned as a 
cautionary tool to add asymmetric protection/equity replacement (“watch out”). 
4. What assets can the BRI be computed on? 
Given the BRI relies only on price-based inputs, it can be computed on any asset with a 
sufficient history of daily price data. Indeed, by inferring sentiment and positioning from 
price action, the BRI can be applied to assets without explicit fundamental valuation 
methodologies (e.g., commodities) or those with opaque positioning visibility.

seven of the eight bubbles saw their peaks concurrent to a BRI reading above 0.8.

6. Can an asset with a high BRI reading not be in a bubble? 
Yes. One of the risks stemming from the BRI’s exclusive use of price data is instances of 
‘false positives’ when the indicator appears stressed, but the asset doesn’t appear to be 
in a bubble based on other metrics. For example, semiconductor stocks exhibited frothy 
behaviour in the immediate aftermath of ChatGPT’s release in Nov-22 but also saw a 
fundamental shift and expansion of their business model in the AI era.  
Another scenario of a high BRI level that may not point to a bubble is in the immediate 
aftermath of an exogenous shock (e.g., COVID), where vol/fragility remain high and 
prices are retracing their drawdown. In such cases, the interpretation of a high BRI level 
requires additional context.

7. Can an asset in a bubble have a low BRI reading? 
Yes. A related limitation of the BRI’s reliance on price data is the implicit assumption 
that the asset bubble in question would result in historically elevated instability. While 
the typical evolution of a bubble sees this happen as stretched positioning and 
exuberance translate into volatile & fragile rallies with high momentum into the peak, 
outlier cases where the bubble peak sees the bull run end in a whimper can occur and are 
a challenge for the BRI (e.g., the Nikkei bubble in the 1980s, which saw its most unstable 
rally earlier rather than later).  
8. How can one use the short-term to long-term sub-indicators of the BRI? 
As discussed above, the BRI aggregates short‑term, medium‑term, and long‑term 
sub‑indicators to capture asset price dynamics across multiple horizons. In addition to 
examining the sub-indicators to understand shorter vs longer-term trends, we also find 
the range across sub-indicators provides a confidence level on the BRI reading, with 
tighter ranges providing higher confidence. For example, a 0.7 BRI reading is likely more 
informative when the sub‑indicators span 0.6 to 0.8 than when they range from 0.4 to 
1.0, where disagreement across horizons suggests greater uncertainty. 
9. What other indicators can help confirm elevated bubble risk? 
At its core, the BRI is simply a parsimonious transformation of prices, avoiding a 
“kitchen‑sink” approach that incorporates explicit positioning, leverage, or PB data. This 
keeps the framework robust to overfitting and makes it applicable across assets, 
regions, and historical periods where such data are often unavailable or unreliable.  
Despite the appeal of such an approach, we find the BRI most effective when used in 
conjunction with complementary tools that can help assess whether frothy price action 
is supported by fundamentals – for example, more traditional valuation (e.g., PV of cash 
flows, valuation multiples, etc.) and/or positioning metrics (e.g., leverage, inflows, retail 
trading volumes etc.) where available. 
10. What universe of assets will be covered in the BofA BRI Landscape? 
We plan to include BRI levels on a broad set of assets covering global equity indices, 
large cap US equity sector indices (including the “Magnificent 7”), major commodities 
(including the BCOM Index), and major cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin and Ethereum). In 
addition to this core cross-asset universe, we plan to apply the BRI framework to popular 
equity themes that have experienced significant inflows over the prior week. This 
thematic lens is designed to spot emerging pockets of froth that may not yet be visible 
at the broader index or sector level.
