JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Pre-market price action points to a bit of an ‘Everything Rally’ and some stabilization in both 
energy and bond markets a welcome respite for Equities. Parts of the AI trade feel supported 
with the combination of NVDA and AVGO earnings and that could become the safety haven if 
inflation prints hotter than expected in the near-term. As we gear up for NFP, I think the print 
has minimal impact on the Fed decision unless you see an extreme outcome, e.g. +125k or -
25k jobs, given the view that the US is at full employment and that wage growth is not driving 
inflation. We remain Tactically Cautious in the near-term given the bond market’s current 
sensitivity to oil prices and the uncertainty of returning to the MoU or signing a new version. 
There remains the risk that a deal is signed over the holiday weekend pointing to the potential 
for the market to de-risk on Friday.

services activity may be fading, the early PMI print rose to one of the highest levels of the 
current cycle. Meanwhile, the ISM services series has moved sideways in recent months, with 
both business activity and new orders edging higher in July. Inflationary pressures remained 
firm in July, as the ISM prices index rose to 70.3 from 67.7 in June and the PMI price 
measures climbed to their highest levels since the post-Liberation Day spike early last year. 
However, the August PMI report pointed to some moderation: input prices fell to 59.3 from 63.0 
in July, while output prices declined to 55.4 from 58.5. This was accompanied by a notable rise 
in the employment index, which increased to 53.2 in the early August survey from 50.8 in July.” 
  
POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE – Prime Time / Monthly Wrap | A Further Reset on Many 
Fronts 
BRIEF SUMMARY:  In many ways, August appears to have been a further reset following 
the July volatility.  HF performance and market returns rebounded, but in a modest fashion 
with continued chop underneath.  The choppy HF performance has likely been tied to the 
lingering de-grossing that we saw throughout much of Aug, but with seasonal trends 
suggesting re-grossing (even if only modest), it could support a better alpha environment going 
forward.  Relatedly, we continued to see Momentum reduced and a rotation out of Semis / AI 
winners into Software / AI laggards.  Aggregate positioning moved back to neutral in most 
regions (e.g. US rebounded, while Japan fell a little).  Taken together, these suggest a lot 


[Page 5]
more neutrality on many fronts, even if some of the prior skews still persist when looked at 
on a longer-term basis. 
  
KEY POINTS: 
1. HF performance rebounded a little in Aug (+0.6%).  Asia was a small positive though 
alpha was mixed and crowded shorts were a drag in the US, particularly in HC and 
Tech.  
2. Gross flows bottomed in the US: The de-grossing persisted through the first few 
weeks of Aug, before flipping to re-grossing over the last week.  Global gross flows 
were only -0.2z last month.  Seasonal trends suggest a further re-gross. 
3. US Positioning back to neutral (similar in other regions): Our US TPM’s 4wk 
change rebounded back to +0.5z from a low of nearly -2z in late July.  In line with this, 
the level of US positioning is back to ~40th %-tile since 2015. It’s similar in other 
regions, while HF net leverage is also around the 50th %-tile on a 5yr basis.  While net 
positioning seems neutral, the near-term directional set-up for stocks appears very 
mixed (Weekly Wrap on Aug 28) 
4. Momentum continued to be sold, though 1H build-up has now been reversed: 
Global net exposure to momentum is back to YTD lows and the buying we saw in the 
factor in US, EU, and Japan have been almost fully reversed.  However, performance 
going forward is still unclear (Weekly Wrap on Aug 21). 
5. US Software still covered, while Cyclicals mostly sold: Combined (HF + Retail + 
ETF) flows showed strongest selling in Financials (a reversal MoM), while Industrials 
and Materials led the HF selling and Energy was still sold into strength.  AI Vulnerable / 
Software was the most bought theme last month and mostly short covering, though we 
did see some longs added at the end of the month.   
6. Europe anti-AI rotation continues; Cyclicals de-risked ex-Energy: EU AI Disruption 
(JPEUAIL) was bought mostly via short covering, but EU AI Facilitators (JPEUAIID) was 
net sold on short adds and Semis was most sold industry. Cyclicals (JPDEECYC) kept 
seeing long reductions + short adds, while Europe Pure Oil (JPEUOIL1) saw renewed 


[Page 6]
net buying; Banks were net neutral (after July selling) and Health Care (Pharma) was 
bought. 
7. Asia: Japan bought broadly, while Korea/Taiwan still de-risked; China/HK mixed: 
Japan saw broad-based buying in Aug led by Financials and Cyclicals, China local was 
sold while HK was bought, and thematically there was buying in China 
Defensives/Health Care. Korea and Taiwan continued to be sold (as in July); Korea 
selling slowed MoM but there’s still no clear shift back to buying even as performance 
stabilized.

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the Aug 24, 2026 Morning Briefing 
We are moving to a Tactically Cautious / Neutral view, which is to say that Equity market 
fundamentals remain strong but that near-term variables are likely to cause markets to chop 
sideways. Here is what is driving the change in our tactical view: (i) The Fed – Equity bull 
markets tends to end with either a hiking cycle or a recession. Currently, a recession is highly 
unlikely to manifest over the next few quarters, but Warsh’s comments now make Sep a live 


[Page 13]
meeting. What is unclear is if the Fed hikes, what is the scale / length of the hiking cycle, e.g., 
would he go in Sep / Dec and conclude the hikes or potentially go 50bp in Sep? NFP and CPI 
are the critical inputs with CPI on Sep 11 the more important of the two prints given Warsh’s 
view that the US is at full employment ad Friday’s print is unlikely to drive a material change in 
the U.3 unemployment rate. (ii) Positioning – make sure to read the weekly update from 
Positioning Intel but the TL; DR is that positioning is not giving a clean read on market 
direction and history suggests a choppy next 3-4 weeks. The team also have flagged that a 
sharp rebound in Momentum factor is unlikely given recent activity. (iii) Credit Spreads – 
Historically, there is a spike in issuance in the two weeks post-Labor Day driving spreads wider 
which can pressure Equities. Given the recent headwinds to the AI trade stemming from CDS 
spread widening, this is another variable to monitor. (iv) Seasonality – September screens as 
the worst performing month for the SPX followed by Q4 being the strongest quarter of the year. 
Seasonal moves may be exacerbated by midterm election seasonality, which tends to be 
negative into the election and then strongly positive post-election. Since 1990, the SPX rallied 
+5.4%, +11.4%, +12.5%, and +14.7% at 3 month (78% hit rate), 6 month (100% hit rate), 9 
month (100% hit rate), and 12 month (100% hit rate) tenors after midterm election days. (v) 
Equity Rotation / Momentum Unwind – Momentum (JPMPURE Index) is down more than 
21% from its June ATH and ‘Classic’ Momentum (JPPQMO Index) is down nearly 34% from its 
June ATH. These moves fall outside the typical drawdown ranges of 10-15%. (vi) AVGO 
Earnings – may prove spectacular and fail to drive meaningful stock appreciation in AI / 
Memory / Semis. 
  
In summary, we think market fundamentals (econ & earnings) remain intact but see the next 2-
3 weeks being choppy. The index may not see a dramatic pullback, but it would not surprise to 
see additional turmoil at the sub-index and single stock level. This argues for reducing net-
longs and potentially adopting a $-neutral or market-neutral approach. Keep an eye on (i) US / 
Iran to see if a deal is possible or if we see actions that drive oil prices higher and (ii) does the 
US / Canada trade debate expand into something more serious.

positions.

the near-term. Regardless, Precious Metals remain attractive. For those concerned on Midterm
