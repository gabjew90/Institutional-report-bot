GS Quinn - S&P 500 Futures Stagflation Resurfacing 3 Sep 2026
Robert Quinn  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, FICC & Equities
Thu 3 Sep 2026, 7:29am ET
Commitment of Traders covering August 18th–25th exhibited a resumption of Non-Dealer selling in S&P
500 futures. Categorical shifts revealed some potential stagflation concerns. Subsequently, solid NVDA
earnings / guidance helped S&P endure another jump in real rates due to Jackson Hole and more warring
in the Middle East. However futures flows apparently remained negative, given the drop in funding.
Perhaps this reflected ongoing growth vs inflation worries and/or stronger-than-expected month-end
rotation.
Non-Dealers sold −$11.2bn, ending a 2-week streak of buying. Liquidation (−$5.7bn) and new shorts
(+$5.5bn) were equal contributors.
Hedge Fund led all sellers with −$15bn in total. Of this, −$9bn represented long unwinds, marking the 3rd
straight week whereby Hedge Fund liquidated alongside an increase in GS’s Long / Short Stagflation

basket. The rolling 3-month correlation between gross long changes and the basket became quite negative.
Asset Manager sold −$3bn. Other and Non-Reportable bought +$3.7bn and +$3.1bn respectively.
Over August 25th–September 2nd, S&P fell just −0.1% alongside a +12bp rise in USGGT10Y. However
futures flows apparently remained negative: the September S&P Adjusted Interest Rate BTIC shed
−17bps. Changes through month-end and close to expiration are somewhat distorted; that said, the
December BTIC also declined. During the same window, Longs / Short Stagflation gained +0.9%.
