Goldman Strategist 'Global Reflections' On Iran, 
Midterms, Bond Whiplash, And Why Index Vol Is 
Lying To You 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SUNDAY, SEP 06, 2026 - 06:15 PM 
Top Goldman Sachs strategist, Natasha Tiwana, welcomes traders back from what was 
supposed to be the last relatively quiet week before year-end, with the must read weekly 'Global 
Reflections' note (available her in full for pro subs). 
Simply put, she writes, it wasn't... 
Iran flared. Energy inflation reasserted itself. Global bonds sold off, then snapped back. Election 
anxiety leaked into every politics-sensitive sleeve that still had any duration left in it. And US 
momentum closed August another painful leg lower, now down 5% year-to-date, while 
commodities and real assets put up the kind of month that makes the rest of the book look like a 
rounding error. 
Her back-to-school 'transcript' of the 'rest of the year movie' is not pretty. The composition of 
momentum is changing. What is not changing is high thematic and factor vol sitting underneath 
contained index vol - the market's favorite way of telling you everything is fine while the things 
that actually move P&L are not. 
Tiwana's "global reflections" offer five key points. None of them say buy the dip and go to the 
beach...

US Midterms 
We are currently in a full Republican government where the President, the House, and the 
Senate are all Republican. 
This means the outcome of the election is either: unchanged full Republican government or a 
divided government where Democrats take majority of either/both House and Senate.  
 
Mirroring the pre-election patterns in uncertainty, volatility, and investor flows, US equities have 
typically traded sideways in the few months ahead of midterms. 


[Page 6]
 
 
The more actionable question for investors is which policy areas become easier to negotiate, 
receive greater regulatory attention, or gain leverage in future spending debates. 
We see noise impacting 3 parts of the equity market in a scenario where Democrats gain 
majority of House/Senate: 
(1) Affordable Care Act exposed equities (GSHLCACA) are expected to benefit while companies 
that produce drugs (GSHLCBPH) are expected to face challenges if there are controls on the 
price of meds.  
Democrats have pushed heavily to revive the enhanced ACA premium tax credits that expired 
at the end of 2025, which has since led to premium increases and a decline in participation in 
ACA marketplace plans. Reauthorizing these subsidies is a top priority to mitigate coverage 
losses. More patients are expected to become uninsured or unable to afford rising premiums. 
Restoring subsidies would likely return patient volumes to providers, who are currently seeing 
increased bad debt and uncompensated care as more patients transition to "self-pay" status. 
Marketplace-focused insurers have direct earnings exposure to enrollment swings each time 
this gets litigated. Hospitals and outpatient providers face rising uncompensated-care costs the 
longer the lapse continues. Inclusion of ACA affordability measures and Medicaid related 
provisions in future spending negotiations remains high on the Democratic agenda. 


[Page 7]
 
 
(2) Republicans have boosted defense spending (GSXUDFNS) while cutting spending in other 
areas (remember DOGE?). We could see non-defense government spending boosted 
(GSXUGOVT).  
 
(3) Public sentiment assumes datacenters are disliked across the board. US households blame 
datacenters and AI for higher electricity prices. If more electricity is generated to support US 
households and the AI build, electricity prices will normalize. We like buying Power 
Infrastructure (GSENEPOW). 
The Democratic AI and energy agenda focuses heavily on "ratepayer protection," specifically 
calling for a shift of massive AI power infrastructure costs away from households and onto 


[Page 8]
 
datacenters. Polling suggests that concerns surrounding datacenters are bipartisan, although 
opposition is more intense among Democrats, largely driven by concerns voiced by US 
households around electricity prices and infrastructure strain. We believe this may be one of the 
more underappreciated election-sensitive themes in US equities. The debate is increasingly 
moving away from whether datacenters should be built and towards who will pay for the 
incremental power generation and transmission required by datacenters. To the extent 
policymakers converge around datacenters cost-bearing, utilities and grid operators could 
benefit from regulatory clarity, while economics for certain datacenter projects become less 
attractive. 
 
European Entropy 
Across the pond, French election uncertainty has meant French Domestics (GSXEFRDO) are 
the worst performing European thematic over 1m but valuation at the upper-end of history 
(~80%ile 5Y) implies scope for more downside particularly if OAT-Bund spreads widen further. 
We are more constructive on the fiscal outlook in Germany, though high energy prices and state 
elections in September present a near-term overhang. 
Nonetheless, YTD underperformance in some pockets like Defense (GSSBDEFE) looks 
increasingly hard to ignore.

Watch European gas (not oil) into winter 
Brent is once again hovering close to $100/bbl territory as the market prices a ‘no-Midest-deal’ 
status quo but our commodity strategists note increased market adaptability to the conflict, 
including a rise in dark transits. 
GS Research estimates total oil exports significantly above visible exports, limiting incremental 
crude price upside (pro subs can read Goldman's 'Dark Transit Rises' note here) 
 
This is not the case for gas, given Qatar LNG loadings continue to miss relative to expectations, 
and European gas storage levels are running lower than seasonality would imply ahead of 
winter heating season. 


[Page 10]
 
In a scenario where gas exports only normalize gradually through 2027, Dec26 TTF could rally 
toward 100 EUR/MWh (vs ~71 EUR currently), supporting higher earnings for Gas producers 
(GSXENGAS) and driving margin pressure for Gas consumers (GSXETTFX). 
 
So that's the playbook... 
Equities are stuck between a rock: elevated risk appetite, growth-shock risk, an AI complex still 
heavy enough to ruin a vanilla index hedge; and a hard place: the de-rating already happened, 
hawkish September hike odds just got talked down by Waller (but back up a bit by payrolls), and 
the vol reset has made the options (protection) they keep telling you to buy look "attractive" 
again. 
'Attractive' is the word the sell-side uses when they want you to pay up for something that has 
already started moving. 
Non-profitable tech spent a year pricing a lower-rates world that still hasn't arrived, and is now 
reverting. French domestics are the worst European thematic over a month and still sit at the 
rich end of history if OATs decide to remember they are not Bunds. Defense in Europe has 
underperformed for long enough that even Goldman is tired of ignoring it. And while the market 
stares at Brent hovering near $100 as if 2008 is loading, the winter problem is not oil. It is 
missing Qatar loadings, European storage already behind seasonality, and Dec-26 TTF with a 
path toward 100 EUR/MWh if gas only normalizes through 2027. 
Midterms, historically, are when the index goes sideways and Tiwana says the real money gets 
made - or lost - in the baskets as the odds of a divided government (or Dem Sweep) rise: 
Obamacare, pharma pricing, defense vs the rest of government spend, and the fight over who 
pays for the power that keeps the datacenters on while households stare at the electric bill.
