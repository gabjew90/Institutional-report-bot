VIX Hits Trump-Term Lows As Yields Hit 30-Year 
Highs; Goldman Partner On The Markets' Paradox 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SUNDAY, SEP 06, 2026 - 12:05 AM 
So here we are in the middle of a long Labor Day weekend... with 10Y JGB yields at levels last 
seen when most of today’s fund managers were still learning how to use a Bloomberg (or just 
evolving from diapers), and Gilt 30s back at 1998 highs - global duration is getting clubbed like a 
baby seal (or dolphin) all summer… and the VIX is printing fresh lows. 
MSCI World is +13% and kissing record highs... 
Growth is reportedly 'impressively resilient'... 
Stagflation talk is apparently cancelled because one payrolls print landed the right side of the 
line... 
It’s almost as if the only thing that still matters is that the tape hasn’t broken yet. 
Waller, at least, had the decency to remind everyone that next week’s CPI and PPI still exist. 
Everyone else seems content to treat multi-decade highs in developed-market yields as a 
colorful footnote while momentum gets cut in half and multiples quietly de-rate underneath 
“staggeringly uniform” earnings. 
"Resilience" is the word that the talking heads use when stocks refuse to price the discount rate. 
Which brings us to Goldman Sachs Partner, Mark Wilson, who has produced his must-read 
'Weekly Mash' (full note available here for pro subs) of what the desk is actually looking at as 
the kids go back to school. 
Read it as a sell-side translation of a summer the bond market already lived through...

Much of the late summer macro focus has been on rates – Japanese 10yr yields are at their 
highest since 1996, Gilt 30yr yields are at their highest since 1998; these are levels that most of 
the people active in  markets today have never seen before. 
After Friday’s payroll report, its hard to give credence to talk of ‘stagflation’ – US and global 
growth continues to be impressively resilient; but, nevertheless, as Waller made clear, next 
week’s CPI & PPI inflation data’s key. 
In the meantime, at the end of an eventful summer – the VIX is printing new lows for Trump’s 
second term (outside of Christmas dates)...

...and MSCI World is +13% YTD and <1% from the highs... 
 
Here are 4 themes I’m focused on as it relates to the opportunity set as markets get 
back-to-school next week: 
1) Momentum's Malaise & The Mix-Shift 
The mix-shift at play within equities was well covered in July, with clear causal factors diagnosed 
late that month. However, the second-leg lower through August got less headline attention. It 
probably shouldn’t have been a surprise that the required risk-transfer & indigestion of July’s 
events weighed on August price formation – but regardless, ‘momentum’ has had a staggering 
reset this summer, giving up close to 50% from late-June highs, and now down YTD (the chart 
below shows our ‘Hi-Beta Momentum’ pair). 


[Page 4]
 
 
As flagged last week, the composition of what constitutes momentum is changing dramatically 
(ie software is in the long leg on 3mth, but still in the short leg of 12mth), so do not expect 1H 
leaders to re-assert 2h leadership. 
2) Right-Tail Rates Risk, Equity Resilience But Still De-Rating 
Combined with some of the indigestion referenced re momentum, interest rates have also been 
a real headwind for equities. Arguably, equity markets have been notably resilient during this 
move higher in global yields; however, its easy to forget that during that resilient period, 
multiples have de-rated and stocks have failed to reward the staggeringly uniform earnings 
strength seen YTD. 
Its difficult to diagnose with precision how much of the de-rating is attributable to a higher 
discount rate, versus the market efficiently pricing the possibility that we’re in an unsustainably 
strong earnings period. 
Regardless, a halt to the fixed income sell-off and a consolidation in yields would be 
unequivocally bullish for equities. 
We recorded GS views on where to next for rates earlier this week with Jan Schefel & George 
Cole – webcast available here: 
As it relates to the bond market, there’s 2 pivotal & unrelated near-term phenomena at play :  
1/ geo-political risk premia, and the consequent re-pricing of oil & gas, has re-asserted itself 
through August as both the Gulf conflict & Russia/Ukraine risk has increased; 


[Page 5]
 
2/ AI investment has had the dual impact of creating greater competition for capital (as the 
largest capex budgets began to be debt funded) as well as producing price pressures in specific 
areas of the economy. 
With respect & consideration for the possible ‘right-tail’ outcomes in both of those factors, I’m 
also conscious that the majority of focus right now is there already – rather than the risk of lower 
oil prices, and greater evidence of AI efficiency gains.  
3) Politics, Geopolitics, & Cheap EU Defense 
Related to the 1st point on geopolitics, its notable how poorly defense stocks have traded for a 
sustained period. 
Part of the initial de-rating was ‘new tech’ taking over ‘old tech’, but that’s no longer the case as 
both sides of that ledger have suffered. 
The political calendar will come into greater focus with the US with mid-terms, but before that 
we’ve got German regional elections this week (in Saxony), then in Sweden, and post those 2 
outcomes European markets will increasingly focus on the possibility of a Le Pen win in next 
year’s French Presidential election.

4) CyberCabs & AI Use-Case Beneficiaries For The Win 
Re-reading last week’s note I was conscious of sometimes not being explicit enough in what I’m 
aiming to say. 
Sometimes its just simpler to say it outright, rather than expecting the inference to be clear: 
Despite the aforementioned rotation in equity markets, the fundamentals of the AI story are 
strengthening & recent bottoms-up data is corroborating this: 
●​ the scale of Nvidia’s earnings beat & guidance raise was enormous;  
●​ DELL’s results told us the same;  
●​ meanwhile, OpenAi’s Astra model has had impressive reviews &  
●​ Nvidia’s Hugging Face acquisition tells us that broad ecosystem will have open vs closed 
optionality for innovation to proliferate as users see-fit. 
Perhaps the most impressive evidence I’ve seen of AI’s step-change in functionality & 
useability... is the least discussed: the Tesla CyberCab launch. 
This has been a long-time coming, and has had its bumps today – but its mind-boggling to 
imagine the impact when the roll-out gains full regulatory sign-off. 
For all the talk of recursive self-improvement, its not hard to see this being the poster-child of 
that AI phenomena if FSD miles compound at their recent runrate (on July 22nd Tesla said 
during their earnings call that CyberCab had driven 380k total miles, and yesterday that crossed 
1m miles...)  ​

*  *  * 
So the markets, after an eventful summer, has done what markets do when the first-half story 
dies of indigestion: it rotated, de-rated, and started hunting for a new narrative that can survive 
higher yields. 
Momentum is no longer the same factor it was in June. 
Rates have been the silent tax on multiples even as earnings held up. 
Geopolitical risk premia in oil and gas reasserted themselves while European defense stocks - 
the one sector that should have been a straight-line beneficiary - spent a year doing the 
opposite of what the headlines implied. 
And the AI complex, having spent the first half pricing the CapEx check, is now being asked to 
prove the use case. 
None of that resolves the original problem... 
JGBs and gilts at generation highs are not a 'vibe' as the kids say nowadays... They are a higher 
discount rate, more competition for capital from AI debt issuance, and a reminder that “resilient 
growth” and “priced for perfection” can coexist right up until they don’t. 


[Page 8]
 
A halt in the fixed-income selloff would be unequivocally bullish for equities. That is also the 
cleanest tell that it hasn’t halted yet. 
Watch the political calendar from Saxony to Le Pen. 
Read the humanoid note on the long weekend.  
Just don’t confuse think this is a sustainable 'new normal' while the risk-free rate is still making 
30-year highs and the VIX is acting like Christmas came early. 
The market can stay irrational. The bond market, lately, has been less so...
