Rates & Credit 
The blue-nami bloodbath in corporate bonds escalated rapidly today with Data-Center CDS 
spreads soaring above 500bps (a record high)... 
 

 
 
CRWV bond spreads topped a stunning 1200bps!!! 
 
Hyperscaler CDS did widen today modestly, but IG spreads spiked again more notably... 
 

 
 
So that's all a total shitshow... BUT... what's bad for the goose is good for the gander (or some 
such shit). Less Data-Centers (worse credit risk), means weaker growth (UST positive) and less 
supply (UST positive). 
Treasuries surged today, serving as the primary safe-haven destination amid the equity rout.  
The front end of the curve is leading the rally with 2Y yields down 17bps since before the 
election (30Y -8bps)... 
 
But today's move began almost immediately after the equity cash open, with Treasury futures 
holding near session bests as the flight-to-quality bid accelerates through the morning.  
 

 
Bloomberg reports that options markets are also reflecting the bullish tone, with demand seen 
for upside via 10-year calls. 
Arguably, the primary macro narrative driving the UST bid is fiscal: bond markets are cheering 
the Dem sweep as a signal of likely political gridlock between a Republican White House and a 
Democrat-controlled Congress, which traders interpret as a constraint on the Trump admin's 
tax-cut and spending agenda - and therefore a reduction in the expected supply of Treasury 
issuance over the medium term. That thesis is reinforced by Bank of America, which now 
expects Treasury coupon auction size increases to be delayed until November rather than 
August, citing debt ceiling dynamics that push the X-date as early as August 2027.   
Additionally supportive was news that South Korea is also reportedly weighing large-scale 
purchases of long-term US Treasuries as part of efforts to bolster FX reserves and strengthen 
economic ties with Washington, adding a potential incremental demand tailwind.  
Rate-hike expectations plunged on the day, despite Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack 
reinforcing her hawkish baseline this morning in Zurich, reiterating that policy needs to move to 
a restrictive stance to bring inflation back to 2% and expressing a preference for smaller, more 
frequent rate adjustments rather than large one-time moves. 
Dec odds are now down to 65% (and Jan odds are tumbling)... 
 
But it is 2027 rate-change expectations that plunged... no pricing in just one hike next year! 
 

 
 
All of which bull-steepened the curve with the market is pricing in more near-term Fed easing (or 
at least less tightening) while long-end supply/inflation concerns keep the back end relatively 
anchored. 
 
And notably, bonds completely ignored the energy complex today... 
 

 
 
Energy 
The dominant story in energy markets today is a sharp rebound in crude from yesterday's 
politically-driven collapse.  
WTI has recovered to around $91 and Brent is back around $98, clawing back a portion of the 
nearly 9% selloff that followed Tuesday's Democratic midterm sweep (which reflected market 
bets that a Democrat-controlled Congress would accelerate efforts to resolve the Iran war - and 
with it, restore flows through the Strait of Hormuz, which have already recovered to near pre-war 
levels)... 
 

 
 
A lot of traders noted that today's bounce was technical, occurring right at the 100/200DMAs 
(along with some position-squaring rather than a fundamental reassessment, with traders still 
digesting what the political shift actually means for the Iran conflict timeline). 
 
Brent CFDs have flipped into contango for the first time since mid-July - a structurally bearish 
signal - as the market anticipates that Middle Eastern oil flows will continue to normalize. 
 

 
 
Beneath the headline crude move, the physical market is showing clear signs of oversupply. 
Saudi Aramco cut its official selling price for Arab Light to Asian buyers by $2/bbl - $7 below the 
regional benchmark for December - a level not seen in six years, as Gulf producers compete 
aggressively for market share now that Hormuz flows are recovering. In the North Sea spot 
market, Vitol bought Forties crude from Trafigura at a discount of approximately $4 to Dated 
Brent FOB — a level described by traders as one never seen in many years.  
Ukraine's overnight strike on Russia's 340,000 b/d Ryazan refinery - one of the country's largest 
- added a modest upside risk to refined product prices, though the market impact has been 
limited so far. 
 
 

 
All of which signaled the broader thesis: behind the geopolitical risk premium that had propped 
up prices, physical supplies are actually quite comfortable, and the cooling of that premium is 
now laying bare a well-supplied market. 
But it seemed noone was paying the energy complex any attention today.

And finally, we go back to what BTIG's chief technical market strategist, Jonathan Krinsky, 
asked (and answered) earlier: "what happens when the music stops?" 
To be fair, Krinsky's base case is still the benign one: semis eventually find a floor, the 
overbought defensives cool off, and the rotation simply flips again. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky 
made a similar point this morning, arguing that "nets are too low for the broader market to go 
down easily." 
But the setup for the bad outcome is uncomfortably in place. Semis have broken their July lows 
with no exhaustion signal, Japan's chip complex is in freefall, healthcare is the most stretched in 
its history, and Goldman's CTA model now sees $164 billion of S&P supply in a down-tape, right 
 

 
after everyone sold their hedges in one of the biggest vol crushes in two decades. Krinsky's 
closing line says it all: 
"That is the condition for the correlation-one selloff, when oversold can't bounce and overbought 
begins to correct." 
Our take: the "rotation" story has been a convenient way to explain why the S&P went nowhere 
for three months while everything underneath it swung wildly. But rotations need buyers on both 
sides, and with XLV at a record 19% above its 200DMA, it's hard to see where the next wave of 
defensive buying comes from. So it's either one more lap of musical chairs (bullish for the semis 
bottom-fishers) or, more likely, the moment the music stops and everyone discovers there are a 
lot fewer chairs than players. Then again, April 1999 shows a 9% dip can come before one final 
melt-up... it's the second part that seems less likely this time.
