center states have increased the risk of permitting delays,

Newly Democratic-led states have influence over roughly

• We recognize that the shifting political landscape creates 
meaningful risks for the pace and cost of data-center 
development, although several factors could mitigate 
the impact. Already-approved projects may prove harder 
to stop, planned capacity could shift toward more 
supportive states, and hyperscalers are likely to increase 
infrastructure and community investments to improve 
the prospects for future development.
• We favor hyperscalers such as Microsoft, Meta Platforms, 
Amazon, and Google, where tighter future capacity 
could increase the value of existing infrastructure and 
lower future capex could support free cash flow. Within 
AI "picks and shovels", we would stay selective and favor 
quality names with less margin expansion to unwind, 
alongside defensive tech exposures such as Visa and 
Mastercard and potential memory-cost beneficiaries such 
as Apple and Sony.

Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa of Texas campaigned on 
a variety of measures aimed at data centers, including 
a moratorium, the elimination of tax incentives, and 
restrictions on energy and water use. Outgoing Governor 
Abbott also put in place a series of measures that effectively 
amounted to a data-center moratorium, including an 
August 2026 order directing the Public Utility Commission 
and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas to review 
data-center projects before allowing interconnection to the 
power grid, followed by a September 2026 order halting 
environmental and operational permits for data centers.
Looking ahead, the temporary pause is likely to be extended 
until the legislature can enact new guidance aligned with 
the incoming administration's campaign agenda, potentially 
including greater local authority over decision-making, the 
elimination of tax incentives, union hiring requirements, 
and additional environmental protections. With roughly 
six months remaining before construction must begin to 
achieve 2028 deployment, further delays could push some 
planned capacity into 2029.
In 
Georgia, 
Governor-elect 
Keisha 
Lance 
Bottoms 
campaigned on pausing new data-center construction and 
said she would sign an executive order implementing the 
pause upon taking office, while also reviewing tax incentives 
offered to data centers.
With governors-elect in key states poised to pause or 
restrict new data center builds, investors sold stocks across 
the AI complex, particularly semiconductors and hardware. 
We recognize the risks associated with a shifting political 
landscape and highlight the following:
• Approved data centers are likely to be completed. 
Governors exercise significant power, but we think 
it is unlikely that an already-approved data-center 
project could be stopped. In our view, this means that 
approximately 30 GW in states shifting to Democratic 
governors will likely be built to completion over the next 
18 to 24 months. This should limit any major near-term 
disruption to the semiconductor supply chain.
• Some planned data centers may migrate to friendlier 
states, providing a "home" for chips two to three years 
from now. Given continued growth in demand for AI 
services, we think capacity shelved because of moratoria 
is more likely to relocate than disappear altogether.
• Data-center developers and hyperscalers are also likely 
to step up their "good neighbor" efforts, including 
commitments to upgrade electric grids and support utility 
infrastructure, reduce or eliminate water consumption, 
provide educational and workforce support, and increase 
transparency. These efforts could ultimately produce a 
win-win-win for policymakers, data center operators, and 
local communities.
The upshot may ultimately be that data centers are built in 
better-suited locations, to higher environmental standards, 
and in communities that welcome the economic benefits 
they bring. Costs may be incrementally higher as developers 
provide greater support to local communities, but not 
materially so, in our view. In addition, in an environment 
where demand for AI compute exceeds supply, delays in 
bringing new capacity online should increase the value of 
incumbent capacity and support higher pricing.
Lastly, we believe data-center operators have various options 
to mitigate the potential impact of stricter requirements 
and tighter constraints. These range from near-term 
solutions with higher costs but more immediate impact on 
longer-dated efforts focused on policy reform. We expect 
hyperscalers to pursue many of these actions in parallel, 
given continued strong demand and contracted backlogs 
that require additional capacity.

To be sure, this will not be a frictionless process. We see 
potential for execution bumps and increased volatility across 
the AI complex. Any delay in shipments could result in 
inventory digestion and price cuts, potentially weighing on 
certain AI semiconductor and hardware bottleneck stocks 
that have benefited significantly from pricing power over the 
past several months.
Over the longer term, we believe demand for AI services will 
ultimately determine the level of data-center investment. If 
demand continues to grow, data centers will ultimately be 
built to satisfy that demand. This may entail incrementally 
higher costs and delays, but the ultimate outcome is likely 
to be a modestly lower growth trajectory, with pockets of 
inventory air gaps but potentially a lower risk of significant 
oversupply.
Within AI, we believe hyperscalers are well positioned 
amid this uncertainty (Microsoft, Meta Platforms, Amazon, 
Google). Restrictions on future data-center supply make 
existing data-center capacity more valuable and increase 
its pricing power. To the extent that capex expectations 
are eventually revised lower for 2028, free cash flow for 
these large capex spenders should improve. Such a reversal 
could become a relative tailwind, particularly as their stocks 
have initially been penalized by declining free cash flow 
associated with elevated AI investment.
In addition, we like defensive technology exposures such as 
payment networks (Visa, Mastercard), as well as companies 
that have been adversely affected by rising memory costs, 
where the prospect of relief in memory pricing could provide 
a tailwind (Apple, Sony).
Within AI "picks and shovels," we prefer to remain selective, 
favoring high-quality companies that have not benefited 
from significant margin expansion during the recent period 
of price increases, and therefore may have less downside 
should pricing reverse.

CIO Selected Designation and CIO Selection Lists
CIO Selected stocks are intended to support the UBS House View (see global asset class preferences definitions) and are 
chosen based on their overall risk-return profiles and other relevant considerations. The starting universe for the CIO Selected 
designation generally consists of securities covered by IB Global Research and rated Buy or Neutral. From that universe, CIO 
Equities applies its own investment judgment, considering a variety of factors such as the fundamental outlook, expected 
performance relative to an identified benchmark, market, sector and industry trends, risk and return characteristics, downside 
considerations, diversification benefits, and other relevant factors. No single factor is necessarily determinative. Rather, CIO 
considers these factors collectively and holistically in determining which securities warrant inclusion in a CIO Equity Selection 
List.
In certain circumstances, CIO may designate a security that is not covered by IB Global Research as CIO Selected where 
CIO determines that the security presents a relevant investment opportunity and applicable eligibility, reasonable-basis and 
approval requirements for such selections have been satisfied. A security will no longer be eligible for inclusion in any CIO 
Selection List if IB Global Research changes its rating to Sell. In such cases, CIO Equities will remove it from applicable 
Selection Lists within a reasonable timeframe.

sectors, countries, regions, investment themes, or another stated objective. Each portfolio is constructed relative to an 
identified benchmark, which is used to define the relevant opportunity set, but the portfolio is not designed to replicate the 
benchmark exactly. The CIO view applies to the portfolio in aggregate, including how the holdings work together, rather than 
each individual holding. It is a research model, not a client-specific portfolio, managed investment strategy, or guaranteed
