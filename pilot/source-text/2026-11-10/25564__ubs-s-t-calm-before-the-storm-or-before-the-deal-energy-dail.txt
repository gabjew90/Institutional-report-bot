Spec sales comment
Brent is on track to finish the week ~15% down on renewed hopes of a diplomatic resolution to the US-Iran
conflict. As has been the case since the start of the war, I am worried at the market’s willingness to price in
de-escalation without evidence of real progress. Most recently, Reuters reports that Iran submitted (via Qatari
mediators) a proposal on potential nuclear concessions to the US, theoretically paving the way for negotiations
to re-open the Strait of Hormuz (which Iran had previously indicated could happen within 2 days of an
agreement). Also widely reported, though seemingly ignored by oil markets, was Iran’s reiteration of the stance
that concessions from the US are a firm prerequisite for any movement on the Iranian side. Iran’s top security
official said “on the issue of diplomacy, we have announced seven conditions to the Americans. As long as
they do not execute our seven conditions, we will neither open the Strait of Hormuz nor have negotiations.”
Iran has demanded a lifting of sanctions, the release of frozen funds, an end to the US naval blockade of
Iranian vessels, guarantees against renewed US and Israeli attacks and an end to attacks against Iranian
allies in the region, a withdrawal of US forces from the region, payment of reparations for damage inflicted
during the war, and acceptance that Iran controls the Strait of Hormuz (which may involve tolls for transits).
The shock of the midterm swing towards the Democrats may have increased the US administration’s
willingness to negotiate, but even on the view that these demands represent a maximalist Iranian opening
stance for negotiations rather than their endgame, I find it hard to imagine major US concessions on any of
these points. Diplomatic progress is possible but I think it is far more likely that we see a return to military
conflict, risking further damage to regional energy production and export infrastructure. One interesting and
potentially impactful aside – several brokers have begun to argue that lower demand triggered by elevated oil

prices and refining margins have pushed the global market into surplus in October. I’ll wait for a full energy
macro update from the UBS analysts, but this view is not supported by the most recent data I’ve seen. And
with the EU integrated names implying a ~$70/b oil price, there is considerable scope for further correction in
the oil price before  valuation becomes challenging for the sector.

well as panels involving expert speakers including the UK’s Shadow Energy Secretary. Please reach out to

you’d like to arrange a meeting or call for feedback after the event.
