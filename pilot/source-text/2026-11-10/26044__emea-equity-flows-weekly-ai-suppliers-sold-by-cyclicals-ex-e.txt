EMEA Equity Flows Weekly - AI suppliers
sold by; Cyclicals ex-Energy saw inflows
Jamie Briggs
08-Nov-2026, 17:22 ET (EST)
Headlines AI de-risking became a global positioning shock last week, but the pressure was far from uniform. In the US,
systematic and retail selling drove Thursday’s momentum unwind before buy-the-dip flows and Friday’s AI/Semis rebound
suggested investors were re-entering a cleaner trade. Risk Control fund selling should exert more pressure on Monday, but
that should mark the low according to the UBS Market Internals team. Asia saw more aggressive AI-led selling across Korea,
Japan and Taiwan, with options investors monetising hedges rather than rebuilding longs. The counter-rotation was clear:
China and India attracted buying as investors rotated toward less AI-exposed markets.Europe’s flat weekly finish masked a
violent intraweek reversal and thematic rotation, as the AI supply chain, Defence and Energy sold off while lower oil and rate
volatility drove a sharp pro-cyclical rotation into Banks and Travel & Leisure. Flows pointed to selective rather than broad
risk appetite, with LO de-risking offset by HF dip-buying and short covering, while investors continued pressing weakness
across AI suppliers and Energy even as markets stabilised. Global Flow Snapshot Systematic and retail de-risking drove the
AI/Momentum shock, with Friday’s rebound offering the first signs of dip-buying into a cleaner trade. US AI/Momentum
de-risking became an index-level positioning shock Thursday, driven primarily by systematic and retail supply rather than exceptional
HF capitulation. Thursday was the highest Retail volume day since Liberation Day. HFs were selling throughout the week but given
they’d already reduced positioning in previous weeks, the magnitude of selling this week was not especially large. UBS Systematic
Advisory attribution analysis shows HFs were making money on relative trades within AI niches (Neoclouds, Optical & Networking)
despite the overall baskets being down significantly. Retail saw a significant rotation from Tech into short-duration Treasury ETFs.
Thursday's aggressive opening selloff subsequently flipped to buy-the-dip. Friday’s rebound, led by AI Winners, Semis and passive
demand in Memory, is the first evidence of investors dipping back into a substantially cleaner trade. Market Internals expert Rebecca
Cheong expects a market low on Monday, driven by a final bout of Risk Control strategy selling before real money (fundamental
investors) and light positioning of other systematic strategies like CTAs supports the market. In Asia last week, Korea, Japan and
Taiwan suffered a double-digit AI-led sell-off. Korean foreign selling overwhelmed local buyers and erased the prior week's
earnings rebound; Japanese hedge funds sold semiconductor equipment while long-only accounts bought defensives. Concerns about
tighter US AI rules and slower data-centre deployment sharpened the debate over returns on AI spending, while estimated US
systematic selling compounded the pressure. Options clients mostly reduced risk or monetized hedges rather than rebuilding outright
longs. “Anti-AI” markets such as India and China were the countercurrents – China internet and tech-ETF cash buying drew support
from futures and CSI upside demand while India Banks and automakers led the advance and both real-money and fast-money
accounts bought Nifty futures. EMEA Flow Summary European equities finished only fractionally higher, but this masks both a
sharp reversal from a 2.5% intraweek and acceleration in thematic rotation. The week was punctuated midway by the US
midterm Democratic sweep which reengaged the rotation away from the supply-chain side culminating in a major sell-off on Thursday
which spilled into Europe. The SXXP ended the week +0.2%, with European Semis -10% and Energy -8.3%, while Defence Spending
fell 9.9% as RheinMetall’s profit warning drove a 21% stock decline. The counter-rotation was a pro-cyclical ex-Energy one, with
less rates volatility and lower oil trades being well rewarded:  Rate Cut Winners +4.7%, High Energy Costs +4.9%, Airlines +11.7% and
Banks +4.3%, pointing to lower oil and lower rate volatility as important drivers beyond the AI unwind.UBS Trading desks report LO
de-risking against selective HF dip-buying and short covering. High Touch activity ran c.30% above the prior five-week average
but finished balanced at just 51% better to sell; LOs 58% sell skew just managed to offset HF 64% net buy-skew. The Prime book
revealed HFs adding longs in Banks (+2.6std) and Autos (+3.0std), but simultaneously cut European Crowded Longs (-0.9std),
Energy (-2.5std) and Renewables (-1.7std). The Flow Derivatives team saw investors reacting to the daily tide changes in the second
half of the week, selling downside structures on European indices when Wednesday looked like a clearing event, only to revert to
adding protection on Thursday’s sell-off.  HF seemingly chasing tactical opportunities rather than signalling any strong directional
conviction. The AI supply chain was sold on the volatility; investors continued to reduce exposure even as markets stabilised
into Friday. High Touch reported heavy LO selling into the pronounced AI/semi unwind, although HFs selectively added on weakness.
In Industrials, selling was concentrated in AI-related industries like Electricals and Construction, while Defence was also sold on
weakness. Tech was sold throughout the week on the Electronic Desk, even on Friday’s Semiconductor recovery. Investors
were pressing weakness across the AI suppliers rather than sensing an opportunity.Banks and Travel & Leisure stood out as areas
of buying against the broader de-risking, although Bank demand was at least partly short covering. Banks rose 4.3% and
finished 61/39 better bid on High Touch, with HF demand described as largely covering, while Prime showed +2.6std Bank long
buying. Reflecting the pro-Cyclical outperformance, Travel & Leisure was also strongly bought at 79% better to buy as Airlines rallied
11.7%, likely helped by the decline in oil prices following the midterms. In contrast, Energy positioning was reduced overall, with High
Touch seeing considerable selling and Prime reporting EU Energy exposure cut by a further -2.5std, although Electronic flows
showed selective buying into the sector’s 8.3% fall. For another week, Healthcare attracted the demand for defensives across desks.

Desk comments
High Touch Cash Trading – Joshua Jones (joshua-za.jones@ubs.com); Aymen Al-Omishy
(aymen.al-omishy@ubs.com)
• HT activity was c.30% above the previous 5-week avg, with the desk 51% better to sell overall.
Importantly, the investor split was divergent: HFs were 64% better to buy, while LOs were 58% better to sell,
suggesting more monetisation and risk reduction from traditional money against continued tactical HF
deployment.
• Industrials were the busiest sector and finished the week better for sell (56/44) led by LOs, while HFs
were net buyers. Cap Goods saw overwhelming supply, driven by Electricals and Construction. A&D was more
idiosyncratic, with buying in Aerospace offset by selling in Defence. Beneath the surface, the strongest
demand was in Aerospace, Transport, Logistics and Construction, while supply was most pronounced in
Electricals, Defence and Materials.
• Banks were also busy this week (17% of turnover) and finished better bid overall (61/39), with
Wednesday very much the key session in driving the skew. Turnover stepped up considerably following the
midterm relief rally, which saw the SX7E climb c.5%. Demand felt largely driven by HF covering, while LOs
were more cautious in deploying risk. Across the week, both HFs (97/3) and LOs (57/43) finished better bid.
Regionally, Spanish and Italian names saw more balanced two-way flow, while UK and Dutch banks saw
demand.
• Healthcare (13% of turnover) finished firmly better bid (65/35), with demand centred on large-cap
pharma and largely driven by LOs. The sector continues to attract longer term defensive positioning, with the
SXDP now the best performer over the past month (+15% MoM). With earnings season broadly out of the way
and few obvious near term catalysts on the horizon, clients appear increasingly comfortable leaning into the
relative safety of the sector.
• Technology flows were much better to buy, but the underlying picture was more nuanced.  The
broader tech tape saw heavy LO selling into the pronounced AI/semi unwind, while HFs were prepared to add
selectively on weakness. That divergence feels important as the debate shifts from simply owning AI
beneficiaries towards questioning the pace and scale of US data-centre build-out, leaving investors
increasingly focused on resilient “ex-AI” opportunities rather than taking broad directional exposure while the
theme remains under pressure.
• Energy was considerably better to sell as crude sold off sharply on the US midterm results (Brent
~$98.75), along with Personal Care/Staples, Insurance, Retail and Food & Beverage which all saw notable
supply (>70% better to sell), suggesting the week’s selling was not confined to crowded growth but included
pockets of defensive sectors  as well. Conversely, demand remained evident across Financial Services
(65%), Travel & Leisure (79%) and, albeit on small volumes, Media (94%). Overall, the flows highlight that
given the volatility investors are becoming increasingly more selective: LOs are taking risk down after the
recent run, while HFs continue to cover shorts or rotate capital into specific opportunities.

Flow Derivatives – Maximilian Frueh ( )
• Directional activity was relatively muted ahead of the US midterms. Early in the week, flows were
dominated by the systematic community, with investors rolling SX5E calls from Dec26 into Mar27, calendar
activity from vol accounts and systematic vol selling from premium harvesters.
• During Wednesday’s post midterm rally, activity picked up notably, with flows skewed towards buying
delta and selling vol. Investors sold puts and put spreads, unwinding SX5E and SX7E protection built over
recent weeks, while more modest upside demand emerged via SX5E Dec26 call spreads. Following
Thursday’s AI led selloff, investors lifted SX5E Dec26 put spreads, with flows turning somewhat panicky. As
spot stabilised near the session lows and into the European cash close, year end upside buying emerged.
• Outside equities, investors expressed a constructive view on gold through Eurex listed IGLN LN call
spreads versus puts and by selling elevated Mar27 downside vol, taking advantage of the recent selloff and
rich skew.
