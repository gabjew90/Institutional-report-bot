 
JPMorgan Co-Head Of Trading: Market Finally 
Focusing On AI Circularity 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, NOV 06, 2026 - 11:32 PM 
JPMorgan's Market Intel team has been one of the most bullish trading desks on Wall Street, 
keeping its tactically bullish view for the past few months, even as the the S&P slump 
accelerated and the Nasdaq tumbled into a correction, while many of JPM's peers turned far 
more conservative if not openly bearish. Well, this morning, following the worst plunge for the 
Nasdaq since Covid, which wiped out all YTD gains for tech funds, and the biggest drop for the 
S&P since Liberation Day... 
 
... the Andrew Tyler-led Market Intel team is scrambling to preserve their optimism, and in this 
morning note, writes that "despite the challenging October environment and residual headwinds 
to our Tactical Bullish call, we are sticking with the view" and here's why:  
●​ First, when you see a flush like we witnessed on Friday there tends to be a near-term 
rebound. Speaking with John Schlegel and the Positioning Intel team, they ran some 
numbers and find that when the NDX is within ~6% of ATH highs and you see a 3.5%+ 
 

 
decline, the index tends to rebound ~3% the following week. The effect tends to be 
stronger if that 3.5% decline occurs on a Friday, perhaps reflecting a higher level of 
de-grossing driving the move rather than a shift in fundamental views. I understand this 
sounds like a baseball stat and that weekend headlines around AI were negative on 
capital raise (CNBC), circular financing (WSJ), and legal (CNBC) may give some 
investors pause but we think the combination of a strengthening broadening trend plus 
stabilization in Tech will push the SPX higher.​
  
●​ Second, this week’s data can assuage stagflationary / recessionary fears. There was a 
spike in client question on the health of the US economy if AI capex declines and 
whether we see a significant recession, mirroring the Tech Bubble burst at the beginning 
of the century. My views is No given (i) health of the consumer – household net worth is 
at record levels with cash levels also historically strong … consumer checking accounts 
have quintupled since COVID which was at a historical high. Further, there remains 
significant room to ramp debt capacity based on debt service as a percentage of 
disposable income. The strongest period of credit strength in US history was 
late-2021/early-2022 and we are not yet seeing credit card delinquency / charge-off data 
fully normalize to pre-COVID levels; (ii) businesses unlikely to see margin compression – 
public businesses are experiencing record / near-record profit margins and that would 
need to compress before mass layoffs begin; (iii) AI is not a significant driver of hiring – 
generally, AI is thought to be a headwind to hiring and on the construction side that is 
where the human capital demand exists but is tempered by current immigration policy. 
Thought differently, for all of the turmoil caused by CRWV it is a company that employs 
550 people. I do not want to be dismissive of a potential domino effect but given the AI 
monetization questions it may be the case that the biggest effects are capex declines 
(affecting GDP growth metrics but not necessarily a material impact on labor markets or 
spending) and potential hyperscaler layoffs than an economy-wide phenomenon. 
Separately, the health of the consumer and subsequent spending remain robust with the 
biggest impact being a negative wealth effect from a bear market. We are seeing an 
increase in the %age of spending tied to consumers monetizing their investment 
accounts rather than checking or savings accounts. ​
  
●​ Third, the biggest non-AI risk stems from a re-escalation in the Middle East. If you have 
not seen the WSJ article on the considerable damage inflicted to US regional bases by 
 

 
Iran, it is worth a read (here). This situation combined with depleted munition levels 
suggests that another wave of attacks could be limited in scope and duration with 
diminished ability to repel the Iran counterattack. This may point to heightened 
diplomatic efforts. In the event of another series of attacks, oil markets appear to have 
already priced this outcome suggesting limited upside to energy prices and thus limited 
room for bond yields to move and Equities to decline. More color on this can be found in 
the US / Iran bullet in the next section, where our views are shaped by work from 
Natasha Kaneva and Brendan Henrici. ​
  
●​ Lastly, positive seasonality returns. November is the strongest month, on average, this 
century. Post-midterm seasonality is also a tailwind.  
There is more in the full note (available to pro subs) but the gist is clear: the same narrative 
goalseeking we saw on Wednesday when the bulls scrambled to preserve their outlook is once 
again on full display, and Andrew Tyler's bullishness (similar to that of Marko Kolanovic once 
upon a time) will be the last to fall... but it will.  
For a much more sober take on what is really happening and why there was a historic rout 
yesterday, we go to JPM's Global Co-Head of Cash Trading Ron Adler, whose reflection of what 
is happening is much more sober. We starts off by noting that “AI remains the fulcrum point of 
the market, and I think it’s that straightforward. Investors rode their hyperscaler longs for the 
past few years, and as those companies began to fund their ambitions via the capital markets 
vs. capex, traders viewed this as a chance to go further out on the risk curve and buy the picks 
and shovels and bottlenecks."  
But, as Adler admits, "Circular revenues received a lot of the airtime, but the circular impact of 
AI on markets and tech probably didn’t get enough scrutiny while things were going well. That’s 
changed."   
Indeed it has, and we for one have done everything in our power to make that change, having 
explained to readers at every opportunity (like here and here) just how perfidious the circular 
financing web has become, and how catastrophic it will be when it eventually goes into reverse. 
 

 
 
Anyway, we go back to Adler and present the balance of his thoughts which are a must read for 
any remaining AI trade bull (not many left) as it presents a starkly sobering view of what may be 
coming:  
The trade was already teetering. META raising money at 7.5% today, a Chinese chipmaker 
putting its IPO on hold, and MRVL now down ~40% post the LITE deal announcement and HF 
stress aren’t helping. The election may have tipped the scale  
The midterms marked one of the two clearing events left for the year, however many took that 
as a sign for broader risk-on and repositioning (see RSP at the highs yesterday). The election 
will make DC growth harder (see META comments this am), and that ripple effect is impacting 
the aforementioned picks and shovels (just look at Photonics/Optics {JPUXLGHT Index}, 
Memory {JPUXMEMO Index} and the neoclouds today). And while I appreciate that 
campaigning and governing are very different, gridlock might be good, but plenty of unknowns 
remain into the new year and rhetoric will likely intensify until then. And the AI trade was a much 
larger part of the broader economy and wealth effect in the market than politicians appreciate, 
and that’s leading to much of today’s growth scare.   
Crowding remains a pain point {JP9CROW Index}. HF long/short spreads are working today 
(albeit with a bit more length after yesterday’s activity). Client questions on pain levels, factors, 
correlations, unwinding, repositioning, fund stress are multiplying into the late afternoon, and 
activity is matching the anxiety (our volumes are +50% following a busy post-election session). I 
don’t expect too many to rush in to defend (outside of defensives).  
The timing is uncomfortable. Investors were already lining up for a year-end rally…and now 
some are throwing in the towel out of frustration. Part of what we’re seeing is portfolio pain and 
 

 
indiscriminate selling. The candor of the flows today seems directionally rational; it’s the 
magnitude that’s raising eyebrows.  
Being constructive: A lot of the positioning pain has been taken. A more challenging path for 
datacenter expansion could ease pressure on the capital markets and subsequently yields. But 
right now, that’s about all I have.”
