Nearly five months after the largest US IPO on record, the free float of 
n
SpaceX has risen to nearly a third of its shares. Ahead of the public launch, 
many investors expressed concern about the impact the stock would have on 
equity index fundamentals such as concentration and valuations. The relatively 
small 5% float at IPO quieted some of those concerns, but the float has increased 
as investor lock-ups have expired. 
As its float has increased, so has the weight of SpaceX as a constituent of 
n
several major equity indices and as a holding of passive funds. Passive ETFs 
owned roughly $30 billion of SpaceX at the end of October, with roughly $20 
billion of that total attributable to funds benchmarked to the Nasdaq-100. We 
expect upcoming rebalances to drive modest additional increases in index 
weights for the stock. SpaceX is not currently an S&P 500 constituent, and 
current index rules point to potential inclusion in H2 2027 unless a corporate 
action accelerates the process. 
The impact of SpaceX on equity index performance, concentration, and 
n
valuation has been much smaller than many investors expected. Excluding 
SpaceX, the P/E of the Nasdaq-100 would be less than half a turn lower than its 
current multiple. Likewise, the return and volatility of the index in recent months 
would have been very similar including or excluding the stock.

The equity index impact of SpaceX inclusion so far
 
 

The equity index impact of SpaceX inclusion so far 
 
 
Roughly a third of SpaceX shares are now part of the company’s public float, up 
from 5% at IPO. Based on SpaceX’s lockup schedule, about half of its shares should be 
part of the public float by the middle of 2027, consistent with the historical precedent 
from large IPOs with low initial floats. 
 
The average weight of SpaceX in US equity indices has risen with its free float and 
should continue to grow as free float rises, all else equal. The largest US equity indices 
generally weight constituents in proportion to free float market cap, although the exact 
proportion varies across indices, as does the frequency with which each index 
rebalances constituent weights. While SpaceX was the largest US IPO on record in terms 
of both absolute market cap and market cap relative to the size of the equity market, its 
low free float at launch resulted in the company carrying a relatively small weight in most 
indices for the first few months post-IPO. These weights should increase as free float 
rises and indices rebalance.  
One notable exception is the Nasdaq-100, where the ongoing increase in SpaceX 
free float should have a relatively muted mechanical impact on index weight. 
Following an update to its index methodology earlier this year, the Nasdaq-100 weights 
constituents with free float below 33% using a multiplier of 3x the value of float market 
cap. Once float exceeds 33%, the company is weighted using its full listed market cap.

primarily to individual investors. Mutual funds and hedge funds will report their positions

modest increase in passive ownership during the past few months suggest that insiders 
and individuals own the majority of SpaceX shares today.
