Prime Perspective: Spike in ETF shorts confirms
increased hedging yesterday. But single name activity
does not point to signs of panic.
 
Following a challenging October (link), HF performance experienced further erosion yesterday (h/t
Marco Laicini):
 
Global Fundamental L/S managers down -1.3% (a -2 SD move), driven mainly by market/beta exposure.
YTD performance is now +8.7%. 
•    TMT L/S funds fell more than -4% and are now roughly flat to slightly positive YTD, down from about
+24% before the June/July AI sell-off.
•    US-focused funds down -1.7%, now up 5.5% YTD. 
 
• Systematic L/S managers up +0.2%. Gains in the U.S. and EM Asia offset losses in Europe and DM Asia.
Now up +16% YTD.
 
• Multi-Strats managers down -1.4%, driven by sell-off in high-beta names, Info Tech, Momentum (down 3.7
SD), and negative  idiosyncratic returns.  
 
 
 
 
Observations from the GS franchise point to signs of increased market stress and panic (h/t Lee
Coppersmith, Ryan Sharkey):

• Vol Panic Index - Our gauge surged to 9.57 out of 10 yesterday, firmly back in the panic zone (9.5+).
The ~5pt increase was the 3rd largest daily jump ever, which followed Wednesday’s huge decline (4th
largest drop ever). The combined two-day swing was the largest on record.
• VIX volatility (VVIX) saw the 2nd largest one-day increase EVER, behind only “Volmageddon” in Feb ‘18.
• Record Put Volumes - Cboe saw >11mm puts trade yesterday, the largest put volume day on record,
surpassing the previous high in Apr ’25. 
• Broad-Based Market Drawdown Led by Tech: NDX -6.4% (worst 1-day drawdown since 3/16/20), R2K
-4.5% (worst 1-day drawdown since 4/3/25), SPX -4.4% (worst 1-day drawdown since 4/4/25).
• Many of our AI related baskets experienced their biggest down day since the launch of ChatGPT.
 
That being said, while yesterday's Prime flows show increased hedging activity via Macro Products,
from a single stock perspective, they do NOT point to signs of panic and an acceleration of de-
grossing activity from the recent pace.  This suggests discretionary HFs were not the main driver of
yesterday’s market price action, in our view.
 
Consider the following:
 
US-listed ETF shorts increased +7% yesterday - the largest 1-day increase since March and the third
largest increase in the past five years, led by shorting in Large Cap Equity ETFs. 
 
 
 

Global equities saw de-grossing activity for a 4th straight session (8 of last 9), but yesterday's pace of
risk unwinds was relatively modest (-0.5 SDs 1-year).
 
 
 
From a net flows perspective, global equities saw the larges net selling in two weeks (-1.6 SDs 1-year),
driven by long-and-short sales (2.2 to 1).
• Nearly all major regions were net sold yesterday (sans DM Asia), led by EM Asia (Taiwan, Korea, China all
net sold), North America, and Europe.
• 7 of 11 global sectors were net sold, led by Info Tech (long-and-short sales), Consumer Disc (long-and-short
sales), Industrials (long-and-short sales), while Energy (long buys + short covers), Financials (short covers),
and Real Estate (long buys) were the most net bought.
 

 
 
Global AI stocks (GSXGGLAI constituents) collectively were net sold for a 6th consecutive session
(-2.6 SDs 1-year), driven by long-and-short sales (2.2 to 1).
• On a trailing 20-day basis, the recent pace of selling of global AI stocks by HFs is the largest in more
than five years (-3.0 SDs 5-year).
 

 
HF Risk Exposures
• Overall book Gross Leverage -0.7 pts DoD to 314.2% (85th percentile 1-year, 97th percentile 5-year).
• Overall book Net Leverage -1.5 pts DoD to 71.8% (4th percentile 1-year, 37th percentile 5-year).
 
HFs have actively unwound risk in the past month, but it's worth noting that Gross leverage "ratio"
has actually increased month/month due to mark-to-market.  Essentially, while Gross MV (the
numerator) has fallen sharply, Fund Equity (the denominator) has fallen at an even faster pace on the
back of performance losses.
