**Sales & Trading Commentary – For Institutional Use Only – Not a Product of Research**
UBS US Equity Weekly
Happy Friday from UBS, 10 Things I learned this week with my US glasses on..
• Politics has overtaken macro as the immediate market driver
The Democratic sweep shifts the US debate from purely Fed/inflation risk toward policy risk around AI and
Data Centres. AI investment has contributed roughly 1ppt to recent GDP growth, while cumulative AI
buildout plans have been estimated at $10.3tn through 2032. The critical distinction is whether policymakers
stop development or instead require hyperscalers to fund their own power, grid connections and water
infrastructure. Macro remains important. UBS Economics still sees the Fed biased toward 25bp hikes in
December and January unless labour markets deteriorate materially. Inflation remains broad, with around
50% of PCE components running above 3%, while AI investment provides an important growth offset.
Takeaway: The market is shifting from macro toward politics, with AI regulation and funding becoming an
increasingly important bridge between policy, growth and earnings.
• AI is not breaking, but funding and deployment are becoming the real constraints
2027 AI visibility remains strong: cloud growth is expected to accelerate from 64% in 2026 to 97% in 2027,
cloud backlog is ~3.5x forward 12-month sales and US DC vacancy is below 2%. Beyond 2027 the funding
question becomes harder: top-13 hyperscaler capex is forecast at $1.77tn vs $1.47tn operating cash flow,
implying a ~$300bn funding gap.
The opportunity is therefore becoming more selective. Palantir (Buy, PT $250) grew revenue 96% and US
Commercial 148%, validating enterprise AI adoption despite flat RPO. In physical infrastructure, tighter DC
regulation could actually increase electrical and cooling content per MW. The reason is that more
self-sufficient capacity requires additional power generation, conversion and redundancy for the same MW of
IT load, while higher rack densities and tighter water constraints increase the cooling equipment required to
support each MW. After a 35%+ de-rating, AI Industrials trade around 16x 2028E EPS vs 20x+
through-cycle, with Johnson Controls (Buy, PT $190) the preferred exposure; Eaton, nVent, Vertiv, INNIO
and Modine are also among preferred names.
Takeaway: AI is moving from a pure demand debate toward funding + permitting + equipment intensity +
enterprise monetisation.
• The equity sell-off has moved from rotation into correlation, but earnings remain supportive
Thursday marked a meaningful change in market internals: 85% of S&P 500 constituents declined, SPX fell
4.7%, NDX 6.4% and SOXX 12.2%, while VIX jumped almost 12 points toward 29. The 3x-long SOXL fund fell

2
39% and is now ~75% below early-October levels. Stock volatility is finally transmitting into the headline index
rather than merely generating rotations beneath it.
Yet the fundamental picture looks considerably healthier. Q3 SPX EPS is running +31.6%, while the
referenced 8,100 year-end target implies >10% upside. Technical Strategy also remains constructive, with
lower oil/yields and stabilising rate-sensitive assets more consistent with a correction inside a broader equity
uptrend than recession.
Takeaway: The tape has moved from rotation to correlated de-risking, but earnings have not yet confirmed
a fundamental bear case.
• Healthcare offers some of the cleanest 2027 catalyst asymmetry
Amgen (Buy, PT $530 from $470) delivered $10.4bn revenue vs $10.1bn consensus and EPS of $6.45 vs
$5.79. Repatha grew 37%, Evenity 39% and Imdelltra 128%. MariTide Phase III obesity data around Feb/Mar
2027 are the major catalyst, while Imdelltra could support $3-4bn+ peak sales. Vertex (Buy, PT $640 from
$615) has its Povetacicept decision on 30 November, followed by early-2027 Inaxaplin data carrying an
estimated 65-70% probability of success and potentially 10-20% share upside. Protagonist Therapeutics
(Buy, PT $184) provides another differentiated setup, with Mimrylo royalties forecast at $55m in 2027 vs
$28m consensus. UBS is also increasingly constructive on delayed AVANZAR/Dato-DXd data, while Merck's
Sac-TMT could become a $10bn+ franchise.
Takeaway: Amgen = diversified growth + obesity/oncology; Vertex = renal diversification; PTGX =
underappreciated launch trajectory.
• Consumer is increasingly about scale, self-help and avoiding fully valued turnarounds
US demographic work suggests nominal retail growth could slow toward ~3.5% annually through 2045,
increasing the importance of market-share gains and productivity. That structurally favours Walmart (Buy, PT
$130) and Costco (Buy, PT $1,275), while ageing shifts spending toward needs-based categories such as
Healthcare, food and home maintenance.  
Dutch Bros (Buy, PT $75) remains the stronger restaurant growth asymmetry: Q3 system comps reached
4.4%, traffic remained positive and ~250 shops are planned for 2027 vs ~215 consensus. Starbucks
(Neutral, PT $110 from $105) has made its turnaround more credible, with ~4% comps and 13.5-14.0% FY27
margins achievable, but ~33x FY27E EPS already discounts significant recovery.
A geopolitical normalisation adds tactical Softlines upside. UBS's preferred beneficiaries are Gap (Buy, PT
$42), Under Armour (Buy, PT $8), Carter's (Neutral, PT $33), G-III Apparel (Neutral, PT $27) and PVH
(Buy, PT $121) through lower fuel/freight costs, improving purchasing power and recovering Middle East
demand. These are tactical 1-3 month preferences rather than changes to the underlying ratings.
Takeaway: Walmart/Costco offer scale, Dutch Bros offers structural growth at a reset valuation, Starbucks
is improving but priced, and selected Softlines offer tactical geopolitical asymmetry.
• Autos and aerospace offer different but attractive structural inflections
Rivian (Buy from Neutral, PT $17) has several catalysts aligning. R2 demo drives exceeded 103k in Q3 vs
>57k in Q2, UBS assumes ~82k R2 deliveries in 2027, automotive gross margins could become positive in
Q1'27 and approach 9% by Q4, while Software & Services provides a higher-margin second growth leg.
Liquidity of ~$7.1bn, potentially rising to ~$13bn including targeted future capital, materially reduces funding
risk. StandardAero (Buy, PT $34 from $32) offers the more visible analogue: 2027 capacity is essentially
sold out, Q3 EBITDA grew 15%, margins expanded 130bp to 14.4%, LEAP provides a long-duration
aftermarket runway and the $350m accelerated repurchase adds support. The ageing US vehicle parc favours
AutoZone (Buy, PT $4,230) and O'Reilly Automotive (Buy, PT $122), UBS's preferred Auto Aftermarket
exposures. AutoZone's mega hubs can reduce delivery times from ~3 hours to as little as 45 minutes, while
O'Reilly's distribution network supports continued commercial/DIFM share gains.

3
Takeaway: Rivian is a higher-risk margin/product inflection; StandardAero and AutoZone/O'Reilly offer
more visible structural compounding.
• Industrials are increasingly about removing overhangs and converting visibility into cash
3M (Buy, PT $230) settled ~99% of current PFAS personal-injury claims for a $5.8bn present-value cost vs
~$10bn investor expectations, removing a major ownership barrier. The simultaneous $6bn buyback
authorisation adds EPS support, while UBS forecasts 2027 EPS of $10.47 vs $9.92 consensus.  Packaging
Corp. of America (Buy, PT $289) offers another form of visibility. Effective containerboard supply appears
tighter than headline capacity, volumes remain resilient and Greif synergies are already $40-50m annualised
vs a $60m target. UBS expects approximately 30% EPS growth in 2027, although the DOJ investigation
remains the key overhang.
Takeaway: The strongest Industrial setups combine visible demand + operating leverage + cash
generation/capital returns, rather than simply macro sensitivity.
• Demographics are becoming an increasingly powerful US stock-selection framework
The global silver economy could reach ~$8tn annually by 2030, structurally benefiting Healthcare, wealth
management, premium travel, longevity and automation. Labour scarcity simultaneously strengthens the
economic case for AI and robotics. In the US, population growth has slowed from 1.3% in 1993 to 0.5% in
2025, while 74% of the projected 9.1m increase in households over the next decade comes from
households headed by someone aged 65+. Ageing favours Healthcare, food and home maintenance, while
discretionary categories tied to younger households face a tougher backdrop. Walmart (Buy, $130) and
Costco (Buy, $1,275) are particularly well positioned because scale and productivity allow them to grow
through market-share capture even as industry growth slows.
Takeaway: Demographics increasingly favour scale, Healthcare, wealth, automation and needs-based
consumption, while reducing the contribution from organic category growth.
• Visible earnings are winning in a difficult biotech and Specialty Pharma tape
Viatris (Buy, PT $28 from $26) combines improving 2027 EBITDA with potential EXPAREL IP-related
re-rating. Amneal (Buy, PT $28 from $25) provides the cleaner earnings-growth story, with generic
lanreotide, Women's Health and other higher-value products improving product mix and margins. In
Diagnostics, GRAIL (Buy, PT $175) is UBS's preferred post-earnings setup. Galleri volumes missed
expectations, but the Q1'27 FDA decision and subsequent CMS reimbursement remain the more important
catalysts, while repeat patients have increased to ~36% from ~30%. Veracyte faces a genuine growth reset;
Caris delivered a healthier quarter but appears closer to fair value.
Takeaway: In a difficult Healthcare tape, the best risk/reward increasingly sits in commercial earnings
visibility and identifiable regulatory catalysts rather than purely binary clinical exposure.
• The best US opportunities combine a 2027 earnings inflection with a valuation or positioning
disconnect
Kontoor Brands (Buy, PT $125 from $136) has a path toward $7+ FY27 EPS, Helly Hansen growing ~8%
YTD, Q3 gross margin expanding 240bp and ~$450m of planned repurchases. Yet the shares trade around
12x FY27E earnings against ~19% expected five-year EPS CAGR. FICO (Neutral, PT $942) is the inverse
setup. Q4 beat, but FY27 guidance was withheld as mortgage pricing shifts toward bespoke contracts and
VantageScore increases competition. FY27 EPS falls to $45.50 from $51.25, while buybacks are paused.
 Positioning adds another dimension. Hedge funds and asset managers entered the midterms significantly 
bearish USD, only for equity weakness and stronger payrolls to trigger a safe-haven USD rally. China Tech
meanwhile became a relative winner as policy support coincided with short-covering from the crowded
long-memory/short-China trade.
Debates this week

4
• Has politics replaced macro? AI/DC regulation, state permitting and infrastructure funding became
immediate equity variables after the midterms.
• Is AI slowing or merely changing architecture? 2027 visibility remains strong, but the ~$300bn
hyperscaler funding gap makes financing and post-2027 capacity increasingly important.
• Was Thursday capitulation or the beginning of something fundamental? The sharp re-correlation
contrasts with still-strong Q3 earnings.
• Which 2027 inflections are underpriced? Rivian, Amgen, Vertex, StandardAero, Packaging Corp. and
Kontoor offer identifiable catalysts capable of resetting earnings expectations.
• Where has valuation already caught up? Apple and Starbucks retain credible franchises but offer less
asymmetry at current valuations.

Bottom line: five narratives for the desk
1. Politics is the new incremental macro variable.
Midterms shift attention toward AI permitting, infrastructure funding and hyperscaler economics, with
Fed/CPI still the macro backdrop.
2. AI remains structurally intact, but the funding debate is real.
Demand remains strong, but deployment capacity and financing matter increasingly. Palantir validates
enterprise adoption while higher equipment intensity could benefit Johnson Controls and other
Electrical/Thermal suppliers.
3. The sell-off is finally reaching the index.
Correlations have risen sharply, but strong Q3 earnings mean it remains premature to interpret the move as a
fundamental recession signal.
4. 2027 earnings inflections remain the strongest stock-selection tool.
Rivian, Amgen, Vertex, StandardAero, Packaging Corp. and Kontoor have identifiable catalysts capable of
materially changing their earnings profiles.
5. Valuation and positioning matter again.
Apple, Starbucks and FICO represent more balanced setups, while USD and China Tech illustrate how quickly
crowded positioning can dominate near-term performance.
Overall: the US market is becoming less about buying the broad AI/growth basket and more about finding
companies where structural demand, 2027 earnings catalysts, capital availability and valuation still
align.
