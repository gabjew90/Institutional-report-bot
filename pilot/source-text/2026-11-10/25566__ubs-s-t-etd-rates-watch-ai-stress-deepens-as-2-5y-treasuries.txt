ETD Rates Watch: AI Stress Deepens as
2-5Y Treasuries Catch a Safety Bid
Daniel Noorian
06-Nov-2026, 03:53 ET (EST)
When they warned that AI could lead to the extinction of the human race, I am not sure anyone thought it
would start by nuking the stock market, but here we are. No surprises to see stocks battered in Asia trading on
Friday after Thursday’s 5.52% fall in the Nasdaq. The Nikkei and Kospi were both down around 6% as tech
names ran into further selling. In South Korea, rules around maintaining collateral worth 140% of margin loans
were waived in an attempt to prevent forced selling.
The one consolation is that at 0700 GMT on Friday, E-mini S&P 500 futures are down 0.12% while the Nasdaq
is up 0.64%. However, looking at the early weakness in European markets, it is questionable how long that
lasts. Europe managed to escape the brunt of the moves on Thursday, but the concern now will be whether
this turns into a broader market rout.
In fixed income, JGB futures gapped higher at the open, up 125 ticks from Thursday’s close at 128.29. USTs
continued to grind higher but still remain below Thursday's high of 107-16. The SOFR strip was up as much as
four, with a December rate hike now priced at a 66% chance, down from 81% on Wednesday.
There was some huge option flow during Asia hours on Friday, with 50k of the Week 1 TY 107.50/108 call
spreads bought for 10. Those expire Friday and have a 26 delta, so that is the equivalent of buying 13k TY
futures. There was also a block buyer of 8,200 TUZ6. Much of the flow seen on Thursday was focused in the
2-5y bucket.
It is nonfarm payrolls on Friday, but I am not sure that really matters given all eyes will be on the stock market.
Based on the incoming data this week, UBS' US Economics has revised up its projection. They expect
nonfarm payroll employment to have expanded by 75k in October, with private employment also expanding by
75k and government employment flat over the month. Market consensus is 70k. The initial and continuing
claims data, on net, also look better than last week.
Thursday's largest SOFR option flow was a buyer of 35k SFRZ6 95.9375/96.0625/96.1875 call flies from 1.5
up to 2.25, as paper positioned for the Fed to pass on a December hike. I think it is a little early to draw
conclusions from the carnage in AI at the moment. However, the Fed will be considering the wealth effect from
the US equity market going into reverse, as well as the possible impact on employment and inflation from any
reversal in data-centre construction. I suspect that if the moves in equities continue, the market is going to see
a lot more of these Fed-on-hold plays.
Some notable open-interest changes Thursday, according to CME preliminary open interest: in SOFR, SFRU7
open interest rose 5.24%, while SFRZ7 dropped 5.2%. There was heavy volume across spreads featuring
SFRZ7, such as SFRM7/Z7 and SFRZ7/Z8. Across USTs, the largest increases came in TUZ6, up 1.31%, and
TYZ6, up 1.44%.
On that note, the Richmond Fed recently published a piece on data-centre construction in the Fifth District,
which is home to more than 20% of the nation’s data centres. In its October business survey, it asked firms
about their direct or indirect involvement in data-centre construction. Of the 376 respondents, 31% said they
had some involvement. Among those involved, around 68% said data-centre-related activity accounted for

2
less than 10% of their revenue. Firms more closely related to data-centre construction also faced greater
increases in costs, prices and wages. Those directly involved were more upbeat about future hiring.
What’s Rebecca Cheong’s take on all of this? She thinks retail investors could sell more on Friday, particularly
ahead of the weekend as positions are squared, but are close to the capitulation point given Thursday's flow
reversal.
Three capitulation signals have now been triggered. Excess sell flow reached -$365bn, the largest on record.
UBS RMM flow bought the dip at the 68th percentile versus 3y, following 1.25 days of selling that represented
the largest selling episode since Covid. The VIX Cross-Asset Rank reached 96%, suggesting equity volatility is
exceptionally elevated relative to virtually every other asset class. Taken together, these signals point to
significant forced selling and oversold conditions. Her expectation is that SPX could establish a low on Monday
as systematic selling and hedge-fund unwind pressure subsequently begin to fade.
There is still potentially a lot of mechanical selling to get through first. Rebecca Cheong estimates that Risk
Control, Leveraged ETFs and CTAs sold a combined $170-210bn, with Risk Control potentially needing to sell
another $200-270bn over the following two days. After Monday, however, she expects both Risk Control and
CTA sell-rebalancing flows to become much smaller.
The other thing to watch is risk transfers around data-centre loans. There have been reports over the last two
weeks that banks are looking for insurance on loan portfolios. Bloomberg reports ING has put a $500m
portfolio of data-centre loans up for sale via a “bids wanted in competition” process. The loans include debt
tied to Vantage Data Centers and CyrusOne.
Investors will also be watching for signs of fund lock-ups. The Financial Times [paywall] reports that
Blackstone is doubling lock-up periods for some Asian investors in its flagship retail private-equity fund. New
Singapore and Hong Kong clients in the $29bn BXPE fund will be forced to hold shares for a minimum of four
years before selling or pay a penalty fee. There is no suggestion at all that this is related to AI, but it is
something to think about given private-credit exposure to the sector.
In October Reuters reported that private credit could be called on to finance about $1tn of the AI compute
build-out. What happens next is questionable. Something worth considering here is that when investors need
to raise cash, it is normally the most liquid investments that get hit first.
