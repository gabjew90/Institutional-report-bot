Nonfarm Validates December Hike — Cue Inflation 
• 
Nonfarm payrolls posted a solid gain in line with our above-consensus estimate 
• 
Gains were driven by health, professional and AI-related categories 
• 
Wage growth has been accelerating 
• 
Hours worked support solid GDP growth 
• 
Persistent under sampling will continue to feed high revision risk 
• 
The FOMC is focused on inflation, but a solid job market adds to the hawkish case 
 
Nonfarm payrolls m/m 000s / UR %, October, SA: 
Actual: 165 / 4.1 
Scotia: 100 / 4.2 
Consensus: 70 / 4.2 
Prior: 60 / 4.2 (revised from 29 / 4.2) 
Two-month payroll revision: +84k 
 
The US economy broadly met expectations for a solid report and my above-consensus 
estimate. 165k jobs were created with solid details including more upward revisions that 
will keep the heat on the Federal Reserve to 
hike in December pending further information 
like next week’s CPI and other developments. 
19bps of a hike is now priced and this is driving 
higher short-term bond yields. 
Two sectors drove much of the gain (chart 1). 
They were business services and education and 
health services but with the need to break that 
down finer. 
The health and social services sector added 
55k jobs which is the fastest pace of hiring 
since April. 42,000 health care workers were 
hired including 16k at hospitals, 11k at home 
health care services and 5k at outpatient care 
centres.  
In seasonally unadjusted terms this October 
saw elevated hiring of health professionals 
compared to prior years (charts 2, 3). The effect 
was tamped down by going relatively low on 
the health sector SA factor (chart 4). 
I would expect more elevated health sector 
hiring to come given the earlier than normal 
start to the respiratory virus season. 
Where I was a bit surprised was in not seeing 
clear evidence of a weather effect given how 
unusually mild it was been across much of N.A. 
this Fall. Sectors like leisure and hospitality  
(-13k), retail (+6k), and construction (-3k) 
showed no real weather-effects. Perhaps that 
lies ahead with El Niño surging?

Professional and business services were up strongly 
at 70k and hence more than anticipated which 
explains much of the overshoot relative to my 
overall nonfarm estimate. The gain was driven by 
professional, scientific and technical services (33k), 
admin support and waste management (28k) and 
company management (9k).  
Wages are picking up with average hourly wages up 
4.5% m/m SAAR in October after 3.2% in 
September (chart 5). 
Hours worked posted a gain of 0.1% m/m in 
October and are tracking a 1.2% q/q SAAR 
expansion in Q4 which is similar to the prior 1.3% 
rise (chart 6).  
The unemployment rate dipped a tick to 4.1% 
because the companion household survey 
registered a job gain of 463k that exceeded the 
expansion of the labour force at 421k. 
Revisions remain high with 84k extra jobs found in 
the prior two months (chart 7). Under sampling on 
first pass will drive further revisions the next time 
(chart 8). 
Charts 9–10 show that the seasonally unadjusted 
gain was higher than usual and so was the SA 
factor. 
Chart 11 shows private sector jobs outside of 
health care are doing rather well. 
Charts 12-14 break out some sectors.
