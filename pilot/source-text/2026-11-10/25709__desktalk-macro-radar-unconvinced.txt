DeskTalk - Macro radar: Unconvinced
Markets
Preston Pagli
20 hours ago
A partial rebound in tech is helping US equity benchmarks recover from Thursday's historic selloff, while USTs have
settled in after an initial sell-off after October payrolls more than doubled expectations (+165k), leaving yields
modestly higher across the front end.
The dominant story remains a tentative recovery in AI-linked names after Thursday’s reckoning which saw the NDX
suffer its largest single-day market value loss on record, wiping out roughly $2.3tn. Sentiment has improved on
comments from Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa suggesting existing data-center construction projects will be
allowed to proceed while AI-related legislation is finalized.
Beneath the surface, the market is still lacking conviction that near-term risks have cleared, with more than 60% of the
SPX lower and our cash equity desk seeing inflows skewed toward Financials and Consumer Discretionary while
outflows remain in Info Tech, Industrials, Comm Services, Healthcare, Energy, and Consumer Staples.
The rebound in tech is also taking a breather after reports Microsoft (-1%) is scaling back plans to rent AI cloud
capacity from Oracle (-2%), citing changing infrastructure requirements. The NDX (+1.2%) has since pared gains after
being up more than 2%.

Elsewhere, the dollar has retreated from intra day highs but remains firm, while CAD (-0.8%) is underperforming
following softer-than-expected employment data. Most Asian and EM currencies traded lower earlier in the session, with
AUD falling to a seven-month low. On the eFX desk, flows are skewed toward selling NZD, CAD, and CHF, while
NOK and MXN remain net buys.

Today's session reflects a tug-of-war between dueling narratives with a dip-buying bid in tech following an extreme
washout and a hawkish macro backdrop. The result is market lacking conviction after peak event risk. Looking ahead,
Tuesday's CPI report stands as the next major macro catalyst. Our economists expect core CPI to rise 0.19% MoM, with
headline inflation slowing to 0.16%, both below consensus.
