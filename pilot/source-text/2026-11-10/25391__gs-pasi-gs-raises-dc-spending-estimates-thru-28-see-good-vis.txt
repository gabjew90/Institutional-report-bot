GS Pasi - GS raises DC spending estimates thru '28 ... see good
visibility into growth 6 Nov 2026
Alisha Pasi  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Vice President, FICC & Equities
6 Nov 2026
GIR raises data center construction spending forecasts and sees good visibility into continued US data
center construction spending growth through 2028 from projects already under construction and
announced projects viewed as high probability.
Raising data center construction spending forecasts for 2026/27 to $80bn/$100bn from $67bn/$87bn
previously, and introduce 2028 data center construction spending forecast of $125bn.
While midterms results introduce additional risk, a potential outcome is extending the duration of the
cycle, rather than significantly impacting the TAM. Projects already under construction support strong
growth through ‘27 and underpin just above half of ‘28 spending forecasts, with subsequent project starts
layering on top.
Key Points:
The total data center construction pipeline is multiples of current spending activity. At baseline benchmark
of ~$3.5bn construction spending per GW, the ~86 GW currently under construction represents ~$300bn
in total construction spend (~6x ‘25 US data center construction). Layering in high-probability
announcements expands the pipeline TAM to ~$900bn.
High degree of visibility on data center spending growth next year, and solid visibility into ‘28: About 86
GW of data centers are currently under construction, providing visibility on another year of double-digit
construction growth. Layering in announced projects Aterio views as high probability, but have not yet
started, see continued double-digit construction spending growth through ‘28 even if no new data centers
are announced.
‘28 spending is peak on current project timelines, but significant potential for cycle extension. Based on
planned timelines for projects under construction and high probability projects, spending peaks in ‘28, but
with construction spending continuing into 2030 at levels greater than 2026. However, significant
uncertainty combined with a very large pipeline of medium/low probability projects creates potential for
the spending growth to extend further out.
Midterm results potentially extend the data center spending cycle, rather than reduce it in magnitude. The
states where Democrats are likely to further restrict development account for ~45% of planned data center
capacity. However, continue to see deep underlying demand, and importantly, we thought announced
project timelines were already aggressive heading into the election. See potential outcome as smoothing
spending and extending the duration of the cycle, rather than impacting the TAM.

Source: Goldman Sachs FICC & Equities, Marquee, as of 6 Nov 2026.
