What's the Most You've Ever Lost on a
Coin-Toss?
Getting Closer, but No Fat Pitch Yet
WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW: 
Last week finally saw some correlated selling, and as a result the SPX is nearing its 200
DMA, the SOXX is ~40% off its highs, and we saw the elusive 80% NYSE downside volume
day for the first time in nearly a year. While these are all necessary developments to
put in a bottom, it's far from a 'fat-pitch'. In fact, when you see the first 80% downside
day in at least three months, and when SOX closes below its 200 DMA for the first time
in at least three months, it's basically a coin-toss for 20-50 day forward returns. In fact,
the odds of another 80% NYSE downside volume day in the next 30 days are now ~73%
based on the last 30 years of data. So while it's tempting to rush back in given the
carnage we saw last week, it will likely take a move closer to 7,000-7,100 on SPX with
a bigger breadth washout to set up a 'fat pitch'.
■
Far from a Washout. Still ~58% of SPX names above their 200 DMA. A more durable
bottom typically sees less than 30% of names above 200 DMA.
■
Put/Calls Still Not Extreme. Despite the recent carnage below the surface, 20-day
put/call ratios remain well below what we saw in March and July at SPX lows.
■
The Streak is Dead, What Happens Now? The longest streak in at least 30 years
is over as we finally saw an 80% downside volume day on the NYSE. Since 1996
that has happened for the first time in at least three months 15 other times. 11 of
those times saw at least one more 80% day in the next 30 days. Average and median
returns are negative 5-10 days out, and while they turn positive after 20 days, SPX
was only higher 53% of the time 20 days later for a paltry +0.31% average return.
In other words, a coin-toss.
■
SOX closed below its 200 DMA for the first time since May 2025. Similar initial
cracks did see some strong bounces, but again it’s essentially a coin-toss and 50-day
returns are actually negative. While it’s a bit late to press tactical downside here,
we ultimately think a move back to the April breakout point (8k-8300) is necessary
as that would completely retrace the parabolic advance.
■
Long-Term Quality Reversion. This goes a bit against our tactical view because many
of these ‘quality’ names have held up well and thus are vulnerable to a ‘near-term’
unwind. Longer-term, however, high vs. low quality looks to be bottoming precisely
at the 2000 lows after a six-year bear market.
■
XLV Downside is a Fat Pitch. XLV hit an all-time high on Thursday and at one point
was nearly 20% above its 200 DMA. This is the widest spread on record. Not only
does this suggest that the overall market is far from a washout, but tactical downside
on XLV is compelling.
■
AAPL Loses Two Ways. Apple (AAPL, Not Rated) is vulnerable in our view given that
it was a clear beneficiary and hideout during the semi/AI unwind. We think it’s now
poised to fall in either a further risk-off scenario, or if semis/AI see a bounce it should
unwind lower.
■
Gold Miners Strength is Notable. We are still not fans of precious metals, but GDX
strength is worth paying attention to as sometimes it can lead the metal itself. Royal
Gold (RGLD, Not Rated) is a quality set-up here.

What Happens Now? 
 
• Since 1996, there have been 15 prior occurrences when there was an 80% NYSE downside volume day for the first time in at 
least three months.  
• Of those 15 times, 11 of them saw at least one more 80% downside volume day in the next 30 trading days.  
• One-third of the time there was at least five or more 80% days in the next 30 days.  
• Average and median returns are negative 5-10 days out, and while they turn positive after 20 days, SPX was only higher 53% 
of the time 20 days later for a paltry +0.31% average return.  
Bottom Line: An 80% downside day is often a necessary part of a bottoming process, but given it’s been almost a year without one, 
we should not be surprised to see another one, if not several before all is said and done.

unlikely to be overcome anytime soon.
