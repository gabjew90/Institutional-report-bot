A Blue Wave Sends Ripples Through Markets 
Democrats have swept both chambers of Congress, securing at least 
54 Senate seats and a substantial House majority. The result sets 
the stage for divided government from January 2027, reflecting voter 
dissatisfaction 
with 
the 
Iran 
war 
and 
rising 
living 
costs. 
Vice President JD Vance acknowledged the war’s role in the defeat, 
suggesting the outcome was more a rebuke of the administration’s 
record than an endorsement of the Democratic agenda. 
A Narrower Path for Policy
President Trump will face greater difficulty advancing tax and 
spending measures without bipartisan support. A Democratic Senate 
will also become a gatekeeper for judicial and senior government 
appointments, limiting the administration’s ability to reshape 
government.
However, the gridlock cuts both ways. Democrats lack the 60 Senate 
votes required to advance most legislation and the two-thirds 
majorities required to override a presidential veto. Their ability to 
enact their own agenda will therefore remain constrained. With less 
room to legislate, we expect the White House to rely more heavily on 
executive action and foreign policy. A divided government may limit 
major legislative changes, but policy uncertainty will persist.

Data Centres Lost at the Ballot Box
Initial market optimism over policy gridlock gave way to concerns that the election outcome could slow AI 
infrastructure investment.
Data centres became a strong campaign issue amid concerns over electricity bills, water consumption and 
limited permanent job creation.
The political shift comes at a sensitive point in the AI investment cycle. Combined capital expenditure at 
Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon reached approximately USD 202 billion in Q3 2026, while their 
combined free cash flow turned negative at around USD 26 billion. This has sharpened questions about 
how further expansion will be funded and when spending will generate sufficient returns.
Positioning added to the pressure. According to industry estimates, US leveraged ETFs attracted 
approximately USD 15 billion in October, the largest monthly inflow in at least two years, as investors 
bought the dip ahead of the election. When sentiment reversed, leverage amplified the selling.
A More Measured Path for AI Expansion
In our view, a broad US AI ban remains unlikely, given the strategic importance of maintaining 
competitiveness with China. The more immediate risk is tighter state-level regulation that could slow data 
centre development, particularly following Democratic victories in Texas and Ohio.
We interpret the political message as a call to build more responsibly. Requirements for operators to 
secure their own power supply and protect households from higher electricity bills could raise costs and 
delay some projects. Over time, however, these safeguards could rebuild public trust and support a more 
sustainable expansion.
Portfolio Positioning
We had reduced technology hardware exposure ahead of the election amid concerns over AI revenue 
growth and the funding of further capital expenditure. We have since trimmed exposure further and remain 
underweight, pending greater clarity on state-level data centre policies and hyperscaler funding.
We will continue to monitor how incoming governors and legislatures translate campaign commitments 
into policy and assess the implications for the pace and economics of the AI buildout.
