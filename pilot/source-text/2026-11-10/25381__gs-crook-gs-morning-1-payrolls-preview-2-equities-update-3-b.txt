1) Payrolls Preview
Ronnie Walker (US Economics): We’re forecasting an increase of 75k, somewhat above consensus of
+65k and last month’s +29k. Both big data and technical factors argue for leaning toward a stronger
reading. On big data, the alternative measures of job growth that we track averaged around 70k in October.
On the technicals, job growth tends to rebound in October following a slowdown in Septembers with late
Labor Days (as was the case this year), accelerating by an average of about 50k from the September pace.
On the unemployment rate, we’re forecasting an unchanged reading at 4.2% (on a rounded basis), in line
with consensus, and we see risks skewed toward a lower reading.
Richard Chambers (Co-Head of US IRP): Duration markets have been very well supported since the Fed
last Wednesday. Payrolls end another action-packed week. Last month’s payroll U3 was a low 4.2%,
consensus is for another 4.2% , but we only need a modest .03% reduction to print a 4.1%. Given
September jobless claims numbers, this is very doable. When you mix the risk off sentiment, a more
patient fed and oil moving lower, an aforementioned data beat (4.1) on Friday after the market has
overcame so much new information can reprice the front higher and ensure the Fed stay on path for a
December hike and more.
Arvind Giridhar (Global Head of Volex Trading): The option market event weights for NFP have been
lower for much of this year, reflecting the diminished weight of the labour market in moving Fed policy
expectations. The Fed is more sensitive to inflation than the labour market at least in the direction of data
beats. The rally recently has been partly driven by left tail concerns around growth and the health of the AI
trade – upper left vol has been bid into the rally back, there has been demand for wing receivers on the
front end, and skew has reshaped to the receiver side increasing the risk premium for rate cuts. There
hasn’t been any weakness in the labour market supporting this – at least thus far. Given the backdrop – we
think the asymmetry in reaction is to a miss than a beat. While we expect this number to be solid, over the
next several months, we expect to see more labour market weakness driven by enterprises needing to see
an ROI from growing AI spend. So the front end remains a buy on dips.
