"Capitulation Catalysts" 
But first, some context on why the floor gave way. McElligott writes that, coming off the rare 
"Stocks Down" signal he flagged earlier in the day, together with "a handful of additional 
'Capitulation Catalyst' headlines out there (particularly on HF performance side)," clients are 
telling him they feel "further incentivized to take risk down at this point." The theme of the 
conversations: 
...preservation of residual shreds of remaining YTD performance and staying-alive into next yr's 
fresh capital and risk 
 

 
In other words, get flat, keep your job, and try again in January. Those "capitulation catalyst" 
headlines are not hard to find: just today, Walleye returned capital and fired its head of equities 
after its worst month since COVID, while UBS trader Torsten Sippel warned that hedge funds 
are capitulating with HF flows running at a lopsided 23/75/2 buy/sell/short sell, and Goldman's 
John Flood said his desk's "phones are exploding." 
Where is the pain concentrated? According to McElligott, the "tap-out" remains centered on 
legacy "Crowding," specifically: 
●​ "AI Momentum" of Long Enablers vs Short Hyperscalers; 
●​ the "Hiding Place" that has been the Energy sector, which was "your 'Rates Short' proxy 
/ Iran Hedge, and the S&P's best sector YTD by a factor of 2:1 over the next closest 
space"; 
●​ cross-asset, the legacy Paid / "Short Bonds" positioning (CTA trend in particular), which 
"continues getting roasted with rage-covering (Spot USTs Up, Vol Up)"; 
●​ and all of that coming after yesterday's Crude / Energy / Commodities Carry melt-down 
on "Iran-OFF". 
Nomura's crowding scoreboard tells the story: the US Equities Momentum factor is down 11.9% 
in five days and 23.1% QTD, Energy (XLE) is down 10.4% MTD, and the Crude Oil / Inflation 
Tail basket has lost 13.6% in a week. Diversification at its finest. 
 
As for the bond shorts, McElligott's table of CTA trigger levels shows the systematic crowd 
approaching "some massive $notional cover levels" in legacy bond shorts, with a +2% move 
implying some $225 billion in global bond buying, led by $69.5 billion in EUR 2Y, $48.5 billion in 
USD 2Y and $23.9 billion in JGB 10Y. Recall that the last time this happened, just over two 
weeks ago, McElligott said the bond short-squeeze just blew up every "high Sharpe" trend 
trade. 
 

 
 
The "Best Trade Of The Year" Just Got Leg-Swept 
Narrative-wise, McElligott says the two halves of the "Best Trade of the Year," the 
"Semis-Energy Barbell," which was up 66% on a 3.3 Sharpe just a few weeks ago, "just got 
leg-swept in the past 48 hours": 
●​ Energy -10.2% WTD 
●​ Semis -13.1% WTD 
Some of our readers may recall that we first flagged traders using the "Semis-Energy" barbell as 
a "source of funds" back in June, and a month ago we pointed out just how lopsided the payoff 
had become: 
Well, the barbell is now down 13% in the past week alone, with the "AI Enablers vs 
Hyperscalers" pair down 15%, after peaking at +80.4% on June 22. Weekly Sharpe ratios: -17.2 
and -10.1. That's not a typo. 
 

 
 
Two things did the leg-sweeping. First, the election, where in McElligott's words the "US 
electorate votes Dem / Blue on a rather clear 'Anti-Data Center / AI' platform... and the world 
remains STUFFED on this stuff (from Equities HF to Mutual Funds to Retail to VC to PE to 
Sovereigns and Endowments)," while the environmental wing of the party is also "Anti-Energy 
industry / pro heavy Regulation, for kickers." Which is why Goldman now sees moratoria 
freezing 45% of data center capacity, and why we published a list of the AI junk bonds set to 
crater earlier today. 
Second, Iran. McElligott sees "a pretty clear vibe-shift from the WH that they might be losing 
resolve and finally tapping on Iran (despite the ugly underlying issues and disruptions remaining 
intact even if we walk away), so the Energy sector's war premium is getting slapped." Hence 
Brent being on track for its worst week since COVID and, as Goldman explained earlier, why 
gold has become an inverse oil trade.

Today? 
...today we are completely reversing that course and then some, with VVIX +30v to a wildy 
puckered 122, and a monster outlier vs the VIX move

sits in the 94th-96th percentile across every lookback, with 21 years of history. 
 
Tech vol is even more extreme, with the VXN-vs-NDX residual in the 99.8th percentile over 1

Front-Running The Front-Run 
Which brings us to the punchline of McElligott's note. Into these big midday moves, he explains, 
discretionary traders always try to front-run the "potent $notional 'mechanical' mix" of: 
●​ Systematic vol-scalers (Risk Parity, CTA, or for this move, Target Vol Control, which 
"generally executes on a t+1 or even +2 lag"); 
●​ Leveraged ETF end-of-day rebalancings; 
 

 
●​ and the hedging of dealer options positioning. 
And so far, the playbook has worked: these "outlier 1d move" days have recently "tended to 
make the intraday low in that 2pm-3pm window, before the front-runners and hedgers turn / 
monetize / cover," closing off the lows and higher than the "projected" extremes. Translation: 
everyone has learned to sell ahead of the 4pm wave, and then buy it back before the wave hits. 
That's the "assumption." Now for the scary part: 
Obviously the scary thing now becomes this: the numbers are getting so fricking big that *what 
if* we don't make that almost "assumed" late afternoon low-then-rally up the standard percent or 
two off the lows, we can't bounce / make new lows into that 2-3pm "standard TWAP"...and 
instead, we close ding-dong lows after headfaking the market's "assumption" that we bounce in 
that final hour 
So how big are the numbers? McElligott's one-word answer: "LARGE..." 
●​ Dealer options "negative gamma" is -$7 billion per 1%, "actually getting slightly less bad 
-4% vs -2% being 'peak'"; 
●​ Vol Control is "a nasty one at ~$61B for sale implied" - though from an execution 
standpoint, these funds are "*likely* executing yday's -$25B for sale TODAY on lag to 
confirm the move, where today's move is now further loading in the queue"; 
●​ and the levered ETF rebalance of -$47.0 billion discussed above. 
 

 
 
Note the vol control matrix: at the S&P's -3.76% move when the table was run, estimated vol 
control selling is $61.2 billion, and it ramps to $70 billion on a -10% day. Meanwhile, 1-month 
realized vol has jumped to 18.8, a 94.8th percentile reading, which is precisely the input that 
keeps these funds selling into next week. UBS has a similar, if even scarier, read, with its risk 
control rebalance estimates pointing to $95-105 billion of selling on a 2% SPX decline. 
Add it up (very roughly, since some of the vol control flow is lagged) and you're north of $100 
billion in mechanical supply, all of it known to every trader on the Street, all of it front-run, and all 
of it waiting on the same 3pm bounce.
