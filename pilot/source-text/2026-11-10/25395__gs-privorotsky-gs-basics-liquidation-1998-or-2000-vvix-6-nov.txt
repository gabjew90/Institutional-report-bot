Markets: Sharp liquidation in the US. Nasdaq −5.5%, SOX −12%, SPX −4.4%. Day one post election saw
AI underperform while the broader market rallied on a perceived pivot to the political center, led by
quality, cyclicals and short covering. Day two was a violent catch down. If data center construction is
about to slow dramatically, nominal growth expectations look increasingly unrealistic, leaving the
cyclicals that rallied Wednesday mispriced against that reality. Leveraged ETF rebalancing likely
compounded the decline in semis and NDX, with mechanical selling into the close. Combine that with
negative dealer gamma and you have a self reinforcing liquidation. Largest 1d move in VVIX since
volmageddon (Feb 5th 2018). Energy reversing higher as fears of escalation compounded the markets
worries.
Flows: TMT supply in Europe was slightly less acute although picked back up as the US session

skew, extending the Tech unwind to -$27bn since it began ...larger than the July technical unwind's

1998 or 2000? : The speed and magnitude of the moves suggest acute leverage and forced de-grossing.
Correlations are increasing, momentum is underperforming, residual leverage factor is selling off as low
beta/safety/quality is bid. Is this a 1998 style liquidity crisis and buying opportunity, or March 2000 and

the beginning of a structural technology unwind? I lean toward 1998. Russia’s default/LTCM were the
external shocks but US growth remained intact. Once liquidity returned and the Fed eased, markets
recovered rapidly. Today, the AI growth engine may similarly remain intact even as markets violently
reprice the financing and timing of the buildout. Unlike 2000, today’s leaders have real revenues, cash
flows and balance sheets. Delaying data centers isn’t destroying compute demand… constrained capacity
may actually improve existing economics. The uncomfortable parallel with 2000 is the sheer scale of
spending and uncertainty around returns. Are we liquidating a sound growth story or discovering its
economics are wrong? I lean toward the former, but demand, utilization and returns need to hold.
AI: Four concerns underpin the selloff. (1) Insufficient capital to finance the buildout. (2) Insufficient
revenue to justify investment. (3) Insufficient power and permitting capacity to energize compute. (4) An
unsustainable aggregate spending trajectory. The market has latched onto number three, arguably the least
important. Constrained supply isn’t disappearing demand. The real question is whether revenues
ultimately justify the capital employed.
Politics: Texas Governor elect Gina Hinojosa’s rhetoric is more nuanced than the market suggests. “We
can have economic development and we can have protections for Texans. These things are not mutually
exclusive.” Yesterday she reiterated, “It is not that I hate data centers.” This sounds more like a regulatory
reset than ideological rejection. Think actual spending could surprise positively versus what’s now being
discounted.
Energy: Oil rebounded roughly 4% yesterday and is higher again this morning. Markets got ahead of
themselves pricing an immediate Iran off ramp. Negotiations remain unresolved, with renewed escalation
a risk. The energy shock complicates stabilization in rates, growth expectations and risk appetite. Risks
remain two sided here but hard to see the broader tape perform without more confidence on energy.
Risk: De-grossing remains acute. Nasdaq has broken its 200 day, while the S&P sits just above. That needs
to hold. Negative gamma leaves scope for violent moves in either direction. My gut is fundamental
uncertainties justify the de-rating but last leg has overextended in a number of these assets as performance
has entrenched forced de-risking. Think higher quality tech spectrum TSMC/ASML. What changes the
narrative? Political reflexivity around AI investment and growth, stabilization in funding markets, and
evidence that underlying spending and demand remain intact. Big picture: I stand by the pre-election view.
Political change can trigger panic, but the S&P has been higher 12 months after every one of the 19
midterms since 1950. That’s a 12m observation, not a one month forecast. Hard to time locally, think all
will be playing defense into the weekend...but these episodes have historically been buying opportunities.
