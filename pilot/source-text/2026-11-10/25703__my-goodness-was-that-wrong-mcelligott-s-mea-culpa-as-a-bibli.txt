Round-Tripped: The "Best Trade Of The Year" Gives It Back 
Just a few weeks ago, the Semis-Energy barbell was up 66% on a 3.3 Sharpe. As of Thursday it 
is up 39% YTD, after losing 15% in the past week on a weekly Sharpe of -15.2, while the "AI 
Enablers vs Hyperscalers" pair is down 16% on the week and now sits in a 31.2% YTD 
drawdown. These are the trades that made the year, and they are being unwound by the same 
people who put them on. 
 

 
 
It's the same story Goldman's Prime desk told this morning, when it showed that tech hedge 
funds have wiped out their entire 2026 gain, down from roughly +24% before the June/July AI 
selloff to roughly flat. 
So what was the "pivot point"? McElligott offers a hypothesis, and it's one regular readers will 
recognize: 
Hypothetically, the "pivot point" was the reality behind the implications of US midterm election 
"Blue Tsunami" for Democrats running an anti-AI platform, then knocking-into the IPO window 
then closing rapidly at MUCH lower valuations for the Frontier Labs, and structurally, incredible 
forward difficulty in raising more Capital (debt and equity -financing) for the assumed "Capex 
 

 
Trickle-down" in the years ahead...Which ofc has acted as "THE" tailwind for US Nominal GDP 
strength over the past year+, as well as the "AI Enablers / Bottlenecks" which had been the 
legacy YTD "Winners" of the trade, but now meaning that the economic and market "Left Tail" 
just picked-up a ton of Delta 
In other words, the market is repricing three things at once: the political risk to the data center 
buildout (where Goldman sees moratoria freezing 45% of capacity), the frontier lab IPO window 
and, most importantly, the financing. Having pounded the table for over a year that the AI 
buildout runs on debt, we'd simply add that "capex trickle-down" has also been propping up 
nominal GDP, which is why the left tail now belongs to the economy as much as to the Nasdaq. 
And because nothing is ever allowed to be simple, the macro data chose this exact moment to 
run hot. This morning's October payrolls came in at 165K, more than double the 70K expected, 
with a surprise drop in the unemployment rate. As McElligott puts it, that only further increases 
the likelihood of Fed HIKES "against this messy backdrop," with December now about 80% 
priced and roughly 50bps of cumulative hikes in the curve. A hiking Fed into a deleveraging: just 
what the doctor ordered.

Sea Legs Tend To Take Some Time 
So when is it safe to get back in the water? McElligott says those "Long Tails / Long Vol" folks 
with performance and cash to spare can "play offense" by spending options premium on 
bounces, exploiting the risk-management purge and the extreme moves in vol, skew and 
correlation. His preferences: 
●​ Energy, still his "hiding place w Upside since the Defensives have gone so bonkers"; 
●​ and, perversely, the Hyperscalers, where "a curbing of their Capex aspirations could 
then see a bullish re-rating off of the back of FCF shifting back +++". 
That second one deserves a moment: after a year and a half in which the market rewarded 
nearly every capex hike, the bull case for Big Tech may now be spending less. It fits with 
JPMorgan's co-head of trading, who says the market is finally focusing on AI circularity. 
But, and it's a big but, timing is everything: 
The trick is that a lot of the various "Shock" -backtests show either "chop" for the next couple 
weeks to 1 month out...before then much cleaner path thereafter...so you gotta watch your entry 
points, again, especially knowing this year-end PNL and performance overhang is gonna make 
it tough to get involved for many in the immediate window, with ongoing liquidation risk from 
 

 
those slower-moving entities listed earlier who aren't at Vol / risk "reactive" and perhaps then 
needing to sell these nascent "bids" in the days and weeks ahead, before that ~1m "turn" 
The backtests bear him out. Nomura finds eight prior instances of a 4.4%+ one-day S&P drop 
striking with the index at all-time highs (1986, Oct '87, '89, '97, '98, 2000, Feb 2020 and 
Liberation Day 2025). Two weeks later, the median return was -0.5% with a coin-flip 50% hit 
rate; one month later +3.5% (75% hit rate); six months later +20.3%. The October 16, 1987 
entry, which preceded Black Monday by one trading day, is a useful reminder of what "chop" can 
mean. 
 
Same story for tech: across 43 prior cases where Nasdaq vol spiked this much relative to spot 
(99th percentile and up), NDX returns one month out averaged -0.2% with a 55% hit rate, before 
improving to +5.9% at three months (80%) and +12.7% at six months (82%). 
 

 
 
And then there's the momentum unwind itself. At -32.6% from its June 22 peak, the current 
momentum L/S drawdown is now the 5th deepest on record, behind only 2008-09, 2002-03, 
2000-01 and COVID. The SPX's median return one month after the trough of those drawdowns 
was +2.2% (64% hit rate), rising to +8.7% at six months (79%). 
 

 
 
Liberation Day Or LTCM? 
The four names ahead of 2026 on that drawdown list are worth a second look: the GFC, the 
dot-com bust (twice) and COVID. The 2001 trough was followed by a further 7.8% SPX drop 
over the next three months, and the worst 3-month outcome in the whole sample, -17.8%, came 
from the January 2020 trough, right before COVID hit. Which brings us back to the parlor game 
Goldman's Rich Privorotsky kicked off this morning: is this 1998 or 2000? Goldman is betting on 
LTCM, a positioning flush rather than a bubble bursting. The backtests mostly agree, eventually. 
We're a little less sanguine. In 1998 Greenspan cut three times; in 2026, the Fed is probably 
about to hike into the flush, the frontier labs are looking at a closing IPO window, and the 
slower-moving "trapped longs" McElligott flags have barely started to sell. Our guess is the chop 
lasts longer than a month, and that the "cleaner path" on the other side only arrives once the 
market gets an answer on who funds the next leg of AI capex. Maybe it's the hyperscalers' own 
FCF, in which case McElligott's contrarian hyperscaler call will look brilliant. Or maybe it's 
nobody. 
Either way, as McElligott's title says, sea legs take time. For now, Survive and Advance is the 
trade.
