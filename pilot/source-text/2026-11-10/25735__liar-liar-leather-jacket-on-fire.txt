Questioning the future
What a week for the future. AI, semiconductors and other high-growth favorites suffered some of the
sharpest losses, while hedge funds accelerated their selling and liquidity deteriorated.
With positioning stretched in some places and increasingly washed out in others, Monday's open raises
more questions than answers. And then, of course, there is the rather unusual Jensen Huang story
making the rounds this weekend...
Marquee
Hedge funds, are you done yet?
Hedge funds have been cutting risk aggressively, although net deleveraging remains less severe than
during the Iran-war selloff. Interestingly, gross leverage ratios have actually risen as fund equity has
fallen faster than gross exposure.
De-risking is underway, but the positioning picture is not quite as straightforward as it looks.

Jensen, what is going on?
The allegations surrounding Jensen Huang make for extraordinary weekend reading. But let's be clear:
we don't know what actually happened. Intelligence assessments are not proof, and the mainstream
media has certainly got sensational accusations wrong before. 
If it turns out to be true, the market will of course ask: If Jensen can lie to his wife of 41 years, what else
could he lie about or mislead people about?
The most important stock in the universe is now barely up YTD, and RSI levels are hitting levels last
seen during Liberation Day.
Bloomberg
Warren went on winning
Even if the allegations against Jensen are true, it does not mean that NVDA could not be a fantastic
stock to own. Warren Buffett's wife Susan famously introduced him to Astrid Menks, who became his
longtime companion while Warren remained married to Susan. The three reportedly even signed
Christmas cards together. Only Buffett could turn a love triangle into a long-term value investment.
Meanwhile, in the shorter term, Berkshire has outperformed Nvidia by roughly 15 percentage points
over the past year.
