A LOOMING PROFIT SQUEEZE
Freya Beamish
Profit broadening but a coming squeeze?
The reacceleration is broad, spanning manufacturing and services. Manufacturing employment
continues its strengthening - remember total  imports have taken off because of AI, which was
untariffed. Import penetration in other sectors has receded. Datacentre construction could be creating
some direct spillovers, though contruction employment was actually one of the few decliners last
month. Certaintly the broadening of profits growth seems to be centrered around datacentres. But the
wider driver of labour demand will be real income growth picking up from the mid-year trough,
especially if energy prices are contained. Wealth effects reversing if the tech sell-off continues could
be a threat to demand but if that materialises we are still banking on it being a termporary slowdown.
So far so good and this is all consistent with profits continuing to broaden. But wage growth likely is
bottoming. Taking out education and health, that has already happened and job-to-job moves reveal
wages accelerating and that tends to lead the broader market. Measured productivity growth could
well deteriorate as the labour market reaccelerates. And that all means unit labour cost growth should
start to rise. The aggregate profit story is facing headwinds and tech may be facing disappointment
too - see Dario's macro pictures hereand here.
In short, macro growth depends on profits broadening, which could happen on the back of real income
growth picking up and people continuing to spend. But we can think of economies where the cyclical
profits story is less long in the tooth - eg the UK, where the labour market is slack, wage growth is
falling and productivity is surprising to the upside.
Confirmation of labour market reacceleration reinforces our conviction in Fed hikes going beyond
insurance against energy inflation - Q1 will not be the last hike. Energy prices coming down merely
puts purchasing power back in the hands of consumers, spurring labour demand against a backdrop
of still constrained labour supply. 
The labour market is now very clearly reaccelerating - total NFP employment rose 165K in
October
ULC growth will start picking up again, suggesting less favourable aggregate profit growth
We stand by our call that Fed hikes are not just about energy - Q1 will not be the last hike
