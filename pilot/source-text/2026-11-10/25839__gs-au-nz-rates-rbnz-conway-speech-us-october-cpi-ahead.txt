{us} ** USD Rates Update ** {us}
Tonight, focus is on October CPI where GIR are looking for a 0.16% increase in core CPI (vs.
+0.2% consensus), consistent with a larger 0.23% increase in core PCE in October. The
October seasonal factors have been distorted by the approach the Bureau of Labor Statistics is
using to interpolate the data that are missing because of last year’s government shutdown - GIR
estimate that this new source of residual seasonality will unduly boost October seasonally-
adjusted core CPI inflation by 5bp, and provide a disproportionate boost to the public
transportation, apparel, and hotels categories. 
Overnight, US rates twist flattened with the long end outperforming as the front end had to
digest $58bn of 3y UST which tailed 0.4bp with low indirect allotment (55.5% Indirects vs
63.45% 12mo avg) and risk performed well (NDQ +3.5%). Rates finished off the cheaps as oil
plunged in the afternoon (WTI: -6%) following news that Iran may let IAEA inspectors return. NY
sales estimate that CTAs covered a total of 10.7mm/bp in the US last week. This brings total
positioning to 54% of max short. Flows are still very bullish, biased towards the long end as
sizes there grow rapidly vs the rest of the curve. We are just 3bp away from levels that will
further intensify long end buying; the rest of the curve is ~15bp away.

{au} ** AUD & NZD Rates Update ** {nz}
Today, focus is on a speech from RBNZ Chief Economist Conway at 11:15am AEDT at the UBS
Australasia conference. Yesterday, in the Sydney session, the NZ curve bear flattened in an
appropriate response to the election results: pricing out the probability of the dual mandate
being introduced. Initially there was paying of December RBNZ and some unwinds in front end
longs from the HF community but as the day continued, we saw only one way receiving from
systematics, mostly in 5-10y IRS.
In Australia, the market held onto marginally cheaper levels following RBA Sarah Hunters
speech on the impact of AI. The key takeaways: (1) liaison reporting that data centre
construction is adding to demand for some already stretched construction skills, (2) price
increases from AI have been in consumer electronics which have a small CPI weight, and (3)
liaison indicate that AI has not yet impacted firm headcount and isn’t expected to over the next

year. Flows were light to start the week with some modest paying of duration and decent two
way in EFP.
GS Rates strategy note that given the oil price move in October, and risks that a hotter inflation
print could reignite hike risks amid early signs of a shift higher in inflation expectations, they
think risk/reward favors tactically paying the February RBA meeting OIS (Entry: 4.89, Target:
5.00, Stop: 4.85). In New Zealand, they note that while  the RBNZ likely views policy as
somewhat accommodative and sees inflation risks as supporting a more neutral policy stance,
the downside risks to activity will likely keep hikes on a gradual path. They think this non-
committal tightening is likely to support to a steeper curve as hike premium decays somewhat,
while inflation pressures, macro uncertainty, cyclical risks, and the likely looser fiscal stance,
irrespective of the outcome of the elections, all point towards higher term premium (non
committal RBNZ to steepen 2s10s).
🌎 ** Global Macro: What else is interesting? ** 🌎
Today, we will receive a 30y JGB auction. Looking at the curve, Trading think the super-long
sector has become rapidly cheap due to the recent rally in futures, and although it flattened
yesterday, it still looks like a buy from an RV perspective. On the other hand, given the
resilience of futures, it is difficult to enter large flattener positions. It is also concerning that 40-
year bonds continue to look heavy, and there is a worry that if the short positions in 30-year
bonds are covered, the cheapening of 40-year bonds may progress. Looking at the repo market,
short positions in 30-year bonds are about half of what they were last time, and pension fund
rebalancing buys might be limited due to cheaper stocks, suggesting that buying demand at the
auction itself may not be very strong. Therefore, coupled with the decline in market risk appetite,
some tail is likely to emerge, but if the curve steepens further from here, overseas buyers are
likely waiting to step in, so buying on any cheapening might not be a bad idea.
GBP Swaps notes that it was quieter start to the session with most flows focused on selling of
ASW, highlighted in the underperformance vs duration/curve/x mkt. Outright wise there was
broadly better selling of cash albeit not enough to explain the futures outperformance. XCCY
characterised by a better bid tone in outright largely tracing EURUSD higher as that remains
better bid as was the case last week. EUR Swaps saw some steepening interest from fast
money while asset swaps were better offered. Worth noting that they are not seeing fresh
sellers of asset swap but this flow feels more driven by outright receiving in the market. In
Sweden, CTA’s continued to receive across the curve.
In data, the UK KPMG/REC Report on Jobs showed a renewed contraction in hiring in October
on the back of rising uncertainty and increased costs. The permanent staff placements index

dropped to 46.6 (from 50.9), with respondents indicating that employers had scaled back or
delayed hiring because of higher uncertainty and rising costs. The temp staff billings index also
dropped to 48.9 (from 50.9). Candidate supply rose at a faster pace, with the availability index
rising to 59.4 (from 57.2). The press release notes reports of redundancies and lower job
openings driving the increase. The vacancies index rose to 48.7 (from 48.5), indicating a slower
pace of contraction. This is the softest decline since August 2024. The permanent staff salaries
index rose to 54.1 (from 53.4) but remains below historical averages.
Equity sales notes that since the start of October, GS clients have now net sold -$30b of tech
stocks (TMT + Comm Services) on our U.S. trading desk (-$6b of this was last week). For
context, during July’s momentum drawdown our clients net sold -$23b of tech names. On the
green shoots front, Friday (11/6) was the first day in 7 trading sessions our desk ended with a
buyskew in tech (+$1b). Investors are in the process of re-calibrating datacenter build
expectations on the back of last week’s mid-term Election (especially in TX and OH). Investor
feedback suggests current market prices are implying limited y/y capex (or GW) growth in 2028
-- well below prior market expectations for 30-40%+ y/y growth in 2028 … a level in markets that
a lot of our clients believe is an attractive entry point.
