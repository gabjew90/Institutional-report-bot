 
Blue-nami Hangover: Chips Bounce, Breadth 
Doesn't As Hot Payrolls Put December Hike Back 
On The Table 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SATURDAY, NOV 07, 2026 - 02:44 PM 
Tl;dr: After Thursday's "Fireworks Day" carnage, Friday was the morning-after: a dip-buy (and 
short-cover) in the most-smashed AI/semis names lifted the Nasdaq, but under the hood most 
S&P stocks were red. A blowout payrolls print (165K vs 70K est, unemployment down to 4.1%) 
killed the "growth scare" narrative and put a December rate-hike right back on the table, which 
bear-flattened the curve, sent the dollar higher, and weighed on gold and bitcoin. Oil chopped 
around ~$90 as Hormuz mine headlines collided with US-Iran deal headlines. Small caps and 
the Dow Jones were the best performers on the day, while the spoos and Nasdaq lagged.  
 
On the week: Nasdaq's worst day since Covid, a record put day, a record ETF levered/inverse 
flow split, and the S&P down ~2%. 
 

 
 
By sector, today had a distinctly defensive flavor to it, with Healthcare the best performing 
sector, and Tech once again the worst. 
 
Goldman's equity desk summed up the mood best in this afternoon's Mid-Day Color (available 
to pro subs): a "whipsaw week", with SPX "ticking just above its 200dma" even as over 60% of 
its constituents are in the red. 
And Goldman's European desk had a name for it: "Hangover Friday, but NFP a sigh of relief." 
A Bounce In The Wreckage... For Some 
Let's start with the good news (for chip bulls at least). The names that got clubbed hardest on 
Thursday bounced hardest on Friday. Per Goldman, NDX was up 1.3% after its worst day since 
 

 
3/9/20, High Beta Momo (GSPRHIMO) rose 2.4% after closing down 6.2% Thursday (and 
finishing down 51% from its June highs)... 
 
... and the SOX was up 3% after its worst day since 2020, a day that left it 14% below its 
200DMA. 
 
By the afternoon the rebound had extended: the Nasdaq 100 was up 1.6% and a gauge of chip 
giants rallied 4%, while the S&P rose 0.6% and the Dow slipped 0.1%. 
But the bounce was narrow. only 44% of SPX names were advancing, cap-weight beat 
equal-weight by nearly 80bps, and the median S&P stock was actually down 0.24%. 
 

 
 
 In other words, a handful of beaten-up megacaps and chipmakers did the heavy lifting while the 
rest of the market... well, didn't. 
 
Nobody seemed especially convinced. StoneX's Fawad Razaqzada told Bloomberg the move 
"looks more like short covering and bargain hunting than a decisive shift in sentiment," and 
Goldman's TMT desk said there was "a lack of conviction" with activity levels muted: 
Flows are more balanced versus yesterday, with some dip buying present but in smaller notional 
size. 
That said, Goldman's overall flows were a 5 out of 10 on activity and skewed 6.6% better to buy 
(LOs +7.6%, HFs +7.8%), and info tech demand was on track to break a 6-day sell streak. 
Probably the closest thing to "good news" the desk has delivered all week. 
 

 
Bulls will point to valuations. Goldman's European desk notes that SOX multiples are 
approaching 2022 levels, with NVDA at 10x blended 24mf, "lowest since 2013." Then again, as 
the same desk put it, the structural debate (too many semis, not enough data center shells by 
2028) "hasn't been resolved - just paused." 
And speaking of shells, today's biggest AI headline came from Redmond: Microsoft is winding 
down its deal to rent AI cloud capacity from Oracle, part of a wider reassessment of billions in 
cloud partnerships. Meanwhile, Anthropic's IPO talk took haircut No. 2, with Bloomberg 
reporting prospective buyers now see a valuation as low as $1.1 trillion. Not exactly the 
backdrop for a V-shaped AI recovery.

The Week In Pain: Panic Gauges, Record Puts, Record ETF 
Flows 
Friday's bounce shouldn't hide just how ugly this week got. Goldman's Prime Brokerage team 
laid out the damage: 
Global Fundamental L/S managers down -1.3% (a -2 SD move), driven mainly by market/beta 
exposure... TMT L/S funds fell more than -4% and are now roughly flat to slightly positive YTD, 
down from about +24% before the June/July AI sell-off. 
Which is something we discussed yesterday in "Round Trip: Tech Hedge Funds Wipe Out Their 
Entire 2026 Gain." 
The panic metrics were off the charts: 
●​ Goldman's Vol Panic Index surged to 9.57 out of 10, back in the panic zone; the ~5pt 
jump was the 3rd largest ever, and the two-day swing (after Wednesday's collapse) was 
the largest on record. 
●​ VVIX posted its 2nd largest one-day increase ever, behind only "Volmageddon" in Feb 
'18. 
●​ Cboe saw more than 11 million puts trade Thursday, the largest put day on record, 
beating April 2025 (as we detailed earlier). 
 

 
●​ Many of Goldman's AI-related baskets had their biggest down day since the launch of 
ChatGPT. 
 
Contrarians will love this: Goldman traders (including Lee Coppersmith) told Bloomberg that an 
extreme panic reading historically signals a strong rebound over the medium term, adding "the 
setup starts to look more interesting as forced selling runs its course." Though even they 
hedged: "Not necessarily a reason to rush back into risk." 
Here is the interesting part: Goldman's PB data suggests discretionary hedge funds were not 
the ones panicking. Single-stock flows "do NOT point to signs of panic and an acceleration of 
de-grossing." Instead, the action was in macro products, with US-listed ETF shorts up 7% in a 
day (the third-largest jump in five years). Net leverage fell to 71.8%, a 4th percentilereading on a 
1-year basis, even as gross stays at 314% (97th percentile on 5-year). Plenty of exposure, very 
little conviction. 
So who was selling? Retail and the levered-ETF crowd, it seems. Goldman's ETF desk (Chris 
Lucas) notes that ETFs printed $580BN of notional volume on Thursday, their third-largest day 
ever and 33% of the tape, with QQQ breaking its notional volume record at $86BN and levered 
semis (SOXL/SOXS) printing over $37BN. And the chart of the day shows something genuinely 
weird:

Asia Breaks Out The Bailout Kit 
The contagion was even worse in Asia's AI supply chain. Goldman's Korea team says KOSPI 
plunged 12% on the weekamid the "blue wave" and fears of data center restrictions, MSCI EM 
fell 4%, and Taiwan saw its second-largest weekly foreign selling on record. Regulators 
responded the only way they know how: Taiwan curbed short-selling and readied a $15BN fund 
while Seoul waived margin calls. Nothing says "healthy market" like banning the sellers. 
Interestingly, the "anti-AI" trade worked elsewhere: India's NIFTY rallied 9% this week and China 
gained 2-5% despite the global AI selloff, per Goldman's weekly kickstarts. 
 
 

 
Payrolls Pop, December Hike Back In Play 
The day's macro event was a blowout: US payrolls soared by 165K, blowing away the 70K 
median estimate. Prior months were revised up, and per Goldman's economists, the 
three-month average jumped to 137K from 51K before the report. The unemployment rate fell to 
4.1% and average hourly earnings rose 0.4% M/M (vs 0.3% est). 
Goldman's Chris Hussey put it simply: "AI is not the economy," and today's report "provides 
some evidence that whatever impact AI may be having on employment so far is still pretty 
muted." Lombard Odier's Florian Ielpo was blunter, telling Bloomberg the report "all but seals 
the case for a December rate hike... Main Street is doing just fine, thank you." 
Add UMich sentiment unexpectedly jumping to a three-month high (with inflation expectations 
declining), and SF Fed's Mary Daly saying more rate increases are likely needed to tame prices, 
and the hawks had a good day. 
Money markets priced about 20bps of a December hike after the data (per Bloomberg). 
 
Goldman's rates strategists (h/t Will Marshall) offered a more measured read: the print mainly 
"reinforce[s] the labor market as something that is allowing the Fed to focus on the inflation side 
of the mandate," rather than making a strong case for hikes on its own. Translation: next 
Tuesday's CPI is what matters. 
Bonds Give Back Some Haven Gains 
Thursday's flight-to-quality bid partially reversed. The front end sold off roughly 5bps and the 
curve bear-flattened, with 2s10s down to 33bps, while the 10Y yield rose 2bps to 4.85%. Still, 
 

 
the haven bid didn't vanish entirely: Bloomberg flagged demand for bullish 10Y options and a 
big long-bond futures block ($900K/DV01 seller). 
 
 
Credit Blinked... Barely 
Here's a chart-less but important observation: credit has barely flinched. Goldman's Credit 
MarketStrats team noted that on Thursday, when the S&P fell 4.4%, generic IG spreads 
widened only 3.6bps vs 8.2bps implied by regression: 
Credit's downside convexity hasn't kicked in...yet. In a systematic risk-off scenario, credit would 
underperform equity... 
 

 
That from a note aptly titled "Equities Blinked, Credit Didn't (Yet)." And Goldman's research has 
the funding math front and center: hyperscalers are on pace to finance 36% of capex with credit 
this year, only 43% of the $687BN of AI-linked financing in 2026 came from the hyperscalers 
themselves, and state moratoriums could affect ~45% of planned US data center capacity 
(Texas alone 25%). Which, as regular readers know, is exactly why we have been warning since 
last October that AI is a debt bubble too, and why "Blast Radius" is required reading. 
 
Dollar Up, Bullion & Bitcoin Down 
The hawkish data lifted the dollar broadly: the Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index was up 0.4%, the 
euro fell 0.7% to $1.1141, and the yen slid 0.5% to 158.49. 
 
 

 
Gold slipped 0.6% to ~$4,063 as real-rate hike-risk returned (silver gained), giving back some of 
this week's haven bid. 
 
Bitcoin went nowhere, stuck around $75.6K, right about where Goldman said its "Democratic 
sweep" price would be. A $6.3BN Binance hack certainly didn't help sentiment. 
 
Oil: Mines, Deals And A Deflating Premium

needs "practical US actions" before diplomacy progresses. Then Bloomberg reported the US 
 

 
and Iran were edging closer to a deal with the nuclear issue back in play. Net-net: WTI settled at 
$90.83 and Brent at $98.32.

One More Lap, Or Last Call? 
So where does that leave us? The bulls have a case: panic gauges at extremes, put buying at 
records, net leverage near lows, and chip multiples back to 2022. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky 
argued this week that "nets are too low for the broader market to go down easily." 
But the bounce was narrow, the median stock fell, CTAs still have ~$28BN to sell, credit hasn't 
even started to reprice, the dip-buying came via 3x levered ETFs, and now the macro data is 
giving the Fed cover to keep hiking into an AI capex slowdown. Goldman's own desk conceded 
there are "no buyers stepping in to defend anything just yet; the long-only community is frozen." 
Our take: Friday was probably the dead-cat bounce everyone was positioned to sell, rather than 
the bottom. A hiking Fed plus a capex freeze is not the mix that produces V-shaped recoveries. 
 

 
The tell will be credit: if IG finally catches down to equities, Thursday won't be the low, although 
the 200DMA proved a sufficient support for Friday purposes. 
 
The big test, however, is the long-term Nasdaq support. Today, it bounced off... but another 
sizable drawdown and all bets are off...
