Editorial 
Increasing signs of a bottom 
Strategy summary: Democrats’ thumping win in the US mid-
term elections has three key implications: i) policy gridlock is 
back in Washington, likely stalling fiscal expansion and new 
tariffs; ii) the chance of a prolonged Middle East conflict is 
curtailed, lowering oil prices and inflation expectations; and iii) 
the pace of AI data-centre buildout is at risk, curbing AI-linked 
bond supply. Higher corporate and wealth taxes are a long-term 
risk if Democrats win the 2028 presidential elections.   
For portfolios, the election reinforces our mid-cycle playbook 
already in place: remaining broadly balanced across assets, 
while looking for tactical opportunities. As the S&P500 volatility 
index (VIX) surpasses 25, we see rising signs of equity market 
bottoming. We add an opportunistic idea on the US IT sector 
(see page 4), while using elevated volatility to earn income 
through structured strategies. In bonds, we prefer the 3-7-year 
part of the Developed Market bond yield curve and BBB-to-BB-
rated corporate bonds to earn attractive income as AI supply 
eases. In FX, we position for a modestly weaker USD over 6-
12 months by allocating to ex-US assets.  
Policy implications: Oil fell 12% over the past week as 
Democrats captured the US Senate and House. The party also 
flipped governorships in five states – Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, 
Ohio and Texas – securing a majority of governorships among 
50 US states for the first time since 2010. Vice President Vance 
and Republican leaders attributed the defeat to the Iran conflict, 
making it harder for President Trump to sustain it. Democrat 
control of the defence budget from next year further constrains 
conflict escalation. Despite near-term escalation risks, we see 
the geopolitical risk premium in oil prices fading, with oil easing 
towards USD 80/bbl over 6-12 months. Oil-driven disinflation 
should allow central banks to adopt less aggressive policies. 
Adding tactical exposure to US IT sector: Semiconductors 
slumped this week amid concerns over data-centre moratoria, 
while beneficiaries of Democrat policies include defensives, 
affordability-related stocks, tariff rollback beneficiaries and the 
healthcare sector. We use the volatility to add tactical exposure 
to the US IT sector. As we enter mid-cycle dynamics, equities 
should return 8-10% annually. The 2015–2017 mid-cycle offers 
a useful comparison: with an opposition Congress, S&P 500 
returns slowed to 8.3%, but remained positive. Diversification 
across sectors and regions remains a priority amid the rotation. 
Prefer belly of the bond curve: Government bonds rallied on 
expectations of slower growth. We still expect two more Fed 
hikes by H1 2027, given sticky near-term inflation and a robust 
economy. However, cheaper oil should lower inflation towards 
the Fed’s 2% target next year, allowing rate cuts in H2 2027. 
Ultra-long yields remain elevated due to fiscal challenges. 
Bonds, particularly those from non-hyperscaler investment-
grade corporates, offer attractive income. The 3-7-year 
segment – the belly of the yield curve – offers the best balance 
of income and sensitivity to interest-rate changes. 
Credit to benefit from restrained AI supply: Since states 
control data centre regulations, Democrat wins in governorship 
races in leading data centre states, such as Texas and Georgia, 
means growth in this segment is likely to be restricted. Slower 
data-centre construction could improve credit supply-demand 
conditions due to reduced issuance by hyperscalers. However, 
some data-centre-focused deals could become less viable, 
warranting caution towards lower-quality issuers. High-yield 
credit spreads have widened, but BB- and B-rated bonds have 
held up, while CCC-rated debt has reached distressed levels. 
We prefer the BBB–BB range, spanning the boundary between 
investment-grade and high-yield debt, for income. 
A modest headwind for the USD, tailwind for gold: The 
dollar should remain range-bound near term before weakening 
modestly over 6-12 months. Democrat control of the Congress 
limits tariff escalation, fiscal expansion and rapid AI buildout, 
three pillars of US exceptionalism supporting the USD through 
2026. Disinflation should also encourage a less aggressive Fed 
stance, removing another source of USD support. Near-term 
support for the USD index (DXY) remains at 100.7. A weaker-
than-expected payrolls report tonight could accelerate the 
USD’s decline, and vice versa. A softer dollar and sustained 
central-bank buying, led by China, remain supportive of gold.  
—  Rajat Bhattacharya

Top client questions 
  What are the equity market implications of the US mid-
term election results?   
Our view: The US mid-term election outcome adds political 
headwinds but does not alter our outlook for positive equity 
returns. Investors should maintain a Core allocation to global 
equities and continue to diversify their equity exposure across 
regions and sectors. We initiate an opportunistic idea on the US 
information technology (IT) sector, moving our opportunistic 
idea positioning from a defensive tilt to a more balanced 
positioning. Higher volatility also presents opportunities to use 
structured strategies for equity exposure.  
Rationale: The Democrats’ victory in both chambers of Congress 
has supported the outperformance of themes aligned with their 
policy priorities, with clean energy stocks, including solar 
companies, among the notable beneficiaries. Affordability-related 
themes have also benefited, including companies that could gain 
from potential tariff rollbacks. Medicaid-related stocks have also 
risen on hopes that healthcare will gain from policy changes. 
Meanwhile, semiconductor stocks remain under pressure amid 
concerns about potential moratoriums on data centres. Our AI capital 
expenditure (capex) estimates remain conservative relative to 
consensus, but with a potential reset in expectations around data 
centre spending, investors should remain selective.  
We initiate an opportunistic idea on the US IT sector after a 
significant 17% correction this quarter. Historically, this has been 
followed by strong positive returns over the next few quarters (see 
table). Semiconductors, about 40% of US IT, appears oversold after 
correcting more than 30% since we closed our opportunistic idea a 
month ago. Earnings growth for US IT continues to be solid, with 
volume driven growth in semiconductors, AI monetisation 
opportunities, ongoing cloud growth and resilient services revenue.  
As we highlighted in our November 2026 Global Market Outlook, 
the transition to mid-cycle is typically characterised by greater 
political headwinds. The mid-term election outcome reinforces this 
trend. The mid-cycle experience of 2015-17 offers a useful historical 
parallel for returns that moderate, but remain positive. Although we 
trimmed global equities to a Core allocation last week, we continue 
to expect equities to deliver mid-to-high single-digit returns per 
annum during the mid-cycle, even amid political headwinds, 
supported by steady earnings growth in both 2027 and 2028. 
Stay invested and diversify, with Overweight allocations to the US 
and AxJ, which offer the strongest earnings growth. Sector 
diversification is key as we expect market leadership to broaden. We 
are Overweight the US IT, US healthcare, US financial, US materials, 
Europe ex-UK financial and Japan financial sectors. 
—  Fook Hien Yap, Senior Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  What are the key takeaways from Q3 2026 US earnings? 
Our view: Q3 US earnings remain robust, but more modest 
earnings beats reinforce our view that equity markets are 
entering a mid-cycle phase. We remain Overweight US equities, 
but recommend investors to maintain diversified sector 
exposure as returns moderate and market leadership broadens. 
Rationale: As of 30 October, 82% of S&P500 companies that had 
reported had beaten earnings estimates. However, aggregate 
earnings were just 4.5% above expectations, a smaller beat than in 
recent quarters. Still, Q3 US earnings growth remains robust at 
31.6% y/y, supporting expected full-year growth of 36.1% in 2026 
and 16.4% in 2027 for the S&P500. The slower pace of upgrades 
suggests the bull market is maturing as investors focus on the 
sustainability of earnings growth. This raises the hurdle for positive 
share price reactions and makes selectivity more essential. For Big 
Tech, capex commitments remain high, while free cash flow is under 
pressure and expected to remain negative. Financing risks weigh on 
sentiment, particularly for companies with weaker balance sheets.  
We remain Overweight US equities, given the strong earnings 
growth, but we expect elevated volatility and modest returns. We 
prefer quality exposure within technology while maintaining 
diversified exposure across US financials, healthcare and materials. 
—  Ryan Goh, Investment Strategist 
  What to expect from Japanese banks’ upcoming results?   
Our view: We expect Japanese banks’ earnings growth to 
continue over the medium term, supported by higher domestic 
net interest income (NII) and resilient fee income. We remain 
positive on the sector, favouring banks with strong deposit 
franchises and scope to increase shareholder returns. 
Investors will focus on whether full-year guidance upgrades 
and buybacks exceed expectations. 
Rationale: The BoJ’s September rate hike to 1.25% took effect late 
in the quarter, so its benefit should be more visible from the 
December quarter as loans reprice. We expect three further BoJ 
rate hikes by June 2027, supporting further NII expansion. We 
will also look for signs that stronger corporate investment is driving 
faster loan growth and higher financing and advisory fees.  
We prefer to place more weight on guidance upgrades driven by 
recurring lending and fee income than on those driven by gains on 
equity disposals. Continued reductions in cross-shareholdings 
could free up capital for additional buybacks, providing a further 
boost to return on equity (ROE). Key risks: faster-than-expected 
deposit rate repricing, rising credit costs and bond portfolio losses. 
—  Robin Xie, CFA, Senior Investment Strategist

  What are the fundamental reasons behind your India 
equity upgrade? Which sectors offer favourable risk-reward? 
Our View: Indian equities offer an increasingly attractive risk-
reward profile. With India’s valuation premium to Emerging 
Markets (EMs) narrowing, foreign ownership near decade lows, 
Underweight EM positioning and a stabilising Indian rupee 
(INR), conditions are increasingly supportive of Indian equity 
outperformance vs. the broader AxJ region.  
Rationale: We have upgraded Indian equities to Overweight within 
our AxJ equities allocation. While Indian markets have witnessed 
USD 32bn of foreign investor outflows in 2026 year-to-date, strong 
domestic institutional investor inflows of USD 74bn have more 
than offset foreign selling. These outflows and the Indian equity 
market’s underperformance vs. global peers reflect broader EM 
allocation shifts towards AI, rather than weakening Indian 
fundamentals. Capital has gravitated towards markets such as the 
US, Taiwan and South Korea, which offer direct exposure to AI 
beneficiaries. However, we expect market leadership to broaden, as 
the rapid AI buildout is increasingly debt-funded, and US mid-term 
election results raise uncertainty. We prefer to diversify and we see 
investors reallocating towards structurally strong growth markets 
such as India, which offer non-AI driven growth.  
The RBI’s measures have supported INR stability, but underlying 
USD demand remains elevated. Lower oil prices would improve 
external balances, but the INR is likely to follow a path of gradual, 
controlled depreciation rather than a quick downward move. On 
valuation to its own historical average, the Nifty 50 one-year forward 
P/E is trading 18% below its long-term average while ROE at 14.7% 
is above historical averages. With India now marginally Underweight 
in large EM portfolios compared to the c.2% average Overweight 
historically, the case for a recovery in foreign flows is 
strengthening. 
India’s earnings growth remains intact. The Nifty 50 clocked Q1 
FY27 and Q2 FY27 profit-after-tax growth of c.20% y/y (ex-oil 
marketing companies). Large-caps offer greater earnings visibility 
and downside protection, while stronger growth in small- and mid-
caps warrants a selective approach given elevated valuations.

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  Does the emerging US job market data alter your view of 
the Fed’s rate path and government bond yields? 
Our view: We do not expect the recent and incoming US job 
market data to alter our outlook unless there are significant 
deviations from the US economy’s current trajectory.  
Rationale: The US economy continues to exhibit resilience, with the 
latest Job Openings and Labour Turnover Survey data showing job 
openings surprising to the upside at 7.5mn. Initial jobless claims 
remain near three-month lows. There is little evidence of a material 
slowdown in labour demand. Inflation is expected to trend lower 
through H2 2027, but the disinflationary impulse remains a medium-
term story and is unlikely to manifest meaningfully in the near term. 
The market is now pricing in two additional 25bps hikes by H1 
2027 (down from three previously), in line with our view. These two 
rate hikes are largely priced into the US government bond yield 
curve. We estimate 2- and 10-year US government bond yields to 
range between 4.5-4.75% and 5-5.25%, respectively, over the next 
three months. Our twelve-month forecast for 2- and 10-year US 
government bond yields are 4.25-4.5% and 4.75-5%, respectively, 
The 2-year yield is already trading within our expected range and 
would only move materially higher if a significantly stronger-than-
expected payrolls print led markets to price in a third hike. With two 
hikes baked in, long-run disinflation expectations intact and an 
uncertain fiscal outlook, the longer end of the yield curve is 
expected to be less sensitive to a single payrolls release. 
—  Ray Heung, Senior Investment Strategist 
  What is your outlook for the GBP following the BoE’s rate 
hike this week? Do you see any impact on EUR/GBP? 
Our view: The BoE rate hike supports the GBP, but upside 
remains limited. We remain constructive on EUR/GBP, 
expecting a rebound towards 0.8450. 
Rationale: The BoE hiked by 25bps to 4% as expected. The GBP 
was little changed, despite hawkish inflation guidance. With markets 
pricing only around 50bps of additional BoE tightening over the next 
12 months, we see the GBP remaining vulnerable. In contrast, the 
ECB held its deposit rate at 2.50% in October but signalled readiness 
to tighten if inflation remains elevated. We expect Euro area inflation 
to rise further, keeping the ECB’s tightening bias alive. This suggests 
EUR/GBP has fallen too far relative to interest rate differentials.  
Germany’s planned increase in federal debt issuance should 
support Euro area growth, while Moody’s decision not to downgrade 
France has removed an important EUR tail risk. Meanwhile, tighter 
UK fiscal policy is likely to weigh on domestic growth and business 
investment. We expect EUR/GBP to recover towards 0.8450. 
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist 
—  Iris Yuen, Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  Why is the AUD underperforming despite the RBA hike? 
Our view: The AUD has lagged because markets had largely 
priced in the RBA’s November rate hike to 4.85%, and the bank 
gave investors less reason to expect another. Equity weakness 
also weighed on the currency. We remain bullish on AUD/USD 
and would add on pullbacks. Australia’s interest rate advantage 
and a sustained recovery in risk sentiment should support a 
move towards 0.72 in three months and 0.74 in 12 months. 
Rationale: Ahead of the 3 November RBA rate decision, market 
pricing implied an 88% chance of a 25bps hike, leaving little scope 
for an upside surprise. What changed after the announcement 
was the pricing of the next hike. The RBA dropped its reference 
to further rate increases, and three members preferred a rate hold. 
Markets scaled back the odds of rates reaching 5.10% next year to 
around 30%, thus weighing on the AUD. 
The AUD is sensitive to swings in global risk appetite, especially 
the US tech sell-off spread to global equities, triggering margin calls 
and weighing on the currency. An equity recovery would ease that 
pressure. Even after the two 25bps Fed hikes we expect by mid-
2027, Australia would retain a 35bps rate advantage over the top 
of the Fed’s target range, supporting a gradual AUD recovery. 
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist 
—  Iris Yuen, Investment Strategist
