Last week was certainly a week to remember — or forget — in markets, depending on your 
point of view and positioning. Usually we save the weekly recap until the end on a Monday 
but there were some big moves work highlighting as we start a new week. The S&P 500 fell 
-2.68% (+0.31% Friday), marking its worst weekly performance since April 2025. 
Moreover, the most AI-exposed areas saw even bigger declines, with the Philly 
semiconductor index down -12.15% (+4.21% Friday), whilst CoreWeave fell another -
15.44% (+6.28% Friday). However rotation meant Consumers Discretionary (+2.68%) and 
Communications Services (+2.26%) led five sectors out of the 11 top level ones that rose 
last week, with Energy (-11.93%) actually performing worse than IT (-6.93%).   
Outside the US, Japan’s Nikkei fell -9.25% (-5.96% Friday), marking its worst weekly 
performance since the pandemic turmoil in March 2020. However, Europe’s STOXX 600 
managed to eke out a +0.24% gain (-1.00% Friday), reflecting Europe’s smaller exposure to 
the tech sector and a boost from falling oil prices. 
Indeed, whilst AI fears gathered pace, there was a huge slump in oil prices as investor fears 
eased about a potential escalation in the Middle East. The current active Brent crude 
contract for January 2027 fell -13.32% (+0.03% Friday), taking it down to $98.32/bbl. So 
along with the risk-off tone from the AI selloff, that helped to bring down sovereign bond 
yields around the world. So the 10yr Treasury yield fell -7.2bps (+2.1bps Friday) to 4.86%, 
whilst the 10yr bund yield fell -6.9bps (+1.4bps Friday) to 3.25%. In part, that recovery on 

Friday followed the US jobs report for October, which showed payrolls rising by +165k (vs. 
+70k expected), along with +84k of positive revisions to the previous two months. So 
that brought the 3-month moving average of payrolls up to +137k, whilst the 
unemployment rate also fell to 4.1% (vs. 4.2% expected). Credit was actually fairly flat 
on both sides of the Atlantic but the risk-off tone in equities meant it was a good week for 
gold, which was up +5.06% (-0.42% Friday) in its best week since August. 
We move on from payrolls to US inflation as the main macro focus this week, with October 
CPI tomorrow and PPI on Friday, alongside a busy slate of Fed speakers. Beyond the 
US, the key releases include China’s trade figures tomorrow, UK Q3 GDP on Thursday, and 
Japan’s October PPI on Thursday, while the corporate calendar includes Cisco, Applied 
Materials, Siemens, Allianz and SoftBank as Q3 winds down after another bumper 
quarter that’s been overshadowed by the recent market volatility. 
Asian equity markets are rallying this morning after Wall Street's rebound on Friday, 
although concerns surrounding an internal investigation involving Nvidia CEO Jensen 
Huang are capping gains. The company is down around -3.5% on platforms that provide 
weekend prices. 
The AI trade is trying to recover though this morning with Taiwan's Taiex (+4.94%) snapping 
a seven-session losing streak after the market regulator and finance ministry unveiled 
measures aimed at restoring investor confidence. Elsewhere, Japan's Nikkei (+2.63%) 
and TOPIX (+3.20%) are also trading sharply higher. The rebound follows a turbulent week 
that saw both Japanese and Taiwanese equities fall into bear-market territory. 
Meanwhile, South Korea's KOSPI (-0.61%) has just turned over after spending most of 
the session higher. Elsewhere the Shanghai Composite (+0.50%), CSI 300 (+0.31%), and 
Hang Seng (+0.08%) are edging higher. S&P 500 (+0.44%) and Nasdaq 100 (+0.25%) are 
continuing to recover some of Thursday's big losses. Meanwhile, the 10-year US Treasury 
yield is up +1bps at 4.87% and Brent down around a percent as I type. 
Earlier this morning, data showed that China's producer inflation accelerated to its 
fastest pace in nearly four-and-a-half years in October, as higher global oil prices 
drove up manufacturing costs, even as consumer inflation softened amid weak domestic 
demand. Producer prices (PPI) rose 5.7% year-on-year, exceeding both September's 
5.0% increase and expectations for a 5.3% gain. In contrast, consumer prices (CPI) 
increased 0.7% from a year earlier, slowing from 0.9% in September and coming in slightly 
below the consensus forecast of 0.8%. The divergent inflation trends highlight the 
challenges facing Beijing as it seeks to revive household consumption while 
maintaining momentum in the industrial sector. Although policymakers have introduced 

a range of measures to support spending and tackle excessive competition, a sustained 
recovery in consumer demand and prices remains elusive. 
Going through the week ahead in more detail, in the US, tomorrow’s October CPI is the 
centrepiece. Our economists expect headline CPI to rise by 0.20% month-on-month, 
down from 0.65% previously, with core CPI also up 0.20% after 0.33% last time. 
Because October 2025 index levels were not produced during last year’s government 
shutdown, year-on-year inflation rates cannot be calculated for this release. Within 
core goods, our economists expect softer medical-care commodity prices to offset some 
strength in recreation and information-technology goods. In services, they expect owners’ 
equivalent rent to moderate from September, although recreation services could be firmer 
following streaming-price increases, while the annual health-insurance reset may provide a 
downward contribution. 
Friday’s October PPI will be important for the read-across to core PCE inflation. Our 
economists expect producer prices to rise by 0.20% month-on-month, compared with 
0.32% in September, leaving the year-on-year rate broadly steady at 3.1%. The inflation 
releases arrive alongside a heavy run of Fed commentary, with around two-thirds of 
officials due to speak during the week. You’ll see them listed alongside the other major 
events in our day-by-day calendar at the end as usual. Attention will be on how 
policymakers assess the recent tightening in financial conditions against still-solid 
economic and labour-market momentum and inflation that remains above target. Our 
economists continue to expect 25bp rate increases at the December and March FOMC 
meetings, absent a further material tightening in financial conditions. US bond markets are 
closed on Wednesday for Veterans’ Day. 
In Europe, Thursday’s UK GDP report is the main event. Our economists expect 
September GDP to contract by -0.1% month-on-month but expect Q3 growth overall to 
reach 0.7% quarter-on-quarter. Norway and Denmark also publish October CPI 
tomorrow. 
Japan’s calendar includes the October Economy Watchers survey and September 
household spending tomorrow, alongside the BoJ’s summary of opinions from its October 
policy meeting. October PPI follows on Thursday. Elsewhere in the region, New Zealand 
holds a general election tomorrow. 
On the corporate side, the US earnings season is winding down, with close to 90% of S&P 
500 companies having already reported. KKR reports today, followed by SoftBank 
tomorrow; Thursday brings Cisco, Applied Materials, Siemens, Allianz and Cerebras, 

among others. CoreWeave tomorrow and Cerebras on Thursday should also offer another 
read on AI-related demand and investment. 
Our asset allocation team have reviewed the global earnings season here and highlighted 
how growth has remained remarkably strong in Q3 despite a very high hurdle after 38% 
year-on-year growth in Q2 and persistent upgrades ahead of the reporting season. 
Global earnings growth likely eased to 33%, still one of the highest rates on record, with 
Tech, Energy and Materials leading but growth excluding those sectors also a robust 12%, 
towards the top of its historical range, while record margins provided further support. US 
earnings growth likely held at 34%, with the median company growing 14% and growth 
excluding Tech, Energy and Materials at 13%, one of the strongest rates since the GFC. 
But Europe was arguably the standout, with growth accelerating from 22% to a four-
year high of 28%, leaving the gap with the US at its narrowest in 3½ years, while Europe 
is now matching or exceeding US growth in 6 of 11 sectors. Japan (33%) and EM (39%) 
slowed sharply but remained very strong, and forward estimates continue to be upgraded, 
particularly in Korea and Taiwan. So although consensus sees some moderation in 2027, 
the striking feature of this earnings season has been how broad-based the strength 
remains even against an exceptionally demanding starting point.
