Equity market turbulence and steep energy relief since the end of October have

n

markets that either have a strong beta to energy—such as Italy and Greece—or have

increasingly stands out amid similar difficulties in addressing elevated deficits and 
political uncertainty, with PM De Wever offering his resignation this week. In this 
context, Belgium OLOs have traded less as a beta to OATs. And in contrast to other 
recent episodes of heightened idiosyncratic focus on France, OLOs have also 
underperformed relative to France and Austria (Exhibit 6). Against this, this week 
also provided evidence that the semi-core is highly levered to global developments, 
benefiting more than global peers from improving geopolitical sentiment in the 
Middle East or sell-off in risk assets. This gives us confidence that energy relief and a 
decline in rates volatility can anchor sovereign spreads lower next year, including in 
places where fiscal risks are elevated. Because of this, we continue to forecast 10y 
OAT-Bund at 130bp by the middle of next year. Compression beyond that point, 
however, likely requires more credible signals towards deficit reductions in France 
and Belgium. This suggests to us the correlation between OATs and OLOs is likely to 
remain more subdued than usual, with markets attuned to the specifics of fiscal 
news.
