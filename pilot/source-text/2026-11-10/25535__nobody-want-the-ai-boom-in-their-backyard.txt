The AI backlash
The AI boom has a new enemy: politics. Morgan Stanley warns that the Democratic midterm sweep
could threaten the massive data center buildout, with Texas emerging as a major flashpoint.
Meanwhile, BofA highlights overwhelming public opposition just as Goldman raises its global capacity
forecasts aggressively. The disconnect is striking: Wall Street expects unprecedented infrastructure
expansion while politicians and voters increasingly stand in the way. The second-order
consequences could create major winners and losers across the AI infrastructure complex.
Not in my backyard
BofA's Hartnett highlights the growing political backlash against AI infrastructure. A remarkable 76% of
surveyed voters oppose new data centers in their communities. This is becoming a serious problem for
the AI buildout, particularly as the Democrats gain political influence. The industry needs enormous
new capacity, but voters increasingly don't want it anywhere near them.

Texas changes everything
Morgan Stanley sees Texas as the biggest political risk to the AI buildout. The newly elected
Democratic governor has advocated a moratorium on new data center approvals, threatening
significant project delays. The scale of the problem is extraordinary: grid interconnection requests in
Texas have reached 474 GW, more than five times the state's record peak electricity demand,
although many requests may be speculative. The key question is no longer just how much capacity the
industry wants to build, but how much will actually receive approval.
The great AI capacity paradox
Meanwhile, Wall Street is still raising its AI infrastructure forecasts. Goldman has increased its 2030
global data center capacity estimate from 217 GW to 273 GW, with US capacity revised up from 125
GW to 152 GW. That's an extraordinary 170 GW of additional global capacity after 2025. GS
acknowledges growing risks from power constraints, regulation and leverage, but still expects massive
expansion. The irony? Capacity expectations keep rising just as the political obstacles to delivering that
capacity are multiplying.

The bond market twist

The implications extend beyond equities. Morgan Stanley highlights that slower data center
construction could reduce AI-related corporate debt issuance, potentially easing long-duration bond
supply and supporting longer-dated Treasury prices. With the AI financing boom becoming an
increasingly important source of duration, political restrictions could have unexpected consequences
for the bond market. Bad news for future AI construction doesn't necessarily mean bad news for all
assets.
Scarcity is the new premium
Here comes the interesting second-order effect. Morgan Stanley argues that restrictions on new data
center construction could actually benefit existing facilities with secured power connections. Less
future supply means greater scarcity value and potentially stronger pricing power. Established
operators such as EQIX and DLR could benefit, while speculative developers relying on future
approvals face mounting uncertainty. The AI trade may increasingly be about who already has access
to power, rather than who promises to build more capacity.
