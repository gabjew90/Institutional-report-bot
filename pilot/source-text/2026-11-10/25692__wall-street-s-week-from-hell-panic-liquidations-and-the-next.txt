Front-running the front-runners
Everybody knows the mechanical selling is coming. Nomura highlights how discretionary traders are
increasingly front-running systematic deleveraging, leveraged ETF rebalancing and dealer hedging.
Meanwhile, dealers are sitting on roughly $7bn of negative gamma per 1% SPX move. The theoretical
risk is a self-reinforcing selling spiral.
But knowing the gamma exposure doesn't mean the flows are easy to front-run. Anyone who has
managed a large short-gamma book knows the agony of deciding when to hedge. Dealers may have
already sold in anticipation, adjusted hedges intraday or reduced exposure well before the close.
Knowing the potential selling pressure is one thing. Knowing when, or whether, it actually hits the
market is another. More on the latest vol moves.
Nomura

The mechanical selling machine
Vol-control funds have absolutely puked risk over the past week. These strategies trade mechanically
based on volatility, with no room for discretion. When everyone is forced to sell simultaneously, the
process becomes dangerously self-reinforcing. Liquidity becomes the real problem, as we saw
yesterday. The irony? Most models still fail to account for liquidity risk in any realistic way, precisely
when it matters most.
