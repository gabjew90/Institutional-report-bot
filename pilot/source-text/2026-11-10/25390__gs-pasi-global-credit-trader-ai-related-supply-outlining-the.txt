GS Pasi - Global Credit Trader: AI Related Supply: Outlining the
Directional Risks 6 Nov 2026
Alisha Pasi  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Vice President, FICC & Equities
6 Nov 2026
5 November 2026 | 4:46PM EST | Research | Credit Strategy
• Market participants are viewing capital structures of AI-related issuers as part of a holistic framework.
This reflects the recognition that access to debt financing is a strategic competitive advantage in financing
the AI buildout. Since October 29th alone, there have been five separate bond deals among the hyperscaler
universe, totaling $67 billion. This has pushed hyperscalers’ year-to-date, global debt issuance to $296
billion—now well above our full-year 2026 forecast for $250 billion.
• The year-to-date hyperscaler issuance represents 36% of the aggregate Capex forecast by our colleagues
in GIR equity research for 2026; this is above our initial expectation for 33% of 2026 Capex to be debt
funded. In some instances, this reflects a pull-forward of planned 2027 supply. We see scope for more
front-loading of issuance, albeit highly dependent on the market tone. Between now and early December,
we continue to view the risks to our forecasts as firmly tilted towards more widening.
• We are still penciling in that 35% of hyperscalers' Capex will be debt financed in 2027, which equates to
roughly $440 billion of direct issuance. We also continue to expect an additional $300 billion of project
finance issuance in 2027, across IG and HY. We believe the HY primary market may be in a 'price
discovery' phase in the near term, with a sharp focus on structures for project finance deals, once issuance
resumes.
• Delayed monetization and growth in purchase commitments—which we view as additional and sizable
uses of cash, separate from Capex—are the key risks to more debt issuance, subject to market conditions.
Acceleration in near-term monetization of AI investment is the key risk to less debt issuance, in our view.
• Our economists have noted the US midterms outcome is less likely to change the outlook for data center
and AI regulation. In the near term, short-term data center moratoriums are possible, potentially affecting
about 45% of the planned US data center capacity expansion (with a large portion in Texas). Over the long
term, however, they expect the moratoriums will not be long-lived. This underscores a bottom-up,
selective approach to project finance deals with a greater focus on structures.
Source: Goldman Sachs FICC & Equities, Marquee, as of 6 Nov 2026.
