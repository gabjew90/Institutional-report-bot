 
Hyperscalers Rip, Semis Rot In One Of The Widest 
Splits In A Decade 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, NOV 05, 2026 - 04:35 AM 
On the surface, Wednesday looked like a classic post-election relief rally. Under the surface, it 
was a demolition.  
Nasdaq 100 futures ripped roughly 400bps off the overnight lows hit when Texas turned blue, 
and yet, as Goldman's TMT sector specialist Peter Callahan points out in his latest US TMT 
Today note (available to pro subs), the Nasdaq outperformed the Philadelphia Semiconductor 
Index (SOX) by nearly 460bps, "a top 10 daily spread in 10+ years."Apple, Amazon and 
Microsoft did the heavy lifting... 
... while semis, the one time "picks and shovels" favorites of the momentum crowd, "continue to 
move further below their 200-dma," with AI names like Intel (INTC), Cerebras (CBRS), Marvell 
(MRVL), Teradyne (TER), Caterpillar (CAT) and F5 (FFIV) all in "oversold" territory. Flex (FLEX) 
puked 14%. 
Or, as we put it in today's market wrap, the money moved "from the Check Receivers into the 
Check Writers." Which raises the obvious question: why would a Democrat sweep that 
threatens to freeze up to 45% of planned US data center capacity be good for the companies 
that rent out data centers? 
If You Can't Build It, Why Buy The Chips? 
Goldman's trading desk had the cleanest answer, describing "the clear rotation into hyperscalers 
with Semis being used as a source of funds." The logic comes in two steps: 
●​ "If compute is more scarce because you cannot build more capacity, you can sell it for at 
a higher price." 
●​ "If you cannot build more capacity, you are spending less, which in turn may lead to 
buying back stock again." 
 

 
In other words, a moratorium turns every data center already standing into a toll road, and the 
hyperscalers own most of the toll roads. Goldman's top IG credit trader Kevin Boova made the 
same point from the bond side, seeing "pretty big supply/demand imbalances in compute... 
which should be a big positive for infrastructure already in place and thus the hypers." 
Hyperscaler CDS duly tightened, even as data center CDS blew out to fresh 2026 highs. 
The pricing side of that argument already has some data behind it. In a note published this 
morning, Wells Fargo's machinery team points out that H100 rental rates are up 26% y/y, 
GB200 rental pricing is up 50% YTD (and 12% in the last month alone), and Oracle reported 
97.9% AI infrastructure utilization with a 20% average renewal/resale premium. Put that next to 
what happened to the chipmakers' multiple, and you get the day's trade in one picture: 
 
As for the buyback half of the thesis, well, that's a bigger leap. As we showed last Saturday, the 
hyperscalers collectively burned $31 billion of free cash flow in Q3, and Microsoft needed a $25 
billion bond deal the morning after its "clean" beat. A slower buildout would help, but the 
companies would first have to stop borrowing before they start buying back stock. 
The market didn't wait for the math. Callahan tipped his hat to Microsoft for "registering a new 
ATH high today," calling it an "impressive turnaround from the narrative overhang the stock was 
facing for much of 2H'25/1H'26": 
 

 
 
Inbound questions to Goldman's desk tell the same story: "why is AMZN outperforming by so 
much?" (Amazon surged more than 8%) and "'really', with Apple back at ATHs?" Really. 
28x To 16x In Two Months 
Then there are the check receivers. Away from "triangulating around campaign policy vs 
practical implications," Callahan thinks the bigger semis debate after today "will just be about 
what got 'priced in'": 
"... as Semiconductor multiples compressed >40% over the last 2-months (from 28x to 16x – top 
chart). Can see below, but the SOX's current ~2x fwd P/E discount to the market (S&P500) is 
near the wides over the last 15 years(bottom chart)." 
 

 
 
Said otherwise, the AI chip complex now trades at a lower multiple than the S&P 500 it spent 
three years carrying. Semis traded at a deeper discount only in 2017-18 and, briefly, in the 2022 
bear market, back when chips were still treated as a cyclical afterthought. 
Regular readers saw this coming. One day earlier, we tweeted the observations of Goldman's 
Delta 1 head, who noted that the SOX was trading "through Liberation Day and Iran escalation 
lows" on a blended 24-month basis, with Nvidia at its lowest multiple since 2013, and asked the 
right question: 
"Potentially 2027 is a peak" is the key line, and Tuesday night's results only made it scarier: if 
Texas, Georgia and Michigan freeze new projects, the 2027 chip orders tied to those projects 
will be the first thing to slip.  
That's exactly why semis didn't get the hyperscalers' bounce (and why last week we asked 
which 1999 we are in, Santa rally or SOX crash). Even Goldman derivatives guru Brian Garrett, 
who saw seven signs of "healing" over the weekend as semis snapped a 355-day streak, 
cautioned that "we just have to survive 2026 first." Make that survive 2026 and the new 
governors. 
Earnings didn't help either, even when they were good. AMD round-tripped from +9% after 
hours to flat, despite checking "the 2 big boxes investors were worried about": Goldman nudged 
its 2027 gross margin estimate up to 55.7%, and the company offset PC weakness with data 
center strength, guiding to ">80% revs growth in '27." Callahan's verdict: the debate "likely shifts 
 

 
to the 'multiple' as GPUs mix in and as broader Semis de-rate(d)." Arista (ANET) faded from 
+10% to +5% after what Callahan calls "one of the better prints of this AI cycle," with revenue 
growth accelerating into the 50s and an initial 2027 guide of +38-40% vs. expectations near 
30%.

history, down ~$18 over the last five days," good for a ~15pt spread between Energy and

Which, maybe, is the point: the money that left semis stayed close to home, rotating into the 
cheapest consumer names on the board, the oil-sensitive ones, and the megacaps whose 
capex bill just might get capped by Austin. 
 

 
Narrative, Meet Outcome 
The open question, per Callahan, is whether the market is now "over-pricing" policy outcomes, 
"esp should these moves continue beyond today (e.g. CSPs > Semis, drawdowns in IPPs or 
E&Cs, sharp rally in consumer discretionary, etc)." Goldman's economists offered the semis 
bulls a lifeline in their initial election recap: 
"However, since data centers are low on the list of priorities for voters this election, we expect 
that the issue will fade somewhat in importance in the ensuing months and that these moratoria 
will not be long-lived if they are enacted." 
Maybe. But the Texas pause probably runs at least through the end of the legislative session on 
May 31, 2027, per Wells Fargo, which puts it squarely on top of the year the Delta 1 desk 
flagged as "potentially... a peak." And the bigger problem is that today's pair trade contradicts 
itself. Boova himself doesn't "think we see capex numbers come down anytime soon." If he's 
right, the hyperscalers keep spending, the buyback story stays a story, and semis at a 2x 
discount to the S&P are the cheap side of the trade. If he's wrong and capex really does get 
capped, then 2027 chip estimates are too high and the de-rating - premise on years of 
relentless capex expansion - has a lot further to run. 
Our bet is on the second: the projects that can't get permits in Texas will probably move to 
Louisiana or Oklahoma eventually, but "eventually" is a long time for a sector whose entire 
multiple rests on next year's orders. The hyperscalers can wait out a moratorium; the companies 
selling them chips, all of which have massive debt loads, can't. Then again, a week ago the 
consensus said the same about everything AI.
