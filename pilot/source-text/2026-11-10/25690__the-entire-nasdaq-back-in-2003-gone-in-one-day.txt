The week that broke the tape: 14
extraordinary charts
It was an extraordinary week across markets, with some of the largest moves in years, technology
under intense pressure and the AI trade showing further signs of fatigue.
From the Nasdaq's $2.5 trillion one-day wipeout to historic semiconductor moves, collapsing
momentum and extreme valuations, here are the charts that stood out.
The entire Nasdaq back in 2003 — gone in
one day
The Nasdaq shed a staggering $2.5 trillion in market capitalization on Thursday. For perspective, the
ENTIRE Nasdaq market was worth just $2.95 trillion at the end of 2003. Almost the value of the entire
Nasdaq back in 2003 — erased in a single trading session.
Dow Jones Market Data
Worse than the dot-com crash
The SOX suffered its second-worst daily decline on record, according to Morgan Stanley. Not even the
bursting of the dot-com bubble in 2000 produced a worse single-day drop.

AI & the bubble club
BofA's Hartnett notes that the AI Big 10 now accounts for 41% of US market capitalization, matching
the concentration reached by TMT at the dot-com peak and approaching the extremes of Japan in
1989 and the Nifty Fifty. History doesn't say concentration has to reverse immediately. But it does
suggest the AI trade is now operating in rather exclusive company.
