Outlook 2027-28: Global Economy On Solid, But Unusually Narrow FootingGlobal Economics: The AI
infrastructure build-out has neutralized tariff and energy headwinds and is driving over half of global trade
growth, a large share of global GDP growth, and half of year-to-date equity gains. UBS outlook hinges largely
on that build-out continuing and on some resolution in the Middle East, the main source of growth acceleration
in 2027. Bottom-up aggregation points to $1.75 trn of hyperscaler capex next year, but public opposition to
data centres could delay and reprofile near-term spending. How the equity market responds to that has a
significant bearing on these forecasts. Enterprise adoption remains low, and AI implementation spending is
unusually concentrated. Although economist Arend Kapetyn can’t yet see AI in the productivity data, wage
restraint during this energy shock has been notable, and global equity earnings growth is running at the 91st
historical percentile. He expects the environment to become progressively more disinflationary in 2027, as
inflation from tariffs, energy and memory price fades. Arend expects only modest additional tightening, and
then a reversal of the hikes in 2028. In equities, favour Nasdaq over RTY, industrials over consumer, and
growth over value. In fixed income, terminal rates look mispriced across most markets; favour steepeners in
the US, and flatteners in Europe. In FX and commodities, favour long gold vs. EUR and long JPY vs. CHF.
(Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Circuit Breakers Fall Short: Caution Warranted Absent Sponsorship – Expect US equities to remain
vulnerable in early trading this week with another test of technical support / the 200-day moving average likely.
Squeeze risk is acute; if that clearing event comes without a corresponding deterioration in credit, rates or FX,
look to add risk rather than protection. Over the past 2 months, none of the circuit breakers discussed in this
note as catalysts to fresh highs have manifested. Rate pressures spiraled into funding pressures, oil prices
soared as Mideast conflict continued, hyperscaler earnings fell short, and the Democrats dominated the
mid-terms. Despite continued AI demand, the result has been a violent repricing in AI as investors have
demanded a large discount to add AI risk. Instead, investors have shifted incremental dollars away from Tech,
and even away from equities and into a credit, where AI exposure is growing exponentially. The rotation away
from Tech crescendoed last week as systematic selling triggered over 365bn of excess sell flow on Thursday,
the largest on record. While most of the systematic selling is behind us, with a residual amount Monday, the
bigger issue for the market is absence of long-only demand. This is the focal point for flows from here. See the
full note. (Michael Romano, Head of Hedge Fund Derivative Sales, Americas)

China’s Producer Price Inflation Accelerates Even As CPI Cools – China’s October CPI rose 0.7% y/y,
below September’s 0.9% and the 0.8% consensus forecast. Prices were unchanged m/m after September’s
0.2% rise and core inflation eased to 0.8% from 1.0%. Meanwhile, PPI rose 5.7% y/y, versus 5.0% previously
and 5.3% expected, its highest since June 2022. Higher oil prices lifted energy-intensive industries’ costs,
while weak household demand restrained consumer inflation. The divergence suggests limited upstream price
pass-through so far and could complicate Beijing’s efforts to revive domestic consumption while supporting
industrial activity. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
RBA’s Hunter On AI: "Some Upward Pressure On Inflation" – RBA Chief Economist Sarah Hunter
sounded overall positive/hawkish on the impact of AI at the UBS Australasia conference, saying there’s limited

Increase in demand for investment could push up the neutral rate of interest, she said. Economist George
Tharenou still expects the RBA to keep the cash rate restrictive for a prolonged time. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro
