So, let's explore that shitshow next... 
Energy 
The dominant catalyst behind today's sharp selloff in crude oil is the Democratic sweep of both 
chambers of Congress in Tuesday's US midterm elections, which markets are interpreting as a 
meaningful step toward a quicker resolution of the ongoing Iran conflict.  
Brent crude has plunged roughly 10% to a session low of $93.22 - its lowest level in two months 
- while WTI has fallen approximately 9% to around $87–88 a barrel.  
 
 

 
The political read is straightforward: the midterms were widely seen as a referendum on the war 
and on affordability, and the Democratic victory is being priced as a reduction in the geopolitical 
risk premium that has kept crude elevated for months.  
Adding further downward pressure, a social media post from Vice President JD Vance explicitly 
blaming Republican losses on the Middle East war reinforced the narrative of a potential 
de-escalation. 
Bloomberg's Michael Ball noted that both future and option markets are moving decisively. 
Crude put volume has been running around 1.6 times calls, with heavy interest in December 
$85 and $83 WTI puts. Brent saw similar bearish flows with its call skew falling notably. That 
increasingly looks like higher conviction positioning for declines to stick.  
More importantly, physical markets are validating the move. North Sea physical crude markets 
reflected the same dynamic, with the Forties grade offered down $3.80 to a premium of just 
$1.35/bbl over Dated Brent.  
 
JPMorgan separately noted that the global oil market had already moved back into surplus in 
September and October, as recovering Middle Eastern flows and rising production elsewhere 
outpaced weak demand - providing a fundamental backdrop that amplified today's geopolitical 
repricing. 
On diesel specifically, the picture is more nuanced.  
US distillate stockpiles fell below 100 million barrels for the first time since 2003 - a structurally 
tight supply signal - with the steepest declines concentrated in the Midwest, where inventories 
hit their lowest since November 2014.  
 

 
 
Despite that tightness, diesel prices crashed lower in sympathy with the broader crude complex, 
as the geopolitical risk unwind overwhelms the supply-side support.  US diesel prices hit near 
two month lows... 
 
The reflexivity of this is too ironic to ignore - the thing (Trump's Iran War) that basically triggered 
the Blue Wave (higher cost of living) is now crashing (to lower the cost of living) benefiting 
Americans as Dems take over (and Trump considers ending the War... too late). 
Nevertheless, lower oil meant lower rates... 
 

 
Rates 
The collapse in oil dragged down US inflation breakevens, with 10Y BEs dropping to their 
richest levels since August... 
 
...supporting a strong bid in Treasuries overall, but it was a wild ride. Yields are down 6-8bps 
across the curve with the belly modestly outperforming... 
 
The 10Y Yield held firmly below 5.00% again - to its lowest closing level in two months... 
 

 
 
Arguably, traders interpreted the Democratic win in Congress as a constraint on the Trump 
admin's fiscal agenda - limiting the scope for large deficit-expanding tax cuts - which reduced 
the supply premium embedded in longer-dated bonds. 
The Treasury's quarterly refunding statement added a secondary catalyst: the department 
maintained auction sizes for notes and bonds unchanged "for the next several quarters," 
dropping the prior "at least" qualifier - a subtle but meaningful signal that dealers interpreted as 
opening the door to potential future supply increases, though not imminently. 
Interestingly, given the decline in oil prices, Dec rate-hike odds actually rose (hawkish) modestly 
today... 
 

 
 
Obviously, Friday's payrolls print is now the key near-term catalyst, with the potential to trigger a 
significant repricing of the Fed path if they surprise to the upside. 
Fiat & Anti-Fiat

Gold mirrored the dollar with its best day since August, back above $4050... 
 

 
 
Bitcoin's move was more nuanced with weakness to start overnight (Crypto sceptics taking

above $80,000... 
 
Finally, we return to where we started above with a reminder of the shocking consistence of 
post-midterm seasonality. JPMorgan trader, Craig Cohen, notes that the SPX  has been up a 
year after every midterm election since 1950 with an average gain of around 15%.  
 

 
 
The worst one-year forward return came after the 1986 midterms but this, of course, had more 
to do with the 1987 crash rather than anything going on in Washington.  
 "I suspect that at least part of the reason for this tendency is that the midterms tend to see the 
incumbent party loses seats and gridlock in Washington tends to be good for Wall Street.”  
Which fits with JPMorgan's pre-results expectations... 
JPMorgan's Market Intel folks also noted that on a 3-month basis, the SPX has averaged a 
+5.4% return, 11.4% on a 6-month basis, 12.5% on a 9-month basis, and 14.7% on a 12-month 
basis.  
 

 
 
The six, nine, and twelve month returns have a 100% hit rate dating to 1990.
