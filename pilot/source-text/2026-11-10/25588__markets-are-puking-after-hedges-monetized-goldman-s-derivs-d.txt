 
Markets Are Puking After Hedges Monetized: 
Goldman's Derivs Desk Says Buy The Tails Now 
That Nobody Is Paying For Them 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, NOV 05, 2026 - 05:45 PM 
The day after the midterms, everybody's hedges paid out at once. 
This morning, with hedges lifted, the plain-old selling is under way... 
 
NDX has broken back below its 200DMA... 
 

 
 
As one veteran trader MSGd us: "this feels very liquidation-y, someone just got carried out..." 
Algo Fingerprints 
The high-volume selloff in S&P e-minis around the open this morning bears the hallmarks of 
programmatic sales rather than indiscriminate de-risking, according to Bloomberg's Alyce 
Andres. 
The move was sharp and fast, with about a 150-point in the benchmark contract drop in roughly 
35 minutes from the open. It’s characteristic of algorithmic execution, and computer-generated 
sales were likely triggered by Commodity Trading Advisors as the contract slid through 7665 -- 
that’s a level where programs would trigger sales and reduction of long positions, according to 
strategists at TD Securities.  
 

 
 
The momentum-following nature of CTAs risks creating a continued chain reaction. Volumes in 
the contract are more than a million, which is elevated for this time of day. Meanwhile, price 
action is consistent with large spikes in volume, which also aligns with the possibility of chunky 
programmatic activity. 
"What I Got Wrong" 
In a must-read note this morning, Goldman's Delta One desk-head, Rich Privorotsky, explained: 
This is what I got wrong. Tech flows (at least in Europe) were overwhelmingly bearish, with long 
only and hedge funds selling everything from chips to infrastructure. We're back to somewhat 
unfalsifiable concerns about AI spending. Legislative restrictions are difficult to quantify 
(particularly in out year), but building will become more expensive, complicated and slower. 
Multiples have compressed enormously, positioning was already derisked and another 
aggressive wave of selling followed... The market is definitively pricing peak CapEx growth. 
Goldman's European industrials specialist Jeremy Elster summed it up well... 
"...we are past the peak of good news and now in a de-rating cycle for the AI complex..." 
But the Vol desk had some ideas before this morning's carnage... 
 

 
Own The (Cheap) Tails 
 
As event premium drained from the options surface after Tuesday's Democratic sweep, vol 
didn't just ease, it was, in the words of Goldman's derivatives desk, "absolutely crushed." In its 
latest GS Vol Views note (available to pro subs), the desk writes that its flows "signaled 
investors were sufficiently hedged during last Friday's turbulence," and then: 
As event premium came out of the surface post midterms, however, vol got absolutely crushed 
and risk parameters reset sharply -- our Panic Index saw its second largest 1d drop in the last 
20 years. 
In other words, the market sold its insurance in one of the biggest single-day fire sales in two 
decades. Here's Goldman's Panic Index, showing Wednesday's -4.25 move, beaten only by a 
-4.68 print back in 2012-13 (chart source GS FICC and Equities): 
 

 
 
Earlier this morning Goldman's Rich Privorotsky, whose note we covered in ""This Is What I Got 
Wrong": Top Goldman Trader Flips On AI As Market Prices Peak CapEx...", described the same 
day as a "remarkable collapse in index vol against the violence underneath." The Vol Views note 
is the options desk's take on just how remarkable, and the punchline is that the calm on the 
surface may be the most dangerous part of the setup. 
VIX Down 20%, VVIX Down 17 Points, Skew Relaxed 
Everywhere 
Start with the scoreboard. The VIX closed at 16.61, down 4.22 points (about 20%), while the 
Nasdaq's VXN fell 18% to 25.08. Goldman's index vol desk, in its Equity Index Vol Update on 
Wednesday night (available to pro subs), described a universal wipeout: 
Vols opened hammered across the board. As the market chopped within a tight range in the 
afternoon, vols melted even further. The vol crush was also universal across all three major 
indices. NDX Dec fixed strike vol was down more than 3v, RUT Dec fixed strike vol was down 
almost 2v, and SPX Dec fixed strike vol was down more than 1v. Risk parameters reset lower 
dramatically, with skew relaxing meaningfully across the board and vol of vol crushed. 
The VIX futures curve, which had been inverted into the vote, "shifted lower and reverted to 
contango (Nov VIX fut 17.74 vs Dec 18)," per Vol Views. And the vol of vol collapsed: VVIX fell 
17 points on the day "as investors monetized hedges."That's one of the largest one-day drops in 
 

 
the past five years (the red line marks Wednesday's -16.99; only a handful of days, mostly 
around the August 2024 and April 2025 vol spikes, did worse):

Semis Vs Hyperscalers: Second Only To DeepSeek Monday 
Then there's what was going on under the hood. As we detailed last night in "Hyperscalers Rip, 
Semis Rot In One Of The Widest Splits In A Decade", Wednesday's tape was a rotation out of 
the AI "check receivers" and into the "check writers." Goldman's derivatives desk now puts a 
number on just how violent: 
The semis to hyperscalers rotation was the most prominent theme under the hood yesterday -- 
the spread between 1d returns of GSXUHYPR and GSTMTDAT was the second largest on 
record, only behind Deepseek Monday. 
The spread between Goldman's hyperscaler basket and its data center/semis basket hit 9.23% 
in a single session, topped only by the 11.02% on DeepSeek Monday in January 2025: 
 
Apple, Amazon and Microsoft all ripped more than 5% as the money moved: 
 

 
The desk's explanation is the same one we heard across the Street: "lower overall spending on 
new capacity is a positive, reviving hopes for a stronger free-cash-flow outlook." And Goldman's 
prime brokerage data shows room for more, with positioning in the Mag7 having "reset hard in 
late October (back to the 55th %ile vs the past year), opening room to add length again." 
We'd note, however, that the "stronger FCF" leg still has to survive the hyperscalers' $31 billion 
of negative free cash flow in Q3, not to mention the off-balance sheet tab, which as we pointed 
out overnight Goldman tallies at $1.7 trillion in leases and $1.9 trillion in purchase commitments, 
with $685 billion due by the end of 2027: 
Scarcity may be good for those who own the capacity, but someone still has to pay for it. And if 
Texas, Georgia and Michigan follow through on the moratoria that Goldman thinks could hit 45% 
of planned data center capacity, the bill may get smaller, but probably not soon enough to show 
up in 2026 buybacks. 
How Goldman Would Trade It 
With vol cheap and the tails fat, the desk's trade ideas lean into both: 
●​ Upside, capped: SPY 30Nov26 782/797 call spreads for $3.29 (19-delta, ref $766.98). 
The logic: "reducing net vol exposure to the upside makes sense as we've rallied back 
into a pocket of dealer long gamma." 
●​ Downside, outright: SPY 30Nov26 750 puts for $4.75 (26-delta, ref $766.73), which 
"capitalizes on the sharp vol reset and dealers' shorter gamma to the downside." 
●​ The AI-shock hedge: an SPXXAI 18Dec26 95% lookback put (daily lookback) for 1.81% 
vs a 1.3% offer on the vanilla, which "eliminates timing risk if the market keeps 
broadening out before a correlated shock lower." 
●​ The blue-wave reversion play: TAN 15Jan27 55 calls for $1.00 (16-delta, ref $46.18), a 
bet on "sharp reversion in the beaten-up solar theme that should benefit from 
Democratic support." After a 5.65% jump, TAN still sits 38% below its YTD highs. 
Note the asymmetry: the upside trade is a spread, the downside trade is naked. Goldman will 
rent you the rally, but it wants to own the crash. 
On the earnings front, the desk notes about 78% of S&P market cap will have reported by this 
weekend with EPS growth on track for 33% y/y. The median beat has outperformed the S&P by 
152bps the next day, above the 95bps historical median, but "earnings beats among TMT 
stocks, however, have generally gone unrewarded." Which is all you need to know about how 
much good news is priced into AI. 
 

 
Calm Is Cheap, And That's The Problem 
Privorotsky's read this morning was that "nets are too low for the broader market to go down 
easily," and that vol compression, stable rates and lower oil make for "a constructive grind 
higher." His derivatives colleagues don't exactly disagree; they just don't want to bet on it 
without a hedge. Their line on the week ahead is a reminder that the election was only one of 
the overhangs: 
Geopolitical overhangs remain topical as we emerge from the bulk of earnings season -- the 
implied move through the rest of the week goes out at 0.80%. 
Our take: Wednesday's crush priced the end of uncertainty, but very little has actually been 
resolved. The Iran war isn't over (oil is merely "pricing as if the war is over," per Privorotsky), the 
AI capex hangover is just starting, and Nasdaq futures were already underperforming again this 
morning as the memory selloff spread. Meanwhile $164 billion of CTA supply sits under a 
market where everyone just sold their puts. When fear gets this cheap this fast, history (and 
Garrett, who warned on Sunday that "we just have to survive 2026 first") suggests the next 
move in vol is far more likely to be up than down. Goldman's own skew tells you which side it 
would rather own.
