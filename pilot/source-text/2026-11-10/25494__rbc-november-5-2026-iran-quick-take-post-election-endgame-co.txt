Iran Quick Take: Post Election Endgame 
Conflict Calculations Following the US Midterm Elections’ Blue Wave 
• 
While voter anger over high energy prices propelled Tuesday’s blue wave, a deal to bring the 
unpopular Iran war to a near-term conclusion will remain challenging. From our standpoint, 
the most likely course of action for the remainder of the year appears to be either a military 
escalation or a continuation of the blockade/ongoing tanker attack stalemate despite the 
public airing of grievances about the war by Vice President Vance. 
• 
During our recent trip to the Gulf, we encountered significant skepticism about the 
likelihood of a negotiated settlement that would lead to the full reopening of the Strait of 
Hormuz given Iran’s continued insistence on the full implementation of the June MOU. At 
the same time, the US embassy drawdowns, regional travel advisories, and cancellation of US 
government and corporate participation in major conferences seemingly underscored the 
clear and present risk of further kinetic action. 
• 
Throughout the week, we were struck by the divergence between deepening escalation 
concerns in the Gulf and the headline driven off-ramp optimism of market participants 
thousands of miles away. The selloff in the days preceding the midterms seemed predicated 
on the idea that the White House was extremely incentivized to stave off a democratic 
landslide by announcing a peace deal. Now that the electoral debacle has occurred, the theory 
of the deal case has shifted to one of the President cutting his losses in the face of public 
rebukes from the America-first camp and Senator Chuck Schumer’s (D-NY) pledge to end the 
war. 
• 
Certainly, we think the prospects of a sustainable agreement would rise if the Iranians 
offered major concessions on the nuclear program, cash transfers, and Hormuz control 
rights. Hence, we will be monitoring the Qatari diplomatic track for any signs of a material 
shift. However, there are no indications yet that the Iranian leadership has moderated such 
demands, and, if anything, they have seemingly increased their social media trolling of the 
White House post midterms. Attacks on vessels continue, with the IRGC reporting that several 
oil tankers have hit waterway mines and caught fire since last night, while separate reports 
suggest a tanker off the coast of Qatar was struck by projectiles. At a minimum, the White 
House will likely keep the current CENTCOM-enforced blockade in place if this disruptive state 
of affairs continues. Moreover, the appeal of an “escalate to exit” to “finish the job” could 
still climb now that the electoral imperative has receded, and we think President Trump will 
endeavor to avoid capitulation optics following the GOP’s historic losses.

With foreign policy the primary preserve for the President, the options for Congress 
curbing his war powers come primarily through the purse strings. The immediate test 
comes December 11, when government funding expires. With full-year appropriations 
facing significant political hurdles, another continuing resolution could push negotiations 
into 2027, giving Democrats greater leverage over spending levels and potential 
restrictions on the Iran war. Importantly, deferring funding decisions would not 
immediately constrain Trump's ability to conduct military operations, but could 
significantly reshape the administration's longer-term funding outlook. Even then, it is still 
an open question how willing Senator Schumer will be to back his words with action come 
January.
