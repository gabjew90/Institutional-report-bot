Key Highlights:
(1) Power Outage - Median Power Stock Has Halved. $2.5 trillion shed from the Nasdaq on Thursday—a record. Since 
their respective peaks, our BTM, Nuclear & IPP baskets have shed ~$550bn, $160bn and ~$90bn in market cap, respectively. 
The median drawdown across the names that underlie these baskets (from 52WH) is now 50%. 
(2) Hormuz Tanker Attacks. Iran reported three additional tanker incidents on November 5, bringing the total to at least 
five since Sunday, versus 35 across October. Tehran said two tankers struck mines and another caught fire; the causes and 
supply losses remain unverified. (Reuters⁠)
(3) Russian Refinery Strike. Ukrainian drones reportedly struck the industrial area housing Russia’s 340 kb/d Ryazan 
refinery amid an attack involving more than 200 drones. Reuters verified footage of a fire nearby, although refinery damage 
and lost throughput remain unconfirmed. (Reuters⁠)
(4) Iran Diplomacy. Iran submitted a nuclear proposal to Washington through Qatar, conditioning substantive negotiations 
and a Hormuz reopening on acceptance of seven demands, including sanctions relief, unfrozen assets and reparations. 
Washington is reviewing the proposal; no agreement has been reached. (Reuters⁠)
(5) Polish Gold Buying. Poland’s central bank reached its 700-tonne gold reserve target after purchasing an estimated 27 
tonnes in October. Gold represented 31% of reserves in September, with Governor Glapinski signaling purchases remain 
possible. (Reuters⁠)
(6) Renewables Buildout. The IEA forecasts 4,800 GW of renewable additions during 2026–31, up 60% versus the prior six 
years, with China accounting for ~2,550 GW and solar more than 75% of additions. Advanced-stage renewable and storage 
projects awaiting grid connection rose 60% to 3,000 GW, roughly 4x annual connections. (IEA⁠)

Investors are generally waiting for this weekend to lapse without escalation for an “all clear” but there are some developments on 
the negotiations front in the meantime that perhaps offset the uptick in kinetic activity. Iran submitted a nuclear proposal to 
Washington through Qatar, conditioning substantive negotiations and a Hormuz reopening on acceptance of seven demands, 
including sanctions relief, unfrozen assets and reparations. Washington is reviewing the proposal. Our Center for Geopolitics team 
wrote a piece last night that helps think about some of the various paths forward. Repurposed in bullets below: 
•
The commander-in-chief has several potential escalation options: an Epic Fury 2.0 that revisits the kinds of military targets 
struck in the spring; broader attacks on energy and civilian infrastructure; or a “final blow” aimed at a nuclear target—a 
largely performative strike that would allow Washington to declare “mission accomplished.” 
•
But here’s the thing: Washington has had these same options on the table since the MOU collapsed in June. The White 
House hasn’t reached for them because, frankly, the options ain’t good. Every option risks Iranian retaliation, potentially 
triggering an escalatory spiral that sends energy prices even higher and inflicts considerably more economic and 
psychological damage across the Gulf. 
•
Still, the U.S. and Iran remain far apart on the core issues and fundamentally distrust one another. That makes a narrower 
arrangement focused on the Strait probably the best we can realistically hope for in the near term. That wouldn’t resolve 
the underlying conflict—not by a long shot. But it would reduce the immediate economic and military pressure.

said, DVN is committed to $1bn of share buybacks in 4Q26 and to increase the pace in 2027 after the Eagle Ford deal closes 
in January 2027. We would be buyers on weakness given the discounted valuation and our expectation that the go-forward 
portfolio can continue to add value, particularly with the higher capital allocation to the Delaware Basin.

IEA: Energy security concerns spur faster renewables growth. Renewables 2026, this year’s edition of the IEA’s annual report 
on the sector, sees global renewable power capacity increasing by almost 4 800 gigawatts (GW) in the 2026-2031 period, almost 
60% more than was added in the previous six-year period. Solar PV is set to account for more than three-quarters of global 
renewable capacity growth to 2031, followed by onshore wind.
China will remain the largest contributor to the global increase in renewable power capacity, but its share is set to decline from 
over 60% in 2025 to an average of 53% over the forecast period amid changes to its renewable energy policies. India is emerging 
as the world’s second-largest growth market, with its total renewable capacity set to more than double by 2031. Growth is also 
increasing across Southeast Asia, the Middle East and North Africa, and sub-Saharan Africa. Across these regions, the amount of 
renewable capacity added in the 2026-2031 period is forecast to almost triple compared with the previous six-year period.
Trump-Backed Critical Minerals Deals Face Growing Pressure After Blue Wave (Bloomberg): Mining companies backed 
by billions of dollars in support from the Trump administration are facing a new risk: heightened scrutiny after the Democrats’ 
midterm sweep. Even before a blue wave overtook Congress, Democratic lawmakers were questioning the methods used for 
President Donald Trump’s push to rebuild the nation’s critical minerals industry. His administration made a flurry of 
unconventional financing arrangements — including taking equity stakes and issuing stock warrants — to support mining and 
processing companies in a bid to lessen reliance on China.
Some of the beneficiaries of Trump’s deal-making bonanza, including USA Rare Earth Inc., Vulcan Elements Inc. and Kaz 
Resources, were singled out by Democratic lawmakers, including Massachusetts Senator Elizabeth Warren, earlier this year over 
potential conflicts of interest from their connections to the Trump family and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. The deals 
have drawn criticism even from within the Republican Party. Former Senator Pat Toomey pointed to the rare-earth agreements in 
a post on X on Thursday.

resumed buybacks, repurchasing 1.4MM shares for $23.6MM and leaving ~$176MM of buyback authorization. Since 
announcing its framework to return up to 50% of annual FCF in 2Q25, the company has repurchased 14.5MM shares for ~
$160MM at an average price of ~$11 per share, or ~8% of shares outstanding at the time the framework was announced.
