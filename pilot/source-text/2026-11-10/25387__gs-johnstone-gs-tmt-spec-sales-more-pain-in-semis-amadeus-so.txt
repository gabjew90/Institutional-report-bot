GS TMT SPEC SALES – Friday 6th November
US equities sold off sharply overnight, with the Nasdaq 100 ( down 6.36%, the S&P 500 ( down 4.36% and SOX (
-12%. Post-election concerns over tighter AI regulation and data-centre permitting added pressure to an already
fragile trade, prompting investors to reassess the 2028+ capex outlook. Selling spread across AI infrastructure,
power and neoclouds, with little defensive support from hyperscalers. ETF-led de-risking and surging demand for
protection dominated, while buyers remained reluctant to step in.
So far there is little relief in Asia. Despite still-firm bottom-up demand indicators and sharply compressed
semiconductor valuations, investors want clearer signs of stabilisation before adding risk; the debate is
increasingly whether potential delays to AI infrastructure spending are already more than priced in. One
development worth noting: Lam Research ( announced a $25bn share repurchase programme, equivalent to around
10% of its market cap. I would not be surprised to see similar announcements across the semiconductor sector in
the coming days and weeks.
The US trading desk saying Wednesday was one of our busiest days of the year on the equities trading desk (from
a number of trades and notional executed perspective) but yesterday was the exact opposite; our phones were
exploding with asks for colour but our HT activity was MUCH quieter relative to the day before with price action
likely a function of top-down pressure being applied via macro instruments (massive notionals traded across ETF
complex).
Despite the negative headlines around Texas data centres, Gina Hinojosa (Texas governor-elect, Democrat) has
said that all currently permitted projects will be allowed to complete construction—essentially covering all of
2027 and likely well into 2028. She also plans to pass laws requiring newly permitted data centres to pay taxes,
commit to no increase in consumer power prices and avoid affecting water availability for residents, among other
conditions. This sounds more reasonable than many of the headlines suggest. Her comments begin at 6:30 in this
interview: Link (
Our PB desk’s hedge-fund performance data highlights how tough markets have been. Global fundamental
long/short managers fell 1.3% (a -2 standard-deviation move), mainly due to market/beta exposure, leaving YTD
returns at +8.7%. TMT funds fell more than 4% and are now roughly flat to slightly positive YTD, down from
around +24% before the June/July AI sell-off.
Many of our AI related baskets just had their biggest down day since the launch of ChatGPT (Sharkey)
On the credit side -post-Democratic midterm sweep we are seeing the most leveraged AI infra names hit with
bonds now firmly distressed; CDS implies >60% 5Y default probability. See Amanda Lynam (GS Credit strategist)

take she highlights that hyperscalers are using access to credit as a competitive advantage, raising $67bn across
five deals since late October and taking YTD issuance to $296bn—funding 36% of 2026 capex and exceeding the
$250bn full-year forecast. Bringing forward 2027 borrowing increases the risk of further credit-spread widening
through early December. For 2027, projected issuance includes $440bn of direct debt, covering 35% of capex, plus
$300bn of investment-grade/high-yield project finance. Future borrowing will depend on the pace of AI
monetisation and rising purchase commitments, while high-yield markets enter a structural period of price
discovery. US midterm outcomes are not expected to change long-term federal AI policy structurally, but near-
term regional data-centre moratoriums threaten around 45% of planned US expansion, mainly in Texas. Project
selection and financing structure will therefore matter: well-insulated projects with secured grid access should
attract tight pricing, while speculative developments without power could face wider spreads, stricter covenants or
delays. Full note (

• Cellnex ( delivered an in-line 3Q26, with guidance reiterated, but the results are unlikely to materially improve
investor confidence given subdued underlying growth. The positives were a value-accretive Poland agreement and
strong cost efficiencies, which drove impressive EBITDA margin expansion; however, management cautioned that

• Microchip ( delivered a solid quarter with revenue, margins and EPS broadly in line with expectations, but the
stock is likely to trade lower as FY3Q guidance came in below both GIR and Street forecasts. Investor
expectations had risen into the print following positive commentary around restocking trends, strong margin
expectations and encouraging results from analog peers. The key focus now shifts to management's outlook on
datacenter demand, trends across non-AI end markets, and the sustainability of margins amid higher input costs.
While the weaker guidance is a company-specific disappointment, the read-across for the broader analog sector
appears limited given most peers have already reported.

and Q4 guidance of $845–846m beat Street expectations by around 6%, despite tougher comparisons. Agentic

37% and Act IV generated its first revenue. The key debate is profitability: gross margin fell 287bps year on year
to 72.4%, missing Street expectations by 84bps, operating margin met expectations at 17.5% despite the revenue
beat, and free cash flow margin fell to 10.5% from 13.3%. (
• TEAM ( fell 20% as cloud results and guidance beat Street forecasts but missed higher investor expectations. Q1
cloud revenue grew 30.5% to $1.30bn, slowing for the first time in six quarters and delivering the smallest
guidance beat since Q3 FY25. Total revenue of $1.77bn met investor expectations, with Data Center contributing
$33m of the $62m beat against guidance. Q2 cloud growth guidance of around 27% fell short of investor
expectations of 28–28.5%, while the FY27 upgrade to 26% merely reflected the Q1 beat, implying second-half
growth of 23.6% versus Street forecasts of 24%. Full-year operating margin guidance of 25.5% beat Street
forecasts, but implied second-half operating profit remained around $79m below consensus.

TECH
• NEM’s call centred on reduced visibility in Design that missed in Q3, with no clear steer on Q4 growth or where
revenue would land within the guidance range (GIR is HSD at circa. 8% street similar and can see street moving
lower post call or debating doing it). Management maintained that performance is on plan, with growth
moderating after a strong H1 as subscription migration reaches smaller customers, but the Q4 commentary left
uncertainty over whether visibility is limited, or management is simply being cautious after this quarter.
Conversion remains strong among mid-sized and larger customers, while smaller customers with 1–3 seats are
taking longer, particularly at Graphisoft given its reseller-heavy model. The transition will continue to affect 2027,
with targeted incentives rather than aggressive pricing or promotions used to encourage migration. Key will be
how they do in Barcelona and on the road and whether they give more clarity
• Memory pricing—reported inflection risk: DIGITIMES said consumer NAND procurement has largely frozen
after roughly 18 months of price increases; October US-supplier quotes were flat and 512Gb TLC wafer spot
prices fell about 6.5%. DRAM pricing may still rise in 4Q26, but at a slower pace, making 4Q26–1Q27 a critical
inventory and pricing test. (DIGITIMES)
• AI-server orders remain firm despite the selloff: Taiwan supply-chain sources told DIGITIMES that customer
demand remains strong and current shipment volatility partly reflects Nvidia ( platform transitions rather than
order cancellations. Inference: the equity correction is presently driven more by financing, regulation and
valuation concerns than by verified supply-chain cuts. (DIGITIME

• INWIT said Fastweb+Vodafone’s proposed early termination of its Master Service Agreement (MSA) in March
2028 remains disputed in court, rather than agreed. Under INWIT’s interpretation of the contract, an operator-led
exit requires an agreed handover plan lasting at least three years, with all sites returned fully vacated by 31 March
2031 on an “all-or-nothing” basis. It rejected Swisscom’s ( claim that Fastweb+Vodafone could retain tower access
during a migration lasting until 2040 and called for a binding timetable so it can remarket the entire portfolio. To
protect mobile service continuity, INWIT also urged regulators to make frequency renewals conditional on
confirmed rights to use passive infrastructure, warning that the proposed TIM–Fastweb+Vodafone Towers JV is
still at an early stage and could be anticompetitive. Press Release (
• BT ( CALL TAKEAWAYS: The call sounded more bullish on current Consumer trends and the run-rate than IR’s
comments on H2 earlier this morning. Consumer EBITDA is expected to be broadly flat for the full year, versus a
1.5% decline in H1, suggesting upside. This excludes TalkTalk, ( and with Netomnia in play, BT has little
incentive to talk up the market. On pensions, the presentation highlighted greater dividend headroom from the
bond swap, while JPM ( pressed the CEO on potential free cash flow upside. AI cost savings are not yet scaling
materially—a pattern seen across the sector, including at DT’s AI CMD, where it was too early to commit to
savings. However, BT expects to see in the coming months how much AI could add to its £3.5bn medium-term
gross cost-cutting target, suggesting potential upside. Overall, Street estimates are likely to edge higher following
the Openreach beat. The key drivers from here are Netomnia, with the CMA response deadline on 15th December,
and TalkTalk, where government approval of the deal is still pending.
• SWISSCOM CALL TAKEAWAYS: Competition in Switzerland is largely unchanged, but higher churn
following this year’s price increases left the company sounding cautious about further near-term rises.
Restructuring charges peaked this quarter, reflecting the new B2B structure and headcount reductions during the
year. Management described the quarterly charge as being at the high end, suggesting charges should fall. In Italy,
competition remains broadly stable, with a similar environment expected into 2027. Swisscom continues to focus
on value while some competitors prioritise volume, but highlighted the need to rethink pricing given Italian ARPU
levels. On INWIT, there are no active negotiations, although discussions are taking place about migration starting
in 2028, with contractual provisions allowing access during the migration phase. Participants in the Swiss
spectrum auctions must pre-register by early December. One analyst appeared concerned that Starlink could bid,
although I would be surprised to see it enter.
• BOUYGUES ( CALL TAKEAWAYS: Like Orange, ( Bouygues confirmed that the deal remains on track.
Telecom EBITDA pressure is in line with guidance, although Street expectations were higher, and the competitive
environment is unchanged. Equans is delivering organic growth, and management sounded positive.
Construction’s organic growth was around 4%, in line with expectations, with acquisitions driving the higher
overall growth rate. The response on civil works lacked clarity: the business is affected by the macro backdrop, but
this is masked by the rest of the group. Colas ( is also affected by macro conditions, and the bottom-line beat
reflects a provision reversal. TF1 remains weak, with consolidation seen as the way forward, although the previous
attempt was blocked. Overall, Equans beat expectations and raised guidance, with the CMD ahead.

• US TRADING: ( VIOLENT sell-off with continued pressure in Semiconductors (and AI adjacencies) as the
market works to re-calibrate datacenter (GW) build forecasts and the execution path following Election newsflow
this week – moves most evident across: E&C stocks (datacenter construction), IPP / Power, Neoclouds and

downside risks to GWs/Capex outlooks. (Callahan)
