Europe Open - AI'nt looking good
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Sharp risk off moves overnight as Asia stocks plunged, following US equities who had their worst day since Apr 2025
(Liberation Day). Kospi unsurprisingly leads losses down -6%, weakness in US equity futures stalls with Nasdaq actually
+0.8% higher at print. USTs calm with slight pickup in USD overnight, no flight to quality with CHF and JPY slightly
weaker on the day. The pickup in oil prices is not helping risk, but we did see more constructive Russia/Ukraine
headlines with President Trump predicting there will be a "big agreement" soon. 
We anticipate majority of the participants are waiting for US NFP ahead to determine next moves. AHE is the most
important measure within the report with softer trends limiting any hawkish reactions to headline payrolls (Citi at 65k,
cons. at 70k and whisper 82k). Canada labor report also due where our economists expect a more modest drop in
employment but steady u/e rate. BoJ Ueda speaks at 16:00 GMT. Monitor Eurozone and Hungary retail sales.

In Focus‌
Equities: Bloodbath in Asia equities with Nikkei down -5%, Kospi -6% and Taiex -4.5% after US equities had their worst day
since 2025's Liberation Day. Concerns of a Dem wins in data center states which could result in data center moratoriums
remain in the forefront, but note that state-level regulation will still not be enacted until next year. So is it yet time to buy? At
best markets are at "fair" value but not yet "cheap", our Asia strategist says. We are probably going to see a round of cuts to
excessively optimistic earnings estimates. European futures set for a negative start though surprisingly SPX e-minis are flat
and Nasdaq +0.8% higher. We expect intraday bounces in e-minis to continue to be sold and a break of 7100, which has
persisted since the start of AI, will be tested. A failure here would herald more negative implications, he warns. HK/China
stocks continue to outperform on relative terms, "only" down -1%. 
Rates: UST moves are flat to end the week. Positioning is now tactically long and today’s payrolls need to validate the growth
concern for duration to extend cleanly. Key desk flows can be described at tentative steepening in futures, a trade that many
were watching already and looking for entry points, steepening trades were also entered via SOFR bull steepeners. JGBs

were best performers with front-end trading +15bp higher. NZ bonds slightly on the backfoot into Saturday's general elections.
Polling indicates a very tight race with the two main parties' National (right) and Labour (left) party and suggests a likely
coalition outcome dependent on smaller parties. Not much market implications expected from the results but Labour have
stated that they intend to restore the RBNZ dual mandate of both inflation and employment. Recall we don't expect any more
RBNZ moves this year. 
Commodities: Oil's slight correction higher not helping risk sentiment with Brent close to returning to the $100 handle.
Positive developments on the Ukraine/Russia front with Trump meeting with Russian presidential envoy on Thu and predicted
there would be a “big agreement” that is “going to involve more than Ukraine” and “might involve all of Europe,” without
elaborating. Gold down -1% overnight though $4000 handle currently holds. 
FX: Risk correlated currencies outperform but we aren't seeing much safe haven demand in JPY which is -0.2% weaker on
the day (CHF flat). Vols were significantly higher in AUDUSD, NZDUSD and USDCNH but activity was generally quiet into
NFP with only cares in intraweek contracts as market looks to patch their weekend risk. Seems like a standstill in FX as
market positioning feels quite light. We currently have 25bps priced in for NFP as market awaits some indication/clarity on the
health of the US labor market, our options trader writes.
Must reads:
Global Macro Strategy - Views and Trade Ideas - Buying Risk Off Insurance Citi’s POLLS signal is working well and suggests
staying out of US equities until late November. Our generals are also close to triggering as another warning signal. This is in
line with the view that the market does not seem in the mood for the typical post midterm year-end rally, as the blue sweep is
compounding AI concerns already present before the election. Just in case the AI bubble has burst already, which would likely
create recessionary forces, we buy a far out of the money receiver swaption.
European Rates Weekly - A midterm turn? This week: (1) Only time will tell what the US midterms mean for the Middle East.
Energy prices would need to fall significantly to derail further ECB/BoE hikes. (2) Signaling by the MPC suggests a second
hike could come as soon as December: We enter Dec/Feb MPC SONIA flatteners. (3) Following Schnabel’s speech on
Thursday, we argue that ad-hoc LTROs could be used as second line of defence against a threat to monetary policy
transmission, policy rate stance being the first. (4) We recommend buying Schatz spreads to monetize domestic and cross-
market cheapness. (5) SSA investors increasingly favor tier 1 names, whilst demand for French agencies and Green bonds
have suffered the most. Domestic bid has weakened, and € SSAs are increasingly dependent on “the kindness of strangers”.
Elsewhere‌
USD: The most important detail this month will be average hourly earnings, where a slowing trend could limit any hawkish
reactions to the payrolls print. Citi Economics expects AHE at 0.3% MoM, with NFP to be a solid 65k increase (in line with
consensus, whisper numbers at 75k) and unemployment rate staying low at 4.2%. U Mich Sentiment releases at 15:00 GMT
for November. Before data, Fed’s Logan moderates a discussion at 00:00 GMT, followed by Williams at 09:30 GMT.
EUR: Germany IP just out printed at 0.7%, MoM stronger than consensus expectations though easing from 1.4% prior.
 Eurozone Retail Sales for September due at 10:00 GMT. 
JPY: BoJ Governor Ueda delivers a lecture at 16:00 GMT, text releases the same time in the BoJ website.
GBP: BoE's Pill gives a briefing post Nov MPC at 12:15 GMT.
CAD: Labor Report at 13:30 GMT for October. After large declines in employment in the last few months, we expect another
-5k decline in Oct (below consensus at 15k). U/e and participation rate should remain unchanged at 6.5% and ~65%. Any
downside surprises should support our base case of no more BoC hikes this year and for cuts to resume next year.
HUF: Retail Sales at 07:30 GMT for September. Our economists expect that retail sales rebounded in September to 3.5%
YoY from 2.4% recorded in August. August data was likely an outlier owing to some extent due to weather conditions
(heatwave and drought) which resulted in low contribution of food and fuel sales in the headline figure. Expect this effect to be
reverted in September.
CLP: CPI MoM at 11:00 GMT for October. Bloomberg consensus expects inflation to accelerate towards 4.6% YoY vs 4.1%
prior.

BRL: Watch trends in the Trade Balance Monthly at 18:00 GMT for October.
