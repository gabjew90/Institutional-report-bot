SEPTEMBER 10, 2026 
  
20 SECONDS:  Oil in a forever conflict. ECB may decouple EUR 1Y1Y from TTF. 
Three implications of lower Equity Risk Premia. US CPI Scenarios. Winner takes all 
vs multipolarity. 
  
•         Higher oil, rising global yields and key catalysts are weighing on risk assets. 
Middle East tensions escalated further: Iran warned of a more intense war, 
while Trump’s advisers said the conflict could last through his term. 
•         Meanwhile: a) Astra/Muse and Tech conferences are supporting AI optimism; b) 
Trump promised a $5,000 dividend if Republicans retain control of Congress; and c) 
Treasury buybacks underwhelmed. 
  
•         Yesterday’s equity sell-off accelerated as Brent crossed $100/bbl, pointing to 
greater sensitivity to energy prices. JPM’s Kaneva argues crude may not rise 
materially even in a “forever conflict” scenario, given excess supply, non-Middle 
East production, weaker demand from electrification and ample inventories. 
•         That said, China’s oil-buying recovery is worth watching. Some estimates 
suggest its 1H cut in oil imports lowered Brent by $30/bbl. 


[Page 2]
  
•         I think today’s ECB market reaction could be dovish. Depending on the press 
conference and scenarios, EUR 1Y1Y may decouple from TTF even if economic 
surprises do not normalise near term. 
•         Why: a) three hikes are already priced by June, while EUR 1Y1Y is nearing 2022 
levels—hard to justify given limited second-round effects, lower realised and 
expected inflation, and a softer labour market; b) mounting fiscal concerns are 
tightening financial conditions. The 10Y Bund is near 3.5% nominal and above 1% 
real—both well above the 2023 highs. 
  
•         There are three implications from the shrinkage of Equity Risk Premia towards 
2%, 100bp below historical averages: 1) higher sensitivity of equities to bond yields, 
2) greater inclination by longer-term investors to rebalance from equities to bonds, 
and 3) reinforcement of the positive bond-equity correlation.

IDEAS & INSIGHTS – IN BRIEF 
•         FLOWS & LIQUIDITY: Going forward, we see three implications from the 
shrinkage of Equity Risk Premia towards 2%, 100bp below historical averages: 1) 
higher sensitivity of equities to bond yields, 2) greater inclination by longer-term 
investors to rebalance from equities to bonds, and 3) reinforcement of the positive 
bond-equity correlation. 
•         GLOBAL STRUCTURAL FORCES: Fed prepared to hike but bigger variable is 
potential “regime change”; long-term yields won’t be anchored until fiscal action; 
market structure shifts and US Treasury ownership changes are structural drivers of 
higher term premium; national security shapes economic policy but risk is where 
governments draw the line; world is in an interregnum, not a settled new order; 
middle powers reject “winner-takes-all” narratives in AI and US-China competition; AI 
investment is a macro shock fusing market and economic risk; investment booms 


[Page 10]
rarely end gently; China isn’t built to maximize shareholder value, shaping how it 
competes abroad; tech is rapidly evolving payments/settlement rails but governance 
and security frameworks will determine winners. 
•         GERMAN POLITICS: Germany’s post-election policy uncertainty appears 
contained, preserving the fiscal and reform backdrop that underpins our constructive 
growth outlook. While the strong AfD showing in Sachsen-Anhalt initially raised 
concerns that the coalition might reconsider parts of its fiscal and reform agenda, 
recent comments from Chancellor Merz and Finance Minister Klingbeil suggest the 
government remains committed to the broader package. Some modest adjustments 
to pension reforms remain possible, but a reversal of the overall fiscal easing looks 
unlikely. The more important question is whether growth, reform delivery, and 
improved business sentiment gain traction quickly enough to stem further gains by 
the AfD ahead of the next round of state elections later this month.  
•         CHINA INFLATION: August CPI held at 0.8%oya while PPI accelerated to 
3.8%oya as higher oil, metals, chemicals, and tech-related prices lifted upstream 
inflation and consumer electronics prices continued to firm. However, core CPI rose 
only modestly to 1.0%oya, reflecting weak household demand, a still-soft housing 
sector, and limited pricing power. Upstream reflation is therefore still not transmitting 
into stronger consumer price inflation, in contrast to the rest of the region. 
•         CATALYSTS TODAY:  ECB Policy rate decision. GE CPI. US PPI. Trump & JD 
Vance deliver speeches at Republican midterm convention. BOJ Board Member 
Masu speaks. ADBE, ORCL Earnings. US Treasury begins expanded buybacks of 
longer-dated bonds

EMERGING MARKETS MONETIZATION – Ellen Wang / Victoria Campos 
The global momentum/tech rebound drove EM 7% higher since the July-end low, 
despite choppy price action. Broadening and AI continue to dominate the narrative in 
EM, with the recent global macro backdrop net supportive. We are bullish on EM 
given (i) improving macro; (ii) weaker USD; (iii) AI CapEx tailwinds, but continue to 
expect divergence in EM performance at the country/sector level and view country-
relative trades as more relevant for the monetization strategy in this environment.  
•         EM MONETIZATION MENU – Key themes and trades include: (i) 
TACTICALLY LONG China H-shares over A-shares: the pair has been trading well 
in July given the momentum rotation. Despite this outperformance, H-shares still lag 
A-shares YTD and we see further room into China Tech earnings next week. (ii) We 
also express this view via a TACTICALLY LONG in China Internet (KWEB). (iii) 
TACTICALLY LONG Taiwan following the Global Tech selloff in July. Taiwan offers 
a less volatile exposure to AI and Asia Tech relative to Korea. 
• HEDGES – (i) We like long LatAm (ILF) as a diversifying play 
against weakness in the EM AI theme and for relative resilience 
against geopolitical risks. (ii) India (INDA) equity strength has been 
driven in part by a rotation during the AI de-risking phase, but

foreign investor flows have also turned more supportive again, 
supported by improving macro. You may consider a L/S pair with 
LONG India and SHORT Southeast Asia (ASEA).

POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE IS NOT A PRODUCT OF RESEARCH 
Summary: Not a lot has changed at the aggregate level over the past week, despite 
market gyrations, but there are some potential themes emerging such as further re-
grossing by HFs, a pause in US Health Care, and a bottoming of Cyclicals vs. 
Defensives. 
Key Points: 
•         HF Re-grossing picked up this week:  Globally HFs added gross 
exposure on 4 of the past 5 days for a +1.3z increase in the past week, the 
highest since late June. 
•         Net changes were muted this week…: HF flows were mixed day-to-day 
but ended with a small sell skew.  We saw a similar thing for our US TPM 
and the level of positioning for our US TPM remains at 40th %-tile, while 
global HF nets are 40-50th %-tile on a 5yr basis. 
• …But sentiment turning less constructive:  Both retail and institutional 
sentiment appear to be reversing lower / pausing, after prior increases. 
• US Health Care could be topping out for now:  Retail sentiment is 
starting to drop from ATHs, HFs are selling, and ETF flows are pausing at 
highs. 


[Page 14]
•         Cyclicals vs. Defensives…will strong growth put cyclicals back in 
favor?  Energy is being bought by HFs and relative net positioning between 
the groups is fairly neutral when excluding TMT and Financials.  In addition, 
Retail sentiment between the two is quite low.  The potential for cyclicals to 
outperform seems present, as long as yields don’t rise so quickly that 
equities turn very risk off.
