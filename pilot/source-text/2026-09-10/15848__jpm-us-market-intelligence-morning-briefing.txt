PPI PREVIEW (FEROLI; here) 
We [Feroli and team] project that the final demand producer price index (PPI) rose 0.2% 
m/m in August (5.0% oya), with the headline series remaining firm but slightly below its 
recent peak of 5.9% oya in May. Energy prices have driven much of the recent volatility in 


[Page 4]
the headline readings: the energy PPI rose a cumulative 15.9% from March to May before 
falling 9.5% over the following two months. For August, we expect a moderate 0.5% decline 
in the energy complex, reflecting mixed signals across products. Lower propane and natural 
gas prices during the month’s survey window are likely to weigh on the LPG and utility 
natural gas PPI indexes, while higher gasoline prices could result in renewed price 
pressures in that segment. We expect food PPI to tick up 0.5% after two consecutive 
monthly declines, as agricultural commodity prices edged higher in August. Excluding food 
and energy, we expect core PPI prices to rise 0.2% m/m (4.6% oya), reversing some of the 
improvement since the recent peak of 4.9% oya in April. We expect core goods prices to 
rise 0.2% m/m, resulting in an over-year-ago pace of 4.8%. A major contributor to firm 
inflation in this category has been sales to government or exports, which edged down 
slightly in July. Prices of finished consumer goods were running at 3.6% oya in the latest 
report, led by non-durable goods inflation, which has been running above its durable goods 
counterpart since April after moving at a similar pace for much of the second half of last 
year. We have been tracking growth in core goods inputs to goods producers at various 
intermediate stages of demand; this measure stepped down from 3.5% m/m in May to 0.3% 
in June and -0.3% in July, signaling some relief in pipeline inflation pressures. We forecast 
core services PPI to rise 0.2%, similar to the prior month. The soft July print was driven by a 
sharp 1.8% drop in transportation and warehousing services, along with a moderate 0.1% 
decline in trade services—two components that had driven much of the firm inflation in 
services earlier in the year. The prior decline in energy prices from recent peaks contributed 
to the recent drop in transportation service prices.

pricing a ~63% probability of a 25bp hike at the Sep 16 FOMC meeting. This print should 
provide clarity on the Sep 16 Fed meeting, with the tail risk for Equities being a material 
hotter print that spikes Oct or Dec rate hike expectations, which are currently ~27% and 
~54%, respectively. If the data supports Feroli’s hypothesis for a Hold or Hawkish Hold, 
look for stocks to react positively. Given the neutral positioning environment and room to re-

US MKT INTEL VIEW 
We maintain our Tactically Cautious / Neutral view ahead of Friday’s CPI report. The 
near-term setup remains binary: a dovish print could ease the pressure from yields and 
rates volatility, allowing investors to refocus on resilient consumer and corporate earnings 
growth, while a hawkish print shall keep the September Fed meeting live with rising bond 
vol pressuring stocks. Beyond these immediate catalysts, our base case remains 
constructive in absence of renewed inflation/bond yield spikes. We think it is prudent to 
remain neutral/cautious ahead of the CPI and Fed hurdles.

ROADMAP TO 26Q4 
As we wind down the quarter, we wanted to share some of the catalytic events as we 
approach Q4. So far, midterm election seasonality has failed to materialize; and, at this 
point, it seems unlikely to do so with the dominance of A, an unresolved conflict in the 
Middle East, and the potential for 26Q3 earnings season (which spans the election) to 
deliver another quarter of more than 20% EPS growth with widening margins. 
If we are wrong on midterm seasonality, buy Healthcare, which is typically the best 
performing sector into midterms. Then, size up on Election Day as the post-election setup is 
positive.
