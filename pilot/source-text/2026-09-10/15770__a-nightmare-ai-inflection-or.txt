Cracks?
The AI trade is well off recent highs and the backdrop is getting considerably more complicated.
Adoption is still growing, but slowing; spending among the biggest customers just had a disturbing
kink; Anthropic's IPO is slipping; and investors have dramatically reduced exposure. Is this the
inflection AI bears have been waiting for — or just another pause in a boom whose earnings continue
to accelerate?

Where is the S1?


[Page 3]
Anthropic's march to the public markets is already hitting a few speed bumps. Its S-1, expected as
early as September, has slipped to late September at best, pushing IPO marketing to mid-October —
and delaying investors' first real look at GAAP revenue, compute costs and the economics underneath
that rumored $2 trillion valuation. That valuation increasingly requires some heroic assumptions about
2028 and beyond. Meanwhile, OpenAI's IPO has moved firmly to the right: 2026 now looks like wishful
thinking, with 2027 the more plausible window — if it happens at all. Private markets can live on TAMs,
run-rates and 2028 forecasts. Public markets eventually ask to see the P&L.

Earnings monster
And that brings us to the main problem with calling the AI inflection just yet. Adoption may be slowing,
IPOs may be slipping and positioning has been washed out — but the one thing that hasn't rolled over
is earnings. AI infrastructure EPS growth is still running circles around the rest of the market. For now,
the nightmare inflection remains a disturbing question mark.
