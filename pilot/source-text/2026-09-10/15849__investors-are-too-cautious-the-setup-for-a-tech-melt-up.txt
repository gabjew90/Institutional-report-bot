Trapped
Tech remains trapped at resistance, but the setup underneath is becoming increasingly skewed to the
upside. Earnings are strong, sentiment is bearish, hedge funds have de-grossed aggressively, NDX
shorts are up 35% and cash balances remain elevated.
Meanwhile, rates have yet to create a genuine financial-conditions shock, and the large AI call buyer
has returned, reviving the spot-up, vol-up dynamic. With bullish seasonality approaching, the
ingredients for an upside vacuum are falling into place.
Time to move
NDX futures remain trapped inside the large dynamic formation, trading at the same levels as in May
and sitting right on the 100-day moving average.
A breakout risks creating a powerful vacuum move.
LSEG Workspace
Why be bullish here?
The GS sales desk highlights a clean combination of strong earnings and light positioning.
S&P 500 EPS grew roughly 30% year-on-year in Q2, yet sentiment remains firmly bearish across both
AAII and GS’s Sentiment Indicator. NDX shorts have increased 35% since June, while sharp Q3 de-
grossing has pushed US fundamental long/short net leverage into the 6th percentile over the past year.
Gross exposure to US tech is down to the 43rd percentile, with cash balances sitting at $163 billion.
Strong earnings, light positioning, elevated shorts and plenty of cash: the pain trade remains higher.

Are rates a threat to the AI boom?
Long-end yields have surged to multi-year highs as fiscal concerns return, but the tightening remains
far less disruptive than in 2022.
The US 30-year Treasury’s maximum drawdown is roughly 7% YTD, versus more than 25% in 2022. The
S&P 500 has fallen a maximum of around 9%, compared with 25% during the 2022 shock.

Worried about rates?
Tech bubbles can overpower rising rates. During the late-1990s dot-com boom, the Nasdaq rallied
more than 200% as the 30-year yield rose roughly 200 basis points, and gained over 100% while the
Fed hiked rates by 100 basis points.
Don't forget: the NDX and the 30-year have moved higher since the launch of ChatGPT.

The AI vol buyer returns
McElligott highlights the return of a large “mystery buyer” in the concentrated AI names hammered
during the summer unwind. Across Friday and the post-Labor Day reopening, the buyer spent roughly
$315 million in options premium, adding $1.1 billion of delta and $5.8 million of vega through large
FLEX calls.
The flows are reviving the “spot up, vol up” dynamic in single-name tech.
Semis skew is very well bid, although overall semis volatility remains dormant.
