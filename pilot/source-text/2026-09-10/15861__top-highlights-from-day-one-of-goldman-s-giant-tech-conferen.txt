Top Highlights From Day One Of Goldman's Giant 
Tech Conference 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, SEP 09, 2026 - 10:30 PM 
The closely watched Goldman TMT Conference is taking place in San Fran this week, and 
yesterday it was day 1 with presentations from OpenAI and Google among others. Below we 
summarize some of the key views from Goldman's Sales and Trading  team, but as one can 
imagine (from what is basically a giant attempt to boost investor excitement), the overall 
message from the conference presentations has been decidedly bullish. 
Here is the agenda from Day 1: 
 


[Page 2]
 
 
As Goldman's Nelson Armbrust explains, the presenting companies "delivered a highly resilient 
and growth-oriented outlook, driven by robust consumer demand and massive structural 
tailwinds from AI infrastructure, custom silicon, and agentic AI networking. Across the board, 
management teams reiterated or raised guidance, highlighted strong customer engagement, 
and pointed to multi-year growth runways (e.g., Broadcom's $230 billion FY28 AI target and 
Cisco's 5x bandwidth multiplier from Agentic AI)." 
Why the Message Was Bullish? 
Massive AI Infrastructure & Networking Demand:  
●​ Broadcom (AVGO): Reaffirmed its massive FY27/FY28 AI revenue targets of $115 billion 
and $230 billion, respectively, while noting that supply remains constrained (indicating 
demand exceeds supply). 
●​ Alphabet (GOOGL): $100m+ deals are growing more than 2x both QoQ and YoY, while 
AI-server payback is less than two years, and roughly half that on proprietary silicon 
●​ Cisco (CSCO): Highlighted that Agentic AI is driving a 5x increase in network bandwidth 
consumption, catalyzing a durable, multi-year networking supercycle across all customer 
segments. 


[Page 3]
 
●​ Arista Networks (ANET): Reaffirmed its 2026 guidance, seeing strong demand for 
back-end networks connecting GPU accelerators, with pricing power and efficiencies 
supporting 2027E margins. 
Guidance Reaffirmations and Upward Revisions:  
●​ TKO: Raised its 2026 WWE partnerships growth expectations to "in excess of 20%" (up 
from mid-single-digit growth in 1H26) and indicated a potential upward revision to its 
$1.2 billion global partnerships target by 2030. 
●​ Axon (AXON): Reiterated its strong full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance of 32% to 
34%, emphasizing a massive, underpenetrated $160 billion total addressable market 
(TAM). 
Resilient Consumer and Enterprise Engagement:  
●​ eBay (EBAY): Reported that US consumer demand remains resilient, with 
value-conscious and enthusiast buyers driving engagement. 
●​ Reddit (RDDT): Highlighted strong user and engagement growth driven by product-led 
improvements, supporting its high >60% growth rate. 
Clear Pipelines for Future Monetization:  
●​ Qualcomm (QCOM): Expressed high confidence in its $5 billion FY27 datacenter 
revenue target and highlighted its custom silicon partnership with Amazon, paving a 
clear path to its $15 billion FY29 target. 
●​ Samsara (IOT) & Klaviyo (KVYO): Both reported rapid adoption of new AI features (with 
Samsara seeing a 4x increase in AI feature usage over the past two months), 
demonstrating clear pathways to future monetization. 
More details on a company by company basis, from Goldman's US S&T team: 
●​ Broadcom  – Sales (h/t Giulio Esposito) joined a strong presentation with CEO Hock Tan 
for the first trillion dollar club keynote to a full ballroom. CEO noted that the majority of 
value will continue accruing to leading frontier model developers. Broadcom noted that a 
1GW AI cluster can generate roughly $30bn of revenue versus ~$10bn of annual 
operating costs, and highlighted that frontier models generate roughly 75% of industry 
revenue despite similar levels of investment to open-weight ecosystems. Management 
also highlighted its long-term TPU partnership with Google through 2031, its OpenAI 
Jalapeno custom silicon program, and its strategic relationship with Anthropic – 
emphasising its deepening relationships with frontier AI labs and hyperscalers to 
orchestrate high leverage ROI. Finally, management reaffirmed its FY27/FY28 AI 
revenue targets of $115 bn /$230 bn, while the $35 bn XPV partnership is designed to 


[Page 4]
 
accelerate AI infrastructure buildouts, funding more than 20GW of compute capacity 
while minimising balance sheet risk. 
●​ Databricks – Sales (h/t Giulio Esposito) saw Founder & CEO on stage to a packed main 
ballroom, arguing that while AI is viewed as strategically critical 90% of enterprise 
leaders, adoption remains early, with relatively few organisations deploying autonomous 
agents at scale across core business workflows. The focus is increasingly shifting from 
AI as a chatbot to AI as an operational layer that changes how companies run functions 
such as finance, sales, and compliance. Management emphasised the importance of 
allowing customers to leverage multiple foundation models while automatically routing 
workloads to the most cost-effective provider. They also highlighted Lapis, a key 
development initiative aimed at disaggregating compute and storage to target the large 
traditional data warehouse market. Looking ahead, management sees growing 
convergence between AI, cybersecurity, and enterprise data platforms as autonomous 
agents begin interacting with sensitive information and higher-value transactions.  
●​ Snowflake – CEO and CFO presented to a nearly full ballroom, with Sales (h/t Giulio 
Esposito) in attendance. A key theme was that enterprise applications are evolving into 
agentic workflows running against governed enterprise data. Management highlighted 
strong demand for ROI-driven use cases across invoice processing, supply chain 
optimisation, customer data platforms, and machine learning, noting that even a 1% 
efficiency gain on ~$10bn of annual payments can be economically significant for 
customers. Management also argued that differentiation will come from governance, 
security, reliability, cost management, and AI workflow orchestration rather than 
proprietary storage. They also highlighted ongoing work to support lower-latency AI 
applications, with response times currently in the 2-3 second range and a roadmap 
toward ~500 milliseconds for real-time agentic workloads. 
●​ Cisco Systems – Presentation featured CFO Mark Patterson and President & Chief 
Product Officer Jeetu Patel, who argued that agentic AI is creating a multi-year 
networking supercycle, with AI agents consuming roughly 5x more bandwidth than 
humans while operating continuously, driving demand across networking, security, and 
observability. Sales (h/t Jacob Malmstrom) noted that management see growth across 
hyperscalers, neoclouds, and enterprise customers, with campus networking 
accelerating from historical 3-4% growth rates to double-digits, while physical AI could 
provide an additional leg of demand. Management also highlighted the rapid scale of its 
hyperscaler AI business, which has grown from almost zero to $9.3bn of AI orders in 


[Page 5]
 
FY26, while reiterating a >$100bn infrastructure refresh opportunity driven by end-of-life 
and end-of-support assets across both Cisco's installed base and competitors'. Looking 
ahead, Cisco expects to be fully independent of merchant silicon providers by 2029, and 
reiterated its F2027 revenue outlook, which includes 15% revenue growth at the 
midpoint with $7.5 bn of hyperscale AI revenue. 
●​ SAP – Sales (h/t Fahad Javid) saw the CEO on stage to a packed ballroom. A key 
theme was the substantial runway remaining in the cloud migration cycle, with roughly 
60% of SAP's installed base still not migrated to the cloud. Management also noted that 
ERP modernisation remains a significant opportunity ahead of the 2030 standard 
maintenance deadline. On AI, management positioned Joule Work as the primary 
engagement layer across the SAP ecosystem, with 150 pilot customers and 80,000 of 
SAP's 100,000 employees already using the platform internally. They reiterated plans to 
have 400 AI agents available by year-end, focused on complex enterprise workflows 
rather than commodity LLM use cases. Looking ahead, management highlighted a shift 
in both the go-to-market model and workforce deployment towards AI consumption and 
platform adoption. They remain confident in its margin trajectory, citing 30-35% 
development productivity gains from AI-assisted coding and approximately 20% 
productivity improvements in functions such as finance and HR. 
●​ Visa – CEO presented to a well-attended main ballroom session. Sales (h/t Giulio 
Esposito) saw management highlight that spending trends remain remarkably healthy 
despite softer sentiment indicators, with Visa's $7tn U.S. payments footprint tracking 
~9% growth QTD through August, global payment transactions growing around 10% 
YoY, and cross-border volumes accelerating to 14% growth, driven primarily by 
e-commerce. Also, VAS is seen as a major growth engine, now representing 
approximately 30% of net revenue and growing 34% YoY, driven by risk, identity, issuer 
processing, and tokenisation solutions. On AI, management highlighted significant 
internal productivity benefits, including an 80% reduction in product design and build 
times, an 80% increase in code commits, and 65% faster feature deployment across 
product and technology teams. Finally, management emphasised and digital identity as 
strategic priorities, and also currently supports more than 200 stablecoin programs 
across 50 countries and sees growing adoption opportunities in cross-border payments 
and B2B flows. 
●​ Reddit – CEO Steve Huffmann delivered a strong presentation, delivering a bullish 
message around user growth, profitability, and Reddit's unique position in an AI-driven 


[Page 6]
 
internet. Sales (h/t Fahad Javid) saw management highlight strong business momentum, 
with 60% revenue growth for eight consecutive quarters and 90% gross margins, with 
user engagement trends remain particularly encouraging, with new user retention 
improving 50% last quarter, driven by feed optimisation, onboarding improvements, 
AI-powered recommendations, and better web-to-app conversion. A major theme was 
Reddit's focus on driving direct app-based engagement, which monetises meaningfully 
better than web traffic. Management noted roughly 100k daily organic app downloads, 
while emphasising that feed relevance remains its largest product investment area. AI 
and machine learning are increasingly being leveraged to improve recommendations, 
onboarding, moderation tools, advertising products, and user retention. Finally, 
management argued that the rise of AI-generated content should increase the value of 
Reddit's authentic, human-led discussions.  
●​ Airbnb – Sales (h/t Jacob Malmstrom) saw CEO Brian Chesky, who outlined Airbnb’s 
evolution from a pure accommodation marketplace into a broader travel platform. 
Management argued that the core business, now approaching ~$100bn of GBV, still has 
substantial runway, with roughly 70% of business concentrated in five countries, while 
markets such as India, Japan, Korea, and broader Asia remain underpenetrated. The 
core message was their progress on category expansion, where category expansion 
include hotels (~1/3 of users who book a hotel return to book a home on Airbnb), 
experiences, services, car rentals (one of the fastest growing new category on platform), 
longer-term stays, and other travel adjacent offerings, which expands TAM while 
simultaneously strengthening the core marketplace. ABNB also noted the strength of the 
consumer franchise, with 90%+ of traffic coming from direct or organic channels. On AI, 
Chesky argued the consumer AI opportunity remains in its early innings, though remains 
skeptical that chatbots become the dominant interface for travel, arguing that travel 
discovery is inherently visual, collaborative, and contextual. Instead, he envisions an 
AI-native platform built around personalisation, rich discovery, and conversational 
experiences that sit somewhere between traditional e-commerce and today's chatbot 
interfaces. 
●​ TKO Group – Sales (h/t Giulio Esposito) saw President & COO Mark Shapiro in a 
packed session, striking a bullish tone on the long-term value of live sports and 
entertainment content. Mark reiterated confidence in the financial outlook, highlighting 
39.6% EBITDA margins in 2026 (+600bps YoY), normalised free cash flow conversion 
above 60%, and leverage falling below 2x by year-end, while also announcing an

Investor Day in 1Q27 to outline a longer-term growth framework and potentially revisit its 
$1.2bn global partnerships revenue target by 2030. A key theme was the strength of 
media rights and sponsorship momentum. Management highlighted WWE Raw's strong 
performance on Netflix, while UFC continues to be a major engagement driver for 
Paramount+, where roughly 25% of subscribers engage with UFC content and UFC 
viewers are approximately 15 years younger than the average subscriber. International 
media-rights renewals across China, Japan, Korea, France, and Canada were

management sees WWE sponsorship in 2026, while Zuffa Boxing continues to gain 
traction with 100 signed fighters, 10 events staged, and plans for 2-3 Saudi-backed

Here is the recap from Goldman's European S&T team: 
●​ OpenAI: OpenAI bullish on both demand and monetisation, highlighting Astra as its 
largest-ever training run and pointing to frontier-level performance at materially lower 
cost. Customers are increasingly focused on cost per task completed rather than cost 
per token, while enterprise adoption is accelerating, agentic products now have 25m 
users, and usage intensity remains well ahead of peers. Despite compute constraints, 
particularly in voice, revenue per GPU is rising and unit costs are falling. Strategically, 
OpenAI sees cybersecurity as the biggest 12-month opportunity, with life sciences the 
longer-term value-based monetisation opportunity. Pricing is expected to shift from seats 
to consumption and ultimately outcome-based models, while consumer momentum 
remains strong at c.1bn users, with subscription upsell and ads already contributing 
meaningfully. 
●​ Prometheus: Prometheus was deliberately light on detail, but the message was centred 
on AI for the physical economy — using AI as an engineering and manufacturing tool to 
build complex products faster. The company discussed the idea of an “artificial general 
engineer”, with likely early use cases in jet engines, rockets and complex casting. The 
broader read-across is that life sciences is becoming an increasingly crowded AI 
opportunity, with both OpenAI and Google also repeatedly highlighting it as a major 
strategic focus. Certainly not the bearish near term catalyst for DSY I had expected. 
●​ Alphabet: Alphabet emphasised that its AI advantage comes from a vertically integrated 
stack spanning TPUs, Gemini models, data, security and applications. Management 
argued this delivers superior price-performance and lets Google capture value across 
multiple AI monetisation layers. Enterprise adoption of Gemini is strong, with over 90% 


[Page 8]
 
of Fortune 100 companies using Gemini Enterprise as an agentic platform to automate 
workflows and reason over private data. Google Cloud is scaling the opportunity through 
industry-specific solutions, system-integrator partnerships, proprietary data integrations 
and forward-deployed engineers helping customers build frontier AI use cases. 
●​ SAP: SAP was notably more confident than last year, with Christian Klein sounding 
upbeat on both the AI roadmap and financial delivery. The company expects to reach 
200 AI agents by month-end and remains confident in hitting 400 agents by year-end, 
focused on complex enterprise use cases. Early feedback on Joule Work is positive, with 
more than 100 customers ahead of the autumn launch, and SAP is moving sales 
incentives toward AI consumption units. A key message was model and front-end 
agnosticism: SAP expects agents to run across multiple models, including cheaper 
options from Mistral and open-source providers, reducing inference costs versus relying 
on a single frontier model. Management also sounded more relaxed on gross margins 
(been model agnostic helps), the geopolitical backdrop (no longer a major concern to its 
FY guide) and CCB, marking a clear positive change in tone. 
●​ Snowflake: Snowflake’s message was that its AI opportunity is expanding beyond data 
warehousing into becoming the orchestration, governance and application layer for 
enterprise AI. Near-term upside comes from AI accelerating migrations and 
deployments, while the longer-term moat is around governed data, agentic workflows, 
security, cost optimisation and application development. Management highlighted 
growing AI consumption and strong demand for ROI-led use cases such as invoice 
processing, supply-chain optimisation and customer data platforms. Snowflake also 
stressed an open ecosystem approach, positioning itself as the layer that lets enterprises 
use whichever models they choose, with retention increasingly dependent on platform 
value rather than proprietary data formats. 
●​ Databricks: Databricks struck a bullish tone on the convergence of data, AI and security, 
positioning its advantage around one copy of enterprise data, governed through Unity 
Catalog, with AI built natively on top. Ali Ghodsi argued that enterprise AI adoption is still 
constrained by the gap between model capability and real operational deployment, 
which Databricks is trying to close through products such as Genie and Unity AI 
Gateway. Genie turns governed enterprise data into a conversational BI and analyst 
layer, while Unity AI Gateway routes tasks to the best and cheapest model while 
centralising access, spend, logging and guardrails. Security was also a major theme as 
agents handle more sensitive workflows, with Lakebase highlighted as a large 


[Page 9]
 
opportunity and Databricks framing the next phase of AI as governed, cost-aware 
production deployment. 
●​ Broadcom: Broadcom remains one of the clearest beneficiaries of the AI infrastructure 
buildout, with Hock Tan reaffirming FY27/FY28 AI revenue targets of $115bn / $230bn 
and stressing that demand visibility is strong while supply remains constrained. The 
company’s moat is its combination of custom silicon, advanced packaging and AI 
networking, supported by deep partnerships with strategic customers including frontier AI 
developers. Broadcom also highlighted the $35bn XPV partnership, designed to 
accelerate more than 20GW of AI compute buildout while limiting balance-sheet risk. 
The key constraint has shifted from demand to physical infrastructure — especially 
power, permitting and data-centre construction timelines — while management expects 
strong AI-driven revenue and cash-flow growth to support ongoing dividends and 
buybacks. 
●​ Arista: Arista reiterated its 2026 revenue and margin guidance, supported by improved 
supply-chain visibility and strong demand across AI infrastructure and enterprise campus 
deployments. The key AI opportunity remains networking for GPU clusters, especially 
back-end networks connecting accelerators. Customers are increasingly using 
scale-across deployments as power, cooling and data-centre space constraints force 
clusters across multiple locations. Looking ahead, price increases and supply-chain 
efficiencies could help Arista sustain gross margins into 2027 despite lapping tariff 
refunds. 
●​ Infineon: Infineon struck a confident tone, with near-term growth constrained more by 
supply than demand, especially in AI data-centre power. Management said execution is 
ahead of plan, pricing remains favourable and capacity is being expanded faster, 
supported by growing customer interest in multi-year reservation agreements. Infineon 
also highlighted its strong position in Nvidia’s MGX ecosystem as power architectures 
move toward 800V, increasing content per rack. Beyond AI, backlog has rebounded to 
around €30bn, inventories have normalised and autos growth is running at roughly 
9-10% on content gains and share expansion. IR also sounded confident that the FY27 
data-centre target of €2.5bn will be materially raised in November, with management 
suggesting €2.8bn would not qualify as a material upgrade. 
●​ Visa: Visa painted a resilient picture of the US consumer, with payment volumes holding 
at 6-8% growth over the past 18 months despite weak sentiment. Global transactions are 
still growing around 10% YoY, while cross-border volumes have accelerated to 14%, led 


[Page 10]
 
by e-commerce rather than travel. On AI, Visa sees itself as a trust and infrastructure 
layer for agentic commerce, using generative AI internally to improve productivity while 
developing a Trusted Agent Protocol for autonomous payments. The key takeaway is 
that trust will determine adoption: only 25% of consumers are comfortable letting AI 
agents pay autonomously, but this rises to over 60% when Visa is involved. 
●​ Muse / Instinct: While not at the conference Investors are discussing Instinct, an 
invite-only personal agent that users can text or call like an EA. It connects to email, 
messages, calendar and a cloud computer to book flights, shop, cancel subscriptions 
and complete web workflows, with invites metered as compute scales. The debate is 
whether Instinct can win as a standalone assistant now that Meta Muse has launched 
with broader distribution through WhatsApp and app channels. Trust remains the main 
concern (some also asking if this is an Apple opportunity), particularly around inbox 
permissions, autonomous emails and data retention. Instinct’s edge is its zero-UI, 
action-completion model, while competitors benefit from distribution, confirmation gates, 
self-hosting or tighter platform integration. 
●​ Nebius: Nebius remains focused on scaling AI infrastructure, with demand visibility 
extending into 2028 and a continued emphasis on maintaining a diversified customer 
base across contract durations. The Palantir partnership strengthens enterprise 
distribution and supports the company’s full-stack platform strategy. Capacity expansion 
appears on track, while regulatory complexity could become an industry-wide supply 
constraint and potentially support margins. Contract economics are also improving, 
creating better payback periods, stronger margins and more financing flexibility. 
●​ REA interesting M&A comments: REA’s comments were interesting in the context of its 
prior attempt to buy Rightmove. Management suggested any cross-border move would 
need to target a highly profitable, market-leading business with clear AI monetisation 
potential — criteria that still point back toward a Rightmove type business...

Bottom line: stay long AI infra as scarcity across compute & energy remains a theme well into 
2027, but early innings on monetization across the full value chain. We have now moved past 
issues around “tokenmaxxing” excessive bills as frontier labs remain committed to slash costs 
for older models to increase utilization…and this is happening. OpenAI commented on how the 
80% drop for GPT-40 mini (referred to as “Luna”) resulted in a 10x increase in usage.  
●​ It’s not only a cost story at all, Jevons’ Paradox is fully at play: technological progress is 
increasing the efficiency with total consumption increasing. Again OpenAI added that 
“Frontier firms” (top 10% of customers) use 33x more tokens than average users and 
expect this hunger to use to carry on as Nebius see orders now being placed for 2028 
capacity and they added they could absorb excess capacity if hyperscalers choose to 
sell it.  
●​ Newer models are more efficient & moving to “cost per task”: investors have focused a 
lot on OpenAI’s GPT6 Astra, the latest model able to score 100% in math and cyber 
benchmarks. Whether Astra is the closest example of AGI is hard to say but seemingly it 
has been able to solve the Navier-Stokes equation – a 90y old math problem in a space 
of a week using 100k GPUs (there is however speculation that OpenAI merely "distilled" 
the solution from a math professor who was working on Codex at the time). What 


[Page 14]
 
matters here is another couple things: (1) Astra requires 68% fewer output tokens than 
previous models to achieve comparable results, lowering the “cost per task”; and (2) the 
concept of “cost per task” is key as models able to score excellently are shifting the 
paradigm from experimentation to production use cases.  
●​ "GPU-buyers" are getting a pretty fast payback on AI servers: despite compute 
constraints, revenue per GPU is rising and unit costs are falling. Bears might argue 
about returns but – in the words of Rich Privo – “topline going up too fast to matter”. 
Additionally Google commented that payback period on AI servers is under two years, 
and half that on own custom silicon. Two silicon types offered by Google: Nvidia GPUs 
and proprietary TPUs. TPUs: 2.7x better price/performance for training, 80% better for 
inference. 
●​ On the open source vs frontier debate: again OpenAI – which stated a goal to reach a

from June to July in Enterprise ARR, they commented that frontier models provide better 
reliability and security for enterprise deployments compared to open-source alternatives, 
which still incur significant inference costs.  Customers care about cost-per-task, not 
cost-per-token — important framing shift.
