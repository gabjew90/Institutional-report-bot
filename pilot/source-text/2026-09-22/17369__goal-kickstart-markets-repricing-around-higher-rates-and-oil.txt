Markets repricing around higher rates and oil prices
Last week, the Fed and the BoJ hiked policy rates by 25bps 
(Japan policy rates are at the highest level in over 31 years), 
while the BoE left rates unchanged. Brent crude prices 
moved back above 100$/bbl, in a convergence of supply 
disruptions, escalating warfare, and refining constraints - it 
has moderated again today. This week investors will turn 
their attention to PMIs (US/Euro area), the US durable goods 
report and the meeting between President Trump and Xi 
Jinping. 
Front-end rates led the cross-asset repricing last week - G4 
front-end volatility rose ahead of the meetings, especially 
relative to equity vol (Exhibit 1). The response was less 
pronounced at the back end, with long-end yields anchored 
by Treasury buybacks, markets still pricing a hawkish BoJ 
trajectory and the UK’s gilt-friendly QT plan. The US 2s10s 
curve is now at its flattest level since March 2025. 
Our Risk Appetite Indicator (RAI) has softened since its peak 
in August, before a small rebound last week to 0.8 (Exhibit 
10). The gap between ‘safe assets’ and ‘risky assets’ RAI 
components widened to its largest level since 2022, driven 
by the rise in nominal yields (Exhibit 2). Last week,
