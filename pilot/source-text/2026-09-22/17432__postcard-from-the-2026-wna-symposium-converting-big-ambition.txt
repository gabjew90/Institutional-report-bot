BofA remains constructive on nuclear energy  
We recently attended the 202  edition of the annual World Nuclear Association (WNA) 
Symposium. Attendance was a record, up 1 % vs. last year. Key idea: while the nuclear 
skepticism of the 2010s has yielded to ambitious plans for deployment, supported by 
decarbonization, energy security, and national security goals, the industry must now take 
bold action. In our assessment, policy makers, end users, power providers, and fuel cycle 
players are moving in the right direction. We remain constructive on nuclear energy and 
point to the following buy rated  
 listed stocks for exposure: CEG, TLN, VST in power, 
CCJ and OKLO among vendors, and CCJ and STDN in the nuclear fuel cycle. 
Ambitious goal to more than triple global nuclear power 
At COP28 in December 2023, WNA set a goal to triple global nuclear capacity from the 
2020 baseline of just under 400 (392) gigawatts electric (GWe) to nearly 1,200GWe in 
2050 (a nearly 4% annual growth rate). Based on current global aggregate targets, global 
nuclear power generating capacity would reach 1,457 GW  by 2050, a near quadrupling. 
Policy, technical standards, finance, are keys to success 
Panelists broadly view  supportive policy,  chnical standardization, and effective 
financial structures a  key to growing global nuclear capacity. Policy should be 
supportive and incorporate industry input; and many improvements have been made in 
this direction, notably in the US and Japan. Standardization on technical specifications 
are seen aiding repeatability  to optimize execution. Reliable costing and timelines are 
seen as critical to making nuclear a widely investible asset class.  
End users of power stepping up to support the build out 
Having identified the decarbonization and energy security strengths of nuclear, end 
users are stepping up to support deployment. Amazon is providing financial and advisory 
support to nuclear reactor start up X Energy. Datacenter provider, Equinix is supporting 
nuclear developers Oklo, Radiant, and others with purchase agreements; as examples. 
Path to 10 large reactor builds in the US by 2030 
In May 2025, a US Executive Order called for 10 large reactors under construction in the 
US by 2030. The AP1000 is viewed widely by industry participants as a likely reactor 
candidate. Th  Department of Energy later announced a $17.5 billion loan program for 
long lead procurement for five, 2 reactor sites, with letters of intent signed with seven 
unnamed counterparties. Some attendees expressed optimism for 1 2 site 
announcements  by the US mid terms.

Conversion market prices 
Conversion pricing in North America and the European Union is declining after reaching 
an all time high of $97 and $95 respectively in February 2024 and could continue 
appreciating given reports of still tight supply (i.e., Kazatomprom 10% production 
curtailment and U.S. ban on Russian uranium products). See Exhibits 6 and 7 for a pricing 
history. 
While Western conversion capacity is gradually expanding, near-term relief remains 
limited. The ConverDyn Metropolis facility is the only operating commercial UF6 
conversion plant in the U.S. that is ramping output by ~20% to over 10,000 tonnes in 
2026, though this alone is insufficient to meet growing demand.  
Orano's Philippe Coste facility, meanwhile, has faced compounding setbacks with a 
slower-than-planned ramp and an unplanned three-month weather-related outage in 
early 2026, with full-year output now expected at just 9kt down from 13kt in 2025. 
Longer-dated additions, including UEC's planned U.S. conversion facility remain years 
from first production. Until meaningful new capacity comes online, conversion tightness 
is likely to persist, implying continued pent-up demand for U3O8 as feedstock and 
sustained upward pressure on conversion prices, which are currently near all-time highs.

Where we are in the cycle: price & volume 
Below we summarize uranium market volume trends and how they relate to price. 
Spot vs. Term Market 
Uranium (yellow cake, conversion, and/or SWU) is bought and sold in one of two 
markets, either the term or the spot. While the spot market is defined by any activity 
that calls for delivery within the next 12 months, spot price indicators are largely driven 
by activity that calls for “prompt” delivery over the next three months. Meanwhile, the 
term market covers product that will be delivered 12 months out (or longer).  
The below exhibit below maps the relationship between the spot and term prices for 
U3O8 (Yellowcake). Term prices generally tend to trade at a premium to spot. However, in 
late 2023, this trend flipped as spot began trading at a premium to term. This marked 
the start of another bullish period for uranium, similar to the period from 2004-07. While 
the 2004-07 period suggests that there may be a correction once supply and demand 
balance. Our view is that this will only occur when the uranium deficit levels out, no 
earlier than 2026, with risk to this being extended.

Historically, brokers, traders and other financial institutions have been the dominant 
group on the selling side of the spot market (see Exhibit 21). On the buying side, utilities 
were long the dominant group, but were dethroned in 2005 with the invention of the 
uranium carry trade, when brokers, traders, and other financial institutions used their 
balance sheets to help utilities offload inventory from their balance sheets. In 2005, 
brokers, traders, and other financial institutions accounted for 60% of annual spot 
purchase volumes (see Exhibit 22). In 2025, brokers, traders, and other financial 
institutions accounted for ~74% of annual spot purchase volumes. 
In the term market, buyers are almost exclusively utilities while sellers are predominantly 
producers. Over the last three years, producers have consistently accounted for a smaller 
share of annual term selling volumes, decreasing from 92% in 2023 to 76% in 2025. 
Rising utility participation in the term market is a driver of higher U3O8 prices, as churn 
(when brokers, traders, and other financial institutions trade amongst themselves) is 
replaced by end-user consumptive demand.

Price objective basis & risk 
Cameco Corporation (YCCO / CCJ) 
Our C$190 ($133) PO is based on 1.0x our NAV and 35.0x 2026E & 34.5x 2027E 
EV/EBITDA (all 3 equally weighted). We use a CADUSD FX rate of 1.42. The 35.0x 2026E 
EV/EBITDA is above its long-term average of c.15x, but below the long-term peak of 
c.40.0x that occurred when nuclear energy growth was last very strong. The 34.5x for 
2027E is a slight discount to 35.0x used for 2026E given time-related risk. The 1.0x 
P/NAV is above the longer-term average of c.0.9x but below peak of 1.35x. We think 1.0x 
is justified given Cameco's world-class tier one assets in favorable jurisdictions (Canada) 
& potential exploration upside partially offset by voluntary idling of one of those tier-one 
assets (but is in the process of restarting). 
 
Downside risks: 1) slower-than-expected global energy demand growth, 2) continued 
push-out of a Japanese nuclear fleet restart, 3) any worsening in sentiment toward 
nuclear or more favorable sentiment toward alternative power fuel sources, and 4) any 
production problems at Cameco's only operating mine, Cigar Lake. 
Upside risks: 1) additional potential mine disruptions that may further improve supply-
demand dynamics, 2) better pace of reactor development in key future demand countries 
(China, Japan, and India), 3) more stringent carbon emissions restrictions in key 
countries, encouraging nuclear power as an environmentally friendly base line energy 
source, 4) a material rise in natural gas prices making nuclear power generation 
competitive in the US.

Standard Nuclear (STDN) 
Our $15 PO is based on a DCF, which we believe is the most appropriate methodology 
given the company's early-stage commercialization profile and limited set of direct 
public comparables. To reflect the varying levels of commercial certainty across the 
business, we separate future cash flows into three categories: contracted revenue, 
qualified customer revenue, and future market opportunity. 
Contracted revenue is discounted using a market-based cost of equity, reflecting the 
highest level of commercial visibility within our framework. Qualified is discounted using 
a fuel-manufacturing peer framework, reflecting greater uncertainty than contracted 
revenue but lower risk than broader market adoption. Future market opportunity is 
discounted using an advanced-reactor peer framework to reflect the speculative nature 
of long-term advanced nuclear deployment. We apply a 2.0% terminal growth rate, which 
we view as appropriate for a mature advanced nuclear fuel business. 
Combining the three cash flow buckets results in an enterprise value of $1.27bn. 
Incorporating our 2030 balance sheet assumptions, which we use as the common 
valuation year across our nuclear coverage universe, produces an equity value of 
$2.19bn. Based on the shares outstanding, this supports our $15 PO.
