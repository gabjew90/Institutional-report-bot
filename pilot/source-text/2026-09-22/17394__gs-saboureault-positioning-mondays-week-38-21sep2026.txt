GS Saboureault - Positioning Mondays Week 38 21 Sep 2026
Pierre Saboureault  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Vice President, FICC & Equities (Europe Sales)
Mon 21 Sep 2026, 7:14am ET
What stands out : 1) Too short. US equities saw the largest net buying in five weeks, and European
equities saw the largest net buying in five months, last week, according to our PB stats ; 2) Too lean. CTAs
have now sold $47bn of global equities over the past month, including $38bn over the past week. Our
models predict that post a small overshoot to the downside short-term, CTAs will flip to buyers over a
one-month horizon ; 3) Too cautious. Net flows into global equity funds saw a meaningful pickup in last
week (+$80bn) after several weeks of more subdued inflows ; 4) Too bearish. Our GS SI is at its lowest in
over a year, and the AAII Bear survey points to highest level of bearishness (53%) in 16 months. As per
Rich Privorotsky : “Experience says stay optimistic when everyone has the same list of things to worry
about” ; 5) Too late ? CFTC data and anecdotal evidence from our floor concur : Long Onlys have
continued to trim exposure and are yet to reengage. All eyes on Trump/Xi summit this week.
HEDGE FUNDS - Prime Brokerage:
- Global : Overall book Gross leverage +4.6 pts to 309.2% (65th percentile 1-year) and Net leverage +0.7
pts to 77.5% (34th percentile 1-year). Overall book L/S ratio -0.3% to 1.670 (25th percentile 1-year).
Fundamental L/S Gross leverage +2.7 pts to 207.7% (30th percentile 1-year) and Net leverage +0.1 pts to
56.4% (65th percentile 1-year).
Global equities were net bought (+0.5 SDs 1-year), as gross trading activity continued to increase driven
by long buys outpacing short sales (3.9 to 1). Regional flows were split, as buying in North America and
Europe outpaced selling in Asia (EM + DM). Macro Products were net bought, driven by long buys and
short covers, while Single Stocks were modestly net sold, as short sales slightly outpaced long buys.
Comm Svcs, Cons Disc, and Industrials were the most $ net bought sectors this week, while Health Care,
Energy, and Info Tech were the most net sold.
- Europe : European equities were net bought at the fastest pace in 5 months, driven by short covers and to
a lesser extent long buys (1.9 to 1). Germany, France, and Ireland were the most $ net bought markets on
the week, all driven by short covers and long buys. Macro Products (long buys + short covers) and Single
Stocks (short covers) were both net bought and made up 63% and 37% of the total $ net buying,
respectively.
7 of 11 sectors were net bought, led in $ terms by Industrials (short covers), Info Tech (long buys + short
covers), and Cons Disc (long buys), while Energy (short + long sales), Financials (long sales), and Staples
(long sales) were the most net sold.
Gross/Net allocations to Europe (as a % of the global Prime Book) now stand at 17.4%/15.8%, which rank
in the 63rd/14th percentiles vs. the past year and in the 58th/3rd percentiles vs. the past five years.

SYSTEMATIC : In their latest update, our models estimate CTAs sold -$39bn Global Equities over the
past week, bringing their length down to around $91bn of Global Equities (62nd percentile). Looking
ahead, our models predict CTAs will remain sellers on a one-week horizon in a flat market scenario;
however, they also forecast buying in a flat market scenario on a one-month horizon. I.e. CTAs would
swiftly reduce exposure, overshooting to the downside before quickly turning buyers, with the potential
for this buy impulse to accelerate with indices moving higher. Note : they have already turned buyers of
US equities.

CORPORATES: Supply (IPOs) vs Demand (Buybacks):
- Issuance: $6.69bn worth of IPOs/Addl in North America on the WEEK / $497.83bn of IPOs/Addl in
North America YTD.
- Buybacks: From our dedicated US buyback desk, for the week ending 11-Sep-2026 : “We estimate the
next blackout window begins this week, on 9/15. At that point, approximately 40% of S&P 500 corporates
are expected to enter their quiet periods. This transition to blackout typically tempers one of the market’s
more consistent sources of demand. Reflecting this transition, our desk has seen an increasing shift toward
10b5-1 plans. The current execution mix stands at 30% OMR vs 70% 10b5-1 (vs. 33% / 67% the prior
week). Despite the looming blackout, our desk activity last week remained steady. Volumes finished 1.5x
vs 2025 YTD ADTV and 1.3x vs 2024 YTD ADTV, with flows primarily skewed toward Tech, Financials,
and Consumer Discretionary sectors.

GS Sentiment Indicator: Negative. Our US Equity Sentiment Indicator dropped to -0.9 from -0.5 a week
ago, further into negative territory and at its lowest since August 2025.
The Sentiment Indicator (SI) is a measure of aggregate positioning and risk sentiment in the US equity
market. The Sentiment Indicator tracks investor positioning across the more than 80% of the US equity
market that is owned by institutional, retail and foreign investors. To calculate the Sentiment Indicator we
run a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on six weekly and three monthly indicators that span these
three investor types.
Source: GIR, as of 08/28/2026. Past performance is not indicative of future results
