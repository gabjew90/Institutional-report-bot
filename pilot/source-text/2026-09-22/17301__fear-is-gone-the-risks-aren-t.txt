Fear is gone. The risks aren’t
Volatility has collapsed even as the macro backdrop remains anything but calm. The VIX is back at a
14 handle, Goldman’s Vol Panic Index just recorded its biggest one-day decline in a decade, and
convexity across indices, small caps and China is trading near historic lows.
The fear reset has been extreme. With momentum still violent and rates vol refusing to disappear, this
looks like the moment to buy convexity, not sell it.
The fear reset
Vols have reset sharply in recent sessions and are approaching their natural floors. Volatility is a mean-
reverting asset, so don’t assume that a further upside squeeze in markets will necessarily drive vols
much lower.

Equity Clock
Buy it when you can
Garrett highlights the disconnect: a hawkish Fed, global tightening, $100+ oil, escalating Middle East
risk, 10-year yields near 5%, growing AI backlash and approaching US midterms, yet VIX closed Friday
with a 14 handle.
Index convexity sits below the 10th percentile, while some single-stock convexity is at the 0th
percentile. At the same time, the underlying data suggest investors have started to re-risk.
Timing remains difficult, but the vol market is offering limited-loss expressions at levels not seen in
months or even years. Buy convexity when you can, not when you need to.

Cheap in China
The lack of interest in trading FXI options into the Trump–Xi summit has been notable. Options volumes
remain flat ahead of the meeting, with no meaningful kink in the vol surface according to GS. As with
many other equity vol measures, FXI implied volatility is sitting at multi-year lows.
Why not some FXI straddles into the meeting?

Not all is dull
GS’s TMT momentum basket fell 11.53% on Monday before rebounding every day for the rest of the
week: +2.08% Tuesday, +3.98% Wednesday, +2.56% Thursday and +2.97% Friday. Over the past three

GS
Watch rates/rate vol
Probably the biggest macro risk. As Privorotsky notes, Friday delivered the first meaningful break in the
energy/rates relationship: WTI fell around 1.6%, yet 2s sold off to 4.74% and 10s pushed back through
5%. MOVE re-bid 6% to 80.6, while front-end vol remains supported by the unusually wide range of
outcomes across the Fed, BoE, ECB and BOJ.
The risk is that rates are becoming disanchored from spot energy: oil is falling, but second-round
inflation and the broader global rates shock may already be embedded. That conclusion still feels
premature after two extremely difficult weeks for the STIR crowd. Rate vol remains the key thing to
watch, although our base case is that rates ultimately continue to follow energy.
The chart shows the short-term gap between MOVE and VXN has widened sharply. Equity vol is cheap,
but rate vol may decide whether it stays that way.
