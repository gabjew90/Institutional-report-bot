GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Muse & CPUs Europe Breadth
Impaired 21 Sep 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets
21 Sep 2026
Markets starting the week firm despite some tense moments over the weekend. Opening gambit still looks
like restraint. Trump spent Sunday talking up escalation with Iran, but no corresponding military move
and diplomatic channels remain open. With Bessent/He meeting over the weekend and Trump-Xi
Thursday, feels like the pieces are there to at least try to bridge something that looks like a deal. Far from
certain, but pathways remain open.
Energy: no news is good news. No fresh disruption overnight, crude lower, Saudi exports recovering
despite the East-West pipe still impaired. Physical market coping better than feared. Some hope of SPR
releases.
Rates/Rate vol: probably the biggest macro risk. Friday was the first real break in the energy/rates
relationship… WTI down ~1.6%, yet 2s sold off to ~4.74% and 10s pushed back through 5%. MOVE re-
bid ~6% to 80.6 and front end vol remains supported as the range of outcomes around Fed/BoE/ECB/BOJ
stays huge. The risk is rates becoming disanchored from spot energy… oil comes down but 2nd rd
inflation and rolling global rate shock are already cemented. Premature to make that conclusion, think very
difficult price action for STIR crowd last couple weeks...rate vol remain the thing to watch (but still of the
view this all just follows energy).
Flows/breadth: last week showed a decent amount of covering. Prime and CFTC both point the same
way… people got a bit too gloomy pre Fed and seems like move down was met with covering. Energy
stocks also saw notable selling. So despite all the doom, positioning is already pretty negative and on the
margin people may be starting to anticipate de-escalation. Breadth is still horrendous. Outside a handful of
equities, most things are trading badly and a lot of the market is still below longer term trends (see below).
In a sense that makes this a coiled spring. If macro improves, we could get one of those violent snapbacks
where the short leg rips (think all those consumer stocks no one likes....I prefer civil aero/airlines). Think
about some Russell calls to protect the right tail while the mood is gloomy.
AI: despite the disastrous Monday open around the “Pace the Frontier” nonsense, the trade recovered
through the week. I still think Monday was a buying opportunity. Product cycle has not slowed. Anthropic
looks close to another release, Polymarket still has Opus odds very high into this week, and there is plenty
of speculation around new OpenAI models coming down the pike.
Muse: probably the more interesting story. Has the No. 1 spot overall among free apps on the Apple U.S in
just over a week after its September 8 release. Meta has basically built a direct to consumer agent
factory… shopping, calls, subscriptions, admin, increasingly acting on your behalf. For anyone already
running agents on a server, the idea isn’t new. What is new is packaging it for everyone with safety rails


[Page 2]
and a credit card attached. The end state is pretty obvious… everyone has dozens of agents buying
groceries, paying bills, booking holidays, sorting schools. Your productive capacity becomes you plus
your team of agents. Massive efficiency unlock, massive compute multiplier. Every major lab will build
one. Watch the CPU/cloud compute trade, not just GPUs.
Europe: German results were challenging for Merz. AfD first in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, CDU
struggled, Die Linke first in Berlin. Last time an AfD shock forced Berlin to actually get moving on
reform/spending....the risk now is the opposite: a severely diminished domestic mandate leading to
prolonged policy gridlock.
Week ahead: Apsara Sep 22-24, Meta Connect Sep 23-24, Fed speak, PMIs Wednesday. Then the big one
is Trump-Xi Thursday. Bessent/He laid some groundwork over the weekend. Setup into it feels one of
hopefulness. Best case is a genuine double whammy… energy relief plus some easing in US-China trade
friction. Last week we managed to climb the wall of worry… Fed, rates, oil, midterms, AI regulation.
Usually when the market gets that gloomy and positioning follows, it just grinds higher. Experience says
stay optimistic when everyone has the same list of things to worry about.
side-note: Breadth not quite to March lows but most stocks have done pretty badly in Sept.
