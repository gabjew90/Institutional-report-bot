Credit is cracking at the bottom
US credit is not experiencing a market-wide break. Investment grade, broad high yield and single-B
spreads remain relatively contained. The fracture is lower down.
CCC and lower spreads have detached sharply from the rest of the quality ladder. This is not broad
contagion. It is a concentrated repricing of borrowers with the least capacity to absorb higher funding
costs.
Credit has repriced the weakest borrowers and small caps have begun to respond. The options market
has not.
The bottom rung
Broad credit indices are dominated by companies that still have earnings, liquidity and access to
capital. Their stability can conceal a much harsher refinancing environment at the bottom of the
market.
The credit market is separating borrowers that remain financeable from those increasingly exposed to
refinancing or restructuring risk.
The credit ladder remains orderly until the bottom rung. The jump from single-B to CCC is
approximately eight percentage points.
BofA
The bottom has detached
The CCC minus single-B spread has widened to 799bp, close to the top of its available three-year
range and well above the roughly 575bp median.
This does not establish an all-time extreme. It does show that lower-quality borrowers are being
treated very differently from the rest of high yield credit in the current cycle.

Small caps have noticed
The same distinction is appearing in equities. BTIG finds that Bloomberg Caa spreads reached 9.2%,
their widest since 2023, while the Russell 2000 lost more than 8% relative to the S&P 500 from late
June.
Smaller companies tend to have narrower financing options, weaker interest coverage and greater
exposure to refinancing conditions. When lenders ration capital at the bottom of the credit ladder,
small-cap equities can deteriorate before broad credit displays visible stress.
CCC borrowers and Russell 2000 constituents are not the same companies. The connection is that both
are more sensitive to tightening marginal financing conditions.
BTIG identifies 11 similar occasions since 1996. The clearest historical pattern was not an immediate
S&P 500 collapse, but further Russell weakness over the following 40–60 trading days. The sample is
small, but the window is worth watching.

Small-cap volatility has not
Small-cap equities have weakened, but the options market has not charged an exceptional premium
for that weakness to continue. That is the more unusual signal.
The six-month ATM implied-volatility spread between IWM and SPX has fallen close to the bottom of its
two-year range. Credit has repriced the weakest borrowers and small caps have underperformed, yet
investors can still buy Russell volatility at an unusually small premium to large-cap volatility.
This does not mean IWM options are cheap in absolute terms. It means small-cap volatility is
inexpensive relative to SPX despite the greater financing risk visible beneath the Russell.
GS
The trade
The expression is simple: own an IWM put spread a few months out while small-cap volatility remains
unusually cheap versus SPX.
This trade does not need a credit crisis. It only needs the options market to catch up with what lower-
quality credit and small-cap equities are already saying.
Credit has cracked at the bottom. Small caps have noticed. Volatility has not...yet.
