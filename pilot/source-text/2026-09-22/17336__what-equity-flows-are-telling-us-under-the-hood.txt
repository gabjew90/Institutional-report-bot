Retail are starting to load up on calls across cyber stocks
1. INSTITUTIONAL INVESTORS HAD LOADED UP ON SEMIS & SOFTWARE... BUT BUYING IS
STARTING TO SLOW. Institutional positioning across the AI complex had turned increasingly bullish
ahead of Monday's sell-off, but the pace of buying has faded in recent sessions. The chart below shows
how the improvement in call-put ratios across Mag 7 and SOXX names has started to stall of late – a sign
that institutional investors are taking a breather for now.
2. RETAIL'S NEWEST OBSESSION APPEARS TO BE CYBER... PARTICULARLY CROWDSTRIKE.
Retail option activity across cyber stocks has picked up sharply from a low base, with the call-put ratio


[Page 2]
seeing its biggest 5-day improvement in our data. CRWD accounts for 53% of all retail call activity across
the basket, while 1M call buying now sits in the 97th percentile of its own history. Importantly, CRWD is
also the one major cyber name where retail are buying the underlying stock alongside the calls. This
increasingly looks like a new theme catching the attention of retail investors, particularly after Dario's
weekend AI warning.
3. THAT DOESN'T MEAN RETAIL HAVE TOTALLY GIVEN UP ON SEMIS... FAR FROM IT. Retail
cash buying across SOXX names has swung by more than $2bn over the past month, with NVDA
accounting for 63% of all semi inflows. Over the past two weeks, NVIDIA alone has more than accounted
for all net retail single stock buying. So in theory, retail would've been net sellers of single names without
this NVDA buying! But there is some breadth underneath the retail bid in semis: 19 of 30 names have seen
positive retail buying over the past month, led by Intel and Broadcom. The 2026 retail investor is still
looking to buy dips in their favourite AI names, just increasingly selectively.

Bottom line
THERE'S PLENTY HAPPENING UNDER THE HOOD... BUT AGGREGATE RISK APPETITE
REMAINS CAUTIOUS. Equity positioning has continued to clear with systematic investors cutting
exposure and institutional buying slowing. That's not necessarily a bad thing: positioning is becoming less
of an overhang, while retail are still willing to put money to work in the themes they like. For now this
remains a selective rather than indiscriminate risk-on tape (chart below). The next leg higher probably
needs the Fed & geopolitical overhang to fade enough for these bottom-up flows to translate into broader
index-level buying.
