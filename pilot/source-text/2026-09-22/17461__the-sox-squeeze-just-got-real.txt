Korea confirms the semi bid
South Korea’s exports jumped 78.3% YoY in the first 20 days of September, led by a 259.4% surge in
semiconductor exports. Korea is one of the clearest real-time windows into the global memory cycle,
so the strength matters as SOX pushes higher.
The distinction: export values reflect both volumes and prices. This confirms the strength of the chip
cycle, but does not mean every part of SOX is seeing the same demand.
Bloomberg/Steno
What was priced?
The market treated slower frontier AI development as a capex slowdown and took SOX from roughly
23x to 13.85x on Bloomberg’s blended 24-month forward numbers. But spending plans remain intact,
Korean chip exports are surging and earnings estimates have held up. That is the gap the squeeze is
closing.
The capex cut that isn’t coming
Privorotsky sees little evidence that slower frontier AI development will change hyperscaler capex
plans through 2027/28. Compute remains scarce, workloads are growing and spending intentions are
still rising.
Permitting is the bigger constraint. Local opposition and proposed moratoriums could slow where and
how quickly data centers get built. That matters for electrical, power and cooling names, but the
impact will differ across the supply chain. Grid constraints could make behind-the-meter power more
valuable, while rising inference demand still requires substantial optical bandwidth and interconnect
capacity.
