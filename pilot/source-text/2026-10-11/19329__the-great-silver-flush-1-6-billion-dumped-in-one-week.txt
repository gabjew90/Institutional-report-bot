Silver: The reset isn't over
Silver is approaching a critical technical juncture.
The dollar remains a headwind, speculative positioning has taken a hit and CTAs are adding to the
selling pressure.
Meanwhile, volatility has collapsed back to pre-mania levels, even as demand for downside protection
remains elevated.
The question is whether the reset has gone far enough, or another leg lower is needed to clear the
decks.
Soggy silver
Silver is flirting with a major trend line, trading well below its 200-day MA.
A close below the key $60 level would be an ugly development, with $56 emerging as the next support.

The dollar connection
Silver and the inverted DXY continue to track each other closely.
Current dollar levels don't necessarily suggest silver needs to catch down immediately, but dollar
strength remains a short-term headwind for the metal.
Historically, GS notes that stronger dollar environments have coincided with managed-money long
liquidation.

The silver flush
GS highlights a sharp deterioration in speculative silver positioning.
Managed Money, Other and Non-Reportable traders sold a combined $1.6bn in the final week of
September, the largest weekly selling since February.
Interestingly, the selling was evenly split between liquidation of existing longs ($800mn) and fresh
shorts ($800mn).
This wasn't just profit-taking. New bears have entered the trade.
GS
CTAs joining the selling
Systematic flows are adding to the pressure in silver.
GS estimates that CTAs have been cutting silver exposure as the technical picture deteriorates.
With silver testing major support levels, the question is whether further weakness triggers another
wave of systematic selling.

GS
The silver vol reset
Silver volatility has continued to reset following the mania that began around this time last year.
Vol surged from around 37 to a peak of 110. We are now back to where it all started.
But beneath the surface, downside protection remains expensive.
GS notes that normalized 25-delta put-call skew has richened into its top decile over the past two
years.
Headline vol has collapsed, but demand for downside protection remains elevated.
