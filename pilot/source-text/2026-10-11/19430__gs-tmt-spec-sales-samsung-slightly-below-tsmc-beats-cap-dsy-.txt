GS TMT SPEC SALES – Thursday 8th October
Market overnight: The QQQs fell 0.25% but recovered from morning lows, with weakness beneath the
index as semis and software declined while internet stocks gained. Analog, auto, semicap, neocloud and
cybersecurity names were the main laggards, while memory, AI hardware, storage and beaten-up internet
stocks outperformed. The session felt more like rotation and digestion than a change in investor sentiment,
with firm memory pricing and supply constraints continuing to support Micron and the broader memory
outlook. The MU potential strikes viewed as positive for memory stocks as it could see supply disruption.
Samsung and TSMC: Samsung reported preliminary operating profit of ₩107.4tn, slightly below street
and marking a fourth consecutive record quarter, although the shares slipped 1% as investors debated
whether the result cleared a high bar (looks good enough to me esp. given how high the bar – but getting
2-way investor debate part looks like FX – full divisional split on 29th Oct). Sentiment remains positive
on strong memory fundamentals, with Samsung reportedly qualifying its 12-high HBM4E chips (press,
not company-confirmed) with Nvidia and hyperscalers, while AMD continues to explore a foundry
partnership.
TSMC’s 3Q revenue reached a record NT$1.49tn (US$46.7bn), above the top end of guidance, driven by
strong AI-chip demand.
Next up ASML next week where the key question after the substantial Q2 guidance raise is whether there
is still room for ASML to surprise positively in Q3 on the guide, or whether other semicap names now
offer better upside. Sell-side views remain broadly bullish, but buyside sentiment is more mixed and flows
have been two-way. US hedge funds appear to be using ASML as a sector funding short in favour of other
semicap names, despite the stock looking inexpensive relative to its own history and peers.
Terafab: Elon Musk clarified that his companies will build and operate the chip plant independently, with
TSMC potentially subleasing part of the facility rather than leading the project; Intel is still expected to
participate, including through its reported collaboration with UMC on 12-inch fabrication.

TELCOS
• Satellites: The FCC has authorised SpaceX to launch 15,000 next-generation low-orbit satellites for
Starlink Mobile and to sell direct-to-phone service without relying on a terrestrial carrier’s spectrum
agreement. Using EchoStar and T-Mobile frequencies, the network aims to deliver 5G speeds of up to 150
Mbps to standard smartphones, with lower latency and approval for international expansion, creating
greater competition for traditional telecom operators. (ADSLZone)
• UK TELCOS: VMO2 CEO Lutz Schüler has urged DCMS not to fast-track BT’s £400m acquisition of
TalkTalk Consumer and PlatformX from administration, covering around 2.5 million retail and wholesale
lines. He argues that Openreach’s position as a major creditor effectively left BT as the only viable bidder
and that using the public-interest process risks bypassing normal competition scrutiny, with the CMA due
to report as early as 19 October. However, many altnets have balance-sheet problems of their own,
including VMO2’s high leverage, and it is unclear why BT should be expected to fund further rescue
attempts when this deal preserves service continuity. Sources: Mobile Europe and Telecoms.com
• CityFibre: Independent auditor BDO has warned of a “material uncertainty” over CityFibre’s ability to
continue as a going concern, citing limited headroom under its debt covenants by June 2027, as rising
interest costs and slower growth put pressure on its debt-funded model. The company plans to raise £900m
from shareholders to support acquisitions, but the warning raises questions about its financial resilience
and credibility as a consolidator of the UK fibre market.
• Telefónica: The Spanish government has restarted the award of the €100mn-plus CORA III cybersecurity
and data-centre interconnection contract to Telefónica, ending a 14-month legal dispute after confirming
that its joint venture meets all legal and technical requirements. The process had been delayed by a
funding shortfall, prompting a temporary nine-month extension for Telefónica and Indra to protect state
networks. (Cinco Días)

• Online Travel Q3 Preview - As we head into Q3‘26 Online Travel earnings, we frame key industry
debates, refresh our operating estimates for industry data and channel checks, and update our 12-month
price targets for names under coverage. In total, our industry work points to a broadly stable to strong end
demand environment (most pronounced in the US travel market in the September quarter). Looking
forward, we expect investor attention to be centred around a few key topics: 1) whether forward
commentary reflects a continuation of industry trends and/or more conservatism around sustained elevated
unit pricing in the travel industry; 2) how the framing of opportunities vs. challenges around the rise of
