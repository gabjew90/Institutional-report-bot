 
"Looks Unsustainable...": SPX Resilience Looks 
Increasingly Fragile 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 08, 2026 - 08:05 PM 
Authored by Michael Ball, Bloomberg macro strategist, 
The S&P 500 is holding up remarkably well, but the margin for error is narrowing as higher 
yields, rising oil, a stronger dollar, and a more leveraged AI capex cycle all add headwinds to 
concentrated equity leadership. 
 
The market mood is increasingly one of resilience under pressure. Equities remain close to 
record highs, but headwinds from the macro backdrop have notably changed leadership. 
Treasury yields are near multi-decade highs, oil is back above $100 and the dollar is surging, all 
underpinned by persistent geopolitical and fiscal risks and a Fed that remains tilted hawkish. 
Wednesday’s exceptionally strong 10-year auction offered some temporary relief, but overnight 
pressures have moved yields right back to around pre-auction levels across the curve. 

 
The tension around AI is growing, turning the space into more of a stock-picker’s market than 
the broad, high-beta momentum trade of the past. 
Demand to grow compute remains exponential, with Samsung and TSMC keep posting huge 
growth, yet the market is becoming harder to impress. 
At the same time, the AI buildout is increasingly turning to debt financing as free cash flow dries 
up, with signs of stress beginning to emerge at the lower end of tech credit.

As a result, it’s safer to say cross-asset signals are becoming more defensive. Investors are 
leaning long dollars but short duration, while equity positioning remains concentrated in 
mega-cap tech, aka quality. 
Credit spreads remain remarkably calm at the investment grade but stress is building further 
out. In a recent note, JPMorgan sees a growing level of leveraged loans below 60 cents, while 
39% of distressed loans are in tech as a refinancing wall approaches. This is driving CCC 
spreads to around 1,000 bps. 
Meanwhile, geopolitics is keeping the inflationary tail alive through sticky energy prices and 
increasingly higher logistical costs. The vibe is not outright risk-off, but one of shrinking room for 
error: a narrowing group of equities is being asked to absorb tighter financial conditions as rising 
estimates of neutral rates crash against an increasingly leveraged AI investment cycle driving 
broader growth. 
This increasingly looks unsustainable.
