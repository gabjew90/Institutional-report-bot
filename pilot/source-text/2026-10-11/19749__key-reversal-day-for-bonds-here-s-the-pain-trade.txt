 
'Key Reversal Day' For Bonds... Here's The 'Pain 
Trade' 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 08, 2026 - 05:55 PM 
30yr yields are flashing signs of exhaustion, according to BTIG chief technical market strategist, 
Jonathan Krinsky. 
 
30Y yields made a new high overnight (by less than 1bp), but have now faded ~8bps from 
intraday highs...  

 
 
As Krinsky notes, that is a 'key reversal' day... 
 
While we continue to think the primary trend for rates is higher, over the next couple of weeks a 
reversion lower would not be surprising, with the risk/reward for tactical trades now firmly lower 
for yields. 
All else equal, falling rates should help RTY stabilize after its nearly -10% drawdown. 

 
However, yields finished well off session highs today and RTY barely finished off its lows. 
Further, in '07 falling yields coincided with falling RTY in July/August, so it's far from a 
guarantee. 
Furthermore, Krinsky notes that this morning we got another sentiment signal from a widely read 
publication with the title: 'Will bonds blow up?' 
 
Those familiar with this publication will know they have a long history of putting out covers well 
after a move has been underway, and often create a contrarian signal. 
What Does a Bond Rally Mean for Stocks? 
Anecdotally, the consensus view is that a pullback in yields will be bullish for stocks. 
While we could sympathize with that view for something like small-caps (IWM) or equal-weight 
(RSP) which have been punished during the surge in yields, we find it curious that something 
like semis or QQQ would benefit from lower yields when they were immune to the surge in 
rates. 
The Pain Trade. 
In fact, the pain trade would likely be lower rates, and LOWER SMH, QQQ, XLK etc. 
RTY/NDX Extreme.  
We wrote yesterday (full note here for pro subs) how the RTY/NDX ratio had the 8th lowest RSI 
(12.77) on record back to 1985. 

 
 
A reversion lower in yields should be supportive to that ratio, but the question is: which way?  
RTY is down ~10% from highs and into its 200 DMA. 
More often than not that should lead to a bounce, so we wouldn't fight that. 
 
But we also have more conviction that NDX moves lower, and again we don't think many would 
expect that to happen during a bond rally
