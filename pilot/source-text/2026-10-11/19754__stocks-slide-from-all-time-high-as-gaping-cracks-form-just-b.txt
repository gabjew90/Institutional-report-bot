 
Stocks Slide From All Time High As Gaping Cracks 
Form Just Below The Surface 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 08, 2026 - 11:45 AM 
The record-breaking run in stocks hit a wall as still-elevated oil prices stoked concerns that 
potential inflationary pressures could trigger more interest-rate hikes. 
It started with the overnight session, with futures sliding as they followed a material drop in 
Korea's Kospi, which closed at session lows as the 55 trillion won buyback quota of Samsung 
Eletronic and SK Hynix - which had singlehandedly propped up the Korean market for the past 
two months - are now ending far sooner than previously expected (mid-November) as both 
companies went on tilt buying back their stocks... 
 
... and accelerated during the European session following a sharp slide in European bourses 
driven by a plunge in European banks... 

 
 
... as the selloff in French bonds returned with a vengeance, pushing the FRA-GER spread back 
to 140bps as the brief euphoria over Le Pen's "Shadow budget" (which will never be 
implemented) faded and after the ECB made it clear that French bond woes are nowhere near 
enough to reactivate bond buying by the central bank. And just like that, Europe's "Doom Loop" 
reared its ugly head, and sparked contagion shockwaves across the continent's banks, which 
ironically may be sufficient to force the ECB to actually do something.  

 
 
Meanwhile, across the Atlantic, after sliding into the open, US equity futures accelerated their 
drop but then slammed the brakes and quickly reversed higher just as Europe closed... 
 
... and got a second wind after today's closely watched 10Y auction printed far stronger than 
expected, stopping through the When Issued by 1.7bps, the biggest stop through in over a year, 
and erasing concerns of another catastrophic auction... 

 
 
... despite the 10Y auction pricing at a yield of 5.300%, the highest since November 2000. 
 
The auction was so strong it helped send 10Y yields sliding by 5bps to a session low of 5.27%, 
after the 10Y earlier hit a new multi-decade high yield of 5.36%. 

 
 
Yields remained subdued, helped by a gradual drop in oil prices even though there was no 
geopolitical news to merit such a move... 
 
... despite the latest NY Fed survey showing that 1Yr inflation expectations jumped to a 
three-year high of 3.9% from 3.6%.... 

 
 
... and despite surprisingly hawkish FOMC minutes which showed that "most" officials assumed 
at least one more rate hike before year-end, while making it clear that the inflationary impact of 
AI is starting to spread across the economy, to wit: 
●​ "Several participants observed that the rate of price increases in the core goods 
category also remained elevated, as effects of the AI buildout appeared to increase while 
the effects of tariff increases waned" 
●​ "Some participants commented that increased energy prices and the ongoing AI buildout 
were contributing to cost pressures faced by businesses, including higher costs for 
transportation and input materials." 
●​ "Some participants commented that the AI buildout could cause aggregate demand to 
outpace aggregate supply over the medium term, putting upward pressure on inflation." 
●​ "Some participants observed that strong demand for skilled workers in sectors related to 
the ongoing AI buildout had been driving strong wage gains for these workers" 
●​ "Participants noted that the ongoing AI buildout was boosting business investment. 
Several participants commented that the scale and pace of the AI buildout had continued 
to surprise to the upside" 
Taking a closer look at stocks, it was a relatively muted session at the index level (SPX intraday 
band just 43bps) but under the hood the rotation is more extreme as Russell underperforms 
NDX for the 4th straight day and (17th time in the last 20 sessions) as small caps continue to 
bear the brunt of the rates pain... 

 
 
... while tech does all the heavy lifting as usual. 
 
As usual, the equal-weighted S&P continues to underperform... 

 
 
... as the ratio of the RSP (equal-weighted) SPX ETF to the plain old SPY just hit a new all time 
low... 
 
... although notably, for the second day in a row, we had more 52 week highs than lows. 

 
 
 
Sector wise there is was a flight to safety with Healthcare, Staples and Utes outperforming... 
 
... though Goldman's desk notes that hedge funds are adding to risk with the bank's Most Short 
basket down -130bps... 

 
 
... while ETF volumes are light. Something notable here, and perhaps the most important chart 
in today's wrap: the Quality pair is screaming higher while most shorted is dumping to a 2+ 
month low. The market is finally starting to reward healthy balance sheets. 
 
Yet while most shorted stocks did get sold - ostensibly amid more shorting - there was no offset 
on the long side, and instead Goldman's High Beta proxies got smoked following yesterday's 

 
leadership: Rare Earths (GSXURARE, -5.5%), Robotics (GSXUROBO, -3%), Uranium 
(GSXURANI, -3.5%) all tumbled, reversing yesterday's post-Constellation Energy news, while 
the PowerUp America (GSENEPOW) basket gave back -2% following its 3rd best day in the last 
5yrs 
 
And speaking of Goldman's desk, it was a one way sell-day there. According to their mid-day 
update, overall activity levels were down -3% vs. the trailing two weeks, while market volumes 
are flat vs. the 10dma. The action on the Goldman floor tilted -9% better for sale, strongly driven 
by LOs while HF short ratio is at the highest level in 2wks 
●​ LOs are -25% better for sale with massive sell skews across Macro Products and Tech 
names, while demand is mutes across Staple and Industrials 
●​ HFs -8% better for sale, though behavior feels risk on with short supply most elevated 
across Utes, Energy and Industrials amid modest demand for Industrials 
While the move in stocks was more of the same - tech propping up everything as S&P ex AI 
sells off - things in credit land are starting to go bump in the night, or day as the case may be, 
starting with Paramount CDS which exploded to the highest since the Global Financial Crisis 
following yesterday's downgrade by Fitch... 

 
 
... we saw SpaceX bonds slide to new all time lows after the overnight report the company is 
preparing to issue another $40BN in an Apollo-led SPV... 
 
... the entire Oracle credit deck crumbled again, with its bond yields hitting a fresh all time high 
after more bad news, this time that its Project Lighthouse data center in Wisconsin will also be 
delayed... 

 
 
... while the proxy bond for the entire data center space - Meta's Project Beignet - which is the 
world's single largest corporate bond, just dropped to a fresh all time low. 
 
Unlike stocks, crypot was a one way carnage all day, with the cohort puking overnight at exactly 
10pm ET, and then failing to sustain a modest attempt at recovery. 

 
 
In commodities, despite oil closing lower on the session, the Diesel crack spread again shot up 
to a two week high despite the Trump admin's attempts to contain the move and last week's 
emergency release of 50 million barrels of Diesel which eased the crack spread... for a few 
hours. And with Diesel prices again set to spike...  
 

 
.... as producers pass through astronomical tanker rates which now amount to $40 or more per 
barrel... 
 
... Dated Brent just hit $130 and is about $10 why of its all time high and at an almost $30 gap to 
Brent futs, as physical crude remains extremely scarce...  
 

 
... all of which means that inflation in the most important fuels for the industrial economy remains 
anything but contained.

Appendix: The Goldman chart of the day today came from the bank's baskets team, which 
highlighted Buying the Consumer Experiences cohort. The GS Consumer Experiences Basket 
(GSXUCEXP) has underperformed SPXXAI Index despite the fact that spending growth on 
experiences accelerated from 1% in Q1 2025 to 6% in Q1 2026, versus 2% for broader 
services.  
 
Demand for Consumer Experiences has remained high and the basket's exposure to higher 
income cohorts has helped insulate it relative to the broader complex, leaving it well positioned 
if oil prices stay elevated or in a potential de-escalation scenario. The Consumer Experiences 
Basket recently exhibited negative correlation with AI, but we expect that relationship to become 
more positive as the value of experiences that cannot be replicated digitally may continue to 
rise.
