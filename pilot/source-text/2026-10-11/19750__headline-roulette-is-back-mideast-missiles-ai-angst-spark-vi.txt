 
Headline Roulette Is Back: MidEast Missiles & AI 
Angst Spark 'Violent Rotations' 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 08, 2026 - 11:00 PM 
Tl;dr: After a couple of days of relative calm, chaos came back today with multiple escalations in 
the Mideast and Gulf shut-ins smashing oil higher (Dated Brent near war highs, shrugging off 
Trump flip-flop). Stocks were steady until reports that OpenAI's revenues were disappointing 
sparked selling across the entire tech stack (NDX ugly, DOW unch). Bonds whipsawed from 
yesterday's highs (oil up) to 50day lows (oil dip, tech easing supply fears). The dollar was 
choppy ending flat, gold green as crypto crumbled (USG wallet move and tech wreck). 
Quite a day... as Goldman's vol desk summarized: "Violent rotations across the board today as 
both geopolitical and AI headlines roiled markets." 
 
The correlation regime between stocks, bonds, and oil snapped again today as various 
idiosyncratic factors hit each market separately... 

 
 
So, let's start with crude which is back in pain territory once again... 
Energy 
We are back to headline roulette (and make sure you're watching the right price!!). 
The cost of real wet barrels of oil - Dated Brent - is soaring, back near war highs... 

 
 
A lot of headlines today: 
Late Wednesday reports said Pentagon was ordered to prepare Iran attack options for before 
midterms. 
Iran-backed Houthis struck two airports in Saudi Arabia, killing three people as the group 
intensifies its attacks on the kingdom while also fighting Riyadh-backed forces in Yemen. 
Smoke was seen rising from a stationary aircraft at Riyadh airport according to a witness and 
three people familiar with the incident. The aircraft was believed to be empty, and Saudi 
authorities have not confirmed the incident. 
In retaliation, the Saudi-led coalition said it had destroyed 82 targets belonging to the Houthis in 
Yemen. 
Iran expanded tanker attacks beyond the Strait of Hormuz into the Persian Gulf and Gulf of 
Oman have slowed traffic once again. 
Multiple projectiles struck a tanker located off the northwest coast of Qatar, around 10 p.m. local 
time last night, according to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations, which also 
reported casualties. 
“The whole story that flows through the Strait of Hormuz have returned has been turned upside 
down,”said Arne Lohmann Rasmussen, chief analyst at Global Risk Management. 
Oil tanker assembly site reportedly explodes near UAE, Mizan reports; "Ocean sources reported 
the detection of a fire in the Gulf of Oman, approximately 30 nautical miles east of Fujairah" 

 
Then oil plunged after Trump said on Truth Social the US will not attack Iran before the 
November midterms. 
"We are having productive discussions with the Islamic Republic of Iran... we will not be 
attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections" 
Iran shrugged it off (which maybe why oil didn't actually end lower) as officials say Iranian 
leaders are deeply suspicious of Trump's statements. The officials said Iranian leaders want to 
avoid a third surprise attack, reports Axios. 
While such attacks can slow or even temporarily halt some transits from the gulf, the bigger 
problem is the perilously low global oil supply. 
Simply put, the cushion for more pain - global reserves - are well down from pre-war levels. 
"The supply resilience cushion is scarily thin," Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser said.  
That can have much broader repercussions, including slower economic growth and higher 
inflation, he added. 
"The longer the disruption continues, the risk of this happening only grows," he added. 
But, that's not all. A hurricane in the Gulf of America has forced some US producers to halt 
production, adding to the upward pressure on prices.  
“With depleted inventories supporting the case for sustained replenishment demand, the 
long-term direction of oil prices will remain tilted to the upside,” said Fawad Razaqzada, a 
market analyst at Forex.com.  
“In the short-term, it would take some positive developments towards a potential US-Iran deal to 
bring some relief.” 
The result of all that is oil prices up over 4% (WTI Crude futures) on the day... 

 
 
Refined product prices are on the march higher again... 
 
...with diesel prices, a key fuel for industries, surging this week... 

 
 
The impact on the refined product that helps power agricultural harvests and ground transport 
for goods globally is set to go on feeding into inflation readings and expectations for some time 
to come. The severe dislocations to global shipping that are driving tanker rates to records will 
add to such concerns (shipping oil from the Middle East to China cost $1.4 million a day on 
Wednesday, the most on record). 
“The recent months have been have been the best of all time for crude tankers,” said Georgios 
Sakellariou, a freight analyst at Signal Ocean. “The major issue is that the ship-to-ship system 
outside Hormuz is inefficient and that’s really stretching vessel supply there and elsewhere.” 

 
 
The resultant inflationary pressures add to the potential for benchmark government bond yields 
to ratchet higher... 
Rates 
But, today, yields ended lower with the long-end outperforming... 

 
 
Bond yields tested up to yesterday's highs overnight as oil prices spiked, but a wave of bond 
buying hit as oil dipped... 
“There was a lot of rumbling about potential pre-midterm strikes, so this takes some pressure off 
near-term oil,” said Izaac Brook, US rates strategist at RBC Capital Markets.  
“This is just a near-term removal of Iran escalation tail risk.” 
...and then a more aggressive wave of bond buyers returned after the OpenAI report as, at the 
margin, it likely means Treasuries will compete less with AI bubble spend for demand (though 
we can make the case that lower revenues actually means more debt will be required). 
"Washington needs capital. Hyperscalers need capital. Data centres need capital. Power 
generation needs capital," Stephen Innis, managing partner at SPI Asset Management and a 
global market strategist, wrote in commentary Thursday.  
"If the desired level of investment rises faster than the available pool of savings, the system 
needs a higher return to make the pieces fit." 
The short-end underperformance reflects very little change in rate-hike expectations (even after 
a multi-decade low in initial jobless claims confirmed the 'low-hire, low-fire' economy... 

 
 
The latest long-end Treasury buyback executed a full $6 billion ticket on 20-30 year 
repurchases, with a little under $15 billion worth of bonds tendered for offer. 
The 30Y auction today was 'mediocre' but that - arguably - is better than many auctions have 
been recently... 
 

 
The 30-year Treasury yield still matters for investors, because it is "the one maturity the [Federal 
Reserve] does not control, and the one the world has to want," said Vincent Ahn, portfolio 
manager at SLW Investments, on Thursday morning. "So, a 30-year auction is the one place 
where the question is not what price, but who shows up," Ahn said. 
“Demand held up,” said Molly Brooks, rates strategist at TD Securities.  
“It’s a good sign for those that want to dip their toes back in the long end.” 
And, as we detailed earlier, the long-bond appears 'exhausted'... 
 
...lower yields, higher Small Caps (most rate-sensitive)... 
Stocks 
NDX was the standout biggest loser (DOW was the prettiest horse in the glue factory, 
scrambling back to unch) amid a defensive tilt from MidEast tensions (and tech bubble fears). 
Today was NDX's worst day since July... 

 
 
Goldman traders reported overall activity levels were around a 4 out of 10, with their flow 
skewed definitely better for sale from both LOs and HFs 
●​ Asset managers are skewed 5% better sellers led by selling in macro products, 
industrials, and hcare vs demand in energy, consumer staples 
●​ HFs are skewed much better sellers led by supply in info tech, macro products, comms 
svcs, consumer staples, with modest demand only in materials 
AI stocks were monkeyhammered today (especially relative to S&P Ex-AI) as OpenAI’s 
annualized revenue is about $20bn less than has been previously signaled, according to 
financial documents shared with investors, a massive gap likely to damp optimism about the 
growth of AI demand. 

 
 
According to Ramp Economics Lab’s data on adoption, OpenAI has been losing its edge to 
Anthropic for some time. 
 
So OpenAI’s pain is Anthropic’s gain for now. The end result may still be solid demand for chips, 
regardless of where it comes from... but OpenAI still has hundreds of billions in commitments to 
fulfill to keep this bubble from bursting... 

 
 
And the beneficiaries of that spend - ORCL, for instance, were slammed. NeoClouds and 
Optical Networking were the hardest hit (down 7%!)... 
 
In addition, Goldman's trading desk noted three other factors contributing to semis / AI 
weakness under the hood: 
1) underwhelming prints vs. high expectations from Samsung & TSM,  

 
2) reports of major AI players plans to further tap debt markets (ORCL, AVGO, SCPX),  
3) lack of updates on China optical ban weighing on US optical (COHR, LITE, GLW).    
 
Energy was the outperformer (as oil jumped) but Staples were bid while Tech was the worst 
sector on the day... 
 
And lastly, before we leave equity-land, until today, we have had notably low realized vol... 

 
 
...which matters because it allows systematic strategies such as vol-control and risk-parity funds 
to maintain or increase equity exposure, creating a steady source of demand beneath the 
market. 
That can reinforce the rally by drawing in CTAs and discretionary strategies more sensitive to 
directional momentum and trend, and SPX Gamma remains very thick around 7750-7800, 
according to SpotGamma... 

 
 
The danger is that if realized volatility finally jumps (like it did today), those same strategies can 
flip from buyers to forced sellers quickly. 
Everything Else 
The dollar whipsawed quite violently intraday but ended basically unchanged... 

 
 
Gold was up modestly... 
 
Gold/Silver surged back up to 70x - its highest in two months... 

 
 
Bitcoin tumbled today, testing down towards $80,000 again... 
 
Finally, while bond vol remains elevated and economic policy uncertainty is soaring... 

 
 
...equity risk expectations remain muted... for now. 
As Goldman's Rich Privorotsky noted earlier, AAII Bull/bear sentiment is back to neutral, so we 
no longer have as much of the extreme negative sentiment tailwind we enjoyed previously... 
 
Today saw RTY notably outperform NDX (after underperforming in 17 of the last 20 sessions)... 

 
 
Everything else remains in the doldrums, with high oil and high real rates an increasingly difficult 
combination for the consumer, small businesses, and leveraged balance sheets. 
The juxtaposition remains pretty remarkable... the AI micro continues to accelerate while the 
macro backdrop gets progressively more difficult.  
Strong earnings simply aren’t enough to offset the pressure from rates, energy and capital 
supply. 
...and today's OpenAI reports may just have given us a look behind Altman's curtain on the AI 
micro.
