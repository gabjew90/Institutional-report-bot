The asymmetry changed
Back in late August, the AI setup was unusually clean: positioning was light, implied vol was low and
macro uncertainty was pushing capital toward the few parts of the market still delivering growth.
That setup has changed.
GS points out that since August 27, the S&P 500 is up 1.3%, while the S&P excluding the AI names is
down more than 5%.
The gap between the two is now close to the widest since the AI trade really began in early 2023.
AI hasn't just been leading the market. It has been holding the index together.
GS
Everyone found the same shelter
There is another important difference versus six weeks ago: positioning.
GS Prime data shows Mag 7 exposure has climbed to around 22% of total US exposure, the highest
level since their data began in 2022.
Tech was also the only US sector net bought by hedge funds in September, with the largest monthly
buying since February 2025.
The AI story may still be working. It just isn't the lonely trade anymore.
GS
The macro vs AI disconnect
The real tension is between AI and the macro.
Rates have repriced sharply, oil remains elevated and large parts of the real economy have already
taken the hit.
The mega-cap AI complex largely hasn't.
That leaves two very different paths from here. Either the macro becomes friendlier and the market
broadens out, or rates and oil stay difficult and eventually start biting the parts of the market that have
so far been immune.
Something has to give.

The crowding is local, not global
While positioning inside AI and mega-cap tech has increased sharply, overall equity positioning
remains remarkably light.
GS has US fundamental long/short net exposure sitting in just the 2nd percentile over the past five
years.
So the distinction matters:
Crowded in the winners. Under-positioned in the market.
That should continue to provide some cushion on meaningful dips.
GS
The next right tail
The next leg higher may need to look very different from the last one.
GS argues that a friendlier macro backdrop would likely unlock the biggest upside in the areas that
have been left behind rather than in the AI winners that already sit near the highs.
Housing, the middle-income consumer and other macro-sensitive pockets have seen both positioning
and multiples crushed.
In other words, lower rates may not simply mean more AI. They could mean breadth.
GS
Protection went on sale
The macro backdrop has become more difficult, leadership is more concentrated and positioning in
the winners is much fuller than it was six weeks ago.
And yet equity vol has collapsed.
For investors who still want to stay long, that changes the equation. You don't necessarily need to sell
the upside when protection has suddenly become much cheaper.
The long trade got harder. The hedge got cheaper.

Stay long, differently
None of this says the equity bull case is dead.
Overall positioning remains light, dips should still find buyers and any relief in rates or oil could unlock
a powerful broadening trade.
But the asymmetry has changed.
Six weeks ago, the opportunity was owning AI while positioning was clean.
Today, it may be staying long while protection is cheap.
