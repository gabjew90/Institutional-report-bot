GS TMT SPEC SALES – Friday 9th October
AI equities sold off after the FT reported that OpenAI’s annualised revenue was around $50bn at the end
of September, below the widely cited $68–70bn figure. The gap appears to reflect reporting differences
rather than weaker demand: OpenAI reports revenue net of cloud-partner sales, while Anthropic reports
closer to gross revenue, meaning OpenAI’s underlying progression was nearer $30bn to $50bn than $40bn
to $70bn—still roughly 70% growth, but below elevated expectations.
Press now suggesting that OpenAI still targets around $70bn of annualised revenue by year-end, but
Nvidia, Microsoft, Oracle and CoreWeave remain sensitive to further clarification, while Oracle and
Broadcom credit spreads are important indicators of concern over debt-funded AI capex. The headline also
triggered broad selling in US large-cap technology, including Meta, Microsoft, Oracle, Micron, Sandisk
and Nvidia; greater disclosure from publicly listed frontier AI companies would make revenue
comparisons easier and reduce this uncertainty.
AI credit back in focus Oracle’s five-year CDS closed at a record 261bp (its up 36% on the month) on
Thursday as nearly $500bn of 2026 AI-related borrowing (Oracle, Broadcom, SpaceX among others) is
being repriced; Broadcom’s five-year CDS printed a record 136bp (Bloomberg via Mint / ICE).
US telecom stocks sold off after SpaceX (watch for weakness in Europe esp. DT) agreed to acquire a
nationwide portfolio of low-band 800 MHz spectrum from Grain Management, including up to 14 MHz of
paired spectrum previously bought from T-Mobile. The price has not been officially disclosed, although
reports have suggested around $8bn, and the deal remains subject to FCC approval. SpaceX said the
spectrum, which offers stronger range and building penetration than its existing mid-band holdings, is the
final component needed to expand Starlink’s direct-to-device mobile coverage across the US. Not really
sure whether these is simply a play to extract MVNO terms or whether SpaceX will now turn up at the up
coming auctions (its need more spectrum) either way today will be sell first and US names given the price
of mobile are far more exposed
The announcement sent AT&T, T-Mobile US and Verizon down roughly 6–7% in post-market trading. The
spectrum could support a hybrid satellite and terrestrial service on existing smartphones, particularly in
rural areas, weak-coverage locations and bundled Starlink offerings; however, becoming a full national
mobile carrier would still require substantial terrestrial infrastructure, capital investment and regulatory
support, especially in dense urban markets where satellite capacity and latency remain constraints. The
deal therefore strengthens SpaceX’s competitive position but does not represent an immediate replacement

for the incumbent US wireless networks. Do note that US Towerco were up – so will be interesting to see
if Cellnex holds – thesis seem to be SpaceX will need also to build a terrestrial network to infill the
satellite coverage
Rightmove shares jumped on a low-quality Betaville report repeating unverified takeover speculation
involving “either a Gulf-backed trade buyer or REA Group”, although Betaville labelled the story

Betaville report was low quality and there are no talks and would expect the stock to fade today

TECH
• OpenAI Revenue Clarification – Gross vs Net = Investor confusion. Interesting reaction last night to the
FT report that OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue run-rate was closer to ~$50bn at the end of
September, versus the ~$70bn figure that had been circulating. In theory most should know the difference
BUT these names are not listed and many do not adjust. AI names sold off hard on Thursday (Oracle –5-
6%, Nvidia/AMD –3%+, CoreWeave lower, Microsoft weaker). This was not a growth collapse —
OpenAI is still growing very rapidly (enterprise more than doubled since July). It was a reset of

expectations after many had priced in the higher, adjusted number. Overnight, futures have stabilized and
there has been no further negative revision. Bottom line - The underlying demand signal remains strong.
The episode highlights how sensitive the complex has become to the precise trajectory of two private
companies that use different reporting conventions. The overnight clarification that OpenAI itself is
guiding to ~$70bn by year-end helps re-anchor expectations. Until both labs are public (or adopt fully
consistent metrics), this type of apples-to-oranges volatility is likely to persist at the margin.
• Gross vs net – the source of the gap: Anthropic reports closer to gross revenue (full customer spend
through cloud partners, partner cut booked as a cost). OpenAI has reported closer to net (mainly its own
share). When some investors tried to put the two on a like-for-like basis by grossing up OpenAI’s figures,
they produced the higher ~$40bn (August) and then ~$70bn numbers. OpenAI’s own investor update used
its more conservative framing and landed near $50bn at end-September.
• The distinction has been out there for sometime but these are not listed entities — OpenAI had flagged it
internally as early as April, and it had been covered in the press. However, many still treated the
circulating run-rate numbers as roughly comparable, which is why the grossed-up figures gained traction
and why the clarification produced a material one-day reaction. For context Gerstner’s mid-September
framework - Brad Gerstner noted the top three labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceX/xAI) were at a combined
~$100bn run-rate in July and needed to reach $180–$200bn by year-end to keep the AI trade intact.
Importantly, he did not adjust all three onto a consistent gross basis. He used the circulating reported
figures as they stood: Anthropic on gross, OpenAI closer to net, and SpaceX on a standard (closer to
gross) basis for its compute hosting contracts. The later OpenAI gross-up exercise was therefore
overlaying consistency onto a framework that had never been fully consistent….

• Palantir: GIR has upgraded Palantir to Buy as investor sentiment improves and the stock increasingly
features in the “inference economy” debate. Despite gaining 9% YTD, Palantir has lagged high-growth
software peers and trades at a discount on EV/FCF/growth. The upgrade argues that its addressable market
could expand through sovereign AI, bespoke applications and industry-specific products, while its
differentiated Forward Deployed Engineer model and use of AI FDEs should help sustain its competitive
advantage.
• TietoEVRY: Updating our estimates ahead of 3Q26 results; Mo has become incrementally more cautious
on near-term revenue trends, reflecting continued macro-driven demand weakness across most business
segments. GIR reduced their FY26 organic revenue growth forecast to around -5% year-on-year, slightly
below company guidance of -3% to -5%, citing ongoing softness in Tech Consulting, legacy contract run-
offs in Banktech and Caretech, and emerging weakness in Indtech. Despite the weaker top-line outlook,
GIR are not trimming margins as cost optimisation initiatives continue to support earnings.
• Indra Q3 expectations: We expect another solid quarter, with underlying FY26–30 revenue growth,
margins and EPS broadly unchanged despite FX updates. Investor focus will remain on Defence, where

revenue reached almost €1bn in 1H26 and should maintain similar momentum in 2H26, supported by a
€11bn-plus backlog, Special Modernisation Programmes and potential new contract awards. Attention will
also centre on the outlook for ATM, Mobility, Space and Minsait, margin and free-cash-flow trends, capex,
M&A integration and any indications ahead of the 27 November Capital Markets Day, where Indra is
expected to outline how it can achieve its €10bn revenue target before 2030. We remain constructive on
the multi-year European defence opportunity and retain our Buy

TELCOS
• UK mobile loyalty: A Lebara-commissioned Opinium survey suggests customer inertia could cost UK
consumers up to £9bn a year, with 56% staying with their mobile provider despite only 9% feeling
strongly rewarded; 31% have remained with the same network for more than five years and 12% have
never switched. The £9bn estimate should be treated cautiously because it is based on potential savings
from moving to Lebara and excludes factors such as coverage and service quality, but the findings suggest
AI shopping agents could make comparisons easier and increase pressure on operators.
