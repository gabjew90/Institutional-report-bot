back from America 
 
A few quick observations from an intense client trip on the US East Coast this week. 
1. What a narrative turn. AI is disinflationary and the US Treasury will not allow bonds to sell-off was the 
story this time last year. AI (issuance) is a big driver of higher yields and the US Treasury has lost control of 
the long-end is the narrative this year. There were mumblings of a coming suspension in 20-year treasury 
issuance and a sharp maturity shortening in supply but most clients were very cautious on fixed income. This 
author could not resist the conclusion that the narrative pendulum has swung a little too much “the other 
way”. 
2. French questions. As can be expected the bias was overwhelmingly bearish, along the lines of Paul 
Krugman overnight. We explained why we disagreed with parallels of 2010-2015 but nonetheless it is 
clear it will take time for the market to regain confidence given the huge dislocations last week. This is a 
fresh weight on the EUR we were not expecting to materialize this year. 
3. The real risk. The discussion on AI was surprisingly nuanced. This author participated in a conference 
panel with data center experts all of which expressed concerns around slipping underwriting standards, the 
availability of energy supply to meet construction demand, and whether the anticipated surge in inference 
capacity can be met with enough demand and revenues. This Brookings paper estimating that AI labs need to 
generate close to $4trillion of revenue to earn a return on the data center investments provides useful 
background reading. 
Our main message in client meetings was the following: the biggest systemic risk in the market next year is 
not France but “something going wrong” in the AI ecosystem: a safety event, a failed IPO, or disappointing 
revenues. Concentration risk is immense, and it is this dollar negative (and very bond positive) event risk that 
is most under-priced in markets at the moment.
