The gold problem
The gold bull case is becoming remarkably simple:
Central banks keep buying.
Maybe they do. But with the dollar rising, gold breaking down and traditional macro tailwinds fading,
the question is getting more interesting:
Who is the next buyer?
Massive levels
Gold is flirting with the huge trend line. The shiny metal is well below the 200-day MA and continues to
struggle.
A close below the trend line, and the big $4,000 level comes into play.

Who is the next buyer?
The structural gold bull case is becoming increasingly dependent on one thing: central banks
continuing to buy.
GS expects gold to rise 23% through end-2027. Remarkably, nearly all of that upside is attributed to
central-bank purchases.
That's a powerful flow while it lasts.
But it also means the bull case increasingly requires someone to keep paying a higher price.
GS
The new normal?
Central-bank gold purchases are currently running at more than 5x the pre-2022 monthly average.
GS assumes purchases average 60 tonnes/month through 2026-27. Maybe they do.
But at some point, extraordinary demand becomes the assumption rather than the catalyst.
GS
Reflexivity works both ways
Gold call positioning remains around three times historical averages. GS argues that this can
mechanically amplify further upside through dealer hedging.
Fair enough.
But that's increasingly a flow-driven market: central banks buy, calls stay elevated, dealers hedge and
price rises.
Reflexivity is wonderful until the underlying flow changes.

GS
Some are getting nervous
Gold skew is moving sharply higher as investors increasingly pay up for downside protection.
The bull story remains intact. The confidence around the path clearly doesn't.
GS
Interesting agreement
The bulls and bears may actually agree on the next important level.
GS's hawkish scenario takes gold toward $4,070. UBS says pullbacks toward $4,000 should be used to
add.
$4,000 is quickly becoming the line in the sand.
