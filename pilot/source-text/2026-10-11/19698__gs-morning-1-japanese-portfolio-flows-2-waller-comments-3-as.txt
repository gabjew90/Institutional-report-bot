1) FX RESEARCH (FISHMAN) – Japanese Portfolio Flows: Still No Signs of Rotation Among Unhedged
Investors
BOTTOM LINE: We recently turned structurally bullish Yen as the domestic policies that have weighed
on the currency for much of the past 10-15 years appear to be shifting in a more supportive direction. The
BoJ has seemingly shifted to a faster pace of hikes and, most importantly, there appears to be a focus
within the government on encouraging some reallocation by domestic investors back towards domestic
assets. As a result, investor attention has turned to the monthly International Transactions in Securities
(ITS) data to watch for any signs of reduced demand for foreign assets by key investors. We have been of
the view that any shift in flows would likely be a slower-moving process, and so far that looks true among
mostly unhedged investors.
International Transactions in Securities (ITS) Data: The trust accounts category (which includes the public
pension fund) suggests net purchases of foreign bonds continued in September but at a slower pace than in
August ($7.6bn vs $14.7bn) and below the six-month average ($8.6bn). In contrast, investment trusts
(retail/NISA) net purchases of foreign equities accelerated slightly to $8.7bn (vs $8.5bn in August). Our
preferred estimate of key unhedged investor flows shows demand for foreign assets has generally persisted
at a similar pace over the past year with no obvious signs of rotation - but if that were to change, these data
would be the first place to see it.
Hedged Investors Selling Foreign Bonds: While less relevant for the Yen, repatriation flows have been
evident among typically hedged investors (mainly banks and roughly 40% of Lifer flows). Net sales of
foreign bonds by banks accelerated from $8.3bn in August to $15.9bn in September. Lifers also net sold
slightly more foreign bonds ($1.9bn vs $0.9bn). The trend in total 12-month flows looks increasingly
negative - similar to the period of rising global yields in 2022. These data predate the sudden volatility in
the European bond market over the past week, and France represents the biggest share of Japan's foreign
debt holdings after the US.

2) Waller Comments & Thoughts on the USD

David Mericle (US Economics): Fed Governor Christopher Waller suggested that he likely expects two
additional rate hikes to be appropriate. He said that “If the economic data continue to come in as expected,
I anticipate additional hikes to support a timelier return of inflation to our 2 percent goal. But there is some
flexibility about when those hikes will occur. The hikes do not need to come at consecutive meetings, but
they should be in place in an acceptable period of time.” Waller said that the lack of a resolution to the
Middle East conflict, signs that the artificial intelligence buildout is raising consumer prices for
technology goods, and the risk of new tariffs had undermined his faith that inflation is progressing toward
the 2% target. We continue to expect one additional 25bp rate hike in December and have lowered our
probability that the FOMC could pass on a December hike because Waller’s speech represents a hawkish
shift relative to his emphasis in previous comments on the three-month annualized rate of core PCE
inflation, which is now just 2.0%.
Stuart Jenkins (FX Strategy) on our Morning Call: “Waller is one of those on the committee that is giving
the most open forward guidance. His shift to flagging ‘multiple’ hikes (energy plus broadening AI-driven
pressures) is important, however he did at the same time push against the idea of consecutive hikes and
that is consistent with our Econ’s call for a hold in October and a hike in December… The USD rally has
been built on three legs – rate differentials, relative equity/tech outperformance, and Brent back above
$100 – but only energy and AI look structural; Fed pricing is the shaky one, and we're the most dovish in
G10 (vs mkt pricing) there alongside the BoE. All three drivers have been mean-reverting this year so we
think EUR may have run a little ahead of fundamentals in the near term and USDJPY for example, has
structural downside asymmetry, so it's a bit early to press USD strength here… Overall, yes, you have
some of those fundamental supports, but it is a fairly mean reverting environment and I think it's also very
difficult to rule out some of the USD negative risks that we saw throughout the year on the fiscal front, on
the policy uncertainty front….”

3) ASIA PORTFOLIO STRATEGY (MOE) - Investing in a Differentiated Asia
BOTTOM LINE: We remain positive on the region with a focus on tech-driven earnings and alpha
opportunities. After 15 years of sub-par growth, regional earnings will grow 34% from 2026-2028,
propelled largely by tech, with info tech expected to grow at a 20% CAGR over the next 5 years on AI-
driven compute demand. We revise our 10-year MXAPJ annual earnings growth forecast up 1pp to 10%.
We estimate 16% valuation upside for hard tech, 3% for non-tech, and 10% for MXAPJ as a whole to
11.3x P/E. We expect 26% 12m USD price return and 14% 10yr annual average USD total return, with a
focus on tech-driven North Asia and selected diversification. Key themes: AI, power, non-tech earnings
compounders, and shareholder return.
Composition Shift: Taiwan is now the largest MXAPJ market at 29% index weight and Korea second at
22% - together half the regional index. China's weight has fallen from a 40%+ peak in late 2020 to 19%,
and India from 19% to 10%. Info tech now accounts for 43% of index cap, having displaced financials.

The top 10 stocks comprise a record 41% of regional cap (4.0 s.d. above the long-term mean of ~24%),
with TSMC, Samsung Electronics, and SK Hynix alone at 29%.
