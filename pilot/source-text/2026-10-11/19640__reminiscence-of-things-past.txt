Reminiscence of things past
The S&P 500 keeps making new highs, but most stocks are going nowhere. Market breadth is
deteriorating, concentration is hitting historic extremes, and valuations are approaching dot-com
levels. Even Juniper Networks and Robert Shiller are making a cameo. Nine charts on a market
increasingly reminiscent of 1999.
Breadth
New highs for the S&P 500, but not for most stocks. More than 75% of index constituents are at least
10% below their 52-week highs, while 37% are down more than 20%. Some bull market.

Concentration
How can the index keep making new highs when most of its constituents are in correction territory?
Simple: concentration. The five largest stocks now carry as much market capitalization as the bottom
434 combined. Never before has so much depended on so few.
Compound
Only getting wider
The Nasdaq 100 keeps pushing higher, while the equal-weighted Value Line index has been rolling
over since August. The gap between the winners and everyone else keeps widening.

Variant Perception
Falling, not rising
And it is not just an equal-weight phenomenon. The cumulative advance-decline line has plunged back
toward its spring lows, even as the S&P 500 holds near record highs. Fewer and fewer stocks are doing
the heavy lifting.
Variant Perception
AI vs ‘the rest’
Strip out the AI trade and the market looks very different. The gap between the S&P 500 and its ex-AI
counterpart has widened sharply. Increasingly, this is less a broad bull market than a bet on one
theme.
And if a market dominated by one technology theme sounds familiar, the next two charts should bring
back some memories.
GS trading desk
The “Juniper Networks” analog
Remember Juniper Networks? During the dot-com bubble, Cisco peaked in March 2000, but Juniper
continued rallying until October as speculative capital rotated into the next generation of technology
winners.
Fast-forward to today. Nvidia continues to hold up, while second-tier AI names have already suffered
substantial drawdowns. CoreWeave is roughly 50% below its 2025 high, while SpaceX is around 25%

below its post-IPO peak. Different companies, different technologies, but a potentially familiar late-
cycle pattern.

AI Big 10 concentration hits the bubble line
Every great bubble has its concentration moment. The AI Big 10 now account for 42% of the market,
right alongside the Nifty Fifty, Japan and the dot-com bubble at their respective peaks. Only the railroad
mania managed to go materially further. So yes, there is still upside precedent — if you find comfort in
railroad bubbles.
And concentration is not the only metric flashing 1999.

Reminiscence of Shiller's 1999
At 41.8x, the Shiller PE is approaching its November 1999 peak of 44.2x. But valuations are notoriously
poor timing tools, particularly when animal spirits take over.
The similarities with 1999 are striking. The inconvenient part is that nobody knows which
month of 1999 we are in.
