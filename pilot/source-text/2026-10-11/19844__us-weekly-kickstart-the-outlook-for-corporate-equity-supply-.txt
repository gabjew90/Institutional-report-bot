Updating our outlook for US equity issuance  
 
 
The pace of US equity issuance slowed sharply in Q3 following a record quarter in 
Q2. US corporates raised $93 billion of equity in Q3. This issuance reflected growth of 
20% on a year/year basis but a steep decline from the record $252 billion of equity 
issuance in Q2. Follow-ons represented the bulk of issuance in Q3, totaling $45 billion, 
and IPOs raised just $7 billion. While half of the Q2 dollar issuance was concentrated in 
just two deals, the number of offerings also slowed materially in Q3 relative to Q2. 
September’s total of 19 follow-ons and 5 IPOs registered the lowest monthly counts in 
each category since the tariff shock in April 2025. 
Despite the sequential slowdown in issuance during Q3, IPO and follow-on proceeds 
in 2026 are on track to register the largest total since the boom of 2021. Year to date, 
equity issuance proceeds have totaled roughly $430 billion, up 98% year/year and in line 
with the amount companies raised in the first three quarters of 2021. However, dollar 
volumes this year have been boosted by a handful of large deals. The number of 
offerings so far in 2026 indicates less of an issuance boom than a return to normal 
following four years of muted activity. 
 
Macro volatility, including the sharp recent increase in interest rates, has 
contributed to the slowdown in equity issuance. 10-year US Treasury yields have risen 
by about 125 bp during the past year and more than 50 bp since late August, with real 
yields driving the bulk of those increases. These equate to moves of roughly 2 standard 
deviations relative to the past 20 years. Sharp increases in interest rates usually pose a 
challenge for equity prices and create similar headwinds for equity issuance activity.

Looking forward, we expect $600 billion of primary corporate equity issuance in 
2027. The macro backdrop should support continued issuance in 2027 as economic 
growth remains solid and interest rates retreat from current levels. On the other side of 
the ledger, we expect corporate equity demand via buybacks and cash M&A will exceed 
$2 trillion. Including expiring lockups, net US public equity supply will likely increase next 
year by the largest amount since 2000. However, it is unrealistic to assume that all 
unlocked shares will be immediately sold. In addition, we expect that much of the liquid 
wealth created by the sale of unlocked shares will eventually result in additional equity 
demand. We exclude equity issuance via stock-based compensation from our analysis.  
We do not expect additional equity supply to derail the bull market in the near 
future. The shifting balance between corporate equity supply and demand adds to the 
various macro and micro factors pressuring equity valuations. However, Federal Reserve 
data show US household equity purchases exceeding $800 billion in H1 2026, with 
non-US investors adding more than $350 billion in additional demand. Our outlook for 
continued earnings growth alongside declining yields should support continued investor 
demand for equities.
