Breaking in bonds
The US 10-year is breaking higher, and 5.5% may be just the beginning.
Bond vol remains stressed, oil is pushing higher and the AI boom is creating an unprecedented
demand for capital.
Meanwhile, hyperscaler credit spreads are tracking yields higher, while semiconductor vol remains
surprisingly subdued.
The question is no longer just how high rates can go.
It's what starts breaking if they keep rising.
The upside vacuum in yields
The US 10-year yield continues to push higher.
The breakout from the multi-year range has opened up a massive upside vacuum, with little obvious
resistance overhead.
Depending on how the range is drawn, the medium-term technical setup suggests room for yields to
move considerably higher.
The upper trend line connecting several major highs comes in around 5.75–5.80%.

The great capital squeeze
Privorotsky argues that a much bigger problem is developing beneath the surface.
Post-GFC, the world operated on abundant savings and structurally low rates. Governments built their
finances around the assumption that cheap capital would last forever.
That world is changing.
Sovereign financing needs are enormous, while AI is creating unprecedented demand for capital.
SpaceX reportedly seeking $40bn and Broadcom exploring more than $50bn in financing only add to
the pressure.
The potential crowding-out effect is massive.
If AI can generate 20–30% IRRs, borrowing at high single-digit rates makes perfect sense. But what
happens to everyone else competing for that capital?
This may explain why real yields refuse to fall. Why buy sovereign duration when so much high-quality
private-sector paper is coming to market?
Privorotsky sees two outcomes: AI becomes increasingly self-financing, or capital gets rationed
elsewhere and weaker parts of the economy start to crack.
CCC spreads may already be flashing an early warning.
Blame AI
The US 10-year yield has been moving remarkably closely with selected hyperscaler CDS spreads.
The same AI boom driving equity enthusiasm may also be contributing to rising financing costs and
credit stress.
An increasingly important cross-asset relationship to watch.

The semis vol disconnect
This isn't arbitrage, but we find the divergence between rates/hyperscaler CDS spreads and
semiconductor volatility intriguing.
With stress building in rates and credit, semis vol still looks relatively cheap.
Hedging potential spillover risks through semis volatility makes sense to us.
