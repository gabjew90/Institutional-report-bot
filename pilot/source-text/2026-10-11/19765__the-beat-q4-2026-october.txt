Rates Are Up. Growth Is Strong. Stay Constructive.
The views and opinions expressed are those of the Portfolio Solutions Group at the time of writing of this presentation and are subject to change at any time due to market, economic 
or other conditions, and may not necessarily come to pass. Not to be construed as an investment or research recommendation.
TOP VIEWS
Equities: Earnings over rates. Healthy earnings and 
hyperscaler CapEx (capital expenditure) are broadening 
growth into less rate-sensitive parts of the market.
Broadening Beyond Tech: Financials, managed care 
and selected cyclicals remain attractive, with improving 
opportunities in Europe and Japan, mainly in financials.
Fixed Income: Manage duration, embrace credit. Rising 
yields hurt passive duration, but growth-driven rates can 
keep corporate fundamentals strong and spreads tight.
Policy Still Supports Risk: Fiscal spending, investment 
incentives and regulatory easing remain important 
tailwinds for nominal growth and risk assets.
Yields are rising largely because nominal growth remains strong, supporting earnings, cash flows, employment, 
consumption and credit quality. Central banks are removing accommodation, not imposing outright restraint —
and that distinction matters. Geopolitical, energy and inflation risks can turn “good” yield increases into bad 
ones, but for now, the balance remains constructive. As the forces driving yields higher begin to fade, we expect 
the bull market in risk assets to reassert itself.
Dollar Stability Matters: A broadly resilient U.S. dollar 
(USD)—and efforts to stabilize the yen—help contain a 
potentially disruptive source of global market volatility.
Energy Is the Geopolitical Fault Line: Tight refined-
product markets, particularly diesel, create a direct 
transmission channel from disruption to inflation.
Inflation Risk Changes Everything: If inflation becomes 
uncontained, central banks must move from removing 
accommodation to actively destroying demand.
Emerging Market (EM) External Debt — Carry, Credit 
and Selectivity: Attractive all-in yields and resilient 
sovereign fundamentals support hard-currency debt, but 
tight spreads and higher U.S. rates make country 
selection and duration management critical.

COMMENTARY
Bonds
Duration
DURATION (U/W): Following the collapse of the Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) and subsequent 
rebound in oil prices, U.S. Treasury (UST) 10-year yields have risen back to ~5%, marking their highest level 
since late 2023. At current levels, we remain slightly U/W duration as nominal GDP growth remains strong and 
well above policy rates.
Credit
CREDIT (U/W): With corporate credit spreads sitting near all-time tights, we continue to feel the risk/reward 
profile is suboptimal and prefer selective exposure to higher yielding credit, namely MBS and Bank Loans.
Equities
Risk Level
EQUITIES (O/W): We move O/W equities. A higher nominal growth environment represents a favorable backdrop 
for equities, while structural tailwinds linked to AI also remain supportive of global growth and corporate earnings. 
Alternatives
Commodities
COMMODITIES (Neutral): We remain neutral on commodities overall. The war in Iran has driven energy prices 
higher, but any resolution would likely cause a sharp reversal. If supply remains disrupted, we expect to see the 
most stress in refined oil products. Industrial metals have benefited from strong global growth, but are most at risk 
if higher energy prices disrupt the economic cycle. Precious metals could face headwinds from the recent hawkish 
shift from the Fed, especially if it also drives USD appreciation.

FIXED INCOME
COMMENTARY
Bonds
U.S. Treasuries
USTs (U/W): With energy prices still elevated and growth data resilient, UST 10-year yields have risen back to 
~5%, marking their highest level since late 2023. At current levels, we remain slightly U/W duration as nominal 
GDP growth remains strong and well above policy rates.
Inflation-Linked Bonds
Inflation-Linked Bonds (O/W): Inflation breakevens have risen again following the collapse of the MOU and 
subsequent rebound in oil prices. We continue to see some value in this space, particularly on the longer sections 
of the curve should inflation remain sticky for the remainder of 2026, into 2027. 
Eurozone Govt. Bonds
Eurozone Govt. Bonds (U/W): We remain U/W duration in the eurozone, as the current energy crisis stokes 
renewed inflationary pressures. We remain O/W Spain and Portugal vs. France and Germany, on the back of 
more favorable growth and debt dynamics, as well as a lower sensitivity of electricity prices to fossil fuels.
EM Hard Currency Govt. 
Bonds
EM Hard Currency (Neutral): With spreads back to near all-time tights, we believe the risk/reward asymmetry to 
EM Hard Currency bonds is less favorable, particularly when considering that fundamentals across most EMs 
have weakened of late. As such, we move our exposure to the asset class to neutral.
EM Local Currency Govt. 
Bonds
EM Local Currency (O/W): We remain O/W EM Local exposure through an O/W to Brazil bonds. With interest 
rates above 13% and inflation around 4%, we expect the central bank to continue cutting interest rates. Lower 
rates will boost growth, improve the fiscal deficit and drive investor flows, all supporting the local currency. 
Public Credit
Municipal Bonds
Municipal Bonds (O/W): Muni/UST ratios have returned to the higher end of their trading range over the past 
several years. We like the asset class for taxable investors, thus move tactically O/W.
Investment Grade
Investment Grade (U/W): IG has poor risk/reward asymmetry with spreads near all-time tights.
MBS/ABS
MBS/ABS (O/W): ABS remain our highest conviction fixed income O/W. Yield per unit of credit quality remains 
attractive, as the asset class continues to benefit from structural tailwinds. 
High Yield
High Yield (Neutral): We remain neutral on HY with the risk/reward asymmetry unfavorable at current valuations. 
We see more attractive opportunities elsewhere in credit.
Bank Loans
Bank Loans (O/W): We remain O/W Bank Loans given our constructive view on growth and high carry.

EQUITY
COMMENTARY
Regional
U.S.
United States (O/W): We remain O/W U.S. equities given ongoing growth resilience and earnings strength in 
2026. While further escalation in the Middle East remains a key risk, the U.S. is more insulated to energy-related 
disruptions than the rest of the world. Fiscal stimulus from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, deregulation and 
continued AI adoption all remain supportive of growth. 
Eurozone
Europe (U/W): We remain cautious on broad eurozone equities as we see lingering headwinds related to the 
effect of the Iran war on energy prices. We prefer banks and the beneficiaries of Europe’s energy security 
investments, such as utilities and grid equipment providers.
Japan
Japan (Neutral): Japanese equities are underpinned by a strong AI CapEx tailwind, fiscal stimulus, corporate 
governance reforms, domestic reflation and BoJ monetary policy normalization. We are O/W Japanese banks as 
main beneficiaries of the rate hike cycle.
Emerging Markets
Emerging Markets (Neutral): Divergence remains a key theme. Headline equity valuations appear undemanding 
while earnings revisions are strong, mainly led by AI-beneficiaries Taiwan and Korea. Meanwhile, weak domestic 
momentum in China and restrained policy stimulus present downside risks, while India is navigating a cyclical soft 
patch amid AI and energy related headwinds.
Style
Growth vs. Value
Growth vs. Value (Neutral): We see select opportunities in both Growth and Value indices, with preferences for 
both tech/AI exposure and global banks as examples. However, we do not have a strong preference for one factor 
over the other and maintain a neutral stance on Growth vs. Value. 
Large-Cap vs. Small-Cap
Large-Cap vs. Small-Cap (U/W): We maintain our tactical overweight to U.S. SMID exposure based on a view 
that growth will continue to broaden out beyond a narrow set of companies, alongside strength in domestic 
industrial activity.
Cyclical vs. Defensive Sectors
Cyclicals (O/W): We remain O/W Cyclicals relative to Defensives. Our Cyclical exposure in the U.S. and Europe 
rests mainly on structural drivers from policy efforts.

About Risk
Bank Loans – There can be no assurance that the liquidation of collateral securing an investment will satisfy 
the issuer’s obligation in the event of non-payment or that collateral can be readily liquidated. The ability to 
realize the benefits of any collateral may be delayed or limited. Commodities – The value of commodities 
investments will generally be affected by overall market movements and factors specific to a particular industry 
or commodity including weather, embargoes, tariffs, or health, political, international and regulatory 
developments. Credit – Investments in income securities may be affected by changes in the creditworthiness 
of the issuer and are subject to the risk of non-payment of principal and interest. The value of income securities 
also may decline because of real or perceived concerns about the issuer’s ability to make principal and interest 
payments. Duration – Securities with longer durations tend to be more sensitive to interest rate changes than 
securities with shorter durations. Equity – Equity investment values are sensitive to stock market volatility. 
Foreign – Investments in foreign instruments or currencies can involve greater risk and volatility than U.S. 
investments because of adverse market, economic, political, regulatory, geopolitical, or other conditions. In 
emerging countries, these risks may be more significant. Gov’t Agency – While certain U.S. Government-
sponsored agencies may be chartered or sponsored by acts of Congress, their securities are neither issued 
nor guaranteed by the U.S. Treasury. Income Market – An imbalance in supply and demand in the income 
market may result in valuation uncertainties and greater volatility, less liquidity, widening credit spreads and a 
lack of price transparency in the market. There generally is limited public information about Municipal issuers. 
Inflation-Linked – Interest payments on inflation-linked securities may vary widely and will fluctuate as 
principal and interest are adjusted for inflation. Investments in inflation-linked securities may lose value in the 
event that the actual rate of inflation is different than the rate of the inflation index. Interest Rate – As interest 
rates rise, the value of certain income investments is likely to decline.. Lower-Rated – Investments rated below 
Investment Grade (typically referred to as “junk”) are generally subject to greater price volatility and illiquidity 
than higher rated investments. Maturity – Longer-term bonds typically are more sensitive to interest rate 
changes than shorter-term bonds. Preferred Stocks – When interest rates rise, the value of preferred stocks 
will generally decline. Prepayment - MBS – Mortgage-backed securities are subject to prepayment risk. 
Prepayment - Bank Loan – Bank Loans are subject to prepayment risk. Real Estate – Changes in real estate 
values or economic downturns can have a significant negative effect on issuers in the real estate industry, 
including REITs.
