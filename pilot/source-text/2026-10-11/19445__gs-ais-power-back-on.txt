Tech / AI asymmetry has shifted.
As Shawn Tuteja points out in his recent note (here), macro is more difficult, and the positioning within AI
and large-cap tech no longer seem to be tailwinds. Clients have certainly re-engaged in tech as a result of
macro uncertainty. The chart below shows that net exposure on our prime book to Mag7 as a % of Total
US exposure is near 22%, which is the highest on record since the start of 2022.
Overall net exposure remains low and there are many pockets of the market that have extremely low
positioning and relative cheap multiples.
US Power (GSENEPOW) is a theme where we are seeing renewed interest. Positioning is extremely clean
(15th percentile on a 3yr lookback per our metrics) and midterms could be a clearing event.
The desk like buying our Power Up America (GSENEPOW) basket here.
This is the most underappreciated AI bottleneck. Positioning is low, valuation has normalized and the
fundamentals are intact. (TY Faris Mourad)
The concerns of the midterms and the politicization of Power are starting to fade. As Adam Wijaya (spec
sales) pointed out, there are signs of life for the IPPs. Specialists aren't tilted as negative and are starting to
warm again. Generalist feedback has been moving with headlines – pointing to more appetite.
At this point in time, the desk expects potential incentives to promote increased power supply in the US
through additional spending on the grid’s infrastructure, behind-the-meter solutions, and more.
The key dated catalysts are FERC ruling on the PJM large-load framework (October 12), PJM RBP filings
(link), and the ERCOT data center interconnection audit.
We also expect higher earnings revisions as independent power producers announce long-term
partnerships with mega-cap technology companies: CEG Announces a 20-year nuclear PPA for ~700 MW
in PJM; positive for industry broadly and CEG Announces PPAs with Google for total of ~3.6 GW of
nuclear in PJM; continued positive momentum on contracting.
Another trade idea is Palladium Calls. Given the evolving and fragmented power markets along with the
different political climates across the 50 states, Industrials, IPPs, and Commodities will all react and trade
differently at various times. (TY Justin Wong)
This is important against the backdrop of recent sentiment. The playbook for the last couple of months
generally has been: "power deal announcements likely to be more muted near term with polls / midterms
in focus, but post November and into YE, it could be off to the races". It was clear on the desk there were
more concerns on the headwinds vs tailwinds for the power trade. Headwinds: midterms, rates, diesel,

OEM overcapacity, lack of incremental DC contracts from BTM players, PJM / ERCOT reforms,
OH/TX/PA/NY headlines and NIMBY. Tailwinds: resilient hyperscaler capex, recent moves in PJM power
price, bullish previews into YE for BTM, general tightness / need for power over next couple of years.
(TY Adam Wijaya)
On this data center theme, GIR also published 2x topical notes today:
1/ Data-center outlook with Dell'Oro — ANET, CLS, HPE, CSCO: Data-center capacity is likely to
increase by approximately 200 GW over the next five years, reaching approximately 250 GW by 2030,
with on-site generation reaching roughly 30% of total power deployment over that period. Within
networking, ANET is among the best positioned in scale-across networks, while CLS and HPE likely lead
in scale-up networks; CSCO has shown impressive progress in scale-out.
Data Center Outlook with Dell'Oro
2/ EU data-center economics deep dive — Solaria, PPC, Drax, Fortum, RWE: DC connection points are
currently worth approximately €20 billion for the sector. Alberto/GIR estimates the European Utilities in
coverage currently own approximately 25 GW of connection points to the power grid, a key prerequisite
for data-center development. Based on recent transactions, these assets are worth approximately €20
billion. Alberto simulates a range of conversion rates for these connection points and concludes that
Solaria monetizing its grid access could ultimately add approximately 45% to the share price. PPC, Drax,
Fortum, and RWE also offer meaningful exposure to this theme.
Powering up Europe
Also reflagging our Behind-the-meter power solutions deep dive from 9/23: Michele/GIR raise our
outlook for BTM power generation from 40GW to 67GW by 2030 vs. our previous estimates. Reflecting
this more constructive outlook, we now forecast fuel cells supplying c.12GW of US data center capacity
and c.18GW globally by 2030, equivalent to a cumulative equipment opportunity of c.$35bn and c.$55bn,
respectively. BTM gas-power generation could serve c.28% of US and c.25% of global data center power
demand by 2030, representing a significant increase vs. our prior expectations and reinforcing our view
that all of these technologies will be required to deliver the data center growth we expect. Michele and
