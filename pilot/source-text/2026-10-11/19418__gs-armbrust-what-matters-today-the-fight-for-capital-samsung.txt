1) The Fight for Capital
I think there is a much bigger issue developing here. Post GFC, we lived in a world of abundant savings
and structurally low rates. Governments effectively extrapolated that equilibrium indefinitely, expanding
fiscal deficits and entitlement spending on the assumption that capital would remain plentiful. That world
is changing. Global sovereign financing requirements are enormous, and now we have a technological
boom with capital demands unlike anything we’ve seen before. Yesterday’s report of SpaceX seeking
$40bn, followed by Broadcom reportedly exploring more than $50bn of financing, only compounds the
point. The crowding out effect is potentially immense.
If you believe scarce compute can generate 20–30% compounding IRRs, you will happily borrow at high
single digit rates to build it. But that places enormous strain on everyone else… consumers, small
businesses and ultimately governments whose balance sheets were built for a world of cheap capital. It
may help explain why real yields refuse to come down. Why rush to buy sovereign duration when an
extraordinary amount of high quality private sector paper is coming at you? Credit spreads remain
relatively contained because perversely the all in yield investors are receiving appears relatively attractive.
But extrapolate this forward and you have two outcomes. Either AI generates sufficient organic cash flow
to become increasingly self financing, making the next hyperscaler earnings particularly important, or
capital gets rationed elsewhere and the weaker parts of the economy begin to break. We may already be
seeing the beginnings of that in CCC spreads. (TY Rich Privo)
2) Samsung and TSMC Lower After Numbers
In Tech, the big focus is on 1) Samsung reporting preliminary operating profit slightly below street
(005930 KS traded -3% in Korea, dragging Kospi -260bps … Korea now lower in 6 of 8 sessions), and 2)
TSM -1.5% in pre following “solid” Q3 and September sales number.
Despite the weak stock performances, both demonstrating exceptionally strong underlying demand.
Samsung’s ₩107.4tn operating profit is extraordinary, while TSMC’s Q3 sales are roughly 50% higher
YoY and ahead of expectations. You can point to the weakness elsewhere within Samsung, particularly
smartphones being squeezed by higher memory costs, and argue that expectations were simply too high
for the numbers to matter. But the underlying trend remains incredibly supportive. LG Electronics is
perhaps the more interesting negative read through… earnings disappointing despite revenue growth, with
logistics and input costs an increasing burden. Another reminder that outside AI, the broader corporate
picture is becoming considerably more complicated.

3) Bank Earnings Preview
What’s Most in Focus for Bank EPS? (views from the desk)
• Capital Markets: How much does trading normalize after a strong 2Q26? IB outlooks – was 3Q26 just an
air pocket or a signal of a more prolonged slowdown to come?
• NII: Outlooks given the sharp upward move in rates & more hawkish forward curve. Strong asset growth
and fixed rate asset repricing vs rising deposit costs.
• Deposit Costs: How will banks manage deposit costs given rate hike expectations and elevated deposit
competition? Potential for deposit beta lag to benefit 4Q26 NIMs?
• Loan Growth: Durability in higher rate environment (with the market questioning growth & activity
outlooks elsewhere).
• Expenses: Levers to pull to manage expenses amidst potentially slower near term capital markets
environment vs will better NII (for asset sensitive banks) flow through to bottom line?
• Capital: Outlook for capital deployment given less excess capital, continued strong loan growth, and
higher rates impacting AOCI – potential for more buyback pauses in Regionals (similar to USB) or slower
pace at money centers?
• Credit: Benign heading into prints supportive of potential reserve releases — watching if the move in
rates creates any early stress with focus on Multifamily CRE.
• Consumer: What is the current state of the consumer? How have higher oil prices and rates weighed on
health & spend?
• Agentic AI: What does agentic AI mean for deposits, funding costs, profitability, and customer growth?
Overblown or are banks taking this seriously?

4) Equity Capital Markets in 3Q26
Investors are looking ahead to the upcoming Q3 earnings season and point to the mid-term elections as a
potential clearing event – historically, markets have underperformed heading into mid-terms (median of
down (3.5)% in the month prior to mid-terms since 1974) but outperform post (median of up 6.5% in the 4
months following the elections) which sets up well for ECM heading into yearend.
Global ECM issuance continues to remain resilient. YTD activity of $856bn (+60% YoY) remains at the
highest YTD level excluding 2021 Americas $514bn (+90% YoY), EMEA $113bn (+15% YoY), APAC
$229bn (+40% YoY). Q3 issuance also outpaced 2025 and ended at $271bn (+35% YoY).
Strongest IPO Issuance Since 2021: Global IPO issuance YTD is $280bn (+174% YoY) across 499
offerings – Americas $200bn (+264% YoY), EMEA $17bn (+13% YoY), APAC $62bn (+94% YoY). GS
Leadership: GS remains at the forefront of IPO activity, involved in ~50% of all global IPO volumes YTD
(~56% of Americas IPOs (ex-SPACs) YTD). GS has led the most IPOs YTD globally, acting as lead left
on 35 IPOs.

Primary Issuance: Corporates continue to utilize the equity markets to raise primary capital with north of
50% of primary volumes being used to fund AI capex. Primary issuance YTD stands at $481bn (+118%
YoY) with GS being involved in ~50% of all global primary issuance.
Sponsor Monetizations: Global sponsor ECM activity stands at $122bn (+10% YoY) across 222 offerings,
with sponsors remaining nimble in hitting open windows.
Convertible Issuance: Convertible issuance YTD remains robust and is currently at $200bn across 250
offerings (+65% YoY and on pace for another record year), skewed ~70% to the Americas.
Source: Goldman Sachs FICC & Equities, Marquee, as of 8 Oct 2026.
