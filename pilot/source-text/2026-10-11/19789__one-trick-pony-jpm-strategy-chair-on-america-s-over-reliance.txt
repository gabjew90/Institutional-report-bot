 
"One Trick Pony": JPM Strategy Chair On America's 
(Over)-Reliance On AI 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, OCT 09, 2026 - 07:45 PM 
Anyone concerned about US reliance on AI, look no further... 
In the following charts, Michael Cembalest, Chair of Market and Investment Strategy for 
JPMorgan Asset & Wealth Management, breaks down the so-called 'one-trick-pony' of the 
concentrated contributions of AI to US markets and economic growth... 
(i) a basket of 42 AI stocks accounts for 60%-80% of all S&P earnings growth, price returns and 
capital spending since Jan 2024; 
 
(ii) at the same time, 60% of S&P 500 stocks are down 20% or more from their all-time highs 
despite the market being close to all-time highs… 

 
 
(iii) only 30% of stocks are outperforming the S&P 500, one of the lowest figures since 1991... 
 
(iv) AI is primarily benefitting AI supply chain companies rather than the companies adopting it… 

 
 
(v) the technology share of GDP growth has risen from almost zero to 2022 to ~33%... 
 
(vi) only 20% of US states are growing faster than the national average, almost the lowest figure 
since 1979… 

 
 
(vii) AI accounts for the only positive trend in US construction spending, having risen by 38% 
since January 2024. 
 
Unfortunately the other three main categories are down since then (manufacturing -28%, 
commercial real estate -13% and residential -6%). 

 
 
That’s why the decline in planned data centers is something to watch. 
What about valuations and investor exuberance on AI? 
I would not say tech multiples are cheap, but since January 2024 when the AI boom began to 
take off, technology stocks around the world have mostly been driven by earnings growth rather 
than by P/E multiple expansion... 

 
 
Markets driven by earnings growth are in my experience less susceptible to rapid declines than 
those driven mostly by multiples.  
However, Cembalest notes a sense of foreboding on the SpaceX IPO given the track record of 
other large IPOs since 2010 as lockup expirations approached, but as shown in the table, 
SpaceX is holding up better than most after 116 days. 
 

 
Maybe, it’s too early to judge since only ~20% of SpaceX shares have been released into the 
free float.
