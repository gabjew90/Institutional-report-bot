Power Back On?
Sharing some of my observations/ thoughts on desk conversations with investors over the last couple of
days. As always - feedback welcome.
The playbook for the last couple of months generally has been: "power deal announcements likely to be
more muted near term with polls / midterms in focus, but post November and into YE, it could be off to
the races". It was clear on the desk there were more concerns on the headwinds vs tailwinds for the power
trade. Headwinds: midterms, rates, diesel, OEM overcapacity, lack of incremental DC contracts from
BTM players, PJM / ERCOT reforms, OH/TX/PA/NY headlines and NIMBY. Tailwinds: resilient
hyperscaler capex, recent moves in PJM power price, bullish previews into YE for BTM, general tightness
/ need for power over next couple of years.
IPPs: The main source of pushback on the group for the last couple of months has been: "we might not see
existing capacity obtain contracts with hyperscalers / DC customers". That view has generally been axed
over the last 48 hours. Here's what we would say - there are signs of life for the group. Specialists aren't
tilted as negative and are starting to warm again. Generalist feedback has been moving with headlines (felt
rudderless for some time, but stacking wins on the contracts has been directionally positive) - still think
there's some appetite. The trade has been PJM vs ERCOT for most - healthy debate if that trade is still
middle innings or later innings as we approach end of year.
BTM: Specialist positioning on the group is closer to 8/10 at this point, and was likely closer to 10/10 in
the late summer. As we head into year end, there is still optimism around more deal announcements hitting
the tape – and there were a number of positive updates over the last couple of weeks (AESI, PUMP, SEI) –
with continued focus on path to min 500 MWs for LBRT into YE & WMB potential BTM
announcement(s) at EPS / before year end. Financing + balance sheet capacity has been topical and don't
expect there to be much changing there. Clear focus from dedicated community on Tier 1 vs Tier 2
operators and how customers think about which names fall into respective buckets - reach out to debate.
Nuclear: The group has been under pressure over the last couple of months – though it’s clear from the
CEG announcement yesterday for example (890 MW of uprates)– hyperscalers want reliable, clean,
baseload power – and nuclear fits that bill well. This isn’t necessarily new – most convos have highlighted
end users / customers happy to strike deals on nuclear offtakes / PPAs – but the construction and cost risk
has kept more on the sidelines from announcing FIDs (for example). Read through for uranium should
also be positive here … uprates = more demand.
Source: Goldman Sachs FICC & Equities, Marquee, as of 7 Oct 2026.
