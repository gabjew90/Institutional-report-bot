Business Cycle, Momentum and Earnings
•
Last week’s retail flows were punctuated by hesitation, with sentiment 
showing meager signs of improvement as attention slowly turns to the 3Q 
earnings season. The S&P 500 reached record highs on Tuesday, only to re-
evaluate the next day as market participants weighed rising yields, geopolitical 
risk and fluctuating oil prices against resilient macro fundamentals—our 
business cycle continues to show strength, lifted by its growth component, see 
US Business Cycle.  As we enter October, the weakest period for retail 
seasonality appears to be behind us, Figure 1Retail Seasonality is typicaly strongest in January and weakest in Q3. The data so far supports 
historical patterns: flows have rebounded from September lows, returning 
to their 12-month average levels. The improvement was driven by ETF 
flows, which recovered from depressed levels to a healthier 32%ile. 
Meanwhile, single-name flows increased modestly amid inconsistent 
positioning —alternating between buying and selling—closing the week at the 
43%ile, Figure 13Retail Investor Daily Purchases of Stocks and ETFs.
•
Retail investors appear to have regained their appetite for Tech: inflows 
into Tech Sector ETFs climbed to their 73%ile, a steady increase from the 
4%ile four weeks ago, Figure 2Retail Purchases of Tech ETFs have rebounded ; inflows into the Mag 7 remained resilient, 
while flows into Tech ex Mag 7 strengthened, led by SNDK and MU (3rd and 
4th most bought stocks last week, respectively). Overall, retail investors were 
net buyers across Tech—not only Semis and Hardware, but this time also 
favoring Software. Within the realm of AI, SPCX was reported to raise $40B 
to buy chips from NVDA; as the stock soared 15% last week, retail investors 
continued to trim their sizeable SPCX holdings after building a substantial 
position following the company’s IPO, with the pace of selling slowing in 
October, Figure 57Cumulative Retail Imbalance in SPCX.
•

Commodity and Crypto ETFs. International ETFs only saw minimal

Retail Trading Activity in Numbers for the Week: Oct 1 to Oct 7
•
Retail flows were $5.7B this week, below the 12-month average of $6.7B/week. Retail investors continued to favor ETFs (+
$4.7B) over Single Stocks ($1.0B).
•
Outside of Broad Based Large Cap Equity ETFs (+$1.9B), retail ETF buying was influenced by Equity Sector Technology 
(+$203M), Equity Broad Based Multi Cap (+$183M), Equity Style Dividend (+$175M), and Equity Style Call/put 
writing (+$172M).
•
This week, retail investors continued to buy AI datacenters and electrification (JPAMAIDE), Mag 7, Growth, Top 30 AI/
Datacenter Beneficiaries, AI Software/Product/Monetization, along with US Companies with High Direct China 
Revenue Exposure, see Figure 4,Retail Single Stock Activity by Themes.
•
Activity in Mag7 this week: Retail investors bought NVDA (+$834M), GOOG/L (+$265M), TSLA (+$122M), AMZN (+
$81M), MSFT (+$34M), META (+$4M), and sold AAPL (-$15M).
•
Outside of Mag7, retail investors favored Tech (+602M) and Industrials (+$143M), and were net sellers of all other sectors, 
led by Communications (-$231M), Financials (-$184M), Health Care (-$153M) and Materials (-$120M).
•
Top five retail stocks last week: NVDA (+$834M), GOOGL (+$265M), SNDK (+$150M), MU (+$135M), STX (+$129M, 
5.3z). Bottom 5 retail stocks: SPCX (-$177M), INTC (-$148M), MRVL (-$54M), SMCI (-$51M), MRNA (-$48M).

In options, retail share is off its highs, Figure 82Retail Options Trading Share. The most traded options were in the following names, echoing previous 
weeks: TSLA, NVDA, MU, AMD, META, AMZN, SNDK, SPCX, GOOG/L and AAPL. For the most bought/sold delta and 
gamma names, see Retail Activity in Options. Also see Retail Investors Options Activity by Sectors.
•
Futures Activity (Non Retail) : Over the past week, Futures traders net bought ~$0.6B, primarily driven by net buys in ES (~
$2.1B) and partially offset by net sales in NQ (~$1.3B) and RTY (~$0.2B).

lenders, and a consortium of private credit providers regarding financing transactions to address its capital needs. While retail 
inflows were strongest in May–July 2025 (~$14M) and subsequently turned to net outflows, the stock has seen renewed 
inflows of ~$4M over the past two months, alongside moderate short interest of ~20% of float, up 4% over the same period.
