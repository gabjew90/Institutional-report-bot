 
"Only Fear Is Of The Right-Tail": McElligott Shows 
Why The S&P Is Blind To The Carnage Beneath It 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, OCT 09, 2026 - 07:00 PM 
What do you call a market where the S&P has gone nowhere for two months, 85% of its 
members are in a correction, and the only options anyone is willing to pay up for are upside 
calls? According to Nomura's cross-asset guru Charlie McElligott, you call it 2026. 
In his latest "BINARIES" note (available to pro subs), McElligott lays out a market that is 
simultaneously asleep at the index level and in a full-blown rolling bear market underneath, all 
while energy - specifically Europe's diesel shortage - keeps the whole thing one headline away 
from a rates tantrum. 
And as if on cue, within hours of the note hitting inboxes, both of McElligott's big binaries got a 
live-fire test: Trump took Iran strikes off the table until the midterms, and the FT took a 
sledgehammer to the AI "excess demand" story. More on both below.

Put differently, the hedge for your AI longs wasn't bonds, it was oil. The classic 60/40 
Equity-Bond portfolio is up a whopping 8% YTD with a 1.2 Sharpe, versus 65% for the 
Semi-Energy barbell. Or as Nomura's chart puts it with admirable economy: "bonds don't work." 

 
 
"Running To Stand Still" 
Zoom out to the index, however, and the picture is one of total paralysis. Since early August - 
i.e. since the July "Momentum De-Gross / AI Unwind" shock - the S&P has moved less than 1% 
in aggregate, as McElligott puts it, chopping "ourselves into a million little pieces." The vol 
metrics read like a sedation chart: 
●​ SPX realized vol: 7 (10-day), 10 (1-month), 9.5 (2-month) 
●​ SPX 3m ATM implied vol: 2nd percentile (1Y) 
●​ SPX 25-delta put/call skew: 0.4th percentile (1Y), with put skew also at 0.4%ile 
●​ SPX 1DTE ATM straddle: 9th percentile (2Y) 

 
 
Why so calm? Because, as McElligott explains, the name of the game is "High Dispersion = Low 
Correlation," the "Thematic Bifurcation" he has been flagging for most of the year. S&P 1M 
implied correlation sits at the 1.8th percentile, with 1M realized correlation at the 0.7th 
percentile; the Nasdaq looks the same. 
 

 
Regular readers know record low correlation has been masking a lot since July. They also know 
what history says about it, courtesy of McElligott's peer at Goldman, derivatives guru Brian 
Garrett, who last month noted that only two other periods in the past 25 years saw realized 
correlation this low: 
Nothing says "healthy market" like a correlation chart whose only precedents are the eve of the 
GFC and Volmageddon. 
Ten Stocks And A "Rolling Correction" 
The low correlation, of course, is just the mirror image of extreme narrowness. Nomura 
calculates that ten stocks drove 70% of the S&P's 23% rally since March 30, with Nvidia alone 
responsible for 13% of it, and Micron, Apple and Microsoft another 9% each. The other 490 
stocks? 30%. Combined. 
 
Meanwhile, single stocks are moving like it's a crisis while the index snoozes: over the past 
month the SPX moved 0.8%, while the average stock moved 8.9%. That 8.1% spread ranks in 
the 95th percentile of the past 30 years. AMD (+39.5%) and Intel (+27.6%) on one side, Netflix 
(-18.2%) and the banks on the other. Diversification at its finest. 

 
 
In McElligott's words (and pretty much everyone else's) it is a world where a small cluster of "AI 
Winners" does all the index heavy lifting, while everything else languishes on the "Crowding 
Out" of capital and sentiment, the perception of AI disruption risk, and lately, simply being 
"Rates Losers." Since Aug 3, the Mag7 is up 7.7%, the S&P 2.6%, and the equal-weight S&P is 
down 3%. 
 

 
Which is why McElligott has little patience for the "equities aren't pricing in rates" / "whistling 
past the graveyard" crowd. They are, just not where the pundits are looking: 
...when you look at the way Rates Sensitive-sectors have been CRUSHED over the past month 
and a half...especially when separately paired with my points above on the "Everybody Else" 
and / or "Crowding Out" of Capital and Sentiment for the "AI Losers"... Equities have already 
been undergoing a "ROLLING CORRECTION" of substantial proportions 
Over the trailing 30 days, the only S&P sectors in the green are TMT and Energy - i.e. the two 
"AI Constraints." Everything else got hit: Healthcare -3.5%, Consumer Discretionary -3.6%, 
Utilities -4.6%, Industrials -5.8%, Financials -7.7%, Materials -7.9% and REITs -9.8%. And 
through the "rotational destruction" lens: 
●​ 85% of S&P 500 names are more than 10% below their all-time highs 
●​ 59% are more than 20% below 
●​ 41% are more than 30% below 
●​ 26% are more than 40% below 
●​ 17% are more than 50% below 
 
In other words, the index is a few points from a record and one in six of its members is in a 
50%+ drawdown. 

 
Stuffed On Gamma 
So why doesn't the carnage underneath ever show up in the index? Because the vol sellers 
won't let it. McElligott notes that index "Vol Supply" remains massive, especially intraday in 
0DTE/1DTE, which looks like QIS VRP strategies, "stuffing Dealers on Gamma and insulating 
us from large moves." Nomura's 0DTE customer gamma range is at +$14 billion, the 100th 
percentile. 
 
And why wouldn't they sell? Look at the trailing Sharpes: selling a daily 25-delta SPX put has a 
10-day Sharpe of 13.9; a daily strangle, 14.2. Picking up nickels in front of a steamroller has 
rarely paid this well (which is usually when the steamroller shows up). 

 
 
It's not just QIS. As we discussed in August when McElligott first spotted a "$300 Billion 'Coiled 
Spring' Of 'Exotic' Market Chaos," the exotics/structured notes and derivative-income complex 
keeps "pummeling Dealers with more 'Vega Supply.'" Derivatives/income fund AUM is now $322 
billion, up $222 billion since 2020, with combined fund and exotic vega supply running at 415 
million vega per month. 
 

 
The low realized vol is self-reinforcing: it has allowed the vol-scaling systematics to 
mechanically re-lever after being forced out post-Iran and the summer AI unwind. Vol Control + 
CTA + Risk Parity collective US equity exposure is back to the 86th percentile, near the highs of 
the year. 
 
The Only Fear Is The Right Tail 
Which brings us to the most perverse part of the setup. Nobody wants downside protection: 
skew "just chokes daily," as McElligott puts it, because investors don't need hedges when their 
nets are this low, and are tired of the theta bleed. Instead, "The Only Fear is of the Right-Tail 
Equities Rally," with SPX call skew at the 99.6th percentile (100th on Nomura's chart). 
...with many Institutional Investors in just the "Beta" either not owning enough of the narrow 
sleeve of AI stocks which are actually driving Index higher and "under-capturing," or conversely, 
the incremental $ of Leveraged Equities Money at Hedge Funds now being controlled [by] 
Market Neutrals, you just don't have the Net on to capture the low-breadth rally 

 
 
Plenty of people tried to de-risk ahead of next week's earnings kickoff, McElligott says, citing 
macro/rates risk, the midterms, the "Can They / Can't They" of the Anthropic IPO, and the 
endless parade of AI capex financing (OpenAI hunting $50 billion for AVGO chips, the 
ORCL/Apollo/GS chip financing, etc). Regular readers know that last part is something we have 
been warning about for a while. 
But the wall of worry keeps getting climbed, and McElligott's bull case rests on the "AI Capex 
Trickle-Down" finally showing up in the earnings of the "Other 493." The Mag 7's contribution to 
S&P earnings growth halved from 15% to 7% in Q2, while the Other 493 accelerated from 7% in 
Q4 2025 to 13% and then 18%. 

 
 
Add nominal GDP bouncing between 7% and 9% (the nowcast is at 7.05%), and McElligott 
argues that corporates and equities "can absorb the Rates." Rate-insensitive maybe... but not 
stupid.

The Binaries 
So how to trade it? McElligott lays out three permutations: 
●​ Chase the right tail. "In a twisted kinda way," MegaCap Tech call spreads into an 
earnings rally over the next month "might be the best play (99%ile Call Skew doesn't 
hurt)," with the buyside still underpositioned for an "SPX 8k" backbreaker if AI earnings 
lift all boats again. 
●​ Fade the energy premium on peace. Any conciliatory Trump "Iran Deal Talk" into the 
midterms would see "Energy and Rate Vol SMOKED off recent levels," with the trades 
then in "the bombed-out stuff like Gold and IWM Upside." 
●​ Own rates vol regardless. Through the midterms and beyond, McElligott wants to be 
"hedged with Long USD / EUR Rates Vega = Own long-dated Swaption Straddles," 
because even in a great-earnings world, terminal rate expectations likely go higher "and 
we have to reprice Fed path into actually 'restrictive' territory." The risks: a further energy 
price shock (resumed attacks, or "the ongoing risk STILL of a US Diesel ban in a few 
months"), or higher rates and energy "Break Something," triggering demand destruction 
and a growth scare - which is why tail funds have been "poking-around on Low Strike 
Receivers and Curve Caps." 

 
Nomura's backtest shows why the swaption straddle is the hedge of choice: USD 20y20y 
straddles have delivered their best returns, over 1% per month, in the highest-inflation quintile, 
almost entirely from vega.

Bottom Line 
McElligott's map is, as usual, unmatched: an index pinned by $14 billion of dealer gamma and 
$322 billion of income-fund vol supply, a rolling bear market underneath it, and a diesel-driven 

 
rates fuse running into November 3. His own conclusion is that the path of least resistance 
remains a forced re-risking higher, as underpositioned beta chases the right tail: 
All of this impetus to "Force-In" a re-risking...as we tend to keep climbing the wall of worries with 
the improvement in Earnings Breadth and the "AI Capex Trickle-Down" only moving higher 
Perhaps. But the entire bull case runs through ten stocks and one premise - that AI demand is 
real and growing - and that premise just took its first big hit from OpenAI's own investor 
documents. With correlation at levels last seen before Feb 2007 and Volmageddon, skew at the 
0.4th percentile and everybody short the left tail at Sharpes of 14, we think the starburst Garrett 
warned about is a much likelier outcome than McElligott's "SPX 8k" backbreaker. Either way, 
McElligott's swaption straddles look like the cheapest insurance on the board. 
Then again, when the only thing anyone is hedging is the upside, the market rarely obliges.
