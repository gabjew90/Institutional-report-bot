DB CoTD: 21 Out of 23 Ain’t Bad 
 
 
I wonder whether title puns based on classic rock songs from 1978 would be lost on many readers but 1978 
does make an appearance in today’s CoTD so it felt doubly appropriate. 
First this year. Between the end of May and the end of September, the S&P 500 rose by less than 1%. 
However, we’ve now entered what has historically been a much stronger seasonal period which is worth 
bearing in mind as nervousness remains high in markets. According to work from our equity strategists Binky 
Chadha and Parag Thatte, the S&P 500 has risen in 21 of the last 23 midterm election cycles over the period 
from one month before the election to two months after. With the midterms now less than a month away, we 
entered that sweet spot this week. The median return over this three-month window is 7%, well above a 
normal quarterly equity return and suggesting the pattern is unlikely to be pure coincidence. Ahead of most 
of these rallies, markets were also fairly flat from around mid-July to early October, broadly echoing the 2026 
experience. 

If you’re looking for reasons to doubt the historical pattern, the two exceptions are worth examining. In 1978, 
equities did fall around the midterms as inflation and interest rates rose. But the backdrop was considerably 
more extreme than today: the dollar was in crisis, falling around -8% in October, before the Fed was forced to 
raise rates by 100bps to 9.5% on November 1st. So the rate moves were far larger than today’s, and more 
urgent, while the dollar was slumping rather than rising. 
The other exception was 2018, also a midterm year during a Trump presidency. Again, the sell-off had a 
substantial rates component, alongside major concerns about the US-China trade war. On October 3rd, 
Powell said the Fed was “a long way from neutral” as the first post-GFC hiking cycle progressed. It wasn’t 
until Powell turned much more dovish in the first week of January 2019 that markets took off again. 
So, there is a rates parallel between the two historical exceptions and today. But neither had an earnings 
season about to begin with YoY growth expected to be above 30%. This season kicks off next week, and we 
should see some bumper results. All else equal, that ought to provide a useful tailwind for the usual midterm 
seasonal pattern to reassert itself, even after the recent rates sell-off. See Binky and Parag's preview of US 
Q3 earnings here where they see a stunning 34% YoY EPS growth.
