The gold squeeze setup
Gold has taken a beating, but the setup for a squeeze is getting increasingly interesting.
CTAs have sold aggressively, downside hedging is picking up and positioning is stretched.
Meanwhile, structural buyers haven't gone anywhere, and the surge in yields that helped trigger the
selloff may be running out of steam.
The technical damage is real, but so is the potential asymmetry.
The sellers are already there. All we need is a catalyst.
The big trend
Gold continues "fighting" with the huge trend line. We dipped briefly below it, but are back above it
again. We are well below the 200-day MA, and while nothing good happens below the 200-day MA, we
could easily see a bounce.

The gold resilience
Gold has been hit by the sharp move higher in yields, but perhaps not as hard as one would expect.
UBS highlights that gold's sensitivity to real rates has weakened, suggesting structural demand is
absorbing some of the pressure.
Meanwhile, the US 10-year yield has moved from the bottom to the top of its trend channel.
We outlined the case for a potential reversal in yields yesterday (here).
If gold has held up this well with yields rising, imagine what happens if yields start falling, especially
with positioning this stretched.

The gold divergence
Gold has been under pressure, but look beneath the surface.
UBS notes that the recent decline has been driven largely by speculative accounts cutting their net-
long futures and options exposure.
Meanwhile, ETF holdings have remained remarkably resilient, with continued inflows despite the
selloff.
The fast money is selling. The structural buyers aren't going anywhere.
UBS
The buyers who don't care about CTAs
While speculative accounts have been cutting gold exposure, central banks continue accumulating.
UBS highlights that China purchased 23 tonnes in September alone and expects global central-bank
buying to reach 750–1,000 tonnes annually.
The bank sees structural buying helping establish a floor around $4,000.
CTAs trade momentum. Central banks trade decades.

Gold vs bonds
Goldman's McGeoch makes an interesting point.
With bond yields at these levels, buying duration looks tempting. But you only buy bonds today if you
believe they won't be cheaper tomorrow.
And if you believe yields are close to peaking, why not buy gold instead?
Both trades benefit from a similar macro outcome, but there's one crucial difference.
Gold has structural support from the PBOC and other central banks. Bonds remain exposed to fiscal
pressures and political uncertainty.
Same macro trade. Very different buyers.
