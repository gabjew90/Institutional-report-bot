Unplugged
The AI boom is supposed to be driving an unprecedented surge in electricity demand. Yet one of its
most obvious beneficiaries, the companies supplying the power, has been absolutely crushed. Hedge
funds have spent months dumping the trade, positioning has collapsed to multi-year lows, and
valuations have followed. The irony? The fundamental story has barely changed. And now Goldman is
gathering hundreds of clients to explain why it may be time to plug back in.
Underperformers
The Power Up America theme is composed of 5 categories, all of which underperformed the S&P 500
Index excluding AI except for the Grid Infrastructure theme.
Marquee
Goldman calls an emergency conf call
Apparently, the carnage has become too interesting for Goldman to ignore. Last week, the bank
gathered more than 400 clients on short notice to pitch what it calls the most underappreciated
bottleneck in AI.
"Last week, we hosted 400+ clients on short notice on the POWER UP AMERICA theme that has
underperformed other AI themes and the S&P 500 substantially YTD. We like buying the Power Up
America basket here. This is the most underappreciated AI bottleneck. Positioning is low, valuation has
normalized and the fundamentals are intact." (GS)

The stocks weakened. The demand didn't
This is where the disconnect gets interesting. While investors have been liquidating the power trade,
Goldman still expects US data center electricity demand to surge from 39 GW in 2025 to 108 GW by
2030 — a 23% CAGR. Total US power demand is expected to grow 3.5% annually through 2030, with
data centers accounting for the lion's share of that growth. Meanwhile, nuclear capacity is increasingly
being locked into long-term contracts.

Deals are back
And now comes the potential catalyst. Constellation's latest power purchase agreements with Google
follow a deal with Amazon just last week. More importantly, these agreements demonstrate that
hyperscalers are still willing to commit to long-term nuclear power supply despite lingering regulatory
uncertainty in PJM.
GS: "We also expect higher earnings revisions as independent power producers announce long-term
partnerships with mega-cap technology companies: CEG Announces a 20-year nuclear PPA for ~700
MW in PJM; positive for industry broadly and CEG Announces PPAs with Google for total of ~3.6 GW of
nuclear in PJM; continued positive momentum on contracting."
Wall Street noticing
Morgan Stanley analyst thinks contracting activity accelerates in 2027 and sees “extremely attractive”
entry point for the group. MS sees IPPs trading at levels that imply no further contracting upside. MS
thinks the strong stock performances earlier this week indicate investors' increasing optimism with the
space.
Switch it back on
So here we are: hedge funds have abandoned the trade, valuations have compressed, and the
underlying demand for electricity continues to accelerate. Meanwhile, hyperscalers are signing new
long-term power deals and Wall Street is rediscovering the sector. The question is no longer who pulled
the plug on Power Up America, but who will be first to switch it back on.
