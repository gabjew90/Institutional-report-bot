Portfolio Manager’s Summary 
US stocks traded higher Friday with the S&P 500 up +1.2% on the week, as investors are positioning ahead of 3Q earnings season and await the 
next move from the Fed with the CPI report due out next Tuesday. IPPs, AI Software, and Power Infrastructure were among the themes that 
outperformed this week, while NeoClouds, Bitcoin Sensitive Equities, and Non-Profitable Tech were among the themes that underperformed.  
 
Prime: US equities were slightly net sold on the week, driven by short sales modestly outpacing long buys in Single Stocks. HFs rotated into 
Industrials and out of Info Tech (most notably on Thursday) this week, a notable reversal from the trends seen since the start of September. At 
the same time, Health Care stocks saw de-grossing activity for a 4th consecutive week amid continued intra-sector volatility and price gyrations.  
 
Shares: Asset Mgrs finished moderate net sellers while HF flows were balanced on the week. Macro chop (Oil / Rate volatility still high) + 
anticipation into earnings contributed to a lack of client activity for the majority of the week. Flows picked up on Thursday, where our desk noted 
a heavy wave of selling in Semis / AI complex around OpenAI revenue headlines that continued through Friday despite the clarification update. 
In addition, we saw long supply in Telcos on the SPCX announcement and profit-taking in Managed Care post the Medicare STARs results.  
 
Futures: While Nasdaq’s rally stalled, leveraged positioning seemingly increased given outright and relative funding strength. In fact, Nasdaq’s 
funding premium to S&P reached a 98% 1-year rank on the October 8th close. If this signaled futures buying, watch the footprint of Asset Managers. 
From September 8th – 29th, the largest and most important cohort sold $3.2bn alongside higher index prices. This brought the historically positive 
correlation between Asset Manager Gross longs and price to effectively nil. 
 
Derivatives: Earnings season kicks off next week and Financials have underperformed meaningfully into their respective prints (XLF -6.5% from 
the highs despite SPX hitting records), where implied move on earnings days is not reflective of heightened uncertainty as the ratio of recent 
drawdown vs earnings straddle is ~5x (implied move cheap) – pace/complexion of trading activity and AI funding calendar will be the most 
debated. On an index level, there is a notable difference in market’s perception and cost of skew – VIX upside convexity has gone extremely bid 
while SPX downside convexity remains very offered, as we have lived in a word of very low realized correlation and very high dispersion.  
 
ETFs: There has been a tremendous focus on Brazil this week as local elections have directed a wave of market participation into the region. EWZ, 
the largest/flagship ETF for Brazil exposure, witnessed over $8 billion of volume print to the tape on Monday -- the largest notional day in the 25yr 
history of the fund. The fund’s underlying index is on track to post its best week of performance since 2020, and we continue to see a strong bid 
on the desk. Bullish expressions haven't been limited to EWZ, with Brazilian small caps (EWZS) also proving to be a strong area of client interest. 
 
Baskets: We like buying the Power Up America (GSENEPOW) basket here as this is the most underappreciated AI bottleneck. Positioning is low, 
valuation has normalized, and the fundamentals are intact. The concerns of the midterms and the politicization of Power are starting to fade: we 
expect potential incentives to promote increased power supply in the US through additional spending on the grid’s infrastructure, behind-the-meter 
solutions, and more. We also expect higher earnings revisions as IPPs announce long-term partnerships with mega-cap technology companies. 
 
Sector Specialists: TMT: The NDX grinded out another ‘win’ on the week (4 in a row and 6 of 7), yet, the ‘vibes’ remain measured out there as 
index level action (NDX + VIX) continues to mask a dynamic stretch of trading under the hood (Software o/p Semis by ~800 bps this past week). 
Franchise activity levels pointed to profit taking (and/or hedging) as investor optimism that earnings will support current ‘consensus’ positioning 
(e.g. beats in Semis and Mag 7) appears to be balanced by an emerging list of ‘questions’ around execution, expectations and linearity. Next week, 
all eyes on TSM and ASML earnings alongside a handful of industry events: WDAY & ACN analyst day + Semicon West (AMAT, LRCX). Financials: 
the sector outperformed this week, led by a barbell of 1) high-quality defensives like a) Exchanges – amidst fading concerns around how market 
structure may evolve away from traditional incumbents – and b) diversified P&C Insurance carriers (while personal insurance remains debated 
amidst broader agentic / competition focuses), and on the other side of the risk spectrum, Payments & Fintech also outperformed amidst positive 
views on the trajectory of consumer spend into October, combined with greater comfort around the position of bellwethers V & MA in the broader 
agentic commerce debate. Next week we kick off earnings with the big banks on Tuesday, of which sentiment has notably turned cautious.

• 
Macro Products (Index and ETF combined) saw little net activity (+0.1 SDs 1-year), driven by long buys offset by roughly equal

(long sales > short covers) and Biotech (short covers). On a subsector level, it’s worth noting that 1) Biotech stocks collectively have

terms on a trailing 8-week basis, magnitude of the recent de-grossing in Biotech stocks is the largest in more than five years. US 
Biotech long/short ratio now stands at 3.22 (vs. peak level of 5.36 in Dec ’25), in the 7th/37th percentiles vs. the past year/five years.

TMT (Pete Callahan and Alexander Joseph) 
The NDX grinded out another ‘win’ on the week (4 in a row and 6 of 7), yet, the ‘vibes’ remain measured out there as index level 
action (NDX + VIX) continues to mask a dynamic stretch of trading under the hood (Software o/p Semis by ~800 bps this past 
week). Franchise activity levels pointed to profit taking (and/or hedging) as investor optimism that earnings will support current 
‘consensus’ positioning (e.g. beats in Semis and Mag 7) appears to be balanced by an emerging list of ‘questions’ around 
execution, expectations and linearity.  This coming week, all eyes on TSM and ASML earnings alongside a handful of industry 
events: WDAY & ACN analyst day + Semicon West (AMAT, LRCX). 
 
Financials & Real Estate (Christian DeGrasse) 
Financials outperformed this week, led by a barbell of 1) high quality defensives like a) Exchanges, amidst fading concerns 
around how market structure may evolve away from traditional incumbents, and b) diversified P&C Insurance carriers (while 
personal insurance remains debated amidst broader agentic / competition focuses), and on the other side of the risk spectrum, 
Payments & Fintech also outperformed amidst positive views on the trajectory of consumer spend into October, combined with 
greater comfort around the position of bellwethers V & MA in the broader agentic commerce debate. Next week we kick off 
earnings with the big banks on Tuesday, of which sentiment has notably turned cautious. We’re watching whether the expected 
slowdown in 3Q activity is 1) as bad as feared, 2) more importantly, either a brief ‘air pocket/pause’ or a signal of a more 
prolonged slowdown to come. On the depository side, investors are tactically positive about how net interest margins will come 
in for the banks (particularly for 4Q guides), in part due to an expected lag in deposit repricing through the end of 2026, though 
the setup in 2027 remains debated. Stepping back, it’s somewhat notable just how negative sentiment is across financials given 
how little focus there is on credit risk (which is viewed as benign across consumer and commercial, with perhaps one eye open 
on CRE), with much of the debate happening outside of the micro (rates, cycle/growth, etc).
