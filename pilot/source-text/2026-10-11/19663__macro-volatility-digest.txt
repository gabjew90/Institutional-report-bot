WEEKLY MARKET COMMENTARY 
Equity Markets Remain Resilient Amidst Continued Yield Surge 
 
Cross-Asset Volatility: Cross-asset implied volatilities 
edged higher as cooler-than-expected core inflation and 
weak job growth momentarily quelled escalating bond 
yields. Interest rate volatilities notched another leg up with 
the MOVE Index advancing +13nms to 110 mid-week 
(3nms shy of its 1-year high) as the 30-year yield breached 
5.63%, its highest level since 2002.  Following “dovish-
hike” comments from Fed Vice Chair Jefferson and NY Fed 
President Williams with both signaling a case for a hike 
deferral, the probability of an Oct rate hike has collapsed 
from 70% to 20%.  Worth flagging is that the probability of 
a rate hike by Dec remains around 80%, suggesting that 
Fed Funds futures are implying that any shift in Fed path 
policy would be more about the timing of the next rate hike, 
rather than a moderation or reversal of the tightening bias. 
Corporate bond volatilities have both continued to climb 
with investment grade (VIXIG) and high-yield (VIXHY) vols 
jumping from 6th and 11th percentile lows 2 weeks ago to 
their 79th and 84th percentile highs today. Note, however, 
that the tandem rise in yields for both IG & HY tiers masks 
a decoupling of duration and credit risk.  Specifically, while 
IG effective corporate bond yields have notably risen over 
the last 2 weeks, the OAS credit risk premium IG investors 
demand over Treasuries still sits near historical tights.  By 
contrast, high-yield OAS spreads (particularly lower rated 
CCC- rated credits) have risen over 300bps YTD to 12.1% 
and now trade above their 25-year average levels.  The 
rise of IG implied vols thus primarily reflects the same rate 
uncertainty that's driving the MOVE index spike, while the 
rise of HY implied vols has been driven primarily by 
investor demand for meaningfully more compensation for 
lower-quality credit risk.  
French sovereign credit spreads widened sharply over the 
past week, with the OAT-Bund spread reaching 140 bps 
and 5yr French CDS jumping to its widest levels since the 
European Sovereign Debt Crisis.  The widening spread of 
French debt vs the eurozone’s anchor credit suggests that 
traders currently price this risk as a France-specific rather 
than a general Eurozone credit concern.  
Equities: Although French companies have the highest 
weighting (~1/3) in the Eurostoxx-50 Index, the majority are 
global diversified multinationals and not tied specifically to 
the French economy. Accordingly, potential contagion risk 
to the pan-European equity volatility markets has so far 
been fairly contained (VSTOXX +2pts w/w to 19.2).  In the 
US, volatility, correlation, and skew arced higher on the 
prior week’s hawkish Fed sentiment but ended the week 
virtually unchanged with the VIX® Index closing at 15.3, 
correlation at 8.0, and skew trading at 7th percentile lows.

How to read this chart:
The chart shows how various asset class implied 
volatilities are trading relative to their own history, using 
a 10-year lookback. 
The volatilities are ranked by z-scores, which compares 
the current volatility level for an asset versus its own 10-
year history. A z-score of +2, for example, would mean 
that the current volatility level is trading 2 standard 
deviations above its long-term average (i.e. rich) while a 
z-score of -2 would mean it’s trading 2 standard 
deviations below average (i.e. cheap)
Because each asset class volatility trades at different 
levels and often defined differently (e.g. lognormal vs. 
normal vol, price vs. bps vol), by standardizing using z-
scores, we’re able to compare different asset class 
volatilities on the same scale - and see at a glance if 
there are any divergences or dislocations in the cross-
asset volatility markets
