Based on a series of conversations with industry experts, supply chain checks, and third
party data, we extend our analysis of Nvidia Blackwell and Vera Rubin AI data center pod-
level economics to include estimates for AMD and ASIC architectures. This note includes our
analysis. Excel backup is available on request.
We revise our Vera Rubin estimates and land at $7.5M per rack vs. our prior estimate
of $9.1M. Biggest drivers of the revision are 1) lowered estimates of per-rack NAND
content and 2) more conservative expectations for HBM 4 pricing. We also 3) revise
our estimates for networking content, as well as 4) our estimates for cooling and power
delivery, based on Asia channel checks, and 5) reflect latest DRAM and NAND pricing. Our
estimates are now much more in line with supply chain expectations of $7-8M per rack.
We estimate AMD’s MI455X architecture to be a $7.3M rack, relatively similar to
Vera Rubin. In terms of the high level differences, we think AMD is earning lower margins
on GPUs and thus has lower computing content, despite higher CPU value than Nvidia’s
Vera. Conversely, Networking content appears significantly higher, due to AMD including
“up to 3” NICs per GPU.
We expect OpenAI Jalapeno to be $5.0M per pod. We had expected Jalapeno to be
a white-label version of the NVDA architecture other than replacing the GPU with a
cheaper ASIC. However, on closer scrutiny, the design is radically different - notably,
OpenAI intends to expand the scale-up domain and hold data locally in HBM (and
DRAM), and reduce dependence on scale-out and NAND. OpenAI’s disclosure are less
complete than NVDA, AMD. However, we find it reassuring that our estimates are generally
fairly consistent with OpenAI’s stated design goals.
Our best guess is TPU v7 Ironwood is a $2.7M, however we found disclosures to be
lacking, and lack conviction in very granular estimates as result. We’re hopeful Google
will disclose more details as external sales roll out; we will likely make significant revisions
to our estimates as disclosures come in. We also await further details on TPU v8, and hope
that as Google’s external customer base broaden, they will also disclose more to customers
(and thus to the public).
We also update our estimates for out-of-rack cost based on our prior work, and
construct a build of hourly cost per GPU. Based on reported power consumption for
each XPU pod, we then reach estimates of total capex / GW, ranging from $35B / GW for
OpenAI Jalepno to ~$40B / GW for VR NVL72.
While our estimates imply notably higher GW shipments than our US data center
model, we believe the disconnect could be explained by the increasing importance
of international builds amid US data center delays.
Available compute continues to accelerate, which we see as an unlock for AI
adoption.

to increasingly be turning to non-US data center partners, such as OpenAI’s deal with Firmus for AI data center capacity across
Australia, Signapore, Indonesia and Malaysia. Accordingly, we belive the % of data center capacity deployed in the US may
decrease compared to historical norms.

growth among AI labs. With the Rubin cycle as well as more widespread adoption of AI ASICs driving a further step-up in
available compute, we believe that this could help to unlock further AI adoption.

• Direct-attached storage content. We remind investors that direct-attached storage is somewhat less transparent.
Storage is excluded from Nvidia’s specs, and there is likely room for configuration variance. Our previous estimate
assumed that it does not make sense to add external storage arrays until in-rack NAND content is maximized, given the
lower performance of external storage. Since external storage appears to be significant, we assumed that we could safely

is still upcoming) from the Vera Rubin NVL 72 pod bill-of-materials. It remains somewhat unclear to us why AI data center
vendors are buying external storage arrays when there is still capacity to increase in-rack NAND content, which would
improve performance and save on facilitate floor area.
