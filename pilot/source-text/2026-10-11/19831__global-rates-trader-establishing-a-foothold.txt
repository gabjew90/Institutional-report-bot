It has been a challenging stretch for valuation-based arguments for stable or lower 
G10 yields, but longer-term forwards are elevated versus fair for most major 
markets, and a range of factors are reinforcing the case that duration is attractive at 
these levels. Recent credit spread pressures have revealed clearer cracks in markets’ 
ability to digest a high vol selloff without more acute financial conditions tightening, 
most notably in Europe. In the US, positioning signals now point to an investor-base 
better hedged against a further yield rise. And while recent volatility has heightened 
focus on potential amplifying factors from market stress and hedging flows, 
measures of market function look generally benign and convexity-related risks on 
further selloffs appear to have diminished. We think that heightened macro 
uncertainty and caution amid market volatility have been the key ingredients for the 
more acute bout of pressure on long-end rates. While certain drivers—most notably 
energy risks—have not subsided, we see tactical value to fading the latest phase of 
10y underperformance—we favor receiving 10s on the 5s10s30s SOFR fly. OAT 
widening has outstripped fundamental drivers; with spread compression dependent 
on support from lower core rates and rates volatility, we think lingering spread risks 
should provide a brake to Bund selloffs, where we see more routes to a rally. For Gilts 
ahead of the late-October UK budget, day-to-day volatility continues to hinge on the 
energy backdrop, but limiting near-term fiscal slippage is a necessary ingredient to 
preventing a build in risk premium.

Europe 
More paths to lower Bund yields. Unresolved uncertainty around energy prices 
n
remains the main risk to our bullish Bund forecasts, but we think asymmetry is 
building toward longs. First, energy upside has increasingly translated into curve 
flattening rather than outright selloffs at longer maturities, and the Bund curve has 
tended to out-flatten G3 peers. Second, the recent broadening signs of sovereign 
stress have started to result in a safe-haven bid for Bunds; we expect this to continue 
should focus on political risk stay acute in France or intensify in Italy (more below). 
Third, the renewed tightening in financing conditions across the Euro area could 
challenge the positive growth sentiment, which has so far supported higher yields 
and the notion of a higher equilibrium rate. We continue to forecast 10y Bund yields 
at 3.25% at end-2026. 
Tentative stabilisation in OATs. OATs have found a better footing this week 
n
although spreads remain close to the wides, including at the front-end of the curve. 
The sharpness of last week’s selloff prompted questions about the possible role of 
selling by foreign holders, such as Japanese institutional investors. We note historical 
evidence of reduced Japanese flows / divestment around episodes of elevated 
French political risk, for instance following the snap elections of 2024 (Exhibit 5). But 
Japanese investors have not been a meaningful source of positive net demand for 
EGBs for some time and are thus likely playing a marginal role in recent price action. 
Instead, we see the rise in rates volatility and impact on vol-adjusted carry as the 
more likely factor behind spread widening. Separately, we think the Rassemblement 
National’s counter budget proposal offered only incremental news for the 
medium-term fiscal outlook. The emphasis on deficit reduction is a positive signal to 
the market, but substantial deficit reduction likely faces implementation challenges. 
We continue to expect OATs to stabilise tighter as we think the widening has 
outstripped fundamental drivers, but are mindful that this may hinge on support 
from lower core rates and rates volatility, or improvement in the domestic macro 
outlook.

BTP underperform energy, but political risk not priced. We see a range of 
n

stabilisation, there is still little political risk premium in BTPs at this stage. For this

n
