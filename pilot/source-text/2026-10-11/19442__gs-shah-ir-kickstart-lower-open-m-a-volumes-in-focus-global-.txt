Global - Key morning news:
• European markets have opened lower (STOXX 600 -0.42%) dragged down by a rebound in oil prices
amid ongoing threats to Middle East energy supplies. Attention today will be on the Fed’s minutes from its
last monetary policy meeting in September, set to be published later today.
• Yesterday: European equities closed higher (STOXX 600 +0.5%), with cyclicals leading the advance.
The constructive tone was supported by a pullback in oil prices, which briefly dipped below $98/bbl
intraday, helping to ease inflation concerns and improve risk appetite. Continued AI and technology
momentum, alongside resilient US growth data and increasingly supportive signals around power demand,
also provided a favourable backdrop. Beneath the surface, there were significant factor-driven rotations
throughout the session, with rates firmly in the driving seat. Healthcare was the standout performer at one
stage, rising more than 2% as European real yields moved lower, before closing around +0.6%.
Meanwhile, defence stocks underperformed, giving back much of the previous session's gains as
geopolitical tensions eased following comments from Iran's Foreign Minister, who described recent talks
with Qatar's Emir as "constructive". In France, domestics rallied following a speech from Marine Le Pen
outlining her economic vision through to 2032. Notably, she increased her proposed savings programme
from €125bn to €140bn to reflect the higher cost of servicing government debt. Across the Atlantic, US
equities moved higher in a broad-based rally, supported by a more constructive macro backdrop. A pause
in the bond market sell-off and stable oil prices helped fuel a tech- and AI-led advance, with investors
entering the Q3 earnings season with renewed optimism. Both the S&P 500 (+0.58%) and Nasdaq 100
(+0.48%) closed at fresh all-time highs. On flows, both asset managers and hedge funds skewed modestly
to the buy side, driven primarily by macro and technology-related activity, although overall volumes
remained relatively subdued. Market breadth was notably strong, with 10 of 11 sectors finishing in
positive territory. Utilities (+3%) led the gains as the AI power theme continued to attract attention, while
semiconductors lagged amid ongoing concerns around intensifying competition.
• M&A Volumes: The value of deals announced worldwide in the first half of the year was $2.77 trillion,
the highest amount during the first half of a year since 2002 (link). However, volumes declined sharply in
the third quarter, which saw the lowest level of transactions worth more than $10 billion since the fourth
quarter of 2024 (link).
• US Inflation: The ISM Services Prices Paid Index climbed to 74 in September, its highest reading since
July 2022. Tariffs and fuel costs were the most frequently cited pressures on supply chains, with no

industry reporting a decline in prices paid (link).
• Imbalances: Figures on the US trade deficit showed that record imports in August pushed the trade gap
out to its widest in 17 months. While record imports point to resilient consumer demand, the resulting
trade deficit acts as a drag on GDP growth. Following the release, the Atlanta Fed's GDPNow estimate for
Q3 growth was revised down slightly to 3.7%. Set against China's record trade surplus, the latest figures
also serve as a reminder of the risks associated with widening global imbalances (RTS).
• Stat of the Day: Nvidia's market capitalisation, now above $5.7tn, exceeds the combined market value of
the smallest 251 companies in the S&P 500 (link).
