David vs. Goliath
RTY vs. NDX at Historical Extreme
WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW: 
There are a million different ways to describe what's been happening over the last
several months. Since mid-August, Technology is +9.3% while eight other sectors are
-3% or worse, and five are -5% or worse. Another way to look at it is the Russell 2k
(RTY) vs. Nasdaq 100 (NDX). That ratio is -16% since late July, bringing its 14-day RSI
to 12.77, the 8th lowest day on record. Since 1985, there have only been three prior
unique periods when the RTY/NDX ratio's RSI fell below 14: July '07, Aug. '11, and March
'20. While a sample size of three is statistically insignificant, it's more about the rarity of
what we are seeing right now. With that said, let's take a look at each of these periods
and what it might mean going forward.
■
The Dates. The exact dates of the three events when the RTY/NDX 14-Day RSI first
closed below 14: 7/23/07, 8/5/11, 3/18/20. What's notable is that the 2011 and
2020 period came after the broad market (SPX) had already experienced extreme
stress, while 2007 occurred with SPX near the highs, like it is now.
■
Oversold Doesn't Mean Over. As far as the absolute ratio of RTY/NDX, a 13-RSI does
bring out our contrarian senses. It's worth nothing, however, that in 2007 and 2011
the ratio kept moving lower over the next 10 and 30 days. Oversold does not mean
over.
■
Similarities to '07. In July '07, SPX had just broken out of a 6-week trading range to
a new high, but after a few days that breakout failed and SPX fell nearly -12% during
the so-called 'quant quake'.
■
Bond Weakness Preceded Equity Weakness. On July 19th, 2007, the VIX closed
at 15. Interestingly, 30yr yields had gone up ~75bps from mid-May to mid-June,
and CCC spreads had gone up ~150bps early June to mid-July. So you had a sharp
treasury bond sell-off and widening junk spreads preceding the RTY/NDX oversold
condition.
■
History Rhymes. Currently, 30yr yields +60bps in a month, CCC spreads +150bps in
a month, 400bps in 6 months, and the VIX closed at 15. History doesn't repeat, but
it often rhymes.
■
Yields Showing Exhaustion. 30yr yields showing a 10-count on Bloomberg candle
sessions, a sign of exhaustion. They also faded ~4bps from intraday highs. While we
continue to think theprimary trend for rates is higher, over the next couple of weeks
a reversion lower would not be surprising.
■
Rates Down, RTY Up? All else equal, falling rates should help RTY stabilize after its
nearly -10% drawdown. However, yields finished well off session highs today and
RTY barely finished off its lows. Further, in '07 falling yields coincided with falling
RTY in July/August, so it's far from a guarantee.
■
The Bottom Line. RTY/NDX ratio closed with a 12.77 RSI, the 8th lowest reading in
history. Yields are showing upside exhaustion. All else equal this should help RTY
stabilize especially with it once again testing its 200 DMA. With that said, history
shows it's no guarantee and our conviction remains higher that NDX 'catches down'
rather than RTY 'catches up'.
