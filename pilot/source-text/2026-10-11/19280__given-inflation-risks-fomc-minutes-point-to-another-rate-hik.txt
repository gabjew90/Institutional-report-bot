Given Inflation Risks, FOMC Minutes Point 
to Another Rate Hike Later This Year
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Headlines 
 
The FOMC voted unanimously to increase the fed funds rate at its mid-
September meeting citing high inflation, inflation risks tilted to the upside, 
and balanced risks to the labor market. 
 
 
Participants expect inflation to move toward the committee’s 2% objective 
in the medium term. 
 
 
The minutes say most participants expected to hike the fed funds rate 
again later this year. 
 
 
PNC’s forecast is for two fed funds rate hikes in the months ahead. 
Details
At their meeting on September 15 and 16, the 12 
members of the Federal Open Market Committee 
voted unanimously to raise the federal funds rate, 
their key short-term policy rate, by a quarter of a 
percentage point to a range of 3.75% to 4.00%. 
According to the minutes from that meeting, 
participants were satisﬁed that the labor market 
was “near full employment, with some signs of 
strengthening,” but noted that “inﬂation remained 
elevated.” 
Most participants viewed 
risks as 
weighted toward higher inﬂation, but labor market 
risks as balanced. Given this balance of risks, “all 
participants viewed a higher target range for the 
federal funds rate as appropriate. Participants 
judged that this would support a timelier return of 
inﬂation to the Committee’s 2% goal.” The minutes 
also 
say 
that 
many 
participants 
viewed 
a 
September rate hike as “providing insurance 
against inﬂation remaining persistently above 
target due to stronger-than-expected demand or 
further adverse supply shocks.”  
Participants provided several reasons for supporting 
a rate hike in September: to protect against further 
unexpected inﬂationary pressures; to slow inﬂation 
in the most likely economic outcome; to prevent 
consumer, business, and ﬁnancial market inﬂation 
expectations from moving higher; and to prevent 
strong AI-demand from driving inﬂation up further. 
Given that several participants viewed monetary 
policy as “not restrictive or only mildly restrictive,” 
that is, either not weighing on economic growth at 
all, or weighing on it only slightly, “most participants 
assessed that another increase in the target range 
for the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate 
by year end.”

According to the minutes, “despite the recent rise in 
longer-term Treasury yields, ﬁnancial conditions 
appeared to be supportive of economic growth, with 
equity prices having risen substantially this year and 
spreads on corporate bonds having remained

Growth remained solid heading into the fall

commented that the underlying momentum in the 
economy appeared to have increased.” Some 
participants also noted that AI investment was 
surprising to the upside. 
According to the minutes from the September 15 
and 16 FOMC meeting, a shift in the balance of risks 
supported a fed funds rate hike. Inﬂation remained 
above the 2% objective, had risen over the past 
year, and risks were tilted toward higher, not lower, 
inﬂation. At the same time risks around the labor 
market were more balanced. Given this balance of 
risks, the committee voted to unanimously raise the 
fed funds rate. 
The minutes point to an additional rate hike this year, 
in an attempt to make monetary policy somewhat 
more contractionary. PNC’s baseline forecast is that 
the FOMC keeps the fed funds rate unchanged at its 
next meeting on October 27 and 28, just a week 
before the midterm elections. PNC then expects 
another 25-basis-point hike in the fed funds rate at 
the Committee’s mid-December meeting, with 
another rate hike in mid-March. This would take the 
fed funds rate to a range of 4.25% to 4.50% by the 
early spring of next year.
