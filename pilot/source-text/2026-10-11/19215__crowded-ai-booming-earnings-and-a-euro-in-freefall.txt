The market's contradiction
AI is crowded, concentration is extreme and the rest of the market is struggling.
Yet the broader upside setup is getting interesting.
Earnings remain powerful, positioning has cooled, seasonality is turning bullish and vol has collapsed.
The risks are obvious. So is the upside.
The asymmetry changed
Back in late August, the AI setup was unusually clean: positioning was light, implied vol was low and
macro uncertainty was pushing capital toward the few parts of the market still delivering growth.
That setup has changed.
GS points out that since August 27, the S&P 500 is up 1.3%, while the S&P excluding the AI names is
down more than 5%.
The gap between the two is now close to the widest since the AI trade really began in early 2023.
AI hasn't just been leading the market. It has been holding the index together.

Everyone found the same shelter
There is another important difference versus six weeks ago: positioning.
GS Prime data shows Mag 7 exposure has climbed to around 22% of total US exposure, the highest
level since their data begins in 2022.
Tech was also the only US sector net bought by hedge funds in September, with the largest monthly
buying since February 2025.
The AI story may still be working. It just isn't the lonely trade anymore. More here.

Nobody told earnings
Everyone has been waiting for the earnings cycle to slow.
Consensus finally has it happening in Q3, with S&P 500 earnings growth expected to decelerate from
34% to roughly 27%.
That is already the highest consensus growth estimate entering an earnings season on record outside
recession recoveries.
And Deutsche still thinks it's too low.
They see growth holding at an extraordinary 34%, putting their forecast around 6 percentage points
above consensus. More here.

AI refuses to slow
The biggest earnings engine isn't showing many signs of cooling either.
Deutsche points out that semiconductor production and exports across South Korea and Taiwan
accelerated again in Q3, while GPU rental prices continued rising.
They see MCG & Tech earnings growth holding around 54% YoY.
The AI earnings machine is still running hot.

Where are the longs?
SPX is sitting around record highs, but long/short appetite has been moving the other way.
That's hardly the positioning backdrop you would normally associate with an equity market pressing
against all-time highs.
If earnings deliver, there is still plenty of potential fuel sitting on the sidelines.
Price looks euphoric. Positioning doesn't.

The calendar turns bullish
And then there is the calendar.
Deutsche notes that the S&P has rallied in the fourth quarter in 21 of the past 23 midterm-election
years, with an average gain of roughly 7%.
Earnings season itself has historically carried a positive bias as well. With positioning far from euphoric
and earnings potentially surprising higher, the seasonal tailwind suddenly matters a lot more.

Use cheap convexity
This may be the most interesting part of the setup.
Equity vol has imploded just as earnings season arrives, positioning remains relatively cautious and
one of the strongest seasonal windows of the year begins.
Chasing SPX at all-time highs may not feel particularly attractive.
Using cheaper vol to structure the upside is a different proposition.
One way to express that view is the SPY Nov 780/820 call spread, which offers defined upside exposure
into the earnings and seasonal window.
