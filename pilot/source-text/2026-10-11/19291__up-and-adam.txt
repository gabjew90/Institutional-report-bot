In contrast to the S&P 500’s near record high, the Russell 2000 is down 9% from its Aug. 14 record high amid the jump in bond yields. 
Technical momentum has also deteriorated, with the index breaking below an uptrend line that’s been in place since the April 2025 
Liberation Day lows. While the 200-day moving average is an important near-term support level, we continue to favor large-caps given 
their more diversified revenue streams, stronger margins, greater supply-chain flexibility, and lower sensitivity to borrowing costs. 
The $39 billion auction drew exceptionally strong demand, clearing at 5.30%, the highest yield since 2000 but 1.7 bps below prevailing 
market levels. The bid-to-cover ratio, a gauge of overall demand, rose to 2.77, its highest since January 2016. Indirect bidders, a proxy 
for foreign demand, took down 80% while dealers were left holding just 2.5%—a record low for a 10-year auction. The results suggest 
investor appetite for US Treasuries remains robust with yields at multi-decade highs. On deck: today’s $22 billion 30-year auction.
