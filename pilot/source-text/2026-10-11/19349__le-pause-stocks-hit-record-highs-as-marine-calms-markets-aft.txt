 
'Le Pause': Stocks Hit Record Highs As Marine 
Calms Markets After The Storm... 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, OCT 07, 2026 - 02:58 AM 
Tl;dr: Today saw a pause (however brief, we'll take it) in the chaos as a fiscally responsible 
budget from France's leading contender to be the next French President calmed EU angst 
(shadow budget savings), which helped lower bond yields, lower the dollar (easing financial 
conditions) and lifted stocks (SPX/NDX record highs on GOOG energy deal, RTY lagged). Oil 
was supportive (lower) early on but attacks/explosions triggered a rebound back to unch. Gold 
was up, bitcoin down, and VIX hit a 14 handle. 
Breathe... as some decoupled serenity came to markets... 
 
Early on it was a classic 'oil down, yields down, stocks up' day but then big boom headlines 
pushed oil up (stocks down, yields shrugged)... 

 
 
So, where to start - let's go with the energy complex since that was the most active 
Energy 
Oil eased overnight on logistics, not peace, as MidEast exports are proving resilient (G7 is 
releasing emergency stockpiles, and Hormuz crude flows are reportedly near 76% of prewar 
levels). However, the diesel shortage persists as Ukraine and Russia trade drone attacks on 
refineries and infrastructure. 
Diplomatically it's a stalemate: there has been no direct US-Iran confrontation since the UN 
General Assembly, which markets are treating as good enough. That is a fragile basis for a rally, 
as Iranian tanker attacks are still threatening the export rebound with explosions heard from the 
sea at the key Qeshm Island facility. 

 
 
Yet, as Bloomberg macro strategist, Michael Ball notes, more oil moving doesn’t mean the 
system has normalized. 
Higher volumes are being achieved through more expensive and operationally complex 
workarounds as attacks persist. The CEO of Vitol estimates roughly 12 million barrels a day of 
crude and another 2 million of products have recently exited the strait, near the 10 million-14 
million barrels of crude it says are needed to balance the market into winter. He also warns that 
Western inventories have effectively been exhausted after months of offsetting the disruption. 
The bigger problem remains further down the barrel. Middle East product exports have 
recovered more slowly, while Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries have pushed Moscow to 
export more crude while restricting diesel sales. Heating-oil and gasoil cracks back near their 
recent highs show just how strained refining capacity remains. The G7’s planned release of as 
much as 100 million barrels of crude and diesel provides a bridge, not new structural supply. 

 
 
Crude curves reflect very little relief: Brent backwardation is high (though implied volatility and 
call skew have eased). 
 
The oil market is loosening from exceptionally tight conditions but remains far from being 
oversupplied, with recovering crude flows masking how little margin for error remains in refined 
products. 

 
Nevertheless, the rebound in crude today was shrugged off by bonds... 
Rates 
French bond yields (and spreads) compressed today as the market desperately reached for the 
pause button after Le Pen's shadow budget offered some hope for fiscal sense... though the 
election is a long way off still and nothing is set. The OAT/Bund spread is 30bps narrower... 
 
That eased the panic selling around the world (which had actually led to some small haven 
flows into the US) but overall offered term premium relief for UST yields (also aided by lower oil 
prices overnight). 
Some are calling this France's "Liz Truss moment", meaning the market forced a policy reversal. 
That matters beyond France because it shows what the pressure valve looks like when a 
sovereign's long end gets disorderly, which is the same question hanging over Treasuries.  
Rate-hike odds were flat today. 
Bonds were also reassured by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent who insisted the government’s 
debt load can be tamed, telling investors that a mix of economic growth and spending restraints 
will “very quickly” start to alter the path of US government borrowing. Speaking at a fireside chat 
in Pennsylvania on Monday night, he said the government would start “bending that curve.” 
By the close, UST yields were lower (ignoring the bound back in oil prices) 

 
 
TSY yields ignored the rebound in crude this afternoon... 
“To see a meaningful rally across the curve, the number one thing you need to see is energy
