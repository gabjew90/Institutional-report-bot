The weekend top 10 list
Stocks may be holding up, but the rest of the market is telling a rather different story. Credit risk is
surging, banks are getting crushed, hedge funds have retreated to the sidelines, and market breadth is
approaching levels last seen during the dot-com bubble. Here are ten charts worth watching.
Oracle's 20% problem
Oracle’s 5-year CDS trades near a record 261bps, implying a roughly 20% probability of default over
the next five years (assuming a 40% recovery rate).
Bloomberg
The weak get weaker
US CCC-rated credit spreads have breached 1,000bps, while the ratio of CCC spreads to overall high-
yield spreads has reached a record high. The credit market is increasingly punishing its weakest
borrowers.
MS
MS
Only worse in 2000
S&P 500 market breadth has deteriorated to levels rarely seen in four decades. The only worse
reading came around the dot-com bubble. A remarkable divergence between headline index
performance and the broader market.

Bank run
Nowhere is that weakness more apparent than in the banking sector. Financials have suffered their
largest weekly outflow since March 2026.

As goes the banks...
US banks are now the most oversold relative to the S&P 500 since 1993, according to Morgan Stanley.
An extraordinary relative momentum extreme.

Hedge funds at zero
Hedge fund long/short ratio is in the 0th percentile on a 1-year look-back.

The rally nobody owns
The rally has been painful for underexposed hedge funds. Goldman reports that fundamental
long/short net exposure sits in just the 2nd percentile of its five-year history.
GS Prime
1 sigma negative
Goldman's US Equity Sentiment Indicator has fallen to -1.3 standard deviations, confirming that
positioning has shifted decisively toward the bearish end of its historical range.
The bottom line: credit is nervous, breadth is deteriorating, and banks are under pressure. But with
hedge funds already positioned at historic extremes, the bigger paradoxical question may not be
whether investors are worried — it's whether there is anyone left to sell.
