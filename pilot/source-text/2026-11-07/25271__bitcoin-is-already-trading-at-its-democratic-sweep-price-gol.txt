So much for "Uptober." 
Bitcoin's most reliable seasonal tailwind ended up as a headwind in 2026: BTC finished October 
down 7.4% at $77,401, ether lost 16.2% and solana 19.3%, while the US-listed spot ETFs for all 
three bled a combined $2.3 billion. And most of the damage came in the final week, courtesy of 
North Korea's elite hackers walking out of Binance with $6.3 billion in about 20 minutes. 
That's the backdrop to the latest Goldman monthly crypto data pack from Dominika Nestarcova 
and Won Gi Hong on the bank's FICC desk in Singapore  "GS Crypto: Monthly On-chain & 
Market Data (Oct 2026)", available to pro subs), which landed just as Americans were finishing 
voting in a midterm that could hand the Senate Banking gavel to the crypto industry's favorite 
villain. Here is how the desk summed up the month: 
"October 2026 broke from Bitcoin's historical 'Uptober' strength, with BTC ending the month 
-7.4% MoM, and ETH & SOL down -16.2% and -19.3% MoM respectively. The bulk of the move 
came during the final week due to security incidents, and the outflows from the US-listed spot 
ETFs did not help either, with the products for BTC, ETH and SOL each marking net aggregate 
outflows of -

North Korea Stole Uptober 
First, the month in one table. Spot ETF assets fell 8.4% to $98.2 billion, and ETF flows swung 
from a $2.39 billion inflow in September to a $550 million outflow in October. Year-over-year, 
BTC is down 29% (chart source GS FICC, Coinmetrics, Bloomberg): 
 
The week before Halloween started well enough, with ICE agreeing to bring Hyperliquid-based 
perps onshore and BTC briefly back at $81.8K. Then, as Goldman's Kenny Lee wrote in his 
Weekly Recap ("Roller Coaster", also available to pro subs), the floor fell out: 
"That positive impulse was then overshadowed by the Binance breach late on Thursday, which 
triggered selling and forced liquidations across crypto... the largest crypto hack on record, far 
exceeding the previous record of roughly $1.5b from the Bybit hack." 
As we reported in real time, blockchain sleuths at TRM and Chainalysis quickly pinned the theft 
on North Korea's TraderTraitor unit, and the FBI confirmed it over the weekend. Bitcoin hit a 
six-week low near $76,350. Binance then plugged most of the hole with its own cash; by 
Sunday it said it had covered about 95% of the affected tokens, while industry players including 
Tether and Circle helped freeze roughly $3 billion of the loot. It didn't stop the stampede: 
Goldman analyst James Yaro noted on Monday that $19.9 billion, or 14% of assets on the 
platform, have left Binance since the disclosure, and total crypto market cap is down 4% since 
the day before the hack. 
Of course, the timing was impeccable, as we noted at the time: 
Here is the part that should worry the bulls more than one (admittedly enormous) heist. Lee 
points out that the ETF selling in ETH was there before Binance got breached: 
"Unlike BTC, ETH had already been losing ETF demand before the Binance incident, indicating 
that its weaker demand profile extended beyond the immediate reaction to the breach." 
BTC ETFs, by contrast, took in $441 million in the four sessions before the hack... then gave 
back $643 million on Friday alone. 
 

 
Ether Is Now Trading At Its Cost Basis 
Which brings us to the ugliest table in the pack. ETH ended October at $2,252, against a 
realized price (the average price at which every ETH last moved, i.e. the market's aggregate 
cost basis) of $2,213. That's an MVRV ratio of 1.02: the average ether holder is now roughly 
breakeven. ETF assets fell 19% in the month to $13.2 billion after $1.6 billion of outflows, CME 
open interest is down 65% from a year ago, and trusted spot volumes collapsed 17% even as 
prices fell (chart source GS FICC, Coinmetrics, Bloomberg): 
 
For comparison, bitcoin's MVRV is still 1.43, with a realized price of about $54,000. Put 
differently, BTC holders have a 30% cushion; ETH holders have about 2%. 
The flip side is that nobody is moving their ether. Goldman's ETH "chart of the month" shows the 
share of supply active over the past year sliding to roughly 34%, the lowest in the five-year 
window shown, even as price rolled over: 
 
Staking keeps grinding higher too: 44.15 million ETH is now staked, up 23.6% YoY, or roughly 
36% of supply, with the yield stable around 3.3% since ETH issuance adjusts to the amount 
staked. ETH burnt jumped 72% MoM: 
 

 
 
Diamond hands, or just nobody left to sell to at a loss? We'll know once price tests that $2,213 
cost basis. 
Solana: Faster Blocks, Smaller Yields 
Solana had the worst month of the big three. SOL fell 19.3% to $95.29, down 49% YoY, and lost 
12.9% against BTC. Spot ETFs, barely a year old, saw $144 million of outflows, shrinking AUM 
by 23% to $1.4 billion, and both spot and CME volumes fell 28.7% (chart source GS FICC, 
Coinmetrics, Bloomberg): 
 
The structural story here is yield. Unlike ETH, most SOL staking rewards come from a fixed 
disinflation schedule that cuts new issuance by 15% a year, "recently voted to accelerate to 
30%." The result: SOL's staking yield is down 20.6% YoY to 5.29%, while staked supply rose a 
"modest" 5.1%.: 
 
None of which stopped the engineers. Solana activated Alpenglow on October 21, cutting 
finality "from several seconds to a fraction of a second," while Ethereum pushed its 
Glamsterdam upgrade onto the Sepolia testnet, aiming to "safely triple baseline processing 
capacity." In Goldman's words, Solana is "cementing its competitive advantage in 
ultra-low-latency finality" while Ethereum builds the "high-capacity settlement layer." 
Great tech, terrible tape. Diversification at its finest. 
 

 
Leverage Didn't Even Blink 
The curious thing about the biggest theft in financial history is how little the derivatives crowd 
cared. Per Lee, liquidations across perpetual futures on October 29-30 reached roughly $2 
billion ("notable, but still relatively contained given the scale of the breach"), BTC perp open 
interest fell just 0.55% versus a 3.66% price drop, and annualized BTC funding stayed elevated 
at ~10% all week: 
"The relatively modest liquidation totals, limited reduction in open interest, and still-high funding 
suggest that bullish leveraged positioning had not been especially stretched going into the 
episode, and that perpetuals traders on aggregate treated the pullback as an opportunity to add 
exposure rather than cut risk." 
Meanwhile, on-chain, miners are still not dumping. Miner revenue rose 8.9% MoM and flows 
from miners to exchanges fell another 6.6%, down 79% YoY (chart source GS FICC, 
Coinmetrics): 
 
Exchange balances, roughly 2.65 million BTC, are well below the 3 million-plus of 2023-24: 
 
So, to sum up: holders aren't selling, miners aren't selling, leveraged longs are buying the dip... 
and price still fell 7%. That's what happens when the marginal buyer, the ETF, disappears. 
Which brings us to tonight. 
 

 
Chairwoman Warren? 
Regular readers know how we got here. On September 15, the Senate blocked the CLARITY 
Act 49-50, with every single Democrat voting against even starting debate on the crypto market 
structure bill. The sticking points: ethics language covering the president's own crypto income, 
and stablecoin yield. 
Goldman's equity sales team summed up the pricing this morning in "Midterms - what is priced 
in?" (available to pro subs): "A Democratic House looks largely priced, with the Senate 
representing the main source of election related surprise," with prediction markets at ~77% for a 
Democratic House. That's where crypto's real exposure sits. TD Cowen's Jaret Seiberg laid out 
the three scenarios last week, and the crypto-specific ranking is the mirror image of the polls: 
●​ Democratic sweep, which he calls "the most negative scenario for bitcoin and the 
broader crypto sector": Elizabeth Warren takes the Senate Banking gavel and runs tough 
oversight of the regulators; CLARITY is effectively dead for two years. 
●​ Split Congress (GOP Senate): Tim Scott keeps Banking, Maxine Waters might trade 
pieces of market structure for investor protections. Better for bitcoin. 
●​ Republican sweep: the "best path for crypto policy," with a wider Senate majority 
possibly reviving CLARITY in 2027-28. 
But here's the twist: Seiberg also concedes that "Warren cannot stop regulators from adopting 
policies that favor the industry," and Trump would veto any bill clipping his regulators' wings. 
Which is precisely what happened the last time Democrats killed a crypto bill. Three days after 
CLARITY died, the SEC issued its tokenization order and cryptos ripped: 
Bernstein made the same argument back in August: a failed bill means a "knee-jerk" selloff first, 
then more rule-making from the SEC and CFTC under Project Crypto. (Bernstein also expected 
crypto to "bottom and start showing momentum towards late Q3 and early Q4 prior to the 
mid-terms." October would like a word.) Goldman FX strategist Stuart Jenkins agrees the policy 
range is narrow, since the President "can continue to lean on Executive action instead of 
Legislation." 
And how much of the worst case is already in the price? A lot, by one measure. Bloomberg's 
Markets Pulse survey of 301 pros in early October put the median year-end BTC call at $90,831 
under a GOP sweep, $84,141 under a split Congress and $76,456 under a Democratic sweep. 
Bitcoin closed October at $77,401: 
 

 
 
Put differently, bitcoin is already trading at its "blue wave" price, and has been for a week. Which 
is either a sign the market has done the work, or a reminder that surveys tend to follow price, 
not the other way around. 
 
As for last night, it was bloodbath for Republicans with Texas (Senate and Governor raising 
anxiety over data-centers) and Ohio (vehement crypto-sceptic Sherrod Brown) falling to 
Democrats. 
Then again, Goldman's crypto equity team flagged one more problem the hack created, 
regardless of who wins: "There could be a yet higher bar in any future crypto regulation or 
 

 
legislation." Nothing says "regulatory clarity" like a $6 billion North Korean heist landing five 
days before Chairwoman Warren's job interview. 
Priced For The Worst, Positioned For The Best 
So where does that leave crypto? The bulls will say every weak hand is gone: miners are 
hoarding, ether supply is dormant, leverage just shrugged off history's largest theft, and the 
regulatory downside is capped by a White House that can (and will) keep doing it by executive 
order. As Michael Saylor put it after CLARITY died, "The only clarity you need is Bitcoin." 
We are less sanguine. The one buyer that powered the last leg higher, the ETF, has gone 
missing, ETH is sitting on its cost basis with no flow cushion, and a Democratic Senate means 
two years of subpoenas aimed at the very regulators the industry is now relying on. Add perps 
funding at 10% into a falling market, and the "positioning is clean" argument looks a lot like 
positioning is long and hasn't noticed yet. Our bet: the blue wave is largely priced, but the flush 
probably isn't, especially if ETH's $2,213 cost basis gives way.
