Meta Muse and Instinct: Product Deep Dive + Contribution
To META + Benefits & Risks
September 14, 2026
 
The Wolfe Byte
Meta's launch of Muse & popularity of Instinct have created fears of platform & marketplace disruption. We est.
Muse to offer $30-$50B rev opportunity in the M/LT for Meta. This note discusses: a) how these agents work; b)
risks & benefits; & c) why some fears are overdone.
 
Since Meta's launch of Muse on Sept. 8, and the rising adoption of Instinct, several names within Internet universe have
sold off (EXPE -5.4%, BKNG -9.0%, DASH -3.5%, SHOP -8.9%, UBER -5.2%). A large part of this underperformance
is due to fears related to agents (like Muse and Instinct) offering a turn-key, easy to use solutions to regular, non-
developer users, enabling them to allow agents to take action on their behalf from booking flights, ordering rides,
to comparison shopping.
Our team used both agents extensively - Muse (available to everyone in the U.S.) and Instinct (invite-only) - to
understand how these agents compared to each other, performed against key use cases, and if the fears around the
risks they impose on marketplaces (Travel/OTAs, ride-sharing, food delivery, and eCommerce platforms) were valid.
What are these agents - Muse and Instinct?: For the first time ever, these are turnkey, ready to use agents that all
regular users (non-developers) can simply download and/or activate, allowing agents to take actions on their behalf -
i.e. browse mobile web pages + comparison shop + unsubscribe to emails + integrate credit card information and
book a ride or plane ticket + place a delivery order.
Impact of Muse on Meta - we estimate $30 - $50B revenue opportunity: a) subscription: $30B opportunity M/LT -
at 5% of 3B users subscribing at $20/month; b) take rate model: Assuming 5% of global eCommerce penetration (ex-
China) and a 3% - 5% take rate implies M/HSD $B revenue. Meta's current advantages are clear: a) Data - decades of
user data across the world on personal preferences; b) Distribution - 3B+ users across apps; c) First mover advantage
- no competing consumer agents from OAI or Google. That said consumer trust with Meta is lower than Google as it
relates to credit card information - per our prelim survey results. Google (and OAI + Anthropic) will likely release
similar agents.
Similarities between Muse and Instinct: Both agents are: a) OpenClaw-like; b) offer turnkey solutions; c) take action
immediately for users; and d) are text based.
Differences between Muse & Instinct: a) Muse is free to use (up-to a limit and then subscription prices apply) and can
be downloaded via app in the U.S. Instinct is invite-only and global; b) Muse is a fully-functioning app with several
features and Instinct is set up via a text message conversation in WhatsApp or SMS via phone with email functionality.

Key benefits of these agents:
●Saves time for high intent actions: When a user knows exactly what they want and/or when they know their
preferences, the ability of an agent to act on the user’s behalf is highly effective and efficient.
●Personalization: Companies that have a data advantage can make agents highly personalized. Muse distinctly
has personalization aspects already embedded in the app, and as users engage more with the agent, the
personalization would continue to improve.
●Small business creation and efficiency gains: Many small/medium-sized businesses do not have structured
back-office processes or scaled customer service operations. Also, new ideas could be translated directly into
action using agents enabling faster business creation. Personal agents can work as 24x7 customer service reps
+ personal agents for day-to-day users.
Key Risks for eComemerce, Ride-Sharing, and Food Delivery.:
●Advertising revenue pressure from lost traffic if eyeballs shift to agents, risking Ad revenue.
●Take rate pressure: If an agent becomes a material demand channel, 3P platforms that plug into platforms like
Muse and Instinct may need to pay for the conversion of traffic.
●Market share loss should long-tail retailers surface more, taking away traffic from large platforms or if competing
platforms for rideshare or food delivery surface more through agents.
●Commoditization of marketplaces: Greater competition across rideshare and food delivery options if consumers
become platform agnostic (i.e. they request the agent to choose the best ride or food delivery option based on
their preferences)
Our view: While we acknowledge the risks to eCommerce, food delivery, and rideshare players, we believe the
magnitude of risk expectations may be a bit overdone. Here is why we believe the risk is MODEST (better-than-feared):
●Power of the full value chain: Amazon offers fulfillment + delivery + inventory management + returns + customer
service – all of which create a deep moat. UberEats and DoorDash offer delivery + returns + customer service +
ETA + promotions/loyalty benefits + increasingly inventory & fulfillment services – all of which create a deep moat.
●Data advantage: We expect Amazon, Shopify, Etsy, eBay, Uber, DoorDash, and Instacart to be highly protective
of their decades of consumer product search and purchase data that is unique to the platform.
●Platform-based AI agents: Amazon has Alexa for example, Uber/DoorDash/Instacart have their own in-app
agents, that will continue to drive engagement and conversion on their respective platforms.
●Depth and breadth of product offering: Shopify has the broadest and the deepest product offerings with very
high merchant retention rates.
●Unique inventory & vertical position: Etsy & eBay’s presence in unique products & categories including trading
cards + P&A + recommerce creates a unique moat specific to those platforms.
●Benefit of browsing: Vast majority of customers browse their food options before making a decision. High intent
food orders account for minority of total, increasing the need for consumers to land on marketplace platforms.
●Three-way MP & Supply moat: UberEats, DoorDash, and Instacart have integrated well with merchants and
grocers, which is very difficult to replicate at scale. In order for agents to integrate with that supply, the best option
is for them to integrate with the marketplaces. Three-way marketplaces have deeper moats than two-way.
●Subscription & cross platform moat – share economics only on incremental orders: 50MM Uber One members
account for over 50% of Bookings, 35MM+ DashPass members account for meaningful % of total GOV – unless
orders through agents are incremental, the 3P marketplaces are LESS likely to share economics with agents;
i.e. if I am already a DashPass or UberOne member, placing an order through an agent, they get the order
either way + data + less incentive to share economics (it’s not incremental).

Muse and Instinct: How They Work – Examples Of Use Cases 
Muse:  
• 
Product: Meta launched Muse – it is a personal AI agent (OpenClaw-like), a standalone app that is a turnkey solution 
to take action on behalf of the user.  
• 
Release date: September 8th, 2026. 
• 
Availability: Currently U.S. only 
• 
Who can use the agent: Users on iOS, Android, muse.ai, and via Meta’s AI glasses soon. 
• 
Adoption & scale: Top 3 on the iOS App (as of September 9th, 2026). 
• 
Monetization: Currently free for all users up to certain token usage. i) Meta will have two subscription tiers – Power 
($20/month, 500MM Muse tokens/week) and Maximum ($100/month, 3B Muse tokens/week). ii) We anticipate Meta 
will also have a take rate model with businesses that plug into Muse. 
Instinct: 
• 
Product: Instinct is also a personal agent that connects to a user’s phone applications and web sources to converse 
via text messaging (SMS or WhatsApp) and calling. The product is currently available in private access (invite only 
and/or waitlist). 
• 
Release date: Late August 2026. 
• 
Who can use the agent: Users who are given access on iOS and Android through texting and calling. 
• 
User count, engagement, & scale: 305K total visits from June – August (Similarweb). 
• 
Monetization: It is currently available for free, future monetization methods unclear. 
Why this matters: Both Muse and Instinct are the first turnkey personal AI agents that enable non-developer users 
(regular users) to activate and allow them act on their behalf. We think this is especially relevant for Meta as they have 
3B+ users globally.

Benefits & Risks: Our View On Agents’ Disruption Potential   
Key Benefits Of Agents: 
1. Saves Time For High Intent Actions: 
• 
When a user knows exactly what they want and/or when they know their preferences, the ability of an agent to act 
on the user’s behalf is highly effective and efficient.  
Examples: an agent can comparison shop across web pages and apps + un-subscribe users from email lists + book 
travel and manage trips + respond to emails and actually send them.  
 
2. Personalization: 
• 
Companies that have a data advantage can make agents highly personalized. Muse distinctly has personalization 
aspects already embedded in the app, and as users engage more with the agent, the personalization would 
continue to improve.  
Example: i) email responses are summarized and phrased per user preferences; ii) food orders account for dietary 
restrictions and allergies; iii) travel recommendations and booking for time slots, airline and hotel preferences; iv) 
comparison shopping for user preferences (price vs. reviews vs. delivery speed vs. U.S. made).  
 
3. Small Business Creation and Efficiency Gains 
• 
Many small/medium-sized businesses do not have structured back-office processes or scaled customer service 
operations. Also, new ideas could be translated directly into action using agents enabling faster business creation.  
• 
Examples: An agent can help with inventory management + customer service + new business creation + customer 
acquisition + online web/app management. 
 
4. 24/7 Assistance 
• 
Peronsonal agents can work as 24x7 customer service reps + personal agents for day-to-day users.  
Examples: Agents can work into the night on deeper analyses & research and act in the morning upon user approval 
+ global businesses can offer 24x7 customer support to customers around the world, around the clock.  
 
5. Meta’s Unique Advantages 
• 
Data + Distribution + Personalization + A Head Start: Meta has over 3B users globally across apps and user data 
is unique to Meta, globally – that allows for scale and distribution advantage. Against that, Meta has been 
exceptionally good with personalization – arguably one of the best product platforms in the world – and with a head 
start on consumer agents, Meta could take a lead on personal agents.  
Examples: Muse agent can see that a user liked a new restaurant opening on Instagram and can remind them of 
said restaurant as the weekend approaches. Muse can then subsequently book the reservation at the restaurant 
and send the user the plans.

Key Risks: There is rising fear that the value prop across Travel, Ride Sharing, Food Delievery, and eCommerce will be 
marginalized with the rise of agents. From most at risk to least at risk the market believes: Travel is most exposed 
followed by Ride Sharing, then eCommerce, and then Food Delivery.  
Below, we share our thoughts on key risks and why some of these risks may be a bit overdone.  
Key Risks – eCommerce 
• 
Advertising revenue pressure from lost traffic if eyeballs shift to agents, risking Ad revenue.   
• 
Take rate pressure: If an agent becomes a material demand channel, 3P platforms that plug into platforms like 
Muse and Instinct may need to pay for the conversion of traffic. 
• 
Market share loss should long-tail retailers surface more, taking away traffic from large platforms.  
Our View: While we acknowledge the risks to eCommerce players, we believe the magnitude of risk expectations may be a 
bit overdone. Here is why we believe the risk is MODEST (better-than-feared) for eCommerce:  
• 
Power of the full value chain: Platforms like Amazon offer fulfillment + delivery + inventory management + returns 
+ customer service – all of which create a deep moat. 
• 
Data advantage: We expect Amazon (and others) to be highly protective of their decades of consumer product 
search and purchase data that is unique to the platform. 
• 
Platform-based AI agents: Amazon has Alexa for example, its in-app agent, and we expect every large eCommerce 
platform to have their own, unique AI agent for consumers/buyers and merchants/sellers. 
• 
Depth and breadth of product offering: Platforms like Shopify have the broadest and the deepest product offerings 
with very high merchant retention rates – we don’t expect merchants to unsubscribe or shift away from the platform 
towards standalone agents. 
• 
Unique inventory & vertical position: Etsy & eBay’s presence in unique products & categories including trading 
cards + P&A + recommerce creates a unique moat specific to those platforms. 
Key Risks – Delivery 
• 
Take rate pressure: If an agent becomes a material demand channel, 3P platforms that plug into platforms like 
Muse and Instinct may need to pay for the conversion of traffic. 
• 
Commoditization of marketplaces: Greater competition across delivery options if consumers become platform 
agnostic (i.e. they request the agent to choose the best delivery platform based on user preferences). 
• 
High margin advertising revenue headwinds: Should eyeballs shift to agents, away from delivery platforms.  
Our View: While we acknowledge the risks to Delivery players, we believe the magnitude of risk expectations may be a bit 
overdone, especially for food delivery. Here is why we believe the risk is MODEST (better-than-feared):  
• 
Power of the full value chain: Platforms like UberEats and DoorDash offer delivery + returns + customer service 
+ ETA + promotions/loyalty benefits + increasingly inventory & fulfillment services – all of which create a deep moat. 
• 
Data advantage: We expect Uber, DoorDash, and Instacart to be highly protective of their decades of consumer 
product search and purchase data that is unique to the platform. 
• 
Platform-based AI agents: All three delivery platforms have their own in-app agent, and we expect users to 
increasingly leverage those agents to drive cart fulfillment + place orders. 
• 
Benefit of browsing: Vast majority of customers browse their food options before making a decision. High intent 
food & beverage orders account for minority of orders, likely increasing the need for consumers to land on 
marketplace platforms to find options.
