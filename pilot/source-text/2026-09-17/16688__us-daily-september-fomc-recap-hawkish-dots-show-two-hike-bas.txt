September FOMC Recap: Hawkish Dots Show Two-Hike Baseline; Adding an October Hike 
 
 
The FOMC raised the funds rate by 25bp to 3.75-4% at its September meeting today. 
Chairman Warsh attributed the decision to hike primarily to stronger labor market and 
activity data, “inflation summer trends [that] weren’t passing the test,” and the effect of 
geopolitics on commodity prices. 
The meeting was more hawkish than we expected in a few ways. First, a 16-2 majority 
projected at least one more hike this year, with four participants projecting two more 
hikes this year, against our expectation that a narrow majority would show no additional 
hikes. There were no dissenting votes against today’s hike, against our expectation of 
one dissent. 
Second, the median funds rate projection remained quite elevated all the way through 
2029, where it remains at 3.5-3.75%, noticeably above the median neutral rate 
projection (Exhibit 1). In addition, the median neutral rate estimate rose more than we 
expected from 3.06% to 3.25% (vs. our forecast of 3.19%), a larger jump than is usually 
seen at a single meeting. 
Third, Chairman Warsh described the hike as “removing a dose of accommodation” 
three times, which might imply that he thinks that the neutral rate is above the 
3.5-3.75% range where the funds rate stood before today, though he declined to give a 
neutral rate estimate and said that the neutral rate does not have any “operational effect 
on decisions that we make today.” He also said that other FOMC participants “widely 
shared” the view he had previously expressed that broad financial conditions are not 
restrictive.

most likely timing for the next hike because it is more natural to deliver hikes that the 
FOMC presented today as supporting “a timelier return” to the 2% target at consecutive 
meetings rather than at an every-other-meeting pace. 
We have kept our forecast of the terminal rate unchanged at 3.25-3.5% by adding to the 
September and December 2027 cuts we already expected a third cut in March 2028. 
The high 2029 median dot presents an upside risk to our forecast because it might signal 
that the FOMC sees the appropriate terminal rate for the coming years as above the long 
run neutral rate, perhaps because it expects the AI boom to keep investment demand 
stronger than usual. But it is also possible that participants simply saw projecting a 
higher path for the funds rate for now as a way of signaling a strong commitment to 
bringing inflation down and will rethink it later when inflation returns to the target. 
Market pricing of the probability of further hikes this year rose 8pp to 51% for October 
and 9pp to 78% for December today, with particularly strong reactions to the release of 
the dots and the “removed a dose of accommodation” comment (Exhibit 2). We suspect 
that some investors could see the midterm elections in early November as more of an 
impediment to a hike at the October meeting than we do. We think that the midterms 
are less of an obstacle to a second hike than they might be for an initial hike.

Additional hikes beyond October are possible but not our base case. One reason is that 
our forecast for core PCE inflation remains below the median FOMC participant’s 
forecast at 3.2% (vs. 3.4% for the FOMC) in 2026 Q4/Q4 and 2.2% (vs. 2.5% for the 
FOMC) in 2027 Q4/Q4.  
Some of the gap in 2026 inflation forecasts could come from a reluctance on the part of 
some FOMC participants to pencil in a downward revision from methodological revisions 
that will be implemented later this month, which we estimate will be worth -0.2pp on the 
year-over-year rate. Warsh noted that the Fed Board staff’s forecast for year-over-year 
core PCE inflation is 3.2% in August, which matches our forecast on a rounded basis and 
suggests a similar estimate of the impact of the revisions. But the impact is uncertain, 
and some FOMC participants might have preferred not to incorporate it yet.
