What exactly is going on in tech?
That may sound like a strange question in the middle of the greatest technological investment boom in
decades. But the more charts we look at, the less obvious the answer becomes.
As Goldman's Peter Callahan puts it:
"All-in, has felt like growing tension around what the ‘central’ debate is in AI / Tech right now that the
market is ‘trying’ to solve (bottoms up? top down? both? neither?) – and, in turn, locally, if earnings can
help provide price stability (and, in turn, confidence) at a time when investors are looking for a signal
…"
On the one hand, Apple is flirting with $5 trillion, fund managers are the most overweight tech since
2020, and just two semiconductor companies are expected to generate 36% of S&P 500 earnings
growth.
On the other hand, semis have suffered their longest losing streak in 17 months, market breadth is
deteriorating, and Deutsche Bank is warning that the latest tech rally may be running out of steam.
Is this a healthy rotation, a market running out of steam, or simply the growing pains of the AI
revolution? We don't have the answer. But we do have the charts.

Megacap correlation
And here is where things get particularly interesting. Nvidia, Apple, Alphabet and Microsoft now
account for roughly 33% of the S&P 500, up from just 9% in 2015.
Yet their average three-month correlation has collapsed to a mere 3.8%.
A third of the market concentrated in four stocks that increasingly can't agree on which direction to go.
How's that for diversification?

Charts are like arseholes
Perhaps the most interesting thing about tech right now is that almost any argument can be supported
by a convincing chart.
Bullish? Look at AI earnings growth. Bearish? Look at valuations and deteriorating breadth. Expecting a
rotation? Apple is already behaving defensively while semis stumble.
The charts aren't necessarily contradicting each other. They may simply be telling us that several very
different things are happening at once.
Welcome to tech in 2026. Everybody has a chart. Nobody has the full picture.
