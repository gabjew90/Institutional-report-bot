remains off YTD highs with choppy price action in recent months. We are bullish on

monetization strategy in this environment.

Report: Weekly Wrap | HFs buy Banks and sell Semis, while EU Positioning 
Low vs. US 
  
Summary: At a high level, there weren’t major changes this past week with HF 
performance still challenging, flows lackluster, and positioning neutral in US 
vs. fairly low in Europe.  However, more focus was arguably below the surface with 
EU Banks continuing to sell off as concerns around France remain elevated, oil still 
volatile and Energy stocks outperforming, Semis taking a hit on Thursday, small caps 
still underperforming, and Biotech selling off again.  While there are many things to 
potentially watch next week with earnings and CPI coming up, the keys from our 
side include whether Banks can rebound (as they typically do in 4Q), if Semis / 
AI can hold up after positioning generally increased last month, and if Europe 
can stabilize 

Key Points: 
• HF performance — October still choppy: HF performance remains 
challenging in Oct (-0.8% MTD) as long books are still dragging and shorts 
aren’t offsetting. Quants have cooled to ~flat MTD (vs. strong gains last 
month). Equity L/S is the main laggard (-1.2% globally) and EU managers are 
feeling the most pain (-1.3% MTD), with EU Financials (Banks) a notable 
detractor via negative crowding returns. 
• Flows — flat HF activity; retail still soft while ETF seasonality improves: 
Aggregate HF flows were lackluster with turnover ~0.5z below average and 
global gross/net flows both roughly flat. Net flows also look increasingly 
correlated to markets in the US (buying into up days late last week/Monday, 
then turning more negative Thursday), suggesting HFs could amplify market 
direction. Retail flows/sentiment remain low, while ETF flows have started to 
rebound and seasonality argues for positive ETF flows and potential Retail 
buying. 
• EU vs. US positioning — US steady, EU sliding and difference is getting 
stretched: US positioning is relatively unchanged MTD (US TPM ~46th %-
tile), but bearishness in Europe continues to build (EU saw -1.7z net selling 
this week; aggregate positioning down to ~13th %-tile). The EU vs. US 
positioning gap is starting to look meaningfully stretched. 
• Banks — US Banks bought ahead of earnings (Large Caps > Regionals), 
while EU also bought despite high positioning: HFs bought US Banks for 
a second straight week (just over +2z), leaning into a potential 4Q rebound 
setup.  Flows still favor Large Cap Banks (+1.5z / 5d; positioning near highs) 
while Regionals were sold (-0.9z; low 12m positioning). EU Banks 
underperformed, and while HFs still modestly bought (<+1z), positioning 
remains elevated (+3z vs LT), making EU Banks a key risk if Europe de-risks 
further. 
• Other sector themes — Semis de-risked ex-AxJ, Utilities still unloved by 
HFs but ETFs bid; Biotech stabilizing: Semis stayed volatile and were the 

most net sold industry by HFs in N. Am. (-1.5z) and EMEA (-3z) and also sold 
in Japan, with AxJ the lone buyer (+1z).  Despite the selling, Semis remain a 
top-2 net exposure industry in every region (more stretched on LT basis than 
vs the last 12m). Defensives outperformed in the US, but HFs showed limited 
appetite to add this past week. Utilities remain near historically low HF 
positioning even as ETF flows into the sector have turned positive. Biotech 
remains weak, but saw a pause in de-grossing.
