Asia/ASML: Headline indices going nowhere, but plenty of chop beneath the surface as AI related
concerns continue to build. Asia had been finding its feet after yesterday’s sharp semiconductor
underperformance. KOSPI was +1–2% after falling 5.5% yesterday but now small down, as had Samsung
and Hynix but now fading post the initial take on ASML. Headline blowout numbers, raising lower end of
2026 guidance and highlighting extraordinary demand extending into 2029/30... becoming very clear that
given the growth their experiencing they wont have enough capacity to meet customer demand. . But the
sticking point is the lack of an upgrade to 2027 expectations. Given the recent pullback I would not have
thought the bar is that high so we will watch to see how this trades but first glance market wanted a more
meaningful upward reset to next year’s earnings (optimistic it is fine).
Rates: Williams yesterday lowering the temperature on 2027 hikes. Pretty clear message that this Fed
wants to stay on hold in October unless absolutely forced to act. CPI today is the key litmus test, with
consensus looking for 0.6% headline and 0.2% core. Interestingly, bonds have actually been rallying, rate
vol compressing and some of the financial tightening of recent weeks beginning to reverse. The original
source of much of the stress is getting easier, despite energy remaining elevated. Feels like very one sided
positioning a tail wind + worries on the ex-AI underlying economy supportive (seen consumer confidence
lately?).
Energy: Heating oil threatening to retest the highs. This is probably the price we should be watching most
closely. Still no real solution to the distillate shortage, and Russian diesel exports are unlikely to change
the near term equation materially. We remain in a real quagmire of refined products. European Gas took
out the highs earlier in this week and now at 86 for TTF. This is both an inflation problem and increasingly
an economic problem as higher fuel costs filter through the system. Politically not much new. Zelensky
showing some willingness to engage, but little evidence that the odds of a Russia/Ukraine resolution have
materially improved. Saudi escalation against the Houthis raises the risk of further retaliation against
energy infrastructure, although the latest attack hit Aramco corp headquarters as opposed to actual Oil
assets.
AI: Google’s Gemini 4 Argon is out and competing at the frontier across a remarkable range of
benchmarks. You now have Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta all genuinely in contention. Can’t
remember a time when the frontier was quite so competitive. X floating hopes of Fable 5.5, although
nothing confirmed. Anthropic’s closed door investor event today, so watch for headlines. The pace of

progress remains extraordinary, and the promise of recursive self improvement feels tantalizingly close.
Hard to argue that underlying demand for intelligence isn’t accelerating rapidly.
Credit: CCC spreads around 1,250bp, over 200bp wider since September, while BB and single B have had
very less dramatic widening. Some of this is idiosyncratic distress in legacy media, cable and building
products. But notably HY has been widening in aggregate and at a very high level gigantic sovereign
deficits and enormous demands to financing AI spending through 2028 create an unprecedented need for
capital. At these yields there is a lot of paper competing. Not necessarily convinced AI crowding out is
driving CCC weakness as that seems more idiosyncratic, but the broader issue is whether the cost and
availability of capital become prohibitive for lower quality borrowers. There are a few in the AI vertical
with notable increases in CDS that we should be watching. Think we need to be vigilant and watch for
migration into single B, leveraged loans and primary issuance. If credit stress broadens, the implications
extend well beyond AI to SMEs, consumers and the broader economy.
Risk: Why are we here? I remain fundamentally bullish on the technology. Four enormous labs racing
towards the frontier, accelerating inference demand and intelligence gains coming at a remarkable pace.
The promise of recursive self improvement feels close, and I have little doubt underlying demand will
continue to accelerate. The issue is whether the cost of capital and physical bottlenecks prevent that
demand from translating into actual spending. There are legitimate constraints around financing, power,
construction and equipment that will frustrate the ability to deliver. If you’re an intermediary sitting
between robust end demand and supply that simply cannot be delivered, at some point you risk an air
pocket in orders. I suspect this is increasingly what concerns the semi supply chain, particularly around
whether that becomes a 2027 or 2028 event. The winners remain those who control scarce compute and
scarce capacity. The market has correctly shifted its perception of hyperscalers as secular beneficiaries of
an exploding inference economy. More agents, more tokens, more traffic on their highways, with the
added advantage that they own much of the scarce infrastructure. Competition among the labs may
compress model economics without necessarily undermining the infrastructure owners. So fundamentally
bullish on the technology and the acceleration towards the next leg of intelligence. But increasingly
conscious that the limiting factor may not be demand, but our willingness and ability to finance and
deliver the supply. Watch CPI today.
