If you want a trade after the Sep CPI data, bet that the 
Dec hike is 50 rather than 25 
 
As sure as eggs is eggs, employment follows profits and inflation follows employment, and 
that is how the tale for 2027 promises to lay out. If not for war and tariffs, better inflation 
would’ve been the story this year, but that run of ifs, buts, and maybes is ending. The 
economy is now rounding past the one-year mark for the low in payroll growth in 2025, 
meaning base inflation should begin to accelerate. It is already evident in the rent data, 
which runs with unemployment, and service inflation, which follows private payrolls with a 
lag. 
  
With inflation where it is, and the base pace about to accelerate, a funds rate nearing 5% 
rather than 4% makes more sense. Unless, of course, Warsh is trying to slow the economy 
with higher term yields, which matter more to private investment spending than short rates 
– something I wrote about in “Inverting policy is Warsh’s challenge”  Perhaps he can get 
away with this, ignoring market credibility concerns, but I doubt it, Warsh has shown that 
he cares very much what markets think about him. In the meantime, we will continue to 
hear the Administration’s view that this is the late 90s redux, replete with capital 
deepening raising real rates and lowering inflation. It is a comparison without merit, but 
any WH is particularly good at pushing meritless arguments from seemingly 
unimpeachable scholars of the realm. 
  
Could Warsh hike 50 in Dec? We will know on Nov 4 – how lame of a duck is Trump for the 
coming two years. My guess is that the Democrats take 219 seats in the House and 51 in 
the Senate (their candidate in Michigan looks like it will cost them a state). Read “Da’ 
Votes! Red, White or Blue?”. If you are wondering about the title, it is my small nod to Mike 
Ditka’s passing that rekindled memories of the SNL skit (IYKYK). 
  
Inflation is broadening, based on my diffusion index, though still not above 50%. 

  
 
  
Core CPI and core service inflation (excluding rents) follow payrolls with a 12-month lag. 
For this cycle, given the non-recession recession, the lag may compress to only 6-months. 
Higher base inflation is baked in for the coming first half of 2027.  
  
 
  

Rent runs with unemployment. Some of the recent weakness in rent has been in cities that 
boomed during COVID and overbuilt. Excess supply is being absorbed and, in some of 
those cities, rents are beginning to firm. 
  
 
  
In the end, the challenge to Warsh will not be hiking but how fast he eases once the 
economy begins to slow, likely kicked off by a poor equity market. As I wrote in “Original sin 
redux”, when monetary policy cuts recessions short, allowing a new upcycle to begin 
before disinflation runs its course, is one reason why the 70s became what they were. But 
let’s not get too far ahead of that story – the probability of a 50BP hike in Dec is not 0. 
Markets are pricing a 90% chance of a Dec hike – the pricing should jump to 150% 
(100% 25 + 50/50 on another 25).
