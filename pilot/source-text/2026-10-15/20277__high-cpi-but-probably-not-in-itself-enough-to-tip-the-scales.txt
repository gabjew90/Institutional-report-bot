High CPI but probably not in itself enough to tip the scales for October
August CPI surprised on the upside for a 0.3% m/m increase, beating the consensus (0.2% m/m) for
the second consecutive month. The surprise was broadly based and the annualized 3-month change
for core CPI is now back above 3%. Several members have indicated that they want to see more data
before hiking again, which has raised the bar for another increase already at the 28 October meeting.
We think the totality of data this week will decide but stick to unchanged in October for now.
Key points
Core CPI 0.3%, 2.6% y/y, consensus 0.2%, 2.5%, previous 0.3%, 2.4%
Headline CPI 0.6%, 3.7%, consensus 0.6%, 3.6% y/y, previous 0.4%, 3.4% y/y

Broadly based increases
Core CPI increased by 0.33% in September, above our and the consensus expectations for a slightly above
0.2% month-on-month increase. The year-on-year rate increased from 2.4% to an above expectation of
2.6%. The surprise was broadly based. The monthly increases in goods prices (core goods 0.25%, core
goods ex used cars and trucks 0.18%) and rents (0.33%) were all 0.1-0.15 percentage points above our
forecast. Services excluding rents were also higher than expected. Tourist services, air tickets (1.2%) and
hotels (2.0%), contributed to faster price increases though in both cases slowing from the previous
month. Headline CPI (0.6% m/m) was in line with estimates. Energy prices increased slightly less than our
forecast but at a solid pace (5% m/m) while food prices were unchanged.
Second consecutive disappointment 
The monthly price changes for the core CPI are still within the volatile range during the past two years.
Monthly core CPI has tended to surprise on the downside this year but has been higher than expected
during August and September. The 3-month annualized change is now at the high end of the recent range
and above 3%. This will concern some members of the Fed, who often focuses on this measure.

Preliminary estimate for core PCE is 0.31%
The core PCE has remained the Fed’s key inflation objective. Our preliminary estimate for core PCE in
September, given the information in the CPI, is 0.31%. Tomorrow’s September PPI will provide more input
for the core PCE, however.
Enough for an October rate hike?
Several Fed members have indicated that they want to evaluate the effects of the September rate hike
before tightening again, most likely at the December meeting. This raises the bar for another increase at
the 28 October meeting. Before the September CPI, markets priced a small 20% probability for an
October hike. After the data, markets are back to pricing almost 50% probability for an increase. Data
during the rest of the week (PPI, retail sales and industrial production) and anecdotal comments in the
Fed’s Beige Book will also be important to watch for both the implications for PCE inflation and for growth
and pricing power. August retail sales surprised strongly on the upside and another very strong report in
September may signal increased scope for companies to pass through cost increases to customers. We
stick to our view of an unchanged rate at the October meeting but the totality of data this week, as well as
the events in the Middle East and energy prices, will be important. 
Also of interest will be the fireside chat with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh and the IMF’s managing direction
Kristalina Georgieve early Friday morning. Kevin Warsh does not want to guide markets but his hawkish
comments at both Jackson Hole and after the September meeting have tended to raise market
expectations for rate hikes. Kevin Warsh has said that he is focusing on the broader trend rather than
individual months, but had already earlier concluded that the slowdown in the CPI was not enough to
convince him that the trend had meaningfully improved. The September data will not change his views on
the trend.
Short-term upside risks have increased
The renewed increase in energy prices has delayed the slowdown for headline inflation and has raised the
risks for indirect effects from, for instance, higher transportation costs. Some signs of higher goods prices
are emerging, and we have revised our near-term forecast slightly higher. There are also some signs that
rents could start to rise at a slightly higher rate again, judging from the changes in market-based Zillow
rents. This could reflect increased demand for rentals, driven by deteriorating affordability to purchase a
home on the back of higher mortgage rates. Wage inflation continues to slow, however, which implies that
any upturn in inflation will be temporary. The inflation outlook is significantly more uncertain than normal,
but we continue to predict that underlying inflation trends will improve as temporary impulses from tariffs,
energy prices and the AI build-out fade. Our forecast implies that both core CPI and core PCE will back at
the target by the end of 2028.
