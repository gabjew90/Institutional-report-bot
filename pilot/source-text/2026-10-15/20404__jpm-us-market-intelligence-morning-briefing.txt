JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
After five straight down sessions in SOX (its longest losing streak since July), ASML's beat-
and-raise drew a positive stock reaction but didn't restart the broader Semis / Memory trade 
(MU -1.2% pre-market; SK Hynix / TEL closed lower in Asia). The market is waiting into a 
catalyst-heavy stretch: chatter around a frontier model’s investor event today and TSMC's 
print / guide on Thursday. With tech front and center of the theme tape, watch for a possible 
squeeze post-CPI today. For CPI, Feroli sees Headline MoM of 0.55% (3.6% YoY) and 
Core MoM of 0.18% (2.4% YoY); his views reflect a MoM increase in Headline but decrease 
in Core inflation. That type of print should be enough to keep the bond market from re-
pricing an October hike, instead continuing to see a skip and a December hike as most 
likely. If core MoM comes in around 0.18%, we'd expect yields to move lower globally, 
which tends to support rate-sensitive baskets. Yesterday's bullish read-through on the US 
consumer (see below) also helped Retail, especially with positioning light. We're 
relaunching our earnings macro read-through for the quarter, see below.  
  

•         VIEWS ON ASML EARNINGS (SCOTT SILVER) – Last print revisions were ~30% and 
stock went down and this print revisions are LSD and stock is up"… So are we just in a 
different tape? One PM claimed “this is good news as everyone in Asia was so pessimistic 
(me too) thinking we get a repeat of July i.e. Good earnings = bad price action”. That said, 
stepping back, it is encouraging to see a fundamentally good set of numbers being 
rewarded with positive price action, something we haven’t seen for some time. Importantly, 
the initial disappointment was never really about the underlying numbers, with gross 
margins in particular an the main positive, but rather the lack of clarity on 2027 and, more 
specifically, what ASML can realistically deliver in terms of additional capacity.  
•         MACRO READ-THROUGH FROM BANK EARNINGS – We published below in the 
October 13th Morning Briefing: Day 1 of bank earnings told a consistent story: consumer 
spending and credit are still strong, and the labor market is what's holding both up. Card 
spending grew 10% year over year at JPM and 13% at C. Delinquencies and net credit 
losses are falling year over year at C and WFC. Corporate credit looks just as healthy, with 
C reporting low corporate non-accruals and net credit losses. WFC noted that higher prices 
are weighing on sentiment and that lower-income households are more sensitive to 
financing costs, inflation and fuel. Even so, that group is described as stable, not getting 
worse. Overall, the read-through supports a resilient US consumer view, and WFC summed 
up the bullish case best: “as long as the economy continues to grow and employment stays 
strong, that should continue. And we're just not seeing signs of that changing at this point."

EARNINGS MACRO READTHROUGH 
•         SUMMARY – Day 1 of bank earnings told a consistent story: consumer spending and 
credit are still strong, and the labor market is what's holding both up. Card spending grew 
10% year over year at JPM and 13% at C. Delinquencies and net credit losses are falling 
year over year at C and WFC. Corporate credit looks just as healthy, with C reporting low 
corporate non-accruals and net credit losses. WFC noted that higher prices are weighing on 
sentiment and that lower-income households are more sensitive to financing costs, inflation 
and fuel. Even so, that group is described as stable, not getting worse. Overall, the read-
through supports a resilient US consumer view, and WFC summed up the bullish case best: 
“as long as the economy continues to grow and employment stays strong, that should 
continue. And we're just not seeing signs of that changing at this point."  
•         CONSUMER HEALTH & RESILIENCE 

o   [Consumer Health] C – "In the US, whilst much has been made of the AI 
boom, the consumer is still driving much of the growth, the labor market is 
stable, and companies remain active." 
o   Consumer Resilience] JPM – "… the consumer has been quite resilient in 
the face of a number of headwinds that under different circumstances you 
might have expected would present a challenge." 
o   [Consumer Resilience] WFC – "The consumer remains one of the 
economy's most important sources of resilience though higher prices continue 
to weigh on consumer sentiment." 
o   [Income Cohort Divergence] WFC – "High income households continue to 
benefit from strong asset prices, substantial home equity and stable labor 
income. And while lower income households remain more sensitive to 
financing costs, inflation and higher living and fuel expenses, they appear to 
be stable." 
o   [Macro Outlook] WFC – "And as long as the economy continues to grow 
and employment stays strong, that should continue. And we're just not seeing 
signs of that changing at this point, either from the way our -- the sentiment 
across our commercial client base or just in the actual metrics that we're 
seeing on the consumer side." 
•         CONSUMER SPENDING 
o   [Consumer Spending] C – "Spend volume up 13% and average loans up 
9%, both partially offset by declines in private label cards." 
o   [Consumer Spending] JPM – "A few points to highlight debit and credit 
spend grew 10% year-on-year with continued momentum across lending 
categories, including gas and travel supported by consumer demand." 
•         CREDIT QUALITY 
o   [Consumer Credit Quality] C – "… And we continue to see strong consumer 
credit performance as both net credit loss and delinquency rates in our overall

o   [Consumer Credit Quality] WFC – "At the same time, consumer credit

loans as well as corporate net credit losses remained low."

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the October 12 Morning Briefing 
•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – we reiterate our view as we head into earnings. Last week, 
we had a test of the bullish hypothesis with last week’s OpenAI news where an accounting 
misinterpretation led to a 3.4% decline on Thursday. Though the story was updated across 
multiple media sources, Semis ended the week on a 3-day losing streak. Now, does the 
market regain its confidence in Semis ahead of earning, post-earnings, or not at all? The 
SOX is ~20% off its July lows and ~15% below its June ATHs, which points to an inflection 
point given recent price action. We think Semis can climb this latest wall of worry as recent 
earnings updates from Hynix / Samsung point to on-going thematic strength. Separately, 
breadth continues to deteriorate which we think can reverse into / out of earnings season 
which will buttress the rally. 
•         RISKS – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI Credit Fears / 
Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – (i) Macro Data – CPI on Oct 14, Retail Sales & PPI on Oct 15, Flash 
PMIs on Oct 23, and Fed Day on Oct 28; (ii) Lower Bond Yields – while growth continues 
to drive yields higher, dovish inflation prints could remove further hike expectations out of 
the market which is currently pricing a Dec hike and then 2x hikes in FY27; (iii) Banks 

Earnings – kicking off earnings season with stronger earnings and a favorable consumer 
and lending outlook would be helpful in restarting the Cyclicals trade and broadening the 
bull market. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with a 
preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 
Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your 
shopping list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and 
Retailers given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy 
prices. Other elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower energy 
prices. Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and Japan. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long but 
would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / yields, 
which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, there is 
likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the Tech tape. 
Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus bullish play. The 
AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we like Tech in the 
near-term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we think you need to 
see the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; excluding AI-related 
plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper yield curve, and 
favorable capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real Estate, Telecom, and 
Utilities may participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most likely to lag the SPX, 
ex-AI plays. The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which would squeeze EU and 
RTY higher; plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, Cruise Lines, 
Discretionary, Hotels, etc) is another area of interest with a preference to play via options.  
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (SEP 21) – We maintain our core view of +Tech vs. -
RTY and that Tech view is a mix of Mag7, Memory, and Semis, and we would consider 
keeping the pair $-neutral or market neutral. Early in hiking cycles, we tend to see USD bid 
higher and the yield curve flattening; together, this is supportive of a move towards Secular 
Growth / MegaCap Tech. Given the likelihood of higher yields and a stronger USD, we think 
the US can tactically outperform DM. That said, the fear / uncertainty around AI may push 

the market into non-AI plays, which puts Latam in focus as a long play; we like Brazil / 
Latam irrespective of your views on AI.
