China data: China's headline CPI inflation edged up to +0.9% yoy in September from +0.8% yoy in
August, mainly on higher energy and transportation services prices. Headline PPI inflation rose to +5.0%
yoy in September from +3.8% yoy in August, mainly due to higher prices for energy goods and chemicals.
Incorporating the September CPI/PPI print, as well as our commodity strategy team's latest oil price
forecasts, we are revising up our 2026/27 full-year PPI inflation forecasts to 2.8%/0.3% (vs. 2.0%/0.0%

previously). Under the revised path, year-over-year PPI inflation is likely to turn negative as early as Q2
next year, due to a high base this year. We also revise down our 2026/27 full-year CPI inflation forecasts
to 0.8%/0.6% (vs. 1.0%/1.0% previously), driven by weak domestic demand and our expectations of
fading boost from energy prices in 2027.
China's trade growth accelerated in September and came in significantly above consensus expectations
(exports: +36.3% yoy, imports: +31.0% yoy). The acceleration in headline trade growth is consistent with
the strong container shipping volume during the month. By region and in sequential terms, Chinese
exports rose sequentially across major trading partners. Imports from major trading partners also rose
sequentially, except for the US. By product, export value rose broadly from August to September, with
apparel/footwear/suitcase products rising the most. Imports by products also rose broadly in September,
led by energy products (mostly natural gas). Overall, the trade surplus widened to US$133.5bn (NSA) in
September from US$119.1bn in August, marking the largest monthly surplus on record.
Watching CPI this morning: CPI GS preview: We expect a 0.24% increase in September core CPI (vs.
+0.2% consensus), corresponding to a year-over-year rate of +2.46% (vs. +2.5% consensus). We expect a
0.59% increase in headline CPI (vs. +0.6% consensus), reflecting higher energy prices. Our forecast is
consistent with a slightly smaller 0.20% increase in core PCE in September. We highlight three key
component-level trends we expect to see in the report. First, we expect a pickup in autos inflation,
reflecting a 0.5% increase in used car prices, a 0.2% increase in new car prices, and a 0.2% increase in the
auto insurance category. Second, we forecast benign readings for the shelter categories—a 0.20% increase
in the OER category and a 0.17% increase in the rent category—reflecting the continued slowdown in
their underlying trends. Third, we expect a 3.0% increase in airfares, reflecting the impact of the further
increase in jet fuel prices. Looking beyond September, we expect a monthly core CPI increase of around
0.2% in October—reflecting upward pressure from distortions to the seasonal factors caused by missing
data from last year’s government shutdown—and increases of around 0.1% in November and December—
reflecting the continued slowdown in the shelter categories and shrinking contributions from tariff-related
price increases. Full note here.

Over the past year, HVAC stock performance has been bifurcated as Applied HVAC (JCI, TT) has
materially outperformed Resi HVAC (LII, CARR) with dispersion being very wide (JCI: +47%, LII:
-33%). We expect the trend to continue. Three key reasons for our more favorable view of Applied HVAC
vs. Resi HVAC over the next 12 months. First, we expect positive FY27 estimate revisions for both JCI
(GS: +6% vs. consensus) and TT (GS: +4% vs. consensus), while our estimates for LII (GS: -4% vs.
consensus) and CARR (GS: -3% vs. consensus) are below. Second, the structure of the Resi HVAC market
could be changing with competition increasing and pricing harder to come by. Additionally, while we
believe inventory levels have normalized post the 2024/2025 disruption from the refrigerant change,
demand remains tepid given low housing turnover and affordability concerns. Third, we expect the
demand for Applied HVAC to remain robust despite backlog rising ~50% on average this year for JCI and
TT as the longer-cycle parts (e.g., power generation) of the AI/DC value chain are now booking orders
beyond 2030. Full note here

GIR – Mark Delaney out looking at the pickup market and product refreshes – full note here:
Given the high level of profits that Ford and GM generate from pickup trucks, and with GM's upcoming
full-size pickup refresh cycle for MY27 (i.e. the Sierra and Silverado), we dig into the importance of the
pickup market for OEM earnings and examine how impactful past refreshes have been. Our key
conclusions are as follows: 1) OEMs typically see a 2-3 year period of better sales after a refresh/new
generation of trucks is launched, absent large macro/cyclical events (e.g. COVID-19); 2) pickups and full-
size pickups have become an increasing part of the US auto market; and 3) full-size pickups likely drive a
significant portion of EBIT for F and GM.
Refresh cycles drive higher sales for 2-3 years. We analyzed a key truck refresh cycle for each of Ford
(2015), GM (2013), and Stellantis (2002) to see how full-size pickup sales for these companies post major
refreshes compared to competitors and the US auto market overall. We analyzed these periods, as we think
other recent refreshes were heavily affected by macro events (e.g. COVID-19 and the ensuing supply
chain volatility, and the Global Financial Crisis). While we acknowledge that multiple factors can drive
product sales, we think that performance relative to the US auto market and competitor pickup sales
highlights the ability for a refresh cycle to generally drive 2-3 years of higher sales volumes as shown in
Exhibit 1-Exhibit 3, absent a significant macro/cyclical event.
Given that brand loyalty can be common in the pickup market, we believe part of the strength post a new
launch could be due to pent-up demand.
For context, the last refresh cycles for Ford, GM, and Stellantis were in 2021, 2019, and 2019 respectively.
In terms of upcoming refreshes, GM is launching its next-gen Sierra and Silverado later this year (2026 as
MY27), Ford has committed to launching a refresh of its F-series by 2029, and Stellantis (Ram) had its
last refresh 10 years apart (implying a 2029 refresh for the light duty model). GM has commented that its

truck volumes into the back half of 2027 to the end of 2027 could be flattish as it ramps supply of the new
variants, but the refresh could be more material to its sales in 2028. New launches can also help with net
pricing, although GM has commented that pricing even late in the cycle on the current generation has
remained strong and therefore the net pricing improvement could thus be more limited with this launch.
