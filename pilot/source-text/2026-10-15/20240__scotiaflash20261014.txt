US CPI Lights Up Fed Hike Expectations 
• 
Core CPI was hotter than consensus, matching Scotia’s estimate 
• 
Early mapping points to elevated core PCE inflation 
• 
Pass through into goods and services prices is evident 
• 
Seasonal adjustment factors tamped down the jump 
• 
Markets raise Fed hike pricing for October onward… 
• 
…as Scotiabank Economics forecasts two more hikes 
US CPI / core CPI, m/m %, SA, September: 
Actual: 0.65 / 0.33 
Scotia: 0.6 / 0.3 
Consensus: 0.6 / 0.2 
Prior: 0.40 / 0.29 
 
US inflation surprised consensus and market expectations while matching my estimates. 
Core CPI climbed 0.33% m/m SA with total CPI inflation up 0.65%. Core CPI inflation is 
clearly not waning (chart 1). The results raised the year-over-year rates to 3.7% (3.4% 
previously) and 2.6% y/y (2.4% prior) for total CPI and core CPI respectively. 
Core goods prices (ex-food and energy) were up by 0.3% m/m SA and at an annualized 
rate are on an accelerating trend (chart 2). This reflects pass through of commodities and 
tariffs and is consistent with soft survey guidance as a leading indicator. 
Core services CPI (ex-housing and energy services) was up by 0.4% m/m SA and at an 
annualized rate continues to post sharp increases (chart 3). Here too, we are seeing pass 
through effects. 
PCE is tracking a rise of 0.45% m/m SA and core PCE is tracking 0.27% m/m SA. This is 
derived by taking the CPI figures and adjusting for weighting differences. We’ll reassess 
the implications for PCE inflation when we get producer prices tomorrow including the 
components that are included in PCE. The bottom line is that underlying inflation in the 
US economy remains rather warm. I wouldn’t rule out another hike on October 28th 
despite some guidance from FOMC officials. Accordingly, market pricing for that meeting 
shot up from about 5bps to about half of a 25bps rate hike.
