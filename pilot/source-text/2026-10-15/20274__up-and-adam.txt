Wednesday, October 14, 2026
After 42 months of YoY declines, the Cass Freight Index—a gauge of North American shipping volumes—turned higher in both August 
and September. Whether by truck or train, ground shipping requires diesel. Higher volumes point to rising diesel demand, which helps 
explain why the YTD jump in diesel prices is outpacing oil. While expensive diesel is weighing on transport firm earnings, we expect 
prices to moderate. With the S&P 500’s Industrials sector down 9.5% from recent highs, we view the pullback as a buying opportunity.

Banks Paint An Upbeat Picture Of Consumer Spending
Source: Cass Information Systems
Chart of the Day: Freight Train
We highlighted yesterday that real-time indicators point to sustained strength in consumer spending, a message reinforced by big bank 
earnings. Credit card sales volumes were robust at Citigroup (+13% YoY), Wells Fargo (+12%), and JP Morgan (+10%). Also encouraging: 
card charge-off rates—the percentage of credit card debt the bank writes off as a loss—decreased for all three companies. Despite 
headwinds from gasoline prices near post-COVID highs, the data suggests that aggregate consumer spending remains on solid footing.
Heading into 3Q earnings season, consensus is calling for the MSCI Europe index to post a second quarter of double-digit EPS growth on 
a YoY basis. While impressive on the surface, the strength is narrowly concentrated, with Energy generating an estimated ~50% of the 
EPS growth, despite comprising only ~5% of the market cap. Given our view that oil prices will decline over the next 12 months, the 
narrow earnings breadth could become a headwind. Among developed markets, we continue to favor the US and Japan over Europe.
