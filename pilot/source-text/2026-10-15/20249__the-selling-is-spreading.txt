The cracks are spreading
King Dollar refuses to take a breather, while tech is getting hammered. Semis are down 12% from their
highs, Korea's memory meltdown is spreading, and China's hard-tech stocks continue to implode
despite increasingly oversold conditions.
Meanwhile, Europe's fiscal and energy headaches are mounting, and silver is breaking critical
technical levels.
The common theme? Crowded trades, deteriorating momentum, and growing risks of a much broader
positioning unwind.
DXY
DXY continues its impressive march higher. After breaking above the main range, the index briefly
paused around the 102 area before resuming its move higher.
Even dollar bulls like ourselves, who have been bullish for some time, are surprised by the dollar's
refusal to take even a mini breather.
RSI is elevated, but not yet at extreme levels. The next major resistance comes from a longer-term
downward-sloping trend line around 105.
Perhaps we get there before the dollar finally takes a breather. Full note here.

Overbought, but who cares?
The dollar is getting increasingly stretched, but that doesn't mean the rally is over.
ING highlights that net USD longs versus G10 currencies, excluding JPY, have reached 24% of open
interest, while options risk reversals are approaching previous extremes.
Yet the bank sees no obvious catalyst for a reversal. Fed expectations remain supportive, while global
bond market weakness continues to underpin demand for the dollar.
Overbought is one thing. Finding a reason to sell is another.

Au revoir, euro bulls
France's fiscal troubles are increasingly becoming embedded in the euro's risk premium, and further
French bond weakness could add to the pressure. Meanwhile, markets have already priced in a
December ECB hike, leaving limited room for additional hawkish surprises.
Not exactly a welcoming environment for euro bulls.

The next tech V-turn?
Immaculate timing: Deutsche Bank turned tactically cautious on tech just before the selloff, arguing
that the latest rotation into the sector is approaching exhaustion.
Mega-cap growth and tech have roughly 4 percentage points of relative upside remaining before
reaching the upper end of their long-term trend channel, versus potentially 16 points of downside if
previous rotation patterns repeat.
This is the fifth major rotation into tech in just two years, and previous reversals have tended to be
sharp, inverted V-shaped moves.
The risk/reward is becoming increasingly asymmetric. More here.

China's semiconductor valuation problem
China's hard-tech selloff is becoming increasingly uncomfortable, but valuations remain anything but
cheap.
Goldman Sachs highlights an extraordinary disconnect: Chinese semiconductor stocks trade at a 237%
forward P/E premium to their global peers.
The push for technological self-sufficiency and strong government support have helped justify
elevated multiples, but GS warns that overpaying for growth and policy support remains a key risk.
The question is whether China's semiconductor stocks have corrected enough to reflect
those expectations.
