 
The Great Flight To Quality: Goldman Sees 
Investors Panic-Buy 'Safe' Stocks At Pace Not Seen 
Since COVID While Rest Of Market Rots 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, OCT 13, 2026 - 01:15 AM 
One of the more interesting dynamics in US equities remains the large disconnect between the 
S&P and the average stock. 
While SPX sits within 1% of all-time highs, the median S&P stock is now 16% below its own 
high. By Goldman's preferred measure, breadth has now deteriorated to its weakest level since 
the Dot-Com Bubble. 
 
As Goldman's flows guru, Lee Coppersmith, details below (full note available here for pro subs), 
the headline index continues to mask just how difficult the tape has been underneath. 

 
 
I think the clearest sign of investor apprehension has been the extraordinary flight to quality 
taking place. The GS Quality factor has rallied more than 16% over the past 8 weeks, a move of 
this magnitude we haven’t seen since the COVID shock in 2020. That’s a pretty remarkable 
development for a market sitting near all-time highs. Despite the fact that the index has barely 
flinched, investors are favoring quality at a pace typically associated with periods of significant 
market stress. 
There is clearly a lot more risk aversion beneath the surface than SPX would suggest. 

 
 
The big question we’re getting from clients now is whether the market narrowness is a warning 
sign for the broader market. I’m increasingly of the view that it isn’t, and continue to favor SPX 
higher over the next 1-3 months. In one study, extreme concentration has been a bullish signal 
over the medium term. Bloomberg looked at 13 episodes since 1995 when the 10 largest S&P 
stocks outperformed equal weight by more than 8% over three months. The median subsequent 
SPX return was +10.1% over six months and +19.6% over 12 months (vs. historical medians of 
+6.9% and +15, respectively). Today, that same performance gap is 855bps. Narrow leadership 
has historically been entirely consistent with a market that continues to move higher. 

 
 
Arguably the most constructive part of the setup is just how light positioning has become, 
particularly for a market still hovering near its highs. Our GS US Equity Sentiment Indicator, an 
aggregate of various positioning measures, is now at -1.3 – now at a level where forward 
returns look more attractive. 
 

 
Global fundamental L/S net leverage sits in the 6th percentile over the past year (2nd percentile 
when only looking at the US), while overall HF net leverage is in the 10th percentile . Gross 
exposures, meanwhile, remain relatively elevated.Plenty of risk on the books, but increasingly 
little directional conviction. 
 
And you can understand why... Last week was another difficult one for fundamental stock 
pickers. Our latest GS Prime data shows fundamental L/S funds lost 70bps between October 
2-8, driven almost entirely by negative alpha on BOTH their longs and shorts. Global equities 
were net sold again, driven by short sales, while US single stocks were net sold for a third 
consecutive week, with short sales outpacing long buys. A difficult environment for generating 
alpha, and not exactly one encouraging investors to put more risk to work. 

 
 
Perhaps the more interesting development was the sharp reversal in Tech flows. After being the 
most net bought sector in four of the prior five weeks, Tech was by far the most net sold last 
week, with a combination of long sales and incremental shorting. Tech’s L/S ratio has fallen to 
1.8, just the 17th percentile over five years, although gross allocations remain in the 97th 
percentile; link. Pretty interesting divergence - still a lot of capital committed to the sector, but 
the marginal buyer has become considerably less aggressive. 

 
 
On the other side, Industrials saw their fastest pace of buying since January (+1.8 SD), almost 
entirely through new longs, following five consecutive weeks of selling. The sector’s L/S ratio 
remains in just the 22nd percentile over five years. Financials also attracted buying, while 
Software showed impressive relative strength. Some early evidence of capital looking for 
opportunities outside the usual leadership. Worth watching whether this develops into a more 
persistent trend. 
A couple of other extremes worth flagging. Staples has now been net sold in seven of the past 
eight weeks, entirely through shorting, pushing its L/S ratio to the 3rd percentile over five years. 
Biotech has experienced its largest cumulative eight-week percentage de-grossing in more than 
five years. Neither is necessarily a buy signal, but there has been a tremendous amount of risk 
taken out of these parts of the market, and positioning is becoming increasingly one-sided. 

 
 
The other key topic remains rates + oil. After the move above 5% in 10-year yields, our Rates 
team sees the asymmetry skewed toward LOWER yields, while rates vol has finally started to 
reverse. For equities, we don’t need a massive Treasury rally - simply some stability could go a 
long way; link. The obvious complication is oil/geopolitics, and arguably what’s keeping 
sentiment at bay. While our Commodities team expects Brent to moderate toward $90 by 
December and average $80 in 2027, they continue to see risks skewed meaningfully higher, 
particularly near term. With more than $20/bbl of risk premium still embedded in prices and the 
potential for renewed supply disruptions, oil remains the biggest threat to a more constructive 
rates and equity backdrop. 

 
 
One other observation from the options market worth highlighting is NVDA implied volatility. The 
world’s largest company, and arguably the most important stock in the entire AI trade, is offering 
downside vol at levels we haven’t seen since before COVID. For anyone looking to hedge 
against an unwind in the AI trade or the crowding in quality, this feels like an opportunity hiding 
in plain sight. 
 

 
Stepping back, earnings remain supportive, positioning is light, and history suggests that 
extreme concentration alone is hardly a reason to get bearish. A broadening in participation 
would help, but isn’t necessary for the index to keep making highs. Oil and rates remain the 
obvious risks, but absent another meaningful move higher in both, the path of least resistance 
still looks higher.
