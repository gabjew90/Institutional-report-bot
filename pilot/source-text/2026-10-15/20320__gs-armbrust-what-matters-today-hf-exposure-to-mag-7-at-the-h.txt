2) Large Bank Earnings
MS (+/=): stock flattish pre mkt. Headline beat driven by Trading (equities $5.8bn vs $4.9bn cons more
than offsetting FICC Miss) and NII (high teens beat vs cons – looks like market based lending) .. Wealth
flows were very strong, with fee based flows of ~$80bn and total flows of $232bn (!) .. The one thing to
pick at is the miss in PT income & margin in wealth, which looks like wealth compensation as a % of rev
went up a couple of points .. CET1 flat at 14.9% w/ pace of buyback (~$1.5bn) consistent .. What’s going
on in the wealth margin may have some competition implications on handful of other names and can see
some picking at this at MS given wealth PT margin expansion has been a big part of the multiple
expansion story (welcome thoughts) but big picture, most of the line items people were worried about
regarding ‘normalization’ off 2Q look pretty strong .. Stock -17% since mid July.
BAC (-/=): stock is -4.6% pre mkt, 4% PPNR miss with the largest impact coming from trading, which
was down ~5.5% YoY vs them guiding to roughly flat YoY … Both equities and FICC missed
expectations, with FICC -17% YoY. Investment banking a slight beat (debt underwriting) but not enough
to stop a headline fee miss – against this fee miss, expenses were in-line leading to -370bps of operating
leverage in the qtr. NII looks ~30bps better than expectations. They bought back $6bn in stock, so no
slowdown here QoQ (despite being in focus around capital) and TCE/TA is practically unchanged at 6%
and the AOCI marks don’t look that large on first glance (this doesn’t include HTM) – CET1 was down
-40bps. Think people care about 3 items here:
• What is going on in trading to cause the weakness vs peers
• looking for more color on expenses going forward, including levers if fees remain weak (was
underwhelming this qtr w/ the operating leverage update at conf) as well as confirmation around to what
extent remediation efforts from last nights’ money laundering fine are in the run rate (press reports indicate
BAC is saying it’s all in th expense base)

• the NII guide, with bulls playing for better trends here amidst the more hawkish rate environment – think
a guide up on NII can help the stock here, watching for that on the call. (TY Chris DeGrasse)

4) US Equity Positioning is a Tailwind
Technical set up is changing for US equities. Supportive flows, in the form of corporate buybacks, may be
met by MF selling pressure into their year-end, but will soon be joined by positive retail flows and a
historical foreign investor bid post-midterms.

November is the 2nd highest month of corporate repurchases on our desk and will bring back a key source
of demand to the marketplace. Positioning remains a moderate tailwind for US equities, and we expect this
dynamic to continue to play an influential role in tactical trading. The midterms have historically been a
catalyst for foreign buying with inflows reaching about 1.75% of AUM in the six months following the
elections. The market is seeking the next incremental buyers to push equities out of this band, and foreign
investors have been one of those sources in previous episodes.

Spooky selling season is nearing its end as October mutual fund year-end takes out a central stream of
supply. November and December see consecutive positive inflows only rivaled by the Feb-Mar period.
The tail scenarios for bonds both turn CTAs buyers while the asymmetry exists firmly to the downside for
the S&P 500.
5) HF Performance in September

Hedge funds were flat in September on a global-basis. Systematic Macro / CTA funds led by a decisive
margin at +3.8% for the month, marking the strongest single-month gain for the strategy this year, and
making it the top performing strategy on a year-to-date basis. Managers in this group cited short fixed
income and long energy positions as key contributors for performance in September. Quant managers also
had a strong month with gains driven mainly by short-side momentum and momentum alpha.
Fundamental strategies lagged in September, and regionally, Asia was the only cohort in positive territory.
