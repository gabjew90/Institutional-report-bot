 
"Sprinting To Stand Still": Goldman's Molavi Spots 
Two Of Three CapEx-Peak Signals Already Flashing 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, OCT 13, 2026 - 02:15 PM 
If the past few months have felt like an expensive treadmill, you're not alone. According to 
Goldman's top macro trader Bobby Molavi, the whole Street is running hard and going nowhere. 
In his latest "Ruminations" note, published on Monday (available to pro subs), Molavi doesn't 
mince words: 
"The last two to 3 months have felt like everyone has been sprinting to stand still. No matter 
what one does with their gross, nets or beta… the volatility of their pnl remains high… and the 
net output for their efforts have little or negative. It's a very challenging market that has lost 
confidence and lost momentum... The rate of change has gone from week to week to intra week 
and with violent swings from risk on to risk off at the stock, sub sector, sector, factor and theme 
level." (emphasis ours) 
He then lists everything the market had to digest in just three weeks of AI news flow: "the 
frontier and end of humanity," new model drops, consumer agentic, new hacks and liabilities, 
revenue run-rate doubts, slowing growth and the open-weight vs frontier split. His verdict: "How 
does one trade that?" 

 
 
In other words, the index is at record highs and the professionals can't make money owning it. 
Below we walk through Molavi's sprawling note (he warns it "will be pun filled," and he delivers), 
from bonds and oil to a liquidity mirage, a P/E ratio that flatters, and the one chart sequence that 
should worry AI bulls most: the gap between the stock peak and the capex peak. 
Records For The Index, Lows For The Pros 
Molavi's diagnosis is that "it's hard to run nets as the violence of the moves and the random 
walk nature of them makes gross and nets harder for mid duration strategies (in reality all 
strategies)." Add yields, geopolitics, central banks and fiscal pressure, and he calls it "an 
unprecedented market to navigate." 
The chart he includes says it more bluntly: the S&P is circled at all-time highs while US 
fundamental L/S net exposure has slumped to roughly 46%, near the lowest level since the April 
2025 "Liberation Day" puke. Regular readers will recall we flagged exactly this split in 
"Conviction Collapse: Hedge Fund Leverage Hits 'Liberation Day' Lows As Everything But The 
VIX Screams Fear" (Oct 4). 

 
 
And beneath the surface, things are even stranger. The spread between average single-stock 
realized vol and index realized vol has exploded to around 26-28 points on a 3-month basis, far 
above the 2009 peak of around 20. Translation: individual stocks are swinging wildly while the 
index glides higher, because the moves cancel each other out. That's dispersion at its finest, 
and it's a stock picker's nightmare when the picks keep flipping. 
 

 
Five Stocks = 434 Stocks 
The reason the index can float while the pros sink is concentration. Molavi does the math: 
"Top 5 companies in the S&P have a combined market cap of close to $22 trillion or 30% of the 
index.... just 5 companies. This means 5 companies carry as much benchmark weight and value 
as 434 constituents. To put that in context... the average in 2013 and 2014 was about 200 
stocks to equal the top 5.... or 350 in the 2000 dot.com bubble peak." (emphasis ours) 
So we are more concentrated than at the peak of the dot com bubble, by a wide margin. And 
the scoreboard is lopsided: "AI winners +58%, S&P +13%, Nasdaq +22% and median stock in 
S&P -15%." The "AI 10" now make up roughly 40% of the S&P's market cap. Goldman's tech 
desk said much the same two weeks ago, when it found the median stock 16% underwater as 
breadth hit dot com lows (Sep 28). And on earnings, recall "The S&P 2" (Oct 6): Micron and 
Nvidia alone are expected to deliver a third of Q3 EPS growth. 
There is a second problem hiding behind concentration: liquidity is thinning even as volume 
explodes. Per Molavi, average daily top-of-book liquidity has fallen 21% since 2023, from $14 
million to around $11 million. Top-of-book depth per billion dollars traded is down to just $11K, a 
93% drop from $162K in 2017. Meanwhile daily volumes have tripled since 2017, from 6.5 
billion shares to nearly 19 billion. In June, when volume hit a 2026 high of 22.8 billion shares, 
top-of-book depth fell 33%. 
"While the market may be trading more than ever... the ability to trade scale efficiently is getting 
harder.Also... a market that trades a lot but remains thin... especially at top of order book... is a 
market where signal and signal protection becomes increasingly important." 
Put differently, everyone is trading, and nobody can get size done. That's how you get a VIX that 
barely moves while single names gap 10% on a headline. 
The Problem With 'E' 
Molavi also takes on the Street's favorite comfort blanket: the S&P "isn't expensive" on forward 
P/E. He grants that it's true on the surface, then asks: "how many times in last 50 years has one 
of the biggest market caps been focused on colonising mars?" 
His history lesson is worth quoting: in March 2000 the forward P/E was 25.2x, but 39.3x on EPS 
actually earned and roughly 60x on a GAAP basis. In 2007 the forward P/E was a tame 15.1x, 
yet 31.6x on earned EPS and over 100x on GAAP. Why the gap? Because at market tops: 

 
"EPS at the top of the market (not saying we are there) is often wrong for a few consistent 
reasons... peak margins getting extrapolated, growth rates over optimistic, analysts late in 
cutting, operating EPS flattering and earnings quality decaying... In this market of ever faster 
valuation growth and rev run rate growth... these slip ups will be much more painful." (emphasis 
ours) 
Or, as we'd put it: the 'E' is only cheap if it shows up. 
Twin Peaks: Stocks First, Capex Next 
This is the section that should keep AI bulls up at night. Molavi sizes the full AI "orbit" 
(megacaps, hyperscalers, neoclouds, labs, chips, compute) at something like $25 trillion to $30 
trillion across public and private markets, much of it built on infrastructure spend. Then he looks 
at what history says about the two peaks, stock and capex: 
"Looking back through history... we saw the UK railway capex peak 2 years after the equity 
peak, US utils 1 year, Japan 18 months, Dotcom 7 months, US shale 5 months.... each time the 
end of capex being signalled by a central bank policy shift and rate rises. Now... equity prices 
stalling... check..... central bank policy shift and hikes... check... capex... watch this space." 
(emphasis ours) 
Two out of three. And Goldman's own chart shows where the third is heading: hyperscaler 
capex growth is set to peak at 116% y/y in Q3 2026, before consensus sees it decelerating 
every quarter, to the low 20s by the end of 2027. 

 
 
Even if the capex keeps flowing, who pays for it is the question. Molavi notes "over $500bn of 
debt issued this year to finance the AI capex build out," meaning "a lot of future AI revenue 
being spent up front." The CDS market is already doing the math: 
"Oracle 5 year cds trades near record 261bps... or pricing a 20% default rate..... SpaceX cds 
prices 16% probability.... even Nvidia a 7% probability."

The Issuance Window Is Closing 
The equity market is getting pickier too. Molavi sees "signs of fatigue starting to appear": poor 
aftermarket performance for secondaries and deals being pulled, with "Firmus the latest 
example following on from likes of Bamboo and Oura." We covered the Firmus saga as it 
unfolded, from the 25% IPO price cut (Oct 8) to the withdrawal later that same day. 
The numbers back him up. Monthly US equity offerings fell from 65 in May to just 24 in 
September: 
 

 
And quarterly issuance dropped from a record $252 billion in Q2 to $93 billion in Q3 (still up 
20% y/y, per Goldman, but a sharp step down): 
 
Yet the demand for capital isn't going away: Broadcom and SpaceX are both signaling raises for 
AI capex. Molavi says the AI capex financing has shifted "from cash to other forms including 
primary equity and IPO's," with higher rates and debates over terminal value and ROI "causing 
a more discerning buyer than we saw in H1... even if the private markets are still backing AI 
plays that are pre product.... pre launch.... and in some cases pre idea!" 
Which brings us back to a point we made last week: the public debt pipeline is full, so the 
money is moving into the shadows. 
The hyperscaler and AI pipeline for IG/HY debt is done: no more can fit. That's why everyone is 
scrambling to use SPVs/Project financing/chip-collateralized structures to fund capex before 
that last door slams shut too.

Bonds, Oil And The Staycation Trade 
On bonds, Molavi points to France as "the epicentre," with the OAT-Bund spread hitting 140bps, 
"a level not seen since 1990", and growing fears of contagion with the UK budget approaching 
and Spain's snap election (Oct 5). His aside: "who would have thought Italy would rise to 
become one of Europe's leading lights in terms of stability." Who indeed. 
On oil, he describes a week of escalation and de-escalation headlines, from the Riyadh airport 
attacks to the G7 reserve release and the Trump/Putin diesel deal, and concludes that the 
stopgaps are running out: 
"It does feel like the re-routing, reserves release and stockpile selling solutions are running dry 
and as such pressure is building... Long story short… Oil can go lower… if lots of things go 
right… but even then… how low is a different question. At the same time... the reasons for it to 
go higher are growing by the day." (emphasis ours) 
His reasons: reserves will be refilled on any dip ("think covid stockpiling"), shipping and 
insurance costs stay elevated, and refining capacity is "structurally impaired." Jet fuel is trading 
between roughly $1,100 and $1,400 depending on tenor, vs a long-term average of $700. With 
the K-shaped consumer already squeezed by food, fuel and financing, he doubts airlines can 
pass that on without hitting demand. "Odds of a staycation for many of us rising." (Molavi admits 
he learned what a crack spread was "this weekend." Better late than never.) 
Odds And Ends 
●​ Security is king: Cyber names have crushed the index YTD, with Okta outperforming the 
S&P by 155%, Fortinet by 138%, CrowdStrike by 124%, Palo Alto by 116% and the 
HACK ETF by 56%. 
●​ Scarcity pricing: 10-year-old supertankers now trade at a premium to new ships, and 
GPU rental pricing is up 17% to 21% across every generation (H100, H200, B200, 
B300). 
●​ Retail fatigue: Searches for "stocks to buy" and options trading are at their lowest weekly 
rate since August 2025, even as Robinhood shows leverage and margin use rising. 
●​ Token volume vs token spend: Ramp data show token usage hitting a new high above 
700 in September (from 100 in January), but token spend fell 5% last month. Molavi 
frames that as net positive for AI infra demand. We'd note that falling revenue per token 
is precisely what the labs' valuations can't afford. 

 
●​ EM: Emerging markets have beaten MSCI World in 6 of the last 9 market "crashes," and 
Molavi argues the EM trade "has much further to run." 
●​ Factories and artisans: Scale is winning everywhere in asset management. The four 
largest multi-asset firms own the vast majority of multi-asset AUM, a16z raised close to 
20% of all VC dollars in 2025, and OpenAI raised $122 billion in a single round at an 
$865 billion valuation. 
Bottom Line 
Molavi says Goldman anchors on three themes, AI, oil and yields, and relies on three things: 
"the strength of the few (megacaps), narrowness, earnings and belief in the future." That's a lot 
of weight on a very small number of shoulders. 
His own "twin peaks" checklist reads two out of three, with the capex peak the only box left to 
tick. Goldman's chart has hyperscaler capex growth peaking this quarter, credit is already 
repricing the AI borrowers, and the equity window is narrowing. The bull case is that the circle 
keeps growing faster than the cost of funding it. We think the opposite is more likely: the market 
is about to learn that a capex peak and a stock peak are not the same thing, and that history 
usually puts the stock peak first. 
Then again, a market where five stocks equal 434 has never needed much to tip it over. We'll 
check back after the hyperscalers report their Q3 capex numbers later this month.
