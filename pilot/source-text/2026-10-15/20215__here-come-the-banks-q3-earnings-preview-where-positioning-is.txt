TUESDAY, OCT 13, 2026 - 02:00 PM 
Earning season is upon us once more, with big banks such as JPMorgan, Goldman, Wells and 
Citi on deck to report second quarter results tomorrow morning. 
 
Let's start with the big picture before pivoting to the banks in particular.  
For the S&P500, consensus Q3 expectations are for 12.4% revenue growth, 29.6% earnings 
growth, with 15.0% margins. For reference, Q2 was 15.6% revenue growth, 52.3% earnings 
growth and 17% margins. Q4 is expected to print 12.4% revenue growth and 28.0% earnings 
growth.  FY26 is expected to print 12.5% rev and 32.7% EPS growth. FY27 is expected to print 
9.4% rev and 15.8% EPS growth. Furthermore, according to JPMorgan's market intel note this 
morning (available to pro subs), 11 of 11 sectors are expected to see positive revenue growth 

 
and positive earnings growth: "this would be the second time in history that this occurs, 21Q2 
being the other time." 
That's the good news: the bad news is that just like the "market" has become essentially a 
handful of stocks and "everyone else", so earnings too are seeing an unprecedented divergence 
with AI infrastructure stocks expected to drive more than half of S&P 500 EPS growth in Q3, 
while the median stock barely grows: as shown below, the top 10 contributing stocks are 
expected to account for over two-thirds of aggregate S&P 500 earnings growth this quarter, with 
just Micron (MU) and Nvidia (NVDA) together accounting for 34% of index growth. 
 
Micron's results two weeks ago were a strong start to the quarter, with the company reporting 
year/year earnings growth of 1,003%, beating consensus EPS estimates while providing 
forward guidance that was just generally good if a touch light... and yet the stock has crumbled 
since, suggesting all the good news is priced in and the market is trading at perfection if not 
beyond (translation: any misses or guidance whiffs will be punished harshly). 
Last week we laid out just how perverse the earnings concentration has become in "The S&P 
2": Micron And Nvidia Alone Will Deliver A Third Of Q3 Earnings Growth As Median Stock 
Barely Grows, and for those who missed it, here again are the highlights.  
Start with the table that matters most. the chart below breaks down the contribution of individual 
stocks to consensus year/year S&P 500 EPS growth (excluding the "other income" distortion we 
discuss below). Micron alone accounts for 19% of Q3 index growth, Nvidia for another 15%. 
Then come Meta (7%), Alphabet (6%) and Broadcom (5%), followed by... Exxon, Chevron and 

 
Marathon Petroleum, courtesy of the Iran war oil shock, and Boeing. All told, the top 10 
contributors account for 68% of Q3 growth, up from 47% in Q1 and 48% in Q2. 
 
Now zoom out: for the typical company, the boom is already fading: consensus expects the 
median S&P 500 stock to grow EPS by just 9% in Q3, down from 14% in Q2, with analysts 
projecting a sequential deceleration "in every sector except for Info Tech and Comm Services." 
Input costs are also biting: analysts have cut the median stock's Q3 profit margin estimate by 
11bp since the start of the quarter, "with estimates trimmed in every sector except Info Tech." 

 
 
Returns look exactly like earnings. Per Goldman, "while the market is only 2% from its all-time 
high, the median stock is 17% below its all-time high, pushing S&P 500 market breadth to its 
lowest level since the Dot Com Bubble." Regular readers may recall that in May, Goldman first 
warned that crash risk was spiking as breadth plummeted to Dot Com lows, and just last week 
the bank's TMT desk found the median stock 16% underwater. It's now 17%.

Today we turn to JPM's Market Intel take which laid out 4 bullet points of the main things to 
watch this earnings season:  
●​ (i) Breadth – Bloomberg flags that 57% of the SPX is in a bear market (down 20%+ from 
ATHs) and that this weak performance is now coinciding with declining dispersion (BBG 
Terminal). If Tech falters, then we may see a correction.  

 
 
●​ (ii) Whether Beats are Rewarded – In 4 of the last 6 reporting periods, we have seen the 
1-day reaction to earnings beats come in below historical median levels, with misses 
punished to a greater magnitude relative to historical performance. Earnings 
expectations for Tech and the SPX are high but potentially less for the remaining sectors. 
 

 
●​ (iii) Capex – the consensus on how to end the AI-induced stock market boom is a cut to 
capex from one or more hyperscalers. That feels highly unlikely to occur in 2026 
earnings, but the market will search for clues including a reduction in shareholder 
distributions.  
 
●​ (iv) Inflation – JPM is thinking about this through the lens of the Consumer sector which 
appears to be trading as though the Consumer is in a recession (consumer confidence is 
at all time lows, see below) rather than via the Fed reaction function / bond yield lens. 
Already, we have seen DAL cut its profit guidance on energy prices and an unwillingness 
to pass on costs to the consumer. How price sensitive are consumers and is that 
sensitivity uniform across sectors are questions that we seek to have answered.

Subsector Sentiment Trends 

 
 
Sentiment Thoughts: 
●​ Large banks:  Mixed (near term softness vs 2Q vs longer term pipeline .. where we are in 
the cycle) – sector has often served as source of funds for money either leaving 
Financials (generalists) or rotating to nonbanks. 
●​ Trust banks: Generally positive amidst investor view EPS estimates are still biased 
higher .. Some questions around whether 2026 is peak 
●​ Regional Banks: Positioning down from the early summer highs – but have Seen HF 
buying (since mid qtr conference) on view the group has near-term upside to 4Q 
numbers … buying appears somewhat tactical 2027 remains debated though 
●​ Consumer Finance: Notably negative among both HF and LO – with view macro 
(summed up in fact we aren’t early cycle + tail risk from oil) will keep valuation multiples 
contained  
●​ Payments: Sentiment is warm with the group increasingly viewed as agentic-insulated or 
a winner, while spend trends are showing improvement to end 3Q 
●​ Alternative Asset Managers: We see HF positioning sharply whip around as the market’s 
digesting rates – though activity & conversations suggest HFs (specialists) are trying to 
be constructive on the group & buy on weakness, though perhaps less confident vs 
August given renewed focus on macro/rates and what that means for sponsors & activity 

 
●​ Traditional Asset Managers: The group is generally favored among HFs (little LO 
sponsorship we’ve seen) and has served as a ‘risk on’ hideout during periods of 
‘narrative volatility’ .. systematic > active remains a popular trade – with the most 
frustration coming in from BLK longs arguing performance of the stock hasn’t aligned 
with fundamental success this year 
●​ Retail Brokers: Mixed … bulls pointing to valuation discounts that now put some at just a 
narrow premium with capital intensive regional banks – bears unclear what gets them 
working again with cash sorting narrative remaining persistent  
●​ Exchanges: In favor – though lingering uncertainty around how market structure may be 
changing (tokenization, perpetual futures / potential new entrants 
●​ Boutique Investment Banks: Very LOW amongst specialists (amongst the lowest) .. Easy 
to be negative on the narrative (where are we in the cycle) when the near-term numbers 
are going lower (3Q airpocket in activity) alongside fears boutiques have limited levers 
(both comp & noncomp) to support margins given competition  
●​ Commercial Carriers & Reinsurers: Remains negative from the specialist community 
(pricing remains in focus) – though some generalists have looked at this group for more 
defensive exposure (with a bias towards high quality & diversified underwriters); some 
debate in CAT heavy reinsurers (price vs book value) 
●​ Personal Insurance: Lean negative amidst pricing concerns (NPW, margins), potentially 
high storm losses in 3Q, and longer term debates on AI agents and what that means for 
growth & margin 
●​ Insurance Brokers: Overall P&C is still viewed negatively; however, the brokers are more 
mixed .. Additionally, Brokers are amongst the most asked on subsectors by the LO 
community and would note multiples have reached levels where the group has seen 
support at prior points this year 
●​ Life Insurance: The group has seen an uptick in long-interest alongside a favorable 
macro backdrop (higher rates + positive equity market) & a desire for value (single digit 
P/E) stocks that don't have an overt perceived AI connection (for better or worse). Single 
stock dispersion has been driven by shifting views on M&A, while bears are watching 
credit spreads + trends in specific products (ie disability) 
●​ Info Services.. Mixed / neutral (despite the fact AI is firmly in focus) with a dispersion 
trade ongoing with investors now comfortable enough with AI catalyst path & each 
company’s position to express a view around ‘winners & losers’  

 
●​ Mortgage Originators: Negative with consensus view being the group won’t work under 
the current rate backdrop with estimates needing to rebase lower .. bulls focus on 
balance sheet strength + names with lower rate optionality 
Best positioned and topical stocks 
Goldman is constructive on Buy-rated WFC (on CL), BAC, and C. Below we except from 
Ramsden thoughts on his favorite banks: 
●​ WFC, Buy, on CL: WFC offers an attractive mix of top-line growth across both NII1 and 
fees, supported by investment in businesses where it remains underindexed, alongside 
meaningful operating leverage from only modest expense growth over the next two 
years. Amidst a higher rate environment, we believe WFC remains on track to achieve 
its 2026 NII target of $50bn, with stronger balance sheet growth and repricing more than 
offsetting higher deposit funding costs, as we expect NIM to stabilize in 2H26. We see 
tailwinds across consumer and commercial channel to support further loan growth: 1) 
increased line utilization could drive C&I growth upside; 2) continued inflection of CRE; 
3) sustained expansion across card account and auto origination; and 4) elevated AI 
related financing need.  
●​ BAC, Buy: We remain constructive on BAC’s NII trajectory through 2026E/27E, and view 
the high end of the 2026 guidance as achievable with potential upside as well, especially 
driven by a better NIM outlook. Further, given BAC’s asset sensitivity, and less exposure 
to capital markets revenue risk, we see an increasingly attractive top-line growth story. 
We see BAC’s NII growing ~7.5% YoY in 2027E, ~0.5pp higher than the large bank 
average for 2027E1 (Exhibit 44). We are ~1% above consensus 2027E NII driven by a 
combination of: 1) BAC’s asset sensitivity; 2) better than expected deposit repricing in a 
competitive deposit gathering environment, given it is not as constrained by funding; and 
3) accretive fixed rate asset repricing, especially after the long end of the curve rose 
further in 3Q26.  
●​ C, Buy: We continue to view C as a compelling investment opportunity, as it has 
continued to execute on improved earnings and returns, delivering EPS beats on all but 
4 quarters over the past decade (vs. peer average of 6). We remain confident on C’s 
path to higher near and medium term returns (we project ~11.5%/13.2% ROTCE in 
2026E/28E), driven by: 1) a robust 4% 2026E-28E core revenue CAGR, supported by 
share gains in capital markets and Wealth, where C currently under index, as well as 

 
through the cycle strength in services business especially with a rapidly shifting rate 
environment; 2) meaningful efficiency improvements, driven by both structural 
productivity gains, and continued reduction in stranded/transformation expenses in 
non-core businesses
