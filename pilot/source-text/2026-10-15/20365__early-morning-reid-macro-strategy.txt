Markets have put in a subdued performance over the last 24 hours, as investors are grappling with various 
crosscurrents. First, chip stocks sold off yesterday on the back of AI fears, with the Philly semiconductor 
index (-0.23%) posting a 5th consecutive decline even as the stocks saw an afternoon recovery off the 
lows. Second, US banks struggled as earnings season got underway. The KBW Banks index closed near 
the lows of the day down, having fallen -1.39%. And third, inflation fears were still prominent, as the 
12-month Brent future (+1.12%) hit a new high for this year of $87.37/bbl. On the plus side French 10yr 
spreads tightened -4.9bps to bunds. All that before today’s US CPI print, which is expected to be a 
strong one. 
Let's start with US CPI , which occurs just two weeks out from the Fed’s next decision, and a bit of 
hawkishness has crept back into the market narrative. For instance, market pricing for an October 
rate hike was back up to 24% yesterday, the highest since October 1, before the underwhelming jobs 
report (and a day after the initial French stress) led to mounting questions as to whether they actually 
could deliver back-to-back hikes. But with oil prices still elevated, the speculation has persisted going 
into the CPI. 
This report is likely to be a strong one given the run-up in energy prices over September, and our US 
economists expect that motor fuel prices were up +9.6% over the month. So they think that would see 
headline CPI come in at +0.57% on a monthly basis, which would be the strongest month since April, 
shortly after the Iran conflict began. And in turn, that would take the year-on-year rate back up to +3.6%, 
its highest since May. To be fair, they think core inflation will be relatively softer, at a monthly pace of 
just +0.16%, leaving the year-on-year rate at +2.4%. But even so, any surprises either way will help 
shape the narrative going into the next Fed meeting, as we don’t get the PCE inflation readings (which they 
officially target) until the day after the next decision. 
Federal Reserve Bank of New York President Williams yesterday reiterated his support for another 
25bp Fed hike this year, but added that “I don’t think at this time I have any reason to believe that we 

would need to do more.” This puts him in line with the median from the September meeting dot plot. 
Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond President Barkin also spoke yesterday about the need for the 
Fed to grow more restrictive as he said that “It seems clear to me at this point that these shocks are 
continuing, and that inflation is enduring, and that doing something about it makes some sense.” He also 
asserted that the Fed’s goal of 2% was “actually something” that you can hit. 
With that backdrop in mind, front-end yields generally moved higher around the world. For instance, the 
US 2yr Treasury was up +2.1bps on the day to 4.78%, while the 2yr UK yield (+1.7bps) also rose even 
as 2yr Bunds were unchanged. However, this wasn’t echoed further out the curve, as the risk-off tone 
put downward pressure at the long end. So the 10yr Treasury yield fell -2.9bps to 5.21%, whilst the 10yr 
bund yield fell -0.8bps to 3.47%. The one piece of (relative) good news came from France, where the 
10yr yield fell -5.8bps to 4.84%, but even that still left the Franco-German 10yr spread at 137bps, 
clearly above its levels before the stress began a couple of weeks ago. 
As all that was going on, it wasn’t the best day for equities either, as AI concerns meant there was a fresh 
drag from chip stocks and data centers. While chips are just one sector, semiconductors and 
semiconductor equipment make up over a sixth of the S&P 500, so what happens there can easily 
drive the overall index. And that was clear once again yesterday, as the Philly semiconductor index (-
0.23%) posted a 5th consecutive decline for the first time since July. So that exerted a drag on the 
overall S&P 500, which was just worse than unchanged (-0.02%) on the day, whilst Europe’s STOXX 600 
also fell -0.10%. Data centers also lagged yesterday with CoreWeave’s stock down -3.05% alongside 
Applied Digital (-3.48%) and GDS Holding (-2.60%). CoreWeave, who has been a large issuer in the $HY 
market this year, saw their 5Y CDS contract close +79bps wider yesterday after having briefly traded 
above 1000bps intraday.   
Otherwise, yesterday also brought the start of US bank earnings, with several names reporting. JPMorgan 
(-0.86%) reported an unexpected decline in fixed-income trading revenue, as the bank joined all 
other 24 members of the KBW Banks Index in closing lower on the day. Goldman Sachs (-0.23%) also 
missed on fixed-income revenue relative to estimates, but saw equity trading beat estimates. The 
worst performing bank in the index was Citi (-5.60%), who saw its fixed income revenue beat 
expectations, but its share price struggled despite that. 
In the meantime, yesterday also brought a fresh rise in energy prices that led to renewed concerns on 
inflation. That was particularly clear in Europe, where the front-end natural gas contract (+3.40%) hit a 
post-2022 high of €87.22/MWh. Admittedly, Brent crude oil (-0.90%) came down slightly to 
$105.69/bbl, but concern about a longer period of high prices persisted, as the 12-month Brent 
future (+1.12%) hit a new high for the year of $87.37/bbl – also its highest level since August 2022. So 
near-term inflation expectations continued to rise, with the 1yr Euro inflation swap (+7.3bps) back up to 
3.59%. 
Asian equity markets are mostly trading higher this morning as semiconductor stocks rebound from 
the recent selloff, though rising oil prices are capping gains. Across the region, South Korea’s KOSPI 
(+1.13%) is leading advances, supported by a recovery in chipmakers. Japan’s Nikkei (+0.89%) is also 
posting solid gains, while Hong Kong’s Hang Seng (+0.16%) is modestly higher. In contrast, Australia’s 
S&P/ASX 200 (-0.66%) is under pressure, and China’s CSI 300 (-0.31%) is lagging regional peers. 
Meanwhile, the Shanghai Composite (+0.01%) is hovering around flat levels. S&P 500 (+0.22%), Nasdaq 
100 (+0.24%) and Stoxx (+0.34%) futures are higher.   
Earlier this morning, data showed that Chinese consumer inflation remained relatively subdued in 
September, as holiday-related spending provided only limited support to prices, while producer price 
inflation accelerated sharply to a more than four-year high. Consumer prices rose +0.9% y/y, coming 
in just below expectations of +1.0% and slightly above August’s +0.8% increase. In contrast, producer 
prices climbed +5.0% y/y, exceeding forecasts of +4.4% and accelerating from +3.8% in August. The 

stronger-than-expected increase in factory-gate prices reflected resilient business demand, rising 
input costs linked to AI-related investment, and supply disruptions associated with the Middle East 
conflict, pushing PPI to its highest level since June 2022. 
Separately, China’s trade data surprised to the upside in September. Exports surged +36.3% y/y, 
marking their strongest growth since February, while imports rose +31.0%, the fastest pace since 
June and an acceleration from August’s +28.2% increase. Both figures comfortably exceeded market 
expectations of +25.7% for exports and +25.0% for imports.   
Finally, there wasn’t much data yesterday, but we did get a couple of US releases. One was the NFIB’s 
small business optimism index, which fell a bit more than expected to 97.9 in September (vs. 98.2 
expected). Conversely though, the existing home sales for September beat expectations, coming in at an 
annualised rate of 3.99m (vs. 3.95m expected). 
Looking at the day ahead now, the main data highlight will be the US CPI print for September. Otherwise, 
central bank speakers include ECB President Lagarde, the ECB’s Elderson, Pereira, Escriva, Makhlouf 
and Schnabel, and the Fed’s Bowman and Barkin. Otherwise, we’ll get the Fed’s Beige Book. Finally, 
today’s earnings include Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, and BlackRock.
