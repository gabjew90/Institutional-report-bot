On Our Minds 
September 2026 FOMC Postmortem: The First Hike 
 
The September FOMC delivered a hawkish message, raising the Fed funds rate by 25bp to 
3.75%-4% and signalling increasing concern about the broad-based nature of inflation. 
The updated SEP reinforced this shift, with stronger growth, a lower unemployment-rate 
outlook, higher inflation projections and a median policy path consistent with further 
tightening. Chair Warsh’s press conference underscored the Committee’s focus on 
persistent inflation and his view that policy remains accommodative. We continue to 
expect two additional 25bp rate hikes in December and March, with the Committee likely 
to coalesce around removing the accommodation from the 2025 insurance cuts, though 
the wide dispersion in the 2027 dot distribution highlights growing disagreement about 
how much further tightening may ultimately be required. 
 
The Rate Decision and Statement 
In with SG and consensus expectations, the FOMC decided to raise its Fed funds target range by 
25bp to 3.75% - 4%, without any dissenters. The statement’s language on inflation was the main 
change, as it changed from “Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent 
goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors, including 
energy.” to “Inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will support a timelier return to the 
Committee's 2 percent goal.”. By de-emphasizing the temporary factors from energy price shocks 
when considering elevated inflation, the Fed signalled it’s now more worried about how broad-
based the pickup in inflation has become. A clear signal that more hikes are in the pipeline. 
The SEP Update 
At this meeting we also had an update of the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP). As before 
the Chair did not submit forecasts. Overall, the SEP update came in line with the SG expectations 
in the September FOMC preview. In terms of the economic forecasts, GDP growth for this and next 
year were revised up 10bp to 2.3% and 2.4%, respectively. The unemployment rate forecast for 
revised down notably to 4.1% for all of 2026 through 2029 – below the Fed’s long-run estimate of 
4.2%. Core PCE inflation for 2026 was revised up again from 3.3% in June to 3.4%, closer to our 
own forecast of 3.5% for this year, whereas the median forecast for 2027 remained at 2.5%.

The SEP continued to show that most FOMC members see upside risks to core PCE inflation. In case 
of the unemployment rate outlook, however, none of the Committee members see downside risks 
in their outlook. So, many Fed officials have become more upbeat on the unemployment rate 
outlook, which signals that the Committee sees less risks of a stagflationary outcome. Inflation risks 
are now squarely the focus for monetary policy, while the employment side of the Fed’s mandate, 
for now, not perceived to be a main issue.

Post-Meeting Press Conference 
In his post-meeting press conference, chair Warsh emphasized the recent pickup in real activity 
and improvement in the labour market. in terms of inflation, the chair built on his Jackson Hole 
speech emphasizing the slow progress in getting inflation structurally towards 2%, de-
emphasized individual inflation releases in favour of the trend in inflation and the broad-based 
pickup in inflation across PCE price components. 
It’s clear chair Warsh favoured a rate increase, as he’s (i) worried about the inflation trend, not 
just individual data points, and (ii) the main reason he singled out strong real activity and labour 
market trends was that these data shows that at the moment the employment side of the Fed’s 
mandate is not an issue for the Fed. He clearly indicated that he, for now, squarely focused 
attention on the Fed’s ongoing inflation problem. 
Also, notable was the Warsh

of views regarding the neutral rate and long-run estimate of the unemployment rate (see right-

Beyond the September Meeting 
Our Fed outlook calls for two additional 25bp rate hikes at the upcoming December and March 
FOMC meetings. Warsh’s press conference, as expected, did not shed light on upcoming policy 
rate actions. But given the statement and the chair’s remarks it’s clear, given the worry about the 
broad-based persistent pickup in inflation within the Committee, more hikes are coming. In our 
Fed call change I laid out the case that agreement on rate hikes will not come easy and it will be 
the inflation data that will eventually drag a majority to agree with policy rate increases. 
Especially next year, however, that appetite for hikes will likely wane and the SEP Fed funds rate 
distribution for 2027 clearly suggest a high degree of disagreement amongst Fed officials. Given 
that the Warsh’s “remove accommodation” remarks at the presser I think there’s consensus 
possible within the FOMC to eventually remove the accommodative effect of the three 2025 
‘insurance cuts’, but, at least for now, not much restrictiveness beyond that. Hence, we stick with 
our outlook for a total of 75bp of rate hikes. But if inflation data continue to disappoint into early 
next year that view might change.
