JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 


[Page 3]
Last week, the SPX fell 0.8%, NDX fell 0.6%, and RTY lost 2.4% as the yield curve bear-
flattened, with the 2Y and 10Y adding 26bp and 18bp, respectively, with most of the moves 
occurring after Thursday's PPI release. Oil rose ~9% during the week, with WTI and Brent 
now at $100.05 and $104.61, respectively. The key drivers were inflation data, rising 
geopolitical tensions, and positive Tech headlines.  
  
We maintain our Tactically Cautious / Neutral view into Wednesday’s Fed Meeting. As a 
reminder, this is not a fundamentally based view, as we see both the macro (above trend 
GDP growth) and micro / earnings environment (>20% EPS growth for 26Q3 and 26Q4) as 
supportive of stocks. We see our view supported by a 10Y approaching / breaching a new 
cycle high (4.99%), credit spreads likely to widen given elevated issuance, and heightened 
volatility especially if Warsh maintains opaque communications. Further, positioning is not 
yet giving a clear directional signal. 
  
Historically, when the 10Y yield makes a new cycle high, it takes up to 8 weeks for Equities 
to fully digest. Our colleague, Craig Cohen flags that over the last 60 years, when the 10Y 
yield has a 2 standard deviation move higher, then Equities have negative monthly returns. 
Currently, a 2z move is ~50-55bp and MTD the 10Y yield is +21.7bp, with more than 50% of 
the move coming after Thursday’s PPI print. We view Craig’s data of supportive of our view 
that bond vol is a bigger headwind than bond yield levels. Yields are resetting higher in 
response to stronger, persistent growth. In the 1990s, the 10Y yield averaged ~6.65% for 
the decade as SPX gained 316%, NDX 1556%, and RTY 200%. For comparison, since the 
COVID low on Mar 23, 2020 through Friday’s close, the SPX is +242%, NDX +319%, and 
RTY +190% with the 10Y yield averaging 3.16%.

JAY’S SCENARIOS 


[Page 7]
•         NO HIKE: A very low probability event, as inaction risks institutional credibility, and 
would make the reaction at the July FOMC meeting appear pale in comparison. In this 
event, we think 1y1y OIS rates (as a representation of the market’s terminal Fed policy 
expectations) likely decline 25bp, which, using the observed sensitivity of Treasury yields to 
policy expectations in recent months, likely translates to a 20bp decline in 2-year yields.  
•         HIKE, NO GUIDANCE: The FOMC raises rates 25bp to address above-target inflation, 
but Warsh offers no forward guidance. This ambiguity could lead to a modest decline in 
front-end rates. 
•         HIKE, GUIDE TOWARD UNWINDING 2025 EASES: Given the Chair’s view that labor 
markets are at full employment, he indicates the 75bp of risk management cuts in 2025 
were unnecessary, and the front end converges temporarily toward pricing in 75bp of total 
hikes. 
•         HIKE, R* IS HIGHER: The Chair admits policy is not restrictive and also admits that 
large-scale AI investment could drive productivity higher, resulting in higher trend growth 
and a higher neutral policy rate. Markets seem somewhat priced for this outcome, though 
1y1y is back near its cycle highs and longer-dated forward expectations have eclipsed their 
2023 highs, indicating markets believe in higher for longer, likely due to structural changes 
to the US economy (Figure 4). Given that perceptions of neutral tend to evolve slowly over 
time, we do not think the front end would move much, but there is room for longer-dated 
forwards to rise further. 
•         HIKE, CRUSH INFLATION: To borrow Bruce Kasman’s analogy, the Fed moves from 
a forgiving, New Testament Committee to a more vindictive, Old Testament central bank as 
it commits to quickly bringing inflation back to target, allowing markets to price in a higher 
terminal rate, but longer-dated forward OIS rates likely decline as markets interpret the Fed 
as being willing to sacrifice growth and labor markets to restore price stability. 
•         Overall, we [Jay & team] are not willing to attach probabilities to these outcomes, but 
the ‘No Hike’ and ‘Crush Inflation’ scenarios appear less likely than the other three 
outcomes in our mind, which could help the 2-year sector find firmer footing next week.

POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE – Weekly Wrap | Macro Risks Reflected among ETFs, 
CTAs, and Equity Themes + YTD Wrap Up 
SUMMARY: This week the market was faced with higher oil and higher yields, which put 
some pressure on stocks.  This coincided with small selling among HFs, but overall a fairly 
neutral response.  At a thematic level, there are signs of macro extremes among 
systematic/CTAs with some of this also reflected by HFs at an industry level. 
  


[Page 11]
KEY POINTS: 
1. HF flows/positioning — quiet tape, mild re-grossing, neutral risk:  Aggregate 
flows were boring: global re-grossing +0.5z WoW (smaller than last week), while net 
flows were -0.3z. US TPM remains neutral (1wk -0.5z, 4wk -0.3z; level -0.2z / 
40th %-tile), and HF net leverage basically unchanged around 50th %-tile. 
2. ETF flow bearishness — equities & credit flashing risk-off:  US equity ETF flows 
-2z on both 1wk and 4wk (most negative since late 1Q26 / post “liberation day” 
2025). Credit vs. short-term Treasury ETF flows have also reached close to -2z 
bearish level, which have often lined up with tactical lows. 
3. CTA positioning — macro extremes building, equities still middling:  CTAs are 
stretched in key macro trades: crude up to ~85th %-tile LT (~70th 12m), and US bond 
positioning down near the 5th %-tile (back at prior lows seen with similarly high 
yields). Equity CTA positioning is off MoM but still roughly neutral (US/EU/Japan 
~56–65th %-tiles; HK lower at ~34th). 
4. Macro-risk themes — defensively positioned beneath the surface:  Despite 
neutral top-down equity positioning, several themes have very bearish positioning 
(due to macro risks), e.g.: Homebuilders (rates), Airlines (oil), Footwear/Sportswear 
(consumer + costs), Restaurants (gas + inflation) — have all been net sold with L/S 
ratios in the low single-digit %-tiles. 
5. International highlights — EMEA leadership, EU rotations, AxJ net selling:  EU 
Energy flipped back to buying (+1z) with positioning already rich (SXEP L/S 
~90th %-tile LT); EU Semis sold into strength; EU HC saw flows flip to ~1.5z selling 
on the leg lower. AxJ had -1.3z net flows (Semis/Energy bought, most other sectors 
sold), while Japan saw small net buying (<1z) led by Telecom/Industrials/Energy and 
selling in Consumer.

o   BUYBACK IMPACT – Fabio Bassi and our cross-asset team flag the 
deleterious impact on buybacks from capex increases (full note is here). In 
1Q26, only Microsoft bought back stock among the four biggest AI spenders, 
and even that was just $3.4bn, the lowest combined outcome for the group in 
nearly a decade. More importantly, the capex cycle is now showing up in 
cash conversion. AI capex is not just pulling forward spend, it is changing the 
earnings to cash conversion of the hyperscaler cohort. LTM net income is 
roughly ~$600bn versus ~$170bn of free cash flow, a $430bn gap that was 
close to zero at the end of 2023. The market implication is not that buybacks 
disappear across the index, but that the incremental bid has been 
concentrated in mega caps, so a moderation in that cohort can have an 
outsized influence on index support and drawdown behavior. From a market 
mechanics perspective, the more relevant question is whether the buyback 


[Page 14]
bid continues to offset incremental supply in the largest index constituents. 
With repurchases moderating in parts of hyperscaler tech and equity 
financing reappearing as a tool, the path of least resistance is a more mixed 
supply backdrop.
