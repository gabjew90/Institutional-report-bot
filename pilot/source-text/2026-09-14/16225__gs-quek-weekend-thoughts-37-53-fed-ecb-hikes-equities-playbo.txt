29/53. FOMO… Fear of Momentum.
The market narrative has snapped into focus around two risks that have lingered on for months -
geopolitical re-escalation and rising yields. Yields have been grinding higher throughout the year but
Jackson Hole was probably the reminder that mattered. Add in the oil spike from a US-Iran re-escalation,
and the two risks compounded into the same move.
Point is volatility is dormant, not dead. 15 weeks to year end and there is not much room for complacency.
X-asset strategist CMG is tactical neutral on Equities over next 3m, but still constructive on a grind higher
over 12 months. Positioning-wise, I would continue to flag the asymmetry - US Tech (vs bullish
takeaways from our flagship conferences, see last section) and China looks very light versus Korea,
Taiwan and Japan.

1/ Macro tweaks - Fed, ECB, Oil.
Post CPI print we now expect a 25bp hike at the upcoming Sept FOMC. Interesting to note the rationale is
less of an economical one, but rather we think there is a reluctance by Fed to surprise a market, which is
now pricing a nearly 90% chance of a hike. Our view remains one that overshoot of the 2% inflation target
can be attributed to fading one-time factors.


[Page 3]
Across the Atlantic we now expect another 25bp ECB hike in Dec on back of recent macro resilience,
renewed spike in energy prices and the Council's recent hawkish message. Keeping our view that the ECB
will normalize the policy rate when inflation slows but timing is pushed back by two quarters to 4Q27.


[Page 4]
BOJ remains set to hike at next Friday's meeting.
Meanwhile our oil analysts have raised their Brent forecasts by $5/bbl to $85 by end of year, and $80 in
2027, reflecting the assumption that Middle East shipping disruptions persist into next year.
2/ Equities vs Higher Rates – History Playbook.
US equities typically struggle in the subsequent 3 months post Fed hikes, but have generated average
returns of +9% in the following 12 months. Worth noting current markets are already pricing more than
three hikes within the next year - the ‘hawkish’ bar is high. What’s key is earnings momentum, and so far
corporate results and balance sheets are both robust.


[Page 5]
Sectorial view. Energy and Tech have delivered the strongest returns in the 3 months post hike. But results
have been inconsistent across different episodes.
