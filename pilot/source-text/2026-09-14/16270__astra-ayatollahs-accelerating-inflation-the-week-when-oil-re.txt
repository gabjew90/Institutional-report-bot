Astra, Ayatollahs, & Accelerating Inflation: The Week 
When Oil Rewrote Warsh's Playbook 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SUNDAY, SEP 13, 2026 - 12:30 AM 
The four-day week began with investors nursing Friday’s payroll-induced hangover and ended 
with them pricing an almost certain Fed hike after hot producer prices, sticky CPI, and triple-digit 
oil. Bonds were monkeyhammered (10Y near 5%), equities spent three days reconsidering their 
life choices, with Friday’s oil-dip-driven relief-rally proving that the cure for inflation is 
apparently... temporarily cheaper inflation. Meanwhile, Astra (AGI?) arrived (briefly reviving AI 
dreams)but fund investors quietly hit the eject button. 
Spot the odd one out for the week... 
 
Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb turned crude into a critical inflation input again with $100+ crude 
and record diesel doing the PPI/CPI dirty work. The dollar was flat while gold chopped on real 
rates, copper’s tariff premium cracked, bitcoin held the mid-$70ks, and fund-flow tape screamed 
de-risk US equity / grab short-dated bonds - with the FOMC statement now the only thing that 
matters before the quarter turns. 


[Page 2]
 
 
Labor Day may have shortened the trading calendar, but Friday’s jobs report ensured investors 
returned to their desks on Tuesday with a lingering macro hangover. 
The report transformed the Fed debate from “how long can it wait?” to “can it credibly avoid 
hiking?” 
September hike odds jumped above 60% immediately after payrolls, from roughly 35% earlier in 
the month. 
That hawkish reset supplied the week’s starting conditions: higher front-end yields, a firmer 
dollar and considerably less enthusiasm for paying heroic multiples for long-duration growth 
stocks. 
Payrolls Lit the Match, Hormuz & The Houthis Poured the Fuel On 
It 
'Two chokepoints, One bigger crisis' as the Iran war moved from being a 'transitory risk 
premium' story into a 'higher for longer' reality check 
Ship-trackers had Strait of Hormuz vessel transits stagnant (though 'dark fleet' transit headlines 
continue to hit) - versus roughly 125 commodity vessels and about a fifth of global oil and LNG 
flows before the conflict lit up in late February. 
Iran said it hit ten ships near the strait after U.S. strikes on Iranian tankers; the IRGC promised 
escalation. 


[Page 3]
 
 
Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthis seized Mocha on the Red Sea and, on Friday, reached Perim 
island in the Bab el-Mandeb, tightening the other choke point that actually matters for product 
and Asian crude runs. 
Saudi crude supply, per the IEA on Friday, fell 2.3 mb/d month-on-month to 6 mb/d in August - a 
more-than-three-decade low after attacks on Saudi energy facilities - and satellite imagery 
showed smoke near the East-West Pipeline that Riyadh uses to dodge Hormuz. 
Trump’s own framing that the war likely runs past the November midterms didn’t exactly calm 
the front month...

did the Real Damage 
Despite a decent retracement of around 3% on Friday as Gulf-Iran shipping discussions (and 
'temporary Hormuz deal' chatter) offered a sliver of hope, both Brent and WTI were still heading 
for weekly gains over 9% for the second week in a row...

That left crude once again the week’s dominant cross-asset variable: bullish energy, bearish 
duration, supportive of the dollar and toxic for rate-sensitive equities. 
When diesel is ripping and Hormuz traffic is in single digits, the inflation channel into next 
week’s FOMC writes itself.. 
PPI Prepped, CPI Clarified: The Fed Hike Went From 'Maybe' To 
'Don't Embarrass Yourself'... 
Thursday's PPI slowly pushed the door closed on Waller's dream of a 'pause', then Friday’s CPI 
slammed it shut - close enough to expectations to trigger a relief rally in equities, but hot enough 
beneath the surface to seal the hawkish policy message. 
The equity market’s positive response reflected the avoidance of a true upside disaster - not 
evidence that inflation had disappeared. And it was the latter that spiked September (next week) 
Fed-hike expectations to as close to a certainty as it gets at 90%... 


[Page 8]
 
 
Futures also placed a roughly 97% probability on at least one additional hike before year-end 
(with 51bps of hikes now priced in for 2026 - massive swing from the 60bps of cuts priced-in in 
February)... 
 
...and Bessent's buy buyback bazooka didn't help the rest of the curve... 
Bond Vigilantes Returned... 
The whole Treasury curve ripped higher this week with the short-end clubbed like a baby seal. 
2Y Yields climbed above 4.60% (up a stunning 28bps on the week), the 10Y approached 5%, 
and the 30Y reached roughly 5.38%, its highest area since 2007. Friday’s oil pullback allowed 


[Page 9]
 
the long-end to ease modestly, but the global sovereign selloff was still the worst since the Iran 
war began... 
 
The curve’s (bear flattening) message was uncomfortable: the market believes the Fed is 
tightening enough that growth will slow. It is a late-cycle / restrictive-policy signal, not an 
immediate “recession starts tomorrow” siren. 
 
This isn't a conventional growth scare yet, it's an oil-driven inflation and discount-rate shock for 
now: 
Resilient claims → hotter PPI & 0.3% CPI → higher Fed-hike probability → higher global yields 
→ stronger dollar → pressure on equities, metals, and duration-sensitive credit. 


[Page 10]
 
But, the principal tail risk is that geopolitical supply disruption converts an already uncomfortable 
inflation print into a persistent energy shock. 
The bullish escape route is equally clear: oil stabilizes, CPI/PPI rollover, and strong earnings 
regain control of the tape. 
Until then, the market is trading less like “soft landing” and more like “no landing, with tankers on 
fire.” 
Credit hasn’t blown out (yet), but it isn’t asleep... 
 
This was not simply a U.S. repricing... 
The ECB unexpectedly raised its deposit rate by 25 basis points to 2.50%, its second increase 
of 2026, and warned that inflation could remain above target through 2027. German ten-year 
yields reached their highest levels since 2011, while long-dated French and UK borrowing costs 
moved toward multi-decade highs. Markets began pricing additional ECB tightening, potentially 
as soon as December. 


[Page 11]
 
 
Meanwhile, the Bank of Japan is expected to lift rates by 25 basis points next week to 1.25%, 
the highest in 31 years. That prospect strengthened the yen and kept the carry-trade unwind 
lurking in the background. 
 
The Bank of England is expected to hold at 3.75% next week, but the market has shifted toward 
additional tightening as oil threatens another inflation wave. 
And so with the geopolitical and macro-economic bombshells dropping like, well, bombshells, 
every other asset class spent four (or five for some) sessions translating it into risk.

more as the cost of capital rose...

Copper was a mess on policy confusion - prices lost more than 4% Thursday and headed for a

Fund Flows finally Cracked...but investors Did Not Abandon 
Bonds 
Global equity funds recorded $15.52 billion of outflows in the week through September 9, the 
largest withdrawal since March 18. 
US equity funds accounted for a striking $32.27 billion of selling, including a record $40.44 
billion withdrawal from large-cap funds. 
Europe and Asia still attracted $11.16 billion and $3.03 billion respectively. 
Yet this was not indiscriminate liquidation.  
Technology funds received $1.89 billion, financials took in $1.25 billion and energy funds 
returned to inflows - the familiar “sell the index, keep the narrative” split. 
Global bond funds attracted another $8.95 billion, extending the preference for income but with 
a major caveat: $6.65 billion went into short-term bonds, while corporate-bond funds lost $2.37 
billion. 


[Page 22]
 
 
Source: Reuters 
Translation: Investors wanted yield without volunteering for duration risk. Retail and intermediary 
money is selling US equity beta into the oil/inflation scare, parking in short duration and 
(selectively) overseas equities, while commodity fund flows finally flipped positive into the crude 
spike - and the HY complex has started to ask for a little more spread without panicking. 
Bottom line: 
Growth is too strong to justify easing, inflation is too sticky to ignore, oil is too geopolitical to 
forecast, and AI spending is too large to abandon... or every wall comes crumbling down.  
That leaves markets confronting the least Goldilocks combination imaginable: healthy payrolls, 
shrinking real wages, soaring energy costs, and synchronized central-bank tightening - with 
Oracle’s backlog serving as the week’s lone $664 billion comfort blanket (and even that 
collapsed). 
Markets are being repriced for "higher-for-longer plus tightening" making duration hard to own, 
oil's sustainability is capped by midterm politics (a Democratic Senate flip raises the odds of a 
post-November retreat below $100), and the combination of extreme positioning, multiple
