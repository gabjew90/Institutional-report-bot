The trap breaks
After months inside a broad consolidation, NDX is now threatening to break lower. Both NDX and SMH
are breaking key technical levels just as HFs have aggressively rebuilt tech longs, while broad-index
fear remains relatively modest.
This is where positioning meets catalyst. AI safety headlines are pressuring multiples, energy risks are
compounding and technical deterioration suggests VIX could have further to run.
The seasonal volatility window has arrived right on cue.
Untrapping
NDX has spent months trapped inside a major consolidation, allowing considerable positioning and
psychological pressure to build. As we outlined last week, any break from the range risked releasing
that stored volatility. We are seeing that today.
As of writing, NDX futures are taking out the rising trend line and falling below the 100-day MA.
There are a few short-term support levels below, but if this develops into a genuine breakdown, the
first major support does not arrive until the 200-day MA.

Calm index, ugly tape
As BTIG’s Jonathan Krinsky notes, nearly 5% of Russell 3000 stocks made a 52-week low even as the
index itself remained less than 3% from a 52-week high.
Historically, this setup has been little more than a coin flip over the following 20 trading days. However,
it has also appeared ahead of several meaningful market declines, including March 2000, October
2007, July 2015, September 2018, January 2020, December 2021 and January 2026.

Just in time
GS PB shows HFs bought US TMT in 10 of the past 11 sessions, led by semis and semi equipment. The
two-week pace of long buying now sits in the 97th percentile of the past five years.
That rebuild matters because dispersion remains extreme. The 200-day vol of GS TMT Momentum
Winners versus Losers is around 76, compared with just 13 for SPX. Tech price action has been
almost 6x more violent than index vol.
Net/net: newly rebuilt tech longs are vulnerable to AI and Iran headlines. According to Lee
Coppersmith, near-term vol makes sense; a break in the bigger AI story does not.

AI worry, energy fear
A tricky weekend for both AI and energy.
Amodei’s call to “pace the frontier,” backed to varying degrees by Altman, Musk and Hassabis, hit
Asian AI hard, with SoftBank down double digits. The concern is obvious: if safety risks slow the
frontier, regulation becomes another reason to compress AI multiples and question capex durability.
Privorotsky believes the amount of spending that ultimately changes will be limited. Microsoft, Meta
and China are not about to leave the race. The more likely outcome is tighter safety gates and
independent oversight, not a capex stop.
Those closest to the technology are effectively saying it is advancing so rapidly that it may need
slowing down. The warning itself contains the bull case: this stuff really works.
Privorotsky’s bigger concern is energy.
Saudi Arabia’s East–West pipeline, which had been moving roughly 4–5mb/d around Hormuz, remains
shut. Repairs could take days or weeks, while Yanbu inventories may cover exports for only another
five to seven days.

If the outage persists, around 4% of global supply could be at risk, on top of the barrels already
disrupted through Hormuz. Meanwhile, the Houthis are pushing towards Bab el-Mandeb, turning a
single-chokepoint crisis into a two-front energy problem.
The Iran–Gulf meeting in Oman has also been postponed. There is no obvious off-ramp.
AI looks like a valuation scare. Energy looks like a genuine compounding risk.
Agony
We entered the weekend with rebuilt tech longs, continued dispersion and much less broad-index fear
priced. The GS Panic Index fell three points on Friday, its third-largest one-day decline in three years.
