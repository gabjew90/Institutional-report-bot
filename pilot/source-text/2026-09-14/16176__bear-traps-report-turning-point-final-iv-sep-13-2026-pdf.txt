effect” that Alan Greenspan put on the map. His Fed 
model said a dollar of household wealth adds roughly three 
to five cents of spending. Later work puts the stock-market 
channel closer to two point eight cents. Either way, the 
channel is real -- and it is now concentrated in fewer hands 
than at any point in the data.

more.

that equity wealth. So, the spending engine and the asset 
base sit in the same hands.

How Do You Finance This? 
 
Two signals hit the same weekend -- Dario Amodei 
calling for a slowdown, OpenAI pushing its IPO back   
-- and both point the same direction: the AI buildout is 
cooling. Those hyperscaler bonds were priced on revenue 
growth outrunning debt service. If the buildout slows, the 
cash flows that justify a century bond or a forty-year 
tranche don’t materialize on schedule. That’s the credit 
risk: not default tomorrow, but a repricing of the entire long-
end stack once the market stops believing the AI revenue 
curve. 
 
*A slowdown in capex growth isn’t the same as revenue 
collapse -- the hyperscalers have enormous cash flow, 
and a slower buildout could actually improve their 
credit profile by reducing the debt they need to issue. 
The repricing risk is real, but it’s a risk, not a verdict.

Why Have CCC Spreads Widened So Much? 
 
1. Higher-for-Longer Rates Hitting the Weakest Borrowers 
Hardest -- Hawkish repricing in Fed Funds futures -- with 
the market now pricing a near-certain hike next week and 
a materially higher rate path into 2027 -- disproportionately 
pressures CCC-rated issuers. These borrowers carry the 
highest leverage, the most floating-rate debt exposure, 
and the least ability to refinance at elevated rates.  
 
Refinancing risk is acute: CCC issuers that loaded up on 
cheap debt during the low-rate era now face a wall of 
maturities at structurally higher costs, with limited market 
access.  
 
2. 
Idiosyncratic 
Stress 
and 
Distressed 
Name 
Concentration 
 
A small number of large distressed names heavily 
influences the CCC index. CSC Holdings (cable) has been 
cited as a primary driver of index-level softness, with 
pressure on its curve pulling the broader CCC OAS wider.  
 
Other notable individual movers include Accendra Health, 
the worst performer in the index on multiple recent 
sessions.  
 
3. Structural Quality Upgrade of the Broader HY Index 
BB-rated bonds now form a record share of the US HY 
index, while CCCs represent only ~10% of the index -- the 
lowest proportion since 2002.  
 
This compositional shift means the broad HY index is 
increasingly a BB/B story, insulating its OAS from CCC-
specific stress. Higher-rated buckets have tightened 
substantially, reinforcing the quality bias.  
 
4. Investor Quality Rotation and Underweight Positioning 
Investors have been actively rotating up in quality, with the 
market showing a clear underweight in CCCs.  
 
This positioning dynamic is self-reinforcing: reduced 
demand for CCC paper widens spreads further, which in 
turn discourages re-entry. “The divergence between 
BB/B tightening and CCC widening reflects dispersion 
in the credit market rather than a broad selloff, making 
issuer selection more important than market beta.” 
This is the type of sell-side BS you hear at major 
inflation points. They want all the sheep invested, and 
they will NOT say otherwise until we are down hard in 
a crisis. 
 
5. AI Disruption and Fundamental Deterioration 
Geopolitical tensions (Middle East conflict pushing oil 
above $100/bbl) and AI-driven disruption risks -- 
particularly in software and IT -- have weighed on weaker 
credits.  
 
Private credit default rates are projected to rise from 4-5% 
to 9-10% by year-end 2026, with AI disruption accounting 
for a significant portion of incremental default risk, and 
spillover effects into public CCC markets through cross-
market correlations.

It legitimizes the wrapper. Much of the hesitation around

Copper Inventories, Prices and LME Warrants  
 
An LME warrant is the title document for a specific lot of 
exchange-approved metal sitting in an LME-registered 
warehouse. When a holder instructs a withdrawal, the 
LME's electronic system marks that warrant "canceled" -- 
the formal first step in pulling metal out of the LME system, 
done before the physical load-out happens. Canceled 
tonnage is removed from the "live" or "on-warrant" pool 
(the stock genuinely available to satisfy exchange 
deliveries), even though it's still sitting in the warehouse for 
the moment. 
 
A rising canceled-warrant ratio tells you someone wants 
physical metal badly enough to pull it out of the exchange 
system, rather than just holding a paper claim on it. That 
shrinks the effective deliverable supply, and it's watched 
as a leading indicator -- cancellations today often show up 
as declines in headline LME stock a few weeks later, once 
the physical loading-out process catches up. It tends to 
coincide with tightening spreads (cash trading at a 
premium to forward contracts, i.e., backwardation). For 
months, canceled warrants went up because metals 
traders moved physical copper from outside the US into 
the US to preempt higher tariffs that Trump said he might 
put on copper imports. Lately, the number of canceled 
warrants has fallen sharply, suggesting traders are 
reluctant to build more onshore inventory because they 
think Trump might cancel or postpone these tariffs 
indefinitely.
