Bonds, Bitcoin, Bullion, & Big-Tech Battered As 
Black Gold & PPI Spike Rate-Hike Odds Ahead Of 
CPI 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, SEP 11, 2026 - 03:55 AM 
Tl;dr: An ugly day all around that seemed to be triggered by the hot PPI (all energy-driven) 
sending rate-hike odds for next week soaring. Hormuz angst and Houthi attacks piled the 
pressure on, smashing crude oil prices dramatically higher (diesel crack new record highs) The 
dollar spiked, driving gold and crypto lower. Stocks were a shitshow with Small Caps ad Big 
Tech leading the plunge. 
Among the 100s of MSGs we get every day, this one caught our eye for its conciseness... 
"The flow, in one line: war → oil → diesel → PPI → hike odds → yields → everything with 
duration or no coupon gets sold, while crude and energy equities keep the bid. Bonds, bitcoin, 
and bullion are the wreckage. Black gold is the wrecking ball. CPI is tomorrow. The FOMC is 
next week." 


[Page 2]
 
 
A six-month war that was supposed to be “over soon” is now being priced as a 
midterm-and-beyond oil shock (Trump claimed prices would collapse after the Midterms), 
wholesale inflation re-accelerated on diesel, and the market has spent four sessions selling 
duration, growth, gold, and crypto while crude does the opposite... so let's start there... 
Energy 
WTI broke above the psychologically critical $100 mark today, back at the highs of the war... 
 
There was no specific driver (although the Houthis seem to be getting more confident). Iran 
signaled it has no intention of backing down in the face of an American naval blockade and will 


[Page 3]
 
escalate its strikes if the US continues attacking its territory. Meanwhile, Tehran-backed Houthis 
in Yemen are targeting Saudi Arabian assets, with the kingdom warning its crude production 
plunged last month to the lowest since 1990. 
Some technicals at play as well today... 
“Crude is trading at its highest levels since May as the market reprices both the escalation and, 
increasingly, the duration of geopolitical risk,” said Rebecca Babin, senior energy trader at CIBC 
Private Wealth Group. 
“As prices push into levels where options dealers have meaningful short-gamma exposure, that 
positioning is adding fuel to the move higher this morning.” 
Across other technical measures, trend-following CTAs flipped to 100% maximum long on 
Thursday in Brent, suggesting they’ve exhausted buying capacity for this session, according to 
Kpler. Robot traders positioned in West Texas Intermediate futures are currently 91% long, the 
firm added. 
As Goldman  stated back on the 7th, risks to their base case remain significantly tilted to the 
upside on net, especially near-term, with the potential for Brent to exceed $120/bbl if average 
Gulf output remains 4mbpd below pre-war levels. 
But, of course, it's not just crude that matters - in fact refined products, especially diesel likely 
matter more... 
 
The 3-2-1 crack spread has stabilized as crude has soared... 


[Page 4]
 
 
But the Diesel crack broke above 110 today (a new record high)... 
 
US diesel inventories are projected to fall this month to their lowest in more than two decades, 
according to the Energy Information Administration. Weekly data from the agency showed that 
distillate stockpiles rose 2.1 million, though supplies still held at lowest seasonal level ever. 
Retail diesel prices (which dominated the rise in PPI today) in the US are within a tick of $6 - a 
record.... 


[Page 5]
 
 
...and Goldman's Adam Widjaya has faced a number of questions on the view of ‘economic rent’ 
shifting from refining margins to crude price – with the takeaway being diesel cracks have likely 
topped while crude can keep grinding higher. 
From my POV – feels like more chasing / trying to explain the move higher in crude over the last 
few weeks and wanting to fade the broader refining momentum that has been topical for the last 
couple of months.  
Crux of the thesis essentially (1) China is coming back to the market as a crude buyer and (2) 
diesel exports should start to recover – and since this is a global market at the end of the day – 
the r/r on diesel is skewed more negative.  
Would think recent headlines on China fuel imports + exports jumping over the last month the 
main driver of this debate – but many pushing back on this saying ‘known that this was coming 
and global inventories still at lows’.  
Bulls highlighting we can see a scenario where both crude and diesel grind higher from here 
(especially with renewed focus on escalation in the ME) with nothing materially changing on 
balances while bears looking for signs of demand destruction ex US and highlighting recent 
headlines on more stronger than expected flows out of the Strait. 
And of course, soaring oil prices meant higher Treasury yields... 
Rates 
Bloodbath doesn't quite capture today's troubles in rates-land. 


[Page 6]
 
Let's start at the beginning. The shortest dated signals pushed rate-hike odds for next week up 
to 75%... 
 
That means (barring a 'cool' surprise from CPI - which would not surprise us) if The Fed were 
not to hike next week, it would be the biggest surprise ever (and traditionally The Fed likes to 
keep the market on-side, which clearly is not the case under Warsh). Will we get a Waller-esque 
dove-nado before the black out period starts? 
That, of course, dragged rates higher across the Treasury curve with the short-end 
underperforming, up a shocking 13bps (while the long-end rose just 7bps)... 
 
5% 10Y yields were looming until 1300ET when the 30Y auction printed 3bps through - 
signaling massive demand... 


[Page 7]
 
The record low share awarded to primary dealers was also the lowest-ever primary dealer 
award in a conventional Treasury auction of any tenor. 
“For years investors yearned for these levels, maybe not under these conditions, but given 
interest for all-in yield, the auctions are in our view reflections of true demand,” said George 
Goncalves, head of US macro strategy at MUFG.  
...but that didn't last as the 10Y went out at its highs... 
 
Rising energy prices hit government bond markets globally, with UK two-year yields climbing 17 
basis points. Euro-zone bond markets slumped after the European Central Bank raised interest 
rates by a quarter point to 2.5%, as expected. The second increase since the Iran war began 
was based on signs inflation will remain “well above target for an extended period,” the ECB 
said. 
Bond Vol (green) is starting to break out (not a good sign, as VaR shocks loom)... 


[Page 8]
 
 
TSY yields are almost at their highest levels since 2007... 
 
“Crude oil drives inflation, and if it starts getting into the system it’s going to be hard to contain 
it,” said Tony Farren, managing director in rates sales and trading at Mischler Financial Group. 
“There’s no reprieve for yields to go lower if inflation remains elevated.” 
...and for once, soaring yields are actually impacting stocks (especially long duration growthy 
stuff)...

investors are wary of whether higher oil prices will be sustained.
