Follow the money
The bond market is where all the action and increasingly all the attention is right now. Forget all the
advanced macroeconomic analysis for a minute. Here is the fixed-income setup through the one thing
that ultimately matters: where the money is actually flowing.
Winning
Fixed income is winning the battle for investor dollars globally in 2026. YTD inflows equal 7.9% of AUM,
versus just 1.3% for equities, with high grade, munis and loans all attracting serious money.
BofA
Furious flow
The US picture is equally clear. Fixed-income flows remain strong, with government bonds doing most
of the heavy lifting.
EPFR
Well above
And this isn't just another decent month. Global bond-fund flows are running well above the historical
median for this point in September.

Under the hood
Investors are still showing a preference for protection. Short-duration and inflation-protected bond
funds continue to see sustained inflows, with the latter trending higher over the past year.

Retail too
This isn't just an institutional trade. Retail investors are joining the bond party, with their flows
providing support to bond ETFs as well.

recent pullback. The bond love affair is already well advanced.

Dialing for duration
The duration bid is broader than one ETF. Flows into long-dated Treasury ETFs accelerated in August
and outpaced IG credit, pointing to demand for duration rather than simply fixed-income exposure.
GIR
One small problem
Investors may love bonds, but the bond market isn't necessarily loving them back. The recent sell-off
has been remarkably broad and global, with higher term premia, rate expectations, inflation
compensation and real rates all contributing to the move.
"The significance of the recent bond market sell-off has been its breadth. The sell-off has been global,
with no single major bond market the obvious driver of higher yields. Forwards across both bonds and
swap curves are near multi-decade highs in several markets driven by pressure from several factors,
including term premium, rate expectations, breakeven inflation and higher real rates." (George Cole,
GS)

The real problem
Global long-end rates are pushing back toward pre-GFC levels. More importantly, much of the latest
adjustment has come through higher real rates, rather than simply higher inflation expectations.

The $5tn elephant
Then there is supply. Goldman estimates roughly $5 trillion of 10-year-equivalent gross issuance this
year and $5.2 trillion next year. That's a lot of duration for even enthusiastic bond buyers to absorb.

 AI enters the bond market
And government supply isn't the only competition for capital. BofA argues that the incremental credit
demand needed to finance the AI investment boom could be the “canary in the coalmine” for rates.
BofA
Strangely calm
Perhaps the most interesting chart to finish on: despite elevated yields, huge supply and increasingly
crowded positioning, bond volatility remains remarkably muted relative to the level of the 10-year.
Everyone is buying bonds. Everyone wants duration. Everyone remains remarkably relaxed. The flows
are bullish, the positioning is crowded and the supply is enormous. Something has to give.
