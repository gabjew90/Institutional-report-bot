Performance Estimates: 1 Global Fundamental L/S managers finished -0.55% in September, outperforming the market 
benchmark. 
Fundamental L/S 1 asset weighted returns -0.55% in September vs. MSCI TR -1.19% 
• 
On an asset weighted basis, global Fundamental L/S total returns finished -0.55% for the month of September, as beta losses were partially offset by 
positive alpha. YTD global Fundamental L/S total returns now stand at +12.6%. 
• 
Excluding beta, global Fundamental L/S funds alpha finished +0.73%, driven by short side gains outweighing long side losses.  
• 
From a factor perspective, Info Tech, Medium-Term Momentum, and Asset Selection were among the largest alpha contributors to Fundamental L/S 
returns in September, which were partially offset by losses from Long Concentration, Size, and Materials.  
• 
Asia L/S managers finished +1.4%, while Europe L/S managers finished -1.1% and US L/S managers finished -0.7%. YTD, Asia, Europe, and US L/S returns 
now stand at +23.4%, +8.3%, and +8.4%, respectively.  
• 
TMT L/S funds gained +4.4% in September (now up +17.6% YTD), while Health Care L/S funds lost -2.4% (now up +9.9% YTD). 
• 
The equally weighted average and median Fundamental L/S returns for September finished at +0.85% and -0.55%, respectively. YTD average and median 
Fundamental L/S returns now stand at +13.1% and +2.5%, respectively.  
 
Systematic L/S 1 asset weighted returns +3.46% in September 
• 
Systematic L/S returns (our proxy for quant performance) rose +3.46% in September based on our estimates – the best monthly performance YTD, driven 
entirely by positive short side alpha.   
 
• 
From a factor perspective, Medium-Term Momentum, Crowdedness (Short and Long), and Asset Selection were among the largest alpha contributors to 
Systematic L/S returns in September, which were partially offset by losses from Short-Term Momentum, Size, and Health Care.

Trading Flows: Global equities were modestly net sold in September, as short sales slightly outpaced long buys, and gross 
trading activity increased at the fastest pace in 4 months.  
 
• 
The global book was modestly net sold in September, as short sales modestly outpaced long buys. Gross trading activity increased at the 
fastest pace since May. 
• 
Single Stocks collectively were net sold, driven by short sales outpacing long buys (1.2 to 1). Communication Svcs, Info Tech, and 
Financials were the most $ net bought global sectors in September, while Industrials, Health Care, and Staples were the most net sold.  
• 
On the other hand, Macro Products (Index and ETF combined) were net bought, led by short covers.  
o 
US-listed ETF shorts increased +4% in September, led by shorting in Credit and Small-Cap ETFs.  
 
o 
ETF shorts now make up 10.1% of the US equity short book excl. Index products (vs. 10.2% at the end of August), in the 2nd 
percentile vs. both the past year and the past five years.

Regional Positioning: Net selling in EM Asia and North America was mostly offset by large net buying in Europe.  
• 
North America was net sold for the first time in five months, driven by long buys outpacing short sales (1.3 to 1). North America’s weighting vs. 
the MSCI World ACWI fell -2.9 pts to 17.3% U/W (in the 23rd percentile vs. the past year and in the 5th percentile vs. the past five years). 
• 
Europe saw the largest % net buying in more than five years, led by Macro Products and driven by long buys + short covers (2.0 to 1). Europe’s 
weighting vs. the MSCI World ACWI rose by +2.3 pts to 2.8% O/W (in the 91st percentile vs. the past year and 57th percentile vs. the past five years). 
France, Ireland, Denmark, and Sweden were among the most $ net bought European markets in September, while the Netherlands, Finland, and 
Portugal were among the most net sold.   
• 
DM Asia saw little net activity, as long buys were offset by a roughly equal number of short sales. Buying in Hong Kong and Australia was offset 
by selling in Japan. DM Asia ’s weighting vs. the MSCI World ACWI rose +0.7 pt to 3.4% O/W (in the 74th percentile vs. the past year and in the 95th 
percentile vs. the past five years).  
• 
EM Asia was heavily net sold in September, driven by long and short sales (1.3 to 1).  Selling in Korea and to a lesser extent China was partially 
offset by modest buying in Taiwan. EM Asia’s weighting vs. the MSCI World ACWI fell -0.4 pts to +9.5% O/W (in the 50th percentile vs. the past year 
and in the 90th percentile vs. the past five years).

US Sector Positioning: HFs rotated into TMT stocks in September, while nearly every other sector was net sold.    
• 
US TMT stocks were significantly net bought in September as they were the only net bought sectors, driven by long buys outpacing short sales 
(2.2 to 1). 
o 
Info Tech saw the largest % buying since Feb ‘25, driven by long buys outpacing short sales (2.1 to 1).  Semis & Semi Equip, Software, 
and IT Svcs were the most net bought subsectors, while Electronic Equip and Tech Hardware were the only net sold. US Info Tech 
long/short ratio now stands at 1.81, in the 40th percentile vs. the past year and in the 25th percentile vs. the past five years.  
 
o 
Comm Svcs saw the largest % net buying since Nov ’25, driven by long buys outpacing short sales (2.5 to 1). Nearly all subsectors were 
net bought, led by Interactive Media & Svcs and Diversified Telecomm Svcs. US Comm Svcs long/short ratio now stands at 3.23, in the 
34th percentile vs. the past year and 55th percentile vs. the past five years. 
 
• 
Consumer Discretionary stocks were net sold, driven by short sales outpacing long buys (1.5 to 1). Automobiles, Textiles, Apparel & Luxury 
Goods, and Household Durables were the most net sold subsectors, while Specialty Retail and Hotels, Restaurants & Leisure were the most net 
bought. US Consumer Discretionary long/short ratio now stands at 2.06, which is a 1-year low and in the 63rd percentile vs. the past five years.    
• 
Non-Consumer Cyclicals saw the largest net selling in five months as all sectors were net sold, led in $ terms by Energy, Industrials, and 
Financials. 
 
o 
US Energy stocks saw the largest % net selling in nearly 3 years, driven almost entirely by short sales. Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels and 
to a lesser extent Energy Equip & Svcs were both net sold. US Energy long/short ratio now stands at 1.54, in the 61st percentile vs. the 
past year and in the 54th percentile vs. the past five years. 
• 
Defensive sectors saw the largest % net selling in nearly 1 year, as Health Care and Staples were net sold, while Utilities saw little net activity.  
o 
US Health Care stocks were net sold for a second straight month (3 of the last 4), driven almost entirely by long sales. Biotech, Health 
Care Tech, and Pharmaceuticals were the most $ net sold subsectors, while Life Sciences Tools & Svcs and Health Care Providers & 
Svcs were the only net bought. US Health Care long/short ratio now stands at 2.29, in the 2nd percentile vs. the past year and in the 22nd 
percentile vs. the past five years.  
 
• 
Most net bought US industries in September (notional) – Semis & Semi Equip, Software, Life Sciences Tools & Svcs, Interactive Media & Svcs, 
Capital Markets, IT Svcs, Diversified Telecomm Svcs, Specialty Retail    
 
• 
Most net sold US industries in September (notional) – Biotechnology, Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels, Electrical Equip, Automobiles, Electronic Equip, 
Instruments & Components, Household Products, Machinery, Tech Hardware, Storage & Peripherals
