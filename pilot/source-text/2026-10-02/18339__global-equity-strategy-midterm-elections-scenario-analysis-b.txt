In a little over a month, one of the year’s most anticipated political catalysts will 
come to pass in the form of the US midterm elections. At stake will be the 
Republican majority in Congress that has so far directed key tax, spending and 
regulatory decision-making. Early indicators suggest Republicans will face a 
strong challenge in maintaining their majority given current generic Congressional 
vote polling (Dems +8.3pts, see RCP), betting odds (Dems 92% House, 63% 
Senate, see Figure 20Beting Ods on US Senate Control) and historically challenged outcomes for the incumbent 
party (e.g. 20 of 23 historical midterms resulted in a significant loss of seats for 
incumbents). In the 2024 presidential election, it was easier to map the equity 
impact of potential policy priorities across sectors, industries, themes and factors. 
However, the equity impact for the upcoming midterms across election 
outcomes scenarios will likely be more nuanced and favor stock picking. For 
now, S&P 500 options suggest only a modest event premium around the election, 
with no pronounced kink in the term structure (see Figure 3S&P 50 options price in limited risk premium around the midterm election and Report). 
Historically, US equity volatility has tended to rise in the months leading into the 
midterm elections, often trading above typical seasonality patterns (see Figure 5VIX 
tended to increase ahead of the midterms). The VIX has typically peaked roughly one month before election day and then 
reset lower post-election (see Figure 6And peak around 1M
 before the election, then reset lower post-election).

Investor concern for the midterms has largely centered on the pace of the AI/
datacenter buildout. A comprehensive AI regulatory bill seems unlikely to win 
Congressional approval in the near term as the focus has been on affordability (see 
report). In fact, States continue to lead on datacenter restrictions, including tax 
policy rollbacks/increases, energy upgrades and community grants creating a 
fragmented regulatory landscape. We expect AI capex momentum to remain 
intact after the midterms and potentially even strengthen in 2027 and 2028, 
as companies seek to meet AI demand and advance investment before political 
risks intensify around the next presidential cycle. Long-end rates could be 
impacted by the midterm election outcome though we expect Gridlock/Blue Wave 
to be marginally more favorable versus Red Wall. Overall, Midterm should serve 
as an important market clearing event. Historical performance in the months 
following the midterms tends to be positive. Irrespective of election outcome, the 
key issues of trade/tariffs, sanctions, regulation (e.g. AI, permitting) and foreign 
policy will remain sensitive to Presidential decision-making.

Midterm Election Outcome. In the past 1-2 months, markets have had to increasingly 
consider the possibility of a Blue Sweep versus a consensus Gridlock scenario. Polymarket 
suggests Democrats’ odds of retaking the House are as high as 92%, while the Senate has 
noticeably strengthened to 63%. The generic ballot supports this outlook, with Democrats on 
average leading by ~8.3 points across major trackers (RCP), a substantial improvement from 
the Democrat’s ~2 point edge in 2024 and ~1 point edge in 2022 at the same time in the 
calendar year. On average, the sitting president’s party has lost ~27 House seats and ~3 
Senate seats over the last 23 midterm cycles (i.e., back to 1934). For reference, the current 
Senate majority is 53 in favor of Republicans and a House majority of 218 (see Press Release). 
In the scenario where Democrats gain control of both chambers of Congress, their policy 
ambitions could still be checked by a Republican White House (i.e. Presidential veto 
authority) and a Senate filibuster. Therefore, strong Democrat-led tax, regulatory, healthcare 
or climate legislation becoming law likely remains constrained over the next two years. Must-
pass legislation such as the government budget, debt ceiling, defense authorization and tax/
healthcare extensions will be familiar battleground.

Sector and Industry Performance. Midterm election cycles are typically less 
meaningful than the Presidential election cycle because of the lower odds of unified 
government. Historical S&P 500 Sector and Industry performance through midterm 
election cycles have been mixed since the 1990s with no perfect hit rates. Investors 
seeking to avoid timing the midterms can favor industries that tend to outperform 
(relative to index) across the full one-month-before through one-month-after window 
(see Figure 36Sector Performance Around Elections and Figure 37Industry Group Performance Around Elections), led by Semis (+7.2% cumulatively), Software (+4.2%) and 
Technology Hardware (+3.7%). More tactical investors can buy the typical pre-election 
dip and position for a post-election rally in Consumer Durables & Apparel (-0.6% before 
to +2.9% after), Consumer Discretionary Distribution & Retail (-2.0% to +1.5%), Media 
& Entertainment (-1.1% to +1.0%) and the standalone Materials sector (-1.5% to +0.9%). 
The trend also appears in Transportation (-0.2% to +0.9%), Health Care Equipment & 
Services (-1.9% to +0.6%) and Utilities (-1.5% to +0.2%), although the post-election 
rallies have been smaller. Conversely, investors may want to avoid the usual rally-to-dip 
pattern in Telecommunication Services (+1.1% to -2.4%) and Household & Personal 
Products (+1.6% to -0.2%).
•
Equity Volatility. The S&P 500 options market appears to price only a modest event 
premium around the election, as reflected in the absence of a pronounced kink in the term 
structure (see Report). Historically, US equity volatility has tended to rise in the months 
leading into the midterm elections, often trading above typical seasonality patterns (see 
Figure 5VIX tended to increase ahead of the midterms). The VIX has typically peaked roughly one month before election day, and then 
reset lower post-election (see Figure 6And peak around 1M
 before the election, then reset lower post-election). The VIX curve currently sits well below levels 
seen ahead of prior midterms, with limited election-related risk priced in, even as macro 
concerns loom over markets, including rates, Iran/oil, AI disruption, etc. As such, we 
favor positioning for a higher VIX ahead of the event, given the potential for VIX levels 
to mirror their historical pre-election moves and/or for one of the other key macro risks 
to intensify ahead of the election.
•
Mini-Reconciliation during Lame Duck Session of Congress. Depending on the 
extent of the majorities generated in a potential Gridlock/Blue Wave scenario, 
Republicans may opt to utilize the lame-duck session of Congress after the midterms to 
compromise and pass a budget reconciliation bill. Sizing remains unclear but the 
incorporation of priorities such as higher Defense spending and support for farmers could 
see the bill sized at $3-500B (see Report). Thematically, this could be viewed as net 
positive for Defense spending (JPRAEROD, see Report) and the Low-end Consumer 
(JPRLOWCO, see Report).
