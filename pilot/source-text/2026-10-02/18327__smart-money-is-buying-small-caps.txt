The small-cap reversal setup
Small caps have been one of the biggest casualties of the rates shock, but the setup is starting to get
interesting.
The Russell is sitting on major support, positioning has been washed out, professional investors have
started buying the weakness and small-cap vol has remained surprisingly contained.
Now the big question is rates. If yields finally reverse lower, the conditions for a sharp small-cap
reversal are increasingly falling into place.
The pain has been real. So is the potential for a reversal.
The big Russell level
The Russell is trading right on the huge trend line, around the 200-day moving average.
The last time we undershot the 200-day, it turned out to be a great buying opportunity.
Let's see how this little undershoot plays out.

Buying the Russell reversal?
The Russell has been crushed by the rates move, but professional investors have started buying the
weakness.
Goldman's Robert Quinn notes that non-dealers bought around $3bn of Russell 2k futures between
September 1 and 22. Asset managers dominated the buying with $2.2bn, with smaller participation
from hedge funds and non-reportables.
The starting point matters: non-dealer positioning entered September at a multi-year low. The
question now is whether this contrarian buying is the beginning of a reversal, or gets overwhelmed by
deteriorating credit.

Rates have gone too far?
The rates shock is getting stretched.
The 30-year has gone from 5.25% to 5.69% in just seven sessions, while bond Daily Sentiment has
collapsed to 10%.
BTIG, which has been looking for higher yields for months, now sees increasing risk of a sharp tactical
reversal lower.

Green shoots
A possible reversal in the 10-year could quickly feed through to small caps catching a bid.
The rates connection has been huge for small caps, making any meaningful reversal in yields
particularly interesting here.

The rates paradox
Here's the counterintuitive one.
BTIG argues that a reversal lower in yields may not necessarily rescue tech. Instead, it could trigger a
reversal in QQQ/IWM and similar relationships.

That could mean better breadth and stronger small caps while QQQ and Mag 7 actually move lower.
Lower yields don't necessarily mean higher tech.
BTIG/Bloomberg
Russell vol
Small-cap vol, RVX, has moved higher, but the pickup has been remarkably ordinary.
Given the magnitude of the sell-off, one would have expected RVX to react much more aggressively
relative to VXN.
For a market down this much, the vol market is showing surprisingly little panic.
