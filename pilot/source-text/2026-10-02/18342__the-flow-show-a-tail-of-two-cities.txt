Q3 Winners & Losers: winners... bitcoin 43%, oil 42%, software 18%, Mag7 11%; 
losers...  zero-coupon bonds -15% (Chart 3), semis -11%, industrials -10%, Korea -8%.  
Zeitgeist: “Rates don’t matter when you’re curing cancer.” 
The Price is Right: best of times, worst of times... tighter financial conditions crushing 
equity breadth... 400 SPX stocks trading below 50dma, 300 stocks <200dma as market 
trading “long artificial intelligence” (NDX), “short artificial irrelevance” (SPW), confident 
MAG7, AI, biotech (Chart 4) impervious to higher rates, 1999 analog intact (Chart 5).  
Tale of the Tape: it’s risk-off until US$ peaks; US$ spiking on credit event risk... Aussie 
dollar (Chart 6), French bonds, risk parity (RPAR), bond insurers (AGO), tech CDS (ORCL)... 
deleveraging price action; threat of policy panic (open-ended UST buybacks, YCC... can’t 
let yields hit AI capex boom/stocks into midterms) limits downside unless small/mid-cap 
(MDY <$666, IJR <$135) follow banks breakdown... “peak growth optimism” = -ve tech.  
The Biggest Picture: bull case is “it’s the railroads this time”; 40% peak concentration 
for internet, Japan, Nifty Fifty, roaring ‘20s bubbles, but 63% for railroads in 1881 (Chart 
2); good news... AI capex 3.5-4% GDP vs. 5% railroads peak + price of semis soaring vs. 
deflation of freight rates topped railroads; bad news... railroad boom’s backdrop was 
falling UST yields, and big top in railroads caused by credit/liquidity events.

On Railroads vs. Data Centers:  
• 
1st railroad bubble ended (1873) by excess capex & credit crunch... 1861-72 
railroad stocks up 3x (Chart 10); massive US railroad buildout from 35k to 70k miles 
until capital spending peaked at ~5% GDP ($400mn) in early-1870s after Franco-
Prussia war caused US credit crunch, capital repatriation to Europe from US, ending 
in collapse of Jay Cooke & Company in 1873, bankruptcy of ~115 railroad companies 
following 12 months.  
• 
2nd railroad bubble ended (1881) by excess capex & deflation... 1877-81 
railroad stocks up 2.5x, railroad stocks as % US equity market peaked at 63% in 
1881 as railroad construction (4x in four years to 1882) caused excess capacity & 
deflation of freight rates (Chart 11), revenues, profits, funding for capex; 42% 
collapse in railroad construction in 1882 sparked banking & credit panic (failure of 
Marine National Bank, Grant & Ward), gold outflows ($150mn from US to Europe), 
big contraction money supply, and 3rd longest US recession of all-time (1882-85).  
• 
Railroads vs. AI data centers: good news... a) while GDP proxies of AI capex (e.g. 
fixed investment on “computers & peripheral equipment” – Chart 13) soaring, AI 
hyperscaler capex ($1.2-1.4tn) on course to be 3.5-4% GDP in ‘27, still below 5% 
peak levels for railroads early-1870s and fiber-optic network buildout in late ‘90s 
internet bubble, b) the price of semiconductors currently soaring (Chart 14) in 
contrast to 5% p.a., deflation of freight rates in 1870s/80s that ultimately made 
revenues/profits unable to sustain capex boom; bad news... railroad booms
