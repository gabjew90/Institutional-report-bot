1) European Rates & EURUSD Update
George Cole (Rates Strategy) and Kristian Brauten-Smith (G10 FX Spot Trading) on our morning call this
morning. Their views below on the European selloff yesterday.
GEORGE COLE: Yesterday was the return of European sovereign risk — not the slow, benign spread
widening we've grown used to, but a violent, two-sided one: Italy and France both sold off, the Bund
caught a safe-haven bid, equities wobbled and EUR/USD got hit hard.
The tell was the front end — 2y BTP-Bund spreads blew out, which smells like genuine stress spilling
over from France, not just a long-end credit drift. You can call it a carry unwind — steepeners and
sovereign longs getting deleveraged in a hawkish, energy-shocked, higher-vol regime — and maybe it is.
But don't reach for the 2011 playbook: back then the ECB had low inflation and room to be generous.
Today it doesn't. Remember SVB — markets priced dovishness, then learned the economy was still hot
and got run over by a duration sell-off.
For EUR/USD the mechanism is clean: if the ECB trades tighter sovereign conditions for more hikes, or
goes dovish and supportive on the balance sheet, euro goes lower. The only bullish path is spread control
via the balance sheet — moderating full-pace QT, reinvestments, a BoE-2022-style backstop — while still
hiking.”
KRISTIAN BRAUTEN-SMITH: EURUSD still trades very technically – the break down through the
1.1310/25 region held as resistance on a brief test early afternoon, as did the critical 1.1200/25 support
zone we highlighted last month – remember this was the breakout level from Q2 2025, and highs back in
September 2024 in the lead up to the election. From an orderbook perspective we are now much cleaner –
the stops that we’d usually characterise as magnetic are no more, and we have a degree of symmetry back
in Dollar pairs. Continued widening in OATs allowed for the move, but we would be wary of a slight
reversal today.
The rates picture yesterday was different especially in the afternoon. NFP today could be an important
input, and much like Williams earlier in the week Jefferson is erring towards a skip at the October FOMC

meeting. This lines up with the “reluctant hiker” commentary from the RBA, ECB and BOE already this
week.
2) US ECONOMICS (MERICLE) - NFP Preview – LINK
BOTTOM LINE: We estimate nonfarm payrolls rose by 80k in September, slightly below consensus of
+88k. On the positive side, the level of layoffs remains low and big data indicators of job growth picked
up sequentially. On the negative side, September payroll growth has tended to underperform its recent
trend when Labor Day is later in the month—like it was this year—and last month’s sharp increase in
nonfarm payrolls was boosted by outsized increases in leisure and hospitality and local educational
services payrolls that we do not expect to repeat. We estimate that the unemployment rate edged down to
4.0% (vs. our previous forecast that was in line with consensus and the prior month at 4.1%), reflecting the
decline in continuing claims. We forecast a 0.2% month-over-month increase in average hourly earnings,
below consensus, reflecting negative calendar effects.
In FX, we would note the implied moves ahead of NFP today are 38bps in EURUSD, 43bps in AUDUSD,
and 47bps in USDJPY.

3) ADAM CROOK – 2 Mins Read: AI Issuance Pulse-Check - LINK
BOTTOM LINE: The impact of hyperscaler + broader AI-related debt issuance remains a key focus for
macro markets, as the global Bond-market remains under duress and as we approach the window of
potential max-escalation in the Middle East….. The sense that investment plans (and the issuance needs
behind them) are relatively rate insensitive is clearly among factors weighing on duration risk appetite. ..
Also for equity markets, the sheer magnitude of AI-related issuance/spend in 2027 + beyond makes it
critical that Hyperscalers continue to demonstrate positive ROIC momentum in upcoming Q3 earnings,
through revenue growth + cloud margins, to maintain confidence in this ‘generational Capex cycle’….
We asked some of the thought-leaders from the Credit floor -> What does the issuance schedule look like
for the rest of the year? Are issuers sensitive to the impact they are having long-ends? Are they shortening
tenor of issuance?... and could this be of source of further pressure on long-end rates markets?
Are issuers sensitive to the impact they are having on long-ends? Not the hyperscalers - Interest expense is
a very small portion of their earnings.
Are they shortening tenor of issuance? Looking at the 2026 issuance in H1 and H2, noticeably a bit more
issuance in shorter-dated tenors: In Jan – Jun, 29.9% was in the 0-5yr bucket, after June this was 46.4%.
The numbers: 2026 projected hyperscaler issuance of $250bn (2024 was sub $20bn for context!), with
most of this year’s funding ($230bn) completed. 2027 Hyperscaler forecasts of $420bn of gross issuance,
based on: i) Bottom-up CapEx forecasts; ii) Continued expectation that 35% of 2027’s hyperscaler CapEx
spending will be debt funded. Not just ‘Hyperscalers’: Hyperscalers ‘only’ 40% of the $575bn+ of global
gross AI-related debt issuance this year, additional supply from project finance issuers (both IG and HY)

to fund data centers and chips, and (non-hyperscaler) issuers within the technology/AI ecosystem ->
Causing significant issues for asset allocators managing overall AI-related exposure – across credit and a
range of other asset classes.
More in EUR markets: Expect share of AI-related issuance from the EUR market to increase, given its
year-to-date share has lagged a range of other global IG markets: ~5% of gross supply in Europe vs ~23%
in US IG + ~18% in US HY.
Macro implications: Debt-issuance behind the ‘generational’ Capex cycle will raise long-end yields’
sensitivity to debt supply and increase the risk that yields overshoot fair value by a larger margin….
Skewing the risk that longer-term yields move significantly higher, and more detached from, underlying
macro drivers…. the only thing we see halting or reversing this is: i) Credibly lower energy vol + levels;
or ii) Emergence of genuine growth worries)…

4) GLOBAL MACRO STRATEGY (CHANG) - Cross-Asset Opportunities in Oil Tail Scenarios - LINK
BOTTOM LINE: The tail scenarios of oil sharply lower or higher are outcomes that could pose risks or
opportunities for many investors, and we are often asked where leverage might be best for those oil-driven
scenarios using our cross-asset frameworks. Compared to options pricing, oil higher or lower screens well
for both tail scenarios because oil implied volatilities have not risen to their old highs. High implied
volatility in rates combined with the offsetting effects of growth and policy for rates in an oil shock mean
that they do not screen as the best exposure to a move in oil prices. Europe’s energy exposures mean that
Europe-linked assets across asset classes screen well. Low FX implied volatilities mean that European FX
screens as well as, or better than, equities as an expression of either scenario.
Our baseline views on both oil and rates are more benign than current market pricing. We think there are
more paths to relief than there have been in some time and that it makes sense to position for higher
equities. But with risks to that view still easy to envisage, we would want that upside in protected format,
or to protect against the tail risks to that view. There may be a role for hedges against some of the tail risks
to a more benign view through optionality for higher index volatility and lower European equities or FX,
which should perform well in an adverse oil scenario but may also be protective against broader risks.
On an options-adjusted basis:
Oil screens well for both tail scenarios. In a relief scenario, equity upside screens well. European equities
screen particularly well, but so do the S&P 500 and Nasdaq in the US. European FX also stands out—in
particular CZK, HUF, and PLN. In an adverse scenario, European equity downside and VIX upside screen
well. European currency weakness vs. USD also stands out.
