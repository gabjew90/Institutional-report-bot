1) Rates & the Fed - from accommodative to restrictive. The Fed made a hawkish 
pivot at its September meeting introducing a Fed funds rate hike that was supported by 
almost the entire committee. Additionally, projections suggest that the Fed thinks rates 
should remain elevated through 2029, and the move higher was described as removing a 
dose of accommodation rather than introducing a dose of restriction. But while markets 
have been quick to price in a string of more rate hikes, we forecast only one more (in 
December), with a strong chance that the FOMC will ultimately conclude that additional 
rate hikes are unnecessary as inflation recedes (yoy core PCE inflation is trending 40bp 
BELOW what Fed members expected) and as oil prices recede. And Dominic Wilson and 
Kamakshya Trivedi highlighted this week how rates, energy, and AI remain the three 
biggest risks in markets today, but are well priced now, suggesting that we may see more 
risk relief than risk escalation in the month ahead. 
The move higher in rates, however, has taken us to levels not seen since before the 2008 
Global Financial Crisis — raising the question once again: are the high rates we are seeing 
today abnormal, or were the ultra-low rates of the post-GFC era the abnormality that is 
unlikely to repeat? If the latter is true, rates may have pivoted to a new ‘old normal.’

On June 1, 2023 we introduced this new investment list which highlights a selection of 
fundamental Buy-rated US stocks across the Goldman Sachs Americas Global 
Investment Research department. These ideas are sourced from our US research 
analysts, but chosen by members of our Investment Review Committee. This new 
‘Conviction List - Directors’ Cut’ is designed to provide investors with a curated and 
active list of 20-25 of what we believe to be our most differentiated fundamental Buy 
ideas across our US stock coverage. 
The subcommittee, which includes the Americas Director of Research and the other 
senior research managers listed as authors of this report, will collaborate with each 
sector analyst to identify top ideas that offer a combination of conviction, a 
differentiated view and high risk-adjusted returns. The subcommittee will then choose 
what they view as the top 20-25 ideas across the department for the list. We intend to 
refresh and publish the list monthly to allow the list to become part of the investment 
process of Portfolio Managers. 
Importantly, we do not express thematic or factor views with this list. Rather, this list 
focuses on bottom-up fundamental analysis from our analysts. While our analysts will 
incorporate their view of macro impacts on the fundamentals of their stocks, we will not 
intentionally express factor, rates, inflation or other macro views in the construction of 
this list. Our analysts appropriately incorporate the impact of these factors in their 
estimates which are a critical component to our selection process. 
Finally, inclusion on this list is not a stock rating and addition to or removal from this list 
does not necessarily represent a change in the analyst’s investment rating for such stock.

Johnson Controls International Plc (JCI) - covered by Joe Ritchie  
 
 
HVAC OEM in the early innings of transformation as growth accelerates 
JCI is in the early innings of a dual top and bottom-line transformation that is poised to 
drive consistent, highly visible earnings growth, >500bps improvement in adjusted 
operating margins, and a more than doubling of EPS through 2028. Now led by CEO 
Joakim Weidemanis — whose prior 13-year executive experience at Danaher is 
increasingly visible in the rollout of a proprietary business system — JCI is executing a 
structural shift from a cyclical commercial construction player to a mission-critical 
thermal management platform. This continuous improvement operating model is 
already unlocking tangible improvements in manufacturing throughput, innovation 
velocity, customer-facing time, and cash conversion. Simultaneously, JCI’s AI-driven data 
center cooling business is scaling rapidly, with organic order growth exceeding 30% for 3 
consecutive quarters and pushing the total backlog to a record $21 bn (up 32% 
organically in F3Q26). As these dynamics play out, we believe FY27 organic revenue 
growth can accelerate into the LDD-MT% range, significantly outperforming 
management’s longer-term HSD% framework and current consensus expectations. 
Accelerating organic growth powered by structural AI and data center cooling 
n
tailwinds. F3Q26 results represent a clear inflection point for JCI’s organic growth 
algorithm, with growth accelerating to +10% in the quarter from just +4% in the 
prior year. This trajectory is anchored by the company’s data center business, which 
is projected to grow from 12% of revenue (~$2.5 bn) in 2025 to approximately 33% 
(~$10 bn) by 2029. Crucially, data centers move through their building lifecycles 
roughly 60% faster than traditional commercial buildings, accelerating the 
higher-margin downstream service and replacement cycle. As the only OEM with 
complete in-house ownership of all chiller subsystems (compressors, CDUs, cold 
plates, and air handlers), JCI has leading system-level optimization, faster innovation, 
and strict COGS control. This is illustrated by its newly introduced CDU line, which 
has already amassed a backlog exceeding $1 bn ahead of its 2027 shipping 
schedule. Driven by three quarters of average organic order growth > 30% and a 
record $21 bn backlog, we think JCI possesses excellent forward visibility to deliver 
FY27 organic growth in the LDD-MT% range (GS: +11% with Americas +15%) 
compared to the current consensus of +HSD%. 
Enterprise-wide Danaher-style business system driving an underappreciated 
n
self-help narrative. JCI is unlocking massive internal efficiencies by deploying a 
rigorous continuous improvement system across its global operations. Led by Mr. 
Weidemanis, the proprietary business system utilizes value stream mapping, Kaizen 
events, integrated supply chain planning, and visual daily management to optimize

Occidental Petroleum Corp. (OXY) - covered by Neil Mehta 
 
 
Management-driven to FCF harvesting inflection company 
OXY is employing advanced recovery techniques to extend the life of depleting shale oil 
reserves at the same time as it ramps up its focus on capital efficiency and deleveraging 
under new leadership that arrived in March.  Advanced recovery efforts could add 2 
billion barrels of oil equivalent (BBOE) to OXY’s reserves. And cost containment 
initiatives could also drive as much as $4bn of incremental cash flow by 2030.  Look for 
excess cash to be delivered back to shareholders as a rising dividend or opportunistic 
buybacks. 
Advanced recovery techniques could extend reserve life. US shale oil fields face a 
n
broad problem of high depletion rates as the ‘easy oil’ has been extracted from a lot 
of acreage.  But OXY’s leading edge in advanced recovery techniques has the 
potential to add an additional 2 BBOE to its reserves as it boosts its estimated 
ultimate recovery rate (EUR) by 70%-140%.  OXY is currently working on 3 
commercial developments, with each development expected to deliver ~$125 mn in 
cumulative free cash flow over 10 years and OXY has identified 30 more 
development-ready projects the company can pursue over time. 
A trio of efficiency programs to drive strong incremental FCF growth. OXY is 
n
embarking on a self-help initiative that aims to address management, operational, 
and financial costs with a goal to cut $4bn of cash expenses from its financials.  
Targets: (1) oil & gas efficiencies: $0.8bn; (2) corporate savings: $1.7 bn; (3) capital 
and midstream savings: $1.5 bn — all independent of the price of oil. On the oil & 
gas efficiency front, a 12% improvement in US onshore well costs, a 10% reduction in 
production costs, a decline rate improvement to 20% from ~25% prior, and a 10% 
workforce efficiency improvement. Additionally, the company is shifting away from 
new, capital-intensive ventures, such as its legacy investment in low carbon 
solutions, which the company has begun to unwind in 2026.  Mehta forecasts that 
the company can achieve $3.4bn of these efficiencies (see charts below). 
With deleveraging comes dividends. OXY is on a path that could result in delivering 
n
back to shareholders as much as $4.00/share (in an upside scenario).  The company 
has reduced principal debt by 60% to ~$11.8 bn since peaking in 3Q24, paving a 
viable pathway for the company to reach its long-term principal debt target of $10 
bn. As it approaches an ideal debt target, look for OXY to focus its cash use on 
dividend growth.  And rising cash balances also provide OXY with the flexibility to 
enact an opportunistic share buyback program if management sees dislocation in 
the stock price relative to strip oil pricing. 
Where we are different. 
Mehta holds a more constructive view on 1) management’s renewed focus on the 
organic development of its advantaged US shale portfolio relative to a prior focus on 
resource expansion and 2) potential for measured dividend growth alongside OXY’s 
strong outlook for balance sheet improvement.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
