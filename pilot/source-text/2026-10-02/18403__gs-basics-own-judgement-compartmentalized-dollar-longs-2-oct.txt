Asia: Bit softer overnight but hardly a breakdown. Nikkei -0.94% after yesterday’s +3.3% rip, Korea
roughly flat/slightly green. HK -2.7%, but looks more technical than informational… reopening after
yesterday’s holiday into the global rates shock with Southbound shut for Golden Week. Financials are
doing most of the damage… HSBC/AIA down ~5% versus tech ~-2%. Hard to read much China macro
signal into it until mainland/Stock Connect reopen.
Equities: Can’t keep this market down. Another two ton freight truck hit the rates market yesterday…
USTs sold off hard, OAT/Bund blew through 130bp and the stress finally started bleeding into BTPs. Yet
the S&P barely moved, Nasdaq finished green and Russell green. Europe/rates blew up… US equities
compartmentalized it. Pretty impressive.
Fed: Jefferson was important. Inflation is still too high and upside risks remain, but on the next move:
“My colleagues and I will need to come to our own judgment, which may take more time.” Combined
with the tightening in financial conditions from higher yields, that is another Fed voice (Williams) arguing
against blindly following through in October. October pricing has come down… still leaves another hike
later in the year on the table.
Rates/Europe: France is now front and center. OAT/Bund pushed through ~130bp and for the first time
this episode you saw meaningful contagion into BTPs. Italy’s fiscal position is objectively much better…
running a primary surplus with a materially smaller deficit, albeit helped by EU recovery funds. If BTPs
are moving with OATs, the market is starting to price European sovereign risk rather than simply French
fiscal risk. My interpretation remains that a France political premium is creeping in… a future government
prioritizing domestic issues/spending over broader EU constraints creates a much bigger question than the
deficit alone. Seems like a notable pick up in bearish Euro flow/long dollar (see below). We get a long list
of ECB speakers… how disorderly does contagion have to become before they begin talking up support.
AI: Party continues. Compute remains scarce and pricing firm. Effective October 7, 2026, AWS
implemented an approximate 15% rate increase on its EC2 Capacity Blocks for machine learning, marking
its fourth consecutive quarterly price increase, with A100 instances included in the price hike for the first
time since Jan. Amazon is reportedly looking at moving ~$8bn of Nvidia GPUs into an SPV, selling it to
outside investors and leasing the chips back. Pretty standard evolution of the financing stack as capex gets
enormous… I don’t read it as Amazon suddenly worrying about the collateral, but it is another way to
keep spending without swallowing all the balance sheet intensity. The one I like less is Broadcom lending

up to $42bn to support leases of compute built around its own chips. Supplier financing the customer
buying the supplier’s product… circularity is undeniably increasing but not today’s problem.
Oil: The physical system is adapting. Alternative routes/ship to ship transfers appear to be getting
meaningful barrels out despite continued tanker attacks. So brute force is working. The durable
consequence increasingly looks like shipping cost/risk premium rather than outright lost barrels… ships
still get hit, freight/insurance remain astronomical and dated Brent has to compensate you for getting
physical oil through the system. I keep staring at Brent curve vs spot price, we've meaningfully eased on
backwardation but spot has continued higher...the rational explanation is cost of delivering the cargo is
through the roof. The US is reinforcing the region (another carrier force deployed) while diplomacy
remains stuck. Take away a credible threat to Hormuz and Iran’s remaining leverage becomes attacks on
infrastructure/shipping around the Gulf. The window for an easy off ramp feels like it is narrowing…
meanwhile diesel remains the important second order inflation channel.
Data: Manufacturing was materially stronger than the headline ISM miss… new orders, backlogs and
employment all accelerated, inventories remain very low, while S&P PMI hit its highest since May 22.
The common thread is demand running ahead of available capacity, led by AI/data center capex,
government spend and some precautionary inventory building, colliding with shortages plus energy/freight
costs. This is why rates aren't coming down...ISM prices paid exploded 71.1 to 77.9. So this isn’t really a
slowdown/stagflation signal yet… it looks more like reacceleration into a supply constraint. Good for near
term production given low inventories/backlogs, but 2nd order effect is stickier goods inflation and less
room for rates to come down.
NFP: Given rates are the epicenter, today is mostly about avoiding the tails. We don’t want accelerating
average hourly earnings or a hot payroll print that forces October back into play… but we also don’t want
a mega low NFP that suddenly creates a growth problem. Weaker is fine, just not too weak. My broader
view remains that the AI/post-Covid economy is one of continuous investment by corporates while they
grow top lines without adding meaningful headcount. Modest job creation with contained wages is
therefore the sweet spot. One of the core reasons not to worry about inflation (that and housing/rents).
Risk: Gotta be impressed. Rates vol blows out, OATs structurally challenged, BTPs finally get dragged in,
credit wobbles… Europe goes down and the S&P basically doesn’t care. Gross still feels incredibly robust.
AI continues to do exactly what it should, while Europe/Russell have already had enormous relative
deratings. At these levels of real rates it is getting harder to press the shorts. So I’m still constructive.
There are litany of different things that can go wrong… rates, France, oil, circular AI financing, payrolls…
but earnings are still moving too fast and multiples have already compressed. Without some radical
breakdown on the rates side, you don’t need a macro miracle… a modicum of stability and equities can go
higher pretty quickly*. Consensus outcome today is somewhere down the middle on payrolls, vol comes
out and we grind into the weekend. Everyone is afraid of everything… yet the market keeps going ten
rounds.
