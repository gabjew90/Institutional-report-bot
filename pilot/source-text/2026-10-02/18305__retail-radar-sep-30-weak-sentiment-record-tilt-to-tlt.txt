Retail Trading Activity Overview
•
September ended much as it began: retail flows remained subdued this week 
at the 12%ile, extending the softness seen earlier this month. In aggregate, 
September closed as the weakest month for retail activity since Dec 2024, 
Figure 2Retail Purchases of Cash Equities are Slowing. Retail investors remained apathetic to this week’s market 
developments: daily flows remained steady in the 15-30%ile range regardless 
of price action, Figure 11Retail Investor Daily Purchases of Stocks and ETFs. Overall, ETF flows continued to probe new lows 
(3%ile), while single stock flows edged down to the 35%ile, with only minimal 
single name activity on Tuesday and Wednesday, Figure 11Retail Investor Daily Purchases of Stocks and ETFs.
•
ETF demand remained under pressure— September’s monthly ETF flows 
approached levels last seen during the 2025 Momentum Unwind, which 
preceded the Liberation Day “Buying-The-Dip” episode, Figure 1Retail Purchases of ETFs are Slowing. This week, 
the weakness was driven by declines in International, Crypto and Commodity 
ETF flows. Partially offsetting this, fixed income ETF inflows picked up, led 
by the largest rotation into long-end Treasury ETFs on record (+5.4z) — and 
driven mainly by outsized inflows into TLT (6.1z, Figure 4Cumulative Retail Imbalance in TLT) as yields kept 
drifting higher, report.
•
The Mag 7 continued to dominate single-stock purchases; outside of Tech, 
every sector was net sold when excluding Mag 7 names. In line with previous 
weeks, retail favored Semis and Hardware over Software, with NVDA driving 
Semis purchases. Interestingly, retail investors made no discernible 
incremental move to position ahead of Micron’s earnings announcement on 
Wednesday (0.0z). The company delivered strong earnings and guidance 
pointing to healthy chip demand; the setup for another beat-and-raise was 
constructive heading into the print, with a memory backdrop that remains 
structurally tight across both DRAM and NAND, report. 
•
Small caps have continued to underperform large caps as Treasury yields 
tested new 22-year highs—consistent with the view outlined in our report; 
indeed, smaller companies rely more heavily on short-term bank financing, 
generally sub-5-year maturities at floating rates. Retail investors are typically 
net buyers of R1000 stocks, and net sellers of R2000 stocks, a trend that has 
been accelerating since March, Figure 7Retail Investors are net selers of Rusel 20 stocks. and Figure 8…
 but are typicaly net buyers of Rusel 10 stocks. This has coincided with 
a further rise in median Short Interest (as a % of shares outstanding, “SISO”) 
across Russell 2000 companies, now at the 99.8%ile, Figure 5 Short Interest in the Rusel 20. S&P 500 SISO 
has also risen recently, but the July drawdown during the Momentum Unwind 
pushed it to comparatively lower levels (96.2%ile, Figure 6Short Interest in the S&P 50). 
•
Across Energy ETFs and single stocks, retail flows have flipped from 
mildly positive/flat to mildly negative as flows through the Saudi E/W 
pipeline were restored last week. Oil prices have held near $100 this week, 
despite Middle East oil exports now just 11% below pre-war levels, report. 
That headline improvement masks an uneven recovery: crude flows have 
rebounded to 98% of pre-war levels, while product exports remain at 58%. 
Hormuz flows have now nearly returned to their late June highs.

Retail Trading Activity in Numbers for the Week: Sep 24 to Sep 30
•
Retail flows were $4.1B this week, well below the 12-month average of $6.8B/week. Retail investors continued to favor ETFs 
(+$3.4B) over Single Stocks ($0.7B).
•
Outside of Broad Based Large Cap Equity ETFs (+$1.4B), retail ETF buying was influenced by Fixed Income Treasuries 
(Long-term) (+$260M), Equity Style Call/put writing (+$188M), Equity Broad Based Multi Cap (+$169M) and 
International Equity EAFE (+$148M).
•
This week, retail investors continued to buy AI datacenters and electrification (JPAMAIDE), Top 30 AI/Datacenter 
Beneficiaries, Mag 7, Growth, AI Software/Product/Monetization, along with US Companies with High Direct China 
Revenue Exposure, see Figure 4,Retail Single Stock Activity by Themes.
•
Activity in Mag7 this week: Retail investors bought NVDA (+$1,286M), TSLA (+$514M), GOOG/L (+$153M), AMZN (+
$146M), and sold AAPL (-$18M), MSFT (-$27M) and META (-$48M).
•
Outside of Mag7, retail investors favored Tech (+194M) and were net sellers of all other sectors, led by Financials (-$278M), 
Communications (-$276M), Communication (-$235M) and Materials (-$199M).
•
Top five retail stocks last week: NVDA (+$1,286M), TSLA (+$514M), SNDK (+$327M), GOOG/L (+$153M), AMZN (+
$146M). Bottom 5 retail stocks: INTC (-$199M), SPCX (-$171M), ORCL (-$88M), NBIS (-$60M), BE (-$54M). 
•
In options, retail share is off its highs, Figure 43Retail Options Trading Share. The most traded options were in the following names, echoing previous 
weeks: TSLA, META, MU, NVDA, AMD, SNDK, AMZN, MSFT, GOOG/L and AAPL. For the most bought/sold delta and 
gamma names, see Retail Activity in Options. Also see Retail Investors Options Activity by Sectors.
•
Futures Activity (Non Retail) : The past week witnessed heavy buying from futures traders at $33B, majorly led by ES 
($19.7B), NQ (~$12.8B) and RTY (~$0.5B).

expected to reach tens of EBs in FY28 (vs. double-digit EBs in FY27). The stock is up +18% since its 9/23 (link). Note that 
the enterprise storage solutions company was just added to the S&P 500 in its most recent rebalance.

core to MGM (link). MGM is down ~10% since the news vs. PPLI +15%.
