Oil's $25 fear premium
The obvious question at $100 Brent: why is oil still here when Gulf exports have normalized, visible
inventories remain above early-2025 levels and GS sees September roughly balanced?
The answer is increasingly about risk premium rather than today's physical balance.
GS estimates roughly $20–25/bbl of risk premium remains embedded in crude. The physical shortage
has eased considerably, but the buffer has changed. Ex-OECD commercial inventories are around
record lows and the availability of spare capacity is less certain.
The barrels came back. The fear premium didn't.

The buffer is thinner
A balanced September isn't really the point.
The tail is what happens if another attack takes Gulf flows back below 50%, this time with a much
thinner inventory cushion. GS argues stocks remain comfortably above operational minimums, but the
system has considerably less room to absorb another major disruption.
That's why $100 oil can coexist with a currently balanced market.

A different oil setup
Oil vol has been remarkably well behaved despite the surge in crude since July.
OVX is trading around the same levels as when oil was below $80, even with crude now near $100.
That's a very different setup from February/March, when rising oil was accompanied by a sharp
repricing of oil volatility.

The weirdest part
Despite $100 Brent and a huge geopolitical premium in flat price, oil vol hasn't followed.
Call skew has actually moved back toward pre-war levels, while the options bid has been concentrated
in puts. Meanwhile, macro funds that are vulnerable to another oil spike have increasingly been buying
outright delta rather than upside calls.
That leaves an unusual disconnect:
Flat price is pricing geopolitical fear. Upside vol isn't. One of them eventually has to move.
