Less Fed fear, same bond problem
Softer PCE has taken some of the Fed fear out of the market, with October hike odds falling from
around 45% to 37%.
The problem is that the long end isn't getting the memo. The 30-year remains around 5.64%, meaning
the dovish repricing at the front end has so far offered very little relief to long duration.
Less Fed fear isn't translating into easier financial conditions.
The long end isn't listening
The divergence across the curve is getting increasingly difficult to ignore.
The 2-year is around 4.89%, while the 30-year remains near 5.64%. As the long end has sold off faster,
the gap between the two has widened sharply.
The Fed may be becoming less threatening at the front end, but the market is still demanding
considerably more compensation further out.

Steepening is back
That divergence is showing up clearly in the curve.
2s30s has re-steepened from roughly 50bp to 75bp as long yields have risen faster than the front end.
We're still well below the August extreme near 113bp, but the direction of travel is clear.
Less Fed fear is steepening the curve, not rescuing the long end.

Bond vol isn't buying the calm
MOVE is back around 110 after the latest spike, while VIX remains relatively contained near 16. We've
seen this divergence repeatedly lately: equities continue to absorb the rates shock remarkably well,
even as uncertainty in bonds remains elevated.
The Fed fear may be fading. Bond uncertainty isn't.

The equity problem
This is where the setup potentially becomes much more important for equities.
Normally, weaker growth eventually brings lower yields and some valuation relief. But if growth starts
to weaken while the long end remains stubbornly high, equities could face softer earnings
expectations without getting the usual valuation cushion from falling rates.
That's the real test from here. Less Fed fear sounds bullish, but if it doesn't bring the long end down,
equities may get considerably less relief than investors expect.
The question isn't whether the Fed gets more dovish. It's whether the bond market finally cares.
