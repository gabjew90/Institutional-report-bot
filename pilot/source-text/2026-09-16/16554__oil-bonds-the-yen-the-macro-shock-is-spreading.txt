The energy shock is spreading
Energy is beginning to infect the rest of macro. China is rebuilding rather than releasing reserves, oil
volatility is spilling into bonds and demand for upside rates protection is approaching extremes.
Equities still treat it as noise. The risk is the sequence: another leg higher in energy ignites rates
volatility, negative convexity amplifies the move and broader asset beta finally breaks, with the yen
potentially adding a second deleveraging channel.
The world is short energy
The world is increasingly short crude, refining capacity, diesel and VLCCs. This broader energy
shortage is now driving the entire macro regime, turning the shock into an inflation and rates problem
rather than simply an oil story.
Bonds, equities, gold, credit and crypto are weakening, while the dollar, energy equities, commodity
carry, rates trend and long volatility remain the preferred hiding places, according to McElligott.

China stops cushioning the shock
China helped absorb the first energy shock by releasing crude from its emergency reserves. This time,
it is doing the opposite: rebuilding inventories and lifting offers into an already tight market.
Shanghai crude has surged well beyond Brent, suggesting China is no longer acting as the global
shock absorber. McElligott argues Beijing knows exactly where the pressure lands: higher US inflation,
weaker risk assets and greater stress on the Trump administration.
Nomura
Oil vol is infecting bonds
Oil volatility has spiked lately, although the move remains more contained than previous spikes this
year. Some of that stress now appears to be spilling over into bond volatility.

On the MOVE
Rates are already becoming the pressure point. MOVE has caught a strong bid and is separating
sharply from the VIX, suggesting the bond market is taking the energy and inflation risk more seriously
than equities.

All assets are short rate vol
Demand for upside rates protection is becoming increasingly extreme. High-strike payer demand and
range-accrual hedging are pushing swaption skew and “vol of vol” towards multi-year highs.
The danger is negative convexity. If another leg higher in energy forces rates volatility to break out,
dealer hedging in swaptions and VIX options could amplify the move across already fragile markets.
Nomura
It still needs to get worse
The tail risks are building, but they have not yet broken the market’s low-volatility malaise. Rates
moves remain largely contained within daily straddles, crude continues to chop around the headlines
and equities are still sitting close to their highs.
McElligott’s sequence is clear: energy must first break to a new extreme, then ignite rates volatility,
which finally drags down broader asset beta. Until that happens, the AI slowdown story looks more like
noise than the immediate macro catalyst.

Don't forget the big one
The yen has reversed part of its aggressive move over the past two sessions, but the initial surge
matters. A renewed JPY rally would add a global carry-unwind channel to the energy and rates-volatility
shock. Add that to the mix above, and suddenly things no longer feel “just noisy.”
