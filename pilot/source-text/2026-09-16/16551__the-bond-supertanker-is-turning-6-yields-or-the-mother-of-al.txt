Higher for longer?
The 10-year has spent the past three to four years consolidating “up here.” The 200-week MA is now
positively sloping, and a decisive break and close above 5% risks triggering a major vacuum move.
Take out 5%, and this could become the supertanker finally turning, from a multi-year consolidation
into a structural acceleration higher.

Oil is the engine, AI is the fuel
Brent is closing in on $110 after Houthi strikes shut the Saudi East-West pipeline, with Riyadh’s output
at a 30-year low of roughly 6m b/d and Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb presenting a two-front chokepoint
risk.
This is a supply shock, which means it doesn't fade on its own, and the WTI/10Y correlation has hit 0.96.
Every barrel higher is a basis point in the long end.
Then there's the quiet second claim on the same buyer pool: the AI build‑out. Hyperscaler capex now
runs >100% of operating cash flow, implying ~$1.3tn of debt by 2028, with tech leading the $380bn
2026 bond supply, competing directly with Treasuries while bidding up chips, power and labor. The
trade funding the equity bull is now financing the tightening cycle.

6% before it is over?
Once the Fed starts tightening, it rarely stops after one move. Historically, hiking cycles persist for
some time, with the 10-year yield generally rising by more than 100bp along the way.
Judging by the history surveyed by DB’s Jim Reid, investors should brace for the 10-year to reach 6%
before the Fed is done. More here.

SPX struggles
Using the past seven tightening cycles as a guide, GS notes that the S&P usually struggles at the start,
falling an average of 2% over the first three months. The pinch typically does not last long, however,
with the index gaining an average of 9% over the following 12 months. The bruising exception was
2022.
Ultimately, earnings growth determines the market’s trajectory, but the speed and volatility of the
move in rates can heavily influence stocks along the way.

Energy is stirring the drink
The world is increasingly short crude, refining capacity, diesel and VLCCs. This broader energy
shortage is now driving the entire macro regime, turning the shock into an inflation and rates problem
rather than simply an oil story.
Bonds, equities, gold, credit and crypto are weakening, while the dollar, energy equities, commodity
carry, rates trend and long volatility remain the preferred hiding places, according to McElligott.

Gold’s cheap Fed hedge
GS’s Tony Kim expects a choppy near-term path for gold. A Fed hike, higher energy prices and rising
yields remain headwinds, although much has already been priced and gold continues to hold its recent
range. Institutional and sovereign demand emerged around $4,000, while the longer-term central-
bank and fiscal-support story remains intact.
Into the Fed, GS favors cheap upside convexity. Call skew and risk reversals are not pricing much
upside tail risk, while GLD gamma could amplify the move. Wingy calls carry reasonably well and
would rally sharply in the unlikely event that the Fed does not hike. More on gold here.
