Markets pressured by 5% US yields, high oil, and tech 
concerns 
MARKET LINES 
Monday’s session delivered a triple blow to global markets. Driven by inflation fears as Brent crude 
surged past $109/bbl, the 10-year US Treasury yield crossed the 5% threshold for the first time since 
October 2023. In response, money markets aggressively repriced rate expectations. Investors are now 
pricing in nearly four rate hikes from both the Fed (including this week’s meeting) and the ECB by 
July 2027, alongside five hikes from the BoE over the next 12 months. This hawkish shift propelled the 
Dollar Index (DXY) back above 99 to 99.6, dragging the EUR/USD down to 1.1538. Emerging market 
currencies and equities also faced heavy selling pressure, with the MXN, PLN, HUF, and CLP among 
the hardest hit. Equities bore the brunt of the risk-off wave, further pressured by a sell-off in the AI 
sector. Calls from tech leaders to slow down AI development weighed heavily on semiconductor 
stocks, dragging the Nasdaq 100 down 1.2%. Gold also plunged over 2%, slipping below $4,300/oz. All 
eyes are now on Wednesday’s FOMC decision, which is set to dictate the direction of risk assets for 
the remainder of the year. 
Rates 
► The higher-for-longer rates theme has been reaffirmed once again. Fresh multi-year highs were 
reached in EUR rates, with the 10Y Bund closing above 3.5% and the 10Y OAT briefly touching 4.5% 
before closing at 4.47%, marking its highest level since 2008. EUR curves flattened sharply during 
the session (by -5 to -8 bps), with the EUR 2s-10s swap falling to 5 bps (lowest since late 2024) and 
the EUR 10s-30s dropping to -13 bps (lowest since early 2025). 
► Despite the renewed rise in Brent crude, expectations for ECB rate hikes by year-end remained 
stable around 40 bps, but were revised upward for H2-2027, with nearly 90 bps of additional hikes 
priced in compared to the current €STR level. 
► Underperformance of the Italian curve, as the 10Y BTP-Bund spread widened by 2 bps to 87 bps, 
in line with the widening in credit spreads. 
FX 
► 
DXY rebounds to 99.6, driven by surging Brent crude ahead of the Fed meeting. 
► 
EUR/USD slips to 1.1538 despite firmer ECB rate hike expectations. 
► 
GBP showed resilience on Monday, with EUR/GBP down to 0.856, supported by strong pricing of 
BoE rate hikes (with 5 hikes now priced in). 
► 
Fueled by dollar strength, USD/JPY touched 155 before ending Monday’s session at 154.4. 
Equities 
► 
The equity market sell-off that began yesterday in Asia (Kospi down 3.3% on Monday) continued 
in Europe and the United States. Reports of a possible slowdown in AI innovation, the ongoing 
bear flattening of the yield curve, and rising oil prices weighed on the semiconductor sector 
yesterday. The SOX was down more than 5% at the close of European trading.

Banca Monte dei Paschi (NR/Baa3/BBB-) – Credit Neutral: Banca Monte dei Paschi (MONTE) 
is pressing ahead with its bids for Banco BPM (BAMIIM) and Banca Generali despite the 
takeover attempt from Intesa (ISPIM). MONTE has confirmed that its separate, "irrevocable" bids 
will proceed, remaining entirely unconditional on the outcome of ISPIM’s rival offer. These 
acquisitions offer premiums of 3.4% for BAMIIM and 12.8% for Banca Generali (as of 11 September), 
aiming to deliver annual pre-tax run-rate synergies of €1.4bn and €400mn respectively. MONTE’s 
CEO must now secure shareholder approval at an EGM on 29 October to counter ISPIM's plans. 
As noted in our Credit News, Banca Monte dei Paschi: The best defence is a good offence, we 
view these concurrent offers by MONTE not only as a defensive move to counter ISPIM’s 
unsolicited offer, but also as a compelling strategic alternative. 
Real Estate  
► 
Realty Income (A-/A3/A) and KKR have formed a JV to own a diversified European net lease 
real estate portfolio. KKR will invest €528mn for a 49% equity stake, while Realty Income retains 
51% and will manage the 54 properties across Spain, Ireland, Poland, and the Netherlands. The 
transaction, valued at a 5.9% initial cap rate, extends Realty Income's private capital strategy into 
Europe. 
► 
Offices in Peri-Defense – according to CoStar, in July the LVMH group signed a lease of 11,000 
sqm in the Sense building in Puteaux, owned by AGC Equity Partners. Good news supporting 
the recovery of the Peri-Defense sector, where take-up in H1-26 has returned to its average since 
H1-19, while the Paris Region market is recovering by 14%. This move, meant to achieve savings, 
concerns the editorial offices of Les Échos and Le Parisien, their support functions and the group’s 
advertising department. They will occupy the premises vacated by Ceva Logistics in this building 
already housing Hewlett Packard (BBB/Ba2/BBB+). 
Infrastructures  
► 
SoftBank Group Corp. (BB+/Ba2/NR) has secured an $11.87bn, two-year loan from around 20 
banks to fund its investment in OpenAI, surpassing its initial $10bn target. This latest borrowing 
adds to SBG’s debt-financing activities linked to the AI firm, as SoftBank plans to repay $25.9bn 
of an earlier $40bn OpenAI-related loan ahead of schedule on September 15.

► 
MBDA’s new Polish facility signals a shift from traditional exports to shared sovereignty. Facing 
Russian threats, localizing production and transferring key technologies secures the supply chain 
closer to the front. These massive state contracts guarantee predictable, long-term cash flows, 
but introduce higher risks of technological dilution and increased physical exposure within a 
high-tension zone.
