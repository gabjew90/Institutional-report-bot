 
Micron Earnings Preview: Another Beat And Guide 
Above Street... But SCAs May Cap Upside Surprise 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, SEP 30, 2026 - 08:50 PM 
Three months ago, heading into Micron's May-quarter print, sentiment was "11/10" and the stock 
had already soared over 260% YTD. It didn't matter: on June 24, Micron blew away Q2 
expectations anyway, printing $41.5BN in revenue vs $35.7BN expected, an 84.9% gross 
margin vs 81.9%, and guiding the August quarter to $49-51BN, some $7BN above where the 
Street was. By the next morning, SK Hynix was up 11% and Kioxia as much as 15% in 
sympathy. 
Fast forward to today, and the memory giant reports again after the close, with the setup 
noticeably less euphoric. So what does Goldman expect? Here is a summary of what the bank's 
analysts and trading desk are saying. 
The numbers: it's all about the November guide 
As Goldman's Jay Narain puts it, "the Q4 print itself is largely well understood; the market is 
focused on the November quarter outlook and forward-looking commentary." For that November 
quarter, GS semis analyst Jim Schneider is above the Street across the board: 

 
 
Here is the bigger picture: median sellside estimates (which are traditionally far more 
conservative than buyside bogeys) are as follows: 
●​ Revenue $51.49 billion 
●​ Gross Margin 86.2% 
●​ EPS $31.83

For context, here is how fast this thing has been growing. If Goldman is right, Micron will have 
gone from $41.5BN in quarterly revenue in May to nearly $58BN by November, a 40% jump in 
two quarters for a company that, not that long ago, was the textbook example of a boom-bust 
commodity chipmaker (and will be again, only question is when do we have a pulled corporate 
bond sale that is funding all this DRAM inflation that is pushing core CPE by 0.5% for 2026 - 
with 10Y at 5.30% won't be too long). 

 
 
As for tonight's August-quarter print itself, GS' TMT specialist sales, Sean Johnstone, noted that 
"after June's large beat, expectations are high," with consensus at roughly $50-51BN of revenue 
and $31-31.50 in EPS. Goldman research expects "persistent memory supply-demand tightness 
to drive another strong quarter, upside to consensus and robust guidance," and is 
mid-single-digit percentages above consensus EPS for Q3-Q4 2027 on better mix and pricing. 
Goldman's London sales desk summarized GIR's view as "another strong beat, with ~3% 
revenue upside to Street as persistent DRAM/NAND tightness continues to support pricing." 
Positioning: "relatively balanced"... and the SCA catch 
This is where it gets interesting. According to Goldman's US-based TMT specialist, Peter 
Callahan, positioning into the print "feels relatively balanced," though he notes that Micron is just 
10% below its all-time high while Samsung and Hynix are still 20-40% below theirs. Investors 
broadly expect a beat and guide above, but here is the catch: 
investors broadly expecting the mix towards SCAs to somewhat limit the beat size (.. at least 
relative to MU beating EPS by 10-30% over the last ~6 quarters) 
In other words, the more of Micron's output gets locked into Strategic Customer Agreements 
(long-term contracts with floor pricing), the less spot-price upside flows through, which is great 
for earnings durability and slightly less great for the kind of monster beats traders have gotten 
addicted to. 

 
 
Callahan's five talking points into the print: 
1.​ The November-quarter revenue guide relative to the Street (up ~10% q/q), i.e. the 
magnitude of y/y and q/q deceleration 
2.​ Any additional updates on SCAs 
3.​ Mix of capital allocation into FY27: capex vs. capital returns 
4.​ HBM market share (investors want Micron to hold or grow its ~20% share) 
5.​ Industry supply/demand, especially regarding China 
The buyback wildcard 
The item that could steal the show is capital returns. As Narain points out, any commentary on 
share repurchase plans "post CHIPS restrictions" will be top of mind given Micron's net cash 
position of ~$24.4BN and $18.3BN in adjusted FCF last quarter. JPMorgan's Harlan Sur goes 
further, noting that the December 9 CHIPS Act anniversary is "a capital return inflection point," 
after which Micron has committed to returning 100% of excess cash (buybacks primary), and 
that cumulative FCF could approach $200BN through year-end 2027. With Nvidia just adding 
$150BN to its own buyback, the bar for "eye-popping" has admittedly been raised. 
Sur's other key call is the SCA disclosure in the 10-K: his base case is that bit production 
coverage has stepped up to 35%+ (from ~20% for DRAM and ~33% for NAND last quarter), 
possibly already 50%+, with floor pricing that locks in gross margins "well above" Micron's prior 

 
~62% peak. Goldman's desk also flagged "progress on the 16+ long-term Strategic Customer 
Agreements, including deposits and visibility on the previously referenced $100bn+ of minimum 
commitments" as a key watchpoint. 
Why Asia cares more than anyone 
"All eyes in Asia will be on Micron earnings as memory stocks in the region are still materially 
below their peaks," writes Narain, who has been canvassing regional investors. His read: 
memory stocks "remain fundamentally compelling but have suffered from relative OW 
positioning in a period of intense de-grossing." The key debates are now: 
●​ Short term: data center construction (per GS, 60% of US data centers planned for 
completion in 2027 haven't started construction yet, mostly due to power constraints) 
●​ Medium term: earnings expectations for 2028 
●​ Long term: whether new LTAs have structurally changed the nature of the memory cycle 
●​ Across all horizons: the sustainability of AI funding and supply threats from China's 
CXMT (a name longtime readers will recognize) 
There is also a flow angle. Goldman's Seoul desk (Heather Oh) notes that Samsung's W15TN 
buyback is 87% complete and SK Hynix's W40TN program is ~75% done, with both set to finish 
early in October. Those buybacks have been doing most of the heavy lifting: in September, 
foreigners and retail were joint net sellers of both names, which "has sparked concerns about a 
potential liquidity void once the buyback program ends." Translation: Korea could use a strong 
Micron print more than Micron does. 
Meanwhile, GS' Graham Ambrose says US investors keep asking him why Asian investors 
aren't as bullish on the tech cycle, pointing to the underperformance of Hynix (local) and Kioxia 
vs. the Hynix ADR and Micron since July. Kioxia, he notes, trades at 3.3x March-2028 P/E with 
a 101% ROE and 116% upside to GS' ¥38,667 target, which is either a screaming bargain or a 
very loud warning about where investors think we are in the cycle. 
Bottom line 
Goldman expects a beat and a November guide above consensus ($57.7BN vs $56.7BN), with 
the real swing factors being (1) how much SCAs dampen the upside surprise, (2) the size and 
timing of buybacks once the CHIPS handcuffs come off in December, and (3) HBM4 share. With 
Micron only ~10% off its highs and the Street already expecting a beat, the stock needs a lot 

 
more than "in line" to repeat June's fireworks... and Seoul and Tokyo will be watching even more 
closely than New York.
