GS TMT SPEC SALES – Thursday 1st October – where has this year gone?
Noticeable Software > Semis tilt yesterday, with most pointing to earnings the JBL -10% on a beat/raise weighing
on sentiment into MU the print …. The print however was good end enough and the better visibility looks pretty
supportive for memory and semicap (see the +ve moves in Asians names). Mega-caps were mostly higher, with
Meta the exception; Google gave back some gains on reports of internal doubts over Gemini 4, although the post-
close “Argon” launch looked solid. Gemini 4 “Argon,” a larger flagship AI model designed to strengthen its
position against Anthropic and OpenAI. Early reaction from AI watchers is mixed: Google claims strong coding
performance, but some reported benchmarks and employee feedback appear less convincing, and limited hands-on
access means its real-world quality is still unclear. The key test will be how Argon performs once developers begin
using it in practice.
Oracle/chip demand the FT/Reuters report Tencent’s five-year ~$7bn Oracle Asia lease for ~100k advanced AI
chips (press, not company-confirmed). Supports Oracle remaining capacity and backlog narrative (OpenAI +
ByteDance + now Tencent) – will be interesting to see how Oracle CDS reacts today. For Tencent it gets frontier
compute without waiting on domestic silicon(Positive Prosus).
Accenture later today: Investors will focus on bookings and book-to-bill, signs that AI work is converting into
durable revenue, any stabilisation in US federal demand, and initial FY2027 guidance. Consensus expects Q4
revenue of around $18bn and EPS of $3.18–3.19, while GIR forecasts FY2027 constant-currency growth of 3–4%,
including 50–150bp of organic growth, roughly 100bp from lapping federal headwinds and 150–200bp from M&A
(GIR take). The broader IT-services backdrop remains subdued as clients redirect spending and use AI to lower
costs: GIR’s Indian IT Services preview forecasts organic growth of 1.8% in FY2027 and 3.2% in FY2028, with
flat margins, broad estimate cuts and LTM downgraded to Sell.
MICRON: delivered a strong quarter and guidance, with Q4 revenue of $54.23bn and adjusted EPS of $33.42
ahead of expectations, while Q1 guidance of $61.5bn revenue and about $38.15 EPS was also well above
consensus. BETTER VISIBILITY: AI and HBM demand continues to outstrip supply, most 2027 HBM capacity is
already contracted at higher prices, long-term customer commitments have increased and management expects
DRAM and NAND markets to remain tight through 2027–28 despite rising capex. The shares were broadly flat
after hours because expectations and valuation were already high, but the broader read-through is positive for
memory suppliers SK Hynix and Samsung, as well as wafer-fab equipment and test names such as LCRX, AMAT,
ASML, Tokyo Electron and Advantest. Asian trading this morning reflected this, with Japanese equipment and
memory stocks leading gains while Korean names were more subdued, suggesting the cycle still favours memory
and selected equipment suppliers, with the main risks now higher capex, valuation and eventual scrutiny of AI

customer returns rather than near-term demand…See Jim’s take he raising his revenue estimates for the next 3
years by >14% per year and EPS > 12% per year. On my IB this this morning this lead to some debate on whether
one should . favor memory and select WFE over pure foundry/logic for the next few quarters of the cycle….feels
like it. Separately this is not great for names make use a lot of memory and ths morning get Ericsson pushed a
negative given the rising DRAM prices and Ericsson EMS partner Jabil describing a subdued 5G market it expects
to remain muted near-term, no signs of a volume-side offset to the margin pressure.
While on Memory the GS Memory pricing tracker points to continued strength in 4Q26, with TrendForce
expecting PC, server and mobile DRAM prices to rise 13–18%, 10–15% and 0–5% quarter on quarter,
respectively, and NAND prices to increase 15–20%. The GS DRAM Sentiment Indicator remained moderately
positive in September, supported by rising DDR5 and DDR4 pricing, record 130% year-on-year server ODM
revenue growth and improving expectations for both conventional memory and HBM pricing.
IFX - AI data-center buildout is now hitting power semis as hard as memory. Infineon Taiwan MD Chen (陳志豪)
this morning flagged structural tightness driven by rack power density scaling toward hundreds of kW / 1 MW,
800 V architectures and higher SiC/GaN content. He sees supply only normalizing in 2–3 years once incremental
capacity ramps.
Taiwanese names are already following the IDM lead (Infineon, onsemi): non-contract product price hikes of 10–
15%, concentrated in MOSFETs.

AI updates:
| • | US government launches AI -The Trump administration has launched America.gov as an AI-powered front
door to the federal government: it currently answers questions using official sources and models including Gemini
and Grok, but is expected to support agentic tasks such as passport renewals and Medicare enrolment from early
2027. Led by Airbnb co-founder and US chief design officer Joe Gebbia, the platform aims to replace fragmented
government websites and paperwork with a simpler digital experience. If it works, it could make AI’s benefits
tangible to millions of citizens, reduce bureaucracy, improve trust in public services and create a visible, hard-to-
reverse model for reform—effectively treating government more like a technology product, as argued in this
commentary on X. | | • | Interesting article on AI Assistants vs Marketplaces, and where the profit pools go -The
central debate is whether AI agents become the new gateway to commerce, shifting customer ownership and high-
margin discovery economics away from marketplaces while leaving incumbents to provide fulfilment. The
outcome will depend on whether agents generate genuinely new demand or simply reroute existing orders, how
much platforms rely on advertising and commissions, and which side has greater bargaining power: Amazon and
Airbnb can defend differentiated inventory and direct relationships, while Shopify, Toast and Square may benefit
from agents as an additional sales channel. Physical networks remain valuable but also need volume, so
incumbents may accept lower profit per transaction if agents expand the overall market; if incremental demand is
insufficient, however, a meaningful share of today’s advertising and commission pools could migrate to the agents

that control discovery. | | • | DeepSeek is partnering with Huawei to open-source programming and
communications tools optimised for Huawei’s Ascend chips, strengthening China’s domestic AI ecosystem and
reducing reliance on Nvidia. | | • | ElevenLabs has completed a $300m employee tender offer at a $22bn valuation,
double its February level, led by Wellington and T. Rowe Price with backing from existing and new investors,
reflecting strong demand for AI voice agents. |

TECH
| • | Samsung: GIR has cut its 3Q26 operating-profit estimate by 5% to W106tn, mainly because the Korean won
was stronger than expected, but still expects a solid quarter with profit above W100tn, supported by robust DRAM
and NAND fundamentals and continued HBM growth. The revised 3Q26 estimate is broadly in line with
consensus, while 2027–28 forecasts remain 8–17% above the Street because stronger DRAM pricing, particularly
for HBM, should more than offset lower conventional DRAM shipments. With memory demand expected to
outpace supply through 2028 and Samsung prioritising HBM—helping tighten conventional DRAM supply—GIR
expects next year’s HBM revenue to exceed W100tn on higher pricing and volumes. | | • | Amadeus – Ben flagging
the IATA data on air passenger demand in August 2026 was released yesterday (September 30), with global
Revenue Passenger Kilometers (RPKs) decreasing by -0.8% yoy (vs. July: +0.3% yoy restated). Within this,
domestic RPKs decreased by -0.5% yoy (vs. July: +0.6% yoy), and international RPKs decreased by -0.9% yoy
(vs. July: -0.1% yoy). Global Available Seat Kilometers (ASKs) rose by +0.3% yoy, while load factors fell by
-0.9pp yoy to 85.1%. On forwards trends, global seat capacity is set to grow by +1.6% yoy in September and
+2.0% yoy in October. YTD RPK growth of +0.4% (through to August) is tracking below IATA's latest outlook for
FY26 as of Jun'26 (+2.1% RPK growth), primarily driven by softness in the Middle East and APAC. | | • |
Synopsys' Investor Day: GIR came away from the event favorably impressed by the company's financial targets
and growth vectors tied to AI, and expect the stock to hold its gains (up ~5%), driven by three key themes: (1)
Long-term financial targets: ~15% revenue CAGR from FY26-30, with 50% operating margin in FY30, and mid-
20% EPS growth to $35.00 in FY30) that are above Street expectations; (2) Greater visibility into AI monetization
and accelerating AI-driven revenue opportunities, including an OpenAI partnership and new agentic AI tools; (3)
Clearer roadmap to accelerate the Design IP business through higher-value, custom platforms for hyperscalers and
other customers | | • | Infineon: Infineon is investing 48bn baht to expand semiconductor assembly and packaging
in Thailand and could eventually manufacture chips in the country. Management sees Thailand’s strong logistics
network, established semiconductor supply chain and neutral geopolitical position as key advantages, while the
government is offering tailored incentives and targeting at least 500bn baht of semiconductor investment in the
first five years and 2.5tn baht by 2050. |

| • | DT: the Handelsblatt again doing a hatchet job on DT (they have in the past been bearish DT)- It cites
previously confidential US court documents reveal that Deutsche Telekom and SoftBank allegedly shortchanged
minority shareholders during the 2020 T-Mobile and Sprint merger to secure corporate control. Internal text
messages show executives orchestrating pressure against the head of T-Mobile's independent directors' committee
while facing potential damages of up to $10.1 billion. Handelsblatt. No comments from DT. | | • | Slower China kit
removal = Capex relief: EU member states are pushing to replace the proposed mandatory 36-month deadline for
removing high-risk vendors such as Huawei and ZTE with a more flexible, case-by-case timetable based on

security risk, equipment lifecycles and the availability of alternatives. The move follows warnings that a full
replacement across mobile, fixed and transport networks could cost up to €40bn and limit investment, particularly
as Deutsche Telekom and Vodafone still rely heavily on Huawei in several European markets. (Telecoms) | | • | TI:
Poste Italiane has formally begun managing and coordinating Telecom Italia after securing an approximately 86%
controlling stake. New guidelines will give Poste visibility into TIM’s strategic decisions and establish
information-sharing procedures, while preserving TIM’s legal independence and protections for minority
shareholders; CEO Matteo Del Fante and General Manager Giuseppe Lasco will oversee the integration and
pursue industrial synergies. (La Milano) | | • | TalkTalk: TalkTalk has given up its controlling 51% stake in
Horsham-based Telecom Acquisitions Group, allowing the company to operate independently. Companies House
records show that TalkTalk Group and TAL CEO Nigel Barnett ceased to have significant control on 8 September
2026; the separation had been planned for more than a year and is unrelated to TalkTalk’s urgent sale discussions,
while normal wholesale and trading arrangements will continue. (ISPReview) | | • | Sky: Sky has extended its
satellite television agreement with SES in the UK and Ireland into the next decade, retaining access to high-power
capacity at the 28.2° East orbital position to deliver linear channels to millions of homes. The renewal extends a
relationship dating back to 1988 and shows that satellite remains an important and resilient part of Sky’s
distribution network alongside its growing streaming services. (Telecom Paper) |
