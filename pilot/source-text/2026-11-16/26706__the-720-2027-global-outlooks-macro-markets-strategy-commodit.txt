In Focus | 2027 Outlooks 
Global Macro Outlook 2027: Steady Baseline, Downside Risks. We expect global GDP 
growth of 2.8% in 2027, as lower energy prices and strong AI capex outweigh tighter 
financial conditions. We see risks skewed to the downside from a renewed energy 
shock, bond market turbulence, and a weaker AI impulse. We expect core inflation to 
slow in 2027 as US tariff pass-through ends, forecasting US core PCE to fall to 2.4% 
from 3.2%. Following two more Fed hikes and one ECB hike, we expect both central 
banks to return to cuts in late 2027. With our policy rate forecasts generally below 
market pricing, we expect longer-term rates to come down modestly. Amid positive 
return forecasts for equities and bonds in 2027, we recommend an overweight 
allocation in the US and Japan and an underweight in Europe. As downside growth 
risks rise, we think bonds are regaining their hedging value while credit spreads will 
likely widen. Jan Hatzius 
Global Markets Outlook 2027 – Growing Pains. We expect a friendly 2027 macro 
baseline with stable growth and declining inflation, but an aging market cycle faces 
downside risks from higher real rates and wider credit spreads. We forecast the AI 
investment impulse as a share of GDP to peak in early 2027. We expect S&P 500 
earnings growth to decelerate to 11% in 2027 from 36% in 2026. Given a more 
two-sided risk picture, we recommend diversified allocations and see renewed 
hedging value in bonds. We forecast US 10-year Treasury yields to reach 4.6% by the 
end of 2027. We project a decline in the broad trade-weighted Dollar in 2027, 
especially versus Asia’s revaluing currencies including the Japanese Yen and Chinese 
Yuan. We highlight emerging market assets for their exposure to high, diversified 
yields and a regionally diversified earnings mix. Kamakshya Trivedi, Dominic Wilson

growth to moderate slightly to 4.5% in 2027 from 4.6% in 2026, slightly above

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
