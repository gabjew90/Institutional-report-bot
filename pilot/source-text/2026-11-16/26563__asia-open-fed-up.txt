Asia Open - Fed up
By Preston Pagli
Hawkish Fedspeak and President Trump's dismissal of an Iran deal sent oil surging and cemented the sharp repricing in
the Fed's policy path seen this week with OIS markets now pricing a 73% probability of a January hike, up from just 25%
before Tuesday's CPI print. The front-end led the selloff in USTs, with 2s surging ~14bps to 4.881% and 10s breaking
above 5.10%, the highest level in nearly a month. In FX, the dollar strengthened broadly, pushing USDJPY briefly
through the highly monitored 160 level before a sharp reversal, though no signs of intervention. Equities were relatively
resilient at the index level with SPX -35bps and NDX -30bps, despite weaker breadth with over 350 SPX constituents
lower as rotation into Nvidia (-4.5%) alternatives (AMD +6%, Broadcom +6%, Arista +5%) offset the drag from semis and
neo's. Brent crude gained 4% to $96, above both its 100dma and 200dma, while gold slumped nearly 3% to a fresh 52-
week low. Looking ahead, lots of central bank commentary to come with NY Fed President Williams at 23:30 GMT, ECB
speakers include Simkus and Schnabel in Vilnius at 07:00 GMT, Sleijpen at 06:15 GMT and Zigman in Budapest at 08:30
GMT.

In Focus‌
FX: The dollar strengthened across all major pairs on continued hawkish rhetoric from Fed officials and renewed geopolitical
concerns surrounding Iran with President Trump stating "we don't need a deal with Iran", just one day after the NYT
reported Vice President Vance was open to direct talks with Iranian hardliner Rezaei. EURUSD hit a fresh YTD low at
1.1046 while CHF was a clear haven, leading gains among peers. Our G10 FX strategy desk re-entered a short EURNOK
trade by buying a 2m EURNOK 10.35 put, with the belief the US midterm election results won't materially reduce the risk of
escalation in the Middle East. On the eFX trading desk during the NY session, activity was raised in EUR pairs, as well as
USDZAR, and USDTHB. Net inflows leaned toward AUD, NOK, and PLN, while the leveraged segment were buyers of MXN.
Outflows led in SEK while the leveraged segment were sellers of ZAR. 
USDJPY briefly broke above 160 before reversing sharply, falling from an intraday high of 160.05 to 159.33. Flow data from
our eFX trading in the time period of 11:40 and 12:05 EST showed volumes of roughly $3.1bn. Near-term support is seen
at the 100dma (158.99) followed by the 200dma (158.72). Our Japan Economics team expects the BoJ to hike in December
in their base case scenario. Looking ahead, 160 remains the key line in the sand. Our G10 FX Strategy team has

highlighted intervention risks around this level, arguing policymakers may seek to push USDJPY back toward 155 if yen
weakness becomes disorderly. Our G10 spot trader Pat Hu remains short AUDUSD and AUDJPY.
Rates: USTs bear flattened with front-end 2y yields surging ~14bps to 4.881 while the benchmark 10y trades +13bps
higher to 5.125%, the highest level since nearly mid-October. Hawkish Fedspeak continued with a slate of officials striking a
similar tone ahead of Friday's PPI where our economists are expecting a below consensus headline print of +0.3%. OIS
traders further increased their bets of a Jan FOMC hike with a 73% chance vs. just 25% before Tuesday's CPI print. US
initial jobless claims remained low at 200k despite topping estimates of 194k, with our economists noting the continuing
claims (1697k) imply the chance of a further decline in the u/e rate to around 3.9%-4.0% in early 2027. 
US Treasury Auction Recap: $25 billion of 30-year bonds. The auction tailed the 1pm market by 0.4bp to yield 5.441%. The
bid/cover ratio came in at 2.36 which is at the 18th percentile of the past 12 auctions.
Equities: Headline indices traded relatively resiliently despite the resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, as potential
winners such as AMD (+6%), Arista (+5%), and Broadcom (+6%) benefited from the rotation. SPX traded -35bps and NDX
-30bps while the RTY dropped 1.7% on broader rate sensitivity in the face of rising yields. Beneath the surface, breadth was
weak with >350 SPX constituents lower. Index vols remained firm with Jan ATM: SPX +0.2v, QQQ +0.1v, IWM -0.35v. On the
derivatives desk, vols were bid while customer activity skewed toward selling of index options with unwinding of downside
hedges in SPX vs selling of upside in QQQ, mostly Nov-Dec expiries. Semis vols were also bid, though more in singles than
SMH, given the dispersion in price performance.
Commodities: Brent crude gained 3.5% as President Trump dismissed the prospects of a near-term deal with Iran, saying
"Those days have long since passed." Brent trades $96.13, moving past its 100dma and 200dma at 94.99 and 94.22
respectively. Citi Global Head of Rates Deirdre Dunn was joined by Stephen Hadley, Former US National Security Advisor to
discuss the conflict in Iran, among other pressing topics heading into 2027. See the replay here.
Gold slumped nearly 3%, hitting a new 52-week low amid broad de-risking with my colleague Rui Ding flagging the 100WMA
which has held since 2022 (aside from Oct 2023 when we had a false technical break before the rally resumed) but is in
danger of being broken. Our sales colleagues think physical demand could re-emerge on a deeper correction, particularly as
we get meaningfully below the $3875-3900/oz area, alongside a gradual return of central bank buying should funding costs
and sovereign stress indicators stabilize.
Elsewhere‌
BRL reached its weakest level since June 2025 with USDBRL (+1.3%) to 5.64. The government's new fiscal package is said
to save 0.5% of GDP though our trader notes the market does not seem to have confidence in its implementation. Local CPI
(IPCA) came in at +0.14% MoM, below estimates of (+0.25%). Flows on the desk have been mainly from the RHS via
offshore accounts (RM and CTAs). Our trader Gabriel Groh notes the current set up lacks RR and prefers to remain flat. 
Ahead:
PEN: Reference Rate Decision at 23:00 GMT. Peru’s central bank has kept rates on hold since September 2025, even in the
face of above target inflation. Our trader notes analysts are expecting a hike, while the market is more divided between a hike
and a pause, so the decision should have some effect for the market tomorrow.
EUR: ECB's Simkus and Schnabel speak in Vilnius at 07:00 GMT, ECB's Sleijpen Speaks in Antalya at 06:15 GMT, followed
by ECB's Zigman in Budapest at 08:30 GMT, then Eurozone GDP SA YoY for Q3 (second estimate), Trade Balance SA for
September at 10:00 GMT.
USD: PPI at 13:30 GMT for October, followed by Fed's Daly in a moderated discussion at 17:00 GMT. Consensus expects
final demand to ease towards 0.4% MoM vs 1.3% prior, and ex food, energy at 0.3% MoM. Despite deceleration in both
PPI/CPI, this will unlikely move the needle on expectations of a Fed hike next month, despite it being the last inflation print
into the meeting.
RON: GDP QoQ at 07:00 GMT for Q3 (advance). Our economists forecast growth to rise 0.5% YoY.
PLN: GDP SA QoQ at 08:30 GMT for Q3 (preliminary). Our economists forecast growth in Q3 moderated to 3.5% YoY (down
from 4.1% in Q2), though risks remain skewed to the upside. This puts the Q3 growth slightly below the full-year forecast of

3.6%, supported by strong H1 2026.
