From panic to the next squeeze?
NVDA has suffered a Liberation Day-style collapse, while the momentum trade has been crushed. But
the positioning pendulum may have swung too far. QQQ shorts are surging, hedge funds are
underexposed, and JPM sees the potential for another wave of buying. Meanwhile, India is winning the
AI rotation and the dollar keeps ripping. The selling was brutal. The next squeeze could be just as
violent.
Liberation Day déjà vu
At today's lows, NVDA was down nearly 40% from its early October ATHs, approaching the drawdown
seen during the Liberation Day collapse earlier this year. That move exceeded 40%, but unfolded at a
much slower pace. Meanwhile, NVDA traded nearly 30% below its 200-day MA at today's lows,
matching the extreme undershoot seen during the Liberation Day panic. The setup remains fluid, but
however you slice it, this has been a brutal crash. More here.

NVDA: The Apple 2011 playbook
Goldman's Jim Schneider draws an interesting parallel between Nvidia today and Apple during Steve
Jobs' departure in 2011. Apple saw its forward P/E compress from 17x to 12x, eventually bottoming
near 9x in 2013, before recovering as investors gained confidence in management's ability to execute
without Jobs. Schneider sees a similar period of multiple uncertainty for Nvidia until leadership and
business momentum become clearer. However, with Goldman forecasting roughly 80% EPS growth, he
sees attractive risk/reward at current levels. The key question is whether Nvidia can prove, as Apple
eventually did, that the business is bigger than its iconic leader.
Cheaper than the market
The relative derating is even more extraordinary. Ahead of the latest selloff, NVDA had fallen to its
cheapest valuation versus the S&P 500 in almost a quarter of a century, long before AI was even on
investors' radar. The ultimate AI growth stock is now trading at a historic discount to the broader
market.

Nvidia is losing China
China's domestic chip substitution is accelerating. Nvidia's share of Chinese data-center AI accelerator
shipments fell to 34% in H1 2026, with Huawei closing the gap at 28%. TS Lombard argues that US
export restrictions are accelerating the development of China's domestic AI ecosystem. The
unintended consequence of keeping China out of Nvidia's technology may be creating Nvidia's next
major competitor.

The shorts are back
The positioning backdrop has changed dramatically. JPM highlights QQQ short interest reaching its
highest level since 2023, while Nasdaq futures positioning has been heavily reduced. Hedge funds
entered the November selloff having already cut significant exposure. That leaves a potentially
powerful source of incremental demand if the market stabilizes. Some of the elevated QQQ short
interest may reflect hedges against existing longs rather than outright bearish bets. Full note here.
JPM
Momentum capitulation
The momentum crash has been historic. JPM estimates that the momentum long/short factor has
declined approximately 40% from its peak, its largest unwind since the post-GFC recovery. October
alone delivered an 11% decline in the long momentum-versus-market portfolio, a roughly six-sigma
event. Crowding has fallen sharply, with shorter-term measures approaching historical extremes. The
momentum trade has gone from crowded to crushed. The question now is whether the unwind has
finally exhausted itself.
JPM
The next wave of buyers?

This is where the positioning story becomes particularly interesting. JPM argues that hedge funds have
already completed substantial de-risking, leaving their exposure significantly below earlier levels.
Continued retail support could stabilize AI stocks through November, potentially allowing hedge funds
to rebuild positions from late November onward. That would introduce a second wave of demand into
a market where momentum and leverage have already been reset. Retail may be providing the
bridge. Institutional re-risking could provide the next leg higher.
JPM
India: The anti-AI trade
India has emerged as one of the biggest beneficiaries of the global AI unwind. TS Lombard highlights
that foreign investors have poured roughly $6.5 billion into Indian equities so far in November,
following heavy selling throughout most of 2026. India is now the best-performing emerging market
this month, with the Nifty recovering to its pre-war February levels. Having missed much of the AI
boom, India is suddenly benefiting from the rotation away from the very stocks that dominated global
markets. The AI unwind is becoming India's positioning tailwind.
