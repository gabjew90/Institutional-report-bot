*** This week I joined my colleagues Henry and Shreyas on their latest Rate Check podcast, which came out 
yesterday. It's the first filmed edition since the podcast began earlier this year, and we discuss everything 
from AI to the bond vigilantes. You can find it here on the DB Research Institute or it's also on Apple and 
Spotify. Search for "Rate Check" *** 
My favourite guilty pleasure returned last night, namely the TV show "Rivals". If you think real life has in recent 
times bordered on crazy, then feel free to watch this as you'll end up feeling a bit better about the sanity of 
the current world and get some great 1980s music and some garish clothes thrown in for free. The crazy 
whipsawing post midterms continued yesterday as after the post-CPI selloff on Tuesday, markets bounced 
back again over the last 24 hours, as the positive momentum from CoreWeave’s (+24.71%) earnings drove 
a sharp rally in tech stocks. So the Philly semiconductor index (+5.66%) helped drive the S&P 500 
(+1.31%) back above its levels before the midterm elections. Who said markets didn't always go up after 
midterms! And over in Europe, both the STOXX 600 (+0.72%) and the DAX (+1.20%) moved up to fresh 
records. However, the current volatility in the tech trade continues this morning as the WSJ reported after the 
US closing bell that the Nvidia board are planning to oust CEO Jensen Huang after the last few days of stories 
around him have surfaced. Their stock fell -8.6% in after-hours trading, which has pushed Nasdaq (-
1.09%) and S&P (-0.45%) lower. So a big deal for one of the largest companies in the world. 
This is also impacting Asian equity markets with the Kospi (-1.04%), Hang Seng (-0.86%) and Nikkei (-
0.78%) all notably lower as I type this morning. The Shanghai Comp is -0.22%. European futures have 
maintained their gains overnight though with DAX futures +0.66% as I type, which hints at some rotation after 
the latest Nvidia story.  
Before the overnight news the AI trade was firmly back in action yesterday after CoreWeave’s earnings on 
Tuesday night. Its share price (+24.71%) saw its biggest daily jump since June 2025, a few months after 

its IPO, although it remains around -45% below August levels. CoreWeave has often been viewed as a 
bellwether of the AI trade, given the company’s significantly heavy debt burden and capital-intensive model 
of building data centres and leasing them out to major AI customers and hyperscalers.  
Those upbeat earnings from CoreWeave were a boon for tech and chip stocks, with the Philly 
Semiconductor Stock Exchange up +5.66%, while the Nasdaq Composite (+1.6%) also climbed. So with 
tech stocks rallying, that lifted the S&P 500 (+1.31%) above its levels before the midterm election results. 
European equities followed a similar pattern, with gains led by the DAX (+1.20%), while the FTSE 100 
(+0.78%), STOXX 600 (+0.72%) and CAC 40 (+0.66%) also advanced. In fact, the Stoxx 600 reached a record 
high for the first time since August.  
Whilst US Treasury markets were closed yesterday, the hawkish implications from the CPI print continued to 
be felt in futures pricing yesterday. Indeed, Fed funds futures continued to shift in a hawkish direction, with 
pricing for the December meeting edging up to 28.5bps. And further out the curve, expectations for the 
September 2027 meeting surged another +7bps to 82bps, although that’s edged back down to 77bps 
overnight. The prospect of a more hawkish Fed helped push the dollar index (+0.66%) to its highest level 
since April 2025. Conversely, that also saw the euro (-0.66%) reach an 18-month low against the dollar, 
closing at around $1.109. 
Given the US holiday, there wasn’t any fresh data or Fed commentary, so all eyes are now on the latest round 
of speakers today, along with tomorrow’s PPI print. 
Turning to the Middle East, oil prices were back up again yesterday, albeit with no obvious headline driving 
the moves. So after two days of consecutive declines, Brent crude rose +1.2% after being higher earlier in 
the session. And in turn, we saw the impact on inflation pricing, with 1yr Euro inflation swaps (+9.3bps) 
rising to +3.02%. So that led investors to price an increasingly hawkish ECB path as well, with the number of 
hikes priced in by the ECB’s July 2027 meeting up another +3bps to +63.5bps yesterday. So that meant 2yr 
German yields (+1.7bps) rose to 3.11%. However, there was also a fresh flattening in the yield curve, with 
the 10yr bund yield (-0.2bps) falling to 3.31%, alongside declines for yields on 10yr OATs (-2.1bps) and 
BTPs (-3.8bps). However, overnight Brent has fallen around a percent taking it back to roughly where it was 
this time yesterday. So a big round trip. That’s helped 10yr US yields reopen half a basis point lower this 
morning after the holiday. 
Elsewhere, focus remains on Russia-Ukraine peace efforts after US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared 
Kushner met President Vladimir Putin in Moscow, with officials citing some progress in talks despite 
territorial issues remaining a key sticking point. The sides agreed to a sizable prisoner exchange, while the 
US delegation is set to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss potential next steps, 
keeping hopes of a diplomatic breakthrough alive. 
Early morning data showed Japan's PPI rose a stronger-than-expected +7.1% y/y in October, up from 
+6.8% in September and above consensus expectations of +6.5%. The hope would be that this now declines 
after recent oil price falls, assuming they hold.   
To the day ahead now, data releases include the UK’s Q3 GDP. In the US, we’ll also get the October existing 
home sales and weekly initial jobless claims. A host of central bank speakers will be on the docket as well 
today, including the Fed’s Hammack, Barr, Goolsbee, Daly and Williams, and the ECB’s Lagarde, Vujcic and 

Nagel. Notable earnings include Applied Materials and Cisco. Finally, the US will also be holding a $25bn 
auction for its 30yr bonds.
