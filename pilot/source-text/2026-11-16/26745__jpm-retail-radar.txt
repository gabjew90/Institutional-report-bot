Despite heavy liquidations on Thursday, retail were net 
buyers of Tech over the week 
•
If the week covered in our last publication was “one of the most consequential 
of the year”, report, this past week may have been the most consequential yet. 
Last Thursday, the Nasdaq recorded its largest decline since March 2020, 
falling 6.4%. Retail investors became record sellers of cash equities early in the 
session, with activity reaching -6z by 10am, Figure 20Retail Investor Intraday Purchases of Stocks and ETFs (as of 10am). This was driven by 
Thursday experiencing the strongest single-stock selling in the first 30 
minutes of trading in over 5 years. Tech stocks accounted for ~75% of 
single-stock liquidations (Tech ex-Mag 7: -6.2z; Mag 7: -3.9z) — for context, 
retail investors have historically favored AI/Tech stocks at the expense of other 
sectors, Figure 18Retail Activity continued to purchase AI/Tech at the expense of SPX 470. ETFs also saw significant offloading at 10am on Thursday 
(-2.3z): flows into broad-based equity ETFs halved (-1.1z), while Style and 
Sector ETFs (led by Tech) each recorded net selling of -2.4z. 
•
Notably, ~80% of the day’s $ selling had already occurred in the first half 
hour of trading. Selling then paused after 11am for the rest of the day, 
suggesting retail investors had largely completed their repositioning early in 
the session, see Reviewing last week’s exceptional retail activity. The daily 
move was subsequently exacerbated by other participants: CTAs had turned 
net short US equities while vol targeting funds’ target leverage had fallen from 
the ~65%ile to 30%ile, report; in addition, support from dealers’ long-gamma 
positions also waned, falling to the 12%ile, report and report. Many clients 
have asked us whether retail investors drove Thursday’s market decline: while 
difficult to quantify, the available data does not rule out that possibility. 
•
Markets rebounded on Friday, with retail buying the recovery (Fri: ~49%ile 
and Mon: ~70%ile), before stronger-than-expected CPI data temporarily 
weighed on sentiment again on Tuesday. Retail nevertheless bought the CPI-
led weakness (~68%ile). On Wednesday, Tech advanced again amid renewed 
AI-led optimism fuelled by CoreWeave’s upbeat outlook (see Earnings 
Takeaways), along with IPO activity scheduled for next week: retail 
involvement was initially strong (58%ile at 11am) but soon faded (8%ile at 
4pm).
•
Despite sharp daily and intraday swings, including Thursday’s liquidation, the 
week stood at 13%ile, with ETFs at 16%ile and single stocks at 19%ile. 
Beneath this overall activity, retail investors ended the week as net buyers 
of Tech, though buying remained average (Tech stocks: 0.2z; Tech ETFs: 
0.1z). Within stocks, buying was concentrated in the Mag 7 (+1.1z), while 
Tech stocks ex-Mag 7 saw net selling (-1.1z). NVDA was a retail beneficiary 
this past week, with strong buying-the-dip flows on Monday (3.7z) and 
Tuesday (4.7z, Figure 16Retail Imbalance in NVDA).

Crowdings and Rotations
Anatomy of the Momentum Unwind
•
Momentum crowding peaked on May 11, providing an early signal of unwind risk (see report and report). The initial unwind 
was concentrated in Residual Vol and continued through June 22, which marked a turning point in risk positioning (Figure 1Risk Positioning In Equities ). 
In July to September period, triggered by Asian Tech deleveraging, US investors rotated out of Beta and Momentum in 
support of the Low Vol and Value barbell trade. By October, the unwind had become more US-centric, with risk-off 
positioning accelerating.
•
Since the peak, Momentum (L/S) has declined by ~40%—its largest unwind since the post-GFC recovery. October was the 
worst month in ~40 years for the long Momentum-versus-market portfolio, which fell 11%, a ~6 sigma event. Over the same 
period, equity multiples de-rated by roughly 4x from their January highs. Despite the severity of the move, we view the 
pullback primarily as a deleveraging event—not a fundamental break in the AI cycle. 
•
Several indicators suggest the capitulation process is well advanced. Momentum crowding has fallen from the 100%ile to 
81%ile relative to its 40 year history. As of November 5, it stood at the 0%ile, 5%ile, and 43%ile vs. 1yr, 3yr and 5yr histories, 
respectively. This repricing reflects deleveraging that has been underway since the June peak: our proprietary broad-based 
leverage index recorded its largest deleveraging episode since the post-COVID downturn in 2022, surpassing the deleveraging 
seen during the Liberation Day shock, report and report.
