Should we fear the upside?
The macro backdrop remains challenging, but the ingredients for another powerful equity squeeze are
falling into place. Goldman sees a pathway higher into year-end following one of the most aggressive
deleveraging episodes in a decade. Seasonality is turning supportive, corporate buybacks are
returning in size, and the semiconductor trade has undergone a brutal reset. Add a market that has
absorbed plenty of bad news, and even the upside could become violent.
The next rally may have more to do with positioning and flows than fundamentals.
Can Christmas come early?
Goldman's Codacci-Pisanelli sees a pathway for equities to squeeze higher into year-end, despite the
recent turmoil. The bank highlights one of the most aggressive deleveraging episodes of the past
decade in its US prime book, leaving positioning considerably cleaner. Add lower multiples and
supportive seasonals, and the ingredients for another rally are there. The macro backdrop remains
challenging, but that doesn't necessarily prevent another powerful squeeze.

The seasonal squeeze
The calendar could be working in the bulls' favor. The final stretch of the year has historically been a
supportive period for equities, and this time the setup comes after a particularly violent positioning
reset. Seasonality is another potential driver of a year-end squeeze. The market doesn't necessarily
need a major improvement in fundamentals to move higher. Sometimes lighter positioning and
seasonal demand are enough.

The biggest buyer is back
Corporate America is stepping back into the market. S&P 500 buyback demand is estimated at
approximately $5bn per day, with 90% of companies expected to be in their open repurchase window
by Monday. Trading volumes have already picked up significantly in the back half of the week. With
positioning much cleaner after the recent washout and seasonality turning supportive, corporate
demand could provide another powerful tailwind for a year-end squeeze.

The market refuses to break
Goldman highlights how much bad news equities have managed to absorb this week. Hotter CPI,
hawkish Fed commentary, surging yields, an oil rally and negative Nvidia headlines have all hit the
tape. Yet the market has held up better than the headlines might suggest. That doesn't eliminate the
macro risks, but it points to a potentially more supportive market structure. When bad news fails to
produce sustained selling, the next squeeze can arrive surprisingly quickly.

The semis reset
The semiconductor trade has undergone a brutal reset, both in positioning and valuations. But
Goldman argues that the longer-term debate around AI monetization and returns on investment may
not be resolved until the next earnings cycle. In the meantime, the fundamental uncertainty remains,
but the positioning backdrop looks considerably cleaner. That creates room for another squeeze, even
without a major improvement in the AI narrative. The next rally doesn't necessarily need a new growth
story.

When rallies create volatility
The usual relationship between equities and volatility is being challenged. Nomura highlights how
hedging flows from exotic and structured products can force dealers to buy volatility as equities rally
and sell volatility as markets decline. This creates positive spot-vol correlation, working against the
traditional pattern. A rising market doesn't necessarily mean falling volatility. Even the upside can
become disorderly.
