Listen To Fed’s Williams; His Hawkish View Is Important
New York Fed president John Williams warned that US inflation was too high and that the Fed needed to take
action over time to ensure inflation shifted back to 2%. His remarks are important for a couple of reasons. First,
his policy bias has shifted. Only a few weeks ago he said one more hike was appropriate this year, but there
was no need for rates to rise further in 2027. Now his remarks have shifted towards a gradual but continued
shift up in rates. He had previously described September’s rate hike as for risk management purposes. But his
latest remarks indicate his moved beyond that. Williams said “the data is speaking very clearly to us — the
economy is strong, labor market continues to strengthen and inflation is persistent”.
The second reason is that Williams has moved up the hierarchy in the Fed. Chairs typically lean on one or two
governors to act as their most senior lieutenants. Chair Powell relied upon Chris Waller and Phil Jefferson, for
example. Chair Warsh appears to have swapped Waller for Williams. Though Warsh says he doesn’t like
guidance, those two governors are able to convey the direction of travel.
(Simon Penn, Macro Sales)

Nvidia's Huang Ousted; Impacts Likely To Be Felt Far And Wide - Nvidia said on Thursday that CEO
Jensen Huang will step down from the role, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress will take on the role of
interim CEO. Huang has agreed to testify before Congress ahead of Thanksgiving. Analyst Timothy Arcuri
wrote on Nov. 11 before the confirmed departure that while the company would likely find a strong candidate,
Huang has obviously been a singular force in many ways so the impact will likely be felt far and wide. He
highlighted that the company has become a key source of funds for a host of AI industry participants including
neo-clouds, LLM developers, ecosystem partners, and suppliers, and Huang has been a key driving force for
this effort. Even if a new leader ultimately maintains a similar approach, there is likely to be some "pause" in
the company's willingness and ability to continue pursuing these deals in the near term. The potential for new,
tighter,  restrictions could see the US government enforcing even tighter restrictions that could impact NVDA's
(and other companies like AMD's) ability to ship product into China but even more importantly into other
regions like Singapore or Malaysia. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
Japan's Katayama Warns Of FX Action As USDJPY Breaches 160 - Japan's Finance Minister Satsuki
Katayama said ministry officials will continue to work closely with the US Treasury to take appropriate action
as needed in response to one-sided, rapid moves in the currency market, after the yen briefly fell below a key
threshold. She spoke after the yen weakened past the key psychological threshold of 160 per dollar during
New York trading Thursday. The breach revived speculation that Tokyo might intervene in the currency market
to support the currency, as it did several times earlier this year. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

Microsoft Suffers Major Global Outage - Microsoft is still working to fix a major global outage that leaves
tens of thousands of users in 74 countries and territories unable to access their emails and other programs,
including Microsoft 365, Outlook and Teams, for more than 10 hours. The disruption started around mid-day
New York Thursday and remains unresolved as of 10:00 London Friday. The company attributed the outage to
“elevated load” on its infrastructure with no indication of a cyber attack. OpenAI said it was investigating an
issue with its Microsoft connectors, warning that new connections might be broken and that some tools were
not working as expected. Anthropic said it had identified an issue with an upstream provider affecting its
Microsoft 365 connector. The incident comes weeks after Microsoft reported an outage of its Azure cloud
platform, which impacted its suite of productivity software and a range of industries worldwide, The Telegraph 
reported [paywall]. (Jolie Ho Macro Sales)

US Cannot Reach Russia-Ukraine War Deal Without Europe, Says Kushner - The US cannot reach a deal
to end the war in Ukraine without Europe’s involvement, US Special envoy for peace Jared Kushner said on
Thursday after separate meetings with Russia and Ukraine’s leaders, the Financial Times [paywall] reports.
Kushner believed Russian President Putin was willing to give European involvement “a try” as Ukrainian
President Volodymyr Zelensky said US officials had made progress on securing Europe a voice in the talks.
Later on Thursday, Kushner said in a post on social media that Russia and Ukraine had “now agreed to
expand the next round of negotiations to include our European partners”. One speaker at the UBS European
conference said that the Trump administration may look for a conclusion to the Ukraine-Russia war to add to
Trump’s legacy. The speaker said his second term had been marred by greater cost of living pressures for US
citizens from a self-inflicted energy and tariff war. Bringing a conclusion on Ukraine-Russia and concluding on
Iran could help paint a better picture of his presidency. Watch for greater urgency from the US on this. (Nana
Antiedu, Macro Sales)
IAEA Says It Has Lost Track Of Iran's Uranium Stocks – Reuters - The IAEA said it is unable to determine
the extent of Iran’s uranium stocks. It declared that its inability to access sites and carry out checks
represented a “matter of serious proliferation”. Reuters reported that the IAEA’s comments came in a
confidential report which had been sent to member states. It said the report noted the IAEA was unable to
determine whether there had or had not been diversion of nuclear materials from peaceful activities towards
weapon research. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
Australia Headline CPI In October To Rise 4.7% Y/y, A New Cycle High - Australia Economics: Stephen
Wu expects Australia headline CPI moved further away from the RBA's target, to 4.7% y/y in October 2026 (no
consensus yet; after 4.3% in September), similar to his earlier estimate. This is expected to be the peak.
Unfavourable base effects from earlier policy changes should start to fade from annual CPI from here. The
month-on-month is expected to be a solid 0.3% m/m, driven by: a surge higher in fuel that adds around 24bp
to inflation and the expected impact from the ban on card surcharging from Oct. 1. Overall, Stephen still
expects the RBA to keep the cash rate restrictive for a prolonged time with the first RBA rate cut in November
2027, and a shallow 50bp cutting cycle; but the risks are now shifting to an earlier and/or larger easing cycle.
(Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
