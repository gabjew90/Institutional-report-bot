**WHAT WE’RE WATCHING:
 
*Macro Outlook 2027: Steady Baseline, Downside Risks
- The global economy grew 2.7% in 2026, in line with potential, with strong AI capex, easier financial
conditions, and fiscal expansion offsetting the energy supply shock. We expect 2.8% in 2027, as lower
energy prices and still-strong AI capex outweigh tighter financial conditions and more neutral fiscal policy.
Risks are tilted to the downside: 1) renewed escalation in Iran could result in another energy price surge;
2) large fiscal deficits could push up long-term yields more sharply, especially in France; and 3) AI capex
could disappoint because of capacity constraints, regulation, credit market stress, or weaker demand. 
- Following upside surprises in 2026, inflation should slow in 2027. US tariff pass-through is ending, the
energy impulse will fade as we lap the outbreak of the Iran war, and AI-related pressures should ease as
production capacity rises. 
- We expect two more rate hikes from the Fed and one from the ECB, but see both returning to cuts
in late 2027. With most of our forecasts for other central banks also below market pricing, we expect longer-
term rates to come down modestly despite large government deficits and heavy AI-linked borrowing.
- We expect positive returns for equities and bonds, but the path could be bumpy. The AI investment
boom has had a much larger impact on earnings and market values than on GDP growth. As the impulse
peaks, markets may worry more about whether the record share of GDP going to US earnings can persist.

- The risk of further oil price spikes could put inflation and rate fears back in focus, underpinning the value of
commodities as a hedge. As we move through 2027, the risks are increasingly skewed towards lower
growth and lower rates. Alongside the highest real yields in many years, we think bonds are starting to
regain their hedging value. With equity index volatility more likely to rise than fall, we think longer-dated
options can offer useful portfolio protection.

*2027 Outlook: Finding Value in Growth
- 2026 was the year of strong earnings. Global equity returns were unusually broad-based across regions,
supported primarily by robust earnings growth — valuations have moderated.
- Earnings growth is poised to slow. As we enter 2027, earnings growth is on course to slow sharply across
all major regions, growth in AI capex spending is likely to peak and the cost of capital remains elevated ...
- But equities have de-rated sharply; with more attractive valuations, positive but slower nominal economic
and earnings growth, and lower bond yields, inflation and oil prices, we expect equities to continue to rise,
but with high volatility.
- We focus on Growth balanced with selective Defensives and beneficiaries of lower bond yields. Attractive
opportunities are emerging in long-duration Growth companies, which should become more valuable as
growth rates slow, particularly US Hyperscalers, which continue to enjoy strong (although slower) earnings
growth and balance sheets.
- We balance this with selective defensive areas that offer reasonable valuations and areas that benefit from
potential lower bond yields and oil prices.
- While forecast returns are highest in Asia, we focus on risk-adjusted returns and are overweight the US and
Japan, neutral in Asia and EM, and underweight Europe, reflecting relative growth, profitability and valuation
opportunities.
- Risks are becoming more two-sided. Bonds, oil prices and AI capex shifts could all increase volatility. We
recommend diversification across Growth and selective Defensive strategies.

*Momentum makeup (h/t Gui Soria)
- The momentum regime shift that started this summer is re-emerging as 12m Momentum
(GSPRHIMO) had its best outperformance day in our records vs 3m Mo (GSPRHMO3) and 6m Mo
(GSPRHMO6) yesterday. In fact, correlation between 12m momentum and 3m momentum is now negative
as the AI trade has unwound. The number of AI names in the short leg of 3m and 6m momo has sky
rocketed as the number of AI names in the long leg has moved lower. This is also consistent with the net
sector flows based on momentum tenor, where info tech was net sold in both 3m and 6m momentum, but
slightly bought in 12m mo. There is also industry level dynamics within each sector like Semis vs Software
(where 12m Mo will be a net seller of semis and buyer of software), but still tells the same story: the make
up of momentum is changing.
- Positioning in momentum has collapsed with exposure on our PB Book falling sharply from its 2026
peak to the 23rd percentile of its 5-year range, as the consensus winners are no longer crowded (Data
Center basket crowding at the 1st percentile over 6 months). We have flagged that any resumptions in the
rotation could provide opportunities to tactically short former 12m momentum short names or broadening
(lower quality software, staples, etc.) where the fundamental story has not supported the move higher
