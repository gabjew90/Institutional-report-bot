From Panic Selling To FOMO Buying
From panic to FOMO in record time. Tech is back, volatility is collapsing, and the positioning washout
has left plenty of fuel for another squeeze. CTAs are short, vol-control funds have slashed exposure,
and investors are chasing the very stocks they dumped days ago. The speed of the reversal is
extraordinary. The question now is whether the squeeze has enough fuel to challenge the top of the
range.
Tech is back
NDX futures are back above the 200-day MA as of writing. The shakeout has been absolutely brutal,
catching most players offside. We bounced right off the major long-term trendline and are now back
inside the familiar range. Once again, investors sold the lows and are desperately chasing the squeeze
higher. From panic selling to FOMO buying in a matter of days. These are extreme markets.

Range mania
One of our core SPX themes has been to trade the range. This remains a mean-reverting market: buy
the range lows, sell the range highs. Unfortunately, plenty of players have been doing the exact
opposite. We are now back above the midpoint of the range and comfortably above the 200-day MA.
From panic at the lows to chasing the highs. How long before the ATH narrative returns?

Semis: Bear market bounce or mid-cycle
correction?
The semiconductor roller coaster continues. The sector remains roughly 25% below its YTD highs, yet
has already surged nearly 20% from the recent lows, technically approaching bull-market territory.
Even more impressive, the rebound has come with limited participation from NVDA, while AMD has
exploded 40% in just four sessions. The debate now is whether to chase high-beta, high-gross-margin
names or stick with quality as AI volatility persists. The selloff was too fast for many to reposition, and
the rebound has been equally unforgiving. Welcome to the semiconductor whipsaw.

CTAs: from long to short
The trend-followers have gone from aggressive longs to net short. UBS estimates CTA global equity
exposure at -5%, the 5th percentile over the past year and 23rd percentile over five years, after
peaking near +150% earlier in the cycle. The positioning reset has been extreme, leaving significant
room for exposure to be rebuilt if markets continue higher. A sustained rebound could force CTAs
back into equities, adding another powerful tailwind to the squeeze. Full note here.
UBS
Vol panic sellers
Vol-control funds have slashed exposure as volatility exploded higher. We have already seen a rather
aggressive reset in vols. Keep volatility drifting lower, and the vol-control bid will return sooner rather
than later. The same flows that amplified the selloff could soon become a powerful tailwind.

The defensive panic trade
The rotation out of tech has reached extraordinary levels. BofA highlights that the one-month
performance spread between defensives and technology has stretched to extremes last seen during
the dotcom era. The rush into safety has become extreme, leaving the trade vulnerable to a violent
reversal. Combine this with the massive positioning reset and collapsing volatility, and the ingredients
for another powerful tech squeeze are firmly in place. The next pain trade could be defensives
underperforming as tech comes roaring back.

China: The ingredients for a squeeze
China has added $5 trillion to GDP since Covid, yet its equities have underperformed global markets by
a staggering 110 percentage points. The disconnect between economic growth and shareholder
returns has been extraordinary. But Goldman sees 10% trend earnings growth and potential valuation
upside of 10–15%, while several key China indices are beginning to stabilize around important
technical levels. The damage remains severe, but the ingredients for a powerful squeeze are starting
to fall into place. Is the China bear finally running out of sellers? More here.

Silver: The sellers are running out
Silver positioning has undergone a brutal reset. Goldman highlights $2.3 billion of cumulative non-
dealer selling over the four weeks into November 3, driven by $2.6 billion of new shorts, partly offset by
$300 million of long buying. Managed Money and non-reportable traders each sold roughly $1.4
billion, leaving net speculative length at just the 2nd percentile of the past two years in notional terms.
The positioning washout has been extreme. The question is whether the market is
running out of sellers.

GS
Silver's fear premium is evaporating
The options market is sending an interesting signal. Goldman notes that silver's three-month
normalized 25-delta put-call skew has retreated from its highest levels since COVID. The extreme
demand for downside protection is beginning to unwind, even as silver remains vulnerable to sharp
two-way moves. Combined with historically depressed speculative positioning, the retreat in fear
creates an increasingly interesting setup. The panic hedges are starting to come off. The next question
is whether speculative longs return. More here.
