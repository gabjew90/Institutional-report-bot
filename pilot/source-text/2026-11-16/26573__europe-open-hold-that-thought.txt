Europe Open - Hold that thought
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
US bond markets reopened overnight but mostly drifted, with US 10s sticky at the 4.99% level. BoJ dove Asada warned
that the bank needs to be careful about the pace of rate hikes. USDJPY close to 160 unsurprisingly saw verbal
jawboning from currency official Mimura, which did little to influence moves. Tech sentiment weaker again dragged by
Nvidia, with Taiwan's Taiex index lower thanks to the addition of geopolitical comments.Oil traded mostly flat, US envoys
are meeting Russian President Putin today. US-Iran talks remain standstill. 
US data is back in the form of PPI at 13:30 GMT - watch for details that matter for core PCE, with our economists
expecting final demand at 0.3% MoM. Printing simultaneously is weekly jobless claims. Fedspeak in more focus with
Hammack, Barr, Goolsbee, Daly and Schmid speaking today, first two are Dec voters. In EM, keep an eye on Argentina
and Brazil inflation data, rate decisions in Romania and Peru (holds expected).

In Focus‌
Rates: US bond markets opened slightly weaker though momentum failed to follow through, with duration trading largely
sideways. Flow-wise, our Tokyo desk saw decent buying in the intermediates alongside some interest in rolling over in 2-year
zone. Fedspeak comes in the form of Hammack (Dec voter) at 14:00 GMT, Barr (Dec voter) at 15:00, Goolsbee at 16:15 for
the second time this week and Daly and Schmid to end the day at 18:10 and 18:30 respectively. US PPI in focus after
stronger CPI - our economists expect Final Demand a 0.3% MoM vs 1.3% prior with ex Food, Energy and Trade at 0.3% MoM
(vs 0.5% prior). Core PCE is currently tracking at 0.33% MoM but we are closely watching details within PPI such as airfares,
medical services and portfolio fees. 30y auction due later. 

Elsewhere, BoJ dove Asada said that he isn’t taking a stance to go against raising interest rates no matter what, but the bank
needs to be careful about the pace of its moves. He wouldn't go so far as to propose a rate cut, though. Decent 5y auction
supporting that tenor in the curve, with the rest tad weaker in line with USTs. 
FX: USD may have rallied to May 2025 highs on Wed though our traders had skepticism behind moves with volumes much
weaker due to Veterans Day. Rally slightly extends overnight with EURUSD breaking below 1.11 and USDJPY just under 160.
Implied vols reacted in tandom with 1m JPY vol 0.5v higher with franchise demands concentrated in the front-end topside
USDPY options. Verbal jawboning from Japan Mimura was unsurprising as USDJPY climbed (overnight highs of 159.88) but
we still think actual intervention remains slim at current levels. "I have a strong impression that currency moves over the past
few days have been too fast," Mimura said. Our trader Anthony Kemp highlights that on a trade-weighted basis, JPY is
considerably stronger which also limits chances of intervention. Overall, interbank volumes were 48% higher than 30d
averages in the Asia session with continued leveraged interest for USD buying. 
Equities: Net negative bias across Asian indices with Taiex underperforming peers, down -2.6%. A combination of
geopolitical headlines (China President Xi said that China's reunification must and will be achieved) and ongoing Nvidia
weakness is weighing on the index. Citi Research think that a leadership transition creates an overhang on the stock given his
central role in strategy, product vision, customer relationships, and investor confidence. We believe CFO Colette Kress could
serve as interim CEO while the board evaluates external candidates.
Nasdaq futures giving back Wed gains, down -1.1% overnight. S&P e-minis are -0.5% weaker. 7600 holds as support with a
break suggesting a move to 7568, courtesy of our futures desk. ICYMI latest CTA model sees CTA flip back long SPX above
7676, with first buy levels in NDX and RTY are further out 29798 and 2891 respectively. Levels for max long are at 7738 for
SPX. CTAs are now long SX5E but levels to return to short are not far at 6313. 
Commodities: Oil prices bottoming and now nearly flat at print, Brent trading at the $92 handle. Al Jazeera quotes Iran lead
negotiator Ghalibaf who said that the "The ball is clearly in the US' court. The Americans have still not formally or clearly
responded to our letter". Meanwhile US envoys Witkoff and Kushner are planning to meet Russian President Putin today in
Moskow, following US-Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi earlier this week. While Kremlin policy aide said that earlier talks
were substantive and useful, key sticking points, such as territorial issues, persist. Elsewhere gold broke through the $4000
handle overnight.
Elsewhere‌
GBP: Q3 GDP just out printed at 0.7% QoQ/1.9% YoY. We await our economists' detailed take but resilient growth is
supportive of another rate hike - we still think Feb is more likely than Dec, but its ultimately dependent on energy prices. 
EUR: ECB publishes its Economic Bulletin at 09:00 GMT.
RON: Oct CPI into the NBRO later today printed tad stronger than cons. expectations at 6.94% YoY vs 6.61% prior. The NBR
should keep the policy rate unchanged as inflation remains elevated.
TRY: Turkey Central Bank publishes its Inflation Report at 07:30 GMT.
INR: CPI YoY at 10:30 GMT for October. Our economists look for headline inflation to advance to 5.9%YoY in October from
5.5%YoY in September.
IDR: Indonesia VP Gibran has formally submitted his resignation to the national assembly overnight after wide speculation.
The motion to elect a successor will begin, with President Prabowo expecting to nominate two candidates with the process
complete within 60 days.
BRL: Brazil government unveiled a package of measures that would tighten current fiscal rules, expecting to improve fiscal
results by BRL 50-60bn ($10.8bn), with a larger impact in subsequent years. The bill would also cap real spending growth at
2% from 2027 onwards, down from 2.5%. 
On the data front, watch IBGE Inflation IPCA MoM at 12:00 GMT for October. Our economists expect inflation to rise 0.28%
MoM, down from 0.82% in the month prior, driven by lower electric prices (-1.9% MoM). More importantly, both average core
measures and underlying services should come in at 0.4% MoM.

ARS: National CPI YoY at 19:00 GMT for October. Our economists will be looking for whether the modest recovery in real
wages continues, and whether the monthly inflation decline that began after the peak in March continues. No forecasts but
last month’s print came at 1.90% Mom/33.4% YoY.
PEN: Reference Rate Decision at 23:00 GMT. Peru’s central bank has kept rates on hold since September 2025, even in the
face of above target inflation. We continue to expect a hold into the foreseeable future.
