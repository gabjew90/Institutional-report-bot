The next buyers
The AI trade has been through a brutal positioning reset, but the next question is who steps in to buy.
Retail is already returning, leveraged exposure has been dramatically reduced and QQQ short interest
has surged to its highest level since 2023. Meanwhile, hedge funds remain underexposed after one of
the most violent momentum unwinds in decades. JPM sees the potential for a second wave of buying
into year-end. The forced selling may be largely behind us. The next question is who gets
forced back in.
Retail refuses to quit
Retail investors are back buying the dip. JPM highlights equity ETF purchases running at $20bn per day
last week, accelerating to $30bn on Monday. Leveraged equity ETFs attracted nearly $5bn over
November 5–6, equivalent to roughly $15bn of leveraged exposure, while inverse ETFs saw
approximately $5bn in outflows over the past week. The buying impulse is returning despite the violent
AI unwind. Retail is once again providing an important cushion for equities.

The leverage reset
The leverage washout has been extraordinary. JPM estimates that by November 5, leveraged equity
ETF exposure relative to underlying market capitalization had unwound nearly 90% of the increase
accumulated between April and June. The recent rebound in leveraged ETF buying is therefore taking
place from a dramatically reduced base. The leverage that helped fuel the earlier rally has largely
been flushed out, creating room for a new positioning cycle.

The shorts are back

The positioning backdrop has changed dramatically. JPM highlights QQQ short interest reaching its
highest level since 2023, while Nasdaq futures positioning has been heavily reduced. Hedge funds
entered the November selloff having already cut significant exposure. That leaves a potentially
powerful source of incremental demand if the market stabilizes. The caveat? Not all QQQ shorts are
directional bets. Some represent hedges against existing long exposure, limiting the potential squeeze
fuel.

The options reset
Retail's speculative appetite has also been reset. JPM notes that small-lot call buying has retreated
sharply from its early-June peak, with the retail options impulse weakening further during the recent
unwind. Retail's share of options trading is now close to its one-year low, according to JPM's Retail
Radar. The cash-equity buying has returned, but the options crowd has yet to fully re-engage. That
leaves another potential source of upside demand.
JPM
Momentum capitulation
The momentum crash has been historic. JPM estimates that the momentum long/short factor has
declined approximately 40% from its peak, its largest unwind since the post-GFC recovery. October
alone delivered an 11% decline in the long momentum-versus-market portfolio, a roughly six-sigma
event. Crowding has fallen sharply, with shorter-term measures approaching historical extremes. After
one of the most violent momentum unwinds in decades, the question is whether positioning has
become sufficiently washed out to support a sustained reversal.

Still buying the generals
Retail investors have not abandoned their favorite stocks. JPM estimates $1.6bn of net NVDA buying
over November 5–11, making it the week's most purchased stock. Buying intensity surged to 3.7
standard deviations on Monday and 4.7 on Tuesday. Even after the violent drawdown, retail continues
to treat Nvidia as the ultimate buy-the-dip trade. Whether that conviction survives the next leg of
volatility is another question.
JPM
The narrow AI bid
Beneath the headline recovery, retail participation remains surprisingly narrow. JPM reports that retail
bought Mag 7 stocks at a 1.1-standard-deviation pace over the past week, while selling technology
stocks outside the group at a corresponding -1.1 standard deviations. Semiconductor buying persisted,
but hardware and software continued to see selling. The AI trade is attracting buyers again, but the
recovery is far from universal. Leadership remains concentrated in a handful of names.
JPM
The next wave of buyers?
This is where the positioning story becomes particularly interesting. JPM argues that hedge funds have
already completed substantial de-risking, leaving their exposure significantly below earlier levels.
Continued retail support could stabilize AI stocks through November, potentially allowing hedge funds
to rebuild positions from late November onward. That would introduce a second wave of demand into
a market where momentum and leverage have already been reset. Retail may be providing the bridge
to the next phase of the AI trade. If hedge funds start rebuilding exposure, the market could
transition from forced selling to forced buying. The risk? Retail runs out of ammunition
before institutions return.
