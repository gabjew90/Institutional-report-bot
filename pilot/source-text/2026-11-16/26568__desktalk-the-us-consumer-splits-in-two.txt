DeskTalk - The US consumer splits in two
By Varshi Karamsetty
The recent CPI report and Fedspeak may have strengthened the case for further tightening and higher for longer rates.
However credit card data reveals nuances beneath headline resilience and show an economy increasingly getting more K-
shaped. Holiday spending and next week's consumer earnings are the next test. 
New report from the NY Fed shows that credit card balance hit a record $1.29tn in Q3 2026, a 1.7% increase from Q2. Total
household debt, including mortgages and student and auto loans, also expanded by $334 billion. It's reassuring that
delinquency rates are falling, but the gap between borrowers living in higher-income neighborhoods and lower-income
neighborhoods has expanded.
It's a similar observation from U Mich data. Headline sentiment beat expectations last month, though underlying data points to
an increasingly K-shaped consumer. Following the recent AI-led equity selloff, confidence rose among lower- and middle-
wealth consumers, while it fell 5% for those with the largest stock portfolios. However, the widening trend between high and
low income households persists.

For the Fed, cooling demand could raise the bar for further tightening. A deeper equity selloff would weaken higher-income
consumption, while wider credit spreads would intensify pressure on lower-income borrowers. The impact may take time to
emerge, but could limit chances of delivering 75bp hikes currently priced by OIS markets. 
Of course, we are heading into holiday season which should provide some relief to the sector. Mastercard data is forecasting
sales to increase 5.5% YoY in 2026, while earnings should also indicate solid outlooks. "The headwinds of persistent inflation
& new pressure from higher gas prices continue to be overshadowed by the tailwinds of a solid jobs backdrop and positive

wealth effect from higher asset prices" Citi Research writes. Key names that report include Home Depot (Nov 17), TJX Cos
and Target (Nov 18), Walmart (Nov 19), Costco (Dec 10). The latter two remain consensus longs but recently used as funding
shorts for many hedge funds. Our Airlines team finds that supermajors are well positioned to capitalize on K-shaped trends.
