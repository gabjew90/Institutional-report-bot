Portfolio Manager’s Summary 
US stocks traded lower Friday but the S&P 500 still gained +2.5% on the week, as investors have been navigating a hot CPI print, ongoing 
volatility in the AI infrastructure trade, and a debate about how stocks might trade once we hit peak earnings growth. Memory, IPPs, and 
Datacenters outperformed this week, while Quantum Computing, Goldminers, and Memes were among the themes that underperformed.  
 
Prime: HFs net sold US equities for a 2nd straight week (5 of last 6), driven by short sales in Single Stocks partially offset by short covers + long 
buys in Macro Products. US ETF shorts decreased -8.6% – the largest % covering in 7 months, while 8 of 11 sectors were net sold, led by Info 
Tech/Financials/Healthcare/Staples. This week's % net selling in US Financials was the largest in 8 months (99th percentile on a 5-year lookback). 
 
Shares: On the week, both LOs and HFs finished moderate net sellers. This week’s market bounce looked more rotational, and this thematic was 
also present in our flows with a glaring drumbeat of supply in NVDA from the LO community which rotated into hyperscalers and other pockets 
of large cap tech. Still ongoing debate on the broader Semis landscape as investor continue to digest recent news flow (Nvidia + Election).  
 
Futures: On November 11th, Russell 2k funding richened significantly, reflecting probable demand for leveraged expression. Based on cash basket 
performances, the Other and Non-Reportable cohorts potentially initiated longs in Russell 2k futures. However, these categories represent a small 
portion of open interest. And an extended rate hiking cycle still presents an imminent threat. Earlier in 2026, Asset Manager gross longs were very 
positively correlated to SOFR. Said differently, when the market priced more or higher-for-longer rates, Asset Manager liquidated Russell 2k futures. 
 
Derivatives: Investor positioning is light (US L/S net leverage near 5-year lows), the vol complex is jittery (Panic Index at 6.5/10), and options 
volumes have ballooned (recall November 5th was the largest put volume session ever). Index skew is bid, allowing clients to enter costless collars. 
Given the amount of volatility in the largest weights in SPX (NVDA especially), last week has been one of the most volatile “average stock” periods 
since March 2020. VIX downside continues to resonate with clients as following last Thursday’s extreme moves there had been more confidence 
in “vol lower” than there was in “spot higher” – VIX puts have traded more volume in the last 10 sessions than at any other point this year.  
 
Baskets: The momentum regime shift that started this summer is re-emerging as 12m Momentum (GSPRHIMO) had its best outperformance day 
in our records vs 3m Mo (GSPRHMO3) and 6m Mo (GSPRHMO6) on Wednesday. The number of AI names in the short leg of 3m and 6m momo 
has skyrocketed as the number of AI names in the long leg has moved lower. There is also industry level dynamics within each sector like Semis 
vs Software which tells the same story: the makeup of momentum is changing. Positioning in momentum has collapsed, and the consensus 
winners are no longer crowded. We have flagged that any resumptions in the rotation could provide opportunities to tactically short former 12m 
momentum short names or broadening (lower quality software, staples, etc.) where the fundamental story has not supported the move higher. 
 
Sector Specialists: TMT: The NDX broke a 4-week losing streak, closing up ~370 bps on the week led by Semis/SOX up ~840 bps – that said, it 
was anything but smooth sailing as the market digested some unexpected headlines from NVDA, an outage from MSFT and an increasingly 
difficult macro backdrop following another hotter CPI print. One thing that’s apparent in our convos is that there appears to be relatively LITTLE 
CONSENSUS in the market right now. Lots of debate remains around where we are in the AI/Semi cycle, what’s priced in, and if this is a bear 
market bounce or a bull market forming. Software feels like dispersion has picked up, and Internet has gotten difficult to trade. Put together, our 
sense is one of more of a ‘blank slate’ re: consensus on positioning and sentiment, arguably a healthy investing landscape into 2027. Financials 
& Real Estate: Financials underperformed the SPX by ~200bps with no clear skew between defensives (P&C Insurance underperformed whereas 
exchanges outperformed) and offensives (Fintech, Consumer Finance, Boutique Investment Banks, and Regional Banks underperformed while 
Asset managers, Moneycenters, and Life Insurance outperformed), suggesting positioning amidst notable sector-selling was having a greater 
impact on this week’s moves. Conversations indicated the most negativity on Consumer Finance on concerns against a potentially shifting 
regulatory backdrop, while debates intensified in cyclicals & capital markets around ‘where are we in the cycle’. Next week in Real Estate is a 
historically well attended industry conference – where the top focuses are 1) is Industrial leasing activity seeing any potential ripple effect from 
greater scrutiny in some states’ datacenter buildout? 2) How are some triple nets changing their growth algorithms amidst higher cost of capital, 
and 3) Are there any green shoots in sunbelt apartments (underwhelmed expectations) and single family (investor optimism rising)?

Trading Flows (November 6th – November 12th): HFs net sold US equities for a second straight week (5 of last 6, -0.4 SDs 1-year), 
driven by short sales in Single Stocks partially offset by short covers + long buys in Macro Products.  
• 
Macro Products (Index and ETF combined) were net bought for a third straight week (+1.2 SDs 1-year), driven by short 
covers + long buys (1.3 to 1). US-listed ETF shorts decreased -8.6% – the largest % covering in 7 months – led by covering in 
Large Cap Equity, Credit, and Tech ETF.  
 
• 
In contrast, Single Stocks collectively were heavily net sold again (-1.7 SDs 1-year), driven mainly by short sales as long 
flows were relatively muted. 8 of 11 sectors were net sold, led in $ terms by Info Tech, Financials, Health Care, and Staples, 
while Consumer Disc, Energy, and Real Estate were the only net bought sectors. Info Tech was by far the most $ net sold 
global (and US) sector again this week – US Info Tech long/short ratio now stands at 1.5 (vs. YTD peak of 2.14 seen in early 
June), the lowest level on our record (since 2016).  
 
• 
Financials was the most net sold US sector in SD terms this week (-2.2 SDs 1-year), driven by long-and-short sales (~4 to 1). 
This week’s % net selling in US Financials was the largest in 8 months and ranks in the 99th percentile on a 5-year lookback. 
Nearly all subsectors (sans Large Cap Banks) were net sold on the week, led by Transaction & Payment Processing, Capital 
Markets (IBs & Asset Mgrs), Consumer Finance, and Insurance. US Financials net exposure (% of total US net MV) now stands 
at 12.1%, in the 98th/86th/58th percentiles on a 1-year/3-year/5-year lookback.  
 
• 
Consumer Discretionary was the most $ net bought US sector this week (+1.0 SDs 1-year), driven by long buys as well as 
short covers. This week’s % net buying in US Consumer Discretionary was the largest in more than 3 months – Hotels, 
Restaurants & Leisure, Autos, and Diversified Consumer Svcs were the most net bought subsectors, partially offset by net 
selling in Textiles, Apparel & Luxury goods. In contrast, Consumer Staples was among the most net sold US sectors this week 
(-2.0 SDs 1-year), driven entirely by short sales far outpacing long buys (5.6 to 1) – nearly all subsectors were net sold, led 
by Staples Distribution & Retail and Beverages.

The momentum regime shift that started this summer is re-emerging as 12m Momentum (GSPRHIMO) had its best 
outperformance day in our records vs 3m Mo (GSPRHMO3) and 6m Mo (GSPRHMO6) yesterday. In fact, correlation 
between 12m momentum and 3m momentum is now negative as the AI trade has unwound. The number of AI names in 
the short leg of 3m and 6m momo has sky rocketed as the number of AI names in the long leg has moved lower. This is 
also consistent with the net sector flows based on momentum tenor, where info tech was net sold in both 3m and 6m 
momentum, but slightly bought in 12m mo. There is also industry level dynamics within each sector like Semis vs 
Software (where 12m Mo will be a net seller of semis and buyer of software), but still tells the same story: the make up of 
momentum is changing.

percentile of its 5-year range, as the consensus winners are no longer crowded (Data Center basket crowding at the 1st 
percentile over 6 months). We have flagged that any resumptions in the rotation could provide opportunities to tactically 
short former 12m momentum short names or broadening (lower quality software, staples, etc.) where the fundamental 
story has not supported the move higher.

lagging while Quality can extend its outperformance. Realistically, momentum volatility remains elevated (60-day, about 
3.5x the S&P 500), so rotations and reversals are likely to stay a feature of the factor until the market settles on new long-
term leadership.

TMT (Pete Callahan and Alexander Joseph) 
The NDX broke a 4-week losing streak, closing up ~370 bps on the week led by Semis/SOX up ~840 bps on the week – that said, 
it was anything but smooth sailing out there as the market digested some unexpected headlines from NVDA, an outage from 
MSFT and an increasingly difficult macro backdrop following another hotter CPI print (cue 10yr Yields back at 5.15% and Oil 
back towards $96/brl). To our eyes, the strength this week was largely an output of cleaner positioning + intra-market rotations 
(large-cap Tech, Defensives, Low Vol, etc – see also charts of Pharma as of late), creating some late-week debate about how 
much further room there is to run. One thing that’s apparent to us in conversations is that for the first time in a while – there 
appears to be relatively LITTLE CONSENSUS in the market right now (perhaps away from positive sentiment on the 
hyperscalers?). Lots of debate remains around where we are in the AI/Semi cycle, what’s priced in, if this is a bear market 
bounce or a bull market forming (YE rally?!). Under the surface, Software feels like dispersion has picked up (even within Cyber & 
Data Infra cohorts), and Internet has gotten squeezy and difficult to trade. Ideas continue to pour in outside of the “same” buy-
the-dip pitch on AI names. Put together, our sense is one of more of a ‘blank slate’ re: consensus on positioning and sentiment, 
arguably a healthy investing landscape into 2027. Next week, focus turns towards earnings from NVDA/PANW & China Internet, 
plus MSFT Ignite conference and the long awaited launch of GTA VI (TTWO). 
 
Financials & Real Estate (Christian DeGrasse) 
Financials underperformed the SPX by ~200bps with no clear skew between defensives (P&C Insurance underperformed the 
index whereas exchanges outperformed) and offensives (Fintech, Consumer Finance, Boutique Investment Banks, and Regional 
Banks underperformed the index while Asset managers, Moneycenters, and Life Insurance outperformed), suggesting investor 
positioning amidst notable sector-selling was potentially having a greater impact on this week’s moves. On an acute basis 
amidst the selling, conversations indicated the most negativity on Consumer Finance on investor concerns against a potentially 
shifting regulatory backdrop, while debates intensified in cyclicals & capital markets around ‘where are we in the cycle.’ A 
specific focus debate this week came around SCHW, with bears focusing on margin risk amidst lower cash in October (& 
potential for another cut to earnings estimates), with bulls arguing it was temporary (Retail activity?) & the accelerating organic 
growth is a unique standout in an industry that’s seeing more competitive pressure. Next week in Real Estate is a historically 
well attended industry conference – where the top focuses are 1) is Industrial leasing activity seeing any potential ripple effect 
from greater scrutiny in some states’ datacenter buildout? 2) How are some triple nets changing their growth algorithms amidst 
higher cost of capital, and 3) Are there any green shoots in sunbelt apartments (underwhelmed expectations)  and single family 
(investor optimism rising). 
 
Healthcare (Jon Chan) 
Healthcare mostly tracked the market this week, as investors picked up the pieces post the factor-led unwinds in the back-half 
of last week post elections, though quickly moved onto new debates – as a hotter-than-expected CPI print on Tuesday catalyzed 
a bounce in the 10Y back through 5% contributing to heaviness/volatility in higher duration corners of HC (SMID biotech, 
Tools/Diagnostics, etc.). While the controversies/volatility in the semis space (NVDA -13% on the week, -33% since early Oct) 
have kept “broadening out” rotations at play – a dynamic that’s net-net aided HC performance, most evident in a persisting bid in 
Large Biopharma (LLY +6% on the week, etc). And while this cohort has also benefitted from a strong showing this 3Q earnings 
cycle – have been sensing an uptick of vertigo around current valuations (PFE vs. NVDA P/E Ratio increasingly circulated 
comparison) – similar to inbounds received during August’s run. Meanwhile, in a backdrop of higher rates + dampened animal 
spirits on contagion risk from the AI uncertainty – the margin of error around growthier/risk assets seemed especially narrow 
this week (TWST -7% on F4Q beat + Initial ’27 comments vs high expects, HTFL -18% Friday on 3Q beat + Raise though some 
picking at PA, VIR lower on the week after data update + raise, etc) or where negative updates were particularly punished (see 
IONS -21% Monday post significant sHTG miss and lowered guide, LEGN -24% on the week after warning on 4Q + ’27 cons, etc.) 
 
Industrials & Materials (Ryan Novak) 
Industrials were up 350bps on the week while materials lagged up 50bps. Broader markets were in between up 230bps led by 
energy with housing lagging on higher rates. Small / mid cap AI names led on the week with MOD up 43% including 30% Friday 
with headlines around a potential takeout, AAON up 40% on the week after a similar beat on earnings, CECO up 26% guiding 
2027 well ahead of expectations. LGN AVEX FLY DPC HAWK were other SMID names that saw upside last week due to earnings. 
On the downside CTVA was down 22% after a sharply lower implied Q4 outlook with earnings, FMC down 10% traded in tandem. 
GFL down 9% on the week after the Board concluded the sale process intending to remain standalone. Housing related names 
DFH LGIH NX HOV NVR were all weak on a surprise uptick in rates following the US CPI. Next week is relatively quiet on all 

fronts. Off cycle / late EPS from WWD ACM VIK KEYS will be in focus with multiple analyst days BLDR ECL GXO EXPD ROK and 
MOD with November conferences ongoing. 
 
Energy & Utilities (Adam Wijaya) 
‘Flight to safety’ conversations on the desk remain elevated in the Energy complex – especially given the uncertain oil macro 
(have seen a significant reduction in risk premium over the last two weeks), continued pressure on gasoline margins and less 
certainty around the power complex given midterm election risk. Investor inbounds have largely centered around the natural gas 
complex (E&Ps and Midstream). On E&P, while Henry Hub remains weaker relative to expectations earlier this year, given the 
move lower in oil/products – there’s a preference to hide in Gas E&P (EQT, AR) where we note specialist positioning has been 
more negative for the majority of the year. On Midstream (WMB, DTM) – while the market continues to digest potential 
implications for the power exposed names near-term specifically in TX/OH/GA, there’s still a view that gas volumetric growth in 
the US surprise to the upside and power demand forecasts longer-term remain less at risk vs the near-term concerns.
