NVIDIA Corporation
Heavy Is the Head That Wears the Crown; Downgrading to Market Perform
What You Need to Know: After a head-spinning week of news and allegations around CEO
Jensen Huang, Nvidia’s board is reportedly preparing to remove him, according to the Wall
Street Journal. The full story will take time to emerge and may require several investigations,
including the board’s own independent probe and a requested U.S. House briefing. What is
already clear is that Huang’s likely departure and heightened scrutiny of the company will
be a significant overhang on NVDA shares in the near term. Meanwhile, the potential for a
slower AI buildout and more intense competition from other chipmakers will only add to
execution risk. We continue to believe Nvidia holds a leadership role in the AI buildout, and it is
entirely possible that the stock may not have much further to fall from current levels. However,
governance and regulatory pressure, the potential for more serious fallout, and a sudden
change in management are likely to muddle both execution and sentiment. As a result, we are
downgrading NVDA to Market Perform and will wait for the dust to settle before reassessing.
Key concerns underpinning our downgrade:
•
Increased Government Scrutiny. Over the weekend, Jensen Huang acknowledged
infidelity after the Financial Times reported that a U.S. intelligence memo alleged a romantic
relationship between him and Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive whom U.S. intelligence
believes was recruited by China’s Ministry of State Security. Megaspeed is a Singapore-
based cloud provider, spun out of a Chinese gaming company in 2023, whose Malaysian
subsidiary is at the center of U.S. and Singaporean investigations into the suspected
diversion of Nvidia chips to China.
The New York Times reported that Megaspeed bought more than $4.8B of Nvidia chips
over two years, and that a Commerce Department probe of the company was moved to the
Department of Justice after Nvidia’s general counsel contacted Commerce investigators
about the case. Nvidia maintains that its dealings were proper, and its board has opened an
independent investigation. Our concern is that, whatever the outcome, the policy response
will create additional hurdles for Nvidia and the broader chip industry. We see the potential
for tighter licensing for Southeast Asian and sovereign buyers, a revival of diffusion-style
country caps, or broader entity listings to gate access to key growth markets outside the U.S.
and weigh on Nvidia’s top line.
•
Leadership Vacuum. We may be getting ahead of events, and Jensen Huang may yet keep
his job. But after reports of a second affair, we think the odds that he is replaced have risen
sharply. Nvidia has a deep bench of seasoned executives, yet Huang would leave very big
shoes to fill. Internally, we see CFO Colette Kress, Vice President of Hyperscale and HPC Ian
Buck (widely credited as CUDA’s creator), and Jonathan Ross, the Groq founder and TPU
architect who joined Nvidia in December 2025, as credible candidates.

Our concern, though, is that without Huang, Nvidia loses the creative, relationship-driven dealmaking that has underpinned its
market leadership over the last two years. Huang was the driving force behind Nvidia’s creative financing schemes at customers,
including a $30B equity stake in OpenAI, investments in Anthropic and xAI, and capacity backstops for neoclouds such as
CoreWeave (CRWV $57.94; Outperform). He was equally central on the supply side, securing leading allocations of memory,
wafers, and optics through prepayments, multiyear purchase agreements, and equity stakes in suppliers such as Lumentum (LITE
$639.99), Coherent (COHR $230.89; Market Perform), and Corning (GLW $120.00).
While existing contracts will survive a CEO change, what’s at risk is the next round of deals, and with it Nvidia’s ability to defend
its share into 2027 and beyond. With Huang gone, or severely hampered by investigations and scrutiny, we believe Nvidia could
open itself to more significant competition from vendors like Broadcom (AVGO $353.80; Outperform) and AMD (AMD $575.38;
Outperform).
•
The Competitive Moat Is Narrowing. Beyond the latest developments and concerns about Huang’s possible departure, we have
some concerns that Nvidia’s lead in AI infrastructure is narrowing. In our view, the moat is noticeably smaller than it was even
a few years ago, as ASIC and GPU alternatives show they can win at scale. Broadcom and Google (GOOG $291.60; Outperform)
have had significant success with the TPU—Anthropic alone could to deploy roughly 5GW of TPU v8i in 2027. SemiAnalysis
argued last week that Google’s TPU is staking a credible claim on agentic inference with its current Ironwood chip (TPUv7) being
competitive with or ahead of Nvidia’s Blackwell systems in tokens per dollar. AMD has also made significant progress with its
Helios racks, which now carry gigawatt-scale commitments from OpenAI, Meta (META $627.74; Outperform) and Anthropic, even
as its interconnect and software stack still lag Nvidia.
Even the CUDA moat is not what it used to be. The lock-in around CUDA was built largely on the cost of rewriting and tuning
kernels for another chip. That cost is falling as frontier workloads now run through framework layers such as PyTorch, JAX and
vLLM, and coding agents increasingly do porting work that used to tie up engineering teams for quarters. Anthropic made this
point in its IPO roadshow. It said bringing up a new accelerator historically took 6–12 months, and that Claude Mythos Preview
brought up prompt processing for one model on AMD’s MI355X in roughly three days, porting or writing almost all the kernels
itself. The claim is narrow (prefill only, one model, no training), but it points toward a more fungible approach to compute. CUDA’s 6
million developers still create real stickiness, and its broad set of libraries and kernels remains critical for training performance. But
as inference becomes a larger share of workloads and competing software stacks improve, we believe CUDA could see its stickiness
lessen over time.
Stock Thoughts: Following reports that CEO Jensen Huang was likely to be ousted, NVDA shares have fallen nearly 9% in the after-
hours market, after a 7% decline so far this week, and a 27% fall from its highs in October. At a price of roughly $160 per share, NVDA
trades at a record low price-to-earnings multiple of only 10 times 2027 and 7 times 2028 consensus expectations. While we do not
believe the near-term fundamentals have changed meaningfully, and the AI supply chain continues to signal that demand far exceeds
supply, uncertainty around the company is likely to cap share performance over the coming quarters. Even in the best case, heightened
scrutiny, government oversight, and reputational damage are likely to make Nvidia’s path forward more challenging. In the meantime,
we expect investors to gravitate toward AI infrastructure names with less governance risk.
Risks: Risks to Nvidia include China exposure, competition from other merchant GPU vendors and custom ASIC projects, increased
regulatory constraints and tariffs, supply chain constraints, the potential for deployments to be pushed out, and heightened execution
risk following the investigations and potential departure of long-time CEO Jensen Huang.
