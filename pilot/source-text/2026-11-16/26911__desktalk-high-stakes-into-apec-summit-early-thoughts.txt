DeskTalk - High stakes into APEC summit, early thoughts
By Varshi Karamsetty
APEC summit will take place next week, Nov 18-19 with many global leaders and CEOs gathering in Shenzhen, China. The
last Trump-Xi meeting took place in Sep, but latest Nvidia developments, and the possibility of Putin attending gives the
summit greater significance. Official schedule not out, but some early thoughts on potential themes that we are closely
monitoring: 
Nvidia brings US-China chip policy into focus, given its central in the debate over export controls. H200 chip sales are
currently allowed but have a 25% import tariff. The risk of tighter restrictions on China's access to advanced chips is high, with
Axios reporting that Commerce Secretary Lutnick recently met with Treasury and Defense Secretary Bessent and Hegseth,
alongside Secretary of State Rubio about ongoing concerns. Citi Research writes that some measures will need no
congressional or presidential authorization and can be invoked immediately, such as the FCC rules.
On trade, China has extended the suspension of the 24% additional tariffs on US imports for two months until Jan 9, while
keeping the 10% blanket tariff in place. US has also extended Section 301 tariff exemptions also to Jan 9. US companies
have still been reporting lower volume of rare earth metals despite a truce agreed last year, so eyes on further commitment.
Meanwhile, FT reports that Japan has asked US to urge China to ease rare earth export controls. Xi-Trump are expected to
meet again in Florida next month.
Geopolitics remains firmly on the agenda on several fronts. Putin proposed a trilateral meeting between US-China-Russia
last month, confirmation of attendance (first since 2017) would be a constructive signal. Taiwan is participating in this year's
summit, but assets underperformed overnight after Xi said complete "reunification" with China is inevitable. Iran is not an
APEC member, but the summit could provide the platform for China to act as possible mediator. Ultimately, substantial deal
progress remains unlikely. 
For markets the current geopolitical and AI uncertainty should keep headline risk elevated. Practical progress remains
unlikely, and next week we also contend with Nvidia earnings, and a potential listing of a major IPO. Nvidia has already
meaningfully dragged tech sentiment, while US inflation/Fedspeak amid elevated oil has been the most significant driver for
rates/FX markets.
