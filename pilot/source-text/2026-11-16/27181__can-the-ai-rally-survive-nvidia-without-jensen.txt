 
Can The AI Rally Survive Nvidia Without Jensen? 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, NOV 12, 2026 - 03:35 PM 
After 33 years, Nvidia no longer has Jensen Huang. 
 
On Thursday morning the board announced that Huang has resigned as President, CEO and 
director, effective immediately, capping a two-week saga that began with the NYT's report on his 
relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset and ended with a second affair, this time 
with an Nvidia executive who reported to him. CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO (while 
keeping the CFO job), Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first-ever Chair, and the search 
for a permanent successor will consider internal and external candidates. The stock slid 10% 
from last night's close, to its lowest since July 2025 and more than 35% below its highs. 
All of which makes the timing of Goldman's latest morning note especially interesting. A few 
hours before the board acted, Goldman's head of EMEA Delta One trading Rich Privorotsky was 
marveling at how well the rest of the market had absorbed Nvidia's slow-motion governance 
crisis. With Treasuries closed for Veterans Day, Goldman's Retail Favorites basket 
(GSXURFAV) jumped 495bps, its 3rd best day in three years, while just three stocks priced 
under $4 accounted for 32% of all US tape volume. 
 

 
Here is how Privo opens his note ("GS Basics - Retail Favorites. Vol Curve Un-Inverts. 
European Consumer", available to pro subs): 
"Quite impressive how much we've managed to rally in the face of the macro backdrop and 
increasingly idiosyncratic problems at Nvidia. Yesterday was emphatically not a broad based 
move." 
Impressive, yes. Durable? That was the question even before Huang walked out, and as we 
discuss below, Privo's answer is a qualified "probably not", at least not until oil is fixed and rates 
come down... two things that may well be the same thing. 
"Emphatically Not A Broad Based Move" 
Just how narrow? Semis did the heavy lifting, with the SOX +5.5%, and ARM and MU each 
+12%, "while MSFT, META and AMZN all finished lower," Privo writes. The S&P rose 1.3% to 
7,619 and the Nasdaq 100 gained 2.3%, while the Russell (+0.9%) and the Dow (+0.5%) mostly 
watched. 
 
It is the Retail Favorites line that stands out, and Privo calls it "the standout flow yesterday." 
Actually, the sub-$4 detail may be generous: Goldman's mid-day desk color, which we flagged 
in real time, put the three names at all under 4 cents. Either way, nothing says "durable new bull 
market" quite like a third of the day's volume trading below the price of a gumball: 
(The basket faded into the close from +6.46% intraday to +4.95%, which still made it the 3rd 
best day in three years.) 
 

 
As we put it in yesterday's wrap, "With Bond Vigilantes Away, Retail Will Play": no macro data, 
no FedSpeak and no bond market to impose discipline made for a vol-crushing, short-squeezing 
rip, with hyperscalers serving as the source of funds. Goldman's Callahan, quoted in the bank's 
US Equities Color, marveled that "the NDX is back above pre-Election levelsand the SOX is 
knocking back on the door of its own 200-dma." 
"Forcing A Significant Rebalancing Of Capital" 
Writing before the resignation, Privo noted that "the resilience of the broader tech complex is 
remarkable given how much market cap is concentrated in Nvidia," adding that "the governance 
issues are clearly forcing a significant rebalancing of capital." 
In hindsight, that was the understatement of the week. By Wednesday's close the WSJ was 
reporting that the board was preparing to oust Huang, Semafor reported that some directors 
favored Kress as interim CEO (they got their wish), and Polymarket's "Jenxit" odds had jumped 
to, appropriately enough, 69%. NVDA slid roughly 9% after hours, and as we noted overnight, 
the stock had already wiped out a third of its value in a month: 
The more interesting point for Privo is what didn't happen: "Breadth remains narrow, but the fact 
we can absorb this much Nvidia specific pressure without a broader capitulation is noteworthy." 
Put differently, money has been rotating within AI (semis ex-NVDA, memory, ARM) without 
leaving the theme, or as we put it this morning, rivals feast. 
Thursday is the real test of that thesis. Even before the board's announcement, Nasdaq futures 
were down 1.5% and S&P futures 0.7% as money rotated out of tech, and Wedbush's Dan Ives, 
among the loudest Nvidia bulls, says the company is worth 25% less without Huang. 
Meanwhile, Kress now has to run Nvidia, run its finances and front next Tuesday's (Nov 17) 
earnings call, where the market will want answers on governance as much as on margins. 
That's a lot of hats for one interim CEO. 
Vol Has Reset, Nets Have Been Flushed 
Part of the explanation is mechanical. According to Privo, the vol complex "has largely reset": 
the SPX vol curve un-inverted, dealer skew flattened, and systematic L/S managers posted their 
best two-day return in nearly three years (+2.15%). 
Goldman's Prime desk puts numbers on the quant rebound: systematic L/S funds rose 1.25% 
on Wednesday, a +2.6 standard deviation move with roughly three-quarters of funds up, "driven 
by renormalization of momentum (medium and short horizons) and recovery in crowded trades." 
 

 
Translation: the stuff everyone was short got squeezed, and the stuff everyone was long 
stopped falling. 
 
The derivatives desk was even blunter. Shayna Peart described "a sharp shift from last week's 
panic to this week's complacency," with the VIX down 992bps to 17.53 and Thursday's straddle 
priced at just 57bps. With "nearly the entire post-election panic retraced, investors are beginning 
to reach for upside," buying December SPX call spreads toward peak dealer gamma near 
7,800. One week. Panic to complacency. Very healthy. 
Regular readers know this is precisely the setup Privo flagged on Monday, when he picked his 
AI bear case as hedge fund nets hit 5-year lows, and again yesterday, when he argued that 
hedged-to-the-gills funds are propping up stocks. Today he frames the open question: 
"Feels like we've reached a point of relatively lower risk, considerable confusion around AI and 
persistent concern about the political/geo-political backdrop. Yet the market keeps finding a bid. 
The question is whether this is simply the mechanical consequence of lower positioning and 
short covering, or the beginning of something more durable." 
Callahan sounds similarly agnostic, noting that "for the first time in a while... there is relatively 
LITTLE CONSENSUS in the market right now," with debate over "if this is a bear market bounce 
or a bull market forming (YE rally?!)". When the Goldman floor itself can't decide, maybe nobody 
knows. 
 

 
Two Weeks, A Third Carrier And 10% Ceasefire Odds 
Geopolitics offers little help. On Iran, Privo sees "conflicting headlines everywhere": reports of 
an "extremely contentious" phone call between Netanyahu and Trump over renewed strikes 
(disputed by Netanyahu's office), suggestions that Vance is exploring direct talks Tehran has so 
far resisted, and Iranian officials claiming Washington has signaled roughly a two-week window 
before deciding on further military action. Meanwhile, a third US carrier group is heading for the 
region, possibly arriving mid-month (although it may simply relieve an existing deployment). 
"Diplomacy and military preparation are proceeding simultaneously. Hard to have conviction 
about which direction this goes." 
The classic two-week window: long enough to rattle oil, short enough to be extended. We'll see. 
On Russia/Ukraine, Polymarket odds of a ceasefire by December have fallen toward 10%, with 
March only around 25%. The Abu Dhabi talks produced agreement in principle on a large 
prisoner exchange, "but little progress on the fundamental territorial and security questions." 
Privo's verdict: the market "can reasonably hope for progress at some point in 2027, but 
increasingly difficult to argue for anything imminent over the next two months." 
AI: Rent The Rallies, Don't Marry Them 
On AI hardware and infrastructure, Privo keeps separating "fundamental compute demand from 
upstream/downstream valuation multiples." Hyperscalers "remain best positioned to navigate 
tight supply given balance sheet capacity and contracted infrastructure," while downstream the 
market "continues to question multiples." His four tactical overhangsremain: i) a higher cost of 
capital, ii) power/permitting delivery constraints, iii) the supply/demand balance entering 2028 
and iv) monetization velocity relative to capex. 
Near-term permitting and logistics worries have eased, he says, but "the real worries are over 
the LT supply absorption and ROI realization." Hence his trading playbook: 
"Expect the AI infrastructure trade to generate episodic, high beta trading rallies rather than pure 
buy + hold,...strong micro results coexisting with multiple compression." 
Having pounded the table on the cost of capital behind the AI buildout for much of the past year, 
we'd only add that "higher cost of capital" is doing a lot of work on that list, with Goldman's own 
economists now expecting a third rate hike in March 2027. 
 

 
Meanwhile, Europe's Consumer Just Rolled Over 
In a side note, Privo highlights Goldman's new euro area outlook ("Euro Area 2027 Consumer 
Outlook - Winter Bills, Delayed Chills", also available to pro subs), and the chart he picked says 
it all: every measure of real retail sales has plunged to roughly -0.5% on a 3m/3m basis, with 
sales ex-fuels and food lower still (chart source GS GIR). 
 
Goldman's economists now see real consumption growth stalling at around 0% qoq over the 
winter and staying below trend for most of 2027, with only 0.1% qoq growth in H1 before a 
pickup to 0.2% in H2 as inflation moderates and confidence rebounds. Winter bills first, 
recovery... eventually. 
"The Market Needs Oil Fixed And Rates Lower" 
Which brings us to Privo's bottom line on risk. He sees "plenty of reasons to be constructive," 
but a durable breakout "probably requires lower rates, lower energy and broader leadership." 
 

 
The logical candidates outside the AI infrastructure complex are hyperscalers, transportation, 
cyclicals and aerospace, with defense also looking washed out. The problem: 
"It's difficult for many of these groups to work sustainably against an aggressive hiking cycle and 
stubbornly high oil prices. Ultimately, the market needs oil fixed and rates lower… and those two 
things may well be connected. Absent that, I suspect we chop rather than break out. That said, 
positioning has been reset materially, volatility remains elevated and the market has already 
absorbed an extraordinary amount of bad news. Low positioning and high vol are powerful short 
term tailwinds." 
We agree with the first half and are skeptical of the second. A short-covering rally with a 57bp 
straddle, penny stocks running a third of the tape, and the most important company in the AI 
trade losing its founder-CEO overnight is about as clean a "bear market rally" fingerprint as they 
come. And credit wasn't buying it even before Thursday: Goldman's own credit monitor shows 
USD CCC spreads at 1,098bps, up 270bps in three months and a whisker from their 3-year max 
of 1,127, while the VIX sat at 17.5. 
Yes, low positioning means fewer forced sellers. But the "absorb Nvidia without capitulation" 
thesis was built on Nvidia's troubles staying a governance sideshow, and as of this morning the 
AI trade's bellwether is run by its CFO, without a permanent CEO, heading into earnings. With 
the Fed hiking into an oil shock and Iran on a two-week clock, "chop" may well turn out to be the 
optimistic scenario.
