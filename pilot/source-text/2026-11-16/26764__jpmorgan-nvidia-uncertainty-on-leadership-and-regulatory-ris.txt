Style Exposure
Late last night, news broke that NVDA’s CEO Jensen Huang allegedly had a 
personal relationship with an executive at NVIDIA (see NYT article here). 
Unfortunately, this marks the second such disclosure just this week. The initial set 
of news broke on Sunday evening, when Jensen internally emailed employees to 
acknowledge that he had a personal relationship outside of his marriage. Various 
news outlets identified the relationship as being with Alice Huang (a former 
executive at Megaspeed), whom U.S. officials concluded was a Chinese 
intelligence asset (according to the Financial Times). The relationship with Alice 
Huang has obviously drawn scrutiny on several fronts, given her alleged ties to 
China and the importance of semiconductors as a national security concern, but the 
new, alleged relationship with an NVIDIA executive opens up an entirely new set 
of consequences, adding to the existing independent investigation by the Board of 
Directors and the external probes by the U.S. government. NVDA’s code of 
conduct explicitly prohibits employees from engaging in activities that may create 
a conflict of interest (e.g., engaging in personal relationships), and so the 
relationship with the NVIDIA employee could expose Jensen to serious 
ramifications, and potentially a removal from his position as CEO (see WSJ report 
here). That being said, NVDA’s chip, system, and software product roadmaps have 
already been laid out for the next ~3 years with the Vera Rubin / Rubin Ultra / 
Feynman ramps and the CUDA software stack. BUT who will lead the company 
through that roadmap is now an open question, and for a company that is so closely 
tied to its founder, a potential dismissal brings real near-term uncertainty to shares 
(which may help explain why NVDA shares fell ~9% AMC last night). Should 
Jensen be removed from his CEO role, we think the most important question 
for the stock in the near term will be whether NVDA’s board can successfully 
put together an interim executive team that can maintain the company’s 
strong track record of execution and continue delivering on the existing 
roadmaps. However, beyond the next 2-3 years, we think the main focus will 
be whether the executive team has the vision / intuition to recognize the next 
inflection in accelerated computing, and build not just the silicon, but also the 
ecosystem and platform around it. All told, given the national security concerns, 
we think the risk of more restrictive guidelines on shipments directly or indirectly 
into China has risen, and we are reflecting that in our numbers. We are specifically 
removing the company’s reported “China + Other” shipments (~8% of F1H27 
revenues) from our FY28 revenue estimates. This drives a ~10% EPS cut to our 
FY28 estimates and applying a ~15x multiple to our new FY28 EPS estimates 
results in a new $215 Dec 2027 PT.
•
We expect the team to maintain its leadership in AI as the silicon / software 
/ platform roadmaps have been defined for the next 2-3 years… however, 
history has shown that one execution misstep often leads to more... 
Maintaining flawless execution to these roadmaps will be critical, but does 
the NVDA team have the internal management capabilities to maintain 
the strong execution near-to-mid-term? If we assume that NVDA’s CEO

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
NVIDIA Corporation (Overweight; Price Target: $215.00)
Investment Thesis
NVIDIA's vertically integrated platform (spanning multiple chips, rack systems, and the 
software stack that ties them together) is difficult to replicate and represents a competitive 
advantage relative to AI compute peers.  Multi-year demand visibility remains exceptionally 
strong and is explicitly framed as a floor, excluding several incremental revenue streams 
while being layered on top of a structural, underappreciated shift of traditional enterprise 
workloads to accelerated compute that is independent of the AI training/inference cycle. 
We see constructive demand fundamentals coupled with an inflecting capital return 
supporting material upside to the stock. 
Valuation
Our $215 Dec 2027 PT assumes NVDA trades at ~15x our new FY28 (CY27) EPS estimate 
of $14.45 (in line with the co’s average P/E multiple over the past 3-4 months).
Risks to Rating and Price Target
•
TAM share capture by alternative compute platforms (AI ASICs/XPUs primarily, but 
also merchant competitors (AMD) and specialized accelerators) has been an ongoing 
narrative headwind, and one that is challenging to dispel entirely given the significant 
program ramps planned over the next several years; we expect GPU and ASIC/XPU 
share of the AI compute TAM to trend toward parity in coming years.
•
Memory cost inflation and supply constraints have pressured platform specifications 
and raise questions over the longer-term durability of gross margins, introducing 
uncertainty around both top-line mix and profitability as the company scales back 
memory capacity to meet compute volume commitments.
