 
Goldman Now Expects A Third Rate Hike In March 
2027, Followed By Rate Cuts Starting In December 
2027 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, NOV 11, 2026 - 03:55 PM 
Following yesterday's surprisingly hot (for a 3rd consecutive month) CPI print, Goldman 
economist David Mericle published his take on the October CPI surprise - "the​
upside surprise was driven by firmer-than-expected readings in a broad set of categories" - and 
updated his Fed outlook, adding a third 25bp hike in March 2027, pushing the peak funds rate to 
4.25–4.50% and delaying the start of cuts to December 2027.  
Here are the highlights from this revised view:  
●​ Yesterday’s core CPI surprised meaningfully to the upside at +0.30%, marking a third 
straight month of worse inflation news. The strength was fairly broad-based and 
appeared to go beyond just the direct pass-through from higher oil prices. The bank 
estimates the report implies a 35bp increase in core PCE prices for October. 
●​ Governor Waller, in a speech prior to CPI release, said that while his baseline was a 25 
bp rate hike in December, a 50bp December hike should be considered if core CPI came 
in around 0.3% or higher, arguing that moving just 25bp could put Fed credibility at risk. 
That said, Goldman still thinks most FOMC voters will be reluctant to jump to a 50bp 
hike, but suspect the recent inflation prints will push most participants to signal a third 
hike in the December dot plot. 
●​ David notes that a third hike could come in January, but March looks more likely as 
Goldman expects softer inflation in November/December before the “January effect” 
pushes inflation firmer again. Importantly, oil prices have fallen substantially since the 
elections, so the bulk of the energy pass-through should now be behind us and we still 
expect inflation to be meaningfully lower by end-2027. 
●​ Further out, Goldman's view on the eventual 3.25–3.50% terminal rate is unchanged, but 
the hotter inflation path pushes the timing of cuts back one quarter. The bank now 
 

 
expects the first cut in December 2027 rather than September 2027, followed by further 
cuts in March, June and September 2028. 
 
And some more details on why the forecast may be wrong: 
The recent inflation news has not been entirely one-sided. Oil futures have fallen substantially 
since the elections, and our analysis suggests that if prices follow either the futures or our 
commodity strategists’ similar forecast, then the bulk of the passthrough from the energy shock 
to consumer prices should be behind us already. More broadly, we remain confident that 
inflation will be meaningfully lower by the end of 2027 as the effects of tariffs, the energy shock, 
and AI price pressures diminish. 
It is possible that by the time of the March meeting or even the January meeting, the energy 
shock will feel largely over. But we suspect that the last two inflation prints will lead most FOMC 
participants to signal a third hike in the December dot plot—this would only require two 
additional participants to raise their forecasts—and that firmer January inflation data would 
make delivering it the path of least resistance even if the energy shock appears over. 
We have left our forecast for the eventual terminal fed funds rate unchanged at 3.25-3.5%, but 
we now expect rate cuts to begin one quarter later in December 2027 (vs. September 2027 
previously), followed by additional cuts in March, June, and September 2028.
