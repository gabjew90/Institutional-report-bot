As we noted yesterday, mortgage rates remain near three-year highs and are weighing on housing demand. Case in point: October’s 
existing home sales fell 5.5% MoM to 3.75 million, the lowest since August 2010. Weakness was broad-based, with sales declining across 
most regions. Despite inventory rising to 5.1 months’ supply—up from 4.4 a year ago—prices remain near record levels, ticking up 0.4% 
YoY. There is no panacea for housing affordability, and we expect housing market activity to remain subdued.
Friday, November 13, 2026
Friday the 13th is back for the third time this year, and history suggests that there is little reason for concern. Looking at daily S&P 500 
returns dating back to 1928, Friday the 13th has been the strongest day relative to other days of the week (including Fridays generally), 
posting average gains of +0.09% and positive 58% of the time (vs. +0.02% and 52% all other days). Of course, correlation is not the same 
as causation—this is just fun historical trivia. As always, we recommend staying invested rather than trying to time the market.

Following Tuesday’s hotter than expected CPI report, attention turns to the PPI release for further insight into inflationary pressures. 
The consensus estimate is for headline PPI to accelerate to +6.6% YoY, driven largely by fuel prices, with core PPI—which the Fed tends 
to focus on—remaining at +5.0%. A higher-than-expected core figure would reinforce concerns that cost pressures are spreading 
beyond energy. Taken together, we expect this week’s inflation reports to strengthen the case for a 25 bp Fed rate hike on December 9.

Cleveland Fed President Hammack, who dissented in favor of a 25 bp hike at last month’s FOMC meeting, poured cold water on the idea 
of a 50 bp hike. Recall, this idea was raised earlier in the week by Gov. Waller, a fellow hawk. Hammack wants policy in restrictive 
territory, citing broadening inflation pressures, but she doesn't see the Fed as behind the curve and prefers smaller moves that would 
be less disruptive to the economy. We agree and—as noted above—expect a 25 bp hike in December, followed by an extended pause.
Shrugging off an abrupt leadership change at the world’s largest chipmaker, the PHLX Semiconductor Index was down only 0.3% 
yesterday—and excluding NVIDIA, semiconductor stocks actually gained 2.3% and are up 19% WTD. Setting aside NVIDIA’s company-
specific uncertainty, the big picture is that the AI infrastructure buildout continues to propel demand for chips, with a high degree of 
visibility into 2027. Consensus is calling for the industry to post 67% EPS growth in 2027, keeping us positive on the Tech sector overall.
