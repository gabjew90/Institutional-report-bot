Market: Quite impressive how much we’ve managed to rally in the face of the macro backdrop and
increasingly idiosyncratic problems at Nvidia. Yesterday was emphatically not a broad based move. Semis
did the heavy lifting, with the SOX +5.5%, ARM +12%, MU +12%...while MSFT, META and AMZN all
finished lower. The resilience of the broader tech complex is remarkable given how much market cap is
concentrated in Nvidia. The governance issues are clearly forcing a significant rebalancing of capital. The
standout flow yesterday. Retail Favorites (GSXURFAV) +495bps, its 3rd best day in three years, (three
sub $4 stocks accounting for 32% of US tape volume US Equities Mid-Day Color). Overnight NVDA
dropping roughly 9%, weighing on Asia tech tape.
Vol/Positioning: Vol complex has largely reset. SPX vol curve un-inverted, dealer skew flattened and
systematic L/S managers posted their best two day return in nearly three years (+2.15%). Feels like we’ve
reached a point of relatively lower risk, considerable confusion around AI and persistent concern about the
political/geo-political backdrop. Yet the market keeps finding a bid. The question is whether this is simply
the mechanical consequence of lower positioning and short covering, or the beginning of something more
durable. Breadth remains narrow, but the fact we can absorb this much Nvidia specific pressure without a
broader capitulation is noteworthy.
Iran: Geopolitical concerns continue to linger. Conflicting headlines everywhere… reports of an extremely
contentious phone call between Netanyahu and Trump over renewed strikes, disputed by Netanyahu’s
office, alongside suggestions Vance is exploring direct talks with Iranian officials, which Tehran has so far
resisted. Iranian officials suggest Washington has indicated roughly a 2w window before deciding on
further military action. Meanwhile, the third US carrier group is heading toward the region, potentially
arriving around mid month, although it may ultimately relieve an existing deployment. Diplomacy and
military preparation are proceeding simultaneously. Hard to have conviction about which direction this
goes.
Russia/Ukraine: Polymarket pricing for a ceasefire by December has fallen toward 10%, with March only
around 25% on the comparable contract. Talks in Abu Dhabi produced agreement in principle on a large
prisoner exchange but little progress on the fundamental territorial and security questions. Plenty of
rhetoric, but cementing a lasting resolution looks elusive. The market can reasonably hope for progress at
some point in 2027, but increasingly difficult to argue for anything imminent over the next two months.
AI Hardware & Infrastructure: We continue to separate fundamental compute demand from
upstream/downstream valuation multiples. Hyperscalers remain best positioned to navigate tight supply
given balance sheet capacity and contracted infrastructure. Downstream, the market continues to question
multiples. The four tactical overhangs remain: (1) higher cost of capital, (2) power/permitting delivery

constraints, (3) supply/demand balance entering 2028, and (4) ultimate monetization velocity relative to
capex. While near term permitting and logistical concerns have eased, the real worries are over the LT
supply absorption and ROI realization. Expect the AI infrastructure trade to generate episodic, high beta
trading rallies rather than pure buy + hold,...strong micro results coexisting with multiple compression.
Risk: Time will tell whether this was anything more than a bear market rally. There are plenty of reasons
to be constructive, but a durable breakout probably requires lower rates, lower energy and broader
leadership. The logical candidates outside the immediate AI infrastructure complex are hyperscalers,
transportation, cyclicals and aerospace. Defense also looks washed out. But it’s difficult for many of these
groups to work sustainably against an aggressive hiking cycle and stubbornly high oil prices. Ultimately,
the market needs oil fixed and rates lower… and those two things may well be connected. Absent that, I
suspect we chop rather than break out. That said, positioning has been reset materially, volatility remains
elevated and the market has already absorbed an extraordinary amount of bad news. Low positioning and
high vol are powerful short term tailwinds.
