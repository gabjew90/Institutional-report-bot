 
"The Biggest Opening Bid We Have Ever Recorded 
In NVDA": Retail Greets Jensen's Exit By Buying 
41x More Nvidia Than Normal 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, NOV 12, 2026 - 09:25 PM 
Wall Street spent the night working out what Nvidia is worth without Jensen Huang. Retail 
needed about 30 minutes. 
Just hours after Nvidia announced that its co-founder and only CEO in 33 years had resigned, 
effective immediately, and with 14 sellside desks busy cutting ratings and price targets, the 
stock opened down about 12% before clawing back roughly half of that by mid-morning. 
Somebody was clearly buying, and according to the retail flow gurus at Vanda Research, we 
now know who. 
In a note published this morning, Vanda's Viraj Patel writes that mom and pop met the 
resignation with "the biggest opening bid we have ever recorded in NVDA": $111 million of net 
buying in the first half hour of trading, or 41 times a normal open! 
Regular readers will feel some deja vu. Back in February, when Nvidia reversed from up 5% to 
down 5% in a single session, we ran a post titled "Biggest Retail Dip Buying In NVDA... Ever!". 
Well, that record lasted less than nine months, and all it took to break it was a spy scandal, two 
affairs, an FBI investigation and the departure of the most important CEO in the market. 
$111 Million In 30 Minutes 
Start with the open itself. Vanda's intraday tape shows retail buying Nvidia in size every single 
day since the scandal went nuclear last weekend, but nothing like the purple spike at this 
morning's bell, which came just as the stock gapped down to around $159, the lowest price 
since the summer of 2025. 
 

 
 
How unusual is that? Vanda has 2,480 sessions of opening data going back to January 2017. 
The median Nvidia open sees $2.7 million of net retail buying. The 99th percentile is $49 million. 
Today's $111 million is more than double the 99th percentile, and it edges out the $107 million 
from Feb 26 (the day of that ZH headline) and the $102 million after last August's earnings. 
Tuesday's open, when retail bought $93 million in half an hour, now ranks fourth. So two of the 
four biggest retail opens ever recorded in Nvidia have come in the past three sessions. 
 

 
 
One detail in the fine print deserves a second look: that $111 million of net buying came on $799 
million of gross retail turnover. Napkin math says retail bought roughly $455 million of Nvidia in 
30 minutes and sold roughly $345 million. Put differently, for every $4 that mom and pop bought 
at the open, they also sold $3. A record bid, yes, but probably not the unanimous vote of 
confidence the headline number suggests. 
If History Holds, A $450-770 Million Day 
The open is only the appetizer. Patel's more interesting point is what opens of this size have 
historically turned into by 4pm: 
This is a day when you want to keep intraday retail flows of NVDA open. Opens this size have 
historically converted into full days of 4-7x, pointing to retail buying anywhere between 
$450-770mn today. That would be well within the top 10 days of retail net buying we’ve seen in 
any stock or ETF in our data since 2014. 
Translation: if the usual pattern holds, retail will have bought somewhere between $450 million 
and $770 million of Nvidia by tonight, which would put Nov 12 among the ten biggest days of 
retail net buying in any US stock or ETF since Vanda's data begins in 2014. 
 

 
For reference, the day retail investors bought the DeepSeek crash in January 2025, when 
Nvidia lost 17% and nearly $600 billion of market cap in one session, Vanda counted $562 
million of net buying, a record at the time. The top half of today's range would blow through that. 
 
61 Cents Of Every Retail Dollar 
None of this came out of nowhere. Vanda notes that Nvidia was the most bought security in the 
entire US market on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, and by a mile: retail bought 9.7x, 14.4x 
and 3.7x as much of it as the next name on the list. Tuesday was the second-biggest day of 
retail Nvidia buying on record. 
Add it up and the three-day total through Wednesday's close is $1.31 billion, the sixth-highest 
reading in 3,735 sessions going back to 2012. More striking is the concentration: Nvidia has 
absorbed 61% of all retail net buying across every US stock and ETF this week. 
 

 
 
More napkin math: if $1.31 billion is 61% of the total, then retail's net buying of everything else 
in the US market over three days was maybe $840 million. That's quite a turnaround for a cohort 
that, as we noted in September, was seeing its buying momentum roll over, and that only two 
weeks ago was "buying the dip, but hedging the risk" with a fifth of its leveraged ETF flows 
going into bearish products. 
And yet the stock is down about 15% in five days. $1.3 billion sounds like a lot until you set it 
against a company that was worth more than $4 trillion on Friday: it's about 0.03% of the market 
cap. Retail can set records all week. It can't set the price. 
Naturally, There's Leverage 
It wouldn't be a retail stampede without a 2x wrapper. Vanda finds that the dip-buying has spilled 
into leveraged Nvidia products, with retail putting $42.5 million into the six long 2x NVDA ETFs 
from Monday to Wednesday. That is the fifth-largest three-day inflow on record and a 99.8th 
percentile reading. The only comparable burst of leveraged long demand came around, you 
guessed it, DeepSeek. 
 

 
 
This fits with what Goldman flagged at the start of the week, when the bank counted record 
inflows into leveraged ETFs during the worst week for the S&P since April 2025, with NVDL 
already among the beneficiaries: 
What about the other side? One might think that a founder-CEO resigning over an affair with a 
suspected Chinese spy would tempt at least a few retail traders to bet on more downside. 
Apparently not. Patel's read on the inverse funds: 
In contrast, inverse NVDA funds saw no net buying – albeit very strong turnover – which tells us 
this is very much a one-way flow story for retail. 
In other words, lots of trading in the inverse products, no net buying of them. The hedging 
instinct from late October is gone. Retail is long, levered long, and has stopped paying for 
protection. 
So Who Is On The Other Side Of The Trade? 
The same people as in February, probably. Our post that day carried the subtitle "It's Not Retail 
That's Selling", and the same is true this week: hundreds of billions in market cap don't vanish 
 

 
because of mom and pop, and with three downgrades and five target cuts overnight, institutions 
have had no shortage of excuses to hit the bid that retail is so generously providing. 
To be fair to the dip-buyers, there is a case. Nvidia now trades at about 12x forward earnings, 
the lowest since November 2012 according to Reuters, the stock hasn't been this oversold since 
2019, and those who bought the DeepSeek Monday were eventually paid handsomely. It was, 
after all, retail that drove Nvidia to become the world's first $4 trillion company in July 2025. 
But "eventually" is doing a lot of work there. The DeepSeek buyers first had to sit through 
another 20% drop into the April 2025 lows. And one month after February's "biggest dip buying 
ever", we were writing that "Buy The Dip" was dead as the same retail army sold the bounce, 
Nvidia included. Record retail buying has marked plenty of days. It has rarely marked thelow. 
Which brings us to what this particular dip has that DeepSeek didn't: the news keeps coming. 
While retail was busy setting records this morning, Semafor reported that multiple Justice 
Department probes threaten to sweep up Nvidia, including one on antitrust grounds aimed at 
the company itself. 
Add Huang's possible testimony before Congress, a permanent CEO search with no candidate, 
and third-quarter earnings on Tuesday delivered by an interim chief who is also the CFO, and 
there are at least three more chances in the next week for the stock to test just how deep retail's 
pockets are. 
Maybe $160 is the floor and this goes down as the dip-buy of the decade. More likely, in our 
view, the smart money is using the biggest retail bid in Nvidia's history as exit liquidity, and will 
keep doing so until someone other than mom and pop steps up. Vanda is right that today is a 
day to keep the intraday flow screen open. We'd watch the afternoon most closely: this fall, the 
afternoon is when retail's morning enthusiasm has tended to turn into selling. 
We'll check back after Tuesday's earnings to see how much of the $1.3 billion is still there.
