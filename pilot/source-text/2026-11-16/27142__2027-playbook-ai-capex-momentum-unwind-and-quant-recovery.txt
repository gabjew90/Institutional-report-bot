What we are watching this morning
2027 is arriving early. Goldman Sachs has already unleashed some 300 pages of outlooks for the year
ahead. We did the reading so you don't have to, picking out the five charts that matter most.
Throw in JPMorgan's latest numbers on the $1.4 trillion AI spending machine, a momentum unwind,
and the quants' biggest two-day comeback in almost three years, and you've got your morning chart
fix.
$1.4 trillion
Goldman Sachs estimates that annualized US AI investment will reach an astonishing $1.4 trillion by
the end of 2027. What began as a semiconductor spending boom is rapidly becoming an economy-
wide capital expenditure cycle. The question is no longer whether AI spending is enormous, but how
much larger it can get.
GIR
That AI contribution
AI has accounted for just 21% of US GDP growth, but 62% of earnings growth and a staggering 92% of
market value growth since ChatGPT launched. In other words, AI has already conquered Wall Street.
Main Street? Not quite yet.
Goldman
JPM puts a number on it
And here is why the spending boom matters so much for earnings. JPMorgan estimates that every
additional $100 billion in AI capex translates into approximately $9 of S&P 500 EPS, or 2.5% earnings
growth, in 2027. At the midpoint of its spending assumptions, that could mean roughly $18 of upside to
consensus EPS, adding 4–5 percentage points to earnings growth. The AI capex machine isn't just a
technology story anymore. It's an index earnings story.

Corporate America never had it this good
S&P 500 earnings now represent a record share of US GDP. Whatever one thinks about the
sustainability of the current profit boom, corporate America has rarely captured this much of the
economic pie. The obvious question for 2027: how much more can the profit share expand?
FactSet
Expensive bubble?
For all the endless bubble talk, forward P/E and PEG ratios are hovering around their 35-year historical
averages. That doesn't make equities cheap, particularly with today's extreme concentration, but it
does make the blanket "everything is a bubble" narrative a little harder to defend.
Datastream
Momentum exposure
So much for the fundamental outlook. Back in the trading trenches, things are considerably messier.
Goldman Sachs Prime Brokerage reports that momentum exposure in its prime book has collapsed to
the 23rd percentile of the past five years. From the crowded highs earlier this year, hedge funds have
dramatically reduced their exposure to one of the market's most important factors.
