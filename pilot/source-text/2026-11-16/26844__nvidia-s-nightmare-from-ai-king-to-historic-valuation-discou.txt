The succession shock
Nvidia's board is reportedly preparing to replace Jensen Huang, putting management depth firmly in
focus. Huang has been central to Nvidia and the broader AI narrative, but UBS highlights the
company's deep management bench. The leadership uncertainty adds another layer of risk alongside
China exposure and Washington's demands. The earnings story remains intact for now, but the
leadership risk has increased dramatically.

Here we are
NVDA is getting crushed again, down another 7% in early Frankfurt trading. The stock is now
approaching major support levels and its long-term trendline. We are trading almost 20% below the
200-day MA, an extreme dislocation. The last comparable undershoot came during the Liberation Day
chaos, when NVDA traded roughly 30% below its 200-day MA. The technical damage is accelerating,
but so is the proximity to critical support.

Nvidia at just 11x earnings
Goldman sees NVDA trading at just 11x its CY27 EPS estimate of $16.70, maintaining a Buy rating and
$300 price target. Despite the recent carnage, the bank expects fundamentals to outpace consensus
and sees attractive risk/reward heading into earnings. The stock has collapsed, but the earnings
machine remains intact.

Cheaper than the market
The relative derating is even more extraordinary. Ahead of the latest selloff, NVDA had fallen to its
cheapest valuation versus the S&P 500 in almost a quarter of a century, long before AI was even on
investors' radar. The ultimate AI growth stock is now trading at a historic discount to the broader
market.

The earnings bar
Goldman expects another strong quarter, with both Q3 and Q4 EPS estimates running 6% above
consensus. Q3 revenue is projected at $110 billion, rising to nearly $125 billion in Q4, with better-than-
expected margins driving the upside. The setup looks favorable following the positioning washout, but
expectations remain demanding. Another earnings beat is Goldman's base case. Whether that is
enough to repair sentiment is another question.

The $700 billion question
The Nvidia debate is shifting beyond near-term demand. Goldman forecasts FY2028 revenue of $718
billion, but investors are increasingly focused on whether power availability and datacenter capacity
can support the expansion. The bank sees limited execution risk in 2027, with 2028 visibility becoming
the bigger uncertainty. Customer funding and AI monetization add another layer of complexity.
Demand is one thing. Building and financing the infrastructure to deliver it is another.

GS
Bigger than Nvidia?
The Huang scandal raises a question that goes well beyond Nvidia. Is Jensen Huang simply the key
man at Nvidia, or has he become the key man for the entire AI industry? So far, the broader market
has held up relatively well despite Nvidia's troubles. For now, investors are treating this as an Nvidia
problem, not an AI problem. That distinction could prove critical.
LSEG Workspace/Authers
Nvidia is losing China
China's domestic chip substitution is accelerating. Nvidia's share of Chinese data-center AI accelerator
shipments fell to 34% in H1 2026, with Huawei closing the gap at 28%. TS Lombard argues that US
export restrictions are accelerating the development of China's domestic AI ecosystem. The
unintended consequence of keeping China out of Nvidia's technology may be creating Nvidia's next
major competitor.
