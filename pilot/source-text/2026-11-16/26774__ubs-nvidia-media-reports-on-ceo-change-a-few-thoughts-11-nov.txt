Media Reports on CEO Change; A Few Thoughts 
Summary
Media reports (here) suggest that NVDA's Board of Directors is now preparing to oust 
Jensen Huang as CEO and working with an outside firm to determine candidates as  
successors. While the company would likely  find a strong candidate, Jensen has 
obviously been a singular force in many ways so the impact will likely be felt far and 
wide. We would especially highlight three key areas: 
1. Financing - the company has become a key source of funds for a host of AI 
industry participants including neo-clouds, LLM developers, ecosystem partners, 
and suppliers, and we have always seen Jensen as a key driving force for this effort. 
Even if a new leader ultimately maintains a similar approach, there is likely to be 
some "pause" in the company's willingness and ability to continue pursuing these 
deals in the near term. We estimate NVDA is generating ~$50-60B/Q in FCF - a big 
portion of which has been put back into the system, so  this could easily take ~
$50B or more out of the system - at least temporarily - at a potentially key time 
when interest rates are rising and certain segments of the credit market have 
become less liquid.
2. Supply chain - the company has often been far ahead of peers in terms of long-
term supply agreements in a host of key areas including memory, substrates, and 
other areas. While this will obviously remain a key element of the company's 
strategy, we always viewed Jensen  as somewhat singular in this regard and out in 
front of some key peers from a procurement perspective.
3. Potential for new, tighter restrictions - we could see the US government 
enforcing even tighter restrictions that could impact NVDA's (and other companies 
like AMD's) ability to ship product into China but even more importantly into other 
regions like Singapore or Malaysia. NVDA changed its geographic revenue 
disclosures in mid-C2025, but Singapore was at that time nearly 20% of revenue. 
If we assume a portion of this, together with some additional portion of its China 
revenue (which is now quite small), could be at risk, we estimate this could be in 
the range of ~10% of revenue.  
Obviously there are other issues to consider - including the longer-term product 
roadmap - but for any company, this goes beyond the CEO and any impact would take 
years to manifest itself. 
Valuation: PT $300 (unchanged)
Our PT remains unchanged at $300, based on a ~14x C2028E P/E multiple (unchanged) 
applied to C2028E EPS of $22.08 (unchanged).

Company Description
NVIDIA Corp. designs and manufactures graphics processing units, add-in boards, and 
related low-level software. Its Graphics Processing Unit segment includes GeForce for high-
end video games and professional graphics workstations; Quadro for designers; DGX and 
Tesla Accelerator cards for data center and AI scientists; and GRID for cloud-based visual 
computing users. The Tegra Processors segment provides processors that enable embedded 
computer vision applications in automotive video surveillance end markets, as well as 
delivering multimedia experiences on mobile gaming platforms, while consuming minimal 
power.
Valuation Method and Risk Statement
NVDA: Our price target is based on applying a P/E multiple. NVIDIA company-specific risks 
include: 1) competition from AMD in graphics processors (GPUs) and professional 
visualization products, which historically have driven the bulk of the company’s gross profit, 
2) intense competition in ARM-based applications processors, where rivals seek to leapfrog 
NVIDIA's early lead in dual-core technology, 3) emerging competition from Intel with its 
many-integrated core (MIC) processor family vs. the company's professional GPU computing 
products, where Intel's approach could be more flexible and easier to develop software. In 
addition, NVIDIA faces the semiconductor sector risk where revenue trends have historically 
been linked to corporate profitability, which could be challenged for robust growth, as the 
economic recovery has been more subdued vs. prior deep downturns.
