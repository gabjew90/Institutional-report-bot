What we are watching 
Today’s PPI release will help fine tune PCE expectations, but this week’s CPI print 
already reset expectations materially higher. Our economists are tracking 0.32% mom 
for core PCE (see US Watch 10 Nov 26). Next week’s FOMC Minutes will be closely 
scrutinized for the reasoning behind the October pause, in particular in light of this 
week’s inflation data and discussions around a more hawkish hiking path from different 
FOMC members (see below). We reiterate a short front end bias and maintain our 
flattening bias on the curve (see Rates – US).  
Retail sales are expected to come in close to consensus, helped by seasonal factors. The 
set-up for the holiday season looks healthy, however, the share of discretionary spending 
warrants monitoring (BofA on USA 12 Nov 26).  
In the Euro Area (EA) soft data from France and Germany may highlight diverging 
fortunes, with our economists increasingly worried about a technical recession in France 
(see Europe Economic Weekly 6 Nov 26). We continue to see activity data support a 
bullish bias in the belly of the EUR rate curve.  
UK inflation and labour market data next week could change the implied likelihood of a 
December BoE hike (19 bp priced). Our economists’ base case remains February, but wage 
growth and core inflation could move the needle. We continue to think that an acceleration 
of the hiking cycle leaves the totality of hikes priced as somewhat excessive.  
The data calendar is rounded out by Canadian inflation and Australian labor market data, 
as well as RBA Minutes. We maintain our 5s30s box (flattener in CAD, steepener in USD), 
and Sep ‘27/ Sep ’28 (U7/8) AU bill flatteners.  
What we learned 
The rates rollercoaster rattled markets again this week. Following last week’s bumper 
payroll report, inflation surprised to the upside, including in core services inflation (see 
CPI Review 10 Nov 26). Governor Waller and Governor Bowman meanwhile seemed to 
debate the merits of hiking by 50bp in December vs accelerating the pace of hikes to 
sequential meetings. 2y yields sold off hard and retraced most of the rally since mid-
October.

Hawk affair 
In the past week: 2Y = +40bp, 10Y = +25bp due to NFP, CPI, & Fed hawks. The market is 
now pricing in a ~10% chance of a 50bp Fed hike in Dec. Don’t fade it.  
Last week we wrote: “consensus now seems centered on 3 themes: (1) Iran de-
escalation … (2) global growth at peak and will likely slow… (3) central bank cycle is well 
priced…. We still lean contrarian.” Consensus shifted quickly. Iran de-escalation view has 
faded (Trump = “we don’t need a deal with Iran”), central bank cycle no longer looks well 
priced (Waller = 50bps hike “should be considered”, Logan = “move more rapidly”).   
This week’s big rate move was likely exacerbated by position unwinds that were long or 
received in the front-end. While the risk-off move in Oct led to a reduction in crowded 
positioning across the curve, the stabilization in equities earlier this week after the 
passage of key event risk led investors to position long front-end rates and back with a 
short vol bias. These likely amplified the rates sell-off & the curve flattening pressure.  
Moves this week validate our pay front end bias & 2s30s nominal curve flattening trade. 
We are most frustrated on our pay 2Y trade that reached stop last Thursday, which 
pushed us to then adopt a “pay front end bias”. Hindsight says we should have just reset 
pay 2Y, but hindsight always 20/20. Our bias is still to pay 2Y; we refrain from doing so 
given +40bp move in 2y this past week.  
Our bias to be paid front end is rooted in: (1) 3m annualized core CPI back to early ’24 
levels (Exhibit 7); (2) front end pricing that lags sharp shift in Fed rhetoric (Exhibit 8); (3) 
Taylor rule that says Fed is wrong by 2-handles, i.e. FF at 5%+ (Exhibit 9).

Fed speculation: 50 risk & hawkish Warsh 
Key question for markets: what could get Fed to hike 50bps? A 50bp move in Dec 
becomes more likely under 3 conditions: (1) a Nov U3 unemployment rate of 4.0%, (2) 
Nov average hourly earnings of 0.4% m/m, and (3) stabilization of tech sentiment. Client 
pushback = hiking 50bps in Dec suggests the Oct hold was a mistake; our view = it’s 
better to address mistakes early before they become even larger. Don’t fade 50bp risk.  
We suspect Warsh to sound hawkish next time he speaks on policy. It would fit (1) hawk 
/ dove pattern of recent communications (2) Fed Chair that does not want to seem out 
of touch with rest of Committee (3) mid-term voter dissatisfaction signals around 
affordability & inflation. Warsh likely got the inflation message from the midterms. He 
will speak M at SF Fed conference; this is likely to be intro remarks & light on policy.  
Oct CPI: real curve flattening strengthened 
Tuesday’s solid CPI print helps underpin our bias towards flattening in the nominal curve. 
We would not want to wildly extrapolate one month's inflation print, but this is also a 
reminder that longer term inflation pricing seems complacent. Although forward nominal 
rates like, say, 5y5y Sofr, are at a level that prevailed immediately following the financial 
crisis, the composition is quite different. 5y5y real rates are much higher now than then, 
while 5y5y inflation is much lower. Despite the relatively high level of bond yields, 
inflation expectations seem too sanguine, we would argue. See: October CPI.  
Debt limit: long M7 SOFR/FF  
The US debt limit (DL) is likely to become binding in Feb ’27. DL bind will trigger a debt 
issuance suspension period that will likely lead to months of TGA drawdowns and bill 
supply cuts. TGA drop will likely place downward pressure on funding and bill supply cuts 
will lead bills to richen until the debt limit is resolved. We like long M7 SOFR/ FF. See: 
Front end – US & Debt limit. We close long Z6 SOFR/FF (see: Close Dec ’26 SOFR/FF). 
Vol outlook: sustained higher in ‘27 
Policy and macro uncertainty should sustain higher '27 vol, even as the Fed shifts to an 
extended hold in 1H27. We therefore expect a higher US rates-vol regime in '27 (see our 
note US vol – A higher regime in ’27, 8 Nov ‘26) with 1y10y fair value at c.90-95bp and 
an 80-105bp range. Significantly, these are rates vol levels that have historically been 
relatively adverse for the risky asset backdrop. We favor: 2s10s floor ladders, long 
receiver spreads and calendars in the belly, scaling into short left vs right side vol. 
Bottom line: we stay underweight front end & in flatteners with Fed hawk shift & CPI.

We enter an outright long duration trade: rec 2y1y OIS  
Since the shift in ECB communication in Oct, we have been voicing a bullish bias for 
EUR rates, esp. around the belly of the curve. We favoured the belly after noting that 10y 
Bunds were finally able to richen vs their relationship to 1y1y (Global Rates Weekly 23-
Oct), with the curve also experiencing more frequent episodes of bull flattening (Global 
Rates Weekly 30-Oct), but with 1y1y-2y2y still not inverted (Global Rates Weekly 6-Nov).  
Our concerns over a potential renewed rally in energy prices meant we limited ourselves 
mostly to option expressions. We now close two of these options trades as they hit 
target following the October rates rally and the recent flattening of the money market 
curve (the short 3m2y EUR vol vs US and the short 6m2y receiver vs long 18m2y 
receiver - see Exhibit 23 for closed trades). We maintain our 3m2y receiver 1x2 entered 
on Sep 8th at zero cost, currently at 1bp, target of 16bp and stop at -8bp). 
But beyond options, we now recommend going outright long via a received 2y1y 
OIS position (at 3.15%, targeting 2.6% with stop at 3.46%). The sell-off in the last few 
days on higher gas prices, stronger than expected US CPI and more hawkish Fed creates 
an attractive entry level. The risk-reward is also improved by the clear acknowledgment 
by the ECB of downside risks to growth from the persistent energy shock and tight 
financial conditions in the back-end of the curve.  
This acknowledgment can strengthen the case for rate cuts in the coming two years, 
esp. if further hikes are delivered. With that, and as observed in recent days, a sell-off in 
2y1y should be more limited than in shorter dated forwards. The changes in the ECB 
board due early 2027 could also add to the perception that the CB will ultimately be less 
hawkish or at least more open to cutting rates when inflation falls from 2H27. As a 
reminder, our economists pencil in at most two other hikes, with cuts starting in Sep-27.

Weak business survey: we recommend Sep 27 (U7)/ Sep 28 (U8) bill flatteners 
We recommend U7/U8 flatteners (current: -23bp; entry: -23bp; target: -50bp; stop: -6bp), 
which roll positively at 10bp/qtr. A sharp decline in the value of housing investor lending 
(-32% q/q) and forward orders (NAB survey) reinforced our weak growth outlook and 
strengthened our conviction in this trade. We continue to expect the RBA to deliver more 
cuts than markets currently price towards end of 2027. But in the near term, the RBA is 
likely to remain cautious about cutting rates, with the October CPI print expected to be 
strong, and given the subsequent policy reversal following the 2025 easing cycle.  
RBA minutes and labour market data could nudge December pricing higher 
The RBA minutes next week should provide greater clarity on divisions within the Board, 
particularly how close the November decision was to a hold and the hurdle for further 
tightening. The unemployment rate could retrace some of last month’s increase, while 
quarterly wage growth could pick up as the WPI incorporates the 4.75% minimum wage 
increase. Markets currently assign little probability to further near-term tightening, with 
December OIS pricing around 1bp. If the minutes suggest that a majority of members 
remain open to another hike, and this is followed by a strong jobs print, we think 
December pricing could increase towards 5bp. 
AU to continue outperform US over medium term 
AU-US spreads narrowed sharply over the past week following strong US NFP and CPI 
data and a hawkish repricing of Fed expectations. We retain a bullish bias on AU rates 
through 2027 and see further scope for AU rates to outperform the US as monetary 
policy increasingly diverges between the Fed and RBA. We also expect AU curves to 
steepen on a relative basis. 
Lower offshore bank funding = lower XCCY basis 
Cross-currency basis (BBSW vs SOFR) and FX-OIS basis have declined sharply over the 
past month. In our view there are two structural drivers: 1) strong Kangaroo issuance 
this year and expectations for issuance to remain elevated in 2027 and 2) an expected 
decline in offshore debt issuance by domestic banks amid the housing market downturn.

JGB futures: Longs in profit, shorts under pressure 
JGB futures open interest data are available on a daily basis, but daily changes in open 
interest alone do not reveal whether the underlying move reflects an increase or 
decrease in long or short positions, and their relationship with JGB futures prices is not 
necessarily straightforward.  
Based on our estimates, cumulative positions built in the December contract since 12 
August currently show a strong long bias. The P&L profile of outstanding positions also 
points to a clear asymmetry between longs and shorts. Remaining long positions have an 
average unrealized gain of around ¥1.06, while short positions have an average 
unrealized loss of around ¥1.45. 
Given this asymmetry, a modest decline in JGB futures prices is likely to generate only 
limited pressure to unwind long positions, while a further rise could trigger loss-cutting 
by shorts. From a position-adjustment perspective, this suggests that the current 
positioning structure is more sensitive to further upside in JGB futures prices. 
Bottom line: On 6 November, JGB futures rallied sharply amid a significant decline in 
equities. However, our estimates suggest that a substantial amount of OTM shorts 
remains outstanding, leaving the balance of risks skewed toward further short-covering.

Debt limit dynamics drive downward funding pressure

dynamic is largely driven by UST spending down cash in the TGA to meet financing

demand for borrowing. As a result, repo rates tend to decline during a debt limit episode.

Debt limit => bill supply cuts => richer T-bills 
During a debt limit episode, Treasury will typically maintain coupon auction sizes but cut 
bill supply. Our debt limit forecasts project bill cuts starting in Mar ’27. Net bill 
paydowns will lead to a broad richening in bills across the curve except for bills maturing 
close to a projected X-date, which tend to cheapen. The cheapening is often driven by 
money market funds (MMFs). MMFs, which hold 30-40% of T-bills outstanding, often 
avoid T-bills maturing near an X-date due to the higher risk of a government default.   
Post debt-limit resolution, we expect Treasury to ramp up bill supply to quickly refill the 
TGA. Bill issuance will depend on debt limit resolution timing. If the debt limit does not 
get resolved until our Nov ’27 X-date forecast, Treasury will likely need to ramp up bill 
supply quickly with several months of elevated issuance to refill a depleted TGA (Exhibit 
18). However, if Treasury guides to an earlier X-date and the debt limit is resolved by 
Aug ’27, Treasury can likely rely on a smaller wave of bill supply and Sept ’27 tax inflows 
to help re-grow the TGA (Exhibit 19). Funding conditions will be sensitive to these bill 
supply and TGA fluctuations. 
Fed “sterilization” risk reduces funding volatility risk 
While not our base case, we see risk of the Fed “sterilizing” TGA outflows by offsetting 
what would be an increase in reserves with bill redemptions or sales. Our projections of 
Fed T-bill holdings in Feb imply the Fed could potentially offset up to $700b in TGA 
outflows. Benefit would be more stable funding at the debt limit rather than the typical 
downward pressure. Once the debt limit is resolved, however, the Fed would have to 
ramp up bill purchases much faster to avoid reserves draining too far during TGA rebuild. 
Fed sterilizing TGA is not our base case because it would be challenging to forecast and 
difficult to communicate with only a limited reduction in funding volatility.  
Bottom line: the debt limit will bind early next year. TGA drawdowns will place 
downward pressure on funding and bill cuts should lead bills to richen until the debt limit 
is resolved. Consistent with this view, we like being long M7 SOFR/FF.
