Our Thoughts on the NVDA CEO Situation
We've stayed silent on the NVDA CEO situation thus far, due to the voyeuristic
nature of the press coverage of the situation. We certainly had no information to
offer regarding the allegations, and we thought that NVDA's board would make every
effort to keep him at the helm given his strategic importance to the company.
That naturally changes given the NY Times reporting last evening that he also had an
affair with a subordinate executive at NVDA. We still think the board will make their
best efforts to keep him, but these new allegations make that more challenging. Media
has reported that the board was meeting last evening, and we think it's possible for
some announcement to be made as early as today given the coverage.
If the CEO is asked to step down, we see zero impact on fundamentals / earnings
through at least CY28, since NVDA is mainly sold out through that period. We think a
CEO transition would have no impact on orders or even the roadmap, which already
runs well through CY28.
We've also read media reports over the last week worrying about the impact of further
export controls negatively affecting NVDA's business. We think those worries are
misplaced, and they are not a concern of ours. NVDA's AI shipments to China are now
de minimis. Even though NVDA has permission from the US government to ship the
H200 into China, the Chinese government has restricted use by Chinese companies.
For that reason, we don't think further export controls would have any effect.
Nonetheless, we think it's inarguable that the CEO represents key person risk, since
Huang has been key in identifying where AI is going, and allowing NVDA to get
there first, as he did years earlier in graphics. We believe NVDA's board will make
every effort to maintain his leadership, particularly since there's no one we can easily
identify that's currently at NVDA who would easily step into the role. News reports
last evening suggested that the board was already working with an outside firm to
identify replacement candidates.
That said, NVDA's deep IP library, their software advantage, and the assembly of the
world's most experienced AI, semiconductor and software engineers wouldn't leave
with the CEO. Huang is no doubt important, but he would leave a legacy that we
think drives a lingering competitive advantage, even if the company were to lose his
leadership.
If Huang is indeed asked to step down, we think that impact would be best expressed
in the effect on NVDA's multiple. But how much of a multiple discount Huang's
absence would or should have on the stock is very difficult to gauge. However, since
the stock is already so inexpensive (under 8x our view of FY29 earnings power), much
of this multiple impact could be already factored into the stock.
