Nvidia leadership crisis, higher oil prices and yields, weighed on

Global key stories
After admitting earlier this week that he had a relationship with a suspected Chinese spy, Jensen Huang
resigned as CEO of Nvidia yesterday. This, along with higher Brent oil prices and interest rates, negatively
impacted risk appetite with stock market declines in the US (S&P 500 -0.4%, Nasdaq -0.4%) although
European stocks advanced (Euro Stoxx 50 +0.1%). The euro weakened against the dollar while the price
of gold fell. In Asia, stock markets are opening upwards in Japan, down in China. 
Geopolitics. President Trump said the U.S. doesn't need a deal with Iran and called on Vice President J.D.
Vance to focus on corruption in opposition-controlled electoral districts. The president also threatened to
double tariffs on Mexico if the country's government doesn't stop a group of about 1,500 migrants from
walking toward the U.S. border. Volodymyr Zelenskyy, the President of Ukraine, stated that Starshield
service has once again been limited for the country's armed forces and therefore makes its drone defense
difficult.
Statistics. Today, October's final inflation data for France, Spain and Poland will be released. In addition,
the second estimate of euro area GDP and employment for the third quarter will be published. In the US,
producer price inflation for October will be published, in Russia inflation and in Turkey, September's
current account will be released. Today's only interest rate announcement comes from Peru, where a 25
basis point hike is expected.
Speeches related to central banks are expected from the Fed's Mary Daly, Michelle Bowman, Susan
Collins, and from the ECB's Isabel Schnabel, Ante Žigman and Olaf Sleijpen. Updated credit ratings are
expected for Bulgaria (S&P), Germany (Fitch) and Ukraine (Moody's).

the Moderate Party's Ulf Kristersson and the Social Democrats' Magdalena Andersson, and the proposals
