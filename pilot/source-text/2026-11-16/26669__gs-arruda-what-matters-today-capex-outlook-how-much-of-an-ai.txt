1) 2027 Capex Outlook: How Much of an AI Boost?
2026 has been a strong year for capex: real business fixed investment grew at an 11% annualized rate in
2026Q1-Q3, and we expect another quarter of strong growth in Q4 at 8.4%. AI investment will remain the
dominant driver of capex growth in 2027. We estimate that spending on AI-related hardware and
intellectual property will exceed $900bn annualized by the end of 2026 (reflecting around $800bn
annualized on hardware) and $1.4tn by the end of 2027 ($1.3tn annualized on hardware), roughly 4% of
US GDP.
We estimate that the impact on true GDP growth will be a more impressive 0.8pp in 2026 and 0.7pp in
2027 after adding back investment in semiconductors and exports of chip design services. We also
estimate a few indirect spillover effects on GDP, including the boost to consumer spending from stock

market wealth effects attributable to AI companies; the hit to household real income and consumer
spending from higher electricity and other prices caused by spillover effects from AI demand; and our
estimates of the limited crowding out effects on other spending.
Beyond AI, we expect declining spending on manufacturing facilities subsidized by the IRA and CHIPS
Act to continue to weigh on capex. Energy investment should be a positive in 2027. Sustained higher oil
prices since the start of the Iran war have prompted US oil producers to boost capex, contributing 0.5pp to
capex growth so far this year. We forecast real business fixed investment growth of 8% on a Q4/Q4 basis
in 2027, above the consensus forecast of 4.2%, we forecast 2027 real GDP growth of 2.4% on a Q4/Q4
basis.

2) Should We Expect More Upside Inflation Surprises?
September and October inflation data in the US surprised to the upside, providing a reminder that the
inflation pressures from higher energy prices and supply chain disruptions remain relevant. While the
impacts on some energy-sensitive core components (e.g., airfares in the US and Canada) tend to be
realized quickly, upstream price increases on average take 6-12 months to fully pass through to consumer
prices, suggesting residual pressures will continue to materialize into the first half of 2027. This pattern is
reflected in our country teams' forecasts, which show upward revisions (vs. February 2026) through June
of next year.
Although core inflation forecasts (including our own) should already incorporate the delayed passthrough
of upstream price changes, consensus forecasts have historically been somewhat conservative in
incorporating these shocks. The left chart shows that monthly inflation prints typically surprise consensus
expectations on the day to the upside by 5-10bp in the 3-9 months following a 10% upstream price shock.
Applying the historical response of monthly inflation surprises to realized price increases since the start of
the Iran War implies that inflation could surprise consensus expectations to the upside by a cumulative
0.1-0.2pp over the next 9 months (right chart), some of which may be realized via an upward surprise from
a “January effect” at the start of the year. These patterns highlight that risks around near-term inflation
news are skewed toward upside surprises. If realized, such surprises could prompt modest further
tightening from major central banks in early 2027
