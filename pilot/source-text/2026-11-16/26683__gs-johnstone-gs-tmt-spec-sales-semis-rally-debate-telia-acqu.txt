• Fractile, a London- and Bristol-based AI chip startup, has raised a $720mn
Series C led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed Venture Partners and Redpoint, with backing
from Britain’s Sovereign AI Fund. The government puts its valuation above £5bn, while the FT and
Reuters (11 November 2026) report $7bn, making it one of Britain’s most valuable tech startups.
Alongside Axelera, Etched and OLIX, it is among the best-funded startups developing purpose-built AI
chips. Fractile aims to make inference—running AI models to generate responses—faster, cheaper and
more energy-efficient by combining DRAM stacked above each chip with software that optimises model
execution. This targets growing memory demands as AI agents perform longer tasks without sacrificing
speed. CEO Walter Goodwin said substantial work remains, but the funding will enable its first chips and
systems to ship in 2027.

• INWIT’s call focused on contract disputes, with no discussion of organic performance in the Q&A. GIR
believes investors may underestimate the importance of the authorities’ involvement, viewing it as the
closest the Italian government can come to influencing a commercial dispute. Three key takeaways: 1)
Management welcomes action by three authorities: AGCM is reviewing the Telecom Italia/Swisscom
tower JV on antitrust grounds; CONSOB says INWIT did not undergo a change of control in 2020,
challenging the basis for Swisscom and Telecom Italia’s efforts to renegotiate contracts; and AGCOM is
seeking to bring Telecom Italia and INWIT together for talks that have not happened over the past year. 2)
INWIT sees AGCM’s review as the most promising route to a positive outcome, with AGCOM’s
involvement offering an additional avenue. 3) An AGCOM-facilitated meeting could take place as early as
26 November. INWIT expects this to clarify whether both parties are willing to hold talks—a potential
positive catalyst.
