After the close, The New York Times reported that Jensen Huang had a relationship with another
NVDA executive, and The Wall Street Journal reported that the board is preparing to remove
him as CEO, with Bloomberg adding that a search firm has been engaged, and an interim CEO
is under consideration. Nothing has been announced, but we now treat a leadership transition
as our base case. The fundamentals are not the issue as we expect a modest F3Q27 beat on
11/17, supply remains the binding constraint, and we continue to believe FY28 estimates skew
higher. The issue is the multiple. With no successor publicly identified, we would focus on who
leads and on what timeline, retention of senior leadership, and channel and export-compliance
posture. We reiterate Buy but lower our target to $205 (from $315), now 13x FY28E EPS of
$15.65 (from 20x).
 Key Points
Leadership Transition Is Now Our Base Case; Execution Is the Variable: NVDA's board
opened an independent review on 11/8 and at the time expressed confidence in Mr. Huang and
the management team. After the market close on 11/11, The Wall Street Journal reported that
the board is preparing to remove Mr. Huang, following a New York Times report of a relationship
with another NVDA executive. Bloomberg subsequently reported that NVDA has engaged an
outside firm to identify successor candidates and is weighing an interim CEO. We have no
insight into the board's deliberations.
Mr. Huang has led NVDA since its 1993 founding, with a broad set of direct reports, and the
company has not publicly identified a successor. In our view, what matters for the stock from
here is less whether a change occurs than how it is executed. We would look for (1) a named
interim or permanent leader, where an internal appointment would likely read as continuity and
an extended external search as added uncertainty; (2) whether Mr. Huang retains a board or
advisory role; (3) retention of the senior engineering, operations and field leaders who report to
the CEO; and (4) whether the 11/17 date and call format hold. Congressional scrutiny continues
in parallel, with three House committee chairs requesting a closed briefing and Rep. Mast

Investment Thesis
We believe NVDA remains the primary beneficiary of the shift from general-purpose to accelerated compute, and that its positioning has
moved beyond merchant silicon in ways not yet reflected in the shares. The company increasingly sells rack-scale systems rather than
components, organizes rather than absorbs the third-party capital funding the buildout, and is beginning to attach software and networking
economics to installed compute on a per-GPU and utilization-linked basis. We think that combination supports a durable revenue trajectory
and a more defensible margin structure than a merchant supplier would command. Much of that advantage is embedded in the organization
rather than any one individual: a multi-generation roadmap through Rubin, Rubin Ultra and Feynman, the CUDA developer base, supply
commitments and rack-scale customer engagements. We therefore view a leadership change as a risk to execution, to the multiple and to
NVDA’s standing in Washington more than to near-term demand, which we expect to remain supply-gated and led by hyperscaler and AI
cloud deployments, with enterprise, sovereign and edge demand, and CPU and networking attach, adding content per rack.
Fundamentals Intact; We Expect a Modest Beat, in Line With Recent History: At the midpoints, management guided F3Q revenue to
$108.0bn and adj-GM to 74.0%. Consensus sits at $108.8bn in revenue, 74.1% adj-GM and $2.47 adj-EPS. NVDA has beaten revenue
consensus in each of the last eight quarters, by 1.5% to 5.8% and ~3.3% on average. At that average, F3Q revenue would land near ~
$112bn. With supply still the binding constraint, we see little reason for the pattern to break. Based on management commentary during the
F2Q call, we anticipate Vera Rubin will contribute roughly a fifth of F3Q data center revenue.
Estimates Bias Higher; the Multiple Sets the Range: Management's first year-ahead outlook called for ~70% FY28 growth, with demand
supporting closer to ~100% and supply the constraint. Fabless peer commentary and hyperscaler 2027 capex signals support that view, and
we see risk to our FY28 estimates skewed higher. As Exhibit 2 shows, even if FY28 tracks closer to the ~100% growth management says
demand supports, the multiple rather than the estimate sets the range of outcomes.

Colette Kress Carries the Call: We expect CFO Colette Kress to carry more of the messaging burden than usual on the upcoming earnings
call; she has also been cited in press reports as a possible interim option. We would focus on four things. The first is operating continuity,
meaning customer engagements, the Rubin ramp, supply commitments and next-gen innovation, Feynman and beyond, all running as
planned. The second is whether the ~70% FY28 framework moves higher; with consensus already there, reaffirmation may not suffice,
and hedging would likely hurt sentiment. The third is disclosure posture, including any 10-Q risk factor changes around key personnel,
government inquiries or export compliance. The fourth is capital return, where buyback pace at current multiples would signal confidence.
In our experience, Ms. Kress is direct on financial frameworks and restrained on governance. If she is named interim CEO, CFO coverage
becomes a secondary question
The ~70% FY28 Framework Holds; Fabless Peers Point to a Supply-Gated 2027: Management's first year-ahead outlook called for
~70% FY28/CY27 growth, with demand supporting closer to ~100% and supply the constraint. FY28 consensus of $693.2bn already implies
~69% growth on FY27 consensus of $410.0bn, so upside requires supply to exceed the framework. As Exhibit 3 shows, fabless peers
describe calendar 2027 the same way. They are growing faster in percentage terms, but ~70% growth on ~$410bn adds roughly $285bn
of FY28 revenue, several times Broadcom's incremental AI revenue, per our math. We therefore read peer commentary as confirming the
bottleneck rather than signaling share loss. CPU attach adds a second leg, with AMD now seeing its server CPU business roughly doubling
in 2027, consistent with NVDA's outlook for CPU revenue to more than double in FY28.

We See Limited Downside to Margins From Memory; Kyber Yields Are a Variable to Watch: Our estimates are in line with how
management has framed margins: 74.0% for F3Q, 71.5% for F4Q and 72.5% for FY28, against management's indication that FY28 adj-GM
settles between 72% and 73%. Investors have asked whether HBM pricing could rise more sequentially than expected. If so, we see more
limited downside risk to margins from memory. HBM pricing for NVDA and other large purchasers is contracted and usually renegotiated
annually, so we would not expect a q/q impact. Supplier commentary points to firm 2027 pricing, with SK hynix describing HBM as essentially
sold out through 2029, but we view that reset as already embedded in the 72-73% FY28 framework (which already accounts for some
absorption of memory pricing). If we are wrong on FY28 margins, we think it is more likely to come from new-rack yields, Kyber in particular,
than from memory. On the call, we would expect management to reaffirm the F4Q and FY28 margin range. We would not expect it to break
out how much of FY28 growth comes from price versus volume, a question it did not answer last quarter.
Rubin Timing Is a Kyber Debate, Not an Oberon Debate; We Lean Optimistic: Management has described Vera Rubin as being in full
production , and says its revised Rubin specifications reflect what the system sustains at volume production. Supply-chain reports, which
the company has not confirmed, point to NVL72 volumes shifting one to two quarters versus the supply chain's original plan, with GB300
extended into 1H27. Even if accurate, we view the shift as largely a Blackwell/Rubin mix question rather than a unit question, since supply
caps total shipments and GB300 demand fills the gap. We therefore think the print and the January guide are safe. On Kyber, we are more
constructive than the headlines suggest. Mr. Huang has called delay reports "not true" and said the original Kyber design was replaced by a
better solution, and supply-chain work points to Rubin Ultra ramping in Oberon-based NVL72 racks in 3Q27. We would expect some color
on these nuances, but more broadly anticipate confirmation that Rubin is ramping and that demand exceeds supply, with roadmap detail
reserved for GTC or a later date.
Valuation: The Discount Now Reflects Leadership, and We Think It Persists Until a Successor Is Named: NVDA trades at ~11x FY2
P/E on our estimates and lower still after hours, versus ~24-25x in January 2026 when it traded in line with AMD, AVGO and MRVL (Exhibit
4). We do not expect the gap to close until the board names a leader and the market can judge continuity. Our 13x target multiple sits at
the low end of the ~13-14x range NVDA held in early October, before the leadership questions emerged, and implies ~18% upside from the
11/11 close. A move to high single digits remains possible in a disorderly transition, which we view as the principal downside case.

Export Risk Moves Closer to Estimates: China data center compute is already excluded from our outlook, but the channel question has
broadened. The Information has reported that a network of China- and Singapore-linked companies tied to a former Megaspeed executive
acquired roughly $7.5bn of NVDA chips, and The New York Times has reported that the Department of Justice is conducting a criminal
inquiry into Megaspeed. These remain reports and allegations, NVDA has not been found to have acted improperly, and Megaspeed has said
prior reviews found no evidence of illicit transfers. Still, we think tighter customer screening across Southeast Asian channels is likely, and
revenue tied to those channels is not excluded from the outlook the way China is. We would expect management to reiterate that it complies
with export regulations and would look for any sizing of exposure. With APEC and the G20 providing two Trump-Xi touchpoints ahead of the
1/10/27 trade truce expiry, we think policy visibility builds into mid-January rather than at the print. U.S. data center permitting, following the
midterms, is in our view a deployment-timing risk concentrated among smaller, less capitalized builders rather than a demand risk
What We Want to Hear on the Call: We will be listening for (i) who leads the call, any interim leadership structure and the timeline for
a permanent successor, (ii) whether the ~70% FY28 framework moves higher, (iii) the F4Q bridge across Blackwell and Rubin mix and
the duration of the GB300 tail, (iv) reaffirmation of the guided 71-72% F4Q adj-GM bottom and 72-73% FY28 range, (v) Rubin Ultra and
Kyber timing, and the roadmap through Feynman and beyond, (vi) customer screening, channel controls and any sizing of Southeast Asian
exposure, (vii) 10-Q risk factor changes around key personnel, government inquiries and export compliance, (viii) backstop and revenue-
share utilization and CPU attach in FY28, and (ix) buyback pace at current multiples.
Lowering Target on Valuation: Our estimates are unchanged, and we believe FY28 estimates likely skewed higher given supply-gated
demand, peer commentary and hyperscaler 2027 capex signals. We lower our target to $205 (from $315), now based on ~13x FY28E EPS
of $15.65 (from 20x), to reflect a likely leadership transition and heightened governance and policy uncertainty. We reiterate our Buy rating.
Target Price Methodology/Risks
Our target price of $205 is based on ~13x our FY28 adj-EPS estimate of $15.65. We believe a ~13x multiple could prove conservative should
the board name a leader the market views as preserving operating continuity, given NVDA's positioning in AI infrastructure, longer-term
growth prospects and profitability metrics. Risks to our target price include: 1) the outcome of the board's deliberations on CEO leadership,
including the timing, identity and execution of any transition, retention of senior leadership, and related congressional and federal inquiries;
2) tighter U.S. export controls or enforcement actions affecting customer channels, including in Southeast Asia, beyond China data center
compute already excluded from the outlook; 3) supply constraints or yield issues on new rack-scale platforms, including Rubin Ultra and
Kyber, that delay revenue timing; 4) memory and component cost inflation pressuring gross margins beyond management's framework; 5)
data center permitting, power availability or customer financing constraints, including exposure through NVDA's backstop and guarantee
commitments; 6) competition from custom accelerators and merchant alternatives; 7) a potential digestion period following several quarters
of significant investment; and 8) general macro-related events, including interest rates, that could impact our revenue growth assumptions
and impede the realization of our target price.

Company Description
Santa Clara, CA-based Nvidia pioneered the three-dimensional (3D) graphics processor unit (GPU) technology and associated software.
Today, Nvidia designs full-stack accelerated computing platforms spanning GPUs, CPUs, networking silicon and systems, and the CUDA
software layer. The company sells increasingly at the rack and data center level rather than discrete components. Nvidia reports in two
operating segments, Data Center, which comprises the hyperscale, and AI cloud, industrial, enterprise and sovereign deployments, and
Edge Computing, which covers PCs, gaming, workstations, AI RAN, robotics and automotive.

Sell - We expect a total return below -5% over the next 12 months with total return equal to the percentage price change plus dividend yield.
