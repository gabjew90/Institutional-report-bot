NVIDIA Corporation
Lowering PT on potential CEO transition risk
Our view: In light of recent board investigation and unconfirmed media
reports regarding CEO Jensen Huang, we are lowering our PT from $330 to
$230, but are maintaining our Outperform Rating. The investigation could
lead to a change at the top which could create a period of uncertainty. In
addition, potential congressional hearing could create further overhang.
That said, we see limited impact to near-term business fundamentals as we
believe: 1) demand is currently outpacing supply by ~50%; 2) company's
product roadmap is set for the next 3 years; and 3) NVDA's full-stack
moat combined with industry-wide component supply tightness limits
NT competitive risks. That's not to say longer term impact should be
underestimated given Mr. Huang's singular vision, execution track record,
and technology acumen which have been central to NVDA's dominance.
However, at 10x CY27 EPS, we believe the stock is more than reflecting the
fundamental risks from a potential CEO transition.
Key points:
Lack of formal CEO succession plan adds to the uncertainty: The CEO
situation remains fluid as unconfirmed media reports point to board
contemplating a replacement. NVDA has a strong bench with highly
capable senior leadership including CFO Colette Kress, VP/GM Ian Buck,
SVP Jeff Fisher, EVP Debora Shoquist, and Chief Scientist Bill Dally to
name a few. However, whether any individual can replicate Huang's
singular combination of technical vision, ecosystem-building acumen, and
ability to serve as the global face of the AI revolution remains an open
question. While we won't speculate on potential candidates, we believe it's
reasonable to expect the process to take time in case the board decides to
appoint a new CEO.
Potential congressional hearings present additional overhang: Potential
congressional hearings and/or national security scrutiny around export
controls create a multi-layered overhang. The resolution timeline is unclear,
and additional headlines could pose downside risk to sentiment. That
said, the company disclosed that China accounted for ~1% of data center
revenue last quarter (total ~8%) and that its data center outlook does not
include any China contribution, which we believe limits potential risk to
estimates.
Risk to NT estimates modest; valuation compelling: As discussed in
our recent preview, we expect NVDA to report a strong quarter and
at least reiterate its 70% revenue growth outlook for CY27. Demand

demand (incl. at Neoclouds) all point to robust end-demand even for older
generation NVDA GPUs. While potential data center moratoriums present
a material sentiment risk, a space/power constrained environment could
work in NVDA's favor given its tokens/watt advantage. We also believe

assumes NVDA continues to trade near its current NTM P/E

assuming that the stock will trade near the low-end of
historical average as strong topline growth and solid margin
execution are partially outweighed by slowing momentum
due to higher base, and the overhang from potential CEO
transition. Our price target supports an Outperform rating.

Investment summary
Key debates on NVDA are: 1) AI infrastructure spending
sustainability, and 2) competition. We expect Cloud capex
to remain elevated for 12-18 months driven by intense
competition among Big 4 hyperscalers who are well-
capitalized. NVDA's $500b+ backlog, surging inferencing
demand, and accelerating enterprise AI adoption give us
further conviction. We expect any spending slowdown to
be gradual and view sovereign projects/China as potential
offsets. We are not overly concerned about competition
given NVDA's full-stack performance leadership and mature
ecosystem. We believe B/S strength is an added advantage
and valuation looks compelling vs. Semi peers/Mag7. Last,
we acknowledge that a potential CEO transition could create
uncertainty, and while not our base case, longer-term impact
could be meaningful given Mr. Huang's singular vision,
execution track record, and technology acumen which have
been central to NVDA's dominance.
Risks to rating and price target
• Cloud capex spending cuts: Hyperscaler spending is a
leading indicator for AI demand. Lower capex guidance from
major cloud service providers could limit revenue growth.
• Substantial share loss: While market share is expected
to decline over time as AMD and ASIC providers ramp
designs, any unexpected share shifts or greater adoption of
competitors' hardware could suppress further growth.
• Supply chain bottlenecks: Growing demand for accelerators
is leading to production constraints in advanced packaging
and HBM allocation. Higher TDP racks also face energy
constraints in data center deployments while geopolitical
tensions remain a tail risk. Any capacity constraints could
limit revenue upside or delay GPU shipments.
• Potential CEO transition: Potential CEO transition resulting
from ongoing BOD investigation could create uncertainty,
and while not our base case, longer-term impact could
be meaningful given Mr. Huang's singular vision, execution
track record, and technology acumen which have been
central to NVDA's dominance.

Key fundamental questions
Our view
How sustainable is NVDA's
competitive advantage?
While market share should decrease over time as ASIC platforms and AMD designs
ramp, we believe this is largely priced in and expect NVDA to maintain majority share
long-term. The company is launching new products at an annual cadence and retains
a first-mover advantage with CUDA moat.
Given the nearly $5T market cap,
is there much more upside to the
stock?
Cloud capex appears on track to grow ~90%+ in 2026 while we see AI accelerator
TAM growing at a 35%+ CAGR through 2028. In addition, NVDA is positioning itself to
address Robotics, Quantum, and other emerging markets. As such, we see sufficient
growth drivers for the next few years.
How long can AI capex spending
sustain at current levels?
Consensus for cloud capex suggests continued growth through at least 2028. We
estimate capex currently accounts for 90%+ of hyperscaler operating cash flows but
as ROI on AI adoption continues to improve, we see room for further spending, in
part enabled by strong access to capital markets at U.S. hyperscalers.

Key sustainability questions
This section is intended to highlight key sustainability discussion points relevant to this company, as well as our views on the outlook. Both the
questions we highlight and our responses will evolve over time as the dialogue between management, analysts and investors continues to
advance. We welcome any feedback on the topics.
Our view
What are the most material
sustainability issues facing this
company?
Energy consumption is a key sustainability issue as AI compute requires greater
power. By extension, the company also faces data center-related issues such as
water consumption and land management. NVIDIA is committed to responsible
mineral sourcing with a goal to use only conflict-free gold, tantalum, tungsten, and
tin (3TG) and to achieve 100% Responsible Minerals Assurance Process-compliant
3TG processing facilities.
Does the company integrate
sustainability considerations into its
strategy?
NVIDIA powers the top supercomputer on the November 2024 Green500 list and
NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs are generally over 50x more energy efficient than traditional
CPUs for LLM AI inference workloads. In addition, NVIDIA DPUs can reduce power
consumption by offloading essential data center networking and infrastructure
functions from less-efficient CPUs.
What is diversity like at board /
management level?
The Board of Directors consists of 12 members, of which 33% are female.

Company description
NVIDIA is a global leader in advanced computing technologies. The company initially focused on GPUs for gaming and mobile
processors but has since become a pioneering force in AI and accelerated computing. NVIDIA's Data Center segment is now
its largest business line and encompasses its leading-edge GPUs for AI/ML, CUDA parallel computing platform, and networking
technologies for large-scale server clusters. In addition, the company is pushing into emerging markets such as autonomous
vehicles, quantum, and humanoid robotics. NVIDIA was incorporated in California in April 1993 and reincorporated in Delaware
in April 1998 with headquarter facilities in Santa Clara, California.
