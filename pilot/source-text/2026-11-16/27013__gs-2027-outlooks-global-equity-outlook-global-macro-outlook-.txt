GLOBAL EQUITY STRATEGY - 2027 Outlook: Finding Value in Growth
• Earnings growth is poised to slow. As we enter 2027, earnings growth is on course to slow sharply across
all major regions, growth in AI capex spending is likely to peak and the cost of capital remains elevated ...
• ... But equities have de-rated sharply; with more attractive valuations, positive but slower nominal
economic and earnings growth, and lower bond yields, inflation and oil prices, we expect equities to
continue to rise, but with high volatility.
• We focus on Growth balanced with selective Defensives and beneficiaries of lower bond yields.
GLOBAL ECONOMICS ANALYST - Macro Outlook 2027: Steady Baseline, Downside Risks
• We expect two more rate hikes from the Fed and one from the ECB, but see both returning to cuts in late
2027. With most of our forecasts for other central banks also below market pricing, we expect longer-term
rates to come down modestly despite large government deficits and heavy AI-linked borrowing.
• We expect positive returns for equities and bonds, but the path could be bumpy. The AI investment boom
has had a much larger impact on earnings and market values than on GDP growth. As the impulse peaks,
markets may worry more about whether the record share of GDP going to US earnings can persist. If AI
capex still rises strongly in 2027 as we expect, positive earnings growth should cushion this transition,
though pressures from ongoing financing needs will likely continue to push credit spreads wider.
Nvidia Corp. (NVDA): Our thoughts on the company's announced management transition
• We believe the current situation is clearly fluid, and there may be additional information that comes to
light that further shapes our investment view. But based on the available information thus far, we believe
these developments are likely to have a measured direct impact on Nvidia's financials and earnings power
over the next 12-18 months - given the company's long-dated backlog and order book (12-24 months) and
clearly-defined product roadmap (for the next 2-3 years) which is well into the execution phase.
Siemens AG (SIEGn.DE): Takeaways from management meeting following 4Q26
• Last evening, the CEO and CFO hosted a sell-side meeting in London.
• We concluded that: (1) Siemens has had a strong start to fiscal FY27 in automation, with double-digit
software growth expected for the year; (2) data center growth visibility remains strong despite potential
near-term order volatility and supply-chain dynamics; (3) the company intends to maintain M&A

discipline while prioritizing software assets, particularly in building services, as well as selective
hardware; and (4) Mobility is core to Siemens’ AI strategy, while greater platform conformity is being
enforced to protect segment margins.

Ottobock SE & Co. (OBCK.DE): Reimbursement uncertainty in focus, but sharp underperformance post
Q3 26 results looks overdone; reiterate Buy
• Overall we are cutting our core revenue/EBITDA/EPS estimates by 0-6% in FY26/27/28e.
• We lower our 12-month target price to €65 (from €75 previously). We remain Buy rated. To drive better
performance from here we believe management needs to (1) provide great clarity and specificity on what
the reimbursement challenges actually are, and confirm whether these are transitory vs structural shift in
the market, and (2) deliver consistent - and importantly predictable - performance.
