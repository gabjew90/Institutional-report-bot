Nvidia's Huang Ousted; Impacts Likely To Be Felt Far And Wide
Nvidia said on Thursday that CEO Jensen Huang will step down from the role, effective immediately. CFO
Colette Kress will take on the role of interim CEO. Huang has agreed to testify before Congress ahead of
Thanksgiving. Analyst Timothy Arcuri wrote on Nov. 11 before the confirmed departure that while the company
would likely find a strong candidate, Huang has obviously been a singular force in many ways so the impact
will likely be felt far and wide. He highlighted three key areas:
1. Financing - the company has become a key source of funds for a host of AI industry participants including
neo-clouds, LLM developers, ecosystem partners, and suppliers, and Huang has been a key driving force for
this effort. Even if a new leader ultimately maintains a similar approach, there is likely to be some "pause" in
the company's willingness and ability to continue pursuing these deals in the near term.
2. Supply chain - the company has often been far ahead of peers in terms of long-term supply agreements in a
host of key areas including memory, substrates, and other areas. While this will obviously remain a key
element of the company's strategy, Timothy and team see Huang as somewhat singular in this regard and out
in front of some key peers from a procurement perspective.
3. Potential for new, tighter restrictions - Timothy could see the US government enforcing even tighter
restrictions that could impact NVDA's (and other companies like AMD's) ability to ship product into China but
even more importantly into other regions like Singapore or Malaysia.
(Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

Williams Says Inflation Is Far Too High As Hawkish Fedspeak Continues - New York Fed President John
Williams joined a growing group of hawkish policymakers Thursday, saying that "inflation is far too high and it
continues to be high even in the latest data." He said the central bank needs to take actions over time to
ensure that "we get inflation back to our 2% goal in a timely manner and on a sustained basis." The data is
speaking "very clearly" to the Fed, showing a strong economy, a strengthening labor market, and persistent
inflation, and that there is still work to do in terms of getting monetary policy to the right place, he added. San
Francisco Fed President Daly said she was comfortable with the September rate hike and that further
tightening is needed. She added that inflation pressure is up, not down, and that firms have said they will pass
on higher costs. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee urged a "sober-minded" approach to inflation and
the policy response, noting inflation remains well above target even as AI investment provides a form of
stimulus to the economy. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, seen as one of the most hawkish voters,
said that she would like to see smaller, steadier movements in rates as they are less disruptive to the
economy. She added that the central bank will need to move to a restrictive policy stance. Hammack said she
doesn't believe the Fed is currently behind the curve, but if they do get behind, a larger move could be needed.

Microsoft Suffers Major Global Outage - Microsoft is still working to fix a major global outage that leaves
tens of thousands of users in 74 countries and territories unable to access their emails and other programs,
including Microsoft 365, Outlook and Teams, for more than 10 hours. The disruption started around mid-day
New York Thursday and remains unresolved as of 12:00 HK Friday. The company attributed the outage to
“elevated load” on its infrastructure with no indication of a cyber attack. OpenAI said it was investigating an
issue with its Microsoft connectors, warning that new connections might be broken and that some tools were
not working as expected. Anthropic said it had identified an issue with an upstream provider affecting its
Microsoft 365 connector. The incident comes weeks after Microsoft reported an outage of its Azure cloud
platform, which impacted its suite of productivity software and a range of industries worldwide, The Telegraph
reported [paywall]. (Jolie Ho Macro Sales)
OpenAI Backs AI Chip Control Measures On China -- Bloomberg - OpenAI is backing a bipartisan effort in
Congress to block China’s access to artificial intelligence chips and curb China’s access to chipmaking
equipment, Bloomberg reported [paywall], citing unnamed sources. Its support is set to add further weight to
legislation gaining momentum on the export control measures after the ouster of Jensen Huang as Nvidia
chief. Congress is weighing three proposals that would seek to put restrictions on future sales of high-end
chips, reduce the diversion of Nvidia’s AI processors and block China from accessing sophisticated machinery
from ASML. The measures would be attached to annual defense legislation that must pass before the end of
the year, as reported. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

Fed Governor Barr Flags AI Job Shock Risk - Fed Governor Michael Barr warned that the US should be
prepared for “serious short-term disruptions” in the labor market from AI, sharpening the focus on employment
risk even as worker displacement has so far been limited. Barr said there are already signs AI is substituting
for labor in some sectors and cautioned that rapid adoption could result in large dislocations. He also pointed
to the declining share of income going to labor, calling it an open question whether AI will further boost returns
to capital. The longer-term distributional impact could hinge on competition. Barr said greater market
concentration could widen inequality, while broader access to AI and stronger competition could spread the
gains more widely. (Antonya Allen, Macro Sales)

spike. The bank said that its decision doesn’t necessarily imply the start of a series of monetary tightening,
adding that it will adjust policy as needed to bring inflation back within the target band. Higher global fuel prices
and weather disruptions linked to El Niño have pushed up transport and food costs, Peru nevertheless retains
some of the lowest inflation and borrowing costs among Latin America’s largest economies. (Jolie Ho, Macro
