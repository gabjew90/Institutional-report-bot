Tim yesterday on Nvidia
• Financing - the company has become a key source of funds for a host of AI industry participants including
neo-clouds, LLM developers, ecosystem partners, and suppliers, and we have always seen Jensen as a key
driving force for this effort. Even if a new leader ultimately maintains a similar approach, there is likely to be
some "pause" in the company's willingness and ability to continue pursuing these deals in the near term. We
estimate NVDA is generating ~$50-60B/Q in FCF - a big portion of which has been put back into the system,
so this could easily take ~$50B or more out of the system - at least temporarily - at a potentially key time when
interest rates are rising and certain segments of the credit market have become less liquid.
• Supply chain - the company has often been far ahead of peers in terms of long-term supply agreements in a
host of key areas including memory, substrates, and other areas. While this will obviously remain a key
element of the company's strategy, we always viewed Jensen as somewhat singular in this regard and out in
front of some key peers from a procurement perspective.
• Potential for new, tighter restrictions - we could see the US government enforcing even tighter restrictions
that could impact NVDA's (and other companies like AMD's) ability to ship product into China but even more
importantly into other regions like Singapore or Malaysia. NVDA changed its geographic revenue disclosures
in mid-C2025, but Singapore was at that time nearly 20% of revenue. If we assume a portion of this, together
with some additional portion of its China revenue (which is now quite small), could be at risk, we estimate this
could be in the range of ~10% of revenue
