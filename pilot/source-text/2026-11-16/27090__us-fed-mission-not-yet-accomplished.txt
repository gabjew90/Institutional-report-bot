US Fed: mission not yet accomplished 
Two more hikes to come 
Upper limit of Fed funds target rate, in %. Solid orange line shows new 
Berenberg forecast. The dashed lines show the old Berenberg forecast 
and market pricing of Fed funds futures. Sources: Bloomberg, Berenberg. 
• 
Solid gains in demand. After a lull in late 2025, the US economy has regained momentum over the course of this year. 
The 0.3% mom rise in core consumer prices in October points to underlying domestic inflationary pressures beyond 
the simple pass-through of higher oil prices. While wage pressures have not picked up yet, they may do so if 
unemployment continues to fall. As a result, we change our Fed forecast. Instead of a final 25bp hike on 9 December, 
we now expect a further 25bp move on 17 March 2027 to bring the Fed funds target range to 4.25-4.5%. In light of the 
new Fed call, we now project that the US dollar will fall merely to 1.20 instead of 1.24 per euro by the end of next year 
despite the gradually worsening fiscal situation in the US. 
• 
The message from the labour market: Having added merely 0.1m jobs last year, the US economy is on track to raise 
its non-farm payrolls by c1m this year. The welcome drop in the unemployment rate from 4.4% in late 2025 to 4.1% 
today, and the increase in average weekly hours worked from 34.1 at the beginning of 2025 to 34.4 now, show that 
labour demand is outstripping labour supply. The harsh clampdown on immigration constrains the available labour 
supply. So far, this has not caused higher wage inflation. Instead, the increase in average hourly earnings has 
decelerated to 3.1% yoy this October from 3.9% a year ago. However, wages often react with a lag to changes in the 
labour market. The Fed thus has to take the risk of more wage inflation serious. 
• 
AI boom: In addition to the fiscal stimulus, most of the increase in US domestic demand reflects investments in or 
market bets on artificial intelligence (AI). Almost two-thirds of the 2.1% yoy increase in real GDP in Q3 stems from the 
30% yoy surge in investment in information processing equipment and the 13% rise in software investment. Adding 
the outlays to build data centres and to supply the energy to run them, while subtracting the rapid rise in US imports 
of IT equipment, we conclude that more than one-third of US demand growth is directly tied to AI-related investment. 
At face value, the 2.5% yoy gain in private consumption remains the major driver of US demand. But the drop in the 
personal savings rate to 4.1% in Q3 2026 from 5.2% one year ago suggests that consumers are spending some of their 
increased personal wealth, which in turn depends on buoyant equity markets in which bets on AI play a huge role. 
• 
Moderation ahead: Markets have started to question whether the investments in AI will generate the necessary returns 
to fully justify current market pricing. The midterm election results also show rising resistance against the rapid 
rollout of new data centres. We thus expect the AI investment boom to moderate over the course of 2027. The boost to 
real disposable incomes from a likely decline in oil prices in 2027 will not fully suffice to offset the absence of any new 
tax cuts for households. Higher Fed rates should also contribute to a US growth slowdown towards 1.5% in 2028.  
• 
Shaky foundations: Our almost Goldilocks scenario of a gradual deceleration towards trend growth by 2028 comes 
with an obvious risk. If a bubble were to burst in AI valuations, a plunge in equities and in AI-related bonds could 
trigger a sharp slowdown or even a drop in AI investment. Uncertainty and evaporating wealth gains could then force 
US consumers to spend less. In an extreme case, the Fed may then have to cut rates significantly to prevent a recession. 
We see that as an unlikely but not fully impossible tail risk. To minimise the risk that excessive rate hikes could trigger 
such a calamity, we thus expect the Fed to proceed cautiously. That argues against a 50bp move on 9 December and 
against more than one additional hike in 2027.  For more details of our calls, see Forecasts at a Glance.
