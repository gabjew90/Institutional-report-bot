  Rates Forecast Update – More hikes to come 
Central banks are in inflation fighting mode to avoid second-round effects from energy prices. The 
RBA was the first out of the gate and is probably finished tightening, with the ECB not far behind (one 
more hike to come). For the later starters, the BoE should follow up the November hike with another 
one and the Fed probably hikes a minimum of two more times, while the BoC stands out as having a 
lot of blue sky before reaching a restrictive setting. Bond yields are off the peak but could be upwardly 
biased in the near term if equity markets stabilize, but once tightening cycles become advanced or 
finished, yields should edge slightly lower. The main exception is the UK where the market is pricing 
significantly more tightening than we think is possible, setting the stage for Gilt absolute and relative 
outperformance.  
Policy rates  
We see a relatively high probability of the Fed delivering back-to-back hikes in Dec/Jan and/or having to 
maintain a quarterly pace beyond March. In contrast, the probability that they get stopped short of removing 
the remaining 50bp of “insurance cuts” from last year is very low. The BoC should hike again in December 
and barring downside growth surprises is on track to reach our long-held 3.25% terminal forecast, with risks 
leaning to more rather than less if the fundamentals evolve as expected. The ECB has hiked 50bp so far and 
one more hike in December is very likely in our view, which we would then also think is the last one for the 
time being and leave the terminal rate at 2.75%. The BoE has hiked once so far and should go again in the 
coming months. We think that after two 25bp increases, from a base that was already seen as being restrictive, 
the BoE will hold rates steady for quite some time to evaluate. The RBA is probably finished raising rates and 
the next move should be a cut in late 2027.  
Bond yields  
Bond yields are expected to be flat-to-higher to year end / Q1 but then be stable-to-lower over the rest of the 
forecast horizon. Unless growth disappoints or central banks fall short of market pricing the scope for a large 
and sustained bond market rally is limited. The exception is the UK, where Gilt yields could fall if our 
expectation for the BoE (one more hike) is realized against the market pricing of ~90bp of hikes.

US Rates Forecast 
 
Policy Rate: 
We still expect 25bp hikes at the Dec 2026 and March 2027 meetings. We have been pushing back against the idea 
that the Fed might skip a Dec hike on equity weakness or Iran positivity. The Fed will likely be viewing further equity 
declines through their impact on the “real” economy (via wealth effect or any slowdown in AI-related growth/inflation) 
or financial stability/contagion risks (which still seem a way off). On Iran, even a lasting ceasefire is unlikely to change 
our base case for two more hikes. Some energy-related inflation pressure will likely remain in the pipeline, and energy 
prices may not move immediately lower alongside crude. These factors may help take the more hawkish tail outcomes 
out of market pricing, but the Fed must ultimately deal with the economic reality on the ground – stable labor markets 
and problematic inflation data, with risks skewed to the upside. All told, we see a relatively high probability of the Fed 
delivering back-to-back hikes in Dec/Jan, and/or having to maintain a quarterly pace beyond March. In contrast, the 
probability that they get stopped short of removing the remaining 50bp of “insurance cuts” from last year is very low. 
Yields: 
Yields overshot on the retracement from post-Sept FOMC yield highs amid cracks in AI/broader risk sentiment. But hot 
NFP and CPI reports, and the Fed rhetoric that followed, served as a reminder that underlying fundamentals remain 
hawkish and the Fed is still on a hiking path. The ensuing selloff pushed yields back to fairer levels, but we still see 
them biased higher through Q1 of next year as the Fed continues to hike. From there, we see yields falling modestly 
in Q2 as the Fed moves into a more extended hold, before climbing modestly in H2 as economic fundamentals remain 
resilient, the market holds on to a hiking bias, and Treasury begins long-awaited coupon supply increases.

Canada Rates Forecast 
 
Policy Rate: 
The BoC delivered the expected hike at the October meeting, with hawkish comments from Governor Macklem on 
the sidelines of IMF meetings spiking market pricing for a hike ahead of the blackout period. While higher inflation 
forecasts were flagged in advance, annual GDP growth was raised for each of 2026, 2027 and 2028, providing 
fundamental motivation for tightening alongside the passthrough effects from higher oil prices. We think the BoC is 
likely to hike again in December to get to the middle of their 2.25-3.25% neutral range, given they are unlikely to 
think one hike is enough to allay their fears on passthrough. Universally softer data (Oct CPI, Q3 GDP, Nov jobs) 
could de-rail an immediate follow-up hike, however. Beyond December, we forecast two further hikes to 3.25% and 
see risks leaning to more rather than less if the fundamentals evolve as expected.  
Yields: 
CORRA futures markets are pricing the overnight rate to reach our forecast of 3.25% by the end of 2027, two quarters 
later than our Q2 timing. That suggests stable-to-higher yields from here and we see no major shifts in curve levels from 
current settings. Given our risk lean to more hikes in 2027, we see upside potential for yields and possible further 
flattening of the curve. It also suggests CAD rates could underperform against their counterparts south of the border, 
though that will depend on the magnitude of Fed tightening that is ultimately delivered.

EUR Rates Forecast 
 
 
Policy Rate: 
At its October meeting, the ECB kept rates steady at 2.5%. It signaled heightened concern that tighter financial 
conditions would weigh on growth but also suggested that the ECB was close to a hike at that meeting. That makes 
one more hike in December very likely in our view, which we would then also think is the last one for the time being 
and leave the terminal rate at 2.75%. Recently, energy prices have come down, but still there is greater uncertainty 
surrounding the Iran War and risks are tilted to the upside. Beyond December we continue to think that the key to 
further rate increases are second round effects of inflation, i.e. the transmission of higher energy prices into wages, 
of which the ECB suggests there is little indication of thus far. 
Yields: 
Bund yields have rallied strongly over the past month aided by both growth concerns in Europe and more recently 
lower energy prices. In general, higher yields have led markets to circle around those countries that are most 
vulnerable (low growth, high debt) such as Italy and particularly France. In particular, France looks fiscally precarious 
with strong budget cuts necessary this year to the get the fiscal trajectory on a sustainable path. The political paths 
to achieving that fiscal consolidation remain narrow. We continue to expect a dip in Bund yields over the next couple 
of months before continuing to trend slightly high in the second half of 2027. Once front-end rates vol starts to ease, 
we think the higher term premia theme will reassert itself and we see steeper curves going into 2027

UK Rates Forecast 
 
Policy Rate: 
The MPC has hiked 25bp in its last meeting in early November, but it is fair to say that the outcome was more hawkish 
than most people, us included, expected. Not only was there a dissenter to the upside (Mann voted for a 50bp hike) 
also the single members’ paragraphs and the press conference skewed more hawkish. Andrew Baily said clearly that 
more tightening was required unless the energy situation in the Middle East changed dramatically and the chief 
economist, implied a further hike this side of Christmas. We have changed our call (see here) and now expect another 
25bp increase at the next MPR backed meeting in February. We still believe that the BoE communication clearly 
suggests that there should not be a hiking cycle and thus we think that after two 25bp increases, from a base that 
was already seen as being restrictive, the BoE will hold rates steady for quite some time to evaluate if the so far 
elusive ‘second round effects’ of inflation, aka higher wage growth, is actually materializing.   
Yields: 
Despite the change in Bank Rate, we leave our yield forecasts and our general calls on the market unchanged. 
Forward SONIA rates imply approx. three more rate hikes and we simply think that this is too steep a forward curve 
to be realised. We thus continue to see the shorter end of the curve being anchored below current spot levels which 
should also help to anchor longer term yields. The budget (see our review here) did not scare markets as far as the 
UK’s fiscal picture is concerned either and thus we do not think a substantial increase in term premium is required at 
this stage either – particularly not as growth rates have started to show some signs of recovery lately.

Policy Rate: 
The RBA hiked to a post-GFC high of 4.85% last week, but chances are they’ve now hit terminal for the cycle. A split-
board vote (6-3) and more balanced forward-looking commentary gave a dovish flavour to the hike, and markets have 
been quick to take out the risk of further hikes. There’s barely another 4bp of hiking priced into the RBA strip now, 
followed by roughly a single cut by 2027 year-end and close to two by mid-2028. While that’s in line with our 
unchanged base case (no movement from 4.85% until a cut in Nov next year), and we’re fine with the implied cutting 
trajectory, we think the risk of one more hike between now and mid next year is somewhat underpriced. Inflation 
reports for October (due 25th Nov) and 4Q (27th Jan) will be key, with the risk that hike pricing for Feb through to May 
next year needs to lift. It won’t be December though, with the RBA signaling a pause to “take stock”.  
Yields: 
With the RBA either at or very close to terminal, we see front-end yields continuing to slip lower over the course of 
the next year. Longer end yields will increasingly take direction from the US, with some slight outperformance likely 
on reaching terminal earlier and as growth continues to fade. In STIRT space, we favour paying Feb and May-27 OIS 
on a risk-reward basis against core longs a little further up the curve in 1-5y rates. We also continue to see curves 
steepening into the end of this year.
