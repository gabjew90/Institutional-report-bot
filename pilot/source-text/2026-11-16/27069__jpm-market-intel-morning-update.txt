JPM RESEARCH: NVDA 
•         CREDIT (ERICA SPEAR) –  We remain Underweight on NVIDIA as we expect continued 
spread weakness while the leadership transition and the legal and regulatory fallout remain 
in flux. This reinforces concerns about circularity within the AI ecosystem, particularly where 
NVIDIA’s financial support helps sustain the spending that drives its revenue. 
o   NVIDIA itself is a relatively small direct credit story, with only ~$35bn of 
debt outstanding, although CDS reached new record highs this morning 
following reports that Huang may be removed. 
o   The bigger implications are for the rest of the AI trade, particularly GPU 
financing and infrastructure deals in which NVIDIA would presumably be 
involved as a supplier, investor or provider of contractual support. 

o   We do not assume existing commitments change, but new deals could take 
longer or require more compensation as investors look for reassurance about 
NVIDIA’s role. 
o   We do not expect hyperscalers to change their near-term infrastructure 
plans solely because Huang has departed. However, this adds uncertainty at 
a time when the financing needs across the AI ecosystem are already 
substantial. 
o   The export control implications could prove more consequential than the 
leadership change itself. 
o   Following last week’s blue wave, we expect a more challenging oversight 
environment when the new Congress takes office in January, with greater 
pressure to examine both NVIDIA’s engagement with the Trump 
administration and the enforcement of existing restrictions. 
o   Congressional control does not automatically change export policy, but it 
increases the prospect of hearings, investigations and legislation that could 
make further easing politically difficult. 
•         EQUITY (HARLAN SUR) –   We expect the team to maintain its leadership in AI as the 
silicon / software / platform roadmaps have been defined for the next 2-3 years 
o   Beyond the next 2-3 years, we think the main focus will be whether the

US MKT INTEL VIEW – a repost from the Nov 11 Morning Briefing 
•         US MKT INTEL – I view yesterday’s (Nov 10) moves, catalyzed by the CPI print as an 
opportunity to reset bond yields higher to more directly match economic growth, which 
printed ~3.4% for 26Q3 as the economy remains on track to grow above-trend into 2027. 
US GDP averaged above-trend growth since 22Q2. Combine this with another robust 
earnings season and, from a macro, fundamental perspective the market remains in a good 
place and on a bullish trajectory. We are Tactically Bullish. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU – We agree with Dubravko’s view:  The Tech washout 
appears to be at/near its completion though there remains some risk of another Semis 
decline, we like owning broad-based Tech plays and would consider US Memory and Semis 
plays via options. CRWV, OpenAI Q3 revenue, capital markets reopening are all supportive 
of a return to the AI theme; but the moves may remain choppy as markets consider another 
shift higher in bond yields. The barbell trade (Tech + Cyclicals) makes sense but think you 
can get more upside from playing Cyclicals via European names/sectors over their US 
counterparts due to relatively lower positioning and valuation. 
•         BOND YIELDS – As we saw AI / Tech selloff the last few weeks, the market narrative 
shifted towards an increase in recession fears, with many suggesting that AI Capex was the 
sole reason for US growth; this is inaccurate, but it fueled a power risk-off / rotation 
narrative. Our recession basket (JPRECES Index) is +14.9% since Sep 30 while SPX -
1.7%, NDX -8.8%, SOXX -22.7%, and DRAM -32.7%. A decline in AI capex is unlikely to 
drive the US into recession, given it did little to increase jobs. Certainly, there is a wealth 
effect via the stock market, but that is second order and likely not a linear relationship for 
the top income decile which is ~50% of US consumption. AI capex has an offset by making 
Net Exports more negative given the volume of the AI trade that uses imports. Consumers 
remain the engine of the US economy. They have historically large sums of cash (checking 
+ savings + MMFs) in a labor market that is essentially at full employment with signs 
pointing to improving wage growth. Rick Shane’s latest Chase Spending Data update is 
here with some charts posted below. That said, there are clear financing / financial risks 
though they are unlikely to be as systemic as what was seen in the lead up to GFC.

DUBRAVKO – his full note is here 
•         Dubravko reiterates his YE26 SPX price target of 8,000 with $365 EPS, 21.9x. This 
implies 8.4% upside from Friday’s close. 
•         TRADE IDEAS – (i) Long 12M Momentum vs. Short 3M Momentum – which is 
essentially Tech vs. Energy / Defensives; (ii) Low Vol is less compelling – after moving 
form 0%ile to 25%ile, the trade is less attractive as the factor will need to see an increase in 
recession risk to maintain its outperformance. If you want to stick with Low Vol, he sees the 
best opportunity in Healthcare followed by Utilities, and then staples; (iii) AI Upstream is 

attractive – the positioning flush has created an attractive entry point. While hyperscalers 
have less upside in the near-term, their medium-term prospects are attractive with room to 
re-rate higher. The Blue Wave will limited the capex curve, but tighter compute capacity 
should benefit pricing power, margins, and operating cash flow for hyperscalers; (iv) 
Consumer Cyclicals remain challenged – affordability remains the headwind tied to oil 
prices, borrowing costs, and housing shortages. That said, he sees longer-term value in 
Apparel, Restaurants, and Retail vs. Energy. Regarding Housing, mortgage rates need to 
approach 6.0% to see inflows into the group. 
•         WHAT’S THE MACRO VIEW?  Looking ahead to 2027, JPM Economics expects US 
GDP growth to slow but remain trend-like with modest disinflation keeping the broader 
business cycle dynamics intact, while we [Dubravko & team] estimate EPS growth of 
~20%.  
•         WHY THE UNWIND? This pullback is a de-leveraging event. Investor were chasing AI 
and AI-adjacent plays with record high levels of leverage. Leverage is at-risk when liquidity 
dissipate, which began occurring in late-June. Liquidity dries, equity de-leveraging follows 
triggering a rapid reversal of the Momentum trade. The unwind was magnified by systematic 
& vol strategies plus a liquidation of buyside funds. October was the largest single month 
unwind for Long Momentum, falling ~11% or ~6 standard deviations contributing to 
Momentum L/S declining ~40% from its peak. 
•         WHAT ABOUT MULTIPLES? Equity multiple have compressed ~4x since January, 
one of the most extreme periods of de-rating when excluding recessions. This de-rating is 
magnified by the fact that earnings have been growing double-digits this year. AI companies 
have seen multiples compress ~35% since May, including: Semis compressing 52%, 
hyperscalers 14%, Memory 60%, and Processing Units 50%. 
•         HOW MUCH SELLING IS LEFT? He sees the process as being far along pointing to (i) 
a reset in Momentum crowding from 100%ile to 76%ile; (ii) Levered ETF sales of ~$60bn 
on Thursday and $35bn the previous Friday which have dropped exposure below Jan 2026 
levels; (iii) CTA have flipped to net Short as vol targeters have seen leverage decrease 
from 65%ile to 30%ile with maybe $50bn left to sell; (iv) dealer net gamma imbalance is 
12%ile; (v) corporate buyback, with $1.2T in announced buyback, are likely to provide 

support; (vi) QQQ have seen their RSI dip below 30 which has, on average seen ~4% rally 
over the next five trading days. 
•         WHAT ARE THE CATALYSTS? (i) developments in the Middle East; (ii) MegaCap 
IPOs coming to market; (iii) the ERCOT meeting next month, which has AI Capex 
implications; and (iv) December Fed meeting. More generally, he sees US Midterm 
Elections as a clearing event and expected data center headwinds to be less onerous than 
the market is pricing. 
•         WHAT ARE THE SPX EPS RISKS FROM AI CAPEX? Dubravko and team estimate 
that  every $100B increase in capex translates to ~$9 for S&P 500 EPS (~2.5% earnings 
growth) in 2027. Thus, even if next year’s capex spend comes in at the middle of the range 
at ~$1.5T, that would roughly translate to an EPS impact of ~$18 vs. consensus, adding 4-
5% to 2027 EPS growth. While there are concerns around future (2028) data center 
projects after the recent midterm election results, J.P. Morgan credit analysts expect 
hyperscalers to be able to fund their AI capex needs next year, absent any exogenous 
shocks in credit markets. However, the funding mix will need to see more participation from 
other sources including equity.
