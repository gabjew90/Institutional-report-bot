2027 Outlook: Finding Value in Growth
 
 

Value in Defensive Growth as earnings slow 
 
 
Our theme for 2026 focused on a broadening equity bull market, with greater 
participation across regions, sectors and themes. Since then, the equity bull market has 
continued, and has widened out across the major regions despite rising oil prices and 
bond yields. The main driver has been strong corporate earnings growth across all 
regions. As we look to 2027, profit and AI capex growth look poised to slow in most 
regions. Nevertheless, recent price action has reflected this slowdown and equity 
valuations have come down substantially, particularly in the Technology sector. 
With economic activity and earnings on course to grow in 2027, albeit at a slower pace 
in nominal terms, and given the prospect of moderating inflation, bond yields and oil 
prices, we expect the equity bull market to continue. The range of potential outcomes 
has widened and, given stock concentration in the US and Asia, is particularly 
dependent on AI capex progress. Our recommended weightings reflect our 
assessment of risk-adjusted returns. We focus on: 
Growth — as earnings growth rates slow and margins moderate, investors are 
n
likely to reward these companies more highly, particularly as valuations become 
more attractive. AI-related companies have de-rated sharply, reflecting anxiety 
about peak AI capex spend and the possibility of slower returns. This is leaving 
attractive entry points, particularly in large capex Hyperscalers, which have strong 
balance sheets and diversified income streams. Significant upside potential has also 
opened up in more cyclical growth areas in memory and chips, although this comes 
with much higher risks and likely volatility.  
On a risk-adjusted basis, we are overweight the US and Japan, neutral Asia and

The key drivers for 2027 
Equity markets typically rise so long as nominal GDP is growing, valuations are not 
excessive, and interest rates are not rising sharply.  Nevertheless, the momentum of 
growth matters. The shift lower in equity markets in recent weeks — centred around AI 
concerns — has rapidly priced in the prospect of weaker growth rates, and the sharp 
sector and factor rotations within equities have pushed up the valuation of Defensive 
and Value parts of the market. From here, we expect equities to generate positive 
returns (Exhibit 4), albeit with higher volatility than in recent years. We expect investors 
to seek companies that can sustain growth as it becomes more scarce.

Rapid recent market moves have also opened up some valuation opportunities in 
more cyclical growth, for example in Semiconductors and some Consumer Cyclicals

Part of the cyclical growth opportunity is also now in areas related to Technology. Both 
Software and, particularly, Tech hardware have seen their premium valuations erode 
(Exhibit 15). Our Asia strategists calculate that Korea’s semiconductor memory sector is 
currently pricing a 70% cut to 2027 consensus earnings, which appears excessive given 
recent company disclosures of sold-out DRAM and HBM products until 2029. 
Furthermore, the sector is trading on a 0.7x 2028 P/B ratio, which has historically 
signaled strong forward returns. That said, their prospects are highly dependent on AI 
capex and so might remain more volatile.  
 
Europe offers a region where lower oil prices would provide a particularly strong support 
for the consumer, where valuations have fallen sharply. Overall areas of focus would 
be: In the US—Hyperscalers, AI monetisation beneficiaries, Power and Growth. In 
Asia—Selective AI exposure, particularly Korean memory, optical networking, 
physical AI and Power. In Europe, we would include Technology, Defence and 
Renewables.

What are the risks? 
We see four in particular: bond volatility, oil volatility, weaker AI capex, and momentum 
concentration — each of which could generate both downside as well as upside risks for 
investors.  
1. The bond market 
The rise in bond yields through 2026 has, so far, largely been offset in equity markets by 
strong earnings growth. That said, it has contributed to lower P/E ratios, and the 
underperformance of sectors that are most sensitive to interest rates, such as Real 
Estate and Defensives, while contributing to the relative strength (until recently) of 
Cyclicals.   
Any relief in bond markets, leading to lower yields, would hamper the relative 
performance of Cyclicals and Financials and favour some of the more defensive 
areas of the equity markets — another reason to have some exposure to selective 
defensives and also sectors that are highly negatively correlated with bond yields.  
Sovereign debt concerns could result in a further spike in bond yields, with the risks 
highest in Europe during an election-heavy year, while an alleviation of inflationary 
pressures might lead to a rally.  
2. Oil prices  
The combination of rising oil prices and bond yields has been central to the movement in 
equity markets through 2026. Our commodity analysts forecast oil at $88/bbl for Brent

3. AI capex spend 
AI capex spending has been an important support for equity markets over the past three 
years, driving earnings growth across the Technology ecosystem, and beyond into the 
capex beneficiaries. Our economists expect the AI capex boom to slow. This could lead 
to a de-rating in sectors that have become the beneficiaries of the capex boom, 
including parts of the Industrials sector, which has re-rated, but also memory and chips 
stocks. The latter have de-rated sharply, making the entry point more attractive, but 
volatility is likely to remain high.   
4. Narrow Breadth & Concentration 
Related to this point, the US and Asia equity markets have a high degree of stock 
concentration in their indices. Narrow breadth can create more stock volatility, with the 
dominant stocks rising or the rest of the market falling. Either way, the momentum factor 
risks high volatility and having some diversification across Growth and some Defensives 
should help to improve risk-adjusted returns.  From a geographic perspective, the two 
most concentrated regions are the US and Asia. That said, given recent market action, 
we think the large cap technology names in the US are more defensive that those in Asia. 
On a risk-adjusted basis, therefore, we think the momentum and concentration risks are 
higher in Asia and imply lower risk-adjusted returns.
