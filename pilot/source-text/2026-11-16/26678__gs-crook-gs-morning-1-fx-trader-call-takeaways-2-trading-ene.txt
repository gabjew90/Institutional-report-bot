2) FX STRATEGY (JENKINS) – Trading Energy Relief
BOTTOM LINE: Energy prices have declined significantly month-to-date, and the imprint on FX markets
has been clear, with our Energy FX factor down ~2.2% so far in November. We previously highlighted
signs of an asymmetry in FX markets’ reaction to energy prices, with greater sensitivity on oil-higher days
than on oil-lower days. That dynamic has generally played out over the past two weeks, partially through
equity market fluctuations clouding the beta in some cases. Brent prices are now just under midway
through their energy shock range, while TTF prices are closer to the highs. NOKSEK having held onto a
larger portion of its gains is consistent with our previous findings of stickier energy passthrough to the
cross, even if it remains one of the most clearly levered FX pairs to energy prices. AUDNZD meanwhile is
further off the highs, with domestic factors playing a greater role, in our view.

3) US ECONOMICS (RINDELS) – 2027 Capex Outlook: How Much of an AI Boost?
BOTTOM LINE: 2026 has been a strong year for capex: real business fixed investment grew at an 11%
annualized rate in 2026Q1-Q3, and we expect another quarter of strong growth in Q4. AI spending - much
of it on imported capital goods - accounted for over half of that growth. AI will remain the dominant
driver of business investment growth in 2027, though its impact in the official statistics will be smaller
than company spending data suggest. We estimate that spending on AI-related hardware and intellectual
property will exceed $900bn annualized by end-2026 (a $550bn increase from 2025) and $1.4tn by end-
2027 (a further $500bn increase), roughly 4% of US GDP. We estimate that AI-related investment will
directly boost 2027 true capex growth by 5.3pp but measured capex growth by only 4.3pp, and will boost
true GDP growth by 0.6pp but measured GDP growth by 0.2pp. Taken together, we forecast real business
fixed investment growth of 8% on a Q4/Q4 basis in 2027, above the consensus forecast of 4.2%, and 2027
real GDP growth of 2.4% Q4/Q4.
Midterm Risks: The midterm election results pose only modest risks to AI investment, for now. We expect
newly elected Democratic governors in Texas, Georgia, and Michigan - which account for over a third of
planned US data centre capacity - to impose moratoria on new data centres. However, these will likely
only impact projects not yet permitted, leaving the vast majority of planned 2027 construction unaffected,
and spending is usually concentrated in the back half of the 2-3-year construction timeline. Moratoria that
are stricter or longer-lasting pose downside risks, particularly in 2028-2029.
4) GLOBAL ECONOMICS (PETERS) – Should We Expect More Upside Inflation Surprises?
BOTTOM LINE: Core inflation has mostly surprised to the downside in major economies since the start
of the war in Iran, but September and October inflation data in the US surprised to the upside, providing a
reminder that inflation pressures from higher energy prices and supply chain disruptions remain relevant.

Global import prices and producer prices have risen and broadened in recent months, indicating increases
in upstream price pressures. While the impacts on some energy-sensitive core components (e.g., airfares in
the US and Canada) tend to be realized quickly, upstream price increases on average take 6-12 months to
fully pass through to consumer prices, suggesting residual pressures will continue to materialize into the
first half of 2027. This pattern is reflected in our country teams' forecasts, which show upward revisions
(vs. February 2026) through June of next year. Consensus forecasts have historically been somewhat
conservative in incorporating upstream price changes, and the historical relationship between such shocks
and monthly inflation surprises implies 0.1-0.2pp cumulative upside to consensus projections over the
next 9 months. If realized, such surprises could prompt modest further tightening from major central banks
in early 2027.
5) Equities Update
Rich Privorotsky, Head of EMEA Flow Intermediation and European One Delta Trading, out with his
latest views. Key takeaways below; full piece here
Resilience: Quite impressive how much we’ve managed to rally in the face of the macro backdrop and
increasingly idiosyncratic problems at Nvidia. Yesterday was emphatically not a broad based move. Semis
did the heavy lifting, with the SOX +5.5%, ARM +12%, MU +12%...while MSFT, META and AMZN all
finished lower. The resilience of the broader tech complex is remarkable given how much market cap is
concentrated in Nvidia. The governance issues are clearly forcing a significant rebalancing of capital. The
standout flow yesterday. Retail Favorites (GSXURFAV) +495bps, its 3rd best day in three years, (three
sub $4 stocks accounting for 32% of US tape volume US Equities Mid-Day Color Overnight NVDA
dropping roughly 9%, weighing on Asia tech tape.

Vol & Positioning: Vol complex has largely reset. SPX vol curve un-inverted, dealer skew flattened and
systematic L/S managers posted their best two day return in nearly three years (+2.15%). Feels like we’ve
reached a point of relatively lower risk, considerable confusion around AI and persistent concern about the
political/geo-political backdrop. Yet the market keeps finding a bid. The question is whether this is simply
the mechanical consequence of lower positioning and short covering, or the beginning of something more
durable. Breadth remains narrow, but the fact we can absorb this much Nvidia specific pressure without a
broader capitulation is noteworthy.
AI Hardware & Infrastructure: We continue to separate fundamental compute demand from
upstream/downstream valuation multiples. Hyperscalers remain best positioned to navigate tight supply
given balance sheet capacity and contracted infrastructure. Downstream, the market continues to question
multiples. The four tactical overhangs remain: (1) higher cost of capital, (2) power/permitting delivery
constraints, (3) supply/demand balance entering 2028, and (4) ultimate monetization velocity relative to
capex. While near term permitting and logistical concerns have eased, the real worries are over the LT
supply absorption and ROI realization. Expect the AI infrastructure trade to generate episodic, high beta
trading rallies rather than pure buy + hold,...strong micro results coexisting with multiple compression.
Risk View: Time will tell whether this was anything more than a bear market rally. There are plenty of
reasons to be constructive, but a durable breakout probably requires lower rates, lower energy and broader
leadership. The logical candidates outside the immediate AI infrastructure complex are hyperscalers,
transportation, cyclicals and aerospace. Defense also looks washed out. But it’s difficult for many of these
groups to work sustainably against an aggressive hiking cycle and stubbornly high oil prices. Ultimately,
the market needs oil fixed and rates lower… and those two things may well be connected. Absent that, I
suspect we chop rather than break out. That said, positioning has been reset materially, volatility remains
elevated and the market has already absorbed an extraordinary amount of bad news. Low positioning and
high vol are powerful short term tailwinds.
