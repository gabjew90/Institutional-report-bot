An impressive feat of parallel processing
Goldman is invoking Steve Jobs. BNP has discovered Shakespeare. Bernstein is counting ants in the
kitchen. And the sell-side is desperately trying to put a price on the drama. Welcome to Wall Street's
attempt to value a potential love triangle. Here are the charts and quotes that matter.
Heavy is the head
Shakespeare famously warned that uneasy lies the head that wears a crown. Four centuries later, BNP
titles their NVDA research piece the same and sees risks. A lot of risks.
"While we think Nvidia will retain significant share, we believe these events could favor AMD and
Broadcom. We expect custom silicon could approach three-quarters of AI xPU units by 2030. We also
believe increased government scrutiny could lead to fewer GPU exports to China and Taiwan
(collectively ~29% of sales), though Nvidia reported no H200 sales in FY26 to China."
Congressional overhang
RBC warns that potential congressional hearings and export-control scrutiny could create yet another
overhang. Washington, it seems, may have questions of its own.
"Potential congressional hearings and/or national security scrutiny around export controls create a
multi-layered overhang. The resolution timeline is unclear, and additional headlines could pose
downside risk to sentiment." (RBC)
A haircut

And while the facts remain contested, Wall Street's spreadsheets have already reached a verdict: the
multiple needs a haircut.
"We are Buy rated on NVDA. However, we reduce our 12-month price target to $220 from $300, based
on 22X P/E multiple (down from 30X previously given limited visibility, management transition, and
execution risk) applied to our normalized EPS estimate of $10.00." (GS)

Cheapest on the block
Stifel looks at peer group valuation comparisons but warns that cheap can get cheaper.
"A move to high single digits remains possible in a disorderly transition, which we view as the principal

Floor thoughts
"Fears of share loss and margin compression will likely push shares lower near term. Calling a floor is
challenging, but our sense is 9x CY27 EPS or $150 would be appropriate." (Cantor)
Jobs Jensen
One analog that Goldman believes is potentially relevant in this situation is Apple, relative to founder
Steve Jobs' extended leave of absence in January 2011 and unfortunate passing in October 2011.

"Over the ~18 months leading up to that point, Apple's stock de-rated from ~17X to ~12X NTM P/E -
and the stock traded as low as ~9 NTM P/E in 2013 before recovering to ~15X in the following quarters
once the company's ongoing continuity of execution with its iPhone and other products became
apparent." (GS)
The chart shows AAPL’s NTM P/E multiple from 2010 through 2015.
Bloomberg
Betting on Buck bucking the bearish trend
"Ian Buck the VP of Nvidia's Hyperscaler and HPC business and 22-year veteran of NVDA is a strong
internal candidate to step-up, in our view. He is well liked and respected by investors. Fundamentally,
we don't see a change of CEO as changing the investment case. Granted there is higher risk. However,
this does not change the direction of travel. In CY27 co should grow revenues +70% and drive $15
EPS." (Piper Sandler)
LLY vs. NVDA
LLY hit a new all-time-high close yesterday, with price action since summer showing some inverse
correlation to NVDA/AI as broadening out rotations remain in focus.
Bloomberg

Where did he find the time? 
Bernstein asks the question we have all been thinking: "Where did he find the time?"
Then Bernstein totally throws in the towel: 
"We find it hard to advocate stepping in here in size as there will no doubt be more allegations to come
(there is a trope that says if you find two ants in your kitchen the best estimate of the total number of
ants in your kitchen is not two...). We admit to struggling mightily with this call, and can sympathize with
the case to buy the dip. But our gut tells us that we probably haven’t yet fully probed the depths of
what might be found here; we’ve had enough, and prefer to watch from the sidelines. We downgrade
to Market-Perform, and lower our PT from $400 to $200."
