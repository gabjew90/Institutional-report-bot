Data Intelligence Sentiment Deep Dive: Institutions Turn Bearish on Stocks; Fed Hike Focus Persists, European 
Risk Tone Improves 
 
Brief Summary: Institutional sentiment toward SPX and NDX recently turned very bearish and comparable 
sentiment levels have coincided with periods of market stress and subsequent rebounds in index performance. On the 
macro front, US inflation is back in focus, and institutional mentions of Fed hikes remain elevated (though off June 
highs), consistent with expectations for a December hike. In Europe, interest in OATs and iTraxx Main has been 
easing, suggesting some moderation in market concerns.

Key Points: 
 
1) SPX and NDX: Renewed tech and AI concerns weighed on both SPX and NDX last week, pushing 
institutional sentiment (i.e. relative bullish vs. bearish views) towards particularly bearish levels (c. -2z, 
six-month rolling basis). Historically, similar sentiment levels have coincided with periods of notable market stress 
and were followed by rebounds in both indices. However, SPX is broadly unchanged as of Nov 11, compared to 
its level when sentiment fell below −2z, and its 50-day and 100-day moving averages remain key resistance 
levels. 
2) US CPI: As inflation comes back in focus, institutional mentions of Fed hikes remain elevated despite 
easing from their June peak, consistent with expectations for a December hike. A sustained shift towards Fed 
cut-related discussion could signal a shift in investors’ policy expectations. 
3) Cross-Asset View: Institutional mentions of French government bonds (OATs) have continued to ease 
from multi-year highs, alongside a decline in the 10-year OAT yield to c. 4.7%, suggesting market concerns 
may be starting to stabilise. At the same time, institutional mentions of iTraxx Main are now back to average 
levels (one-year rolling basis), alongside a tightening in spreads below 60bps.  
Thematic Charts & Additional Detail 
SPX: As highlighted in our previous note, institutional investors have remained cautious on stocks despite
more supportive underlying signals (here). Since then, the S&P 500 has declined c.-2%, primarily on renewed
concerns around tech and AI (XLK US c.-8%), with sentiment falling to particularly bearish levels (-2z, six-month
rolling basis). Since 2021, we’ve seen five comparable episodes, all around notable periods of market stress; each
episode was followed by a rebound in SPX, with the strongest performance typically seen over the next 20 and 60 days.
So far, however, SPX is broadly unchanged from its level when sentiment fell below −2z (Oct 22).   
That said, the technical backdrop remains cautious - a break above key resistance levels (50-day and 100-day
moving averages) would provide further evidence of an improving technical picture for SPX. 

 
NDX: Nasdaq shows a similar contrarian setup – since 2022, we’ve seen nine episodes where institutional sentiment
fell below -2z (six-month rolling basis). Although sentiment is currently above that threshold (-1.8z as of Nov 6), it sits
close to year-to-date lows – levels previously associated with reversals in NDX performance. 
 
 
US CPI: Inflation is back in focus after Tuesday’s stronger-than-expected CPI report, with markets now pricing in
a higher likelihood of a December hike. Our institutional dataset reflects this broader shift towards tighter policy 
expectations: since the start of the year, mentions of Fed hikes have exceeded mentions of cuts, peaking in June
(c. 4z, one-year rolling basis). This contrasts with 2024 through late 2025, when cut-related discussion dominated and
remained elevated. With attention now on inflation and the Fed’s next move, we are watching whether hike-related
mentions remain dominant despite easing from their June peak. A sustained decline, alongside renewed interest in 
cuts, could signal a shift in investors’ policy expectations. 
 
              
 
 

 
Cross-Asset View – Institutional mentions of French government bonds (OATs) have eased from multi-year
highs, alongside a decline in the 10-year OAT yield to c. 4.7%. While French bonds remain exposed to domestic political 
and fiscal uncertainty, easing investor attention and lower yields may point to some moderation in market concerns. 
Institutional mentions of iTraxx Main are now back to average levels (one-year rolling basis), as Main tightened
below 60bps; spreads remain above levels seen earlier in the year, but below those recorded at the onset of geopolitical 
tensions.

Our data toolkit 

By leveraging proprietary data and signals from our industry-leading Markets business, we utilize a combination of tools to inform our insights and trade 
ideas including equity market timing indicators, positioning, flows and sentiment. 
These toolkits are available to our institutional clients via written

More details on our flagship datasets Signal from the Noise, Tactical Positioning Monitor, Through the Retail Lens, Strategic Index Fundamental 
Toolkit (SIFT) and Quantitatively Selected Themes (QUEST) are shown below. Contact us here if you would like access to the datasets or to learn more.
 
Signal from the Noise 
Signal from the Noise reflects market timing signals for the US equity market (S&P 500) based on fundamental and (externally derived) positioning 
data. For the framework to be bullish on the S&P500, either the Positioning or Fundamental signals need to be flashing ‘Stron
g Buy’ (and likewise for a 
low conviction bullish view). The ‘Strong Buy’ strategy demonstrated an 8.6% annual return and 1.7x Sharpe ratio (69% per trade hit ratio) from 2010 
to 2021. 
Tactical Positioning Monitor 
Tactical Positioning Monitor (TPM) reflects the level of broader positioning data. For the framework to be bullish on the S&P500, short
-term 
positioning data points either need to be very light (-1.5z), or rising (+1.5z) but with markets below peaks. The strategy demonstrated an 8.9% annual 
return and 1.0x Sharpe ratio (69% per trade hit ratio) from 2015 to 2021. 
Through the Retail Lens 
Through the Retail Lens focuses on US retail trading activity and social media volumes. The product combines proprietary and high frequency retail 
flow data from our QDS Research team, with analysis of social media volumes at a single stock, thematic, sector and market le
vel. 
Strategic Indices: Fundamental Toolkit (SIFT) 

Strategic Indices: Fundamental Toolkit (SIFT) is a proprietary toolkit of 21,000 aggregate global equity indices with an extensive cross section of 
46 different metrics across each index from the 1960s – created by our colleagues in the J.P. Morgan Equities Structuring team. With its comprehensive 
metrics and long history, the database can be used for a variety of investment analyses for the market, sectors & factors.
 
QUEST (Quantitatively Selected Theme)

investment themes in the market – created by our colleagues in the J.P. Morgan Equities Structuring team, in partnership with the J.P. Morgan Chief 
Markets Strategist Marko Kolanovic and team. The database is comprehensive and customizable, and allows clients to screen for themes a
t a granular 
level across a wide universe of stocks.
