BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, SEP 08, 2026 - 03:44 AM 
While the Street anxiously awaits next week’s inflation print, Goldman’s flows guru Lee 
Coppersmith says in his latest note (available here in full for pro subscribers) the more important 
tape is AI: OpenAI’s Astra drop, GS’s Communacopia+Tech circus (OpenAI at the podium 
Tuesday 9:30am PT), and a washed-out positioning book.  
 


[Page 2]
 
US fundamental L/S net leverage sits in the 4th percentile (gross 27th), Semis vol has been cut 
in half, and Goldman’s Tech L/S momentum pair is still ~50% off the late-June highs even after 
Friday’s near-7% bounce - all with the SPX week straddle priced at a sleepy ~1%.  
Coppersmith’s research colleagues add that risk appetite already has growth optimism near the 
top of its post-COVID range; further upside needs lower rates, not stronger growth. 
Goldilocks would be cheaper energy (Hormuz reopen) plus cooler CPI. Anything else - growth 
miss, another geo shock - and there’s less cushion, because this year’s earnings have been 
Tech/AI CapEx and mark-to-market investment gains while multiples refuse to pay up for 
increasingly cyclical, more-levered AI earnings. 
 
Lee Coppersmith’s Sunday note (available here in full for pro subscribers) is less a CPI preview 
than a coiled-spring setup into a week that can break the tape three ways: the inflation print, 
OpenAI’s Astra launch, and whether the Goldilocks bid - already stuffed with post-COVID-high 
growth optimism while the policy leg of Goldman’s risk-appetite gauge is still negative - finally 
runs out of road. 
A lot of investors seem to be holding their breath for what next week’s inflation data might bring, 
but the latest on the AI front may be more important for stocks. The release of OpenAI’s Astra 
model, lighter positioning and a big round of micro updates - headlined by our marquee 
Communacopia + Technology Conference - make for a pretty interesting setup... All with the 
SPX straddle for the upcoming week at ~1%...

On the macro front, our research team has flagged that further increases in risk appetite likely 
need lower rates more than stronger growth expectations (note available here in full for pro 
subscribers). Our Risk Appetite Indicator’s growth component is already near the top of its 
post-COVID range, while the monetary policy component remains negative. Lower energy 
prices with a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, alongside further progress on US inflation, 
would be a friendly combination (i.e. more Goldilocks pricing). But with a decent amount of 
growth optimism already embedded across assets, there is less room for growth 
disappointments or another negative geopolitical shock. 
 
The earnings/valuation dynamic is also worth keeping in mind. It’s no secret a lot of this year’s 
earnings strength has come from Tech, supported by AI capex and gains on equity investments. 
Investors have been less willing to pay higher multiples for increasingly cyclical earnings, 


[Page 4]
 
particularly with leverage rising across the AI ecosystem. Higher long-end yields have also 
pressured valuations outside Tech. Our strategy team expects lower equity Sharpe ratios from 
here, though lower rates alongside resilient growth could help multiples again - especially as AI 
adoption broadens beyond the infrastructure buildout (note available here in full for pro 
subscribers). 
 
The market needs fresh catalysts, and next week could deliver. OpenAI kicks off our 
Communacopia conference Tuesday at 9:30am PT, with 40 public/private companies across our 
research team’s lineup. The focus: AI spending, returns and competition into 2027 and beyond. 
Our team still sees upside to capex, with compute demand outstripping supply well into 2028. 
Beyond AI, expect updates on the digital consumer and the trade-off between growth, 
investment and margins (note available here in full for pro subscribers). 
Our PB data shows exposure levels are still quite friendly. Gross leverage for US fundamental 
long/shorts is in the 27th percentile over the last year, and net leverage is in just the 4th 
percentile. There has been some rebuilding recently, but positioning looks very different versus 
two months ago (note available here in full for pro subscribers).

The reduction in positioning is also reflected in the options market, namely in tech vols. Semis 
volatility in particular has seen a dramatic reset, in what has been a painful stretch for long-vol 
positions. VXSMH - a proxy for semiconductor implied volatility - has fallen from roughly 65 in 
July to 36. Nearly cut in half, and back toward where we started 2026. 
 
But the market hasn’t really pivoted on its micro optimism. Put-call skew on the average S&P 
stock is still very flat, near the lows of the last three years. The vol reset has compressed the 


[Page 6]
 
implied range of outcomes, particularly across AI, without much of a bearish tilt taking hold. 
Less being priced for how far stocks can move, but still very little premium for downside relative 
to upside. 
 
Speaking of things cut in half - our Tech Long/Short Momentum pair has now seen a ~50% 
drawdown since the late-June highs. That’s even after Friday’s near-7% bounce. It’s been 
anything but easy for a lot of investors trying to navigate recent rotations in the equity space. 
 
Some interesting charts here too. MU, SNDK, EWY and DRAM are starting to break above their 
recent downtrendsafter spending much of the summer consolidating…

On Korea specifically, the last four weeks of equity flows have been supported purely by 
buybacks despite net selling from the rest of the investor base. Still room for broader 
participation. It will be interesting to see if demand returns post summer; link. 
 
The takeaway for me: owning right-tail expressions through options makes a lot of sense here 
given these dynamics. Light positioning, a dramatic reset in implied vols and plenty of potential 
catalysts in the coming days. It’s worth noting Friday started to show a little of this excitement 
returning to our space, with decent upside buying. 
If we’re framing where to hedge risk at the moment, downside on our SPX ex-AI index (SPXXAI) 
offers really attractive convexity. It’s still sitting at the highs as investors were forced to rotate 
away from AI for most of the last two months. A reversal of those flows could leave it vulnerable. 
 
Even pairing long Broad AI (GSTMTAIP) vs short SPXXAI looks compelling at these levels in 
our view.

addition to the traditional LatAm/EM investor base. Outsized upside prints continue across EWZ 
and Ibovespa.

Bottom line 
The options market has compressed the range without ever buying a real downside tilt - 
average single-stock put/call skew is still near three-year lows - so Coppersmith’s trade is 
simple (and painful to traders instincts: Don't reload the factor that just killed you, don't short the 


[Page 11]
 
only thing that worked, and don't buy vol after vol already died)...: own the right tail in vol, hedge 
the crowded rotation via SPX-ex-AI (SPXXAI) which is still sitting at the highs, or just run long 
Broad AI vs short the ex-AI basket. 
That is exactly why Coppersmith thinks the payoff is there. If it were easy to click, the 
4th-percentile net and the halved semis vol would not exist. 
Korea’s last four weeks of “inflows” were buybacks covering everyone else’s selling; 
MU/SNDK/EWY/DRAM are poking above summer downtrends; Brazil is overbought (RSI 76) 
with a one-point Bolsonaro-over-Lula runoff flip juicing EWZ. 
Light books, crushed tech vol, a bigly conference week, and an inflation print that could change 
everything. 
Either Astra/Communacopia re-ignites the melt-up they just de-risked… or the 1% straddle is 
the last cheap thing in a market that already spent the Goldilocks bid.
