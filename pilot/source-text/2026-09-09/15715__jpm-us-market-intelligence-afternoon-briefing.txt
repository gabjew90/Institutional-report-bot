•         PPI PREVIEW (FEROLI; here) – We [Feroli and team] project that the final demand 
producer price index (PPI) rose 0.2% m/m in August (5.0% oya), with the headline series 
remaining firm but slightly below its recent peak of 5.9% oya in May. Energy prices have 
driven much of the recent volatility in the headline readings: the energy PPI rose a 
cumulative 15.9% from March to May before falling 9.5% over the following two months. 
For August, we expect a moderate 0.5% decline in the energy complex, reflecting mixed 
signals across products. Lower propane and natural gas prices during the month’s survey 
window are likely to weigh on the LPG and utility natural gas PPI indexes, while higher 
gasoline prices could result in renewed price pressures in that segment. We expect food 
PPI to tick up 0.5% after two consecutive monthly declines, as agricultural commodity 
prices edged higher in August. Excluding food and energy, we expect core PPI prices to 
rise 0.2% m/m (4.6% oya), reversing some of the improvement since the recent peak of 
4.9% oya in April. We expect core goods prices to rise 0.2% m/m, resulting in an over-
year-ago pace of 4.8%. A major contributor to firm inflation in this category has been 
sales to government or exports, which edged down slightly in July. Prices of finished 
consumer goods were running at 3.6% oya in the latest report, led by non-durable goods 
inflation, which has been running above its durable goods counterpart since April after 
moving at a similar pace for much of the second half of last year. We have been tracking 
growth in core goods inputs to goods producers at various intermediate stages of 


[Page 4]
demand; this measure stepped down from 3.5% m/m in May to 0.3% in June and -0.3% in 
July, signaling some relief in pipeline inflation pressures. We forecast core services PPI to 
rise 0.2%, similar to the prior month. The soft July print was driven by a sharp 1.8% drop 
in transportation and warehousing services, along with a moderate 0.1% decline in trade 
services—two components that had driven much of the firm inflation in services earlier in 
the year. The prior decline in energy prices from recent peaks contributed to the recent 
drop in transportation service prices.

•         US MARKET INTEL – The debate is 0.2% (rounded) vs. 0.3% (rounded). Feroli sees 
0.2% being low enough to hold rates and 0.3% as high enough to hike. The bond market 
is pricing a ~63% probability of a 25bp hike at the Sep 16 FOMC meeting. This print 
should provide clarity on the Sep 16 Fed meeting, with the tail risk for Equities being a 
material hotter print that spikes Oct or Dec rate hike expectations, which are currently 
~27% and ~54%, respectively. If the data supports Feroli’s hypothesis for a Hold or 
Hawkish Hold, look for stocks to react positively. Given the neutral positioning 
environment and room to re-gross / re-lever, there remains significant upside and we 
would point to Momentum, Tech, and Cyclicals are the drivers of gains. Our colleagues in 
Positioning intel have seen HF re-grossing increasing, “Globally HFs added gross 
exposure on 4 of the past 5 days for a +1.3z increase in the past week, the highest since 
late June.” Current market pricing combined with positioning point to the risk/reward being 
skewed to the upside. We think markets remain choppy into the print, the key driver 
behind our move to Tactically Cautious / Neutral from last week despite underlying 
fundamentals remaining strong, both macro and micro, which over time will be supportive 
of stocks.

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the September 9, 2026 Morning 
Briefing 
We maintain our Tactically Cautious / Neutral view ahead of Friday’s CPI report. The 
near-term setup remains binary: a dovish print could ease the pressure from yields and 
rates volatility, allowing investors to refocus on resilient consumer and corporate earnings 
growth, while a hawkish print shall keep the September Fed meeting live with rising bond 
vol pressuring stocks. Beyond these immediate catalysts, our base case remains 
constructive in absence of renewed inflation/bond yield spikes. We think it is prudent to 
remain neutral/cautious ahead of the CPI and Fed hurdles. 
  
•         MONETIZATION MENU (Unchanged WoW; AUG 31) – We start with a +NDX vs. -
RTY position. Within Tech, we like Mag7 as the favored near-term play given the inability 
for AI / Memory / Semis to sustain a bid. Software remains attractive especially if Semis 
continue to fall. Generally, we think that SMID-caps are likely to underperform as part of 
this tactical call. Parts of Cyclicals can perform especially if we see stronger than 
expected ISM prints this week. Here, we like Large-Cap Banks given the economic 
growth story accompanied by loose financial conditions and Consumer Discretionary if 
energy prices move lower. Within Defensives, Healthcare tends to be the best performing 
sector into midterms. We do not have a strong view on Cyclical vs. Defensive 
outperformance over the next two weeks. As hedges we like shorting Credit ETFs and 
buying VIX products. Until we get through the Fed Meeting on Sep 16, the Dollar 
Debasement trade may struggle, so makes sense to switch cash positions into derivative 
positions. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (AUG 24) – We like the barbell approach but with eye 
towards Momentum having some more downside risk we think it prudent to play the 
broadening theme. Within our barbell approach, that would spreading risk from 
AI/Memory/Semis to include Mag7/Software. Within Cyclicals, spreading risk from AI-
related Cyclicals to include Financials, Energy, and Metals/Miners. These moves will 
benefit from further broadening and take advantage of the ‘Dollar Debasement’ trade

ROADMAP TO 26Q4 
As we wind down the quarter, we wanted to share some of the catalytic events as we 
approach Q4. So far, midterm election seasonality has failed to materialize; and, at this 
point, it seems unlikely to do so with the dominance of A, an unresolved conflict in the 
Middle East, and the potential for 26Q3 earnings season (which spans the election) to 
deliver another quarter of more than 20% EPS growth with widening margins. 
If we are wrong on midterm seasonality, buy Healthcare, which is typically the best 
performing sector into midterms. Then, size up on Election Day as the post-election setup 
is positive.
