JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Last week, the SPX/RTY were mostly unchanged flat and NDX added +0.4%. WTI surged 
nearly 10% last week as US and Iran resumed attacks. 2y and 10y yields rose 3bp and 6bp 
last week, respectively. The main drivers for the bond market are NFP and Fedspeaks 
(Warsh at the G20 and Waller). Thematically, Memory, Renewable, Autos and Banks were 
among the top performing baskets last week, while AI vulnerable baskets (Software, 
Alternative Managers) lagged. Momentum L/S pair added 2.5%; Cyclicals added 2.1%. 
•         YESTERDAY’S PRICE ACTIONS – [From September 8th Afternoon Briefing] Two main 
drivers of price actions yesterday: Middle East and AI. First, on the Middle East escalation, 
the main headlines today were the Houthis’ attack on Saudi and US strikes on Iranian 
targets near Kharg Island; WTI added 1.7% to $93.03; 2y and 10y yields were 3bp and 1bp 
higher, respectively. Airlines, housing and consumer were among the top underperformers. 
On AI, OpenAI’s Astra release drove another move higher across AI compute bottleneck 
trades: SOXX added 1.6%; software fell 2.3%. Outside these two themes, trade war 
tensions also further escalated: there is still no trade deal between US and Canada after the 
retaliatory tariffs; Trump said he would seek to bar Canadian companies from selling to 
government contractors.

MACRO UPDATES 
•         US/IRAN – Brent hits $100 a barrel for the first time since July 24 this morning. While 
there have been headlines around mediators trying to bring both parties back to the MOU, 
progress remains stalled. On the other hand, oil flow through the Strait of Hormuz has been 
improved: in the week before fighting erupted again on August, roughly 8-9 mbpd had been 
flowing through Hormuz using alternative routes and means, per Reuters, comparing to the 
pre-war level of 16 mbpd. In a separate report by CNN, it highlights that the US military 
escorted 40 commercial vessels carrying 18mn barrels of oil through Hormuz. Yesterday, 
two more headlines that drove WTI above $93: (i) the Houthis said they again targeted 
Saudi’s Jazan refinery and facilities; Saudi said operations at several of its energy facilities 
were halted by Houthi attacks; (ii) US officials told a Fox News reporter that the US hit 
Iranian targets near Kharg Island. Meanwhile, on the demand side, Chinese crude oil 
imports, one of the key factors that limited the surge in oil prices earlier this year, rebounded 
(+6.2% in August vs. July). 
•         NFP THOUGHTS – [We sent below immediately after Friday’s NFP release] This is a 
monster print and seems to answer the question, “if growth is awesome, where are the 
jobs?”. This is supportive of the growth reboot and increasing the probability of US growth 
remaining above trend into YE26 and beyond. Now, this likely lower the CPI bar to see a 
hike and that will likely be reflected in asset prices now and into next Friday’s CPI. After the 
dust settles, this is an economy that remains in good-to-great shape with a consumer that 
has cash / spending said cash, businesses doing great despite the uncertainty from 
geopolitics. There will be a time to get long, potentially irresponsibly long, but we think it 
prudent to wait until we see the CPI print. The rationale is that we don’t have a strong 
indication on how many hikes the Fed deems appropriate, 25bp? 50bp? 100bp? Obviously 
this is data dependent, but the general view is that we are looking at 1-2x hikes and not an 
elongated hiking cycle. The former can be endured by Equities and that latter is likely to end 
the bull market. With an assumption of ~50k jobs added, JPM ECON (Joe Lupton) sees 
Waller’s speech as flagging Core PCE MoM of 0.30%, 2.8% YoY, as the bar that needs to 
be exceeded for the Fed to HIKE.

market is currently pricing a 63% probability of a 25bp hike at the Sep 16 FOMC meeting.

US MKT INTEL VIEW 
We maintain our Tactically Cautious / Neutral view ahead of Friday’s CPI report. The 
near-term setup remains binary: a dovish print could ease the pressure from yields and 
rates volatility, allowing investors to refocus on resilient consumer and corporate earnings

growth, while a hawkish print shall keep the September Fed meeting live with rising bond 
vol pressuring stocks. Beyond these immediate catalysts, our base case remains

POSITIONING INTELLIGENCE – Weekly Wrap | Let the Re-Gross Begin? 


[Page 6]
Summary:  Not a lot has changed at the aggregate level over the past week, despite 
market gyrations, but there are some potential themes emerging such as further re-grossing 
by HFs, a pause in US Health Care, and a bottoming of Cyclicals vs. Defensives. 
  
Key Points: 
1. HF Re-grossing picked up this week:  Globally HFs added gross exposure on 4 of 
the past 5 days for a +1.3z increase in the past week, the highest since late 
June. 
2. Net changes were muted this week…: HF flows were mixed day-to-day but ended 
with a small sell skew.  We saw a similar thing for our US TPM and the level of 
positioning for our US TPM remains at 40th %-tile, while global HF nets are 40-
50th %-tile on a 5yr basis. 
3. …But sentiment turning less constructive:  Both retail and institutional sentiment 
appear to be reversing lower / pausing, after prior increases. 
4. US Health Care could be topping out for now: Retail sentiment is starting to 
drop from ATHs, HFs are selling, and ETF flows are pausing at highs. 
5. Cyclicals vs. Defensives…will strong growth put cyclicals back in favor?  
Energy is being bought by HFs and relative net positioning between the groups is 
fairly neutral when excluding TMT and Financials.  In addition, Retail sentiment 
between the two is quite low.  The potential for cyclicals to outperform seems 
present, as long as yields don’t rise so quickly that equities turn very risk off. 
  
VIEWS ON TECH MOMENTUM FROM JPM TMT SPECIALIST MARK SCHILSKY: full 
note is here 
•            Momentum Died! Long Live Momentum!: A lot of your favorite Internet and 
Software longs have bounced hard off the June bottom. Take for instance: DASH 
(+40% since mid-June), ABNB (+40%) SHOP (+30%), BKNG (+20%), TEAM 
(+150%), NOW (+58%), MSFT (+42%), etc. Is a chunk of these moves attributable to 
idiosyncrasies related to solid earnings prints? For many, yes. But for both sub-


[Page 7]
sectors, let’s just take a step back and acknowledge that momentum peaked 
and started ‘dying’ right around June 22nd, as exemplified by the JPMPURE. 
That was two days before MU reported Q3 earnings - long memory was the most 
crowded trade that I’ve seen in a very long time. Was it all MU’s fault? No. It was 
likely triggered by sheer buyers’ exhaustion. But the fact remains the same: 
momentum peaked and many of your favorite Internet/Software longs ‘magically’ 
came back to life. Does this mean that fundamentals universally got better across 
FDN and IGV? No. But it does mean that Semis continue to drive the bus in 
tech…for now.  
• 
Last Schilsky Whispers I wrote: “AI is the Only Trade in the Market - Still: The market 
remains bifurcated – either your company is an AI Winner (Semis) or an AI Loser 
(Internet & Software). On any given day, SOXX and IGV move in different directions. 
As such, most of my high level investor discussions focus on one major topic: when 
will the party end? Put another way, tech investors have made so much money in 
Semis this year so quickly that they are looking for potential warning signs that the 
music is about to stop. Predicting such an end is incredibly difficult. As such, 
investors are searching for forward-looking indicators that might suggest the AI party 
is nearing a peak. Two are worth highlighting: 1) A slowdown in the growth of the 
annualized run-rate revenues of the major AI labs. If there is any sort of second 
derivative ‘kink’ in their growth algorithms, that could portend a future problem for the 
AI trade. 2) Scaling laws being upheld. We really haven’t had fully Blackwell-trained 
models on the market quite yet. No one truly knows if Blackwell will result in scaling 
laws being upheld (although it seems evident to me that Blackwell will result in a 
step-change in LLM capabilities). It is highly likely we will be getting some major 
upgrades from several of the major AI labs by year end. They better be great…” I 
think this still holds up.  
  
THE JPM VIEW – full note is here 


[Page 8]
•         The DM monetary policy repricing has not dented risk assets, consistent with our mid-
year outlook view that slow and gradual hiking would not derail the fundamentally 
supportive macro backdrop into year-end. 
•         In equities, despite investors’ sentiment being quite cautious here, we reiterate our 
constructive outlook into year-end with a gradual, rotation-led advance, rather than an 
indiscriminate, beta-driven surge. After Jackson Hole, the ‘Fed-on-hold Goldilocks’ scenario 
looks less likely, delaying conviction in the rally broadening. Nasdaq/S&P 500 
outperformance vs. equal weight/Russell 2000 keeps Tech, Quality Growth and 
Hyperscalers in the lead into an expected Fed hike. 
•         AI capex is reshaping hyperscaler capital allocation, shifting the mix away from 
buyback-driven float shrinkage toward incremental equity supply (equity raises and the IPO 
pipeline). This dilutes the mega-cap “marginal bid” that has disproportionately supported 
index levels and raises the hurdle for drawdown resilience unless earnings breadth 
improves or the equity risk premium adjusts. 
•         Market hike pricing is running above our projected monetary policy paths across DM. 
Even so, risks are skewed to more hikes than we currently forecast. We have a bearish bias 
on the UST belly (valuations still rich), but real money positioning screening already 
underweight makes us cautious on timing. We continue to recommend long 10Y Bund 
cross-market vs. UST. 
•         In FX, hawkish Fed repricing has paused USD weakness and triggered a modest 
rebound. After strong payrolls today, the CPI report next week will remain the short-term 
catalyst into the September meeting. A slow and gradual Fed hiking remains USD 
supportive but does not derail the broader FX carry theme and our strong preference for 
high yielders. We re-enter USD longs vs. DM low yielders (SEK and CAD). We continue to 
hold tactical long JPY positions, expressed via DM low yielders (short SEK/JPY) and stay 
short CAD/MXN as our benchmark carry expression. 
•         In commodities, we remain low-intensity bullish gold, using Fed repricing bumps 
(September meeting and terminal) as buying opportunities, while the debasement trade has 
lost traction after Jackson Hole. In oil, persistent uncertainty around conflict duration leaves 


[Page 9]
a looming threat of higher Brent risk premiums into year-end, with potential impacts tilting 
our 4Q path upward. 
•         In US HG credit, even after heavy August supply, we expect the market can absorb 
September issuance given the constructive macro narrative and attractive all-in yields. The 
main challenge is excess rates volatility, not fundamentals. 
  
 
 
ROADMAP TO 26Q4 
As we wind down the quarter, we wanted to share some of the catalytic events as we 
approach Q4. So far, midterm election seasonality has failed to materialize; and, at this 
point, it seems unlikely to do so with the dominance of A, an unresolved conflict in the 
Middle East, and the potential for 26Q3 earnings season (which spans the election) to 
deliver another quarter of more than 20% EPS growth with widening margins. 
If we are wrong on midterm seasonality, buy Healthcare, which is typically the best 
performing sector into midterms. Then, size up on Election Day as the post-election setup is 
positive.
