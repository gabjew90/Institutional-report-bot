All eyes on inflation and what it means for a Sep hike 
Focus this week will be on August CPI report (Fri) and what it implies for core PCE, 
which could decide whether the Fed hikes rates in September, especially after a hawkish 
payroll print last week. Our economists expect the data to be firm enough for the FOMC 
to follow through with a hike (they forecast core CPI to rise a trend-like 0.22% m/m in 
August, see preview on page 3), and they continue to expect 75bp of hikes this year. Our 
strategists suggest owning Large Cap value in the case of Fed hike concerns. 
Watch this week's long-end buyback operation 
Secretary Bessent's decision to raise the cap on 10y+ buybacks is consistent with a 
more activist approach to debt management (see: UST buybacks = activist debt 
management), a shift that was also reflected in the revised forward guidance at the Aug 
refunding (see: Aug refunding recap). The focus now shifts to this week's long-end 
buyback operation, the first since the higher limits were introduced. A UST that doesn’t 
take down near the max will temper market expectations for additional steps and likely 
see long-end rates cheapen further. 
Fed rate hikes concerns? Value over Value traps 
Any catalyst that pushes credit concerns to the forefront, be it Fed rate hikes, off 
balance sheet financing, private lending hiccups, or an unanticipated increase in long 
rates, could hasten the pain. Across major credit stress cycles since the 90s, the first 
half of the move in spreads from trough to peak took six times longer than the second 
half, and the second half was always accompanied by negative S&P 500 returns. Large 
Cap Value has provided a ballast for investors, despite AI-fueled spending. Value-
oriented industries that screen well include Oil, Gas & Consumable Fuels, Distributors, 
Metals & Mining, Banks and Leisure Products, and Insurance and Consumer Finance have 
improved in August. But watch out for the Value Traps - cheap for a reason - which 
include Media and IT Services, where price deterioration is outpacing estimate cuts. See  
S&P 500 Relative Value Cheat Sheet and Stealth strength: Large cap value. 
Oracle: Attractive risk/reward into Sep 10th 1Q27 release 
Oracle reports Sep 10th (Thu). The stock is down -25% since 4Q26 earnings, and BofA 
analyst Tal Liani likes the risk/reward for Oracle. He believes Street consensus already 
captures the challenging balance sheet fundamentals, yet is not fully incorporating the 
likelihood of revenue growth acceleration related to reaching DC buildout milestones.

Large cap value: Large cap value ETFs offer exposure to favored sectors with strong 
returns. Invesco Large Cap Value ETF (PWV; 1-FV) is up 12% in three months (vs. S&P 
500 up 2%), with 65% allocation to financials, energy, materials. Putnam Focused Large 
Cap Value ETF (PVAL; 1-FV) has returned 16.8% CAGR over the last five years (vs. 12.8% 
for S&P 500) and holds less than half the tech & communications of the S&P 500 (23% 
vs. 48%). Avantis US Large Cap Value ETF (AVLV; 2-FV) has 40% exposure to financials 
and energy, with 7ppts higher expected YoY 2026 earnings growth than the Russell 1000 
(36% vs. 29%). 
TRFK: TRFK aims to track tech exposure to both hard & soft tech, along with over 20% 
exposure to machinery and electronic equipment. We have a 2-NV rating. They target 
companies generating at least 50% of their revenue from “the use, manipulation, 
transmission, or storage of data and the ancillary services”. The top 5 holdings (Nvidia, 
Broadcom, Micron, AMD, SK Hynix) are 39% of the portfolio, and ORCL is the 6th largest 
at 4.2%.

A compounding problem 
Crossing the $40tn debt threshold was likely not a catalyst for the recent rise in yields. 
However, a renewed focus on deficits is warranted given the rise in rates. The fiscal 
challenge is increasingly about interest expense, which now exceeds spending on both 
defense and Medicare and is still rising. The biggest long-run risk is a feedback loop in 
which higher rates raise deficits, larger deficits increase Treasury supply, and higher 
term premiums push borrowing costs even higher. 
The week ahead: All eyes on inflation 
All eyes will be on the Aug inflation data this week (PPI on Thur and CPI on Fri, preview 
below). We also get NY Fed inflation expectations (Tue), existing home sales (Thur), 
University of Michigan consumer sentiment (Fri) and federal budget balance (Fri). There 
is no Fedspeak since the Fed is in blackout before the Sep FOMC meeting. 
Data preview: Inflation to keep Sept hike on track 
This week's inflation data could be the decisive factor for a September hike. We expect 
the report to be firm enough for the FOMC to follow through. Specifically, we forecast 
core CPI to rise a trend-like 0.22% m/m in August and core PCE to increase 0.24% m/m. 
If correct, core PCE would be running at a 2.9% annualized pace and the year-over-year 
rate would edge up to 3.4%, both well above levels consistent with the Fed's 2% target. 
In our view, that should be enough to convince Chair Warsh that underlying inflation 
trends have not improved sufficiently and that higher rates are warranted.

Tal Liani

Oracle reports 1Q27 results on Thursday, September 10th. The stock is down -25% 
since 4Q26 earnings, primarily related to investor concerns around data center 
profitability as component costs increase and credit spreads widen. Into the print, we 
are focused on three areas: (1) data center capacity deployment and the pace of RPO 
conversation to revenues, (2) capital expenditures and funding requirements, and (3) 
trends across the core software business. We favor the risk/reward of Oracle, as we 
believe Street consensus already captures the challenging balance sheet fundamentals, 
yet is not fully incorporating the likelihood of revenue growth acceleration related to 
reaching DC buildout milestones.
