IDEAS & INSIGHTS – IN BRIEF 
•         SEMIS JULY WSTS:  Total industry sales fell to $142B (-10% M/M) in July, with 
Y/Y growth decelerating to +129% Y/Y, down from +143% Y/Y in June. The 
weakness in sales was primarily ASP-driven, with blended industry ASPs declining -
9% M/M, and total industry unit volumes remaining ~flat M/M. The M/M decline in 
sales was directionally in-line with seasonal trends and heavily skewed by monthly 
lumpiness in memory sales (total semiconductor sales ex-memory were ~flat M/M). 
We would also separately flag that company commentary across our sector over the 
last ~90 days points to demand continuing to accelerate from current levels, and thus 
we contend that fundamentals will likely follow suit.  Memory pricing growth 
decelerated but remains objectively strong. 
•         ASIA SEMIS: Gokul and team  met with 22 companies & attended the wider 
semicon event. Leading edge capacity shortage likely to worsen in 2027 & remains 
the primary bottleneck. Advanced packaging investments broadening out, with a 
focus on CoWoS in 2027 and SoIC in 2028. CoPoS development seems slower, 
EMIB-T progressing with variants being explored in the supply chain. High level of 


[Page 12]
interest in CPO, but lack of clarity on testing process flow, with Insertion 2 test likely 
skipped in initial production. Equipment suppliers and market share dynamics for 
CPO test insertion 1-4 and SiPh optical test are investors’ key focus. 
•         JAPAN EQUITIES:  At present, we think a range of ¥155-160/$ would be desirable 
for Japanese equities.  Considering the FY2025 average of ¥150/$ and corporate 
assumptions in earnings guidance averaging ¥153/$ for FY2026, we think this range 
would have a modest positive impact on corporate earnings in Japan and limit the 
drag on dollar-denominated returns for overseas investors. Sectors with large 
negative exposures to yen appreciation include transportation & logistics (mainly 
shipping, operating profit −1.6ppt), autos & transportation equipment (−1.1ppt), steel 
& nonferrous metals (−0.8ppt), machinery (−0.6ppt), and trading companies & 
wholesale (−0.6ppt). In contrast, the only positive exposure is in electric power & gas 
(+0.4%).  
•         JAPAN FLOWS & FX:  Net foreign equity purchases of ¥0.6trn (JPMe: -¥1.7trn) 
and net foreign bond purchases of ¥2.3trn (JPMe: +¥0.9trn) by “Trust Accounts,” 
which reflect pension funds’ activities. Both figures point to continued net 
accumulation of foreign assets — contrary to prevailing market speculation which 
expects a shift towards domestic assets from foreign assets. 
•         CATALYSTS TODAY:  China PPI, CPI. Taiwan Trade. Apple to unveil new 
iPhones. US Treasury begins expanded buybacks of longer-dated bonds. 
Republicans open their first national midterm convention in Dallas.

EMERGING MARKETS MONETIZATION – Ellen Wang / Victoria Campos 
The global momentum/tech rebound drove EM 7% higher since the July-end low, 
despite choppy price action. Broadening and AI continue to dominate the narrative in 
EM, with the recent global macro backdrop net supportive. We are bullish on EM 
given (i) improving macro; (ii) weaker USD; (iii) AI CapEx tailwinds, but continue to 
expect divergence in EM performance at the country/sector level and view country-
relative trades as more relevant for the monetization strategy in this environment.  
•         EM MONETIZATION MENU – Key themes and trades include: (i) 
TACTICALLY LONG China H-shares over A-shares: the pair has been trading well 
in July given the momentum rotation. Despite this outperformance, H-shares still lag 
A-shares YTD and we see further room into China Tech earnings next week. (ii) We 
also express this view via a TACTICALLY LONG in China Internet (KWEB). (iii) 
TACTICALLY LONG Taiwan following the Global Tech selloff in July. Taiwan offers 
a less volatile exposure to AI and Asia Tech relative to Korea. 
• HEDGES – (i) We like long LatAm (ILF) as a diversifying play 
against weakness in the EM AI theme and for relative resilience 
against geopolitical risks. (ii) India (INDA) equity strength has been 
driven in part by a rotation during the AI de-risking phase, but 
foreign investor flows have also turned more supportive again, 
supported by improving macro. You may consider a L/S pair with 
LONG India and SHORT Southeast Asia (ASEA).

Summary: Not a lot has changed at the aggregate level over the past week, despite 
market gyrations, but there are some potential themes emerging such as further re-
grossing by HFs, a pause in US Health Care, and a bottoming of Cyclicals vs. 
Defensives. 
Key Points: 
•         HF Re-grossing picked up this week:  Globally HFs added gross 
exposure on 4 of the past 5 days for a +1.3z increase in the past week, the 
highest since late June. 
•         Net changes were muted this week…: HF flows were mixed day-to-day 
but ended with a small sell skew.  We saw a similar thing for our US TPM 
and the level of positioning for our US TPM remains at 40th %-tile, while 
global HF nets are 40-50th %-tile on a 5yr basis. 
• …But sentiment turning less constructive:  Both retail and institutional 
sentiment appear to be reversing lower / pausing, after prior increases. 
• US Health Care could be topping out for now:  Retail sentiment is 
starting to drop from ATHs, HFs are selling, and ETF flows are pausing at 
highs. 
•         Cyclicals vs. Defensives…will strong growth put cyclicals back in 
favor?  Energy is being bought by HFs and relative net positioning between 
the groups is fairly neutral when excluding TMT and Financials.  In addition, 
Retail sentiment between the two is quite low.  The potential for cyclicals to
