Day 1 of Communacopia reinforced a broadly bullish view on AI infrastructure, software and enterprise
adoption, with the central theme being the shift from experimentation to production use cases (its only
Day 1 so lets see how rest of week shapes up). Across OpenAI, Alphabet, SAP, Snowflake and Databricks,
the debate is moving from model capability to cost per task, governance, model choice and workflow
automation, with enterprises increasingly looking for model-agnostic platforms that can route work to the
best and cheapest model while protecting data. OpenAI and Google both sounded highly confident on
demand and monetisation, while SAP (notable more upbeat than last year, investors will debate the
multiple given the bounce), Snowflake and Databricks positioned themselves as critical enterprise AI
control layers built around agents, governed data, consumption-based pricing and open ecosystems (will
be interesting to see more software names this week as investor debate here certainly 2 way and asking
how much more than can bounce without revenue acceleration). On infrastructure, Broadcom, Arista,
Infineon and Nebius all pointed to strong AI demand and improving visibility, with constraints shifting
toward supply, power, data-centre capacity and execution rather than end demand. The broader conclusion
is that AI is accelerating across the stack — from semicap and networking to enterprise software,
payments and personal agents (while not at the conference Instinct and personal AI agents getting some
debate that caused online travel names to roll.) Also note later today Apple new launch with the new Apple
CEO. Company Notes OpenAI
OpenAI bullish on both demand and monetisation, highlighting Astra as its largest-ever training run and
pointing to frontier-level performance at materially lower cost. Customers are increasingly focused on cost
per task completed rather than cost per token, while enterprise adoption is accelerating, agentic products
now have 25m users, and usage intensity remains well ahead of peers. Despite compute constraints,
particularly in voice, revenue per GPU is rising and unit costs are falling. Strategically, OpenAI sees
cybersecurity as the biggest 12-month opportunity, with life sciences the longer-term value-based
monetisation opportunity. Pricing is expected to shift from seats to consumption and ultimately outcome-
based models, while consumer momentum remains strong at c.1bn users, with subscription upsell and ads
already contributing meaningfully. Prometheus
Prometheus was deliberately light on detail, but the message was centred on AI for the physical economy
— using AI as an engineering and manufacturing tool to build complex products faster. The company
discussed the idea of an “artificial general engineer”, with likely early use cases in jet engines, rockets and

complex casting. The broader read-across is that life sciences is becoming an increasingly crowded AI
opportunity, with both OpenAI and Google also repeatedly highlighting it as a major strategic focus.
Certainly not the bearish near term catalyst for DSY I had expected. Alphabet
Alphabet emphasised that its AI advantage comes from a vertically integrated stack spanning TPUs,
Gemini models, data, security and applications. Management argued this delivers superior price-
performance and lets Google capture value across multiple AI monetisation layers. Enterprise adoption of
Gemini is strong, with over 90% of Fortune 100 companies using Gemini Enterprise as an agentic
platform to automate workflows and reason over private data. Google Cloud is scaling the opportunity
through industry-specific solutions, system-integrator partnerships, proprietary data integrations and
forward-deployed engineers helping customers build frontier AI use cases. SAP
SAP was notably more confident than last year, with Christian Klein sounding upbeat on both the AI
roadmap and financial delivery. The company expects to reach 200 AI agents by month-end and remains
confident in hitting 400 agents by year-end, focused on complex enterprise use cases. Early feedback on
Joule Work is positive, with more than 100 customers ahead of the autumn launch, and SAP is moving
sales incentives toward AI consumption units. A key message was model and front-end agnosticism: SAP
expects agents to run across multiple models, including cheaper options from Mistral and open-source
providers, reducing inference costs versus relying on a single frontier model. Management also sounded
more relaxed on gross margins (been model agnostic helps), the geopolitical backdrop (no longer a major
concern to its FY guide) and CCB, marking a clear positive change in tone. Snowflake
Snowflake’s message was that its AI opportunity is expanding beyond data warehousing into becoming the
orchestration, governance and application layer for enterprise AI. Near-term upside comes from AI
accelerating migrations and deployments, while the longer-term moat is around governed data, agentic
workflows, security, cost optimisation and application development. Management highlighted growing AI
consumption and strong demand for ROI-led use cases such as invoice processing, supply-chain
optimisation and customer data platforms. Snowflake also stressed an open ecosystem approach,
positioning itself as the layer that lets enterprises use whichever models they choose, with retention
increasingly dependent on platform value rather than proprietary data formats. Databricks
Databricks struck a bullish tone on the convergence of data, AI and security, positioning its advantage
around one copy of enterprise data, governed through Unity Catalog, with AI built natively on top. Ali
Ghodsi argued that enterprise AI adoption is still constrained by the gap between model capability and real
operational deployment, which Databricks is trying to close through products such as Genie and Unity AI
Gateway. Genie turns governed enterprise data into a conversational BI and analyst layer, while Unity AI
Gateway routes tasks to the best and cheapest model while centralising access, spend, logging and
guardrails. Security was also a major theme as agents handle more sensitive workflows, with Lakebase
highlighted as a large opportunity and Databricks framing the next phase of AI as governed, cost-aware
production deployment. Broadcom
Broadcom remains one of the clearest beneficiaries of the AI infrastructure buildout, with Hock Tan
reaffirming FY27/FY28 AI revenue targets of $115bn / $230bn and stressing that demand visibility is


[Page 3]
strong while supply remains constrained. The company’s moat is its combination of custom silicon,
advanced packaging and AI networking, supported by deep partnerships with strategic customers
including frontier AI developers. Broadcom also highlighted the $35bn XPV partnership, designed to
accelerate more than 20GW of AI compute buildout while limiting balance-sheet risk. The key constraint
has shifted from demand to physical infrastructure — especially power, permitting and data-centre
construction timelines — while management expects strong AI-driven revenue and cash-flow growth to
support ongoing dividends and buybacks. Arista
Arista reiterated its 2026 revenue and margin guidance, supported by improved supply-chain visibility and
strong demand across AI infrastructure and enterprise campus deployments. The key AI opportunity
remains networking for GPU clusters, especially back-end networks connecting accelerators. Customers
are increasingly using scale-across deployments as power, cooling and data-centre space constraints force
clusters across multiple locations. Looking ahead, price increases and supply-chain efficiencies could help
Arista sustain gross margins into 2027 despite lapping tariff refunds. Infineon
Infineon struck a confident tone, with near-term growth constrained more by supply than demand,
especially in AI data-centre power. Management said execution is ahead of plan, pricing remains
favourable and capacity is being expanded faster, supported by growing customer interest in multi-year
reservation agreements. Infineon also highlighted its strong position in Nvidia’s MGX ecosystem as power
architectures move toward 800V, increasing content per rack. Beyond AI, backlog has rebounded to
around €30bn, inventories have normalised and autos growth is running at roughly 9-10% on content gains
and share expansion. IR also sounded confident that the FY27 data-centre target of €2.5bn will be
materially raised in November, with management suggesting €2.8bn would not qualify as a material
upgrade. Visa
Visa painted a resilient picture of the US consumer, with payment volumes holding at 6-8% growth over
the past 18 months despite weak sentiment. Global transactions are still growing around 10% YoY, while
cross-border volumes have accelerated to 14%, led by e-commerce rather than travel. On AI, Visa sees
itself as a trust and infrastructure layer for agentic commerce, using generative AI internally to improve
productivity while developing a Trusted Agent Protocol for autonomous payments. The key takeaway is
that trust will determine adoption: only 25% of consumers are comfortable letting AI agents pay
autonomously, but this rises to over 60% when Visa is involved. Muse / Instinct
While not at the conference Investors are discussing Instinct, an invite-only personal agent that users can
text or call like an EA. It connects to email, messages, calendar and a cloud computer to book flights,
shop, cancel subscriptions and complete web workflows, with invites metered as compute scales. The
debate is whether Instinct can win as a standalone assistant now that Meta Muse has launched with
broader distribution through WhatsApp and app channels. Trust remains the main concern (some also
asking if this is an Apple opportunity), particularly around inbox permissions, autonomous emails and data
retention. Instinct’s edge is its zero-UI, action-completion model, while competitors benefit from
distribution, confirmation gates, self-hosting or tighter platform integration. Nebius

Nebius remains focused on scaling AI infrastructure, with demand visibility extending into 2028 and a
continued emphasis on maintaining a diversified customer base across contract durations. The Palantir
partnership strengthens enterprise distribution and supports the company’s full-stack platform strategy.
Capacity expansion appears on track, while regulatory complexity could become an industry-wide supply
constraint and potentially support margins. Contract economics are also improving, creating better
payback periods, stronger margins and more financing flexibility. REA interesting M&A comments
REA’s comments were interesting in the context of its prior attempt to buy Rightmove. Management
suggested any cross-border move would need to target a highly profitable, market-leading business with
clear AI monetisation potential — criteria that still point back toward a Rightmove type business...
