From AfD To Astra: Goldman Delta-One Boss Says 
'Micro Is Fine, Macro's Getting Harder' 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, SEP 08, 2026 - 03:56 AM 
While Americans digest the fruits of the Labor Day grill, the market is still chewing on a monster 
August payrolls print, with unemployment sat at 4.1%, and September hike odds lurched back 
toward 60% (after the Warsh-Waller whiplash). However, Friday’s CPI print is now the binary 
event of the week.  
 
Goldman Sachs Delta-One desk head Rich Privorotsky’s morning note (pro subs can read the 
note in full here) to clients is less interested in the barbecue and more interested in the split 
screen: models that keep getting cheaper and better versus an oil market that will not stay in its 
lane. 
Payrolls print and "more pressure on The Fed" 
Net-net, Privorotsky calls the NFP report firmer. 


[Page 2]
 
There are seasonal/payback excuses after the soft summer prints, but the front-end still repriced 
for Fed expectations. 
The long end is stable - Treasury flagged a maximum-size buyback for the 9th - which is the 
kind of color that trading desks notice when duration is the only thing keeping the equity multiple 
from slamming into $97 crude. 
 
The setup into Friday: a benign CPI gives the Committee room to wait. A hot core “probably 
solidifies” a September hike. Markets have about 60bps of tightening priced through year-end; a 
mechanical September reprice could take that to 75. The offset to that (which is doing a lot of 
the heavy lifting), is strong nominal growth. 
In that world, equities can stay resilient even as the funds rate gets a little more real. 
"Oil remains the biggest macro problem" 
Brent is back around $97, (real) tanker traffic through Hormuz has fallen to its lowest since May, 
but diesel is the product that actually hurts. 


[Page 3]
 
 
European winter gas inventories are about to start mattering... 
 
...and the SPR is down to roughly 287 million barrels - the policy cushion that used to exist 
when Washington wanted a headline and a release is gone. 


[Page 4]
 
 
The US is still trying to keep the routes functioning, including a months-long mine-clearing effort. 
Iran is now threatening a more formal restricted zone around the Strait. 
On the face of it, Privorotsky writes, things look bad. 
Then comes the trader’s hedge: skate where the puck is going. Iran’s maximum political 
leverage is probably now, ahead of U.S. political events, while Washington has an equally 
obvious incentive to get energy prices lower. Evidence of real diplomacy remains thin. 
Ever the optimist for a incentivized (low energy prices, higher approval rating) reflexive/TACO 
move, Privorotsk's gut says:"there is still scope for a diplomatic headline because the pressure 
on both sides is becoming acute." 
Until that headline arrives, energy is not just color, it's the constraint. 
Astra "Feels Important" 
The micro side of the note is the AI complex, again. 
Privorotsky flags Astra as important: materially more token-efficient on a run of agentic 
workloads, which means cost per completed task at the frontier can look a lot better than the 
last generation. The capability jump is "real" and he points at SoftBank up another 10% as the 
tape’s opinion. 


[Page 5]
 
 
The market read-through, in his words, is simple: better models reduce friction, improve 
economics, and encourage more usage, which ultimately supports more infrastructure spend. 
The leap from one generation to the next is happening at an exponential pace. 
That is “positive for the broader AI chain and another reason not to be a hater on tech/AI here.”  
He even drops the ARC-AGI-3 / GPT-6 Astra side-note for anyone still pretending the evals are 
a rounding error.

Europe: AfD, Bardella, watch OATs 
Away from chips and Hormuz: AfD took 43.8% in Saxony-Anhalt and 39 of 83 seats, three short 
of an outright majority and a touch stronger than the 40–41% polling. Expected, he says. 
The question is whether it lights a fire under the German establishment to deliver the 
growth/reform agenda it keeps promising and not delivering. 
France is the one he would actually trade.  
Budget season is starting. National Rally’s Jordan Bardella: “What is certain is that in this 
budget we will very firmly oppose tax increases - increases in taxes on French people.”  
 
Watch OATs is Privorotsky's simple advice. 
The week you actually have to trade 
PPI Thursday. CPI Friday. ECB Thursday. Apple Wednesday. Oracle and Adobe Thursday. 
A packed TMT conference slate, including Goldman’s own Communacopia. 
Apple’s foldable, if it shows up, makes the event “unusually interesting.” 
Oracle is the AI-infrastructure data point. Adobe is another software print. 
The ECB is “nailed on” for 25bps; the only question is whether it is a dovish hike or a hawkish 
one. 
Pricing of three hikes, he adds, feels ambitious / fully priced.

The Bottom Line 
“Micro is getting more encouraging while the macro is getting harder.” 
Privorotsky sees AI capability accelerating, and the conference and earnings calendar this week 
should spit out plenty of constructive TMT data. Asia tech is already trading like it believes that. 
Against that: energy is objectively problematic, and inflation and rates are back in the game. 
So he stays constructive on the AI trade and treats the real risks as oil, inflation, and rates. 
Institutional positioning, in his read, is light. That is the wall-of-worry setup - the one that lets the 
complex grind higher until the front end, or the Strait, decides the micro story is no longer 
allowed to ignore the macro one. 
Skate to where the puck is 'going' (forgive the earlier error) they say... well, traders are heading 
towards Friday at full speed, but what may really matter is wherever Hormuz is by the close.
