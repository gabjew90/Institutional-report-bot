AI Bull, Macro Bear
The fundamental AI bull case keeps strengthening, but the tape is becoming harder to read. The yen is
surging, tech CDS spreads have widened and rates have moved higher, yet NDX refuses to properly
break.
Is leverage quietly draining from the system, with the real downside still ahead? Or is tech absorbing
every macro shock before the next AI leg higher? With correlation and straddles near the floor, the
market is offering a relatively cheap way to play the answer.
AI bull case
As Privorotsky argues, AI keeps breaking barriers. Frontier models are making real progress on
previously unsolved math and science problems, including OpenAI’s proposed Navier–Stokes solution.
Astra, trained across more than 100,000 GPUs and requiring 70–100MW at peak, roughly a small
city, is already moving materially beyond prior models. This is not fully self-recursive learning, but AI is
increasingly accelerating the research behind the next generation of AI.
The demand tape is equally hard to fade. Google Cloud’s $100m-plus deals are growing more than 2x
QoQ and YoY, while AI-server payback is under two years, and roughly half that on proprietary silicon.
Broadcom says demand exceeds its $115bn FY27 outlook, with power, site readiness and supply now
the constraints.
The bear case is not demand, but who ultimately captures the economics. For now, the topline may
simply be growing too fast for that to matter.
The implication: if tech breaks from here, weak end-demand is unlikely to be the catalyst. The more
credible threat is macro: higher rates, a stronger yen and leverage leaving the system.

The yen stress test
Bessent was unusually explicit overnight: “I am the house now … and you can bet against me if you
want.” Whatever the rhetoric, the yen continues to strengthen as markets price BOJ tightening and
repatriation.
The second-order risk is a yen-funded carry unwind, with capital returning to Japanese bonds and
equities and leverage quietly draining from US markets. A stronger yen normally means risk-off and
higher vol, yet NDX has largely absorbed the move. Is the recent heaviness an early warning, or
another sign of resilience before the next AI leg higher?

Tech and CDS
Tech CDS spreads have paused lately, but the widening since mid-August remains notable. The July
spike coincided with an NDX sell-off. This time, NDX has barely reacted. Credit is flashing caution, but
equities are refusing to confirm it.

Tech and rates

increasingly looks like resilience.

Convexity is the trade
Realized correlation and straddles are dead. Six-month realized correlation across the SPX has fallen
to just 11. Only two other periods in the past 25 years have seen similarly depressed levels: February
2007 and January 2018, just before “Volmageddon.”
Garrett notes that such extremes have rarely proved sustainable, typically ending in an explosive
return of index vol convexity. Meanwhile, SPX straddles are hovering near their yearly lows.
The setup is unusually clean: AI fundamentals remain strong, macro and credit are flashing stress, yet
price refuses to confirm either direction. With correlation, straddles and midterm event premium near
the floor, convexity remains cheap. Long SPX straddles offer a clean two-sided expression of the
