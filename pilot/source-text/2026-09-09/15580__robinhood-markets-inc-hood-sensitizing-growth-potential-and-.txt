Robinhood Markets Inc. (HOOD): Sensitizing growth potential and value 
in HOOD’s scaling Rothera prediction markets exchange
7 September 2026 | 9:21PM EDT
Since shortly after its May launch, Rothera, Buy-rated HOOD’s predictions 
market JV, has been the 3rd-5th largest global prediction markets exchange by 
trading volume. After ~$150mn of annualized top-line in its first quarter of 
operation, we believe that Rothera’s growth could continue, driven by 1) 
potential depth of liquidity across the exchange, enabled by HOOD’s large installed 
base of retail customers combined with market maker engagement on the platform; 
2) low fee rates relative to other prediction markets exchanges, which could entice 
other brokers to Rothera over time; and 3) expansion opportunities by adding other 
brokers and products to the exchange. 
We believe Rothera has meaningful equity value, and we conduct a 2027E 
base/bull/bear case scenario analysis to assess revenue, profitability and 
potential value. In our base case, we estimate $307mn of Rothera revenue (5% of 
HOOD 2027E GSe total revenue), equating to 1% market share, and $5.1-5.4bn of 
equity value. In our bull case, we estimate $359-906mn of Rothera revenue (5-13% 
of HOOD 2027E GSe total revenue), equating to 2-4% market share, and 
$6.7-19.5bn of equity value, representing 32-258% upside to the base case. In our 
bear case, we estimate $91-242mn of Rothera revenue (1-4% of HOOD 2027E GSe 
total revenue), equating to 0.5-1% market share, and $1.0-3.6bn of equity value, 
representing 35-79% downside to the base case.  
Rothera could account for a meaningful portion of HOOD’s total prediction 
markets revenue, and we see it is a key driver of our above-consensus 
predictions markets revenue forecasts and a contributor to our above-Street 
total revenue estimates. Our 2027E/28 forecasts for $307mn/444mn of Rothera 
revenue (2027E based on the implied midpoint of our base case) and 
$943mn/1,150mn of total prediction markets net revenue reflect 10%/14% upside 
to consensus. Our 2027E/28 total revenue forecasts are 3% above the Street. 
HOOD currently trades at 39.3x FY+2 GSe P/E, or a 87th %ile P/E since 2021. 
However, in both our base and bull cases, after backing out estimated Rothera 
equity value, the balance of the HOOD business appears to trade at a less 
expensive, and thus more attractive, P/E. Excluding our base case Rothera equity 
value, remaining HOOD equity value (based on current HOOD market cap) trades at 
only 39.2-39.3x FY+2 GSe P/E, or 87th %ile trailing 5-year levels. In our bull case, the 
balance of HOOD would trade at only 37.0-38.9x FY+2 GSe P/E, or 83th-86th %ile 
levels. In our bear case, the balance of HOOD would trade at 39.6-39.8x FY+2 GSe 
P/E, or 87th-88th %ile levels.

PM summary 
 
 
Rothera, a prediction markets exchange/designated contract market (DCM) JV 
between Robinhood and Susquehanna International Group (SIG), has scaled rapidly 
since its late-May launch to ~2bn/1bn contracts traded in July/August. We believe 
that growth could continue from here. Rothera ownership is split 45%/45%/10% 
across HOOD/SIG/MIAX, with HOOD the controlling partner. HOOD consolidates 100% 
of Rothera revenue and expenses and distributes 55% of Rothera net income through 
non-controlling interest. Rothera began by offering sports contracts but has recently 
started to diversify into politics/economics contracts (each 1% of Aug. 2026 volume). 
Over the first 41 days of operation in 2Q26, the company generated $17mn of revenue, 
or ~$150mn annualized, at an estimated ~45% pre-tax margin. We see growth and 
improved profitability from here, catalyzed by the following factors: 
Rothera is positioned to develop meaningful depth of liquidity. In our view, 
1.
liquidity is important in prediction markets, given the breadth of available contracts 
that is more similar to fixed income than equity markets and results in more 
fragmented liquidity. Rothera can drive liquidity due to 1) SIG’s expertise as a highly 
scaled market maker on the platform, with its 45% equity stake aligning incentives to 
Rothera’s growth and 2) HOOD’s large retail customer base (~14mn monthly active 
users) whose FCM flow we expect to be increasingly routed to Rothera over time. 
Rothera offers the lowest retail fee rate across DCMs, which should attract 
2.
incremental FCM and retail adoption. Our analysis suggests that most of the event 
contracts trade on Rothera are priced between $0.25-0.30 (or $0.70-0.75 - these 
two price ranges yield the same fee rates, as pricing is symmetrical, and based on the 
distance from a 50/50 contract [i.e., $0.50]). This suggests one-sided retail taker 
DCM fee rates of 0.38-0.42% per $1 notional contract, meaningfully below the 
~1.17-1.31% rates for peers. We lay out the Rothera pricing model, which is dynamic 
and based on both the type of customer trading on the DCM and the contract price 
at the time of execution; lower-priced trades have lower fees, which is designed to 
drive liquidity. 
Rothera could drive further growth by adding other FCMs and diversifying into 
3.
other exchange-traded products, e.g., perpetual futures. Currently, HOOD is the 
only FCM on Rothera. Over time, Rothera could add other FCMs, potentially 
attracted by lower pricing, which would deepen liquidity. Further, while Rothera 
currently only offers event contracts, its exchange licenses could allow it to add 
other products over time, e.g., perpetual futures. 
As Rothera scales, we expect its margin profile to improve as upfront 
4.
investment needs decline and as the exchange generates greater fixed cost 
leverage on a growing revenue base. We estimate Rothera’s 2026E pre-tax margin 
at ~45%. However, over time, we believe margins could approach the ~55-70% 
range of established derivatives exchange peers with greater scale. 
With the launch of Rothera, HOOD can now capture both DCM and FCM prediction 
markets fees (vs. only FCM fees previously), enabling it to largely maintain its 
previous fee rate on prediction markets, even while reducing fees for customers. We

Overviewing Rothera: Background, pricing model, profitability profile and growth potential 
 
 
Rothera is a prediction markets exchange / DCM established as a joint venture 
between Robinhood and SIG - see our report on the announced JV here), which has 
seen healthy growth since its late-May launch to 1.7bn/0.6bn of July/August 
volumes, representing the 3rd/5th largest prediction markets DCMs, with ~3%/1% 
of July/August notional volume share (Exhibit 1, Exhibit 2). The company was 
profitable in its first 41 days of existence during 2Q26, reaching a $150mn 
annualized revenue run rate and delivering an estimated ~45% pre-tax margin. We 
expect growth to continue, given 1) the ability to deliver breadth of liquidity 
through a combination of strong distribution through HOOD’s retail customers and 
SIG’s market making expertise and 2) Rothera’s below-market fee rates that could 
act as a customer acquisition tool, especially for other FCMs that might wish to 
execute on the exchange, with Rothera’s current two-sided fee rate range 
(reflecting that each contract has a buyer fee and a seller fee) at 0.79-0.90% 
(Exhibit 12, Exhibit 14). Finally, we expect Rothera’s profitability to increase as well 
through scale. We provide further detail on the JV’s structure, HOOD’s accounting, and 
profitability below. 
What are prediction markets / market structure: Prediction markets are fully 
n
funded futures contracts. A buyer buys the contract for a price of between $0-1. 
When the contract expires, the contract settles at either $0 or $1. In terms of market 
structure and how the business is monetized, there are two fee streams that are 
monetized, Exchanges, also known as DCMs, charge fees for prediction markets 
execution. Brokerages, also known as FCMs, charge brokerage fees on each 
predictions market trade. 
Legal/regulatory backdrop: Notwithstanding the CFTC putting out a proposed 
n
rulemaking in June 2026 proposing that it should regulate prediction markets (NPR 
here), there are a number of outstanding lawsuits related to the legality of prediction 
markets. The litigation mainly centers around whether certain prediction markets, 
specifically sports-related contracts, are in fact sports gambling and thus would be 
subject to state gambling regulations. Litigation has come from a variety of sources, 
including a number of US states’ attorneys general. Most recently, the US 9th Circuit 
Court of Appeals ruled on August 28th that Nevada litigation against Kalshi, a 
prediction markets exchange, could proceed. Separately, there is an ongoing 2026 
legal challenge from CME as to whether perpetual futures (perps) are futures or 
swaps. If the challenge were successful in classifying perps as swaps, this would 
prevent exchanges from adding or offering these products to US retail customers. 
We take no view on the outcome of any outstanding litigation. 
What is Rothera: Rothera, a HOOD/SIG JV acquired a 90% stake of MIAXdx, a 
n
CFTC-licensed DCM, from MIAX, with the equity ownership and hence economics of 
the resulting exchange split 45%/45%/10% across HOOD/SIG/MIAX (Exhibit 9). 
HOOD is the controlling partner in the JV. Rothera operates a traditional exchange 
trading system, also known as a central limit order book (CLOB). The CLOB is the 
organization and matching engine for buy and sell orders for a specific asset in 
Rothera’s markets.

Rothera product offering: Rothera offers a growing selection of event contracts. Its 
n
initial contract offerings were within sports, although it has since added 
macroeconomics and political outcome contracts, and HOOD has highlighted plans 
to expand from here into other predictions markets, e.g., crypto contracts, over

product set (Exhibit 10). We believe that the sports focus, specifically initially on the 
World Cup, drove the large step up in July volumes to 2bn, and why volumes have

n

(Exhibit 11). We believe that this margin could rise as the business scales, as we see 
with other, larger exchanges. Rothera currently has much less scale over which to

remains at ~45%. We estimate Rothera’s 2026E margin using two methodologies: 
The average 2026E pre tax margin of exchange peers who offer options, and 
o
apply a 20% haircut to reflect lack of scale and estimated upfront investment, 
yielding a 2026E pre-tax margin of ~45%.

Rothera’s current fee rates...: Rothera uses a dynamic pricing model, with 
n
one-sided fee rates charged to a Rothera client in a range of 0.02-3.00%. Each trade 
executed involves two exchange fees - one paid by the buyer, and one paid by the 
seller. Rothera fee rates are based on the following formula: fee rate = K * P (1 - P), 
meaning that the fee rate depends on two variables. 
The type of trader, with different fee rates depending on if the trader is retail, 
a.
a market maker, or a professional trader. Rothera applies a discrete K value 
constant to each customer type. Retail traders/market makers/professional 
trading firms have K values of 0.02/0.03/0.12, meaning that a market maker 
could pay up to 50% more in fees than a retail trader, and a professional 
trader could pay up to 6x that of a retail customer. 
The certainty of the outcome in the contract. Specifically, the closer the price 
b.
(P) for a contract is to $0 or $1, the lower the fee rate, as the company looks to 
incentivize liquidity in the contract through lower fees in contracts that are

distribution across contract prices, which means that a contract with a price of

n

income markets than to equity markets, given the breadth of available 
contracts, which can fragment liquidity across individual prediction markets.

Rothera impacts on HOOD prediction markets pricing 
 
 
With the roll-out of Rothera, HOOD can monetize both the FCM and DCM fee 
components of prediction markets vs. only the FCM fee previously. We expect this 
monetization of Rothera along with HOOD’s recent FCM pricing cuts to allow 
HOOD’s all-in gross prediction markets fee rate (across FCM + DCM fees) to rise up 
to 14% in the near term, although after distributing the 55% non-controlling 
interest on Rothera, HOOD’s all in effective fee rate could fall 5-12% in the near 
term (Exhibit 20, Exhibit 19). Given HOOD’s pricing cuts and given that Rothera’s 
pricing is well below all competitor exchanges, e.g., Kalshi, we estimate HOOD’s 
retail customers could pay 29-34% less than previously when orders were routed to 
Kalshi and ForecastEx (Exhibit 14, Exhibit 17). Further, if executing through HOOD 
FCM + Rothera, we estimate that customers could pay up to 3% less than 
transacting directly on Kalshi (Exhibit 18). Finally, we see potential longer-term 
upside to HOOD’s all-in prediction markets take rate, if Rothera diversifies its 
customer mix, especially on the institutional side, although this could be partially 
offset by if other FCMs are added to the platform, for which HOOD only earns a DCM 
fee.  
Further detail on HOOD’s all-in prediction markets monetization, HOOD’s recent event 
contracts pricing changes, and how Rothera fits in is listed below. 
What a HOOD customer pays all-in on prediction markets trading. 
n
Previous to 6/1/26, on orders that it routed mostly to Kalshi, although also to 
o
ForecastEx, HOOD charged a 1.00% per trade FCM fee, on top of a 1.00% 
DCM fee from the venue on which the order is executed (either Kalshi or IBKR’s 
ForecastEx). Thus, all-in, a customer paid 2% in fees (Exhibit 17). 
Post 6/1/26: On 6/1/26, HOOD changed its FCM pricing. While at the high 
o
end, the fee rate is capped at 1%, FCM pricing uses the same pricing formula 
of K * P (1 - P) as Rothera, meaning that the pricing can fall notably below 1%, 
if the price for the contract is closer to $0. The K value for HOOD Gold 
customers is 0.05, and for non-Gold customers, it is 0.1, meaning that the low 
end of pricing for non-Gold customers is 0.10%, and for Gold customers, the 
low end is a lower 0.05% (Exhibit 20). Given that daily volumes suggest that 
contracts on Rothera are currently priced between $0.25-0.30 (or 
$0.70-0.75), this would yield a HOOD FCM fee of 0.94-1.0% across Gold and 
non-Gold customers (Exhibit 20). In terms of the DCM fee rate, if a HOOD 
customer’s order is routed to Kalshi, the economics would be unchanged (i.e., 
resulting in a 1.00% customer fee). If the order is routed to Rothera, the 
one-sided fee rate that the customer would pay is 0.38-0.42%, as discussed in 
Exhibit 12. This equates to an all-in gross fee that a customer pays of 
1.31-1.42% (Exhibit 20). 
Impact of pricing changes to customer: Our analysis suggests that if average 
o
contracts are in the $0.25-0.30 (or $0.70-0.75) price range, customers would 
save between 29-34% from the old FCM pricing model + execution on 
Kalshi/ForecastEx to the new FCM pricing + execution on Rothera (Exhibit 17).

What HOOD earns all-in on prediction markets trades. 
n

terms of DCM fee, if the order is routed to Kalshi, the economics would be

routed to Rothera, the one-sided fee rate that would correspond to the FCM

gross fee in its revenue, we estimate an effective fee rate, after HOOD’s 
payments to partners, of 1.11-1.19%.

o

However, when factoring in the non-controlling interest, the effective fee rate 
would fall by 5-12%.

HOOD earns, and a customer pays - these could impact HOOD’s take rate in a given 
quarter and over time. Specifically, Rothera could see customer mix shift away from 
retail traders to market makers and professional trading firms, which have 1.5x/6x 
higher fee rates than those of retail, and this could push up the DCM fee that Rothera 
earns. However, if Rothera adds other FCMs to the platform beyond HOOD, Rothera 
could generate DCM fees, but HOOD would not earn an FCM fee on those transactions, 
which would be fee rate-dilutive for HOOD.

to HOOD (Exhibit 28, Exhibit 29). In our base case, backing out estimated Rothera equity 
value out of the current equity market value of HOOD, the remaining equity value 
implied by the current market cap of HOOD would be $108.7-108.9bn, or $119.4-119.6 
per share, implying that the balance of HOOD is at only 39.2x-39.3x FY+2 GSe P/E, or 
87th %ile levels (Exhibit 41). In our bull case, the equity value of the balance of HOOD’s 
business would be $102.4-108.2bn, or $112.5-118.5 per share, implying that HOOD is

equity value of the balance of HOOD’s business would be $109.6-110.7bn, or 
$120.4-121.6 per share, implying that HOOD is trading at 39.6x-39.8x FY+2 GSe P/E, or 
87-88th %ile levels. 
The implied base case P/E for the balance of HOOD would be ~8.0x above the 
brokers & crypto average of 31.1x, which we view as reasonable, given we forecast 
much stronger growth at HOOD than peers - we model a 2026E-28E top-line CAGR of

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
