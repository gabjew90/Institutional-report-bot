Vol is waking up
Index vol has moved higher, but remains rather low in absolute terms. Meanwhile, the warning signs
are multiplying: oil vol, skew, VVIX and JPY vol are all moving sharply higher, while realized correlation
sits at record lows.
The repricing may have started, but cross-asset signals suggest it may have further to run. With CPI,
the yen carry unwind and a potential return of correlation all in play, optionality still looks too cheap.
The convexity window
Summer is over, positioning is neutral and the catalyst calendar is packed, yet vol remains extremely
cheap. GS’s derivatives desk sees an attractive window for adding convexity on both sides: right-tail
exposure as AI returns to focus, alongside tactical protection against near-term macro risks.
The compression is especially extreme in tech. One-month ATM vol for the average NDX stock has
fallen from roughly 57 to 40, while put/call skew is near its flattest in 20 years. With CPI, the GS tech
conference and key earnings ahead, the market is pricing just a 0.91% move through the week-end.
Optionality looks too cheap.

Correlation extinction
The S&P has just recorded its lowest six-month realized correlation ever, according to the GS
derivatives desk. History suggests such extremes are unsustainable and have typically ended in an
explosive return of index vol convexity.

Not all vols are sleepy
Oil vol continues to move higher, leaving VIX behind. Skew is spiking again as well. The signal has been
erratic lately, but current levels point to a renewed bid for downside protection on a relative-vol basis.

The yen warning
The latest JPY move has been brutal, raising the risk that the classic carry unwind spills into broader
markets. You do not trade VIX solely off USDJPY vol, but the gap has become huge, and hardly
comforting if VIX is supposed to provide a macro hedge.
