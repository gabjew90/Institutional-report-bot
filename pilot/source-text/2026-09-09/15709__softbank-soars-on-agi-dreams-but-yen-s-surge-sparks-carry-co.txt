Softbank Soars On AGI Dreams But Yen's Surge 
Sparks Carry Concerns; Goldman Delta-One Desk 
Warns Energy Is Still What Breaks The Market 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, SEP 08, 2026 - 04:10 PM 
"Back to school, but hardly a quiet start," is how Goldman Sachs Delta-One desk-head, Rich 
Privorotsky begins this morning's note (available here in full for pro subs) into a 
holiday-shortened week in the US. 
He's watching the Yen, the Strait, and a Copper tape that will not die. 
 
He notes that Asian tech and semis ripped. SoftBank about +30% in three days on AGI dreams 
about Astra (OpenAI's GPT-6 - read here for more on the 'AGI' fallacy) further supported by 
optimism about tech conferences this week and earnings (AMD, SNDK, SMCI, CSCO, VRT, 
NBIS, CRWV) which should give us a better read on whether spending intentions are still rising 
or beginning to level out.  


[Page 2]
 
 
However, we do note that KOSPI reversed a 2.5% gain to close lower... 
 
...despite record buybacks (PPT-maxxing)... 
The bigger surprise has been the yen’s sharp appreciation, Privorotsky warns. 


[Page 3]
 
 
Stronger Japanese wage data overnight reinforces the case for further BOJ tightening. 
Think raises some questions about yen carry trade unwind and the impact to global 
assets...particularly equities and credit.  
No surprise Japanese exporters underperforming as they are the intersection of a stronger 
currency and higher energy prices.  
But, for the delta-one desk-head, Energy remains the biggest tangible risk to equities.  
Iran’s proposed exclusion zone could constrain ship to ship transfers. 
Vitol's Hardy puts Hormuz flows at roughly 10m barrels a day, mostly crude, but warns that 
product markets remain tight. 
Priovo also notes - as we pointed out last night - that Shanghai crude futures have been 
outperforming, raising the question if China has returned to the market as the marginal buyer 
(having meaningfully reduced apparent demand in early to mid summer).  


[Page 4]
 
 
Some grounds for optimism on diplomacy: Iran says discussions with Oman are nearing 
agreement, although that is not yet an operational solution or a broader US deal.  
He adds that the incentives favor compromise now... Washington needs time for lower energy 
prices to feed through before political deadlines, while Tehran risks losing that source of 
leverage afterwards. Incentives are there but limited tangible progress so far... 
Russia/Ukraine remains another risk for Europe.  
Moscow is closing Germany’s St Petersburg consulate following the dispute over the attempted 
Leipzig drone attack. 
Separate sabotage investigations around German infrastructure add to the concern, although 
attribution needs care. 
Russia’s national parliamentary and regional elections culminate on 20 September, with Duma 
voting running 18–20 September. 
Worth watching for any change in escalation around that window.  
Shifting from the geopolitical, Privorotsky focuses on Copper, Bonds, and The Fed... 
Copper is quietly making new LME highs despite the stagflation concerns.  


[Page 5]
 
 
Supply constraints and tariff driven distortions are part of the story, so this is not purely a clean 
growth signal. 
 
Still, it fits the broader view: a spending heavy, late cycle economy in which equities and 
commodities can keep working until energy or rates rise enough to break demand. 
The question is what stops the spending, not simply whether the Fed hikes. 
Bonds are in a holding pattern.  
3yr  auction today, with the Treasury buyback announcement tomorrow (will it be >$4bn?). 
PPI on Thursday, and CPI on Friday are the main macro tests. 


[Page 6]
 
Not expecting the 3yr  alone to change much. 
 
The Fed 
The market's pricing already captures most of a hypothetical three hike cycle, with 61bps priced 
through September of next year... 


[Page 7]
 
 
Don't get me wrong the inflation data at end of the week is quite binary locally and we have 
some room to price from 2.4 to 3 hikes (wont be good on the day). 
That said, unless inflation is genuinely hot, I struggle with the idea of a huge additional repricing. 
This still looks more like credibility hikes as opposed something intended to derail the spending 
cycle. 
[CPI] could produce a painful dip at end of week, but I would still lean towards buying it if the AI 
narrative remains in tact. 
In conclusion, while AI optimism can carry the market for a day or two, a yen carry unwind is a 
market-wide wrecking-ball. But, Privotorsky warns that the bigger threat is an energy breakout 
with no diplomatic off ramp, adding that this is "the scenario in which I would be much less 
comfortable with risk..."
