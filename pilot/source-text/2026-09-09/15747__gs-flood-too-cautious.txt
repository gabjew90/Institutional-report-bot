GS Flood - TOO CAUTIOUS 9 Sep 2026
John Flood  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
Wed 9 Sep 2026, 2:32pm ET
“The strong survive, but the courageous triumph.”
― Michael Scott
I just got back from a week in Asia and was taken aback by how cautious / skeptical a lot of this
community currently is on the AI trade (aka the stock market). Plenty of scar tissue still exists - with a lot
of Asia based HFs dropping 20+% of their ytd performance in July (our pb data shows that despite July’s
drawdown Asia HFs are still +21% on the year).
My main take away from the trip: investors in the Americas are likely underestimating China’s AI (open-
weight) adoption rates while investors in Asia are simultaneously likely overestimating them. This
probably means it eventually shakes out somewhere in-between.
After July’s historic momentum drawdown and investors’ lackluster performance in August I am currently
witnessing a lack of willingness to play any meaningful offense. Positioning and sentiment remain muted
with concerns RE geopolitics / rates / inflation surfacing in every client conversation.
I believe the upcoming IPO pipeline has potential to be the spark that motivates investors (both
institutional and retail) to start playing offense again.
Below I highlight several signals that suggest investor sentiment / positioning might be too cautious at the
moment (in front of some potentially high profile deals and post a truly stellar Q2 earnings season).
Double clicking on the earnings point, S&P 500 EPS growth in Q2 2026 was ~30% year / year excluding
the "other income" related to some private investment stakes. Earnings for the hyperscalers and the AI
infrastructure companies benefiting from their capex spending increased by 54% year / year in Q2,
accounting for about 50% of S&P 500 EPS growth during the quarter. However, earnings growth for the
rest of the market has also been strong and accelerating. Excluding the Energy sector profits that were
boosted by higher oil prices, the rest of the S&P 500 posted year/year EPS growth of 14%...
