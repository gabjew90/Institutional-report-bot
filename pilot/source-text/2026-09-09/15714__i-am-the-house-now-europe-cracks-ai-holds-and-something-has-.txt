Macro cracks, AI holds
Europe is beginning to crack under the combined weight of surging energy, rising yields, French
sovereign stress and a stronger yen. Yet US tech refuses to properly break, supported by an AI demand
story that continues to strengthen even as credit and macro signals deteriorate.
The market is caught between strong fundamentals and increasingly fragile plumbing. With correlation
and straddles near the floor, owning the eventual break may be cleaner than guessing its direction.

AI bull case
As Privorotsky argues, AI keeps breaking barriers. Frontier models are making progress on previously
unsolved math and science problems, including OpenAI’s proposed Navier–Stokes solution. Astra,
trained across more than 100,000 GPUs and requiring 70–100MW at peak, roughly the power
consumption of a small city, is already moving materially beyond prior models. This is not fully self-
recursive learning, but AI is increasingly accelerating the research behind the next generation of AI.
The demand tape is equally difficult to fade. Google Cloud’s $100m-plus deals are growing more than
2x QoQ and YoY, while AI-server payback is under two years, and roughly half that on proprietary
silicon. Broadcom says demand exceeds its $115bn FY27 outlook, with power, site readiness and
supply now the constraints. The bear case is increasingly about who captures the economics, not
whether demand exists. More here.

The yen stress test
Bessent was unusually explicit overnight: "I am the house now."
Whatever the rhetoric, the yen continues to strengthen as markets price BOJ tightening and
repatriation.
The second-order risk is a yen-funded carry unwind, with capital returning to Japanese bonds and
equities while leverage quietly drains from US markets. USDJPY vol has surged far ahead of VIX. You do
not trade VIX solely off FX vol, but the gap is hardly comforting if VIX is supposed to provide a macro
hedge.
NDX has absorbed the move so far. Whether that reflects genuine resilience or simply a delayed
reaction remains the key question.

Who's dead?
Realized correlation and straddles are dead. Six-month realized correlation across the SPX has fallen
to just 11. Only two other periods in the past 25 years have seen similarly depressed levels: February
2007 and January 2018, just before “Volmageddon.”
Garrett notes that such extremes have rarely proved sustainable, typically ending in an explosive
return of index vol convexity. Meanwhile, SPX straddles are hovering near their yearly lows.
The setup is unusually clean: AI fundamentals remain strong, macro and credit are flashing stress, yet
price refuses to confirm either direction. With correlation, straddles and midterm event premium near
the floor, convexity remains cheap. Long SPX straddles offer a clean two-sided expression of the
eventual break; for a more targeted hedge, BofA prefers selling the October 16 VIX put and buying the
November 22–30 call spread.
