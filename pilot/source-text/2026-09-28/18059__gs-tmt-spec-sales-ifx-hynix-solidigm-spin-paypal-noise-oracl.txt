GS TMT SPEC SALES – Monday 28th September
US equities closed higher on Friday, ending a three-day losing streak as oil eased on hopes of renewed US–Iran
talks, while Treasuries saw front-end yields fall but the 30-year yield rise to 5.53%, its highest since 2004. Over
the weekend, President Trump rejected Iran’s latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, although indirect
talks are expected to continue, pushing Brent up; Asian equities were mixed on Monday, with Chinese
semiconductors weaker, while the Trump–Xi meeting delivered only a two-month trade-truce extension and a
potential AI hotline, leaving deeper tensions, including over Taiwan, unresolved. On AI, the two sides agreed to
hold a dialogue on the technology's risks and benefits, with the next round of discussions set for November.

Last week’s key theme was the shift from chat-based AI discovery toward agentic commerce, where personal
agents can complete tasks across shopping, payments, travel, administration and subscriptions, potentially


[Page 2]
disrupting search monetisation, digital distribution and consumer-inertia models while increasing the strategic
importance of payments, identity, fraud prevention and trust infrastructure. Adoption is likely to be gradual over
the next three to five years as issues around trust, liability, regulation and merchant control are resolved, but
Microsoft’s latest Copilot move reinforces the direction of travel: it is turning Copilot into a unified “super app”
with Home for chat and Office apps, Code for natural-language app building, and Autopilot, an always-on digital
teammate that can work independently with enterprise controls. Microsoft is also introducing consumption-based
pricing for advanced agent and coding usage, with Home and Code entering early access and Autopilot expanding
in private preview from late September, ahead of broader previews later in 2026. Aside: Banking: Muse and other
AI agents could sweep household cash automatically into accounts paying 3.3% to 5.0%, instead of the 0.1%
national average on checking accounts; a potential risk to cheap bank deposits; good chart published by Apollo.
This week Micron (consensus long) and ACN (consensus short with SI near 5 year highs) are in focus, will have
read to CAP) plus OpenAI DevDay 2026, tomorrow that Industry reports suggest may use or preview security-
focused tools or updates around this timeframe, following recent platform developments
| • | MICRON GIR are expecting persistent memory supply-demand tightness to drive another strong quarter,
upside to consensus and robust guidance; GIR is mid-single-digit percentages above consensus EPS for Q3–Q4
2027 on better mix and pricing. After June’s large beat, expectations are high, with consensus at roughly $50–51bn
of revenue and $31–31.5 of EPS, so investors will focus less on the headline beat and more on whether gross
margins can hold near or above the guided ~86%, pricing remains firm, HBM4 ramps on schedule and AI demand
continues to absorb new supply. Other key watchpoints are progress on the 16+ long-term Strategic Customer
Agreements, including deposits and visibility on the previously referenced $100bn+ of minimum commitments, as
well as FY2027 capex, capacity additions, capital returns and management’s view on whether tight conditions can
persist beyond 2027. | | • | ACCENTURE Q3 FY2026 delivered solid profitability, but bookings softened and
revenue growth guidance was narrowed to 3–4%, or 4–5%. For Q4, consensus expects around $18bn of revenue
and $3.18–3.19 of EPS (see GIR take) , with investors focused on bookings and book-to-bill, the quality of growth
and any stabilisation in federal work, evidence that AI projects are converting into durable revenue, initial FY2027
guidance(GIR expect CC revenue growth of 3.0% - 4.0%, which we model as ~50 - 150bps of organic growth,
~100bps tailwind from lapping prior Federal headwinds, and ~150 - 200bps of M&A), and the outlook for margins
and capital returns. The key upside would be stronger bookings, clearer FY2027 acceleration and tangible AI
monetisation, while another weak bookings print or further caution on growth would reinforce concerns over
discretionary spending and AI disruption. |

Interesting: Anthropic says its new Bay Area wet lab used Claude to identify a potentially novel CRISPR-like
enzyme system in bacteriophage DNA in 21 hours, using around 950 agents and 210 million tokens, although
independent researchers still need to validate the finding and Stanford has reported a partly similar system, as
Dario Amodei noted. The work comes amid growing concern over increasingly capable AI models and their
potential misuse in bioterrorism, though Amodei also believes AI could help cure most diseases within 5–10 years.

Anthropic stressed that its BSL-1 and BSL-2 lab does not handle human pathogens and that all physical
experiments are performed by scientists, not Claude, although future automation may be possible with safeguards.

DeepMind’s AlphaFold. See TechCrunch

| • | SK Hynix: Solidigm, its US subsidiary, is considering an IPO as early as next year that could raise around
$15bn, potentially making it the largest US semiconductor listing on record. Formed in December 2021 after SK
Hynix acquired Intel’s NAND flash memory and SSD business for $9bn, Solidigm designs and sells NAND chips
and solid-state drives, with a particular focus on high-performance products for enterprise and data-centre
workloads. | | • | Infineon will formally open a semiconductor facility in Thailand on 1 October, investing more
than THB48bn ($1.44bn) in power-module manufacturing, semiconductor testing and an R&D centre. The facility,
Infineon’s third power-module production hub after Germany and China, will make components for electric
vehicles, energy systems and data centres, employ more than 5,000 Thai workers, train over 600 local science and
technology personnel and send more than 130 employees abroad for specialist training. Infineon will also spend at
least THB2.8bn on R&D, develop at least 14 Thai suppliers and aim to source 60% of materials and components
locally, supporting Thailand’s semiconductor strategy to attract at least THB500bn of investment in its first five
years and THB2.5tn by 2050. |
| • | Neobank consolidation: Nubank is in preliminary talks to acquire Monzo, according to Sky News, with any
deal likely to include a combination of cash and shares and value Monzo at £8–10bn ($10.6–13.25bn). However,
Monzo is also considering a new funding round that could value the business at more than £8bn. |
TELCOS
| • | Telefónica: Telefónica Deutschland is targeting an operational inflection in 2027 and full recovery by 2028
after the loss of wholesale partner 1&1 drove a 9.8% decline in H1 2026 German revenue to €3.693bn. Its plan


[Page 4]
centres on expanding enterprise services, growing wholesale partnerships such as Aldi Talk and offering more
bundled digital and fixed-line products, while using AI, closing 60 stores and cutting 1,100 jobs to simplify the
business and reduce costs. Management also supports consolidation from four German network operators to three
and remains open to acquisitions alongside organic growth. (Cinco Dias) |
| • | TalkTalk: The UK government is on national-security alert over concerns that the debt-laden telecom provider
could collapse within days, potentially disrupting critical networks used by the Ministry of Defence, with security
officials in daily contact with Ofcom and senior civil servants to protect service continuity. The crisis has
intensified after Octopus Investments’ exclusive talks for wholesale unit PXC collapsed and Opus Broadband cut
its consumer-business bid to around £100m; Epiris is now negotiating to buy PXC for about £200m, potentially
through a pre-pack administration led by Alvarez & Marsal. TalkTalk said on 25 September that it was in the final
stages of the sale process and expected deals for both divisions to conclude imminently. (Telecom Paper) |
| • | Poste Italiane increased its stake in Telecom Italia to about 85.8% after the final tender window closed,
cementing control of the former phone monopoly, but falling short of the 90% threshold required under Italian law
to trigger a mandatory purchase and delisting, meaning Telecom Italia shares will continue to trade in Milan for
now. Poste had owned around 66.6% after the initial acceptance period ended on 11 September and expects the
combination to generate more than €700 million in annual cost and revenue synergies, while its stated objective
remains acquiring the entire share capital and delisting the company. |
| • | Liberty Global selected a shortlisted group of infrastructure investors to submit binding offers for a minority
stake in Wyre, its Belgian fiber and broadband joint venture with Telenet and Fluvius. |

| • | MUSIC: Sony (SONY) and Universal Music Group (UMGNF) are suing Suno again, claiming its new v6
model still infringes on their copyrights since it is trained on user outputs from previous models, which were
trained on unlicensed music scrapped from YouTube and elsewhere, The Verge's Terrence O'Brien reports. The two
labels are notable holdouts who did not sign licensing deals with Suno, the author notes. "Training a 'new' model


[Page 5]
on the outputs of an infringing model does not eliminate the infringement; it launders it, passing the value of
Plaintiffs' expression from the copied recordings into the tainted models, from those models into their outputs, and
from those outputs into v6 ... V6 is not a fresh start; it is the fruit of the same poisoned tree," reads the complaint,
which was shared with The Verge. Link |
MACRO AND TRADING
| • | US TRADING: US equities ended the week higher, with the S&P 500 up 1.21% and closing just 71bp below
its all-time high, while the Nasdaq 100 gained 3.25% and the Russell 2000 fell 0.68%. Markets balanced
enthusiasm around Meta’s Muse launch against continued US–Iran tensions, higher oil prices, strong September
flash PMIs and hawkish Fed commentary, which pushed Treasury yields to post-2007 highs before some relief on
Friday after reports that Iran had proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz and resuming nuclear talks; the two-
month extension of the US–China trade truce also supported sentiment. Meta rose about 11% on Monday as Muse
topped the US Apple App Store and Google Play charts with more than 902,000 downloads in its first six days,
helping revive momentum trades, with the High Beta Momentum basket up 6.61% on the week. In flows, asset
managers were net sellers, particularly across consumer discretionary, communication services, technology and
healthcare, while hedge funds also sold overall, with demand focused on technology and healthcare. |
