Something's Gotta Give
Breadth and Credit Divergences are Unsustainable
WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW: 
Last week mirrored trends since August: SMH +5.9%, QQQs +3.2%, SPY +1.3%, RSP
-0.6%, and IWM -0.8%. Two breadth metrics triggered unseen since 2000: <50% of SPX
names above their 200 DMA, and more 52wk lows than highs for nine straight days—
both with the index near highs. Who blinks first? Like 2000, could breadth and equal-
weight improve while SPX sells off? Short-term direction hinges on Iran de-escalation.
While a 'deal' won't fully reverse recent damage, hardest-hit sectors (discretionary,
financials, REITs, Utilities) will likely rebound most while tech/AI lags. Big picture, credit
markets show increasing signs of stress; CCC spreads have widened 11 of the last 14
days, approaching '22 levels. We're also seeing divergences in high-quality tech, with
IG Tech CDS at their widest since '23.
■
Breadth Divergence Keeps Widening. No secret breadth has been weakening, but
the question is, who blinks first? Or like we saw in 2000, could we see both with
breadth and equal-weight improving while SPX sells off?
■
Last Seen in 2000 Part 1. Since '90 only 25 other days that had < 50% of SPX above
their 200 DMA and the SPX itself was closer to a 52wk high than it was last week.
All of those occurred between 1998 and 2000.
■
Not Since 2000 Part 2. Since '90 the only times there have been nine straight days
of 52wk lows > highs while SPX was within 2% of a 52wk high: Dec. ’99, Jan. ’00,
Sep. ’26.
■
What Happened in 2000? From July ’99 through Feb. ’00, SPW -16% vs. SPX +8%.
They then reversed with the SPX -17% into late Dec. while SPW +20%. Possible we
see a similar dynamic this time around?
■
Still No High Correlation Selloff. It’s now been 213 trading days since the last 80%+
NYSE downside volume day. The average calendar year sees 21 such days, and we
have never seen a year with fewer than five. While the constant rotation has kept us
from seeing a correlation-one selloff, we continue to think the clock is ticking, and
we see one sooner than later.
■
Bonds are Separating from Oil. Despite their strong intraday correlation, crude has
traded sideways since mid-March while 30-year yields have climbed to 5.50%. Even
if a Middle East resolution causes a short-term pullback, broader drivers like fiscal
issues and global yields mean the primary upward trend in rates remains firmly
intact.
■
HY vs. IG Spreads Widening. It’s subtle, but HY spreads are widening vs. IG. They
are back to the wides of the summer when SPX was closer to 7300.
■
Tech Spreads Diverging. HY Tech CDS back to spring levels, and IG Tech CDS widest
since '23. Meta (META, Not Rated) bonds have fallen below 93 while its stock has
surged.
■
Consumer Back to Support. Discretionary has been one of the hardest hit areas
given the surge in crude/rates. Structural issues remain, but if there is any sort of
resolution in the Middle East it's also the group that likely rebounds the hardest.
■
Bounce Candidates: These names have all been hit hard but coming into strong
support levels. Burlington Stores (BURL, Neutral), Brinker (EAT, Not Rated), Yeti
Holdings (YETI, Not Rated), Acushnet Holdings (GOLF, Not Rated).

first? Does breadth catch-up to the index, or does the

whether a real de-escalation with Iran is reached.

Bonds are Separating from Oil 
 
While the intraday correlation between 
crude and rates remains extremely high, 
taking a step back we can see that crude has 
essentially been moving sideways since mid-
March, while 30yr yields have continued to 
march higher from ~5% to 5.50%.  
There are more factors than energy driving 
interest rates, including fiscal issues and 
global yields.  
So while a resolution in the Middle East will 
clearly cause crude prices to fall, and rates to 
fall in the short term, it’s unlikely to change 
the primary trend which is now firmly higher.

Company Valuation and Risk Disclosures
Burlington Stores, Inc. (BURL, Neutral)
Valuation: We are Neutral on a P/E valuation methodology. BURL is trading at a slight premium to its 5-year average forward P/
E multiple of 24.4x, but we believe there is better value in other areas of retail. BTIG does not provide price targets for Neutral-
rated stocks.
Risks: Downside risks include increased tariffs, a weakening macroeconomic environment, and increased competition among
off-price retailers. Upside risks include if new store openings and “Burlington 2.0” strategies continue to outperform, the
company could deliver stronger-than-expected sales and margin gains. Better-than-anticipated consumer demand, successful
merchandising, or further supply chain efficiencies could drive upside to both revenue and earnings. Additionally, if tariff-
related disruptions create more buying opportunities for discounted branded goods, Burlington may be able to expand market
share and profitability faster than current guidance suggests.
