The earnings test beneath the rally
The S&P 500 is near its highs, but only about a quarter of its stocks are above their 50-day averages.
Earnings estimates show a similar divide. The index’s Q3 estimate rose 1.2% from 30 June to 31
August, while estimates fell in seven of eleven sectors.
The coming earnings season isn't really a test of the S&P 500. It's a test of everything underneath it.
Q3 reports and, more importantly, Q4 guidance will tell us whether the narrow market is justified, or
whether the average stock finally has room to catch up.

The index can hide a lot
FactSet’s pre-September Q3 revisions were led by Energy (+11.8%), while Materials fell 9.1%. The
aggregate increase was unusual, but it did not reach most sectors.
That makes the next revision cycle more important than the index-level number.
If Q4 upgrades spread beyond the current winners, the market may have underestimated the rest of
the S&P 500. If cuts remain widespread, weak breadth isn't just a technical divergence. It's an earnings
story.
Margins enter the test
Goldman’s September flash PMIs show input-price pressures accelerating across developed-market
manufacturing and services. Output-price gauges rose too, but by less.
The question for earnings season is whether companies can pass those higher costs through. If they
cannot, margin pressure becomes another reason earnings breadth stays narrow.
For the average stock to catch up, broader earnings need to improve just as the cost backdrop is
becoming less friendly.

The hurdle rate just moved higher

Margins aren't the only problem.
The US 10-year real yield rose from 2.44% on 1 September to 2.76% on 23 September while S&P 500
breadth deteriorated.
Higher real yields make it harder for a broad set of equities to rerate. Strong earnings from a small
group can still hold up the cap-weighted index, but the hurdle for an equal-weight catch-up just got
materially higher.
TradingView
What would change the trade?
The catch-up case is straightforward.
If post-report Q4 estimate upgrades begin reaching a majority of sectors while RSP/SPY remains
depressed, the earnings argument for equal weight starts getting much stronger.
A turn higher in RSP/SPY and a recovery in the 50-day breadth reading would show that buyers are
following the earnings change. Waiting for every signal to fully normalize would give up much of the
move.
The tell
If Q4 estimates are cut across most sectors and RSP/SPY continues to weaken, the index can still be
supported by a handful of earnings winners. But that leaves an increasingly narrow market dependent
on those winners continuing to deliver.
Higher real yields make the catch-up harder. Lower yields would help, but rates cannot substitute for
broader earnings.
RSP versus SPY is not a pure breadth trade. It is also a sector and stock-weight bet. But the setup gives
us a clean decision point: the first post-report Q4 revisions will tell us whether the
divergence beneath the index is an opportunity, or simply fundamentals catching up with
price.
