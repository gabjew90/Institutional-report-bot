Rates refuse to stop
The rates move is getting extreme, but the pressure refuses to break. The 10-year is deeply stretched,
5% has given way and oil continues to push yields higher.
Bond and credit markets are already screaming stress. Equities, for now, are still refusing to listen.
Higher for longer
The US 10-year is trading at the most overbought levels since the autumn 2022 rates upside panic.
This is starting to look like real pain.

Vacuum
Rates have entered something of a vacuum since breaking above the big 5% level. The next major
level comes in around 5.5%.
Above that, and the chart starts looking less like a rates move and more like a stock undergoing a
fundamental repricing.

The oil connection
It didn't take much of a move in oil for rates to print fresh recent highs. The tight relationship between
the two continues to hold.

Oil owns the bond market
The correlation between WTI and the US 10-year yield is the tightest in 35 years.
Even more striking: the relationship between the average US stock and the 10-year yield is now the
most negative since 1960.
Oil → yields → equities. That is increasingly the macro chain that matters.
MS QDS
Vol dispersion
The theme we have been highlighting continues. Equity volatility remains depressed, while fear in
bonds and credit has exploded higher.
Chasing bond volatility here looks late to us. If bond stress really does spill over into a broader global
crisis, equities should be where the next adjustment happens, and the gap between bond and equity
vol should shrink.
