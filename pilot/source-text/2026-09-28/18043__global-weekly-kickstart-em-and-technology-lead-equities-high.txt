AI Spending Surges, Returns Matter 
AI capital expenditure remains exceptionally strong, 
n
but its growth rate should slow. US hyperscalers are 
expected to spend $800 billion in 2026, $1.2 trillion in 
2027 and $1.4 trillion in 2028. 
The key question is whether AI revenues can justify 
n
the investment. Hyperscalers may need around 
$300bn of annual AI revenue to break even, but 
monetisation is accelerating, with 48% cloud revenue 
growth in Q2 2026 and $1.7tn of backlog across the 
three largest cloud providers. 
US earnings are elevated, but the team expects 
n
deceleration rather than collapse. S&P 500 earnings 
grew 51% YoY in Q2, driven by AI investment, 
semiconductor margins and investment-related 
income. Our US team forecasts 11% EPS growth in both 
2027 and 2028, with a preference for hyperscalers and 
semiconductors.

what’s priced in the AI trade and US Equity View: Over-earning, 
but not an earnings bubble

GLOBAL WEEKLY KICKSTART 
EM and Technology Lead Equities Higher
Global equities advanced 0.9% last week, led by EM (+1.3%), 
while Europe remained broadly flat at +0.1% (Exhibit 1). 
EM’s gains coincided with a further rise in Momentum 
relative to the market (Exhibit 31). Sector leadership was 
concentrated in Technology (+3.3%) and Communication 
Services (+1.6%), while Utilities fell 2.5% (Exhibit 3). In 
commodities, Copper rose 1.3% amid persistent tariff 
uncertainty, Gold declined 1.6%, and Brent remained 
broadly stable at +0.3% (Exhibit 4).  
Macro data this week 
US: Core PCE report and Employment report. There are 
many speaking engagements with Fed officials this week.  
Europe: Flash HICP reports in France, Spain, Italy, Germany 
and Euro Area; Italy, Euro Area and Norway unemployment 
rate and final manufacturing PMIs across the regions. 
Japan: September BOJ Tankan, BOJ Summary of Opinions, 
and September Tokyo CPI.  
AEJ: PMI across the region, Indonesia CPI, Korea IP and 
exports data. 
Pricing is as of the close of Friday, September 25.
