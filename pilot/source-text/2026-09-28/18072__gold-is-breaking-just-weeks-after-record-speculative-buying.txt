Gold is cracking
Technicals are deteriorating, speculative length is vulnerable and the macro backdrop has turned
increasingly hostile. The structural gold story may still be intact, but right here, the short-term setup is
getting ugly.
Heavy
Gold is printing a monster down candle as we write. The major trend line that has defined the move
since 2025 is being tested right here.
We are already well below the 200-day MA and are now breaking the 50-day. A close below the mega
trend line would be a major technical deterioration, with $4,000 the next obvious downside level to
watch.

Speculators
Gold speculators are still carrying a lot of recently added length. That was fine while price was trending
higher. With the tape now breaking down, it becomes potential fuel for the move lower.
The question is how quickly those longs start heading for the exit.
LSEG Workspace
A month ago
Remember what happened just a month ago, right around the time gold reversed lower. Over a three-
week period, speculators bought a record amount of gold futures in nominal terms, according to COT
data. And it wasn't isolated to one cohort, every category participated.
That is a lot of fresh length sitting above the market.

China is cutting length
Shanghai open interest fell by 11k contracts, or 2.6%, during the day session as longs were liquidated
into the open.
The headline positioning probably overstates the true speculative long, as a large +SGE/-SHFE
onshore EFP position inflates SHFE open interest. But Chinese specs are still long, according to
Goldman's gold desk.
Now add the calendar. SHFE is closed 1–7 October for National Week, and with Iran and rates both
moving, there is little incentive to carry large speculative length through a week-long shutdown.
Expect more liquidation into the holiday.

The dollar connection
Believe in the great central-bank gold-buying story or not (more here), but if you trade gold, you are
basically trading the dollar. That relationship has held remarkably well for almost a year now.

Volatility falling
Gold typically trades with an upside skew. Lately, however, gold vol has continued to bleed lower,
despite GS showing elevated call demand versus puts. Calls are bid, but vol keeps falling. The options
market is simply not pricing meaningful upside urgency here.

Opportunity cost
As GS’s Privorotsky points out, front-end real rates have moved sharply higher over the past few weeks,
with the 2-year back around its highest levels in more than two years. Inflation expectations have not
risen nearly enough to offset the move.
That is a tough setup for a zero-yielding asset. Gold can hedge inflation, fiscal stress and geopolitical
risk, but when cash suddenly offers a large positive real return, the opportunity cost starts to bite.

Short-term bear case
Hawkish hikes are now becoming reality, and the macro-policy hedge that helped drive gold higher is
being unwound faster than expected. Higher real yields are also hitting the rate-sensitive ETF crowd,
with global outflows already accelerating.
Add Hormuz escalation and $4,000 becomes the level that matters. GS trading has flagged
sovereign/institutional buying around there. Lose it, and $3,887 is next, followed by the $3,500 area
highlighted by the World Gold Council.
This is not necessarily a structural gold bear story. Central banks are still accumulating around 91
tonnes per month, roughly 5x the pre-2022 pace, while the longer-term fiscal-sustainability bid
remains intact. The structural bid is still there. The short-term problem is that the marginal buyer is
stepping away.
