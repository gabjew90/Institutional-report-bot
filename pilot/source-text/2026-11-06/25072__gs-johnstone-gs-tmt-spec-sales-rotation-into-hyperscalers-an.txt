GS TMT SPEC SALES: Rotation into hyperscalers and out of semis;
Starlink aspirations; NEM mixed; BT beat; Swisscom OFCF raise;
DT mixed; JCD beats
Sean Johnstone
5 Nov 2026
GS TMT SPEC SALES – Thursday 5th November 
Tech saw a sharp rotation into hyperscalers and out of semis and datacentre infrastructure, as
investors viewed the Democratic win as potentially making new builds harder and existing, power-secured
compute more valuable. QQQ finished up 1%, having gained as much as 2.5% early on before fading
through the afternoon, while SPY rose 1.6% and VIX fell 2–3 points. Semis fell 3%, after opening higher and
dropping as much as 5%, underperforming QQQ by 4 percentage points. Our US desk’s largest thematic
flow was buying in hyperscalers versus selling in semis, across a mix of client types. Investors debated the
scale of the hyperscaler rally, but bulls argued that scarcer powered capacity could lift prices and strengthen
existing operators’ competitive advantage, while leaving future-capacity providers behind. AMZN rose 7–8%,
MSFT 4–7%, GOOGL 5–6% and META 3%, with Apple also strong and combined Mag7-style market-value
gains approaching $750bn–$1tn. In contrast, CoreWeave fell 7–11%, Nebius 4–6%, and other neoclouds
and datacentre builders. Memory and some semis, including MU, were weaker, while NVDA was only slightly
lower, highlighting pressure on companies building, supplying or powering new datacentres.
 
The debate: In tech the main debate from the midterm’s outcome was does the Democratic win slows the
US AI data-centre buildout via tighter permitting, water/power rules, fewer tax breaks and temporary
moratoria. Brad Gerstner’s comments on All-In did not help sentiment as he argued that regulatory friction
could stop 10-15 GW of expected 2027 capacity. Note he was already somewhat below the more bullish
street estimates for the US as he only thought ~25 GW was realistic in September because “atoms and
energy are hard.” GIR is only at 20GW in the US well below street and had push back on the past (GIR see
more risk on 2028. Plus SemiAnalysis was out saying they 16.7GW of planned 2028 Texas capacity at risk).
The main point is that the election now adds political risk on top of already bullish street forecasts well
above 30GW. The counterview is more sanguine around 2027 where most of the capacity is already
under construction or permitted, Texas moratoria would be constrained by the legislature, and many local
bans hit areas with little real activity. Broad shutdown is unlikely; delays and higher costs are the real risk.
GIR also of the view that datacentre focus will fade somewhat after the midterms dies down as it low on
voters priorities and focus will be elsewhere.
Investors of the view that IF there are short-term delays and questions: installed, power-secured
compute becomes more valuable with the hyperscalers near term winners AMZN/MSFT benefit; pure
new-build names (ORCL expansions, NEOS, SPCX, pure-play AI cloud) lag. (look at the moves overnight)
Also had some AI/Semis sceptics asking if there is a risk now is that the semis narrative flips to
inventory correction if power/sites lag chip deliveries. They point to Microsoft prior comments that it had
chips sitting idle because it lacked power and data-centre space. Note this was back in Nov 2025 however
more recently the FT reported that AWS is doing the same quoting that internal documents show it taking
delivery of ~$3bn of Nvidia GB300 racks per month and staging some in warehouses because certain sites

have space but insufficient power. So, while there may not be a demand issue there could be a near term
semi’s inventory issue if we do see delays similar to 2022–23 post-pandemic correction. 
The other investor debate was that structurally, sustained AI growth requires the cost of compute to
keep falling esp. for further major advances and higher compute costs reduce the displacement pressure on
spaces like traditional software and media. 
What the flow is showing: Our US desk is seeing a clear rotation into Hyperscalers from both LOs and HFs
(Mag7 unsurprisingly a top performer on the screen) while Semis remain a source of funds (see
underperformance). Pretty similar in Europe as we saw only one way LO supply across AI names (ASML,
IFX, ENR)
 
US Telcos: More press on SpaceX US mobile aspirations as its just signed a deal with Qualcomm for its
Dragonwing Cellular Infrastructure platforms to support Starlink Mobile’s terrestrial base stations. The US
telcos ignored this as near term seen as safe place to hide. But looking at the release it looks like Starlink is
starting to actively assemble the physical infrastructure needed to challenge incumbent Tier-1 mobile
operators. By combining its LEO satellite constellation with tens of thousands of terrestrial cell towers
powered by Qualcomm’s high-capacity Massive MIMO silicon. Release suggests SpaceX plans for U.S.
nationwide deployment rollout to begin in 2027, with tens of thousands of sites deployed over the multi-year
agreement. so will keep the debate going regards US telcos and from a Telco equipment view negative for a
Ericsson and lesser extent Nokia
 
RESULTS THIS MORNING
• NEM: Mixed, given run not enough today – the reported revenues inline, but organic growth at 12.5% is light of
street 13.6%. Market will focus of the Design weakness at 6.5% vs. street at 8.5% despite Build beat at 23.6% vs.
street 22%. Media also missed but is small. EBITDA 1% ahead due to recurring beating. FCF conversion above
100% better than street but not sure on consensus quality. Group Guidance reiterated despite lowering the Mange
(low DD to MSD) & Media (stable vs MSD) outlook. See GIR take
 
• SINCH: Top line, organic gross profit growth, and EBITDA broadly in line, better FCF. broadly in-line topline growth
and broadly in-line organic gross profit growth of c.4% yoy ex-fx. This was supported by Americas which remained
solid and broadly in line with expectations, with organic gross profit growing c.10%, driven by momentum in
Network Connectivity and continued growth in the API business. Across the other regions, we note an
improvement in APAC, which returned to positive topline growth in the quarter, helped by growth in email, as well
as some temporary low-margin messaging traffic. Organic gross profit growth in APAC was also ahead of
expectations at -10%, despite continued challenges in India and Australia. FCF came in significantly stronger vs.
consensus at €967mn, above usual seasonality. More colour likely on call but these are good enough See GIR
take
 
• NEXI: revenues inline at +1.4% vs. street 1.5% within it mix Card & Digital beat helped deliver the inline topline
while Merchant Services also improved to +1.7% after having been negative for the last 2 quarters. Lower costs
saw EBITDA 2% ahead. Guidance reiterated.   
 
• DT: Mixed results, with pretty solid numbers and decent German KPIs, offset by a lower buyback and press reports
suggesting that TMUS merger aspirations are not over. Group revenues beat expectations, with Germany (service
revenues 0.4% ahead) and Europe ahead. EBITDA AL also beat expectations, although Germany was a small

miss. Lower capex drove an OFCF beat. FY guidance was raised marginally to reflect the US, while non-US
guidance was reiterated. For Germany specifically, EBITDA growth is now expected to come in at the low end of
FY26 guidance of 2.5–3%. Growth was +1.5% YoY in 3Q26 due to cost phasing, according to DT. Growth will need
to be at least +3.5% in 4Q26 (consensus models just over 4%, which DT notes has not historically been delivered
in Q4). DT continues to guide to c.€11bn (consensus: €10.98bn). German KPIs are positive, especially in
broadband, where DT is growing. The buyback is €2bn versus Street expectations of €3bn, disappointing those
who had hoped for more, although DT wants to retain flexibility. The HANDELSBLATT article will not help
sentiment, as it claims DT continues to actively pursue a merger with its 54.95%-owned US subsidiary, T-Mobile
US, despite Bloomberg reporting last week that the process had been put on hold.   
 
• BT: revenue (0.6% at service revs), EBITDA (3% ahead) & line loss all beat while higher capex in Q means OFCF
misses. FY guidance reiterated and does not yet include the impact of the TalkTalk acquisition; that will come later
in the fiscal year. Within the mix Openreach is doing much better esp the line loss (175k vs. streets 197k), the
offset the consumer market that BT is saying is highly competitive and in H2 is likely to be the same if not worse
than H1. Pension update (neutral)its latest triennial pension valuation deficit is at £2.7B inline with street. The
change is that the contributions will be substantially replaced with zero-coupon bonds to be issued shortly; BT will
provide additional funding of £0.3B net present value to ensure the BTPS remains on track to be fully funded by
2030 (that is new); the measures will substantially reduce the funding deficit to around £0.3B; wider package
agreed, including enhanced dividend headroom. 
 
• Swisscom: Revenues beat by 1.4%, reported EBITDAaL missed by 1.2%. ex the E39m restructures it’s a beat
and lower capex means OFCF beats. All guidance reiterated except for OFCF now guided at above CHF 2.0B vs.
around CHF 2.0B prior to this. Would note that its lowered the integration costs (mostly CAPEX) so EUR ~-50mn
into 2028. 
 
• SES: Q3 beat, FY Revenue/EBITDA guidance reiterated, capex guidance reduced. Revenue 1.3% ahead of cons
driven by Media (this missed last Q) and excluding one offs in Mobility. Adj EBITDA 12.6% ahead. For FY Capex
now sees around €600mn (€700mn previously) on the back of optimisation initiatives vs cons €706mn. Rest of
guidance reiterated. The small negative is they are delaying the CMD from December to March with Q1 results, but
they have said they will update on financial targets potential to bring forward a portion of anticipated C-Band
proceeds which will be taken well. Overall very strong results in Video, last Q they missed so this will reinstall
confidence in mgmt that they can meet guidance.
 
• Bouygues reported a solid set of results, with a c.1% beat on the top line and c.2% beat on COPA. Guidance for
Equans COPA (EBIT) has been upgraded from a 5.2% margin to at least 5.5%, while Group guidance has been
upgraded to slight growth. Less supportive trends, including TF1, were pre-reported. We expect low-single-digit
upgrades to FY estimates, although more muted telco trends, while consistent with guidance, may limit upside
today. See gIR take  
 
• JCD: 3Q26 organic growth and Q4 guidance slightly ahead was a modestly positive update, with 3Q organic
revenue growth of +6.6% coming in ahead of both company guidance (~5%) and consensus expectations, driven
by stronger-than-expected performance in Street Furniture and Transport. Digital Out-of-Home remained a key
highlight, with organic growth accelerating to +15.3% and programmatic revenues up +37.5%, underscoring the
group’s continued digital transition. Management guided to around 6% organic growth in 4Q, slightly above market

expectations, suggesting trading remains resilient. Overall, the results support a neutral-to-slightly positive share
price reaction, although the beat was relatively modest and does not materially change the earnings outlook.
 
• AUTO LN -2% H1 Revenues below (mostly due to weaker than expected Vehicle & Accessory sales) while EBIT
better; FY27 Guidance reiterated. See Adam’s take
 
 US overnight
• QCOM (-7.5% AH): In-line quarter with better revenue guidance, but weaker margins was broadly in line, with
revenue meeting expectations and upside from Automotive and IoT helping offset continued weakness in
Handsets. The key debate is that while December-quarter revenue guidance was slightly better than expected and
management highlighted the early ramp of Datacenter revenues, earnings and margin guidance disappointed,
suggesting mix and investment pressures remain. Jim feels that investors are likely to focus on the sustainability of
an Android handset recovery, the pace of Datacenter growth in FY27 where upside is “constrained by supply rather
than demand,” per CFO, and whether Qualcomm’s diversification strategy can drive meaningful earnings growth
outside smartphones. See GIR take. GIR cut estimates by 8% on average, reflecting ongoing weakness in
smartphones and lower gross margins. On Handsets Qualcomm highlighted increasing headwinds in
smartphones, driven by supply constraints and memory-related demand softness that has expanded into the
premium segment. The company now expect the smartphone market to decline in the low-to-mid teens range in
CY26, and to be flat to down slightly in CY27.  
 
• ARM (down 1.7% AH): GIR expect the stock to remain range-bound despite revenue and EPS guidance above
Street expectations, as strong licensing revenue was offset by another significant royalty revenue miss. Licensing
benefited from four new CSS customers, while management highlighted stronger AGI CPU demand and greater
confidence in achieving $2bn of AGI CPU revenue in FY27–28. However, we believe the royalty shortfall reflects
near-term smartphone market pressure. Investor expectations appeared relatively balanced heading into results,
with optimism about the datacentre CPU revenue ramp tempered by the recent sector sell-off.   
 
• COHR +7.6%: In line on the quarter (GM missed by ~10-30bps), better guide. FY27 pro forma growth >60%,
which puts it at >~$11.2B on the ~$7.0B FY26 base vs. Street $10.63B.  
 
• HUBS beat Q3 but guided below expectations for Q4 raising the AI at risk debate again. Q3 revenue grew
17% in CC, ahead of expectations of around 15.5–16%. However, Q4 growth guidance of 12% fell short of the
roughly 14.5–15% expected, which management attributed to AI changing how customers find and buy software.
The CFO also lowered key operating expectations: full-year net revenue retention is now expected to fall around 2
pp, net new ARR to decline year on year, and Q4 net customer additions to be below 5,000. Asked whether 2027
would start with 12% growth, below expectations of around 13%, she said these headwinds would continue
through Q4 and into 2027.  
 

• APP +20% after its biggest revenue beat since Q4 2025, guidance well above expectations and a $3.2bn buyback
announcement. Its crowded short that run into the print, and now trades at 15x P/E. key themes: Revenue growth
again comfortably exceeded expectations, driven by core gaming growth of nearly 50% YoY and stronger
consumer momentum. Total consumer spend grew by triple digits YoY in Q3, with positive trends continuing into
the Q4 holiday season. New model improvements and architectures are showing early performance gains,
supported by investment in datacentre hosting. Despite targeted spending, strong operating leverage and free
cash flow generation continue to support management’s priority of returning capital through buybacks.
• SNAP reported mixed Q3 2026 revenue results, despite progress on growth initiatives and continued operating
leverage. Total revenue grew by a mid-teens percentage YoY, broadly in line with expectations and guidance, but
advertising growth slowed to low-to-mid single digits as brand advertising weakness offset progress in new ad
formats. Subscriptions remained a bright spot, with paid subscribers reaching around 30 million, alongside solid
incremental margins and free cash flow generation. Near-term investor focus remains on whether core ads and
newer revenue streams, including subscriptions and SPECS, can reaccelerate growth. Beyond the next 6–12
months, management remains optimistic about AR and spatial computing, with its platform and SPECS hardware
evolving to support that multi-year strategy. See GIR take
