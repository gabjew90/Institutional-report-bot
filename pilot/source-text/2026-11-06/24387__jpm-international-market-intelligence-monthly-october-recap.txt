KEY THEMES IN OCTOBER 
  
  
MOUNTING AI CONCERNS  
Souring sentiment across sector driven by infra spend & capex concerns as elevated yields create 
funding challenges.  

•         Capital emerged as the key bottleneck, with neoclouds amongst main casualties (CDS spreads 
spiked and pressured equities). 
•         Q3 earnings not enough to support sector, raising questions around AI monetization amid 
increasingly negative FCF at the hosting layer. 
  
(FORCED) BROADENING 
•         Tech selloff drove another episode of Global Momentum unwind, with shorts squeezing  
•         Rotation out of Cyclicals into Defensives. Quality outperformed. 
•         Broadening returned (SPW vs SPX, EU vs US), but felt more technical rather than fundamental 
  
POSITIONING 
•         ‘Nowhere to hide’ from a strategy perspective as there have been challenges across the board.  
•         Surprising that the broader indices in the US and Europe were basically flat to up in October, 
which makes the HF pain stand out even more: Global HF net returns were down ~4%, with declines 
across all regions. 
•         US Tactical Positioning Monitor fell to the 33rd %-tile as of Oct 29, though 4wk change was 
neutral. EU Positioning hit a low of the 5th %-tile before rebounding to the 14th %-tile.  
  
RATES RE-ESTABLISH THEIR ROLE AS SAFE-HAVEN ASSETS 
•         Despite hot macro data (incl. US inflation & growth), rates rallied in October, boosted by safe-
haven flows and Tech weakness easing funding pressures 
•         UST yields became positively correlated with NDX, reflecting a flight-to-quality pattern. 
•         Investors questioned the scale of hyperscaler and neocloud funding needs—and their willingness 
to absorb further supply—while high-yield spreads briefly revisited their 2026 wides.  
  
Q3 EARNINGS 
•         Despite the positive EPS surprise (3%pts in the US and 13%pts in Europe), the proportion of 
companies beating estimates is lower vs last quarter 
•         EPS growth for SPX is at 19% YoY. All sectors are positively contributing to aggregate EPS 
growth, with Tech, Industrials and Energy the biggest drivers 

•         EPS growth for SXXP is 35% YoY. While the Energy sector continues to be the biggest driver of 
earnings (ex-Energy EPS growth 17% YoY), delivery is robust even beyond this sector: 8 of remaining 
10 sectors recorded double-digit earnings growth.  
•         The weaker Euro has helped the relative sales beats in Europe vs the US 
  
GEOPOLITICS & POLITICS 
•         Middle East: Renewed flare-ups in the Middle East (incl. tensions between Houthis and Saudi 
Arabia) drove oil prices higher in October. 
•         UK budget: largely delivered on its promise as a ‘do no harm’ budget, with muted market reaction. 
Banks surcharge surprised positively (below consensus expectations), with other policy 
announcements generally in line with expectations.  
•         France politics: Budget negotiations, protests and fiscal-credibility concerns drove OAT 
underperformance and wider intra-EMU spreads. Some of the widening reversed late in the month 
after Moody’s did not downgrade France and protests calmed 
•         US Midterms: Drew’s scenario analysis is here. A soft blue wave is priced in, but a big blue wave 
could be a risk for Tech. Focus on Texas.  
•         Brazil:  Lula’s surprise runoff victory, after trailing Flávio Bolsonaro in the first round, reversed the 
earlier election-driven rally. Equities, BRL and rates came under pressure as fiscal uncertainty rose 
and expectations for consolidation deteriorated.. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

 
 
  
  
MONTHLY RECAP 
  
  
GLOBAL EQUITIES IN OCTOBER  
Source: JPM Int Market Intelligence, Bloomberg 
  
The MSCI ACWI was lower in October (-1.7%), extending losses from September. Both 
Developed Markets (-0.8%) and Emerging Markets (-8.1%) closed lower. Tech was the key driver 
behind global weakness, and EM notably underperformed, weighed down by Asia Tech & LatAm 
(Brazil elections in focus).  
  
In the US, macro data remained constructive through October, but the signals for 4Q were 
mixed. Real GDP grew at a 3.4% annualized pace in 3Q, broadly in line with expectations, as 

private domestic demand rose 4.9% on strong consumer spending and business investment. 
October’s all-industry PMI remained elevated at 57.6, suggesting resilient activity (~5% annualized 
growth) and upside risk to research’s 1.75% growth forecast for 4Q. Yet consumer-spending 
momentum slowed late in 3Q, inflation remained sticky, and hiring softened. September core PCE 
rose 0.32% month on month, lifting the year-ago rate to 3.1% and the three-month annualized pace 
to 2.9% from 2.2%. Despite the PMI’s firm employment signal, September nonfarm payrolls rose just 
29k, below the 90k consensus; the prior two months were revised down by a net 60k, and 
unemployment edged up to 4.2%. 
  
US equities were generally lower in October (SPX -0.9%, NDX -6.7%, RTY 0.5%), with Tech the 
key underperformer – reversing August/September momentum. Broadening returned with the 
Tech/AI unwind, with S&P Equal-Weight ending the month up 4.9%. Similarly, all sectors closed 
higher (led by Energy and Consumer Staples), with Tech and Comm Services bearing the brunt. 
Mag7 (-7%) weighed down on index-level performance, following disappointing Q3 earnings & still-
elevated spending plans. Alongside broadening and the momentum unwind (-7.9%), Defensives 
(9.6%) notably outperformed Cyclicals (-13.4%). Thematically, AI themes were amongst the worst 
hit: Semis (-18%), AI Positive Sentiment (-11.5%), Data Centers (-8.2%), Memory (-26%) all sold off 
notably. Neoclouds (-35.3%) were especially in focus, after Coreweave CDS moves sparked a 
broader equity selloff. 
  
In Europe, October data pointed to resilient but increasingly uneven growth. GDP rose 0.8% 
annualized in 3Q excluding Ireland, with Germany expanding 0.9% and Spain 2.6%, while France 
contracted 0.5%. Early 4Q surveys suggested growth was continuing, albeit more slowly: the 
October composite PMI fell to 51.9, broadly consistent with research’s 1.5% annualized 4Q forecast. 
Germany’s PMI held at 52.8, but France’s dropped to 48.4, making it the clearest source of 
weakness. Sentiment also softened, with the EC Economic Sentiment Indicator slipping to 96.9, led 
by weaker consumer and retail confidence. Inflation remained a headwind: CPI rose to 3.8% YoY 
(from 3.2%), prompting research to raise its forecast for EA headline inflation to 4.0% YoY, largely 
on energy, while expecting core inflation to remain broadly stable at 2.55%. UK macro data 
published in October pointed to resilient growth but mounting inflation pressure. August GDP 
rose 0.1% MoM after 0.4% gains in both June and July, leaving 3Q growth on track for close to 3% 
annualized; research nevertheless expected growth to slow toward a 1% pace from 4Q as higher 
energy bills weighed on real incomes. Activity surveys remained constructive: the October flash 
composite PMI rose from 52.0 to 53.1, consistent with roughly 1.5%–1.6% annualized growth. Its 

employment index also returned to expansion, reinforcing a tentative improvement in official labour-
market data: payroll employment resumed growth and the unemployment rate fell from 4.9% to 
4.7%. Inflation remained the main headwind. September headline CPI rose to 3.5% YoY and 
research lifted its forecast for a peak near 4.6% in 1Q27 as energy costs fed through. 
  
European equities were mixed in October (SXXP 1.9%, SX5E -2.2%), reflecting regional 
differences. Germany and Switzerland outperformed, while Spain (-4%) and Italy (-3.6%) were 
weighed down by Banks’ weakness – EGB spread widening drove a -11.8% selloff in the sector 
(SX7E). Overall, European equities benefitted as the Anti-AI trade, with SXXP vs SPX relative 
performance notably bouncing (SXXP had its best month since June). At the sector level, Basic 
Resources (-8.5%) lagged, with Tech (-1.5%) relatively outperforming US peers. Defensive sectors 
outperformed, led by Healthcare and Staples. Overall, Defensives outperformed by 8.2%, in line with 
MOMO down -14.5% (Banks a key driver on the long end). Semis (-13.8%) also sold off, in line with 
the US, while MOMO shorts incl. Chemicals (4.9%) and Defence (8%) rallied. In the UK, FTSE 100 
(1.2%) outperformed FTSE 250 (-0.4%), reversing recent trends. UKX gains were driven by 
Energy, and the Defensives sector skew. Despite a market-friendly budget, Homebuilders (-2.8%) 
and UK Banks (-13.4%) ended the month lower. 
  
Asian equities were lower in October (MXAP -6.1%), with Korea and China’s Star50 (-24.1%) 
amongst worst global performers as the AI/semiconductor unwind broadened into a wider North 
Asia de-risking. Research writes  In Asia, large-scale foreign selling in Korea and Taiwan ($15bn 
and $32bn, respectively) highlighted flow challenges – particularly in Korea where the end of the 
previous round of buybacks complicated the balance. As a result, North Asia markets struggled 
(Korea: -15.4%; Taiwan: -8.3%; Japan: -4.1%) and China (-7.0%). By contrast, Australia (+3.7%) 
and India (+1.4%) stood out, benefiting from their lower exposure to AI-related stocks.  Japan’s 
decline was milder than Korea/Taiwan but still reflected a sharp rotation out of AI semiconductor 
stocks and overseas-demand cyclicals into domestic-demand defensives, high-dividend, low-
volatility and quality names. Medium-term support remained tied to resilient corporate earnings, AI 
demand and the structural shift of household assets from cash/deposits toward financial products.

Source: JPM Int Market Intelligence, Bloomberg 
  
In October, global rates were lower, regaining their ‘safe haven’ properties, with long-end 
yields outperforming. Overall, US 2Y -30bps, 10Y -36bps; GE 2Y -17bps, 10Y -26bps; UK 2Y -
2bps, 10Y -14bps; Japan 2Y -6bp, 10Y -28bps. In the US, despite hot macro data (incl. inflation), 
rates outperformed global peers as investors returned to USTs as a safe haven asset on the back of 
Tech concerns and a potential slowdown in issuance (eased long-end pressure). The Fed held rates 
at 3.75–4.00%, despite two dissents in favor of a hike. Research noted that Chair Warsh did not 
clearly explain why the Fed chose not to hike; markets read his press conference as softer than the 
tightening-biased statement, although research still expects a December hike given resilient growth 
and sticky inflation. Credit spreads widened as AI funding fears weighed on sentiment, particularly 
around neocloud financing and heavy hyperscaler capex, though the pace of investment-grade 

widening slowed late in the month. In Europe, the ECB held rates but retained a tightening bias, 
with research expecting hikes in December and March. France remained the focus for intra-EMU 
spreads as fiscal and political uncertainty drove OAT underperformance; spreads partly recovered 
after Moody’s did not downgrade France and protests calmed, but research stayed cautious. 
Lagarde said the ECB was attentive to unwarranted, disorderly market moves and had tools to 
respond, without signaling a specific intervention. In the UK, the Budget generally delivered in its 
goal of ‘doing no harm’. though the OBR’s pessimistic growth forecast in focus. Research expects 
the BoE to hike in November. In Japan, the BoJ held rates at 1.25% in October, but the decision 
was viewed as a hawkish pause and research continued to expect a December hike. 
  
In FX, the dollar extended monthly gains in October (DXY 0.4%, USD trade-weighted 0.7%). EM 
FX weakened (-0.6%) on the month, with BRL amongst worst performers – down over 5% and 
wholly unwinding intramonth gains following a surprise presidential election outcome. EUR 
weakness extended as political worries remained elevated in France, with continued pressure in 
intra-EMU spreads. Notably, EUR screens as the most short currency across our FX strategists’ 
metrics. Meanwhile, USDJPY held steady in October, below the 160 level. 
  
The BCOM was lower in October (-0.4%), with Precious Metals the key driver behind 
weakness. Energy extended gains, up another 7.6% on the month amidst escalating Middle East 
tensions. Industrial Metals (-2%) ended the month lower, but Precious Metals were the main point of 
weakness: Gold sold off -6.8% while Silver notably underperformed, down 18.7% in October. these 
moves were broadly in line with the equity momentum unwind.
