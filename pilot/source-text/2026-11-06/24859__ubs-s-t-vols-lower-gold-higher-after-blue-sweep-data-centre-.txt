Equities Rebound, Volatility Fades After Democratic Sweep
Volatility down, gold up – those are the main market moves as investors digest the results of the US midterm
elections, in which the Democratic Party secured majorities in both houses of Congress. The immediate
response to the extent of the Democratic wins was a knee-jerk sell-off in equities, but that has reversed.
European equities are up 1.1% on the day, and US equity futures point towards a 0.5% move higher. The
dollar is bruised, with the DXY slipping 0.5% lower, while the US 10y Treasuries yield is slightly lower at
4.92%. In options, the VIX index is down below 19 vols and MOVE has edged below 100 vols; gold,
meanwhile, is up 2.9% at $4,044/oz.
NBC News has called 54 Senate seats for the Democrats, against 44 for Republicans, with the last two leaning
towards the Democrats. In the House, NBC estimates the Democrats will end up with 258 seats versus 177 for
the Republicans. Democrats will control the agenda in both houses, but President Trump retains the ability to
veto any legislation. Democrats will also have significant influence over major executive and judicial
appointments, and can control committees and investigations.
Some potential market observations sourced from across UBS:
• US rates lower: reduced risk of geopolitical escalation means steady/lower oil prices; gridlock means slower
increase in government spending
• Higher equities: lower oil, lower rates mean fewer Fed hikes; US economy remains strong
• Stronger dollar: strong US economic data and continued weakness in the rest of the world (FX Strategy)
• Tech stocks under some scrutiny: tougher rules on data center construction could drive up costs/slow
buildout, but momentum remains healthy

AMD Results Upbeat On Data Center Strength; Shares Surge - AMD is up 8.5%, roughly in line with the
~8.6% implied move, after a Q3 beat and Q4 revenue outlook above expectations, with Data Center strength
the standout and Client coming in slightly light as anticipated. Expectations had been reset lower following
INTC’s report, leaving the results as a clearer signal of execution and accelerating Data Center growth. Q3
revenue was $13.38 bn versus $13.1 bn expected, with EPS of $2.09 versus $1.95. Data Center revenue of
$8.55 bn topped the $8.25 bn bogey, while Client revenue of $2.92 bn missed $3.08 bn. Embedded revenue
was $1.11 bn versus $1.08 bn, gross margin was 55.9% versus 56%, and opex of $3.56 bn was below the
$3.66 bn estimate. Capex was $648 mn versus $861.3 mn, while free cash flow was $554 mn versus $793
mn. For Q4, AMD guided revenue to $17 bn, plus or minus 3%, versus $16.3 bn expected, with gross margin
around 55% versus 55.3%. Management pointed to strengthening Instinct and EPYC demand and expects
growth to accelerate further in Q4 as Helios and Venice production scales. (Robert Ruple, Specialist Sales)
Eurozone Inflation Up To 4%, Matching Expectations - Eurozone October HICP inflation rose 0.2pp to
4.0% y/y, in line with consensus and our expectations (which we updated on Friday following the flash country
prints). The key driver behind the increase in headline was a rise in energy prices (+1.9pp to 20.8% y/y),
largely due to a big jump in electricity tariffs in Italy, more than offsetting a temporary drop in Germany on the
back of the temporary fuel tax cut. Following its first increase in September after a surprising summer
slowdown, food inflation was up 0.3pp to 1.6% y/y, although slightly below our forecast of 1.7%. Core inflation
remained in line with consensus but slightly below our (updated) forecast of 2.6% y/y. Within core, both good
and services inflation edged up. As always, we will get a better idea of the drivers within each category with
the release of the final estimate on 18 November, but at this stage, based on limited details from the country
releases it appears that volatile seasonal components (related to transportation, accommodation and holidays)
have played a role in a pick-up of services inflation. (Anna Titareva, Research - Economics & Strategy - Pan
European Economist)
Nagel Says December ECB Hike “Very Likely Necessary” - Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel added
to the case for a December ECB rate hike, saying a move is “very likely necessary” unless the inflation outlook
improves significantly. Nagel said inflation is approaching the ECB’s adverse scenario and that price pressures
are likely to run above the bank’s September projections. His comments add to a growing group of
policymakers arguing for further tightening after the ECB held rates last week. (Antonya Allen, Macro Sales)
ECB Wage Tracker Shows Higher Growth In First Three Quarters Of 2027 - The latest ECB wage tracker
points to negotiated wage growth remaining relatively firm into 2027, with the headline measure at 2.7% in the
second half of 2026 and 2.8% in first half of 2027, before moderating to 2.4% in Q3 2027. Excluding one-off
payments, wage growth rises from 2.6% in 2026 to an average 2.8% over the first three quarters of 2027,
suggesting underlying wage pressures remain somewhat sticky even as the headline measure eventually
eases. The wage tracker is one of the many data points ECB President Lagarde said would help to inform their
rates decision after they held last week. At 10:00 London, keep an eye out for Eurozone CPI which may
influence market pricing for the next meeting. The Dec. 17 meeting is currently pricing 22bp. (Nana Antiedu,
Macro Sales)
BoC's Macklem Says Further Rate Increases May Be Necessary - In a speech before the House of
Commons Standing Committee on Finance, Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem said the Bank raised its
policy rate 25bp to 2.5% as persistently high oil prices worsened the inflation outlook while the Canadian
economy proved more resilient than expected. He said inflation is projected to remain at or above 3% through
much of 2027 before returning close to the 2% target by mid-2028, as higher energy costs increasingly pass
through to consumer prices. Macklem said the combination of persistent inflation and economic resilience
leaves less scope for monetary accommodation and that further rate increases may be necessary, although
policymakers will proceed carefully and make decisions meeting by meeting amid uncertainty over energy
prices and US tariffs. (Jonathan Garber, Macro Sales)

Riksbank Raises Rates By 25bp To 2% As Expected, To Fund Part Of Its FX Reserves With Foreign
Currency Loans - The Riksbank raised rates by 25bp to 2% on Wednesday, as broadly expected. In the
press release, the Riksbank said "if the outlook for inflation and economic activity holds, it is likely that the
policy rate will be raised further in the coming year, in line with the policy rate forecast in September." The
Riksbank sees two-sided risks, with stronger growth and supply disruptions posing upside inflation risks, while
labour-market uncertainty, geopolitics, AI concerns and weak global demand present downside risks. The
Riksbank also said it will fund SEK200 bn of its foreign currency reserves with loans via the national debt
office. It said this is not a monetary decision and should not affect the size of its reserves. (Nana Antiedu and
Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
Fed To Shrink Balance Sheet; Fed Will Need To Make Reserves Expensive To Hold - Fed Chair Warsh’s
balance sheet task force is expected to hand in its findings early next year. It’s likely to conclude that the only
way to really pull balance sheet size down would be to disincentivise banks from holding more reserves than
they need. Former Fed official Bill Nelson, now chief research officer at the Bank Policy Institute, but formerly
one of the architects of GFC era balance sheet policy discussed the Fed’s options with UBS Knowledge
Network. He said there was an important distinction to be made: asking banks what quantity of reserves they
needed depended upon the environment the question was being asked. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
US Treasury Quarterly Refunding Presents Near-Term Steepening Risk – The US Treasuries borrowing
estimates were just $12 bn above UBS Rates Strategy forecasts, or $38 bn below after adjusting for
cash-balance assumptions. The financing estimates leave strategist Phoebe White comfortable with her call
Treasury will hold coupon auction sizes unchanged for the upcoming quarter in its policy announcement
Wednesday morning (see preview here). Phoebe expects Treasury to adjust its forward guidance, removing
the phrase "at least" or signaling that auction size changes are likely during this fiscal year. She sees the first
round of coupon auction size adjustments in May 2027. To the extent that the possibility of immediate
long-end cuts has attracted increased attention, a decision to keep auction sizes unchanged could pressure
the 10s/30s curve steeper and long-end spreads cheaper. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
New Zealand Unemployment Up To New Cycle High; UBS Still Sees December RBNZ Hike - New
Zealand Economics: Employment fell 0.1% q/q in Q3, well below expected, and the y/y rate slowed to 0.7%,
reversing some of the progress made in Q2. The unemployment rate lifted further to 5.7%, well above
expected, and the highest since 2015, while the under-utilisation rate held at 13.8%. The unemployment rate
has increased in every quarter for nearly five years now. Economist Stephen Wu still expects the RBNZ to hike
by 25bp in December to 3.00% and again in February next year, as inflation remains too high. Governor
Breman, who voted to hold in October, has said the chances of a December hike are 'high'. While a
higher-than-expected unemployment rate supports a downshift in the cadence of hikes and reduces the need
to tighten into restrictive territory, Stephen thinks it does not remove the need for further hikes, with Q4 CPI
likely to top 4% y/y (UBS expects 4.2% and RBNZ estimates 4.4%). He however flags risk of a delay in the
hike until February, after the CPI release. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
RBA Likely On Extended Hold - Australia Economics expects the RBA to hold the cash rate at 4.85%, at a
restrictive level, for a sustained period. The risk of an additional hike has reduced after three members of the
RBA's Board voted against a hike at the November meeting, and the RBA surprisingly dropped their tightening
bias. Nonetheless, if the RBA does end up hiking again, the more likely timing remains in February 2027, when
the RBA staff will present updated forecasts in the SMP. The market has already shifted towards pricing rate
cuts from as soon as March, but economist George Tharenou thinks this is too early given inflation is still too
high. A rate cut cycle is more likely to start in late 2027. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
