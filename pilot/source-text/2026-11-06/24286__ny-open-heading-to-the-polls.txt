NY Open - Heading to the polls
By Varshi Karamsetty
It's US midterms today - polling stations are currently open with the majority closing between 19:00 - 21:00 ET. As a
reminder 435 seats of the House of Representatives and 35/100 Senate seats are up for election, alongside
gubernatorial races. Prediction markets see a high probability of a Dem sweep though margins matter and is what will
drive markets on Wed. Senate outcome is most important, with swing states results likely from 22:00 (according to
historical call times). Texas is the key bellwether, polls close at 20:00 ET and a win by the Democrats could signal a
broader blue wave. 
It was a quieter session overnight - RBA hiked by 25bp but a 6-3 vote split and Bullock indicating a wait and see
approach saw OIS curves reprice and AUD weaken. We don't expect any more hikes but depending on trimmed mean
inflation still see hawkish risks in 2027. USTs were closed overnight and open weaker following Europe, thanks to the
slight correction in oil. US confirmed that they conducted targeted strikes on Iran, but there are headlines that diplomatic
talks continue. US equity futures struggle for direction, large cap positioning has eased amid AI jitters with Nasdaq now
flat. USDCNH spiked higher after PBoC surprisingly announced a 2% hike to the FX reserve requirement ratio to 6%,
likely aimed to narrow the spot-fixing spread. Bottom line, the broader RMB appreciation trend remains intact.

Cheatsheet - US Midterms: A final markets guide
It's Election Day ‌
US midterms are underway. 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 out of 100 Senate seats are up for election.
Republicans currently hold narrow majorities in both chambers, controlling the House 219-213 (with three vacancies) and the
Senate 53-47, including two independents who caucus with Democrats. 36 gubernatorial (governor) races will also take place
simultaneously. 
Prediction markets place a high probability that the Democrats retake the House and Senate, but margins matter. Cook
Political Report sees Dems winning 227-240 seats (218 seats needed for majority) but the Senate outcome matters more. Of
the 35 seats being contested, Republicans hold 22, and Democrats hold 13. 11 states are considered competitive, with Texas
as the key bellwether: a Democratic victory here would signal a broader blue wave. 

Results are released after initial polls close from 18:00 ET, with majority closing between 19:00 - 21:00 ET. The earliest clues
on the House should emerge from 19:30-20:00 ET. Seven competitive House races close by 19:30, with Democratic gains in
Kentucky or Ohio and holds in Florida providing an early signal that the party is on course to reclaim the House. By 21:00 ET,
results from North Carolina and Texas should begin offering a clearer picture. 
For the Senate, while polls in most key battlegrounds will have closed by 21:00, several competitive Senate races have not
been historically called until later in the evening. We expect NC, NH, GA, KS, OH and TX results potentially on the night, as
early as 22:00 ET, but we might need to wait until the following day for MA, MI and MN. 
How markets may move will depend on margins. A Dem sweep is increasingly priced in, though our equity strategists see
incremental pressure only if Dems win over 53 seats in the Senate. FX spot jumps are minimal (EURUSD at 35bp) but reflect
risks beyond the election outcome (e.g. geopolitical escalation). Q4 refunding announcement is due on Wed and arguably
more in focus for USTs. Our strategists see higher risks of escalation on a divided Congress. More broadly, our quantitative
team finds that credit, rates and equities all underperform in election years, but a post-election relief rally unwinds this
volatility into year-end.

Elsewhere‌
What's happening across markets:
Rates: USTs were closed overnight due to Japan holiday and open slightly weaker, following European FI trends. Gilts are
reversing Monday's gain as geopolitical pressures persist. AU bonds outperformed - while the RBA hiked rates by 25bp, the
split decision (6-3), removing the reference to hike further if needed, and Governor Bullock stting that there is "time to take
stock" skewed dovish. Dec hike is off the table barring a shock Oct CPI, but our strategists still see Feb as live if trimmed-
mean is in line with 0.9%. which could prompt RBA to hike again. We see value being paid in February and received further
out, making front-end flatteners appealing. Our economist base case is no further hikes, but we still see risks clearly skewed
towards higher rates in 2027.
Equities: Negative tone across Asia and Europe with Kospi closing -3.5%, among the worst performers overnight. US equity
futures diverge with SPX e-minis -0.1% while Nasdaq futures are +0.3% higher. Conviction is likely lower today, implied
moves post midterms look are at 0.91% for SPX, 1.63% for QQQ, and 1.21% for IWM. After the midterm elections, our CETS
team are hopeful for relief in areas like Power Generation (CGUSPGEN), but uncertainty is too high to pursue that quite yet.
ICYMI new Equity Markets Positioning Model shows further moderation in US large-cap positioning with S&P 500/Nasdaq
positioning closer to neutral. After the close on Monday, Apple earnings beat, but December outlook was mixed with higher
product revenues partially offset by lower services growth and gross margins. Despite the mixed outlook, we remain
constructive on its long-term trajectory.
FX: USDJPY rally picking up, following moves in FI but the rest of the complex traded in ranges (AUD underperformed post
RBA). Total interbank volumes during the London morning were higher by 35% versus the 30d average, led by GBPUSD and
EURUSD. Recall our trader has recently gone long EURUSD/bearish USD, as USD is expected to remain under pressure to
further AI strains. Flow & Positioning report highlights long USD positioning was reduced last week but still elevated at +5.8,
mostly by RMs and retail investors. Risks appear skewed towards USD selling this week ahead of events, as HFs have
already shifted towards a bearish view. Post RBA, our London trader maintains his AUDNZD short - the RBNZ's decision not
to hike was less dovish than the headline reaction, and Nov 7 elections may not prove to be as negative as expected.
Seasonal patterns are also bearish.

Commodities: After a near -8% oil selloff on Monday, some negative headlines are driving a slight correction, (Brent up
1.57% at print). The US conducted targeted strikes on some Iranian targets in the SoH on Monday, WSJ reports, including an
attack on Qeshm Island. US/Iran continue to review latest proposals of a possible deal, though Reuters reported overnight
that Iran and US are far from reaching a deal to end the war. Gold down -0.9% amid headlines, watch whether $3900 holds
as support.

 
Best of the rest:
USDCNH spiked higher above 6.67 overnight after PBoC unexpectedly announced a 2% hike in FX reserve requirement ratio
to 6%, draining the supply of dollars by an estimated $20bn. This seems to be targeting the basis gap - likely aiming to narrow
the spot-fixing spread to around -250 to -300 pips from the extreme levels of ~700pips before. Our local trading desk expects
spot USDCNH to consolidate for a few days before encountering strong resistance around the 6.6750-6.6800 zone. That said,
the broader RMB appreciation trend remains intact, though the pace is now firmly managed.
CHF: Oct CPI printed stronger than expected at 1.5% YoY vs 1.0% prior, and 1.2% cons. MoM trends remain modest picking
up slightly towards 0.2%. The next SNB is not until Dec so we will have a few more inflation prints to assess whether the trend
is re-accelerating. For now, the bank are expected to hold rates in the indefinite future. 
EUR: ECB's Nagel speaks in Berlin at 12:00 ET followed by Escriva in Madrid (no time). In recent comments, Nagel stated
that the decision to hold rates in Oct “wasn’t an easy one”, keeping hikes on the table for future meetings.
HKD: Q3 GDP surged to 5.2% YoY vs 3.7% cons. and 4.3% prior. The trend was likely supported by AI-related merchandise
trade and rising investment, with QoQ growth also strong at 1.9% QoQ recovering from -0.6% prior.
TRY: Oct CPI printed at 29.77%, broadly unchanged from 29.7% prior with MoM printing at 2.58%. Data continues to reflect
persistent pressures, and our economists look to the Nov 12 inflation report for further guidance on the inflation outlook.
