US Midterms: Democrats Eye Sweeping Gains
Democrats flipped six Republican-held seats in Maine, Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, North Carolina, South Carolina
and Texas so far, NBC News reported. Betting markets have pushed the probability of a blue wave from the
mid-to-low 80s to above 98% during the Asia morning. Risk assets have weakened alongside that repricing,
with NDX futures down 1.3%, UST yields 6bp lower and the US dollar down 40bp. One interpretation is that
markets are attaching greater risk to a Democratic sweep, including potentially more friction around
AI/data-centre investment, as well as greater uncertainty around the debt ceiling, fiscal policy, and the odds of
government shutdown, etc. 
The tech sell-off in early Asia morning was somewhat at odds with the message from the UBS APAC Hedge
Fund Leaders Conference on Wednesday. Panelists argued that opposition to AI data-centre development is
increasingly bipartisan rather than a uniquely Democratic policy risk. Concerns span land, electricity and water
consumption, local control, corporate concentration and job displacement, with Texas and Pennsylvania
highlighted as key states to watch for potential gubernatorial policy shifts and restrictions on development. 
Equities later pared back those losses and USTs gave back some of their earlier gains. Interestingly, gold had
been resilient throughout given its recent correlation with equity corrections and it later saw a breakout above
4,000.
(Jason Cheng, Macro Sales)

probability of Democratic control at 97% in the House, versus 7% for Republicans, and 81% in the Senate,

Nagel Says December ECB Hike “Very Likely Necessary” - Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel added
to the case for a December ECB rate hike, saying a move is “very likely necessary” unless the inflation outlook
improves significantly. Nagel said inflation is approaching the ECB’s adverse scenario and that price pressures
are likely to run above the bank’s September projections. His comments add to a growing group of
policymakers arguing for further tightening after the ECB held rates last week. (Antonya Allen, Macro Sales)
BoC's Macklem Says Further Rate Increases May Be Necessary - In a speech before the House of
Commons Standing Committee on Finance, Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem said the Bank raised its
policy rate 25bp to 2.5% as persistently high oil prices worsened the inflation outlook while the Canadian
economy proved more resilient than expected. He said inflation is projected to remain at or above 3% through
much of 2027 before returning close to the 2% target by mid-2028, as higher energy costs increasingly pass
through to consumer prices. Macklem said the combination of persistent inflation and economic resilience
leaves less scope for monetary accommodation and that further rate increases may be necessary, although
policymakers will proceed carefully and make decisions meeting by meeting amid uncertainty over energy
prices and US tariffs. (Jonathan Garber, Macro Sales)
New Zealand Unemployment Up To New Cycle High; UBS Still Sees December RBNZ Hike - New
Zealand Economics: Employment fell 0.1% q/q in Q3, well below expected, and the y/y rate slowed to 0.7%,
reversing some of the progress made in Q2. The unemployment rate lifted further to 5.7%, well above
expected, and the highest since 2015, while the under-utilisation rate held at 13.8%. The unemployment rate
has increased in every quarter for nearly five years now. Economist Stephen Wu still expects the RBNZ to hike
by 25bp in December to 3.00% and again in February next year, as inflation remains too high. Governor
Breman, who voted to hold in October, has said the chances of a December hike are 'high'. While a
higher-than-expected unemployment rate supports a downshift in the cadence of hikes and reduces the need
to tighten into restrictive territory, Stephen thinks it does not remove the need for further hikes, with Q4 CPI
likely to top 4% y/y (UBS expects 4.2% and RBNZ estimates 4.4%). He however flags risk of a delay in the
hike until February, after the CPI release. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
RBA Likely On Extended Hold - Australia Economics expects the RBA to hold the cash rate at 4.85%, at a
restrictive level, for a sustained period. The risk of an additional hike has reduced after three members of the
RBA's Board voted against a hike at the November meeting, and the RBA surprisingly dropped their tightening
bias. Nonetheless, if the RBA does end up hiking again, the more likely timing remains in February 2027, when
the RBA staff will present updated forecasts in the SMP. The market has already shifted towards pricing rate
cuts from as soon as March, but economist George Tharenou thinks this is too early given inflation is still too
high. A rate cut cycle is more likely to start in late 2027. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
Russia Looks To Test A Weakened Trump After Midterms - Moscow plans to explore further easing of US
sanctions after the midterms, seeking to persuade President Trump that Russia and the US can expand
commercial ties without first reaching a peace agreement in Ukraine, Bloomberg reports [paywall], citing one
person close to the Kremlin. This follows an agreement on the US procurement of Russian diesel supplies and
could eventually extend to areas including fertilisers, gas and frozen Russian assets. Regardless of the
outcome of the midterms, a speedy resolution to the Ukraine-Russia conflict is unlikely, especially one
brokered by the US. The Kremlin has already lost confidence in Trump's ability to pressure Ukraine into
accepting a settlement on Russian terms, Bloomberg reported. After a defeat to the Democrats, Trump will be
unable to exert pressure on either Ukraine or Russia. And even if the Democrats sweep both Congressional
chambers, Trump can veto any legislation to support Ukraine as he desires, only to be overridden by a
two-thirds majority. (Wayne Gouro, Macro Sales)

China Services Sector Growth Slows To Three-Month Low  - China’s RatingDog services PMI fell to 50.8
in October from September’s 51.6, indicating the slowest expansion since July but remaining above the
breakeven 50 level that separates expansion from contraction. Encouragingly, new business growth held
broadly steady, export orders rose for a sixth month, and hiring continued. Still, stiff competition prompted
firms to lower selling prices for a second month, while material, fuel and wage costs pushed input inflation to a
five-month high. Consequently, business confidence fell to its lowest since July. The survey suggests
continued expansion, but rising costs and discounting risk squeezing margins. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Turkey’s Core Inflation Declines, Paving Way For Rate Cuts - UBS Economics: Turkey’s core inflation
dropped 40bp to 28.3% y/y in October, supporting the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye’s (CBRT)
easing narrative. Despite headline inflation ticking up slightly to 29.8% y/y, declining core and services inflation
signal reduced domestic price pressures. Economist Gyorgy Kovacs forecasts another 100bp rate cut to 35%
at CBRT’s December meeting, aligning with its forward guidance. Risks include external shocks, such as
geopolitical tensions, which could pause easing. Looking ahead, Gyorgy expects headline inflation to exceed
30% y/y by end-2026 due to rising fuel prices and less favorable food inflation base effects. Elevated inflation
expectations among households and corporates may sustain inertia in services inflation. (Jonathan Garber,
Macro Sales)
