US Election Monitor: Final Update and What to Watch Tonight
3 November 2026 | 8:51AM EST
This is our final edition of the Election Monitor, including a summary of where the Senate 
and House races stand followed by a guide to watching the results as they are reported.  
As of November 2, prediction markets imply over a 70% chance that Democrats win 
the Senate majority, a significant increase from the roughly 60% chance two weeks 
ago (Exhibit 1). 
 
Democratic candidates continue to lead polls in all of the seats they hold plus in 7 
Republican-held seats (Exhibit 2). However, the marginal races are still within the 
margin of error and polling errors have become more polarized in recent midterm 
Senate elections, with Republicans outperforming their final preelection polling in 
red states (e.g., Alaska, Ohio, and Texas).

What to Watch as the Results Come In 
The key question tonight is how large the nationwide swing toward Democrats will be. 
Some early House bellwethers will signal the size of the Democratic wave:   
KY-06: An early signal might come from Kentucky’s 6th District (R+15 in 2024), where

will likely take more than three hours. Like many jurisdictions, the early returns will 
include pre-Election Day votes, which lean more Democratic (Democrats outnumbered 
Republicans in early voting by 4pp). If Dembo, the Democrat, comes within a few points 
of winning here, it would suggest Democrats are set to have as good an election as polls 
have suggested.

demographically similar to Texas and could shed some light on how much the vote might 
shift there. A recent Emerson poll had Rep. Castor (D) leading by 5pp here; a Democratic 
loss might suggest polls overstated Democratic support. 
NC-1 and NC-Senate: We expect 90% of the votes to be counted by 10:30 PM. 
Republicans redrew NC-1 into a district that would have supported Trump by 11.5pp in 
2024, but it is currently seen as a toss-up. If the incumbent Democrat, Rep. Don Davis, 
wins tonight, this would suggest Democrats are performing in line with the polls. In the 
Senate, former Gov. Roy Cooper appears very likely to win, with polls showing him 
leading by 11-12%. Democrats had one of their strongest early vote showings in North 
Carolina relative to other states, so the read across to some of the states in the middle of 
the country with tight races (IA, KS, MI, OH, TX, etc.) might be limited. 
OH-Senate: Polls close at 7:30 PM ET, and we expect 90% of the votes to be counted by 
10:30 PM, so this could be the earliest of the marginal Senate races to be called. A 
Democratic loss in Ohio combined with signs of underperformance in Texas would make 
a Democratic Senate majority unlikely. Democrats would then need to win Alaska, Maine, 
and Iowa in addition to North Carolina, and Iowa is seen as a harder seat for them to win 
than either Texas or Ohio. 
VA-01, VA-02, and VA-05: By 10:30 PM, we should begin to get a read on the likely size 
of the Democratic House majority with results in a few competitive races in Virginia 
(VA-01, VA-05, and VA-02). The results in VA-02 will show us if a strong Republican 
candidate in Rep. Jen Kiggans can survive a nationwide shift toward Democrats which 
could in turn shine light on the prospects for Collins in Maine and Hinson in Iowa. 
TX-Senate: Most polls in Texas close at 8:00 PM ET, and we expect over half the vote to 
be counted within an hour. Democrats have increased their share of the early vote 
compared with two years ago (Exhibit 3) but, as with many states, the remaining pool of 
Election Day voters leans more Republican (Exhibit 4). This is likely to mean that early 
counts in Texas will lean several points more Democratic than the final result. If the race 
is tight, as polls suggest it will be, media outlets are unlikely to make a call until late in the 
night (or morning). 
Calls in the remaining key Senate races will likely take longer. Michigan and Maine are 
unlikely to be called before Wednesday, and Alaska could take several days if the race is 
tight due to its ranked choice voting system.
