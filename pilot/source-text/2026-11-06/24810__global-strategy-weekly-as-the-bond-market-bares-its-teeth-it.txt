As 10y French bond yields rise above 5%, it is clear investors are concerned about 
what the recent riots in France might mean for the fiscal arithmetic – link. The French 
fiscal position already looked perilous, but is it any worse than that of the UK, the US 
or indeed China? Warren Buffett famously said, “You don’t find out who’s been 
swimming naked until the tide goes out”. But is the 2026 global bond bear market being 
driven by macro fundamentals, or is a downturn in the global liquidity cycle really 
what is dominating financial market events? And if the liquidity cycle has turned 
down, are equities next in the firing line?  
 I was listening to my friend and former client, George Noble, interviewing Larry Jeddeloh 
of The Institutional Strategist – link. I’d never seen Larry interviewed before, and he came 
across as soft-spoken and thoughtful. His macro analysis is similar to mine – that we are in a 
long secular bear market for bonds (nothing like a bit of confirmation bias). This is a mirror 
image of the Ice Age thesis, which saw a secular descent into secular stagnation from 1980 to 
2020. This new theme could well be the backdrop for decades!  
 Larry’s 10% target for US 10y is not for this cycle, but for a couple of cycles further out. It 
reminds me of Ray Dalio of Bridgewater, who said a while back that investors would be 
surprised by how high interest rates would need to go, not in the current cycle, but in the next 
one (more from Ray later). 
 For those of us who take a long view, identifying the secular backdrop is almost more 
important than pinpointing exactly where we are in the current economic or credit cycle. I 
know clients are currently being told not to worry that bond yields have risen back to 5% as 
this is the old ‘normal’ (the negative bond yields we saw in the aftermath of the Covid 
pandemic being the last gasp of a QE/ZIRP-inspired fantastic bubble in bond prices). 
 I’ve had the pleasure this week of reading and reflecting on the thoughts of two of the 
great thinkers from the bond space, Ray Dalio of Bridgewater and my former Kleinwort bond-
market colleague from yesteryear, Edward Ballsdon who writes The Grey Fire Horse blog.

markets (and shortly perhaps the equity market) more to do with receding ‘excess’ liquidity than 
macro-fundamentals? Twas ever thus? Ray Dalio’s analysis dovetails very closely with the king of 
global liquidity – David Howell of CrossBorder Capital. Howell has been warning that the excess 
liquidity tide is going out as a combination of a robust real economy and heavy demands on the 
capital markets drain the font of golden spring waters that has floated market valuations higher 
– read here. Maybe the chart below is the most important of all for bonds, equities and much else. 
Forget macro-fundamentals? China’s credit impulse leads the US equity market… lower!
