 
Speculators Are Gunning For No Fed Hike In 
December 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, NOV 03, 2026 - 01:40 PM 
Authored by Simon White, Bloomberg macro strategist, 
A surge in speculators’ positioning in fed funds futures indicates they see a lower policy rate 
than priced. 
US equity futures extended the ugliness of Friday’s price action yesterday with NDX now in 
correction (down over 10% from its highs)... 
 
...and the SOX semiconductors index down over 10% in the last two days (with only 0.4% of 
days posting a worse daily return in the index’s 32-year history than Friday's calamitous decline) 
to its lowest since April... 
 

 
 
Hedge fund selling and rotation were fingered as the main drivers of the move, as portfolio 
managers contend with mounting AI-related trade losses. The fat drop in gold, 5.4%, on a day 
when yields and the dollar were lower, signaled a market in need of liquidity to cover losses and 
margin elsewhere. 
With so much of the fortunes of the economy and the market hitched to the AI train, hiking rates 
becomes a precarious pursuit. Nonetheless, the market overall still sees an almost 75% chance 
the Fed hikes in December. 
 
Speculators and leveraged funds, though, are rapidly coming to another view.  
 

 
Their net long in fed funds futures as a percentage of open interest, based on Commitment of 
Traders data, has surged in the last couple of weeks to its highest level since 2020 (latest data 
as of last Tuesday and published on Friday). 
 
Interestingly, the position in SOFR futures remains net short.  
Some of this could be spread trades (SOFR has widened versus fed funds of late as the size of 
the Treasury’s account at the Fed has grown), but I suspect the main driver is views of tighter 
Fed policy expectations. 
Positioning in fed funds futures is probably a cleaner read on what speculators are thinking 
about Fed policy than SOFR futures, which extend much further down the curve and are heavily 
used to hedge other products, such as rate swaps, cross-currency swaps, etc. 
That’s not to say speculators are necessarily correct on the Fed.  
Their historical record based on their positioning shows they have no clear edge.  
 

 
 
But given the turmoil in the markets, and what’s at stake if the AI trade falters, a lower expected 
chance of the Fed hiking in December seems appropriate.
