JPM CREDIT RESEARCH: AI CAPEX / DATACENTERS – full note is here 
Early Thoughts On Blue Wave Implications On DC & Power Funding 

•         B.L.U.F. – Slowing overall data center development and deployment is not necessarily 
all bad. We don’t anticipate any material changes to 2027 capital plans (if developers are 
spending money on projects in 2027 they are already under construction), but all else equal 
we would expect some impact in 2028 and beyond. Shallowing the growth curve should 
allow more time for monetization to catch up to spending, and likely keep compute in short 
supply longer than the counterfactual case. From a funding market standpoint, most 2027 
projects have been funded. So, the real question is when does the market begin to fund 
2028 and beyond projects. Realistically, we expect bond supply to slow in the near-term as 
the market wrestles with the implications of the election, with the first set of new deals to 
launch likely focused on data centers and GPUs deployed in “safe” jurisdictions. The likely 
issuance slowdown should bode well for relieving at least a portion of the technical pressure 
on the sector. 
OVERVIEW 
•         Focus on Governors … Texas, Georgia, New Mexico, Nevada, and Ohio 
•         Local ballot measures to restrict have passed or are about to. This is ~45 ballot 
measures across 13 states 
•         Independent Power Producers (IPP) – expect weakness in the group esp. those with 
exposure in Texas, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Georgia (ALPGEN, CEG, LONRID, NRG, TLN, 
VST) 
  
POWER & UTILITIES 
•         IPPs such as VST, TLN, and CEG could see increased contracted revenue as their 
existing PPAs come online in the next few years, which could help insulate balance sheets 
from power price volatility longer-term. 
•         Behind-the-Meter (BTM) Power Providers to see the largest near-term impacts. We 
expect the more bespoke BTM/bridge power providers (SEI, VOLTAG) to see the most 
negative near-term impact, as extended data center construction timelines could soften 

demand for bridge power solutions before new supply hits the grid in 2029, particularly in 
Texas and PJM. 
•         Regulated utilities could see some reset of growth expectations, but data centers have 
been more of a “nice to have” for credit. 
•         ***the team look at state-by-state impacts in the full note*** 
  
DATA CENTERS 
•         We expect the primary investor focus to be on any changes to TX’s regulation of data 
centers, as ~27% percent of the MW of data center capacity funded in the HY project 
finance market are being built in TX. Much of this risk will be concentrated within 2028/29 
energizations which could face delays or even, in a draconian case, terminations 
(somewhat offset by termination rights outlined in Figure 3). 
•         Assets in the ground are going to be at a premium … “we used to pay a premium for IG 
leases, now we are going to pay a premium for stuff that is already built”. 
•         Data centers and power infrastructure that have already been built or at least are fully 
permitted and under construction are worth more today than they were yesterday. However, 
the path to get new data centers permitted and constructed in critical states like Texas, 
Georgia, New Mexico, and Nevada is likely to be much more difficult, slower, and more 
expensive.

US MKT INTEL VIEW  
Despite the challenging October environment and residual headwinds to our Tactical 
Bullish call, we are sticking with the view. First, when you see a flush like we witnessed on 
Friday there tends to be a near-term rebound. Speaking with John Schlegel and the 
Positioning Intel team, they ran some numbers and find that when the NDX is within ~6% 
of ATH highs and you see a 3.5%+ decline, the index tends to rebound ~3% the following 
week. The effect tends to be stronger if that 3.5% decline occurs on a Friday, perhaps 
reflecting a higher level of de-grossing driving the move rather than a shift in fundamental 
views. I understand this sounds like a baseball stat and that weekend headlines around AI 
were negative on capital raise (CNBC), circular financing (WSJ), and legal (CNBC) may give 
some investors pause but we think the combination of a strengthening broadening trend 
plus stabilization in Tech will push the SPX higher. 
  
Second, this week’s data can assuage stagflationary / recessionary fears. There was a 
spike in client question on the health of the US economy if AI capex declines and whether 
we see a significant recession, mirroring the Tech Bubble burst at the beginning of the 
century. My views is No given (i) health of the consumer – household net worth is at 
record levels with cash levels also historically strong … consumer checking accounts have 
quintupled since COVID which was at a historical high. Further, there remains significant 
room to ramp debt capacity based on debt service as a percentage of disposable income. 
The strongest period of credit strength in US history was late-2021/early-2022 and we are 
not yet seeing credit card delinquency / charge-off data fully normalize to pre-COVID levels; 
(ii) businesses unlikely to see margin compression – public businesses are 
experiencing record / near-record profit margins and that would need to compress before 
mass layoffs begin; (iii) AI is not a significant driver of hiring – generally, AI is thought to 
be a headwind to hiring and on the construction side that is where the human capital 
demand exists but is tempered by current immigration policy. Thought differently, for all of 
the turmoil caused by CRWV it is a company that employs 550 people. I do not want to be 
dismissive of a potential domino effect but given the AI monetization questions it may be the 
case that the biggest effects are capex declines (affecting GDP growth metrics but not 
necessarily a material impact on labor markets or spending) and potential hyperscaler 

layoffs than an economy-wide phenomenon. Separately, the health of the consumer and 
subsequent spending remain robust with the biggest impact being a negative wealth effect 
from a bear market. We are seeing an increase in the %age of spending tied to consumers 
monetizing their investment accounts rather than checking or savings accounts.  
  
Third, the biggest non-AI risk stems from a re-escalation in the Middle East. If you have not 
seen the WSJ article on the considerable damage inflicted to US regional bases by Iran, it is 
worth a read (here). This situation combined with depleted munition levels suggests that 
another wave of attacks could be limited in scope and duration with diminished ability to 
repel the Iran counterattack. This may point to heightened diplomatic efforts. In the event of 
another series of attacks, oil markets appear to have already priced this outcome 
suggesting limited upside to energy prices and thus limited room for bond yields to move 
and Equities to decline. More color on this can be found in the US / Iran bullet in the next 
section, where our views are shaped by work from Natasha Kaneva and Brendan Henrici.  
  
Lastly, positive seasonality returns. November is the strongest month, on average, this 
century. Post-midterm seasonality is also a tailwind.
