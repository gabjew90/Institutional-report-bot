 
Splash Zone: Hedge Funds Go Underweight AI For 
First Time Since 2023 As Record De-Grossing 
Meets Record-Low Correlation 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 02, 2026 - 02:00 AM 
Trick or treat? 
For most of this earnings season the answer seemed to be "treat": 80% of S&P companies beat 
on EPS, blended Q3 profit growth is running at 30%, and US GDP just grew 3.4%. And then 
came the last 48 hours of October, which Morgan Stanley's Nick Savone, in his latest weekly 
"Global Reflections" (available to pro subs), says felt "a little like 'Friday the 13th,' with an 
exhausting succession of earnings reactions leaving investors struggling to catch their breath." 
That's one way of putting it. Another way: Intel just had its worst day since 1974, Alphabet 
suffered the biggest drop in its history, the Nasdaq 100 briefly slid into correction and... the 
average stock had a great week. In fact, Thursday was so strange that it has never happened 
before: 
Thursday's price action revealed a rather "spooky" divergence: the S&P 500 declined even as 
more than 80% of stocks advanced, marking the strongest positive breadth on an S&P 500 
down day since at least 1990, while stock dispersion reached the 88th percentile of its five-year 
history. 
 

 
 
MS adds that Thursday was the first time in its data history (nearly 40 years) that the market 
was down while more than 80% of stocks closed up. For context, the average S&P return on 
such a day is +1.63%; nearly two-thirds of stocks gained more than 1%, a third rallied more than 
2%, and 8 of 11 sectors closed higher. In other words, the index is now just a handful of AI 
stocks wearing a 500-stock costume, which is fitting for Halloween. 
And as the AI costume came off, the people wearing it, i.e., hedge funds, ran for the exits. As 
Savone puts it, investors may be "approaching the final drop of the 'log flume' ride, with the 
hyperscalers still facing a potential washout and the 'splash zone' extending across the broader 
AI ecosystem." Let's see who's getting wet. 
The Largest De-Grossing On Record 
We start with positioning, because that's where the real horror show is. According to Savone, 
hedge funds have now moved underweight the broader AI trade for the first time since early 
2023, and the combined summer and October de-grossing marks "the largest sustained stretch 
 

 
of L/S fund de-risking in over 15 years, as gross exposure never fully recovered between 
episodes." 
The MS Prime Brokerage data is brutal: cumulative gross flow is now down 8% from the June 
peak, the largest sustained de-grossing on record (as our friends at The Market Ear flagged this 
morning). And yet, the bulk of this week's buying came from short covering, which pushed US 
L/S net leverage up 5 points to 51%, while gross slipped 1 point to 207% (still in the 92nd 
percentile since 2010, but just the 10th percentile over the past year). 
 
Where did the money go? The MS PB Content team says US funds covered index hedges and 
added Tech exposure, particularly in Memory, Agentic AI, Internet and Cybersecurity (oops, 
more on that below), while selling Hyperscalers, Energy, Materials and Real Estate. Meanwhile, 
~35-40% of L/S funds are now flat or negative YTD, and the October scorecard still looks ugly: 
Americas L/S down 1.7% MTD vs +1.0% for the S&P, Europe L/S down 1.7% vs +2.4% for the 
STOXX 600, and Asia L/S down 4.0% vs -5.0% for MSCI Asia Pacific (at least they beat 
something). 
 

 
Translation: funds can't rebuild exposure because every time they try, the thing they bought 
blows up. Which may explain why investors are "reluctant to rebuild exposure," as Savone 
delicately puts it. This is also, of course, the precondition for a washout bottom... if only 
someone could tell us when the washout ends. 
Record Single-Stock Vol, Record-Low Correlation 
The flip side of a market where the index is a handful of AI names and everything else trades on 
its own is that stock-level chaos has never been greater. MS QDS's Amanda Goldsmith notes 
that S&P 500 dispersion over the past two weeks sits in the 88th percentile vs. the last 5 years 
(+5 percentiles WoW), single-name vol is in the 83rd percentile (+13 WoW), and the ratio of 
between-sector to within-sector dispersion is in the 79th percentile, "which suggests that 
dispersion has been more driven by sectors/factors/macro and thematic moves, rather than 
moves at the single-stock level." Put simply: AI vs. everything else. 
 

 
 
But the two charts that really jump out are these: single-name vol relative to index vol is at a 
record high (going back to 2005, and well above both the GFC and Covid), while the six-month 
pairwise correlation of S&P stocks has dropped to a record low below 10%. 
 

 
 
Regular readers know that this is a theme we have been pounding the table on since the 
summer, when we first noted that "We Have Never Seen Such A Decoupling Between Price And 
Breadth" (Aug 19). Well, now we have... again. And it's even worse. 
Equal Weight Tests The Line, Mag 7 Takes A Tumble 
All of this is starting to show up where it matters for mutual fund managers heading into their 
October 31 fiscal year-end: in relative performance. Savone's chart pack asks "will managers 
defend yesterday's winners?" and the early answer is... no. The RSP/NDX ratio is testing the 
downtrend that has been in place since Nvidia's May 2023 blowout quarter, while equal-weight 
S&P (RSP) is now up ~15% YTD vs. ~13% for the SPX and just ~6% for the Mag 7. 
 

 
 
Something we also discussed on Friday when we noted that "Beyond The AI Massacre: 
S&P500 Ex-AI Stocks Head For Best Month Since 2022," with the S&P ex-AI cohort on track for 
a 6% monthly gain even as AI-related trades got clubbed like a baby seal. 
"Behind The Mask" 
So what happened? The short answer: earnings were good, just "not quite good enough," and 
the hoped-for "ballast" from stronger results gave way to concerns that growth may be "peaking" 
just as multiples come under pressure. As Savone writes, with 80% of reporters beating on EPS 
and 69% beating on sales, "the modest 2.8% median EPS surprise has nevertheless offered 
 

 
limited support for positive revisions." And with 44.5% of S&P market cap having reported this 
week, there are few big catalysts left: 13.2% reports next week, then it's mostly over. 
 
Note the sector table: blended 3Q EPS growth of 30% is being carried almost entirely by Energy 
(+143%) and Info Tech (+65%), while Health Care is negative and Staples, Discretionary and 
Financials are in the low single digits. Which is why, when the hyperscalers disappoint even 
slightly, there's nothing to catch the index.

Wilson: Three Clearing Events Before The Final Low 
So how close are we to the bottom of the log flume? MS chief equity strategist Mike Wilson, who 
we profiled on Tuesday in "Three Clearing Events To Capitulation," thinks the mid-cycle 
correction is "entering its final stages," although the S&P may still converge toward weaker 
market breadth. His three clearing events: i) AI financing concerns, ii) the midterm elections and 
iii) tightening financial conditions driven by Fed policy and higher energy prices. Wilson sees 
liquidity as "the biggest near-term risk and potential catalyst for a final market low," and expects 
semiconductor weakness to find support around mid-November, "potentially after another 
10–15% decline." 
The good news is that the gap between the S&P (as a % of its 200-DMA) and the share of 
stocks above their 200-DMA, which was 6% in SPX terms, is "starting to converge as 
predicted." The bad news: the MS Sentiment Indicator is only back at "neutral" and "could push 
to extremes," i.e., capitulation levels last seen on Liberation Day. 
 
 

 
For now, Wilson sticks to his 8,000 year-end S&P base case and continues to favor quality. His 
Fresh Money Buy List: Delta, EQT, Knight-Swift, Northrop Grumman and AbbVie (also available 
to pro subs). Not a single AI name among them, which tells you all you need to know. 
The Fed Is Hiking Into This 
As if that wasn't enough, the policy backdrop is no help. Savone notes that the familiar 
regulatory, monetary and fiscal "holy trinity" has provided little support, "leaving markets to do 
some of the Fed's tightening themselves" (a theme Wilson has been hammering for weeks, see 
"Doing The Fed's Work"). Warsh held at 3.75-4.0% this week after much waffling, but MS chief 
economist Michael Gapen still expects 25bp hikes in December and March, taking the target 
range to 4.25-4.5%. And if AI investment fuels broader demand and tighter labor, "the Fed could 
require more aggressive tightening." 
Yet the market reacted dovishly, which raises Savone's very good question: "are lower yields 
reflecting greater confidence in inflation, or growing concern about earnings, equity markets, 
and the economy?" We suspect the latter. Rate-insensitive maybe... but not stupid. 
The Midterm Wildcard 
Which brings us to the second of Wilson's clearing events, now just days away. Prediction 
markets are pricing 66% odds of a Democratic sweep, and MS flags NC-03, FL-07, FL-13 and 
VA-05 as the seats to watch: a Dem win in any of them implies 97% odds of a wave election. 
 

 
 
The market impact matrix is instructive. A Republican win (8% odds) is "status quo" with an 
OBBBA tailwind; a Democratic sweep (65%) brings "regime uncertainty/DC pushback concerns" 
on the index and, crucially for the AI trade, an "expectation for more durable & sweeping 
restrictions" on data centers; a narrow Democratic win (29%) is the "most fiscally restrictive" and 
carries "downside risk via brinksmanship." 
 

 
 
On the consumer side, MS economist Heather Berger estimates the scheduled SNAP and 
Medicaid cuts would subtract 20bps from real consumption growth in 2027, with her baseline 
slowing from 2.5% to 2.0%. A Democratic sweep would likely delay the cuts, lifting 2027 
consumption growth by just 0.1pp to 2.1%, while a broader reversal (including existing SNAP 
work requirements) could add 0.4pp. Of course, delayed benefit cuts would also mean bigger 
deficits... and as we noted in our October recap, bond vigilantes are already spooked. Interest in 
MS's Democrat vs. Republican policy-exposed baskets is picking up accordingly (see the first 
chart in the SaaS exhibit above): the Dem basket is up to 107 while the GOP basket has 
slumped below 97. 
Meanwhile, Brazil just showed what happens when an election goes "the wrong way." Investors 
had assigned an estimated 80-95% probability to a Flávio Bolsonaro win; instead Lula won with 
51.8% of the vote, and in the two days that followed local investor portfolios fell 15-30% amid 
~$2.5 billion in futures selling (as we chronicled in "Many Macro Fund Casualties"). MS's Thiago 
Machado sees USD/BRL potentially testing 5.50 before moving toward 5.60-5.70, while the bear 
case points to 130K on the Ibovespa by mid-2027. Not exactly an advertisement for trusting the 
prediction markets. 
Ghosts In Asia, Skeletons In Europe 
Elsewhere, Korea's early stabilization had investors hoping the region could lead a broader AI 
recovery, but Friday's selling suggested "a few 'ghosts' of the recent unwind may still be 
lingering." The KOSPI fell 3.5% (Taiwan -2.5%, Nikkei -2.1%), SK Hynix dropped 7.1% despite 
its favorable memory outlook, and the KOSPI's drawdown since late September has reached 
~16-17% vs. ~3-4% for the Nikkei. Kioxia's ADR fell 17% after a disappointing 
 

 
December-quarter outlook raised fresh questions around NAND pricing momentum (something 
we discussed in "Flash Crash: Kioxia Warns NAND Price Surge Ending"). In China, the CSI300 
lost 2.9% and the CSI1000 plunged 8.5% WTD, although suspected state-backed ETF buying 
helped stabilize sentiment (MS's Laura Wang says the "National Team" bought US$15 billion of 
equities during October 9-20). 
Europe had its own "skeletons in the closet," with fiscal uncertainty and rising energy costs 
lurking behind decent earnings. France avoided a Moody's downgrade last week, but on Friday 
Fitch cut its outlook to negative in a surprise warning. More ominously, European gas prices 
briefly breached €100/MWh for the first time since 2022, with Pan-European storage at just 
74.9% heading into winter. And with Iran conducting its first limpet mine attack on a tanker since 
2019 on Friday, a winter energy squeeze looks a lot more likely than it did a month ago. 
MS's chief European equity strategist Marina Zavolock asks "are we there yet?" The 13% 
pullback in European cyclicals vs. defensives is comparable to past growth scares, European AI 
enablers have surrendered nearly two-thirds of their peak relative gains, and the AI 
enabler-vs-challenged trade has fallen ~40% since June. She sees potential recovery 
leadership in Semis, Tech Hardware, Cap Goods, Metals & Mining and Banks, with Top Picks 
Nokia, ASML and ABN AMRO. 
 

 
 
The One AI Trade Still Working: Power 
If there is a "treat" in Savone's note, it's power. MS's Stephen Byrd and Mayank Maheshwari 
raised their global power consumption estimates by 10% to ~36 trillion units by 2030, as 
electricity demand broadens "well beyond data centers" to industrial and household 
consumption ("Agents, Aircons, Allshoring"). They see the electrification cycle unlocking ~$500 
billion in market value by 2027 and driving ~14% upgrades to consensus 2028 EPS for 
preferred stocks, with Asia expected to absorb ~15GW of US data-center demand "as power 
constraints intensify." 
 

 
 
Byrd also updated his US power shortfall model, which now shows a net shortfall of 38GW 
through 2028 even after accounting for front-of-the-meter and behind-the-meter capacity under 
construction, available grid GWs, nuclear, turbines, Bloom fuel cells and spillover into APAC. 
That's down from the "44 nuclear power plants" shortfall Byrd first flagged last November, but 
still enormous. 
 
Meanwhile, MS's updated global data center forecasts imply capacity will grow by +180GW, or 
23% per year, from 2026 to 2030, with the US (+88GW) and China (+49GW) leading and 
Europe lagging (+12GW). The team's AlphaWise survey confirms energy remains the primary 
obstacle to building DCs, although new constraints have emerged: financing, memory, powered 
land and chips.

Waiting For The Final Splash 
Savone ends on the log flume one more time: 
Yet with positioning already considerably lighter, another round of selling could also help clear 
the way for a more durable recovery, provided earnings expectations stabilize and AI financing 
concerns ease... As Halloween gives way to November, the question is no longer simply "Trick 
or Treat?", but how close investors really are to the "final splash" and what awaits on the other 
side of the ride… Stay nimble! 
It's a reasonable setup: positioning is lighter, the de-grossing is the biggest on record, and the 
average stock is doing just fine. But note the conditions: earnings expectations need to stabilize 
(with growth "peaking"), AI financing concerns need to ease (with the AI off-balance sheet time 
bomb hitting $4.1 trillion), and the Fed needs to stop hiking (it isn't). Wilson wants another 
10-15% out of semis first, and the sentiment indicator is only at "neutral." So maybe the splash 
is close... but the hyperscalers haven't hit the water yet.
