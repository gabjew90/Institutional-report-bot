1) AI x Hyperscalers x Energy
AI: Legislative restrictions are difficult to quantify (particularly in out year), but building will become
more expensive, complicated and slower. Multiples have compressed enormously, positioning was already
derisked and another aggressive wave of selling followed. Some moves felt capitulatory but time will tell.
The market is definitively pricing peak CapEx growth. Does it also need to price an outright deceleration
in spending? I think not necessarily. Delayed projects aren’t cancelled demand, but could create an
inventory glut across chips, turbines, cooling and electrical equipment. Has the industry overordered
against an energisation schedule it can no longer deliver? Does that ultimately undermine semiconductor
capacity expansion at ASML and TSMC? The risk is an inventory cycle, not necessarily demand
destruction.
Compute Owners: The flipside is that existing energised compute becomes more valuable. Hyperscalers
potentially enjoy better economics on scarce capacity, while constrained expansion implies less CapEx
and higher FCF. Amazon rallied aggressively despite last week’s FCF concerns. Scarcity is good for those
who own capacity, potentially disastrous for those whose valuations depend on building it.
Energy: The physical market has effectively collapsed with physical Brent trading at a $2.87 discount to
Dated Brent yesterday, reportedly record extremes. This was >$20/bl just 3 weeks ago. November CFDs
and the front three weeks of Dated Brent in contango. Prompt Brent fell ~$8.50 across the session and
after-hours to ~$94. Oil pricing as if the war is over...some arguing fair value below $70. Physical scarcity
disappearing well ahead of any confirmed resolution. Hugely supportive for rates, consumer and
everything the market hasn’t owned. A little worried locally we are a bit ahead of ourselves, are we sure
there wont be any weekend escalation? (TY Rich Privo)
2) The Impact of State Election Results on Power and Utilities
We believe Democratic wins could increase near-term uncertainty around data center development,
particularly in Texas and Georgia, though we continue to see these markets as important longer-term areas
for load growth.
Texas: the gubernatorial outcome could drive negative reactions for exposed stocks including Buy rated
VST, NRG, SRE, and XEL, as well as Neutral rated CEG and AEP.
Georgia: we believe the PSC election outcome could be negative for Neutral rated SO.

Ohio: we believe the gubernatorial outcome could be less constructive for Buy rated FE and Neutral rated
AEP, with more limited impact on Buy rated DUK.
California: we now await clarity on a potential special legislative session before November 15th. If called
and depending on the outcome, it might be a positive for Buy rated PCG and SRE as well as Neutral rated
EIX.
3) 4Q26 Treasury Refunding Recap
Treasury’s estimates for marketable borrowing came in a touch above our expectations for 4Q26 ($618bn
vs. our $571bn projection), while the 1Q27 borrowing projection came in above our forecast ($800bn vs.
our $713bn estimate), driven primarily by a higher-than-expected TGA cash balance target of $1tn (versus
our $900bn assumption).
Treasury also emphasized demand considerations and cost effectiveness as it evaluates potential issuance
shifts. Debt managers highlighted growing demand for bills from the private sector and SOMA purchases,
signaling comfort with bill absorption for now, though TBAC noted signs of increasing price sensitivity
amid sluggish MMF AUM growth. We think this, coupled with cited structural demand trends, will limit
auction size increases to the 2-to-10y part of the curve. Our baseline remains for long-end auction sizes to
remain unchanged, but risks skew towards modest reductions.
