From panic to squeeze
Gold is back with a vengeance after a brutal positioning washout. Nearly $20 billion in speculative
selling, sizeable CTA shorts and a surge in downside hedging had set the stage for a violent squeeze.
Meanwhile, falling oil prices, dollar weakness and renewed Chinese demand are providing additional
support. Beyond the short-term positioning reset, a new structural gold buyer is emerging in the form
of Tether, now one of the world's largest physical accumulators. The squeeze has been impressive, but
is this the beginning of something bigger? The technicals still have some work to do.
Gold technicals
Gold's shakeout has confused many investors. We are trading back above the short-term trend line
that has been in place since late August, but the shiny metal needs to reclaim the 50-day MA ($4,205)
to establish a more persistent bullish move. The 200-day MA remains well above current levels, sitting
just below $4,500.

Recall the gold dump?
As we highlighted a few days ago, citing Goldman's Quinn, gold had already experienced a massive
positioning washout ahead of the latest squeeze. Five consecutive weeks of speculative selling saw
$19.5 billion dumped, with the final week alone accounting for $8 billion. That included $5.2 billion of
long liquidation and $2.8 billion of fresh shorts. Other and Non-Reportable traders accounted for
roughly 95% of the latest selling. A huge amount of selling had already taken place before gold started
squeezing violently higher.
GS
Panic at lows
Goldman's Quinn pointed out a few days ago that the options market was flashing extreme demand for
downside protection. Three-month implied volatility was rising, while normalized 25-delta put-call skew
had reached its 85th percentile over the past year. Traders were paying unusually high premiums for

downside puts relative to calls. Just days later, gold is squeezing violently higher. The timing of that
rush for downside protection was particularly striking.
GS
CTAs in gold
CTAs were running sizeable gold shorts heading into the latest violent squeeze. Some of that exposure
has likely already been covered, adding fuel to the reversal. The question now is how much short-
covering firepower remains. The positioning reset helps explain the violence of the move, but doesn't
necessarily guarantee follow-through.
GS
China's AI trade: Buy gold, sell copper
Goldman Sachs flags a sharp pickup in Chinese activity, with traders buying gold and selling copper.
GS sees this as an expression of concerns around the AI investment cycle rather than a broader move
out of equities. Gold remains bid, but open interest is flat as short covering is offset by fresh longs, with
volumes still relatively light. Goldman warns that the trade is increasingly dangerous and vulnerable to
a reversal.

The inverse oil trade
Goldman's Privorotsky argues that gold may have become an inverse oil trade. Emerging-market
central banks have spent years accumulating gold as a reserve asset, but soaring energy bills may
have forced some to sell holdings or at least pause purchases. As oil reverses lower, funding pressure
eases and gold catches a bid. The theory remains unproven, but could explain recent price action
better than real yields alone. The trade implication: Long gold may now be a more attractive de-

escalation trade than short oil, given how much geopolitical premium has already been priced out of
crude.

The gold buying monster
SocGen highlights that Tether has quietly become one of the biggest physical gold buyers in the world.
Since 2023, the stablecoin giant has accumulated 87.5 tonnes of gold, making it the fifth-largest net
buyer globally, behind just three central banks and one ETF. Tether's gold holdings have grown at an
annualized 31% since early 2023, compared with 13.6% for ETFs and just 1.8% for central banks. The
new gold whale is coming from crypto.

Soc Gen
Tether vs central banks
Tether is starting to look more like a central bank than a traditional crypto company. SocGen estimates
the group held 146.5 tonnes of physical gold as of June, ranking it alongside sovereign reserve holders
such as Libya and ahead of the Philippines. The bank estimated holdings could reach 161 tonnes by
September, equivalent to the seventh-largest gold ETF globally. A completely new institutional
heavyweight has emerged in physical gold.

The gold setup
The positioning flush has removed a significant amount of speculative selling pressure, while falling oil,
dollar weakness and emerging structural demand offer additional support. But the distinction between
short covering and genuine fresh buying remains crucial. The next test is whether gold can reclaim the
50-day moving average and sustain the recovery. The squeeze has delivered. Now the bulls need to
prove this is more than a squeeze.
