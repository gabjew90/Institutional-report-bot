From terrific to terrified
Tech has gone from terrific to terrified in less than a month. Volatility is exploding, AI fragility is
reaching historical extremes and hyperscaler cash flow is collapsing. Taiwan is cracking as the
semiconductor selloff spreads, while India is emerging as the unexpected winner of the great AI
rotation. Meanwhile, Treasury yields have plunged, but systematic bond shorts remain sizeable.
Markets are moving fast, positioning is getting shaken out and the next surprise could be a violent
move higher. Don't forget the upside.

The great AI fragility
BofA highlights an extraordinary development beneath the surface. US tech is on track for its second
most fragile year in over three decades, surpassed only by 2025. Earnings reactions are becoming
increasingly violent, despite fundamentals not deteriorating at the same pace. The problem is no
longer just earnings. It is collapsing conviction, AI uncertainty and reflexive positioning. Investors are
reacting more aggressively to the same information.
BofA
The cash flow collapse
Goldman's Brad Shelofsky highlights a dramatic deterioration in hyperscaler cash generation.
Combined quarterly free cash flow has collapsed to negative $31.4 billion, with Microsoft now the only

major hyperscaler still generating positive FCF. As Bobby Molavi points out, these companies are
transitioning from cash-printing machines into cash-spending machines. AI demand isn't the problem.
The cost of delivering it is. More here.

The 10 year
Back on October 8, with the US 10-year pushing 5.30–5.35%, we pointed out that everybody was
expecting yields to move higher. That was precisely the problem. Fast forward to today, and the 10-
year has moved from the top of its trend channel around 5.3% all the way down to channel support
near 4.9%. The 100-day moving average sits slightly lower, around 4.8%.
If you played this move, time to say thank you and move on.

Don't forget the upside
Tech is getting slaughtered, but upside convexity is becoming interesting. BofA highlights IGV and SMH
December call spreads as offering some of the most attractive upside payout ratios among liquid ETFs.
The interesting asymmetry is that a clearing event around the midterms could trigger a violent
recovery, while defined-risk call spreads limit losses to the premium paid. Don't confuse a bearish tape
with an absence of upside opportunities.
