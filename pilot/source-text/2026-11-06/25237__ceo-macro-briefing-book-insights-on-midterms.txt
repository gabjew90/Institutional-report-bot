•
Gridlock generally seen as more stable. The White House’s 
spending plans face greater resistance by a Democratic-controlled 
House and Senate, which should anchor longer-term yields. 
Despite gridlock, negotiation historically has been possible and 
even beneficial for reducing federal debt—think of the Clinton 
surplus years with a Republican Congress and Senate during the 
late 1990s. 
•
Longer-term rates still elevated thanks to structural fiscal 
issues. The FY2026 deficit was USD 2.0te, or 6.2% of GDP—
historically wide—adding to the overall debt levels and pushing 
up the premium investors demand on longer-term US Treasuries. 
Reducing tariffs now would lower revenue and widen the deficit, 
adding upward pressure on rates.
•
The Fed’s independence is better protected. Every Fed board 
nominee needs Senate confirmation, and Elizabeth Warren is 
expected to chair the committee that vets them. That lowers the 
odds of politically driven rate cuts but also reduces upward 
pressure on the long-end because an independent Fed has more 
credibility. 
•
What to do:
o Lock in fixed rates where you can; don't wait for much lower 
long-term rates.
Will gridlock lead to lower 
Treasury yields? 
At the margins – but fiscal 
concerns matter more.

costs fell.

has ended.

Credit spreads widened as markets priced in a Blue Wave 
Odds of (D) Senate Control, % & IG Tech OAS, bp
Source: Bloomberg, UBS, as of 5 November 2026 
•
Democrats campaigned on limiting AI. Democratic governors 
in Texas, Ohio, and Georgia campaigned on restricting data-
center development; Texas holds about 27% of US data center 
capacity. During the past election, voters rejected AI initiatives by 
approving 40 ballot measures limiting AI infrastructure. 
Reportedly, once seated, House Democrats could establish a 
select committee on AI.
•
Markets repriced accordingly. As odds of a Democratic Senate 
control rose, so did spreads on hyperscalers. The magnitude of 
the nationwide shift towards Democratic led to a chipmaker sell-
off. Companies with strong cloud capabilities rallied as existing 
cloud capacity would be more valuable.  
•
What it means:
o States may start rolling out regulation to slow or stop 
additional data center construction plans. Prices on existing 
cloud and compute power may rise as a result. 
o Electricity costs are now political; expect pressure on data 
centers to pay a greater share of grid costs.
Democrats may tighten the reigns 
on data centers.

M&A deal value is strong, but a few mega-deals account

concentrated in a few giant deals led by well-capitalized 
strategics.

Democrats have limited control on antitrust enforcement. 
Antitrust enforcement is under the purview of the M&A-friendly 
White House. Democrats may generate headline risk through 
anti-trust hearings focused on big-tech and data-centers. 
•
What it means:
o Selling: the next 12–24 months offer a stable regulatory 
window and motivated PE buyers.
o Buying: expect competition; corporate buyers pay a median 
11.6x EBITDA, above the multiple paid by PE buyers.

time for review and possible congressional attention.
