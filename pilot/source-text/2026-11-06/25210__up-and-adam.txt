As we noted yesterday, new governors in key states may tighten rules on data center projects. With the four largest hyperscalers 
gaining an average of 4.4% yesterday, the market reaction may seem counterintuitive, but it makes sense. If new builds slow, the AI

benefit if smaller players cannot expand. Less capex spending and capital raising could also ease pressure on balance sheets.
Thursday, November 5, 2026
Looking back at the first post-midterm trading session since 1930, the S&P 500 has risen 72% of the time. Yesterday’s 1.6% rally, the 
second-strongest since 1982 behind only 2018, reinforces a point we often make ahead of elections: while election outcomes matter, 
markets also tend to welcome the reduction in uncertainty that follows. Consumer Discretionary and Financials led the rally, while 
Energy pulled back alongside lower oil prices. Bottom line: investors are cheering a clear-cut election outcome.

Chart of the Day: The Midterm Effect
ADP private employment topped expectations in October, rising 86k and matching September’s downwardly revised gain. Hiring was 
broad-based across firm sizes, with services leading job creation and construction benefiting from the data center buildout. Alongside 
the stronger than expected JOLTS report, the data reinforce our view of a broadly healthy labor market despite pockets of some sector-
level weakness. Looking ahead to Friday’s nonfarm payrolls, we expect a 70k gain, with the unemployment rate holding at 4.2%.
Having already declined amid signs of improving US-Iran dialogue, WTI crude fell another 8%, closing at its lowest level since August 31. 
The decline followed Vice President Vance’s social media post stating: “The reason we lost is the war.” The oil price reaction suggests 
markets are interpreting Vance’s comments as signaling a more dovish stance toward Iran. However, as we noted earlier this week, 
diplomatic efforts have yet to deliver a durable settlement, warranting some caution around the latest optimism.
