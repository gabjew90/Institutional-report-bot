gained 2.4%, its best month since December 2023. Markets are now pricing in two Fed rate hikes over the next 12 months, down from

refunding announcement. Longer term, we expect cooling inflation to pull the 10-year toward our target of 4.50%–4.75%.

November Is Here—What Does History Suggest About Market Seasonality?
Source: FactSet
Chart of the Day: Treasury Triumph
Dating back to 1950, November and December have been the strongest two-month stretch of the year for the S&P 500, gaining more 
than 3% on average and delivering positive returns 77% of the time. Needless to say, history is never a guarantee of future results. Case 
in point: the S&P 500 declined 0.9% in October, compared to the long-term average of a 0.9% gain. Beyond seasonality, investors will 
remain focused on earnings season and the post-election political landscape in the weeks ahead.
As we noted last Friday, a wide range of metal prices have recently come under pressure. Gold is a case in point: Friday marked its first 
close below $4,000/oz since July and its lowest close since October 2025. Notably, gold fell ~7% in October even as the 10-year Treasury 
yield declined to its lowest level since Sept. 9. This is a reminder that gold is a sentiment-driven commodity. Traditional trading 
relationships, including gold’s typical inverse correlation with bond yields, can break down for periods of time, as we’ve seen recently.
