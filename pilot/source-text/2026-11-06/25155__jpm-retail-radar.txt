Midterms, Earnings, Momentum Unwind
•
This week was one of the most consequential of the year—following major 
hyperscaler earnings reports last week, Momentum experienced its second +6-
sigma unwind day in ~a week (Figure 2Highest Negative Sigma Episode in M

and producing its largest single-month underperformance in more than 40 
years at -5.8z (long versus the market, cap-weighted, Figure 1 Largest Single M

saw large volatility swings, pushing the Nasdaq into correction territory as 
markets awaited the results of the Midterm Election. 
•
Overnight on Tuesday, Democrats swept Congress. Their House majority was 
largely expected, but surprisingly, they also now control at least 54 Senate seats 
and won the Texas governor's race. Markets reacted positively when trading 
resumed after an initially cautious start to the Asian session. Oil fell about 8% 
on Wednesday, yields eased, and Consumer stocks outperformed while 
Semiconductor and Energy shares lagged. As volatility dampened risk 
appetite, retail purchases this week fell to near their lowest level of the year 
(3%ile), driven by single-stock activity hovering near its 1y low (0.4%ile), 
while ETF trading edged up modestly to 40%ile. In options, retail share of total 
volume declined steadily in October, Figure 13Retail Investors Option Volume Share, as retail volume fell ~1% while 
total volume rose ~9%, reflecting increased institutional trading.

56%ile day, led by healthy broad-based ETF purchases at 96%ile and Mag 7 
at 94%ile), they promptly capitulated on Monday, resulting in the first

-3.7z, Figure 16Retail Investor Daily Purchases of Stocks and ETFs). Wednesday saw a similar level of single stock selling (-

Earnings
Last week, GOOG/L fell ~13% the day after its earnings miss, drawing significant concern. 
In this context, we conducted a 30-year study of 1-day, post-miss performance for S&P Tech 
names: the median reaction to a miss is just -2.3% absolute (-2.2% vs. the S&P), though that 
penalty has risen to roughly -5% since 2025— likely as misses have become rarer,  so each 
one lands as more of a surprise. While GOOGL's drop is well outside this typical range, it is 
in line with recent episodes involving other big-Tech names: META (-26% Feb-22, -25% 
Oct-22), NVDA (-19% Nov-18), AMZN (-14% Apr-22), and TSLA (-15% Jul-26).
This week, overall, retail investors largely refrained from engaging with these companies 
ahead of, or following their announcements, see Single Stock Stories with Elevated Retail 
Activity.

Retail investors stepped in to buy the dip in GOOG/L this week (~5z, Figure 32Retail Imbalance in GOGL), after 
it fell -13% post-earnings when it announced that the magnitude of Search deceleration 
was steeper than expected, with AI Mode becoming a bigger percentage of search queries 
and likely lagging traditional search in coverage and ad load, resulting in lower overall 
monetization (link).
