The big news this morning is that the Democrats have made significant gains in the midterm elections, with 
multiple news organisations projecting they’ll win both the House of Representatives and the Senate. The 
gains have been substantial, and the AP have already projected they’ll pick up Senate seats in North Carolina, 
South Carolina, Ohio, Kansas, Iowa and Texas from the Republicans. Moreover, the New York Times needle is 
now pointing to a 55-45 Democratic majority in the Senate when all is done, which if realised would leave 
them with 8 gains. In fact, the last time the Democrats had a Senate majority that big was after the 2012 election, 
when Barack Obama won a second term. 
Although votes are still being counted, this means that when the new Congress convenes in January, it will be 
the first time the Democrats have controlled both chambers since the first two years of Joe Biden’s 
presidency. In addition, it’s the first time in either of the two Trump administrations that the Democrats have 
both the House and the Senate, as in Trump’s first term, the Republicans still had a Senate majority after the 
2018 midterms. So this will add to the constraints on the Trump administration. 
Most obviously, a major constraint is that all legislation must be passed by a majority in both chambers of 
Congress, so any new laws will require at least some Democratic support. That includes spending packages to 
prevent a government shutdown, as well as any increases in the debt ceiling. Remember as well that the Senate is 
the chamber that votes to confirm new appointments, including for the Cabinet, Supreme Court Justices, and 
Fed Governors. So this will be the first time in either of Trump’s terms that Democrats could block appointments to 
key positions. Nevertheless, Trump still retains a veto over legislation, so even if Democratic majorities 
passed something through both chambers, Trump’s veto could only be overridden if two-thirds of both chambers 
agreed. 
In terms of the results so far, the direction of travel became clear early in the night. For instance, AP have said the 
Democrats have already made 22 pickups in the House so far, well above the 3 gains required to take the 
majority. That currently leaves them on 205-123 on the AP count, with 218 needed for the majority. So even 
though they haven’t reached the majority line yet, multiple news networks are already projecting a Democratic win in 
the House, including CNN and NBC. Then in the Senate, the 6 Democratic gains so far mean they’re already 
over the majority line, with the AP count currently at 52-41. And elsewhere, the Democrats have also made gains 
in the Governor’s races, with AP projecting Democratic pickups in Ohio, Iowa, Georgia and Texas.  

When it comes to the market reaction, there’s been a clear selloff in US equity futures, led by the tech sector. In part, 
that reflects investor expectations that Democratic control in Congress might lead to tougher regulation on AI, 
and futures on the S&P 500 are down –0.42% this morning, whilst those on the NASDAQ 100 are down -
0.49%. However, S&P futures were down over -1% earlier, so they’ve pared back a decent chunk of those 
earlier losses, and there’s been a rally for US Treasuries as well, given that divided government has generally made 
it harder for fiscal stimulus to be passed. So that’s eased concerns on the fiscal trajectory a bit, and the 2yr Treasury 
yield is down -3.5bps overnight to 4.60%, whilst the 10yr yield is down -2.9bps to 4.94%. Investors are also 
pricing in a more dovish path for the Fed too, with the amount of rate hikes priced by the June 2027 meeting 
down -3.3bps this morning to 55bps. So that’s pushed the dollar index down -0.40% overnight as well. 
In Asia, there’s been a similar pattern overnight, with several of the most tech-exposed indices posting sharp 
losses, although they’ve recovered from earlier in the session. Nevertheless, Japan’s Nikkei is currently down -
2.09% this morning, whilst South Korea’s KOSPI (+0.03%) is now basically flat, but was down more than -4% 
at the lows earlier. Meanwhile other indices have posted gains, including the Hang Seng (+0.89%), the CSI 300 
(+1.02%) and the Shanghai Comp (+0.83%).   
Before we knew about the midterm results, a bit more positivity crept back into markets yesterday. Most notably, oil 
prices continued to fall after Iran’s President Pezeshkian said that the country’s Supreme Leader had granted 
“additional authority”, which would allow talks on issues previously considered “non-negotiable”. Moreover, 
Reuters also reported that Qatari mediators expected feedback from the US and Iran on a new proposal to 
advance talks within 36 hours. So that added to the optimism about some kind of diplomatic resolution, and Brent 
crude oil prices fell -1.12% over the session to $103.60/bbl. And there was a decent decline in European 
natural gas too, which came down another -3.44% to €91.60/MWh. However, there were still some signs of 
caution, and oil prices picked up from the lows after the Secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council said 
on X that “The US response to Iran’s rights and demands was not satisfactory”. And then soon after, Iranian 
state TV cited a spokesman for Iran’s negotiating team, who said that no new developments had been reached on an 
agreement.   
That backdrop helped US equities to finally stage a recovery, with the S&P 500 (+0.36%) picking up after 3 
consecutive declines. That was led by the tech sector, with the NASDAQ (+0.83%) posting an even bigger 
advance, whilst the Philly semiconductor index (+2.63%) rebounded after falling by -11.6% over the previous 
three sessions. And to help matters further, the latest data pointed to ongoing resilience around the US economy. 
For instance, the JOLTS report showed job openings rising to a 4-month high of 7.523m in September (vs. 
7.164m expected). Meanwhile, the quits rate of those voluntarily leaving their job hit a 15-month high of 2.1%, 
and separate data on factory orders showed growth of +1.8% in September (vs. +1.2% expected).   
With that hawkish data in hand, that cemented investor confidence that the Fed would deliver another rate hike at 
their next meeting in December. Indeed, futures raised the probability of a hike from 74% to 78% by the close 
yesterday. And in turn, that hawkish momentum and risk-on sentiment helped to push up Treasury yields as well. So 
the 2yr yield (+3.6bps) was up to 4.63%, whilst the 10yr yield (+5.1bps) rose to 4.96%. 
Over in Europe, investors also became more confident that the ECB would hike rates in December, with market 
pricing for a hike next month up from 83% to 89% by the close. That followed comments from the ECB’s Radev, 
who said that “If incoming data confirm inflationary pressures are becoming more persistent, further rate 
increases may be necessary”. This was also echoed by Nagel, who said “there is likely no way around an interest-
rate hike unless the inflation outlook improves significantly” and Kocher was suggested that a December hike was 
likely if the situation does not change. So that supported investor conviction that the ECB would still deliver a 
December hike, and yields on 10yr bunds (+3.8bps), OATs (+5.1bps) and BTPs (+3.8bps) all moved higher. 
However, European equities underperformed their US counterparts, largely as they caught up to the late 
selloff in the US session the previous day, with the STOXX 600 closing -0.58% lower. And this morning, 
European equity futures are also trading lower, with those on the STOXX 600 down another -0.66%.   
Looking at the day ahead now, data releases include the Euro Area flash CPI print for October, and in the US there’s 
the ISM services index for October, the ADP’s report of private payrolls for October, and the trade balance for 

September. Otherwise, we’ll get the final October services and composite PMIs from multiple countries. From central 
banks, we’ll hear from the ECB’s Lane and Moulin. Finally, earnings releases include Qualcomm, McDonald’s and 
Uber.
