The risk-off machine
Tech is losing its footing. The Nasdaq and semis are breaking major levels, credit stress is flashing
warning signals and volatility is waking up. Meanwhile, the machines are sitting on massive downside
selling potential, Goldman's risk indicator is plunging and oil longs are getting slaughtered. Valuations
are finally resetting, but don't confuse cheaper with cheap enough. Is this another violent shakeout, or
are we watching the early stages of something much nastier?

Serious convexity
CTA flows look negligible in a flat tape this week, but the one-month setup is anything but calm.
Downside selling could reach a massive $164bn in a falling market, while an upside move could trigger
$120bn in buying. Serious flow convexity in both directions.
Key thresholds to watch: short-term 7,713, medium-term 7,544 and long-term 7,082. The medium-
term level is the most important.

Anything but straightforward
History offers little comfort. Following the 11 largest declines in Goldman’s RAI since 2005, the S&P 500

The oil long trap
Trend followers entered today's selloff heavily long energy, leaving the market vulnerable to a
potentially violent unwind. With key technical levels under pressure, systematic strategies could turn
into aggressive sellers. CTA downside convexity is serious, raising the risk of a self-reinforcing
liquidation.

GS
Oil's volatility trap
According to Goldman's Tallulah Adams, speculative and macro accounts returned as sizeable buyers
of crude calls, while implied volatility surged and one-year volatility reached three-year highs. The
options market has been pricing escalating upside risks, leaving a potentially nasty mismatch between
bullish positioning and today's price action. More on oil here.
