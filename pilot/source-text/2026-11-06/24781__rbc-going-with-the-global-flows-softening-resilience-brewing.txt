Going With The Global Flows: Softening Resilience, Brewing Rotation
About This Report
Our Going with the Global Flows report is a collaboration between the US Equity Strategy, Canadian Equity Strategy, Sustainability, and Commodity Strategy teams in which we examine trends in monthly and weekly funds 
flows for major asset classes and important subgroups within them. We anticipate this publication will come out monthly. We leverage EPFR data through September 2026 on a monthly basis and October 28th on a weekly 
basis. Flows are tracked as a percentage of AUM. Below, we highlight the biggest things that jump out from the perspective of each of strategy team. Across our coverage, one theme we see is softening or fading resilience 
in previously strong areas and the early signs of rotation. For a complete rundown on the contents of the report, please see the Table of Contents on page 2. 
What Jumps Out By Global Asset Class, Regionally, In The US, and For Sectors, Factors & Themes (Lori Calvasina, Head of U.S. Equity Strategy Research)
Looking across asset class, geography, sector, and theme, here’s what jumps out to us from our seat in US equity strategy. 
▪
First, the global equity story appears to be one of moderating resilience, though it is not unique in this description. Global equity flows are positive yet clearly softening from recent highs, as are global ex-US equity flows 
following a recent pop (reflecting a push to diversify outside the US within equities). Global bond flows remain constructive but are also moderating, particularly ex-US. Meanwhile, global money market flows are choppy 
but picking up, Gold is weakening and Crypto has turned negative.  
▪
Second, the geographical equity story we see is one of DM moderating, EM improving, and Europe as the weak spot. Developed Market equity flows remain positive across North America, the US, Canada, Asia, Japan, 
and Australia, but most of these, particularly the US, have softened from recent highs. Asia and Japan are showing some renewed strength in recent weeks. EM, China, and South Korea are bright spots with positive 
flows and improving trends. Western Europe stands out negatively — not only are flows in outright outflow territory across France, the UK, and Germany, but the modest improvement we had been seeing for Western 
Europe broadly has begun to reverse. 
▪
Third, looking at the US across asset classes and equity size and style, hints of rotation can be seen.  Large Cap and Blend equity flows remain positive but are below peak, mirroring the broader global softening trend. 
Meanwhile, Investment Grade, TIPS, and Muni bond flows are positive and improving. US Treasury bond funds have also generally seen steady inflows throughout the summer and fall. Digging deeper within US 
equities, Mid Cap flows are negative, Growth and Value are generating no strong signals, and Small Cap is weak but showing signs of stabilization. We view the nascent Small Cap improvement as less of a risk-on 
signal and more of a market searching for rotation destinations. 
▪
Fourth, global DM sector flows are only showing isolated pockets of clear strength. Energy and Health Care/Biotech stand out with both strong flow levels and stable-to-improving trends. Several previously strong 
sectors have reversed sharply and are negative again — Consumer, REITs, and Utilities. Financials and Industrials have been weak but have shown signs of stabilization in the latest weekly updates and are worth 
monitoring closely. Tech and Telecom flows are positive, but volatile trends underneath are masking a flatter underlying tone. Key regional divergences: US Financials are weaker than DM broadly; European Utilities are 
stronger than DM broadly, while European Financials, Industrials, and Health Care are notably weaker. 
▪
Fifth, on themes and industries we see evidence of previous hot trades cooling. In particular, Aerospace & Defense, BDCs, Industrial Metals, Rare Earths, and Space & Satellite have all faded meaningfully from dramatic 
surges and in some cases turned negative. AI flows remain positive but are also well off prior highs. Semiconductors are volatile but currently positive.  Cybersecurity is a standout — flows are positive and accelerating.
What Jumps Out In Canada & Sustainability (Sara Mahaffy, Head of Canadian Equity Strategy & Global Sustainability Strategy Research)
Canadian Equity flows have been strong this year. While weekly flows have eased a bit from recent highs, positive trends remain largely intact. Examining trends within Canadian equity funds, we’ve found that both domestic 
and foreign flows have driven constructive trends, though passive flows have been much stronger than active flows. From a market cap perspective, inflows remain in place for Large Cap funds (though they’ve eased a bit) 
while outflows remain in place for Small Cap funds (though they’ve improved). From a style perspective, Value and Growth flows have deteriorated recently, while Blend flows have remained positive. 
Looking at sustainable fund flows, weekly flows for sustainable equity funds have turned negative again. While flows have generally been more resilient for sustainable bond funds, inflows have been easing (and turned 
slightly negative for Europe domiciled funds this past week). From a thematic perspective, outflows are in place for most sustainability themes with key exceptions being Nuclear & Uranium (slightly positive but down from 
highs), Cybersecurity (positive overall), and Gender funds (which recently turned positive again). Most themes linked to AI/electrification narratives (including Smart Grid/Power, Sustainable Infrastructure, Sustainable Tech, 
Clean Energy) have weakened from their highs seen earlier this year. 
What Jumps Out In Commodities (Christopher Louney, Commodity Strategist)
Commodity markets remain at the mercy of headlines and broader uncertainty, with volatility seemingly unavoidable. Energy overall remains understandably linked to the Iran conflict, with more internationally exposed Brent 
flows reflecting some of the growing appreciation of crude's likely post-midterm dynamics and the Iran conflict's trajectory. Metals flows, however, have mostly faded despite pockets of resilience, while agriculture seems to 
be lagging generally, with few exceptions.
When you break it down, in the energy space natural gas leads with the strongest positive inflows, but Brent crude also improved alongside the August/September price recovery, while WTI remains mixed amid uncertainty 
despite recent weekly recovery. In precious metals, gold momentum is fading as monthly strength in September was offset by recent weekly outflows, while silver has been relatively weak after a strong 2025–early 2026 
period. Among industrial metals, copper and nickel are lagging with negative/neutral flows, and aluminum looks stable but has become weaker. Lastly, there is wide dispersion among agricultural commodities, with livestock 
as the sole leader with positive momentum. Most crops (corn, wheat, soybean, and sugar) are declining after mid-year peaks. Overall, early-year momentum does seem to be fading across most commodities outside of 
energy and perhaps livestock, signaling investor caution.
