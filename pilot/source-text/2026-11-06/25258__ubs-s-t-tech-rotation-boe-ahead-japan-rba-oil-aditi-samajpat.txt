Post-Election Tech Rotation Hits Semis and AI
The post-election bounce is playing out everywhere outside Tech, with a sharp unwind in momentum and
semiconductors as investors rotate toward quality and more defensive growth. The key debate is whether the
midterms have pulled the AI capacity ‘peak in 2028/29’ discussion forward to 2027. If tighter restrictions on
data-center development are coming, hyperscalers have an incentive to accelerate capacity, secure power
and get permits in hand. That could pull more spend into 2027, but it also raises the risk of an earlier rollover in
EPS revisions for semis. The more constructive case is that regulation ultimately elongates the cycle, but
either way, the path is unlikely to be linear and the added uncertainty is weighing on price action.
That dynamic is also showing up in flows. Investors are favouring hyperscalers over neoclouds as they look for
quality, potentially reflecting the scarcity value and stronger pricing backdrop for existing compute capacity if
scrutiny of new builds increases. The cash desk is a better buyer of hyperscalers and semi-cap equipment
names and better for sale in memory. There’s selective buying in Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Lam
Research, while Intel, ServiceNow, CoreWeave, Advanced Micro Devices and Sandisk are better for sale.
AI Power remains front and center, with UBXXVOLT falling to a 52-week low as Texas stays in focus and
investors price greater regulatory and execution risk. At the same time, the broader macro tape is helping
cushion the indices, with oil down about 10%: Brent near $93/bbl, DXY 40bp lower and yields falling.
Tactically, some of the excess positioning looks to be clearing. Semis and AI have absorbed a meaningful
amount of bad news, and Wednesday’s washout may be getting closer to pricing that risk. The question is
whether the clearing of election uncertainty can now allow the trade to find a floor. With Friday’s nonfarm
payrolls the main remaining macro catalyst this week, attention turns back to earnings for differentiation.
(Robert Ruple, Specialist Sales)

Investors Focused On De-Risking Even As Some Major Risks Fade – Despite major macro events such
as the US midterm elections, investors are focused almost exclusively on trying to de-risk further. UBS’s equity
positioning data shows hedge funds have made their third-largest reduction in overall market exposure this
year, leaving their net exposure close to the lowest levels seen over the past five years. And yet with this and
other major events out of the way (Q3 earnings, October Fed), and given the strength of the US economy,
there’s scope for an equity rally into year-end. The main consequence of the Democratic sweep is likely to be
a boost for US Treasuries (and lower yields), as divided government likely means gridlock and no significant
increase in spending. Tech stocks are under scrutiny given the attention on data-center buildout, which could
slow given there will likely be requirements on companies to take on power, water and infrastructure costs.
That said, AMD on Tuesday projected revenue growth of 80% for 2027 while Meta issued $15 bn in bonds,
suggesting there’s no major slowdown (although admittedly, Meta debt was priced 220bp over US Treasuries).
Inflation, another important factor in the elections, is largely a consequence of the Iran war and tech build –
about which the Fed can do very little. Chair Warsh will be hoping time brings inflation back down – UBS
expects core CPI to start edging lower in the spring. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)BoE Set To Hike Thursday –
UBS Economist Anna Titareva expects the BoE to hike 25bp to 4.0% on Thursday, in line with Bloomberg
consensus, after the hawkish hold in September. She sees a 7-2 vote split, with Swati Dhingra and Alan Taylor
likely voting to keep rates on hold. The updated macro projections and scenarios should show upward
revisions to both inflation and growth. Anna expects the MPC to stick to its previous message that it “stands
ready to act as necessary to ensure that CPI inflation remains on track to meet the 2% target in the medium
term”, underscoring its data-dependent approach and leaving the door open to further rate hikes. Further
ahead, Anna expects the BoE to pause in December before one final 25bp hike in February to 4.25%, more
dovish than current market expectations. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
BoJ Minutes Point To Further Hikes; Member Flags Faster Pace If Inflation Overshoots – BoJ
policymakers saw a need for further tightening at September’s meeting, the minutes showed, with one
member saying signs of an upward deviation in prices would require the bank to “accelerate the pace of rate
hikes”. Another said hasty action was unnecessary but policy should be guided appropriately “to prevent
excessive and persistent price rises”. The BoJ raised its policy rate to a 31-year high of 1.25% in September,
shifting its focus towards preventing inflation from overshooting its 2% target. Many members expected
inflation to accelerate from autumn as surveys pointed to additional food-price increases. They also saw
elevated inflation risks from renewed crude-price increases linked to prolonged conflict in the Middle East. The
BoJ subsequently held rates steady in October but maintained its vigilance towards inflation risks, reinforcing
market expectations for a December hike. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)

Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser for signalling that rates were very likely to rise before this week’s meeting,

the board voted 6-3 to lift rates to 4.85%. Some board members were also surprised when assistant governor
Sarah Hunter said before the meeting that the RBA had “not quite yet” done enough to tame inflation,
Australian Financial Review reported [paywall], citing unnamed sources. The trading desk noted that the
headline has not materially translated into AUD weakness, despite being fundamentally unhelpful. AUDUSD at
0.7035-0.7045 should be difficult resistance, with the short bias reconsidered only above approximately
0.7065-0.7070. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

Why Banxico Will Opt For A Cut – Latin American Economist Rafael De La Fuente expects Banxico to
deliver a 25bp policy rate cut on Thursday, lowering the benchmark to 6.25%, despite only five out of 30
surveyed economists anticipating this move. According to Rafael, the board’s dovish majority is likely to act on
signals for further fine-tuning, even as arguments for holding rates remain strong—such as MXN weakness,
higher oil prices, upside inflation surprises, and accelerating growth. Orderly FX trading, declining core
services inflation (now at 3.37%), and the Fed and ECB’s pause create a window for easing. Banxico’s March
"surprise" cut and the current output gap reinforce the case for action, though the decision won’t be
unanimous, he said. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales)
Midterms Offer No Clear USD Catalyst; Gold Rally Looks Fragile – Midterms do not provide a clean
directional catalyst for USD, according to FX strategist Shahab Jalinoos. Stronger institutional checks
challenge the de-dollarization narrative, while potentially slower AI investment and lower oil pull the other way.
Resilient US growth, constructive capital flows and firm equities keep the core USD-positive framework intact,
leaving EURUSD downside as the preferred expression. Client meetings across Hong Kong and Singapore
show limited G10 conviction, with major pairs largely range-bound and investors reluctant to chase EUR
weakness. Meanwhile, gold’s return above 4,000 looks more like a relief rally than a durable breakout. Lower
oil can support gold by easing reserve-manager FX pressures, but renewed oil strength could reverse the
move, Shahab said. Longer term, he remains sympathetic to accumulating gold at lower levels because the
US fiscal outlook, especially alongside an eventual US slowdown, could be supportive. Near term, though,
another rise in oil could put gold under pressure again. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)

US Inflation: Gasoline Repricing Drives Breakeven Bear Steepening – Markets responded forcefully on
Wednesday: equities staged their strongest session in months, the nominal curve bull steepened (10y -8bp at
4.885%, 5s30s +3bp at 59.2bp), while gasoline futures fell around 20c as traders priced a higher probability of
de-escalation or political pressure to end hostilities. In the front end, Dec-26 to Mar-27 pricing remained
roughly unchanged, however, consistent with a data-dependent Fed still biased toward additional tightening if
core inflation proves sticky. On the inflation side, the main driver of Wednesday’s bear steepening was the
repricing in gasoline futures. The front end led the move lower, with 1y ZC ending the day 23.5bp lower at
2.16%, slightly underperforming the implied energy move. Further out, the BE curve continued to bear steepen
under pressure from lower energy prices and the duration rally. Looking ahead, the primary drivers will be the
path of the Iran conflict and energy prices; the post-midterm fiscal outlook under divided government, which
may modestly constrain further deficit expansion; and labour-market resilience, with ADP at +86k ahead of
Friday’s NFP. (Bogdan Doubovick, Rates Options Trading)
ARM Slips On Royalty Miss – ARM fell 3.8% versus roughly 10.4% implied volatility after a headline Q2 beat
and broadly in-line Q3 guidance failed to lift expectations, with weaker-than-expected royalty revenue and no
increase to the AGI CPU sales target weighing on the shares. Its Q2 revenue came in at $1.44 bn versus
$1.38 bn expected, while EPS of $0.60 beat the $0.48 consensus. The mix was less clean, however, with
royalty revenue of $670 mn missing the $707.3 mn estimate, while license and other revenue of $773 mn was
well ahead of $659.1 mn expected. For Q3, ARM sees revenue of $1.49-1.59 bn and EPS of $0.52-0.60,
bracketing consensus at $1.52 bn and $0.55. Management also reiterated confidence in achieving up to $2 bn
of AGI CPU revenue across fiscal 2027 and 2028, rather than raising the target despite accelerating customer
commitments. (Robert Ruple, Specialist Sales)
