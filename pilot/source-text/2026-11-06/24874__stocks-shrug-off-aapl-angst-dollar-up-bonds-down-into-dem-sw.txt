Down Into 'Dem Sweep' Midterms 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, NOV 03, 2026 - 10:00 PM 
Tl;dr: Stronger than expected macro (opposite-land from yesterday) and lower physical oil prices 
(flood of supply and diplomacy hope trumped US attacks) offset any angst about AAPL's 
overnight disappointment, pulling stocks (AI/Tech led) and bond yields higher on the day. The 
long-end underperformed as growth hopes dominated inflation fears (plus heavy corporate 
supply) and that helped pull the dollar higher, spoiling the early gains in gold. Bitcoin bounced 
along with tech stocks ahead of AMD and Midterms results. 
 
Choose your own adventure as oil-bonds-stocks decoupled after the open (that is oil futures, not 
physical, more below)... 
 

 
 
But, it appeared that macro was a bigger driver today... 
Macro 
Much stronger than expected jobs data (JOLTS) combined with an ongoing surge in factory 
orders pushed Citi's US macro surprise index to its highest level of the year. 
 
The US Labor Department’s October employment report is slated to be released on Friday, and 
is expected to show improvement from September.  
 

 
And also on the economically bright side, though of no use to Trump now that the Midterms are 
upon us, energy prices continue to believe in diplomacy... 
Energy 
Today's oil market was defined by a sharp supply surge out of the Persian Gulf following the 
easing of Hormuz transit disruptions, with physical crude prices collapsing across Atlantic Basin 
and Asian markets - Forties, for instance, swung from a nearly $20 premium to a $1.40 discount 
in just three weeks. Dated Brent plunged... 
 
Saudi Aramco's CEO confirmed the company will maximize production this quarter and push 
additional barrels to market, while combined Arab Gulf exports from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, 
Iraq, and Kuwait climbed roughly 10% in October to 15.53 million barrels per day, near pre-war 
levels. 
However, Brent futures swung more than $6 intraday, settling near $103, and WTI dropped and 
popped, as traders parsed conflicting signals on US-Iran peace talks - Iran's Supreme Leader 
reportedly granted negotiators "additional authority" on previously non-negotiable issues, fueling 
speculation of a diplomatic resolution that could further normalize supply 
 

 
 
On the demand side, Vitol's CEO projected global oil demand will fall by approximately 3 million 
barrels per day in 2026, adding a bearish fundamental backdrop, while Russia's crude exports 
edged higher to 3.65 million barrels per day even as Ukraine resumed drone strikes on Russian 
refineries, including a 124-drone attack on the Yanos facility.  
However, despite the plunge in crude, diesel prices bounced back as the New Jersey governor 
warned port and terminal operators against accepting Russian diesel shipments authorized by 
the Trump administration, arguing the Treasury license conflicts with federal law and of course, 
Inventories remain critically low and refining capacity is still constrained, which analysts warn 
keeps the market vulnerable to sharp spikes on any supply disruption 
 
 

 
Of course, this is all too late to help Trump in the Midterms... 
 
But, bonds decided to ignore the drop in oil prices... 
Rates & Credit 
The primary macro catalyst for bonds today was a stronger-than-expected JOLTS report - US 
job openings jumped to a four-month high in September, with layoffs declining - which traders 
interpreted as evidence of labor market resilience that could give the Fed room to raise rates 
further... 
 
 

 
"The JOLTS numbers for September are firm," said Padhraic Garvey, head of research for the 
Americas at ING Groep NV.  
"The edge higher in yields looks about right against this." 
But the long-end underperformed (supply - more below) with yields 2-4bps higher across the 
curve... 
 
Looking ahead, the dominant focus is Wednesday's quarterly refunding announcement: dealers 
are split on whether Treasury Secretary Bessent will signal a reduction in long-end auction 
sizes, with five recent developments - including a subtle language tweak in the August refunding 
statement - fueling speculation of a bullish supply cut.  
Additionally, the midterm election results are also being closely watched, with analysts noting 
that a divided Congress could offer some relief for the long end, though Mizuho cautioned 
against chasing that narrative given Trump's willingness to negotiate on spending. 
The 10Y remained below the 5.00% Maginot Line... 
 

 
 
Another potential drag on bonds (especially the long-end) is the flood of corporate supply with 
another $15BN debt offering from META today with traders noting how wide initial price talk is 
relative to their secondary bonds - implying very large new issue concessions to raise the 
AI-linked capital. 
 
As Bloomberg's Sebastian Boyd noted, higher yields and rising spreads have forced borrowing 
costs for all the AI issuers higher. But at least part of what is driving their spreads wider is the 
companies’ own tendency to reprice their debt. It’s hard for bonds to maintain their value when 
the same issuers keep coming out with debt that ranks pari passu and pays higher spreads. 
 

 
 
Hyperscaler CDS did decline today rather notably, and IG spreads narrowed very modestly... 
 
But Data-Center CDS was flat near record highs...
