Capitulation?
Markets have gone from panic to a potentially massive reversal. SPX and NDX futures are printing
enormous hammer candles after testing critical support overnight, while risk appetite has suffered its
biggest monthly collapse since 2021. Sentiment is approaching capitulation, short positioning appears
to be building and tech valuations have reset sharply.
Meanwhile, the post-midterm playbook points to significant upside potential. The washout may not be
complete, but the ingredients for a violent squeeze are falling into place. Don't forget the upside.
SPX hammer
SPX futures are printing a massive hammer after an aggressive overnight selloff, reclaiming the range
that has been in place since August. Note that futures came within touching distance of the 200-day
moving average at the overnight lows.
The bigger picture? SPX is essentially flat since May, with no meaningful medium-term trend. Chasing
momentum at range extremes has consistently been a losing strategy. Trading SPX has required a
mean-reversion mindset for months.

The massive tech hammer
NDX futures are printing one of the biggest hammer candles we have seen in a very long time.
Overnight lows tested the major trend line and the crucial 27,500 support level, briefly undershooting
the 200-day moving average before reclaiming it.
As we pointed out yesterday, tech has gone from terrific to terrified. This massive reversal could mark
a capitulation low, at least for now.

Sentiment nears capitulation
Another correction, another sentiment gauge flirting with capitulation without actually getting there.
Morgan Stanley's Composite Sentiment Indicator is approaching the panic zone, but has yet to deliver
the textbook -2.00 flush. The message? We haven't seen full capitulation, but markets don't necessarily
need it to squeeze.

The shorts came
According to Quinn at Goldman Sachs, the latest tech puke appears to be attracting fresh S&P 500
futures shorts. Aggregate E-mini open interest has jumped $13.5bn while December funding
cheapened by 3bps. With AI semis down 11.6% and hyperscalers off 6% since October 27, the selling
pressure may be spreading beyond tech into broader index positioning. Whether this is hedging or
outright directional shorting remains unclear. But if fresh shorts are building into the lows, a sustained
reversal could force an aggressive unwind.

The midterm tailwind
According to JPMorgan's Market Intelligence team, history suggests the US midterms could provide a
much-needed boost to equities. Since 1990, the S&P 500 has delivered average gains of 5.4%, 11.4%,
12.5% and 14.7% over the following 3, 6, 9 and 12 months, with a 100% positive hit rate at 6, 9 and 12
months. Meanwhile, JPMorgan argues the recent de-risking is not quite capitulatory, with quant selling
and elevated tech positioning still posing risks. However, widespread deleveraging and momentum
exposure collapsing to just 30% of its peak suggest the stage may be set for an alpha rebound.

Don't forget the upside
Upside convexity is becoming interesting. BofA highlights IGV and SMH December call spreads as
offering some of the most attractive upside payout ratios among liquid ETFs. The interesting
asymmetry is that the clearing of midterm uncertainty could trigger a violent recovery, while defined-
risk call spreads limit losses to the premium paid.
