Blue, blue, and more blue: Democrats broadly sweep key elections. The 2026 mid-term elections
resulted in a blue tsunami, with Democrats winning key races almost fully across the board. The
most impactful surprise is the magnitude by which Texas voted Democratic, imperiling the outlook
for data center developments. We highlight key takeaways below.
Winners are few and far between: Clean energy, especially high-short-interest / unprofitable names;
most utilities on lower interest rates; and NEE, with Florida holding Republican. We reiterate our
call to move NXT to our Franchise Pick among Buys and see the recent rally in the most shorted
names continuing given the scale of the win. We are not convinced of meaningful national or state-
level clean energy reform, but expect a bid regardless. Watch for restoration of manufacturing tax
credits for clean energy and electrification as a key debate in coming weeks.

Utility Underperformers
Blue wave makes utility winners and losers harder to disentangle. Democratic turnout swept nearly every
major battleground, including Texas, Georgia, New Mexico, Nevada and Ohio, creating two overlapping
pressures for utilities: (i) broader weakness in datacenter-exposed names and (ii) company-specific risk
where election results directly change commission control, appointments or the legislative agenda. We seek
to separate the macro move from which utilities are actually worse off, with the greatest state-specific risk
concentrated in PNW, CNP, SO and PPL.
Remember AI trade will roll over, but the question is which states see more pragmatic statements. Quietly,
we already see substantive engagement with our coverage across new governors to support development.
We watch carefully new Gov in Texas as the most important indicator, but other states to watch include
NM, MI, OH and WI on development trends. We see subtle positives in each of these around datacenter
development (still) under new leadership; but timing is absolutely critical to watch. Has been disappointing
thru results on total DC developments in 3Q on BTM deployments, while utilities have remained robust.
SO remains the clearest commission-control risk after Democrats sweep Georgia’s two PSC seats.
Victories by Peter Hubbard in District 3 and Shelia Edwards in the open District 5 seat flip the Georgia
PSC to 3-2 Democratic, putting a more consumer-focused commission in place ahead of Georgia
Power’s expected 2028 filing for 2029 rates. The result matters given Georgia Power represents
roughly 60% of SO’s earnings, while much of the stock debate had hinged on whether the 2025
Democratic wins represented a temporary affordability backlash or a durable change in commission
control. We see the outcome extending the affordability and recovery overhang into the next rate case;
the key question now shifts from whether the commission flips to how aggressively the new majority
approaches bill growth, capital recovery and authorized returns. Question will be extent of bill credits from
datacenters that can be lifted to minimize GA rate impacts in next case; focus on how narrowly case be
focused on rate tariffs by customer class is our overarching focus.

Washington: Control of Congress and the Federal
Policy Read
Result: Democrats won the House (CBS estimate up to ~259 seats; final count pending) and the Senate
(seven Republican-held seats flipped: TX, NC, SC, OH, KS, IA and ME). Mississippi (Hyde-Smith and
Colom separated by a few dozen votes at ~97% counted) and Alaska remain too close to call.
Bottom line: A Democratic Congress raises the odds of a federal ratepayer protection bill in 2027 and
lowers the odds of a lame-duck permitting deal. For utilities that is a multiple headwind more than an
EPS event; clean energy gets a better tone.
Where we stood going in. A Democratic sweep of the House and Senate had been the favored
outcome since mid-September, with Polymarket odds near 58% on October 20 and ~65% by October
30. Affordability had become the defining sector theme, and we saw the market pricing real volatility
rather than an asymmetric downside skew, after election risk had been under-appreciated earlier in
the year.
What the sweep means. A Democratic Senate improves the odds of a federal ratepayer protection bill
in 2027. H.R. 9340 passed the House, but Senate cloture fell three votes short (57-43) on September
30. As drafted, it requires states to consider data center rate standards rather than mandating them,
with exceptions for prior state action, so we see it raising the floor on cost-causer-pays: more a sector
multiple headwind than an EPS event, with states that already have large-load frameworks (OH, TX,
PA) better insulated at the margin. For clean energy, we had flagged that a Democratic sweep could
bring back some positive momentum; that is now the base case.

Bottom line: Georgia Power's 2028 case now goes before a Democratic majority. The ~$950m/yr of

What was at stake. The governor does not appoint the PSC, so the investable binary was commission
control, decided by two statewide seats. A sweep by Hubbard and Edwards flipped the commission

a statewide analysis of impacts. The governor does not appoint the PSC, so the readthrough to SO

office.

takes the open District 5 seat (Pridemore stepped down in September). Edwards says data centers
