The $61 billion volatility trap
The volatility explosion is creating another potential selling problem. Nomura estimates roughly $61bn
of implied vol-control selling following today's shock. The important nuance is timing. These strategies
typically react with a lag, meaning today's price action may be loading up tomorrow's selling queue
rather than translating into immediate liquidation. The volatility shock could therefore have
consequences well beyond today's close.

CTA trap door
Vanda Research warns that today's selloff, combined with the explosion in volatility, is bringing CTA
selling back into play. Markets appear to have avoided major systematic selling on election night, but
the break below 27,600 could now push CTAs outright short. A clean break below 26,800 would likely
see CTA exposure collapse toward Liberation Day lows, adding another layer of selling pressure to an
already stressed market.

Front-running the front-runners
Everybody knows the mechanical selling is coming. Nomura highlights how discretionary traders are
increasingly front-running systematic deleveraging, leveraged ETF rebalancing and dealer hedging.
Meanwhile, dealers are sitting on roughly $7bn of negative gamma per 1% SPX move. The theoretical
risk is a self-reinforcing selling spiral.
But knowing the gamma exposure doesn't mean the flows are easy to front-run. Anyone who has
managed a large short-gamma book knows the agony of deciding when to hedge. Dealers may have
already sold in anticipation, adjusted hedges intraday or reduced exposure well before the close.
Knowing the potential selling pressure is one thing. Knowing when, or whether, it actually hits the
market is another.

Recall the gold dump?
As we highlighted a few days ago, referring to Goldman's Quinn, gold had already experienced a
massive positioning washout ahead of the latest squeeze. Five consecutive weeks of speculative selling
saw $19.5 billion dumped, with the final week alone accounting for $8 billion. That included $5.2 billion
of long liquidation and $2.8 billion of fresh shorts. Other and Non-Reportable traders accounted for
roughly 95% of the latest selling. A huge amount of selling had already taken place before gold started
squeezing violently higher.
