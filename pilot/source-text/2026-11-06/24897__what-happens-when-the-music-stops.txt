What Happens When the Music Stops?
Don't Get Caught Without a Chair
WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW: 
The game of musical chairs continues. In late September, 18% of SPX names we're
oversold. At that point the RSP was -7% off highs nearing its 200 DMA, while the
SOXX had just rallied nearly 20%. Today we are the complete inverse of that. Semis are
oversold while RSP back at the highs. So all the market has done is sold one cohort of
stocks to buy another, without any net progress. As a result, 22% of SPX components
closed with an RSI above 70, yet the SPX RSI itself was just 50.4. That's the second-
lowest SPX RSI ever when more than 22% of components we're 'overbought' (RSI > 70).
The only reading that was more extreme was 4/19/99. If semis begin to stabilize, our
assumption is the overbought areas will begin correcting. i.e. another rotation. But what
if semis can't bounce? How much more staples/utilities/healthcare can be bought here?
That is the condition for the correlation-one selloff, when oversold can't bounce and
overbought begins to correct.
■
Internal vs. External Extreme. Yesterday 22% of SPX components closed with an RSI
above 70, yet the SPX RSI itself was just 50.4. That's thesecond-lowest SPX RSI ever
when more than 22% of components we're 'overbought' (RSI > 70). The only reading
that was more extreme was 4/19/99.
■
What Happened in '99? Well interestingly that occurred about a year before the
actual dot-com peak. SPX would fall ~9% into Oct. '99 before rallying ~26% into the
final March '00 high.
■
What Was the Third Biggest Divergence? The next closest occurrence was much
more recently on 2/11/26. 23% of SPX names had RSI > 70, with SPX RSI at 52.5. SPX
would immediately begin its decline and fall ~10% into the March lows.
■
What Does it Mean? It's just another representation of what we already know. The
semi/AI complex is weighing on SPX, while internally nearly a quarter of all S&P 500
names are now overbought.
■
180 Degree Swing. In late September, 18% of SPX names we're oversold (sub-30
RSI). At that point the RSP was -7% off highs nearing its 200 DMA, while the SOXX
had just rallied ~20%. Today we are the complete inverse of that. Semis are oversold
while RSP back at the highs. So all the market has done is sold one cohort of stocks
to buy another, without any net progress.
■
What Now? If semis begin to stabilize, our assumption is the overbought areas will
begin correcting. i.e. another rotation. But what if semis can't bounce? How much
more staples/utilities/healthcare can be bought here? As we have been discussing
XLV now 19% above its 200 DMA its widest spread on record, and gapping up again
pre-mkt.That is the condition for the correlation-one selloff, when oversold can't
bounce and overbought begins to correct.
■
Japan Semiconductors -12% overnight, -22% last five days. They were up 100% at
the end of June. They are now up 27%. Still a decent year, but looking more like a
meaningful top here.
■
Rare Dow Stat. Yesterday was just the third time in the last 30 years when the INDU
gained 3%+ while closing within 3% of a 52wk high. The last one was 11/6/24 during
the last election. Before that you have to go back to 5/16/01 during the dot-com
bust. Statistically irrelevant, but for those curious both saw meaningful weakness
over the ensuing six-months.
