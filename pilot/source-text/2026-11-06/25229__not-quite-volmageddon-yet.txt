Not quite Volmageddon... yet
Volatility is back with a vengeance. VIX, VVIX and tech volatility are exploding higher, while the short
end of the volatility curve is flashing serious stress. Yesterday's all-clear signal has turned into another
painful reversal, catching complacent traders off guard. Yet beneath the surface, the picture is more
nuanced. Skew remains relatively contained, implied correlations are far from extreme and sentiment
has been even more bearish before. This is a market in panic mode, but not yet a full-blown
Volmageddon. The risk is that increasingly complex market dynamics turn today's volatility shock into
something much uglier.
Volatility panic
The stress is particularly striking beneath the surface. VVIX is showing even greater signs of stress than
VIX, while tech volatility is moving into extreme territory. VXN is rapidly approaching Liberation Day
panic levels. The volatility of volatility is sending an increasingly uncomfortable message about market
stability.

Term structure panic
The short end of the VIX term structure is exploding higher as the crowd rushes to put on immediate
downside hedges. This is creating serious pain, especially as so many saw the all-clear signal after
yesterday's rebound. Those picking up pennies in front of the steamroller are getting properly smoked
today.

Under the hood
The VIX 2/8-month futures ratio is moving sharply higher today. Regular readers of TME know this is
one of our preferred "under the hood" indicators for monitoring market stress. The sharp move reflects
the market aggressively repricing near-term risk relative to longer-dated volatility, a classic sign of
mounting short-term stress.

CTA trap door
Vanda Research warns that today's selloff, combined with the explosion in volatility, is bringing CTA
selling back into play. Markets appear to have avoided major systematic selling on election night, but
the break below 27,600 could now push CTAs outright short. A clean break below 26,800 would likely
see CTA exposure collapse toward Liberation Day lows, adding another layer of selling pressure to an
already stressed market.
Vanda
Not so skewed
Noteworthy is that, despite volatility exploding higher, "demand" for downside protection, as measured
by the SDEX skew index, remains below the levels seen in mid-October. This could suggest that
investors are not expressing outright downside panic through options, or simply that they are choosing
to cut delta exposure rather than chase increasingly expensive downside protection.

Implied correlations
Implied correlations are spiking today, but we remain far from extreme levels. Despite the volatility
explosion, the market is not yet pricing an outright correlation shock.
The distinction matters. Volatility is screaming panic, but neither skew nor implied correlations are
confirming the same degree of stress. The danger is that persistently elevated volatility triggers further
deleveraging, turning today's volatility shock into something much uglier.

Extreme fear
CNN's Fear & Greed Index has plunged into extreme fear territory, but we saw even more bearish
sentiment in October. Panic is spreading, although sentiment has not yet reached the extremes seen
during the previous selloff. Another reminder that while fear is elevated, we are not necessarily at
capitulation levels.
