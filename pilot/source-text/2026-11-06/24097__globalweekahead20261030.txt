Checks and Balances 
Buckets of sweat will be dripping from pollsters over whether they’ve called Tuesday’s US midterm elections correctly. They’ve certainly 
gone out on a limb given high odds of a so-called ‘blue sweep’ by the Democrats as shown in charts 1 and 2 compared to the start of the 
year. Democrats are projected to win majorities in both chambers of Congress by this site and its sister site, this one and this one. This site 
provides a break down of House seats by party leaning and this one does the same for the Senate. Chart 3 shows that betting sites are 
similarly aligned. Also see the chart of the week from Jay Parmar on the front cover. 
Suffice it to say that it would be a shock if the Trump 
administration retained one, let alone both 
chambers of Congress. I won’t speculate on next 
steps but hope the results are respected come what 
may. At least so far, the democratic process is 
working in the direction of providing judgement on 
almost two years of Trump 2.0. The aftermath could 
inform risks to trade policy, geopolitical risk with 
sabres rattling over Iran and other theatres, and 
fiscal policy plus broad regulatory stances including 
toward AI. Voters will cast judgement on a 
somewhat below-average president in terms of 
cumulative GDP growth during his term so far (chart 
4). 
The immediate aftermath may include escalation of 
US strikes against Iran after the vote. 
Results will be counted well into the night. We might not have full results for days, possibly weeks, especially if some results are contested. If 
the margin is tight, then the overall composition of Congress may hang in the balance for a while. A decisively large margin like the pundits 
are predicting could settle the matter fairly quickly. 
A wave of central bank meetings, Canada’s federal 
budget two days after the midterms, and dual US 
and Canadian jobs reports will offer plenty of 
potential market volatility.

CANADIAN JOBS—A STICKY MESS 
Canada refreshes job market readings for the month of October on Friday.  
I’ve estimated a modest gain of 15k and an unchanged unemployment rate of 6.5%. This 
follows a loss of 68k jobs in September and 42k in August. It’s a conservative angle on a 
rebound from some quirky developments in the prior month of two. 
Canada has lost 100k or more jobs in back-to-back negatives only thirteen times. Chart 12 
shows what happened the next time but we’ve excluded March 2020 at the start of the 
pandemic because the unusual shutdown-driven loss would swamp the scale. 11 of the 13 
times (including March 2020) were in recessions and/or during extreme shocks. Only one—
February of this year—was not in a recessionary period. The point being that the one time 
when the sharp hits were not in recessions saw a mild rebound the next month. The rest of 
the occasions are not helpful to present circumstances. The punchline is therefore that while 
it’s not impossible, it would be highly unusual for a third consecutive drop in employment 
outside of recession or a truly major shock. New US tariffs on three-wheeled Canadian 
vehicles is not a major shock. 
Further, there remain strong reasons to discount September’s lost jobs: 
- Supply-side: The labour force shrank by 37k in August and 53k in September for a total of 
90k which is close to the 110k lost jobs. Fewer available workers played a major role in 
reduced employment due to exits.  
- Unwinding a hot summer job market: Youths aged 15-24 were the biggest demographic 
category to lose jobs at a pace of almost 50k in September. When the loss is concentrated

RBA—A Buck and a Quarter 
Another 25bps increase in the cash rate target is widely anticipated on Monday night (ET). 
Almost all of consensus expects it and 20bps is priced in OIS. A hike would represent 125bps of 
cumulative tightening this year.  
CPI recently surprised a touch on the upside and remains too warm even before the full

recent parliamentary testimony about materializing upside risks to inflation (chart 16), while

Most expect the Selic rate to be held at 13.75% with a minority still of the view that a cut could be delivered. Markets have downplayed the 
prospect of easing since the election. One reason is that as capital flight kicked in, the real sank by about 9% to the dollar this past week

Banxico—Inflation Hawks Likely to Dominate 
Mexico’s central bank is widely expected to remain on hold at an overnight rate of 6.5% on Thursday, thereby extending the holding pattern 
since the last cut in May. Uncertainty surrounding the Federal Reserve’s next move may inform a pause. So may upside risks to inflation. 
September’s CPI reading jumped 0.2% to about 3½% y/y with core CPI at 3.8%. Rising energy prices and the peso’s depreciation of over 
10% to the USD since September will keep the inflation hawks on high alert.  
Riksbank—Are You the One? 
Wednesday’s decision by Sweden’s central bank is significantly priced for a rate hike with 70% market odds while consensus is unanimous 
that the policy rate will rise to 2%. That would be the first increase since 2023. There is some uncertainty over whether this is the meeting at 
which they will hike versus December that is priced for 30bps of cumulative tightening. The central bank has guided that a rate hike is likely 
as early as this meeting.  
Norges Bank—Cashing in on Insurance 
Thursday’s decision by Norway’s central bank is expected to whiff. A hold at a deposit rate of 
4.5% is widely expected by consensus and priced by markets. They have room to do so given 
two hikes in May and September in anticipation of firming inflationary pressures. The bias 
remains hawkish but the bank has signalled that it will be on hold for longer than previously 
guided.  
BoE—Buckling Under Pressure  
The Bank of England is widely expected to deliver a 25bps rate hike on Thursday morning (ET). 
It’s basically fully priced in markets and consensus is unanimous that the week will end with 
Bank Rate at 4%. This central bank fought the good fight against hike talk but is under 
insurmountable pressure to buckle. Confidence that it could look through a transitory shock to 
inflation has waned as the BoE raises concern toward second-round pass-through effects of 
soaring energy costs (chart 17) into broader prices. Updated forecasts may do much of the 
talking.  
Bank Negara—The Fiscal-Monetary Swap 
Malaysia’s central bank is widely expected to hold its overnight rate at 2.75% on Thursday. A slim minority leans toward a hike. Fuel 
subsidies transfer inflation’s costs to the state’s coffers but are keeping reported inflation low at 1.8% y/y.  
CANADIAN BUDGET—HEY BIG SPENDER 
I asked our Rebekah Young to share her best-of thoughts on the Canadian federal budget for this section. 
Federal Finance Minister Champagne will table Budget 2026 on November 5th. Another expansionary budget is expected, with its signature 
measure, the Productivity Mega Deduction, announced on the margins of September’s Investor Summit and costed at $36 bn over five 
years. We estimate a cumulative $60 bn in new spending has been announced since last April’s update. While purely speculative, a final 
package in the order of $80 bn on budget day would not surprise (charts 18, 19). 
Material fiscal windfalls should partially offset new spending, as economic growth this year, especially in nominal terms, has so far outpaced 
projections in the government’s April update. The net result would be a modest deterioration in the fiscal outlook, with deficits still 
narrowing toward the mid- to high-1% range of GDP and debt stabilizing about a percentage point above the spring trajectory (charts 20,

The Governor’s opening statement will be posted on the bank’s website. Banco de México: Banco de México is expected to keep its overnight policy rate unchanged 
at 6.50% on Thursday. The September meeting minutes struck an unexpectedly dovish tone, with some members seeing room for a cut at this meeting, although the 
Board maintained its data-dependent stance. Since then, the Mexican peso has weakened further and headline inflation has picked up, while core inflation has 
remained broadly stable. Meanwhile, economic activity has surprised to the upside, foreign trade has maintained solid momentum, and the labour market has 
tightened further. Taken together, these developments reduce the urgency for further easing. With upside risks to inflation set against downside risks to growth, 
Banxico is expected to remain on hold and maintain a data-dependent stance until there is clearer evidence that either side of the risk balance is materialising.
Bank of England (BoE): Markets have fully priced in a 25bp rate hike by the Bank of England on Thursday amid growing upside risks to the inflation outlook from 
persistently elevated energy prices, which the bank warned at its previous meeting could increasingly feed through to other prices and wages the longer energy costs 
remain high. Meanwhile, recent monthly GDP and retail sales data point to resilience in economic activity and household spending despite the energy shock. As a 
result, Thursday’s expected hike is likely to mark the start of a gradual tightening cycle, with the pace dependent on the evolution of inflationary pressures and 
economic activity. The bank will also update its forecasts at this meeting, incorporating more persistent energy-price pressures and potentially the impact of the 
recent Budget. Riksbank: A lean consensus expects the Riksbank to deliver its first 25bp hike in three years on Wednesday, while markets price in only 17bp of 
tightening, rising to 30bp by year-end. At its last meeting, the bank signalled that “the policy rate should be raised more going forward,” with tightening expected to 
begin this year. Since then, the Board’s communication has remained hawkish, with Governor Erik Thedeen noting that the “economy has performed better than 
expected, whilst the risks of inflation overshooting the target have increased,” and revealing that he had argued for a rate hike as early as November at the previous 
meeting. Norges Bank: Norges Bank is expected to hold its deposit rate at 4.50% on Thursday, following two pre-emptive 25bp hikes early in the energy shock. At its 
last meeting, the bank signalled that rates would remain near their current elevated level for longer than previously expected, while leaving open the possibility of

Banco Central do Brasil: Banco Central do Brasil’s Wednesday meeting is expected to be “live,” with consensus split between a hold and a 25bp cut, while markets 
have removed all easing previously priced for the meeting following the election. The surprise election outcome relative to pre-election polls has driven a reversal of 
the early-October market moves, with the Brazilian real depreciating sharply and financial conditions tightening. At the same time, expectations of fiscal easing could 
add to inflationary pressures and reduce the room for further monetary easing. Against this backdrop, the bank’s forward guidance will be key to anchoring 
expectations around the inflation outlook and the path back to target.
Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA): The Reserve Bank of Australia is expected to deliver its fifth 25bp rate hike on Monday, taking the cash rate to its highest since 
2008. Following its September hike, the RBA left the door open to further tightening, warning that inflation remained too high and could prove more persistent than 
expected. Since then, the case for another hike has strengthened. Energy prices remain elevated, the labour market has softened but remains relatively tight, and Q3 
core inflation was stronger than expected. Governor Bullock reinforced the hawkish message last week, warning that upside inflation risks were materialising amid the 
AI boom, weather disruptions, Middle East developments and domestic capacity constraints. Crucially, she stressed that a further period of subdued growth is 
needed to contain inflation, with excess demand still part of the problem. With the RBA also likely to revise its inflation forecasts higher, the combination of recent 
data, hawkish communication and market pricing leaves a high bar for a hold. Bank Negara Malaysia: Bank Negara Malaysia is unanimously expected to hold its 
overnight policy rate at 2.75% on Thursday, given low inflation and contained pass-through despite strong growth. However, the bank is likely to remain cautious 
about the inflationary effects of elevated global commodity prices and domestic demand, after replacing its view that 2026 price pressures would remain contained 
with a warning about uncertainty in global commodity price.
