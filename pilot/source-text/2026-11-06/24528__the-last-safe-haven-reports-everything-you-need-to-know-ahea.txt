 
The Last Safe Haven Reports: Everything You Need 
To Know Ahead Of Apple Earnings 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 02, 2026 - 08:49 PM 
For most of October, Apple was the only megacap tech stock that didn't need an AI story. 
While semis were puking and the Nasdaq 100 slid into its third correction of the year, Apple 
quietly reclaimed the title of world's most valuable company on Friday, and its correlation to the 
Nasdaq 100 turned negative for the first time ever. As our friends at The Market Ear put it on 
Oct 19, "Apple is doing something it has never done before." Here is what that looks like, 
courtesy of Bloomberg: 
 
The result: Apple is up 17% since the end of June, while the Nasdaq 100 is down 6%, and that 
includes a 7.4% puke after the China and Services miss on July 30: 
 

 
 
Tonight, after the close, the haven has to report. It will also be John Ternus' first earnings call as 
CEO (Tim Cook took his final call in July), and Goldman's Giulio Esposito calls it "the ultimate 
test for consumer hardware elasticity and self-funded AI deployment." In other words, can Apple 
keep raising prices faster than people stop buying, at a time when memory chips cost multiples 
of what they did a year ago? 
"In-Line" Is The New Beat 
Start with the bar. The Street is looking for fiscal Q4 (September quarter) revenue of $113.5 
billion, right at the top of Apple's $111.7-113.7 billion guidance range, with EPS of $2.00 and a 
gross margin of 47.6% (guidance 47-48%). iPhone is expected at $56.5 billion and Services at 
$31.6 billion, per Bloomberg: 
 

 
 
Nobody on the sell-side is reaching for the top end. UBS Sales & Trading gives Apple a 
sentiment score of just 6.5 out of 10(it had Amazon at 9 last week), and analyst David Vogt 
(Neutral, $296 PT) "made limited estimate changes ahead of Apple earnings." His numbers sit a 
hair below consensus: 
"Apple is expected to report iPhone revenue of $56.0 bn and total revenue of $112.8 bn, versus 
consensus of $56.2 bn and $113.6 bn. The slightly below-consensus iPhone forecast reflects 
roughly 5% fewer units, partly offset by around 5% higher average selling prices. iPhone 
revenue is expected to grow close to 14% y/y." 
Translation: fewer phones, at higher prices, adding up to roughly the same dollars. Hold that 
thought. 
Goldman's Michael Ng (Buy, $390 PT) is a touch more constructive but lands in the same place. 
In his F4Q26 preview, "iPhone 18 family + Duo to drive growth in F2027" (available to pro subs), 
he writes: 
"We expect AAPL to deliver in-line F4Q26 results with (1) iPhone revenue growth of +15% yoy 
as price increases on the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max and older generation phones, as well as 
the launch of the Duo offset modest volume declines as a result of price-volume elasticity and 
the delayed launch of the iPhone 18; (2) Services revenue growth of +10% yoy despite slowing 
App Store growth as iCloud+, AppleCare+, subscription services, and advertising grow 
 

 
double-digits; and (3) gross margins of 47.8% which includes a 1pp benefit from tariff refunds 
and reflects headwinds from component cost inflation." 
Ng's growth rates work out to roughly $56.4 billion of iPhone and $31.6 billion of Services, i.e. 
right on consensus. JPMorgan's Samik Chatterjee has revenue at $112.9 billion (+10% y/y), 
"modestly above the guidance midpoint." Put it all together and the sell-side has converged on 
one word: in-line: 
 
Vogt sees EPS of $1.97 vs. $1.99 consensus, and flags where any upside would come from: "A 
few cents of upside is possible from lower operating expenses, although an opex-driven beat is 
unlikely to be rewarded by investors." Which is a polite way of saying that cutting costs to make 
the quarter is what companies do when the product isn't making it for them. 
The Guide Is The Print: Price Up, Units Down... A Lot 
As usual, the quarter matters less than the December-quarter guidance. Goldman's TMT desk 
(Peter Callahan) lists the "4Q revenue guide" as one of three things investors care about 
tonight, noting that GIR "expects Apple to guide to total revenue growth of LSD%-MSD% yoy." 
JPMorgan's Chatterjee is on the same page ("look for low to mid-single digit growth with 
iPhones ASPs offsetting lower volumes"), and his December-quarter model is where it gets 
interesting: 
 

 
"He forecasts $147.9bn of revenue, +3% YoY with iPhone revenue of $84.0bn, -2% YoY vs. 
69mm units, -18% YoY." 
Read that again: iPhone units down 18% in Apple's biggest quarter of the year, with price doing 
all the work. Part of that is self-inflicted timing: this year Apple launched only its premium models 
in the fall (the 18 Pro, Pro Max and the ~$2,000 foldable Duo) and pushed the base iPhone 18 
to spring 2027. Part of it, though, looks like plain sticker shock. 
Recall that back in June, Apple jacked up Mac and iPad prices by 15-25% overnight because of 
the memory crunch, and the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max then launched at $1,199 and $1,299, 
both $100 above their predecessors. The early read has not been great: 
●​ Bernstein found that US Pro and Pro Max demand was down low single digits y/y (China 
was up 12% in launch week). 
●​ Nikkei reported that Apple cut October component orders for the two Pro models by 
15-20%, with one supplier calling the handsets "just too expensive." 
●​ Loop Capital downgraded the stock to Hold on Oct 20, citing iPhone shipment 
reductions. 
And then there's the Duo. Pre-orders sold out instantly (reportedly), resellers demanded steep 
premiums, and then, within days of delivery, screens started cracking down the middle: 
Apple acknowledged "display problems" and offered replacements, and "Crackgate" was born. 
UBS Evidence Lab still had average Duo wait times at 89 days as of last week, but Vogt is 
watching for signs of buyers deferring to a second-generation foldable or trading down to the 
Pro models. Goldman's Rich Privorotsky says tonight matters for "what it says about iPhone 
demand and early Duo orders." Expect that question on the call, probably more than once. 
Goldman, for its part, thinks FY27 is fine: Ng counts on "the full-year contribution from price 
increases on the iPhone 18 Pro/Pro Max, a replacement cycle improvement catalyzed by the 
iPhone Duo, the Spring 2027 launches of the base iPhone 18, iPhone 18e and iPhone Air 2," 
plus the new touchscreen MacBook Pro unveiled at last week's "Pro and Tell" event. JPM's 
Chatterjee has FY27 revenue of $518.3 billion (+9%), "just below consensus," on iPhone units 
of only ~247 million (+3%). Sure, so long as consumers keep paying up. 
Tariff Refunds In, DRAM Out 
Gross margin is the second thing on Callahan's list ("cost inflation, pricing & services mix"), and 
here the headline number flatters. Both banks have it inside guidance, but both get help from a 
one-off: UBS's 47.5% "includ[es] a roughly 100bp tariff-refund benefit," as does Goldman's 
 

 
47.8%. Strip that out and, per UBS, "gross margin is expected to decline due largely to higher 
DRAM costs." 
That's no surprise to anyone who has looked at a memory price lately. Back in June, Vogt 
estimated that memory had gone from about $50-60 (a mid-single-digit share) of a $1,000 
iPhone's bill of materials to "~20%" depending on long-term agreements, which is why he 
expected Apple to push through roughly $100 price increases just to keep product margins in 
the 37-38% range. Since then, things have gotten worse: South Korean DRAM exports hit 
another record $34 billion in October, up 595% y/y (per Goldman's Korea tech team), and 
TrendForce sees mobile DRAM contract prices up another 0-5% q/q in Q4. Somebody pays for 
that, and so far it has been the consumer. The question for tonight is how long that lasts, and 
what happens to the margin guide once the tariff refund is gone. 
Services: The Golden Calf Slows Down 
Services was supposed to be the part of Apple that didn't care about DRAM. In July it missed by 
almost $1 billion, growing just 12%, which prompted this: 
Three months later, the Street has caught up. Everyone is now at about +10% y/y ($31.6 billion), 
and UBS notes that "App Store growth slowed to low single digits." Callahan's first question for 
tonight is whether Apple can "hold DDs y/y growth on this line," and Ng names "the 
sustainability of DD% Services growth given slower App Store trends and continued regulatory 
pressure on App Store monetization" as one of the two key debates. Probably the most 
important one, given it's the highest-margin line Apple has. 
A Haven... At A Record Multiple 
Here is the problem with being the safe haven: everyone hides in the same place. Callahan 
points out that Apple "goes into earnings on Monday night at an all-time high multiple - both on 
an absolute basis (36x fwd P/E...) and on a relative basis (17x premium to the S&P 500)" (chart 
source GS, Bloomberg): 
 

 
 
That is a forward P/E nearly double the S&P's 18.4x for a company whose unit volumes are 
expected to shrink next quarter. Our contributor Quoth the Raven asked last week whether 
Apple has peaked, and did the math on a trailing basis: "If Apple's earnings remained 
unchanged and its valuation contracted from 40x to 30 times, the stock would fall 25%... at 20x 
earnings, investors would be looking at a 50% haircut without the company losing a single dollar 
of annual profitability." Larry McDonald's Bear Traps Report went further on Friday, telling clients 
it is "long AAPD, high conviction" (that would be the inverse Apple ETF). 
The bulls aren't hiding, though. Last Monday and Tuesday, Nomura's Charlie McElligott flagged 
"MASSIVE Upside Vol BUYERS" in megacap tech into earnings, with Apple leading the pack: 
We know how the Alphabet leg of that trade went. 
Options are pricing a big move for tonight. On our math, the Wednesday at-the-money straddle 
implies about 4.4%, roughly double Apple's 2.3% average absolute move over the past 12 
reports. In fairness, that expiry also covers Tuesday's midterms, but Goldman's Brian Garrett 
notes that at the index level "excess variance for the midterms remains nonexistent" (the 
Wednesday SPX straddle "captures aapl earnings and midterms and yet costs 30bps less than 
Friday's single session move"). So most of that premium is probably Apple. And the last time it 
reported, the stock fell 7.3%: 
 

 
 
Elasticity Has A Limit 
So here's the setup. Apple walks in at an all-time high multiple, with a moderate sentiment score 
(6.5/10 at UBS), a sell-side that expects "in-line," a gross margin propped up by a tariff refund, 
Services growth slowing toward 10%, and a December quarter in which JPMorgan sees iPhone 
units falling 18% while price does the heavy lifting. It also walks in as the stock people bought 
because it isn't an AI trade, the same week Deutsche Bank's strategists flipped back to positive 
on US tech after an 11% drawdown. If the AI names bounce, the haven money has somewhere 
else to go. 
Can Ternus pull it off? Maybe, if the Duo backlog is real and the guide lands at the high end of 
"LSD-MSD." But we suspect the market is about to find out what we flagged in July: a 36x 
multiple is a lot to pay for fewer phones at higher prices, and an opex beat won't cover for that. 
UBS put it best: a few cents of upside from lower costs "is unlikely to be rewarded by investors." 
Then again, nothing says "safe haven" like a stock whose earnings growth increasingly depends 
on how much more people will pay for a phone that cracks.
