GS TMT SPEC SALES – Wednesday 4th November
The SOX rebounded 2.5% ahead of the US midterm election results, although it closed below its intraday
high of +4.2%. US desk activity was subdued at 4 on a 1–10 scale, but semiconductor and AI flows were
more balanced as selective buying absorbed some of the ongoing selling. MU rose 2.6%, Hynix ADRs
2.1% and NVDA 2.3%. After hours, AMD and Super Micro each gained 7% following better-than-
expected revenue outlooks, with AMD delivering a strong Datacenter beat (+97% in Q). Arista rose 10%,
with its outlook pointing to AI revenue doubling to $7bn. Semiconductors gained a further 2% after the
close.
This morning, attention turns to the implications of the Democratic sweep for data centres and AI,
particularly in Texas (where we saw a democratic win), which accounts for circa. 27% of planned US
data-centre capacity (see chart below). The initial concern is whether Texas’s shift will weigh on data-
centre development, although some X commentary suggests the new policies may be less negative than
first feared as the existing players are already in process of meeting many of Hinojosa asks.
Following the recent semiconductor and AI sell-off, the question is how much policy risk is already priced
in. Yesterday’s sharp rallies provide context, rather than proof that those risks have been discounted: CIFR
rose 18.4%, HUT 16%, WULF 14.5% and GLXY 15.4%, alongside neoclouds IREN (+11%), NBIS
(+7%) and CRWV (+7%), and power names BE (+9%), GEV (+6.5%) and VST (+3.7%). These moves
preceded the election results. Early trading suggesting Europe is waiting to see which way the US takes
things when it opens..

Would note that our US TMT trader yesterday highlighted a more balanced two-way market on the desk,
with selective buying absorbing some of the ongoing semiconductor and AI selling. This contrasted with
recent sessions, when there was little buying support. It was also encouraging to see Meta and Oracle trade
well following their recent capital raises: with issuance still heavy, particularly in AI, the market’s ability
to absorb new supply remains important. Despite difficult recent price action, some of the conditions
investors have been looking for are arguably starting to fall into place, at least partially: earnings delivery,
financing, some relief in oil and rates for now, and resets in positioning and valuations.

RESULTS
• AMD: Strong Data Center demand drove Q3 revenue of $13.38bn (+45% y/y), ahead of $13.11bn
consensus, with Data Center sales up 97% to $8.55bn versus $8.25bn expected. Q4 revenue guidance of
~$17bn (+66% y/y) topped $16.29bn consensus and reached the upper end of investor expectations; lower
operating expenses imply EPS of ~$2.92 versus $2.65 consensus. Margins were the caveat: Q3 gross
margin of 55.94% missed expectations, Q4 guidance of ~55% was 30bps below consensus, and Client
revenue fell 3.7% q/q versus guidance for slight growth. GIR reiterates Buy, citing CPU share gains and
broader AI accelerator adoption, with its commentary pointing to visibility for >80% company-wide

revenue growth in 2027 despite PC headwinds; the release itself gave no fresh numerical 2027 outlook,
versus August’s guidance for Data Center revenue to more than double and server CPU growth above
70%.
• Arista (ANET): Michael Ng highlights a Q3 revenue, EPS and margin beat, raised 2026 revenue
guidance implying 48% growth, and an initial 2027 outlook for 38–40% revenue growth, 62–64% gross
margins and ~49% EBIT margins, with AI revenue doubling to $7bn and campus reaching $2bn. Growing
AI complexity and custom ASIC deployments support demand for Arista’s networking and EOS platform,
while enterprise security and upgrade needs underpin campus growth. Management targets 1–2 new 10%
customers by year-end and expects >10GW of additional XPU deployments across 10+ accelerators in
2027. GIR raises 2027/28 EPS estimates by ~10% to $6.13/$7.83; declining deferred revenue and slowing
bookings growth remain the key investor concerns.
• Wolters Kluwer: slightly positive, but essentially a rounding beat, with 9M26 organic growth reported at
6% versus GSe’s 5% despite all divisions being in line, implying minimal consensus changes. The main
positive was Clinical Solutions accelerating to 6% growth from 5% in H1, while continued print declines
remained a 100bps drag on group organic growth. Constant-currency adjusted EBIT grew 10%, in line
with H1, although product development spending is weighted towards Q4. Full-year guidance was
reiterated, including an adjusted operating margin of around 28%, high-single-digit adjusted EPS growth
and adjusted free cash flow of €1.30–1.35bn. See GIR take
• Scout24: BEAT Q3 and guide raised catch is street is there. Revenue and adjusted EBITDA beat
expectations by around 1%, with stronger Spain performance and private-customer pricing offsetting
softer German professional subscriptions. Revenue rose 19.7% to €199.2m, including 10.1% organic
growth, while adjusted EBITDA reached approximately €121m at a 60.9% margin. Professional revenue
grew 23.3%, supported by Spain and transaction services, but German professional subscription revenue
was 2% below expectations as subdued development activity weighed on new-home developer spending
(grew 11.5% vs. GIR 13.5%); the slowdown in subscription revenue per customer was also greater than
anticipated. Private revenue beat consensus by 3%, helped by pay-per-ad sales and 9% growth in average
revenue per customer, with 40% of subscribers on higher-priced tiers and approximately 534,000
subscribers versus 535,000 expected.
FY26 revenue growth guidance moves to approximately 18% from 16–18%, reflecting a larger 7–8
percentage-point contribution from the Spain acquisition versus 6–7 previously. Adjusted EBITDA margin
guidance remains unchanged at up to 61% including Spain and up to 64% organically, implying limited
changes to consensus forecasts. See GIR

• SMCI: F1Q27 Review: Beat & raise on broadening demand with improving working capital highlighted
a strong F1Q27 beat, with EPS of $1.81, revenue of $19.9bn and gross margins of 12.8%, driven by
continued AI infrastructure demand and a favourable mix of higher-margin products, prompting
management to raise FY27 revenue guidance to $72-82bn from $65-72bn while citing customer
deployment constraints and component availability, rather than demand, as the main limiting factors. The
company also reported a significant improvement in working capital, with its cash conversion cycle
improving by 52 days QoQ to 97 days, supported by $3.1bn of customer prepayments, and said it expects
to fund future growth through operating cash flow and its balance sheet without needing additional capital.
While Goldman was encouraged by the stronger outlook, it noted ongoing risks from customer
concentration, with one customer representing 44% of F1Q revenue, and increasing competition from
ODMs and OEMs that could continue to drive margin volatility.

TELCOS
• Liberty Global: the call felt aimed at the CMA rather than investors with management strongly
challenging its approach to Netomnia and arguing that BT/TalkTalk should receive comparable scrutiny.
Management will participate in a CMA hearing and has requested a supplementary report and a
reassessment of the counterfactuals; a final report is expected in mid-December, with no certainty on the
outcome. The UK strategy is shifting towards capital discipline: Liberty will not build a third fibre
network, restricting new build to areas where it would form a duopoly, while retaining renting, partnership
and acquisition options. Existing HFC delivering 2Gbps is seen as sufficient for the next few years, with
no UK DOCSIS 4.0 rollout implied. Management is targeting revenue stabilisation, not assuming a near-
term recovery, with cash-flow improvement driven by cost savings, simplification, AI and lower fibre
spending. All asset-sale options are being considered to deleverage, subject to Telefónica alignment,
including towers and potential HFC/fibre transactions. VMO2 leverage is well into the 6x range versus a
4–5x target (and mgt continue to buy back stock), although positive free cash flow, no maturities before
2029 and base-rate hedging provide runway; further financing detail is due with Q4/early-next-year
updates. Separately, management reiterated the Q2 Ziggo Group spin, barring force majeure, and its 2028
targets of 4.5x leverage and 500m equity free cash flow, but the detailed financial bridge remains
outstanding.
MEDIA & INTERNET
• WPP: interesting Digiday article on WPP that argues that Apple’s crackdown on browser-based identity
tracking has minimal direct impact on its data offering because InfoSum, now integrated into WPP Open,
enables companies to analyse data without moving it out of their own systems and operates outside the
browser infrastructure Safari is blocking. The article frames this as a potential strategic advantage over
Publicis following its $2.2bn LiveRamp acquisition, with LiveRamp now on Apple’s blocklist and WPP
having stopped using it by June. However, WPP is not immune: weaker Safari signals and restrictions on
third-party ad-tech tools could still affect targeting, while consumer identity remains an input into
InfoSum. Separately, WPP Media returned to modest growth, with Q3 like-for-like revenue excluding
pass-through costs up 0.3% versus a 2.8% decline in Q2. Its decision not to pitch for Coca-Cola’s North

American media account also reflects a more selective approach to pitching, prioritising delivery on
business already won elsewhere over another costly process.
