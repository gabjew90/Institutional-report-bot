ETD Rates Watch: Equity Futures Rebound
As Markets Digest Democratic Sweep
Daniel Noorian
04-Nov-2026, 03:54 ET (EST)
The Wednesday Asia session was another example of how the first move in markets is not always the right
one. As news came through that the Democrats had enjoyed a blue wave, taking both the Senate and the
House, risk markets fell, with E-mini S&P 500 futures down 1.63% at the lows, trading down to 7,490.
However, dip buyers soon emerged, with the market rallying 1.24% from the lows. It was a similar story in
E-mini Nasdaq futures, while Asian exchanges also saw some volatile swings. The biggest was in the Kospi,
which initially rallied 3.2% on the open before falling 4% and then bouncing back to currently stand up 0.62%
as at 0705 GMT.
It was a similar story in fixed income markets, which initially rallied on the back of equity weakness before
giving back earlier gains. There has been little shift in front-end pricing, with markets pricing 19.3bp of hikes for
December, more or less unchanged from Tuesday. Although futures volumes were almost three times the
recent average, with TY trading 452k, options were actually quiet by recent standards.
In regular TY expiries, no strike has traded over 5,000 lots as at 0710 GMT. The most popular plays came in
FV, with upside once again the preferred play. The FVZ6 104.75/105.75/106.50 broken call fly was bought for
17.5 in 2k and was also bought against selling the 104.25 put for 6.5. SOFR options were also quiet, with the
only flow of note a buyer of 4k 1y March midcurves 96/96.50 call spreads for 8, underlying SFRH8 95.535.
SOFR futures spreads were more active, although it was the three and six-month gaps that dominated flows.
For example, SFRH7/M7 (12.13) traded 10k while SFRM7/Z7 (1.5/2) traded 8.5k.
President Trump did congratulate “a formidable opponent” and said the Democrats “did a good job.” He
acknowledged that the Iran war had contributed to his defeat but warned that he would use his veto powers in
the final two years of his presidency. He also said he could work with a Democratic-controlled Congress “if
they want to work with me.”
There are now two things for markets to focus on: Iran and the AI trade.
On Iran, there has been a pick-up in headlines around a possible deal. President Trump told the Financial
Times [paywall] the US had no plans to strike Iran and suggested he was content with the state of the stand-off
with Iran. Ghalibaf, the speaker of the Iranian parliament, said the Republicans’ defeat was America saying no
to war and that they endorsed this message.
On the AI trade, given how prominently data centres featured in campaigns, there will be more concerns over
the tech sector. I mentioned above that the dip was bought over the Wednesday Asia session, but there will be
concerns over a halt to data-centre construction. For example, in Texas, Gina Hinojosa, who was elected, ran
on a campaign calling for an immediate moratorium on data-centre developments. Data-centre development
has been a massive tailwind for the US economy.
The question now is whether that continues. The AI trade and its associated investment have been huge
drivers of US economic growth. If that tailwind is removed, how will the economy perform? There are also
suggestions that this growth has helped mask the impact of higher energy prices, while the wealth effect of US
equity markets has been well documented. One positive could be a resurgence in housebuilding if resources

earmarked for data-centre development find themselves being redeployed. How does this all play out for the
Fed? Unfortunately, none of these questions will be answered today.
In other news, ECB Governing Council member Nagel, speaking last night, said the ECB would probably raise
rates next month. He warned that the inflation picture has deteriorated since the September forecasts and that
repeated spikes in energy prices also raise the risk of second-round effects. The only way a hike can be
avoided is if the inflation picture changes dramatically. His colleague on the Governing Council, Kocher, also
echoed his comments.
Accounts have been focusing on the 97.0625 strike in ERH7 as they position for December to be the last hike
of the cycle. Call flies and condors have been the expressions of choice. The largest flow came in the ERH7
96.6875/96.8125/97.0625/97.1875 call condor. That was bought for 6.5 on Tuesday and paper has now
accumulated around 50k of this in-the-money structure.
It doesn’t look like there are too many concerns over private credit, with Blackstone raising around EUR 13bn
for its European direct lending strategy. The firm has now closed its fourth European Senior Direct Lending
fund and is targeting mid-market and upper mid-market borrowers. The fundraising follows news that Ares is
raising money faster for its latest flagship US direct lending fund than for the previous record-setting vintage.
The best-performing equity market in Asia on Wedensday was China, with the Shanghai Composite up 2.16%
and the Shenzhen Composite up 3.7%. Markets were boosted as state-backed funds stepped in. A basket of
ETFs favoured by state funds drew $11.5bn of inflows on Tuesday, the second-largest on record.
