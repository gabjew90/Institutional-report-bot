From AI panic to squeeze?
The epicentre of the global AI trade has gone from euphoria to panic. Korean memory stocks have
been crushed, hedge funds have aggressively cut exposure and valuations have fallen to
extraordinary lows. Yet the underlying memory cycle remains remarkably strong, with demand
outstripping supply and earnings estimates holding firm. Meanwhile, KOSPI has just printed a massive
hammer off a critical long-term trend line. Positioning has reset, valuations are deeply depressed and
fundamentals refuse to break. The ingredients for a violent squeeze are falling into place.
The KOSPI hammer
KOSPI, the epicentre of the global AI trade, has actually outperformed most AI-related markets during
the recent selloff. The overnight hammer is particularly striking. Note that KOSPI bounced off the major
trend line that has been in place since the bull market began, while the 200-day moving average
remains well below current levels.
Could this be the start of a violent short squeeze?

Hedge funds dump Korea AI
The great Korea AI pile-in has gone into reverse. Goldman's Stankard highlights heavy net selling in
October, with gross exposure falling from a peak of ~7% to ~4.9% and net exposure collapsing from
~8% to ~3.5%. The fast money that chased the AI trade into Seoul is now heading for the exits.

The memory massacre
Memory stocks have been absolutely crushed. JPMorgan highlights that the sector has plunged 45%
from its 2026 peak, compared with a 29% drawdown in the SOX. SK Hynix has collapsed 53% from its
highs, while Samsung is down 40%. The market has gone from pricing an AI supercycle to aggressively
discounting a memory downturn. The question is whether the selling has already gone too far.

Too cheap to ignore?
The valuation reset in Korean memory is extraordinary. JPMorgan highlights SK Hynix trading at just
2.6x 2027 estimated earnings and Samsung at 2.8x. The broader memory group trades at only 3.6x
despite projected EPS CAGR of 98% between 2026 and 2028. Crucially, earnings estimates have not
followed share prices lower, with JPMorgan seeing little risk of cuts over the next six months. The
disconnect between valuations and fundamentals is becoming difficult to ignore.
JPM
The cycle refuses to break
The market is increasingly pricing an imminent memory downcycle, but the underlying data continue
to tell a different story. JPMorgan highlights DRAM prices rising 23% quarter-on-quarter in Q3, while
memory makers report demand exceeding supply beyond 2027. Long-term supply agreements are
expanding, providing greater earnings visibility even as AI funding concerns dominate sentiment. The
stocks are trading like the cycle has already broken. The fundamentals suggest otherwise.
