GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Land & Power. 2027 vs 2029. Vol
Compression 4 Nov 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
4 Nov 2026
Markets: With legislative margins and key regional leadership races now decided, equity futures saw a
sharp overnight dip followed by a meaningful recovery. Texas governor race: Gina Hinojosa defeated
Abbott (AP). Resilience is interesting. A lot of hedges were put on over the past few days that now need to
be monetized. Multiples have already been aggressively de-rated, particularly across AI infrastructure, in
anticipation of these concerns. The question is how much is already in the price? My suspicion is that vol
will struggle to sustain a bid and the initial selloff gets absorbed as people begin picking apart what they
actually want to own.
Land/power: This is where it gets interesting. The debate is no longer just about the cost of compute, but
whether you can actually permit, energize and deliver the capacity required in 2027/28/29. Moratoriums,
local opposition to data center development and a potentially more restrictive regulatory environment
could materially constrain the supply curve. Two very different implications. First, if you sell chips,
cooling, electrical equipment or the nuts and bolts of data centers, there is a legitimate risk that customers
have overordered relative to the capacity they can actually energize. Some of that demand could migrate to
other geographies or BTM solutions, but not necessarily quickly enough. This is potentially negative for
the picks and shovels, although much of that risk now appears reflected in multiples. The second, more
perverse implication is that compute itself becomes even scarcer. Assuming demand continues to
accelerate, how exactly do you deliver the supply everyone was expecting? If you can’t, the scarcity value
of existing, energized compute should rise. That potentially benefits the hyperscalers and owners of
deployed capacity. Neoclouds are a more nuanced debate… some have substantial existing compute and
contracted demand, while others remain dependent on new facilities, financing and power delivery.
They’re not all the same. The distinction between owning compute today and promising compute
tomorrow becomes enormously important. Ultimately, throttling the supply of compute doesn’t necessarily
destroy demand… it could just push pricing higher for longer.
2027 vs 2029: Beyond the immediate AI implications, the focus turns to the broader policy and corporate
planning horizon...naturally arise regarding future corporate tax rates, the antitrust/M&A landscape, and
administrative oversight. These are legitimate uncertainties that can cap terminal multiples. However,
there is a compelling counterargument: with current administration leadership in place through 2028,
corporate decision makers face a distinct incentive to pull forward capital deployment, infrastructure
permitting, and strategic transactions into 2027 to lock in existing regulatory conditions. The market may
increasingly look past theoretical 2029 policy changes to focus on tangible corporate activity incentivized
over the next two years. Historically, the S&P 500 has been higher 12 months after all 19 midterm election
cycles since 1950.

Geopolitics: Unclear what comes next. Escalation or continued negotiations. Mediators still exchanging
proposals and attempting to bridge the remaining differences. A limited deal is possible, although the two
sides remain far apart. Add the scheduled trilateral Russia/Ukraine/US talks in Abu Dhabi on November
10–11 and there is at least a pathway toward a more constructive geopolitical backdrop. Equally, we could
be discussing another weekend of escalation.
AMD: Remarkably robust numbers and exactly the micro evidence the AI trade needed. Revenue +45%
YoY to $13.4bn, data center +97% to $8.5bn, with Q4 guidance implying another 27% sequential
acceleration. Worth contrasting with Intel, which was a pretty poor read through for the broader AI
complex. Intel’s data center business actually grew 78% YoY, so demand clearly wasn’t the problem.
AMD, meanwhile, is benefiting from both EPYC CPU demand and the ramp in Instinct GPUs, with no
obvious signs of deceleration. Intel was a supply/execution and profitability story, not a demand warning.
Hard to reconcile AMD’s accelerating growth with the scale of multiple compression across the AI
complex, even allowing for legitimate concerns around future power availability.
Risk: Already a lot in the price and a lot of hedges outstanding. I think the market will increasingly anchor
on what happens in 2027 rather than trying to discount every possible outcome in 2029. Geopolitics
remains the swing factor and could take us in either direction very quickly. But my gut is that AI
infrastructure has been sold to death, and investors will start differentiating between the capacity that
exists and the capacity that may never arrive. The micro remains strong, electoral uncertainty has
diminished and positioning looks considerably cleaner. Vol likely comes in quickly and that drives spot in
the short term.
