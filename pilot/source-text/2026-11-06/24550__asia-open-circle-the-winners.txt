Asia Open - Circle the winners
By Preston Pagli
Markets repriced aggressively in the wake of the election outcome, with the most clear indicators in equities where the
Mag7 and AI hardware sector bifurcated as investors re-assess CapEx implications against a more rigid regulatory
backdrop. Oil prices collapsed further with Brent nearly 20% lower since Friday. USTs rallied on the move while the
Treasury's quarterly refunding announcement offered little additional support to the long-end, keeping nominal coupon
sizes unchanged. The dollar's sharp sell-off was mostly short-lived as attention turns back to the still sticky inflationary
backdrop and upcoming US data implications of the Fed's policy path, with NFP due Friday. Looking ahead, a busy
macro calendar across Asia includes BoJ meeting minutes at 23:50 GMT, Philippines and Thailand CPI at 01:00 GMT
and 03:30 GMT respectively.

US Midterms: Cross Asset First Take (REPLAY)
In Focus‌
Equities: SPX (+1.58%) posted its best single-day performance since August driven by a surge in Mag7 constituents on the
presumed slowdown of the CapEx spending that has concerned investors this earnings cycle. On the contrary, semis
remained under pressure on the presumed slowdown with SOX -3.13% and -23% over the past 30 days. Beneath the
surface, vol capitulated as peak event risk has passed while NFP implied vol compressed meaningfully with SPX: 0.71%,
QQQ: 1.3%, IWM: 0.98%. SPX (ESZ6) closed above the CTA trigger to flip long at $7,670, pointing to the likelihood of
systematic buying flows on Thursday.
Rates: Global FI rallied following the sharp drop in oil with UST yields 8bps lower led by the belly. The market remains
structurally short and tactically long driven largely by systematics while the macro framework remains stuck between
reflationary and goldilocks. Our US Rates Strategy desk initiated a tactical long position in 30y breakevens as the
valuation seems to be diverging from the broader macro themes of solid risk asset performance, a relatively dovish monetary
policy, and above average growth. The Treasury QRF announcement for Q4 came in line with expectations while the
coupon issuance guidance being the more interesting read through as the Treasury opened the door for auction size
adjustments in 2027.
Commodities: Crude repriced sharply lower with WTI and Brent slumping 8% to $89 and $95 respectively. Citi Macro
Strategist Jim McCormick alongside Commodities strategists Eric Lee joined my colleague Varshi Karamsetty for a discussion
on Wednesday with Eric Lee noting the short-term outlook could remain choppy until 2027 while the broader outlook is

bearish for oil. On the supply side, EIA data showed US diesel stockpiles fell below 100 million barrels for the first time
since 2003. Elsewhere, gold (+3.4%) surged back above $4,000, posting its largest gain since mid-August.
FX: The dollar initially weakened on the Dem sweep on the basis of lighter AI-driven inflows and geopolitical safe-haven
qualities. EUR strengthened as Eurozone CPI came in line with consensus, rising +0.4% MoM, supporting the case for a hike
in December with 20bps priced. LatAm currencies rallied after a sharp sell-off on Tuesday where BRL, COP, and MXN all
weakened more than 1% each. On the NY eFX trading desk, activity was heavy in EUR pairs while net flows favored CZK,
EUR, and JPY. Outflows were tilted toward CNH, USD, and DKK.
Elsewhere‌
PLN: The MPC held rates steady at 3.75%, in line with expectations while the statement was held mostly the same, providing
little guidance for the policy path forward. PLN strengthened nearly 1% as staff inflation forecasts were revised higher to 3.2%
in 2026 and 4.2% in 2027. 
Ahead:
Client Call at 03:00 GMT: US Midterm Elections - Post Results Assessment & Implications for Asia
JPY: BoJ Minutes of September meeting at 23:50 GMT should be too backwards looking to influence markets. Following the
Oct meeting, we retain our view of a 25bp rate hike in Dec.
SEK: CPIF YoY at 07:00 GMT for October P. Bloomberg cons. looks for headline CPI to ease to 1.4% YoY from 1.5%, while
ex-energy inflation is seen unchanged at 0.5% YoY. However, coming after the Riksbank decision, the inflation print is unlikely
to be a major market mover, especially if policymakers opt to hike rates.
NOK: Deposit Rate Decisions at 09:00 GMT. With 1bp priced in, wide consensus is for a rate hold. Our STIRT desk doesn’t
expect a hike in December either, given their clear communication this year, but at 9.25bp priced in the step, the risk-reward
profile is not favorable to us currently
PHP: CPI YoY at 01:00 GMT for October. Our economists see headline inflation accelerating to 8.1%YoY in October from
7.2%YoY in September.
THB: CPI YoY at 03:30 GMT for October. Our economists expect headline inflation to rise sharply to 3.5%YoY in October from
2.8%YoY in September, while core inflation may edge up to 1.6%YoY.
IDR: GDP QoQ at 04:00 GMT for Q3. Our economists forecast GDP growth at 5.2%YoY, broadly stable from 5.3%YoY in Q2
2026.
MYR: BNM Overnight Policy Rate Decision at 07:00 GMT. Our economists expect BNM to stay on hold at the Nov MPC,
albeit with some risk of a 25bps hike. Taking recent data in totality, our economists now expect a 25bps OPR hike in Jan
which may or may not be hinted at with stronger language at the Nov-26 MPC.
TWD: CPI YoY at 08:00 GMT for October. Our economists expect headline and core inflation both moderating to 2.4%YoY in
October, from 2.7% YoY and 2.5% YoY, respectively, in September.
