The 10 year
Back on October 8, with the US 10-year pushing 5.30–5.35%, we pointed out that everybody was
expecting yields to move higher. That was precisely the problem. Fast forward to today, and the 10-
year has moved from the top of its trend channel around 5.3% all the way down to channel support
near 4.9%. The 100-day moving average sits slightly lower, around 4.8%.
If you played this move, time to say thank you and move on.

Nailed the rates top
In early October JPM warned that CTA momentum signals in Treasuries and Bunds had reached
extreme bearish levels. Trend followers were heavily short bonds, leaving positioning vulnerable to a
sharp mean reversion. JPM absolutely nailed the setup. Another reminder of what happens when
everybody is on the same side of the trade. The chart shows just how stretched momentum was at the
time.
JPM
Bond volatility remains stressed
Bond volatility has eased lately, but remains elevated by recent standards. The MOVE index was
averaging around 75 before the rates chaos erupted. At 100, bond volatility is still pricing in
considerable stress, despite the recent retreat.

LSEG Workspace
Still shorty
According to DB, CTAs have covered only a fraction of their bond shorts despite the sharp rally in
Treasuries. The positioning unwind may be far from over.
DB
The CTA bond squeeze
According to Goldman Sachs, trend followers are turning increasingly bullish on US Treasuries, with
CTAs expected to buy around $2.5mn DV01 each in the 2-year and 5-year sectors, and $1.5mn DV01
each in the 10-year and 30-year sectors this week. The bigger risk is a further bond rally triggering
medium-term trend signals, potentially unleashing another wave of systematic buying. Short-term
signals have already flipped positive, while medium-term signals remain close to turning. The
machines are shifting from bond sellers to buyers, but remember, this is a CTA-driven flow story, not
necessarily a fundamental bullish call on Treasuries.
