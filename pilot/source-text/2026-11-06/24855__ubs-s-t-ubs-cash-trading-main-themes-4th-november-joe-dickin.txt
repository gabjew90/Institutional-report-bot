Trading Desk commentary, not a product of UBS Research. For Institutional Clients only
#Markets : EStoxx -50bps to start, though already c1% off the early morning lows as markets shake off a
much weaker open for tech-exposed markets after Democrats swept control of both chambers of Congress.
The knee-jerk was sharp, with Nasdaq futures down as much as 2.4% before trimming losses, Nasdaq and
S&P -50bps at time of writing. The KOSPI's 4% rally off the lows back to unchanged suggests market pricing
was more in-line with odds markets on Midterm results. The cleaner expression has been the dollar, broadly
weaker with USDJPY back below 158, CHF +0.5% and both EUR and GBP higher. Gold jumps ~$50 to
~$3,980/oz and UST 10yrs ease 2bps to 4.94%, with the market reading a sharp rebuke of Trump and a much
narrower legislative runway rather than a growth scare. Earnings helps cushion the AI complex with AMD,
ANET, ALAB and SMCI all delivered beats with stronger outlooks, led by AI/data-center demand; SWKS sold
off on weak post-Qorvo guidance, FRSH fell on softer billings and a modest FY trim, while VRNS beat but
withheld guidance due to its pending acquisition. China trades broadly higher (CSI300 +3%, SHCOMP +1.9%)
Semis +10% in the region as state measures to support markets continue to feed into price action, China
services gauge unexpectedly dipped for the first time since July, and October A-share new account openings
fell -21.5% MoM (-35% YoY), so retail appetite is cooling despite the State Council's capital markets push in its
report to the NPC. Japan broadly lower (close yesterday). Oil is steady with WTI ~$96 as the US and Iran
continue to pursue diplomacy despite limited American strikes. API showed a crude draw (-5.3m) and
distillates -1.2m, but builds in gasoline (+1.4m) and Cushing (+0.5m).
#Flow : Flows finished balanced on Tuesday (50/50), with participation marginally below the HT YTD average.
Industrials were the busiest sector (26% of turnover), finishing better for sale with supply concentrated in
electricals. Banks were also active (21% of turnover), finishing better bid on strong demand across Spanish
and Dutch names. Healthcare (15% of turnover) was more nuanced, with two-way activity in large-cap pharma
while MedTech remained quiet. Energy (9% of turnover) saw supply as crude faded, while Tech saw marginal
supply on muted volumes (4% of turnover).
