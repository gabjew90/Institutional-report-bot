Markets: The market bought the election dip… thank heaven for everything that wasn’t semi-related. Two
overarching conclusions from the midterms. Anti-AI regulation will likely slow CapEx, while the
overwhelming unpopularity of the Iran war has eroded the oil risk premium, with markets pricing an
accelerated end to the conflict. Asia reflects the rotation… onshore China finally bid, Nifty +2%
benefiting from lower oil, while KOSPI -5% with Hynix and the AI complex catching down to the US. A
more optimistic post midterm tape, provided you don’t only own the AI supply chain.
Vol: Absolutely crushed. VIX -20% to 16.61, VXN -18% to 25.08. Hyperscalers led, quality bid,
defensives and consumer participating (min vol...see below). Remarkable collapse in index vol against the
violence underneath. This is rotation, not wholesale liquidation. With macro uncertainty receding, nets
low/hedges in place the first move was more obvious...from here more complicated.
AI: This is what I got wrong. Tech flows (at least in Europe) were overwhelmingly bearish, with long only
and hedge funds selling everything from chips to infrastructure. We’re back to somewhat unfalsifiable
concerns about AI spending. Legislative restrictions are difficult to quantify (particularly in out year), but
building will become more expensive, complicated and slower. Multiples have compressed enormously,
positioning was already derisked and another aggressive wave of selling followed. Some moves felt
capitulatory but time will tell. The market is definitively pricing peak CapEx growth. Does it also need to
price an outright deceleration in spending? I think not necessarily. Delayed projects aren’t cancelled
demand, but could create an inventory glut across chips, turbines, cooling and electrical equipment. Has
the industry overordered against an energisation schedule it can no longer deliver? Does that ultimately
undermine semiconductor capacity expansion at ASML and TSMC? The risk is an inventory cycle, not
necessarily demand destruction.
Compute Owners: The flipside is that existing energised compute becomes more valuable. Hyperscalers
potentially enjoy better economics on scarce capacity, while constrained expansion implies less CapEx
and higher FCF. Amazon rallied aggressively despite last week’s FCF concerns. Scarcity is good for those
who own capacity, potentially disastrous for those whose valuations depend on building it.
Energy: The physical market has effectively collapsed with physical Brent trading at a $2.87 discount to
Dated Brent yesterday, reportedly record extremes. This was >$20/bl just 3 weeks ago. November CFDs
and the front three weeks of Dated Brent in contango. Prompt Brent fell ~$8.50 across the session and
after-hours to ~$94. Oil pricing as if the war is over...some arguing fair value below $70. Physical scarcity

disappearing well ahead of any confirmed resolution. Hugely supportive for rates, consumer and
everything the market hasn’t owned. A little worried locally we are a bit ahead of ourselves, are we sure
there wont be any weekend escalation?
Gold: Lots of questions about gold and why the rip. My take is that gold has become an inverse oil trade.
Central banks, particularly EM, have spent years accumulating gold as the new defacto reserve currency.
Higher energy bills forced reserve holders to tap that piggy bank or at minimum stop buying. As oil
reverses, funding pressure eases and gold catches a bid. Not proven, but arguably more compelling for
recent price action than real rates alone. Long gold potentially a more defensible de-escalation trade than
short oil, given how much premium has already come out.
Risk: So where does that leave me? The market reversed when Trump signaled willingness to work with
Democrats. The blue sweep is unequivocal, but there are silver linings. Betting markets shifting towards
more moderate 2028 candidates, while a sizeable Democratic majority potentially reduces dependence on
the fringe. The optimistic interpretation is a pivot towards the middle… tougher data center regulation, but
not a binary moratorium. Surely you don’t sacrifice jobs, growth and investment at the altar of regulation.
A lot of bad news may already be reflected in prices (of picks and shovels). Multiples compressed, nets
low, selling extreme. But respecting the flows...will take time to digest. More obvious optimism in
transports, aerospace and consumer as oil falls. I actually think it’s healthy that equities can rally without
AI infrastructure. We’re compartmentalizing the concerns, not seeing wholesale liquidation. Breadth is
improving even as the most crowded growth trade unwinds. My gut is that nets are too low for the broader
market to go down easily. Vol compression, stable rates and lower oil make for a constructive grind higher.
The optimistic pathway… energy normalizes, AI CapEx gets rationalized rather than strangled, multiples
have reflected the damage and capital finds alternative jurisdictions and power solutions. Plenty of
catalysts into year end, but from a macro perspective, if we can check off the midterms and Middle East,
it’s still a supportive environment for equities… even if AI sits in the backseat.
