Commodity Summary: China absorbing macro copper selling; think physical strength will stop out AI
CapEx relates shorts. Gold rallying on CTA covering and (modest) Chinese buying; we’ve not seen

Copper: Modest spec short-covering on Shanghai overnight as China continues to bid for units; social +
bonded inventory -4k vs Monday to -69% y/y, as spot premium (customs cleared) rallied to ¥2,100/mt
supporting cash ARB, whilst Nov / Dec SHFE at ¥1,490b is helping open Nov26 ARB. What’s perhaps
more interesting (as Chinese physical tightness is not new) is that the US export ARB is opening from
Long Beach to China due to CIF premium strength (last $200/mt) and negative prompt CME arb. The bull
thesis for copper this year has largely been predicated on the US absorbing the global surplus, we don’t
think an open Long Beach à Shanghai export arb ends this trade because i) think there is only ~20k MT in
Long Beach, the majority of the metal is in New Orleans which has a closed export arb, ii) the metal is
only leaving the US because of how tight China is, and iii) the administration will likely take a dim view
of US exports if they increase, but disconcerting nonetheless. Franchise flow remains low; get the
impression macros shorted it on rates / AI capex but reluctant to add given China physical. Vol still bid
from post LME put buying. Risky bid to puts which we think is wrong basis China physical; looking to
sell that.

Gold: Macro take from Privo “My take is that gold has become an inverse oil trade. Central banks,
particularly EM, have spent years accumulating gold as the new de-facto reserve currency. Higher energy
bills forced reserve holders to tap that piggy bank or at minimum stop buying. As oil reverses, funding
pressure eases and gold catches a bid. Not proven, but arguably more compelling for recent price action
than real rates alone. Long gold potentially a more defensible de-escalation trade than short oil, given how
much premium has already come out.” Micro take from commods: China has covered shorts on SHFE and
is starting to add modest length but we are not seeing re-engagement from our franchise. Twice this year
we’ve seen significant (re) allocation through vol, think option length got to ~50% of Jan27 highs (we got
too long also, adding a bunch of convexity into FOMC etc) both times price gave up due to rates / oil; hard
to add meaningful risk In Nov when the street has drawn twice this year. Our CTA model is close to max
short – pain trade likely higher. Side-note: GLD + iShares ETF local VWAP ~$4,400

Copers Labour risk - Chile Copper: Labour Negotiations Add Another Supply Risk Into 2027 negotiations
are most relevant for BHP, RIO, LUN and ANTO: Based on the scale of their operations and the unions
involved, BHP has the greatest exposure through Escondida and Spence (GSe ~75% of copper
production), alongside RIO through Escondida (~35%), LUN at Caserones (~40%), and ANTO through
Los Pelambres and Centinela (~55% and ~30%). Escondida and Caserones have a history of industrial
action, while the recent strike at Centinela provides a relevant precedent for ANTO, although the
upcoming Los Pelambres negotiations are with a smaller union. GLEN and AAL have more limited
exposure through Lomas Bayas and El Soldado, respectively, with neither operation having a history of
industrial action. Worth Noting TECK doesnt have any upcoming.
