 
AI/Tech Wrecks As Crude Carnage & Macro Malaise 
Sparks Bond Bid, Gold Gains 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
TUESDAY, NOV 03, 2026 - 10:00 AM 
Tl;dr: Disappointing macro combined with a collapse in crude prices (biggest drop in 5 months) 
provided support for bonds (offset a little by AI supply fears - ORCL/META - which smacked 
credit spreads wider). Relief rally hopes faded fast in stocks with tech leading both ways (NDX 
back at Friday's lows/200DMA, DOW outperformed). Lower yields and rate-hike expectations 
dragged the dollar lower, pushing gold back up towards $4000. Bitcoin gave up the weekend's 
gainsas tech tumbled ahead of AAPL & Midterms. 
 
Let's start with the quiet part first - some uglier than expected macro data... 
Macro 
Not much macro today but what there was disappointed bigly... 
 

 
 
US Manufacturing survey data was mixed this morning with ISM up (prices down), S&P Global 
down (prices up) - so take your pick (but both missed expectations). The market largely 
shrugged it off.  
However, construction spending was a real dud, crashing 1.9% MoM - its biggest drop since 
COVID - driven by a 13.0% drop in home improvements (data centers remaining a strong 
performer, with spending up 8.6%). 
But it was crude prices that really mattered today... 
Energy 
Oil prices crashed over 7% today - the biggest daily decline since May 20th (the day Trump said 
negotiations with Iran were in the “final stages") - with WTI smashing back below $100 near 
three-week lows... 
 

 
 
Brent also fell around 8% on the day, back to $104... 
 
The plunge came after Iran’s supreme leader approved government pursuit of negotiations, 
state-run IRNA reported. The development compounded bearish momentum that followed 
reports that President Trump told Israeli PM Netanyahu he wanted to let talks with Iran play out. 
Then around midday, oil legged lower on a reported breakthrough in US-Iranian negotiations, 
reported by  Al Jadeed citing a diplomatic source, after Qatari mediation successfully overcame 
procedural obstacles. 
 

 
It wasn't all optimistic, however, as UK Maritime Trade Operations, a unit of the country’s navy, 
said it received a report that a ship had been struck in Hormuz. UKMTO also said a vessel was 
hit east of Ras Al Khafji, Saudi Arabia, deep inside the gulf. 
Beyond the Middle East, Kazakhstan temporarily halted crude flows toward Russia via the 
Atyrau-Samara pipeline after a drone strike. The link is one of the main routes for Kazakh oil 
exports, shipping roughly 7.5 million tons, equivalent to just over 200,000 barrels a day, in the 
first nine months of this year.  
Trump - of course - had to say something (in the last hour of trading):

●​ *TRUMP ON IRAN: IT'S COMING TOGETHER NICELY, THAT'S WHY OIL DOWN

TACO came a day early... or two months too late!? His seeming optimism is too late for pump 
prices and we suspect too soon for deal hopes. 
Nevertheless, as Bloomberg reports, the massive drop reverberated across energy markets, 
with benchmark European gas futures falling almost 11%, the most since July 27, while heating 
oil and gasoline contracts also declined.  
“A lot of risk premium is seeping out of both crude oil and European natural gas,” said Darrell 
Fletcher, managing director for commodities at Bannockburn Capital Markets.  
“Ironically, it’s also a day before midterms.”  
 
The squeeze on Persian Gulf supplies, along with escalating Ukrainian attacks on Russian 
refineries, have pushed prices for diesel and other fuels higher in recent months, but today saw 
a serious decline (near one month lows) for both EU and US diesel prices... 
 

 
 
Political pressure on Trump to end the war has intensified but any relief from Monday’s drop will 
come too late to assuage Americans crushed by elevated pump prices. 
Rates 
Lower oil and weaker macro were the double whammy (in a good way) for bonds today with 
yields down across the curve, led by the long-end as the equity cash open prompted a bid 
across the curve... 
 
The 10Y extended its push below 5.00% back at its lowest in almost two months... 
 

 
 
Somewhat interestingly, there was little to no change in STIRs (Dec odd and Jan odds flat) but 
we did see 2027 rate-change expectations slip (dovishly) by a few bps... 
 
Short-term Treasury yields falling less than longer-term ones “is likely a consequence of the 
sharp post-FOMC steepening move” following last week’s decision to leave rates unchanged, 
said Dhiraj Narula, US rates strategist at HSBC’s US investment bank.  
The move also reflects “questions around potential long-end Treasury supply cuts” that US 
officials may answer at Wednesday’s quarterly discussion of their borrowing strategy. 
The quarterly refunding announcement will be "unusually interesting," Antti Ilvonen, senior FX 
and fixed income analyst at Danske Bank, said in a note. 
 

 
"Instead of the issuance amounts themselves, markets are focusing on forward guidance for the 
expected maturity composition, and the toolbox for influencing it," he said. 
"The Treasury could announce decreasing long-end issuance, increasing long-end buybacks, or 
a combination of both." 
In August, the Treasury announced an increase in long-dated bond buyback auction volumes. 
“The historical negative correlation between bonds and equities seems to have returned over 
the past month, and the gains today could be related to investors looking to protect further 
downside in equities,” said Steven Zeng, an interest-rate strategist at Deutsche Bank.  
Stocks & Credit 
The dead-cat bounce in big-tech died an ugly and fast death today - from 1.2% gains around the 
cash open, to almost 1.5 % losses, taking out Friday's lows... 
 
That smashed QQQ down to their 200DMA... 
 

 
 
And left NDX in correction (down over 10% from its highs)... 
 
We note that today's top (around the US cash equity open) was in line with levels from Friday's 
cash equity open highs too. We note that DOW outperformed on the day (Value/Growth) but 
late-day selling pressure pulled RTY red and wiped any rotational lipstick off SPY... 
 

 
 
Energy and Tech stocks led the sector weakness with Discretionary and Financials interestingly 
outperforming... 
 
It wasn't really a rotational day as both Mag7 and the SPX 493 fell in line with each other... 
 

 
 
The massive outperformance of SPX Ex-AI continued today with the broad AI basket down 
around 3% while SPX Ex-AI rose 1%... 
 
And its the NeoClouds and Optical Networking stocks were mullered most (down over 6%)... 
 

 
 
Memory/Chip names were ugly erasing all of last week's hope-filled bounce... 
 
And Momentum puked to its lowest close since September... 
 

 
 
And in partnership with stocks slump, credit markets cracked even wider with Data-Center CDS 
surging back near record wides... 
 
Hyperscaler credit spreads hit a new record wide while IG spreads are really blowing out as 
META/ORCL new issues hit the tape... 
 

 
 
It was ugly... and we're not sure anything AAPL can do tonight (or PLTR) will rescue it for now 
as the giant black cloud of AI funding fears hovers over the whole market - rotation won't be 
enough. 
Everything Else 
Lower yields and dovish shift in 2027 Fed expectations pulled the dollar lower, holding at the low 
end of the one-month range... 
 
The dollar's loss was Gold's gain bu the precious metal was unable to get back to $4,000 after 
Friday's liquidation-y moves.. 
 

 
 
After a solid recovery weekend from the Binance Hack battering, today was bitcoin give it all 
back... 
 
Finally, as a reminder (and as we noted here and here), the SPX has been up a year after every 
midterm election since 1950 with an average gain of around 15%. The worst one-year forward 
return came after the 1986 midterms (but, as JPM Intel noted, this, of course, had more to do 
with the 1987 crash rather than anything going on in Washington). 
 

 
 
We suspect that at least part of the reason for this tendency is that the midterms tend to see the 
incumbent party loses seats and gridlock in Washington tends to be good for Wall Street... of 
course a Dem sweep may negate the gridlock argument, despite Trump's promise to become 
'Mr.Veto.' 
 
And the higher the odds of a Dem sweep... the lower the price of AI stocks go ... 
 

 
 
But then again, we have to get through AAPL earnings first...
