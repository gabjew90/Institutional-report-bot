US Midterms: Implications For AI Data Centers, Fiscal Policy, Fed Appointments
The US goes to the polls on Tuesday in the midterm elections, with the Democratic Party poised to take
control of the House of Representatives and potentially even the Senate, according to the latest polling. UBS
Evidence Lab calculates the implied probability for Democratic control of the House as 97%, compared to 7%
for Republican control of the House. For the Senate, Democrats have an 81% chance of winning compared to
the Republicans with an implied 25%.
US Rates Strategist Phoebe White argues a Democratic sweep of House and Senate would only lead to
modest flattening of the US rates curve, which would likely be short-lived given narrow congressional margins
and the likelihood of gridlock. In the Republican sweep scenario, there could be a significant sell-off in
long-end yields. Phoebe identified at least three separate channels: more pressure on AI data centers to pay
for costs associated with electricity generation and grid updates; wider deficits next year as a split Congress
may not restrain spending; and finally, a Democratic Senate could constrain Fed appointments and preserve a
more independent, potentially less dovish central bank (also discussed in this note from the US Economics
team).
Pricing for the US midterm elections points to the largest one-day breakeven move for the S&P 500 through
the end of the year at just over 1%, according to Connor Lyons in equity sales. Full note here.
James Kanagasooriam of Focaldata identified some key races to watch in the House and Senate for early
signals on the national races. James argued the prevailing view that Democrats have a double-digit lead in
generic polling looks overstatedl he sees the Senate as effectively a toss-up. Here are the 10 races to watch.

split. “The Board will continue to do what it considers necessary to bring inflation sustainably back to target,” its
post-meeting statement said. UBS Economics sees the RBA’s decision to drop the tightening bias, i.e.
'increasing cash rate further, if needed' from the statement and the vote split as clearly dovish, while retaining

ECB Presidency Nominations Due By Friday – Eurogroup president Kyriakos Pierrakakis has urged
Eurozone governments to submit nominations to replace Christine Lagarde as the head of the ECB by close of
business on Friday. Lagarde said last week she would stand down on Feb. 7 next year, nearly nine months
before her official term ends. On Tuesday, Pierrakakis said the group will then discuss the shortlist at a
meeting on Monday, Nov. 9. The only known candidates so far are Pablo Hernandez de Cos of Spain and
Klaas Knot of Netherlands. However, there have been reports Bundesbank president Joachim Nagel and Bank
of France President Emmanuel Moulin are considering whether to stand.  (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
Trump, Vance Tout Iran Talks Just As US Hits Qeshm Island – Oil prices are edging lower as the market
reacts to the potential for new talks between Washington and Tehran, rather than the reality of the US on
Tuesday striking Iranian facilities in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude has dropped 6% so far this week, as
Iranian president Pezeshkian had reportedly been given authority by supreme leader Khamenei to pursue new
talks. It’s unclear whether there has been any change in the standoff. Iran said it cannot consider a reopening
of the Strait until all US sanctions have been lifted; Iran also said offers made by US vice president Vance
were unchanged from past efforts, and therefore it’s not ready for any discussion on its nuclear ambitions,
according to press reports. Iran’s foreign minister Abbas Araghchi said if the US agreed to its Iranian
conditions, then talks could resume. A few hours after Araghchi’s comments (and from Trump that talks were
coming along nicely), the US attacked Iran’s Qeshm Island, which sits within the Strait. The US claimed that
Iran was using the island to launch surface-to-air missiles. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
Trump, Putin And Xi May Meet Soon, FT Reports – The Financial Times reports [paywall] a possible
meeting between Presidents Trump, Putin and Xi at the APEC meeting in a fortnight’s time. FT reports officials
on all three sides were working on whether such a meeting could take place in Shenzen. It said the difficulty is
in putting together a mutally agreeable agenda. An alternative could be a China/Russia and China/US
meeting. Either way around, the signals would be favourable towards President Xi as being the orchestrater
and leading figure. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
US CPI Likely To Show More Moderate Inflation Rise In October – US Economics: Alan Detmeister
projects October’s headline CPI to rise just 0.28%, nearly matching consensus and down notably from
September’s 0.39%—thanks mainly to much lower gasoline price increases (just 0.04pp contribution vs.
0.39pp last month). Core CPI is also set to cool, at 0.23%, led by a sharp 1.5% drop in used car prices. Food
prices climb moderately, while rent and hotel price growth slow. Although 12-month CPI inflation won’t be
published because of missing data due to last year’s federal government shutdown, PCE inflation rates will
remain available and are expected to tick up, with core PCE rising to 3.2%. Alan said there is no clear inflation
risk skew for October. He expects meaningful relief for headline CPI inflation only by next spring, barring major
oil price surges, while core CPI is due to peak early in 2027. UBS has 25bp hikes priced for the FOMC’s
December and January meetings. (Matthew Cowley, UBS Knowledge Network)
Central Banks, Other Institutions' Gold Buying Hits Highest Since Q4 2024 - The World Gold Council
(WGC) on Tuesday reported central banks and other official institutions bought a net 321 tonnes of gold in Q3,
up 42% year-on-year, and the largest quarterly addition since Q4 2024. It said the official sector attributed its
demand to portfolio diversification and risk management. WGC also said physically backed gold ETFs saw a
net inflow of 211 tones in Q3, just 6% below the exceptional demand seen in Q3 2025. The WGC said
although global bar-and-coin demand was down 19% y/y in Q3, demand from China was actually up 7%.
(Simon Penn, Macro Sales)

Swiss October Inflation Print Unlikely To Move SNB In December - The SNB is likely to keep rates on hold
at 0% at its next rates decision on Dec. 10, even after October CPI came in hot. Inflation, as measured by CPI,
rose 0.5pp to 1.5% y/y, well above the Bloomberg consensus estimate of 1.3%. This caused pricing for the
December meeting to tick up near 2bp, however the reading sits comfortably within the SNB's 0–2% target
band, leaving the central bank with limited pressure to adjust policy on inflation grounds alone. March looks
like the next likely move from the SNB, with 15bp of hikes currently priced. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
Apple Slips As Headline Beat Falls Short Of Launch Expectations – Apple (AAPL) shares fell 0.24%
versus 3.9% implied volatility after a headline Q4 beat, with upside in iPhone and Mac offset by softer iPad and
Wearables. The muted reaction suggests the print was a little underwhelming in the context of the iPhone
launch, while softer China sales left forward commentary as the key focus. Q4 revenue came in at $114.48 bn
versus $113.4 bn expected, with EPS of $2.10 versus $1.99. With no Q1 figures in the initial colour versus
expectations for $152.2 bn of revenue and $2.90 of EPS, attention now shifts to guidance, China trends and

South Korea Inflation Eases As Farm Prices Fall – South Korea’s headline CPI eased to 2.8% y/y in
October from 2.9% in September and 3.1% in August, with agricultural, livestock and fishery prices down
1.7%, subtracting 0.14pp from inflation. Meanwhile, petroleum prices rose 14.9%, their fastest increase since
July, adding 0.56pp; diesel climbed 19.7% and gasoline 11.6%. Services rose 2.7%, while dining-out prices
increased 2.9%, contributing 0.42pp. Core CPI, excluding food and energy, stood at 2.8%. The finance
ministry estimated fuel price caps reduced headline inflation by 0.8pp. The data suggest food-price relief and
policy support are cushioning inflation, while energy and service costs remain sources of pressure. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Cross-Asset Correlations Collapse, Reopening Classic Risk-Off Hedge – UBS Global Markets Volatility
Weekly: October saw the largest cross-asset correlation shift since the start of the Iran war. The driver was
US rates with bonds going from being positively correlated to risk (bonds selling off with risk selling off) to
being negatively correlated to risk (bonds rallying with risk selling off). This shift has seen average cross-asset
correlation drop to their lowest level in years with almost every cross-asset correlation down month-on-month.
The shift in cross-asset correlations have not yet been reflected in SPX/Rates or SPX/Yen implied correlation
levels (which remain at similar levels to a month ago). This provides an attractive opportunity to add classic
SPX risk-off hybrid hedges. (Shane Carroll, Structuring Strategist, HKG)
