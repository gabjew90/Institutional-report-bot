 
"Pnl Protection > Pnl Generation": Goldman Warns 
Still-Crowded Mag7 Is The Next "Source Of Funds" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 02, 2026 - 09:00 PM 
October was the month the AI trade finally broke... and the hedge funds that own it broke with it. 
"Another week of extremes to close out month-end," is how Goldman's Ryan Sharkey opens his 
latest weekend wrap,"Extremes" (available to pro subs), and the list that follows reads like a 
stress test nobody signed up for: the Nasdaq 100 suffered its worst month of underperformance 
vs. the equal-weighted S&P since 2002, the SOX broke below its 200-day moving average for 
the first time since June 2025, TMT-focused hedge funds lost 11.9% in October, and Goldman's 
HF VIP vs. VIP Short basket had its third-worst month in 15 years. Meanwhile, the S&P ex-AI 
just posted its best month since the launch of ChatGPT, up 7.64%! 
And according to Sharkey, the unwind is not done yet: 
AI length still being unwound: We net sold -$4.2bn of tech on Friday (most since this episode 
started) and finished the week net sellers, fully reversing a big mid-week buy skew. 
In other words, the dip buyers showed up on Monday and were gone by Friday (and the same 
pattern played out this morning, but fades far faster).  
 
 

 
Which is why Sharkey's most important call is about the one AI cohort that still has not been 
de-risked: the Mag7. 
Re-Risking Lasted About Three Days 
Regular readers know this wasn't supposed to be how the week went. On Wednesday, with 
hedge fund nets at the 0th percentile, we asked whether it was "Always Darkest Before The 
Dawn?", and Goldman's desk was seeing "early signs of offense" in semis and upside vol. Then 
the earnings came. Here's Sharkey's play-by-play: 
LOs were coming back to higher-beta AI early in the week (semis the standout). Things turned 
quickly after a bunch of negative EPS prints (INTC on PC weakness, AMZN on weak FCF & 
higher buyside AWS bogeys, search weakness, and MSFT beat and closed in the red). This 
was extremely deflating for investors who just started to redeploy capital. 
"Extremely deflating" is one way of putting it. Microsoft jumped ~6% after hours on an Azure 
beat (bookings and backlog were another story) and still closed in the red, Google plunged on 
an ugly top and bottom line miss, and on Friday Intel crashed the most on record as Amazon 
slid 7% on surging cash burn: 
Goldman's clients responded accordingly. A +$5bn mid-week buy skew was fully reversed, and 
Friday saw -$4.2bn of net tech selling, the largest single-day tech sell skew in three weeks.

Gross Near Records, Nets Near Lows: Max Frustration 
The Prime Book shows how the pros are (not) positioned going into November. Global equities 
were actually net bought for the first time in a month, but read the fine print: de-grossing 
accelerated for a third straight week, with short covers outpacing long sales 1.8 to 1, and macro 
products (index and ETFs) made up 73% of the net buying. Put differently, the "buying" was 
mostly hedges being covered. 
The leverage numbers say the same: 
●​ Overall book gross leverage: 314.1% (97th percentile on five years) 
●​ Overall book net leverage: 73.6% (10th percentile on one year), with the long/short ratio 
at 1.612, the 2nd percentile on five years 
 

 
●​ Fundamental L/S net leverage: 49.3% (3rd percentile on one year); beta-adjusted net 
leverage at 64.8%, a one-year low 
That's a little better than the 71.8% overall net and 47.0% fundamental net Goldman reported a 
week earlier, but only just. Huge gross, tiny nets: the textbook setup for a "unplayable" tape 
where anyone carrying gross loses money. And they did: 
●​ Global Fundamental L/S: -85bps on Friday (-1.3 SD), -3.3% MTD, still +9.3% YTD 
●​ TMT-focused funds: -3.1% on Friday (a -2.2 SD move), -11.9% MTD and down to +5.7% 
YTD 
●​ Global Systematic L/S: -3.7% MTD, their first negative month of the year, as momentum, 
crowding and short-term signals all hurt

Pnl protection > pnl generation right now will limit offense being played in the near term (buyers

The Last Crowded Long Standing 
Which brings us to the punchline of the note. While everything AI-adjacent has been liquidated, 
one group has barely been touched. Per Sharkey: 
Positioning in Mag7 has been extremely resilient despite a volatile TMT tape. It quickly went 
from under owned in June to near the YTD highs in exposure levels now. This group should 
continue to act as a source of funds limiting any near-term dip buying demand. 
The chart is striking: Mag7 net exposure as a share of total US Prime Book exposure has 
rocketed from about 14% in June to roughly 22%, right at the top of its five-year range, with 
gross exposure close behind (chart source GS PB as of 10/28): 
 

 
 
And with the funds still this long, the selling has already started: Mag7 names were net sold in 4 
of the last 5 sessions, which Goldman reads as "little recovery in risk appetite amid a busy week 
of earnings and a volatile tech tape." So the semis and neoclouds were the first to go; the Mag7 
is what funds sell next to cover everything else. 
Sharkey's one wildcard is retail: "More vol could mean more engagement in these names, and 
that could offset some of the weakness." Maybe. But betting on retail to absorb hedge fund 
supply is a tough sell after Meta, SpaceX and now Alphabet all failed the "reaction to the 
reaction" test this earnings season. 
Peak Growth... And A Bid For Toothpaste 
It's not like the earnings are bad. Quoting Goldman's portfolio strategists ([MARKETDESK 
LINK]), Sharkey writes that "earnings growth is strong but no longer accelerating": 
 

 
EPS growth is on track to register 33% year/year in Q3 2026. This would match Q2 as the 
strongest quarter in recent decades outside of post-recession recoveries. However, it would also 
mark an end to the pattern of acceleration that has characterized the last few quarters. 
Note the gap in the chart below: aggregate EPS growth is 33%, but the median stock is growing 
just 12%, down from 14% in Q2. That is the AI concentration problem in one bar (chart source 
FactSet, GS GIR): 
 
Worse, the beats are shrinking. Twelve trading days into the season, the cumulative Q3 EPS 
surprise is running at roughly 2pp, about half the pace of Q2 and far behind Q1, and barely 
keeping up with the average quarter since 2005 (values approximate): 
 

 
 
Pasquariello made the same point on Saturday: "it increasingly appears that earnings GROWTH 
has peaked," and "the rate of upgrades to forward estimates has clearly slowed" (more on his 
debate with Goldman's Mark Wilson in "$11 Trillion Cloud Backlog Vs. Peak Earnings Growth"). 
Which is all you need to know about where the money went. Per Sharkey, Berkshire, 
Coca-Cola, Philip Morris, AbbVie and Apple ("acting defensive") all hit all-time highs in the past 
two weeks, while the Nasdaq 100 slid roughly 7% (chart source Bloomberg, colors inverted): 
 

 
 
Apple as a defensive and Coke at a record... as BofA reminded us last week, the last time 
defensives got this frothy was right at the peak of the dotcom bubble. Nothing says "healthy 
rotation" like a 1999 analogue on the safe side of the trade. 
As for next week's midterms, Sharkey flags Goldman's three trades: long ACA Beneficiaries

We broke these down (along with the veto problem Goldman only half-mentions) in this

Buyers Live Higher, Sellers Own The Mag7 
So, tally it up. TMT funds: -11.9% in October. NDX vs. equal-weight: worst month since 2002. 
SOX: below the 200dma after 355 sessions. AI vs. AI at risk: second-worst month since 
ChatGPT. Systematic funds: first losing month of the year. And the S&P? Down 0.93% for the 
month, per Pasquariello. 
The bulls have a case, and Pasquariello makes it well: sentiment is "unequivocally downbeat," 
the seasonal swing into year-end is underway, and the market "has spent the past month 
clearing a very significant amount of length." But as he concedes, "the bears could argue that 
the same was said into late 2018." And Sharkey's data show the clearing is incomplete: the 
semis, neoclouds and TMT funds have been flushed, while the Mag7, the biggest weight in the 
index, sits at five-year-high exposure with funds already selling it to fund losses elsewhere. 
 

 
We'd side with Sharkey over Santa for now. With PMs in pnl-protection mode and the AI 
buildout increasingly running on borrowed money (something we have been warning about 
since last October), the path of least resistance for the Mag7 is lower until that crowded length is 
cleaned up, and the cap-weighted index can't really rally without them. The ex-AI rotation can 
keep working, of course... it just can't carry the S&P on its own. 
Then again, buyers live higher. We'll find out how much higher after Tuesday's vote.
