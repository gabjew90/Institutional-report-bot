Trading Desk commentary, not a product of UBS Research. For Institutional Clients only
 #Markets : EStoxx +15bps, Crude -1.3% overnight and Brent now 12% off the WTD highs - a range of both
Democratic and Republican speakers vocalizing the impact that the war in Iran had on the Midterm Elections -
Trump, Vance and Schumer's commentary most impactful in markets repricing the probability of an end to the
conflict - worth noting that Polymarket odds in the immediate future have seen little repricing, the end of March
2027 is the most visible shift - lifting of the US Naval blockade at 71% (from 64% prior). Implying the election
was more of a clearing event (VIX -4.5vol yesterday) with lower expectations for material escalation and
effecting a release valve for risk assets, particularly given the context of the exhaustion of technical selling
onto the event (sent in previous notes - pls ask for colour). No real change in sector rotation in the US post the
European close - Consumer Discretionary vs Energy +11.2% as a function of Oil markets. AI
Semis/Power/Winners -3-6%, Neoclouds -8.4% given the risk for an increase in AI regulation/scrutiny. That
unwind is extending into Asia – Kospi -6%, TAIEX -3%, with clear evidence of broad-based de-grossing across
the APAC AI supply chain. Nasdaq futures -1.4% (S&P -0.3%), not helped by Qualcomm (-7.2% aft-hrs) after
guiding Dec quarter EPS below cons on a broad-based rise in costs, reads negative for handset OEMs
(MediaTek, AAC). China remains the beneficiary of rotation post-midterms – HSTECH +5% and H shares +3%
following KWEB +4.5% in the US session. Cross-asset reinforces the de-escalation read: UST 10yr -5bps to
4.85%, the dollar weaker against all G10 majors (yen back below 158) and gold near $4,110 on lower yields
and a softer dollar. Think US markets continue to underperform on a relative basis provided the change in
market assumptions on the US/Iran prove correct.
#BoE : UBS expects the MPC to hike Bank Rate by 25bp to 4.0% today, in line with market pricing, with the
vote flipping from 7-2 for a hold in September to 7-2 in favour of a hike. Dhingra and Taylor are expected to
favour no change, although UBS sees some risk of a more hawkish 8-1 split.
#Flow: Flows on Wednesday finished balanced (52/48), with participation running at circa 2x the HT YTD
average, as activity remained elevated while markets digested the US midterms. Industrials were the busiest
sector (26% of turnover) and finished better for sale, with supply in electricals, while transport saw some
demand. Banks were also busy (15% of turnover), with broad-based demand across UK, Italian and Spanish
names, as both HFs (97/3) and LOs (61/39) finished better bid. Despite broader sector weakness, Tech (15%
of turnover) finished better to buy, driven by demand in semis. Healthcare saw demand in pharma, while
Energy saw supply as crude faded sharply. Elsewhere, Consumer Staples saw supply across Food & Bev and
Personal Care, while Discretionary saw demand in luxury.
