Notes from the road: waiting for the clearing event 
The concept of “clearing events” - pivotal moments that provide clarity, usually 
identified in retrospect – dominate our recent meetings- “Reactions to the next IPO will 
tell us what we need to know about AI/Tech,” “Get past the midterms before deploying 
new capital,” “Mega cap Tech Q3 results will provide necessary clarity for 2027.” But 
this notion is flawed, in our view as investors never get an all-clear. E.g., the big IPO this 
year shed little light, moving up, down and sideways as lock-up dates came and went; Q3 
earnings have seen minimal reactions; directional moves after recent policy surprises 
haven’t always aligned with expectations. We highlight themes for implementation in the 
here and now around scarcity, mispricing, style shifts and macro risks. 
AI: Spenders, takers, adopters, disruption? All of the above
2026’s roving themes-big capex surprises, eyewatering capital raises, software 
disruption, neocloud credit concerns etc. have investors looking for a singular focus area. 
But change is the constant: semis are spending, hyperscalers are vertically integrating, 
enemies are now bedfellows and even disruptees like IT Services are seeing an uptick in 
demand for AI experts. AI capex and revenue surprises are hard to predict, as are 
changes in capital costs and sources. See AI-focused screens on monetization vs. spend, 
earnings quality risk, and adopters based on real sales to labor change and others.  
Scarcity: AI, inflation protection, diversification 
AI uncertainty has investors looking for safe havens: growth with nothing to do with AI 
(Exhibit 3). Inflation protected income (Exhibit 4) is scarce: cash real yields are negative, 
bonds offer fixed return. Diversification is getting - gold moved into positive co-
movement with stocks since March, and historically, bonds and stocks have grown 
positively correlated when 10y Tsy yields clear 6%. Surprisingly, some of the best 
diversification trades against the S&P 500 sit in the S&P 500: Top 50 by dividend yield 
(Exhibit 8), negative beta equities (Exhibit 7) the number of which sits at record highs. 
Macro (rates, oil, USD) & micro (value vs. value traps) 
Rates, geopolitics and the USD are top of mind - see macro beta screens and long 
duration stock screens for companies hurt or helped by these components. A value tilt 
has been working Value is up +7% MTD vs Growth and Momentum down -2% and -5%. 
FCF/EV has led as it incorporates capex and leverage. But if profits slow as we expect, 
GARP tends to win: our Opportunities screen looks for good value - inexpensive stocks 
with strong growth and price confirmation whereas Value Traps highlight “cheap for a 
reason” stocks. Quintile 2 is our all-weather strategy for income investors. 
Acknowledging the calendar: TLCs and earnings 
Of the calendar-based strategies that could drive performance, the Oct. 31 cutoff is 
behind us and tax loss candidates could bounce. We also include potential movers on Q3 
earnings announcements for co’s that have not yet reported.

Long Duration stocks 
Equity duration measures how far into the future a company’s value is concentrated, 
providing a useful lens on interest-rate sensitivity. Long-duration companies derive a 
greater share of their value from cash flows expected further into the future, making 
those cash flows more sensitive to changes in the discount rate. Conversely, short-
duration companies derive more of their value from cash flows generated sooner, giving 
them a more bond-like, higher-coupon profile. Our Equity Duration framework adapts the 
Dividend Discount Model (see 2026 Quant Primer) to estimate this interest-rate 
sensitivity: higher duration implies greater sensitivity to changes in rates.

business, providing a capital structure-neutral lens on valuation. That discipline is 
particularly important today, as elevated CapEx requirements are weighing on free cash 
flow across parts of the market: companies that can fund investment internally while still 
producing strong FCF have greater financial flexibility and less dependence on increasingly 
costly external capital.

Screening for above-market, not stretched, dividends 
If we are returning to a total return world in which the contribution of dividends to total 
market returns could be higher than during the zero interest rates period, we advise 
investors to seek out companies with above-market but not stretched dividend yields. 
Quintile 2 of the Russell 1000 by trailing dividend yield has been superior as a “first-
pass” screen to other quintiles, as it is the second-highest tranche of dividend yielders. 
These are less likely to be distressed companies that migrate into Quintile 1 (highest 
yield group) if prices fall ahead of potential dividend cuts. Quintile 2 also incorporates a 
“buy low, sell high” valuation discipline: if prices rise faster than dividends grow, 
companies should migrate into Quintile 3. 
Methodology: Each month, we calculate the trailing 12-month dividend yield for each 
company in the Russell 1000 by dividing 12-month dividend per share by the previous 
month-end stock price. We then rank all dividend-paying Russell 1000 stocks by this 
dividend yield. The screen published here, Quintile 2, represents stocks within the 
second-highest quintile of companies that pay a dividend. The stocks in the Quintile 2 
screen do not constitute a recommended list and investors should consider the 
fundamentals of the companies before investing.
