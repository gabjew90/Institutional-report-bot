The momentum massacre
The worst momentum crash in four decades. Two 6-sigma shocks. A 30% drawdown. Hedge funds have
been forced into brutal liquidation, but JPM argues the worst of the de-risking may now be behind us.
Positioning has reset dramatically, potentially setting the stage for a powerful year-end squeeze. The
catch? Retail is starting to blink just as the market needs stability before hedge funds can return.
Disaster
October was a historic disaster for momentum. JPM highlights that the factor suffered its worst monthly
underperformance in more than 40 years, including two 6-sigma unwind days in roughly one week.
Cap-weighted momentum underperformed the market by 10.8%, a staggering -5.8 sigma event. This
was no ordinary rotation. The great momentum trade was being liquidated.

The 6-sigma massacre
Two 6-sigma shocks in roughly one week. JPM highlights that these rank among the most extreme daily
momentum events in recent history. Such moves point to a disorderly positioning unwind rather than
an ordinary market rotation. When crowded trades unwind this violently, price action can become
disconnected from underlying fundamentals.
JPM
The 30% destruction
The pain goes well beyond October. JPM highlights that long/short momentum lost an extraordinary
16% during the month, as both sides of the trade moved against investors. Combining the July and
October unwinds, the cumulative momentum drawdown has reached 30%, the largest since ChatGPT
launched. An entire year of momentum outperformance has been wiped out.

Hedge funds get slaughtered
The momentum massacre has spilled into the hedge-fund universe. JPM estimates that Equity
Long/Short funds lost around 3% in October, Multi-Strats around 3% and Equity Quant funds as much
as 4–5%. Asia-focused Quant funds suffered losses of 7–8%. For Multi-Strats, the damage ranks among
the worst in history, comparable to episodes during the dot-com collapse, Lehman crisis and COVID
crash. JPM argues that forced liquidation of crowded AI exposures may have been even more severe
than in July.
The positioning reset
Here is where things get interesting. JPM argues that the October hedge-fund liquidation has created a
considerably more favourable positioning backdrop for equities, particularly AI stocks. Meanwhile,
momentum crowding has fallen from the 100th to the 86th percentile over its 40-year history, with
positioning down to just the 12th percentile versus the past three years. The positioning reset has been
dramatic, even if longer-term crowding remains elevated. After the brutal washout, the next surprise
could increasingly come from the upside.
JPM
Who buys next?
The forced selling may be largely behind us, but JPM doesn't expect hedge funds to rush back into AI
immediately. The bank sees a few weeks of stability, supported by retail buying, as the potential
catalyst for renewed institutional exposure from late November onwards. The complication is that
retail is losing momentum too. Weekly retail flows have fallen to just $2.5bn versus a 12-month
average of $6.5bn, with single-stock outflows of $2.3bn. The setup is intriguing: institutional positioning
has been violently reset, but the next squeeze may depend on whether retail can hold the line until
hedge funds return.
