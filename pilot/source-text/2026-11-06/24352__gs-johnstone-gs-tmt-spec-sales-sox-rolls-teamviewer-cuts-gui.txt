TECH
• Americas Technology: Hardware: Results Preview - We highlight key themes and expectations heading
in C3Q26 earnings for our US hardware coverage, including DELL, HPE, NTAP, and HPQ. Over the last
two months post C2Q earnings, our investor conversations with hardware investors have been focused
around two key themes: First on durability of enterprise IT spend into C2027, more bullish investors see a
structural uplift in demand for traditional compute, storage, and data canter/campus networking related to
agentic AI and the ongoing broader infrastructure refresh, which should be a durable, multi-year demand
driver despite elevated ASPs (i.e., enterprise IT budgets earmarked for data centre infrastructure should
grow). Investors who are more bearish on this theme attribute the strength in C2026 industry revenue to
memory cost inflation & a pull-forward of demand, and view hardware OEMs as currently over-earning
on server/storage, with downside to margins if memory pricing trends slow or reverse. Second on
heightened investor concern about the sustainability of neocloud demand, investors are looking to
understand both (a) the durability of neoclouds as end customers for hardware OEMs; and (b) the risk of
neoclouds transitioning to cloud-like procurement models (i.e., white-box) for compute, networking, and
storage needs. More constructive investors point to the differentiated design, supply-chain management,
manufacturing, integration, and deployment capabilities of US server/networking OEMs (v. ODM
alternatives) as a competitive moat that enables OEMs to bring AI clusters online more quickly (justifying
their premium pricing). In this note, we dive deeper into the (a) key investor debates for each stock going
into C3Q26 earnings; (b) our view on key metrics and estimates; and (c) highlight an emerging theme
impacting each company that we believe is underappreciated by the market. We reiterate our Buy ratings
on enterprise data centre infrastructure exposed names DELL, HPE, and NTAP while remaining Sell rated
on PC/printer OEM HPQ.
• Nvidia has instructed data-centre clients to reduce their exposure to China, according to the FT, rather
than a company statement. The move builds on tighter customer checks in Singapore, Malaysia and Japan
aimed at preventing chips from being diverted to China, increasing compliance requirements for Asian
neoclouds, data-centre operators and other buyers subject to export controls. It could further restrict
China-linked demand, support domestic chip suppliers such as Huawei and reinforce Nvidia’s shift in
foundry capacity at TSMC towards advanced chips for non-China markets. The near-term revenue impact
is likely to be limited given Nvidia’s already minimal China data-centre exposure, but the report adds
headline risk.

TELCOS
• DT: Getting investor questions on US wireless and whether Starlink is looking at Boost Mobile as a route
into the US MVNO market. This may be possible on paper, but the original agreements suggest significant
hurdles: carrier consent and change-of-control provisions matter, while T-Mobile’s access commitment is
cited as running only to January 2027. A straightforward deal therefore looks unlikely. In my view, the
Washington Post article also overlooks the broader investor speculation around acquiring EchoStar,
including its DISH Wireless assets and spectrum. One potential attraction of SpaceX buying EchoStar is
EchoStar’s stake in SpaceX, equivalent to roughly 2% of SpaceX’s equity. The argument is that SpaceX
could effectively buy back its own shares at a discount while acquiring additional spectrum. Whether
DISH Wireless’s abandoned radios and antennas retain value is another point of debate.
{ The Washington Post report that Musk is considering buying or using Boost puts its network-access
rights in focus, but these should not be assumed to transfer automatically. Following the Sprint/T-Mobile
merger, Boost secured DOJ-mandated T-Mobile wholesale access, cited in the notes as running to 21
January 2027. A 2022 amendment improved pricing, roaming and data handling. Separately, DISH’s ten-
year AT&T agreement, signed in July 2021, made AT&T its primary network provider, with minimum
subscriber and roaming-usage commitments and at least $5bn in payments over the term, subject to
contractual conditions. The non-exclusive deal provides nationwide voice, data, messaging and roaming
access, including in DISH’s own network markets, but restricts resale to third parties. Crucially, AT&T can
terminate following a qualifying change of control involving specified wireless providers, cable
companies or large technology companies. MVNO access can nevertheless continue for up to two years,
with MNO roaming also available during that period; whether a Musk/Starlink transaction would trigger
these provisions requires confirmation. Overall, the reported interest looks more like a potential MVNO
route to supplement Starlink’s coverage and capacity than an independent network build. However,
operators have so far shown little interest in an MVNO agreement with Starlink.

• TEF: BBVA is moving closer to exiting Telefónica after 30 years as a shareholder, according to
Vozpopuli. The bank has cut its stake from 5% to 1.96% and is using derivatives to unwind the remainder
while limiting an equity impact that has exceeded €900m. Falling below the significant-shareholder
threshold means further incremental block sales no longer require mandatory CNMV disclosures. The
accelerated exit follows a reported understanding with the government after BBVA’s failed bid for Banco
Sabadell, the state’s investment in Telefónica through SEPI, the departure of BBVA’s board representative
and Telefónica’s decision to halve its dividend. Commercial ties have also weakened: Telefónica-related
revenues fell 82% after Telefónica sold its remaining BBVA stake earlier this year, although joint ventures
including Adquira, in which each holds 44.44%, and Telefónica Factoring remain in place.
