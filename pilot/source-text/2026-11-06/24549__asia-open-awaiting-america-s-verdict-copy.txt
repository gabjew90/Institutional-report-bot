Asia Open - Awaiting America's verdict
By Preston Pagli
It's election day in the US, with all 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats up for grabs as investors await what is likely to
be the most important political event ahead of the 2028 presidential cycle. While Democrats are widely expected to
regain control of the House, market attention is firmly on the Senate. Citi Velocity will be providing live coverage and
insights as results come in. See the US Midterms Content Feed.
Participation was light across US markets today with the NDX (+68bps) posting a slight rebound driven by semis on a
positive round of earnings reports. In rates, global yields drifted higher with USTs +3-5bps across the curve on a boost in
job openings with focus turning to Wednesday's quarterly refunding announcement at 08:30 EST. In FX, the dollar firmed
against most, with the yen the weakest performer across G10. LatAm currencies lagged on their re-opening with MXN,
COP, and BRL all more than 1% weaker. Commodities were quieter with crude mostly unchanged in the NY session with
diplomatic efforts offsetting "defensive" US strikes on Qeshm island overnight. Looking ahead, data will remain
secondary as US election results roll in throughout the night with NZD unemployment at 21:45 GMT and RatingDog
China PMI at 01:45 GMT.

In Focus‌
Global: It it election day in the US with 435 House and 35 Senate seats up for grabs. The Democrats are widely expected to
retake the House, putting key Senate seats across Iowa, Alaska, Maine, Michigan, Texas, Ohio and more in focus. Citi
Velocity will be providing live coverage and insights throughout the week. See the US Midterms Content Feed.
Rates: Duration sold off globally ahead of the US midterms and noisy geopolitical backdrop. UST yields drifted modestly
higher across the curve despite oil's sharp move lower holding. JOLTS job openings came in well above estimates, in large
part due to the manufacturing sector, where activity has been supported by AI-investment and tax incentives that our
economists see fading in 2027. From a positioning standpoint, Citi's David Bieber has seen new longs in duration in the US
vs. mixed flows in EGBs. The backdrop remains structurally short, with larger moves required by systematics to flip long,
while long end flattener positions remain vulnerable. 

Equities: Headline equity indices traded higher thanks to a narrow rally in tech. The SOX gained +2.6% following
constructive earnings results from ETN, FN, LSCC, and ON, with more to come this week. On the cash equity desk, flows led
in Cons. Discretionary, Comm Services, and Utilities while there were continued outflows in Info Tech, Financials, Healthcare,
Industrials, Energy, Consumer Staples, Materials and REITs. Following last week's sharp sell-off CTAs were signaled to flip
short SPX. CTAs are estimated to need to sell $15bn of unlevered notional in SPX e-minis throughout this week in order to
move into their new short position. With the first midterm results approaching, our equity trading strategy desk is watching key
Governor's races in TX, OH, and GA that have large data center exposure. In credit, IG outperformed HY, with LQD roughly
4.5bps tigher. IG cash was bid while CDX IG saw HF short covering as accounts shored up psoitoing ahead of the midterms. 
FX: The dollar firmed on a rebound in the AI trade and strong USD jobs data. Yen was the biggest G10 underperformer with
USDJPY +0.49% to 158.723. Our G10 options trading desk has reduced USDJPY vol positions in favor of AUDJPY downside
expressions. Latam FX was the biggest underperformer with BRL -1%, MXN -1.4%, and COP -1.2% all under pressure
following the holiday. Our MXN trader expects the peso to reconnect ahead of Thursday's Banxico meeting. Activity on the
eFX trading desk was mostly higher, with EURNOK volumes more than 150% higher than average while USDZAR, and
USDTWD were also heavily traded. Net flows were led by JPY buying from the bank segment, leveraged accounts bought
NOK and GBP. Outflows led in SEK and DKK.
Commodities: Crude traded modestly lower, extending its decline to over 9%, with the US midterms the main near term
focus when thinking of any new diplomacy efforts between the US and Iran. In precious metals, silver (-1.9%),
underperformed gold (-0.62%) as central banks continue to support bullion.
Elsewhere‌
BRL weakened 1% as uncertainty surrounding Lula's cabinet remains high. USDBRL at 5.50 looks increasingly stretched
ahead of Wednesday's rate decision where a hold is expected. Ibovespa lost an additional ~1.6% and trades 14% lower over
the past month. Last week, corporates were the biggest buyer of BRL, followed by RM and the leveraged segment.  
Ahead:
USD: US Midterm Election. Democrats are widely expected to win the House and are slight favorites to win the Senate, albeit
within the margin of error. First polling stations close from 18:00 ET, with the majority closing between 19:00 - 21:00 ET.
Earliest clues for the House majority could emerge from 19:30-20:00, with Senate outcome potentially later in the night from
22:00. Dem sweep is becoming increasingly priced in, suggesting a benign outcome on a divided outcome. See US Midterms:
A final markets guide for more.
NZD: Unemployment Rate at 21:45 GMT for Q3. Unemployment rate should fall to 5.4% (Citi e.) vs 5.6% prior, with positive
employment change too (0.3% vs 0.4% prior). This should be countered by slightly weaker private sector wages at 0.6% vs
0.7% prior.
SEK: Riksbank Rate Decision at 08:30 GMT. OIS pricing stands at ~17bp, while the Bloomberg economist survey leans
towards a 25bp hike. With core inflation running close to the 2% target, the case for remaining on hold also remains
compelling. We tentatively expect a 25bp rate hike with communication of future moves expected to stay balanced.
CNY: Private data RatingDog China PMI Composite at 01:45 GMT for October should not be market-moving after the national
print.
CZK: CPI MoM at 08:00 GMT for October P. Our economists expect headline inflation to hold steady at 2.5% YoY in October,
unchanged from September, as slower disinflation in food and regulated prices offsets more moderate fuel price growth.
