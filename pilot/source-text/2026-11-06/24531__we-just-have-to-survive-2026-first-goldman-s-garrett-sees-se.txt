 
"We Just Have To Survive 2026 First": Goldman's 
Garrett Sees Seven Signs Of "Healing" As Semis 
Snap A 355-Day Streak 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 02, 2026 - 04:05 AM 
Last weekend, everyone wanted to know which inning the AI unwind was in. 
By Wednesday, the answer seemed obvious. Three straight green sessions added roughly 
150bps to the S&P and 300bps to the Nasdaq 100, and traders happily adopted the "8th inning" 
framework: forced deleveraging behind us, earnings ready to carry everything to the year-end 
rally. Then came Thursday (NDX -140bps) and Friday (NDX -350bps), and as Goldman 
derivatives guru Brian Garrett writes in his latest Weekend Prep note (available to pro subs, the 
two sessions were enough "to force significant portfolio re-underwriting / soul searching." 
The tally: the Nasdaq 100 fell about 7% in October, its second-worst month since the AI cycle 
kicked off in early 2023 according to Goldman's TMT specialist Peter Callahan, and is now in its 
third correction of the year. Semis closed down 7%-8% on Friday (depending on which 
Goldman desk you ask), their third-largest day of underperformance in more than 15 years. And 
the S&P 500? Down 0.6% on the week, to 7,580. 
Garrett, for one, is tired of describing it: 
i'm running out of ways to describe the difference between heightened volatility under the 
surface and lack of volatility above the water line … i'm also fully cognizant it's a theme we have 
discussed ad nauseum in 2026 … that said, just because it's in the open doesn't mean it's going 
to change, as a matter of fact next year (2027) is priced to be one of the least correlated (most 
dispersive) 12 month periods in our dataset … spx 12m implied correlation (atmf) trading at one 
of the lowest levels in history (~20%) … we just have to survive 2026 first 
In other words, the options market is pricing another year of the index sitting still while 
everything inside it moves violently. Diversification at its finest. 
 

 
 
And yet Garrett's real point this week is a more hopeful one: "some contra indicators are coming 
to the fore … suggesting that even though spot is lower, it may be providing some healing 
characteristics." Below we walk through his seven, with help from Callahan's Sunday "5 x 5" 
note and the PB desk's Friday "US Equities Weekly Rundown", which Garrett draws on 
throughout (both also available to pro subs). 
355 Days Above The 200dma... Then One Afternoon 
Start with the epicenter. Callahan notes that the SOX closed back below its 200-day moving 
average on Friday after 355 consecutive sessions above it, its third-longest streak in the past 10 
years, and is now retesting the July low. Meanwhile, the forward P/E on the SOX is back at 
AI-cycle lows of roughly 16x (which 1999 are we in again?). 
 
What did it? A week of earnings that "arguably displayed some 'rate-of-change' datapoints" per 
Callahan, though he stresses that "these datapoints are all away from the AI complex", citing 
Amazon guiding to two years of forward AWS revenue visibility. The culprits: 
 

 
●​ Kioxia guided Q4 bit shipments flat as data center SSDs slipped into 1Q27 (though 
CY27 capacity is "effectively sold out, as is the majority of '28"). 
●​ Microsoft capex came in under "budget" on third-party capacity delays (with a 2H 
acceleration still guided). 
●​ Intel guided PC revenue down ~20% q/q and now sees the inventory correction running 
into 1H27. 
●​ AMKR and Vanchip offered "soggy" smartphone read-outs. 
"Yes, all largely explainable," Callahan adds. The T+1 moves less so: NOW -25%, INTC -30%, 
RDDT -30%, FFIV -20%, with a number of names posting "(some of) their worst days ever post 
earnings," which he chalks up to crowded positioning, a truncated calendar and ongoing secular 
debates. Intel's recent history makes Garrett's third contra point, that stocks are no longer 
"crashing up": "for some of the largest companies in the market, volatility in 2026 came from the 
right tail, that is no longer the case." Intel's average rally back in June was 6.5%; its average 
selloff has now jumped above its average rally. 
 
Same story for the semis ETF and the Qs, where the average down day is once again bigger 
than the average up day: 
 

 
 
Put differently, stocks are behaving like stocks again, with "downside volatility more extreme 
than upside volatility," and Garrett notes skew has already repriced for this in the short run.

Index Vol Finally Flinches (Gamma Doesn't) 
Garrett's first contra indicator: nervousness is finally showing up in macro equity vol, "catching 
up to the panic previously priced into both single stock vol and credit markets … index vol 
refused to price any contagion, that is starting to change." 
 

 
The derivs desk's Friday note in the Weekly Rundown fills in the details: after front-end vol got 
crushed into Friday, NDX 1m vol snapped back almost 2 vols in a single session and, "for the 
first time, we are now seeing flows catch up to the bear thesis," as the S&P broke its 
medium-term CTA threshold intraday and clients bought downside on the index and in semis. 
QQQ's realized-versus-implied move was one of the most outsized of the past year: 
 
And vol of vol went parabolic: the VVIX hit 124 intraday, the highest level since the US-Iran war 
started, before fading into the close (chart source GS FICC & Equities, Bloomberg). 
 
Quite a change from three weeks ago, when Garrett warned that "underweight is overcrowded" 
and the next premium dollar would chase calls, not puts. 
The one thing that refuses to move? Gamma. In Garrett's words, "despite the realized daily 
move for the indices, the 0day straddles have remained consistently priced (no flinch)," and 
 

 
excess variance for the midterms "remains nonexistent": Wednesday's S&P straddle, which 
captures both Apple earnings and the midterms, costs 150bps, less than Friday's single-session 
drop of 180bps. That's either complacency or a very confident options market. Probably both. 
It wasn't for lack of trading. US equity options traded almost 100 million contracts on Friday, the 
fourth-highest option volume session of all time, prompting Garrett to wonder where the 
prediction market for the busiest session of 2027 is quoted ("i'm 130/150mm"). 
 
For those counting, there are 42 sessions left in 2026, and the year-end straddles are S&P 
±5.1%, Nasdaq 100 ±7.8% and Russell 2000 ±6.3%. Large cap vol above small cap vol... again. 
Risk-Off Buying And Nets That Usually Come With A 20% 
Drawdown 
Contra point two: net exposure is rising, but as a function of "risk off" flow. Goldman's Prime 
book shows hedge funds net bought US equities for the first time in four weeks (+1.1 SDs), but 
Macro Products made up 73% of that and it was mostly short covering. US-listed ETF shorts fell 
4.8%, the largest percentage covering in more than six months, led by Tech, Large Cap Equity 
and Credit ETFs. 
Tech was the most net bought sector on the week, but the PB team points out this buying 
(through Thursday) amounted to just ~40% of what hedge funds sold in the prior three weeks, 
and Mag 7 names were net sold in four of the last five sessions, "suggesting little recovery in 
risk appetite." 
 

 
 
That said, Garrett notes Mag 7 "seems to remain one of the only pockets of TMT where 
positioning has continued to build, length remains at ~ytd highs." So the crowd has been selling 
everything in tech except the one cohort it's most crowded in. 
Overall, US fundamental L/S net leverage stands at 47%, in the 7th percentile of the past year 
(gross is still a hefty 216%, 66th percentile), extending the slide we flagged last weekend. And 
here is where it gets interesting: Garrett regressed PB nets against S&P drawdowns and found 
that these levels of net have been associated with drawdowns of more than 20%. The S&P is 
barely 3% from its high. 
 

 
 
Translation: hedge funds are already positioned for a bear market the index hasn't delivered, 
which is what makes it a contra signal. 
The selling hasn't stopped, though. Garrett's shares desk sold a net -$4.2 billion of tech delta on 
Friday alone, finishing the week a net seller and "fully reversing a significant mid-week buy 
skew," with almost -$10 billion of skew peak-to-trough. With ETFs at ~31% of tape (highest 
since June), the moves "felt systematic / top-down driven." Next up: the machines. The S&P 
closed just 35 handles above its medium-term CTA threshold while the Nasdaq 100 closed 
below both its short- and medium-term triggers, and Garrett expects "a red sweep across equity 
systematics" when the models update Monday. 
80% Green, Index Red: "Safe Is Expensive" 
The breadth data were stranger still. On Thursday, 406 S&P constituents closed green, one of 
the highest readings of 2026, and the index still fell. Garrett plots every down session against 
the share of stocks that rose on the day; Thursday sits off on its own, an outlier with 80% of the 
S&P higher and the index lower. Even on Friday, the S&P ex-AI managed to close in the green. 
 

 
 
Regular readers know where this ends up: the S&P ex-AI basket is at its highs while Goldman's 
AI winners basket rolls over, which is why ex-AI stocks were headed for their best month since 
2022: 
 
Garrett also shows government bonds (TLT) trading with a roughly -0.7 two-week correlation to 
the AI winners, near the bottom of the past two years' range: when AI sells off, bonds rally, the 
textbook risk-off pattern (and one that matches the "stocks down, oil up, yields down" mix we 
discussed earlier in "Goldman's Last 'Source Of Calm' Is Central Banks"). 
 

 
 
Contra point four: index valuations are "catching down" to earnings. The S&P is up 10.7% on 
the year, and yet its forward P/E sits at the lowest level of 2026, around 19x. 
 
Which brings us to point five: pockets of equities are at extremes, and "safe is expensive". The 
dividend yield factor is in its 90th percentile and the valuation factor in its 80th (10-year), while 
momentum (30th) and growth (40th) are cheap. Just look at what happened to the S&P Low 
Volatility index's forward multiple: 
 

 
 
Nothing says "defensive" like paying a growth multiple for low-vol stocks. 
The rotation is happening, Callahan writes, with conversations nudging investors toward 
"broadening out the portfolio", but it's "easier said than done" given the "unwindy" feeling on 
days when non-AI leads, and how hard it is to find non-AI names safe from AI disruption 
narratives. His list of what's working: AAPL, SNPS, ADI/TXN/NXPI, SPOT, V/MA, EBAY and 
LYV. 
The Week Ahead: Apple At A Record Multiple, Midterms At A 
Discount 
The calendar doesn't let up: Apple Monday night, AMD and ANET Tuesday, the midterms 
Tuesday (Kalshi pricing 65% odds of a Democratic sweep, per Goldman's shares desk), the 
refunding (QRA) details Wednesday, and payrolls Friday after last month's +29K. The S&P's 
implied move through Friday is 1.84%. Callahan's five names to watch: 
Apple (AAPL) goes into earnings "riding high" at an all-time high multiple, both absolute (36x 
forward P/E) and relative (a 17x premium to the S&P). So the one megacap still trading like a 
safe haven is also the most expensive it has ever been. See "safe is expensive" above. 
 

 
 
Uber (UBER) has sold off the day after earnings in 8 of its last 10 prints despite a "steady 
cadence of meets-and-beats," as investors struggle with AVs and agentic commerce. At ~10x 
EV/EBITDA (a PEG under 0.5x), "perhaps this coming week can break the trend.. ?" 
 
Cloudflare (NET) has delivered about five straight quarters of accelerating or stable revenue 
growth, and bulls hope agentic traffic keeps it going into 2027 (consensus has Q3 growth easing 
to ~32% from 36%). 
 

 
 
Microchip (MCHP): despite ISM hovering near cycle highs, analog semis have de-rated for 
months. After solid-ish TXN and NXPI prints, can MCHP make the group a "GARPy, core 
holding into 2027"? 
 
Take-Two (TTWO), "arguably one of the more (most?) idiosyncratic names in all of TMT," is 
back at its spring lows. Can it "find support here and re-base for a GTA 6 fueled YE rally?" 
 

 
 
As for the midterms, Garrett expects them to be "a non event at the index levels" but "the basket 
team's time to shine." The theme is affordability, and the most interesting pair is power: long 
renewables and grid, buy protection on hyperscalers and datacenter equipment, since who pays 
for datacenter power is "one of the more underappreciated election-sensitive themes in US 
equities." 
"We Just Have To Survive 2026 First" 
Garrett's last two contra signals are the softest. Point six: "the *actual* credit experts" are 
suggesting AI financing needs could surprise to the downside, and less need for the bond 
market "would help decrease anxiety but takes time to play out." Having been pounding the 
table on AI debt since last October, we'll believe it when the hyperscalers stop printing bonds. 
And point seven, our favorite: the college friend and father-in-law indicators are flashing yellow, 
with that cohort "moving from buying every single dip to at least asking before buying the dip 
('bg, this is all going to be fine, right?')." Garrett thinks retail is "on the verge of evolutionary 
change with agentic," noting an ad that ran while "the garrett quants" were out trick-or-treating: 
"set up your agentic option strategy in 7 minutes or less." What could possibly go wrong. 
So is the pain healing? Positioning says yes: nets that usually come with a 20% drawdown, 
panic-level breadth outliers, defensives at extreme valuations and a nervous father-in-law. But 
the flows say the unwind isn't done: desks are still selling tech, Mag 7 length is still near the 
year's highs, CTAs are about to flip red and the options market is charging less for Apple and 
 

 
the midterms than Friday alone cost. We lean toward the latter, at least until the systematic 
selling clears, which means the "8th inning" crowd may have to sit through extra innings. Then 
again, with 2027 correlation priced at ~20%, the index may never notice.
