Week 3: Plenty of beats, Big Tech takes the

2/3 of index EPS in: solid results but 2Q was stronger  
With results from 301 S&P 500 companies (2/3 of index earnings) in, 75% are beating 
on EPS, 70% on sales and 58% on both, slightly weaker than at this time last quarter 
(77%/72%/61%), but well above the historical post-Week 3 averages (66%/60%/47%). 
Despite a healthy breadth of beats, 3Q EPS growth has been revised up by just 1% since 
October 1st, now tracking 28% YoY growth. Excluding large one-time charges at Meta 
(litigation) and Alphabet (SpaceX revaluation), 3Q EPS would have been revised up by 
3%, still below the +4% revision at this time last quarter. 
AI capex intact, not rewarded by investors 
Hyperscalers showed no signs of tempering capex plans, with Meta (recap), Alphabet 
(recap) and Amazon (recap) signaling significant growth next year, while Microsoft 
(recap) indicated acceleration in the first half of CY 2027 as delayed capacity arrives. 
Given growing cash burn concerns (all but Microsoft are generating negative FCF), 
investors were quick to punish any softness in results, with Alphabet plunging >12% 
after missing on Search. Hyperscalers and Semis collectively lost >$800bn in market cap 
last week, roughly equivalent to the smallest 67 S&P 500 companies combined. 
Despite Big Tech turbulence, breadth remains healthy… 
With 40% of companies yet to report, median EPS growth is tracking a healthy 12% YoY 
pace, slightly below 2Q’s 14%. Similarly, 79% of stocks are on track to grow EPS YoY, 
down modestly from 81% in 2Q but still above the 90th percentile historically (see 
Exhibit 33). Corporates continue to sound optimistic on earnings calls, with only a 
modest uptick in “weak demand” mentions from still-low levels (see Exhibit 21).  
…the catch: upgrades concentrated in Tech & Energy 
Consensus 4Q and 2027 S&P 500 EPS estimates have both risen 1-2% in October, but 
the upward revisions have been driven entirely by Tech and Energy. Excluding those 
sectors, 4Q EPS has been cut 1%, while 2027 is roughly flat. Guidance has also been 
stronger than usual, with 2.3x as many above- vs. below-consensus EPS guides last 
month vs. the October average of 1.0x. Ex-Tech, the ratio moderates to 1.8x but remains 
above average, with consumer sectors showing some of the weakest trends so far. 
Vol picked up, but beat/miss spread stayed narrow 
Week 3 brought bigger swings, particularly in TMT, which saw five stocks move +/-20% 
or more. However, dispersion between beats and misses remained unusually narrow: EPS 
beats outperformed by an average of just 0.4ppt the next day vs. +0.1ppt for misses, 
tracking the lowest spread since 2001. The worst performers, Reddit (recap) and Intel 
(recap), beat 3Q EPS expectations but fell nearly 30% on weak guidance, as AI disruption 
weighed on the former and higher memory prices pressured the latter’s PC business. 
The last big week of reporting is up next: ~140 companies, including Apple 
(11/2), Palantir (11/2) and AMD (11/3) (see Exhibit 57).

Tech/AI Infrastructure

Vertiv (Electrical Equipment) – “Data center demand is accelerating, despite pacing items like power availability,

AI.”

Generac Holdings Inc. (Electrical Equipment) – “In addition to the significant demand from our hyperscale customers,

across an expanded geographical footprint helps to create an increasingly diversified business as the global data center 
investment cycle matures.”

Vol picked up, but beat/miss spread stayed narrow 
After relatively muted reactions in the first two weeks of reporting, Week 3 brought 
bigger swings, particularly in TMT, which saw five stocks move +/-20% or more. 
However, dispersion between beats and misses remained unusually narrow: EPS beats 
outperformed by an average of just 0.4ppt the next day vs. +0.1ppt for misses, tracking 
the lowest spread since 2001. The worst performers, Reddit (recap) and Intel (recap), 
beat 3Q expectations but fell nearly 30% on weak 4Q guidance, as AI disruption weighed 
on the former and higher memory prices pressured the latter’s PC business. 
What drove dispersion in October? Rotation out of Momentum and Growth, 
into Value and Cash Deployment. FCF/EV was the top-performing factor, +11% 
(99th percentile performance vs. the equal-weighted S&P 500 since 1986).

FX flipped from a tailwind to a headwind in 3Q 
We estimate that the stronger US dollar (DXY +2% YoY in 3Q) reduced sales growth by 
roughly 0.2ppt, reversing a 0.5ppt tailwind in 2Q and a much larger 2.0ppt benefit in 1Q. 
By sector, Communication Services and Discretionary are estimated to have seen the 
biggest drag, though the impact was still modest overall (<0.5ppt). In general, we 
estimate that every 10% move in the dollar translates into a 3ppt inverse move in sales 
growth (all else equal), although the impact varies depending on which currencies are 
driving the move.

Weaker earnings quality  
S&P 500 companies are generating less free cash flow relative to net income than usual, 
driven by a big drop in hyperscalers’ free cash flow from increased capex (see Exhibit 
49). One-time gains from investment revaluations have also had an outsized impact on 
reported S&P 500 EPS growth in recent quarters, with mark-ups at Amazon and 
Alphabet alone contributing nearly 20ppt to 2Q S&P 500 EPS growth (+50% or +32% ex. 
these gains). While investment revaluations have boosted S&P 500 EPS this year, they 
could also hurt if valuations reverse (e.g., SpaceX revaluation detracted 20% from 
Alphabet’s EPS this quarter, a minor hit relative to gains recognized earlier this year).

Following big positive surprises in the last two quarters, healthy beats are the status quo

that growth can be sustained (consensus 2027E EPS +16% vs. 2026E >30%) despite 
rates and commodity pressure. 2027 risks are skewed to the downside in our view (we 
forecast +12% YoY, see Outlook). Ultimately, index EPS remains highly dependent on the 
durability of the AI capex cycle and successful monetization, but the interplay between 
geopolitics, rates and Tech may be underestimated – a higher cost of capital and 
geopolitical bottlenecks could gum up what has so far been a mostly smooth story.

Muted reactions; misses hit more than beats rewarded 
So far, small caps that beat on both EPS and sales have outperformed by 1.8ppt the 
following day, on average, while misses on both have underperformed by 2.3ppt. Similar 
to in large caps, these reactions are both more muted than what we saw last quarter 
(when beats outperformed by 3.0ppt and misses underperformed by 4.7ppt), suggesting 
macro has been trumping micro this earnings season. Mid caps that beat on both have 
outperformed by 1.5ppt the following day, while those that missed on both have 
underperformed by 2.5ppt (a smaller reward for beats but a bigger penalty for misses 
than in small caps).

If a company issues changes to its outlook more than once in a one-month period, we 
incorporate all instances of guidance into our aggregate number. The ratio also includes 
all instances of above- or below-consensus earnings guidance issued by a company (for 
example, if they issue both quarterly and annual guidance). The one-month and three-
month revision ratios are calculated as they are for estimate revision ratios. The data 
source is Bloomberg. For companies that provide both GAAP and Operating guidance, or 
for REITs that provide both EPS and FFO guidance, we remove one data point if both 
data points provide the same guidance direction, otherwise both data points are used.  
Capex guidance: We track the number of instances of above- vs. below-consensus 
management guidance for planned capex over the last three months for S&P 500 
companies, calculated the same way as above; data source is FactSet. 
 
Earnings Revision ratios 
Beginning in April 1986 (and January 2006 for Real Estate), we calculate the ratio 
between the number of companies in the S&P 500 for which Thomson Financial 
consensus earnings estimates have been raised versus those that have been lowered. 
We apply the same calculation to the S&P 500 GICS Sectors (where we use FFO for 
REITs within Real Estate). One-month revision ratios are computed as the number of 
companies for which estimates this month are higher than they were last month, divided 
by the number of companies for which estimates this month are lower than they were 
last month. For example, within a particular subgroup of the market, if consensus 
estimates have increased for 5 companies and have decreased for 10, the estimate 
revision ratio will be 0.5 (5 divided by 10). 
The three-month revision ratio is similarly defined as the total number of earnings 
estimate increases divided by total number of earnings estimate decreases during the 
last three months. For example, in February 2002, the three-month revision ratio is 
computed as the total number of companies with increased monthly estimates, divided 
by the total number of companies with decreased estimates throughout December 2001, 
January 2002, and February 2002. 
We similarly calculate the S&P 600 (small cap) earnings revision ratios (one-month and 
three-month) by the same methodology, with data beginning in February 2001. 
For earnings/sales estimates, we look at the change in the FY1 estimate (current 
unreported year) if there's more than 120 days between the current date and the 
company's fiscal year-end date, otherwise we look at the change in the FY2 estimate 
(next unreported year).
