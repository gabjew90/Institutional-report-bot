Labor Market Watch 
Oct jobs preview: No jump-scares expected 
     
   
 
 
Stable labor market in Oct 
We expect payrolls to rise by an above-consensus 75k in Oct (private: 70k), up from 29k 
in Sep. Claims remain extremely benign, while the recent slowdown in weekly ADP and 
potentially unfavorable seasonals pose downside risks. We expect the u-rate to remain at 
4.2%, as the LFPR falls a tenth and HH employment gains moderate after two strong 
months. A larger drop in participation could push the u-rate to 4.1%. Overall, the labor 
market remains stable, with job growth above breakeven and the u-rate flat/down. 
Benign claims show little signs of layoffs 
Initial claims have been exceptionally benign by historical standards. Continuing claims 
have also largely been trending down. Despite recent high-profile layoff announcements, 
the absence of any pickup in claims suggests economy-wide layoffs remain contained. 
Capex boom to support construction & manufacturing jobs 
Recent 3Q GDP data showed extremely strong business investment. We expect that 
capex strength to continue to support employment in construction and manufacturing, 
both through the data-center buildout and production of AI-related equipment. There is 
some evidence of broader investment strength beyond AI as well. We also expect 
education and health services to continue adding jobs at a solid pace. 
Softer ADP & unfavorable seasonals pose downside risks 
However, downside risks exist. First, weekly ADP job gains averaged around 23k in Sep, 
(when monthly ADP came in at 90k), versus roughly 17k in the available Oct prints. This 
points to some moderation in private hiring. Second, seasonal factors are usually 
unfavorable in Oct. Third, TPS visa revocations could weigh on Oct payrolls. 
U-rate expected to hold at 4.2% 
We expect the u-rate to round up to 4.2%, just as it did in Sep (4.17%). The labor force 
participation rate should fall a tenth to 61.7% as HH employment moderates after 
averaging roughly 490k over the past two months. However, a larger drop in the 
participation rate could push the u-rate down to 4.1%. 
Wages to bounce back up after Sep slowdown 
After the outsized decline in wage growth in Sep, we expect AHE to rebound to 0.3% 
m/m. This would help align the wage data with our broader view that the labor market 
slowdown is being driven more by labor supply than demand. We also expect average 
weekly hours to remain at 34.4. Together, these would point to stable labor income 
growth that is supportive of consumer spending but not necessarily inflationary. 
Jobs unlikely to move Dec Fed pricing much 
Oct jobs should keep markets and the Fed focused on inflation. Chair Warsh continues to 
characterize the labor market as near full employment, emphasizing benign claims and a 
steady u-rate over weak job growth given the ongoing labor-supply shock. Our Oct 
forecast reinforces that view, with job growth above breakeven and u-rate flat/down.
