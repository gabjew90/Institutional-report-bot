US Tech Rotation Hits Semiconductors, AI Stocks
US equities are bouncing after this week’s midterm elections but tech stocks lag, with a sharp unwind in
momentum and semiconductors as investors rotate toward quality and more defensive growth. The debate is
whether the midterms encourage hyperscalers to bring forward as much development as possible, expecting
tighter regulations down the line. That could pull more spend into 2027 but raises the risk of an earlier rollover
in EPS revisions for semis. A more constructive case is that regulation ultimately elongates the cycle, but
either way, the path is unlikely to be linear and the added uncertainty is weighing on price action.
That dynamic is also showing up in flows. Investors are favouring hyperscalers over neoclouds as they look for
quality, potentially reflecting the scarcity value and stronger pricing backdrop for existing compute capacity if
scrutiny of new builds increases. The cash desk is a better buyer of hyperscalers and semi-cap equipment
names and better for sale in memory. There’s selective buying in Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Lam
Research, while Intel, ServiceNow, CoreWeave, Advanced Micro Devices and Sandisk are better for sale. AI
Power remains front and center, with UBXXVOLT falling to a 52-week low as Texas stays in focus and
investors price greater regulatory and execution risk.
At the same time, the broader macro backdrop is helping cushion the indices. Brent fell as low as $93.22/bbl
on Wednesday, its lowest since late August, before recovering to around $95.70/bbl on Thursday morning.
Lower oil, combined with expectations that a Democratic-controlled Congress could constrain fiscal expansion,
helped support US Treasuries, with the 10-year yield falling as much as 10bp. The DXY is also lower as
investors question whether the capital inflows and US growth outperformance that have supported the dollar
over the past two years can persist.
Tactically, some of the excess positioning looks to be clearing. Semis and AI have absorbed a meaningful
amount of bad news, and Wednesday’s washout may be getting closer to pricing that risk. The question is
whether the clearing of election uncertainty can now allow the trade to find a floor. With Friday’s nonfarm
payrolls the main remaining macro catalyst this week, attention turns back to earnings for differentiation.
(Robert Ruple, Specialist Sales)
One Liners
German Factory Orders Accelerate – Germany's factory orders rose 4.4% m/m in September, just shy of the
Bloomberg consensus estimate of 4.5%, possibly indicating more evidence of the fiscal lift. The rebound
follows a revised 10.1% decline in August (previously reported as a 10.6% decline), making the September
recovery look somewhat more modest in context. On a year-on-year basis, orders rose 3.3%, exactly in line
with the 3.3% estimate, and a touch above the revised 3.2% reading in August. The September print arrives
against a backdrop of improving broader manufacturing conditions — Germany's final manufacturing PMI

2
came in at 54.2 in October, the second-highest reading in nearly four and a half years, suggesting the sector's
recovery remains on track. That said, the data coming out of Germany is still mixed. Germany's September
retail sales came in sharply below expectations. Sales fell 2.0% m/m against a consensus estimate of a 0.4%
rise, and dropped 2.3% y/y versus an estimate of a 0.2% decline — a significant miss on both measures.
(Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
France September Industrial Production Misses; Manufacturing A Bright Spot – France's industrial
production fell 0.5% m/m in September according to Insee, missing the Bloomberg consensus estimate of a
0.3% increase and following a downwardly revised 0.4% decline in August. On a year-on-year basis, output
dropped 1.3%, significantly worse than the 0.3% fall the Bloomberg consensus estimated and a sharp reversal
from the revised 0.2% increase in August. Manufacturing production offered a rare bright spot, rising 0.5%
m/m in September versus a 0.3% decline in August, though it still fell 0.9% y/y. The weak print fits into a
broader picture of French economic fragility — GDP unexpectedly contracted 0.1% in Q3 2026, dragging on
an otherwise resilient Eurozone, and the October flash composite PMI slumped to 48.4, well below the 50
expansion threshold and the 50.3 forecast, with new orders falling to their lowest since July 2025. Political
headwinds compound the picture, with nationwide strikes and protests over the cost of living underway on
Thursday and the 2026 budget remaining deadlocked in parliament. Taken together, the September industrial
production miss reinforces concerns in France. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
BoE Set To Hike Thursday – UBS Economist Anna Titareva expects the BoE to hike 25bp to 4.0% on
Thursday, in line with Bloomberg consensus, after the hawkish hold in September. She sees a 7-2 vote split,
with Swati Dhingra and Alan Taylor likely voting to keep rates on hold. The updated macro projections and
scenarios should show upward revisions to both inflation and growth. Anna expects the MPC to stick to its
previous message that it “stands ready to act as necessary to ensure that CPI inflation remains on track to
meet the 2% target in the medium term”, underscoring its data-dependent approach and leaving the door open
to further rate hikes. Further ahead, Anna expects the BoE to pause in December before one final 25bp hike in
February to 4.25%, more dovish than current market expectations. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
Sweden CPI Undershoots, Complicating Riksbank Hiking Cycle – Sweden's headline CPI came in at
0.9% y/y for October, below the Bloomberg consensus estimate of 1.2% y/y. On a monthly basis, consumer
prices were unchanged, also below the 0.3% estimate. The CPIF (CPI with fixed mortgage rate, the
Riksbank's preferred measure) similarly undershot expectations, rising 1.1% y/y against a 1.4% Bloomberg
consensus forecast, with the monthly reading flat versus an expected 0.3%. Stripping out energy, CPIF rose
just 0.1% y/y — well below the 0.5% estimate — and fell 0.2% m/m. The softer-than-expected print arrives just
a day after the Riksbank raised its policy rate by 25bp to 2% — its first hike since 2023 — citing energy-driven
inflationary pressures and a strengthening economy, while signaling the door remains open to further
increases over the next year. The October CPI miss may complicate that forward guidance, as underlying
price pressures appear more subdued than policymakers had anticipated. The market is pricing a skip at the
December meeting (2bp of hikes priced) and then a potential hike in February (18bp priced), March (21.5bp
priced) and May (20bp priced). It is unlikely that the central bank hikes in all three meeting, so receiving that
May meeting looks interesting. Of course, with the ongoing war in Iran, conviction is not as firm. (Nana
Antiedu, Macro Sales)
Norges Bank Holds at 4.5%, As Expected - Norges Bank held its benchmark policy rate unchanged at
4.50%, as widely expected. The Bank reiterated that rates will likely need to remain elevated for some time
and that it remains prepared to hike again if needed. Governor Ida Wolden Bache said “Inflation is too high. It
will likely be necessary to keep the policy rate elevated for a time, and we are prepared to raise the policy rate
if needed to bring inflation down to target within a reasonable time horizon”. Inflation remains well above
target, with domestically generated price pressures proving somewhat stronger than expected, while the
labour market has also been slightly firmer than forecast. This is despite capacity utilisation now appearing
slightly below normal and a stronger krone providing some disinflationary support. Overall, there was little to
suggest a material change in the policy outlook form the September meeting, with the Bank maintaining a
higher-for-longer stance and keeping the door open to further tightening. New forecasts will come at the

3
December meeting. Market pricing looks fairly in-line with the statement from, not anticipating a move from the
Norges bank for the next year. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
RBA Caught Between A Slowing Economy And Sticky Inflation - Australia Economics: Australia’s
composite PMI output in October fell to 47.7 (after 50.8 in September); implying real GDP growth is slowing
significantly, according to economist George Tharenou. The composite PMI output prices lifted to 56.9 (after
55.1 in September); implying CPI over coming months could stay high at around 4% y/y. George thinks the
RBA faces a policy dilemma. PMIs imply the economy is slowing signficantly; but inflation pressure remains
way too high. Indeed, fiscal policy remains loose, with strong Government spending; coupled by the AI-related
global trade & investment boom. Overall, George expects the RBA to hold, but at a restrictive 4.85% for a
prolonged time. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
RBA’s Hauser Still Hawkish - RBA deputy governor Hauser was hawkish in his remarks. Speaking in a Q&A,
he said no matter which measure one used, the fact was that Australian inflation was too high. He said the
RBA’s board has been engaged in a “lively debate” reflecting the group’s diverse range of experience. He
pushed back on suggestions he’d been overly hawkish in the lead up to the last policy meeting, which had
caused the rates market to re-price to the point at which the RBA was forced to move. Hauser said he didn’t
recognise that picture of the board having been pushed into a decision corner. (Simon Penn, Macro Sales)
Swiss Unemployment Unchanged In October; SNB’s Schlegel: Would Act If Price Stability At Risk
- Switzerland’s October unemployment rate was unchanged at 3.0%, for the third consecutive month, in line
with expectations. The seasonally adjusted rate also held steady at 3.1%, matching both consensus and the
September reading.While both the headline and seasonally figures were unchanged, the underlying details
were mildly softer. The number of job seekers rose 1.1% m/m to 230,001 and job openings fell 8.5% to 40,462,
although long-term unemployment declined 0.3% to 23,499. Earlier on Thursday, SNB chair Schlegel said the
central bank was prepared to act against inflation if it felt price stability was at risk. His comments follow a rise
in CPI inflation to 1.5% y/y in October, its fastest pace since December 2023. In an interview with
Handelszeitung, Schlegel said he hadn’t yet seen signs of significant second round effects yet. Still, he
admitted inflationary pressures were higher than in the summer. (Simon Penn and Wayne Gouro, Macro
Sales)
BoJ Minutes Point To Further Hikes; Member Flags Faster Pace If Inflation Overshoots – BoJ
policymakers saw a need for further tightening at September’s meeting, the minutes showed, with one
member saying signs of an upward deviation in prices would require the bank to “accelerate the pace of rate
hikes”. Another said hasty action was unnecessary but policy should be guided appropriately “to prevent
excessive and persistent price rises”. The BoJ raised its policy rate to a 31-year high of 1.25% in September,
shifting its focus towards preventing inflation from overshooting its 2% target. Many members expected
inflation to accelerate from autumn as surveys pointed to additional food-price increases. They also saw
elevated inflation risks from renewed crude-price increases linked to prolonged conflict in the Middle East. The
BoJ subsequently held rates steady in October but maintained its vigilance towards inflation risks, reinforcing
market expectations for a December hike. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Why Banxico Will Opt For A Cut – Latin American Economist Rafael De La Fuente expects Banxico to
deliver a 25bp policy rate cut on Thursday, lowering the benchmark to 6.25%, despite only five out of 30
surveyed economists anticipating this move. According to Rafael, the board’s dovish majority is likely to act on
signals for further fine-tuning, even as arguments for holding rates remain strong—such as MXN weakness,
higher oil prices, upside inflation surprises, and accelerating growth. Orderly FX trading, declining core
services inflation (now at 3.37%), and the Fed and ECB’s pause create a window for easing. Banxico’s March
"surprise" cut and the current output gap reinforce the case for action, though the decision won’t be
unanimous, he said. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales)
Brazil: Hawkish Hold, Higher Bar For Cuts -Brazil’s Copom on Wednesday kept Selic at 13.75%, but the
message in the statement was more hawkish. The central bank said inflation has reaccelerated, and it lifted its
forecasts for 2027 inflation to 4.7% from 3.9% in September. The relevant-horizon inflation forecast increased

4
to 3.5% for Q2 2028, up from 3.2% for Q1, indicating monetary policy is not tamping down on prices over the
timeframe it watches. Market expectations for 2027 have also risen, to 4.8% from 4.3%. Crucially, Copom
dropped its previous guidance around the size of the easing cycle (“the total magnitude of the calibration
cycle”), which implies the effective suspension of additional cuts for now. Slower economic activity and
labour-market cooling argue against renewed hikes for now, but the bar for further cuts is definitely higher.
This higher-for-longer environment should be supportive for BRL relative to local rates, with fiscal policy and
inflation expectations key to when easing can resume. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sale)

produced a relief rally across equities and bonds, alongside lower yields, without significant buying behind the
