Asia Open - Oil plunges, equities focus on funding
By Preston Pagli
Crude was the biggest laggard of the day with WTI tumbling nearly 8% on renewed hopes for US-Iran diplomacy.
Equities largely ignored the move as investors remained focused on financing concerns, stretched AI-related capex
expectations, and a crowded macro calendar. Tech once again bore the brunt of the selling, with the NDX (-1.39%),
falling into correction territory. Apple reported a slight beat with EPS $2.10 on $114.48bn vs. consensus $2.00 on
$113.47bn. Elsewhere, long-dated USTs rallied in a bull flattening move while commodity-linked currencies remained
volatile. Looking ahead, Korea inflation is expected to remain steady at 23:00 GMT, the RBA decision comes at 03:30
GMT with our economists expecting a 25bp hike, later on, Turkey and Switzerland CPI print at 07:00 GMT and 07:30
GMT respectively.

In Focus‌
Equities: Sentiment cratered sharply late in the session with the NDX losing -1.4% and 10% from October's all-time high, to
correction territory. The rotation seen last week continued with tech underperforming cyclicals and consumer discretionary
names. Flows on the cash trading desk were skewed toward Info Tech, Healthcare, Comm Services, and Energy, while
outflows were in Financials and Industrials. Vol nearly revisited Friday's highs with spot VIX trading over 21 while skew
remains highly elevated, underscoring the still heavy demand for downside protection ahead of key events this week.
Midterms implied vol for Nov 4 is as follows: SPX @ 0.91%, QQQ @ 1.63%, IWM @ 1.21%. Our equity trading strategy desk
has closed down their positive views on hyperscalers and growth, remain long strong vs. weak balance sheet stocks and
continue to see Health Care AI (CGUSHCAI) as the best option for growth.
FX: Broad based dollar strength was seen across G10 with the notable laggards in NOK and AUD. Currency strength was
clear in CHF and GBP with the BoE rate decision due Thursday, where a hike is widely expected. Long USD positioning for
investors was reduced last week as initial net buying by investors was offset on Thursday and Friday. On a rolling 1-month
basis, leveraged net USD flows peaked in mid-October and have been declining since. Risk appears skewed toward USD
selling ahead of the midterms. AUD traded 50bps weaker ahead of tonight's rate decision with a hike fully priced. Our trader
reduced part of the AUD longer but sees scope for a hawkish hike. On the eTrading FX desk, volumes were lower than

average aside from EURNOK and EURSEK which were highly active on the move lower in oil. Net flows on the desk were
skewed toward JPY while the leveraged segment were buyers of CNH. Outflows led in CHF, NZD, and NOK.  
Rates: USTs bull flattened on renewed US diplomacy hopes in the Middle East. 10y and 30y yields reached their lowest level
since September before paring gains. Key catalysts for rates this week include US midterms Tuesday, the quarterly refunding
announcement Wednesday, with issuance guidance expected to receive a major overhaul, and finally non-farm payrolls
Friday, with our economists in line with consensus at +65k. Our US Rates Strategy desk recently rolled forward their Oct
FOMC reciever into Dec/Jan FOMC to monetize a Fed hold in Jan. US data today included ISM manufacturing, which rose to
55.5, firmly in expansionary territory. Our economists would watch for signs in manufacturing survey data that activity could
start to slow into 2027. Many anecdotes in the ISM survey were mixed, citing elevated costs and uncertainty but continued
strength in AI-related activity.
Commodities: Crude was the biggest mover across the commodities complex with WTI and Brent tumbling 8% to $96 and
$104 respectively. Reports that Iranian Supreme leader Khamenei gave the green light to negotiate spurred additional bearish
momentum to energy prices after President Trump said he prefers a path to diplomacy over a resumption of military activity.
 US natural gas reversed earlier gains and fell alongside oil. Gold rebounded 2%, clawing back some of Friday's near 4%
slump. 
Elsewhere‌
KRW: CPI MoM at 23:00 GMT for October. Our economists project headline inflation to remain broadly steady at 2.88%YoY,
while core inflation could edge down to 2.72% YoY vs 2.8% prior. Tourism related services and durable goods prices should
offset lower agricultural prices.
AUD: RBA Cash Rate Decision at 03:30 GMT. Citi is in line with consensus expecting a 25bp rate hike taking the cash rate to
4.85%. New economic forecasts should show higher inflation (CPI to 4.1% in Dec 2026, 3.3% in Jun 2027, trimmed mean at
3.5% and 3.1% respectively), wages growth remaining somewhat sticky and the unemployment rate reaching the top of the
RBA’s NAIRU band, keeping upside risks to the cash rate. The press conference should retain a hawkish tone, though given
current market pricing, the bar for a hawkish surprise is high.
CHF: CPI MoM at 07:30 GMT for October. SNB continues to signal little urgency to adjust rates, with a stable monthly inflation
supporting the case for remaining on hold. Headline CPI is expected at 1.2% YoY versus 1.0% prior (Bloomberg cons).
TRY: CPI MoM at 07:00 GMT for October. Our economists anticipate inflation to remain broadly unchanged at 29.7%
YoY/2.5% MoM. Expect food and housing inflation to lose pace during the month, this will be broadly offset by a pick-up in the
transport component.
