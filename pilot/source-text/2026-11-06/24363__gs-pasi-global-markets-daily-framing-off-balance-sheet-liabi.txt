GS Pasi - Global Markets Daily: Framing Off Balance Sheet
Liabilities in the AI Ecosystem 3 Nov 2026
Alisha Pasi  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
3 Nov 2026
3 November 2026 | 4:38AM EST | Research | Economics
• Market participants across asset classes are increasingly viewing the capital structures of AI-related
issuers holistically, recognizing that access to debt financing is a strategic competitive advantage.
• As hyperscalers’ free cash flow has turned negative, debt issuance has accelerated. With more than $40
billion of deals announced since October 29th, the hyperscalers have issued $271 billion of debt so far this
year—above our full-year 2026 forecast for $250 billion. In some instances, this reflects a pull-forward of
planned 2027 supply. We see scope for more of this activity, although we expect capital markets access to
be somewhat uneven across issuers and highly dependent on the market tone.
• The true scale and scope of AI-related debt issuance is also much larger than ‘just’ the five hyperscalers.
Through November 2nd, we estimate $657 billion of year-to-date, global, AI-related debt issuance across
syndicated credit markets—underscoring the importance of managing overall exposure.
• Credit market participants are also analyzing growth in off-balance sheet obligations. For the
hyperscalers, we track $1.7 trillion in lease commitments, 75% of which have not yet commenced. We
also estimate $1.9 trillion of aggregate, unconditional purchase commitments—$685 billion of which is
due by year-end 2027.
• While a portion of this amount is captured in consensus capex forecasts, we believe this (fast-growing)
category nonetheless represents an additional 'use of cash.' This leaves us comfortable with our view that
debt financing—across syndicated and private markets—will play a key role in the AI buildout.
Acceleration in near-term monetization of AI investment is the key risk to less debt issuance. Delayed
monetization and growth in purchase commitments are the key risks to more debt issuance, subject to
market conditions.
• In early August, we flagged that the rating agencies were using different approaches to how they
accounted for off-balance-sheet liabilities, with S&P taking a more conservative stance. Since that time,
their posture has appeared more cautious, with some lowered outlooks and commentary regarding little
“margin for error.” We believe evidence of monetization by 2028 is key to mitigating downward pressure
on debt ratings in the AI ecosystem (albeit from a high starting point).
Amanda Lynam, CPA
