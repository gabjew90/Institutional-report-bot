JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
Dems flip both chambers of Congress driven by a significant increase in voter turnout and a 
breaking of the coalition that Trump formed in 2024 with Trump’s unfavorable rating acting 
to boost Dems. In the Senate, Dems held seats in Georgia, Michigan, Minnesota, and New 
Hampshire. Then, they were able to flip Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South 
Carolina, and Texas; Alaska and Mississippi are pending. This gives Dems at least 54 seats 
in the Senate. Despite the strong showing for Dems, they are unable to form veto-proof 
majorities (requires 2/3 of votes) in both chambers of Congress. The biggest topic into the 
election and overnight is on the Texas Governorship and what it means for Datacenters and 
thus the AI trade. Gov-elect Hinojosa favors an immediate moratorium on data center builds 
in Texas until new laws can be passed to establish guardrails (Hinojosa Campaign 
website). One interpretation is that existing builds / permits would be free to continue and 
all new construction would be halted and thus would move to other states. Another 
interpretation is that the datacenter build is slowing and likely reducing capex; this theme 
had been building into the election. The market initially sold off with NDX futs down more 
than 2% before rallying into the green. The Mkt Intel view is that fears emanating for a 
change in control in Texas were overblown given the multitude of other states where 
datacenters could be built and the likelihood that a change in control would mean regulation 
rather than a cessation of datacenter activity. Given the anxiety around the AI trade, we are 
not yet comfortable saying that this marks the ‘All Clear’ to re-gross into the theme but it is 
another positive step. Clients had previously pointed to the need to see a position flush, a 
reiteration of hyperscalers capex, positive Semis / Memory earnings, and improved capital 
markets, in addition to passing Midterms, before having comfort to re-gross. Here is a 
repost of our Monetization Menu; which we think still holds though we may see some 
squeezy behavior in the very near-term.

Energy sector appreciation and less Tech downside. We would execute this via a barbell of

Value return; in Jan – Feb, US Value was +7.4%, SXXP +7.6%, and UKX +9.7%.  
  
Aside from the datacenter issue, what else are people focused on with the Blue Wave 
having materialized? (i) Do Dems force an end to the Iran Conflict? Once the new 
Congress is seated in January, they may choose to pull funding or sue to recall troops given 
they have been deployed in a conflict for more than 90 days without Congressional 
approval. The markets seem to be expressing a view that a deal is increasingly more likely. 
There may be ramifications for RU / UKR given the bipartisan support for Ukraine and 
Russia sanctions law. (ii) Subpoena power / investigations. While there will be 
heightened focus on Trump and his family, Tech and business CEOs may come under the 
spotlight. That said, it is unclear how the DOJ responds gives comments by AG Blanche 
that he works for the President, dispelling the notion of independence (Yahoo). (iii) 
Reduced deficit spending. FY26 deficit is $1.99T. FY25 was $1.78T. FY24 was $1.8T. 
FY23 was $1.7T. FY22 was $1.38T. This can be achieved by asserting power over the 
budget and likely wading through a government shutdown. (iv) Higher taxes / reversal of 
OBBBA. Given the level of federal debt, raising taxes is the most viable path given the 
traditional inability to materially cut spending. This would require waiting for a new POTUS. 
(v) Dems take the White House. The election results are similar to the 2006 midterms 
which saw Dems successfully defend all of their seats and then flip both chambers of 
Congress. That set the stage for Obama to take the White House in 2008; does history 
repeat itself?  
  

•         POST MIDTERM SEASONALITY – our colleague Craig Cohen notes that the “SPX  
has been up a year after every midterm election since 1950 with an average gain of around 
15%. The worst one-year forward return came after the 1986 midterms but this, of course, 
had more to do with the 1987 crash rather than anything going on in Washington. I suspect 
that at least part of the reason for this tendency is that the midterms tend to see the 
incumbent party loses seats and gridlock in Washington tends to be good for Wall Street.” 
Mkt Intel adds that on a 3-month basis, the SPX has averaged a +5.4% return, 11.4% on a 
6-month basis, 12.5% on a 9-month basis, and 14.7% on a 12-month basis. The six, nine, 
and twelve month returns have a 100% hit rate dating to 1990. Index and sector returns are 
in the chart below. 
 
 
  
•         ISM-SRVCS (FEROLI) – We expect the ISM series composite to improve to 55.5 in 
October from 54.9 in the prior month. The September ISM report saw new orders decline 
1.1-pt from the peak in August, while business activity was down 5.2-pt. That widened the 

wedge between the two series to one of the largest levels in the current cycle. We look for 
business activity in the October PMI series report to remain steady at 58.0. 
o   Services demand has been strong since the middle of the year, as new 
orders in both surveys reached cycle high levels in recent months, running 
ahead of operating capacity. This is evident in the surge in backlogs of work 
in the PMI that have climbed to 55.9 in the early October print, close to levels 
seen during the post-pandemic demand surge. The employment PMI has 
also surged in recent months, reaching 55.2 in September before falling 
slightly to 53.8 in October. Improvement in the PMI employment gauge has 
far outpaced its ISM counterpart, which has remained rangebound at a more 
subdued level for much of this year. 
o   Price pressures also remain a worrying theme in the services surveys. The 
ISM prices index reached a cycle high of 74.0 in September, higher than the 
spike in March following the beginning of the Middle East conflict. The early 
October PMI report displayed mixed signals for any moderation, as input 
prices edged down to a still elevated level of 63.4 from 65.9 in September, 
while output prices rose further to 58.7 from 58.2.

US MKT INTEL VIEW  
Despite the challenging October environment and residual headwinds to our Tactical 
Bullish call, we are sticking with the view. First, when you see a flush like we witnessed on 
Friday there tends to be a near-term rebound. Speaking with John Schlegel and the 
Positioning Intel team, they ran some numbers and find that when the NDX is within ~6% 
of ATH highs and you see a 3.5%+ decline, the index tends to rebound ~3% the following 
week. The effect tends to be stronger if that 3.5% decline occurs on a Friday, perhaps 
reflecting a higher level of de-grossing driving the move rather than a shift in fundamental 
views. I understand this sounds like a baseball stat and that weekend headlines around AI 
were negative on capital raise (CNBC), circular financing (WSJ), and legal (CNBC) may give 
some investors pause but we think the combination of a strengthening broadening trend 
plus stabilization in Tech will push the SPX higher. 
  
Second, this week’s data can assuage stagflationary / recessionary fears. There was a 
spike in client question on the health of the US economy if AI capex declines and whether 
we see a significant recession, mirroring the Tech Bubble burst at the beginning of the 

century. My views is No given (i) health of the consumer – household net worth is at 
record levels with cash levels also historically strong … consumer checking accounts have 
quintupled since COVID which was at a historical high. Further, there remains significant 
room to ramp debt capacity based on debt service as a percentage of disposable income. 
The strongest period of credit strength in US history was late-2021/early-2022 and we are 
not yet seeing credit card delinquency / charge-off data fully normalize to pre-COVID levels; 
(ii) businesses unlikely to see margin compression – public businesses are 
experiencing record / near-record profit margins and that would need to compress before 
mass layoffs begin; (iii) AI is not a significant driver of hiring – generally, AI is thought to 
be a headwind to hiring and on the construction side that is where the human capital 
demand exists but is tempered by current immigration policy. Thought differently, for all of 
the turmoil caused by CRWV it is a company that employs 550 people. I do not want to be 
dismissive of a potential domino effect but given the AI monetization questions it may be the 
case that the biggest effects are capex declines (affecting GDP growth metrics but not 
necessarily a material impact on labor markets or spending) and potential hyperscaler 
layoffs than an economy-wide phenomenon. Separately, the health of the consumer and 
subsequent spending remain robust with the biggest impact being a negative wealth effect 
from a bear market. We are seeing an increase in the %age of spending tied to consumers 
monetizing their investment accounts rather than checking or savings accounts.  
  
Third, the biggest non-AI risk stems from a re-escalation in the Middle East. If you have not 
seen the WSJ article on the considerable damage inflicted to US regional bases by Iran, it is 
worth a read (here). This situation combined with depleted munition levels suggests that 
another wave of attacks could be limited in scope and duration with diminished ability to 
repel the Iran counterattack. This may point to heightened diplomatic efforts. In the event of 
another series of attacks, oil markets appear to have already priced this outcome 
suggesting limited upside to energy prices and thus limited room for bond yields to move 
and Equities to decline. More color on this can be found in the US / Iran bullet in the next 
section, where our views are shaped by work from Natasha Kaneva and Brendan Henrici.  
  

Lastly, positive seasonality returns. November is the strongest month, on average, this 
century. Post-midterm seasonality is also a tailwind.
