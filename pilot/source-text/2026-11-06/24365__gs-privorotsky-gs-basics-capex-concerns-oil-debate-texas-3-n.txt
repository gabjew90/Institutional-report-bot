GS Privorotsky - GS Basics Capex Concerns. Oil Debate. Texas. 3
Nov 2026
Rich Privorotsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
3 Nov 2026
Markets trading poorly ahead of the US midterms. The tech tape really couldn’t hold on yesterday, despite
a huge pullback in energy. Gen AI down 5%, AI healthcare down 4%, optical down 7.5%, neoclouds down
6% … meanwhile homebuilders up 4%, alternatives up 3.5% and value up 1.6%. High momentum down
7%. Feels like the rolling ball of de-grossing hasn’t stopped. I think you can decompose it into two things:
a lack of support for the AI trade, with an early reflexive bounce fading into the US session; and the
collapse in energy driving a bid to discretionary and retail. That translates into a momentum unwind …
shorts going up, longs going down. Apple overnight: The quarter itself beat, but the outlook disappointed,
with the stock down around 3% after hours. December quarter gross margin guidance of 45–46% reflects
higher memory costs, while services growth guidance of 8–9% was below expectations....AI demand is
supporting memory pricing, but also competing for components and squeezing margins elsewhere.
Asia: Follow-through from the US. TSMC Capex basket down 8% (TSMC in Taiwan having broken 100d,
looks could test 200d), Asia AI bottlenecks down 5.6% and Korea AI down 5%. Airlines up 2%. Japan is
closed. So what’s happening? Obviously there’s a lot of anxiety ahead of the midterms. See chart below
with the key Senate and gubernatorial races … Texas is taking the focus and is one to watch for markets.
Capex: At the same time, anxiety around capex is extremely high. A number of bond issues launched
yesterday reinforce the question around the cost and availability of capital. There are bull and bear takes
on both sides, but the multiple compression is striking. The SOX on a blended 24 month forward basis is
around 12.16x p/e, through Liberation Day and Iran escalation lows...approaching 2022 levels. Nvidia is at
10x, its lowest multiple since 2013 (blended 24mf). What is the market trying to tell us? That there is
significant risk to estimates and potentially 2027 is a peak, as the cost of capital and the capacity to
finance the buildout constrain how quickly companies can move. There are parallels to tech in the late
90s… building capacity before there was enough use for it. The bull case, and I’m probably more in this
camp, is that the use cases are still largely untapped. Robotics has barely started, enterprise IT workflow
automation has barely scaled across legacy stacks. But corporate balance sheet capacity and debt market
limits are real and binding.
AI Tests: AMD and Arista report today … two important reads after Intel and the optical sell off. AMD
can speak to accelerator demand and the competitive backdrop; Arista gives us a read on hyperscaler
networking spend. In this tape, the reaction to the guidance may matter as much as the guidance itself.
Oil: Energy dropped around 8% yesterday, with headlines around possible US/Iran talks accelerating the
move. But oil is higher after a "report that the United States had struck Iran's Qeshm Island" (RTRS)… an
odd fact pattern if talks are progressing smoothly. Middle Eastern equities outside Qatar didn’t really rally,
although they closed before the second half of the oil sell off, so we’ll see what they do today. The Saudi

ETF closed near its lows, hardly reflective of a grand bargain in the Middle East. I’m a little nervy that the
market got ahead of itself on oil. I’d love to be optimistic and hope a deal is happening, but it isn’t obvious
that’s where we are, and there’s a risk of re-escalation after the midterms. Brent is bouncing modestly after
yesterday’s meaningful sell-off … a very unclear backdrop.
Risk: The hard part is disentangling how much of this is fundamental versus technical. The market is
nervous about the midterms, energy and the rates backdrop. People have had a very difficult time hanging
on to positions and, having made money in the first half, have been playing defense to protect those gains.
The clearing of the political event should be important. The S&P has been higher in the 12 months
following all 19 midterms since 1950 … no guarantees but gridlock has generally been seen as supportive
for equities, although AI and data center restrictions remain an important risk to watch. We remain on the
back foot in a tricky environment. Everyone seems to be in some variation of risk reduction, trying to
maintain stability across scenarios without getting too far out the risk curve. We saw a lot of index related
hedging Friday. The upside is that sentiment and positioning have come down meaningfully, and my gut is
that the clearing of events will be positive through time. But we also need to find stability in the AI trade
as the market gets comfortable with corporate capital structures and the durability of revenue supporting
the spend. The first hurdle is avoiding an extreme midterm outcome … Texas will be important to watch.
After that, the questions are whether the Middle East moves towards progress or re-escalation, and
whether lower oil can deliver sustained relief in rates
