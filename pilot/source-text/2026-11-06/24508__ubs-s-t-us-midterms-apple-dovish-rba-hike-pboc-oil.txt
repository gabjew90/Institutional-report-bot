RBA’s Dovish Hike – The RBA hiked the cash rate by 25bp to 4.85%, as widely expected, with a 6-3 vote
split. “The Board will continue to do what it considers necessary to bring inflation sustainably back to target,” its
post-meeting statement said. Governor Michele Bullock will hold a press conference at 15:30 Sydney. UBS
Economics sees the RBA’s decision to drop the tightening bias, i.e. 'increasing cash rate further, if needed'
from the statement and the vote split as clearly dovish, while retaining its call for the central bank to stay on
hold from here. (Jolie Ho, Macro Sales)
PBoC To Raise FX Reserve Ratio To Curb Yuan Gains; Fix Offers Limited Policy Signal – The PBoC will
raise the FX reserve requirement ratio by 2ppts, from 9% to 11%, using the tool to manage USD liquidity and
FX flows. The desk views the increase as aimed at slowing CNY appreciation by reducing onshore USD
liquidity. The PBoC last raised the ratio by the same amount in December 2021, from 7% to 9%, when
USDCNH was below 6.400 during Covid. Closely watched following this morning’s announcement, the yuan
reference rate was set at 6.7150 per USD, versus an estimate of 6.6517 and marginally weaker than the
desk’s 6.7105/35 estimate. The desk sees limited evidence in the fixing itself of a major FX policy shift, while
viewing the reserve-ratio increase as a move to temper RMB appreciation. It has seen net USDCNH buying
through eFX, led by hedge funds, likely reflecting an unwind of bullish CNH positions. (Jason Poh, Execution
Sales)

South Korea Inflation Eases As Farm Prices Fall – South Korea’s headline CPI eased to 2.8% y/y in
October from 2.9% in September and 3.1% in August, with agricultural, livestock and fishery prices down
1.7%, subtracting 0.14pp from inflation. Meanwhile, petroleum prices rose 14.9%, their fastest increase since
July, adding 0.56pp; diesel climbed 19.7% and gasoline 11.6%. Services rose 2.7%, while dining-out prices
increased 2.9%, contributing 0.42pp. Core CPI, excluding food and energy, stood at 2.8%. The finance
ministry estimated fuel price caps reduced headline inflation by 0.8pp. The data suggest food-price relief and
policy support are cushioning inflation, while energy and service costs remain sources of pressure. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)

UBS CIO Sees Brent Near $110 At Year-End As Supply Constraints Persist Despite Diplomacy – The
prospect of negotiations may reduce part of oil’s geopolitical risk premium, but does not yet signal a
normalization of the physical market. Middle Eastern flows have recovered, but remain volatile and dependent
on maritime security, costly naval protection, and constrained tanker capacity. Global inventories have also
fallen sharply, strategic reserve releases are largely complete, and recovering Chinese imports are removing
an important buffer. UBS Wealth Management CIO therefore retains a moderately constructive near-term
outlook and expects Brent to trade near $110 a barrel at year-end, before declining to $90 by June 2027 as
traffic through the Strait of Hormuz gradually recovers. Risks around this path remain elevated and depend on
the progress of US-Iran diplomacy, the reliability of Gulf production and shipping, and global demand. For
investors, CIO continues to see broad commodity exposure as one way to strengthen portfolio resilience,
alongside high-quality bonds, capital-preservation strategies, and select alternatives for those seeking to
mitigate downside risks. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
