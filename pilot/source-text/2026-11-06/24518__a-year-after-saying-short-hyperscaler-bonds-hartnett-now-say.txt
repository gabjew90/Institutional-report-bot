 
A Year After Saying "Short Hyperscaler Bonds", 
Hartnett Now Says Buy Them 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
MONDAY, NOV 02, 2026 - 02:44 PM 
Last weekend, BofA CIO Michael Hartnett told us that he would stay tactically risk-off until 
mid-November, and he named the three things that would end the selling. The third one read: 
"AI capex cut or extreme equity issuance announcement (think Boeing 2024) causes peak in AI 
hyperscaler debt spreads." 
Well, he didn't have to wait long. 
On Monday morning, Meta launched an equity raise of up to $95 billion: a $35 billion offering of 
common and mandatory convertible preferred stock, followed by an at-the-market program for 
up to $60 billion of non-voting Class C shares. The stock rose, which, as we said at the time, 
"tells you how much the market feared the alternative was even more debt." We had one 
follow-up question: 
"Everyone else" answered over the next four days, just not the way we'd hoped:  
●​ Microsoft, the cleanest balance sheet in the group, kicked off its first dollar bond deal 
since 2017 and asked for at least $25 billion;  
●​ Alphabet suffered the worst day in its history; and on Friday Intel crashed the most on 
record while Amazon slid on a $33.5 billion cash burn, sending the Nasdaq 100 down 
3.5% to 28,385 and briefly into correction territory.  
●​ Hyperscalers as a group burned $31 billion in Q3, and the off-balance sheet time bomb 
inside AI hit $4.1 trillion, up $1 trillion in 90 days.  
As for Hartnett's other call, that the Fed needed to hike on October 28, Warsh's Fed paused 
instead, with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favor of a hike. Through it all bonds kept rallying, 
and the 10Y closed the month at a six-week low below 4.9%. 
So did hyperscaler spreads peak? Not by a long shot: by Thursday IG spreads were blowing 
back out toward the recent wides, data center CDS was widening and Meta's Beignet SPV 
bonds were getting smashed. But Hartnett has seen enough to make a call that would have 
sounded crazy a year ago. Back in November 2025, he told clients that the best trade into 2026 
 

 
was shorting hyperscaler bonds (which was about as close to perfect prediction accuracy as 
they come on Wall Street).  
So with US tech bonds down 9% YTD and their returns relative to the IG index at a 30-year low, 
he is now taking the other side: buy the tech bond "diamonds in the rough." 
In his latest Flow Show, titled "When AI met ABB" (available to pro subs); Hartnett starts with his 
weekly scoreboard. Oil extended its lead (79.1% YTD from 72.7% a week ago), US stocks 
clawed back almost a point (12.7% from 11.9%) and the dollar firmed (3.7% from 3.4%), while 
govt bonds (-3.3% from -3.0%) and IG (-2.5% from -2.2%) slipped again: 
Scores on the Doors: oil 79.1%, US stocks 12.7%, global stocks 9.6%, US$ 3.7%, cash 3.1%, 
HY bonds -0.5%, IG -2.5%, govt bonds -3.3%, gold -7.1%, bitcoin -8.4% YTD. 
Last week we noted that gold was just 0.2 points ahead of bitcoin in the race for last place. Well, 
the gap is now 1.3 points, and both scores predate the late-week fun: gold broke below $4,000 
to a one-year low, while bitcoin hit a six-week low after North Korea was blamed for the $6.3 
billion Binance heist. So much for the two debasement hedges in a year of record deficits. Keep 
in mind that these numbers run through Wednesday, i.e., before Alphabet, Intel and Amazon 
took turns detonating (it's worse now).

The chart says it all: the last time IG tech bonds lagged the IG index this badly was after the

Having warned since October 2025 that AI is now a debt bubble too (well before Hartnett issued 
his "short AI debt" reco), quietly surpassing the banks as the largest sector in the IG market, 
we'll allow ourselves a modest victory lap: hyperscaler debt issuance went from $17 billion in 
2024 to $182 billion this year, more than a tenfold increase, and the bond market has been 
repricing it ever since. That is what gets you to the yields in Hartnett's Table 1, where Meta's 
2066s yield 7.40%, Oracle's 2066s 8.43% and CoreWeave's 2031s a junk-ish 14.26%. Even 
Microsoft and Apple's long bonds now pay north of 6%: 
 
 

 
(Small quibble: half the names in BofA's "Magnificent 7" table are not in the Mag 7. CoreWeave, 
Galaxy, SoftBank, Oracle, Broadcom, Micron and AMD have all been promoted, which, given 
who has been doing the borrowing, is probably fair.) 
The other half of the story is equity. Add $89 billion in hyperscaler stock sales to the $182 billion 
in bonds and you get $271 billion of new paper YTD, with three of the four biggest quarters on 
record all coming this year: 
 
And the money used to go the other way. Hyperscaler buybacks peaked at about $45 billion in a 
single quarter in late 2021, and ran at $105 billion for all of 2024. This year they total $7 billion, 
or a $14 billion annualized pace: 
 

 
 
Put differently, the biggest buyers of US stocks for the past decade have become sellers. 
Bloomberg's Simon White made the same point on Tuesday, warning that stock buybacks have 
a lot of work to do to counter new issuance, with Meta's deal and Anthropic's upcoming IPO 
adding to the "surfeit of supply." And when Meta's stock does get bought, it's sometimes by the 
people Meta pays: 
Hartnett's logic for the bond trade is straightforward: every dollar of equity a hyperscaler raises 
sits below the bondholders in the capital structure, so Meta choosing shareholder dilution over 
another jumbo bond deal is a gift to creditors, and a sign management finally cares what the 
bond market thinks. We'd add one caveat. The bonds are cheap for a reason, and the reasons 
got bigger this week: if the cleanest hyperscaler needs $25 billion in bonds, who doesn't? With 
$4.1 trillion in off-balance sheet obligations still to be serviced, we suspect the "diamonds" may 
need a bit more polishing (and maybe a few more equity deals) before the rough is cut.

Policing The Bubble Into The Midterms 
Put it all together, and Hartnett's message is that the AI bull has run into the one super trend it 
can't buy its way out of. As long as electorates demand goodies and governments are afraid of 
austerity, investors stay ABB, yields stay high, and the bond market keeps policing the AI capex 
boom one jumbo deal at a time. He wants to own the tech bonds that have already been 
punished, wait for policymakers to cap yields, and look for the real risk-on moment in H1'27, 
when peace, cheaper oil and the end of rate hikes finally ease financial conditions. 
We agree with the diagnosis, and we'd probably skip a step on the prescription. A week that 
started with Meta selling stock, then saw Microsoft borrowing $25 billion, Alphabet having its 
worst day ever and IG spreads widening again is the kind of week that typically comes before 
the peak in hyperscaler spreads. MAGS has already broken $70, SMH is $2.50 above $520, 
and on Tuesday the country votes on whether to make "affordability" the theme of 2027. If the AI 
debt bubble really is deflating, Hartnett's own logic suggests where the money goes next: the 
good old Treasury bubble, with YCC waiting in the wings to make sure it never pops. 
Then again, Hartnett said "short hyperscaler bonds" a year ago and was right. Maybe "buy 
them" will age just as well, only probably not before the votes are counted.
