KEY RESEARCH
China Industrial Tech – 2027 Outlook: Valuation Reset and Sector Preferences. We refresh our 2027
outlook for the sector, favoring the AI robotics supply chain driven by frontier-model commercialization
and resilient automation demand, alongside Data Center Electricals where overseas progress remains solid
despite a delayed 800VDC mass adoption to 2028. We forecast China grid investment to grow 6% year-
over-year in 2027 with increasing emphasis on UHV, while cutting 2026-2030 sector EPS estimates by 5-
10% on average, primarily across battery and energy storage names due to raw-material inflation and
margin pressures. We highlight preferred names including Inovance, Shuanghuan, Sanhua, Kstar,
Envicool, Hongfa, Sieyuan, Huaming, and Nari Tech, and we upgrade Jianghai (002484.SZ) to Buy with a
12m TP of Rmb43.9 driven by an increasing AI product mix and solid earnings visibility. Jacqueline Du
China Grid Tech – Scenario analysis on US regulatory uncertainty. We maintain Buy on Sieyuan and
Huaming but lower our 12m TPs to Rmb145 and Rmb22.3, respectively, to reflect regulatory overhang
from US Executive Order 14421 and easing transformer supply shortages. For Sieyuan, we cut 2027-30E
EPS estimates by 9-14% as global capacity additions increase competition and drive modest declines in
overseas gross margins, though North American orders from cloud service providers remain resilient. We
leave Huaming EPS estimates unchanged but adjust our target price to account for potential margin
pressure on transformer components alongside the US regulatory uncertainty. Zhou Li
Nintendo – 2Q profit miss on softer hardware sell-in; lowering OP estimates – Buy. We maintain our
¥9,800 12m TP following an underwhelming 2Q operating profit of ¥73.9 bn, which missed expectations
as Nintendo Switch 2 hardware sell-in lacked momentum despite a robust software beat. Consequently, we
lower our FY27E operating profit estimate by 6% to ¥401.8 bn and cut our FY28E–FY31E estimates by 2-
7%, reflecting reduced hardware shipment assumptions partially offset by higher software tie ratios. While
hardware penetration has been more gradual than anticipated following recent price hikes, we expect

active consoles and earnings to enter a renewed growth phase as the dedicated software lineup expands.
Minami Munakata
Sony Group – 2Q beat and raised guidance on entertainment strength – Buy. Sony reported a strong 2Q
operating profit beat at ¥622.3 billion, driven by record box office for Spider-Man: Brand New Day and
robust PlayStation engagement. Management raised FY3/27 operating profit guidance to ¥1,870 billion,
prompting us to increase our FY3/27–FY3/31 operating profit estimates by 6% to 9% to reflect a revised
¥160/USD forex assumption and the stronger entertainment and I&SS outlook. We anticipate continued
earnings expansion fueled by deepening PlayStation monetization, Crunchyroll growth, and cross-segment
synergies, and accordingly raise our 12m TP to ¥4,800. Minami Munakata
China Consumer Durables – 3Q26 Wrap: Overseas Outperforming Domestic. We highlight Midea, Anker,
and Roborock as our preferred Buy-rated names following a sequentially weaker 3Q26, where covered
companies reported a weighted average of -5% and -11% year-over-year revenue and profit growth. We
expect mild domestic improvement in 4Q26 supported by easing base effects, alongside a meaningful
overseas revenue boost for companies with developed market exposure entering the festival season,
though gross margin pressures from cost inflation persist. Factoring in recent results and industry trends,
we revise our 2026E-28E EPS forecasts by -41% to +17% and 12m TPs by -14% to +17%, lower
estimates and TPs for Oppein and Apptronics on cautious outlooks, and upgrade XGimi to Neutral
reflecting its rising overseas contribution. Nicolas Yi
Global Credit Trader – AI-Related Supply: Outlining the Directional Risks. We highlight that year-to-date
global debt issuance by hyperscalers has reached $296bn, surpassing our full-year 2026 forecast of $250bn
and representing 36% of aggregate capex. Looking ahead, we expect 35% of hyperscalers' 2027 capex to
be debt-financed, equating to roughly $440bn of direct issuance, alongside an additional $300bn in project
finance supply. With total global AI-related debt issuance tracking at $687bn, we view near-term risks as
firmly tilted toward spread widening amid potential front-loading of 2027 supply and a volatile market
tone. While recent US midterm elections may lead to short-term local data center moratoriums affecting
up to 45% of planned capacity expansion, we expect these to be short-lived, underscoring our selective
approach to project finance deals. Amanda Lynam
