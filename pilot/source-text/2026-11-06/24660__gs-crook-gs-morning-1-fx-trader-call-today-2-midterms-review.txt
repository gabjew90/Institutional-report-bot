2) Midterms Review
Rich Privorotsky, Head of EMEA Flow Intermediation and European One Delta Trading, out with his
latest views on the AI trade following the Midterms. Key takeaways below; full piece here.
BOTTOM LINE: My gut is that AI infrastructure has been sold to death, and investors will start
differentiating between the capacity that exists and the capacity that may never arrive. The micro remains
strong, electoral uncertainty has diminished and positioning looks considerably cleaner. Vol likely comes
in quickly and that drives spot in the short term.
Data Centres: The debate is no longer just about the cost of compute, but whether you can actually permit,
energize and deliver the capacity required in 2027/28/29. Moratoriums, local opposition to data centre
development and a potentially more restrictive regulatory environment could materially constrain the
supply curve.
Two very different implications of a constrained supply curve…
1/ If you sell chips, cooling, electrical equipment or the nuts and bolts of data centres, there is a legitimate
risk that customers have overordered relative to the capacity they can actually energize. Some of that

demand could migrate to other geographies or BTM solutions, but not necessarily quickly enough. This is
potentially negative for the picks and shovels, although much of that risk now appears reflected in
multiples.
2/ Compute itself becomes even scarcer. Assuming demand continues to accelerate, how exactly do you
deliver the supply everyone was expecting? If you can’t, the scarcity value of existing, energized compute
should rise. That potentially benefits the hyperscalers and owners of deployed capacity. Neoclouds are a
more nuanced debate… some have substantial existing compute and contracted demand, while others
remain dependent on new facilities, financing and power delivery. They’re not all the same.
Overall: The distinction between owning compute today and promising compute tomorrow becomes
enormously important. Ultimately, throttling the supply of compute doesn’t necessarily destroy demand…
it could just push pricing higher for longer.

3) RATES STRATEGY (MARSHALL) – QRA Preview – &
BOTTOM LINE: We expect Treasury to keep 10-to-30y coupon auction sizes steady and maintain or
modestly increase buyback envelope sizes. A reduction in long-end auction sizes is a risk, but it is not our
base case. Instead, we expect Treasury to further adjust its guidance, explicitly signalling potential auction
size adjustments, and eventually begin increasing 2-to-7y auction sizes in May 2027. Our expectation for
unchanged long-end auction sizes reflects a few factors, including that a reduction seems unnecessary
from a liquidity or demand standpoint. In fact, our fair value models suggest that long-end spreads remain
wide to fair value, and the spread curve is somewhat steep, while longer-term forwards are only modestly
above our estimate of fair value.
Treasury Guidance: We expect Treasury may make further changes to its guidance, explicitly nodding
towards the potential for auction size adjustments to come. Consistent with the revision in August and
preexisting framing, we expect language would be two-sided (i.e. “changes” rather than “increases”) with
emphasis on structural trends in demand.
Market Expectations: We think expectations likely lean towards reduced long-end auction sizes, following
the tweak in guidance at the August refundingand mid-August increase in long-end buybacks, leaving
some scope for disappointment and long-end underperformance on the spread curve. However, we think

the extent of any impact should be somewhat contained given that Treasury is unlikely to undo any of the
measures it has taken so far, and wider long-end spreads have been a global phenomenon.

4) EM FX TRADING (MEDIRATTA) – CNY Update
BOTTOM LINE: USDCNY onshore fixing at 6.7180, 30 pips higher than yesterday’s fix at 6.7150, largest
one day increase since Aug 18. DXY moved higher yesterday (prior to the fix) and with US mid-term
event risk, the signalling effect reinforces the message sent with the FX RRR hike – PBOC is targeting the
pace of appreciation. USDCNH bounced to a high of 6.6855 and was sold into as the broader USD turned
weaker with the blue sweep in US elections.
What to Watch: With DXY reversing today (for now), tomorrow’s fixing will be an important signal. The
key metric to watch here is the gap between USDCNH and the onshore fixing – traded as low as -700

before bouncing closer to -450; with a weaker USD backdrop, PBOC might use a sideways fixing pattern
to compress the gap. Expect to see more volatility around the fixing pattern going forward.
Levels: If the fix moves 200-300 pips from the lows, then there is a definite change in intent for
authorities.
USDCNH Path Forward: The overall trend does not change – the trade surplus continues to move higher
as China has managed to capture manufacturing supply chains and increase the value-added component of
exports. This trade surplus is continuously increasing China’s market share across the board and this
continues to cause friction with trading partners – the appreciation path followed by China makes the
surplus appear more sustainable. The backdrop of higher trade surplus and consequently higher exporter
conversions, RMB internalization with possible US-China meetings around APEC and the G20 will
continue to support the path lower for USDCNH.

5) COPOM Preview
LATAM ECON (RAMOS): We expect the BCB to hold the Selic rate at 13.75%, pausing the rate
normalization (‘recalibration’) cycle. The inflation outlook has worsened, the BRL weakened amidst
significant asset price volatility, the budget baseline deteriorated with the costly fiscal announcements and
commitments made during the presidential campaign, and the prospects for a credible near-term fiscal
anchor have dimmed. We expect forward guidance to signal a pause rather than a definitive end to the
cycle. A 25bp cut to 13.50% (30% probability), paired with firmer guidance setting a very high bar for
another cut in December, cannot be ruled out. However, we believe a pause rather than a cut is a superior
choice from a credibility and macro risk management standpoint, and that would not preclude resuming
the normalization cycle once the balance of risks for inflation turns more friendly (less now could easily
lead to more later). Full preview here.
BRL RATES TRADING (FONSECA): Our base case is for the BCB to stay on hold at 13.75%, pausing
the easing cycle after five consecutive 25bps cuts. This is consistent with what we highlighted last week:
activity has continued to lose momentum, but the combination of inflation still above target, renewed
deterioration in expectations, higher oil prices, BRL depreciation and a materially more uncertain
fiscal/political outlook makes another cut difficult to justify at this stage. The market closed last Friday
pricing a ~25% chance of a 25bps cut via Digital Options, followed by a series of rate hikes priced in up to
Jan28, of about 170bps.
