No more Teflon Tech
Nasdaq has become the ultimate pain trade. Bulls buy the rebounds, bears chase the breakdowns, and
both keep getting punished. But the latest selloff looks increasingly different. NDX is crashing through
critical technical levels, tech volatility is approaching Liberation Day panic extremes and positioning
data reveals a brutal cycle of selling, shorting and forced covering. Meanwhile, the crowded AI trade
continues to unwind, with relative winners emerging outside the AI complex.
The ultimate frustration trade
The latest collapse makes the preceding short covering even more painful. NDX is now down more
than 5% as of writing, leaving traders who covered shorts into the rebound watching the market plunge
again. More importantly, the technical picture is awful. Nasdaq is breaking below its range lows and the
longer-term trend line, while trading well below the 200-day MA. What started as a frustrating range
trade is threatening to become something much uglier. The make-or-break levels are giving way.

Crowded longs start blinking
The Nasdaq positioning unwind started before the worst of the earnings disappointment. GS highlights
$5.1bn of non-dealer futures selling between October 20 and 27, ending three consecutive weeks of
buying. Yet net length remained at the 96th percentile on a two-year lookback. Despite the selling,
positioning was still extremely elevated. The crowded long trade had barely begun to unwind.
GS
Selling low, shorting lower
Then came the aggressive bearish positioning. Nasdaq dropped 5.6% between October 27 and
November 2, while futures open interest surged by a massive $16.3bn. October 30 alone saw a
$14.3bn increase, the largest daily jump of 2026 outside roll periods. GS interprets the move as
aggressive new short selling. The crowd was pressing the downside just as the market was
approaching another reversal.
GS
The squeeze nobody enjoyed

The punishment was swift. Nasdaq rebounded 1.6% between November 2 and 4, while futures open
interest declined $4.1bn. Meanwhile, Nasdaq funding recovered relative to SPX. The combination
points to short covering rather than a decisive return of bullish conviction. Traders who pressed the
downside were suddenly forced to reverse course. Sell low, buy high, repeat. Welcome to extreme
markets.
