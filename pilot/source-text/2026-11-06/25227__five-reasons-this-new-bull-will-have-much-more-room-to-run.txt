Fab Five
The bears have had plenty to talk about lately, but the bulls aren't exactly running out of ammunition.
From increasingly attractive tech valuations and fresh mega-cap highs to collapsing oil prices, record
buybacks and favorable seasonality - here are five (not so randomly selected) bullish forces that are
lining up.
1- The valuation re-set
The S&P 500 semiconductor index now trades at just 13.8x forward earnings, a 25% discount to the
broader market and the widest relative discount since December 2018, according to Scott Rubner.
Translation: So the crash is now in the rearview mirror... at least in terms of valuation.
Scott Rubner
15-year wides
Continuing on the semis theme (as it is the most important sector by far right now): The SOX’s current
~2x fwd P/E discount to the market (S&P 500) is near the wides over the last 15 years.
Peter Callahan
Bear market low
And the valuation reset extends beyond semis. The Nasdaq 100 forward P/E has fallen to around 19x,
approaching levels last seen during the 2022 bear market. The difference? This time, the index isn't in
a bear market.
Citadel

2 - Leadership is alive & well
So much for the death of mega-cap leadership. Microsoft has printed a fresh all-time high after
spending much of the past year fighting a negative narrative, while Apple surged more than 5% on
Wednesday, its best session in 18 months. Two of the world's three largest companies are now making
new highs. The bulls could hardly ask for better leadership.
Peter Callahan
@barchart
3 - The energy gift
And the macro backdrop is offering the bulls another helping hand. Brent has plunged roughly $18 in
just five sessions, one of its sharpest five-day declines in recent history.
GS trading desk
Gasoline prices present
Lower oil is already feeding through to gasoline, with US wholesale prices hitting a seven-month low. If
sustained, cheaper energy could provide a welcome boost to consumers heading into the holiday
season.

Javier Blas
4 - Bombastic buybacks
Corporate America is stepping up. US buyback authorizations have reached a record $1.3 trillion this
year, more than 10% above 2025 levels. According to Goldman's trading desk, the number of
companies in open repurchase windows is set to rise from roughly 300 to nearly 500 in the coming
days. The corporate bid is about to get considerably bigger just as we head into year-end.
Goldman Sachs FICC and Equities
5 - Marvellous mid-terms
Since 1952, the 12 months after a midterm election have been the strongest stretch over the four-year
cycle for the S&P 500. Across 18 midterms, the index averaged a +7.5% gain through year end,
+13.9% over the following 6 months, and +15.5% over 12 months. The S&P 500 was higher at both
points (+6m and +12m) in all 18 instances. 

GS Market Data
The best year of the cycle awaits
More or less the same as the previous chart, but some things are so good they need to be stated twice.
Year three has historically been the strongest year of the presidential cycle, with average S&P 500
gains of 14%. Next year is year three. The bulls have history on their side, too.
Sean Emory
