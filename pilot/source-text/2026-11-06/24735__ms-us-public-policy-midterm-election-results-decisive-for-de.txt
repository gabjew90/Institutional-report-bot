Results, Policies, & Market Impacts to Focus On
Summary: In a "cohesive" Democratic majority scenario, uncertainty due to a change in 
control can act as a headwind to equity markets on the index level, similar to 2022 
midterm volatility amid the newly Republican-controlled House. However, potential delays 
in SNAP/Medicaid cuts provide a tailwind to consumer sectors & some upside risk to growth 
forecasts. The party-line reconciliation route is closed and removes the main source of 
upside to Treasury's borrowing needs. While delaying SNAP/Medicaid cuts works in the 
opposite direction, this is likely small against a $2 trillion deficit. The front-end matters 
more as a majority can coalesce around policy asks, using December funding deadline and 
2027 debt limit as leverage. In FX, we expect near-term trading to remain consistent with 
our defense regime, favoring JPY, CHF, and EUR against USD. AI-related headwinds could 
leave AUD lagging on crosses, while targeted tariff risks could weigh on CAD against low 
yielders. Stronger congressional resistance to renewed geopolitical escalation could also 
put downward pressure on NOK/SEK.
Plausible Policy Paths
Four things to focus on:
1. Fiscal: We expect the OBBBA SNAP and Medicaid cuts to potentially be rolled 
back, extended, or softened in tandem with negotiations around fiscal deadlines, 
given that Democrats likely have a large enough majority to navigate differences 
within the caucus & coalesce around a small set of policy objectives. See here for 
estimated impacts of rolling back the cuts.  Overall, we think deficits stay around 
6% of GDP, even with these cuts extended.  
2. Executive-led policy: We think policy uncertainty since the beginning of President 
Trump's second term has been tied to executive, not legislative, action. Policies on 
tariffs, geopolitics, trade (particularly the USMCA review) and deregulation, and 
export controls are all likely to persist, meaning we should expect a certain level 
of policy uncertainty to continue.  
3. Data center restrictions: The practical risk that we see is that incremental state-
level rules from newly elected candidates split the country into areas that are 
open to new data centers and those without. The levers that actually bind a 
project, including interconnection, large-load rate design, siting, and tax 
abatements, sit almost entirely with state commissions, legislatures, and county 
boards. Accordingly, changes in control of certain governorships warrant 
monitoring, although the exposure is likely to be limited to specific jurisdictions. In 
Texas, the election of Democrat Gina Hinojosa increases policy risk with respect to 
the pace of data center approvals and construction. Governor Hinojosa's campaign 
platform included a moratorium on new data center approvals, and given the 
Texas Governor's multiple regulatory agencies that are involved in approving new 
data center projects, we see the risk of many projects being delayed significantly. 
By far, the most important election result, from the perspective of data center 
development, was the Texas gubernatorial election. See the next section for our 
full analysis.

Key Market & Economic Impacts
• US equities: The midterms represent the most obvious clearing event we 
discussed in last week's note, with history providing an encouraging signal. Since 
1982, large-cap equities have gained approximately 15% on a cap-weighted basis 
and 16% on an equal-weighted basis in the six months following midterms, with 
both measures rising roughly 22% over the subsequent year. In our view, investors 
broadly anticipate a Democratic sweep with a narrow Senate majority. Thus, given 
that this was accompanied by a Democratic gubernatorial win in TX, this outcome 
could contribute to near-term volatility. However, we think it could mark the final 
phase of the rolling correction we've been discussing since the summer. Renewed 
military escalation with Iran or additional tariffs following the elections could also 
pose near-term risks.
• Cross-asset: We continue to recommend OW global equities vs UW core fixed 
income on the back of the midterm election results. Our constructive view on 
global equities so far this year has been underpinned by our expectations that the 
AI capex cycle will remain intact. With  limited change likely in US tech-related 
investment at a top-down level, we don't expect earnings support across regions, 
including Europe and EM,  to fall away. The outlook for global fixed income also 
remains broadly unchanged. We expect corporate credit issuance to remain 
elevated, supported by digital infrastructure funding needs despite less 
constructive sentiment around AI-related capex. Elevated issuance could put 
upward pressure on spreads, particularly in lower-quality credit. Beyond AI, oil 
prices and ex-US fiscal risks remain important drivers of equities, yields, and stock-
bond correlations, alongside US politics. The midterm election results do not 
materially alter those factors. Until they change, neither does our allocation: we 
remain OW equities, UW core fixed income.

• US economics: Our overall outlook for resilient growth and continued, albeit

• AI-related financing: The focus of AI-related financing could increasingly shift 
toward chip financing relative to power-shell financing. By the time a project 
reaches the chip-installation stage, data-center construction and energization, the 
activities most exposed to potential policy headwinds, would typically have already 
been completed. This shift could contribute to a more sustained flattening of  
credit curves.

Lame Duck: What Happens Next?
We highlight the below policy catalysts to watch into year-end:
• Funding: The government passed a continuing resolution (CR) to keep funding 
levels flat through December 11. At that point, Congress will need to pass either 
another CR or full-year appropriations to avoid another shutdown. Democratic 
leadership has signaled a preference for finishing the bills in the lame duck session, 
but the election results, in our view, should decide whether leaders prolong the 
debate into 2027. Our prior research  highlights that the shutdown impact on 
markets is limited to delayed data releases rather than material economic 
downside risk. We also noted in our DC takeaways note that experts see 
meaningful risk that a CR runs into CY27.
• Defense: Our base case is that the FY27 NDAA passes by year-end. What to watch 
in the bill: We see potential for the partial inclusion of some export control 
legislation, including the MATCH Act (aligning allied controls), AI OVERWATCH 
(codifying Commerce’s chip export policy), Chip Security (location verification), and 
RASA (the Remote Access Security Act). Overall, we think the direction of travel is 
more restrictions, increasingly driven by Congress while the administration 
prioritizes stability with China. On DoW base budget funding vs. supplemental:

meetings unfold throughout the duration of this year.

Data Center Restrictions in Focus  
See Powered Shell Providers: Midterm Implications for the "Time to Power" Stocks for 
more  
Democrat Gina Hinojosa's victory in the  gubernatorial race in Texas could bring greater 
policy uncertainty and constraint on future supply, increasing the relative value and 
pricing power of existing, power-secured facilities. In August, Texas paused portions of 
its data center interconnection process after Gov. Greg Abbott ordered an audit of all data 
center projects in ERCOT’s queue. Interconnection requests had reached approximately 
474 GW, more than 5x ERCOT’s record peak electricity demand, with data centers 
representing roughly 90% of new power requests. Gov. Abbott warned that development 
on this scale could threaten grid reliability and stability, prompting ERCOT to delay its 
Batch Zero transmission planning study for the first group of projects entering Texas’ new 
large load interconnection process. The audit was intended in part to distinguish credible 
projects from speculative requests that may be inflating Texas’ demand outlook. In terms 
of actions a Texas Governor  can take: (1) an executive order to temporarily pause new 
projects until the legislature can put real guidelines in place (but if a Republican-led 
legislature acts quickly, this could be a number of months), (2) make appointments to the 
power regulatory body (the PUCT - these are 6-year terms that come up in 2027), (3) 
provide policy direction to various state agencies on permitting (ie halting permits that an 
agency hasn’t issued yet). Governor-elect Hinojosa has advocated for a a moratorium until 
laws are passed (applying to new permits, not for those already under construction). 
What would require legislation: (1) more durable changes, including tax changes 
including ending tax breaks (sales-tax exemption), (2) giving localities power to reject 
projects (Governor-elect Hinojosa has planned to empower local officials to negotiate 
with developers, which requires a change in the law), (3) codifying ratepayer protections, 
and (4) union hiring requirements all will require consent from the state legislature. In 
Ohio, the election of the Democratic candidate, Dr. Amy Acton, creates significant risk of 
data center project delays in the state. The largest project at risk in Ohio is the 10 
gigawatt project involving NVIDIA and a major US AI lab. Another large project that is 
under construction is the 1.5 gigawatt Amazon facility in Fayette County. In May, Ohio 
enacted a moratorium on consideration of new data center tax exemption requests, while 
the General Assembly studies the growth of data centers in Ohio (tax exemption is a 
critically important consideration when a data center selects a location).  
Stocks with largest Texas data center and Behind the Meter power exposure: CIFR, 
MARA, GLXY, SEI, VST, CEG, AEP, SRE, CNP and NRG, power equipment stocks.

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)
Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including 
standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages 
in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, 
as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment 
speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the 
MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even 
though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any 
premium paid.
