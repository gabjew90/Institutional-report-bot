US Equity Positioning Update: Volatility
Reprices Higher Ahead of Midterms
Conor Lyons
02-Nov-2026, 19:36 ET (EST)
Hedge Funds aggressively cut risk last week, but most of the reduction in exposure occurred on Wednesday
and Thursday prior to Friday’s market sell-off. Last week was the third largest degross week YTD. Long
exposure reductions and increased shorts dropped nets to the 10th percentile on a 5y lookback. Within
sectors, Semis net exposure declined the most over the month of October and is back to the July lows.
Hyperscaler exposure saw large fluctuations during the week; nets finished near local lows, but the group is
still one of the largest net length pockets for the AI trade. Industrials, Financials, Healthcare, and
Communication Services short exposure are all at or near 5y highs.
Composite positioning moved further underweight, driven by big reductions from Balanced and Value Funds.
Risk Control funds sold an estimated $160 bn of equities over the past two days, cutting exposure to the 65th
percentile down from the 93rd percentile last week. This decline in exposure is accompanied by a decline in
their sensitivity to market volatility; however, they are still expected to sell ~$105-120bn of equity risk if the
market has another -2% day. CTA risk saw similar declines, with CTA global equity exposure in the 9th
percentile, after hitting various sell triggers Friday. Levered and Inverse ETFs sold $30 bn of equity risk on
Friday’s move, but their exposure is now below median levels.
Both index and single-name implied volatility has repriced higher in the wake of the price action over the past
few days, with both VIX and VIXEQ closing at their highest levels since July. Second-order risk metrics like
skew also posted sharp increases as the market has increased the relative cost of hedges, suggesting clients
have moved quickly to hedge exposure. Looking ahead, the upcoming US midterm elections are pricing the
largest one-day breakeven move for the SPX through the end of the year at just over 1%.
