As growth becomes scarcer, the composition of growth matters increasingly. The market is likely to favor
compounders with durable growth profiles over high-growth cyclicals, which may face slowing
momentum as higher rates, elevated oil prices, and rising energy costs create headwinds. De-escalation-
driven investment narratives are becoming harder to underwrite in an environment where the Fed is still
tightening, oil remains near $100, and 10-year yields are above 5%, although these themes remain
compelling from a narrative perspective. Our Call on AI over cyclicals/SPXXAI Index has worked well,
and we still like it. Ultimately, midterms beckon and the result probably won’t be risk positive.
A softer PCE and NFP print have helped the market push out the next Fed hike, but exposure in Europe
feels increasingly uncomfortable as fiscal risk is building. Japan is the standout region in Asia with a
$140bn AI datacenter push driving renewed focus on regional AI capex beneficiaries. Five key points:
Rates-sensitive equities have felt the pain. At the index level, equities have continued to grind higher amid
the rates market meltdown, but the market is (once again) being held up by the AI trade. Beneath the
surface, rate-sensitive pockets have been under pressure. Our US Homebuilders (GSXUBLDR) basket has
declined -17.5% over the last 3 months, and while Non-Profitable Tech (GSXUNPTC) had a strong start to
the year, it has drawn down -11.4% since early July. Lending Sensitive Equities (GSXULEND) have
underperformed in tandem while Regional Banks (GSCBRGBK) have been more resilient relative to their
rates beta and could have downside if rates remain elevated. A softer PCE and NFP are pushing yields
lower, which could drive significant relief in these rates-sensitive pockets if sustained. Weak market
breadth also implies scope for a broader beta rally, which we like expressing via our sector/industry-
neutral beta basket (GSPUBETA). For more cautious investors, we recommend hedging via our Beta-ex
AI pair (GSPUBETX).
Fundamentals on POWER remain intact. Demand forecasts continue to be revised higher. GS research
sees 108 GW of datacenter power demand by 2030 (23% CAGR off a 2025 base) and a 3.5% if total US
power demand CAGR, of which datacenters contribute just over 2.5% (1.8pp front-of-the-meter and 0.8pp
behind-the-meter). Equipment demand remains exceptional. GEV is guiding to 125 GW of backlog plus
slot reservation agreements by year end, roughly 6x current capacity. Construction on the ground is
materially stronger than the headlines suggest. Valuations have de-rated across the complex: IPPs are
down ~30% over 12m, gas infrastructure names are down ~13% over 3m. Behind-the-meter is the key
structural shift to the 4.5 year interconnection queue and is expected to make up ~20% of datacenter
power demand by 2030. State-level permitting friction has, on the margin, increased the preference for

“bring-your-own-power”. We are monitoring the upcoming catalysts in this area: FERC ruling on PJM
large-load framework, 3Q Earnings, November midterms, and ERCOT datacenter interconnection audit.
We like owning upside in this theme into year-end, particularly as rising agentic AI adoption drives higher
compute and power needs. The AI power story in Asia is no different and we’ve just launched a basket to
gain exposure to a critical component of next-gen AI datacenters with our Asia Battery Backup Units
(GSXABBUS). The basket provides exposure to the BBU supply chain across batteries, modules and
energy storage systems. Note our research colleagues expect the global BBU TAM to expand 23x between
2025 and 2030E (see here).
French fragility becoming a Euro problem? OAT-Bund spreads hovering around 150bps with signs of
contagion into BTPs this week implying the market is now pricing broader European sovereign risk. In
equities, European Banks (SX7E) have been hit hard following an exceptional 18m rally and given
significant net length in the trade. If French fiscal risk remains contained, we would be buyers of dips in
EU Banks given a solid structural earnings outlook, but we remain cautious on stocks with weak balance
sheets and upcoming debt maturities (GSXECLFF). We also note a pick-up in investor interest on equities
vulnerable to Euro weakness. Our European Domestic vs. US exposed pair trade (GSPEDOAM) has
historically been most reactive to moves in EURUSD.
Japan separates itself from the rest of Asia. Japan’s strong regional performance is being driven by AI
leadership and BoJ normalization that remains gradual enough for equities to absorb. A less hawkish BoJ
summary of opinions has supported sentiment on Japan equities, while flows in recent weeks have
increasingly favored Japan AI versus EM AI beneficiaries. Our favorite Japan AI trade is our Optical
Networking basket (GSXAJONE) as the key beneficiary of the next leg of AI infra spending. See GIR’s
latest note on optical fibers here.
Pulse Check on China Consumer. Golden Week consumer data will be the key thing to watch when China
reopens next week. Another disappointing print would reinforce concerns that stimulus remains
incremental rather than transformative. The macro data shows a widening production-consumption gap,
which our economists argue reflects a structural pivot to high tech manufacturing, supportive of headline
output, but less labor-intensive, and negative for employment, household income, and consumption. We
like positioning via shorts on Asia Consumer Inertia stocks (GSXASWCH) that face pressure from weak
discretionary spend and the longer-term risk of AI-enabled switching behavior. Inflecting China consumer
sentiment is also key for a turnaround in European Luxury stocks (GSXELUXG), though Liberation-Day
low valuation and stable EPS momentum have driven a pick-up in interest ahead of mid-month 3Q
updates.
Bonus point – Brazil first round elections this weekend. We’ve been flagging Brazil Rate Sensitive
(GSBZRATE) as the key equity trade into elections. A Bolsonaro win scenario should be bullish equities,
and in particular rate sensitive equities which could re-rate up to 30% in a 200bp rate cut outcome. More
color in last week’s note. Our Brazil colleagues will host a First-Round Takeaways webinar early Monday
AM. Register HERE.
