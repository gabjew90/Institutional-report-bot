 
Third Quarter Ends With Surging Yields Closing At 
24 Year High... As Stocks Yawn 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 01, 2026 - 11:11 AM 
Another day, another total disconnect between a handful of bubble stocks, and everything else. 
After starting the day on the backfoot, stocks spiked after the cooler than expected PCE... 
 
... which however was largely the result of a change in methodology, while the not-so-closely 
watched Supercore PCE jumped the most in months after "Other Services" jumped by the most 
on record... 

 
 
... which lowered Oct hike odds to 37%, half where they were two days ago .... 
 
... which helped corroborate Williams’s comments from yesterday about needing only one more 
hike "late this year."  

 
But even with rate hike fears receding, ominously rates themselves continued to chug higher, 
and the 10Y yield hit 5.30% (appropriately after Cramer said on CNBC first thing today he is a 
buyer of Treasuries). 
 
This is the 7th consecutive month of yields rising, surpassing the 6 month stretch observed 
during 2023 when stocks slumped on stagflation fears.  
 

 
The real 10y is now up 57bp in a month, which as we noted earlier this week, Goldman warned 
breaks the two-standard-deviation "speed limit" it links historically to negative equity returns. As 
Goldman's own Tony Pasquariello's said the 2-SD (~50bp) monthly "strain" rule "was just 
triggered." In fact, the 1-month real-rate move is one of the worst since the 2013 "Taper 
Tantrum" - when it culminated with a powerful market selloff. Effectively any time the 10Y has 
moved more than 50bps in a month, stocks have gotten hammered... 
 
... but not this time, as complacent investors are clutching their Mag7/memory/semi/chip pearls 
and refusing to sell, ignoring that the bubble only lasts as long as new debt flows in to fund the 
gargantuan cash burn.  
In short, Goldman's MarketStrats desk calls this move in "one of the most significant rates 
repricings of the year."  
Worse, rates are rising, despite oil actually sliding for much of the day, a divergence which 
Goldman Delta-One head Rich Privorotsky said is the biggest risk for stocks. 

 
 
Some more scary stats from Goldman: 
The 2y is up 145bp YTD and the 10y has risen seven straight months. GS credit strats have the 
10y at 5.29%, the 100th percentile on every lookback back to 2004. 
And yet, stocks don't seem to care. Or rather, a handful of stocks - those which define the 
market-weighted S&P - don't care. The same can not be said for the rest of the market, as the 
median stock is now down 5% in the past month, the S&P is unchanged and the only thing 
keeping the S&P afloat is the 6% gain in semis in the past month. 

 
 
Remarkably, we now have a 12th consecutive day of more 52 week lows than highs (and 17 of 
the past 18), Of note, every previous time this has happened, stocks were near the lows after a 
selloff and inflecting higher, only this time the S&P is at all time highs. 
 
It's only fitting that a quarter defined by catastrophic breadth closed with 263 more decliners 
than advancers. 

 
 
And perhaps the most stunning chart: the ratio of the equal-weighted ETF (RSP) to the S&P just 
hit an all time low. 
 
Thematically, today's price action has been somewhat mixed with the usual mega cap tech 
largely higher across the board (ex-META which gives back some of yday’s OpenAI developer 
day-related gains) while under the hood, there are continued signs of an unwind with shorts 

 
getting covered // software > semis taking cue from JBL -7% on a beat/raise and weighing on 
sentiment into MU tonight vs FDS +4% on a mixed print, outlook).  
Of note, Goldman's high beta momentum basket appears to finally be slagging, down for the 
first time after 12 consecutive days higher. 
 
Adding insult to bad breadth injury, the equal-weight S&P 500 just suffered its seventh straight 
weekly decline, something which has been seen only twice before, during 2002 and 2022 bear 
markets. Yet the headline S&P 500 remains broadly stable, as tech megacaps mask the 
collapse below the smooth surface. 

 
 
The gap between index calm and individual-stock volatility is now the widest since the dot-com 
crash in 2000, as is the market breadth calculated between the index and the median stock as 
Goldman noted over the weekend.  
 
Source: Goldman 

 
Goldman's desk summarizes the mood well: Overall activity level today remains muted, despite 
mkt volumes pacing +10% ahead of yday,as most activity with concentrate at the close today 
●​ HFs are small net sellers, but the short ratio below 40% feels like taking risk off ahead of 
MU and into quarter-end 
●​ LOs are +8% better to buy with notable demand across Software and Mega Tech 
But it's not just stocks: credit is also cracking thanks to the continued pile up in data centers, 
with the peak pain focused on CCC-rated junk bonds where OAS is now 998bp: +140bp in a 
month, +226bp in 3 months, at the 100th percentile over 1y and 3y. HY OAS rose +43bp in a 
week to 309bp (97th percentile), CDX HY +36.2bp to 337.2, and IG all-in yields sit at 6.48%, the 
highest since 2004. Privorotsky: credit "widened and rates kept selling off even as oil fell." 
The fulcrum security here is Oracle which has continued to sink, with its CDS hitting a record 
wide this week while its bonds tumble. 
 
And while credit is cracking, rates are getting absolutely rocked, with the MOVE index hitting 
another post-war high of 110, with the divergence from the S&P now the biggest on record. 

 
 
Finally, looking at the 3rd quarter which just concluded, Rabobank's Michael Every had this 
recap: 
●​ With today’s session still to come, the S&P is +2.3% q-o-q vs. +14.9% in Q2 and -4.6% 
in Q1. The Dow -1.9% vs. +12.9% and -3.6%, the Nasdaq +2.2% vs. +21.4% and -7.1%, 
Eurostoxx are flat vs. +13.4% and -3.6%, the FTSE +1.3% vs +3.2% and +2.5%, but the 
Nikkei is -6.5% vs. +37.2% and +1.4%, the Shanghai composite -6.4% vs. +5.2% and 
-1.9%, and the Indian Sensex -5.2% vs. +6.3% and -15.6%. 
●​ Q3 saw the most dramatic up move in bond yield so far this year. With today yet to 
come: US 2s are now 4.90% (+72bp q-o-q), 10s 5.24% (+76bps), and 30s 5.57% 
(+60bps), the highest since 2002; Bund 2s are 3.29% (+76bps), 10s 3.62% (+76bps), 
and 30s 3.95% (+53bps); OATs 2s are 3.64% (+94bps), 10s 4.81% (+116bps), and 30s 
5.39% (+93bps); Gilts 2s are 4.91% (+76bps), 10s 5.41% (+65bps), and 30s 5.92% 
(+44bps); JGB 2s are 1.96% (+59bps), 10s 3.11% (+43bps), and 30s 4.20% (+26bps). 
●​ Q3 didn’t just drop hopes of ‘lower for longer’ but doused them in scarce diesel and set 
fire to them. In Q1, we got just one rate hike from a G20 central bank, the RBA. In Q2, 
we got four, the RBA, ECB, BOJ, and Bank Indonesia. In Q3, we got the RBA (who just 
hiked again yesterday and are seen doing so again in the new year), the ECB, the BOJ, 
plus the Fed – and the issue is how much more might be needed there and elsewhere. 
●​ FX markets were also caught on the hop if expecting a weaker USD, as is so often the 
case, with a notable exception with huge implications for other markets. EUR/USD is 

 
around -1.1% q-o-q and GBP/USD had an unexciting Q3 while the DXY is slightly up 
right now. However, there was huge action in USD/JPY in the opposite direction that may 
carry through to the Yen Carry Trade if it carries on. EM FX are notably also starting to 
slip against the dollar again with the exception of CNY, which is a ballgame unto itself. 
●​ Brent oil is $104 right now, well up over a Q3 it started at $73 while the 3-2-1 crack 
spread is $62 vs. $17, which is what matters more, so we moved from an effective total 
of $90 to $166. The FT today notes that oil prices and US Treasury yields are in tightest 
relationship since 1990 – you mean during the late Cold War, when it was all about 
geopolitics at core? What a surprise! 
And with all that, we're off to the final quarter of the year, which promises to be a rollercoaster, 
heading into the historically volatile month of October, followed with a midterm election where 
the only question is how big will the blue sweep be in Congress.
