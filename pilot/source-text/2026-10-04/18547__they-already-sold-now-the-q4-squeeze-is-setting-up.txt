The squeeze setup
Tech continues to absorb the rates shock, with NDX approaching a major breakout level and SOX
already pushing through resistance.
Underneath the surface, the setup is getting more interesting. AI spending remains remarkably
resilient, CTA positioning has gone from crowded long to below neutral, and the corporate bid is about
to return. Meanwhile, oil remains the obvious macro tail.
Cleaner positioning, returning flows and tech refusing to break. The squeeze setup is getting
interesting.

The AI machine isn't rate sensitive
One of the more interesting features of this cycle is just how insensitive the underlying AI buildout has
become to rates.
GS expects hyperscaler debt issuance to surge to $420bn in 2027, yet notes that interest expense
remains a very small part of hyperscaler earnings. In other words, higher yields haven't been enough
to derail the capex machine.
That's interesting if yields finally reverse lower. The spending cycle hasn't needed lower rates. Tech
multiples certainly wouldn't mind them. (Captures debt issued by the following companies: Alphabet,
Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, and Oracle.)

Plenty of firepower
The spending numbers look enormous, but so do the balance sheets. MS has hyperscalers at just 0.4x
net leverage with cash equal to 132% of debt.
The AI buildout isn't running into a balance-sheet wall yet.

The reset
The interesting part isn't just the fundamental strength. Expectations had already been reset.
GS says expectations for conventional memory and HBM pricing were largely washed out and are now
starting to recover, with more investors looking for upside to current pricing assumptions.
That's exactly the type of backdrop where improving price action can force people back into the trade.
Reset expectations + improving fundamentals + a technical breakout is a decent recipe for a squeeze.

The mother ship
While the market is debating the health of the semi space, Taiwan seems pretty confident.
We outlined our SOX logic last week, and we continue to see plenty of catch-up potential.

They've already sold
One of the more interesting parts of Rubner's latest work is just how much the systematic reset has
already happened.
US equity CTA positioning has collapsed from +2.35σ at the end of August to -0.80σ today, a more than
3-sigma swing in just one month. The flow asymmetry has flipped. (Chart shows US Equity Total – CTA
Aggregate Positioning, Z-Score of Net Positioning, Since January 2024).

The corporate bid is coming back
Rubner points out that US corporates have already authorized a record $1.3tn of buybacks this year.
The important part now is timing. Buyback windows start reopening from October 15, with the
corporate bid historically accelerating into November. A lot of authorized firepower is waiting for an
open window.
Citadel
The midterm setup
Rubner highlights a powerful Q4 seasonal pattern.
Since 1930, the S&P 500 has gained an average 5.6% in Q4 during midterm years, almost twice the
2.9% average across all years. October and November have historically been the strongest months of
the midterm calendar.
The calendar is about to become considerably more friendly.

The buffer is thinner
A balanced September isn't really the point.
The tail is what happens if another attack takes Gulf flows back below 50%, this time with a much
thinner inventory cushion. GS argues stocks remain comfortably above operational minimums, but the
system has considerably less room to absorb another major disruption.
That's why $100 oil can coexist with a currently balanced market. Latest oil note here.
GS
A different oil setup
Oil vol has been remarkably well behaved despite the surge in crude since July.
OVX is trading around the same levels as when oil was below $80, even with crude now near $100.
That's a very different setup from February/March, when rising oil was accompanied by a sharp
repricing of oil volatility.
