Bond volatility has risen near YTD highs as the global rate selloff intensifies. For much of the year, income helped offset the price 
impact of rising yields. However, as of yesterday, all major US fixed income sectors had slipped into negative territory YTD. And this is 
not solely a US phenomenon. Case in point: France’s 10-year government bond yield topped 4.9% for the first time since 2002, while its 
spread versus German bonds (129 bps) reached its widest level since 2012, during the European debt crisis.

CCC-rated spreads rose above 1,000 bps for the first time since March 2023 and have widened nearly 450 bps since February, while the 
gap versus BB-rated bonds is the widest since 2020. Broader high-yield spreads have also widened sharply over the past two weeks (+49 
bps to 318 bps) amid heightened rate volatility, though they remain below their March peak and long-term averages. With investment-
grade spreads holding near 80 bps and IG yields above 6%, we continue to favor higher-quality credit.

S&P 500’s Index-Level Performance Masks Weakness Beneath The Surface
Source: FactSet
Chart of the Day: CCC You Later
Despite the 10-year Treasury yield climbing above 5.3% by the end of September, the S&P 500 was resilient, slipping just 0.5% for the 
month. Under the surface, however, performance was far less uniform. After a period of broadening participation, leadership is 
narrowing, with the S&P 500 Equal Weight Index on track for a 7th straight weekly decline. Meanwhile, 43% of S&P 500 constituents are 
in a bear market, with the average stock down ~21% from its peak. We expect market leadership to remain narrow until yields stabilize.
A mirror image of the yield-driven pressure on gold has been the strength of the US dollar, up 3.7% YTD and at its highest level since 
April 2025. We believe the dollar’s rally is becoming increasingly stretched for two reasons. First, during the Iran conflict, the dollar has 
generally moved in tandem with oil prices, and a meaningful drop in oil could trigger profit-taking. Second, the dollar’s advance 
reflects expectations for nearly four Fed hikes over the next 12 months, while we expect only one more hike in this tightening path.
