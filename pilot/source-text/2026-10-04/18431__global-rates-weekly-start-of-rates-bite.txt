Data & central bank guidance will now determine the extent of future moves. Our views: 
• 
US: we remain paid 2Y & in flatteners, though we had to reset stop in 2s30s 
flattener (see Rates – US). Key focus = today’s NFP. BofA econ expects steady 4.1%

AU: we stay paid Nov RBA OIS. Key focus = Melbourne Institute inflation print that

• 
Japan: bearish front end & bullish back end bias. Key focus = extraordinary Diet

What we learned 
There are early signs that large rate moves are starting to bite financial conditions 
(spread widening in OATs, EU periphery, US HY). Rates restricting financial conditions is 
a precondition for the selloff to stop (unless macro data softens first). Central banks are 
starting to push back but will only be credible if conditions stay tight / tighten further or 
upcoming data softens. Rate moves have also become less tightly correlated with 
commodities as broader market impacts strengthen.

Rates may be biting  
US rates twist steepened on the week ahead of Sept payroll data. Our economists 
expect a below consensus 60k NFP due to unfavorable seasonals but expect U3 to 
remain stable at 4.1% (see: Sep jobs preview). This number or lower would likely drive a 
meaningful bull steepening of the UST curve & see terminal Fed pricing drop from 4.7% 
to 4.55-4.6%. A print above BBG consensus 85k would likely see bear flattening that 
could take terminal Fed pricing to 4.8%+. Labor matters but inflation still drives. 
This week’s core PCE data should give the Fed a bit more breathing room on the policy 
path. A 3.0% core PCE shifts the extent of Fed hikes implied by our 2 preferred 
frameworks: (1) standard Taylor with Fed star variables = now implies a peak FF rate of 
4.8%, close to market pricing (Exhibit 1) (2) real FF history = to achieve the typical lower 
end of real FF rate will now require a 5.5% terminal (Exhibit 8). PCE drop is mostly due 
to method change, but it gives the Fed room & doves case to move slower.

Sharp steepening = real uncertainty & vol spike 
We have been surprised by the extent of sharp steepening in the 2s30s curve. We 
attribute the steepening to elevated uncertainty & limited client interest to fade the 
move. Higher rate hedging flows have also likely exacerbated the move.  
We make the following observations on the recent steepening move: (1) it is almost all 
real rate driven (2) comes amidst some dovish Fed speak, i.e. Williams & Jefferson, which 
suggest higher real rate needed in future if Fed not delivering it today (3) occurs with 
recent soft auctions & limited investor macro conviction (4) consistent with spike in 
MOVE index, which implies wider range of rate outcomes & greater compensation 
demanded (Exhibit 9). The vol move reflects both higher fwds and some normalization of 
the vol regime; this suggests implied vol may ease if yields consolidate, but the market 
likely needs to adjust to a structurally higher rates-vol regime near term. 
The rate vol spike is causing a breakdown in some recent RV relationships & de-risking. 
De-risking was seen in the sharp spread tightening over recent sessions. We hold our 2Y 
& 30Y ASW widening views but recognize they may be pressured with higher rates vol.  
The recent spread tightening & RV de-risking drove UST to take full $6b in the 10-20y 
buyback, which saw largest par amount offered for a long-end buyback (Exhibit 10). The 
buyback reflects “liquidity support” as the program originally intended. It does not 
reflect a UST that aggressively bid to take down the max offering. Buybacks still fall far 
short of Bessent “interventionist” approach & remain as originally conceived.

US midterms: D sweep tech reg vs R sweep fiscal easing 
This week we published views on the US midterm election. Our US econ take: " We 
assume a split Congress in our baseline outlook. A Democratic sweep would yield similar 
policy: limited legislation, flat appropriations, and an administration focused on 
geopolitics & deregulation. While rhetoric around AI regulation would intensify, 
Democrats would lack the Senate supermajority needed to pass meaningful legislation,

likely be the most supportive outcome for growth, but also the most negative for the

gridlock and limited new legislation. Democratic control of Congress would likely be 
modestly negative for growth & risk assets, resulting in a small bull flattening of the

expansion risks, likely leading to a bear flattening of the curve (Exhibit 11).

French bonds: what next? 
6 factors have accelerated OAT-Bund widening this week 
The 10y OAT-Bund spread widened by over 30bp this week. At 140bp, it is now c.10bp 
off sovereign crisis levels.  A confluence of factors has driven this move. We name five: 
(1) stops on long positions (esp in 3-5y and in futures), (2) negative convexity flows (esp 
around futures CTD change), (3) potential portfolio shifts around month-end, (4) rising 
supply concerns (2027 funding plan), (5) incremental noise on the political front, and (6) 
secular bearish technical trends – see technicals below.      
10y OAT-Bunds in 100-150bp, watch incremental news 
Fair value for the 10y OAT-Bund spread stands at 100-110bp, based on the long-term 
joint dynamics of EGB spreads and the short-term directionality to the EUR front-end. 
That said, for buyers to step in and fade this cheapness, we believe the spread needs to 
stabilise first, and the context needs to be favourable. In the note, we present a list of 
potential incremental news on budget, macro, politics and other factors.

5 backstops that would prevent OAT-Bunds > 150bp 
The five backstops that we had flagged in French bonds under the radar are still valid; 
(1) a strong reduction in WAM of supply; (2) more cautious political parties; (3) domestic 
buying; (4) shifts from periphery into OATs on risk-off contagion; and (5) ECB action, via 
communication before any forceful intervention. If OAT-Bunds get quickly over 150bp, 
several of these backstops could be triggered. Contagion would call for ECB action. 
ECB backstop: going through the options  
The ECB has backstops. For too tight financial conditions, rates are the tool (starting 
with verbal interventions and guidance). The balance sheet comes into play for disorderly 
moves in speed and/or magnitude on spreads/long-term rates. Verbal intervention has 
started, but the ECB’s pain threshold tends to be higher than that of markets. 
Economics: we worry a lot, especially about sentiment 
We cut our French growth forecasts again, to 0.3% and 0.6% for this year and next (-
10bp and -20bp, resp). We worry about the negative feedback loop between financing 
costs, growth, budget deficits. The corporate sector may be the most fragile part here.

RBA hikes to 4.60% but terminal rate expectations pared back 
The RBA unanimously decided to increase the cash rate by 25bp to 4.60%, taking the 
cash rate to its highest level in 15 years. The statement maintained a hawkish tone, 
focusing on upside risks to inflation, and noted the Board would hike again, if necessary.  
However, market pricing has now been pared back to 7bp for November and ~36bp by 
mid-2027. The press conference was viewed as dovish, in particular the Governor noted 
that the Board considered a pause and that the impact of previous hikes needed time to 
flow through. Trimmed mean inflation then surprised to the downside, rising 0.24% m/m 
(vs. BofA forecast 0.38%).  
Close short 1y2y AU OIS vs ESTR OIS 
We close our short 1y2y AU OIS vs ESTR OIS trade at its stop following the downside 
surprise to CPI (entry: 175bp; target: 200bp; stop: 160bp). We are still bullish EUR front 
end, but our conviction around an RBA November hike is lower.  
Stay paid November RBA OIS 
That said, we retain our pay November RBA OIS recommendation (current: 7bp). Risk: 
lower oil prices. Current entry levels are now more attractive while inflation risks remain 
skewed to the upside. Next week’s Melbourne Institute inflation print should provide an 
early read on September inflation and could move RBA pricing higher. Overall, our 
economists forecast for 3Q trimmed mean to rise by 0.91% q/q, implying the November 
meeting will likely be a very close call, and market pricing should be closer to ~13bp.  
Sell-off continues but AU rates outperform  
Australian government bonds have continued to follow the global sell-off, with the 10-
year ACGB yield trading around 5.4%, its highest level since 2011. In our view, a 
meaningful rally is unlikely until domestic data show clear signs of slowing demand and 
easing inflation pressures, which we do not expect until 1Q27. Nevertheless, with the 
RBA appearing closer to the end of its hiking cycle while the Fed has further to go, AU 
rates have outperformed in recent weeks and risks skew to further outperformance.

This is an excerpt from Liquid Insight, 01 October 2026 
Why we remain bullish on long-end JGBs 
Given the rise in global bond yields, long-end JGB yields have been trading near record 
highs. However, we do not view the recent increase in yields as being driven by Japan-
specific catalysts. Rather, we see it as a spillover from the global bond sell-off, led 
primarily by the United States. Indeed, despite the extraordinary Diet session scheduled 
to begin on October 5, there have been few new domestic catalysts for a sustained bond 
sell-off. As such, we believe the rise in JGB yields has largely reflected higher overseas 
yields. 
At the same time, net JGB supply as a share of nominal GDP is expected to decline after 
peaking in 2026, suggesting that supply-demand dynamics should gradually improve. 
Moreover, neither our survey nor market flow data points to a meaningful deterioration 
in demand for long-end JGBs. Supporting factors include the preference for flattener 
positions highlighted in our FX and Rates Sentiment Survey, the solid results of the 
September 40-year JGB auction, and ongoing switching demand from domestic life 
insurers into on-the-run issues. Taken together, these developments suggest that the 
environment surrounding the long-end JGBs has not deteriorated materially. 
1. Record-high JGB yields, few domestic sell-off catalysts 
The 30-year compounded JGB yield has risen above 4.1% and is trading near record 
highs. However, the recent increase in yields should be viewed less as a reflection of 
Japan-specific catalysts and more as part of a broader global bond sell-off. The 30-year 
JGB yield rose by a cumulative 15bp between September 10 and 15, before climbing a 
further 7.1bp in a single trading day on September 24 following Japan's holiday period.  
While Bloomberg reported on September 15 that a Takaichi administration was 
considering raising defense spending to 3.5% of GDP, there have otherwise been few 
new Japan-specific catalysts for a sustained bond sell-off ahead of the extraordinary 
Diet session scheduled to convene on October 5. As such, we believe the sell-off in the 
long-end of the JGB curve has primarily reflected spillovers from rising overseas yields, 
exemplified by the 10-year UST yield reaching 5.25%, its highest level in 19 years. 
2. Net JGB supply to decline after 2026 
From a supply perspective, our estimates suggest that net JGB supply as a share of 
nominal GDP will decline going forward. Net JGB supply, calculated as gross issuance 
minus redemptions and the Bank of Japan's net purchases (= gross purchases less 
redemptions of JGBs held by the BoJ), is projected to fall from ¥6.5tn in Q3 2026 to 
¥5.6tn in Q4 2026. As a result, upward pressure on yields arising from supply-demand 
dynamics is likely to gradually ease.

3. Demand at the long-end shows little sign of weakening 
As discussed below, there are currently no clear signals pointing to a deterioration in 
demand for super-long JGBs. 
• 
BofA FX and Rates sentiment survey: According to our FX and Rates sentiment 
survey conducted between September 4 and 9, steepeners have emerged as the 
most common long-end positions across the euro area and the United States. In 
Japan, however, investors continue to favor flatteners, highlighting a clear 
divergence in curve views across major markets (see FX and Rates Sentiment 
Survey, 11 September 2026). 
• 
Robust results from the September 40-year JGB auction: Despite the 
continued rise in global bond yields, the 40-year JGB auction on September 29 
delivered a strong result, with the bid-to-cover ratio reaching 3.10x, its highest level 
since July 2020. The auction underscored the resilience of buy-on-dips demand in 
the long-end JGB sector. 
• 
Switching demand from Japanese life insurers: Japanese life insurers were net 
buyers of super-long JGBs for three consecutive months through August 2026. As 
long-end yields have moved higher, the relative attractiveness of on-the-run issues 
has improved, likely prompting investors to switch from low-coupon off-the-run 
bonds into higher-coupon on-the-run issues. While the estimates are subject to 
several assumptions, our analysis suggests that 30-year JGBs up to JX75 have 
already reached, or are approaching, insurers' impairment thresholds. In contrast, 
on-the-run issues continue to retain a substantial cushion relative to those 
thresholds. Consequently, demand from life insurers to switch out of off-the-run 
bonds and into on-the-run issues is likely to remain intact (for estimates of 
impairment thresholds by issue, see Japan Rates Viewpoint, 03 June 2026). 
Bottom line: Supported by constructive investor sentiment and the prospect of a 
gradual improvement in supply-demand dynamics, we maintain our bullish stance on 
long-end JGBs. At the same time, upward pressure on global rates remains a key 
headwind. In addition, headlines pointing to further fiscal stimulus may emerge during 
the extraordinary Diet session convening on October 5, suggesting that upside risks to 
yields have not been fully eliminated. 
Against this backdrop, the hurdle for establishing outright long positions in the long-end 
sector appears high. Instead, we continue to favor long 30-year JGBs on ASW as a means 
of capturing the relative cheapness of long-end JGBs while limiting exposure to interest 
rate risk (entry 45bp, target 35bp, stop 52bp, current 49bp; risk is further position 
unwinds by non-Japanese investors).

Bills cheapen as MMFs shun intermediate bills 
The recent cheapening of Treasury bills in 3-6m tenors highlights how bill demand can 
be unreliable when uncertainty around the Fed path is elevated (Exhibit 18, Exhibit 19). 
Treasury relies on bills for relatively low-cost funding, which typically benefits from deep 
MMF demand. However, demand can be fickle when Fed path uncertainty is high. We 
review recent T-bill moves, investor shifts, and implications for UST issuance.  
Treasury reliance on bills risky with elevated uncertainty 
Treasury has increasingly relied on bills for financing needs (Exhibit 21). Bills are 
expected to continue growing because of a growing US deficit, no near-term increase in 
coupon auction sizes, larger buybacks, and potential cuts to back-end auction sizes. Bills 
are typically easily absorbed by the market at levels near Fed OIS path due to strong 
investor demand, especially MMFs. However, recent bill cheapening in 3-6m tenors show 
investor bill demand can depend on Fed path and bill supply uncertainty (Exhibit 22).  
MMF demand concentrated in front- and back-end bills 
MMF are large investors in the T-bill market (Exhibit 23). Cheapening in belly bills post 
Sep FOMC however, appears to imply lower demand from MMF due to uncertainty 
around the Fed path. MMFs prefer shorter maturities when Fed hikes are uncertain to 
avoid locking in yields before rate increases. Consistent with this, demand for very short-
dated bills at auction from indirect bidders, a proxy for MMFs, remains strong but 
demand for intermediate bills has weakened (Exhibit 24). The recent cheapening in 3-6m 
bills suggests investors are demanding additional compensation to hold bills in this 
sector, which is most exposed to uncertainty around the Fed & UST supply path.  
FRN demand is flip side of bill cheapening  
Investor uncertainty around the Fed & UST bill supply path supports FRN demand. FRNs 
have been richening over recent weeks as the Fed path and bill supply expectations have 
shifted. FRN demand typically rises with Fed hikes and drops with Fed cuts (Exhibit 20). 
Increased FRN demand and limited desire to increase coupons makes the timing ripe for 
a 1Y FRN. Treasury asked about issuing SOFR-linked FRNs at the Feb ‘26 refunding (see 
Feb refunding recap). A 1y SOFR FRN would likely see strong MMF demand with Fed & 
UST supply uncertainty + MMF excess capacity from low repo rates and low repo 
demand.  
Bottom line: bills have cheapened with elevated uncertainty around the Fed path and 
UST supply outlook. We expect 3-6m bills to remain cheap with Fed hikes in play and 
possible increased bill supply. FRN demand is strong and supports UST 1Y point 
issuance.

Higher rates volatility generally propagates quickly across the spectrum of risk (most 
assets are valued vs risk free rates) and tend to be a material headwind for the dynamic 
of risky assets (particularly spread products). It is, therefore, important to understand the 
broader outlook for rates volatility to construct expectations for the broader market 
dynamic going forward. Here we reflect on the near- and medium-term outlook for vol. 
Valuations – rich vs recent history but fair in the new regime 
Implied vol for intermediate expiries in the belly reflects the medium-term structural 
regime for volatility. When we look at current levels, they look clearly rich vs recent 
history (Exhibit 30).  
However, decomposing the recent rise in normal vol between changes in fwds and the 
logVol regime suggests only a normalization of logVol from very cheap summer levels 
(c.18.1%) to fair levels (c.20.7%; Exhibit 31). At constant fwds, this normalization 
explains roughly half of September's increase in 1y10y normal vol (11bp of 21bp), with 
the remainder driven by higher fwds. The repricing in medium-term vol therefore 
reflects an almost even split between a reset in the logVol regime and the move in fwds.  
In this context, current levels for intermediate vol in the belly look fair, reflecting 
fundamentally a shift in regime for fwds and a normalization of lognormal volatility. In 
other words, the recent vol repricing reflects not merely a transient risk premium 
but an adjustment to a structurally higher rates regime, leaving only limited 
scope for vol normalization absent a meaningful duration rally. 
Term structure 
Despite fair levels in intermediates, the vol term structure is inverted (Exhibit 27 and 
Exhibit 32), reflecting a market view for higher levels of risk near term which naturally 
drive a premium for near- vs medium-term vol. Significantly, however, the degree of 
inversion remains modest vs recent inversion episodes (e.g., Mar ’26 on ME crisis or Apr

Near term outlook… 
In recent publications we noted that vol was quite complacent over the summer given 
the packed calendar event risk ahead and saw the potential for vol to find some support 
from mid-Sep onwards (see report back to school ’26). 
As yields consolidate, implied vol may ease slightly and the term structure is likely to re-
steepen. Our baseline view, however, is that the market needs to come to terms with a 
structurally higher vol regime relative to the levels seen in late ’25 / early ’26. 
Indeed, the decomposition of the normal vol dynamic above suggests that a meaningful 
normalization in rates vol likely requires a reversal of the recent bearish rates 
momentum. We do not see strong catalysts for such a shift over October, with the most 
obvious duration-supportive events only arriving in November via the US mid-term 
elections and the UST refunding announcement: (1) a Democratic sweep could support 
duration and lower volatility, while (2) reductions in long-end issuance could improve the 
supply-demand backdrop. Until then, rates are likely to remain under pressure, the Fed is 
likely to remain hawkish, and volatility should stay supported.  
Therefore, outside of unexpected news flow (e.g., on a ME deal), the best opportunity to 
position for a partial fade of the recent selloff and higher-vol backdrop is likely 
backloaded into November. However, we see little scope for yields or volatility to revert 
to pre-September levels in the near term. 
… vs medium term 
The vol outlook for ’27 is tied to our broader macro view. The first half of the year may 
be quite noisy and vol-supportive, with the market looking for the shift in Fed policy to 
on-hold (which may happen sooner and at lower rates as our econ team expects, but 
there are obviously meaningful risks that it happens later and at higher rates), political 
noise under a new congress, and persistent geopolitical risks. All of these support 
expectations for the persistence of a relatively high steady state for vol in 1H27. 
Over 2H27, however, we see scope for the US economy to fade the recent peak reflation 
momentum. By end-’27, the backdrop for the US economy may be back closer to soft 
landing (market pricing back Fed cuts) or no landing scenarios (Fed on hold), and these 
tend to drive vol (and yields) lower, with the left side of the grid leading the way. This 
view drives our bias towards receiver calendars where medium term (15-18m) belly / 
frontend receivers are financed by selling near term (c.3m) receivers.

Remembering the 2022 RY breakout and risk-asset selloff 
Upside breakout in Jan-2022 coincided w/a risk-averse 1H22. Is this repeating? 
US real yields have broken and trended higher across the curve. We see parallels with

occur in a short period.

encouraged to become familiar with the "Characteristics and Risks of Standardized

with a FINRA Registered Options Principal.
