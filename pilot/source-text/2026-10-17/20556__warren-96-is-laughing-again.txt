Beautifully boring
For the past few years, the market has been obsessed with the next big thing. AI, semiconductors,
momentum, whatever happens to have the hottest chart and the most ridiculous valuation.
Meanwhile, Warren Buffett has been doing what Warren Buffett always does: buying quality, ignoring
the noise, and letting everyone else have their fun.
But what happens when the hottest trades start losing their magic? Suddenly, boring looks beautiful
again.
Quality is surging, momentum is looking vulnerable, and the Oracle of Omaha may be getting ready
for yet another last laugh. The following charts look at how far the resurgence of quality has come.
Warren winning
The king of quality is up 5% in just five days. And the five-year chart suggests this is hardly a new
phenomenon. Boring has been working for quite some time.
Rewarding quality
The major indices remain near all-time highs, but market leadership is quietly changing. Investors are
increasingly rewarding the quality of earnings rather than growth at any price. And with earnings
growth expected to decelerate across both AI and non-AI sectors over the next 12 months, the
backdrop could become increasingly favorable for quality.
GS trading desk: "Our sector, industry, and factor neutral Quality Pair Index (GSP1QUAL) is up 20% over
the last 3 months to a 12m high on just ~13% realized vol."

With a little macro help
And it isn't just about investors suddenly rediscovering Buffett. The macro backdrop is helping too, with
higher rates being priced in and adding another tailwind to the quality trade.

Anti-AI
Here is where things get particularly interesting. Quality is increasingly behaving like the anti-AI trade,
showing a strong negative correlation to AI, momentum and high-beta exposure. In other words, the
very stocks investors spent years ignoring could now provide protection against the trades everyone
owns.

south. That's precisely when quality has tended to shine.

Positioning
Positioning in momentum and beta remains elevated. Momentum positioning is at the 78th percentile
over five years, while beta is at the 81st percentile.
And with positioning still this elevated, there is plenty of room for the unwind to become considerably
more painful.

Quality vs Momentum
And finally, the trade that brings it all together. Long quality versus short momentum is already up 55%
in 2026. An extraordinary move, but the longer-term chart suggests there could be considerably more
upside if this really is the beginning of a structural rotation.
At 96, Buffett may not understand every new technology. But he still understands something Wall
Street keeps forgetting: the difference between a great story and a great investment.
