Markets have just had an incredibly tumultuous 24 hours, as a strong US CPI print was then followed up 
with mounting fears around the AI trade. Indeed, you can see the story of the day from the huge turnaround 
in bond yields, with the 10yr Treasury yield initially surging to 5.33% after the CPI print, before paring that 
all back to close at 5.19%, and this morning it’s now down another -8.4bps to 5.10%. Meanwhile for 
equities, the developments pushed the S&P 500 (-1.11%) to its worst performance since July, and futures 
on the index are down another -0.50% this morning. Moreover, there’s been a huge selloff in Asia overnight, 
with the KOSPI (-7.04%) and the Nikkei (-5.08%) both slumping, and the KOSPI even saw a circuit breaker 
triggered earlier given the scale of the declines. 
In terms of the catalysts, several names most exposed to AI have already been under mounting pressure in 
recent days. For instance, we mentioned in yesterday’s edition how CoreWeave’s 5yr CDS contract went 
over 1000bps intraday on Tuesday, highlighting how nervous some credit investors were about the 
company’s leverage profile. But yesterday, their CDS was then up another +136bps, closing at 1118bps, 
and the company’s share price fell -15.46% on the day. Moreover, this pressure started to be noted more 
widely, particularly among those firms that have been heavily scrutinised by credit investors given their large 
capex needs. So this led to big losses elsewhere, with Oracle (-8.83%) posting its largest decline since 
June, whilst the Philly semiconductor index (-4.30%) posted a 6th consecutive decline for the first time 
in over a year. In fact, that now leaves the index down -11.6% over those 6 sessions. 
Those trends have shown no sign of letting up overnight, with significant moves in Asia this morning. That’s 
been most obvious for the KOSPI (-7.04%) and the Nikkei (-5.08%), but we’ve also seen sharp losses for 
the CSI 300 (-2.00%), the Shanghai Comp (-1.98%) and the Hang Seng (-1.47%) as well. Some of the 
individual names have seen even bigger losses, including SK Hynix (-11.28%) and Samsung (-9.91%). And 
looking forward, there’s no sign of the moves stabilising in the US and Europe, with futures on the NASDAQ 
100 down another -1.08% this morning. 

All that was exacerbated by a strong US CPI print yesterday, which led to mounting expectations about Fed 
rate hikes, adding to the pressure on some of these names. To be fair, the print was already expected to be 
a strong one given the recent rise in energy prices. But when it came out, headline CPI was at +0.649% on 
the month (vs. +0.6% expected), meaning it was just a whisker away from rounding up to +0.7%, and it 
pushed the year-on-year number to +3.7% as well. Moreover, the details didn’t look promising either, as 
core CPI was at +0.335% on the month (vs. +0.2% expected), taking the year-on-year number to +2.6% 
(vs. +2.5% expected). So this wasn’t just an energy surge driving prices higher, as there were broad-based 
moves across the consumer basket. 
For markets, the clearest impact was on Fed pricing, as it immediately drove fresh speculation about an 
October rate hike. Beforehand, that had been priced at only a 22% chance, partly because multiple Fed 
speakers had steered away from it in the last couple of weeks. But after the CPI print came out, that then 
surged to an intraday peak of 58%, and it spent around half an hour above the 50% mark. Nevertheless, 
the AI fears and the broader risk-off tone have seen that move almost entirely unwind since, with futures 
now pricing in just a 27% chance of an October hike this morning, not much higher than before the CPI 
print even came out. So it just shows how much AI is dominating market attention right now, as fears over a 
broader tightening in credit conditions led to growing doubts about whether the Fed would actually move, 
despite the strong print. Indeed, US HY spreads (+11bps) ended yesterday’s session at 324bps, their 
widest since March. 
With all that happening, US Treasuries saw some huge moves in the aftermath, with very large intraday 
ranges. For instance, the 2yr yield was initially up +16.1bps on the day, before paring that back to close 
only +0.8bps higher at 4.80%, and this morning it’s seen a sharp -7.8bps decline to 4.72%. Similarly, the 
10yr yield initially surged to 5.33% after the CPI print but ultimately fell -2.3bps on the day to 5.19%, and 
has since fallen another -8.4bps overnight to 5.10%. And for the 30yr yield, there was initially a move up 
to 5.63%, before ending the day down -3.4bps at 5.52%, and this morning it’s down -7.2bps at 5.45%. The 
impact cascaded across other asset classes too, as there was initially a sharp appreciation of the US Dollar, 
but that’s also unwound entirely since, with the Euro strengthening +0.53% against the US Dollar this 
morning to $1.1215. More broadly, US equities were under pressure from both the inflation and the AI 
stories, but the small-cap Russell 2000 (-1.93%) took a particular hit, with the index entering correction 
territory yesterday, having now shed over -10% from its August peak.    
Otherwise yesterday, oil prices rose slightly, with Brent crude (+0.35%) up to $106.06/bbl by the close. That 
came amidst no signs of progress in the US-Iran conflict, and the moves added to the inflationary concerns 
that had already mounted after the CPI print. However, the risk-off tone has dominated overnight, with 
Brent crude since down -1.75% to $104.20/bbl. Moreover, that’s despite Ukrainian President Zelenskyy 
saying to the FT that Ukraine would resume its strikes on Russian refineries. 
Earlier in Europe, the continent’s assets put in a relatively better performance yesterday, albeit with a strong 
divergence across the board. For equities, there was strength in aggregate, with the STOXX 600 (+0.43%) up 
to a two-week high. However, there were gains for some like the FTSE 100 (+0.70%) and the CAC 40 
(+0.58%), whereas Italy’s FTSE MIB was down -0.61%. Then for bonds, 10yr bund yields (-0.3bps) fell back 
slightly to 3.47%. But sovereign bond spreads widened, with the 10yr Franco-German spread up +4.1bps 
by the close to 141.5bps, just shy of its recent closing peak on Monday, at 142.3bps. And looking forward, 
equity futures are also pointing lower today, with those on the DAX down -0.49%.    

Looking at the day ahead now, US data releases include PPI inflation and retail sales for September, the 
Empire State manufacturing survey for October and the weekly initial jobless claims. In Europe, we’ll also get 
UK GDP and Euro Area industrial production for August. Central bank speakers include the Fed’s Goolsbee 
and Hammack, along with the BoE’s Greene and Breeden. Finally, EU leaders will meet for a summit in 
Brussels.
