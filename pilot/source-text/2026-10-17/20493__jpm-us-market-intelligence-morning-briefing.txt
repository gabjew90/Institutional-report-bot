JPM MARKET INTEL EQUITY & MACRO NARRATIVE 
The two main questions yesterday were (i) the reversal in bond yields and (ii) how much 
longer the tech selloff can run.  
•         On the first, Treasuries sold off hard at 8:30amET yesterday on the hotter-than-
expected September core CPI, then recovered nearly 15bp during the day, with the 2Y 
ending more than 15bp below its morning high. There was no clear catalyst or dovish signal 
behind the move, which left the market confused. We see three main reasons. First, as we 
said in our initial takeaway, it may be prudent to wait for today's PPI and the heavy run of 
Fedspeak, before reading much of a Fed signal into yields. Second, the market clearly 
overreacted when the odds of an October hike jumped above 50%. Those odds have since 
fallen back to ~32%, with ~30bp of hikes priced through year-end. Third, there were signs of 
a move into quality and safe assets, along with concern about a possible AI-related credit 
event. 

o   JAY BARRY ON RATES YDAY (here) – However, much of the move in 
yields unwound through the course of the session amid a weaker equities 
backdrop, and as markets digested dovish comments from Governor 
Bowman, who stressed from the sidelines of the IMF-World Bank meetings in 
Bangkok that she didn’t “feel any urgency for taking further action at this 
point,” echoing similar language from recent speeches by President Williams 
and Vice Chair Jefferson. Though she spoke before the CPI release, the 
comments suggest a more patient Committee overall. 
•         On AI, the SOXX fell for a seventh straight day, with financing risks and IPO timelines 
still at the center of the story. We also think memories of the July selloff played a part, with 
possible investors de-grossing ahead of possible IPO-related updates. Positioning Intel 
told us last Friday that US positioning largely unchanged MTD, with TPM at a neutral ~46th 
percentile. By comparison, TPM fell to around the 19th percentile near its low in late July. 
Given the September price action and where positioning sits vs. July, we could see the 
potential for further de-grossing into key tech catalysts, but we remain constructive on the 
sector and still see a possible opportunity to buy the dip. 
•         VIEWS FROM FX SALES (STEVE BRUNER) – Two things happening, both 
interplaying with each other… First, chatter of today’s CPI strength being smaller in breadth, 
and driven to a material extent by elevated shelter numbers (which will recede given the 
state of the housing market) and one-offs like tuition. Fwiw, Abiel’s take a bit more hawkish. 
Second, from mid-morning the market decided to trade rates based on the equity sell off. 
Demand destruction.  Not just “I can’t afford gasoline and groceries” type demand 
destruction. (Retail index down just 1.6% / yes, I know this sector has already been priced 
to semi-recession territory). But more than that. Eventual tech and related demand 
destruction. SOX down 4% as I type.   SNOW down 6.3%. The hyperscaler index down 
3.1%.  Have rates finally crossed the AI Rubicon? My instinct is Not Yet, but who am I to 
argue with Madame Market today. This article from earlier in the week is worth a read, and 
got some lip service today .. “The Staggering Costs of a Single AI Data Center”, find it at 
{NSN TMSIIEKIJHA8 <GO>}.  Adding insult to injury. This piece (here) got circulated today, 
“Gartner Says Over $3.5 Trillion of AI Infrastructure Spending Could be Exposed to 
Impairment by the End of 2030”. Didn’t help things. Don’t know how smart Gartner is or is 

not. Some discussions that the expected ~200 bps Q3 to Q4 GDP growth deceleration will 
have its effect on alleviating hot prices. What’s the lag on that? Not immediate in my mind. 
And Nora’s recent piece throws that narrow argument into the fire; my gut is that growing 
2%, rather than 4%, doesn’t kill structural inflation. (And, as I said to one client … “7850 to 
7700 doesn’t put the inflation genie back in the bottle. Need like 5800. Ok, so maybe it’s 
6200.  But it’s a lot lower than 7700.”     
•         INITIAL CPI THOUGHTS [We blasted below immediately after the release] – The 
unrounded MoM change of Core CPI is 0.335%, above the Street’s 0.2%/Feroli’s 0.18% 
estimates. This print is interpreted as a hawkish print that drove an 11-16bp spike in 2Y 
immediately after the print. OIS market pricing now sees 50% prob for Oct (and 1x in Dec 
remain the base case), but should keep the probability of 2027 hike elevated despite 
remarks from Fed’s Wiliams earlier this week. Looking at the details of the release, Used 
car, Transportation and Apparel were among the outstanding items in Core, but the 
increase in MoM core inflation was quite broad across categories. Chase credit card data 
and yesterday’s bank earnings all told us that despite inflationary pressures, consumer 
demand and spending remains elevated, keeping the core inflation sticky. For risk assets, 
with 50/50 in October and 2027 hike still in expectation, this will keep rate-sensitive baskets 
under pressure in the short-term. However, we think it may be prudent to wait for 
tomorrow’s PPI to verify the full picture on PCE. 
•         JPM ECON ON CPI (Abiel Reinhart) – Our forecast for core PCE is 0.31%m/m 
(3.1%oya, 2.8% 3m saar), which is firm enough to put the October FOMC meeting in play, 
though we still expect the next Fed hike in December. The increase in core inflation was 
broad-based, with core goods up 0.25% and core services 0.37%. Wireless phone service 
prices, which had contributed 10bp to the August core CPI, held stable in September 
instead of reversing. Rent and especially OER were firm, but supercore also increased 
0.42%. Goods price increases were widespread outside of drugs and household 
furnishings, and the three-month pace on core goods (2.2%) is right back near the highs of 
last year. Headline CPI exceeded swap market pricing by over 7bp, the largest upside 
surprise since the March 2024 CPI report. The reading from a broader slew of inflation data 
surprises over the past month, inclusive of today’s CPI, now points to upside to inflation 
markets, after being broadly balanced prior to the print 

•         ETFs VIEWS (JONATHAN ROGERSON) – SOME LIKE IT HOT: Week to date we 
have been lifted virtually across the board by our customer base, themes are 1) Buyers of 
Energy – MLP’s (MLPA, MLPX, AMLP) have been in demand, as well as IEO (iShares 
Oil/Gas E&P) – this has dovetailed the relentless grind higher in the energy space over the 
last few sessions in tandem with the oil rally, there is a mix of Hedge Funds and L-O’s 
buying here 2) Buyers of Growth – a very large block of IWF (iShares R1 Growth) was lifted 
yesterday on screen, and we have seen offers wanted in companion products (VUG, 
VONG) 3) Buyers in DRAM – opportunistic bids lower as the asset has retraced, could be 
an interesting entry point as we approach hyperscalar earnings. In International, we have 
started to see supply – dollar strength is a headwind for this space (particularly in EM) and 
the benchmark trackers (EEM, IEMG) are trading cheap / closer to redemption bands. In 
DM, a large block of EFA was sold by an asset manager right into the bell yesterday, and 
we have seen continued supply in Japan trackers (EWJ, FLJP). With todays hotter-than-
expected CPI print I’d expect an extension of the International supply trend (witness 
USD/Yields spiking here), we are good buyers of most of the global/EM products (ACWI, 
VT, ACWX, EMXC, VXUS, URTH, IXUS).

US MKT INTEL VIEW – We published below in the October 12 Morning Briefing 
•         TACTICALLY BULLISH – we reiterate our view as we head into earnings. Last week, 
we had a test of the bullish hypothesis with last week’s OpenAI news where an accounting 
misinterpretation led to a 3.4% decline on Thursday. Though the story was updated across 
multiple media sources, Semis ended the week on a 3-day losing streak. Now, does the 
market regain its confidence in Semis ahead of earning, post-earnings, or not at all? The 
SOX is ~20% off its July lows and ~15% below its June ATHs, which points to an inflection 

point given recent price action. We think Semis can climb this latest wall of worry as recent 
earnings updates from Hynix / Samsung point to on-going thematic strength. Separately, 
breadth continues to deteriorate which we think can reverse into / out of earnings season 
which will buttress the rally. 
•         RISKS – (i) Momentum / Tech Unwind; (ii) Tech Earnings Miss; (iii) AI Credit Fears / 
Spread Widening; (iv) Spike in Bond Vol / Equity Vol; (v) Weaker Consumer Data 
•         CATALYSTS – (i) Macro Data – CPI on Oct 14, Retail Sales & PPI on Oct 15, Flash 
PMIs on Oct 23, and Fed Day on Oct 28; (ii) Lower Bond Yields – while growth continues 
to drive yields higher, dovish inflation prints could remove further hike expectations out of 
the market which is currently pricing a Dec hike and then 2x hikes in FY27; (iii) Banks 
Earnings – kicking off earnings season with stronger earnings and a favorable consumer 
and lending outlook would be helpful in restarting the Cyclicals trade and broadening the 
bull market. 
•         MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 12) – Our Menu remains relatively unchanged with a 
preference for Tech and some Cyclicals, with the AI theme being a globally bullish view. 
Previously, we had flagged the COVID Reopening theme as an area to put on your 
shopping list and revisiting that, we would focus on things like Cruise Lines, Hotels, and 
Retailers given the strength of the consumer and relative insulation from elevated energy 
prices. Other elements of the Reopening Theme will need help from materially lower energy 
prices. Internationally, and excluding the global AI theme, we like Brazil and Japan. 
•         OLD MONETIZATION MENU (OCT 5; SEP 28) – We still like Tech as a core long but 
would no longer pair with an RTY short given squeeze risk from a move lower in oil / yields, 
which points to using a derivative expression to play the squeeze. Within Tech, there is 
likely to be a broadening, with Semis and Mag 7 potentially outperforming the Tech tape. 
Software seems to be slowly gaining traction but remains a non-consensus bullish play. The 
AI theme is likely to persist, and we like owning the theme. Overall, we like Tech in the 
near-term with earnings likely to provide additional support. Ex-Tech, we think you need to 
see the yield curve bull steepening to see a persistent rally in Cyclicals; excluding AI-related 
plays, we favor Banks given the growth reboot, potentially steeper yield curve, and 
favorable capital markets outlook. Rate-sensitive sectors like Real Estate, Telecom, and 

Utilities may participate in a squeeze but we find those sectors most likely to lag the SPX, 
ex-AI plays. The biggest catalyst would be a US / Iran deal, which would squeeze EU and 
RTY higher; plus, the COVID Reopening Theme (Airlines, Autos, Cruise Lines, 
Discretionary, Hotels, etc) is another area of interest with a preference to play via options.  
•         OLDER MONETIZATION MENU (SEP 21) – We maintain our core view of +Tech vs. -
RTY and that Tech view is a mix of Mag7, Memory, and Semis, and we would consider 
keeping the pair $-neutral or market neutral. Early in hiking cycles, we tend to see USD bid 
higher and the yield curve flattening; together, this is supportive of a move towards Secular 
Growth / MegaCap Tech. Given the likelihood of higher yields and a stronger USD, we think 
the US can tactically outperform DM. That said, the fear / uncertainty around AI may push 
the market into non-AI plays, which puts Latam in focus as a long play; we like Brazil / 
Latam irrespective of your views on AI.
