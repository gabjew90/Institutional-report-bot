Buy high, sell low
The selling has been brutal, but something interesting is happening beneath the surface.
NDX futures are staging a powerful intraday reversal, semis are testing critical support, and oversold
conditions across AI, Korea and small caps are reaching extremes.
Meanwhile, hedge funds have aggressively unwound AI exposure, speculative shorts in Russell are
heavily crowded, and fear has finally caught up with the selloff.
With positioning increasingly washed out and buyers starting to fight back, are we setting up for the
next big squeeze?
Hammer time
NDX futures are putting in a huge and rather unusual hammer candle. You don't trade solely on a
hammer, but today's reversal is important, suggesting sellers are losing control after the latest selloff.
We saw a similar setup at the April lows, albeit with an inverted hammer. Different candle, same
underlying message: selling pressure is being absorbed, and buyers are starting to fight back.
Get confirmation, and the squeeze could turn violent.

The big sell
Lin highlights aggressive global equity selling yesterday, with net flows at -2.1 standard deviations.
Long sales outpaced short sales by roughly 3 to 1, pointing to outright risk reduction.
The US accounted for 66% of global net selling, with tech leading the retreat. Korea and Taiwan also
saw heavy liquidation, while global AI stocks experienced their second-largest net selling in a year (-2.8
Z-score).

The key takeaway: investors are dumping longs, particularly in AI and tech, rather than simply adding
shorts.

Gone
Hedge funds have net sold global AI stocks for five consecutive sessions. According to Goldman,
cumulative hedge fund net buying in the sector since the start of 2025 has now been completely
reversed.
In flow terms, hedge funds have unwound their entire 2025 net accumulation of AI stocks.
GS
Cleaned up
Hedge funds have aggressively reduced their AI exposure. Net allocation to global AI stocks has fallen
to 26.1%, down from 31.7% at the June peak, and now sits at the 49th percentile over the past year.
Meanwhile, the aggregate long/short ratio has dropped from 4.34 to 3.27, placing it in just the 7th
percentile over the past year.
The AI trade remains a significant allocation, but positioning has been substantially cleaned up.

Leopold low 2.0?
Leopold reportedly loaded up on CoreWeave and other AI-related calls in early September, just weeks
before the latest selloff.
Consider the CRWV November $95 call, which has collapsed from a high of $18 to just $1 since
September 8. We haven't established which specific options Leopold bought, but this contract carries
substantial open interest.
The options unwind may also have amplified the selloff. Dealers who initially bought delta to hedge
call exposure may subsequently have been forced to sell as those calls lost value, adding further
pressure to the underlying stocks.
Meanwhile, DRAM has crashed back to the Leopold lows, while CRWV's RSI has returned to levels last
seen during the initial selloff.
With positioning washed out, options crushed, and technicals deeply oversold, could this
be the second chance? Full note here.

The big short
Since July, CFTC data has shown a positive short-term correlation between changes in Russell 2000
hedge fund short positioning and CDX high-yield spreads.
In other words, as credit conditions deteriorated, hedge funds increased their Russell shorts. With short
positioning now very large, the potential for a sharp short-covering rally is becoming increasingly
interesting. More here.
