CEG – Seguing from one nuke deal to the next
 
The Wolfe Byte
CEG continues to shift the narrative. Today's GOOGL news and last week's
AMZN deal build on the Q2 call's nuke contracts. Nuke uprates are enabling
existing generation deals with 45% of the carbon-free fleet contracted, materially
enhancing the biz profile. Outperform.
 
Going big with GOOGL. Announcing a 20-year PPA on 890 MWs of new nuclear
uprates across several PJM plants. There is also a 15-year offtake for 2,700 MWs of
existing nuclear energy/capacity (ex-attributes). No pricing details, but the total 3.6
GWs are in CEG's targeted $20-50/MWh premium to PTC floor. It demonstrates that
existing generation can still contract. Also notable that GOOGL, who is most stringent
in additionality views, is willing to step in here. Our understanding is any RBP/IRAS
risk would fall on the customer.
Building on AMZN, Q2 deals. CEG announced the AMZN deal at Calvert Cliffs last
week – 190 MWs of new uprates and 500 MWs of existing. A co-lo arrangement was
previously rebuffed locally so they pivoted to a FTM deal. CEG now has nuke contracts
with the big 4 hyperscalers. This built on 890/30 MWs of existing/new contracts
announced on Q2. CEG's nuke uprates are now all spoken for – Byron/Braidwood,
Dresden, LaSalle, Limerick, Salem, Quad Cities.
Estimate implications. The ramp of existing volumes mostly match uprate CODs.
Calvert Cliffs is 2030/2032. GOOGL starts 2028, but mostly 2030+. This is longer-
dated EPS upside, but PJM pricing is elevated near-term, and it is also turning CEG
into a higher multiple infrastructure play with decades of visibility (15-20 years from
2030). We run scenarios on page 3 across all 3 deals totaling 4.1 GWs of existing
– EPS upside to base earnings could be $1.70-2.40 at a blended $25-35/MWh
premium to PTC floor; upside to overall earnings could be $0.35-0.71 at a $5-10/
MWh premium to current market (or $1.00+ off CEG's early-year guide). The mix of
base EPS also increases from 65-70% toward 75%+. The uprates would be additive,
but come with capex.
ECP sale post-close. Little bit of a momentum killer as Bloomberg reported post-
close that ECP was selling 3M shares at $294. Above their last sale at $281, they
would still own ~11M (3%) shares. CEG still has 25M shares from Calpine holders
under lock-up til mid-2027.

Investment Conclusion
CEG's stock began trading in February 2022 and has continued to move higher on a combination of the broader AI
thematic, growing appreciation for nuclear, data center deal potential, and rising power prices. CEG has a price floor on
the entirety of its nuclear generation, plus the complementary gas-heavy Calpine business, with no ceiling to pricing
upside. We see the valuation as attractive at ~14x 2030 EPS without any pricing upside either from PPAs or power
curves. We expect a mix of nuclear and gas data center deals, mostly FTM, with Calpine's fleet providing an enhanced
customer offering (with some geothermal and storage too). CEG sees a 20%+ base EPS CAGR over 2026-2029, that
should sustain at 10%+ longer-term on a rolling three-year-basis. Visibile drivers include nuclear PTCs, long-term
power contracts, and conservative pricing assumptions. On top of that, CEG sees enhanced EPS making up 30-35%
of total earnings longer-term driven by upside to market pricing, margins, and volumes. CEG is investing $1.6B to
restart the Crane/TMI nuclear plant and plans other growth capex, like nuclear uprates and gas new build. Beginning
in 2024, nuclear PTCs became effective, limiting downside to CEG's nuclear fleet with a floor price starting at $43.75/
MWh and growing with inflation through YE32. CEG's business is essentially a contractual one with upside from
inflation in the PTCs, higher power prices, FTM/BTM data center deals, premiums for nuclear's carbon free attributes,
and 24x7 matching. Thus far, CEG has long-term contracted cash flows at premium pricing via Crane/MSFT, Clinton/
META, Calvert/AMZN, GOOGL on PJM nukes/uprates, NY ZECs, and ERCOT CCGTs with CyrusOne/KKR.
Valuation - Not Yet Updated for the 5.2 GWs Contracted YTD (4.1 GWs Existing + 1.1 GWs Uprates)
Our $395 Price Target reflects 16x our pro forma 2030E EPS of $19.39, plus 50% chance of getting $30/MWh more
in margin on available nuclear generation from co-located generation (BTM or FTM) or higher power prices on CEG's
fleet. CEG has a strong, investment-grade balance sheet, with capacity for incremental buybacks. Note that we did
not update our official estimates or our valuation for the 5.2 GWs of contracted MWs YTD given the lack of
disclosure on timing, etc.

EPS Uplift from Contracting Existing MWs
CEG has quickly contracted 4.1 GWs that is incremental to their last long-term guidance update on the 3/31 Business
Update Call. There's a lot of unknowns here (pricing, timing, etc.), but we attempt to frame out how this impacts earnings
below. Using our assumptions, the contracted MWs moderately move up the total EPS, but more will shift earnings
from the "enhanced" bucket to base.
CEG didn't give much disclosure on pricing for each of the deals other than that they fall within their targeted $20-50/
MWh premium to the PTC floor for the combined existing + uprates package. We would also assume that CEG is pricing
each of these at a premium to current energy forwards + capacity. This translates to a wide range of prices that is
highly location-dependent, since NI-Hub energy is at low-$40s/WMh, PJM-West is at low-to-mid-$70s/MWh and BGE
is at high-$70s/MWh. The 500 MWs AMZN offtake is tied directly to Calvert Cliffs in the premium BGE-zone, and our
understanding is that the 2.7 GWs GOOGL contract is roughly half NI-Hub/half PJM-W. The 890 MWs announced on
Q2 is unknown, but we assume the average of NI-Hub and PJM-West below for simplicity.
It's also impossible to know how much of the premium is for the uprates vs. the existing MWs, but we run various
scenarios assuming $0-10/MWh premium to the market for the existing MWs. Bottom-line, we estimate uplift to overall
earnings could be $0.00-0.71 at a $0-10/MWh premium to market pricing on the whole 4.1 GWs based on today's
forwards. If we compare to lower market forwards as of year-end which CEG assumed when they set their long-term
guide, there is an incremental $0.67 of consolidated EPS upside (so long as CEG was still able to strike a price off today's
forwards). Additionally, we estimate $1.70-2.40 of EPS upside to base earnings for a ~$25-35/MWh premium to PTC
floor (shifting from enhanced). The uprates would be incremental earnings, but come with capex.

Carbon-free fleet now 45% contracted
CEG has now contracted ~45% of their carbon-free fleet under long-term agreements. The GOOGL PPA announced
today represents ~15% alone, plus another ~5% between the AMZN/Calvert Cliffs PPA, the WMT/Dresden PPA and
890 MWs existing generation contracted on Q2 call. The nukes under IL CMCs set to expire in mid-2027 represent
another 25% of their carbon-free fleet, though some of this output may be included in the 2.7 GWs existing in the GOOGL
PPA. CEG is fully executing on the thesis for IPPs in this power cycle by locking up long-term contracts at elevated rates.

Company Description:
CEG is based in Baltimore, MD. They have 32 GWs
of generation, producing 200+ TWhs/year. CEG has the
largest merchant nuke fleet totaling 22 GWs across IL/
PA/MD/NJ/NY/TX. CEG also has a large customer-facing
retail arm, which provides energy products/services mostly
to C&I customers. CEG's recently closed acquisition of
Calpine adds a large gas fleet that creates the largest
power company with regional/fuel diversification.
Investment Thesis:
We think CEG offers investors a way to play
decarbonization and the increasing value of flexible gas.
CEG comes with an IG B/S and solid capital deployment
story, including a recurring dividend and buybacks. CEG's
earnings are less exposed to volatile power prices thanks
to the nuclear PTC floor. CEG also has upside opportunities
tied to premiums from its carbon-free 24x7. The Calpine
gas assets only enhance CEG's customer offerings.
Valuation:
Our $395 PT reflects 16x our pro forma 2030 EPS of
$19.39, plus 50% probability of $30/MWh excess margin
on available nuclear generation via DC deals or higher
power prices.
