U.S. Markets Recap
US -1.10% | Nasdaq -1.88% | Dow -0.09%
Synopsis

US equities finished lower in Wednesday trading, ending just a bit off worst levels. The S&P 500 broke a 53-day streak without a 1%+
decline. The AI trade came under notable pressure again, with semis and memory broadly lower, and neocloud weakness (CRWV-US)
received some attention. Software, networking and tech hardware were also down, and big tech was mostly lower. Other laggards included
banks (BAC-US, STT-US), IBs (MS-US), credit cards, PE, E&Cs, multis, airlines, homebuilders and space. Relative outperformers included
integrated energy, pharma/biotech, managed care, hospitals, road/rail, P&C insurance, casual diners, steel, commodity chemicals, sta-
ples and telecom. Treasuries were mixed, well off worst levels from the morning's hotter core CPI print, with long-end yields down 3-5 bp.
The dollar index was up 0.4%. Gold finished off 1.7%, and silver was down 6.1%, its worst day since June. Bitcoin futures were off 1.9%.
WTI crude settled up 1.0%. Stocks spent the afternoon near session lows despite rates erasing a big backup following the morning's
hotter-than-expected September core CPI print; the 2Y was down more than 15 bp from its morning peak, which may be a function of a
flight-to-safety shift amid the sharp selloff across the AI trade and Big Tech, and a rotation toward defensive healthcare and staples.

Macro

September CPI hotter than expected, pushing Fed hike odds higher: September CPI takeaways were largely seen as hawkish, with core
inflation hotter than expected and price pressures more broad-based than in August. Headline rose 0.6% m/m, driven largely by gasoline,
while core rose 0.3% vs 0.2% consensus and annual core accelerated to 2.6%. Core services and goods both firmed, with strength across
shelter, used vehicles, hotels, medical care services, apparel and technology goods, though food and some household categories re-
mained cooler. BofA said pressures were less idiosyncratic than in August, flagged AI-related demand spillover into consumer goods and
argued rent disinflation has run its course. The report was seen as complicating the case for an October pause following recent Fedspeak
noting a lack of urgency; BofA maintained its October hike call, while Citi shifted to a December hike from no further tightening this year
and still sees no tightening in 2027. October hike odds sat near 60% from ~20% pre-release. The reading also heightens anticipation of
Fed Chair Warsh's remarks on Thursday night.

Fedspeak and the Beige Book: Speaking at the IMF meeting in Thailand, Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Bowman said there is no urgency
for further rate hikes following the September increase, and that the Fed needs to take time to understand how things will evolve. Chica-
go's Goolsbee (non-voter) called the morning's CPI report discouraging, though he also said the Fed needs more data; he cautioned that
if core inflation is around 4%, that is not compatible with the Fed's target and policy would need to address it. Richmond's Barkin (non-
voter) said the CPI report was hotter than he would have expected but did not want to predict the outcome of the October meeting. The
Fed's latest Beige Book said economic activity remained subdued, with three Districts reporting no change in growth. Manufacturing was
the primary bright spot, expanding in seven Districts (notably Dallas). Consumer spending was driven mainly by affluent households as
lower- and middle-income consumers were more cautious. Price pressures picked up since the last report in four Districts on fuel, diesel
and tariff costs, while employment was little changed to unchanged overall, with some noting headcounts reduced via attrition and some
forecasting lower job creation as a result of AI productivity. Half of the 12 districts saw robust or intensifying price pressures in recent
weeks. Social Security recipients are to get a 3.6% cost of living adjustment for 2027.

Rate headwinds hit BofA and Morgan Stanley FICC, overshadowing AI financing tailwinds: Banks and investment banks pulled back again
following BofA's and Morgan Stanley's Q3 reports. BofA was hit by a $1.2B AML regulatory charge, while an 85 bp surge in long-term
yields pressured FICC trading (-17% y/y), offsetting strong NII and upbeat multi-year NII guidance. Morgan Stanley delivered record Wealth
Management revenue ($9.07B) with $232B in quarterly net new assets and standout equities outperformance (+41% y/y to $5.80B),
though softer FICC results ($2.01B) and modest deposit mix shifts weighed on NII; it also highlighted a two-phase quarter, as robust
July/August activity gave way to post-Labor Day deceleration. BofA cited rate-driven friction on its trading desk during Q3 but maintained
that consumer spending remained steady through early October. Both management teams emphasized tailwinds from multi-year AI infra-
structure financing, solid credit quality and strong capital buffers ahead of Basel III finalization.

AI trade under pressure as neocloud funding comes under scrutiny: Stocks were under notable pressure, with the S&P 500 snapping a
53-session streak without a 1%+ decline that extended back to 30-Jul, the longest streak since a 74-day string into early 2020. The SOX
was down 10% over the past six sessions and on track for its longest losing streak since May 2025, following ongoing weakness in South
Korea's KOSPI and memory pressure (MU-US down eight of the past nine sessions). There was more scrutiny of neocloud CDS spreads
and funding discipline, particularly CRWV-US: its 2032 debt is yielding more than 15%, drawing attention on social media (Bloomberg,
CNBC), and its CEO said the market is acting "irrationally" (Bloomberg). OpenAI's head of computing capacity is leaving after a year in the
position (CNBC), and Nvidia and Chipagents launched an evaluation for AI-powered chip design (The Information). Elsewhere in corporate
news, Netflix is cutting 6% of staff focused in product, tech, legal and HR teams (Bloomberg), Anduril is in talks for an insider share sale
at ~$90B valuation (Bloomberg), and Berkshire Hathaway revealed an additional $300M investment in Italy-based software firm Bending
Spoons (CNBC).

EU Market Recap
Germany +0.15% | France +0.58% | UK +0.70%
Synopsis

European equity markets closed higher, near best levels after earlier weakness. Property and construction, along with defensives, were
the best performers, while financials such as banks and insurers were the big laggards. Real estate was the best performer, with UK
property stocks supported by falling UK gilt yields, and construction and materials were also higher on a UK housebuilder rally. Technology
was stronger, albeit not one of the top performing sectors, with the spotlight on ASML (ASML-NL) Q3 results, which raised FY26 sales
guidance on AI-driven demand but warned that capacity constraints cloud the 2027-28 outlook and flagged slightly softer-than-expected
Q4 guidance. In software, Softcat (SCTS-GB) was lower after flagging a slower FY2027 growth outlook, while Cirata (CRTA-GB) reported a
decline in closing annual contract value to $5.2M, although its FY outlook was unchanged. Banks were the biggest laggard, with SEB
(SEB.A-SE) down after Sweden's regulator imposed temporary higher capital requirements over internal risk-model deficiencies, and in-
surance was another decliner on negative broker action, with Swiss Re (SREN-CH) lower after a Jefferies downgrade and Admiral (ADM-
GB) lower after a Peel Hunt price target cut. Bonds remained very much in focus, with attention on remarks by ECB President Lagarde,
who said France must solve its debt problems on its own, a pointed signal on ECB intervention. European government bonds were broadly
firmer, with UK Gilts leading as 10-year yields fell 2.6bp to 5.428%; German Bund and French OAT yields edged 0.2bp lower to 3.465%
and 4.879%, respectively. The euro and sterling were notable decliners. In the UK, the focus was on remarks by BoE chief economist Pill,
who expressed skepticism over aggressive market pricing of four rate hikes in the coming year, which helped UK property stocks following
recent weakness linked to prospects of rising mortgage rates. On the fiscal side, Chancellor Healey was said to be ready to fully fund
Budget giveaways without extra borrowing. On the corporate front, LONN-CH raised its mid-term EBITDA margin target to 37-39% at its
CMD versus a prior 32-34%, and EVT-DE preliminary 9M revenue and adjusted EBITDA beat consensus. Press reports suggested the
Trump administration may reconsider the permit for the ORSTED-DK Revolution Wind project.
Macro

ASML raises guidance as AI demand overwhelms supply and flags constraints through 2028: ASML (ASML-NL) reported in-line Q3 revenue
of €11.7B but stronger-than-expected profitability and EPS, raised FY26 sales guidance to €44B-45B from €43B-45B and set gross
margin at 56%, the upper end of the previous range. Much of the focus was on the warning that planned capacity increases will be
insufficient to meet demand in 2027-28, prompting it to explore further expansion beyond existing growth plans (FT, Bloomberg). It cited
strong AI-driven demand, with customers already placing orders for 2028 and expressing interest in 2029-30. Sell-side takeaways were
broadly positive on profitability and AI-driven demand, with supply constraints supporting long-term visibility, although limited FY27-28
capacity detail and slightly softer Q4 guidance were seen tempering share price upside. Barclays leaned into demand outstripping supply,
with 2028 orders and early interest in 2029-30. Jefferies flagged the softer-than-expected Q4 guidance but welcomed the potential for
further capacity expansion in 2028. ODDO BHF highlighted stronger profitability, improved long-term visibility and value-based pricing
benefits from 2027, but said the lack of capacity guidance would likely underwhelm the Street. In the US, ASML-US was lower despite the
beat and raise, as analysts flagged underwhelming guidance and a high bar, and broader AI-complex follow-through was muted.

Lagarde pushes back on ECB intervention as French spreads stay elevated: ECB President Lagarde said the solution to France's rising
borrowing costs lies in domestic fiscal policy, not ECB intervention (CNN), a comment seen as direct pushback against ECB intervention,
with the primary response having to come from Paris. Bank of France Governor Moulin said conditions for ECB intervention are not
currently in place (Econostream, Reuters) and reiterated that the ECB's role is price stability around 2%, with France's answers lying at
home through a deficit-reducing budget. IMF's Georgieva recently said it will take time for France to bring debt and the deficit to adequate
levels. French 10-year yields eased from early-Oct highs near 4.9% (highest since 2002) and OAT-Bund spreads above 140 bps (widest
since late 2011). Goldman Sachs called the broader European sell-off more structural and remains neutral on French rates, citing
uncertainty ahead of the 2027 election, while Morningstar said French spreads stay vulnerable and are weighing on European bonds into
Q4. On equities, French fiscal and 2027 election risks were widely flagged as an overhang (Investing.com, Bloomberg). Goldman noted
France's substantial discount after years of underperformance has not yet drawn buyers, and Nuveen is structurally underweight France
while seeing short-term value in Italy and Spain. Bank of America last week upgraded French stocks to overweight after ~20%
underperformance since spring 2024 left them at a 5% P/E discount (widest since 2016), even though the recent European Fund Manager
Survey showed France as the least favoured EU position, with a record 56% underweight.

BoE's Pill skepticism on the rate path lifts builders as yields ease: BoE Chief Economist Pill, who has voted for rate rises in recent months,
revealed more skepticism than markets about the need for four 25 bp hikes over the next year (Reuters). He questioned whether following
market pricing all the way to four increases was "obvious", while acknowledging that the Iran war's inflationary effects are becoming
clearer and more persistent. Pill noted that the gap in views on the MPC had narrowed and that the slowdown in pay growth was helpful,
though wage rises remained at the upper end of levels consistent with the Bank's 2% target. Investors had been pricing a Nov hike as
near-certain, followed by three further increases by Sep-2027. The comments prompted a decline in UK gilt yields and supported rate-
sensitive sectors, with housebuilders and property stocks leading gains; BWY-GB, VTY-GB and BTRW-GB all advanced as lower yields
eased concerns about mortgage costs and housing demand, extending a relief rally in the sector after recent bond-market pressure.

Healey set to fully fund Budget giveaways: UK Chancellor Healey is expected to pay for all new measures in the 28-Oct Budget through
higher taxes or spending cuts (FT, Reuters). The package is said to be "scorecard positive" in 2029/30, meaning extra discretionary
spending is fully funded by the end of the parliament, consistent with the government's repeated commitment to meet fiscal rules with a
buffer and the IMF's public advice to show discipline (Reuters). The direction was largely anticipated, and the explicit "scorecard positive"
framing may reduce the tail risk of a late loosening signal. The IMF forecasts the UK tax burden to rise faster than in any other major

Asian Markets

Synopsis

Asia equities fell into the close on Wednesday despite a bright start. South Korea's Kospi and Japan's Nikkei 225 initially moved higher
before gains were reversed in the afternoon session. There were losses for mainland China and Australia, while Singapore and Hong Kong
ended flat. Taiwan stayed higher and several Southeast Asia benchmarks also closed with gains. The AUD and NZD were rallying and the
rupee was stronger again. JGB yields were higher across tenors. Technology stocks opened with gains but reversed course on read-across
from European chip-equipment earnings, whose revised 2026 sales outlook and vague 2027 forecast failed to meet expectations. Sam-
sung Electronics (005930-KR) ended flat, but SK Hynix (000660-KR), Tokyo Electron (8035-JP) and Kioxia (285A-JP) all ended lower.
Equities were also hampered by inflation concerns, laid bare by China's producer price inflation print today as well as MAS's reminder
that inflation could last well into 2027. In macro developments, China September exports rose 36.3% y/y to a seven-month high $447.2B

China export growth accelerates in September as the country is on track to hit $1T in surplus this month: Customs exports rose 36.3%
y/y in dollar terms in September, the fastest since February and notably exceeding Reuters consensus of 25.5% and 25.0% growth in the
prior month. Imports rose 31.0%, also beating estimates of 24.5% and 28.2% growth in August. As a result, China recorded a widening
trade surplus of $133.5B, higher than expectations of $118.1B and last month's $119.1B. Bloomberg noted China is on track to hit $1T
or more in surplus this month, crossing a threshold that was not reached until November in 2025. The global AI boom and data center
buildout have fueled strong demand for China-made components, notably exports of integrated circuits hitting nearly $45B in September,
a surge of 136% in value from last year, although the quantity of those exports only gained 1%. Customs officials highlighted industrial
robots, electronic components, computer parts and electrical equipment as driving trade growth in the first nine months of 2026. Auto
exports rose 62% y/y in value in September while rare earths shipments grew 14.3%, and exports to the US and EU rose 39.6% y/y and
25.3% y/y, respectively. Booming exports reinforce their role as the growth engine for the Chinese economy amid still weak domestic
demand but also invite tensions with China's largest trading partners, and came as China and the EU just reached agreement to cap
imports of Chinese hybrid cars, putting brakes on a full-blown trade war (SCMP).

China inflation firmer amid fuel price pressures: CPI rose 0.9% y/y in September, slightly below consensus of 1.0%, following 0.8% in the
previous month, and core inflation was steady at 1.0%. Transportation fuel rose a further 6.6% m/m, accelerating sharply to a 14.9% y/y
increase, and NBS specified that gasoline prices rose 16.6% y/y, up 7.3 ppt vs August. Broader energy prices accelerated to 4.1% y/y
from 0.6%, accounting for 0.47 ppt of the headline. Communication devices also picked up to 14.3% y/y, and almost a quarter of headline
contributions came from surges in IT products, namely data storage devices, tablets, PCs and mobile phones. Food prices remained the
main drag. Broader goods prices firmed to 1.0% from 0.8% and services were steady at 0.8%. PPI rose 5.0% y/y, above consensus of
4.4%, following 3.8% in the prior month, mostly mirroring stronger upstream prices, though downstream prices have virtually stabilized
after a long stretch of declines. PCs, communication and other electronics remained notable gainers, while autos extended declines.

Singapore's MAS holds policy steady as Q3 GDP growth steadies: The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) left its policy settings un-
changed on Wednesday against expectations of modest tightening, saying it expects the AI boom to ease next year and energy prices to
fall. MAS kept the slope, width and center of the policy band unchanged following consecutive tightening in April and July. It said core
inflation is to remain elevated in the quarters ahead before easing 'discernibly' in H2-27, narrowed its FY26 core inflation forecast to 1.5-
2.0% from 2.5% in July, and estimated headline inflation at 2.0% (StraitsTimes). MAS added that the current stance "leans against" an
anticipated rise in near-term imported inflation to ensure easing in price pressures next year. Analysts cited by Bloomberg said the pause
probably reflected successive tightening still working its way through the economy, but MAS may tighten further if inflation risks develop
next year. The decision came as the ministry of energy trade and industry (METI) said Singapore's GDP grew 5.4% y/y in Q3 from Q2's
6.2%; on a q/q basis, the economy grew 1.1% from 1.5% in Q2. Growth was at the top end of MAS's forecast and above the consensus of
5.0%, with main contributions from an 8.8% y/y surge in construction output and 8.0% expansion in services, and a modest slowdown in
manufacturing growth to 7.5% from 12.9% in Q2; expansion in electronics and precision engineering offset contraction in biomedical and
chemical sectors (StraitsTimes).

More indications BOJ is leaning against a back-to-back hike: Bloomberg sources indicated BOJ officials are leaning against a back-to-

absence of an abrupt escalation in inflation risks and will instead look at data to determine whether a December hike is warranted, though

target in the specified time frame. Underlying inflation can finally be assessed as being around 2%, but stabilization at this level is required
to declare achievement of the price stability goal, echoing comments from Governor Ueda in parliament yesterday. Recent input cost
inflation pressures have eased slightly, though the elevated pace in CGPI still supports the rate hike case.

BofA Asia FMS sees AI monetization turning from hurdle to driver: The October BofA Asia FMS continued to highlight AI as the primary
theme, with a strong majority of 70% now seeing AI monetization as the primary APAC equity driver over the next year, dominating other
themes. In the September survey 80% were looking for evidence of AI monetization before adding positions, and this fell to 65% in October.

strategies indicated stronger AI conviction, with 45% either remaining overweight with no hedging or rotating within AI vs 25% in Septem-
ber, as rotation into defensives faded notably. Sentiment toward the underlying economic growth outlook in APAC-ex Japan was little
changed, with some improvements for China and Japan, while inflation expectations jumped 25 ppt to 50%. Valuation DI indicated regional
equities are seen as increasingly undervalued, and perceptions that the AI theme is largely priced in retreated notably. For Japan, a
majority now look to earnings as the key theme, and most expect the next BOJ rate hike in December.
