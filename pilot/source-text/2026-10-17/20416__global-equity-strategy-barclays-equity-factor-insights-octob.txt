US factor analysis and views: October 2026
Equity market performance in September 2026:
• The S&P 500 declined a modest 0.5%, as weakness in Banks (-7.3%) and Capital Markets
(-9.9%) more than offset continued strength in AI-linked areas of the market. Semiconductor
stocks gained 7.2%, while Big Tech advanced 8.1%, helping limit the broader market's
downside.
• Small caps underperformed sharply, falling 5.4% as the surge in bond yields weighed on the
more rate-sensitive segment of the market. The move marks a notable reversal from the first
half of 2026, when small caps outperformed large caps. The decline was led by Biotech and
Banks, which sold off by 6.0% and 4.4%, respectively.
• Within large caps, Growth significantly outperformed Value, with the Russell 1000 Growth
gaining 2.1% versus a 3.3% decline for the Russell 1000 Value. The divergence was largely
driven by Growth's greater exposure to TMT sectors, particularly Semiconductors,
highlighting the renewed strength of the AI theme as the primary driver of Growth's relative
outperformance.

US Factor Discussion & Recommendations
We mainly use three criteria to formulate our factor views – relative fundamentals of our factor
baskets vs. their own history, current macro events along with past event studies, and our
market outlook ahead.
Stay Positive on Value and Neutral on Growth for the following reasons:
The macro backdrop continues to favor Value, with inflation still above target and economic
growth holding up well. At the same time, signs of tighter financial conditions are becoming
more evident. We have recently highlighted widening credit spreads across hyperscaler, data
center, and neo-cloud issuers, alongside a material increase in hyperscaler CDS spreads (see
Evolving AI Risk Premia, 30-Sep-26). The unprecedented wave of credit issuance is occurring as
Treasury yields have moved sharply higher, raising the cost of capital. In this environment,
investor fatigue with the dominant and highly crowded AI narrative could create a relative
tailwind for under-owned segments of the market. As we have argued previously, Value
effectively represents an "anti-AI" style factor and stands to benefit if leadership broadens
beyond AI-linked winners (the current earnings growth concentration may be vulnerable to
downward revisions in semiconductors, which have exhibited some of the largest forecast
errors among S&P 500 industry groups, as argued in Checking the chip stack, 7-Oct-26).
Moreover, Value also continues to benefit on account of its consumer orientation, as consumer
spending has been holding up well (see The consumer who won’t quit, 8-Oct-26). As a result, we
continue to prefer Value over Growth in the near term.

We stay Positive on Large-over-Small for the following reasons:
While our preference for large caps initially faced headwinds as strong risk appetite supported
small-cap performance, particularly in biotech and banks, the backdrop shifted meaningfully in
the second half of the year as interest rates moved sharply higher. This was the key risk we had
identified for small caps, given their greater sensitivity to financing conditions. Since the end of
1H26, both the Russell 2000 and S&P 600 have declined by over 7%, while the S&P 500 has
gained 4.0%, underscoring the divergence in performance. Importantly, the weakness in small
caps has been broad-based, with nearly every sector outside Technology retreating since the
10-year Treasury yield decisively moved above 5%. With Barclays rates strategists expecting the
10-year yield to remain around current levels for the next several quarters (see US midterms:
Priced for gridlock, 22-Sep-26), the macro environment is likely to stay challenging for smaller
companies. At the same time, small-cap fundamentals remain unappealing, with leverage near
post-COVID highs and the share of unprofitable companies still elevated relative to history.
Against this backdrop of restrictive financing conditions and weak underlying fundamentals, we
continue to favor large caps over small caps.

Stay Neutral on Momentum for the following reasons:
The factor has recovered meaningfully from its earlier drawdown, but the rebound has been
driven largely by renewed strength in AI-related stocks. In particular, gains in Semiconductors
and Electrical Equipment, partially offset by weakness in Software, account for nearly half of the
10.1% increase in our long/short Momentum basket since the end of August. Looking ahead,
Momentum retains meaningful exposure to Semiconductors, Oil & Gas, and Biotech, while
remaining underweight Electric Utilities, Specialty Retail, and Media. This positioning leaves the
factor supported by two potentially favorable themes: continued earnings strength across AI-
linked beneficiaries and elevated geopolitical risk in the Middle East. Moreover, among all the
factors we track, Momentum is cheapest style vs its own history. However, a key threat to the
style is the upcoming midterm elections, which could disrupt prevailing market trends and
increase factor volatility. Empirically speaking, in almost every midterm election year since
2006, Momentum has sold off from the beginning of October through the end of
November. Thus, with supportive and adverse forces largely offsetting one another, the outlook
for Momentum is finely balanced, and we therefore maintain a neutral stance.

Stay Neutral on Quality:  Despite several weeks of 10-year Treasury yields holding above 5%,
there are few signs that equity fundamentals are meaningfully deteriorating. Our FY27 earnings
forecasts still imply growth rate in the mid-teens, and even a significant downward revision to
semiconductor earnings, which currently account for more than 70% of expected earnings
growth, would leave aggregate earnings growth in the double digits. In this environment, the
defensive characteristics that typically support Quality appear less compelling. At the same
time, downside risks to equities remain elevated. The midterm elections represent an important
potential catalyst for market volatility, and historically Quality has tended to outperform in the
periods immediately surrounding midterm elections. With resilient earnings reducing the need
for defensiveness, but political uncertainty preserving demand for quality franchises, the risk-
reward profile appears balanced. As a result, we maintain a neutral stance on the Quality factor.

Stay Negative on High-over-Low Volatility: Although the style staged a strong rebound during
the second half of September, that momentum has faded quickly, and the outlook remains
challenging. Looking ahead, High-over-Low Volatility faces several potential headwinds,
including elevated interest rates and heightened political uncertainty surrounding the midterm
elections. Importantly, our previous work shows that the factor has historically struggled during
equity market drawdowns (see Rotation Rattles Risk-on, 11-Feb-26). This reflects its underlying
composition: among the style factors we track, High-over-Low Volatility has the lowest share of
profitable companies, making it particularly sensitive to shifts in risk sentiment and
deteriorating market conditions. With multiple sources of potential stress still present and little
evidence of a favorable catalyst on the horizon, we continue to view the risk-reward profile as
unattractive and maintain our negative stance on the factor.
Stay Neutral on Yield (Dividends + Buybacks): The prospect of interest rates remaining
elevated for an extended period continues to limit the appeal of traditional income-oriented
equities, particularly as higher risk-free yields provide investors with an increasingly
competitive alternative. However, along with being under-owned, Yield also shares some
characteristics with Value as an effective "anti-AI" style factor. Its exposure is concentrated in
sectors such as Banks, Insurance, Oil & Gas, and Software & IT Services, leaving it relatively
underrepresented in the market's dominant AI trade. As a result, any broadening of market
leadership or signs of fatigue in the AI theme could provide a meaningful tailwind for the factor.
While the macro backdrop remains challenging, these potential style-rotation benefits argue
against an outright negative stance. On balance, the positive and negative forces appear
broadly offsetting, leading us to maintain a neutral view on Yield.

European Factor Themes
From a factor standpoint, we see three key themes shaping the market into October: (1) easing
fiscal concerns could unlock Value leadership, (2) improving earnings breadth supports Value,
Growth and Small Caps, and (3) a cleaner positioning backdrop leaves Momentum leveraged to
any reacceleration in the AI trade.
1) A narrowing EU fiscal risk premium would favour Value
Rising Bund yields would normally provide a favourable backdrop for European Value, but the
latest rates move has been accompanied by wider Euro Area sovereign spreads as fiscal
concerns, particularly around France, have come back into focus. Given the factor's greater
exposure to domestic revenues and Financials, spread widening can dilute the traditional
benefit of higher rates by weighing on growth expectations and risk appetite. A normalisation of
fiscal concerns and tighter spreads would therefore remove an important headwind and
strengthen the case for renewed Value leadership.
2) Broad based earnings beats could favour Value, Growth and Small Caps
Resilience in economic surprises points to an improvement in earnings breadth over the coming
earnings season. Historically, a current high EU CESI readings has coincided with a 60/70% of
STOXX 600 companies beating EPS expectations, suggesting consensus may still be
underestimating the resilience of corporate earnings. More importantly, periods characterised
by broad-based earnings beats have typically delivered the strongest support to Growth, Value
and Small Caps, creating scope for market leadership to broaden beyond the narrow cohort of
recent winners.

European Factor Performance Review
Momentum and Yield are leading since September
Momentum has been the standout factor over the past month, supported by strong
performance on the long side and relative weakness among Low Momentum stocks. Yield also
generated positive returns, while Quality and Size were broadly flat over that period. Growth
and Value delivered a slightly negative factor-pair return driven by deeper weaknesses across
long legs. Volatility was the clear laggard, driven by weakness in High Vol and resilience among
Low Vol stocks. The factor-pair regime, however, points to a more nuanced picture. Yield
remains in the leadership quadrant, while Momentum, despite leading outright returns, has
moved into fading territory as short-term momentum turns lower. Value is showing signs of
recovery but has yet to regain positive outright momentum. Meanwhile, Growth and Volatility
remain in laggard territory, with Quality and Size broadly stable near the centre of the regime
map.
Over the past three months, factor leadership has rotated decisively towards cheaper and
higher-yielding areas of the market, while Momentum has continued to recover fron early
summer steep unwind. Cheap Value has extended its outperformance while giving up the lead
to High Yield more recently, which is now the clear leader on the long side. High Growth has
delivered more modest gains, while High Quality and Large Size have remained broadly flat.
High Vol performance remains subdued. On the short side, Low Momentum has gave up most of
it advance since early September, while Low Quality, Low Growth and Small Caps have
outperformed. Expensive Value has stabilized but remains weak. Low Yield remain among the
weaker short exposures, while Low Vol has recently seen some acceleration working as pure
hedge amid EU equities wobbles.
