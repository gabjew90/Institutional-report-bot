Energy 
An odd day with no single catalyst for the whipsaw moves in crude oil futures today as overnight 
hopes (for deals) gave way to reality (of escalating attacks)... like deja vu all over again. 
Hope dominated the overnight decline in oil futures... 
Russian diesel supply hopes were high and then were dashed as Russia struck Ukrainian 
power plants after Trump said the two sides had agreed to stop targeting each other’s energy 
infrastructure. Zelensky accused Moscow of breaking the energy ceasefire and warned that 
Ukraine would respond during winter without a truce. 
And that sent any hopes of a deal to shit with diesel prices surging... 

 
 
Demand and supply signals did help early on: OPEC cut its 2026 oil demand growth forecast 
sharply (to ~400,000 bpd from 1.4 million earlier in the year). There were also notes of OPEC+ 
production recovering in September (led by Saudi Arabia) and Saudi comments on potential 
export capacity additions. 
But, then, prices surged amid lingering risks of a wider conflict (and the pressure from Iran into 
their peak leverage Midterms). 
Perhaps the biggest factor for the rebound was the escalation of the Houthi-Saudi war. 
The Saudi coalition, which is calling itself the Coalition to Restore Legitimacy in Yemen, on 
Tuesday issued a warning for civilians to evacuate a site in Sanaa ahead of a targeted 
operation. The city has been held by the Houthis going all the way back to 2014, and is the main 
base of the Iran-aligned government there. Specifically, civilians are being urged evacuate the 
Yemen Petroleum Company’s Al-Sabahah central fuel station in Sanaa, and the publication 
went so far as to issue an illustrative map of the location. There are emerging reports of active 
bombings of the area. 
This report from Axios late on did not help, pushing WTI to the highs of the day... 
Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and President Trump last week discussed the 
possibility of launching a new operation against Iran before the Israeli and U.S. elections, 
according to two U.S. officials, one Israeli official and a fourth source with knowledge... 
By the settle, Oil futures prices were up... 

 
 
The disconnect between crude and the broader physical complex is notable and rational. Diesel 
cracks, which represents the spread between crude oil and ex-refinery diesel price, have surged 
toward $100. Freight rates for dirty tankers, which carry crude oil as opposed to refined 
products, have climbed to multi-year extremes, reflecting mounting stress across refining and 
transportation. As Bloomberg's Frank Monkam notes, all this leaves Brent vulnerable to a 
catch-up. 

 
 
This divergence suggests stress is building across the supply chain without generating a 
commensurate response in crude futures. If that gap begins to close through higher Brent 
volatility, the next move could prove considerably more forceful than the recent price action 
suggests. 
And that will lift bond vol, yields, and inflation fears... 
Rates & Credit 
Some mixed FedSpeak and a better than expected home sales print pushed rate-hike odds up 
today (ahead of tomorrow's big CPI report) 
HAWK then DOVE: Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee spoke out of both sides of his 
mouth, hinting that the terminal rate is lower, but the trajectory from here to there will likely be 
higher: 
In an interview published by Semafor, Goolsbee says rates can come down if the Fed gets 
evidence that inflation is on a path back to 2% with full employment. 
"But the more you see services inflation going up and the more you see AI demand jumping out 
of its lane and overheating the rest of the economy, then it's going to be a little dicier," he says. 
And he knows where the pain is coming from: 
“Prices are going up in spaces that are not just components in data centers and the directly 
adjacent industries,” he said. 

 
“I’m getting increasingly nervous that that’s just straight, old-fashioned overheating demand,” he 
adds. 
HAWK: Richmond Fed's Barkin also noted that The Fed "can see the impact of AI investment on 
US inflation." 
DOVE: Then NYFed's WIlliams went full Dove-tard, saying that he still sees one more 
interest-rate hike as appropriate this year, but at the moment doesn’t believe it will be necessary 
for the central bank to increase rates again in 2027. 
“I haven’t changed my view that it may be appropriate to have one more rate hike,” Williams 
said Tuesday in an interview with Bloomberg News at the New York Fed. 
“I don’t think at this time I have any reason to believe that we would need to do more” after that. 
That pushed Dec hike odds to cycle highs near 90% and lifted September odds back up near 
25%... 
 
Interestingly Fed's Waller confirmed what we noted earlier: The bond market is doing The Fed's 
job... 
“When yields go up, is that going to impact interest-sensitive sectors like housing and autos and

Delhi on Tuesday. 
While that isn’t the Fed’s intention, higher yields are “doing a little bit of the work,” he said.

There has been a lot of intellectual gymnastics as to why bond vol has failed to pull VIX higher. 
The vol divergence gap could close in several ways, including a drop in bond vol associated

And before we leave credit-land, which is now directly pressuring the leaders in equity-land, 
Bloomberg macro strategist, Michael Ball, notes that the AI debate is shifting from how much 
demand for compute exists to whether investors will keep financing the buildout at levels that 
ultimately allow it to generate acceptable returns. That dynamic puts increasing pressure on 
forward earnings estimates, valuations and broader US equity market leadership.  
Cracks are forming, even as the current earnings outlook remains intact, as my colleague 
Tatiana Darie writes. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan has questioned whether investors understand 
what they own. AI-linked capital raises have also been delayed or canceled. More strikingly, 
JPMorgan and Goldman are leading a roughly $5 billion loan for AI startup Volta at around 11%, 
yet underwrote only $1.3 billion themselves. That suggests lenders want less exposure even 
with higher returns. 
Credit markets are already challenging the idea that AI spending is immune to higher rates.  
 
Data-center bond spreads have widened sharply over six months, CoreWeave debt trades near 
85 cents on the dollar for roughly a 14% yield, and Citi says five-year CDS spreads for megacap 
technology companies have tripled to around 75 bps points from a year ago. 

 
 
To be clear, hyperscalers aren’t running out of money. The problem is increasingly the 
economics of the marginal project. Barclays estimates current AI-related revenue at roughly 
$250 billion against an ecosystem that may eventually require well above $1 trillion simply to 
sustain maintenance capex. Meanwhile, AI-capex beneficiaries are expected to contribute the 
majority of S&P 500 profit growth next year. 
That creates an uncomfortable feedback loop. AI investment supports growth and higher 
neutral-rate expectations, while those higher yields raise the financing hurdle for AI itself. 
Compute demand may look limitless. Investor patience for the math to eventually work will not 
be...

Circling back to the crux of the matter, questions are growing over whether the scale of AI 
investment can ultimately generate enough returns to justify the buildout. BofA says 45% of 
investors expect an AI bubble in 2027, while 39% see hyperscaler CapEx as the most likely 
source of a systemic credit event. Rising financing costs are already pressuring the weakest 
links in the AI ecosystem, while intensifying competition is clouding the outlook for 
semiconductors, which Barclays had written is expected to drive roughly 60% of S&P 500 profit 
growth next year. 
But, as Bloomberg's Michael Ball notes to end our equity section, the rest of the market is not 
yet offering much of an offset. The median S&P 500 stock is notably below its recent high, a 
significant amount of members are down this year and only two of 11 sectors are outperforming 
the index. Recent strength in healthcare and financials hints at rotation, but there is no clear 
replacement in leadership to the longer-term rally in quality. 

 
 
Shifting options positioning may be an early sign that the winds are starting to change. Nasdaq’s 
gamma has flipped negative, according to MenthorQ, meaning dealer hedging flows amplify 
rather than suppress moves. That setup is not yet a directional breakdown, but negative 
short-dated gamma leaves that part of the market increasingly vulnerable to a sharp 
acceleration in volatility if support gives way. The SPX, by contrast, remains buffered by positive 
gamma between 7,700 and 7,800. 

 
 
Source: SpotGamma 
That makes 7,700 the key line to watch for a broader shift in risk sentiment... and with CPI 
tomorrow, vol traders are already bracing...  
 
The current tech wobble can remain contained while the index sits above that support -- but if 
that level is broken, an AI-trade drawdown risks turning into a broader market correction. 

 
Everything Else 
The dollar was steady around its highest level since May 2025 - holding at the top of that 18 
month range... 
 
Bloomberg macro strategist, Frank Monkam, notes that the dollar’s renewed strength is 
increasingly finding support from traditional interest-rate fundamentals, as the Fed's recent pivot 
to hiking restores the relationship between the greenback and relative yield differentials. 
The correlation between DXY and the two-year US Treasury–German Bund yield spread has 
rebounded sharply since the summer, reversing a prolonged period of dislocation during which 
the dollar became less responsive to relative monetary-policy expectations. 

 
 
The recoupling coincides with the repricing of the Fed’s policy path, which has pushed two-year 
Treasury yields higher since the start of September and widened the yield advantage over 
German Bunds. The shift suggests that monetary policy divergence is taking the wheel back 
and driving dollar performance. 
The currency could therefore be transitioning from a predominantly macro-risk driven trade back 
toward a monetary-policy-divergence trade. While geopolitical uncertainty and elevated energy 
prices continue to complicate the inflation outlook and safe-haven demand remains supportive, 
the next directional move for the greenback increasingly depends on the persistence of the 
Fed’s hawkish repricing. 
Gold gained overnight as the dollar dipped but gave it all back as oil rallied and yields rose...
