Seven-Fold In Seven Months 
And that history is staggering. Anthropic's run rate went from $87 million in January 2024 to $1 
billion at the end of 2024, roughly $9 billion at the end of 2025, then $14 billion in February, $19 
billion in March and $30 billion in April. By the end of July, Bloomberg reported that the company 
had told investors its annualized revenue had hit $65 billion, seven times where it ended 2025. 
Last month the NYT reported that Anthropic is expected to finish the year with more than $100 
billion in annualized revenue. 

 
 
Hence the problem. With a print above $65 billion already in the market for over two months, 
today's figure has to show the curve is still compounding toward $100 billion, or the "strength" 
narrative cracks. Recall Brad Gerstner's mid-September framework, which Goldman's desk 
resurfaced last week: the top three labs (Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX/xAI) were at a 
combined ~$100 billion run rate in July and need to reach $180-200 billion by year-end "to keep 
the AI trade intact." Anthropic, booked gross, is carrying the biggest share of that load. 
The price tag assumes it can. Prospective buyers have floated a valuation of $1.8 trillion to $2 
trillion, roughly double the $965 billion post-money of its May round. Reuters Breakingviews did 
the napkin math: take the $16 billion of revenue Anthropic booked in the first two quarters, hold 
it steady, and you get "just shy of $40 billion" for 2026, which puts a 2𝑡𝑟𝑖𝑙𝑙𝑖𝑜𝑛𝑣𝑎𝑙𝑢𝑎𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛(𝑙𝑒𝑠𝑠~
120 billion of net cash) at roughly 47x revenue, above even SpaceX's ~42x. Independent

Recall also that 83% of Anthropic's 2025 revenue came from metered consumption, per the 
prospectus details reported by Reuters, up from 75% in 2024. As Breakingviews put it, those 
customers "can cut usage quickly if budgets tighten or cheaper, alternative models emerge." 
The $518 Billion Tab 
Which brings us to the other side of the ledger. According to the confidential prospectus seen by 
Reuters, Anthropic posted a $42 billion net loss in 2025 (about $34 billion of it an accounting 
charge tied to financing that could eventually convert into shares) on revenue of $4.6 billion, 
with an operating loss of more than $8 billion and $7.33 billion spent on compute. Going 
forward, it expects to spend at least $518 billion over roughly a decade with six partners, and 
"about 80% of that sum is non-cancelable or requires payment regardless of usage": 

 
 
"If our actual spend falls short, we must pay Google the difference," the prospectus says. 
Amazon gets the same terms. And the single biggest line item, $161.2 billion of 
Broadcom-related equipment leases, is being financed by the debt market: Broadcom's $60 
billion chip-financing package for Anthropic ("APCHIP 2.0" in Goldman's IG credit desk 
shorthand) began syndicating at the start of the month, with banks marketing a $42 billion 
senior-secured Class A tranche and Blackstone leading an $18 billion junior piece, as we 
detailed in "The Crowding Out Effect Is Immense" last Thursday.

Wrong Time To Be A Price-Taker 
The timing doesn't help either. In Tuesday's Goldman flow and positioning note, the bank's Tony 
Pasquariello forecast "$600bn of primary issuance, which includes $175bn of IPOs," while 
"lockup expirations could bring $1.7tr of supply to market" and "net US equity supply could 
increase the most since the year 2000." That is the same supply wall Paul Tudor Jones warned 
about on CNBC this morning, when he said he'll be "short the whole year" in 2027. One Bear 
Traps PM pointed out that the Mag 7 fell 15% in the 29 trading days into the SpaceX IPO, and 
with the S-1 due publicly 15 days before the roadshow, all eyes are on a public filing around Oct 
25 for a mid-November debut. 
Meanwhile, the cost of money for anyone financing $518 billion of compute just went up again: 
this morning's hotter-than-expected CPI put an October hike "firmly on the table." Privorotsky, 
who remains "fundamentally bullish on the technology," spelled out the real risk in his overnight 
note (available to pro subs): 
The issue is whether the cost of capital and physical bottlenecks prevent that demand from 
translating into actual spending... Competition among the labs may compress model economics 
without necessarily undermining the infrastructure owners. So fundamentally bullish on the 
technology and the acceleration towards the next leg of intelligence. But increasingly conscious 
that the limiting factor may not be demand, but our willingness and ability to finance and deliver 
the supply. 
Even Broadcom's Hock Tan, whose own credit is paying for many of the chips, warned at the 
weekend that capital may now be a bigger constraint than chips or power, and that "a lot of 
investors have no clue what they are investing in."  

 
Bottom Line 
Goldman's Johnstone laid out what the bulls need: "The clearest positive catalyst would be 
evidence that total compute demand—token volumes multiplied by compute 
intensity—continues to accelerate despite greater competition and lower inference costs." A 
blowout run rate today, say on track for the $100 billion the NYT reported, would do exactly that, 
and given how much negativity is priced into semis (Johnstone notes discussions are 
"increasingly focused on who can make the most negative case"), a squeeze is entirely 
plausible. JPM's desk is already flagging "a possible squeeze post-CPI." 
But even the best possible number answers only the demand question. It says nothing about 
whether ~$400 billion in non-cancelable commitments, the widest hyperscaler credit spread 
since July, half of a $2.3 trillion backlog and an all-time low token price add up to a company 
going public "from a position of strength." Having pounded the table on AI's debt problem since 
last October, we lean toward the second half of O'Rourke's question: a lab growing this fast that 
still needs $100 billion of new equity, on top of $150 billion of chip debt, to pay its landlords is 
going public from a position of need. Need and strength can coexist, of course... until the cost of 
capital decides which one gets priced.
