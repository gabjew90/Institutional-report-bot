US inflation revives the October debate 
MARKET LINES 
September’s US inflation report has reignited expectations of further monetary tightening. Coming 
in above expectations for the second consecutive month, it suggests that the summer’s easing in 
price pressures may have been temporary. Although supercore inflation moderated, renewed 
pressure across goods and housing has cast doubt on the disinflationary trend. Treasury yields 
surged, while the dollar climbed to an 18-month high as markets revived expectations of another 
Fed rate hike. Nevertheless, we continue to favour a hold at the October meeting. While the latest 
report gives the more hawkish members of the FOMC additional ammunition, recent comments 
from Bowman and Williams suggest there is no immediate need to tighten further. We therefore 
expect the Fed to wait for more data before reassessing its position in December, although the 
October decision has become a much closer call. In Europe, yields also moved higher, while ECB 
policymakers remain divided over the need for further tightening. Meanwhile, escalating tensions in 
the Middle East pushed Brent above $106 intraday, reinforcing concerns about a prolonged energy 
shock.
