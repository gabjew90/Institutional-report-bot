1) AI Funding Concerns
Neoclouds sold off hard yesterday, with credit spreads widening for the whole AI ecosystem. GS’s global
neocloud basket is down 20% in five sessions, a roughly 1st pctl move over ten years, with selling
accelerating. Hyperscaler IG spreads widened another 7.4bp last week, If the appetite to finance the AI
buildout is constrained, the consequence is incrementally slower chip consumption, less capex and
ultimately weaker growth.
See Rich Privo's take here:
AI: Where to from here? I don’t think this is fundamentally a crisis of demand. Aggregate token
consumption continues to surprise to the upside. Who captures that demand, through which models and
platforms, remains open to debate, but the trajectory of aggregate compute consumption still looks one
way. All roads lead back to those who own scarce compute, particularly the well capitalized hyperscalers.
The interesting question is whether hyperscalers elect to fund incremental capacity through organic
balance sheet equity and operating cash flows rather than tapping the debt markets, alleviating debt
overhang concerns. The harder part to falsify is physical delivery… getting power, energizing data centers
and delivering the sheer number of gigawatts the market is expecting. Those constraints become much
more tangible into 2027 and particularly 2028. Logistics, permitting and policy are already messy and
likely to get messier given the political backdrop. That creates real execution risk for the middlemen and
helps explain the derating we’ve seen across the complex. But these are ultimately constraints on supply,
not evidence that demand is disappearing.
Risk: So where does this leave us? I think the market is discovering that demand for compute can grow
exponentially, but the capital available to finance it cannot. I’ve learned to respect the credit market, and
the widening across the AI vertical is a warning. But the equilibrium may simply require a recalibration of
what can actually be delivered and when. That increasingly favours those with powered capacity and
strong balance sheets over the leveraged middlemen. The structural thesis can remain intact even if the
investment cycle disappoints. And importantly, a lot of bad news is now getting priced. Neoclouds down
20% in five sessions, a historic liquidation, while quality has rallied in 16 of 18 sessions with RSI at 83. .
We’ve had a substantial correction, discretionary nets are low, and rates are finally rallying. My gut is
we’ve overreacted in pockets. There are legitimate financing constraints, but they don’t invalidate the
underlying demand story, and if rates stabilize lower they improve the equation. We should respect what

credit is telling us, but equally recognize that positioning, valuations and rates are all moving in a more
supportive direction.
2) Circuit breakers in Korea overnight
Color from Korea Desk Overnight as circuit breakers triggered for first time since 7/29, with KOSPI
extending losses for 7th consecutive session (longest losing streak since September 2024)… Hynix
finished -11.1%... Samsung -9.5%...The timing directly reflects the removal of the corporate bid. With
Samsung Electronics completing its program on October 6 and SK Hynix tapering into tag ends,
secondary selling from foreign institutions (~80%–87% concentrated in the two chip heavyweights) has
cleared into an empty domestic bid, breaking below key psychological thresholds and triggering forced
margin liquidations.
Despite the magnitude of the sell-off, Our pad did not see concentrated degrossing nor capitulation in
memory. Pad was better Sell-skewed today, but the nature of outflows were less memory concentrated
compared to the July drawdown. We were 1.2x better Sellers by EOD, after small pockets of dip buying
emerged in Memory, Power Grids, and Holdcos..
More broadly per Exchange data, Foreigners were net sellers for 8th consecutive days, with Tech
accounting for 86% of the selling (-$11.6bn in Tech and -$13.4bn in KOSPI over the last 8 sessions).
Specifically, foreigners extended their 15-day net-selling streak in Hynix and 12-day net-selling streak in

3) Core CPI Above Expectations; Estimating 0.32% for September Core PCE
Core CPI increased by 0.33% in September, above expectations. The upside surprise was partly driven by
a firmer-than-expected reading in the shelter category, with OER increasing by 0.35% month-over-month
and contributing to the core by about 5bp more than we expected. The strength in OER was concentrated
in the Midwest (+0.68%, SA by GS), well above our estimate of the underlying trend in the OER category.
The travel services, autos, and recreation goods categories were also firm. Based on details in the CPI
report, we estimate that the core PCE price index rose 0.32% in September (vs. our expectation of 0.20%
before the CPI release), corresponding to a year-over-year rate of +3.09%. The upward revision to our
September core PCE forecast largely reflects the upside surprise to our OER, hotels, and recreation
forecasts.
