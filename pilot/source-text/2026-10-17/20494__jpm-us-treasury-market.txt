Market views
Treasury yields gapped higher in the New York morning, as core CPI in September rose 
0.33%m/m (consensus: 0.2%), bringing the over-year-ago rate back to 2.6%. The print 
was strong across categories, and with the numbers in hand, we see core PCE rising 
0.31%, lifting the over-year-ago rate from 3.0% to 3.1% (see US CPI & TIPS: On firmer 
footing, Abiel Reinhart & Harry Downie, 10/14/26). The front end sold off 15bp as the 
print thrust the October FOMC meeting back into the spotlight, and money markets 
briefly priced above a 50% probability of a hike in two weeks, with three hikes solidly 
priced by 1H27 (Figure 1Expectations for a hike in October rose folowing the above-consensus Septem
ber CPI print.). However, much of the move in yields unwound through the 
course of the session amid a weaker equities backdrop, and as markets digested dovish 
comments from Governor Bowman, who stressed from the sidelines of the IMF-World 
Bank meetings in Bangkok that she didn’t “feel any urgency for taking further action at 
this point,” echoing similar language from recent speeches by President Williams and 
Vice Chair Jefferson. Though she spoke before the CPI release, the comments suggest a 
more patient Committee overall.

event that will be closely watched: the appearance will represent the final major piece of

dearth of first tier data between now and the October meeting. We see value emerging at

drive yields materially lower. Additionally, technical factors remain a headwind. In our 
Treasury Client Survey released today, the share of outright longs saw its sharpest 
weekly decline this year, leaving the share of longs at the lowest level since mid-
August. However, unlike in prior hiking cycles, the survey index continues to lean 
long overall, and a wholesale reduction in these overweights could still drive yields

obvious catalysts ahead, we prefer to remain patient before adding outright

Prior small spikes in November 2025 and May 2026 reversed in the following print, so 
today’s flat reading reduces the likelihood the August surge will reverse. Together with 
transportation services, this pushed supercore CPI to 0.42% m/m, the highest since 
April. Supercore inflation is the most wage-driven inflation category and therefore 
should give the Committee pause when assessing their priors for the October FOMC.
Upside to shelter. OER was especially firm, rising 0.35%m/m, with rent (0.25%) near 
the upper end of the recent range. Excluding an April spike related to last fall’s 
government shutdown, this is the largest OER increase in 18 months. The rise was 
regionally broad-based, with an outsized gain in the Midwest, whose 0.72% rise almost 
equalled the highest post-COVID reading from 2022. Nationally, the shelter panel is 
split into six groups, each sampled every six months, and the latest month-on-month 
increase reflects the six-month change in the sampled panel. But the OER increase from 
six months ago, 0.28%m/m, does not look unusually small; if anything, it was 
moderately firm, so it is hard to argue this was payback for a small rise the last time this 
panel was sampled.
