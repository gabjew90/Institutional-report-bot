Out of GIR, Jordan Alliger out with transports Q3 preview: We would suggest staying the course with our
Buy rated names across the trucking sector – including Truckload (KNX, WERN, SNDR) and LTL
(ODFL, SAIA, ARCB, XPO, and hybrid TFII). The recent pull-back in trucking names (TL down 14%,
LTL down 20% vs broader transports down 10% since June 1) should afford the opportunity to capture the
historical share price rally that often arises for transports as we roll into peak shipping season, which
usually emerges due to volume growth accompanied by spot rate re-acceleration (Exhibit 7; plus see our
TL Spot Rate Seasonal Analysis note).
While there is risk around high energy prices and consumer resilience as we move into peak shopping
season, we note that channel checks suggest that as of now the demand environment remains stable and
GS economists suggest continued economic resilience (see our recent economists' note). Supporting this is
ongoing rail volume strength (Exhibit 15)--with both US intermodal and industrial traffic up +5% and
+2%, respectively, as we begin the 3Q and following +5% and +3% growth in the 2Q26, respectively--as
well as productive ISM readings with the most recent month closer to 55 (Exhibit 3) alongside an ongoing
positive spread between ISM new orders and inventories (Exhibit 4), as well as two consecutive positive
CASS freight readings (Exhibit 5).
While we cannot say for certain that peak demand will be strong, we would reiterate that truck supply
remains tight due to various regulations – so even in a modest peak freight environment (and barring a
negative volume situation), we would expect spot/contract price-related support for the group (Exhibit 6-
Exhibit 13). We separately note positive trends around used truck pricing (Exhibit 9) as well as ongoing
tightness in supply from a broadly limited Class 8 net order trend (Exhibit 10).
3Q and the Numbers: For the 3Q 2026, our estimate outlook is somewhat of a mixed bag with fuel related
expense pressures likely impacting rails and truckload to some degree due to lags in fuel surcharge
recovery mechanisms versus the very sharp spike in diesel prices during the 3Q (Exhibit 8). At the same
time, the LTL sectors stands to see some tailwind from fuel as it often does in this type of situation. Our
3Q26 estimates increase 1.5% on average vs prior estimates with Brokers/LTL revisions most positive at

+7%/+3%, Rails most negative at -2%, and TL slightly negative at -1% versus prior forecasts – in total we
lowered estimates for 6 companies, raised for 7 and left 3 unchanged (Exhibit 2-Exhibit 1). Full note
A&D – Noah out with takeaways from AUSA: We hosted meetings with 14 companies at the 2026 AUSA
conference. We met with large-cap defense, smid-cap defense, defense-tech, and government IT
companies.
Budget inputs: Most industry participants expect a small level of reconciliation and/or supplemental
spending, on top of the $1.15tn base budget, for FY27. Most see further growth beyond FY27 given
capability requirements, but at slow rates of change given the high base / difficult compares.
Priority areas of spending: There is a high degree of focus on unmanned, counter-UAS, space, electronic
warfare, hypersonics, and munitions re-stocking (both exquisite and low cost). Many companies

showcased UAS, USV and counter-UAS capabilities at the expo. Companies note demand for autonomous
vehicles in aerial and marine domains is driving higher demand for specialized components and payloads.
Companies are looking to further diversify supply chain in critical areas to be able to substantially ramp-
up production and meet demand.
Our stock views: We continue to recommend more stocks in defense tech than legacy defense, as we see
greater growth and margin expansion, driven by exposure to priority areas of spending and better ability to
run commercial models. On the defense side of our coverage we are Buy rated on HII (on CL), LHX,
AVAV, AVEX, CACI, HAWK, KTOS, DRS, TDY; while we are Sell rated on LMT, GD, MRCY, BAH.
