Larger-Than-Expected August Rebound 
in Retail Sales As Consumers Hold Up 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Headlines 
 
Retail sales jumped 1.2% in August. The timing of Amazon’s Prime Days 
provided some of the boost, but the details were solidly positive. 
 
 
The fundamentals for consumer spending are broadly negative, and risks 
are to the downside. 
 
 
PNC expects slower growth in consumer spending in the months ahead. 
Details
Retail sales jumped 1.2% in August from July, 
according to the advance release from the Census 
Bureau (Figure 1). This was better than the consensus 
expectation for an increase of 0.8%. These numbers 
are not adjusted for price changes. Retail sales fell 
0.5% in July (revised from a decline of 0.6%) after 
Amazon moved its Prime Days promotion to June in 
2026 from July in 2025; this shift boosted seasonally-
adjusted retail sales growth in August.  
 
The details of the report were solid, with lots of 
moving pieces. Sales at gasoline stations jumped 
3.1% over the month on higher gas prices. Sales of 
autos and parts rose 0.6%, making up only about 
one-third of July’s decline. Core sales, excluding 
autos and gasoline, rose 1.2%. 
 
Control retail sales—sales excluding autos, parts, 
gasoline, food service, and building materials, and 
which goes into nominal consumer spending in 
GDP—rose 1.4%, after falling 0.4% in July (Figure 2).  
 
Nonstore retail sales, primarily online sales, jumped 
2.6% in August, after falling 1.7% in July and 
increasing 1.0% in June. Over the past four months, 
smoothing out the impact of the Prime Days 
change, online sales rose an average of 0.6% per 
month, slightly slower than the pace over the similar 
period in 2025. 
 
On a year-ago basis overall retail sales were up 6.0%, 
with core sales up 5.6%, both solid gains. Control 
sales were up 5.6%, consistent with the pace of the 
last few years. 
 
Consumers are holding up in the summer of 2026, 
despite drags from high gasoline prices, tariffs, 
rising interest rates, and a labor market that has 
slowed somewhat from a couple of years ago. 
Larger tax refunds earlier in 2026, courtesy of the 
One Big Beautiful Bill Act, supported spending 
growth in the spring and early summer, but that 
positive driver has faded. 
 
Consumer spending growth is likely to slow in the 
months ahead but remain positive. Job growth has 
picked up somewhat this year from last, although 
wage growth is easing. High gasoline prices remain 
a drag, and expected Federal Reserve fed funds 
rate increases will raise borrowing costs for credit 
cards and auto loans. A soft housing market is 
weighing on sales of building materials, furniture,

Risks to consumer spending are weighted to the 
downside. Gasoline prices have moved higher in 
September, and if they remain elevated they will 
become more of a headwind for sales. And with 
stock gains powering much of the growth in 
spending, a large equity market correction would 
turn this positive household wealth effect into a 
negative. 
 
PNC expects the Federal Open Market Committee to 
raise the federal funds rate this afternoon by 25 basis 
points to a range of 3.75% to 4.00%, with two 
additional rate hikes likely to come in the months 
ahead. The higher fed funds rate will translate into 
higher rates on credit card debt and auto loans. 
 
There were gains in most segments in August. Sales 
at restaurants and bars jumped 1.2% over the month 
and were up 5.8% on a year-ago basis in August. If 
consumers were starting to buckle, this is a category
