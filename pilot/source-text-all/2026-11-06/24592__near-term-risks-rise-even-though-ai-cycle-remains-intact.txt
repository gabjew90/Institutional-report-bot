Expert opinion
Near-term risks rise even though AI cycle
remains intact
Global Head of Macro & SAA Salman Ahmed and Global Head of Multi Asset Matt
Quaife analyse the drivers of recent market volatility and discuss the broader
investment implications as we move into the next phase of the AI cycle. While
underlying fundamentals generally remain resilient, they highlight the importance of
maintaining a balanced approach to risk, with the flexibility to adapt quickly as the
macro landscape shifts.
PRO
Multiple authors Global 03/11/2026
The recent AI-related market volatility is a result of changing investor expectations. For much of the past
two years, markets have rewarded companies for capex on AI infrastructure. But now the focus is
shifting onto return on investment. Investors are increasingly asking whether profits and productivity
gains can justify the massive outlay the hyperscalers are making.
This transition comes against a challenging macroeconomic backdrop. Elevated bond yields are putting
pressure on the valuation of long-duration assets, such as AI companies whose present value is derived
from the expectation of cash flows in the future. Higher borrowing costs are squeezing the increasing
amount of AI capex funded though debt, increasing scrutiny on financing even for the hyperscalers.
Meanwhile, oil price volatility and persistent inflation constrains the Federal Reserve’s ability to ease
policy in response to any market weakness. Geopolitical risks could intensify following the US midterm
elections, while fiscal and political developments in Europe add another layer of uncertainty.
Despite the recent volatility, the underlying AI investment cycle remains intact. Our bottom-up analysts
continue to see robust demand and sustained investment across the AI supply chain. However, as
capital expenditure increasingly absorbs free cash flow, companies are becoming more sensitive to
borrowing costs.

We are maintaining a balanced approach to risk. With several macro factors potentially reaching
turning points, it is important to remain nimble and alert to opportunities as they emerge.

A sustained AI correction could have wider implications for financial conditions and economic growth.
AI capex has become a dominant driver of corporate spending and economic expectations. A more
prolonged cooling of sentiment towards AI could tighten financial conditions and weaken the growth
outlook.
Crowded positioning and high expectations have amplified the recent volatility. We believe near-term
risks have increased even though the longer-term opportunity remains. AI demand remains strong. The
next phase of the AI cycle will depend on whether companies can demonstrate a return on their
investments.
We remain cautiously optimistic
Although global markets are navigating a period of heightened volatility, the underlying economic
fundamentals remain more resilient than recent market movements might suggest. Global growth
continues to show signs of broadening, corporate earnings remain supportive, and the AI investment
cycle remains intact. Importantly, our quantitative indicators are neutral rather than signalling a decisive
risk-off shift, suggesting that a more balanced approach to risk is warranted.
Against this backdrop, we continue to hold a neutral stance on equities, reflecting a more finely
balanced risk-reward environment rather than a fundamental deterioration in our growth outlook. While
underlying demand remains robust, we expect greater differentiation between companies and sectors
going forward. We remain cautiously constructive, but believe patience and selectivity are important,
particularly as markets look for catalysts that could restore confidence.
Within fixed income, the recent decline in bond yields has provided some relief, but persistent inflation
risks, fiscal uncertainty, and likely monetary tightening argue against aggressively extending duration.
We continue to see opportunities in high yield credit, where attractive income and resilient corporate
fundamentals offer a compelling source of returns.
Our priority remains maintaining diversified and resilient portfolios, while retaining the flexibility to
respond as conditions evolve. With several political, geopolitical, and policy uncertainties potentially
reaching important turning points, we believe it is important to remain nimble, focused on fundamentals
rather than short-term noise, and prepared to take advantage of opportunities as they emerge.
