Expedia’s Q3’26 earnings report highlighted a number of themes: 1) 
core operating metrics (Gross Bookings, Revenue, Adj. EBITDA) 
exceeded street expectations and management guidance, driven by 
solid execution across both consumer and B2B businesses; 2) B2B 
remained a key growth driver, supported by continued partner 
expansion (now ~75k partners), opportunities to expand wallet 
share with existing partners, and ongoing investments in platform 
capabilities and trip breadth; 3) consumer execution remained solid, 
with management citing improving loyalty engagement, record 
attach rates, continued direct traffic strength (~2/3 of bookings), 
and progress across the Vrbo platform; 4) continued investment 
around B2B (“one-stop travel shop” strategy), AI-enabled 
experiences, and product innovation, while indicating these 
initiatives should remain largely self-funded through continued 
operational discipline and cost leverage; & 5) Expedia continues to 
see AI as a long-term opportunity across both customer acquisition 
and productivity, citing benefits from natural language search, 
customer service efficiencies, faster product development, and the 
potential diversification of upper-funnel traffic sources.  
Short-term, we expect investor debates to center around: 1) 
whether the softer Q4 outlook reflects temporary factors 
(election-related demand disruption, geopolitical impacts, and B2B 
promotional activity timing) or signals broader travel demand 
normalization; & 2) how Expedia’s investments in AI, alternative 
traffic channels, and B2B ultimately translate into sustainable 
growth and competitive differentiation relative to peers. Over the 
long-term, we remain constructive in EXPE’s ability to execute on 
strategic initiatives, execute on Adj. EBITDA margin expansion & 
return capital to shareholders. We reiterate our Buy rating on the 
shares, adjust our 12-month PT from $310 to $320, and update our 
operating estimates for this earnings result and management 
commentary.

Q3’26 Positives & Negatives: 
Positives: a) Q3 Gross Bookings and Revenue came in ahead of GSe/Street (FactSet) 
estimates and company guidance, primarily driven by resilient travel demand 
(particularly in the US market), and continued B2B momentum; b) B2B remained a 
notable area of strength with revenue growing +23% YoY and mgmt. highlighting 
broad-based DD% YoY growth across all regions, reiterating plans for continued 
segment expansion; & c) AI initiatives continue to drive strategic priorities, improving 
customer service efficiency, accelerating product development, and expanding the 
company’s presence across emerging AI discovery channels.  
Negatives: a) Mgmt. expects a moderation in Q4 growth compared to Q3, as growth 
laps stronger prior-year B2B partnership benefits, a softer demand environment, and 
the timing impact of Fall Sale activity shifting into Q3; b) Despite stronger B2B growth, 
mgmt. noted near-term B2B margin pressure as partner growth drives mix toward 
higher-tier agreements, as well as continued investments in the company’s one-stop 
travel shop strategy; & c) Moderating demand environment expected going into Q4, 
reflecting the US election cycle and geopolitical-related travel disruptions across EMEA. 
Q4’26 & 2027 Estimate Changes: 
Q4’26: Gross bookings of $28.19bn (from $28.08bn); Revenues of $3.72bn (unchanged);

Valuation: Maintain Buy Rating; Raise PT to $320 (from $310) 
 
 
Our $320, 12-month price target (from $310) is based on an equal blend of: (1) 
EV/GAAP EBITDA applied to our NTM + 1 year estimates and (2) a modified DCF using 
EV/FCF-SBC multiple applied to our NTM + 4 years estimates discounted back 3 years.

of companies within our coverage universe consisting of: (1) 3% risk free rate (based 
on the normalized 10-year rate); (2) average beta of ~1.3; (3) equity risk premium of 
7%. We lower our absolute multiple on the back of ongoing macroeconomic 
uncertainty and geopolitical volatility impacting forward growth. 
 
Risks to our Buy rating and price target include: a) a slower pace of travel demand 
resulting from potential macroeconomic headwinds and/or consumer discretionary 
spend shifts between services and goods could result in bookings & revenue growth 
underperforming our base case estimates; b) loss of North America market share to its 
peers would likely cause bookings & revenue to underperform our base case estimates 
and result in the need to incrementally invest in marketing; c) industry competitive 
intensity pressures revenue take rates and operating margins; & d) disappointing level of 
shareholder returns in forward years would limit a potential positive catalyst for the 
stock in the future.

normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
