Thoughts and Upcoming Week
Earnings largely wind down this week with LEU and MP both on Thursday (10/5). 
For N-rated LEU, we anticipate pressured margins, reflecting the loss of the cost-plus 
HALEU operations contract and mix headwinds, but we also expect more updates on 
the recently announced Westerman acquisition and incremental derisking (see 
earnings preview - link). For OW-rated MP, we anticipate earnings pressured by 
lagged 2Q maintenance-related costs and commentary focused on various project 
updates, including initial commercial magnet sales, heavies/recycling midstream 
operations and restarting on-site chlor-alkali facilities.
 Key takeaways from last week’s earnings:
•
NUE in-line 3Q (link): Mills was in-line and again reported record shipments, 
while downstream benefited from tubular shipments (+82% Y/Y) and J&D 
pricing (+$110/t Q/Q vs. STLD +$20/t). FCF totaled ~$1.6B (highest since 
3Q23), implying a ~11% annualized yield (vs. STLD’s implied ~8%) despite 
heavier capex. Management reiterated FY26 shipments +10% Y/Y, implying 
4Q step down (seasonality, fiscal days, planned maint.), with earnings 
conservatively guided “in-line or modestly higher.”
•
FCX 3Q beat (link): FY26/27 Cu sales cut ~100Kt each (-3%/-2%) due to 
weather disruptions/mine sequencing and FY26 production costs lifted 2% to 
$3.29/lb driven by US/SA (+4%/+2%). Bagdad FID targeted for 4Q26, with 
initial ~$4.5B budget reiterated. Commercial-scale additive trials on track for 
1H27, which, if successful, could drive upside to ~300Mlbs FYE27 run-rate 
target (prior FYE26).
•

Sector Highlights
•
US-Canada discuss easing S232 steel/auto tariffs, according to Reuters on October 
26 (link). The countries are reportedly working toward an agreement that could 
reduce S232 duties, and Canadian sources have indicated a deal could come before 
the November 3 midterms. Our thoughts: The import arb for Canada HRC remains 
solidly closed, even when assuming a reduced 25% rate, suggesting minimal impact 
to US steel markets still reliant on the marginal import. 
•
US mills tighten 2027 contract terms as flat-rolled/plate pricing and lead times 
stay elevated, according to Platts on October 26. Market participants see mills cutting 
2027 HRC/HDG discounts to 2%-3.5% vs. 6%-8% for 2026, eliminating rebates in 
some cases, reducing monthly volumes, and in certain instances not participating in 
select products/gauge ranges, leaving little room to negotiate. Plate buyers similarly 
said discounts/rebates were being cut and max volumes/guaranteed tons reduced. 
Our thoughts: Tight conditions during the year-end negotiating season have resulted 
in a ‘sellers market’ for 2027 contracts that should benefit domestic mills.
Company Highlights
•
Canada escalates scrutiny of Cliffs’ Stelco job actions and Investment Canada 
commitments, according to Bloomberg on October 22 (link). Ottawa has given Cliffs 15 
days to restore jobs or justify layoffs tied to the idling of Hamilton Works' value-added 
operations, warning it could pursue court-ordered remedies, penalties and/or a forced 
divestment if commitments are not met. Cliffs responded in an October 26 company 
release (link) arguing Stelco is in full compliance and that employment exceeds required 
thresholds, while requesting a meeting with Canada’s Minister of Industry ahead of an 
October 31 reporting-period close. In an October 27 interview with CBC (link) and 
statement to SMU, CEO Lourenco Goncalves said Stelco is not for sale and suggested any 
sale consideration would be contingent on at least one year of higher production and 
recalling the ~175 laid-off workers. Our thoughts: We largely view the move by CLF/
STLC as a push for tougher trade policy for value-add products in particular amid 
ongoing import pressure in Canada.
•
Centrus ships first new centrifuge supporting US enrichment expansion, according 
to an October 29 company release (link). Its Oak Ridge, TN facility has began shipping 
completed centrifuges to OH for final assembly, with manufacturing rates expected to 
ramp over time. Our thoughts: We view this as a tangible de-risking milestone completed 
ahead of the company’s prior year-end target; however, the ramp remains long-dated and 
the current near-term AI risk-off tape keeps us Neutral (recent note linked).
•
Constellation gets $5B US DOE loan commitment, according to an October 27 DOE 
release (link). The conditional commitment would support nuclear upgrades across the 
company’s fleet, adding ~1.1 GW of incremental capacity. Our thoughts: The 
announcement is consistent with the Administration’s broader push to expand US nuclear 
output, and we view this as a constructive long-term signal for domestic nuclear fuel-
cycle demand (uranium/enrichment), though the magnitude and timing will depend on 
project execution cadence and procurement strategy.
•
Audi plans first US production site in Georgia, according to an October 25 media 
release (link). The ~$4B plant will be designed for up to 200K vehicles of annual capacity, 
and construction is scheduled to start in 2027 (with production in early 2030s). Our 
thoughts: Reshoring of auto production is beneficial for ~25% of US steel demand and 
integrated players like CLF in particular, although mini mills like NUE continue to make 
a push to gain share in the most stringent exposed/HSS auto applications, which remains

Rio Tinto set to restart underground mining at Oyu Tolgoi Copper Mine, according 
to MarketWatch on October 30 (link). The company had paused underground activities

GM cancels VAC rare-earth magnet supply deal, according to Reuters on October 23 
(link). GM terminated its strategic sourcing agreement with VAC’s US unit (owned by 
Energy Fuels), removing ~90% of the planned offtake for VAC’s South Carolina rare-
earth permanent magnet plant. VAC and GM are in arbitration, with VAC seeking 
damages tied to the termination. Our thoughts: GM, which has heavily pulled back its 
EV ambitions, has a legacy supply deal with MP (~1Ktpa) for permanent magnets that 
is currently undergoing qualification, with first commercial shipments imminent.

NRC accepts General Matter’s first license application for review, according to an 
October 29 company release (link). US Nuclear Regulatory Commission noted “the 
quality and completeness of the application reflect the effectiveness of General Matter’s 
constructive pre-application engagement with the NRC staff,” enabling the NRC “to 
accept the application in approximately half the timeframe typically allotted for an 
acceptance review.” The NRC also said it “will pursue appropriate efficiencies and 
consider opportunities to expedite portions of the review.” General Matter reiterates its 
plan to submit a license amendment to produce HALEU next year.

Recent JPM Global Research

North America Nuclear: Examining the New Nuclear Debate - Establishing a New 
‘Nuclear Compact’ Could Underpin Next of a Kind Positive Proof Point, Ushering 
In Next Wave of Nuclear Development (link). Nuclear equities traded favorably in 2025 
on accelerating “power scarcity” views tied to data centers. The group has since de-rated 
sharply in 2026 as momentum unwind, data center opposition, and higher interest rates 
weigh on long-dated cash flows. Here, we understand the market’s nuclear concerns 
given data center demand trajectory questions coupled with FOAK-to-NOAK execution 
risk, long construction timelines, and recent history highlighting uncertain capital costs. 
However, we increasingly see the building blocks of an investable nuclear framework 
coming together. Bipartisan federal support, state-level funding frameworks, foreign 
capital, and hyperscaler engagement converge around a new nuclear compact. We believe 
the market will underwrite the nuclear trade if all stakeholders come to the table and agree 
on a framework to share development risks and employ creative financing arrangements. 
In any scenario, we still believe load growth from data centers, electrification, onshoring, 
coupled with the desire for firm, clean power circles back to new nuclear, where first 
development (domestic or abroad) will spark quick followers.
