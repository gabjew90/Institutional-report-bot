04 November 2026
Iranian attacks in the SoH could ease with mission accomplished in
US midterms
A large risk premium for imminent attack on Iran has left crude oil prices. Brent crude fell back 1.1%
yesterday. It closed at $103.6/b after having traded in a wide range of $100.8 - 107.24/b. A huge reset
following the Friday closing price of $117.64/b (the Dec-26 contract). What is clear is that a large risk
premium for the moment has left oil prices as Trump stood down from his threats of immediate strikes on
Iran post the US midterm elections. There is still clearly a risk that Trump-Israel could choose to strike, but
the sense of any immediate overhanging risk has left the market. Thus prices falling sharply. ICE Brent has
traded as high as $104.96/b this morning. Following recent very high risk and prices, the appetite for
piking up Brent Q1-27 at close to "only" $100/b has probably supported the buy side a little. 
But there is still a lot of risk. You cannot fully remove the risk of a new attack. We are still far from a proper
resolution. It is the imminent risk of a Trump-Israel attack on Iran which has fallen back sharply.
Trump has no immediate plans of another wide attack on Iran. Probably because such an attack
would be very costly. Trump has now stated that he has no immediate plans of striking Iran. He also
commented on the fact that there haven't been any full, frontal attacks on Iran for the past six
months: "...if you think about it, we haven’t really had a war with them in a long time, six months, maybe
more.”. The reason for it is of course that the cost of full, frontal attacks on Iran became too high already
back in April. Major energy infrastructure in the Gulf were damaged and so were a range of US military
bases to the point that no US congressmen were allowed to visit them and see how bad it was. Now more
than six months have passed and Iran has regrouped, rebuilt and prepared. The cost for the US and its Gulf
allies (if you can call them that anymore) is now higher than at any time since April.
Iran may ease attacks in the SoH following an October peak of such attack in the runup to US
midterm elections. Trump has already paid a dear political price for his war against Iran: The loss of both
chambers in Congress. Iranian officials in October apparently stated that Iran was ramping up attacks in
the SoH in the runup to the US midterm elections with the aim of impacting the elections through gasoline
and diesel prices at the pump in the US. And Iranian attacks in October have been very high through
October. Probably twice as high as in September. Mission accomplished. Republicans lost both chambers
in the US Congress. Democrats will now have a stronger voice and it will not be so easy for Trump to get
whatever money he wants for his war with Iran. This is a clear strategic win for Iran. It strengthen Iran's
negotiation position. As such we could see Iran easing the attacks in the SoH back to the level in
September. I.e. more oil will escape more easily from the Persian Gulf. Looking at Brent versus Oman Jan-
27 futures we see Oman at $101.3/b which is well below Brent at $103.8/b. A sign that oil is indeed
flowing out of the SoH. The pressure at this geographic point is easing rather than rising.
Iran and the US are indirectly negotiating in Tehran through Qatari mediators. But Iran's security chief,
Mohsen Rezaee says that what the US has offered is not satisfactory. Iran want's an end to the naval
blockade, a release of frozen funds and sanctions relief. It also want recognition that it together with Oman
is in control of the SoH. The US wants a resolution of the nuclear issue. And there it stands.
