COMPANY DESCRIPTION
Founded in 2009 and headquartered in Rockville, Maryland, X-energy develops the Xe-100, an 80 MWe high-temperature gas-cooled
reactor designed for deployment in four-unit, 320 MWe plants. The company also manufactures proprietary TRISO-X nuclear fuel

and Centrica. X-energy completed its Nasdaq IPO in April 2026 and operates through an Up-C corporate structure.

CORPORATE HISTORY AND STRATEGIC EVOLUTION
X-energy was founded in 2009 by Dr. Kamal Ghaffarian to develop advanced nuclear technology, building on decades of high-temperature
gas-cooled reactor research and earlier pebble-bed reactor programs. A key milestone came in December 2020, when the company was
selected for the Department of Energy's Advanced Reactor Demonstration Program (ARDP), followed by a February 2021 cost-sharing
agreement supporting reactor development, fuel manufacturing and its first planned deployment with Dow. X-energy subsequently
expanded its commercial pipeline through Amazon's 2024 investment and development agreement targeting more than 5 GWe of
potential capacity by 2039, Centrica's September 2025 agreement targeting approximately 6 GWe in the United Kingdom, and the October
2026 AEP agreement identifying another potential Amazon-backed deployment in Indiana.
In parallel, X-energy has developed its proprietary TRISO-X fuel manufacturing platform in Oak Ridge, Tennessee, where it has operated
a pilot facility since 2016 and began construction of its first commercial facility, TX-1, in 2024. TX-1 received its NRC Special Nuclear
Material License in February 2026 and completed vertical construction in September 2026. The company has funded development through
ARDP reimbursements, private capital and its April 2026 IPO, priced at $23.00 per share. X-energy operates through an Up-C structure,
with the publicly traded company holding a controlling economic interest in X-Energy Reactor Company, LLC, while pre-IPO investors
retain exchangeable interests. We view these milestones as positioning X-energy to transition from technology development toward
commercialization, with the next phase dependent on customer final investment decisions (FIDs), fuel manufacturing readiness and
conversion of development agreements into binding contracts.

BUSINESS MODEL & UNIT ECONOMICS
An Integrated Reactor and Fuel Model with Attractive Potential Economics
X-energy intends to generate revenue from technology licensing, engineering services and recurring fuel fabrication while customers
finance, own and operate the nuclear plants. Management estimates approximately $2.41–4.73B of cumulative revenue per standard
four-reactor, 320 MWe plant over development and 60 years of operation, comprising $50–300MM of technology licensing fees,
$800MM–$1.3B of services revenue and $1.56–3.13B of fuel fabrication revenue. The illustrative framework assumes gross margins
approaching 100% for technology licensing, 20–30% for services and 30–60% for fuel, implying a blended gross margin of approximately
30–55%. We believe this integrated model offers attractive potential economics, although management's estimates are illustrative rather
than contracted, and realized returns will depend on customer agreements, reactor deployment and manufacturing execution.

Technology Licensing Offers the Highest Margins; Services Provide Revenue Throughout the Reactor Lifecycle.
Licensing fees compensate X-energy for the use of its proprietary Xe-100 design and carry limited associated direct costs. Management
anticipates lower fees for initial customers, followed by higher pricing as the technology establishes a commercial track record and
deployment scales. Services include engineering, licensing support, design integration, procurement assistance, construction support and
ongoing technical services. Unlike licensing, services require labor and other direct costs, with actual revenue dependent on the scope of
work retained by X-energy versus customers and third-party contractors. We believe revenue timing is an important consideration for
valuation, as technology licensing fees and development-related engineering revenue could be earned in the years preceding commercial
operation, while ongoing services and the majority of fuel fabrication revenue would be generated over the subsequent decades.
Fuel Fabrication Represents the Largest Recurring Revenue Opportunity
Fuel fabrication represents the largest component of X-energy's illustrative lifetime revenue framework. The Xe-100 uses spherical
graphite fuel elements containing coated uranium particles that can be added and removed continuously while the reactor operates.
Unlike conventional nuclear reactors that periodically shut down to replace fuel assemblies, the Xe-100 is designed for online refueling,
supporting high availability and creating recurring demand for fabricated fuel. Management estimates approximately 220,000 fuel pebbles
per initial reactor core, with roughly 175 pebbles replaced daily during normal operation. Management estimates $1.56–3.13B of
cumulative fuel fabrication revenue per four-reactor plant, with illustrative gross margins of 30–60%.
X-energy expects customers to provide or fund the underlying high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU), allowing the company to
earn fabrication revenue while limiting direct exposure to uranium commodity prices. We view the combination of proprietary reactor
technology and fuel manufacturing as a potential competitive advantage because it positions X-energy to supply fuel throughout the
operating lives of its installed reactors. However, the company has not disclosed binding long-term fuel fabrication pricing, and its
illustrative revenue and margin ranges are not formal financial guidance. Actual profitability will depend on realized fabrication prices,
production costs, manufacturing utilization and reactor deployment, while HALEU availability remains a potential constraint despite
customers bearing the underlying commodity cost.

COMMERCIAL PIPELINE
X-energy has identified a potential commercial pipeline of approximately 144 Xe-100 reactors, representing 11.5 GWe of capacity,
across the U.S. and U.K. The pipeline is anchored by Dow's 320 MWe Seadrift project in Texas, the most advanced deployment,
alongside Amazon-backed developments with Energy Northwest in Washington and AEP in Indiana, and a broader U.K. opportunity
with Centrica. The October 2026 AEP announcement improves project visibility by identifying a utility partner, site and prospective
electricity buyer, although its proposed 960 MWe is already included in the reported pipeline. We view the growing number of site-specific
projects as encouraging, but most remain in development. Ultimately, construction permits, definitive commercial agreements, financing
commitments and final investment decisions will determine how much of the pipeline converts into revenue.

Dow Seadrift — The First Commercial Reference Plant
Dow's Seadrift project in Texas is X-energy's first planned commercial deployment, comprising four Xe-100 reactors with approximately
320 MWe of combined capacity. The facility is intended to supply electricity and high-temperature steam to Dow's existing chemical
manufacturing operations. Dow subsidiary Long Mott Energy is the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) construction-permit applicant
and intended plant owner and operator, while X-energy will provide reactor technology, engineering services and fuel. The industrial
application could provide a commercial reference for customers requiring both electricity and process heat.
Long Mott submitted its construction-permit application in March 2025, which the NRC docketed in May 2025. The NRC completed its
environmental review and issued a Finding of No Significant Impact in May 2026, with the final safety evaluation targeted for November
2026. Management anticipates construction-permit issuance in 1Q2027, a customer FID in 2027 and first commercial operation around
1Q2032. A separate operating license will be required, with the application expected around 2029.
Dow remains the most important near-term commercialization milestone. The total plant construction budget and definitive technology
and fuel contract economics have not been disclosed, making FID a key indicator of customer commitment and project viability. The
project also benefits from DOE cost-sharing support, discussed separately in Government Support and Incentives.

AEP and Amazon — A Second Utility-Backed Project in Indiana
On October 19, 2026, X-energy and American Electric Power (AEP) announced a joint development agreement to evaluate a proposed
960 MWe facility at the former Breed Generating Station in Sullivan County, Indiana. The project would comprise twelve Xe-100
reactors across three four-unit groups, with AEP subsidiary Indiana Michigan Power providing the proposed site and utility development
capabilities.
Amazon will provide milestone-based development funding and intends to purchase the facility's electricity under a long-term
arrangement if the project proceeds. The proposed structure is intended to support project economics while limiting financial exposure
for existing utility customers. Reuse of the former coal-generation site may offer infrastructure and transmission advantages, although
nuclear-specific licensing, engineering and interconnection requirements remain.
The project is at the feasibility stage. The parties have not disclosed a construction budget, definitive electricity purchase terms, financing
structure, NRC application schedule or expected commercial operation date. The proposed 960 MWe is already included in X-energy's
approximately 11.5 GWe pipeline and should not be treated as incremental capacity or committed backlog. The next milestones are
completion of development studies, definitive commercial agreements and a project investment decision.
Centrica — Long-Term International Optionality
X-energy and Centrica entered into a development agreement in September 2025 targeting approximately 6 GWe of potential Xe-100
capacity in the United Kingdom. Hartlepool has been identified as the preferred initial location, with a proposed configuration of up to
twelve reactors totaling 960 MWe.
The Xe-100 was accepted for entry into the U.K. Generic Design Assessment process in September 2026. This review evaluates whether
the reactor design can meet applicable safety, security and environmental requirements but does not constitute site-specific approval or
construction authorization. Management anticipates an approximately three-year design review, subject to regulatory progress.
The Centrica agreement provides a potential international expansion opportunity but remains at an early development stage. Definitive
commercial agreements, project financing, site-specific approvals and a committed construction schedule have not been disclosed.
Progress through design assessment and toward an initial Hartlepool investment decision will be the principal indicators of development.

GOVERNMENT SUPPORT & INCENTIVES
Federal Funding Supports Reactor Development and Initial Deployment
X-energy was selected for the Department of Energy's (DOE) Advanced Reactor Demonstration Program (ARDP) in December 2020 and
entered into a cooperative agreement in February 2021. The original agreement provides federal reimbursement of 50% of eligible project
expenditures, up to approximately $1.2B, against an estimated $2.4B of eligible costs. Funding supports Xe-100 reactor development
and licensing, construction of the TX-1 fuel fabrication facility and the initial Dow Seadrift deployment. Subsequent commercial reactor
projects and additional fuel manufacturing facilities are not included in the original award.
As of June 30, 2026, X-energy had received approximately $546.7MM in ARDP reimbursements, leaving approximately $653MM
relative to the original award ceiling. This amount is not an unrestricted funding commitment, as future reimbursements depend
on eligible expenditures, available appropriations, DOE approvals and compliance with program requirements. The current approved
budget period extends through March 2027, with additional funding periods subject to continuation approvals. X-energy has also been
selected for incremental federal support, although additional funding requires a formal award modification. The timing and availability
of reimbursements remain relevant to the Dow project, whose targeted commercial operation extends beyond the current ARDP funding
period.
Tax Incentives May Improve Reactor and Fuel Manufacturing Economics
Federal tax incentives may also benefit X-energy’s customers and manufacturing operations. The Section 45Y Clean Electricity Production
Tax Credit (a per-kilowatt-hour credit on electricity generated by a qualifying plant) and Section 48E Clean Electricity Investment Tax
Credit (a credit equal to a percentage of the qualifying capital cost of the generating facility) may improve project economics for qualifying
nuclear plant owners, subject to applicable eligibility and timing requirements. These credits generally benefit customers financing and
owning the generating facilities rather than X-energy directly.
Separately, X-energy’s TRISO-X subsidiary received an approximately $150MM allocation of the Section 48C advanced-energy
manufacturing tax credit (an investment credit on qualifying costs of building or expanding manufacturing facilities) for its TX-1 facility
in Oak Ridge, Tennessee. Realization of the credit remains subject to applicable certification, qualifying investment and placed-in-service
requirements.

TRISO-X FUEL MANUFACTURING
TX-1 Establishes Commercial Fuel Manufacturing Capacity
X-energy's TRISO-X subsidiary is developing the proprietary fuel required by the Xe-100 reactor. TRISO fuel consists of uranium particles
surrounded by protective carbon and ceramic layers and embedded within spherical graphite pebbles. The Xe-100 is designed to circulate
and replace these pebbles during operation, creating demand for initial fuel cores and recurring replacement fuel without conventional
refueling outages. Although TRISO technology has decades of research and operating precedent, commercial-scale TRISO-X production
for an operating Xe-100 has not yet been demonstrated.

TX-1, located in Oak Ridge, Tennessee, is X-energy's first commercial-scale fuel manufacturing facility. In February 2026, the company
received a 40-year Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) Special Nuclear Material License covering TX-1 and planned TX-2 operations
at the licensed site. TX-1 is designed for approximately 6.25 metric tons of uranium (MTU) of annual nameplate capacity and 5
MTU of effective throughput, sufficient for the steady-state refueling requirements of approximately eleven Xe-100 reactors. The facility
completed vertical construction in September 2026 and is progressing through interior buildout, equipment installation and supporting
infrastructure work. Management targets startup in 1H2028.
The NRC manufacturing license authorizes specified nuclear material activities but does not establish that commercial fuel production
and qualification have been completed. TRISO-X must commission its equipment, demonstrate consistent manufacturing quality and
yields, and satisfy applicable fuel qualification requirements before supplying operating reactors.

HALEU Supply and Long-Term Fuel Opportunities
The Xe-100 uses high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU) enriched to approximately 15.5% uranium-235. Domestic commercial HALEU
availability remains limited, making feedstock supply an important requirement for fuel manufacturing. X-energy has received an
allocation of approximately 7.6 MTU through DOE's HALEU Availability Program, which management expects to support the initial
Dow project, and has entered into enrichment agreements with Centrus Energy and General Matter to support future requirements. X-
energy expects customers to provide or fund the underlying HALEU, limiting its direct exposure to uranium commodity costs, although
fabrication remains dependent on timely delivery of qualified material.
We discuss the broader TRISO fuel market, competitive landscape and merchant manufacturing opportunities in greater detail in
our Standard Nuclear (STDN) initiation note. X-energy's strategy differs in that TRISO-X is expected to primarily supply fuel for its
proprietary Xe-100 reactor fleet through long-term supply agreements (LTSAs) with plant owners. This would establish recurring
fabrication revenue tied to the installed reactor base rather than principally to third-party merchant demand. X-energy also intends to
supply other advanced reactor developers and government customers, although these opportunities remain secondary to expected Xe-100
requirements. The company has not disclosed definitive long-term fuel pricing or sufficient binding third-party agreements to establish
a material merchant revenue base.

TECHNOLOGY OVERVIEW
Xe-100 — High-Temperature Nuclear Power for Electricity and Industrial Heat
The Xe-100 is an 80 MWe high-temperature gas-cooled reactor designed for deployment in standardized four-unit, 320 MWe plants. Unlike
conventional U.S. nuclear reactors, which use water to transfer heat from the core, the Xe-100 uses helium coolant and graphite-based
fuel pebbles. The reactor is designed to produce steam at approximately 565°C, enabling it to supply industrial process heat alongside
electricity generated through a conventional steam turbine. This expands its potential applications beyond electricity generation to
industrial facilities that currently rely on fossil fuels for process steam.
X-energy targets a 60-year operating life and capacity factors above 95%, with the ability to adjust output in response to changing electricity
demand. These performance objectives have not yet been demonstrated by an operating Xe-100. The first commercial deployment at
Dow's Seadrift facility is intended to demonstrate the reactor's ability to supply both electricity and high-temperature steam in an
industrial setting.
Passive Safety Is Central to the Design
The Xe-100 incorporates several passive safety features, including coated fuel particles designed to retain radioactive material at high
temperatures, a graphite core that absorbs heat and reactor characteristics intended to limit temperature increases during certain
accident conditions. Helium coolant is chemically inert and does not undergo the boiling or phase changes associated with water-cooled
reactors. These characteristics are intended to reduce reliance on active cooling systems and support a smaller emergency planning
zone. Management has proposed an emergency planning zone extending approximately 400 meters from the reactor, subject to Nuclear
Regulatory Commission (NRC) review and approval.
The design draws on operating experience from earlier high-temperature gas-cooled reactors, although Xe-100-specific safety
performance and operating characteristics remain subject to regulatory review and commercial demonstration.

REGULATORY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE
Licensing Remains a Major Barrier to Commercial Deployment
X-energy is pursuing the Nuclear Regulatory Commission's (NRC) two-step licensing process under 10 CFR Part 50, which requires a
construction permit followed by a separate operating license before commercial operation. Dow's Seadrift project is the most advanced,
with its environmental review complete and final safety evaluation pending. Energy Northwest remains in pre-application activities,
while AEP's Indiana project is at the feasibility stage. Licensing progress will influence construction schedules, customer final investment
decisions and the timing of X-energy's technology, services and fuel revenue.
Fuel manufacturing is subject to separate regulatory requirements. TX-1 received a 40-year NRC Special Nuclear Material License in
February 2026, although commercial production remains dependent on commissioning, operational readiness and fuel qualification.
In the United Kingdom, licensing is a separate, multi-step process. The Xe-100 was accepted for entry into the Generic Design Assessment
(GDA) in September 2026, a voluntary review of the reactor design by the Office for Nuclear Regulation (ONR), the Environment Agency
and Natural Resources Wales, which management expects to take about three years. The GDA does not authorize construction. Centrica’s
proposed Hartlepool deployment would still need an ONR site licence and consent to begin nuclear-related construction, environmental
permits and planning permission. Management expects first power in the mid-2030s, subject to securing these approvals.

TRISO Fuel Competition is Expanding, but Business Models Differ
The domestic TRISO fuel market is transitioning from small-scale production toward commercial manufacturing as advanced reactor
development progresses. X-energy's TX-1 is designed for commercial-scale production under a 40-year Nuclear Regulatory Commission
(NRC) Category II Special Nuclear Material License. Standard Nuclear has begun smaller-scale production under a Department of
Energy (DOE) authorization, while BWX Technologies continues to develop specialized nuclear fuel capabilities. Differences in regulatory
authorization, permitted materials and manufacturing capacity make direct comparisons of commercial readiness difficult.
Importantly, X-energy's fuel strategy differs from Standard Nuclear's merchant manufacturing model. While Standard Nuclear
intends to supply TRISO fuel to multiple reactor developers, X-energy is primarily developing TRISO-X capacity to serve its proprietary
Xe-100 fleet through long-term fuel supply agreements with reactor owners. Consequently, X-energy's fuel revenue will depend principally
on Xe-100 deployments and recurring refueling requirements rather than competition for third-party manufacturing contracts. Although
broader industry capacity could influence fuel pricing, direct merchant competition is less central to X-energy's business model.

VALUATION FRAMEWORK
Valuation Framework
We do not assign formal price targets to Hold-rated companies. Instead, we use a scenario-based valuation framework to assess what level
of reactor deployment is reflected in X-energy's current share price. We believe conventional near-term EBITDA multiples are of limited
use, as the company is not expected to reach meaningful commercial scale by 2030E. Even 2035E EBITDA would not fully capture the
recurring economics of the installed reactor fleet, as earnings during the buildout would include substantial technology licensing revenue
while many reactors have yet to begin generating recurring services and fuel revenue. Consequently, EBITDA in any single year during
this period could be heavily influenced by construction timing rather than the long-term earnings capacity of the business.
We therefore use a 2040E steady-state valuation framework that separates recurring earnings from development-related fees. For each
scenario, we capitalize the recurring EBITDA generated by fuel fabrication and lifecycle services from a fully deployed reactor fleet and
discount that value to the present. We value technology licensing separately based on the present value of fees earned during construction.
This approach distinguishes the long-term recurring earnings opportunity from the timing of one-time licensing revenue.
Our framework considers four deployment scenarios, each assuming the included projects are fully operational by 2040E. The first includes
Dow Seadrift alone (0.32 GWe; four reactors). The second adds 5.2 GWe of Energy Northwest and Amazon-related opportunities, bringing
total capacity to 5.52 GWe (69 reactors). The third incorporates Centrica's 6.0 GWe U.K. opportunity, representing the full identified
pipeline of approximately 11.52 GWe (144 reactors). The fourth assumes an additional 1.0 GWe of unidentified projects, bringing total
capacity to 12.52 GWe. Full deployment of the announced pipeline by 2040E would require a substantial acceleration in reactor
construction and fuel manufacturing, particularly given that initial commercial operations are not expected until the early 2030s. These
scenarios represent illustrative deployment outcomes rather than probability-weighted forecasts.
At approximately $10–11 per share, X-energy trades between our illustrative Dow-plus-Amazon value of $7.60 and full identified-
pipeline value of $12.00. Linear interpolation suggests the market is assigning value equivalent to roughly 9–10 GWe of deployment,
or approximately 75–90% of the announced pipeline. This implies meaningful credit for Centrica's U.K. opportunity, despite its early
development status and lack of binding construction commitments. While this analysis does not establish a formal price target or precise
market-implied project probabilities, it provides a framework for evaluating the commercial execution reflected in today's valuation.
Given the scale of deployment required, we view the risk/reward as balanced, supporting our Hold rating.
