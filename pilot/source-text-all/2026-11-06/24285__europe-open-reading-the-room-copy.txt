Europe Open - Reading the room
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
RBA hiked rates by 25bp but 6-3 vote split, the removal of further hikes in the statement, and Governor Bullock signaling
a more wait-and-see approach so AUD drop and bonds rise as markets adjust future rate hike decisions. Though the
committee stress upside risks, it looks like they have reached terminal rates. USTs were closed overnight, USD drifted.
After a near 8% selloff in oil, prices are correcting higher after fresh defensive attacks from the US on Iranian targets,
meanwhile Reuters also notes skepticism towards a deal. Asia stocks weaker as tech sentiment once again skews
negative amid AI jitters (Apple earnings were also mixed).
It's US midterms day. First polls close in Indiana and Kentucky at 23:00 GMT (18:00 ET), with the last closing at 06:00 on
Wed. House majority will become clear overnight with Senate outcome dependent on key swing races, the first to report
~03:00 ET. Expect conviction to remain light as polls remain open, but US Jolts job openings does print at 15:00 GMT for
Sep. ECB Nagel and Escriva should maintain a hawkish lean. Q3 HK GDP prints shortly.

In Focus‌
RBA hiked rates by 25bp in line with expectations, but the split decision (6-3), removing the reference to hike further if
needed, and Governor Bullock signaling a wait-and-see approach skewed dovish. Rates are now expected to remain at this
level through end-2028, with OIS pricing keeping a slight premium of a hike into Feb (+11bp). AUDUSD down -0.25% at print,
our Asia trader has closed his longs post CPI and thinks topside will be limited from here. 0.6900 marks key support. Our
options desk like owning AudCross gamma given recent USD decorrelation amid cross assets brutal sell offs, and we like
owning Aud long term vols with 1y atm at 8.7v for devoid of vol risk premium.

Rates: USTs were closed overnight due to Japanese holiday, with bullish moves in AU/NZ bonds post RBA. Expect moves to
remain rangebound as its US midterms day, with polling stations not closing until late European session (first polls close in
Kentucky/Indiana around 18:00 ET/23:00 GMT). Watch data in the form of JOLTS job openings at 15:00 GMT for September.
Bloomberg cons. expects openings to remain steady at 7100k vs 7079k with low quits/layoffs rates indicating a low churn
labor market. ICYMI - US Economics - NFP Preview - Slowing wage growth to limit hawkish market reactions. We expect a
solid 65k increase in payrolls and the u/e rate remaining at 4.2%. 
FX: Interbank volumes are ~4% higher than 30d averages with our platforms seeing leveraged names net buying USD, GBP
and selling JPY, AUD and EUR. Overnight moves were modest, so instead we flag two interesting notes from CitiFX Quant -
Flow & Positioning highlights long USD positioning was reduced last week but still elevated at +5.8, mostly by RMs and retail
investors. Risks appear skewed towards USD selling this week ahead of events, as HFs have already shifted towards a
bearish view. They also analyze Trading USD with Citi Equities POLLS Indicator. USD has historically also gained after very
low/equity bullish POLLS readings, pointing to USD smile behavior. The strongest USD gains have historically occurred more
than three weeks after POLLS triggered above 18 which would be consistent with roughly now for the latest sign. 
Don't miss FX eTrading Microstructure Report Q3 and October 2026. Interbank volumes declined by 3% QoQ overall. G7
volumes fell 1%, Scandies declined 14%, CEEMEA fell 8% and Asia dropped 13% QoQ. Scandies saw the sharpest
contraction, while G7 proved most resilient. Interbank spreads tightened across all G10 currency pairs and most EM pairs;
however, the magnitude varied meaningfully at the pair level.
Client call today at 14:00 GMT: October 2026 FX Forecast Update. Join us to discuss the latest developments across the
asset class in our latest update. Q&A and replay will be available. 
Equities: Negative tone once again with Kospi/Taiex down -3.5% after a major AI firm's ~$900bn new capex pledge and all-
share M&A deals signals tech cash strain. US equity futures are more modest but could pick up as the European session
continues. New Equity Markets Positioning Model shows further moderation in US large-cap positioning with S&P
500/Nasdaq positioning closer to neutral. Europe remains comparatively resilient, supported by ongoing long accumulation,
while Asia's consensus short positioning is creating a backdrop where short-covering risks continue to build. Key positioning
risks include further US growth de-risking, a potential European Banks squeeze, and profit-taking across profitable Asian
short exposures. Apple earnings beat, but December outlook was mixed with higher product revenues partially offset by lower
services growth and gross margins. Despite the mixed outlook, we remain constructive on its long-term trajectory. 
Commodities: After a near -8% oil selloff on Monday, some negative headlines are driving a slight correction, (Brent up
1.57% at print). The US conducted defensive strikes on some Iranian targets in the SoH, including an attack on Qeshm
Island. As we go to print, Reuters reports that iran and US are far from reaching a deal to end the war. Gold down -0.9% amid
headlines, watch whether $3900 holds as support.
Elsewhere‌
CHF: CPI MoM at 07:30 GMT for October. SNB continues to signal little urgency to adjust rates, with a stable monthly inflation
supporting the case for remaining on hold. Headline CPI is expected at 1.2% YoY versus 1.0% prior (Bloomberg cons).
USD: US Midterm Election. Democrats are widely expected to win the House and are slight favorites to win the Senate, albeit
within the margin of error. First polling stations close from 18:00 ET, with the majority closing between 19:00 - 21:00 ET.
Earliest clues for the House majority could emerge from 19:30-20:00, with Senate outcome potentially later in the night from
22:00. Dem sweep is becoming increasingly priced in, suggesting a benign outcome on a divided outcome. See US Midterms:
A final markets guide for more.
EUR: ECB's Nagel speaks in Berlin at 17:00 GMT followed by Escriva in Madrid (no time). In recent comments, Nagel stated
that the decision to hold rates in Oct “wasn’t an easy one”, keeping hikes on the table for future meetings.
HKD: GDP YoY at 08:30 GMT for Q3. Our economists anticipate GDP growth at 3.5%YoY, supported by AI-related
merchandise trade and rising investment, although modest consumption growth could contribute to a mild sequential
contraction of 0.3% QoQ vs -0.6% prior.
BRL: Watch trends in the Central Bank Weekly Economist Survey at 11:25 GMT. 
TRY: Oct CPI out at print prints at 29.77% broadly unchanged from 29.7% prior with MoM printing at 2.58%.
