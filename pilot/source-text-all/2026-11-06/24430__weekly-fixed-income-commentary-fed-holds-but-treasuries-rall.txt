Market recap
The U.S. Federal Reserve held rates steady at 3.75% to 4.00% in a 10-2 vote, with 
hawkish undertones citing a strong economy and elevated inflation. Treasuries 
nonetheless bull steepened as Middle East concerns and U.S. midterm election 
anxiety drove safe-haven demand into the front end, pulling short-term yields 
down while the long bond finished relatively unchanged. Technology stocks sold off 
sharply into the week’s end after a disappointing semiconductor revenue report. Oil 
prices rose again, with Brent crude climbing to $117 on concerns the United States 
may resume strikes against Iran.
Fixed income gains were broad, with every major sector except convertibles posting 
a positive total return, reversing two weeks of credit weakness. Municipals led 
as the rally extended into a third week, closing out their strongest month since 
November 2023. Mortgage-backed securities and high-yield corporate bonds were 
the best performers relative to similar-duration Treasuries, as mortgage prices 
moved closer to Treasury prices and investors returned to higher risk bonds that had 
sold off earlier. 
Outlook
We expect the Fed to deliver one more hike by year-end at the December meeting, 
followed by an extended hold through 2027. This path is less hawkish than current 
market pricing and depends heavily on how the energy shock passes through to core 
inflation. With oil prices rising on renewed Middle East tensions, that dependency 
remains acute.
Credit fundamentals remain sound. The recent firming in high yield and mortgages 
suggests the AI-driven decompression was driven by sentiment rather than 
fundamentals. We continue to favor high-quality income and, in municipals, 
longer duration over Treasuries. We forecast the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.95% 
for year-end 2026 and 4.75% for end-2027, and the 2-year at 4.70% this year and 
4.50% next year.

U.S. Treasuries 
 Yields rallied for a third straight week, but the move reversed in shape: The 
front end led while the long end barely budged, steepening the curve after two 
weeks of bull flattening. Rate volatility dropped meaningfully, bringing bond 
pricing back in line with cash markets, and swaps followed the same pattern 
with the front end outperforming. 
The 2-year fell 9 bps to 4.59%, 
the 5-year 9 bps to 4.72%, the 10-
year 7 bps to 4.93%, the 20-year 
3 bps to 5.34% and the 30-year 
1 bps to 5.31%. 
Tax-exempt municipals
Municipals extended their rally for a third consecutive week, closing out their 
strongest month since November 2023. Issuers took a wait-and-see approach 
ahead of the Fed meeting, and lighter new issue supply met strengthening 
demand. Net fund flows were solidly positive, though the tax-loss swap trade 
continued to drive assets out of mutual funds and into exchange-traded funds. 
High yield funds saw outflows for a sixth straight week even as high yield 
performance stayed strong and spreads held stable. Yield ratios leave short-
dated paper looking rich, while 30-year municipals look relatively cheap versus 
Treasuries. With the index yielding 4.21% and high yield municipals at 5.87%, 
we continue to favor longer duration in municipals over Treasuries. 
The Bloomberg Municipal Index 
returned +0.66%.
Taxable municipals
Taxable municipals posted a solid gain as falling front-end yields supported the 
sector, capping a strong month. Absolute yields near 5.56% continue to provide 
a natural clearing mechanism and attract demand from crossover investors. 
We view the current backdrop as an opportunity to lock in elevated yields at 
attractive valuations.
The Bloomberg Municipal Taxable 
Index returned +0.47% with 
spreads at 57 bps.

Investment grade corporates
Credit whipsawed tighter, then wider, as technology and AI developments drove 
sentiment. Spreads rallied early in the week before giving back those gains 
as anxiety over the AI infrastructure buildout and its return on investment 
intensified, compounded by disappointing earnings. Sentiment remains bifurcated: 
Technology, media and telecom names reached decade-wide spread levels, while 
otherwise resilient higher-quality issuers were dragged wider alongside them. 
Issuance surpassed an all-time annual record, with weekly supply running near 
double dealer forecasts. Investors were net sellers across the curve, suggesting 
de-risking has broadened as well as deepened. Fund inflows nearly tripled the prior 
week’s pace, driven by ETFs.
The Bloomberg U.S. Corporate 
Bond Index returned +0.48% with 
spreads at 90 bps.
U.S. high yield corporates
High yield had a firm week. The market opened with liquidity challenging in 
lower-quality technology, media and telecom names while higher-quality credits 
remained in favor. It strengthened materially midweek as beaten-down, higher-
beta names rallied sharply on thin, momentum-driven liquidity. Lower-rated paper 
outperformed through the Fed decision before the tone turned mixed at the end 
of the week. Higher-quality credits held firm while higher-beta names gave back 
ground as decompression returned following the move in rates and several earnings 
misses in technology. Primary activity was active throughout.
The Bloomberg High Yield 2% 
Issuer Capped Index returned 
+0.50% with spreads at 340 bps.
Preferred securities
Capital securities had a constructive week, with bank preferreds higher and the 
prior week’s bank issuance outperforming. Domestic hybrids closed firmer, led 
by telecom hybrids as earnings helped the group rebound from several weeks of 
underperformance. Additional Tier 1 (AT1) bonds rallied, with French names 
outperforming after the sovereign’s credit rating was left unchanged. Retail par 
securities were the notable laggard, declining on continued ETF outflows tied to 
year-end tax-loss selling. It was a quiet new issue week, though the single deal 
that priced drew strong demand and compressed meaningfully from initial price 
talk, with more hybrid supply expected.
The ICE Preferred Index returned 
+0.31% with spreads at 201 bps.

Senior loans
Loans were firm for most of the week. Prices generally rose, and buying and 
selling were fairly balanced except for company-specific situations. The market 
strengthened midweek as investors who package loans into collateralized loan 
obligations (CLOs) bought at current prices, then settled into a calmer, more 
balanced tone by week’s end. New deal activity slowed from the prior week’s 
heavy pace, which helped support prices. Fund outflows totaled -$304 million, 
and new issuance reached $2.0 billion.
The S&P Leveraged Loan 
Index returned +0.36% with 
spreads at 510 bps.
Securitized credit
Mortgage-backed securities gained ground as interest rate swings calmed, and 
the extra yield they offer over Treasuries shrank. Mortgage rates continued 
to recover, and lenders issued a heavy batch of home equity and jumbo loan-
backed deals, though some drew weaker demand. Asset-backed securities 
started the week on solid footing, with new issuance running ahead of last year’s 
record pace, though recent deals priced at wider yields than earlier in the year. 
Demand was healthy for high-quality collateralized loan obligations (CLOs), 
and they outperformed high yield corporate bonds. CLO managers shifted 
their focus from refinancing existing deals to issuing new ones as conditions 
improved. Commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) saw more trading as 
investors bought longer-maturity bonds, while their spreads held steady.
The Bloomberg MBS Index returned 
+0.75% with spreads at 34 bps. 
The Bloomberg CMBS Index 
returned +0.39% with spreads 
at 75 bps. The Bloomberg ABS 
Index returned +0.30% with 
spreads at 51 bps.
Global emerging markets
EM hard currency sovereigns saw another week of spread compression, with 
high yield tightening more than investment grade and several frontier credits 
leading the move. Corporate bond prices moved in different directions. Riskier 
high yield bonds fell in price, while higher-quality investment grade bonds were 
unchanged. Brazilian corporates fell further after an unexpected election result 
over the weekend. Brazil’s local-currency bonds also declined, and Brazil again 
lagged the group. Retail outflows declined substantially from the prior week, 
with hard currency redemptions decelerating and ETF flows turning positive. 
New issue activity was busier than expected despite the Fed taking center stage, 
with high yield outpacing investment grade for the first time in weeks and 
demand improving as books grew more oversubscribed.
The Bloomberg Global EM 
Index returned +0.41% with 
spreads at 197 bps.
