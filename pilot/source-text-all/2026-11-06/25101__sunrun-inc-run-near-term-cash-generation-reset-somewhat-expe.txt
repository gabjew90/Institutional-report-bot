RUN reported mixed 3Q26 results, with strong revenue and 
improving direct channel growth offset by lower overall installations 
and another reduction in FY26 cash generation expectations. 
Importantly, direct installations returned to growth at +9% yoy, 
storage attachment reached a record 75%, and upfront net 
subscriber value margin improved sequentially, providing a more 
constructive setup for 2027. That said, RUN now expects FY26 cash 
generation of ~$200mn excluding equipment safe harbor 
investments, at the low end of its prior $200mn-$375mn range, 
while aggregate subscriber value was similarly revised to ~$4.6bn 
from $4.6bn-$4.9bn. We continue to view growth in the direct 
channel, improving unit economics and potential monetization of 
RUN’s ~5.0GWh networked storage base as key offsets to near term 
volume and cash generation pressure. We reiterate our Buy rating 
and provide further details on the quarter here within.

n
guidance. RUN now expects FY2026 cash generation of ~$200mn excluding 
equipment safe harbor investments, at the low end of prior guidance of 
$200mn-$375mn, with management expecting higher cash generation in 4Q26 
following ~$56mn in 3Q26 excluding safe harbor investments. Aggregate subscriber 
value is now expected at ~$4.6bn, at the low end of prior guidance of 
$4.6bn-$4.9bn. Management noted that the cash generation reduction was 
predominantly driven by lower volume expectations, with higher interest rates a 
secondary factor. We view the revisions as reflecting the weaker overall deployment 
trajectory through 2026, although the return to direct channel growth and 
improving unit economics provide a better setup heading into 2027. Notably, 
upfront net subscriber value increased to ~$3,718/subscriber in 3Q26 from ~$2,016 
in 2Q26, with margin expanding to 7.1% from 3.7%, despite the observed project 
level discount rate increasing to 8.4% from 7.3%. 
Direct sales inflection improves the setup for 2027. Management noted that 
n
direct sales grew high-single-digits to low-double-digits yoy each month from June 
through October, providing further evidence that the direct channel recovery is 
sustaining beyond the quarter. The affiliate channel also improved sequentially but 
remained down 67% yoy as RUN continues to prioritize higher quality partners. 
Looking ahead, management expects overall installations to return to yoy growth in 
2027 and indicated that all sales routes, including affiliate, could be growing yoy by 
~2Q27. Notably, Illinois, Texas and New Jersey are already growing >25% yoy, 
supporting our view of a more constructive volume setup heading into 2027. 
Storage scale builds a new cash generation lever. RUN’s networked storage 
n
capacity surpassed ~5.0GWh, with management expecting capacity to double by 
YE28 while increasing both participation rates and revenue per enrolled asset. 
Management expects distributed power plants to contribute to cash generation in 
2027 and highlighted the PG&E/Google transaction as an early proof point for the 
Tesla Renew consortium, with additional deployment discussions underway. We view 
the growing network as an increasingly important source of recurring revenue 
beyond customer origination, although the timing and magnitude of incremental 
cash generation remain key items to monitor. 
Estimate changes. Our 2026E-2028E MW and revenue estimates move to 
n
738MW/804MW/868MW from 774MW/844MW/911MW and 
$3,447mn/$3,419mn/$3,803mn from $3,241mn/$3,517mn/$3,917mn, 
respectively due to slight changes to deployment forecasts. 
Valuation 
Our 12-month price target of $13 (from $11 prior) is based on a NAV valuation, with 
assets that the company expects to install through 2027 having $11 (unchanged) of 
value, and assigning a 1.0X multiple (from 0.0X due to the positive growth and cash 
generation trajectory into 2027) to future NAV creation of $2/sh (from $0/sh) for 2027 
assets.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
