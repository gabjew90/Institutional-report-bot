1) US Midterms Recap
Alec Phillips, Chief US Political Economist, out with his latest thoughts following the US Midterm
elections. Key takeaways below; full piece here (
Large Democrat Majorities: A larger win could give the Democrats slightly greater leverage in
negotiations with the White House, but will also dilute the influence of small factions within the
Democratic caucus.
Fiscal Deadlines: We expect Democrats to use fiscal deadlines to seek to limit the war with Iran, roll back
selected tariffs, and reverse spending cuts enacted last year. The first test will come December 11, when
spending authority is set to expire. Given continued disagreement on spending levels and other policy
disputes, a short-term extension to February or March looks most likely, but it is possible that Democrats
could seek to use that deadline to win concessions before they take control.
Fiscal Impact: We expect Democrats to reverse spending cuts enacted in last year’s fiscal package, which
would boost spending by an estimated 0.25% of GDP in 2027. A deal on discretionary spending or
expanded health insurance subsidies would add modestly to that total.
AI & Datacentres: Democratic wins at the state and local level are likely to lead to short-term data centre
moratoria, potentially affecting about 45% of planned US data centre capacity expansion. However, since
data centres are low on the list of priorities for voters this election, we expect that the issue will fade
somewhat in importance in the ensuing months and that these moratoria will not be long-lived if they are
enacted.

2) BoE Preview
EUROPE ECONOMICS (STEHN): Energy prices have risen notably since the last MPC meeting, activity
data have surprised to the upside, and the labour market numbers have shown further signs of
improvement. With the latest commentary from Governor Bailey and the Deputy Governors continuing to
signal openness to policy tightening, we expect the Committee to vote 7-2 for a 25bp hike. We now expect
a second Bank Rate hike to 4.25% in February given continued upward pressure on energy prices and the
stronger recent activity and labour market figures. But we continue to think that market pricing for over
100bp of tightening over the next year looks too high given that second-round effects into wages look
contained and consumer spending growth is likely to slow over the winter. Full preview here (
STIR TRADING (DARYL LI): Nov-Mar MPC (Dec, Feb and Mar meetings together) have a cumulative
36ps (down from 41bps y’day). We are still of the mind that this pricing has more to come out with or
without de-escalation, mainly because the data has been good but not exponential – we should be pricing
between 0-1 hikes per quarter, not 1-2. 7-2 expected today (Dhingra and Taylor), and I wouldn't expect an
outsized reaction with 8-1 simply because 1) Nov has been nailed on for a while 2) we still have a decent
amount priced, especially compared to other CBs taking a measured pace. 6-3 I do think takes more out of
Dec MPC, but ultimately, a lot has already come out – moving from 12-7bps. With the war still
unresolved, receiving it is a short vol trade and probably can only get down to a 4bps minimum. It feels
like the right trade and we need a re-escalation, with gas pushing towards 120+, before Dec MPC is truly
on the table.

3) Latam Central Bank Round-Up
Copom Recap: After five consecutive 25bps cuts, the BCB decided to leave Selic unchanged at 13.75%, in
line with market prices (78.5% probability was priced in), in a unanimous decision. The decision and
communication came out in line with our expectations, as hawkish as they could at this point. That should
favour, in our view, a relief to the rates curve, mostly expressed via flattening in longer tenors. H/t Rafael
Fonseca, BRL Rates Trading.
Banxico Preview: GS Economics ( expect Banxico to leave rates unchanged at 6.50% today, due to a more
challenging, hawkish external backdrop (Fed hikes, broader global monetary tightening, stronger USD /
weaker MXN, higher core yields, and substantially higher oil prices). However, they assign a high 40%
probability to a split-decision in favour of a 25bp cut, given that three of the five directors still lean dovish,
relying on optimistic forecasts of inflation convergence to target that diverge sharply from market
consensus. In line with those risks, GS Trading expect a cut, highlighting the notably dovish September
minutes, where three of five directors were open to a cut with little since walked back, even despite
stronger local data. They also feel that on MXN, Banxico’s tolerance band has potentially shifted, with an
18-handle looking more acceptable, with the discomfort may now being the risk of going back to a 16-
handle rather than fearing a 19-handle. H/t Matt Atherton, FX Sales.

4) FX MARKET STRATS (RASTOGI) – USDJPY: The Best AI Hedge – LINK (
Short USDJPY is, in our view, the best hedge in FX for a drawdown in the AI trade. Particularly when
implied correlation for equity down / yields down has already repriced aggressively.
When equities and rates fall together, JPY is the standout long. We look at the largest 20 twin drawdowns
since 2000 and assess performance different FX cross combinations grouped into clusters during
drawdown episodes. The JPY cluster stands out as the preferred long in a twin-drawdown environment.
JPY also stands out against USD.
We compare the option-implied distributions of 10y SOFR and USDJPY by using USDJPY's beta to 10y
SOFR during historical twin drawdowns. The beta during these episodes ~12%. Mapping the 10th-
percentile SOFR outcome into USDJPY implies a level that the FX options market assigns only a ~8%
probability.

5) EM MARKET STRATS (SUN LU) – Beijing Trip Notes – LINK (
Key takeaways from Sun Lu’s trip to Beijing, following meetings with local institutional clients…
Macro: Expectations of large stimulus remain low with YTD GDP growth staying within the targeted 4.5-
5% range. Locals believe if export decelerates meaningfully next year, there may be more domestic
demand-side policies, with focus on investments. Official fiscal deficit may stay around 4% or rise to 4-
4.5% range in the next few years, though concern on debt sustainability and interest expense likely
prevents a more aggressive rise. Meanwhile, special local government bonds quota may not increase in
2027, as there are idle bond proceeds from this year’s issuance due to lack of revenue-generating projects
to invest in. A relatively stable US-China relationship in the very near term is expected to continue due to
scheduled two meetings between leaders before year end (Nov 18-19 APEC and Dec 14-15 G20 summit).
However, there is some expectation on relationship deterioration in non-tariff area(e.g. finance, tech) in
2027 with blue sweep and likely fewer Xi-Trump meetings due to busy political agenda domestically. For
more discussions, please see GIR’s China CIO tour takeaways (
Gradual RMB Appreciation: Trade surplus-driven RMB appreciation is expected to continue per local
clients. Exporters still show herd behaviour and may panic sell USD if RMB appreciates fast. Meanwhile,
importers have delayed USD purchase this year due to the faster-than-carry RMB appreciation. Gradual
RMB appreciation is seen as a positive policy by top leadership, to help keep trade tension contained with

US and EU, support RMB asset confidence & RMB internationalization, and mathematically support
China’s nominal GDP catch up against US. Most don’t see there is a pre-determined target or range for
RMB appreciation, but many expressed breaking 6.0 seems too early to conclude. Some mentioned
authorities’ goal is to limit risk accumulation among exporters in terms of USD hoarding. Thus PBoC
encourages them to increase hedging ratio and offload USD as they could. Another important objective of
the authorities is to limit panic factor and excessive one-sided adjustment of FX within a short period of
time.
USDCNH Entry Opportunity: Recent 2% hike of foreign currency deposit RRR is a such symbolic policy
meant to slow down the recently accelerated appreciation pace, in our view. Once spot-fixing gap narrows
back to 200-300pips, barring any material external shock such as Middle East tension escalation, we think
it’s a good entry opportunity for downside USDCNH again, to position for faster-than-carry RMB
appreciation into Jan driven by seasonal factors and upcoming leaders’ summits. We argued ( that during a
RMB appreciation year, the seasonal increase of exporter repatriation in Dec is higher than average (we
think at least US$70bn increase in Dec for this year), likely pushing USDCNH to 6.55-57 area by end of
Jan. We continue to like 3-month USDCNH binary put ( targeting a move to 6.55-6.57 area.
