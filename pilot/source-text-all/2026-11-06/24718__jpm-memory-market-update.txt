Overall, our impression from memory makers’ 3Q26 results has a positive bias 
(EPS/margin beat against F/X headwinds, pricing upside, successful HBM price 
negotiation, and volume delivery). There were a few company-specific 
disappointments (e.g. SEC’s lack of imminent shareholder return and Kioxia’s 
weak CY4Q26 guidance); however, we view this to be temporary (SEC's 
shareholder return catalyst should be back in Jan-2027 and Kioxia’s CY1Q27 
growth should be above seasonal and ahead of peers). While end demand signals 
continue to be mixed between B2B (steady hyperscaler’s AI capex spending 
growth with consensus upward revisions for next year) and B2C (PC/Mobile 
facing greater challenges), we believe that bottom-up memory S&D fundamentals 
should continue to be very solid through 2028E.
•
DRAM - Fundamentals keep surprising on the upside. In 3Q26, DRAM 
prices once again surprised on the upside, driving mild beats across the board 
(DRAM ASP up 23% Q/Q vs. our expectation of high-teens - 20% up Q/Q). 
Incremental surprise was in the 4Q bit-guidance with memory makers guiding 
DD% Q/Q increase and the industry likely exiting 2026 with high-20% y-y 
B/G. Memory makers began shipping HBM4 and guided to HBM4 bit/revenue 
crossover in 3Q26/4Q26E but more importantly guided to narrowing 
profitability vs. conventional DRAM heading into 2027E upon successful 
price negotiation. We have been guiding that an aggressive HBM ASP increase 
could be a double edged sword (stronger memory earnings vs. demand 
destruction) but believe smooth negotiation across the board implies largely 
manageable risk at the end customers (JPMe: +96% HBM ASP increase in 
27E). Memory makers have unanimously voiced demand outstripping supply 
beyond 2027 (some guiding 2027 memory is de-facto sold-out), and we believe 
such guidance should provide relief once earnings begin to prove resilience 
amidst the market's AI funding and sustainability concerns.

Server sales mix reaching mid-to-high 60% in DRAM; 40%+ in NAND. The CSP-
driven AI upcycle is forcing memory makers to prioritize server memory production, 
tightening the industry ASPs, and raising the server sales mix across the board. Based on 
company filings and guidance, we estimate server sales mix rising to 60%+ in DRAM 
(server DRAM and HBM combined) vs. 50%+ in 25A and on-road to reach 70%+ in 27E. 
For NAND, we believe SEC/SKH carries 60%+ exposure to the server segment thanks 
to long-lasting dominance in eSSD/Solidigm acquisition, while the laggards such as 
SNDK/Kioxia carry relatively less server sales mix (40%-45% in 26E vs. 13%-mid-30% 
in 25A). The unprecedented server demand is likely to push the server sales mix even 
further, crowding out production volume for smartphones/PCs which will likely further 
weigh on the B2C end-market (likely reflected through price hikes (both component/set 
devices), unit correction, etc.). We believe the weak B2C earnings are likely to be easily 
offset by server demand strength, albeit believe the concentration risk is worth 
monitoring.

Sub-sector snapshot: Demand outstripping supply for servers; dwindling B2C 
demand. 3Q26 earnings commentaries across AI ecosystem partners point to firm server 
demand with hyperscalers’ capex rising +87% y-y in 3Q (~US$217bn) and higher 27E 
spend. AI related revenue accelerated in CY3Q26 (Azure: + 48%/GCP: +103%/AWS:
+39) with guidance pointing to further acceleration in CY4Q26E (for Azure/AWS). 
Server ODM monthly results indicate server demand outstripping supply with memory 
being the major bottleneck (report), while consumer-related devices (smartphone/PC) 
struggles from chip-flations and price-hikes evidenced by INTC guiding DD% y-y PC 
unit TAM decline in 26/27E (report). We believe the end-demand outlook that memory 
makers are observing is consistent with the commentaries from the broader AI ecosystem 
partners. We do believe the underlying demand for servers (both general/AI) remains 
robust, backed by frontier AI labs competing for model launches and accelerated AI 
capex which will offset the B2C earnings weakness. Of note, our US H/W team expects 
AI capex across tier-1/tier-2 hyperscalers to reach $1.5trn in 27E vs. ~$800bn in 26E, a 
87.5% growth (report).

Memory trading at <3.5x 27E P/E vs. 98% EPS CAGR in 26-28E. Memory stocks that 
were once trading at 6-7x P/E have significantly de-rated on AI sustainability/funding 
concerns, HBM de-specs, disbelief in the elongated upcycle and now trade at <3.5x P/E 
while the EPS has not seen any correction helped by memory shortages across the 
industry and LTAs. The order visibility is extending as we progress through the year, but 
we believe the market is anticipating AI funding risk and power bottleneck driven 
memory downcycle as early as in 1H27E. This puts memory in a tricky spot as investors 
debate between “too-cheap to ignore” vs. “it is cheap for a reason.”  We believe EPS cuts 
are unlikely to happen in the next 6M as the memory S-D gap is too wide to narrow over 
a short period of time and memory earnings will have a buffer protected by LTAs and 
prepayments.

Memory stocks are down 45% from recent peaks; Bullish on the sector but stay 
selective. Memory stocks have corrected 45% from the 2026-peak (vs. SOX: -29%) as 
the skepticism around AI funding quickly flipped the sentiment amidst the market 
anticipating memory to be the biggest victim once the correction occurs (equally saying 
memory was the biggest beneficiary from AI). Amidst the depressed market sentiment, 
shareholder returns were the catalyst that we hoped for and was well-executed by SKH, 
but the stock further corrected post 3Q26 earnings implying the bearish sentiment will 
likely continue until the end-market concerns are resolved (i.e. AI funding, AI 
monetization, etc.). We believe memory fundamentals are solid with memory pricing 
likely to remain elevated in 27E, but believe the stock will likely remain range-bound in 
the near-term due to a lack of imminent catalysts. On a medium-term basis, we continue 
to view risk-reward as attractive and remain OW across all our coverage companies and 
rank our next three months order of preference based on shareholder return policy upside: 
SKH (OW) > SEC (OW) > Kioxia (OW).
