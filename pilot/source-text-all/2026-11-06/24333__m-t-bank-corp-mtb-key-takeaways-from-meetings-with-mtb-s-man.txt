We recently hosted investor meetings with MTB management, 
including CFO Daryl Bible and SVP of Market and Investor Relations 
Steven Wendelboe. Topics of focus for investors included: 1) outlook 
for loan growth, including potential areas of moderation in 2027 
(C&I), 2) drivers of its assumed relatively stable net interest margin 
as well as driver of NII growth over the medium term, 3) 
expectations for deposit growth, deposit re-pricing and overall 
managing of funding costs, 4) capital management, including where 
it expects to take its capital ratios over time (targeting 7.5% TCE), 5) 
returns into 2027 and drivers of a ~17% ROTCE, 6) updated 
thoughts on M&A and the impact of technology / regulation on the 
industry. Each of these are addressed in the note.   
Overall MTB remains focused on driving consistent returns across its 
platform through steady operating performance and further 
leveraging its capital base. Loan growth has been solid in 2026 and 
while MTB expects it to moderate, it should continue to see L/MSD 
growth.  Additionally, MTB expects its peer leading margin (2nd in 
peer group) to remain in this range as it benefits from fixed rate 
re-pricing and growing core deposits. Fee growth is likely to remain 
a bright spot and this along with continued expense discipline 
should help it drive 17%+ returns. M&A remains a focal point 
although we sense there isn’t much on the horizon in the near-term.  
Putting it all together, shares trade at 10.4x 2027E vs. peers at 9.3x 
and if MTB can continue to generate operating leverage and improve 
returns, shares should have further upside from here. See below for 
key takeaways:  
• MTB reiterated its expectation for its net interest margin to remain 
in the mid-3.60s (likely stable, could improve depending on shape of 
the curve), including fixed rate asset re-pricing and driving low cost

Deposits: Hoping to continue to fund loan growth with core deposits   
MTB’s average deposits came in at $166.8bn, up +2.0% QoQ (+2.6% YoY), as average 
interest-bearing deposits increased +2.9% QoQ (+3.8% YoY) to $123.1bn while average 
NIB deposits declined -0.6% QoQ (-0.8% YoY) to $43.7bn. Deposit growth outpaced 
loan growth during the quarter and allowed MTB to reduce average short-term 
borrowings by $2.8bn QoQ (to $5.2bn). Looking ahead, MTB expects 4Q26 average 
deposits of $169-$170bn (implying 1.6% QoQ growth) and remains focused on funding 
loan growth with deposit growth, although growing DDA balances remain challenging in 
the higher-rate environment.  
MTB is placing increased emphasis on gathering operating accounts and other 
relationship deposits across consumer, business banking, commercial and wealth which 
should hopefully lead it to be able to fund 2027 loan growth with deposits. After 
deposits lagged loan growth in 2Q, MTB looked to have the business lines reorient 
around deposit growth which helped drive the strength in growth in 3Q (+$3.3bn) which 
was enough to both fund loan growth and reduce average short-term borrowings. 
Within the deposit growth, MTB expects to continue to drive deposit growth into 2027 
though the incremental mix will likely be more expensive as DDA growth becomes more 
challenging and it sounded like achieving stable DDA balances in 2027 (and thus a lower 
NIB mix of the 28% in 3Q) would be a solid outcome for the bank. With commercial 
clients increasingly requesting sweep deposits, MTB has responded by increasing 
earnings credit rates to remain competitive for DDA deposits and is orienting growth 
focuses on SMB / operating accounts.

Capital: Expects to deploy part of the Basel III benefit; Looking to run at 7.5% TCE over 
time   
MTB reported a 3Q26 CET1 ratio of 10.2%, or ~9.87% including AOCI (-29bps QoQ), as 
continued capital generation was more than offset by $323mn of share repurchases, 
higher RWA associated with $2.2bn of EOP loan growth, and higher unrealized losses on 
AFS securities given the move in rates. Tangible book value per share declined to 
$115.88 (-1.3% QoQ) and TCE stood at 7.85% (-22bps QoQ). Additionally, in order to 
limit further tangible capital volatility, MTB transferred $8.3bn of residential MBS from 
AFS to HTM during the quarter. Looking ahead, MTB expects to operate with a CET1 
ratio of 10.0%-10.25% through YE26 and thereafter expects 7.5% to serve as a floor as 
it deploys a portion of the Basel III benefit.  
In terms of expected Basel benefits, MTB expects to see a ~90bps benefit under the 
standardized approach with an additional 10-20bps if it elected the advanced 
approach. While this would result in a stated capital ratio that approaches 11%, MTB 
does not expect to be able to leverage all of this capital given constraints from the 
ratings agencies.  Given its view that it expects to run at ~7.5% TCE (7.86% today), MTB 
expects to only leverage 30-50% of this capital once the rule goes through.  MTB has 
been repurchasing shares at a ~300-$400mn quarterly pace and with more modest loan 
growth we would expect buybacks to step up in 2027. If MTB manages to a 7.5% TCE 
target, we estimate MTB could repurchase ~$2.8bn of shares through 4Q27, ~$800mn 
above consensus (Exhibit 16), providing meaningful incremental capital return capacity. 
Additionally, MTB has been increasing its dividend every 5 quarters and we expect there 
could be another raise coming in 4Q.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
