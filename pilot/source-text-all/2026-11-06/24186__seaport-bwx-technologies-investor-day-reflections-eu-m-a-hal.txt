BWX Technologies Inc.
(NYSE: BWXT)
Investor Day Reflections, EU M&A, HALEU and DUECE
Summary:
We attended the 9/29/26 BWXT 2026 Investor Day presentation. We found the
event informative and very well organized, particularly in the thoughtful way strategic
messages were reinforced by management. We review these with references to relevant
slides. We also discuss an EU M&A screen we constructed, and finish with a review
of BWXT’s DUECE pilot and how it fits in with the National Nuclear Security Agency’s
(NNSA) long-term unobligated uranium (U) enrichment program. Finally, we have
updated our 2030 model based on BWXT guidance through 2030 provided at the
Investor Day. We maintain our Buy rating and price target of $245/share.
Highlights
2026 Investor Day Overview
BWXT has a demonstrated record of exceeding its own targets
Management explicitly compared the targets established at the February 2024 Investor
Day with expected 2023–2026 results, showing that BWXT materially outperformed its
original growth commitments. CEO Rex Geveden said the company had “blown out” its
prior Investor Day targets.

In our view, BWXT is using its record of execution to support confidence in its new targets
through 2030. Management acknowledged they are ambitious, but argued that backlog
visibility, differentiated capabilities, capacity investments and operating improvements
provide a credible path to achieving them. We see BWXT as a very execution-oriented
organization based on decades of successful naval work. While there is no such thing
as riskless forecasting, we do not see a history of hyperbole in its guidance.
Nuclear demand is inflecting across both national security and commercial power
BWXT presents its growth opportunity as structural and broad-based: rising defense
requirements, energy security, AI-driven electricity demand, new reactor construction
and the refurbishment of the existing fleet.
●Slide 10: Demand is moving toward nuclear, supported by secular growth drivers and
more than 2× backlog growth since year-end 2023.
●Slide 18: The investment thesis combines the predictable defense platform with large
growth opportunities in Gen III reactors, SMRs, fuel and large-scale new-build.
Company Description: 
BWXT manufactures and fuels all nuclear propulsion systems for the U.S. Navy. It has special
materials and uranium enrichment government programs. It has a commercial division in Canada
wedded to Ontario's nuclear power complexes. With its acquisition of PCG, it has expanded its
capabilities in US commercial nuclear power.

M&A 
During his introductory remarks, CEO Rex Geveden noted BWXT’s geographic footprint was “extending 
into Europe” and had “aspirations to do more there.” Chief Strategy Officer Rik Geiersbach subsequently 
added M&A was focused on high-quality industrial capabilities, attractive margins and earnings, and FCF 
potential. Acquisitions could range from a small targeted bolt-on to something “transformative.” 
 
This stimulated us to investigate what EU M&A opportunities might be available to BWXT. We divided this 
effort into acquisitions that would augment the capabilities of BWXT (emphasis on large component 
manufacturing), PCG (smaller commercial components, testing and inspection), and Kinectrics 
(engineering and services). This list should not be deemed comprehensive.

Investigating Potential EU M&A 
The strategic question was whether an acquisition could fill a genuine gap in BWXT, Kinectrics or PCG—
rather than simply add geographic presence—and satisfy BWXT's Slide 73 criteria. We were also mindful 
of BWXT management’s assertion that its current heavy-manufacturing capacity is sufficient to fulfill its 
current Gen III pipeline, given its NAM assets and the expected expansion of US commercial heavy 
component manufacturing.

While several companies have relevant differentiated capabilities, whether due to their potential cost, 
incomplete strategic fit, ownership focused on growth, or their status within sovereign entities and 
conglomerates, we did not find candidates that seemed like immanent potential acquisitions at this time.                              
 
Our conclusion is that further M&A may be driven by a specific capability gap or a useful motivated seller—
not only the desire to establish a larger European footprint. Greenfield investment, partnerships and smaller 
specialist acquisitions remain alternatives, subject to BWXT's Slide 73 requirements for differentiated 
capabilities, revenue synergies, positive NPV, first-year EPS accretion and margin-accretive growth. 
 
Above all, we believe BWXT management requires project visibility and a logical rationale to transact M&A. 
On 10/22/26, BWXT issued a press release regarding rumors of an impending Polish investment. After 
making it clear no investment decision had been made, it plainly stated “any investment would depend on 
firm orders and the extent of the opportunity.” We believe this also applies to organic investment. 
 
BWXT's DUECE Project: Technology, Enrichment Pathway and Strategic Opportunity 
We noticed management’s references to DUECE being part of a comprehensive NNSA program to provide 
the U.S. military with a completely unobligated uranium enrichment program from natural uranium (NU) to 
highly enriched uranium (HEU). Unobligated means the entirety of the production chain from U ore to 
finished U enrichment has only domestic content. This prevents any foreign agency from attempting to 
interfere with NNSA enrichment programs. What particularly caught our attention was that HALUE was the 
feedstock for BWXT’s DUECE pilot.  
 
Previously, the United States operated an integrated, multi-site enrichment complex that took natural 
uranium through successive enrichment stages to weapons-grade HEU. It was a coordinated system of 
gaseous-diffusion plants at three sites. 
• 
Oak Ridge, Tennessee (K-25): Began operations in 1945 and produced progressively enriched 
uranium, including HEU. 
• 
Paducah, Kentucky: Began in 1952, producing lower-assay enriched uranium (2% enrichment) that 
served as feed for the other plants. 
• 
Portsmouth, Ohio: Began in 1954 and produced uranium at enrichments up to ~97% U-235.  
 
The three plants were operated as an integrated production complex during peak production. Their 
cascades used equipment arranged in successive stages, with different enrichment duties across the 
system. Ultimately, gaseous diffusion was discontinued because gas centrifuges could produce the same 
separative work using dramatically less electricity. 
 
The Government Accountability Office (GAO) August 2026 report (Nuclear Fuel: Actions Needed to 
Enhance Cost Reporting and Economic Analysis for Federal Uranium Supply Efforts) noted existing 
unobligated LEU supplies for tritium production last into the early 2040s, while existing HEU allocated to 
naval nuclear propulsion lasts into the 2050s.  
 
[In our report OKLO’s Future Growth: It’s All About the Fuel; Upgrade to Buy, 6/9/25, we provided analysis 
that suggested NNSA had sufficient HEU supply to operate its nuclear assets through the 2050’s, although 
we will follow the GAO assumptions going forward]. 
 
The DUECE pilot is expected to begin limited operations in 2035 and transition to HEU enrichment around 
2049. 
 
CEO Rex Geveden discussed both BWXT’s DUECE pilot and the government's wider ambition to control 
the entire enrichment pipeline. BWXT's $1.5Bln contract focuses on industrializing ORNL's small centrifuge 
technology and establishing the pilot capability to enrich HALEU to HEU. The question then is where will 
the HALUE come from?

1. The Complete Current Enrichment Pathway 
The GAO report referenced above provides the clearest description of the intended architecture. It 
distinguishes the upstream production plant from BWXT's pilot plant in Erwin, Tennessee.  
 
Stage 1 — LEU production 
Produce unobligated low-enriched uranium for defense requirements potentially using Centrus’ (LEU) 
large AC 100 centrifuges at Piketon, small centrifuges at a new facility, or both. 
Stage 2 — HALEU production 
Further enrich the LEU to produce HALEU feedstock. 
The eventual plant configuration and allocation of responsibilities remain unsettled publicly. 
Stage 3 — HEU production 
Enrich HALEU to HEU for naval nuclear propulsion. 
ORNL's DUECE technology; BWXT's Erwin pilot plant. 
 
The important nuance is that DUECE's pilot plant is being designed to transition HALEU to HEU production, 
while the broader program still requires separate, large-scale LEU and HALEU production capacity. GAO 
projects limited pilot operations beginning in 2035 and the transition to HEU enrichment around 2049.  
 
BWXT’s $1.5 Billion Contract, Encompasses Two Parallel Workstreams: 
• 
One is to scale up ORNL’s DUECE centrifuge technology, prepare that for mass production… 
• 
The second workstream is to prepare the facilities, licensing and requirements to enrich through to 
HEU.  
• 
The contract leaves the intermediate assays—LEU and HALEU—outside its current scope.  
 
On the additional opportunity, Geveden said the preparatory stages could represent “tens of billions” of 
dollars in potential work. He stated that BWXT intends to compete for the LEU and HALEU portions.  
 
When asked about the ultimate commercial potential of the program, it was acknowledged that it could be 
less cost-competitive than commercial enrichment without the fully unobligated stricture.  
 
The current mission for BWXT is to industrialize DUECE, establish reliable domestic component 
manufacturing, and demonstrate competitive lifecycle costs. The larger opportunity is to secure follow-on 
awards. While the upstream opportunity remains competitive, it is reasonable to assume BWXT can 
improve its chances with DUECE success.
