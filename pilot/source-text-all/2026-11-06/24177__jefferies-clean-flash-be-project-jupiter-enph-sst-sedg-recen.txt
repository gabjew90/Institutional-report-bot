BE Fireside Chat
BE: Key Takeaways from our recent fireside chat on New Mexico regulatory approval process
for Project Jupiter. The air permit for Project Jupiter, Oracle's data center in southern New Mexico,
remains the single most critical gating item for the project, with the hearing at the New Mexico
Environment Department (NMED) currently scheduled for early December (12/7 Schedule Details)
and an opinion expected by year-end 2026, although the timeline could extend. Water rights, while
publicly contested, are viewed as legally sound by some parties. Longer term, the New Mexico
legislature is expected to pursue a guardrail bill rather than an outright data center moratorium, though
grandfathering provisions for in-flight projects such as Project Jupiter remain an open question. The
key near-term objective for the Project Jupiter air permit application is to receive clearance before year-
end, ahead of the incoming Governor administration taking office and a potential change in the regulatory
backdrop. At the same time, the current timeline remains compressed, and recent changes to the application
raise the possibility for the extension of the existing process and timeline. Even if air permit approval
is issued by year-end, there remains the potential for an appeal, which we believe opposing parties are
likely to pursue.Importantly, however, the project's substantial economic investment, organized labor
support and job creation could provide continued political support for Project Jupiter even under a new
administration.
Project Jupiter: Overview and Air Permit Status. Project Jupiter is Oracle's large-scale data center
development in Las Cruces, in southern New Mexico. The project has broad economic support in New
Mexico: the physical facility is nearly complete, labor unions and building trades are fully aligned behind
it, and the outgoing governor has been a consistent supporter throughout the process. The energy
transition goals embedded in New Mexico's Energy Transition Act (ETA), which was signed by the
governor and legislature, do not need to be met until 2045, and Oracle has characterized Bloom Energy
and similar providers as short-term bridge solutions until those longer-term clean energy targets are
fulfilled. Oracle has been explicit on this framing, noting the 2045 timeline as the governing constraint,
while community opponents have pushed for compliance on a much faster schedule.
New Hearing Officer Appointed. Our understanding is that NMED selected a well-regarded hearing
officer who is known for thorough, deliberative work. His prior opinions have proven difficult to
challenge, with at least one previous ruling advancing all the way to the U.S. Supreme Court. Both
supporters and opponents of the project have publicly praised his selection, which was deliberately
designed to lend credibility to the permitting process and make any eventual appeal harder to sustain,
following the recusal of the previous hearing officer. Air permit process is being resolved through
New Mexico Environment Department Docketed Matters (select “Environment Department Cabinet
Secretary”; then select “AQB 26-57(P): Yucca Growth Infrastructure, LLC – YGI Microgrid.”).
October 26 Hearing Rejects Motion to Dismiss; Current Timeline Remains Intact. Today, the Hearing
Officer denied New Energy Economy’s Motion to Dismiss, leaving the December 7 merits hearing on the
current schedule. Earlier this month, New Energy Economy (NEE) filed a Motion to Dismiss the Project
Jupiter air-permit proceeding, arguing that the application before NMED was no longer technically
reliable or sufficiently complete for a merits hearing. NEE pointed in particular to administrative-record
emails showing that incorporating more accurate facility information into the air-dispersion model
roughly doubled modeled pollutant concentrations.
The argument followed Yucca Growth Infrastructure’s (YGI) submission of 197 pages of revisions and
new modeling, including reducing fuel-cell stacks from 2,275 to 1,263, increasing stack heights from
30 ft to 45 ft, and changing exhaust and operating parameters.
Importantly, the Hearing Officer indicated that the December 7 hearing date would change only if NMED
makes a specific request to modify the schedule, which he suggested would need to be made “fairly soon.”

Some timing uncertainty nevertheless remains, as NMED did not provide an actual or estimated completion

Appeals Process Could Extend the Project Beyond Year-End Approval. If the hearing officer issues a
favorable ruling in December (assuming no delays in the current schedule), opponents may appeal to
the Environmental Improvement Board (EIB), a governor-appointed body. Under the current outgoing
governor, the board's composition would likely deny any appeal, allowing Oracle and Bloom to proceed
immediately with the energy infrastructure build-out. If the opinion instead comes out in January 2027
or later, the incoming governor would have the opportunity to influence the EIB's composition through
appointments, potentially introducing an additional element of uncertainty around an appeal. Notably,
the EIB itself, rather than the judge or governor, controls the timing of any appeal review, creating an
additional layer of timing uncertainty. There is also a procedural layer before the EIB: even if the hearing
officer recommends approving the permit, NMED as an agency retains final decision-making authority,
and the incoming governor appoints NMED leadership. The NMED Cabinet Secretary holds the
ultimate power to sign off on, modify, or deny permit. A process extending into January would therefore
introduce incremental uncertainty and could lengthen Oracle's timeline. That said, the preponderance of
legal opinion holds that because the permitting process commenced under the current administration,
it would be difficult for the incoming administration to halt it entirely. Moreover, the project's advanced
stage of construction, support from organized labor and building trades, and economic-development
benefits could create meaningful incentives for the next administration to allow the existing process to
proceed, even if its broader policy toward future data centers becomes more restrictive.
Political Dynamics Could Shift with the Incoming Governor? Haaland (D), a likely Governor-elect
based on current polling, has publicly committed to signing a data center moratorium on day one of
her administration, but she has simultaneously stated that she “can't stop Project Jupiter,” as she does
not believe she has the legal authority to intervene. The economic reality of a nearly completed facility
supported by organized labor also creates meaningful countervailing considerations. The incoming
governor is described as politically close to the building trades, particularly given the project's status
as a significant jobs and economic-development driver. We are therefore increasingly less concerned
about the incoming administration as it relates specifically to Project Jupiter, and we believe there could
ultimately be political support for allowing the project to proceed. Nevertheless, pushing the permitting
process into 2027 could create additional delays for Oracle and extend the near-term overhang on Bloom.
Bloom has had several other wins in recent months (Nebius, AEP Wyoming), but Project Jupiter remains a
flagship project, with over 2 GW of fuel cells expected to be deployed once fully operational.
Natural Gas Pipeline: The "Green Chili" Project; an Important Catalyst, but Not the Main Roadblock.
No substantive work on the proposed natural gas pipeline serving Project Jupiter, the "Green Chili"
pipeline, will commence until the air permit is issued and cleared. If and when that occurs, the incoming
Land Commissioner is expected to begin pipeline negotiations with Oracle in Q1 2027. This represents
a meaningful shift from the status quo: the current Land Commissioner has not been open to such
negotiations along state land boundaries, whereas the incoming Land Commissioner has been publicly
and explicitly forthcoming about his willingness to engage directly with Oracle. He has also signaled a
preference to conduct those negotiations himself rather than allow Oracle to pursue alternative federal
pathways. The pipeline is therefore an important second-order catalyst that follows, rather than runs parallel
to, air permit resolution. We ultimately do not see pipeline approval as the principal gating item, even if the
route ultimately needs to be modified to avoid state lands, as ET, the pipeline owner, has indicated. The
pipeline is still pending FERC approval, expected this December.

Legislative Outlook: Moratorium vs. Guardrails. Two competing legislative tracks are being developed
in New Mexico for the 2027 session: a data center moratorium bill and a guardrail bill with meaningful
restrictions. The moratorium has received the most public attention, but the expert's read of the
legislative landscape is that most legislators are more comfortable with a guardrail framework, and
the expected legislative outcome is a guardrail bill “with teeth.” Multiple other data center projects
beyond Project Jupiter are being discussed statewide, and the broader policy tension around data
center permitting, power consumption, and emissions is playing out in virtually every state, making
this a national issue rather than a New Mexico-specific one. A key open question for Project Jupiter
and similar in-process projects is whether any legislation passed in 2027 would include grandfathering
provisions protecting projects already underway. Given Project Jupiter's advanced construction status and
economic and labor support, there could also be a political rationale for distinguishing the project from future
developments subject to a new regulatory framework.
Industrial Revenue Bonds Challenge Pending Too. The Dona Ana County industrial revenue bonds
(IRBs) granted to Project Jupiter are the subject of a separate legislative challenge. Senator Cervantes,
Chairman of Senate Judiciary and himself a Las Cruces representative, has publicly stated his intent
to retroactively “claw back” the IRBs going back two years, which would effectively eliminate the
county-granted tax incentive. Oracle views such a retroactive measure as legally unsound. Neither the
senator's office nor outside observers have articulated a clear mechanism for retroactive revocation,
but the senator believes he can introduce and pass such a measure. The IRBs do not represent a project
viability issue: removal of the IRBs would change the project's economics but would not by itself prevent the
project from proceeding. IRBs for up to $165Bn were approved in September 2025. The bond package
serves as a tax-abatement tool to support the multi-decade private investment by project developers.
Temporary Power Supply. Oracle recently approached El Paso Electric for a backup power
arrangement. However, the El Paso Electric arrangement would supply only approximately 1/4 of
the power required for the data center. Sourcing a materially greater share of power from external grid
providers would appear inconsistent with the terms of the existing IRB, which is specifically tethered to
the project's own on-site generation grid and the proposed natural gas pipeline. Any materially larger
external power import arrangement could therefore require Oracle to negotiate a new IRB. In addition, we
believe additional grid supply would require material infrastructure investment and could extend the timeline
beyond Oracle's current project schedule. As a result, external grid supply appears better positioned as a
supplemental or backup solution rather than a substitute for the project's planned on-site generation and
natural gas infrastructure.
ENPH 900V GaN
Enphase and Infineon announced the world’s first 900 V monolithic GaN bidirectional switch,
which ENPH is integrating into IQ SST development builds, with production qualification expected to
follow. The higher voltage expands ENPH’s GaN architecture beyond 650 V while preserving the high
frequency switching that enables smaller magnetics and higher power density.
The key SST implication is architectural simplification rather than simply faster switching. At 900 V,
ENPH can use fewer series connected power conversion modules to handle medium voltage inputs,
reducing switch count, conversion losses, components and potential failure points. The architecture
also supports a unipolar 800 VDC output rather than split ±400 V buses, while providing a path toward
1,500 VDC architectures through ±750 V generation. ENPH believes this enables a single stage SST
architecture with lower EMI, fewer components and potentially lower cost.

Infineon’s separate collaboration with NVIDIA provides incremental validation for GaN within the
emerging 800 VDC data center power architecture, although we would not read too much into it for
ENPH’s SST specifically. NVIDIA and Infineon are using GaN for the high voltage intermediate bus
converter, where 800 VDC is stepped down to lower rack level voltages, with Infineon highlighting GaN’s
switching frequency, power density and efficiency advantages. This validates GaN at an important
conversion stage within NVIDIA’s 800 VDC ecosystem, but does not suggest NVIDIA has selected
GaN for the entire power architecture or endorsed ENPH’s SST design. If anything, it is a marginal
positive for ENPH because the broader ecosystem is increasingly validating GaN in high density AI
power conversion, while ENPH is attempting to extend those advantages further upstream into the
SST.
The announcement strengthens ENPH’s differentiation around GaN and provides tangible semiconductor
validation through Infineon, but commercial execution remains the key gating factor. The devices have
been successfully tested and are being incorporated into IQ SST development units, but have
not yet completed production qualification. This reinforces our view that the next critical SST
milestones are system level pilot performance, efficiency, reliability and customer validation, rather
than semiconductor capability alone.
