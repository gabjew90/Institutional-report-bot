•
Last week saw a mix of monetary policy, political developments and economic updates alongside 
simmering geopolitical tensions. The Fed, ECB and BoJ kept rates unchanged. Elections in Brazil 
and Israel were in focus. Economic data pointed to resilient Q3 activity in the US and Europe, 
while inflation stayed elevated in Japan. China’s industrial profits strengthened in September, 
while the Fifth Plenum concluded without new stimulus measures.
•
Major global equity markets ended the week mixed, while benchmark US Treasury yields fell, oil 
prices rose, and the US dollar index remained broadly stable.
•
Gold’s recent weakness increasingly reflects a repricing of the Fed’s expected rate path since the 
October meeting, alongside some rotation back into more attractive bonds (C.O.T.W). Selling 
appears driven by leveraged investors, as US gold ETF flows have been fairly benign. COMEX 
futures, hitherto less reliable bellwethers of price, may have had a stronger hand in recent weeks: 
Managed Money positions have fallen ~11t since mid-October, while Other Reportable, Non-
Reportable and Spreading positions have dropped ~70t. For now, gold faces softer demand from 
US investors, even as stickier ETF holders are more positive. This could pressure gold lower until 
an attractive floor is reached – the focus of our upcoming Gold Market Commentary (Wed 4 Nov).

All about Gold
3
Source: CFTC, Shanghai Futures Exchange, ETF Providers, Bloomberg, World Gold Council
•
Gold's weakness continued: the LBMA Gold Price PM ended Friday at US$3,913/oz, losing 2.5% 
w/w and dipping below the US$4,000/oz threshold for the first time since mid-July. Y-t-d, gold has 
fallen by 10.4%.
•
Hawkish repricing of the Fed's rate path, alongside concerns over AI spending and geopolitical 
tensions that rattled equities, led to liquidity-driven selling of gold, mainly from leveraged 
investors. Net longs of gold futures at the COMEX and SHFE both fell, and option traders 
remained bearish. Yet strategic allocation to gold ETFs continued, with both North American and 
European funds recording inflows. 
•
Gold has fallen sharply to test what we see as key technical support at US$3,858/oz, the 38.2% 
Fibonacci retracement of the entire 2015–2026 rise. It is also near what we view as a 'typical' 
downside extreme: 10% below its 200-day average (p6 & appendix).
•
Tuesday’s midterm elections are in focus. A split Congress – a widely expected outcome – could 
weigh on US equities as a Bloomberg survey shows, amid concerns around tighter AI regulation 
and legislative gridlock. Bloomberg survey respondents also see foreign and fiscal policy as key 
post-election risks. For gold, the impact is mixed: volatility could boost safe-haven demand, but 
rates and liquidity-driven sell-off may weigh on gold. Fiscal gridlock could also keep term premia 
elevated, a near-term headwind for gold while strengthening its longer-term case.
•
October non-farm payrolls (Fri) are also key to watch. The market consensus suggests a strong 
uptick in hiring, which should firm the case for another Fed hike next month, while recent Fed 
comments favouring a more gradual pace of tightening add nuance to the outlook.

Gold has seen a further sharp fall as equities come under increasing pressure and a broader “risk off” tone takes hold and this 
has seen the market fall to just shy of what we see as key technical support from the 38.2% retracement of the entire 2015 – 2026 
rise at US$3,858/oz. With the market also seen at a “typical” downside extreme (10% below its 200-day average) our bias is to try 
and look for signs of a floor on a closing basis here, despite the broader negative tone.
Should the “risk off” tone persist though and a sustained close below US$3,858/oz be seen this would suggest the immediate 
trend is likely to remain lower. We would note short- and medium-term measures continue to trend lower and even though net 
long positioning has fallen, we still in our view have not seen a “positioning washout”. Support would then be seen next at 
US$3,820/oz ahead of the 50% retracement of the 2022/2026 rise at US$3,605/oz, with key price support seen at US$3,500/oz.
Above resistance at US$4,083/oz is seen needed to ease the immediate pressure off the US$3,858/oz retracement support, but 
with a more secure low only seen established on a move back above the 55-day average, currently at a distant US$4,275/oz.

Recap of the week  
US
•
The Fed held rates at 3.75%–4.00% and upgraded its assessment of economic activity to 
“strong” from “solid,” while reiterating that inflation remained elevated. 
•
Q3 GDP beat expectations, expanding at an annualized 3.4%, up from 2.2% in Q2, aided by 
business investments and consumer spending. 
•
PCE inflation rose 0.5% m/m and 3.6%y/y in September, highlighting persistent price pressures. 
The rise was broad-based across goods even, with energy a major driver, rising 21% y/y. Core 
PCE rose 3.1% y/y.
•
The Conference Board's Consumer Confidence Index surpassed expectations and rose to 91.7 
in October from 84.2 in September.
•
Q3 corporate earnings results have been strong. Among the  301 of the S&P 500 companies 
that have reported earnings, revenue is up 13.64% y/y and EPS 29.37%, with 61% beating 
revenue estimates and 82% earnings expectations.
Europe
•
The ECB left its key interest rates unchanged, with the deposit rate at 2.5% and the refinancing 
rate at 2.65%. ECB President indicated that she would step before the end of her term.
•
Eurozone Q3 GDP beat expectations, growing 0.4% q/q . Spain grew 0.6% and Germany 0.2%, 
while Italy was stable and France contracted 0.1%. 
Asia
•
The BoJ kept its policy rate unchanged at 1.25% and signaled further rate hikes. Tokyo core CPI 
eased to 2.6% y/y in October from 2.7% in September, with electricity and gas subsidies helping 
contain price pressures. Meanwhile, retail sales growth slowed to 2.3% y/y in September, while 
industrial production beat expectations, rising 2.2% m/m.
•
China’s industrial profits rose 14.4% y/y in September, up from 4.2% in August, taking growth 
for the first nine months of 2026 to 15.5%.Sectoral performance remained uneven, with 
electronics manufacturing profits surging 103.8% while automobile profits fell 22.8%. 
Meanwhile, the Communist Party concluded its Fifth Plenum without announcing new 
economic stimulus, instead emphasizing existing policy priorities and implementation.

Gold Drivers - 10yr US Real Yields complete a small 
top to warn of a further decline…
12
10yr US Real Yields rose to test and reject flagged yield resistance at 2.92%/3.00% as suspected and the subsequent decline has seen support from the 55-day average broken and a small 
yield top established as the “classic” stock/bond correlation returns. With US equities seen under increasing pressure along with other key events such as the US midterms and with the 
aforementioned supports broken this suggests the immediate trend for yields is likely to remain lower, with weekly MACD momentum (lower panel above) seen on the cusp of also crossing 
lower. Yields support is seen next at the 38.2% retracement of the rise in yields from March and July high at 2.47% where we would look to see an initial hold. A break though would see 
support next at 2.32/2.30%, with the long-term 200-day average seen at 2.20%, which would be expected to provide a better floor. Above resistance at 2.68% is seen needed to ease the 
immediate downside pressure on yields and above 2.825% to raise the prospect of a fresh look at 3%.
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council

13
Importantly in our view the fall in yields this time around has been accompanied by a sharp retracement lower in US equities as a “classic” stock/bond correlation returns and bonds act as a 
hedge against falling equities, with the Nasdaq 100 enters correction territory (10% down from its peak). Crucially, ahead of a slew of upcoming market and political events this leaves the 
Nasdaq 100 testing what we see as major supports from its rising long-term 200-day average, currently seen placed at 27927, the 38.2% retracement of the rally from March at 28107 with 
the July low seen not far below at 27176. These key supports are seen as critical going forward. A close below the 27176 July low, if seen, would in our view mark the completion of a price top 
to warn the weakness in October is part of a broader downturn with support seen next at 26182, and lower we think in due course. Such a fall would likely result in a further yield fall. Support 
at 27176 holding though would suggest recent weakness has more likely been a fall in a broader high-level range.

Glossary of Technical Analysis terms
26

Technical Analysis Glossary
27
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council
Advance/Decline Line 
A popular type of Breadth Indicator (see below) which represents the cumulative number of individual stocks in a broader index that have risen during a session, 
 
against those in the index that have fallen.
Bar chart 
 
A bar chart shows the open, close, low and high of the price of an instrument over a specific time-period. A vertical bar shows the low to high move, with the open 
 
a small horizontal bar to the left of the vertical line and with the close a small horizontal bar to the right.
Bollinger Bands 
Shows bands that represent 2 standard deviations above and below a central move moving average, typically a 20 period average. The bands are expected to 
 
 
typically capture 95% of price action under normal conditions. 
Breath Indicators 
Breath indicators describe a range of indicators that aim to show the internal strength of a specific equity market index (see Advance/Decline line).
Candlestick chart 
A method of representing open/high/low/close data, originally from Japan. The candlestick (or candle) is formed of a rectangle which represents 
 
 
the open to close move, called the real body, with this shaded different colours depending on whether a higher or lower close was seen for the session. The low 
 
and high are shown as vertical lines above and below the real body/rectangle.
Continuation Pattern 
A pattern that indicates a consolidation phase which is a pause within the direction of the current prevailing trend.
Divergence 
 
When two separate measures behave differently. For example, when a new high or low in price is not confirmed/matched by a corresponding new high or low in
 
 
a momentum indicator, hence showing a divergence.
Double Top/Bottom 
A Double Top is a type of Reversal Pattern (see below) formed during an uptrend when two price highs occur at approximately the same level. Completion of the
 
 
pattern is signalled when the “neckline” to the pattern (see below) is broken. A Double Bottom is the exact opposite setup.
Fibonacci retracements 
Horizontal lines that can indicate where support and resistance can potentially be found when a market retraces following a trending move. The percentage 
 
 
value shown is how much of the prior trend the price has retraced. The Fibonacci retracement levels typically shown are 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6%.
Fibonacci projections 
Horizontal lines that can indicate where support and resistance can potentially be found in the direction of the current trend. The percentage values are applied 
 
to the prior trending move, projected off the low/high of the subsequent corrective counter-trend move. The Fibonacci projection levels typically shown are 50%, 
 
61.8%, 100%, 150% and 161.8%.

Technical Analysis Glossary
28
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council
Flag 
 
A Flag pattern in a classic continuation pattern, characterised by a sharp rise or fall (the flagpole) followed by a short-lived counter-trend move (the flag). They are
 
 
expected to be resolved in the direction of the prevailing trend.
Head & Shoulders Top/Bottom A Head & Shoulders price pattern is a classic trend reversal pattern that appears with three peaks, where the outside two are seen closer in height and the
 
 
middle peak is the highest. Completion of the pattern is signalled when the “neckline” to the pattern is broken (see below).
Measured Objective 
Most technical patterns, regardless of whether they are reversal or continuation patterns come with a “measured objective”, which is typically based on the size
 
 
or height of the pattern. The objective is a potential indication of where the price may move to after a pattern has been completed.
Momentum 
 
Momentum is the rate of acceleration or velocity of the underlying instrument/security. It is thus the speed at which the price of the security is changing.
MACD 
 
Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) is a trend-following indicator, often also used as a momentum indicator. It shows the relationship between two
 
 
exponential moving averages of a security’s price, know as the MACD line, with an exponential average then taken off this line (the Signal line).
Moving Average 
A classic statistical moving average of the underlying price data of the security to give a guide to the direction of the prevailing price trend. Different periodicities
 
 
are used to define short-, medium- and long-term trends. Also used to identify potential areas of support and resistance.
Moving Average Envelope 
Shows bands which represent the percentage distance from a selected moving average, which can be used to identify potential support and resistance.
Neckline 
 
A trendline which marks the point where a reversal pattern is confirmed, typically found by connecting the lows/highs of the pattern.
OnBalanceVolume 
A cumulative volume indicator constructed by comparing the amount of volume traded seen on positive sessions to those on negative sessions.
Overbought 
 
An overbought condition occurs when a price rally has extended too far to fast and is seen unlikely to extend further and a pause is likely to be seen.
Oversold 
 
An oversold condition occurs when a price decline has extended too far to fast and is seen unlikely to extend further and a pause is likely to be seen.
Pennant 
 
A Pennant pattern is a type of continuation price pattern, formed when there is a sharp rise or fall (the flagpole), followed by a short consolidation period within
 
 
converging trend lines, similar in shape to a small triangle (the pennant). They are expected to be resolved in the direction of the prevailing trend.
