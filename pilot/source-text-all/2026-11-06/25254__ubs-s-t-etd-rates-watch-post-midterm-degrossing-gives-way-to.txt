ETD Rates Watch: Post-Midterm
Degrossing Gives Way to Selective
Redeployment
Daniel Noorian
05-Nov-2026, 05:53 ET (EST)
Pick a narrative that suits your view seems to be the name of the game at the moment, as investors struggle to
rationalise price action in the wake of the midterms. As usual, the first reaction was wrong, with equities
rebounding from the lows on Wednesday and the E-mini Nasdaq making a 4.9% intraday move. My hindsight
take is that price action in equities over the last few weeks has simply been about market degrossing ahead of
the midterms, as reflected in various street surveys.
With that out of the way, investors opted to selectively redeploy capital, although some of the recent themes
continued. Anything with data centre exposure got hit, although no one seemed that bothered about a 9.9%
decline in the CoreWeave share price, while defence names also got hammered. On the rates side, we saw
renewed interest in the belly of the US curve, while the decline in oil led to significant outperformance from
Gilts and OATs. I can rationalise the Gilt move, which has continued through Asia trading on Thursday, even
ahead of Thursday's BoE meeting where they are expected to raise rates by 25bp to 4%. I have a harder time
rationalising the move in OATs given nothing has really changed.
Markets seem convinced it will be a dovish hike from the Bank of England, judging by the ongoing move into
upside plays across SONIA options. Wednesday was another example, where renewed put selling to fund
upside was observed. The pick of the flows was paper paying from 0.75 up to 1.25 for 16.8k SFIM7
95.40/95.50/95.95/96.05 call condors versus the 95.00/94.90 put spread. Hard to argue with the rationale,
although I prefer nearer-dated upside plays such as the SFIF7 95.65/95.75/95.85/95.95 call condor, which was
bought for 2k in 5k. I had suggested that earlier in the morning when it was 1.5 offered.
Put selling wasn’t confined to SONIA, as accounts put on similar plays in Euribor, again playing for the end of
the ECB hiking cycle. For example, paper paid 1.25 for 8k ERF7 96.9375/97.0625 call spreads v 96.75/96.50
put spread.
One thing that has been evident recently is the drop-off in Eurex option flows. That has coincided with less
interest in European fixed income futures in general. It isn’t just Europe, though, with SOFR option activity also
slowing and the larger flows yesterday biased towards liquidations.
There was a decent pick-up in Long Bond (USZ6) open interest yesterday, rising 2.07%. That eclipsed
everything else, with TUZ6 up 1.18% and TYZ6 up 1.02%. I don’t know how much you can really read into the
increase in the Long Bond. There were no surprises from the QRA, with the phrase “at least” removed in
regard to auction.
One view is that President Trump could find his spending plans harder to push through, taking pressure off
long-end yields. Bit early for that, I think, and it was notable that there was a huge block steepener in the US
yesterday: 44.7k TU versus 10.2k WN.
Although I think markets are really just focusing on the equity trade at the moment, it is difficult to get too
bearish rates. I wonder if this is what has helped drive the ongoing call buying. That continued into the London
morning on Thursday, with paper paying 16 for 19k TYZ6 108 calls. If oil continues to weaken, which I am

sceptical about, while the impulse from data centre construction is curtailed, that should all lead to reduced
demand and then less inflationary pressure. In that case, the market is right to challenge current central bank
pricing.
This inflationary impulse has been seen everywhere, so results from Maersk this morning are interesting. They
raised their full-year financial outlook for the second time in three months. It was this comment from CEO Clerc
that stood out to me:
“What we’re seeing is actually demand continues unabated, still fuelled by a lot of investment in electrification,
in AI coming out of Asia and going all over the world….on the land side, both in Asia and at the final
destinations, are continuing also to create some constraints that do absorb some capacity and put the rate
environment in a very, very favourable light.” What happens if the AI infrastructure demand starts to falter?
Even UK high street bellwether Next seems to be having a tougher time. This morning they reported that sales
growth slowed in the prior quarter. Full-price UK sales grew 2% in the 12 weeks to 31st October, down from
3.6% in the first half of the year. The fall was blamed on rising household bills and warmer September
weather. Overall sales beat expectations, but growth was driven by a 32.2% increase in online international
sales.Where is Terry McCrann when you need him? The AFR reports that RBA members privately rebuked
Deputy Governor Hauser for comments made a fortnight before the RBA meeting signalling rates were likely to
rise. They argued that this undermined their independence and risked pre-committing the RBA to hiking.
Assistant Governor Hunter also shocked members of the board by appearing on Channel Nine’s 60 Minutes,
where she declared the RBA were “not quite done”.
As a result, sources report that board members will be more careful in “free-wheeling fireside chats and
television interviews.”
Hauser spoke early in the London morning on Thursday and said he was surprised that public trust in the RBA
had stayed solid after a series of rate hikes over the last 18 months. However, he warned that trust was under
strain and that “gains tomorrow may require pain today”. He referenced “taking away the punch bowl” and
whether people can see why difficult decisions are necessary even when the immediate impact is unwelcome.
In the Q&A, he then didn’t recognise the view that board members had been driven into a corner.
