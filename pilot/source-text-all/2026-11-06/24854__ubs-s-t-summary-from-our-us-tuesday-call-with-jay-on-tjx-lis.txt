Investment Thesis
• Marmaxx weakness a temporary execution issue, not a change in the TJX thesis. Q2 comps were +4%

Key Positives (+)
• Marmaxx appears fixable while the broader business remains healthy. Marmaxx's +1% Q2 comp
reflected higher basket but fewer transactions and was described by management as a self-inflicted
merchandise-mix issue. Meanwhile, HomeGoods +7% comps / 12.4% margin, Canada +6%, International
+7% / 7.3% margin argue against a company-wide deterioration. Jay said roughly four comparable misses
over 30 years were generally corrected within 2-3 quarters, once 4.
• The moat deepens with scale rather than eroding. TJX generates approximately $60.3bn of FY2026

an increasingly important customer to suppliers as it grows, encouraging vendors to work within TJX's
economics rather than creating a sourcing bottleneck. He mentioned $70bn, $80bn, $90bn and eventually
$100bn potential future scale.
• International significantly extends the growth runway. Jay referenced UK, Canada, Australia, Spain,
Mexico, Saudi Arabia/wider Middle East and Russia as evidence of, or examples around, the proposition's
international applicability, with longer-term opportunity across South America, broader Europe and eventually
Asia. The company has around 5,200 stores vs. a 7,500 long-term target, including 300 additional Marmaxx
and 200 additional HomeGoods stores. Rollout constraints are mainly real estate, talent, sourcing
infrastructure, regulation and execution, rather than consumer acceptance.
• TJX has an unusually powerful merchandising engine. Jay said inventory effectively turns roughly every
three weeks and described a typical store as around 25,000 sq. ft. Buyers operate almost like portfolio
managers, owning categories and continuously assessing what is trending, what consumers will pay, what
TJX should pay vendors and whether that spread produces an attractive margin. His illustrative example was a

Key Risks (-)
• Marmaxx does not recover. This is the principal stock-specific risk. Jay believes investors ultimately want
comps back toward mid-single digits, with evidence of recovery around the end of Q4 / Christmas season into
January. Management's nearer-term Q2 commentary separately pointed toward a 2-3% comp cadence by Q4.
• The fashion miss proves more persistent or structural. Jay thinks an important trend slipped between
buying/category responsibilities and TJX reacted too slowly. Continued weak transactions would challenge the
view that this is simply one of the periodic merchandising misses TJX has historically corrected.
• International growth requires execution. Jay sees consumer demand for branded goods at attractive
prices as broadly applicable, but entering new markets requires real estate, local talent, sourcing
infrastructure, regulatory execution and operational know-how.
• Consumer and margin conditions can deteriorate. Jay believes moderate inflation can strengthen TJX's
value proposition, but a sufficiently severe inflation shock/recession could overwhelm that benefit. Q2
merchandise-margin strength also benefited partly from tariff refunds, whose future amount and timing are
uncertain, although management indicated underlying results were ahead even excluding them.
• Positioning creates relative-performance risk. Jay described investor interest in TJX as very high. That
creates buying potential if Marmaxx improves, but in a powerful risk-on environment TJX could relatively lag
more beaten-down or lower-quality names. He also sees investors seeking somewhat more portfolio balance
away from exceptionally concentrated AI/Magnificent Seven exposure rather than a wholesale rotation.
Bull Case
Marmaxx recovers from +1% Q2 / low-single-digit territory toward healthier growth, confirming that the issue
was a temporary merchandising miss. HomeGoods, Canada and International remain healthy, off-price
continues taking share from department stores and weaker specialty retail, and TJX scales internationally
toward its 7,500-store opportunity. The moat of 21,000+ vendors, direct sourcing, highly specialized
buyers, rapid inventory turns and the treasure-hunt proposition supports approximately 6-7% sales
growth + modest margin expansion + buybacks. Investor confidence returns and the stock moves back
toward the high-20s/low-30s P/E range, supporting the UBS $198 PT.
Bear Case
Marmaxx does not recover despite corrective actions, indicating that the weakness is more persistent than Jay
expects. Lower transactions continue, buying execution remains challenged and international expansion takes
longer because of real estate, talent, infrastructure or regulatory constraints .A weaker consumer or margin
pressure compounds the problem, and despite TJX's franchise quality investors become unwilling to restore
the historical premium multiple.
Overall Takeaway
Jay's central message is straightforward: the TJX investment case has not fundamentally changed; the
current Marmaxx weakness looks like a temporary merchandising/execution problem rather than
deterioration in the off-price model or TJX's competitive position. The important distinction is that TJX's
broader engine continues to work. Q2 consolidated comps were +4% despite only +1% at Marmaxx, while

HomeGoods +7%, Canada +6% and International +7% remained strong. Jay therefore sees the problem as
localized rather than company-wide.
Longer term, the case rests on several mutually reinforcing advantages: structural off-price share gains, a
sourcing model supported by 21,000+ vendors and direct sourcing, inventory that Jay says turns roughly every
three weeks, category buyers operating like portfolio managers, a physical treasure-hunt experience, and an
international platform that differentiates TJX from Ross and Burlington.
International is particularly important to Jay's differentiation argument. His core consumer insight is that people
across markets like strong brands at low prices. He referenced Russia, Saudi Arabia, the wider Middle East,
Australia, Spain, the UK, Canada, the US and Mexico, alongside longer-term possibilities in South America,
the rest of Europe and eventually Asia. The question in his view is more about execution and timing than
whether the consumer proposition travels. This combination supports Jay's approximately 6-7% annual sales
growth + 10-20bps annual margin expansion + buybacks framework. UBs estimates 11% five-year EPS
CAGR, 30x P/E and a $198 PT, and also highlighted HomeSense, Sierra Trading Post and TK Maxx's entry
into Spain as incremental growth opportunities.
The key debate is therefore not an elaborate macro call. It is whether Marmaxx comps recover toward
mid-single digits. If they do, Jay believes it addresses the three questions currently hanging over TJX: has
the model changed, is competition gaining, and is the slowdown structural?

Q&A Highlights
1. Can TJX continue finding enough attractive merchandise as the business becomes significantly
larger?
Jay sees no meaningful sourcing constraint. Off-price benefits from inventory mistakes, excess inventory
and market inefficiencies, but TJX also undertakes substantial direct sourcing. At roughly $60bn annual
revenue and ~20,000 vendor relationships in Jay's call framing, TJX is already strategically important to
many suppliers. His argument is that scale can strengthen sourcing because vendors have incentives to
produce merchandise that fits TJX's economics. He used $70bn, $80bn, $90bn and eventually $100bn only
to illustrate future scale, not as dated forecasts.
2. Is e-commerce, and increasingly agentic AI, a structural threat to the off-price
model?
Jay thinks the risk is limited. An online competitor still needs to recreate TJX's price/value economics, which
he believes is difficult, while also replicating the experience. Consumers may spend 20-30 minutes browsing
without knowing what they will find, then get satisfaction from discovering an unexpected deal themselves.
Agentic commerce may grow significantly, but Jay's argument is that it does not necessarily recreate value +
discovery + constantly changing assortment.
3. What has actually gone wrong at Marmaxx, and is it temporary or structural?
Jay's best explanation is that TJX missed a fashion trend and reacted too slowly. In his interpretation,
something effectively slipped between category responsibilities, leaving TJX underexposed to a trend
competitors captured. Q2 Marmaxx comp was +1%, with Q3 being discussed in low-single-digit territory,
while HomeGoods, Canada, Europe/international and Australia remained stronger. Jay said related incidents
occurred roughly four times over the previous 30 years, generally taking 2-3 quarters to correct, once 4.
His base case is therefore that this is an execution mistake rather than structural deterioration.

4. What does the market need to see for the stock to work?
The key number is Marmaxx same-store sales returning toward mid-single digits. Jay expects evidence of
recovery around the end of Q4 / Christmas season into January. In his view, that would help remove the
central concerns around whether TJX's model has changed, competition has strengthened or the Marmaxx
slowdown is structural, potentially restoring confidence toward the high-20s/low-30s P/E range.
5. Is Ross's recent strength evidence that it is taking share from TJX?
Jay does not think that is the main dynamic. His larger industry thesis is that off-price is taking share from
outside off-price, particularly traditional department stores and weaker mall-based specialty retailers. He
used Children's Place as an extreme illustration of pressure on weaker specialty retail, referencing a market
value of roughly $5m versus ~$2bn historically, and noted bearish UBS views on Macy's, Kohl's and
Dillard's, with Nordstrom having previously carried neutral/sell ratings before going private. He pointed out
that Kohl's still represented more than $20bn in revenue, illustrating how much share remains available
outside off-price. He was also constructive on some of Ross's newer marketing ideas and believes industry
innovation can bring more consumers into off-price overall, rather than merely move customers between
TJX, Ross and Burlington.
6. Marmaxx has been seeing larger baskets but fewer transactions. Is that temporary, or could the
inflation dynamic ultimately become advantageous for TJX?
Jay linked the dynamic partly to inflation: higher prices / potentially lower unit volumes → stronger
consumer desire for value → stronger relative appeal for off-price. Moderate inflation can therefore
paradoxically support TJX because consumers become more motivated to hunt for bargains, historically
helping off-price gain share. The limit is an inflation shock severe enough to cause recession and materially
damage aggregate spending, which Jay did not believe described the US backdrop at the time.
7. How strong is the US consumer backdrop?
Jay described spending as quite strong, with US retail sales generally growing at a mid-single-digit pace
through much of the year and stronger in some months. He also referenced UBS consumer work surveying
8,000 US consumers every month, with a 20-year history and backtesting that provides a useful
approximately three-month-forward read on spending. The transcript has a gap in the precise survey
question/result, so that missing part should not be reconstructed. What is preserved is Jay's observation that
department stores had been the number-one answer the prior year, while he interpreted more recent data
as evidence of consumers shifting toward off-price/value.
8. Does TJX's value proposition genuinely work internationally?
Jay was emphatically positive. His basic insight is that people across countries like strong brands at low
prices. He described how local entrepreneurs in markets such as Russia had independently tried to create
similar concepts, which he sees as evidence of underlying demand even where execution was inferior to
TJX's.
He referenced Russia, Saudi Arabia, the wider Middle East, Australia, Spain, the UK, Canada, the US and
Mexico, with further opportunity in Mexico more broadly, South America, the rest of Europe and eventually
Asia. The hurdles are primarily real estate, local talent, sourcing infrastructure, legal/regulatory requirements
and operating complexity, so Jay sees them as time/execution constraints rather than evidence that the
consumer proposition does not travel.
9. Is TJX using AI to optimize costs and operations, similar to initiatives discussed at companies such
as Tapestry?
Jay said he has heard that TJX is working on AI and seriously thinking about how to evolve the
operation. He noted that TJX would have been viewed as relatively technologically unsophisticated 5-10
years ago, yet was already exceptionally good at getting appropriate merchandise into the appropriate store

7
at the appropriate time. He compared that inventory competency with H&M, albeit achieved through a
historically more manual model.
AI therefore represents incremental optimization rather than a fix for an inefficient operation, particularly
around inventory quantity, allocation and timing. Jay described a potential 5-10 year project, explicitly
noting that this timeframe was his own guess.
10. How does TJX achieve such rapid inventory turns, and what role do the buyers
play?
Jay said TJX effectively turns inventory every three weeks. He described a typical store as around 25,000
sq. ft. and illustrated the markdown discipline with a product planned at $50 being reduced toward $25 if
demand is insufficient, thereby clearing merchandise quickly.
The buyers are central to this system. Jay compared them with portfolio managers: each owns a
merchandise category and is expected to understand it deeply, including what is trending, what customers
will pay, what TJX should pay vendors and whether the spread creates an attractive margin. He
described this as the core competency of the business.
11. With earnings on November 18, how should we think about the catalyst path, including the
consumer, TJX versus Walmart/Costco, the Fed and broader macro?
Jay stripped the answer back to Marmaxx. The Fed can influence the overall market but is not especially
TJX-specific. The two pieces of information he wants investors to focus on are(1) real-time credit-card data
showing TJX sales trends and (2) actual reported same-store sales confirming or challenging those trends.
His framework was essentially binary: Marmaxx improves + the rest of TJX remains healthy → the stock
can work. Marmaxx fails to improve → investors remain dissatisfied.
12. What could the election / oil-price backdrop mean for TJX?
Part of Jay's answer is missing from the transcript and should not be reconstructed. The preserved portion
links the election discussion with possible developments involving Iran and oil prices and considers a
scenario in which oil falls.
Interestingly, in such a strong risk-on scenario, Jay argued TJX might not be the best relative performer
despite his bullish fundamental view. TJX is already a popular, well-owned quality stock, while lower-quality or
more beaten-down names could rally harder. The distinction is therefore: bullish TJX fundamentals, but
potentially less compelling relative performance in an extreme risk-on rally.
13. How are investor conversations and positioning changing?
Jay described investor interest in TJX as very high, meaning a visible Marmaxx improvement could potentially
attract meaningful incremental buying. More broadly, investor conversations had been unusually active across
his coverage over the prior roughly two months.
Beyond TJX, Ross and Burlington, he said discussions were increasingly moving toward On Holding, Levi's,
Ralph Lauren and Tapestry, while positioning in Nike and Lululemon remained notably bearish. He also
observed that consumer-discretionary crowding had begun to edge higher after declining during the first half
of the year.
He connected this with a broader desire for somewhat more portfolio balance after the market had been
dominated by AI/Magnificent Seven exposure, while explicitly stopping short of calling it a wholesale rotation.
14. What's the most practical real-time indicator before earnings?
Jay specifically highlighted Bloomberg TJX → ALTD. He said US hedge funds monitor this credit-card data
closely because it can provide a real-time read on TJX's US sales trajectory before reported results.
