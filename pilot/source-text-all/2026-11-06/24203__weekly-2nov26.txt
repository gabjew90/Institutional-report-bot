Global Macro
2
The main story in macro markets last week continued to be the rotation away from AI-related tech hardware stocks. The
Philadelphia Semiconductor index (SOX) dropped 8.2% on Friday alone and is now 27% lower than its June highs (-15% in
October). It has closed below its 200-day moving average for the first time since April 2025. The MSCI Emerging Markets
(EM) Information Technology (IT) index is also now on its 200-day moving average for the first time since April 2025 but is
yet to drop below it, with some support to the Korean memory sector emerging in recent trading sessions.
Because broad market sentiment has been so driven by AI and tech this year, a shift in mood has had a very marked impact
on bond yields, with the 10-year UST yield now back at 4.9% for the first time since September, having round-tripped to
over 5.3% in early October. This likely reflects de-leveraging and positioning dynamics as well as some markdown in bond
investors’ expectations of overheating AI capex driving nominal growth higher.
There has been little fundamental reason behind the sell-off so far, in our view. The tech sector has derated along with
rising interest rates since June, and the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) rate hike in September set the tone for
another leg of the sell-off, with tightening financing conditions first affecting the weak links such as the neoclouds and more
leveraged AI names. The US Federal Reserve (Fed) held rates last week in a 10-2 vote, but commentary remains tilted
hawkish as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh reiterated commitment to bringing inflation back to target. Markets are pricing a 75%
probability of another hike in December.
Pain in leveraged AI names, however, has now broadened. Equity markets have derated companies associated with large AI
capex, even those with large net cash positions. Yet there has been no major across-the-board sell-off, with sectors such as
healthcare and consumer staples – classic risk-off rotation plays – catching a bid: both S&P 500 sectors rose over 11% in
October.
It is notable that while valuation declines have accelerated in recent months, the broad IT derating really began in July 2024
(peaking at slightly lower levels again in October 2025). Since then, relative to the S&P 500, US IT has fallen from around
1.5x the valuation of the broad index (in 12-month forward P/E terms) to 1.0x today (from 32x to 18x earnings). Earnings
have, therefore, driven well over 100% of the rally, with the index up 50% since July 2024’s valuation peak. A 33% derating
relative to the index over around 26 months is comparable with the dotcom bust, where S&P IT fell from 2.3x the index
valuation to about 1.4x over the same period, around a 40% decline. At 18x forward earnings, S&P IT today is also cheaper
than it ever was in the dotcom bust, where the index bottomed – in valuation terms – at 19x in 2002 before a period of
recovery. EM IT stocks at 8.5x earnings are also close to the cheapest levels of the 2000s so far.
Despite being very solid on paper, US hyperscaler earnings have not alleviated concerns, probably due to very elevated
expectations for both earnings themselves and for capex forecasts. On average, hyperscaler capex came in slightly softer
than expected for Q3 2026. However, the only reason for this was supply issues, which is the ‘bottleneck’ trade playing out
in real time – the opposite of an indication of oversupply. If one were to make an argument for the recent tech sell-off being
efficient ‘pricing’, it would be pricing the risk of capex massively undershooting expectations in 2027. However, there is little
evidence that this will be the case. All indications from hyperscaler outlooks are for capex to rise ‘significantly’ in USD terms
in 2027, especially as profitability of AI investments remains, so far, very attractive. Cloud revenue is surging across the
board, with yoy revenue growth also continuing to rise each quarter, at 103% at Google, 48% at Microsoft and 39% at
Amazon.
Taking a step back, it is easy to forget that 2026 was really the first year of mass corporate AI adoption. Given we are still so
early in the enterprise adoption curve, and supply around compute and AI data centre components remains highly
constrained across the board, it is hard for us to believe we are anywhere near ‘the end’ of this investment cycle. However,
even moving to a stage of lower growth in spending may take some time for investors to digest. Risk-off sentiment has set
in and financing concerns remain an issue, even as rates have come down meaningfully in the past few weeks. Hedge fund
de-leveraging has likely contributed to the magnitude of moves in high-beta tech in the last few weeks.
The US midterms coming up this week are another risk event bringing further uncertainty to the sector. Prediction markets
are currently pricing the probability the Democrats take the Senate at 73%. This playing out, assuming the Democrats also
take the House (95% priced), may well lead to some further negative AI sentiment, given the Democrats’ anti-data-centre
rhetoric. Other models that incorporate the past results and poll biases suggest a good chance that the Republicans keep
control of the Senate.

Caution is therefore advisable in the short term, with volatility likely to remain elevated, particularly as the market’s
perception is that escalation risk rises in the Middle East upon the conclusion of the US midterms. The main catalyst for a
switch back to risk-on remains a deal in Iran, in our view. Axios reported that, on a Saturday call, Israeli Prime Minister
Benjamin Netanyahu pressed US President Donald Trump to strike Iran immediately after Tuesday’s midterms. Trump prefers
to let negotiations run and leave the possibility of a ‘deal’ on the table while preparing for a return to major combat. US
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is said to be urging more time for the economic pressure campaign to work. Ayatollah
Mojtaba Khamenei reportedly ordered an immediate response “without geographical limitations” to any attack, even as
Qatari mediators spent over 12 hours in talks in Tehran; Iran has threatened oil and gas facilities in five Gulf states if struck.

India:  The Finance Ministry said price stability will be a key priority in H2 FY27, as elevated crude prices and food inflation
persist, and estimated Q2 FY27 growth at 7.35% after 7.8% in Q1. It noted that the effectiveness of the Reserve Bank of
India’s (RBI) October hike will depend on liquidity management and on limiting second-round effects. A Reuters poll sees
growth slowing to 7.1% in FY27 from 7.7% in FY26, inflation averaging 6.1% this quarter and two further RBI hikes taking the
repo rate to 6.0% by end-March.
Malaysia:  Second Finance Minister Amir Hamzah Azizan said the BUDI95 targeted fuel subsidy programme is still expected
to save MYR 2.5-4bn a year by closing loopholes for non-citizens and ineligible recipients. Fuel subsidy spending has
stabilised at around MYR 4bn a month, allowing the government to restore the monthly subsidised quota to 300 litres from
200 litres.
South Korea:  SK Hynix reported record Q3 operating profit of KRW 83.3trn, up 557% yoy, with an operating margin above
80%, and said virtually all its DRAM and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) capacity through 2029 is covered by long-term
agreements. Its shares rebounded more than 10%. Samsung Electronics’ final Q3 results confirmed operating profit of KRW
107.4trn, with chips accounting for 99% of the total and 2027 HBM capacity sold out, but net profit of KRW 75.1trn missed
consensus and its shares fell.

Argentina:  The government and IMF staff reached agreement on the third Extended Fund Facility (EFF) review, which
would unlock around USD 900m once approved by the board in early December. The IMF said net reserve accumulation
beat the end-June target by around USD 4bn and kept the 2026 primary surplus target at 1.4% of GDP, with no clear policy
changes. Separately, the USD 3bn Vaca Muerta Oil Sur export pipeline is 85% complete, with first shipments due in March-
April 2027 and capacity rising from 180,000 barrels per day (bpd) towards 550,000 bpd by end-2027.
Brazil:  President Lula da Silva won a fourth term with 51.8% of the vote in a surprise runoff victory over Flávio Bolsonaro,
whose Liberal Party recognised the result. The BRL fell around 10% to BRL 5.60/USD and the Ibovespa triggered a circuit
breaker. Brazilian stocks ended the week nearly 25% down in USD terms. Fitch said Lula’s re-election adds fiscal uncertainty
and could limit the Selic easing cycle, warning a failure to improve fiscal credibility could lead to a downgrade from ‘BB’.
However, the Liberal Party will hold the largest caucus in both houses of Congress, making the choice of finance minister a
key signal. Flávio left Brazil for Miami despite a Supreme Court order to surrender his passports.
Chile:  Capstone Copper announced more than USD 3bn of investment over four years to roughly double its Chilean copper
output to 300,000 tonnes a year by 2031, led by the USD 2.3bn greenfield Santo Domingo copper-iron project and
expansions at Mantoverde. The pipeline of copper investment is strong but should help Chile return to previous output
peaks rather than drive a boom, given lagging project execution and falling mine productivity.

Hungary:  The government will postpone the inflation indexation of fuel excise taxes by six months to 1 July 2027, citing
high fuel prices. Indexation will be lower next year at 1.2% than this year’s 4.3%, adding around HUF 2.5 per litre to petrol and

resume talks with the US and Ukraine after 4 November, possibly in Abu Dhabi.

Egypt hiked rates for the first time since 2024
Source information is at the end of the document.
Commentary
Egypt:  The central bank raised its key rates by 100bps in a surprise move, its first hike since 2024, taking the overnight
deposit rate to 20.0% and the lending rate to 21.0%. It cited rising inflationary pressures, risk-off sentiment and global
volatility, as portfolio outflows resumed and the pound hit record lows. It still expects inflation to return to its 5-9% target
range by Q4 2027, with GDP growth stable at around 4.7% in Q3.
Kenya:  The government urged counties to step up flood preparedness as El Niño rains intensify, warning above-average
rainfall could extend into January 2027. Officials estimate up to 1.5 million people could be affected by flooding, including
around 500,000 who could be displaced, and activated a national emergency response plan on 5 October.
Nigeria:  Finance Minister Taiwo Oyedele unveiled a draft National Economic Policy Framework to end policy reversals,
including mandatory impact assessments before major tax and tariff changes and an annual scorecard tracking real wages,
jobs and poverty. He reiterated the target of bringing inflation, at 14.6% in September, into low single digits before end-2027
to allow single-digit interest rates.
Saudi Arabia:  Saudi Aramco has raised the East-West pipeline’s crude capacity to around 7m bpd from 5m bpd before the
war, and a temporary conversion of a gas pipeline adds a further 1.2m bpd until May 2027, taking export capacity to the Red
Sea to about 8.2m bpd. A further expansion to around 9m bpd is under consideration. In the UAE, Abu Dhabi National Oil
Company (ADNOC) is fast-tracking a twin Habshan-Fujairah pipeline to double export capacity outside Hormuz to around
3.6m bpd.
Türkiye:  Minutes of the October meeting, at which the central bank cut its policy rate by 100bps to 36%, said monetary
policy will be tightened if the inflation outlook deteriorates significantly and persistently, with decisions taken meeting by
meeting. The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) said underlying inflation has softened but expects higher food and energy
prices to lift monthly CPI in October, while core inflation remains more moderate.
