Excelerate Energy Inc (EE): 3Q26 Recap: Operational Flexibility Solves 
Hormuz Risk; Caribbean Expansion Supports Multi-Year Growth Runway
5 November 2026 | 4:25PM MST
Following 3Q26 results for Excelerate Energy (EE, Buy), we refresh our estimates. 
Results in the quarter were stronger than expected, and EE narrowed FY26 guidance 
to $495-515m (vs $490-515m prior) which compares to GSe and consensus now of 
$508m/$498m. Most notable were updates on EE’s plans to eliminate Strait of 
Hormuz crossings in 2027 for both its Iraq and Colombia FSRUs via vessel swaps, 
keeping both in-service dates and project returns intact. We see updates here as 
important for both derisking the 2027 outlook (questions on EE’s SoH transit risk had 
been a key overhang for the stock) and a proof point on the value of operational 
flexibility on EE’s fleet. Commercially, EE unlocked its next phase of growth on its 
Caribbean footprint with the 11-year LNG supply agreement with AxDo, 
underpinning the new Bahamas LNG Hub (~$15m/yr run-rate EBITDA for half the 
terminal’s capacity; initial deliveries will ramp in 1Q27 via Jamaica ahead of a 2H28 
dedicated FSU deployment). Lastly, EE also signed an agreement with Petrobras for a 
5-year extension of the Sequoia contract (now through 2038, including plans to add 
a reliquefaction unit). Altogether, we see updates in the quarter as meaningfully 
derisking the near-term growth path, validating the scalability of EE’s integrated 
footprint, and supporting the company’s industry-leading EBITDA growth (we 
estimate an 11% CAGR 2025-2030 vs our midstream coverage average of 7% - we 
detail drivers of potential upside to our estimates within). On valuation, we still see a 
compelling risk-reward following relative outperformance today (EE +3% vs AMNA 
-3%); our PT stays at $49 and we maintain our Buy-rating.

Key Takeaways  
2027 growth outlook derisked via fleet flexibility. EE outlined plans to eliminate 
n
planned Strait of Hormuz crossings for both its Iraq terminal and Colombia FSRU 
deployments. Specifically, EE now expects to utilize an FSRU already within the 
region for its Iraq deployment (instead of the Acadia for an unchanged 2Q27 
in-service) and source LNG supply within the region. Similarly, EE expects to utilize 
other FSRUs in the company’s portfolio for the Colombia project (instead of the 
Express for an unchanged 1Q27 in-service). We see this update as a proof point on 
the value of EE’s underappreciated operational flexibility and should drive investor 
confidence in the 2027 growth outlook. Importantly, management noted that 
despite puts and takes to project economics (the absence of cross-ocean fuel fees 
may be offset elsewhere, as example), project returns were reiterated ($100m of 
annual EBITDA on Iraq and 35% contract uplift on Colombia). Overall, we see EE’s 
plans to swap FSRUs and avoid SoH crossings both as derisking the 2027 growth 
outlook and as a proof point on the value of EE’s operational flexibility, which we had 
seen as underappreciated heading into the release.  
Bahamas LNG Hub represents first major Caribbean expansion since the 
n
Jamaica acquisition. EE announced plans to develop its Bahamas LNG Hub, a 
planned floating LNG storage and distribution terminal near the island of Grand 
Bahama. Initial deliveries are guided to ramp 1Q27 (using the Jamaica platform’s 
existing FSRU, LNG supply, and logistics capabilities for ~$6m of annual EBITDA), 
followed by the addition of a dedicated FSU in 2H28 (for $15m of annual EBITDA on 
the AxDo contract alone, for $85-95m of guided capex). The project is underpinned 
by an 11-year agreement with AxDo Bahamas for 16 tbtu/yr of LNG supply, which 
per management, represents less than half of the asset’s total capacity. On the AxDo 
economics ($15m of EBITDA per year 2H28+ on the 16 tbtu/yr contracted capacity), 
we would expect this terminal to represent ~$30m+ of EBITDA on a run-rate basis. 
Overall, we see this as solid execution on EE’s plans to grow its integrated Caribbean 
footprint by $80-110m by 2030 - just ~1.5 years after the Jamaica acquisition 
closed - a proof point on the rapid scalability, cost-advantaged logistics, and 
repeatability of EE’s integrated infrastructure model across the regional marine 
bunkering and power generation markets.  
Contract backlog boosted by five-year Petrobras extension on Sequoia. 
n
Excelerate executed a five-year contract extension with Petrobras for the FSRU 
Sequoia now through 2038 - representing a $400m increase to EE’s contracted 
backlog. As part of the agreement, Excelerate will install a reliquefaction unit on the 
Sequoia during its next scheduled dry dock in 2028. Management expects the 
agreement to provide modest upside to contracted revenues beginning in 2028 from 
the reliquefaction installation. Overall, we see this as a tangible example on both the 
demand for floating regasification infrastructure and on EE’s ability to capture 
operating leverage on its legacy contract portfolio (via tenor and rate).  
Patricia Camila conversion on track with Seatrium contract award. Excelerate 
n
awarded Seatrium the EPC contract for the conversion of the Methane Patricia 
Camila LNG carrier into an FSRU last month. Total project capex increased to 
~$260m (inclusive of the vessel purchase price), though management reiterated

confidence in achieving a 5-7x build multiple. There were no tangible details on the

Clear bridge to $250m-$300m in EBITDA growth by 2029. Management provided 
n
clear building blocks for medium-term EBITDA growth: ~$100m+ from the Iraq 
terminal, ~+$10m from the Colombia charter rate step-up, +$80-$110m from 
expansions on the Caribbean footprint, and ~$40-$50m from the Patricia Camila 
deployment (~+$100m if EE executes an integrated terminal), and modest upside to 
the Sequoia. Altogether, this represents ~11% EBITDA CAGR 2025-2030 vs our 
coverage average of ~7% - and each incremental FSRU conversion option could 
represent ~6% upside to our 2030 estimates on a standalone basis, or ~13% upside 
on an integrated basis. We continue to see EE’s highly contracted earnings runway, 
solid balance sheet position, and upside to medium-term estimates offering a 
compelling risk-reward.  
Constructive tone on the upcoming regas buildout cycle. Zooming out, 
n
management remains highly constructive on the regasification capacity demand 
outlook, as the upcoming global LNG macro supply addition wave will require 
significant downstream regasification infrastructure additions to provide seasonal 
peak-shaving, fuel switching, and renewables intermittency backstopping 
capabilities. Management highlighted EE’s incumbent position as the largest floating 
regasification operator globally as a key competitive advantage and noted they are 
actively high-grading the backlog, prioritizing high-margin integrated projects to 
maximize long-term returns given the magnitude of opportunities emerging for 
regasification demand globally. Management’s tone on the macro is consistent with 
our views that we are entering a supportive environment for regasification 
infrastructure development, as global LNG import demand growth and new, 
price-elastic demand growth each support incremental regasification development 
in the coming quarters. We continue to see a potential need for ~30-115 mtpa of 
floating regas capacity additions globally by the middle of next decade, which could 
support 2-5 FSRU additions to EE’s fleet through mid-2030s on our math, assuming 
EE’s 25% market share is unchanged.  
Updating Estimates  
 
 
Our EBITDA estimates increase ~1% on average 2026-2032. We update our model to 
reflect 3Q26 actuals, the Sequoia contract extension, the Bahamas LNG hub 
announcement, and our marked to market commodity deck. We also delay the startup 
of the Qatar/Petrobangla LNG supply contract which remains subject to improvement in 
LNG flows through the Strait of Hormuz. On capital allocation, we continue to expect 
low double digit dividend growth through 2028.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
