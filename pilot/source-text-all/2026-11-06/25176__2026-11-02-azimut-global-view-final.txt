As of this writing, reporting for the 2026.Q3 earnings season is over halfway through,
with 55% of companies in the U.S., 49% in Europe, and 18% in Japan having reported
their results. Overall, earnings growth is coming in better than consensus expectations,
supported by firmer activity momentum. Reported EPS growth stands at +19% YoY in
the U.S. and +34% YoY in Europe, implying positive EPS surprise factors of +3% and +13%,
respectively.
In the U.S., 81% of reporting companies have beaten EPS estimates, with positive
contributions across all sectors and the bulk of earnings growth driven by Information
Technology, Industrials, and Energy. In Europe, 56% of companies have exceeded EPS
estimates; performance is strong and broad-based, with the Energy sector acting as
the primary driver (+126% YoY) and eight of the remaining ten sectors printing double-
digit earnings growth. In Japan, 45% of TSE Prime companies have beaten EPS
estimates; aggregate EPS growth stands at -3% YoY, delivering a surprise factor of -2%.
Regionally, European earnings growth has outpaced the U.S. in both aggregate and
median terms (+13% YoY vs. +10% YoY, respectively), aided by a weaker euro which
bolstered European relative sales beats. Cyclicals in the U.S. continue to deliver much
stronger EPS growth than traditional Defensives (+29% YoY vs. +10% YoY), while
European Defensives have seen beats rebound toward their highest levels since 2022.
United States (S&P 500)
For the third quarter, U.S. companies have reported 11% sales growth and a 19%
increase in EPS. Notably, 60% of companies have beaten sales estimates, with an
overall sales surprise of 2%, and all eleven sectors delivered positive top-line growth.

On the earnings side, 81% of companies have exceeded consensus estimates, delivering an aggregate
positive surprise of 3%. Seven out of eleven sectors have recorded double-digit EPS growth or higher, led by
Information Technology (+27% YoY), Industrials (+41% YoY), and Energy (+168% YoY), while Healthcare
remained flat and Real Estate (+3% YoY) lagged. Stock price reactions to earnings have been negatively
tilted, with beats rewarded modestly with a median 1-day gain of +0.2% relative to the market, whereas
misses were penalized more severely with a median decline of -1.0%.
Consensus expectations for U.S.
corporate profits have continued their upward trajectory, with the blended S&P 500 2026.Q3 EPS estimate

growth expectations have been upgraded to 34.8% (up from 15.6% in January), with FY27 EPS growth
pegged at 17.5%.
Big Tech
Microsoft delivered a structurally flawless quarter that easily cleared a rising bar of buyside expectations,
anchored by a massive 48% CCY growth in Azure alongside a resilient 18% CCY growth in M365 Commercial
Cloud. More importantly, management's FQ2 outlook decisively beat any near-term growth concerns by
guiding for further acceleration: Azure is expected to hit 49–50% CCY, and M365 Commercial is modeled at
19% CCY, with consumption revenues layering constructively throughout the fiscal year. While they did trim
FY26 CapEx slightly from $175B to $170B, management made it explicitly clear this was driven by compute
deployment delays rather than any softening in underlying AI demand.
The narrative for Alphabet has firmly split, with Q3 results defined by an uncomfortable 6.3% GAAP EBIT miss
driven entirely by margin compression in the core Google Services segment. While Google Cloud continues
to execute flawlessly, delivering revenue and EBIT well ahead of expectations and teasing meaningful TPU
sales starting in 2027, the legacy Search business has officially re-entered a "show me" phase as investors
scrutinize the margin profile of AI-integrated search. Compounding the mixed sentiment, management
maintained a conservative posture by reiterating its $195B–$205B framework.
Amazon posted a 7% Operating Income beat, demonstrating exceptional leverage across both AWS and its
North American retail footprint. The retail margin story was particularly impressive: management successfully
lowered the overall cost-to-serve through better inventory placement, shorter shipping distances and

Japan (Topix)
With 18% of TSE Prime companies having reported Q2 FY27 results (covering the July–September / Q3
calendar quarter), delivery showed a clear bifurcation between revenues and net profits. Forty-five percent
of reporting companies beat net profit estimates, with aggregate net profit contracting -3% YoY, resulting in
an earnings surprise of -2%. Twelve out of sixteen reporting sectors achieved positive earnings growth, led by

healthy top-line growth of +14% YoY, with all fifteen reporting sectors contributing positively. In terms of
revisions, full-year consensus FY26 EPS growth for the Topix has been revised upward to 22.0% (from 11.1% in
January), while FY27 growth is estimated at 10.7%.

Transcript Analysis, Capital return and Guidance
Earnings call transcripts for Q3 2026 reveal that corporate commentary remains generally constructive
regarding demand, guidance, and margins, though rising input costs and interest rates are emerging as
headwinds. Demand discussions remained overwhelmingly positive, with 88% positive mentions, while margin
sentiment stayed resilient at 74% positive as companies emphasized their pricing power. Sentiment toward
the macro economy is negative at 43%, while foreign exchange transitioned into a tailwind for European
exporters for the first time in several quarters, registering 40% positive mentions. Conversely, input cost
commentary was 82% negative due to elevated energy prices, and interest rate impacts weighed at 60%
negative.
Corporate guidance sentiment was robust, with 62% positive mentions and 34% neutral, led by optimism in
Healthcare, Tech, Real Estate, and Financials. Capex commentary remained supportive at 58% positive and

We have upgraded our recommendation on Developed Market equities back
to Neutral. The upgrade reflects relative value between asset classes rather than
an outright positive view on stocks. After a summer of increasingly attractive
interest rates and steadily rising stock prices, bonds were due to outperform,
which is exactly what has happened over the past two weeks. With the two
asset classes now more balanced, we thought it tactically appropriate to revert
to a neutral recommendation. Weaknesses in the equity market persists, so
caution remains warranted and further declines are possible. On the other
hand, once the U.S. midterm elections are over, one of the market's main
sources of uncertainty will fall away, which should provide support. The ongoing
sector rotation is also helping to underpin the major indices, despite sharp
corrections in some of the most popular sectors.

We have tactically lowered our recommendation on Developed Market
sovereign bonds back to Neutral. The reason is the same—but in
reverse—as the one mentioned in the commentary on developed
market equities. Our long-term view has not changed: we continue to
view bonds as an attractive asset class at these levels. However,
following the significant pullback in yields—particularly at the long end of
the curves—we deemed it appropriate to temporarily adopt a more
cautious stance, aiming to rebuild our positions should rates begin to rise
again. We continue to favor the belly of the curves, even more so after
the outperformance seen at the long ends.
