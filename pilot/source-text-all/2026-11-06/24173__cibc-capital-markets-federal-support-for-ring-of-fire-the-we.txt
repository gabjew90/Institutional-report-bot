Federal Support For Ring of Fire – The 
Weekly Blueprint 
  
Key Takeaway: Ottawa’s commitment to match Ontario’s investment of up 
to $1B in Ring of Fire road infrastructure reinforces a multiyear opportunity 
across our E&C and heavy equipment coverage. We see BDT as the 
clearest beneficiary given its established Marten Falls partnership, while TIH 
and the engineers are also likely beneficiaries. We believe BDT’s expanded 
partnership announced in August warrants some renewed attention, 
particularly as a reminder that it has growth drivers beyond data centres after 
its shares were seemingly caught up in the broader weakness across AI 
“pick-and-shovel” names. The Ring of Fire opportunity spans community 
infrastructure, access roads and eventual mine development, with last week’s 
announcement improving funding visibility for the next stage of activity.  
Funding Confirmation Supports An Existing Pipeline: The October 21 
announcement commits federal funding to match Ontario’s initial investment 
of up to $1B over three years, alongside coordinated permitting and a First 
Nations engagement group. While this commitment was largely expected, 
we view the formal confirmation of funding positively.  
The Broader Road Network: While the announcement does not specify 
how federal funding will be allocated among individual projects, Exhibit 1 
herein highlights the roads for the planned access network connecting 
communities and the Ring of Fire mineral district to Ontario’s highway 
systems. 
BDT’s Opportunity Extends Beyond The Road: The announcement in 
May of the majority indigenous-owned Piinahzii LP established a framework 
for community infrastructure and readiness initiatives, including workforce 
development and local contracting capacity. The August 31 agreement 
expanded that mandate to include construction of the Marten Falls 
Community Access Road. Community infrastructure therefore represents a 
distinct source of work alongside the larger transportation program, while the 
partnership positions BDT to participate in further development within Marten 
Falls’ traditional territory. Neither announcement disclosed a contract value; 
however, third-party project listings estimate the access road’s value at 
~$1.3B. We suspect BDT’s portion of the $1.3B could be reflected in the 
company’s pending backlog for Q3 and we estimate that could be ~$600MM. 
Engineering Opportunities Extend Across The Development Cycle: 
ATRL already had documented involvement through preparation of the 
Webequie Supply Road and environmental assessment/impact statement. 
More broadly, we see opportunities for engineering services spanning 
environmental studies, geotechnical work, detailed design, construction 
oversight and eventual mine and supporting infrastructure development.

Ritchie Bros. "Buy Now" And "Make Offer" Purchasing Options Live Across 
North America 
RBA announced that "Buy Now" and "Make Offer" purchasing options are live across North 
America, adding more than 10,000 additional listings to rbauction.com and giving customers 
a choice of online bidding in unreserved auctions, fixed-price purchase with Buy Now, or 
submitting offers to sellers using the Make Offer format. The company noted that Buy Now 
and Make Offer options give buyers immediate access to more equipment and give sellers 
more flexibility to move equipment and expand liquidity across the Ritchie Bros. marketplace. 
Customers can choose the format that fits how they choose to buy or sell, rather than 
adapting to a single sales format. RBA noted that sellers can sell through unreserved 
auctions, set a fixed price for more price certainty, and accept offers from buyers, with this 
flexibility helping sellers move equipment without waiting for a scheduled auction date. The 
company added that for buyers, equipment is available to buy immediately at any time 
through Buy Now, with the option to name their price through Make Offer. New formats 
launched first in North America, with international rollouts to follow.

Construction Commentary 
U.S. Construction Starts Declined M/M In September 
According to the Dodge Construction Network (Dodge), total U.S. construction starts 
decreased 1.5% M/M in September to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of US$1.33T. Non-
residential building starts declined 6.3% M/M, residential starts edged down 0.3% M/M, and 
nonbuilding construction increased 5.5% M/M. Within non-residential, commercial activity fell 
18.4% M/M, largely due to a sharp decline in hotels after a strong August, as well as 
decreases in office, data centers, and manufacturing, partially offset by gains in parking 
garages. Institutional activity increased 16.3% M/M, driven by strong education and 
miscellaneous projects, while healthcare activity declined. Nonbuilding construction was 
supported by higher activity in highway, bridges, and miscellaneous, while utility pulled back. 
In residential, single family starts declined, but multi-family starts improved. Sarah Martin, 
Director of Economic Research at Dodge, noted that gains in institutional and nonbuilding 
sectors helped cushion the decline in other areas, acting as a counterweight to weaker 
commercial and single family activity. On a YTD basis, total construction starts increased 
14.3% Y/Y, with non-residential activity up 23.7%, non-building up 18.4%, while residential 
activity was down 1.7%. 
ABI Index Declined Sharply In September 
The AIA/Deltek Architecture Billings Index (ABI) declined to 44.7 in September from 47.2 in 
August, marking the largest decline in billings since May. Inquiries for new projects saw a 
modest increase, while value of the newly signed contracts declined after growth during the 
summer. Firm backlogs averaged at 6.2 months at the end of Q3, down from 6.6 months at 
the end of Q1, with the most significant reduction at firms specializing in multifamily 
residential, reaching 4.8 months in Q3 from 6.2 months in Q1. Backlog for large firms held 
steady at 8.1 months, institutional firms at 8.0 months, and smaller firms saw backlog

Nuclear Commentary 
Philippines Advances Plans For Nuclear Energy 
The Philippines is advancing plans to introduce nuclear power into its energy mix, targeting 
1.2 GW of capacity by 2032 and up to 4.8 GW by 2050. The government is prioritizing site 
selection, regulatory readiness, financing arrangements, and technology evaluation, 
positioning nuclear as a complement to renewable energy under its broad energy strategy. 
The Department of Energy is working to establish policy and regulatory frameworks, develop 
procurement processes, and set commercial conditions to make nuclear investments viable, 
following the International Atomic Energy Agency’s milestone approach.  
The government is also focused on developing domestic supply chain and human resource 
capabilities to support a sustainable nuclear program, and is working with international 
partners to build domestic expertise. Philippines’s private sector is also exploring options for 
the SMRs, with the final study from Manila Electric Company for potential sites and 
technology is expected in Q3/27. 
Canada Advances Talks With Türkiye For CANDU 
Last week, Canada's Minister of Energy and Natural Resources, Tim Hodgson, met with 
Türkiye's Minister of Energy and Natural Resources, Alparslan Bayraktar, to discuss the 
potential deployment of Canadian nuclear technology for Türkiye's second planned nuclear 
power plant, highlighting CANDU's strong operating history and Canada's existing end-to-end 
nuclear supply chain. In addition, Prime Minister Mark Carney expressed optimism about 
Canada's prospects of winning the bid for the new nuclear project in Türkiye, noting that 
ATRL's CANDU technology now has a "good chance" of succeeding, compared with what he 
described as a long shot a year ago. While the technology selection process remains 
competitive and no contract has been awarded, we view the commentary positively for ATRL.  
ATRL is currently conducting a feasibility study to assess the potential deployment of CANDU 
technology at the proposed Sinop site in Türkiye. At our Eastern Conference, ATRL indicated 
that it expects a technology selection decision in H1/27. 
Heavy Equipment Commentary 
Used Vehicle Prices Up In The First Half Of October Y/Y 
The mid-month Manheim Used Vehicle Index (seasonally adjusted) increased in the first half 
of October to 207.4, up 0.7% M/M from 205.9 in September, indicating higher wholesale used 
vehicle prices. The figure was up 2.2% versus October 2025. If October ends at 207.4, 
representing a Y/Y increase, it would mark an improvement from September, which was the 
first month in 2026 to record a Y/Y decline amid macroeconomic pressures. 
U.S. Trailer Orders Increase 
U.S. orders for heavy-duty trailers surged in September as fleets looked to have equipment 
secured for the 2027 cycle. Data from industry forecasting firms showed Y/Y and M/M 
growth, supported by pent-up demand for replacement and efforts to get ahead of future 
tariff-associated price hikes. The robust opening to the latest order season points to a change 
in capital spending, with carriers seeking to add capacity while also refreshing capacity. ACT 
Research estimated preliminary September net trailer orders at 42,700 units, up 311% Y/Y
