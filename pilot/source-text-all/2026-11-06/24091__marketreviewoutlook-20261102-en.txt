Review – Fiscal risks add to the list of market concerns 
Global financial markets have become more volatile. Renewed en-
ergy-price pressures and growing fiscal concerns have challenged 
risk appetite in October. The political situation in France added to 
market uncertainty, pushing sovereign risk premiums higher and 
weighed on the euro. Brent crude oil climbed above US-$110 per 
barrel, lifting the broader commodities complex. Government bond 
yields initially continued their upward trend but retreated towards 
the end of the month as investors moved into safe assets. The 10-
year US Treasury yield fell back below 5% after reaching 5.3% ear-
lier in the month.

Equity markets struggled to sustain their earlier momentum. In the 
US, the Q3 earnings season started with mixed results from major 
banks, whose headline profits concealed weaker fixed income 
trading revenues. EM equities represented the laggards in October, 
reflecting the correction in the semiconductor space. Reflecting 
concerns about fiscal sustainability, European High Yield also 
posted a weak performance. The strong dollar and elevated real 
yields remain a challenge for precious metals. 
 
Global Macro – Strong US activity warrants tighter policy 
The US economy continues to show solid growth, supported by AI-
related capital expenditure, resilient consumer spending and solid 
labour market conditions. Investment in computing infrastructure 
has become an increasingly important source of final demand, 
while strong equity markets and associated wealth effects have 
helped cushion the impact of higher interest rates. Manufacturing 
activity also benefits from the investment boom, with signs that its 
positive effects are spreading beyond the technology sector. Yet 
persistent underlying inflation and renewed energy-price pres-
sures suggest that monetary policy is not sufficiently restrictive. 
We therefore expect the Fed to tighten monetary conditions fur-
ther, with a rate hike to 4.25% in December and further rate hikes 
in 2027. 
 
The euro area economy remains more resilient than expected, sup-
ported by improving manufacturing activity, stronger external de-
mand and Germany’s fiscal expansion. Elevated energy prices are 
pushing inflation higher and we expect the ECB to raise its deposit 
rate to 2.75% in December and one more rate hike in early 2027.

stability and the absence of a credible fiscal consolidation pro-
gramme have pushed France’s borrowing cost to new highs. Stabi-

Japan's economy continues to benefit from robust external de-

Strong exports contrast with subdued domestic demand and per-

and private investment remain lacklustre, stronger fiscal execution

Asia, economies closely integrated into global technology supply

Bonds – High uncertainty premia raise yield levels 
Volatility has risen in government bond markets as investors reas-
sessed the outlook for monetary policy and fiscal sustainability. 
Short-term maturities primarily reflect expectations of additional 
central bank tightening, while longer maturities have faced pres-
sure from elevated inflation uncertainty, increased government 
borrowing and higher term premiums. European bond markets 
have been particularly affected by France's fiscal problems, with 
the yield spread between 10-year French and German government 
bonds widening to around 150 basis points. Shorter French matur-
ities already offer more attractive relative value. We continue to fa-
vour intermediate-duration government bonds, where higher start-
ing yields provide attractive carry and roll-down opportunities. Cor-
porate credit spreads have widened from exceptionally tight levels, 
particularly in lower-rated segments, but remain consistent with 
resilient economic activity.

Equities – Moderate gains as AI fears hold markets back 
Global equity markets have struggled to push higher in October as 
higher interest rates and geopolitical uncertainty offset resilient 
corporate earnings. US equities have held up comparatively well, 
despite the weakness of the technology sector. Energy shares have 
benefited from higher oil prices, while financials continued to un-
derperform. Looking ahead, we expect continued AI investment 
and earnings growth to support equities, but the gains should mod-
erate going forward, given diminishing macro support and higher 
discount rates. We continue to favour developed over emerging 
markets, with a regional preference for the US and Switzerland. At 
the sector level, we favour communication services (telecommuni-
cations preferred), consumer staples, and utilities. 
 
Asset Allocation – Slight Adjustment in Bonds 
High nominal growth rates and the positive trend in corporate earn-
ings continue to provide a positive environment for risk assets. Ac-
cordingly, we maintain our slight overweight position in equities for 
the time being. In equities, we continue to favour developed mar-
kets over emerging markets. 
 
Our bond allocation remains slightly underweight. Yet we have 
taken advantage of the recent rise in interest rates to reduce our 
position in emerging market government bonds slightly and in-
crease our position in high-yield bonds accordingly. The latter have 
recently seen a slight widening of credit spreads. Our allocation to 
alternative investments remains unchanged at a slight under-
weight.
