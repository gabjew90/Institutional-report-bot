came away incrementally more positive on the company’s staffing

volumes are nearing an inflection, with reported bill-rate growth of

double-digit declines over the past several quarters. Perm is leading 
the recovery as low unemployment and talent scarcity make it more 
difficult for clients to hire independently, with RHI’s experienced 
recruiter base positioned to capture improving demand. Protiviti

technology consulting demand broadens Protiviti’s recovery beyond 
regulatory work. RHI has also retained sufficient capacity to support 
higher demand and return toward prior peak margins without 
rebuilding its recruiter and consultant base. On AI, RHI has seen no

performance data support a more balanced risk assessment. We

Perm is leading the recovery. Permanent placement is recovering faster than contract 
staffing, supported by low unemployment, talent scarcity and RHI’s experienced 
recruiter base. Permanent placement revenue grew faster than contract staffing in 3Q as 
low unemployment made it more difficult for clients to source qualified employees 
independently. This relative resilience is also evident across cycles: perm declined ~45% 
from peak to trough in the latest staffing downturn compared with ~65% following the 
Dot-Com and Global Financial Crisis cycles, vs ~30-35% for contract staffing. RHI noted 
that low unemployment and post-COVID concerns about talent scarcity have modestly 
shifted client preference toward owning critical talent instead of relying exclusively on 
contractors. RHI is positioned to capture this demand since its most-tenured recruiters 
produce a disproportionate share of perm revenue and remain in place for the recovery. 
Management acknowledged that perm carries greater quarterly volatility, particularly in 
4Q, when year-end bonuses can delay candidate transitions and holiday schedules can 
affect activity. 
No measurable AI displacement to date. RHI has not seen AI-related 
underperformance across its most exposed categories and cited existing automation, 
SMB staffing models and the continued importance of domain expertise as factors 
limiting displacement risk. The company noted that accounting operations, customer 
service, technology support, help desk and PC technician roles have shown no relative 
underperformance attributable to AI, despite concerns that these positions face 
elevated risk. The incremental automation opportunity may also be overstated because 
QuickBooks, Microsoft Dynamics and third-party applications already automate routine 
accounting processes such as invoice scanning, expense coding, purchase-order 
matching and approval workflows. AI is consequently addressing more of the last mile 
than the first, since the remaining work is increasingly centered on exceptions that 
require judgment and domain expertise. RHI’s SMB exposure provides another buffer 
because a 20% productivity improvement in one task does not allow a small company to 
eliminate 20% of an employee who performs several functions. The current transition 
resembles earlier adoption cycles for Excel, bookkeeping software and payroll 
applications, which improved productivity without eliminating demand for accounting 
professionals. RHI remains constructive on domain expertise plus AI, though we believe 
the absence of measurable displacement to date does not eliminate longer-term 
labor-demand risk as adoption broadens and companies redesign workflows.

Margins can recover to prior peaks. RHI has retained the capacity and favorable mix

margins because replacing experienced recruiters and consultants would be costly and 
new hires would require time to reach full productivity. As a result, staffing and recruiter 
capacity is sufficient to support higher demand, and Protiviti’s recent cost actions

capacity. Full-Time Engagement Professionals represents another source of upside, as

the highest contract gross margin within Talent Solutions. RHI has also preserved its 
strong gross margins through several years of wage inflation because pricing is largely a 
pass-through and SMB customers prioritize candidate quality over modest differences in 
hourly rates. 
Data and distribution differentiate RHI. RHI combines proprietary candidate 
outcomes, an established brand and local distribution with AI-enabled matching and 
sales tools, creating advantages that digital-only competitors and general-purpose 
models cannot easily replicate. The company has used vector-based matching for more 
than a decade, allowing its systems to identify semantically similar job titles and skills 
that keyword-frequency searches could miss. As modern LLMs make contextual 
understanding more broadly available, RHI’s proprietary data on actual candidate 
performance, assignment completion and churn become a more important source of 
differentiation than the model itself. Its matching system scores candidates against client 
requirements and separately compares them with historically elite performers in the 
relevant role. The resulting AI score remains visible even when recruiters apply their own 
judgment and filters. RHI uses similar technology in its sales pipeline to compare 
prospects with existing clients, incorporate internal and external activity signals and 
recommend relevant conversation topics. Brand recognition should also matter in an 
answer-engine-optimization environment, and RHI’s local sales force provides a 
distribution advantage because in-person engagement improves win rates with SMB 
clients. 
Dividend protected and buybacks can resume. RHI remains committed to maintaining 
its dividend and returning essentially all FCF to shareholders, with excess cash available

current level because the existing payout already absorbs nearly all of the company’s 
reduced FCF. If FCF exceeds the dividend requirement in 2027, RHI intends to direct the 
excess toward share repurchases. The company noted that cash conversion could 
initially lag the recovery as higher revenue requires additional investment in receivables.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
