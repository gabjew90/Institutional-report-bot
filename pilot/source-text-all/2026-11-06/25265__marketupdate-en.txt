The American people have spoken. The Demo-
crats won a majority in both the House of  
Representatives and the Senate in the US mid-
term elections. In an initial market reaction, the 
USD has lost some ground, stocks are trading 
higher, and US Treasuries are also gaining 
value. We remain constructive on the stock 
markets and are focusing on diversification. 
 
 
The Election Results 
The so-called “blue wave”—named after the party’s color—
has materialized. In the midterm elections, the Democrats not 
only secured a majority in the House of Representatives, as 
expected, but will also hold a narrow majority in the Senate 
going forward. The shift in the Senate majority is attributable 
to the Democrats’ success in winning districts and states even 
in traditionally deep-red, i.e., Republican, regions. 
While ruling parties traditionally face a backlash in midterms, 
the scale of this year's shift in power was historic. The last 
time a sitting president lost control of both chambers of Con-
gress was 20 years ago. The state of Texas, which had been 
in Republican hands for nearly forty years, has switched 
sides. The same is true for Kansas, where voters have over-
whelmingly supported Republicans for nearly 100 years. 
In an initial response, the White House acknowledged the 
Democrats’ victory and expressed a willingness to work con-
structively with them in the future. 
 
The Initial Reaction of the Financial Markets 
In an initial reaction on the financial markets, equities, US-
Treasuries as well as gold are all trading higher. The US-Dol-
lar on the other hand lost some ground. 
Market participants assume that the new political situation 
could slow down various financial market-friendly initiatives of 
the current administration.  
At the same time, greater budget and debt discipline is ex-
pected under the new political landscape, which could support 
the bond markets. 
 
What the New Situation Means Politically 
With both chambers of Congress now under Democratic con-
trol, the White House will face greater resistance. Congress 
will play a larger role and be able to block initiatives—particu-
larly on domestically and economically significant issues such 
as the budget, taxes, and regulation.  
It is also expected that the White House will continue to rely 
on so-called “executive orders”. These are presidential direc-
tives that take effect without congressional approval. It is rea-
sonable to assume that the White House will continue to rely 
on such orders, particularly in the area of trade policy. How-
ever, due to recurring disputes over jurisdiction, these orders 
frequently end up in court and are overturned.  
The President has also already announced that he will use 
vetoes to block congressional initiatives in the future. It is no 
coincidence that the President is referred to as a “lame duck” 
in such a scenario. Many political initiatives are blocked or 
even derailed in such a situation. 
Furthermore, it is likely that the Democrats will attempt to in-
vestigate alleged cases of personal enrichment, government 
contracting, and the handling of conflicts of interest in general. 
On the foreign policy front, various political analysts are rais-
ing the risk of renewed escalation in the Middle East. The 
months following the midterm elections before the new Con-
gress is constituted in January 2027 could be a window of op-
portunity for further military action. 
 
What This Means for the Financial Markets 
Such a situation of political deadlock can be viewed as a posi-
tive sign for the markets. Historically, U.S. equities have often 
generated positive returns in the twelve months following mid-
term elections (see figure 1). This has been observed in all 
midterm elections since World War II. On the one hand, once 
the election results are in, a certain degree of uncertainty 
among investors dissipates. On the other hand, a defeat of 
the ruling party leading to an opposition-controlled Congress 
creates a stalemate in which fewer political experiments are to 
be expected and greater budget discipline prevails. While 
both of these factors are generally viewed positively by the fi-
nancial markets, historical observations do not allow us to 
draw conclusions about future performance. 
 
Currently, market participants also expect that a Democratic-
controlled Congress could lead to a certain slowdown in 
growth, with the Democrats’ greater willingness to regulate 
likely being a key factor. Greater budget discipline—with 
tighter control over government spending and fewer tax 
breaks—could also dampen growth. Conversely, such a po-
tential slowdown would also have a dampening effect on infla-
tion. Overall, the issue of the cost of living remains central,

and the fight against inflation will continue to be high on the 
political agenda. Even with government bonds currently trad-
ing at higher prices—that is, lower interest rates—the money

With greater budget discipline, the issue of a possible “gov-
ernment shutdown”—a partial suspension of government op-

to the fore. Even though financial markets seem to have 
grown accustomed to this recurring situation, such develop-
ments are always accompanied by a certain degree of uncer-

Shifts are also expected at the sector level. It is likely that a 
Democratic-controlled Congress will put regulatory issues—
particularly in the field of artificial intelligence—on the agenda. 
The same applies to the banking and healthcare sectors. 
Furthermore, an attempt to roll back the current administra-
tion’s measures in the healthcare sector is expected. This 
could also be relevant for Swiss pharmaceutical companies. 
The same applies to issues in the areas of environmental and 
energy policy, which in turn are likely to have repercussions 
on artificial intelligence—specifically, data centers. 
These developments could have a particular impact on the 
performance of various market sectors. 
 
What should investors do now? 
Political market movements are short-lived. As mentioned, in 
the past, U.S. stock markets have traded higher twelve 
months after midterm elections, and for now, one source of 
political uncertainty has been resolved. 
Given this outcome of the midterm elections, we continue to 
anticipate a growth-friendly environment with somewhat more 
headwinds. In particular, the issues of increased budgetary 
discipline and a greater propensity for regulation could act as 
brakes on growth. The fight against inflation will also remain a 
key issue, which in particular will tighten refinancing condi-
tions. 
We are maintaining our preference for equities, which we are 
implementing through corresponding positions in emerging 
markets. There, we see compelling growth potential at moder-
ate valuations.  
We also maintain an overweight position in gold. Gold serves 
not only as a diversifier within the portfolio. With inflation de-
clining and interest rates expected to fall again in the medium 
term, the price of the precious metal could recover. 
We finance these two overweight positions through an under-
weight in corporate bonds. Credit spreads in this segment of 
the bond market remain at relatively low levels. 
We also recommend maintaining a high level of diversification 
and selectively focusing on stable sources of income. Both 
measures help stabilize the portfolio during times of height-
ened political and geopolitical uncertainty. 
 
In summary 
The results of the midterm elections are in. As expected, the 
U.S. Democrats have strengthened their position and will now 
control both chambers of Congress—and will be in opposition 
to the Republican administration. 
Historically speaking, at least, such a stalemate is not nega-
tive for the stock markets. Greater budgetary discipline going 
forward is also likely to be viewed favorably in the fixed-in-
come markets. 
Geopolitical risks will likely continue to weigh on investors in 
the coming months, making a diversified portfolio allocation 
essential. In the short term, issues such as the Middle East, 
oil prices, inflation, and the resulting cost of living could re-
main central and dominate the agenda. 
Even with the new political landscape in Washington, we re-
main constructive on financial markets over the coming 
months.
