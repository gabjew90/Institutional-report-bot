The Commodities Feed: False US-Iran
hopes or the real deal?
Oil and gas sold off yesterday amid positive signs for US-Iran talks ahead
of today’s US midterm elections
Energy – Hopes grow for US-Iran talks
Energy markets saw an aggressive pullback yesterday, with ICE Brent settling 7.6% lower and
prices falling back below $105/bbl. The move comes amid new hopes for potential US-Iran
talks. While we’ve seen this before with nothing materialising, positive signals appear to be
coming from both the US and Iran. President Trump responded “no” to a question on whether
he would strike Iran following the midterm elections. Iran’s president said that the government
has been given more authority from the country’s supreme leader to negotiate with the US.
Despite these positive signals, it’s probably too early to think escalation risks have passed. On
Monday, the US carried out strikes on Iran’s Qeshm Island, reportedly in response to Iran firing
at a US aircraft.
What is clear, though, is that strong signals of progress in peace talks would mean more
downside for prices. The market still has a large risk premium baked in, particularly given the
strong oil flows from the Persian Gulf.
European gas prices also came under significant pressure on the back of US-Iran hopes, with
TTF settling 8.7% lower on the day at a little under EUR95/MWh. For the gas market, de-
escalation is crucial, as LNG flows from the region haven’t recovered as much as on the oil side.
This leaves the market vulnerable as it heads deeper into the European heating season.

Metals - Gold climbs back from one-year low
Gold rebounded on Monday after last week’s sharp sell-off, helped by the pullback in oil prices
and bargain hunting at lower levels. The precious metal settled 1.85% higher on the day,
recovering from its lowest level in a year, after signs of easing US-Iran tensions sent crude
sharply lower. The drop in oil eased some of the inflation concerns weighing on gold, providing
additional support.
The recovery follows a difficult period for gold. It's caught up in a broader risk-off move and
heavy selling across technology stocks. Geopolitical risks remain elevated, while expectations
for higher interest rates weigh on sentiment. We expect gold to regain some ground following
its recent correction. But the near-term outlook remains challenging amid expectations for
higher rates and a stronger dollar, limiting the scope for a more sustained recovery.
