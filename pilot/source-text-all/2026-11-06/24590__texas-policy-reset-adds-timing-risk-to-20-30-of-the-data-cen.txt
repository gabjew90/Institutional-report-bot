Our Call
Texas's governor-elect plans to hold data center approvals until the legislature acts, 
adding timing risk to 20-30% of the US pipeline and backlog/production slots. Token 
economics remain attractive. We maintain our barbell: Rental and Waste.
 
The Texas pause likely runs through the 2027 session, and Texas is 20-30% of the US 
pipeline. Hinojosa won on a pledge to hold new data centers until the legislature "can 
meet to put in place real protections." Abbott's ERCOT audit and TCEQ permitting halt
are already gating approvals, and the session runs 1/12-5/31/27. Texas is ~20% of the 
cumulative US pipeline per our EE/MI colleague Steve Tusa and close to 30% of 2028 US 
GW additions per SemiAnalysis.
 
About 3 GW of the ~37 GW in our recip and turbine trackers sits on Texas projects 
now seemingly on hold. As we noted on 10/26, power gen lead times extend into 2030, 
well past ~18 months of permit visibility, and the TCEQ hold covers the air permits on-
site generation and backup gensets need. CMI's 2.0 GW Circe award (deliveries through 
2030) and ~1.1 GW of CAT turbine content (Kilby, Stargate) are exposed; experts at our 
conference said 50%+ of Texas projects could be cancelled.
 
Token economics remain attractive; the debate is which projects move forward. Oracle 
reported 97.9% AI infrastructure utilization and a 20% average renewal/resale premium. 
H100 rental rates are up 26% y/y. GB200 rental pricing is also up 50% YTD and 12% in 
the last month. Equipment demand now depends on which projects clear permitting and 
financing. Data center power names need better project visibility or a backlog reset for a 
more constructive view.
 
Construction rental remains our preferred cyclical exposure amid tight supply, inflecting 
margins off trough, and accelerating semi onshoring. Semi capex is recovering off a ~
$60 bn peak-to-trough decline toward 90%+ growth in 2027E, and power starts (+91% 
LTM) add longer-cycle demand. Supply is tight and margins just turned positive in 3Q. URI, 
which remains on our department's tactical list, and SUNB are our top picks.
 
Environmental Services is the other side of our barbell - idiosyncratic growth at 
historical average valuation. Waste offers growth uncorrelated to data center policy or AI 
credit: the top two operators hold 91% of the average local landfill market, pricing has run 
ahead of inflation since 2018, recycled commodity prices are inflecting, unit profitability is 
growing double digits and valuations sit at around 3-year averages.

Texas Moves From an Administrative Pause to a Legislative One
Texas data center approvals have been frozen by executive directive since August, and the election 
converts that pause from one tied to an audit into one tied to legislation. On August 3 the governor 
directed the PUCT and ERCOT to audit every data center in ERCOT's interconnection process and 
required that the audit be completed before any project moves forward. On September 21 he directed 
TCEQ to halt all permits sought by data centers until the audit is complete. Unlike the August order, 
the September directive reaches projects with behind-the-meter generation, projects below 75 MW 
and projects outside ERCOT. ERCOT is targeting a December 10 audit filing, but that filing does not by 
itself reopen permitting.
The governor-elect has said the pause should last until lawmakers act. Gina Hinojosa pledged 
an immediate moratorium on new data centers until the Legislature "can meet to put in place 
real protections," alongside a directive to the PUC to freeze residential electricity rates. The 90th 
Legislature convenes January 12 and its regular session ends May 31, 2027. SemiAnalysis estimates 
that a freeze lasting through the session would run about eight months from September, and that 
waiting for new rules could extend it close to a year, though a deal or special session could come 
sooner. In NBC's exit polling, 75% of Texas voters opposed a new data center in their community. We 
flagged election headline risk in our 9/8/26 Trading Update, AI Election Headline Risk: Peak or Mid-Cycle 
Pause?

Texas Is 20-30% of the US Data Center Pipeline
Texas is the largest single market in every dataset we use, and the near-term measure carries the 
higher share. Our EE/MI colleague Steve Tusa's pipeline tracker puts Texas at ~81 GW, or ~20%, of the 
cumulative adjusted US pipeline, more than twice Georgia or Virginia. SemiAnalysis has Texas adding 
close to 30% of US GW in 2028. The upper end of the range applies to 2028 additions, which is the 
window where power equipment orders sit today. ERCOT's large-load queue stood at ~474 GW in 
August, more than five times its record peak, with ~90% from data centers.
Exhibit 2 - Texas share of the US data center pipeline
Much of the Texas queue was never going to be built, and the policy reset raises the bar for what 
gets through. Panelists at our Data Center conference said more than half of Texas projects could be 
cancelled. For equipment suppliers the relevant population is the funded, permitted portion of the 
pipeline, and the policy reset now delays even that portion.
About 3 GW of Our Tracked Power Projects Sit in Texas
Power is where the delay bites, because lead times run past the permit window and the Texas 
hold sits on the permits on-site power requires. As we highlighted on 10/26, power gen equipment 
lead times have extended well past the ~18 months of visibility air permits provide, since permits 
generally have to be filed within 18 months of construction, and lead times for both CAT turbines and 
recips extend into 2030. An OEM slot for 2029-30 delivery is therefore matched to a later phase of 
a campus that may not yet hold its state permits. If that phase slips, the slot is deferred, lengthening 
backlog conversion, or resequenced to another customer. Backup diesel gensets need TCEQ air 
authorization and ship ahead of energization, and on-site prime generation, the industry's route 
around the grid queue, is now caught by the September directive as well.
Across the ~37 GW in our recip and CAT AI turbine trackers, about 3 GW of CAT- and CMI-supplied 
capacity sits on Texas projects. CMI's entire 2.0 GW tracked prime position is its Circe Energy award 
for a behind-the-meter West Texas HPC campus, with HSK78 and QSK60 deliveries scheduled from 
2026 through 2030. In our CAT AI Turbine Tracker, at least ~1.1 GW and ~$1.2 bn of estimated CAT 
turbine content sits on Texas projects awaiting state action, led by Microsoft and Chevron's Project 
Kilby in Reeves County and Stargate's Project Ludicrous in Abilene. Outside our coverage, INNIO's 
VoltaGrid orders add 1.5 GW in Shackelford County. On standby, Texas is 12% of the permitted diesel 
recip power in our six-state sample. Several tracker entries are undisclosed, so these figures are floors.
We track 50+ GW of recip and turbine permits. Our checks indicate lead times for some reciprocating 
engine products are now down to 3.5 years from 4 years in August. Our supply-side framework for 
Machinery was focused on permits and lead times over the past two years as data center demand 
inflected and accelerated. Our work centered on physical constraints — machinery supply, permits, 
interconnect queues, lead times — and we treated financing as available. That was the wrong 
assumption. Since September we have tracked CDS, which suggest rising risk.

Token Economics Remain Attractive; The Variable Is Which Projects Move 
Forward
Nothing in the Texas outcome changes the return on compute that is already running. Oracle 
reported 97.9% AI infrastructure utilization in its August quarter, and said GPUs coming up for renewal 
were renewed or resold at a 20% average premium to prior contracts, with most of that cohort four 
years or older. SpaceX is renting compute primarily short-term at prices generating payback in under a 
year, and median H100 on-demand rental rates are up 26% over twelve months.
The question for equipment demand is whether a given project clears permitting and financing on 
time. That is a project-selection problem, and may be impacting credit as well, as outlined in our 10/26 
note. Texas now adds a permitting constraint on the same window.

We need either improved project visibility or a backlog reset before turning more constructive on

Barbell, Part 1: Construction Rental Remains Our Preferred Cyclical 
Exposure
We view construction rental as our core cyclical holding, with a more limited AI project 
resequencing link. First, the non-res inflection comes with visibility: our Non-Residential Construction 
Tracker points to private non-res spending moving from -1.7% in 2025 and -1.5% in 2026E to +9.5% 
in 2027E and +10.4% in 2028E, with data center starts ~140% ahead of LTM capex and the mid-2025 
surge in power starts pointing to strong 2027 capex. Second, semi capex is recovering off a trough: 
despite two years of robust data center capex, private non-res is down ~$25 bn on a ~$60 bn peak-
to-trough decline in semis and electronics, consensus semi capex was revised 10% higher during 3Q, 
and semi capex plans call for 90%+ growth in 2027E, including TSMC's consideration of additional 
expansion in Texas. Third, power adds longer-cycle demand, with power starts up 91% LTM and our 
utilities colleague Shar Pourreza forecasting a mid-teens 2025-27 utility capex CAGR on new baseload 
generation and transmission. Data center construction starts are more than 2x vs. construction 
spending, pointing to visible construction through 2027E, in our view.
Supply is tight, pricing has room to run and margins have turned. Per our Machinery Supply Tracker,
YTD inventories of used aerials are down 9% (seasonally adjusted) and excavators 18%, and used 
earthmoving supply was down 14% y/y in September. In prior upcycles, construction-season pricing 
realizations averaged 50-100 bps per month, implying potential for mid-single-digit plus industry 
pricing growth in 2027E, and margins just turned positive in 3Q. Top picks URI (tactical list) & SUNB.
YTD inventories of used aerials are down 9% (seasonally adjusted) and excavators -18%. Continued 
tightening in used equipment supply, combined with improving non-resi leading indicators, is 
supporting industry time utilization in 3Q. In our cross-sector non-res tracker, we note 40% of private 
non-res verticals are set to drive near double-digit growth in US private non-residential construction.
As a result, we see meaningful pricing upside.

Valuation: CAT’s Premium Has Unwound; CMI Continues to Trade at a Substantial Discount
The technology credit sell-off has shifted the debate from power-demand growth to the financing and timing of that demand.
CoreWeave’s sharper debt repricing has highlighted rising AI infrastructure funding risk. Five-year CDS spreads have widened to ~700 bps 
at CoreWeave and ~150 bps at Oracle, with Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Microsoft also moving wider. We believe the valuation pressure 
reflects greater uncertainty around the timing of future power earnings. Tighter financing conditions could delay project development and 
conversion, even if underlying power demand remains intact.
CAT’s de-rating has largely eliminated its premium to broader industrials. Since Sep CAT’s NTM P/E has declined from 26x to 20x, 
versus 22x to 21x for our industrial composite. CAT has consequently moved from a roughly 20% premium to a 5% discount, a 25-point 
compression in relative valuation. Our view is that the market has removed much of the premium for future power growth before a 
corresponding softening has appeared in near-term estimates.
CMI trades at a large discount to broader industrials. CMI trades at 14.7x NTM EPS and at a roughly 30% discount to the industrial 
composite versus 27% previously. CMI entered the sell-off already discounted. We see more scope for CMI to earn a higher relative
