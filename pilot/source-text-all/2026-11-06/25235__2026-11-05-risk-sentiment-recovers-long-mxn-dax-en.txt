Lower geopolitical and trade uncertainty. Global stocks have seen a sharp reversal in fortunes 
after Trump's signalling that he is willing to work with the Democrats. This should help allay 
concerns over political confrontation or prevent a continuation of the mercurial policymaking 
that has characterized his second Presidency so far. And that, in turn, should lower geopolitical 
and trade uncertainty and boost the attractiveness of international stocks, in particular.  
Long DAX, short Staples & Utilities. In this new environment, we like international laggards such 
as the DAX vs Consumer Staples and Utilities. Staples have already pushed to new all-time highs 
even though earnings trends remain poor (i.e., forward earnings growth rates are falling, and 
revisions ratios are negative). For Utilities, the prospect of slower data-centre construction in the 
US could curb AI electricity demand and weigh on revenue forecasts for the sector. We add a 
long on DAX vs US Staples & Utilities to our portfolio (long DAX vs short XLP & XLU).

Reduced chance of prolonged escalation in Iran. As regards Iran, even if the war switches back 
to a kinetic phase, our political strategist Christopher Granville reckons the risk will eventually tilt 
towards de-escalation. The underlying logic is that the conflict has been stuck in a pattern of 
mutual siege with periodic flare-ups, while incentives such as munitions shortages and casualty 
intolerance should push Trump to curb any renewed air-strike campaign and toggle back 
towards negotiation. The midterm election results further reduce the chances of renewed 
escalation lasting very long (among other things, owing to heightened electoral pressure and 
Trump potentially seeking to rebuild economic credibility with voters). Moreover, the elevation in 
prices seems to be mostly a function of risk premia (which can decline further) as flows from 
the Persian Gulf remain close to pre-war levels.
