UL Solutions reported strong 3Q results, with revenue, EBITDA 
margins and EPS outperforming our estimates and consensus and 
the full year organic revenue growth guide raised. The results 
increase our confidence that ULS can sustain at least 
mid-single-digit organic growth into 2027, supported by volume-led 
certification demand and order growth outpacing revenue growth 
across energy transition, electrification and materials. Industrial 
should remain the primary growth driver, with data centers creating 
a multiyear opportunity as successive product generations require 
additional testing across power distribution, power conversion and 
liquid cooling and subsequently support ongoing certification 
services. We also expect Consumer growth to benefit from the 
recent Eurofins E&E acquisition, which expands geographic reach 
and laboratory capacity and creates cross-selling opportunities. 
Operating leverage, productivity, AI-enabled processes, improved 
laboratory utilization and value-based pricing serve as drivers of 
sustained EBITDA margin expansion, in our view. That said, muted 
Advisory trends could limit Industrial growth, and depressed Risk & 
Compliance Software margins and elevated E&E integration costs 
will likely constrain margin expansion in 2027. We believe current 
valuations balance ULS’ durable organic revenue growth and margin 
expansion potential with near-term integration costs, Advisory 
headwinds and depressed Risk & Compliance Software margins.

3Q 2026 Positives / Negatives 
Positives:  
Volume-led certification demand supports sustained organic growth. ULS’ 3Q 
n
revenue growth was driven slightly more by volume than price, with certification 
testing growth particularly volume-led. Industrial organic growth was ~9% y/y ex 
Advisory, and order growth is outpacing revenue growth in businesses exposed to 
energy transition, electrification and materials, supporting ULS’ revenue pipeline and 
visibility into 2027. 
Data centers provide a multiyear testing and recurring revenue opportunity. 
n
Certification testing revenue increased nearly 11% y/y, which ULS identified as the 
best proxy for data center activity, driven primarily by volume. The company expects 
continued innovation across power distribution, power conversion and liquid 
cooling, with successive upgrades to servers and chipsets requiring corresponding 
changes to cooling, thermal management and power infrastructure. Each product 
generation can drive additional certification testing and subsequently support 
ongoing certification services, extending the revenue opportunity beyond the initial 
data center buildout. 
Consumer combines resilient underlying growth with Eurofins E&E upside. 
n
Consumer organic revenue increased 5.9% y/y despite a ~150 bps headwind from 
planned business exits, supported by consumer technology, retail and both 
certification and non-certification services. Excluding these exits, underlying growth 
would have been ~7.4% y/y. The Eurofins E&E acquisition, which closed on October 
1, should contribute ~$50mn of revenue in 4Q and adds laboratory and engineering 
capabilities in Europe and Asia, particularly Korea. The acquisition broadens 
customer relationships, supports cross-selling of ULS’ broader service portfolio and 
improves laboratory utilization, with go-to-market and lab utilization synergies 
largely expected by the company by the end of 2028. 
Record Industrial and Consumer margins have further expansion runway. 
n
Industrial EBITDA margins expanded 150 bps y/y to a record 36.1%, with Consumer 
margins expanding 170 bps to a record 21.4%, reflecting employee productivity, 
operating leverage, favorable mix and restructuring benefits. ULS has captured most 
of the anticipated benefits from its efficiency program, with the remaining 
headcount actions expected by the company to be completed by the end of 1Q 
2027. The company sees additional margin opportunities over time from AI-enabled 
processes, improved laboratory utilization, portfolio optimization and value-based

Valuation and Risks 
 
 
We raise our 12-month price target from $86 to $88 based on 16.75x (from 17.50x 
prior) our rolled forward NTM +1YR EBITDA estimate of $1,077mn, NTM net debt of 
$137mn and NTM shares outstanding of 204mn. We lower our target multiple to reflect 
reduced margin visibility over the near-term due to evolving Risk & Compliance 
profitability and E&E integration costs. Our target multiple comes at a premium to the 
European TIC peer group median of 12.5x reflecting robust Industrial growth trends and 
improving efficiencies, partially mitigated by its lower FCF conversion from EBITDA 
compared to peers. 
Key risks to the downside include macro and geopolitical uncertainty, premium pricing 
relative to competitors, elevated investments into growth initiatives that could limit the 
pace of future margin expansion and FX. Key risks to the upside include an acceleration 
in the rate of product innovation, effective returns on the company’s recent laboratory 
and testing site investments, regulatory actions that impose more stringent safety 
standards, M&A activity and efficiency gains at testing facilities.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
