Rates Spark: A blue wave in the US and
euro inflation
A decisive election result supports immediate sentiment, but the
prospect of tighter AI regulation could add to jitters about the tech
sector. A president now potentially focusing more on foreign policy
leaves oil and tariff risks in focus as sources of rate volatility. Eurozone
headline inflation is expected to rise to 4%
Data centres were a key issue in the midterm elections
Decisive victory helps stability, but still plenty of uncertainty
ahead
The baseline case was a Democrat win, but the scale of the victory will give markets plenty to
digest. A decisive victory helps ensure a smooth process, as there is little risk of the result being
challenged. Trump is accepting the result, and in January we should have a new Congress
taking office. We also avoid a gridlock situation with Congress split between Democrats and
Republicans. These factors of relative stability should all help market sentiment.
On the other hand, we should also place the Democrats' victory in the context of the current AI
jitters in equity markets. Data centres have been a big topic in these elections, with the
Democrats taking a firmer stance against the free market approach given to the AI sector by

start forming a headwind. Probably not something that will materialise in big changes in the
near term, but definitely a trend to keep an eye on.
With Trump still in charge of foreign policy, inflation remains a bit of a wild card. The war with
Iran is not over, and oil barely budged on the election results. Tariffs could make a return as
one of Trump’s favourite policy measures. Markets are sensitive to any inflation surprises in the
data, and if another vector of inflation uncertainty were to be added, we could easily see rates
rise further. Implied volatility measures suggest we should prepare for significant rate moves
over the coming months.
Wednesday’s events and market view
The data highlight for the day in the eurozone is the flash CPI print for October. With
most countries having reported their numbers already, headline inflation is seen rising
to 4%. The core rate is expected to remain steady at 2.5%. The figures still point to
another rate hike from the ECB in December. The ECB will publish its wage tracker and
chief economist Lane, as well as France’s Moulin, will speak.
The US will publish the ISM services, seen coming in only marginally softer at 54.8.
Ahead of Friday’s official US jobs data, markets will also eye the ADP employment
change numbers. The trade balance and import/exports data round off the releases for
the day.
In primary markets, Germany auctions 15y and 20y Bunds for a total of €2bn. The US
Treasury makes its quarterly refunding announcement, where no changes to coupon
issuance are expected.
