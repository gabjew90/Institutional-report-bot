The violent reversal
Chinese equities have gone from panic to a violent squeeze. ChiNext has bounced 5%, while STAR 50
has surged 10%. Extreme oversold conditions, Beijing's policy support, massive ETF inflows and
resilient tech earnings are creating an intriguing setup. Is this just another dead-cat bounce, or the
beginning of something much bigger?
From panic to squeeze
Chinese equities have staged an aggressive rebound overnight following a brutal selloff. JPMorgan
highlights that the CSI 300 had fallen 22% from its June peak, while the CSI 500 and CSI 1000 had
plunged 31%. Meanwhile, crowding and momentum indicators were approaching depressed levels,
creating the conditions for a potentially violent reversal. The violent rebound is underway, but with
ChiNext still trading well below its 200-day moving average, the technical damage remains substantial.

Star struck
STAR 50, China's innovation index, surged 10% overnight. An extraordinary move, but zoom out and
the scale of the preceding collapse becomes obvious. The bigger the washout, the more violent the
squeeze.

Beijing's put is back
The timing is interesting. JPMorgan highlights two regulatory meetings on November 2 aimed at
stabilizing Chinese equities, including efforts to expand capital-market support tools and improve stock
buyback financing. There are also signs of government-backed buying returning to the market.
JPMorgan sees an emerging policy floor, although sustained inflows are still needed to confirm a
durable bottom. After a 20–30% correction, the policy put is becoming increasingly relevant.

The buying machine
Despite the brutal equity correction, money has been flowing into Chinese ETFs. JPMorgan highlights
RMB 379bn of A-share ETF inflows in October, including RMB 156bn into CSI 300 trackers, RMB 58bn
into ChiNext and RMB 54bn into STAR ETFs. This follows RMB 471bn of inflows in Q3. The combination
of aggressive ETF buying, a government-backed policy floor and deeply oversold markets provides an
interesting backdrop for the latest squeeze. The question is whether these flows can become sustained
buying pressure.

China's oversold tech
The washout in Chinese AI has reached extreme levels. JPMorgan's screen identifies several AI
infrastructure stocks with 14-day RSI readings below 20, including AMEC at just 14.3. Meanwhile,
semiconductor and technology hardware shares had fallen approximately 26% over the preceding
four weeks, with momentum participation deteriorating sharply. The setup was becoming increasingly
stretched. When positioning and momentum are this depressed, even modest improvements in
sentiment can generate powerful rebounds.

AI valuations reset
The selloff has materially improved the risk/reward in China's AI supply chain. JPMorgan highlights that
memory, server/switch manufacturers and semiconductor equipment stocks have moved back toward
more reasonable historical valuations. Importantly, the bank argues that China's domestic AI supply
chain benefits from government-backed investment priorities, providing greater visibility on future
demand. With valuations reset and the market rebounding aggressively, the next question is whether
buyers begin looking beyond the immediate squeeze.

JPM
Price panic, earnings boom
The disconnect in Chinese tech is becoming extraordinary. JPMorgan highlights that Q3 earnings in
China's information technology sector surged 55% year-on-year, with healthcare profits jumping 49%.
Meanwhile, the broader CSI 300 recorded a 4% earnings decline. The market has been aggressively
dumping some of the very sectors delivering the strongest earnings growth. If earnings remain
resilient, the market may have priced in a downturn that never arrives. That's where the real upside
asymmetry lies.
