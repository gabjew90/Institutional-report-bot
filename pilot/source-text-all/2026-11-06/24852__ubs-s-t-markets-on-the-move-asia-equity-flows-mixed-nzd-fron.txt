Cash Equity:
Japan – Semiconductor and electronic components (Taiyo Yuden -12%, Kioxia -12%, Murata -10%) are
leading the selloff as early US midterm results point to a Democratic sweep, triggering a sharp unwind of the
Trump/AI trade. Flows are 62% better to buy, with the desk buying in retail, multilayer ceramic capacitors, and
electric power companies vs. selling in semiconductor-production equipment related names. (Sarath Kutty)
Hong Kong/China – Flows are 52% to buy, with 3% in shorts. The desk is buying healthcare, consumer, and
technology vs. selling financials. (Max Fung)
Taiwan – Flows are 59% sellers as TAIEX turned early gains into steep losses of over 1300 points, now
testing 42K, with US midterm election results so far pointing to prospects of a Democrat sweep. AI continued
to sell off while margin calls remain as a risk with many below the 130% threshold: Delta (2308, -5.9%),
Chroma (2360, -8.4%), TUC (6274, -2.8%), Wiwynn (6669, -2.5%), etc. The desk is better selling
semiconductor bluechips and offloading copper-clad laminates vs. buying AI hardware/panels. (Jessica Wu)
Korea – Foreign cash selling is picking up again, with net outflows of $1.6 bn, as mostly retail investors take
the other side. National Pension has sold only $52 mn so far. Nasdaq futures are dribbling lower, down 2.2%,
with US midterms looking like a Democrat sweep, taking down the region, led by the pro-Trump sectors like AI.
(Hyuntai Lew)
ASEAN – Flows are 59% better to sell, most active in Singapore. Indonesia is reducing exposure to basic
materials and consumer. Financials have two-way flows. Industrials are supported in Malaysia and the
Philippines, while utilities have buyers in Malaysia and Thailand. (Kenneth Sui)
India – UBS pad is buy skewed, driven by inflows into fast-moving consumer goods, capital markets,
healthcare, banks, two-wheelers, and telecoms while the desk is selling retail. (Bhoomi Yagnik)
Australia – Flows are 59% sell skewed, most concentrated in industrials, utilities, and basic materials.
(Samuel Collins)
FX:
New Zealand's weak jobs data make the Nov. 7 election more interesting. The weight has been squeezing
higher over the past week, from the 60bp handle to over 70bp of gap move priced in. NZD risk reversals are
likely to be better bid for puts and more hikes will get priced out should Labour win as an employment objective
gets added back to the RBNZ mandate. That said, front-end supply has been emerging, with 1w1m NZD and
1w1m AUDNZD looking stretched. (Andrew Chok)
