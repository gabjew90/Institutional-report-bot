DeskTalk - Oil sinks as Iran tone eases
By Varshi Karamsetty
Sharp knee-jerk move lower in oil prices as we see a slew of messaging from Washington on Iran. Support for de-escalation
is turning bipartisan, which is supportive of further correction. Brent is now at levels last seen early Oct. 
Oil prices have slid nearly -9% with Brent quickly breaking below $100 and $95 after US VP Vance said that voter frustration
over the war in Iran and the US-Israel relationship was the reason for the Democrats' landslide victory at US midterms. "The
president himself said so last night,". 
Senate Minority Leader (and incoming Majority Leader) Chuck Schumer added further fuel to the de-escalation narrative,
saying the Iran war "must end" and noting that "some Republicans have joined us." The latter is notable, as it points to
emerging bipartisan support. While geopolitical policy remains within the executive branch and President Trump retains veto
authority, a two-thirds Senate majority, with Republican backing, could give Democrats a path to constrain or pause military
action legislatively.
We just held a client call where our macro strategist Jim McCormick highlighted that with the midterms results, the probability
of de-escalation has gone up. Vance's tweet was posted during our live call, but it remains supportive of this view. Receiving
front-end EUR is Jim's favorite expression; for the brave, selling front-end European inflation is also good, but this is purely a
long/short gas trade. Our energy strategist added that while the short-term outlook could remain choppy until official
confirmation/deal-making, the broader term outlook remains bearish for energy markets. Catch the replay here.
Overall, lots of noise and ultimately we keep an eye on further headlines, especially from Trump. As for broader asset classes,
treasuries return to overnight level highs and the equity rally is stalling. Gold continues to gain, now ~3.5% higher. A
meaningful de-escalation would likely see markets move beyond geopolitics and refocus on fundamentals. We discussed
further implications in US Midterms: Cross Asset First Take (REPLAY).
