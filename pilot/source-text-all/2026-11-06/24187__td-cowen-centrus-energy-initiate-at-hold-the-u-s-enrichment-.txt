THE TD COWEN INSIGHT
We initiate coverage of LEU with a Hold rating and $120 target. Centrus is a unique public
company building out commercial uranium enrichment in the U.S. The effort satisfies a
national and energy security need in a market that is seeing constrained supply and rising
prices. However, capital costs appear high, competitors are state owned, and significant
capacity expansions have been announced.
■Centrus is the only publicly traded pure play uranium enrichment company with established
technology pursuing commercial capacity expansion today. With growing commercial
nuclear power demand, limited enrichment capacity growth, and forbiddance of Russian
supply in certain markets, enrichment services are in short supply. As a result, pricing has
been moving higher. Additionally, the introduction of advanced reactors requiring higher
enriched fuels and a U.S. desire to reestablish domestic enrichment capabilities creates an
opportunity for Centrus to deploy capital.
■Following a successful demonstration of High Assay Low Enriched Uranium (HALEU) for
the DOE and the accumulation of a contingent backlog for enrichment services, Centrus is
pursuing a multi-billion dollar project to stand up enrichment capacity at its Piketon, OH site
with first volume expected in 2029.
■While the backdrop appears favorable and Centrus has strong liquidity, total capital
required to build out capacity could approach $11B, which seems to require significantly
higher enrichment prices to justify acceptable returns. Furthermore, with a global industry
dominated by state-owned enterprises, we worry competitors may not behave rationally.
■Along with our initiation, we have also published a deep dive report on the nuclear fuel
cycle, including enrichment. The report includes analysis of planned enrichment capacity
expansions and corresponding costs. We count a total of ~14MM SWU of gross capacity
expansion in the US and Europe, equivalent to ~60% of regional supply.
■Our $120 DCF-based target price assumes an 11.5% WACC. While our model already
assumes lower costs for Centrus to build out enrichment capacity and selling prices above
current levels, it is possible both are conservative, which could drive upside to our target.
We also do not give any credit for enrichment to support national security missions, which
could drive upside. Furthermore, Centrus recently completed an acquisition to support their
enrichment supply chain, although the business operates in other industrial markets that
could provide opportunities for growth outside enrichment.

Company Description
Centrus Energy supplies nuclear fuel and enrichment services to commercial utilities and the
U.S. government. The company's LEU segment sells enrichment services and low-enriched
uranium to utilities, historically sourced largely from third-party suppliers including Russia's
TENEX. The Technical Solutions segment provides engineering, manufacturing and operations
services. Centrus operates the American Centrifuge Plant in Piketon, Ohio, the only licensed
HALEU production facility in the U.S. Centrus is working on expanding enrichment capacity to
3.5MM SWU, with the first new capacity expected by 2029. The company is headquartered in
Bethesda, Maryland and was formerly known as USEC.

Background 
Centrus Energy traces its roots to the U.S. government's uranium enrichment 
enterprise. The company was created as the United States Enrichment Corporation 
(USEC) in 1992 to commercialize the government's enrichment activities and was 
privatized through an IPO in 1998. For many years, USEC's business centered on 
supplying enriched uranium to nuclear utilities, including material purchased under 
the U.S.-Russia "Megatons to Megawatts" program, which downblended Russian 
weapons-grade uranium for use as reactor fuel. As the company's aging gaseous 
diffusion plants became increasingly uneconomic, USEC sought to transition to 
modern gas centrifuge technology through the American Centrifuge project. In 2013, 
under a cost-shared program with the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), USEC 
successfully constructed and operated a 120-machine demonstration cascade at 
Piketon, Ohio. The program achieved key technical milestones validating the 
underlying centrifuge technology and establishing an important technical foundation 
for the company's future enrichment efforts. 
Despite achieving important technical milestones with the American Centrifuge 
demonstration cascade in 2013, USEC was unable to secure the financing and long-
term government support needed to construct the multi-billion-dollar commercial 
enrichment plant originally envisioned for Piketon, Ohio. The demonstration cascade 
was ultimately decommissioned and the equipment shipped offsite for disposition. 
DOE continued to support technology demonstration activities, but not deployment 
of a full-scale commercial facility. At the same time, global enrichment markets 
weakened following Fukushima, enrichment prices remained depressed, and USEC's 
legacy enrichment business was facing structural headwinds. These factors, 
combined with a highly leveraged balance sheet, contributed to Chapter 11 
restructuring in 2014. The company emerged as Centrus Energy Corp., with over $3 
billion of debt eliminated and a strategy centered on two businesses: (1) supplying 
enriched uranium to utilities through long-term purchase-and-sale contracts, and (2) 
preserving and advancing American centrifuge technology for future national 
security and commercial applications.  
Rather than owning large-scale commercial enrichment capacity, Centrus evolved 
into what is effectively a nuclear fuel broker and marketer, sourcing enrichment 
capacity from global suppliers and selling fuel products to utilities. This model 
required far less capital than building enrichment facilities while allowing the 
company to leverage decades of customer relationships and fuel-cycle expertise. 
More recently, the U.S. government's desire to rebuild domestic enrichment 
capability has shifted the strategic focus back toward owning enrichment capacity.

Enrichment 101 
For a more in-depth discussion see our accompanying industry report Heavy Metal: A 
Deep Dive on The Nuclear Fuel Cycle – Ahead of the Curve. 
Most nuclear power relies on enriched uranium fuel, where the concentration of the 
fissile U-235 isotope is increased above its natural level of 0.7%. Most existing light-
water reactors (LWRs) use Low Enriched Uranium (LEU) containing 3-5% U-235, 
while many advanced reactor designs are expected to require High Assay Low 
Enriched Uranium (HALEU), containing between 5% and 20% U-235.  
The nuclear fuel cycle begins with uranium ore (U₃O₈), which is mined and milled into 
yellowcake, converted into uranium hexafluoride (UF₆), enriched, and then fabricated 
into reactor fuel for use in a reactor. 
Today, nearly all commercial enrichment is performed using gas centrifuges, where 
UF₆ gas is spun at high speeds to separate the lighter U-235 isotope from the heavier 
U-238 isotope. Gas centrifuge technology has largely replaced the older gaseous 
diffusion process because it consumes dramatically less electricity and offers lower 
operating costs. An alternative technology, laser enrichment, is also under 
development. Rather than physically separating isotopes with centrifuges, laser 
systems selectively excite or ionize U-235 atoms, potentially reducing energy 
consumption and facility footprint, although further technology readiness is needed.  
Because the same enrichment technologies can be used to produce both commercial 
reactor fuel and weapons-grade material, enrichment remains one of the most tightly 
regulated segments of the nuclear fuel cycle.  
Enrichment output is measured in Separative Work Units (SWU), which quantify the 
effort required to separate U-235 from U-238. SWU requirements increase rapidly as 
product assay rises, causing HALEU to require substantially more enrichment work 
and natural uranium feed than conventional LEU. In addition, HALEU will have higher 
fabrication and handling costs due to its higher enrichment level. 
Global enrichment demand is currently approximately 54 million SWU per year, 
compared with nameplate enrichment capacity of roughly 64.5 million SWU, 
controlled primarily by state owned enterprises Rosatom, Urenco, Orano, and CNNC. 
While global capacity exceeds demand on paper, approximately 43% of worldwide 
enrichment capacity resides in Russia, creating significant geopolitical and supply-
chain risks.  
Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, the United States enacted the Prohibiting 
Russian Uranium Imports Act, which will eliminate Russian enrichment imports after 
2027 absent further legislative action. Historically, Russia supplied roughly 3.5 million 
SWU annually, or about 25% of U.S. enrichment requirements, while domestic 
enrichment capacity currently satisfies only a minority of U.S. demand. As a result, 
concerns over future Russian supply availability, combined with rising nuclear power 
demand and anticipated HALEU requirements for advanced reactors, have tightened 
the non-Russian enrichment market, driven higher SWU prices, and accelerated 
investment in domestic conversion and enrichment capacity.

Business Overview 
Centrus currently reports in two segments, the LEU segment, and the Technical 
Solutions segment. The LEU segment currently accounts for ~75% revenue and 
almost all profit, reflecting the company's LEU brokerage business. The brokerage 
business primarily involves two supply contracts from uranium enrichment 
companies from which Centrus sells to Utility customers. These supply contracts 
extend through 2028 and 2030. The Technical Solutions segment currently includes 
HALEU production as part of a DOE contract, but over time will include LEU and other 
enrichment for national security. Eventually the contracts in the brokerage business 
will expire and Centrus will transition to becoming an integrated enrichment 
company.

LEU Segment 
The LEU segment currently generates ~75% of company revenue and nearly all the 
company's profit. The business primarily consists of sales of enrichment services to 
utilities for low enriched uranium (LEU) that are met by two supply contracts between 
Centrus and enrichment companies TENEX and Orano which run through 2028 and 
2030, respectively. TENEX is a subsidiary of Russian state-owned Rosatom. Orano is a 
multi-national fuel cycle corporation, 90% of which is owned by the Government of 
France.  
Centrus has disclosed the TENEX contract accounts for well over one-half of the LEU 
they expect to deliver to customers through 2027. Since the TENEX contract was 
signed in 2011 when SWU pricing was at a cyclical high, the contract contained a 
one-time market-related price reset which occurred in 2019 and will continue through 
the duration of the contract. The more recent increase in SWU pricing has left 
Centrus in an advantageous cost position. The Orano contract is said to have 
significant flexibility to adjust purchase volumes, subject to annual minimums and 
maximums, and has a market-related price formula with floors and ceilings.

Technical Solutions Segment 
Technical solutions accounts for ~25% of current revenue and generates minimal 
profit. The division includes enrichment activities, which thus far has involved the 
DOE HALEU demonstration program and will eventually include commercial 
enrichment. Along with 4Q25 earnings, Centrus also announced notification by the 
National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) of intent to sole source certain 
uranium enrichment activities from Centrus, which would also be included in 
Technical Solutions. Enriched uranium for national security purposes must come from 
an unobligated supply chain, meaning enrichment equipment and the services 
provided must all be domestically sourced. Centrus states they have the only viable 
production-ready technology that can meet national security needs.  
We note that BWX Technologies was awarded a $1.5B contract by the NNSA to 
develop the Domestic Uranium Enrichment Centrifuge Experiment (DUECE) pilot 
plant. The release states " The DUECE pilot plant is required to reach key 
technological readiness milestones in support of NNSA’s strategy to establish a 
reliable and economic supply of unobligated enriched uranium for defense purposes, 
specifically low-enriched uranium (LEU) for tritium production, and highly enriched 
uranium (HEU) for naval nuclear propulsion." 
Enrichment Capacity Plans 
Since 2022 Centrus has gone from being largely focused on running a brokerage 
business for uranium enrichment services to pursuing a multi-billion-dollar expansion 
into enrichment capacity at their American Centrifuge Plant in Piketon, OH.  
The transformation began with a DOE demonstration project where Centrus 
ultimately produced ~2 tonnes of HALEU. During that demonstration and over the 
past several quarters, Centrus has added contingent commercial backlog, been 
awarded task orders from the DOE and raised capital supporting its plan to have 
commercial enrichment by 2029.    
The existing building at Piketon can support up to 3.5MM SWU. The site has licensing 
available to expand to 7MM SWU, and land to support 14MM SWU. 
While Centrus has not confirmed what their ultimate capacity plans are, at 1Q26 the 
company noted current plans address their $2.5B LEU backlog at the time and 12 
tonnes of HALEU. Furthermore, Centrus has stated the current plan will achieve nth of 
a kind costs.  
Timeline & Costs  
The 3.5MM capacity includes 96 cascades implying ~36.5K SWU per cascade. 
Centrus has guided to a first-of-a-kind to nth-of-a-kind cadence with the first cascade 
taking 42 months, the second six months and then each thereafter taking two 
months. With a Dec '25 start, this cadence implies the first cascade would come on in 
2029 (which the company has confirmed) and Centrus would reach 100K SWU / year 
in early 2030. From there, delivering one cascade every two months adds ~220K SWU 
per year, and it would take till 2045 to reach 3.5MM SWU. 
Datapoints from Orano and Urenco capacity expansions could suggest capital costs 
of ~$1,500 per SWU, meaning the full 3.5MM buildout could cost Centrus $5.25B. 
However, we understand the Urenco/Orano cascade is smaller and has a lower 
CapEx at the expense of higher OpEx vs Centrus. 
Specifically, Urenco and Orano share a centrifuge technology and supplier, 
Enrichment Technology Company (ETC), which has a one part, 20-foot centrifuge unit 
while the Centrus design is a 40-foot centrifuge with five sub-assemblies. As a result, 
in the event of an equipment failure, the ETC unit may need to be completely 
replaced while Centrus may just need to replace one of the sub-assemblies.

Backlog 
In support of an eventual capacity expansion plan, Centrus began building a backlog 
of commercial contracts for LEU and later HALEU enrichment, contingent upon 
securing substantial public and private investment necessary to build new 
enrichment capacity. From 1Q24 to 2Q26 Centrus accumulated a contingent 
backlog totaling $3B, largely in LEU of which $2.4B is definitive LEU.  
The first disclosure of contingent contracts in 1Q24 totaled $900MM of LEU; by 1Q26 
the contingent contract position had grown to $2.4B of LEU, all of which were 
definitive agreements. On 6/18/26 Oklo selected Centrus for a HALEU contract to 
supply "multiple years of Oklo reactor cores, covering up to five Aurora powerhouses 
as part of Oklo's planned 1.2GW Clean Energy Campus" with deliveries to begin in 
2029. This award contributed to contingent backlog at 2Q26 of $3B, still mostly LEU, 
but now with some HALEU from Oklo, and $2.4B of definitive LEU. There have been 
three further announcements in 3Q that we expect to show up in 3Q26 backlog:

Estimates and Target 
We view near-term results for Centrus to be less impactful to the share price as they 
primarily reflect the brokerage business, which will largely be replaced by in-house 
enrichment in 2029+. That said, we see a weak 3Q26 for the brokerage business, 
owing to timing. Management has regularly discussed how timing can create 
significant volatility in results and the business should be evaluated on a 12-month 
trailing basis. Our sense is the company is still comfortable with prior guidance for 
revenue to be between $450-500MM, implying a strong 4Q.  
Also aiding 4Q will be the recently announced acquisition of Westerman, which we 
assume contributes $160MM+ of annual revenue. The acquisition brings key 
centrifuge component supply in-house while also diversifying Centrus' revenue 
outside of enrichment. 
We believe the outlook for in-house enrichment business will be the primary driver of 
the stock. Our model assumes Centrus ultimately constructs 3.5MM SWU of 
enrichment capacity by 2040 for a cost of $6B with most of the volume directed 
toward LEU enrichment at $250/SWU with a 60% EBITDA margin. Under these 
assumptions, with an 11.5% nominal WACC, we compute a DCF-based price target of 
$120/share.  
We assume a lower WACC for Centrus vs other new nuclear technology companies 
as Centrus' enrichment technology has been demonstrated through the DOE HALEU 
program and arguably faces less competition risk being reactor design agnostic. This 
is somewhat offset by existing state-owned competitors who may value financial 
returns differently.

While our target price is above the current share price, we note that each of our 
major assumptions are more optimistic than current market prices, or what the

Specifically; our LEU price assumption of $250/SWU compares to current long-term

~$6B, at the low end of the $6-11B range we believe is generally expected, and our 
EBITDA margin assumption of 60% is at the high end of Urenco. 
Under our assumptions, every $10 of SWU price change translates to ~$5 per share of 
value. 
Orano / Urenco Comparison 
Orano and Urenco, while state owned, disclose financial results. We view Urenco as a 
better comparison as their business generates most of the revenue from enrichment 
services. Orano's business involves the entire fuel cycle and recycling, and while the 
company does report a "front end" segment, this also includes uranium mining and 
conversion. Over the past seven years, Urenco's EBITDA margin has averaged 52%, 
with a range of 38-68%. As discussed, our estimate of 60% is at the high end of 
Urenco's range. Perhaps Centrus' margins could be above Urenco's given what we 
understand is a higher CapEx centrifuge in exchange for lower OpEx, though Urenco 
likely gets some scale benefit producing 17MM SWU.

Investment Risks
Clean Energy:
Risks to the sector may include any regulatory delays or rejections regarding nuclear module
designs would have a negative impact on the stocks. Failure to execute on the construction of
power plants or business development plans would negatively affect stock prices. A design flaw
or issue in a company's manufacturing or supply chain would have negative consequences for
the stocks. Any malfunction or issue with any of the world's nuclear assets could have negative
ramifications for all nuclear companies.
Risks To The Price Target
Upside risks to our price target include an enrichment supply bottleneck that pushes long-term
LEU pricing well above our $250/SWU assumption and an accelerating nuclear buildout that
drives faster offtake commitments. Downside risks include cost overruns and timeline delays
on the cascade buildout, the reentry of Russian enrichment supply into the U.S. market, and a
slower than expected U.S. nuclear buildout.
