ETF Market Pulse 
Week 44 - Value in the Driver’s Seat: Bulgaria Surges, Brazil Stumbles 
 
 
Last week, ETF returns reflected a continuous rotation away from US Growth and 
speculative assets toward cheaper equity segments: US Growth (-1.7%) was hit by 
renewed concerns over stretched tech valuations and the sustainability of AI-related 
capex, while EM equities (-2.0%) suffered from higher US yields. Bitcoin (-5.7%) was the 
main laggard as the risk-off move triggered a sharp unwind of leveraged crypto 
derivatives, with the Binance security incident adding to selling pressure and 
accelerating liquidations. In contrast, US Value and Small Caps (+1.3% each) benefited 
from the rotation toward cheaper exposures, while European equities (+1.5%) 
outperformed thanks to their more value-heavy, lower-tech exposure, which provided 
relative insulation from the US tech sell-off. 
 
The Expat Bulgaria SOFIX UCITS ETF (+13% in USD) was the standout performer, 
while Brazil-focused equity ETFs ranked among the main laggards. Bulgaria 
benefited from its removal from the FATF (Financial Action Task Force) grey list and 
positioning ahead of MSCI’s planned upgrade to Frontier Market status, with the 
market’s limited liquidity amplifying inflow-driven gains. In contrast, Brazilian equities 
suffered from a sharp political and fiscal repricing, as Lula’s narrow runoff victory 
challenged expectations of a more market-friendly policy shift and prompted investors 
to reassess the country’s outlook. 
 
ETF Flows: From Cash to Credit, Financials and Defensives: In fixed income, investors 
redeemed Bloomberg US Treasury Bills 1–3 Months exposure while adding to iBoxx USD 
Investment Grade Corporate Bonds. Despite the Fed maintaining a hawkish pause, ETF 
flows suggest investors viewed front-end cash yields as close to their peak and moved 
further out the curve to lock in attractive IG corporate yields. In equities, MSCI Brazil 
25/50 saw heavy redemptions amid post-election fiscal concerns, while creations 
favored S&P 500 Financials and Health Care, combining higher-for-longer/value 
exposure with defensive earnings. Strong creations also targeted MSCI World ESG Broad 
CTB Select, supporting diversified global equity exposure with a climate-transition tilt.  
 
New ETF Launches: Lower Fees and New Niches in Europe, Active Innovation in the 
US.  In Europe, Amundi repriced its swap-based MSCI ACWI exposure to 9bp vs 45bp for 
its existing ETF. VanEck launched a UCITS version of its China Semiconductor ETF, using 
a 10%-capped, ESG-screened index, while Quest added a global cash-flow quality 
strategy. iShares launched the fourth active global defence UCITS ETF, and State Street 
added a 13th global high-yield UCITS ETF. In the US, AB added another US active equity 
ETF, differentiated by its high-conviction fundamental and quantitative approach, while 
Hartford launched the first active developed-markets ex-North America growth ETF.
