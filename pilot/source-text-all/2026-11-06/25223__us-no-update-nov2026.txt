Nordic Outlook Update – USA 
 
 
 
 
 
Resilient growth, lackluster jobs, sticky inflation  
Growth has remained resilient to shocks from trade and geopolitical conflict, and 
we raise our GDP forecasts again, slightly. AI investments and spending by 
wealthier households have made up for weaknesses elsewhere but are set to 
lose some momentum going forward. Slowing jobs creation and modest wage 
growth should keep underlying inflation contained but temporary upward 
pressures from energy and the AI buildout means that inflation will not be back 
at target until the end of 2028. The Fed raises one more time in December 2026. 
 
 
 
 
 

  
November 2026                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
 
 
• 
Business sentiment (ISM & PMI) has improved further after the start of the war with Iran 
and indicates continued solid growth. We do not expect the boom in Q3 to continue. Still, 
we revise our GDP forecast upwards again to 2.4 per cent in 2026, followed by 2.3 and 2.2 
per cent in 2027 and 2028 respectively; 0.2 percentage points above the forecast in 
Nordic Outlook August for 2026 to 2027 and 0.1 for 2028.  
 
• 
Fiscal policy support has limited the hit from rising gasoline prices on consumption, but the 
effect is fading. Sluggish employment growth, falling real earnings and weak consumer 
sentiment make us expect a slowdown in coming quarters. Positive wealth effects from 
rising equity prices are supportive but makes spending sensitive to setbacks in AI.  
• 
Investments in data centers are expected to continue to rise at a rapid pace though higher 
borrowing costs could weigh on some of the plans. The AI effect on total GDP is, however, 
counteracted by a large import share. More generous tax depreciations rules may have 
contributed to a broadening into other areas. Residential construction continues to decline 
due to high mortgage rates.  
 
• 
The labour market remains in a low hiring, no firing situation. Unemployment remains 
stable slightly above 4 per cent, around the Fed’s view of the longer run. Negative effects 
of AI have become visible in areas such as information and finance, but partly counteracted 
by increased employment in other sectors, such as manufacturing.  
• 
We continue to see upward pressures on inflation from tariffs, energy prices and, to some 
extent, higher prices on AI-related IT, as temporary. However, a prolonged period of 
elevated energy prices carries risks for spreading into other prices. We have raised our 
forecast for core inflation slightly for 2027. Modest earnings growth, of around 3-3.5 per 
cent, and strong productivity growth support a return to the 2 per cent target by end 2028. 
 
• 
Midterm elections and the decisive victory for Democrats in both chambers will have only 
limited effects on the growth outlook. Congress will be gridlocked and Democrats will get 
greater leverage in budget discussions. We think this may result in a slightly more 
expansionary fiscal policy but the door for major new spending initiatives/tax cuts is 
closed. A shift to Democratic governors in some states may temporarily impede the AI 
build-out. Fed independence will come less under pressure now after the Senate shift.    
 
 

  
November 2026                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
 
GDP grew by nearly 3.5 per cent q/q annualized in Q3. Private 
domestic demand – household consumption and business 
investments – increased by nearly 5 per cent. We expect total 
GDP to slow to a trend-like 2 per cent q/q pace in 2027.    
Household spending has outpaced real disposable incomes as 
unusually large tax refunds offset high gasoline prices in the 
first half of this year. Positive wealth effects are reflected in a 
decline in the savings ratio below the historical average.  
 
The AI build-out has boosted IT equipment spending but there 
are some signs of a broadening into other sectors.  
Job creation has picked up since the lows in 2025 but is well 
below the pre-pandemic average. Tight immigration policies 
and an aging domestic population limits the growth in supply.  
 
 
Upward pressures from tariffs and higher energy prices have 
contributed to keeping inflation above target for over 5 years. 
We expect to see some indirect effects from higher energy 
prices in 2027 but only temporary.  
We continue to expect another rate hike by December 
followed by a cut in late 2027 and another two cuts in the 
first half of 2028. Markets are pricing two additional hikes 
until end 2027.
