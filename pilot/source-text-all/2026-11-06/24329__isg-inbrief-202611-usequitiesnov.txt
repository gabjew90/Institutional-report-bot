Is it all Just a Handful of AI Giants?  
Assessing the Breadth of S&P 500 Earnings and Returns 
 
As 3Q earnings season unfolds, clients are asking whether profit growth is being driven by a handful of 
large technology stocks. An article in the Wall Street Journal last week reinforced this concern. It noted 
that optimism about the stock market has been fueled by a surge in corporate earnings, but that "the 
catch is that the profit growth is being driven by a handful of AI giants."1  It also pointed to expectations 
for earnings to decline this quarter for 132 stocks within the S&P 500 (roughly 25% of companies). 
While AI companies are contributing meaningfully to this year's close to 30% earnings growth, we show 
that earnings and returns across the S&P 500 remain broad-based. Moreover, a portion of S&P 500 
companies report declines in any given quarter, which is not unusual by historical standards, and this 
year's share of decliners is in fact lower than the median share of decliners post-global financial crisis. 
 
 
1 Hannah Erin Lang, “Two AI Chipmakers Make Up 34% of America’s Earnings Boom,” Wall Street Journal, October 24, 2026. 
KEY TAKEAWAYS 
• 
Earnings growth across the S&P 500 is broad-based. The median S&P 500 company is growing at a low-double-
digit rate this year. This is well above the 6.5% trend earnings growth, and 7.4% annualized actual earnings 
growth, for the S&P 500 since World War II. 
• 
Breadth is healthy across sectors and companies. Underlying earnings growth is positive in all eleven sectors this 
year, excluding one-time charges in the Health Care sector. About 75% of companies are expected to grow 
earnings year-over-year in Q3, which compares with a 65% median post-global financial crisis. 
• 
Returns have also been broad-based. The S&P 500 excluding AI-related companies has returned 12% year-to-
date, which is the same as the index overall. Gains have come entirely from earnings growth, while valuation 
multiples have fallen. For the S&P 500 overall, forward P/E multiples have declined 16% from 22.0x at the 
beginning of the year to 18.4x through October 30, 2026. 
• 
We preliminarily expect high-teens US earnings growth in 2027 (excluding one-time gains in the 2026 base), 
continuing to outpace non-US developed and Chinese equity markets. 
• 
Our recommendation to stay invested in US equities continues to be anchored by the fundamental strength of the 
US economy and corporate earnings.

US Earnings Growth is Broad-Based  
S&P 500 firms have delivered much better-than-expected results so far this year. As shown in Exhibit 1, reported 
earnings grew by 27% and 50%, in Q1 and Q2, respectively. While reported earnings growth was boosted in 
both quarters by gains related to two technology companies’ equity investments, when these non-recurring 
gains are excluded, underlying growth was still strong at around 20%-30% (shown by the red markers in Exhibit 
1). Consensus expects earnings to continue to grow at a similar pace during the remainder of the year.  
We acknowledge that AI spending beneficiaries2 have contributed meaningfully to this year's S&P 500 earnings 
growth. They have grown by over 90% and contributed around half of the S&P 500’s 28% growth in the first half 
of this year.3  Even so, earnings growth has been strong across the rest of the S&P 500:  
1. The median S&P 500 company has delivered above-trend earnings growth. The median company growth 
rate is one measure of breadth as it reflects the typical company and is not skewed by outliers or a 
particular sector. In Exhibit 1, the light blue bars show the median company in the S&P 500 has in fact 
grown earnings at a low-double-digit rate in the first half of 2026 and is expected to do so in Q3 and Q4, 
exceeding 6.5% trend earnings growth for the S&P 500 since World War II.  
 
2. Underlying growth across all eleven sectors of the S&P 500 has been positive so far in 2026, excluding 
one-time changes in the Health Care sector. Health Care is the only sector with negative headline 
earnings growth so far this year, but that is entirely due to one-time in-process research and 
development (IPR&D) charges, based on company reporting. Excluding these non-recurring charges, 
underlying growth for Health Care is positive as well. For 2026 overall, consensus expects positive growth 
in all eleven sectors.

3. The share of S&P 500 companies with growing earnings is above its historical median. For Q3 2026, 
consensus expects about 25% of the companies in the S&P 500 to decline year-over year (YoY), with the 
remaining 75% expected to grow. Skeptics who view this as a narrowly driven earnings rally may point to 
this statistic. However, some dispersion in earnings growth, and even YoY declines, across companies in 
the S&P 500 in any given quarter is normal. In fact, as shown in Exhibit 2, the median share of companies 
with positive YoY earnings growth has been 65% since the global financial crisis, which implies that 
roughly 35% of companies typically report a YoY decline. The Q3 consensus reading of 75% ranks in the 
83rd percentile since 2009, meaning breadth has been lower in 83% of quarters over that period. Each 
quarter so far this year has also come in above the 65% median, so more companies than usual have 
reported positive earnings growth.

Strong Earnings Growth has Driven Broad-Based Returns in 2026  
AI spending beneficiaries² have outperformed this year, but S&P 500 gains are broad-based outside AI-
related companies (see Exhibit 3). We define AI-related companies as AI spending beneficiaries2 and 
hyperscalers4. As shown in Exhibit 3, S&P 500 excluding AI-related companies returned 12% this year, 
the same as the return for the S&P 500 overall. Returns across AI and non-AI related companies have 
come entirely from earnings growth, while valuations have decreased. For the S&P 500 overall, forward 
P/E multiples have declined 16% from 22.0x at the beginning of the year to 18.4x through October 30, 
2026. More specifically, valuation multiples for AI spending beneficiaries² have fallen 38% this year, 
reflecting a healthy dose of investor skepticism that such rapid profit growth can last. AI spending

We Continue to Expect US Earnings to Outpace Other Regions 
Relative to other developed countries and China, US earnings have outperformed in 2026, even 
excluding one-off gains (see Exhibit 4). We continue to believe that the US will provide more stable and 
reliable earnings growth, supported by the structural advantages that underpin our US Preeminence 
investment theme. For 2027, we preliminarily expect a high-teens earnings growth rate in the US in 
2027 (excl. one-time gains in 2026 base), outpacing non-US developed economies. For this reason, 
along with the expectation that the US economy will continue to expand, we maintain our 
recommendation to stay invested in US equities.
