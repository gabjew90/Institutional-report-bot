Europe Open - Blue skies ahead
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Democrats have won control of the Senate and House, confirming expectations of a sweep. While the overnight reaction
was in typical risk-off fashion (Nasdaq futures plunged -2.5% on AI slowdown/data center moratorium risks after Senate
and Governor wins in Texas), moves have largely reversed - Asian stocks are mostly green with Nikkei still holding
losses and China continuing to outperform. UST gain stalled and USD bottomed, with safe havens JPY and CHF holding
most gains in G10. Oil prices drift, with President Trump stating overnight that they have no immediate plans to strike
Iran. At print, Dems have won 53 seats in the Senate with 5 still to be called, and 223 in the House (majority 218), with
~80 still to be called by AP.
Beyond midterms, it's a packed day ahead with Riksbank rate decision (08:30 GMT) where our economists expect a
cautious 25bp hike. Eurozone HICP due at 10:00 on track to print at 4% YoY, core at 2.5% - its post regional data so
shouldn't be too surprising. US Q4 refunding announcement at 13:30 is a larger driver for treasuries than midterms, we
see a $3bn cut to the 20y and 30y bond auctions. Plenty of moving parts in EM with Czech CPI due shortly, Hungary IP,
rate decisions in Poland (hold, but close call with hike) and Brazil (hold). Its local elections in South Africa.

Midterms recap
Democrats have won control of the Senate and House. In the Senate, they flip 7 states including Texas, Kansas and Ohio,
with an overall 53 seats called by AP. They also won governor races in Georgia, Ohio and Texas, the latter most surprising as
this marks the first Democratic Texas Governor since 1990. Our strategists had been flagging this as a key race, given the
implications for the AI trade/data center buildout. As we go to print, 5 Senate races have yet to be called with DDHQ
projecting Democrats winning a total of 56 seats. As for the House, 223 seats have been officially called for the Dems, flipping
25, with ~80 races still to be called by AP. 

Initial risk-off sentiment following the Democratic sweep has faded sharply, with USTs fading early gains, US 10s only up +3bp
compared to +7bp earlier, with US equity futures also bouncing from the lows (Nasdaq traded as low as -2.3% before
recovering to -0.5% as we go to print, Kospi back positive). USD also bottoms out. No clear reason for the reversal but we did
see more constructive comments from President Trump about being able to work with the Democrats and hoping not to have
to use veto power, and absolutely agreeing with the results.

What's happening across markets:
Rates: USTs are reversing overnight gains as the vote count continues, but NZ bonds surged higher and were best
performers, front-end up as much as 11bp following weaker than expected jobs data. Unemployment rate jumps to 11y highs
at 5.7%, driving Dec hike odds lower to 15bp. Hike pricing towards Jun 2027 still remains heavy at ~89bp (Bloomberg WIRP).
View change from Citi Economics - we no longer expect further RBNZ tightening folowing the Q3 report. Despite above target
inflation, we expect the OCR to hold at 2.75% throughout 2027, with risks evenly balanced. Following a dovish RBA hike, we
still see risks of Feb being a live meeting, so recommend paying AU 1y1y at 4.69% versus receiving NZ 1y1y at 4.06%
(spread -63bps), targeting -100bps with a -45bps stop. Saturday's tight election also adds risk, as a Labour win could restore
the RBNZ's dual mandate.
Equities: Sharp reversals as we go to print with Kospi up 0.6%, Nikkei still negative at -1.6% but largely playing catchup
after being closed on Tuesday. Chinese stocks continue to hold gains after outperforming overnight, CSI up 2% at print.
European and US equity futures remain red but losses have narrowed. Risk off sentiment exacerbated after Gina Hinojosa's
win for Texas governor - she has been campaigning for a moratorium on new data center development until permanent state
rules are introduced. Our D1 salesperson thinks the risk reversal perhaps indicates that much of the negativity around AI was
already priced, and we could see re-grossing through the European morning now the midterms event has passed. Elsewhere,
AMD and ANET results beat supportive of the semis trade, but in a busy night, strong guidance was overlooked. FT reports
that Oracle scrapped a chip leasing contract worth $7bn with Tencent. 
In Europe, our D1 desk is inclined to buy into weakness. AI strains may persist, and any shift into investment away from the
US is supportive for European development/EU Electrification theme. European banks also look attractive as tighter US
financial regulation improves their appeal. CGDORBND Index comprises names hit hard during the recent de-grossing and
could benefit if flows return to Financials/Semis/Electrification.
Commodities: Oil prices hold modest gains, Brent at $103. The US has no immediate plans to strike Iran, Trump said in a
phone interview to FT late last night. "I’m ready, we’re ready. We’re locked and loaded if we need to go, but there may be no
reason for it," when asked about a strike later this week. Bloomberg continues to report that Tehran and Washington are
reviewing proposals from mediators on how to resolve their differences and avoid re-escalation, with no strikes recorded
overnight. Gold sees a strong bounce off the $4000 handle up 2% overnight
FX: Moves are more tepid with USD recovering from lows but key levels across G10 holding - EURUSD at 1.12 and USDJPY
at 158. We had been highlighting that midterms may not be a major event risk for FX given that a Dem sweep was already
priced. In the very near term, QRA event is the anchor event where our strategists are out of consensus expecting $3bn cuts
to 20y and 30y issuance, which should weigh on the USD. FX Strategy like fading USD weakness ahead of data and potential
escalation, but may need to give it a few days while markets remain focus on midterm implications and the potential QRA
dovish surprise. Asia eTrading volumes continue to run x2.2 times 30d averages but is slightly cooling off at print. 
 
Still to come:

SEK: Riksbank Rate Decision at 08:30 GMT. OIS pricing stands at ~17bp, while the Bloomberg economist survey leans
towards a 25bp hike. With core inflation running close to the 2% target, the case for remaining on hold also remains
compelling. We tentatively expect a 25bp rate hike with communication of future moves expected to stay balanced. Given the
dovish nuance in last week's central bank meetings, there is a decent chance that Riksbank hikes but frame in a dovish
fashion, which is a risk to EURSEK shorts. 
EUR: Eurozone CPI YoY at 10:00 GMT for October P. Regional data indicates HICP is on track for a 4.0% YoY print (Citi e.,
above initial forecasts at 3.9%). Core inflation should print stable at 2.5% YoY. With headline inflation 50bp above ECB
forecasts, it justifies another 25bp hike in Dec. Eurozone ECB Wage Tracker also due at 09:00 GMT.
USD: US Treasury Quarterly Refunding Announcement at 13:30 GMT. Citi expects Treasury to cut the 20y and 30y bond
auctions by $3bn each. This would leave 20y new issues at $13bn (reopenings at $10bn) and 30y new issues at $22bn
(reopenings at $19bn) which would be funded by additional T-bill issuance from mid-Jan. TIPS sizes should remain
unchanged for now. Printing simultaneously is trade balance details which will be less market-moving ISM Services Index at
15:00 GMT for October should remain resilient at 55 (Citi e.).
Elsewhere‌
ZAR: Local elections. 10,526 seats are up for election across 257 councils. Final results could take 48-72 hours but this is
subject to recounts. Turnout is a key swing factor with higher turnout correlating with DA popularity. Ipsos numbers see a
~42% turnout. A common thread from recent election results and recent polls is that the ANC is no longer the organizing
center of local politics and as a result, the decisions are now who it partners with. More from EM Strategy.
CZK: CPI MoM at 08:00 GMT for October P. Our economists expect headline inflation to hold steady at 2.5% YoY in October,
unchanged from September, as slower disinflation in food and regulated prices offsets more moderate fuel price growth.
CLP: Central Bank Meeting Minutes at 11:30 GMT.
HUF: Industrial Output % YoY at 13:00 GMT. Our economists expect that industrial output growth slowed down to 6.2% YoY in
Sep from 8.7% recorded in Aug. Improvement in Aug was driven by capital goods with motor vehicles manufacturing and
category of computer, electronics and optical products enjoying strong growth.
BRL: Selic Rate Decision at 21:30 GMT. Our economists maintain their base case that Copom will remain on hold at 13.75%
until mid-next year. Copom is likely to reaffirm their data-dependent approach.
PLN: Poland Base Rate Decision Announcement. Our economists expect that the MPC will keep its policy rate unchanged at
3.75% but it’s a close call between a hold and a 25bp hike. New inflation forecasts should indicate CPI running above 4% for
at least 2-3 quarters. We could re-assess our view following inflation data, where beats would reinforce the NBP’s case to
hike.
CNH: Private data RatingDog China Composite PMI printed at 52.2 but didn't move markets given the national print was
released over the weekend.
