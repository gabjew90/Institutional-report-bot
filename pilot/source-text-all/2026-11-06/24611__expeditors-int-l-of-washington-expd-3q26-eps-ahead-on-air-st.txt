Expeditors Int’l of Washington (EXPD): 3Q26 EPS Ahead on Air Strength 
Continuance; Ocean Supply Acceleration in 2027 Could Weigh on Rates
3 November 2026 | 2:19PM EST
Bottom Line: EXPD (Sell) reported 3Q26 operating EPS of $2.65 compared to 
$2.20/2.21 estimates for GS/Visible Alpha Consensus Data. The beat was driven by 
better than expected gross revenue performance flowing into net revenue - notably 
from higher Air gross revenues of 16% v. GSe due to higher volume and rev/kilo 
flowing to net revenues 16% ahead of GSe.  This led to EBIT that came in at 18% 
ahead of our estimate from slightly lower opex v. GSe, and importantly EXPD saw a 
36.7% EBIT margin as percent of net revenue – which is above the key 30% level for 
assessing health in forwarding. 
Shares: Net, net EXPD saw another strong quarter and based on tight air 
n
capacity and pricing trends we do raise the trajectory of EPS (details below).  
Encouragingly, the company noted favorable demand from hyperscalers for 
goods movements as well as E-Commerce trends looking favorable out of North 
Asia – something we think that could equate to positive read through to 
parcel carriers (UPS, FDX).  
We will continue to monitor rate and volume development for EXPD to 
assess if trends around net revenue and margin persist, however, given the 
strong net revenue and EPS growth in 2026, this does set up 2027 with 
difficult comparables, especially as we anticipate an influx of ocean supply in 
2H27 (see our European ocean analyst recent downgrade of MAERSK).  
Results: Net revenue of ~$1,214M was +6% above our forecast on strong total 
n
revenue as Net Revenue Margin of 29.5% was 50bps below our 30.1% forecast. 
Net revenue in Airfreight beat GSe by +16% on +16% higher total airfreight 
revenue driven by stronger air rates +38% YoY v. GSe +20%, and importantly, 
stronger kilo growth of 13%, generating an inline 24.3% NR margin vs GSe. Net 
revenue in Ocean came under GSe by -4% driven by lower ocean rates of +41% 
on 7% volumes, leading to inline NR margin vs 22% GSe.  We note Air/Ocean 
volume growth at +13%/7% YoY in 3Q26, improving vs +14%/0% in 2Q26 
and above our +10%/+3% forecasts, while implied revenue per unit growth 
came in at +38%/+41%, mixed from +43%/+5% in 2Q26 and above/below 
our 20%/50% YoY forecasts. 
Customs brokerage net revenue beat GSe by +4% on +8% higher total 
o
customs brokerage revenue. Customs brokerage NR margin of 40.9% was 
below GSe 42.5% - lower QoQ from 2Q26 (42.1%).

Current Trends: Looking at trends through the 3Q26, EXPD’s volume growth within 
n
Ocean accelerated through the quarter at +5% in July, +5% in August, and +12% in 
September. Air volume growth also accelerated through the quarter at +8% in July, 
+13% in August, and +19% in September. 
Within airfreight, EXPD noted elevated rates given high demand for 
o
airfreight with constrained capacity due to limited fleet expansion and 
new aircraft delays. Demand for airfreight was robust in the quarter from 
South Asia, with demand sustained by hyperscalers, as well as 
e-commerce recovery on Asia to US lanes lapping de minimis impacts. 
EXPD’s ocean business saw strong growth across Asia export markets, and 
o
congestion at major Asian ports impacted rates higher as capacity and port 
operations were ideld due to inclement weather in Asia. 
Customs brokerage benefited from strong demand across its verticals and 
o
geographies, calling out strength driven by hyperscaler and tech 
customers. Management noted that growth is driven by new business 
wins, higher volumes, and broad based customs services across all regions 
with new and existing customers, providing additional growth following 
tariff related growth that started in 3Q25. 
Looking Ahead: Trade and geopolitical uncertainty remains prevalent in global 
n
trade as higher fuel and tighter capacity drive air prices higher - that could vary 
depending on the duration of the middle east conflict - combined with strong export 
demand out of Asia related to hyperscalers. On ocean, rates remain elevated to start 
the quarter, but we look ahead to 2H27 when we anticipate meaningful supply 
growth, which could offset the positive tailwinds in Air.  
Estimates: Our prior FY26/27/28 EPS estimates of $7.90/$8.35/$8.95 now stand at 
n
$8.55/$8.80/$9.50. Our EPS estimate for 2026 increases by the beat and higher 
trajectory in air net revenues, partially offset by lower ocean net revenue. Our FY 
2027 and 2028 EPS estimates increase on a higher base in 2026. 
Price Target/Risks: Our 12-month price target is $185 (from $180 prior) and is based 
on ~20.0x P/E multiple (from 21.0x) applied to our updated Q5-Q8 EPS forecast ($9.30). 
Our multiple moves lower on the stronger earnings for EXPD and what could be nearing 
the strongest point of net revenue growth in 2026 before potentially slowing in 2027. 
Upside risks to our thesis include strong consumer volume related to re-stocking, 
ongoing uplift to customs brokerage demand and potentially price, contract pricing 
holds firmer than expected, EXPD’s asset-light/high return business model proving more 
resilient vs. peers in further economic slowdowns, unknown duration of the Red Sea 
conflict ceasefire which could lead to tightening capacity and higher rates.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
