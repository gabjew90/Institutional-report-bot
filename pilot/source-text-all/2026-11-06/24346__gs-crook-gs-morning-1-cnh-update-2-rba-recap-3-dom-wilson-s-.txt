1) CNH: End of the Appreciation Trend?
China Macro Trading (Skyler Qian): I don't think this policy shift means that the PBoC is putting a line in
the sand for spot – it is more of a policy goal to narrow the gap between offshore USDCNH/onshore
USDCNY/CNY fixing. Spot-fixing gap still widened from -300 to -700 in Oct, and offshore/onshore basis
widened from around par to -40. After the sharp move in the morning, spot-fixing gap already bounced
from -700 yesterday to -430, offshore/onshore basis recovered to within -10. I think when spot fixing gap
narrows to -300 or higher, it would be good timing to re-engage USDCNH short. This 2% hike in forex
reserve requirement is more symbolic than its actual impact on USD liquidity. CNH DF curve only
lowered modestly in the morning. As long as exporter flow persists and unless CNH funding loosens
significantly enough to change the carry profile.
EM Strategy (Sun Lu): This measure itself has very limited impact. Onshore USD liquidity is flush and
2% RRR hike only removes 24bn out of 1.2tn USD deposits in onshore banks. USD liquidity is ample and
has limited need onshore. It’s mainly a signal from PBoC to push back against recent fast RMB
appreciation which likely caused some panic among corporates. Current spot fixing gap is close to
400pips, I think 200-300pips gap (6.68-6.69) will be good risk reward area to fade (not sure if we can get
to 6.69). Trump is still expected to come to Shenzhen for APEC Nov 18-19. Believe PBoC will engineer a
faster fixing appreciation close to that date. If US-Iran escalates, we could see 6.70-71 max on position
unwind.
Asia Econ (Andrew Tilton), paraphrased from this note overnight: The trade balance is the big picture. 6%
annualised CNH appreciation since mid-2025 makes sense and has occurred despite some attempts to slow
this down – cutting the FX risk reserve ratio for banks’ forward FX sales from 20% to 0 in March 2026
and expanding outbound QDII quotas in March and August 2026 are two such examples. The annualised
pace of CNY strengthening 9.5% against the USD since the end of September is likely too much. We
maintain our view that the central bank still favours gradual appreciation and may seek to manage its pace
to prevent one-way expectations, rather than reverse the trend, as suggested by its Q3 MPC statement and
recent daily fixings. We maintain our 12-month USDCNY forecast of 6.40.

2) ASIA ECONOMICS (BOAK) – RBA: 25bp Hike, 6-3 Split Vote
BOTTOM LINE: The RBA raised the cash rate 25bp to 4.85% at today's meeting, in line with our and
consensus expectations (GSe/BBG: 4.85%). The decision was by majority, with six members of the
Monetary Policy Board voting in favour of the 25bp hike, and three members voting to leave the cash rate
unchanged.
We view this as incrementally dovish for three reasons:

1/ The split vote.
2/ The omission of longstanding language emphasizing a tightening bias - “The Board will continue to do
what it considers necessary…. including increasing the cash rate target further if needed”
3/ The updated forecast for trimmed mean inflation to decelerate to 2.4% by 4Q2028 and slightly
undershoot the 2.5% mid-point of the RBA’s target band (as per August’s SMP).
Press Conference: Governor Bullock said capacity pressures were "greater and contributed more to
inflation than we judged before" while flagging concern about the upside risk to inflation expectations.
Looking ahead, Governor Bullock stressed "there's now five interest rate increases in the system," which
means "it is probably a good point to just take stock for a minute and see what comes in terms of the data"
AUS IRP SALES (GATTELLARI): Despite significant front-end flattener unwinds leading into the
meeting, positioning remained paid the first three meetings and received further out the curve.
Immediately after the statement release, the bill curve re flattened, unwinding much of its recent
steepening, and HFs came to pay the dip in Dec and Feb RBA. The flattening in bills continued as Bullock
delivered an overall dovish presser and we saw mostly t/p paying in 1y1y 2y1y from those who received it
into the morning steepening (was +5bp at one point today). We would rather pay ~3m3m for choice, but
continue to think risks are building further out the curve for rate cuts to get priced (1y1y and out). This
potential hike risk - alongside likely weakening economic data - should support 3m3m 1y1y flatteners
from here as the front end remains anchored, and 1y1y prices in normalisation cuts.

3) GLOBAL MACRO (WILSON) – The Fed as a Driver of Long-Term Rates –
The Latest Shift in Real Forward Rates: Forward real rates shifted above their prior range in July 2026,
after the energy shock and AI capex upgrades, with the obvious catalyst being that the Fed shifted clearly
to signalling - and then delivering - policy hikes, beginning with substantial dissents in favour of hikes at
the July meeting and extending through Jackson Hole and the September FOMC. After an extended period
where the market had assumed the policy rate would ultimately fall, the realization that the funds rate
troughed above 3.6% may have prompted the market to push up its view of the longer-term distribution.
Investors may have lost faith that neutral rates can plausibly be in the 3-3.5% range.
Anchoring the Future Range: History suggests that the range over which the funds rate has moved, and
especially the peak of recent Fed hike cycles, may play a role in anchoring the zones in which these
forward rates can plausibly trade. Pushing longer-dated forward yields above the peak of the last hiking
cycle (5.3%) would be unusual, unless the market came to expect the Fed to hike above those levels, and
markets struggled to hold onto forward pricing approaching that zone earlier in the month. If the Fed funds
rate looks like it is peaking at the 4-4.25% rate as in our modal forecast, or even a hike or two beyond that

point, deep forward rates above 5% are ultimately going to look less plausible as the anchor for the future
range.

4) US Midterm Elections
Stuart Jenkins (FX Strategy): We should get a sense of the degree of the polling error pretty early on, but it
can take days or even weeks, especially on the Senate, to get an official result. The biggest questions out
of this are on Iran and AI regulation, but ultimately we think the outcomes are relatively narrow for those
policies between a blue sweep and a split Congress, given that the ball will remain in the President's court
and he can continue to lean on Executive action instead of Legislation. A blue sweep would probably
mean incrementally higher spending, but I do also think, given where fiscal capacity is right now, there is
probably some lingering premium as well, so it is not clear how positive that would really be for the USD.
Regarding tariffs, Alec has mentioned that the Dems may focus on reducing tariffs on imports from
Canada, which has the potential to be positive CAD medium-term.

5) COMMODITIES RESEARCH (DART) – Raising Our 2027 TTF Forecasts
BOTTOM LINE: Although we maintain our Mideast LNG supply outlook, assuming Qatari LNG exports
reach their operational max in Feb27, we raise our 1Q27 European natural gas price (TTF) forecast to 74
EUR/MWh (from 44) and the 2027 average to 51 EUR/MWh (from 32) to reflect a subjective 45%
probability that we face a low-winter-Mideast LNG supply scenario (worth 26 EUR to 1Q27 prices), and
the inclusion of a small cold-weather risk component (worth 4 EUR).
Risks to our 2027 Price Forecast: Risks remain skewed to the upside. We estimate that a low-winter-ME
supply scenario, combined with a 1 std. deviation colder than average NW European winter, would lift
1Q27 TTF to nearly 150 EUR/MWh. 1Q27 TTF currently at 92 EUR/MWh implies a 47% market-
perceived probability of this very tight scenario. See Scenario analysis below.
