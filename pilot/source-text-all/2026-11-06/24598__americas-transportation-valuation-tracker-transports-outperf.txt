Americas Transportation: Valuation Tracker: Transports 
Outperformance Led By Parcels and Rails in October
3 November 2026 | 4:54PM EST
Transportation stocks were mixed against the S&P in October with 16 companies 
within our coverage outperforming the S&P 500 and 9 covered names 
underperforming; our 25 covered names were +4% on average in October versus 
the S&P 500 down -1% and S&P Equal-Weight Index up +5% (Exhibit 5). Parcels 
were relative outperformers of the subsectors in October at +13% (FDX and UPS 
shares were up +10% and +16%, respectively). Rails and Railcar 
Lessors/Manufacturers also outperformed the transportation subsectors in October 
at +9%/+6%, led by TRN shares +15% within the lessors/manufacturers (CSX at 
+12% for the rails). Trucking was the weakest performing subsector in October with 
TL at -10% (WERN shares down most at -18.5% versus SNDR/KNX shares -8%/-4%) 
and LTL flat (FDXF outperforming the subsector in the month at +15% versus TFII 
down most at -22%), followed by Logistics underperforming the broad transport 
group at +2% (CHRW down most at -16% versus RXO outperforming the subsector 
at +34%). On a YTD share price performance basis through October, we note that 
our Logistics/Rails coverage is up most +28% each (Logistics ex-RXO up 6%), 
followed by Parcels up +22%, Trucking up +13% on average (TL /LTL at 
+6%/+19%) and Railcar Lessors/Manufacturers +1% (vs S&P 500 up +11%). 
Transport subsector multiples: Parcel’s valuation was above its 5-Year P/E multiple 
average of 13.7x as of the end of October at 14.4x, up from 13x as of the end of 
September (Exhibit 11). The average Rail P/E multiple of 21.9x at the end of October 
was up versus the end of September (20.9x), with the multiple above the 5-year 
average of ~19.7x (Exhibit 9); rails are trading above the group’s average historical 
relative P/E versus the S&P 500 (historically 100% of the index’s valuation) given the 
~14% average premium to the index in October (Exhibit 10)—but we note 
bifurcation within the group as the US Railroads trade above their 5-year averages 
(Exhibit 29; Exhibit 23, Exhibit 35), while the Canadian Rails are trading below their 
historical averages (Exhibit 41, Exhibit 47). The Logistics group average P/E of 21.2x 
at the end of October now trades below the group’s five-year average of 22.1x 
(Exhibit 13), potentially due to the legal overhang - with CHRW trading at 17.2x at 
the end of October after the compression from ~22x at the end of September and 
from ~28x in July (Exhibit 65). LTLs’ valuation (ODFL, SAIA, XPO, ARCB, TFII) moved 
further below the group’s +1SD average P/E valuation of 28.7x as of the end of 
October with the group trading close to its 5-yr historical average at 23.4x, as the 
multiple has now compressed ~8-turns compared to the end of June (Exhibit 17). 
Truckload’s average P/E of 16.9x at the end of October (down ~11-turns vs end of 
June) reflects the group now trading below the historical multiple average of ~19.4x

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
