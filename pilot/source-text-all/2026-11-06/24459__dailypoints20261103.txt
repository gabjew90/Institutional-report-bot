RBA hiked, indicated it may be done

US data distractions to include JOLTS, factory orders, vehicle sales 
Mixed signals on the Iran war continue to spark volatility in oil, bonds and stocks on 
midterm election day in the US. After yesterday’s plunge in oil prices, today’s prices are 
slightly higher by 2% or so. That’s enough to pivot bonds back toward higher yields in the 
US but particularly Canada and Europe.  
Stocks are gently lower almost everywhere partly because of oil, partly because markets 
were unimpressed by Apple’s beats as sales growth slows and results in China 
disappointed, and partly because of nervousness toward what happens in the midterms 
and the ensuing aftermath. 
Back to oil. Unquestioning robots believed Trump hook, line and sinker yesterday when he 
did an about-face on attacking Iran. His social media post played up talks and pointed to 
lower oil on the day as prices plunged on the back of his own headlines. Most would see 
through this as an effort to lower energy prices on election day while retaining caution 
toward the ensuing days given Trump’s constant warnings that he could escalate war again 
after the vote. The overnight US attacks on Qeshm Island play to this narrative, as do more 
ships struck by mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Leaning against this is jawboning from Qatar 
that negotiations are ongoing. They’ve been ongoing time and again to no avail as the 
divisions are too great. Possible domestic gridlock raises foreign policy risks in my opinion. 
MIDTERMS—STAGGERED RESULTS TO DRIVE MARKET VOLATILITY 
And then settle in tonight for US midterm results to start rolling as polls close in staggered 
fashion and media outlets are permitted to begin reporting results. The first polls close at 
6pmET in parts of Kentucky and Indiana. More shut at 7pmET (Georgia, Virginia, the part 
of Florida in the eastern time zone, South Carolina and Vermont). Then Ohio, NC and 
West Virginia at 7:30pmET. 8pmET is the biggest single closing period followed by 
9pmET. Polls continue to close at 10pmET and then 11pmET including California. Alaska 
and Hawaii shut after the midnight bell tolls. It’s an inefficient electoral system and any 
close races could be subject to recounts and possible court challenges. It’s also very 
unclear that Trump will accept the results. 
Early results could be misleading, such as the so-called ‘red mirage.’ Markets are likely to 
be volatile as headlines then transition toward counting in true blue country. Unless it’s a 
real blow-out it could take days or weeks to get the full results including any contested 
outcomes. Most pundits and pollsters expect a blue House and possibly blue sweep.

BOC’S MACKLEM WILL PROBABLY FADE POST-HIKE DEVELOPMENTS 
BoC Governor Macklem and SDG Rogers are unlikely to say much of anything new in round one of two rounds of testimony before 
parliamentary committees this week. Macklem’s statement will be released at 11amET and then two hours of scintillating banter will unfold. 
The only news since the BoC’s rate hike decision last Wednesday has included softer than expected monthly GDP that I think he’ll fade in 
relation to forward-looking views, and a drop in oil prices that he’ll probably also fade given mixed signals on next steps in the Iran war. 
RBA HIKED, MAY BE DONE 
The RBA did indeed raise its cash rate target by 25bps to 4.85% in a 6–3 vote as widely expected overnight. Yet the A$ softened and the 
Australian rates curve rallied post-communications. Why? The statement removed reference to doing what’s necessary “including 
increased the cash rate target further if needed.” Governor Bullock said “I’d not be interpreting this as saying we’re not going up. I wouldn’t 
be interpreting it as saying we definitely are going up. We’re basically preserving our options.” Communications further emphasized data 
dependency and the lagging effects of rate increases. Markets are pricing the RBA as basically done after 125bps of hikes with a peak one-
in-four chance of a further hike in 2027Q1 after a hold in December. 
MODEST US DATA RISK 
US data will pass the time while markets await midterm coverage. JOLTS job openings during September (10amET) and an expected jump 
in factory orders for the same month (10amET) will then be followed by an expected dip in vehicle sales by the end of the day.
