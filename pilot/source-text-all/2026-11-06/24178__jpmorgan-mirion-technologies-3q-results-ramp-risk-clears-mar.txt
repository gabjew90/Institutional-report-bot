Style Exposure
We placed Mirion on Positive Catalyst Watch into the print (see here) on backlog 
conversion supporting a material 2H26 step up in organic growth, and this has 
largely unfolded, with FY26 organic revenue growth raised to 7-8% (from 5-7%), 
implying high-teens organic growth in 4Q26 (vs. 1.1%/6.5% in 2Q26 and 3Q26). 
Adj. EBITDA margins, however, landed below both our forecast and consensus, 
driven by Paragon dilution/mix in Nuclear & Safety, one-time costs, and 
incremental AI investment ($4M YTD), though management removed its near-
term 30% margin target. Order momentum remained robust, with Nuclear Power 
orders up 105% ex-M&A, six new build wins in 4Q26 ($80M) not in backlog, and 
backlog covering ~36% of FY27 revenue, above historical averages.
•
Mixed Quarter Shaped by Revenue Growth and Margin Headwinds: 
Mirion delivered a mixed quarter, with revenue strength offset by margin 
pressure. Total revenue was $278M (vs. consensus $276M), comprising ~6.5% 
organic growth, ~18.6% acquisition contribution, and a ~0.6% FX headwind, 
while adjusted EPS of $0.12 was flat YoY (vs. consensus $0.12). Consolidated 
adj. EBITDA margin compressed ~20bps YoY to 23.3% (vs. consensus 
24.4%), as Nuclear & Safety revenue grew ~34% to $194M but margin 
declined ~240bps to 25.7% on Paragon dilution and mix, while Medical 
revenue rose ~6.7% to $84M with margin expanding ~190bps to 37.8%.
•
Revenue Ramp Concerns Eased: Moving to Margins: Heading into the 
quarter, the key concern was whether revenue could ramp into 2H, with 
Nuclear Power implying a strong step-up. With Nuclear & Safety organic 
growth now implied in the high-teens (we estimate ~19%) and 4Q revenue 
~83% covered by backlog (the highest since 2022) we believe this concern has 
moderated. Looking to next year, our ~7.8% organic growth estimate is 36% 
covered by current backlog (figure 12). With revenue visibility improved, we 
believe investor focus now shifts to margins, where the quarter came in light 
on one-time costs and incremental AI spend ($4M YTD).
•
Revised 2028 Margin Target, a Prudent Reset: Margins came in light, with 
both Medical and Nuclear & Safety below internal expectations. One-time 
items (~$1-2M across Paragon and Medical) weighed on results; stripping 
these out implies a ~100bps beat in Medical vs. consensus, though Nuclear & 
Safety remains ~150bps below. Management removed its 30% margin target 
for 2028, citing elevated AI spend and slower Paragon margin realization, a 
move we view as prudent, resetting expectations. We are lowering FY27/FY28 
margins, applying ~50% incremental margins and estimating expansion of 
~180bps/~170bps in FY27/FY28.
•
AI & Cyber Investment Accelerates: Front-End Spending: Mirion is 
leaning further into AI and cybersecurity, with ~$4M of YTD opex already 
exceeding its initial full-year budget; management now expects FY26 costs of 
$5M or more, plus a few million dollars of capex, with further growth into 
2027. The spend drove ~70bps of the quarter's margin headwind, alongside 
~50bps from M&A and ~50bps from one-time items. Management framed the 
investment around data infrastructure, internal productivity, and product 
development, citing it as a key reason for withdrawing the margin target.

Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation
Investment Thesis
Mirion is a global leader in radiation safety technologies, 
supporting 98% of nuclear reactors and the top 100 cancer 
centers worldwide, while commanding a #1 market share in 17 
of 19 markets served. The company’s ~73% recurring revenue 
stream, grounded in decades of customer relationships, provides 
consistency, while a robust pipeline from new builds, SMRs, and 
an expanding installed base drives high-single-digit organic 
growth. As the company optimizes its operations, wins new 
contract deals, and integrates Paragon, we believe the company 
can achieve ~600bps of margin expansion by 2028, fueling 
double-digit EPS growth.  
Valuation
Our $22 Dec 2027 price target is based on 19.5x 2027E EV/
EBITDA, roughly in line with historical averages and a 
premium to current trading levels. Our SOTP and DCF analysis 
reinforces our belief that the company can sustain its historical 
averages, underscored by strong recurring demand, expanding 
nuclear pipeline, and margin expansion.

•
Order Book: Large Awards Build a Multi-Year Runway: Orders delivered the 
standout result, growing 62% YoY excluding M&A to $340M, with total orders up 85% 
to $386M including a $46M Paragon contribution and despite the previously disclosed 
Chinese debooking. Nuclear power orders rose 105% (108% excluding FX, or 134% 
excluding the debooking), while labs and research nearly doubled on two Department of 
Energy awards and medical orders increased ~$19M YoY. Large opportunity awards 
totaled $140M in the quarter ($122M net of the debooking), bringing YTD awards to 
$244M, with no pipeline losses recorded this year. Management is targeting ~$125M of 
additional 4Q awards, of which more than $80M relates to French newbuilds under the 
EDF alliance awaiting EU funding approval. 
•
Backlog Conversion and Cash Generation: Guidance Raised as Leverage 
Improves: Total backlog reached $1.25bn (vs. $816M a year ago), with current-year 
recognition projected at $292M (vs. $212M last year) and 2027 backlog of $443M (vs. 
$285M), together representing ~$735M to be recognized across 4Q26 and FY27. 
Guidance was raised, with total revenue growth of 24-25% (from 22-24%), organic 
growth of 7-8% (from 5-7%), and adjusted free cash flow of $170-185M (from $155-
175M),while  adj. EBITDA was narrowed to $285-292M on margins of 24.7-25.2%. 
•
Model Changes: Revenue Raised, Margin Path Lowered: We are updating our 
estimates to reflect the raised guidance, robust backlog and the removal of FY28 margin 
guidance. On balance, our revisions are net negative on the adjusted EBITDA line, with 
lower margin expansion out to FY28 on incremental AI investment and slower Paragon 
margin improvement, although the demand and revenue growth landscape remains solid. 
We are maintaining our price target at $22, based on 19.5x our FY27 adjusted EBITDA.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
Mirion Technologies (Overweight; Price Target: $22.00)
Investment Thesis
Mirion is a global leader in radiation safety technologies, supporting 98% of nuclear 
reactors and the top 100 cancer centers worldwide, while commanding a #1 market share 
in 17 of 19 markets served. The company’s ~73% recurring revenue stream, grounded in 
decades of customer relationships, provides consistency, while a robust pipeline from new 
builds, SMRs, and an expanding installed base drives high-single-digit organic growth. As 
the company optimizes its operations, wins new contract deals, and integrates Paragon, we 
believe the company can achieve ~600bps of margin expansion by 2028, fueling double-
digit EPS growth.  
Valuation
Our $22 Dec 2027 price target is based on 19.5x 2027E EV/EBITDA, roughly in line with 
historical averages and a premium to current trading levels. Our SOTP and DCF analysis 
reinforces our belief that the company can sustain its historical averages, underscored by 
strong recurring demand, expanding nuclear pipeline, and margin expansion. 
Risks to Rating and Price Target
Nuclear Project Timing: Large RMS and reactor-related orders can be lumpy, with delays 
in government approvals or life extension schedules creating quarterly volatility.
Regulatory Dependencies: Both nuclear safety and medical solutions depend on evolving 
regulations; changes in radiation exposure standards or approval processes could impact 
demand.
Execution on Integration: Acquisitions, such as Certrec and Paragon, require effective 
integration; delays could limit synergy capture.
Geopolitical and Policy Risk: Energy policy shifts, trade tariffs, or defense budget 
reallocations (e.g., NATO, DOE programs) may affect order timing and funding. 
Competition: Large diversified peers such as Thermo Fisher, AMETEK, and Fortive could 
pressure pricing or accelerate digital investments.
Supply Chain and Talent: Securing germanium and specialized talent remains critical; 
disruptions could constrain growth.
Financial Leverage: The recent acquisition of Paragon elevates leverage to ~3.3x on a pro 
forma basis. Higher interest rates on debt could lower our 2026 EPS estimates, and delayed 
deleveraging could constrain capital allocation flexibility.
