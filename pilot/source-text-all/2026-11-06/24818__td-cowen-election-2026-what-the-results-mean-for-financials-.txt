THE TD COWEN INSIGHT
Democrats as of Tuesday night were projected to win the Senate and House. We view this
outcome as negative for our sectors though as we have been writing it will not impact the
ability of regulators to push their de-regulatory agendas over the next two years. Key to watch
now is the lame duck, congressional investigations of Trump, the debt ceiling and populist
legislation like interest rate caps.
Our View
■Debt Ceiling. There will be a push to raise the debt ceiling in the lame duck though we are
dubious it will succeed as we expect Democrats will want to leverage the vote next year to
force Trump to make policy concessions.
■De-Regulation. Bank regulators will advance rules on capital, liquidity and tiering despite
the election. We also expect the SEC and CFTC to remain active including on crypto,
prediction markets and perps. There should not be changes at these agencies.
■Clarity Act. We see Sherrod Brown winning in Ohio as damaging the crypto industry's
political clout as the industry worked to defeat him. We did not think Clarity could advance
over the next two years. This makes us more confident in our prediction.
■More Clarity. Democrats will investigate Trump's crypto entanglements including World
Liberty Financial. We believe the resulting political environment may make it impossible to
advance a version of Clarity that the crypto sector could back.
■Interest Rate Caps. Trump and Sen. Elizabeth Warren agree on capping interest rates.
We view this as a risk though it is likely to be closer to 36% than to the 10% cap Trump has
sought. As such it may impact fintechs more than banks.
■More Pulte. We believe Bill Pulte will remain FHFA director even if he takes other White
House jobs. This means continued risk to FICO, mortgage insurers and title insurers. It also
should mean the expedited refinancing plan advances even if it hurts MBS investors.
■Housing. Rep. Maxine Waters will focus on housing including down payment help and funds
to renovate public housing. We remain dubious it will become law.
■Consumer Protection. We see Elizabeth Warren as the next Senate Banking chair. That
means a focus on the CFPB and consumer protections. It could result in headline risk to
consumer finance companies.
■M&A. We see no impact on bank merger approvals despite Democrats controlling
Congress.
■Confirmations. We do not see Democrats confirming Trump nominees unless Trump fills
Democratic seats at agencies like the FDIC and SEC which Trump is unlikely to do.
■Fed Gov. Lisa Cook. The inability to confirm a replacement may make Trump less inclined to
fire Cook.
