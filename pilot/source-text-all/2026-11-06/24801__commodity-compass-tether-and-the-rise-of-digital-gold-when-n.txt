Tether and the Rise of Digital Gold: When New Money Meets Ancient Gold 
 
◼ 
Gold is no longer the exclusive playground of central banks, ETFs and bullion 
banks. A new generation of fintech firms has emerged as a meaningful source of gold 
demand, leveraging blockchain technology to create digital claims on physical 
bullion. Although tokenized gold remains small relative to traditional gold markets, its 
growth has been exceptional. The 15 largest gold-backed crypto tokens now represent 
more than US$6bn of market capitalization and roughly 45 tonnes of implied gold 
exposure, while aggregate trading volumes surpassed US$150bn year-to-date in 2026, 
already exceeding the full-year record set in 2025. 
◼ 
Yet perspective matters. The global gold market trades roughly 3,000 tonnes per day, 
including around 950 tonnes in spot markets and 2,000 tonnes in derivatives, 
compared with only ~3 tonnes of daily turnover in tokenized gold. Likewise, the entire 
tokenized gold universe represents just 1% of the approximately 4,000 tonnes held by 
physically backed gold ETFs worldwide. Digital gold remains a niche market today, but 
one growing materially faster than traditional investment channels. 
◼ 
The advantages of digital gold. This growth is being driven by structural advantages 
that conventional gold products struggle to match: fractional ownership, 24/7 trading, 
instant settlement and integration into digital financial ecosystems. Some providers 
have gone even further by introducing yield-bearing mechanisms through bullion-
lending programmes, creating a form of gold ownership that offers both price 
exposure and income. 
◼ 
Tether as a case study. Among these new entrants, Tether stands out. The company 
has quietly become one of the world's largest gold holders outside traditional 
institutions. Based on our estimates, Tether's gold holdings reached approximately 
146.5 tonnes as of June 2026, placing it 33rd globally among central-bank-equivalent 
holders, alongside countries such as Libya and ahead of the Philippines. If classified as 
a gold ETF, Tether would rank as the 7th largest gold ETF in the world, requiring only 
around 25 additional tonnes to move into sixth place. 
◼ 
The hybrid role of the Tether Group. We argue that Tether occupies a unique 
position in the gold ecosystem. Unlike ETFs, part of its gold backs a currency-like asset 
ecosystem, while another portion backs its gold token, XAUT. In this sense, Tether 
increasingly resembles a hybrid institution combining characteristics of both a 
reserve-holding central bank and a gold ETF. 
◼ 
Nowcasting Tether Group’s reserves. Tether's next quarterly reserves attestation is 
scheduled for 31 October 2026 and will provide the first official update on the group's 
gold holdings since June. Based on our estimates of balance-sheet growth, XAUT 
issuance activity and gold-price developments during Q3, we forecast that Tether's 
physical gold holdings increased to approximately 161 tonnes by quarter-end. If 
confirmed, this would mark another significant step in Tether's rapid expansion as a 
gold holder, extending the steady rise that has seen the company climb the ranks of 
both institutional and sovereign holders in recent years. Nevertheless, even at 161 
tonnes, Tether would remain the 7th largest gold ETF-equivalent holder globally, 
highlighting both the remarkable pace of its growth and the substantial size of the 
largest established gold investment vehicles. 
◼ 
Looking ahead. The World Gold Council estimates that digital gold could reach 
US$160-170bn by 2031, while also highlighting a longer-term theoretical opportunity 
of US$700bn. We view the latter as an aspirational ceiling rather than a realistic base 
case. Achieving such scale would likely require current digital gold holdings to 
increase by at least eightfold within a few years.

Tether and the Rise of Digital Gold: When New 
Money Meets Ancient Gold  
The rise of fintechs in the gold market 
Among the key participants in the gold market, fintechs are arguably the newest. In just a few years, 
digital asset providers have proliferated, and some have already risen into the ranks of the largest 
holders of physical gold, rivalling central banks and major ETFs and ETCs. 
Not only have their gold holdings grown rapidly, but the pace of accumulation has, at times, exceeded 
both central bank purchases and ETF inflows. Several factors help explain this growth. First, 
tokenized gold products provide retail investors with highly granular exposure, allowing them to 
purchase fractions of a token for only a few dollars. Second, these products trade 24/7, offering 
continuous price discovery and accessibility. Finally, some providers have gone a step further by 
transforming gold-backed tokens into yield-generating assets. By lending the physical gold backing 
their tokens and distributing part of the leasing income to token holders, they have introduced a 
feature traditionally absent from gold ownership. In a period of elevated nominal yields, this feature 
has attracted considerable interest from investors. 
Traditional market venues have begun to respond to this trend. For example, CME launched its 1-
Ounce Gold futures contract in January 2025 and extended trading hours in July 2026, making it a true 
round-the-clock market with 24/7 trading, including weekends. This reflects a broader effort to offer 
retail investors greater flexibility and accessibility while preserving the advantages of regulated futures 
markets. 
Later in this note, we show that digital gold is growing far faster than traditional gold investment 
vehicles. Yet, despite this rapid growth, tokenised gold remains a niche segment of the global gold 
market relative to the size and liquidity of established trading venues. 
As of September 2026, average daily gold trading volumes are estimated at approximately 3,000 
tonnes, comprising around 950 tonnes in the spot market, 2,000 tonnes in derivatives markets, 
and 50 tonnes through gold ETFs. By comparison, gold-backed crypto tokens account for only around 
3 tonnes of daily trading volume. This volume should not be added to total global gold market liquidity, 
as the underlying exposure is likely hedged through established venues such as the OTC market and/or 
the spot market. Similar conclusions emerge when comparing investment holdings. Net central bank 
purchases average roughly 70 tonnes per month, while changes in COMEX gold futures open interest 
average around 105 tonnes per month. On their side, ETFs holdings vary on average by 45 tonnes per 
month.  
Against this backdrop, the 15 largest gold-backed crypto tokens have a combined market 
capitalisation of more than US$6 billion, equivalent to roughly 45 tonnes of implied gold exposure. 
In the next section, we explain the distinction between "tokenised" gold and "vaulted" gold. 
The key point is that, although the sector remains small in absolute terms, investor turnover is 
exceptionally high. Trading volumes of around 3 tonnes per month on a market worth just US$6 
billion, or roughly US$40 billion when vaulted gold is included, are proportionally far larger than those 
observed in traditional gold investment vehicles. By comparison, physically backed gold ETFs trade 
roughly 51 tonnes per day against a stock of around 4,000 tonnes of gold. Similarly, monthly central 
bank purchases of around 70 tonnes are modest relative to official gold reserves of approximately 
36,000 tonnes. 
This highlights an important characteristic of the digital gold ecosystem. While it remains a relatively 
small source of gold demand, it is associated with a much higher velocity of capital than traditional 
channels. In other words, the same ounce of gold changes hands more frequently.

Digital gold 101 
Vaulted digital gold 
Vaulted digital gold gives investors a direct claim on physical bullion held in custody. Ownership is 
recorded in a centralised ledger maintained by the provider, and transactions occur within that 
provider's platform. Examples include BullionVault (US$5bn+) and GoldMoney (US$4bn+).  
Tokenised gold 
Tokenised gold wraps the same economic exposure into a blockchain token. Each token typically 
represents a specified quantity of vaulted gold and can be transferred on public blockchains 
without relying on the operator's internal ledger. The leading examples are Tether Gold (XAUT) with 
a market capitalisation of roughly US$3.25bn and PAX Gold (PAXG) at roughly US$1.8bn. 
 
The risk 
This expansion can be viewed both as an opportunity and a risk. On the one hand, tokenised gold 
broadens access to the gold market and attracts new participants. On the other hand, the high 
turnover suggests that investors in this segment may have shorter holding periods and a more 
tactical mindset than traditional gold investors. Whereas central banks and many ETF investors 
typically hold gold as a long-term reserve or portfolio allocation, participants in digital gold markets 
appear more inclined to trade gold as a liquid financial asset. As a result, this emerging pool of demand 
is likely to be more volatile and more sensitive to price changes than traditional sources of gold 
ownership, particularly amplifying outflows during corrections. 
$700bn? An aspirational ceiling, not a base case 
The World Gold Council suggests digital gold could unlock up to US$700bn of latent demand if 
adoption barriers fall. We treat this cautiously: it's a theoretical upside built from investor surveys. 
The WGC's own base case targets US$160-170bn by 2031, triple today's US$45-55bn market, 
equivalent to roughly 1,235 tonnes at US$4,150/oz, up from about 375 tonnes today. Plausible, if 
ambitious. The US$700bn figure is different: at the same price it implies ~5,250 tonnes, over four 
times the 2031 base case. Using Tether, the largest gold-backed token by assets, as a reference, the 
industry would need to multiply current holdings by at least eight times in five to ten years, far 
beyond anything achieved to date in a segment that still represents less than 1% of the US$13tn 
physical gold investment market. It's a useful ceiling for framing addressable demand, not a base

Tether as a case study 
One major player stands out in the digital asset space: Tether. Not only does it issue the world's 
largest physically backed gold token, but it also operates several stablecoins backed by relatively 
low risk assets, including US Treasuries, secured loans, and gold. In this section, we explore how this 
new category of investor can influence gold prices, if it is not doing so already. 
How does Tether compare to Central Banks? 
In terms of total gold holdings on its balance sheet, Tether's position continues to rise rapidly. When 
we first wrote about the group a year ago, it would have ranked 43rd globally if compared with central 
banks. By February this year, we showed that it had moved up to 36th place. According to the 
company’s June report, Tether ranks 33rd in this notional ranking, with gold holdings comparable to 
those of the Central Bank of Libya and around 13 tonnes higher than those of the Philippines.

We view Tether as a hybrid player, sitting somewhere between a central bank and a gold ETF. Why? 
On the one hand, through its gold token (XAUT), Tether commits to backing each token with one ounce 
of physical gold held in Switzerland. As a result, its gold holdings are directly influenced by investor 
inflows and outflows, much like a gold ETF, where bullion holdings expand or contract in line with 
demand. 
On the other hand, Tether also holds gold on its own balance sheet as part of the reserves backing its 
liabilities, primarily its stablecoins. Gold represents roughly 10% of the group's balance sheet, which 
totals around USD 185 billion. 
As a result, Tether's gold holdings serve two distinct purposes: supporting a currency-like liability 
base and backing a tokenised gold investment product. In our view, this makes Tether a genuine 
hybrid player. It combines characteristics of a central bank, which holds gold as a reserve asset to 
support confidence in its liabilities, with those of a gold ETF, whose bullion holdings are driven by 
investor demand for gold exposure. 
How does Tether compare to ETFs? 
If Tether were treated as a gold ETF, its 161 tonnes (estimated by our model) of gold holdings as of 
30th September would rank it as the 7th largest gold ETF globally. Moreover, a further 7% increase in 
its gold holdings, or 11 tonnes, would be sufficient to move Tether into 6th place, highlighting how 
rapidly its position is strengthening.  
 
Combining both rankings, we estimate that Tether Group is now the 40th largest gold holder 
globally. More importantly, it is far from a passive participant. While the gold holdings of most central 
banks tend to remain broadly stable for years, and sometimes even decades, Tether's pace of 
accumulation has outstripped that of any central bank or gold ETF. For that reason, we described it 
earlier this year as the market's "gold flow monster". 
The significance extends beyond the scale of its purchases. Using the World Gold Council's survey of 
digital gold owners as a proxy1, we estimate that around 65% of these inflows represent new money 
entering the gold market. In other words, a majority of investors buying Tether's gold products 
appear not to have previously owned gold in any investment form. This suggests that Tether is not 
merely competing for a share of existing gold demand but may be expanding the investor base and

We showed earlier that the crypto ecosystem remains a relatively small venue for trading gold-linked 
assets. However, when it comes to the accumulation of physical bullion, it has been the fastest-
growing segment of the gold market. 
Since early 2023, gold ETF holdings have increased by an average of 13.6% per year, while central 
bank gold reserves have grown by 1.8% per year. Over the same period, Tether's gold holdings have 
expanded by 31% per year, making it by far the fastest-growing corner of the gold market. 
In absolute terms, Tether ranks 5th in cumulative gold purchases since 2023, having added 87.5 
tonnes. By comparison, only 1 ETF and 3 central banks accumulated more gold over the same period.  
 
Nowcasting Tether Group’s reserves 
Our model reconstructs Tether Group's daily gold (and broader reserve) holdings by converting each 
quarterly attested dollar figure into a number of units of a market-priced proxy (spot gold for 
Precious Metals, total-return indices for other asset classes). Between two attestations, holdings are 
linearly interpolated in proportion to the growth of the group's total balance sheet over the quarter (a 
"flow" assumption, falling back to straight calendar-time interpolation when the balance sheet barely 
moved), so the reconstructed path reflects when money came in, not just a straight line between two 
points. 
Nowcasting beyond the last attestation. Once there is no next attestation to anchor to, every other 
asset class is extrapolated at its trailing-quarter trading pace, but gold receives a sharper treatment 
because XAUT, an on-chain token backed 1:1 by physical gold, gives a live daily signal of actual gold 
purchases. The nowcast splits the gold move into two legs: a backing leg equal to the raw day-by-day 
change in XAUT supply (since minting is instantaneous and fully collateralized, this leg is never 
smoothed), and a residual leg capturing the rest of the group's gold buying (estimated historically 
as a stable multiple of XAUT issuance), which is smoothed over a short rolling window so that a single 
large, lumpy mint does not get misread as an implausible one-day jump in total reserves.

The $500mn XAUT mint 
Q3 2026 was also marked by a significant expansion in XAUT supply. On 29 September, Tether issued 
roughly 119,700 new gold-backed tokens, corresponding to nearly US$500mn of additional gold-
linked exposure. Although the newly minted tokens were initially transferred to treasury inventory 
rather than directly entering circulation, the transaction nevertheless represented one of the largest 
single expansions of XAUT supply to date. Beyond its immediate impact on the token's market 
capitalization, the issuance highlights both the growing scale of the tokenized gold market and 
Tether's willingness to expand capacity ahead of anticipated investor demand. If sustained, such 
issuance activity would further reinforce Tether's position as one of the largest and fastest-growing 
sources of private-sector gold demand.
