A few quick thoughts on the market impact of the US mid-term elections: 
1.       Risk positive. We agree with the rebound in global equities and risky FX currencies overnight. The 
combination of a large Democratic majority in Congress and President Trump’s statement that he is willing 
to work with the opposition should act as a break on extreme policy outcomes and bring down risk premia. 
The market is increasingly likely to interpret surprising policy shifts from the Executive as time-bound by the 
next Presidential election or the new domestic political configuration. 
2.       No news is good news on Iran. Over the last 48 hours, President Trump stated that no attack on Iran 
is imminent and has acknowledged that the Iran war drove part of the election outcome. We have been 
writing that with oil flows through the Middle East having materially recovered, the market is pricing a large 
imminent risk premium of re-escalation and energy supply disruption. There is therefore negative time decay 
on the oil price, the more escalation is delayed, the bigger the downward pressure on oil. 
3.       All about the debt ceiling (and AI) now. The single biggest source of domestic policy uncertainty 
remaining in the US is the debt ceiling that will become binding by Q2 of next year. It is reported that 
Republicans aim to pass a debt ceiling increase via reconciliation over the next few weeks. If this succeeds, it 
will remove one additional layer of uncertainty on fiscal policy, while also preventing AI and other policy 
matters from getting bundled up in a protracted negotiation with a Democratic Congress. Irrespectively, 
question marks around the build-out of AI will likely persist. 
On balance, we see political developments overnight as supportive of a continued bottoming out in 
EUR/USD: lower energy and global policy risk premium but higher AI implementation risks should on balance 
weaken the dollar. If Congress fails passing a debt ceiling increase this year, we would consider it an added 
dollar negative.
