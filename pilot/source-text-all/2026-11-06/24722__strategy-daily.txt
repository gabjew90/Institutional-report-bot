The midterms delivered the blue sweep markets had increasingly moved towards, with Democrats taking 
control of both chambers of Congress. The initial reaction has been duration-positive and risk-off. 10Y UST
yields fell around 7bp towards 4.90% before giving some back as equities recovered, while the dollar has been hit 
harder, with DXY now just below 101.50. For rates, we think the first read is lower fiscal optionality through the 
remainder of Trump's term, but we would be careful chasing the rally too far as we see risk of Trump flipping to 
“deal-making mode” and keep the fiscal expansion alive. 4.90% remains the key support level in 10Y yields, which 
stands at odds with yesterday's strong JOLTS print and oil still around $104pb (the latter two adding less 
constructive duration drivers). Overnight Iran-related headlines continue to be mixed. Trump has played down 
the prospect of an immediate escalation, but Iran remains far from a deal and disruption risks around Hormuz have 
not disappeared. Today should quickly move the focus back from politics to macro and supply. ISM 
services is the main data point, but the QRA will be the one to watch, especially for the curve. We expect 
coupon sizes to remain unchanged, with incremental funding needs still pushed into bills. There is a tail risk of cuts
to 20Y issuance (not our base case). Given that the 20Y point looks rich on the curve, the lack of changes could 
make this part of the curve vulnerable. Delaying coupon increases can work for now, but simply shortening the 
maturity profile is not a durable answer to the fiscal imbalance. 
EGBs should take their direction initially from the post-election rally in USTs and softer risk backdrop, 
although the combination of oil still around $104/bbl and an ECB that remains uncomfortable with inflation 
should limit how far Bunds can follow. Yesterday's move again showed just how sensitive the market remains 
to Iran headlines, with 10Y Bund yields swinging sharply before ultimately finishing around 3bp higher. EUR/USD 
has meanwhile benefited from the broad dollar sell-off and is trading around 1.1240. Today we get October 
Eurozone CPI alongside an updated ECB wage tracker, with the latter particularly important given markets 
continue to price a high probability of another hike in December, alongside another 25bp in H1-27. The 
tracker is explicitly designed to give a forward-looking read on negotiated wage pressures, and the ECB has 
repeatedly stressed that they are watching wages to assess potential second-round effects. Softer wages/CPI 
would give duration some room to follow USTs, but sticky underlying inflation would quickly put the focus back on 
December and keep us cautious on the front end. Supply-wise, Germany taps DBRs May41 and Aug48 
(combined €2bn).  
Gilts finally have a quieter domestic calendar ahead of tomorrow's BoE meeting, leaving the market caught 
between the rally in USTs following the Democratic sweep and the still-hawkish UK rates backdrop. 
Yesterday's sell-off took 10Y Gilt yields to 5.25%, with Gilts underperforming Bunds. Thursday remains the 
bigger event. A 25bp BoE hike is effectively priced, meaning the debate is increasingly about the path beyond 
this meeting rather than whether the MPC delivers now. Markets still price considerably more tightening than 
economists. For today, final services PMI is unlikely to change the narrative materially. GBP rates are likely 
to be driven mainly by USTs, oil and positioning into the MPC. GBP/USD continues to be well bid alongside the 
broader dollar move and is trading around 1.3480.  
Mixed session in Asian equities, with a rough start due to the US midterms news but some recovery later 
on. JGBs didn’t follow USTs and bear-steepened instead after a weak round of Rinban. USD/JPY experienced a 
sharp fall in the early Asian hours, testing 157.50, before retracing back above 158.
