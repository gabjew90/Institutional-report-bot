Europe Open - Next phase
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Post-midterms moves continue with USD retaining losses, USTs higher. Choppy in US equity futures with Nasdaq
extending weakness again, with regional divergence in Asia (Kospi down -5%, China stocks outperform). Brent remains
lower on the day with no notable headlines since VP Vance comments which drove the initial knee-jerk reaction. Our
experts still see US/Iran escalation risk in the next few months and remain cautious of extrapolating current moves. 
BoE is the main event meeting at 12:00 GMT with expectations of a 25bp rate hike in a 7-2 vote split. Watch out for US
weekly jobless claims trend (13:30 GMT), SNB Schlegel also on the docket. Lots of moving parts in EM with two rate
decisions ahead - Czech (25bp hike) and Banxico (hold). BNM just held rates. Taiwan CPI prints shorty.

US Midterms: Cross Asset First Take (REPLAY)
Tactical Longs, Structural Shorts; Stretched Richness
In Focus‌
Commodities: After a near 9% selloff in oil prices, front-end contracts retain downside, Brent trading at $94 at print. A knee-
jerk reaction lower in the late European evening was driven by US VP Vance who said that voter frustration over the war in
Iran and the US-Israel relationship was the reason for the Democrats' landslide victory at US midterms. "The president
himself said so last night,". Further rhetoric from Senate Minority Leader Schumer who said that "some Republicans have
joined us" aided moves further. New Oil Monitor from Citi Research writes that the Democratic blue wave has added to US-
Iran dealmaking momentum, though re-escalation risks remain in the near-term. The USS Theodore Roosevelt aircraft carrier
is set to arrive in the Middle East by the end of November, which could mean a lull before its arrival, followed by renewed
escalation risk. Our base case (60% probability) remains for a gradual de-escalation before year-end and into next year, with
a more sustained decline in Brent towards $70/bbl in 2027. A bull case (30%) sees Brent prices back to $120. Meanwhile
gold retains gains and is up a further 1.3% overnight. 
Rates: Antipodeans and JGBs play catch up, rallying thanks to energy price downside, USTs also extend their rally with US
10s trading at 4.845%. BoJ Sep minutes indicated that policymakers agreed that further interest rate hikes were needed,
reinforcing expectations of a Dec hike. Monetary policy was not discussed in RBA Hauser's commentary, instead he focused

on public trust. ICYMI - US Rates Strategy - Q4 refunding - Treasury does not deliver long-end support. Treasury kept
nominal coupon sizes unchanged, but revised guidance to open the door for an increase in late 2027 (our base case is fEB),
We expect 20y and 30y sizes to be unchanged when Treasury does eventually grow sizes. 
Equities: Another slide in Kospi, trading as low as -5.28% overnight but as usual, AI/Semi unwinds typically result in
rotation/shorts squeeze in HK/China stocks with Hang Seng up 3.55%. Nasdaq futures flip negative once again with SPX e-
minis losses more modest. The midterms overhang might have been removed, but this was already embedded in the price.
For now, oil prices and AI sentiment will determine whether we will see a year-end rally. "Don't expect a "coast is clear" per
fundamental readthroughs as that will require more concrete policy next steps", our US Equity Strategy team warn. It remains
premature to presume policy regimes translate into stronger S&P 500 fundamentals.
FX: USD remains on the backfoot but our eTrading desks notes USD buying by real money names overnight. G10 volumes
were -3% lower than 30d averages, with leveraged names buying JPY, NZD and CAD versus selling EUR, CNH and HKD.
Despite choppy moves cross-asset, G10 vols have been largely softer. Our options desk have seen fresh interest in putting
on lower USD gamma trades. USDJPY is somewhat uninspiring though cross-JPY expressions remain more interesting.
AUDNZD continues to outperform as AUD and NZD idiosyncratic macro narratives have dominated the cross directionally. FX
Strategy says to be cautious taking signals from post midterms moves. They still want to fade USD weakness, especially
against EUR. US/Iran escalation risk can re-emerge and they look to re-engage EURNOK downside which they took profit on
yesterday. Dig deeper. 
Elsewhere‌
EUR: Germany Factory Orders for Sep prints in line with cons. expectations at 3.3% YoY. ECB's Zigman speaks in Croatia at
08:30 GMT.
SEK: Oct CPIF prints flat MoM with YoY coming at 1.1% from 1.5% prior, ex energy coming at just 0.1%. Overall, a dovish
print and is in line with our economists only expecting one more hike, and that too in early 2027. 
NOK: Deposit Rate Decisions at 09:00 GMT. With 1bp priced in, wide consensus is for a rate hold. Our STIRT desk doesn’t
expect a hike in December either, given their clear communication this year, but at 9.25bp priced in the step, the risk-reward
profile is not favorable to us currently.
GBP: Bank of England Bank Rate Decision at 12:00 GMT. We expect the MPC will vote to raise Bank Rate to 4.0%, with the
most likely scenario being a 7-2 in favor of tightening (although 6-3 also possible). MPC may push back against market
pricing, though a second hike is likely in Feb 2027. Full BoE Preview: The reluctant hike. Our London traders have been long
GBP - with cable trading comfortably above the 200dMA at 1.3426 and there remains plenty of room for longs to build. Year-
end pricing still looks rich, but he struggles to see how today's meeting will create a meaningful obstacle for longs. 
CHF: SNB's Schlegel Speaks in Zurich at 12:40 GMT. His comments post CPI could be interesting.
USD: Watch trends in weekly jobless claims at 13:30 GMT. Fed's Hammack speaks on Panel in Zurich at 13:50 GMT,
followed by Barkin at 14:10 GMT.
 
Best of the rest:
BRL: Copom held rates in line with expectations, warning a tough outlook with weaker growth, faster inflation and high
uncertainty. Looking ahead, as the recent deterioration in market inflation expectations should prove more persistent, our
economists see Copom on hold in coming meetings. Keep an eye on Sep IP at 12:00 GMT. Our economists see production at
0.50 vs. -0.60 in August. 
TWD: CPI YoY at 08:00 GMT for October. Our economists expect headline and core inflation both moderating to 2.4%YoY in
October, from 2.7% YoY and 2.5% YoY, respectively, in September.
CZK: Repurchase Rate Decision at 13:30 GMT. Against the backdrop of rising inflationary pressures and core inflation moving
back above 3%, our economists expect the CNB to raise the policy rate by 25bp to 4%. Prior to the rate decision, our

economists expect retail sales for September (out at 08:00 GMT) to print 4.8% YoY from 4.3% YoY prior, supported by
continued strength in household consumption.
UYU: CPI MoM at 17:00 GMT for October. Headline inflation is projected to exceed 5% in early 2027 due to geopolitical and
climate supply shocks before converging to the 4.5% target in 2H27, with core inflation well contained.
MXN: Overnight Rate Decision at 19:00 GMT. Our economists expect Banxico to keep the policy rate unchanged at 6.50%.
Since the September meeting, the case for additional easing has weakened. Recent activity indicators suggest a faster
closing of the output gap, the currency depreciated, while core inflation has recently re-accelerated. This suggests dovish
risks to the statement.
COP: Colombia Monetary Policy Minutes due at 22:00 GMT.
MYR: BNM held rates in line with Citi expectations. 
PHP: Oct CPI printed at 7.8% YoY, tad lower than 8.1% Citi expectations but stronger than 7.2%YoY in September.
IDR: Q3 GDP beat estimates slightly coming at 5.47%YoY, broadly stable from 5.3%YoY in Q2 2026.
