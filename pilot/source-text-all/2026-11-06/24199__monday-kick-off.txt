First a quick recap:
Last week, markets continued to be dominated by the AI investment cycle, but the narrative shifted from enthusiasm about still-powerful demand to growing concern over the scale and cost of financing it. Early in the week, Anthropic’s filing, record
results from SK hynix, and strong Azure growth at Microsoft reinforced the view that AI demand remains exceptionally strong, helping semiconductors and parts of the broader technology complex recover. That optimism faded, however, as muted
reactions to Alphabet and Amazon brought investor focus back to free cash flow, leverage, and capex intensity, highlighting how the market is becoming more sensitive to how the AI build-out is being funded, not just to its revenue promise. US
equities closed lower across the board Friday, albeit off their worst levels, with the AI trade driving the move and the Nasdaq posting its worst session since June. Technicals however reflected more of an internal rotation than wholesale derisking,
with 9 of the 11 S&P 500 sectors higher for the week. US Treasuries were stronger, but gold finished down 3.3%, falling back below USD 4,000 and posting its worst day since June. Crude was up 2.9%, albeit falling again this morning on hopes around
Iran war talks entering a third day.
The broader macro backdrop remained relatively supportive. US Q3 GDP came in at 3.4%, and September PCE inflation at 3.6% YoY, reinforcing the picture of resilient growth alongside still-sticky inflation. In a busy week for global policymakers, the
Fed held rates at 3.75%–4.00%, the ECB stayed on hold while retaining a tightening bias, and the BoJ left policy unchanged while declaring its 2% inflation target met for the first time since 2013 (more in the sections below). Data out over the weekend
showed China NBS Manufacturing PMI slipped into contraction territory in October.
Price action reflected that uneasy mix of growth resilience, policy restraint, and AI funding anxiety. Semiconductor and technology names were firmer early in the week, but the tone deteriorated as the week progressed, with Intel falling sharply on
weak guidance and Asian equities also coming under pressure. Politics and geopolitics added to the complexity: Brazilian assets sold off sharply after Lula’s victory and its contested aftermath, Israel’s inconclusive election revived coalition
uncertainty, and Brent rose back above USD 115/bbl amid renewed Gulf tensions, tanker disruptions, and fresh strikes around Saudi energy infrastructure.
Overall, the week left markets facing a sharper version of a familiar contrast: growth remains resilient, but investors are becoming increasingly uneasy about the cost of financing the AI boom, just as inflation stays sticky, central banks remain
hawkish, and geopolitical risks keep energy prices elevated.

Precious under pressure
The Technical View
Persistently high real yields and a firmer US dollar have continued to weigh on non-yielding assets, while short-term momentum has weakened across the metals complex. Even so, the longer-term technical structures have so far been tested rather
than broken, with gold and silver remaining the relative anchors and flows showing early signs of improvement at the margin. The next phase for precious metals will depend on whether these structural support areas can hold in a higher-for-longer
rate environment.

Central banks hold
A global pause, not an end to tightening
Last week’s sweep of central bank meetings reflected a globally hawkish message, although, at the October meetings, policymakers favoured patience over immediate action. The Federal Reserve kept rates unchanged on Wednesday, with two officials
dissenting in favour of a hike. While Chair Warsh avoided forward guidance, nothing pushed back against another increase in December. At the same time, the Fed maintained a relatively benign view of the growth-inflation mix, arguing that strong
growth is not necessarily inflationary and that financial conditions, while tighter, are not yet restrictive. By the end of the week, markets were pricing a 74% probability of a December hike, followed by at least another two next year.
In Europe, the ECB held its deposit rate at 2.5% on Thursday, as expected, following September’s 25bp increase. Inflation has risen to 3.8%, driven by energy prices, but President Christine Lagarde noted that the ECB was not yet seeing additional
second-round effects, while underlying inflation and wage growth remained broadly stable. Markets nevertheless interpreted the decision as a hawkish pause, pricing a December hike with an 85% probability and an additional two in 2027. Without
mentioning France directly, the ECB president did warn highly indebted countries to keep spending tightly within fiscal rules, as financing costs could remain elevated, while another comment reminded the room that tools exist to deal with
disorderly market movements, helping keep the French 10-year yield below 4.90% by the end of the day.
The Bank of Japan also delivered a hawkish hold, keeping rates at 1.25% in an 8-1 decision. Crucially, the BoJ now sees underlying inflation as “generally consistent” with its 2% target and raised its FY2027 inflation forecasts, reinforcing confidence in a
more durable wage-price cycle. Further normalisation therefore remains likely, although weak economic momentum argues for a gradual path.
Finally, the surprise came from New Zealand, where the RBNZ unexpectedly held rates at 2.75% in a close 4-3 vote, despite markets pricing a hike at more than 90% beforehand. While the central bank retained a tightening bias, the surprise pause
triggered a sharp repricing lower in December expectations before they rebounded to around 75%, alongside lower New Zealand yields and a weaker NZ dollar.
Overall, the meetings reinforced a common theme: central banks continue to lean towards further tightening, but are increasingly willing to wait for incoming data before taking the next step.

November seasonality
Will the midterms reinforce the seasonal pattern?
November has historically been one of the stronger months for US equities. While midterm years have typically brought greater volatility ahead of election day, the month itself has often proved more constructive thereafter. Rates, gold and oil,
however, have so far this year diverged meaningfully from their usual seasonal patterns.

The Equity quantitative grades
Our equity quant process is one by which we rank and score the stocks within our equity universe according to various short-term (i.e.
EPS growth) long-term (i.e. quality) and positioning (i.e. technical) characteristics, and on relative and/or historical basis as
appropriate. The various scores make up a stock’s total or ‘global' grade’:Growth Grade: The Growth Grade is a proprietary formula
made up of earnings revision momentum, past earnings growth, earnings stability, and current and long-term earnings growth.  A
grade above 55 is considered bullish on a 3-month basis, bearish below 45 and neutral between 55 and 45.
EPS Grade: The EPS Grade is a proprietary formula consisting of current and forward EPS growth, change and surprise data.  An EPS grade
above 60 or below 40 is considered predictive for future out/under performance.Sales Grade: The Sales Grade is a proprietary formula made
up of current and next year’s sales momentum, past sales growth, sales stability, and current and long-term sales growth. A grade above 55 is
considered bullish on a 3-month basis, bearish below 45, and neutral between 55 and 45.
Value Grade: The Value Grade is a proprietary formula made up of estimated P/E, P/B, P/S and P/CF ratios. 40% of the grade is based on
historical values and 60% on current market data.  A grade above 55 suggests a stock is cheap, below 45 expensive, and neutral between 55
and 45.
Quality Grade: The Quality Grade is a proprietary formula that focuses on the balance sheet (i.e. change in accruals, change in free cash flows
and profitability). A grade above 55 suggests a healthy balance sheet.Credit Grade: The Credit Grade focusses on the passive side of the
balance sheet.  It is divided into three sub-components to assess both short and long-term solvency.  A grade above 55 suggests a strong
capital structure, while a grade below 45 suggests a weak one.
Money Flow Grade: The Money Flow Grade is a proprietary formula that gives the accumulation/distribution based on the volume flows of a
stock. A grade above 55 indicates good money flow and a grade below 45 suggests weak money flow.Smart Sentiment Grade: The Smart
Sentiment grade is a contrarian indicator based on investor positioning measures such as the days-to-cover ratio, the put-call ratio, and the
short-interest ratio.  A weak grade suggests ‘too much’ optimism.
Relative Strength (RS) Grade: The RS grade measures the price momentum of a stock over its 1-year price performance.
Global Grade: The Global Grade is a weighted average of the Growth, EPS Sales, Value, Quality, Credit, Money Flow and Smart Sentiment
Grades.
Trend Score
A normalized composite of 10 underlying indicators that look at price relative to key moving averages, moving average trajectories and
crosses, and overbought/sold condition. Absolute and Relative scores range from -10 (worst) to +10 (best).
The Exposure Optimisation Model
The PTS Exposure Optimisation Model (EOM) is a proprietary model designed to give tactical signals on the prevailing short-term
regional equity market regime. The model is applied to each market on a mutually-exclusive basis, with output expressed by way of
exposure scores (US; Europe; Japan and EM); in each case an average of underlying factors scores with dynamic weighting applied
according to the prevailing structural market volatility regime. Exposure score ratings are: very bullish (80-100%); bullish (60-80%);
neutral (40-60%); bearish (20-40%); and very bearish (20-0%). Factors are as follows:
Regime: A combination of technical indicators, moving averages and a Constant Proportion Portfolio Insurance (CPPI) strategy, the regime
score is designed to reflect short-term market momentum in the region;
Valuation: Built around the forward price-to-sales ratio, the dynamic valuation score is based on a calculation that is re-set on bull/bear
market regime changes and calibrated to facilitate regional comparison;
Economics: The economics score draws on real economy activity data (i.e. heavy truck sales and shipping traffic), economic signals from
financial markets (i.e. yield-curves) and medium-term economic indicators (i.e. PMIs and unemployment data); 
Flows: The flows score draws upon both active and passive weekly EPFR fund flows data giving us an indication of risk appetite going into the
week ahead; 
Market Sentiment: The market sentiment score is built around classic regional market risk indices (i.e. VIX; ARMS) as well as soft survey data
(i.e. U.of Michigan Consumer Sentiment). The scoring system is applied in a similar fashion at both regional and market level, while at sector
level we refer only to the Regime, Flows and Valuation factors.

Relative Strength Index (RSI): A momentum oscillator that measures the speed and change of price movements. It ranges from 0 to 100 and
is typically used to identify overbought or oversold conditions in a market. An RSI above 70 suggests that a stock may be overbought, while an
RSI below 30 indicates that it may be oversold.
The Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD): A trend-following momentum indicator that shows the relationship between two
moving averages. It is calculated by subtracting the 26-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) from the 12- period EMA. The result is the
MACD line. A nine-day EMA of the MACD, called the signal line, is then plotted on top of the MACD line, which can function as a trigger for
buy or sell signals. When the MACD crosses above the signal line, it is typically considered a bullish signal, and when it crosses below, it is
considered bearish.
Ichimoku Cloud Technical Analysis: Ichimoku Cloud is a popular technical analysis tool used to identify potential trends and
support/resistance levels in financial markets. It consists of several components, including the Kumo (cloud), the Tenkan-sen (conversion
line), the Kijun-sen (base line), and Senkou Span (leading span).
