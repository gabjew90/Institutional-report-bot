Ahead of YETI’s 3Q results, we refresh our brand momentum 
trackers and address key debates. 
Bottom line: While our trackers point to healthy underlying brand 
metrics, recent US demand indicators have become sequentially 
more muted. Our retailer conversations remained constructive on 
the brand but also highlighted a softer start to 4Q. Bestseller 
rankings, search momentum and observed transaction trends were 
also mixed. That said, YETI did reaffirm its FY26 guidance at its late 
September Investor Day, limiting risk to F2H. International 
momentum also remains strong. Against this backdrop and given 
toughening prior year comparisons, we believe the growth 
momentum of the US and drinkware businesses in 3Q and 4Q will be 
critical for stock performance. Beyond sales momentum, we believe 
focus on the call will center on YETI’s margin comments into 2027, 
particularly the degree that Project Upcycle cost cuts can offset 
inflation in input costs, freight, and the potential move in tariffs back 
to higher rates.  
Key debate

has outlined expectations for a return to US drinkware growth in 
their mid-term plan. We believe the cadence of recovery of the 
US drinkware business in 3Q and 4Q of this year will be critical to 
stock performance.

Innovation pipeline broad, but how are bags performing? YETI has a broad 
n
portfolio of newly launched innovation spanning Drinkware, bags/soft coolers, 
cases/storage, home, and performance apparel. Recent and upcoming launches 
include phone cases, additional gear/case form factors, new bag offerings and 
four-wheel luggage, while YETI is beginning a controlled test of technical apparel 
focused primarily on midlayers and outerwear. We believe the success of new 
categories will be closely watched given the importance of the bags category in 
driving future growth. On this, our checks this quarter for the bags category were 
more mixed and search interest remains limited vs. core categories.  
International growth robust and led by Europe: International remains an 
n
important growth driver, with management pointing to particularly strong 
momentum in Europe as the business gains scale through both door expansion and 
higher existing door productivity. We expect more consistency in F2H as a result of 
expanded category launches, tailwinds from Europe and Asia, and more consistency 
in corporate sales.  
FY27 margin comments critical for the quarter: Cost pressure continues to build 
n
with inflation in stainless steel, resins/oil derivatives, transportation, and logistics. 
Tariffs are also uncertain into FY27 - though YETI’s current FY26 guide 
conservatively models a return to ~20% rates. As we move into 2027, we expect 
management’s commentary regarding the pace of Project Upcycle cost savings 
realization, potential pricing, and other margin improvement drivers will be critical 
as investors contemplate the annualization of F2H cost headwinds. We believe YETI 
can deliver an algorithm margin expansion year as Project Upcycle savings flow, 
though it is not clear if management will guide to algorithm levels to begin the fiscal 
year.  
Brand Momentum Checks and Industry Trends 
Channel checks modestly more mixed this quarter, though brand momentum still 
healthy. Our October independent retailer conversations were generally positive on 
customer impressions and largely consistent with prior-quarter sales expectations, 
although most retailers noted a softer start to 4Q. Separately, YETI purchase intent has 
recently tracked in line with peers, while NPS remains above the peer set and consumer 
perceptions of durability, quality and warranty have improved relative to peers.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
