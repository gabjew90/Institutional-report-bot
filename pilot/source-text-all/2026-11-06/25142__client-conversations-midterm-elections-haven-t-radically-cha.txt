Politicians haven’t completely re-engineered the 
economy or dramatically altered economic growth
Proof points
•	 Investors are often concerned that 
elected officials will drastically 
change the economy. Yet the 
composition of the US economy 
has remained fairly consistent for 
decades.
•	 Economic activity, represented 
by the median annualized 
quarterly percent change in US 
GDP, has been remarkably similar 
during single-party and divided-
government rule.
•	 Despite the focus on the midterms, 
monetary policy often matters 
more for markets. Politicians are 
often helped or hurt by Federal 
Reserve efforts to ease or tighten 
financial conditions.

after midterms regardless of prevailing party

Proof points
•	 Since 1960, the president’s political 
party has lost 22 seats in the US 
House, on average, during the 
midterms. The only presidents who 
led their party to House gains were 
Bill Clinton in 1998 and George W. 
Bush in 2002.
•	 The president’s approval rating in 
the quarter before the midterm 
election has often pointed to 
how their party will perform. Low 
approval ratings have tended to 
result in political change. President 
Trump’s 41% average second-term 
approval rating suggests that 
Republicans could potentially lose 
control of the House.
•	 The stock market has posted 
positive returns in the calendar year 
following midterms for nearly 65 
years, regardless of the election 
outcome.

Markets have performed well under single-party 
rule and different types of divided government
Proof points
•	 Markets have historically tended 
to generate their weakest returns 
in a president’s second year, 
which is the year leading up to the 
midterms. 
•	 Markets have posted strong 
average returns in year three of the 
presidential cycle, directly after 
the midterms. 
•	 A common misconception 
states that markets prefer 
divided government. However, 
performance the year after 
the midterms has tended to 
be strong regardless of the 
election outcome. Historically, 
markets have performed well 
under single-party rule and 
different combinations of divided 
government.
