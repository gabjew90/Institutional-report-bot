Webinar: Rates outlook 2027 – The
Good, the Bad, and the Ugly
Could the US 10-year Treasury hit 6% in 2027? And could markets be too
relaxed about euro rates? AI-driven growth, stubborn inflation and
extraordinary government borrowing could keep global rates under
pressure, while France’s debt challenge adds another potential fault line
in Europe. Join ING experts to explore what could drive rates in 2027 
The US 10-year Treasury hitting 6% in 2027 is not as far-fetched as it sounds. AI and
productivity, extraordinary fiscal deficits, and stubborn inflation could combine to keep global
rates under upward pressure. But which force will ultimately dominate? What could Treasury
Secretary Bessent do if yields surge? And what if inflation proves stickier than market
expectations suggest? Or is all that overblown, and we land in a less dramatic place? We
explore the forces that could drive each scenario.
In the eurozone, the inflation shock may be changing rather than disappearing – is it, and if so,
what are the implications? To what extent could second-round inflation pressures, heavy bond
supply and rising global real rates complicate the outlook, even if the ECB turns more dovish?
Meanwhile, France’s growing debt challenge adds another source of uncertainty for euro
sovereign markets. How bad can this get for bond markets and rates? Will bond supply finally
test investor appetite?
