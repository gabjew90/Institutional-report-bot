post-GeoPura acquisition and outlined its medium-term framework. We think evolution from traditional fuel-cell equipment sales and

Regulatory Updates
Trump administration issues proclamation on wind turbines: President Donald Trump issued a proclamation declaring imports of
wind turbines and related components a potential threat to U.S. national security following a Department of Commerce investigation
conducted under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act. The administration cited concerns related to cybersecurity vulnerabilities
in foreign-made equipment, potential interference with radar and air traffic systems, and growing reliance on imported wind turbine
components. Importantly, the proclamation does not impose new tariffs or trade restrictions at this time. Instead, the administration
directed trade officials to begin negotiations with trading partners and report back within 180 days, with additional actions, including
potential tariffs, remaining under consideration.  Source
Company & Market Updates
GOOG builds on VPP with Voltus: GOOG and Voltus announced an agreement to develop a 1 GW VPP in PJM, aggregating flexible
capacity from residential, commercial, and industrial customers, including batteries, smart thermostats, distributed generation, and
demand response resources. Under the structure, GOOG will fund the capacity, while Voltus will dispatch participating resources during
periods of grid stress and provide bill savings and direct payments to customers. The agreement expands on a three-year, 100 MW
deal the two companies signed in June, as well as extending the commitment through 2040. The initiative is expected to ramp to full
capacity over the next three to four years and reflects growing interest from large-load customers in securing non-traditional sources

SEDG — Nexis IslandDER gains approvals from PG&E and SDG&E: SEDG's Nexis IslandDER meter collar has been approved
for use in the service territories of PG&E and SDG&E, expanding deployment opportunities for the Company's residential solar and
energy storage platform across two major CA utility service territories. Meter collars are installed between a utility meter and the meter
socket in a residential installation and enable a direct, two-way electrical connection between DERs and the homes at which they
are installed. Including the Nexis IslandDER, four meter socket adapters solutions have been approved by PG&E and five have been
approved by SDG&E. The Nexis IslandDER is also scheduled to complete witness testing with SCE.  Source
BE — Project Jupiter air quality permit hearing set for December 7, 2026: The NMED scheduled a December 7 hearing on
the air quality permit for Project Jupiter's proposed 2.45 GW BE microgrid. The hearing follows NMED-requested technical updates
that produced a ~2% increase in estimated emissions but roughly doubled modeled ambient pollutant concentrations vs. the original
analysis, raising questions around the existing permit application. In response, ORCL and YGI proposed a YGI-developed refinement
to the microgrid design that includes an enforceable cap on fuel-cell operating output, which is expected to reduce permitted emissions
by ~35%, while maintaining planned DC IT capacity, with no indicated reduction to the previously announced 2.45 GW of BE capacity.
The December 7 hearing is likely to establish the evidentiary record around the revised emissions modeling and YGI's proposed
operating cap as part of the permit determination, with construction of the microgrid unable to proceed until the permit is issued.
Source, Source, Source
PLUG — PLUG appoints former BLDP COO to lead engineering industrialization: PLUG appointed Dr. Lee Sweetland as Global
Engineering Industrialization Lead, where he will focus on scaling PEM stack manufacturing, improving reliability, and reducing costs.
Sweetland spent eight years at BLDP, most recently as COO, where he focused on cost reduction, capital projects, and manufacturing
scale-up.  Source, Source
FCEL — FCEL receives ~99.9% PJM capacity accreditation for Block System: FCEL's Block System received a ~99.9% unit-
specific ELCC rating from PJM for the 2028/29 RBP process, meaning ~99.9 MW of every 100 MW evaluated may be recognized
as dependable capacity. FCEL did not disclose the specific project evaluated, though we believe it could potentially relate to the Fit
Energy data center campus in Luzerne County, PA, which is located in PJM and plans to use FCEL systems (more here). The rating
compares with preliminary PJM class ratings of 96% for nuclear, 93% for diesel/engine backup, and 67% for gas turbines, validating
the system's reliability and potentially improving customer economics by allowing nearly all accredited capacity to participate in PJM's
capacity market.  Source
Solar News
DUK — DUK launches 75 MW solar facility: DUK completed construction on the 74.9 MW Bailey Mill Solar Center in FL, further
expanding its renewable energy portfolio. Bailey Mill is one of five solar sites DUK has completed since 2025 and contributes to the
Company's solar portfolio of more than 25 facilities totaling ~1,850 MW. The Company plans to add 11 additional solar facilities by the
end of 2028 as it advances toward projected utility-scale solar capacity of more than 5,500 MW by 2033.  Source, Source
NEE — NEE secures approval for 120 MW CO solar project: The CO State Board of Land Commissioners approved a land use
lease for the 120 MW Mirasol Solar Project, which is being developed by a NEE subsidiary. The project will be built on CO state trust
lands, with lease revenues supporting public school education. The project is expected to enter commercial operation in late 2028 and
is authorized for up to 200 MW of solar generation and 200 MW of battery energy storage.  Source
Wind News
Trump administration requests federal court to pull permit for New England Wind: The Trump administration has asked a
federal court to remand approval of Avangrid's 1.8 New England Wind project back to the Department of the Interior for further review,
continuing a broader effort to revisit offshore wind permits issued during the prior administration. The project includes New England
Wind 1 (791 MW), which Massachusetts agreed to procure power from, and New England Wind 2 (1,000 MW), which lacks an off
taker. The administration’s move follows similar actions involving the 704 MW Revolution Wind project and U.S. Wind's Maryland
offshore wind development.  Source
Trump administration requests federal court to pull permit for Maryland Offshore Wind project: The U.S. Department of Interior
has asked a federal court to remand approval of the 2.2 GW Maryland Offshore Wind project's Construction and Operations Plan
for further review, arguing that the original approval may not have adequately complied with the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act.
The Maryland Offshore Wind project is a phased project consisting of the 300 MW Marwin project, 800 MW Momentum Offshore
Wind project, and a future third phase of the Maryland Offshore Wind Project segments. The overall plans of the project consist
of constructing 114 wind turbines, four offshore substations, a meteorological tower, and up to four offshore export cable corridors.

Nuclear News
XE, AEP, and AMZN evaluate 960 MW Xe-100 deployment in Sullivan County, IN: XE and AEP entered into a JDA to advance
feasibility work for a proposed 960 MW nuclear project at AEP's former Breed Generating Station site in Sullivan County, IN, with
AMZN providing milestone-based development funding and serving as a conditional off-taker. The project would consist of 12 80-MW
Xe-100 SMRs and represents the second commercial deployment under XE and AMZN's collaboration targeting >5 GW of new U.S.
nuclear capacity by 2039, with the first being the 2023 JDA XE signed with Energy Northwest (AMZN joined in 2024) for a 320 MW
initial project (up to 960 MW) in Benton County, WA (more here). The Energy Northwest project remains in the NRC pre-application
phase, with construction expected by 2028 and operations targeted for the 2030s. The IN project marks XE's third announced U.S.
commercial Xe-100 project and fourth globally, supporting its ~11.5 GW commercial pipeline, with the first commercial deployment
being the 320 MW Long Mott Generating Station at the DOW's Seadrift, TX, manufacturing site (under the ARDP), which is in the final
phases of NRC review after submission of the construction permit in 2025.  Source, Source, Source, Source, Source, Source
The Nuclear Company proposes 6.6 GW nuclear campus in KY: The Nuclear Company announced Prosperity One, a proposed 6.6
GW nuclear campus along the Ohio River in Lewis County, KY, with plans for up to six Westinghouse AP1000 reactors. The Company
has secured site control and is beginning site characterization, geotechnical, environmental, transmission, and federal licensing work,
with construction targeted to begin in 2028 subject to regulatory approvals. If fully built, the project would be the largest nuclear power
campus in the U.S. and among the largest globally.  Source
