Asian Tech stocks are now back to the levels seen in Jul-26, the fourth 20%+ 
correction we have seen in this rally since 2022. In this note, we outline market 
concerns and feedback distilled from our 200+ meetings with investors and outline 
our roadmap for the AI Infra Tech trade in the next 3-6 months. We continue to 
remain optimistic, given (1) fundamental drivers such as unit economics, AI 
scaling laws, token demand growth all remain strong; (2) valuations have already 
corrected to levels that signal imminent EPS cuts (which are unlikely to happen, 
in our view); and (3) classic cycle-peak signals, such as inventory builds, 
shortening lead times and rapid capacity ramp-up are largely absent. 
Key market concerns are AI infra funding gap for neoclouds, datacenter 
power constraints and the next ARR driver beyond coding: Investor concerns 
primarily center around AI infra capex funding, especially from neoclouds, given 
the rapid increase in compute commitments from AI labs and hyperscalers and the 
increasing reliance on external funding. A smaller set of investors also worry about 
datacenter power availability, especially given increasing constraints in the US and 
worries of a GPU chip inventory buildup. Lastly, for the investors who still hold 
faith in Gen AI evolution, what is needed to get more bullish is some signposts as 
to the next application that can drive revenue growth at AI labs, beyond coding. 
Stocks are pricing in a EPS cut in the next six months, with a likelihood of 
hyperscaler capex cuts in 2028: After a 20%+ pullback in Asian Tech from the 
peak, Asian Tech valuations have already corrected back to mean levels, despite 
accelerating EPS revisions, implying that the stocks are already pricing in a turn 
in the EPS momentum. At this point, we are not seeing any signs of this coming 
through and the 3Q26 earnings season has brought further upward revisions in EPS 
momentum. Investor conversations also suggest widespread concerns that 2028 
hyperscale AI capex may be cut significantly, marking 2027 as the peak of capex 
due to funding and monetization concerns. We believe that hyperscaler capex is 
likely to remain quite robust, with ample avenues for funding. 
Why are we bullish?
1. Unit economics have improved to the best they have been in this cycle: The 
unit economics of AI across hardware, public clouds and AI model vendors have 
all improved materially in the past 12 months, with AI model vendors also earning 
significant inference gross margins, public cloud vendors benefiting from rising 
compute prices ($ per Watt) and the hardware ecosystem benefiting from strong 
price increases. This is all underpinned by strong demand for tokens (both from 
proprietary models and open source ones), with the payback period in the 
downstream foodchain shortening significantly. Even in the past three weeks, 
where the market has become more concerned about neocloud funding, compute 
pricing ($ per Watt) has not seen any meaningful correction. Given the strong ROIs, 
we believe that incremental datacenter projects (from hyperscalers or large 
neoclouds) are still likely to get funded, especially those with strong contractual 
commitments.

model scaling is happening at a faster pace in 2026, with multiple new models from frontier 
model providers taking leadership in the past 3-4 months alone. Fundamental AI scaling laws 
appear to be intact, with most AI model labs anticipating RSI (recursive self-improvement) 
to drive faster model intelligence evolution and higher compute efficiency. We see AI scaling 
laws and model advancements as the indirect signal of new AI TAMs opening up, given this 
has been fundamental to opening up new TAMs for Gen AI (from chatbots to reasoning 
models to coding to agents) – hence, we remain constructive on the future TAM expansion. 
AI model labs allocating an increasing percentage of their token budget to model training also 
bodes well for the longevity of the innovation cycle for AI models, in our view. 
3. Token demand growth shows a strong recovery, although token prices have resumed 
their correction: AI token demand continues to remain very strong, growing ~17x YoY in 
the past few months with a reacceleration after the Aug-Sep wobble, and has been responding 
strongly to recent price actions and the launch of more powerful foundational models. Token 
prices appear to have resumed their correction after a period of upward momentum in 2Q26, 
but we caution that the long-term price curve for tokens has been sharply downward (70-80% 
price decline per year is the norm) and is not a sign of waning AI compute demand. 
4. Compute pricing still strong amidst supply constraints: Neocloud concerns seem to 
be more of a market share re-arrangement: Amidst all the bearish sentiment, supply of 
compute still remains extremely constrained and is likely to get worse into 2027 given the 
strong demand for compute from multiple AI labs, vertical applications and CSPs. Most new 
AI applications (productivity agents, consumer personal agents such as Muse) are all largely 
compute-constrained, which is also reflected in the steadily rising prices for short-term 
compute contracts ($40-50 per watt) through the last three months. Based on our 
conversations with the supply chain, it doesn’t appear that this compute demand is likely to 
fade in the near-medium term. The current neocloud concerns appear to be more of a market 
share re-arrangement within the GPU cloud rental space so far, with larger players (SpaceX, 
other hyperscalers) likely taking more market share and consolidating the market, but not 
impacting overall GPU demand so far. 
5. Public cloud growth continues to accelerate for hyperscalers, as does hyperscaler 
capex intent: Post-3Q26 earnings season, aggregate hyperscaler public cloud growth has 
accelerated further to ~50% yoy, adding $130bn in public cloud revenues, continuing the 
steady acceleration that we have seen in the past few quarters. We believe that public cloud 
growth (an indirect sign of compute demand) is likely to continue to accelerate in the next 
few quarters, with strong compute demand and an increasing share of GPU compute revenues 
going to SpaceX as well (SpaceX is covered at JPM by Doug Anmuth) , given its aggressive 
capacity buildout. We also see that hyperscaler capex intent is likely to remain quite strong, 
with JPM’s IT hardware equity research team expecting ~70% growth in hyperscaler 
datacenter capex in 2027, after 85% in 2026 (see Figure 3 and report from Joseph Cardoso 
and team).  
6. Classic signs of semi / tech cycle peak are absent – inventory buildup, shortening lead-
times, imminent massive capacity growth: While this semi cycle is already the longest ever 
(at 36 months already of yoy revenue growth), the classic signs of a semi cycle peak that we 
typically watch for are absent. First, we do not see any meaningful inventory buildup of GPUs 
or ASICs in the supply chain, which is usually the most important warning signal of a 
fundamental downturn – inventory buildup is usually seen as positive by companies first, 
before it becomes a headwind. Second, lead times for various components are still 
lengthening, indicating that the supply-demand gap is not really contracting – so far, the 
supply response is still much slower than demand growth. Third, capacity growth continues 
to be quite rational into 2027, which limits the risk of a big inventory buildup. Supply should

What are the next positive catalysts 
1. Continued EPS revisions could drive markets up, given the set-up is anticipating a 
EPS cut soon: We anticipate that Asian tech EPS revisions are likely to remain broadly 
positive in the next six months, driven by better pricing and improving margins. Breadth 
of the EPS revisions is also quite good, with ~80% of companies seeing EPS upward 
revisions. Given the market is already anticipating some EPS downward revisions in the 
next 3-6 months, continued EPS upgrades themselves should drive tech stocks up, in our 
view. 
2. New applications from AI Labs – given accelerating scaling laws: Accelerating AI 
scaling laws give us confidence that potential new applications and the new TAM for AI 
labs is likely to open up in the next several months. While it is hard to pinpoint a particular 
new application, we are also seeing multiple new vertical AI startups working on various 
aspects of enterprise workflow, AI for biomedical / healthcare research, while larger AI 
labs are also making strong efforts to expand into these areas. As models get better and 
compute efficiency improves, we believe personal AI agents (such as Muse or Dot) will 
also become more pervasive, driving a new avenue for consumer-led token consumption 
growth. Multi-modal is an area that most AI Labs have largely left untapped due to 
compute overheads, but increased compute efficiencies and cheaper tokens could lead to 
more breakthroughs here.  
What are the risks to watch for 
1. Snowball effect on GPU demand from neocloud funding concerns: A key area that 
we continue to watch is whether the neocloud funding concerns are leading to any 
sizeable amount of uncommitted GPU compute emerging in the market. If this starts to 
become a repetitive event, it could create a snowball effect, leading to a large-enough 
weakening of GPU demand, thus triggering a fundamental downturn. Thus far, the 
neocloud concerns don’t appear to be creating large pockets of uncommitted GPU 
compute in 2027, and any compute that is freeing up, is being quickly snapped up by 
aggressive larger players such as SPCX. Lack of GPU / accelerator inventory in the chain 
also helps manage the risks much better.  
2. Compute price ($/W) compression: We continue to view compute pricing ($/W) as a 
good indicator of the robustness of compute demand from AI labs and application 
vendors. So far, compute pricing has been moving up steadily this year, both for long-
term contracts ($15-25 per Watt), while shorter-term contracts are being priced at much 
higher levels ( $40-50 per watt). As long as compute prices remain stable or rising, we 
believe that the ecosystem’s health is unlikely to be challenged. Note though that we don’t 
use token prices as an indicator, as token prices have always been declining (70-80% 
decline every year since 2022), due to model and compute efficiencies, with a very high 
elasticity on token consumption.  
3. Actual datacenter buildout impediments showing up in changes in behavior: Our 
current read is that GPU/accelerator compute is likely to remain the bottleneck for AI 
compute into 2027 and early-mid 2028. While there are a lot of comparisons of AI 
compute (based on chip shipments) vs datacenter installations (based on committed 
power), we believe that the best way to judge a shift in the bottleneck away from chips 
is to look at hyperscaler/ neocloud behavior.  In a truly power constrained world, we 
should see more replacement of older GPUs (A100s, H100s) with newer chips (GB300, 
VR200, etc.), as the token per watt (and hence revenue generation) can be 50-100x higher 
using newer GPUs. However, we are still not seeing any signs of such behavior, and so

Continue to prefer sub-sectors in Asian tech with positive second derivatives and 
emerging price hike narratives into 2027: At this stage, we continue to prefer tech sub-

Memory valuations are getting close to 1.0x Price to Book and, hence, could see a near-term

concerns on HBM content de-spec. Our key stock picks include TSMC, ASE, Mediatek,

TW Tech has been more resilient than KR and JP, but expect this to continue given 
broader earnings drivers: In this correction, TW Tech stocks have been far more resilient 
than KR and JP Tech, given more broad-based EPS exposure and higher weighting from 
TSMC. We believe that TW Tech is likely to remain more resilient since the drivers for EPS 
upward revisions are more diverse, and margin expansion is still coming through (unlike 
memory where margins are close to peaking).  If fundamental demand starts to worsen, as we 
outlined earlier, then TW tech resilience could also crack, given negative data-points usually 
start showing up in the TW foodchain earlier than elsewhere.
