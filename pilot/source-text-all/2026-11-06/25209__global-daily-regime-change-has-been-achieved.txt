Regime change has been achieved 
 
Global Daily 
Market comment 
Today’s Global Daily is titled in honor of a clever meme—which I did not make—highlighting that 
the Trump Administration has finally accomplished regime change. However, that regime change 
was in the US, at the expense of red seats on Capitol Hill, rather than in Iran. While a blue wave 
was widely anticipated, the extent to which the Democrats swept the House, Senate, and 
Gubernatorial elections was unexpected, with Texas having a Democratic senator for the first time 
since 1988 and South Carolina electing a Democratic Senator for the first time since 1998. 
Trump and the GOP are now left scrambling for answers, and a strategy, as they head into the 
second half of the Trump Administration without Congressional support. In a tweet posted to X, 
JD Vance cited a pollster who asked converted Trump voters what they would need to see from 
the Republicans to return to the party. See Vance’s summary of the responses as follows: 
1. 
End the war 
2. 
Focus on cost of living/inflation 
3. 
Put America First 
4. 
Change the GOP relationship with Israel 
Vance echoed these sentiments in his own words, especially highlighting the impact of the war—
and by extension, the unprecedentedly high cost of gasoline going into an election—on the 
GOP’s loss. Interestingly, Vance’s tweet was enthusiastically promoted by Donald Trump Jr.  
Headlines from CBS also circulated that Trump was considering replacing Hegseth. While Trump 
himself denied the claims, saying that he thinks Hegseth is “doing a very good job,” CBS reports 
that “one source said that GOP Florida Governor Ron DeSantis is being floated as a possible 
replacement for Hegseth as he could go through Senate confirmation in the lame-duck period 
and then take the post when his term as governor expires on January 5.”  
The outcome of the election had largely been priced in both by prediction markets—and grown-
up markets—but Brent crude oil still fell by more than 10% to $93/bbl, making its way below 
$100/bbl for the first time since October 7. Meanwhile the S&P 500 soared by almost 2% to 
$7,708. Rabobank’s Philip Marey provides more color on the outcome of the Midterms for our 
forecasts and markets framework in his report, Blue Tsunami. 
Rabobank’s Global Strategist Michael Every also notes the implications of the midterm elections 
for our geopolitical base case. The result here increases the chance that Trump finally backs down 
over Iran after months of refusing to do so, though such a reversal would neither necessarily end 
Middle East hostilities nor leave the US in a stronger geostrategic position. 
Washington cannot simply leave Iran and its partners to dominate the region without conceding 
significant strategic ground to China and Russia. Reduced appetite for direct US military 
involvement could instead leave regional rivalries as a source of continued pressure on Iran and 
continued instability. That instability could relatively strengthen the US strategic hand as an 
energy exporter, while still imposing domestic costs through higher energy prices. Furthermore, 
our Global Energy Strategist, Joe DeLaura, believes that leaving Iran as master of the Persian Gulf 
would not guarantee flows of oil and other key products like aluminium, helium, LNG and 
fertilizers. The Gulf countries may end up in a new war to dislodge Iranian dominance over the

Every also notes that Moscow could read the election as evidence of diminished US willingness to 
fight, giving it even less incentive to negotiate with Kyiv. This reluctance could persist regardless

Elsewhere, Canada and China announced that they would be extending their trade deal for 
favorable market access for Canadian canola meal, peas, lobsters, and crab through 2028. This 
comes shortly after announcements from Mark Carney that the US is “asking less” and “offering 
more” in trade negotiations, saying also that “some of our red lines have clearly shifted, around 
leverage, around culture, around sovereignty, our ability to have trade deals.” But even though 
Carney sounds optimistic and Canadians are buzzing about the potential for another trade deal, 
talk is one thing and details are another.  
Trump already tried lowering tariffs on some Canadian goods to boost Republican odds in 
northern states. After those attempts failed, the question now is what are the immediate 
incentives for the US to alleviate tariffs on Canada further? Marey highlights in the article 
mentioned above that Trump’s foreign-policy activity is likely to escalate given his constraints at 
home. While our base case remains that the Trump Administration will sharpen its focus on Iran in 
the coming weeks, trade negotiations also offer opportunities for agreements it can present as 
“wins,” particularly if it secures concessions in return.  
The divergent rhetoric surrounding US-Mexico negotiations in 2025 offers a possible analogy. 
Sheinbaum has emphasized Mexico’s sovereignty in the face of trade tensions with the US, but 
also pursued a reduction of trade with China—“coincidentally,” this something Trump has been 
advocating for. This does not establish what Canada would concede, nor that it necessarily will, 
but it illustrates why public messaging alone is an incomplete guide to a deal’s substance.  
As such, a similar dynamic may play out north of the US border, with Carney presenting an 
agreement as beneficial to Canada even if it involves compromises. Carney might be able to land 
trade deals with both the US and China, but the key question is whether maintaining US market 
access would limit the scope of Canada’s agreement with China. Which product categories, if any, 
would be excluded from further market-access concessions to China in an attempt for Canada to 
work its way into better US standing, and would we ever even know what they are? 
Day ahead 
The day ahead holds Eurozone aggregate PPI inflation data for September, expected to mark 
some significant pick ups against the August reading. PPI inflation month-over-month is 
forecasted to register 2.5% versus 1.9% prior, which would be conducive to the accelerating trend 
since early 2023, while an in-line year-over-year print would mark the hottest producer price 
increase since early 2023, back when prices were still falling from their 2022 peaks. Today we also 
have a full docket of ECB speakers. 
The Bank of England will be releasing its interest rate decision. Bloomberg surveyed analysts, 
including Rabobank’s Stefan Koopman, unanimously anticipate a 25bp hike to bring the bank rate 
to 4.00%. OIS curve pricing implies this hike is almost fully priced in by the market as well. Read 
Koopman’s Bank of England Preview here. 
The Bank of England is not alone in its decision today. Banxico will also be announcing its 
decision, which is forecasted by the majority of Bloomberg surveyed analysts, and Rabobank, to 
be a hold at 6.50%. Read more on the upcoming Banxico decision here.
