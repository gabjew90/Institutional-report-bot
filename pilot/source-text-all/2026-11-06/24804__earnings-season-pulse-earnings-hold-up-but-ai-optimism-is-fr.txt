Earnings Season Pulse 
Earnings hold up but AI optimism is fracturing 
Global earnings were better than feared last week, with high beat rates  across US health care, industrials 
and financials, alongside strength in European technology and telecom.  
 AI investment is strong… The AI supply chain company results reiterated the divergent patterns 
from last week as spending continues to accelerate. Microsoft’s Azure grew 48%, ahead of the 44% 
consensus, while Amazon’s AWS grew 39% to $46bn. Tokyo Electron guided to nearly 90% operating-
profit growth, and SK Hynix reported a sevenfold profit increase, with HBM capacity sold out through 
2029.  
 …but returns on AI are uneven. Investors are demanding proof that spending will generate 
returns. Amazon fell 5.9% after quarterly capex reached $77bn, while Alphabet dropped more than 7% 
on weaker advertising and services revenue. Samsung declined 4% despite record profits as investors 
viewed rising capex in absence of higher shareholder returns as defensive.  Caterpillar fell 9.3% despite 
beating estimates, reinforcing that AI exposure alone is no longer sufficient to support premium 
valuations.  
 Semiconductors: a two speed cycle. HBM such as SK Hynix and semiconductor equipment 
(Mediatek) remain the strongest segments. By contrast, NAND and legacy computing are weakening. 
Kioxia’s operating-profit guidance was roughly 15% below consensus, while Intel guided fourth-
quarter revenue to $15–16bn versus $17bn expected, triggering a 27% pre-market decline. China 
memory maker CXMT sequential growth slowed to 19% from 90% in 2Q26, and the stock has lost 8% 
since.  
 China weakness reverbrates across the globe. China remains the main earnings risk for global 
autos. Volkswagen’s Chinese joint ventures swung from a €744m profit to a €113m loss, while Mercedes 
cut its margin guidance to 2–3% from as much as 5%. Audi also lowered its operating-margin forecast 
to 3–5%.  Chinese automakers are also facing pressure. BYD missed estimates, Great Wall Motor’s net 
income declined 41%, and Geely’s shares fell despite a 26% increase in net income, as investors 
focused on European trade barriers.
