F4Q26 preview 
 
 
We estimate F4Q26 EPS of $1.73 (v. Visible Alpha consensus of $1.66) with segment 
EBIT (non-GAAP) of $4.93 bn (v. consensus of $4.85 bn; guidance of $4.9 bn), and 
revenue of $25.43 bn (v. consensus of $25.14 bn). By segment, we forecast 
Experiences revenue and EBIT of $10.06 bn & $2.28 bn, respectively (v. consensus of 
$9.87 bn & $2.24 bn), Entertainment revenue and EBIT of $11.50 bn & $1.26 bn, 
respectively (v. consensus of $11.46 bn & $1.26 bn), and Sports revenue and EBIT of 
$4.48 bn & $1.38 bn, respectively (v. consensus of $4.41 bn & $1.35 bn). Notably, F4Q26 
results will benefit from a 53rd week, which will contribute ~$600 mn to total segment 
operating income and provide an estimated 1.5% to 2.0% lift to total revenues.  
Experiences 
We forecast 15% y/y Experiences revenue growth in F4Q26 to revenue of $10.06 bn 
(v. consensus of $9.87 bn), driven by (1) the launch of Disney Destiny in November 
2025 and the Disney Adventure in March 2026, which increased stateroom capacity by 
~50% y/y; (2) domestic theme park price increases; (3) stable attendance trends at 
domestic theme parks; (4) strategic cross-selling initiatives (e.g., Lightning Lane 
Premiere Pass); and (5) the opening of new attractions like World of Frozen at Disneyland 
Paris (March 2026), partly offset by (1) softer attendance trends at Asian theme parks; 
and (2) Disney Wonder drydock in the quarter. Within domestic parks, we continue to 
expect +9% y/y domestic attendance growth, or +1.5% y/y on an underlying basis (v. a 
3% increase in F3Q26A). This is in-line with management’s commentary that forward 
bookings at Walt Disney World remain robust. Additionally, last quarter management 
noted continued headwinds from international attendance at domestic parks; however, 
these pressures moderated relative to the y/y impact observed in F2Q26. We model 
F4Q26 domestic per caps to increase 3% y/y driven by offerings like Lightning Lane and 
VIP experiences coupled with ticket price increases. 
We forecast Experiences segment EBIT of $2.28 bn (v. consensus of $2.24 bn), 
reflecting a 21% y/y increase in the quarter and a 12% y/y increase for F2026 (v. 
guidance for the high end of HSD% growth ex. 53rd week). Our outlook is supported 
by (1) +1.5% y/y underlying domestic attendance growth (+9% y/y reported); (2) an 
increase in passenger cruise days following the launch of Destiny (November 2025) and 
Adventure (March 2026); (3) new attractions like World of Frozen at Disneyland Paris; (4) 
international visitation stabilization for US parks; and (5) comping the opening of Epic 
Universe (May 2025). The Disney Wonder had a 2-week dry dock during the quarter, 
resulting in a 3-week operational gap from August 17-September 7.  We estimate that 
DIS had a similar headwind in F3Q26, when Disney Magic had a 2-week dry-dock from 
May 2-May 16. Notably, the Disney Wish is reportedly scheduled for a dry dock on May 
8-22, 2027.  
Disney is executing on its $60 bn 10-year capital expenditure plan (F2023-33) for 
Experiences, as the expansion of the cruise fleet and Disney theme parks is expected to 
drive a balanced, multi-year mix of volume and yield growth. A key component of this 
strategy is the measured expansion of the Disney Cruise Line, which targets a 13-vessel 
fleet by the end of 2030. Last quarter, management also outlined its cruise expansion 
pipeline, with the Disney Believe expected in late C2027, Ship 11 and the OLC ship in 
C2029, Ship 12 in late C2029, and Ship 13 in late C2030. Additionally, at D23

Entertainment 
We forecast F4Q26 Entertainment segment EBIT of $1.26 bn (v. consensus of $1.26 
bn), reflecting 15% y/y growth in F2026 (v. guidance for DD% growth, excluding 
53rd week), and a segment revenue estimate of $11.50 bn (v. consensus of $11.46 bn). 
Growth drivers include (1) the pay-1 window for The Devil Wears Prada 2 (July 29, 2026), 
Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu (September 2, 2026), and Toy Story 5 (September 
23, 2026); and (2) new original launches both domestically and internationally. These are 
partially offset by (1) weaker-than-expected box office performance for Moana’s Live 
Action; (2) continued secular pressures from cord-cutting; and (3) a 
softer-than-expected advertising environment, particularly in domestic SVOD. 
We forecast F4Q26 SVOD segment EBIT of $690 mn (v. consensus of $680 mn) with 
revenue of $6.01 bn (v. consensus of $5.86 bn), implying 11.2% margins for F2026. 
The quarter should benefit from (1) the pay-1 window for titles like The Devil Wears Prada 
2, Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu, and Toy Story 5; (2) password sharing 
crackdowns; (3) product enhancements; (4) price increases; and (5) international local 
content investment. These are partially offset by industry supply-driven headwinds to 
SVOD advertising.  
Disney also recently implemented price increases for its Disney+ and standalone 
Hulu offerings in the U.S., effective September 23, 2026. Specifically, the prices for 
Disney+ and Hulu standalone premium plans increased by $2.50 (to $21.49), their 
respective ad-supported tiers increased by $0.50 (to $12.49), and the Disney+/Hulu 
Premium bundle increased by $2.00 (to $21.99). First, we note that this increase was 
implemented ahead of the historical October cadence observed over the last three 
years. Second, we believe that Disney is widening the pricing spread to incentivize a shift 
toward bundling, with the price for the Duo bundles (Hulu and Disney+ ad-free and 
ad-supported) now only $0.50/month more than the standalone price for either Hulu or 
Disney+. 
Looking ahead, Disney+ is evolving into a comprehensive, membership-style 
ecosystem to increase lifetime fan value and lower churn. Domestically, the 
platform’s upcoming Spring 2027 integration of merchandise and interactive 
experiences, coupled with the launch of a new FAST channel to capture sold-out AVOD 
demand, will serve as key churn mitigation tools. Additionally, a new TikTok partnership 
is expected to drive top-of-funnel growth among younger demographics, create a 
creative ambassador program, and expand Disney+’s vertical mobile presence. The 
company also remains focused on driving Disney+ growth outside the U.S. by executing a 
disciplined, market-specific localized content strategy. Disney plans to roughly triple its 
local original series over the next three years and ramp up investments in localized

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
