U.S. Markets Recap
US -1.82% | Nasdaq -2.86% | Dow -1.09%
Synopsis

US equities fell on Friday, though stocks ended off their worst levels: the S&P 500 lost 1.82%, the Nasdaq 2.86% and the Dow 1.09%. The
Nasdaq posted its worst session since June. Breadth was only slightly negative, and the equal-weight RSP, although lower, outpaced the
cap-weighted index by about 100 bp. The AI trade was the biggest drag, with semis, memory and neoclouds sharply lower, and NVDA-US
and SPCX-US fared worst among big tech. Treasuries rallied on flight-to-safety buying, with yields down 2-8 bp. Gold finished down 3.3%,
its worst day since June and back below $4K, while bitcoin futures lost 5.2% after the Binance hack and WTI crude gained 2.1% on skepti-
cism about a diplomatic offramp in Iran.

It was a very light economic day. Q3 ECI rose 0.8% m/m, in line with consensus, while October Chicago PMI printed 55.3 against consensus
of 56.0, with prices paid at their highest since December 2021. Post-FOMC Fedspeak stayed firm: Dallas's Logan said the Fed needs two
more 25 bp hikes before pausing, and St. Louis's Musalem said it may need to raise rates again soon, although rate path pricing was modestly
dovish after a less-hawkish policy assessment. October payrolls are due next Friday, expected at +40K.

Software, networking, tech hardware, MedTech and airlines lagged, while energy, staples, pharma/biotech, insurers and exchanges outper-

Macro

Amazon weaker as AWS growth falls short of elevated buyside expectations
Amazon was a post-earnings laggard. Better-than-expected 39% AWS growth fell short of the elevated buyside bogey, and Q3 capex of $77B
was around $20B above consensus. Funding remained a key area of scrutiny as elevated capex pressured FCF (TTM -$42B), and the com-
pany said it plans to spend 'meaningfully more' in 2027. There was also some concern about a slowdown in non-AI core segments, as both
retail and advertising slowed and missed consensus, and Q4 operating income guidance was below consensus at the midpoint. Bullish
talking points remained: AWS growth is projected at 40%+ in both 2027 and 2028 with a path to $1T in AWS revenue, backlog surged to
$578B, and the retail and ad softness was chalked up to timing (Prime Day shift).

Intel sinks as weak PC guidance and capex ramp overshadow data center surge
Intel Q3 EPS beat though revenue missed, as Data Center/AI upside was offset by weakness in the traditional PC business (Client Computing
revenue down 5.1% y/y to $8.1B, missing the $8.95B consensus). The company guided Q4 EPS and revenue well below the Street and
warned it expects the PC market to decline 10% in 2027 after a 12% slide this year, blaming memory shortages and high component costs.
It also noted $20B in capex expected this year and a significant increase in 2027, with the investment year expected to result in negative
FCF, and an Israeli factory shutdown is expected to reduce revenue by $600M over the coming two quarters. Shares were downgraded to
neutral from buy at BofA, citing lack of traction with external foundry customers and a sharp PC shipment decline.

Renewed Iran strikes remain on the table as diplomatic hopes wane
Oil was higher as hopes waned for a near-term cooling of US-Iran tensions and attention turned to the prospect of renewed kinetic action
after next Tuesday's midterm elections. VP Vance's outreach to Iranian security chief Rezaei appears to have had little effect, with Iran's
Ghalibaf reasserting that the US must first agree to Tehran's preconditions (ending the blockade, unfreezing assets and restarting oil sales)
before nuclear talks resume. Qatari negotiators have returned to Tehran to continue talks that Reuters said have been coordinated with the
US. Meanwhile, the Trump administration appears to be continuing plans for a new round of strikes, with CENTCOM commander Cooper
visiting the White House for an unusual in-person briefing with Trump on the state of the war.

Tech selloff raises AI funding scrutiny ahead of Anthropic IPO
The recent selloff across megacap tech and semis is a growing overhang for further AI capex and capital raises. Bloomberg noted that
Alphabet shares are now 22% below their $85B June equity offering price and Intel is down 33% from its $23B August equity offer, while
shareholders who participated in Meta's $25B offering on Monday are already more than 2% underwater. Fresh equity digestion headwinds
arrive ahead of Anthropic's expected November IPO, which could exceed SpaceX for the largest debut in history. Blackstone told investors
that Anthropic's latest $63B chip financing deal is expected to receive a high junk rating after the IPO, and pre-IPO perpetual contracts of
Anthropic on Hyperliquid are trading at their lowest levels since mid-August, down about 25% from the 9-Sep peak.

Binance says $6.3B of funds impacted in biggest-ever crypto heist
Bitcoin and other crypto assets came under pressure following a suspected hack at Binance, which confirmed that $6.3B of funds were
impacted (Bloomberg, Reuters). The incident is already being described as the biggest ever crypto hack. Binance froze $3B of assets with
deposits and withdrawals temporarily suspended, reassured customers that funds are safe and said the exchange will cover losses. Block-
chain analysis firm TRM Labs said North Korea's elite hacking unit, TraderTraitor, was behind the theft, and stablecoin issuers Tether and
Circle collectively froze $2.54B in assets linked to the incident. Coinbase (COIN-US) said it was unaffected but is monitoring the situation,
and there is some discussion about near-term ripple effects, including risk to investor confidence and potential implications for liquidity.

European Markets Recap
Germany -0.9% | France unch | UK +0.1%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished mixed on Friday: the DAX lost 0.9%, the FTSE 100 gained 0.1% and the CAC was unchanged, while the
broader STOXX 600 fell 0.4% but remained on course for decent gains on the week. Food & Beverage, Health Care, Personal & Household
Goods and Media outperformed, while Basic Resources, Technology and Banks underperformed. Bonds were firmer, with the Bund yield
down 4 bp to 3.33% and gilts down 2 bp to 5.28%. Renewed AI-capex worries hit the tech sector, and Q3 earnings growth is running at
about 36% y/y among the 43% of the STOXX 600 that has reported.

Investors continued to digest central bank policy. ECB takeaways point to a likely December rate hike unless growth deteriorates notably,
and sources signaled a pause after that. Eurozone Q3 GDP beat expectations after mixed national updates, with French and Italian data
missing and German, Dutch and Austrian readings beating. In the UK, the BoE is expected to hike 25bp to 4% next week, while the OBR cut
its 2027 growth forecast to 1.0% from 1.6% and the gilt reaction to the Budget was muted.

Defensives were driven by earnings: SAN-FR rose 4.7% on a Q3 beat and outlook upgrade, AZN-GB gained 7% on a Q3 beat and a $1.5B
savings plan, and BAYN-DE rose 5.5% after winning a US Roundup trial. PROX-BE jumped 11.8% on raised guidance. Banks fell on mixed
results, with NWG-GB down 8.8% on an adjusted PBT miss, although CABK-ES beat consensus Q3 net profit by 8%. ETL-FR lost 10.5% on
a Q1 revenue shortfall and ACX-ES fell 5.5% on a softer Q4 outlook.
Macro

ECB takeaways point to a December hike, followed by a pause
The ECB held rates on Thursday as expected, but takeaways highlighted the likelihood of a December rate hike unless there is a significant
slowdown in activity into year-end. President Lagarde said a hike was discussed, emphasized upside inflation risk and warned on the in-
creased magnitude and duration of the energy shock, and the statement suggests it was only the backup in rates that swayed the decision.
Sources via Bloomberg and Reuters said the ECB expects to hike in December if the inflation outlook does not improve and then pause, and
ECB staff estimates show the rise in bond yields cutting growth by a cumulative 100 bps and inflation by 90 bps in 2027-28 versus the
September baseline. Lagarde also confirmed she will step down on 7-Feb, with de Cos and Knot the leading candidates.

Eurozone national inflation prints diverge but reinforce the case for a hike
Preliminary October inflation data from Germany, France, Italy and Spain showed divergent trends, underscoring the uneven impact of the
energy shock. German inflation declined more than expected to 2.8% y/y from 3.3% in September, with core falling to 2.1% from 2.4% as a
temporary fuel tax cut slowed energy price growth. France moved the other way, with CPI accelerating to 3.4% from 3.1%, the highest since
2023 and above forecasts, and Italy recorded the sharpest jump, with CPI rising to 5.4% y/y from 4.2% against a 4.6% consensus. Spain's
CPI held at 4.9%, and Pantheon raised its Eurozone October inflation forecast to 4% ahead of the 4-Nov flash HICP, seeing a low bar for a
December ECB hike barring a significant decline in energy prices.

UK Budget is low-key as BoE surveys point to firmer inflation expectations
In the UK, Chancellor Healey's low-key Budget was the main event: the OBR cut 2027 growth to 1.0% from 1.6%, the bank surcharge and
gambling tax were milder than feared, and the gilt reaction was muted. The BoE's monthly DMP survey showed firms' year-ahead inflation
expectations rising to 3.3% from 3.1%, with no sign of pass-through into wages, while the Agents' summary pointed to modest growth and
persistent services inflation. The Citi/YouGov survey showed public inflation expectations easing in October, but the market prices more
than a 90% chance of a 25 bp hike next week and about a 50% chance of a December move, with a 6-3 vote split tipped. The Lloyds Bank
business barometer recovered to 47 from 41 in October.

UK Treasury drawing up off-balance-sheet plans to fund defense spending
The London Times reported that the UK Treasury is drawing up plans for off-balance-sheet funding to increase defense spending, after
Chancellor Healey committed to raise it to 3% of GDP by 2030, which an economist estimates will cost the government an extra GBP11B a
year. Healey is in talks with at least three NATO members about setting up an international bank to finance a significant part of the spending
without it counting as borrowing, and a second option would allow private companies to provide upfront funding for equipment that would
then be leased back to the Ministry of Defence. Both proposals would not require extra debt issuance or a break from the fiscal rules. The
Institute for Fiscal Studies criticized the decision to fund part of the defense plan from unallocated contingency in the Treasury's capital
reserve.

STOXX 600 Q3 earnings beat expectations on Energy, although sales disappoint
With 43% of STOXX 600 constituents having reported, earnings are growing 35.8% y/y (blended 26.3%), well ahead of the 18.9% expected
at the start of the season, while sales are up 11.3% y/y. The aggregate earnings surprise is +2.85% and the aggregate sales surprise is -
2.13%, with Energy explaining most of the headline growth and nearly all of the sales shortfall. Energy leads earnings growth at +135.7%,
followed by Technology (+68.7%) and Basic Materials (+26.6%), while Consumer Discretionary (-4.0%) and Telecommunications (-2.9%)
lag. Ex-Energy, growth is about 17%. Analysts expect Q4 earnings growth of +30.5% and full-year 2026 growth of +21.8%, though JPMorgan
has 2027 STOXX 600 EPS growth slowing to +9.5%.

Synopsis

Asian equities ended mostly lower on Friday, with the Nikkei down 2.12%, the Hang Seng up 1.46% and the Shanghai Composite down
1.44%. Technology stocks fell sharply, South Korea's tech names again saw steep declines although they finished off their lows, and Taiwan's
Taiex ended at two-month lows and near a technical correction. Hong Kong, Australia and India posted gains, while Southeast Asian bench-
marks were all lower. The MSCI Asia Pacific ex-Japan index ended nearly 1.0% lower for the day, 1.2% higher for the week and 6.6% lower
for the month. Defensive sectors and alternative assets, including JGBs, Hong Kong energy and REITs and India's software services, gained
at AI's expense, and short-closing provided a small boost into the close.

The Bank of Japan kept its base rate at 1.25%, declared that inflation had effectively reached its target and flagged another hike as likely. It
also surprised markets by accelerating its ETF sales from January. Japan's September retail sales fell 0.7% m/m while unemployment
unexpectedly declined to 2.4%, and Tokyo October core CPI rose 2.6%. South Korea's September industrial output rose 9.0% m/m, the
strongest since February 2009.

BYD-HK missed quarterly estimates despite more than doubling overseas sales y/y, while Tokyo Electron-JP beat forecasts and gave a
strong outlook. Coles-AU fell sharply after saying heightened competitor promotions hurt Q2 sales. Samsung Electronics fell about 6.0% in
Seoul after missing on Q3 net profit. KONAMI-JP rose 15% on raised guidance, while Fujitsu-JP lost 16.2%, Sany Heavy International-HK
15.9% and Kia-KR 11.7% on results.
Macro

BOJ on hold, confirms underlying inflation at 2%, increases ETF selling pace
The BOJ left the policy rate unchanged at 1.25% as widely expected, with an 8-1 vote; Takata dissented, pushing for a 25 bp hike to step up
the pace toward the neutral rate. The main surprise was a change to ETF sale guidelines, increasing the pace to about 0.1% of market trading
value from 0.05%, or annual JPY860B ($5.5B) on book value versus JPY330B, with changes to be implemented in January 2027. The
Outlook Report forecasts were mostly little changed, with the largest revision FY27 core inflation to 2.6% from 2.4%, citing higher crude oil
prices since the summer. The BOJ stated that underlying CPI inflation is generally consistent with the 2% price stability target and sees
core inflation accelerating clearly above 2% amid wage cost passthrough, oil prices, AI pressures and a weak yen, with risks seen neutral for
growth and to the upside for inflation. Nikkei noted genuine surprise at the ETF change but sanguine views on the market's capacity to
absorb the supply, since 0.1% of turnover would amount to only some JPY10B per day, while MUFG Trust Bank warned that annual sales
in the JPY2T range could compound pessimism in a downturn.

Japan industrial production beats while retail sales surprise to the downside
Industrial production rose 2.2% m/m in September, above the 1.2% consensus, following a 2.4% decline in the previous month, with autos,
production equipment (including chipmaking) and business machinery the main drivers. Shipments grew more moderately, leaving inven-
tories notably higher, and core capital goods shipments fell for the first time in four months, though they still posted 8.8% q/q growth in
Q3. METI survey projections point to growth of 5.1% in October (upgraded from +3.1%) and 0.8% in November. Retail sales fell 0.7% m/m
against expectations of a 0.3% increase, extending a 0.9% decline in the prior month, with appliances, fuel and food & beverage weighing
while autos edged higher for the first time in five months. Retail data have attracted more attention as a barometer of inbound tourism
spending, and tourism traffic has turned down this year as China numbers more than halved after the diplomatic spat over Prime Minister
Takaichi's past remarks on Taiwan.

Tokyo core CPI in line at 2.6% while Japan jobless rate edges lower
Tokyo core CPI rose 2.6% y/y in October, matching expectations, following 2.7% in the previous month, while ex fresh food & energy
inflation softened to 2.6% against a consensus of 2.7% and a prior 2.9%. The main driver again reflected volatility in public utilities, with
water rates going from +65.6% to 0.0%, and the drag was largely negated by a positive swing in energy prices. Electricity and gas subsidies
are due to run off from next month, and the ruling coalition is discussing another round for Q1. Non-fresh food moderated notably on sharp
declines in rice prices. The unemployment rate was 2.4% against a consensus and previous month's 2.5%, total employment rose 340K m/m
entirely driven by regular jobs, and the job offers to applicants ratio was 1.19 against a consensus and prior of 1.18.

South Korea industrial production jumps to the strongest growth since February 2009
South Korea industrial production rose 9.0% m/m in September following a revised 4.6% drop in the previous month, marking the strongest
growth since February 2009. The year-ago increase was 1.2%, above the consensus of 0.6%, following a revised 1.7% decline in the prior
month. The strength was largely attributed to a 19.2% rise in semis (DRAM), while autos spiked 39.9% after labor strikes and the summer
vacation season ended (Yonhap). Shipments rose a softer 6.3% m/m while inventories edged up 0.8%, and export shipments were consist-
ently higher on the year, outweighing a second straight decline in domestic demand. The headline drove an 8.0% m/m bounce in operating
rates after a 5.0% drop in August, and services, retail sales, equipment investment and construction completions were also firmer.

Samsung Electronics Q3 net profit misses, absence of shareholder return plan disappoints
Samsung Electronics (005930-KR) posted record Q3 operating profit of KRW107T ($80B) on Thursday, in line with the company's prelim-
inary announcement and almost ninefold higher y/y on strong AI-related memory demand. Revenue of KRW195T was also in line, but net
