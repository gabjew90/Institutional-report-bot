MPLX LP (MPLX): 3Q26 Recap: Confident Message on Growth, Though 
Refining Logistics Sale Tempers 2027 Distribution 
5 November 2026 | 6:47PM EST
We update our estimates for MPLX LP (MPLX, Buy) following 3Q26 results. 
Results were slightly stronger than expected, primarily on Crude & Products 
(products rates and volumes), partially offset by softer than expected Natural Gas & 
NGL (higher opex). MPLX announced a $750m sale of logistics assets tied to sponsor 
MPC’s refinery divestiture at a 9x 2027 EBITDA multiple, per MPLX. (equating to 
~$83m, or ~1% impact to our 2027E EBITDA all else equal; not reflected in our 
estimates prior and guided to close later this year). Looking forward, management 
reiterated expectations for 2H26-weighted growth, with 4Q26 trending higher 
sequentially (we estimate +2% QoQ, even with the Titan expansion not yet 
contributing). Management still expects stronger YoY EBITDA growth in 2027 versus 
2026 despite the sale, and reiterated their mid-single-digit growth outlook going 
forward (GSe: ~5% EBITDA CAGR 2026-2032). On capital returns, MPLX lowered the 
2027+ distribution outlook due to the asset sale, after previously guiding to 12.5%. 
We now model +8.5% in 2027 (vs 12.5% prior), and remain at ~4% growth 
2028-2032E, in line with DCF growth. While our lower distribution rate more than 
offsets the lost EBITDA (~$750m saved vs. ~$500m lost through 2032), the 
opportunity for MPLX to manage to a higher coverage level was likely prudent. 
Separately, management’s comments on the MPLX-MPC strategic relationship were 
notable given the joint divestiture and MPC’s growing cash balance, though we saw 
this as an acknowledgment of a willingness to be flexible in the future, vs. a sign of 
near-term action. We continue to believe there is no meaningful change coming 
(particularly given our view of a meaningful cash tax charge if MPC were to roll in 
MPLX), and point to positive recontracting comments on the call as constructive.  
Overall, we tweak our estimates modestly and our PT moves to $62 (from $63).

Key Takeaways 
Strong producer activity in Northeast - runway for incremental growth. Northeast 
fundamentals remained a highlight in the quarter. The Northeast processing system ran 
at 93% utilization even after Harmon Creek III (300 MMcf/d processing plus a 40 kb/d 
de-ethanizer) entered service in August. Utica was the standout, with gathering and 
processing volumes up 19%/20% YoY (+7%/+23% vs GSe) and processing utilization 
rising to 89% from 73% in 2Q26. Management noted Utica utilization has nearly doubled 
over the past three years, supported by strong NGL and light oil pricing, and we expect 
continued strong producer activity to drive utilization on MPLX assets as MPLX brings 
projects online and ramps volumes.  
On recent Northeast upstream consolidation, management highlighted long-standing 
relationships with both customers, durable long-term commitments, and acreage 
dedications from one customer extending largely over a decade. Management does not 
expect a negative impact from the combination, and sees continued growth with the 
combined entity via its Liberty gathering system in Washington County and the 
Majorsville expansion. That said, on rates, given the combined customer’s greater scale, 
management noted openness to future commercial discussions, and we will monitor any 
implications for rates and contract terms over time. Separately, there was no update on 
the Northeast behind-the-meter power opportunity set flagged at the October 
presentation. 
Project updates were quiet in the release, following the intraquarter Solitude FID. 
Recall, MPLX (10% interest) and its JV partners reached FID on the Solitude Pipeline 
System during 3Q26. The project adds 4.5 Bcf/d of Permian gas egress (Phase I in 2H29, 
Phase II in 2030) and lifts MPLX’s interest in Permian long-haul capacity to ~16 Bcf/d, 
from 11 Bcf/d. Management highlighted Solitude as one of the fastest FIDs in company 
history, moving from commercial to FID in under 90 days, with a partner mix including 
both midstream companies and producers. Elsewhere, management confirmed Titan II 
will complete by YE26 but will not contribute meaningful volumes in 4Q26, with its ramp 
toward 400+ MMcf/d occurring through 2027. MPLX continues to develop its gathering, 
processing, and treating projects (Secretariat II, Marcellus gathering expansion, Titan 
sour gas complex), pipeline projects (Traverse, Bay Runner, Eiger, Solitude, BANGL 
expansion), and Gulf Coast frac and export projects - which management has flagged 
potential for earlier-than-expected commissioning, which would pull forward 
contributions in 2028 and 2029. Looking forward, we will look for continued execution 
converting incremental opportunities in support of MPLX’s wellhead-to-water strategy 
and growth in the Permian and Northeast core basins. 
Management expects the divestiture to be a modest 2027 headwind, but still 
expects 2027 EBITDA growth above 2026. Alongside MPC’s announced refinery sales, 
MPLX announced the sale of its associated logistics assets (an interest in a crude oil 
pipeline, storage capacity, and three terminals) for $750m, with a guided YE26 close. 
The price implies a ~9x multiple on company-forecast 2027 EBITDA, implying ~$83m of 
EBITDA, or ~1% of our 2027E MPLX EBITDA all else equal. We do not include the 
divestiture in our estimates given the deal is still pending. On the call, management 
acknowledged the sale presents a 2027 headwind, but it does not expect the divestiture

2027 framework, likely alongside 4Q26 results. We continue to see MPLX as offering 
among the highest distribution yields in our coverage (~8% vs our midstream coverage 
average of ~5%). 
MPLX-MPC relationship unchanged per management; recontracting expected in 
early 2028. Management characterized the divestiture as the result of MPC’s decision to 
monetize the St. Paul Park and Mandan refineries (vs. the 2025 Rockies sale, which MPLX 
initiated), and noted there has been no change in the strategic relationship with MPC. On 
structure, which has been in focus for investors given MPC’s relative outperformance vs 
MPLX this year and its growing cash balance, management noted it will always look to 
optimize value for both MPLX unit holders and MPC shareholders - importantly, we do 
not view this commentary as a meaningful shift in management’s stance on the topic. On 
the MPC commercial agreements up for renewal in early 2028, management sees no 
reason MPLX and MPC would not recontract, adding that the process typically begins 
12-18 months ahead of expiry and that both parties intend to renew. We will look for 
updates on recontracting terms as that process gets underway over the coming 
quarters. 
Updating Estimates  
 
 
Our EBITDA estimates remain within 1% of our prior estimates for 2026-2032. We 
update our estimates to reflect 3Q26 actuals, stronger Northeast G&P volumes, and 
modestly higher product pipeline tariffs and terminal throughput following the 3Q26 
beat, partly offset by softer Bakken volumes. We leave our Titan II, Blackcomb, Bay 
Runner, and Texas City frac/export project timing unchanged. We do not include the 
pending divestiture given the deal has yet to close. We now model an ~8.5% distribution 
increase in 2027 (vs 12.5% prior), alongside unit repurchases of $50m/quarter 
(unchanged), and we model DPU growth in line with DCF growth 2028+ (~4% on average 
2028E-2032E) to maintain a 1.3x coverage ratio. We expect MPLX to retain significant 
balance sheet capacity, trending below the 3x range by the early-2030s, and

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
