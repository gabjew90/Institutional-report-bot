Industrial Metals Weekly
Base metals equities under pressure despite supply tightness
Our view: Copper equities slid 1.5% w/w while copper was relatively flat as the tech selloff on doubts
over AI spending put the AI demand narrative behind copper's rally under renewed scrutiny. The Intel
and Amazon selloffs hit AI-linked names and the Fed hold, with two dissents in favour of a hike, added
pressure to equities. Views from LME week were mixed around the future AI-driven demand, citing a
stark disconnect between AI capex and actual metals demand; though this is counterbalanced against
tight physical conditions as the Yangshan premium nears $200/t (363% YoY) and Chinese inventories
are drawn lower to 44kt (down 57% YoY). Chilean copper supply notably saw further reductions to
forecasts for 2026 and a milder-than-expected recovery in 2027. Cochilco, the country's state-run copper
commission, now expects 2026 output to fall 8.4% vs. prior estimates of a 2.6% decline, and expects
a 3% recovery in 2027, which is lower than previous expectations. The agency cited lower ore grades,
maintenance, and disruptions as the main culprits for the disappointing copper supply outlook. In this
note, we fine-tune our cost estimates for FCX to match company guidance; see the company page for
details.
• Base metals mixed: Copper prices eased 0.3% to $6.55/lb as nickel fell 1.9% to $6.91/lb and zinc rose
1.5% to $1.79/lb.
• Bulks mixed: Met coal prices declined 0.9% to $264.4/mt, while iron ore prices recovered just 0.4%
to $91.10/dmt.
• Published research: We published our company notes on FCX, FM, CIA, and CS along with Q3 earnings-
related quick takes.
Chart of the Week: Age of copper producing mines (globally)
Mines that have run for multiple decades typically face falling head grades, deeper pits or underground
transitions, longer haul distances and rising sustaining capex, so each tonne of copper takes more
material moved and more spend. Replacing this base is slow as copper projects can take more than
a decade from discovery to production. All while significant new discoveries of copper have collapsed
in recent years. An older base also has less margin for storms, strikes and seismic events, which are
becoming more frequent sources of disruption as we've witnessed this past year alone. We read the
age profile as support for the structural supply case and for a higher incentive price.
Source: Wood Mackenzie, RBC Capital Markets

Valuation
We value Freeport by applying a 50% weight to 1.50x our
NAV and a 50% weight to 6.0x our 2028E EBITDA estimate.
We use a 6.0x EV/EBITDA multiple and 1.50x P/NAV multiple
to reflect challenges at Grasberg and to be more in line with
global peers. Our $85 price target and implied relative return
support our Sector Perform rating.
Investment summary
Freeport offers investors exposure to copper (~77% of
revenue), precious metals (~12%), and molybdenum/other
(11%) in a senior mining company. Freeport remains the
largest publicly traded copper company in the world and is
a component of the S&P 500 Index. Freeport has significant
copper mining operations in Indonesia through the Grasberg
mine (49% ownership), North America (Morenci at 72%
ownership; Bagdad, Safford, Sierrita, Miami, Chino, and
Tyrone all at 100% ownership) and South America (Cerro
Verde at 55% ownership and El Abra at 51% ownership)
and two molybdenum operations (Climax at 100% ownership
and Henderson at 100% ownership) in the United States. In
addition, FCX owns Atlantic Copper Smelting, Rod & Refining.
We value the equity-interest in Freeport's copper (158Mlbs)
and gold (35Moz) resources at 4c/lb CuEq, a slight discount to
historical in-situ precedent valuations of 5c/lb CuEq to account
for option value on future development.
Potential catalysts
• Progress in resuming production at the Grasberg block cave
(2026/2027).
• Successful 
execution 
of 
capital 
projects, 
including
expansions at Bagdad and El Abra, but these remain several
years away from production
• Additional return of capital to shareholders above regular
dividend
Risks to rating and price target
Risks for our price target and rating include near-term funding
challenges, de-leveraging of the balance sheet, political risk in
Indonesia, operating disruptions at its mines, specifically the
Grasberg mining complex, and significant risk to the balance
sheet if copper prices move sharply lower.

An analyst's 'sector' is the universe of companies for which the analyst provides research coverage. Accordingly, the rating assigned

analyst's sector average.
Ratings
Outperform (O): Expected to materially outperform sector average over 12 months.
Sector Perform (SP): Returns expected to be in line with sector average over 12 months.
Underperform (U): Returns expected to be materially below sector average over 12 months.

Not Rated (NR): The rating, price targets and estimates have been removed due to applicable legal, regulatory or policy constraints
which may include when RBC Capital Markets is acting in an advisory capacity involving the company.
