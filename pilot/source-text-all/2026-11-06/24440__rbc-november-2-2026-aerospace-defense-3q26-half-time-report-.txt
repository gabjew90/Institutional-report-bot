Aerospace & Defense 3Q26 half-time report
Defense has been bought, aero sold; positioning ahead of mid-terms,
oil outlook
Our view: The initial A&D results have been strong, pointing to what remains a robust demand
environment across aerospace and defense markets. Across the initial results (majority of large cap
A&D stocks have reported 3Q26 results), companies have beat revenue and earnings consensus by an
average of 2.4% and 3.6% (excl. SPCX 119% EPS beat), respectively. The defense primes have posted
3Q26 organic growth of 10%, with an impressive defense book-to-bill of 3.3x. Not surprising, defense
stocks have been up an average of 3.5% on the day of reporting results and 4.4% in the three days post
results. We believe expectations were low into the results, and there is a view that the mid-terms will
be a clearing event (providing at least some certainty on the defense outlook, with the potential for a
step-up on Iran war activity following the mid-terms). Aerospace aftermarket results have been strong,
with engine AM growth of ~18%, and component AM sales up 8%. However, the aerospace stocks have
been soft as the aftermarket results did not exceed elevated expectations and Boeing sounded more
cautious on the pace of production increases (the MAX in particular).
The highlight of the 3Q26 results has been the strong defense reaction. However, we would highlight
that we believe some of the initial strength is getting sold on November 2, as investors await the results
of the U.S. mid-term elections and the lower views on the FY27 defense budget. We expect a ~$60B
supplemental DoW funding bill prior to the new Congress coming into office, but the DoW will likely
look at a FY27 base budget of ~$1.1T. We believe there will be limited growth off the FY27 base budget
funding levels, creating crowding-out risk, which will keep sentiment on the defense primes in check.
We continue to favor several of the defense tech stocks, such as KRMN and MRCY.
Aerospace results have been mixed. Aerospace stocks have been down (2.3%) on the day of results
and (1.9%) for the three days following printed results. We believe the aerospace aftermarket results
were positive, but against elevated expectations, the results were not enough to shake the more bearish
narrative around 2027 growth and demand destruction risk. For example, GE Aerospace reported total
services growth of 17% (down from ~30% in 1H26) and Safran reported spare parts sales of 17% against
our sense of investor expectations at ~20%. Commentary on the aftermarket remains positive, but
lingering risk associated with higher crude prices remains an overhang. We believe the relative strength
today in the AM stocks (up 5%-10%) highlights the underlying confidence in the aerospace aftermarket.
The aerospace OE outlook has softened. Boeing struck a more cautious tone on the pace of MAX
production increases. We believe this reflects largely internal challenges for BA (MAX wing system
integration), but commentary on the aerospace supply chain remains guarded. We see little risk to the
backlogs at Airbus and Boeing but believe investor confidence in the timing of the backlog conversion
to cash has moved to the right, although we do believe there is an element of conservatism in the mid-
term FCF outlook for Boeing. Note that the 3Q26 OE growth has been up >20% for both engine-related
OE revenues and for component suppliers. We believe investors are taking a more nuanced view on
the aerospace cycle and see the potential for better performance from suppliers until confidence in
2027-2028 delivery growth from Airbus and Boeing increases.
Limited 2nd half adjustments. We believe the set-up remains favorable for the defense suppliers
and 3Q26 results, although the risks associated with the mid-term elections remains a headwind. For
defense tech companies, we continue to like KRMN and MRCY for calendar 3Q26 results. In aerospace
stocks, we continue to like DPC and VSEC into 3Q26 results. Even with the recent strength, we believe
sentiment on aerospace stocks remains mixed at best and believe that confidence in the non-aerospace
outlook (IGT, industrial) is improving, providing some offset (DPC has significant IGT exposure).

RBC Capital Markets Aerospace & Defense 3Q26 half-time report 
With roughly half of A&D stocks (skewed towards large-cap) having reported their 3Q26 
earnings, we are approaching the halftime of the 3Q26 earnings season. Within our coverage, 
15 companies have reported so far. Across our coverage, 73% of companies have beaten 
consensus on 3Q26 revenues while 86% have beaten on margins. Meanwhile, 79% have beat 
on EPS and 86% have beaten on FCF. 
We believe the defense results were strong (and against lower investor expectations), with 
most companies beating on top-line (NOC the exception) and every company beating on 
margin, EPS, and FCF. Investors have rewarded the strong defense results as the defense stocks 
have been up an average of 3.5% 1-day post earnings and sustaining these gains with an 
average of up 4.4% 3-days post earnings. The defense primes’ defense book-to-bill was strong 
at an average 3.3x, with each prime delivering >1.0x defense book-to-bill.

Commercial Aerospace 
Within the commercial aftermarket, concerns on the level of deceleration in growth into 2027 
remains a key headwind for sentiment, in our view, even with the strong 3Q26 aftermarket 
results so far. The engine and component aftermarket growth rates were 18% and 8%, 
respectively, and the commentary on demand remains robust. Fleet indicators continue to 
point to minimal impact associated with the higher jet fuel prices and ~flat ASKs growth, as we 
have yet to see a meaningful increase in parked aircraft or retirements.  
We continue to favor VSEC into the 3Q26 results for commercial AM exposure as we expect a 
beat-and-raise quarter, with longer-term upside as the company integrates recent M&A, and 
we believe the upcoming December investor day could be a positive catalyst as we anticipate 
the company will establish a strong medium-term guidance (we believe ~HSD organic growth 
with 20%+ adj. EBITDA margins). 
Within the commercial OE sector, the outlook has softened as Boeing struck a more cautious 
tone on the pace of subsequent 737 MAX production rate breaks. We believe the aerospace 
engine supply chain remains very tight but believe it is improving, while Boeing has called out 
internal challenges with the wing system integration for the 737 MAX.  
While total commercial aircraft deliveries in 3Q26 (across Airbus, Boeing, and Embraer) were 
down (1%) YoY, engine and component supplier revenues were up 22% and 21%, respectively. 
While we do not believe supplier de-stocking is a significant risk today (less risk with engine 
suppliers vs component, in our view), this will be a watch item into 2027 if deliveries continue 
to disappoint. We continue to favor DPC for commercial OE exposure and IGT upside, and we

Defense  
As mentioned above, the defense results have surprised to the upside. However, we believe 
investors now await the results of the US mid-term elections with a focus on the eventual FY27 
DoW budget. We expect a ~$60B supplemental DoW funding bill prior to the new Congress 
coming into office. We continue to expect crowding-out risk post FY27 as we see limited 
growth off FY27 levels, and we believe this will keep sentiment in check. We continue to favor 
KRMN and MRCY for SMID-cap defense stocks, as we believe KRMN provides attractive 
exposure to the urgent missiles and munitions restocking priority within the US defense 
strategy, and we continue to like MRCY for execution upside and margin expansion (with the 
company’s outlook yet to incorporate upside opportunities across several markets).
