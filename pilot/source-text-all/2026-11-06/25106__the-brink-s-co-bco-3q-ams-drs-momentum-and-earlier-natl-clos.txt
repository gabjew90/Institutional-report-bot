and the company expects another sequential acceleration in 4Q.

DRS/AMS represented a record 32% of quarterly revenue, 
contributing to 90 bps of y/y EBITDA margin expansion and ~50%

Acquisition Updates 
Earlier NATL close pulls forward value capture. BCO now expects to close the 
n
NATL acquisition in early January 2027, nearly a full quarter earlier than it originally 
contemplated, allowing synergy actions to begin sooner. Foreign direct investment 
and money transmitter licensing conditions have been satisfied, and antitrust 
reviews have been resolved in 12 major jurisdictions, with the remaining UK and EU 
reviews progressing as the company expected. Integration teams have identified 
day-one actions and expect to reach the full $200mn run-rate cost synergy target by 
the end of 2029, with the earlier close accelerating the realization curve. 
Revenue synergy potential comes into focus. BCO noted that the combination 
n
would create an end-to-end ATM offering spanning hardware, software, processing, 
maintenance and cash logistics, enabling financial institutions to outsource ATM 
infrastructure and reduce capital requirements. BCO expects the combination to 
expand its addressable opportunity to 2-3x the current $36bn global market, with 
customer discussions already identifying opportunities to reduce cash held within 
ATM networks and improve service quality. The 5,500-location retail win illustrates 
the potential to service DRS devices and Allpoint ATMs in a single trip, increasing 
route density, lowering costs and expanding share of wallet. 
Additional cost synergies identified. Integration planning has identified 
n
incremental savings beyond the opportunities included in the original $200mn 
target, though BCO is still validating and quantifying the upside. Opportunities span 
procurement, corporate real estate, SG&A and service network optimization through 
footprint consolidation, higher route density, lower-cost support centers and greater 
workforce flexibility. BCO is preserving the combined sales infrastructure to pursue 
revenue opportunities and expects to provide more specific synergy details after 
closing, potentially with 4Q results in February. 
UK divestitures remove profitable revenue. The planned NoteMachine and 
n
TestLink divestitures to satisfy NATL closing conditions are associated with 
£120-130mn of revenue and ~£30mn of EBITDA, representing mid-to-high 20% 
margins. Sale proceeds remain uncertain since the buyer process is ongoing, and 
BCO did not quantify whether additional regulatory remedies could be required. 
BCO anticipated the potential disposal in its transaction modeling, excluded 
NoteMachine benefits from the $200mn synergy target and maintained that the 
previously communicated deal returns remain intact.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
