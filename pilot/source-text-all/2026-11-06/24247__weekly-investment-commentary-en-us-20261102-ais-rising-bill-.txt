We will learn much more about the AI theme this earnings season, especially how 
impactful the technology will ultimately be. But the backdrop for mega-cap tech 
stocks is starkly different from just four months ago. After narrowing 
considerably earlier this year, the earnings growth gap has now flipped for the 
first time since 2022, with the S&P 493 outpacing the “magnificent seven” at 
roughly 29% versus 18%. See the chart. The bar remains high. Consensus 
expects S&P 500 earnings to grow about 30% – marking a third consecutive 
quarter of growth above 20% – according to LSEG data. Meanwhile, scrutiny of 
the AI theme has grown. That shifting backdrop contributed to our decision to 
downgrade U.S. equities to neutral last month. Our conviction in AI 
fundamentals, however, remains intact.

Four years of booming AI investment have sent earnings expectations soaring. 
Investors now want evidence that spending can deliver returns, especially as rising 
capital expenditures squeeze free cash flow, pushing it into negative territory for 
some firms. We recently downgraded U.S. equities to neutral to lock in profits, but 
our core conviction in the AI transformation remains intact. That supports our EM 
equity overweight through exposure to bottlenecks in the AI buildout.

Last week’s earnings highlighted a growing tension between strong demand for AI-linked goods and services and the rising 
cost of meeting it. Hyperscaler capital spending has surged to about $649 billion over the past year, from about $357 billion 
in the prior-year period, according to data from Bloomberg, leaving little cash surplus after capex. And while hyperscalers’ 
operating margins have edged up to 27.1% from 26.9% in the previous four quarters, incremental margins have fallen to 
27.9% from 40.4% over that same period. In other words, each additional dollar of revenue is generating less additional 
profit, raising questions about whether returns can keep pace with soaring investment. Still, our analysis finds that 
companies with higher AI adoption outside the S&P 500 technology and communication sectors are on track for 3% higher 
earnings growth this quarter than lower adopters, based on evidence of AI use in regulatory filings. Cloud revenue growth at 
Microsoft and Alphabet also points to sustained momentum in AI demand. But whether that demand will be enough to justify 
the scale of investment is still an open question, one we plan to debate at our 2027 internal Outlook Forum this week. 
As the AI buildout becomes increasingly debt-financed, leverage and differences in funding capacity are coming into sharper 
focus. Credit markets are becoming an important signpost of how much additional AI investment can be financed and at 
what cost. The Treasury rally alongside the tech sell-off this week also fits our view that the ebb and flow of AI investment 
expectations will drive competition for capital and bond yields around structurally higher levels.
Within equities, we’re selective with our AI exposures. We favor companies and listed infrastructure poised to benefit from the 
critical bottlenecks in the AI buildout, a key reason for our move back to overweight EM equities in September. We see that 
opportunity playing out in Asia. Recent results from chipmakers point to tight supply and strong AI demand. But staying 
nimble is crucial, as our EM positioning shows: we upgraded at the start of the year, took profits midyear and returned to 
overweight in September. Brazil’s post-election selloff bears watching, but it does not change our EM overweight. 
Bottom line: AI faces a tougher near-term test as investor scrutiny intensifies. But our conviction in AI fundamentals remains 
intact, keeping us selective on where we take AI exposure while favoring opportunities tied to scarcity in the buildout.

Market backdrop 
The Nasdaq Composite capped its worst month since March 2025 as concerns over returns on massive AI spending 
intensified. The key question now is the economic value of the AI buildout: what returns it generates and who captures them. 
We see a range of possible outcomes for growth, profitability and returns on this investment. U.S. Treasuries extended their 
rally last week, with 10-year yields falling nearly 6 basis points to 4.93% after the Fed kept rates unchanged, as expected.

We are overweight. Strong earnings and cheaper valuations create opportunities. We

We are neutral. We see opportunities in physical AI. Cheap, open-source AI could drive adoption,

Short U.S. Treasuries
We are overweight. Much of the repricing we anticipated has occurred, and expectations for 
further Fed hikes look excessive. We favor the front end for attractive income and a greater 
breakeven buffer.
Long U.S. Treasuries
We are neutral. Higher yields offer attractive income and a bigger cushion against further rate 
increases. We still see room for yields to rise as competition for capital intensifies.

Global inflation-linked 
bonds
We are neutral. We see inflation settling above pre-pandemic levels, but markets may not price 
this in the near term as economic growth could slow.
Euro area govt bonds
We are neutral on short- and medium-term bonds. Current ECB rate pricing looks fairly valued 
given the balance of risks. Higher energy prices could push rates higher. We prefer to deploy 
risk elsewhere. 
UK gilts
We are neutral. We expect periods of elevated volatility given political uncertainty, longer-term 
bonds making up a larger market share, and buyers becoming more price-sensitive.
Japanese govt bonds
We are underweight. The substantial repricing in yields has improved the risk-reward, but 
further rate hikes and heavy issuance could push yields higher.
China govt bonds
We are neutral. China bonds offer stability and diversification but developed market yields are 
higher. A shift in investor sentiment toward equities limits upside. 
U.S. agency MBS
We are neutral and lean into the stable income MBS provide despite tight spreads.
Short-term IG credit
We are neutral. Spreads are tight due to corporate strength, but attractive overall yields help 
offset the risk of increased issuance.
Long-term IG credit
We are underweight. We prefer short-term bonds less exposed to interest rate risk over long-
term bonds. But attractive overall yields help offset the risk of increased issuance.
Global high yield
We are neutral. High yield offers attractive income. We prefer higher-rated U.S. and European 
high yield over investment grade and see dispersion of returns increasing. 
Asia credit
We are neutral. Overall yields are attractive and fundamentals are solid, but spreads are tight. 
Emerging hard currency
We are neutral. Fundamentals have improved, but we see a more attractive risk-reward profile in 
EM local debt.
