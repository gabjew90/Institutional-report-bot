THE TD COWEN INSIGHT
We see nuclear fundamentals improving despite lower stock prices in the sector and see
attractive entry points for XE, BWXT ahead of earnings. Additionally, we recently published a
deep dive on the nuclear fuel cycle, and Initiations on LEU (Hold) and OKLO (Hold). We note
several updates to our SMR tracker on pg 6. See fact sheets on pg 12+ and previous nuclear
reports on pg 23.
Nuclear Fundamentals Continue to Improve Despite Lower Stock Prices
We believe nuclear fundamentals continue to improve despite recent stock price weakness
that we attribute to the sector's high beta and overall weakness in broader power theme
stocks. The latest NEI survey of U.S. Chief Nuclear Officers indicates expectations to add
33.6GW of nuclear generation by 2040, a 44% increase from the 2025 survey.
Momentum is also building around the DOE’s $17.5B long-lead procurement program, with
11 companies submitting expressions of interest (up from 7 originally). At our recent Nuclear
Conference, DOE indicated that 3 to 4 parties have presented highly credible plans. Our
sense is commitments may be approaching, and any announcement likely involves a group
of utilities. We believe an announcement could come by end of year or early 2027. Poland’s
recent move to order long-lead items for a large PWR shows further progress, while X-energy’s
new joint development agreement with AEP adds to broader commercial SMR agreements in
the U.S.
Against this backdrop, we expect nuclear co's to maintain constructive language around new-
build activity during upcoming earnings, with incremental timing commentary representing a
key area to watch.
Ahead of The Curve on The Nuclear Fuel Cycle - LEU+OKLO Initiations
We recently published Heavy Metal: A Deep Dive on The Nuclear Fuel Cycle covering our
thoughts and analysis on Uranium, Conversion, Enrichment and Recycling as potential supply
and demand may evolve with additional nuclear deployments into the 2030s. We're most
bullish on Uranium and Conversion, and our TD Securities colleague Craig Hutchison favors
Cameco (CCO.CN) and Denison Mines (DML.CN) within his coverage.
Along with the report, we initiated on LEU (Hold) and OKLO (Hold).
We saw a particularly active period for nuclear news. See additional updates inside note.

Additional News:
10/29/26 - NuScale and Doosan Sign Manufacturing Contract
NuScale Power signed a contract with Doosan Enerbility to manufacture the first upper reactor
coolant system components for the NuScale Power Module in Korea. No customer project or
contract value was disclosed.
10/27/26 - $5B DOE EDF Loan for Constellation Expected to Add 1.1GW in Uprates
DOE EDF has announced a $5B conditional loan to Constellation for ~1.1GW of nuclear uprates.
Constellation will invest $7B to add 1,080MW. Overnight costs would total ~$6,500/kW.
10/27/26 - BWXT Signs TRISO Fuel Contract with Antares
BWXT signed a multi-year agreement to produce TRISO fuel for Antares, beginning with 1
MTU and the option to increase volumes as deployments grow. The fuel will be produced in
Lynchburg on the same line used for Project Pele and Antares' Mark-0. Antares expects their
Mark-1, expected in 2027, will also be fueled by the BWXT line
10/27/26 - NANO Nuclear and Hyundai Engineering Sign KRONOS MOU
NANO Nuclear signed an MOU with Hyundai Engineering to support planning, procurement
and supply-chain work for the KRONOS MMR project at the University of Illinois. Hyundai's role
could later expand to EPC, manufacturing and support for future deployments.
10/26/26 - Elementl Seeks up to $750MM for U.S. Nuclear Projects
Elementl Power signed a preliminary agreement for a development partnership of up to
$750MM with a Korean consortium led by DS Investment Partners, with Samsung C&T
participating as a potential investor. Funding would support pre-construction work for up to
three projects, including Elementl's planned 1.5GW BWRX-300 project in Ohio.
10/22/26 - NRC Finalizes DOE-to-NRC Licensing Rule
The NRC finalized a rule allowing applicants to use relevant DOE or DOW demonstration data
in commercial license applications. Applicants must still meet NRC requirements and explain
differences or any missing data points needed between the demonstration and commercial
projects.
10/22/26 - BWXT Clarifies Poland Investment Reports
BWXT said no decision has been made on a reported investment in Poland for microreactor
and SMR deployment. Any investment would depend on firm orders and the size of the
opportunity. Previous reports indicated BWXT would invest $1.5B to stand up manufacturing
facilities. Recall that Poland is looking to deploy over 20 BWRX-300s and is procuring long-
lead items for a Large PWR.
10/21/26 - The Nuclear Company Proposes 6.6GW Large PWR Campus
The Nuclear Company proposed a 6.6GW campus in Kentucky that could include up to six
Large PWR reactors. The company has site control and plans to pursue approvals needed to
begin construction in 2028.
10/19/26 - X-energy, Amazon and AEP Announce 960MW JDA
AEP and X-energy signed a JDA to study a 960MW, 12-unit Xe-100 project at a former coal site
in Indiana, with Amazon funding development.
10/19/26 - Indiana Looking to Evaluate CANDU Reactors
The State of Indiana has signed an MOU with Atkins Realis (ATRL.CN - covered by Michael
Tulphome) to explore the deployment of up to 6GW worth of CANDU reactors in Indiana.
10/14/26 - NRC Approves Updated Large PWR Design
The NRC approved and renewed the Large PWR design certification through 2066,
incorporating changes and lessons from Vogtle Units 3 and 4. Future projects can reference
the updated design, although site-specific approvals will still be required.

North America Large Reactor Pipeline Commentary 
U.S: The U.S. government initially announced a strategic partnership supporting at 
least $80B of new U.S. reactor construction, followed by DOE’s conditional $17.5B 
loan commitment to support long-lead equipment for up to 10 Large PWRs across 5 
two-unit projects. We’re on the lookout for 2H26 award announcements on long-lead 
items, as commentary from BWXT, Doosan and CW points to possible awards by 
year-end. Doosan notes negotiations ongoing on 3 to 5 large units for 2026, though 
some of those units may be for builds in Europe. Many utilities have noted interest in 
building large nuclear but highlight cost-overrun protection as a sticking point. We 
view DUK (Buy, $125.90), AEP (Buy, $129.34) , ETR (Buy, $109.97) and D (Buy, $65.12) 
(all covered by Shelby Tucker) as having the most visible activity toward advancing 
new nuclear deployments.  
DUK's latest IRP notes focus on possible large nuclear deployment at William Lee site 
that currently holds an active Combined Operating License (COL) for a Large LWR 
with possible start up in 2037. We note that the 10-year timeline lines up well with 
possible announcement of long lead awards over the coming year.  
TVA is another utility that is strongly aligned with SMR opportunities and new nuclear 
overall. Latest TVA IRP notes up to 5GW of nuclear by 2040.   
V.C. Summer: Brookfield was chosen as the primary developer for the potential 
restart of construction of a dual Large PWR project and is in current negotiations with 
Santee Cooper to progress the project. FID is being targeted for 1Q28.  
NEI's 2026 Survey of Utilities' Chief Nuclear Officer shows plans for 33.6GW of new 
nuclear generation with roughly half attributed to large reactors with the balance 
being SMRs. 
Canada: OPG is currently evaluating technology providers to deploy up to 10GW at 
the Wesleyville site. We view CANDU and a Large PWR as the most likely technologies 
to be selected and our analyst covering ATRL.CN, Michael Tulphome, expects CANDU 
to be favored given the existing infrastructure and Canadian-based technology. 
Though we think the selection is contingent on the MONARK design being completed 
successfully and in a timely manner.  
Additionally, Bruce Power is looking to deploy up to 4.8GW of nuclear, known as the 
Bruce C Project. The status of the project is in pre-development with no current 
technology selection.

X-energy's project with Dow at Seadrift (Long Mott) is the other project with the same 
50% ARDP cost-share and has already submitted their construction permit 
application to the NRC with approval expected early 2027. X-energy's Amazon 
backed project with Northwest Energy is expected to submit a construction permit 
application by 1H27.   
Kairos Hermes 1 & 2 demonstration plants are now under nuclear construction. 
These two projects are truly "FOAK" technology and practical learnings and 
successful deployments of the demonstration plants could warrant future successful 
commercial deployments after signing a 500MW agreement with Google. Hermes 1 
will not produce electricity but Hermes 2 is expected to generate 50MWe with TVA 
signing a PPA for the electricity that is expected to power Google data centers. Kairos 
has recently updated the deployment timeline for Hermes 1 from 2027 to 2028. The 
NRC has approved the latest date of completion to April 2029.  
We remain on the lookout for regulatory updates on Oklo's Aurora plant at INL given 
the shift to a DOE authorization licensing. Oklo recently received PDSA approval 
which allows further construction at INL. We believe a traditional NRC licensing 
timeline and follow on construction may warrant an early 2030s deployment, but this 
may be accelerated dependent on DOE licensing progress and design development. 
Management commentary is targeting a 2028 commercial operation date (updated 
from late 2027/2028), which management has admitted is aggressive.   
NANO's KRONOS project with the University of Illinois has recently submitted a 
construction permit application to the NRC. Given the reactor size, deployment may 
be relatively speedy once licensing is approved if project execution is done 
successfully. Project costs are expected to be between $300MM to $350MM. 
While there are many microreactor projects and additional demonstration projects 
being planned, we see BWXT's designed Project Pele reactor as the furthest along in 
terms of progress and development. We look for updates towards 2028 deployment 
date and evidence of successful proof of concept for a Gen IV HTGR transportable 
microreactor.  
Additional European SMRs - Proposed Buildout  
RoPower continues to progress their SMR project leveraging NuScale's technology 
after approving FID for the project. Timeline has now slipped to 2033/2034 from 2030 
and cost estimates have been updated by the government of Romania to roughly $6 
to $7B or ~$14,000/kW at the midpoint. Reports indicate that RoPower may be open 
to revaluating the SMR technology selection given unmet FID conditions.  
Rolls-Royce SMR won the UK SMR competition and are now working with the British 
government to deploy 3 reactors at Wylfa. The Czech Republic Cez group is also 
planning to deploy a fleet of Rolls-Royce reactors. Both projects expect deployments 
in the mid-2030s.  
Sweden's Vattenfall has selected Rolls-Royce SMR as the preferred technology 
vendor over GEV's BWRX-300. Vattenfall plans to deploy ~1,500MW worth of SMR 
power.  
Holtec Michigan Deployment at Palisades  
Holtec is planning two SMR-300 deployments at the company's Palisades nuclear 
plant. Holtec has already submitted part 1 of the construction permit application to 
the NRC. We'll be on the lookout for a final construction permit submission later this 
year or 1H27. Deployment start-ups are expected end of 2030 and 2031.

VALUATION METHODOLOGY AND RISKS
Valuation Methodology
Clean Energy:
Our valuation methodology is based on a discounted cash flow analysis.
We make investment recommendations on certain early stage, pre-revenue companies based
upon an assessment of their business model, technology, probability of market success,
and the potential market opportunity, balanced by an assessment of applicable risks. Such
companies may not be assigned a price target.
Investment Risks
Clean Energy:
Risks to the sector may include any regulatory delays or rejections regarding nuclear module
designs would have a negative impact on the stocks. Failure to execute on the construction of
power plants or business development plans would negatively affect stock prices. A design flaw
or issue in a company's manufacturing or supply chain would have negative consequences for
the stocks. Any malfunction or issue with any of the world's nuclear assets could have negative
ramifications for all nuclear companies.
