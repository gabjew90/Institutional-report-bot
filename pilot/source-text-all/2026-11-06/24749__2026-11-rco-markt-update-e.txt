Solid Fundamentals Despite Headwinds 
The global economy has recently remained robust. Positive lead-
ing indicators and high levels of investment have supported 
growth in the U.S. in particular. At the same time, the current 
earnings season has been encouraging: Many companies ex-
ceeded expectations, and earnings growth has increasingly 
spread across various sectors. Market breadth has also visibly 
increased on the stock markets recently. Headwinds came from 
persistently elevated interest rates and rising energy prices. Ge-
opolitical tensions in the Middle East, in particular, caused addi-
tional uncertainty in the financial markets at times. On a positive 
note, core inflation continued to moderate in many industrialized 
countries, and long-term inflation expectations have remained 
stable overall so far, despite higher energy prices.

AI Winners in the Next Market Phase 
We remain constructive on equities for the coming months. The 
robust economy and increasingly broad-based earnings growth 
continue to provide a solid foundation, while high interest rates 
and geopolitical risks are likely to cause volatility. The U.S. mid-
term elections could provide additional momentum: Historically, 
stocks have consistently performed well in the twelve months fol-
lowing such elections, while political uncertainties have tended 
to subside. The future direction of the market will depend on how
geopolitical tensions surrounding the war in Iran evolve after the 
midterms. At the same time, the AI cycle is likely to continue. 
Following major investments in semiconductors, data centers, 
and power infrastructure, the question of which companies can 
actually use AI productively is now coming more sharply into fo-
cus. We see interesting opportunities here, particularly in the 
banking and biotechnology sectors.

The Broader Market Is Catching Up  
For much of the past few years, the U.S. stock market was dom-
inated by a handful of large technology companies. Recently, 
however, this picture has changed. Since early October, the 
equally weighted S&P 500 has outperformed the market-cap-
weighted index—a sign that companies outside the largest tech 
stocks are increasingly contributing to the rally. This trend is sup-
ported by broader earnings growth across various industries. 
This is relevant for investors: broader market participation re-
duces dependence on a few heavyweights and opens up addi-
tional opportunities in sectors that have received less attention 
until now. The key question remains whether this broader earn-
ings trend can continue sustainably in the coming quarters.
