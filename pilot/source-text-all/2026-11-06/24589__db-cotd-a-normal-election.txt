DB CoTD: A normal election 
 
The number of seats lost by the incumbent party in the midterms has historically been closely tied to the 
president’s approval rating going into the vote, which in turn tends to track consumer confidence. President 
Trump’s approval rating in the summer pointed to a roughly 40-seat Republican loss in the House when 
compared to other parties at the same point before previous midterm elections. Current consensus across 
live trackers from NBC, CBS, The Economist and others suggests the eventual loss will be around 43 seats. 
So far, then, this looks like a perfectly normal election by historical standards. Markets appear to have 
welcomed that normality, with an emerging relief rally as things stand. 
Ahead of almost every US election, the worries tend to look more concerning than they do immediately 
afterwards. So far, this one is following the same script. AI and Iran may yet test that calm in the weeks and 
months ahead, but yesterday’s CoTD here showed that the S&P 500 has been higher one year after each of 
the last 22 midterms; 1938 and 1930 were the last exceptions. History is never a guarantee, and AI will hold 
the key to whether we'll see the run extend to 23, but the striking message from both the election result and 
the market record is the same: political uncertainty usually peaks before the vote, not after it.
