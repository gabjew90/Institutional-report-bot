New attacks on Iran will lead to nowhere and the cost would be high
Trump gives diplomacy another chance after oil spiked on Friday. The Brent Jan-27 contract rose 1.6%
last week. It traded in a range of $109.02 - 114.29/b and closed the week at $113.43/b while. The Brent
Dec-26 contract rolled off on Friday with a close of $117.64/b. There was some softening in physical
crude prices. Physical premiums for Fortis and Oseberg fell further to $5.9/b and $1.9/b after having
reached unusual highs of $20/b recently. The Dated Brent crude oil price still closed at a strong $118.77/b.
The Brent Jan-27 contract is down 3.3% this morning to $100.8/b as Trump has indicated that he will give
diplomacy another chance. As a result the elevated risk premium in Brent crude lately is partially erased
this morning as an imminent attack post 3 November seems to have been averted for now.
New attacks by the US/Israel will lead to nowhere. There are hectic diplomatic efforts in the Middle
East to avert a new US/Israeli attack on Iran. None of the Gulf countries want another round of hostilities.
The US didn't seek support for this war from any of its allies (except Israel). Not even its Gulf allies were
consulted. The US is now in a quagmire in the Middle East with little support to be found anywhere except
from Israel. The Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said on Saturday that: "A second round will be
more dangerous, more destructive and it will lead to nowhere" (Bloomberg story yesterday). And that is
precisely it. A new round of attacks on Iran by the US and Israel will lead to nowhere politically. Trump is
hoping or maybe praying that a new, large attack on Iran will soften Iran so it bends to the will of the US
and Israel. That is just as fancifully thinking as Putin now is pursuing through its attacks on civilians in
Ukraine.
Iran is well prepared to retaliate and "there will be no geographical limitations". Iran was heavily
attack in March and April with US-Israeli attacking military targets across Iran. Since then it has only been
briefer and more limited attacks like in June and 1 September. The latter lasted only 10 hours. Iran has
essentially been able to rebuild and reestablish a lot of its defensive and offensive capabilities almost for a
full 6 months. Iran has naturally not been standing still as it naturally has expected and prepared for
renewed attacks by the US and Iran. Naturally since Trump has continuously threatened to do so. Iran is
assumed to still hold 3/4 of its prewar ammunition. The Gulf states and the US has burned off a major
share of its defensive rockets (Patriots and Thaad) which are needed to counter Iranian ballistic rockets.
Iran however likely still has a large stock of ballistic rockets in store. Attacking Iran again now is a much
bigger risk and much more dangerous than attacking it for example in May. The Gulf countries probably
understands this very well. Trump hopefully also understands this. Iranian media stated this weekend that
if Iran is attacked it will retaliate without geographical limitations. 
One last attack and then a declaration of victory? We struggle to see any good reason for why the US
would choose to attack Iran again. We totally agree with Badr Abdlatty above: "it will lead to nowhere".
Senior researcher, Karen Young, at Columbia University's Center on Global Energy Policy has a different
angle: "I think we will see a US-Israel attack on Iran, a declaration of victory, and a deal that is favorable to
Iran". I.e. another attack is essentially a process to pack up and leave. We'll see.
