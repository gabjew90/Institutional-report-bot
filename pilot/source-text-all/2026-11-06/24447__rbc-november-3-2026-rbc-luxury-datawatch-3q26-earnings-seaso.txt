RBC Luxury Datawatch
3Q26 earnings season supports preference for BCU, ZGN, ADS, EL
Luxury - The widening growth divergence between absolute luxury (Brunello Cucinelli, Zegna, both OP)
and fashion-oriented luxury (LVMH, Kering, both SP) was the overarching theme from 3Q26 earnings
so far. China was a key disappointment which will likely persist whilst negative wealth effects in the US
and softening EMEA local demand and lack of inbound tourism are unhelpful. Investor interest remains
largely hedge fund oriented. Positioning is skewed towards defensive names (BC, ZGN) whilst Hermes'
perception has weakened, and sentiment is soft for cyclical luxury stocks which require an inflexion
(LVMH, KER).
Sporting - adidas (OP) Sentiment appears to be improving as the market starts to worry less about
decelerating growth profile into 2H26E and tough comps in 1H27E and starts to appreciate better overall
execution and longer term approach to managing revenue and earnings growth with visibility into FY27E
at an attractive 14x CY27E P/E. Nike (SP) has been quiet post 1Q27 results despite upcoming CMD.
EssilorLuxottica (OP) has been more active in recent months, given valuation dislocation, and cleaner
setup from here, as debate shifts back towards quality of Core business, with less emphasis on
Wearables, and the potential for better margin outcomes.
Stock picks - We remain positive on Richemont (strong revenue growth, EPS delivery cycle),
EssilorLuxottica (quality, defensive), Brunello Cucinelli (absolute luxury, elevated growth profile) Zegna
(revenue growth, self-help, valuation) and adidas (execution and FY27E visibility, valuation).
RBC Momentum Scorecard - We have the most upside to our price target for Richemont and adidas,
followed by Zegna and BCU. Earnings momentum is less negative than in recent months with broad
based stabilisation (except for Nike, Kering, Swatch, DOCS and adidas), and positive for EssilorLuxottica,
PANDORA and WOSG. Relative valuation - Most attractive at DOCS, Burberry, EssilorLuxottica and
adidas. Share price momentum – Positive for EssilorLuxottica; most negative for Kering, LVMH, Hermes
and Richemont, flattish elsewhere. RSI – Positive for EL, LVMH and Puma and flattish elsewhere.
Website traffic - Soft luxury trends remain healthy. For Kering, Gucci +18%, YSL +18%, Bottega Veneta
+23% and Balenciaga +9%. For LVMH, improvement at Celine (+9%), Fendi (+34%) and Louis Vuitton
(+13%) and unchanged Dior (+21%). Chanel (+19%) and Burberry (+12%) are sequentially softer,
Hermes (+12%) and Zegna (+14%) unchanged, Brunello Cucinelli (+6%) and Moncler (+1%). Hard
luxury (Jewellery) trends remain positive. For Richemont, Cartier +29% remains strong and Van Cleef
remains flattish. At LVMH, Bulgari +15% and Tiffany +4% little changed. In sporting goods, ON Running
+56%, adidas +31%, Lululemon +20%, Puma +20%, Asics +14%, Hoka +13% and Nike +9% on the third
consecutive month of positive growth, albeit likely reflecting elevated promotional activity.
Macro data - US softer - RBC US Macro Index modestly lower. September consumer confidence 81.9
(miss) and lower sequentially, retail sales +6.3% (beat), non-farm (29k) softer, Services PMI 57.9 (miss),
CPI +3.7% (ahead), personal finances and business conditions lower. China mixed - RBC China Macro
index stable. Consumer confidence index at 89 (lower), industrial output +5.5% y/y (beat), retail sales
+1.1% y/y (beat), manufacturing PMI 50.1 (miss), exports +36% y/y (beat), unemployment elevated,
property indices remain soft. Europe mixed - RBC EU Macro Index broadly stable. Consumer confidence
(Oct) softer, Manufacturing PMIs (Oct) broadly stable, retail sales mixed, inflation increasing. Micro data
- WTI Crude Oil +66% y/y, Polyester +57%, Baltic Dry +27%, shipping costs (China-US) rising, cotton +20%,
wool +22%, viscose +26%, gold/silver normalising y/y. HK/Macau visitor arrivals (Sept) +28%/+16% y/

Recap of 3Q26 reporting period 
Sporting Goods – 3Q26 results were mixed with adidas delivering ahead of consensus and 
with raised FY26E guidance, soft results from Nike with FY27E outlook downgrade and 
mixed results from Puma with topline ahead of consensus but elevated opex resulted in 
EBIT miss. Strong share price gains (+15%) on average which combined with negative EPS 
revisions (mostly Nike driven) resulted in 26% multiple re-rating L1M.  
Luxury – 3Q26 results were softer than expected on average. We note a widening 
divergence between absolute luxury (Brunello Cucinelli, Zegna brand, Hermes Leather) 
which outperformed relative to fashion-oriented luxury (LVMH, Kering, Hermes non-
Leather, Moncler). China was the main drag on performance which will likely persist near 
term in absence of macro improvement. Negative wealth creation effects in the US and 
rising cost of living pressure in Europe (as well as lack of inbound tourism) are likely to be 
incremental headwinds in the coming quarters in our view. Share prices were broadly 
flattish (L1M) on average. Relative winners were Zegna (+10%), Brunello Cucinelli (+7%) 
and Burberry (+6%), whilst Kering (-21%) was the main laggard. EPS revisions were 
modestly positive for Brunello Cucinelli and Zegna and negative for Kering, LVMH and 
Hermes predominantly. 
EssilorLuxottica 3Q26 results were reassuring with growth predominantly from the Core 
business (+6.5% organic) with positive Wearables contribution (+0.7%). The debate is 
shifting towards the quality of its Core business which is set up to deliver sustainable mid-
single digit growth in the mid-term, and less focus on Wearables. We also note potential 
for modest margin upside for FY26E and FY27E supported by less negative product mix 
and favourable FX. Shares are up +27% L1M post strong 3Q26 print on broadly unchanged 
EPS with ~27%/5x multiple re-rating.

Context - The RBC Global Macro Indices track the 
monthly economic activity across four key regions —
the United States, China, the European Union and 
Japan — using a consistent, comparable framework. 
Each index is constructed from a blend of activity, 
sentiment, inflation and financial market indicators, 
normalised to a base of 100.  
US – The RBC US Macro Index modestly lower in 
September, 
largely 
reflecting 
lower 
consumer 
confidence and weak S&P 500 performance, partly 
offset by better services PMI.  
China - The RBC China Macro Index is broadly stable in 
recent months, with September characterised by 
slightly softer consumer confidence, offset by 
improvement in retail sales, output and PMIs.  
Europe - The RBC EU Macro Index has been trending 
downwards since early 2026, however has stabilised 
since May, supported by improving services PMIs, 
retail sales and the stock market more broadly, partly 
offset by modestly lower manufacturing PMIs. The 
index is sequentially stable in September. 
Japan - The RBC Japan Macro Index has been trending 
positively since Nov-25 supported by JPY/USD 
weakness and improving real expenditure index. In
Sept-26 modest sequential softness, driven by lower 
PMIs, softer retail sales and weaker consumer 
confidence.

Sporting goods  
Western sporting goods 
Elevated Lifestyle Footwear inventories remain a road block for the sporting goods sector, 
which is driving promotions and a difficult marketplace to sell into, particularly in China, 
EMEA and the US. As a result, wholesale channel is under pressure which we expect to 
improve gradually into FY27E as supply/demand dynamics normalise, most likely from 
2H27E onwards. 
We believe that adidas (Outperform) is executing well, managing the arguably challenging 
2H26 marketplace with discipline 3Q26 results confirmed broad-based regional growth, 
led by DTC and Apparel for now. The outlook points to improving Footwear performance 
in Lifestyle, supported by disciplined sell-in for much of 2026, which was a solid move and 
demonstrates management are doing a good job of managing lifecycles to deliver a more 
consistent growth profile. adidas offers 16%/24% EBIT/EPS 3yr CAGR at 14x FY27E P/E. 
Nike (Sector Perform) is taking the right steps but the turnaround is unsurprisingly taking 
longer. CEO Elliott Hill understands the business, has a clear plan, and is framing

FX sensitivity  
We sensitise sales, gross profit and operating profit to FX base currency depreciation vs 
key currency pair (EUR, USD, CNY, JPY) for the companies under our coverage. 
Sales – On average, we note a 3-4% uplift to revenues on a 10% depreciation of EUR vs 
USD and 1-2% revenue benefit on 10% depreciation of EUR against CNY or JPY. WOSG, 
Richemont, Essilor and Pandora would benefit the most from EUR depreciation (vs USD). 
Gross profit – On average, we note a 2-4% uplift to gross profit on a 10% depreciation of 
EUR vs USD for luxury goods and 1-2% headwind for ADS and PUM given sourcing and 
manufacturing costs are in USD (as opposed to luxury goods that are mainly in EUR). 
Operating profit – On average, we note a 5-10% uplift to operating profit on a 10% 
depreciation of EUR vs USD in luxury goods whilst operating deleverage leads to material 
negative impact on EBIT for adidas and Puma.
