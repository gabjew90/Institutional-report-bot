Sentiment Check + Two Stocks
Couple of lines on subsector tilts / views below – let me know what sticks out to you.
Refining. Most specialists agree medium term outlook on cracks screens most bullish vs oil or gas. Issue
really is near-term revisions – bears pointing to gasoline crack rolling, less confidence in diesel crack
upside, and potential for de-escalation abroad. Really think the starting point here shouldn’t be products –
but moreso on the crude differential side. Merchant refiners essentially ~20% off highs back in October –
some questions on building more cash vs leaning into buybacks with stocks off peaks.
Oil. Have seen a lot of the risk premium come out of the barrel over the last couple of trading sessions. Oil
E&P has been more well owned by the dedicated community– question really is around how much more
length needs to come out of the group. Have seen a clear preference for the International linked equities vs
the Permian in desk conversations. Capex stepping up for the group has been one of the main talking
points this EPS season (service cost inflation, steel, diesel, etc).
Gas. The one area of O&G where the views on the macro really haven’t shifted one way or another. Strip
still looks challenging, Permian growth still top of mind (and the associated gas volumes coming with it),
International gas trade feedback has been 'this is played out'. Think there’s been more ‘hiding in gas’ with
uncertainty on the path for both oil and products near term. Would say the incremental buyer here has been
specialists, generalists largely not involved with gas E&P (unchanged).
IPPs. No real sense of playing defense (or offense?) yet on the group. We’ve been saying specialists have
been more neutral on the group for some time now. There’s no discussion on valuation at this point –
because there’s no conviction on the floor / FCF yield to step in. Inbounds highlight more caution from
generalist investors – which we think is still telling about length in the group. PJM vs ERCOT has been
the trade over the last few weeks and would think there’s even more willingness to lean into PJM now
given uncertainty on when (if) the demand shows up in TX.
BTM. “Shoot first, ask questions later”… most asked question yesterday on power was ‘even with the
TX/OH updates – should BTM be ok?’ Think there will be a focus today on the dispersion between Tier 1
and Tier 2 players – ie who has the contracts, cash flow coming in the door, equipment already signed, less
financing needs etc. The bull pitch was contracts were at / near the finish line as we head into YE -
slippage the largest risk highlighted by power folks.

Solar. Healthy debate on if the price action over the last couple of trading sessions is real demand is or just
covering. Can make the case following the midterms outcome – the space was due for a bounce given how
light positioning has been / how negatively skewed investors have been on expected downgrades to near
term guidance. We’ve been saying this has felt like a ‘coiled spring’ in some sense. More inbounds on
utility scale front vs resi lately.
Two Stocks
SEI. Down 17% yesterday was a bit perplexing to most – not the fact that the power group was down - but
the extent of the move. Similar to prior quarters – strong beat on EBITDA (above high end of guidance)
while raising 4Q26 and 1Q27 guidance. 15-year agreement highlighted again (largest and longest contract)
– would just note total capacity under LT contracts is now 3.2 GW with average tenor of 11 years. We
think there’s still a preference to own Tier 1 operators in the space - and SEI fits the bill.
COP. EPS print this morning – one of the more well owned stocks in E&P (desk had positioning score of
9/10 – one of the highest scores we have seen for Conoco in some time now). Print itself should be good
enough – EPS ahead of Street with production largely in line and CFO ahead. 4Q guidance is a little softer
vs expectations – likely the one thing that gets picked at from the update (at least for now). I’ve been
focused on the capex update – which importantly highlighted no change to FY guidance (expect them to
get asked on the call on early 2027 thoughts). Aside from that - progress on Willow (first oil early 2029),
and formulaic approach to returns well received. Positioning will matter in this on how it trades today, but
do think there’s more folks willing to defend the story here.
