Summary. On Wednesday, November 4th, we hosted senior 
management of ExxonMobil (XOM) for a group meeting with 
investors in Houston. Key participants included Mr. Darren Woods 
(CEO), Mr. Jack Williams (SVP), Mr. Neil Hansen (SVP and CFO), Mr. 
Jim Chapman (VP, Corporate Finance and Treasurer), and Mr. Matteo 
Pedercini (Director of Investor Relations). In a volatile commodity 
price environment, management continues to draw on unique 
competitive advantages to redirect molecules and move products, 
supplying global needs. On the back of US midterm elections, 
management maintains a long-term focus and leverages a large, 
diversified business model to navigate market shifts, weather price 
shocks, and deliver resilient shareholder returns.  
The Positives. Management highlights strong operational execution 
across assets, with record refining performance in global fuels 
utilization, diesel yield, and North American diesel volumes. This is 
paired with major Upstream production milestones across Guyana 
and Canada, alongside new operatorship status at Papua LNG and 
higher working interests at both Papua and Rovuma, Mozambique. 
Management now guides to $20 bn of share repurchases next year, 
demonstrating consistency through commodity price cycles, and 
highlights strong financial results during the quarter, including $25.7 
bn of cash flow from operating activities, $9.4 bn in total returns to 
shareholders, and cumulative structural cost savings of $16.9 bn 
since 2019.

with shares trading at a 6% free cash flow yield in 2028 at $75/b 
Brent, relative to CVX at 8%. Investors also debate a geopolitical risk 
premium for greater relative Middle East exposure, where 
management assumes a production impact of ~350 kbd next

Upstream Assets. Management continues to advance competitive Upstream projects, 
with strong year-over-year results in Guyana and the Permian Basin. Production from 
the Stabroek Block in Guyana now exceeds 1 bn barrels of oil, with a fifth FPSO on track 
to start up next quarter. We look for progress on a final investment decision at Longtail 
and updates on a potential ninth FPSO. In the Permian, management continues to guide 
to ~1.8 MBOE/d of production this year, leveraging stackable technologies to double 
growth and recovery by the end of the decade. We highlight three automated drilling 
rigs delivering standout performance, with plans to expand use to 25% of the Permian 
fleet next year and roughly half by 2028, supporting low-cost production. Management 
views both assets as key components of post-2030 growth, while also pointing to 
international LNG projects and future exploration efforts. We expect management to 
continue to seek more attractive fiscal, legal, and operational terms before investing in 
Venezuela, where production requires technology and scale to generate competitive 
portfolio returns. In the Middle East, management assumes a ~350 kbd production 
impact next quarter. We note UAE production is recovering to historical levels, though 
sales revenue is delayed due to logistics and shipping constraints.  
LNG Portfolio. Management continues to strengthen the LNG portfolio to capture 
long-term natural gas demand, using a wide geographic distribution of supply points for 
customer flexibility. In Papua New Guinea, cumulative production now exceeds 100 mn 
tons, with plans to grow further via the Papua LNG Project, where the company is now 
operator at a 34% working interest. In Mozambique, management reached an 
agreement with ENI to increase working interest in Rovuma LNG to 50% and exit a 
non-operated acreage position. Management expects to reach final investment decision 
later this year, while seeing early opportunities to improve capital efficiency. While we 
monitor any updates from QatarEnergy on repairs to damaged LNG trains in Qatar, 
management reiterates a strong commitment to maximizing the value of the partnership 
and joint ventures. At Golden Pass, management sees strong progress on Train 1 and 
expects Train 2 to be largely complete by December, with mechanical completion of 
Train 3 around mid-2027. 
Downstream Operations. Management highlights strong operational and financial 
results in refining, achieving record utilization levels of around 84% and running US Gulf 
Coast refineries at crude unit utilization of roughly 100%. Supply chain and trading 
teams continue to leverage unique visibility into major market shifts, securing 
feedstocks, redirecting molecules, and moving products to meet global demand. We 
note a 3x increase in Energy Products unit earnings per barrel on a constant price and 
margin basis since 2019. Management continues to improve competitiveness by 
investing in large-scale refineries with integrated, high-value product portfolios, 
expecting US Gulf Coast projects to increase distillate yields by 100 kbd. We expect 
long-term strategy to focus on further high-grading product slate, improving traditional 
petroleum product yields across refineries, and transforming hydrogen and carbon 
molecules into next-generation products like Proxxima. We note near-term macro 
challenges related to restoration of clean product flows through the Strait of Hormuz, 
recovery of Russian refining capacity following Ukranian attacks, and uncertainty around 
China exports. As a result, management expects healthy margins into next year.  
Capital Allocation. Assuming reasonable market conditions, management expects to 
maintain share repurchases at $20 bn next year, alongside steady dividend growth, 
reflecting a long-term commitment to consistency across commodity price cycles.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank

across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
