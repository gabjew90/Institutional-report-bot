Additional data center demand provides further capex upside beyond the base 
plan. XEL now has ~2 GW of data centers contracted or under construction and remains 
on track to sign another ~1 GW by year-end in 2026 and ~3 GW by 2027. Management 
said it feels good about reaching the 4 GW target, with the strongest near-term 
opportunities in the Upper Midwest and SPS, while Colorado is likely to come later as 
XEL works through its large load tariff. Importantly, the infrastructure needed to serve 
this additional 4 GW is not included in the $70bn plan. XEL estimates $6-$8 bn of 
investment for every 1 GW of data center demand, including third-party generation, and 
currently has more than 20 GW of potential additional data center demand in its 
pipeline. We continue to see data centers as upside to the base growth outlook, 
particularly in the back half of the plan and into the early 2030s. 
EPS growth moves into double digits through 2031, while funding needs screen as 
manageable. Management expects 10%+ average EPS growth from 2027-30, off the 
midpoint of 2026 guidance, before moving to 9%+ average growth through 2031, 
though recognizing that 2031 capex is likely lower due to lack of regulatory clarity that 
far in the future. As mentioned above, we model a ~$79 bn capital plan through 2031, 
which extends the 10% EPS growth cadence through 2031, vs. 2030 in management 
guidance. Per the company, the new capital plan requires ~$8bn of equity and ~$26bn 
of additional debt, with ~40% of the equity already contracted. XEL still sees ~40% 
equity as a reasonable assumption for additional capex in the near term, but said the 
equity need could be lower for projects added in 2030-31 as cash flow improves. The 
company expects FFO/debt to reach ~18% by 2031, leaving more room to fund 
additional growth later in the plan. 
Model Updates 
We update our model for 3Q26 actuals and roll our estimates forward through 2031. We 
now reflect ~$79 bn of capex over 2027-31, ~$9 bn above XEL’s $70bn base plan, as we 
give some credit to the company’s $10bn+ of incremental investment opportunities. As 
shown in Exhibit 1, the upside in our model is mainly driven by the NSP, PSCo and SPS 
generation opportunities, with ~$8.7bn of estimated capex from these projects falling 
through 2031. We do not reflect the full incremental opportunity given the remaining 
regulatory and project-level approvals. We also incorporate the latest rate case updates 
and make adjustments to D&A, O&M, TOTI and AFUDC across subsidiaries. From 
2026-30, our updated EPS estimates are $4.16/$4.57/$5.05/$5.59/$6.20, respectively, 
versus $4.16/$4.56/$5.03/$5.51/$6.12 previously. We also introduce a 2031 EPS 
estimate of $6.77. Following these changes and the roll-forward to 2031, our estimates 
imply a 10% EPS CAGR from 2026-31, above management’s 9%+ average EPS growth 
outlook through 2031. As a result of our estimate changes and rolling valuation forward, 
our price target moves to $95 from $93 previously.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
