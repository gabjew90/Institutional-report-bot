Economic outlook and investment strategy

Economic outlook
Asset class views
Asset allocation
Advanced economies 
•
Global growth is likely peaking and should moderate in 2027; still, we expect most 
major economies to grow above potential
•
The expected slowdown reflects a moderation in the pace of the AI buildout 
alongside tighter macroeconomic policy
•
Reduced economic slack and elevated energy prices should keep inflation elevated, 
with a peak in 1Q27 – assuming no renewed large spikes in crude oil prices 
•
We anticipate central banks to raise rates several times next year, with rates peaking 
in Q2-Q3. Weaker growth and lower inflation should then allow for some monetary 
easing in 2028
Emerging market economies
•
China’s growth dynamics have improved slightly with strong exports and improving 
fiscal execution. We have upgraded next year’s growth to 4.5% as we expect export 
momentum to remain while investment should pick up
•
The AI investment boom dominates the Asian EM macro outlook for the next 6-12 
months. Most Asian EMs have joined the global capex cycle
•
Outside the AI supply chains, the capex momentum in infrastructure and strategic 
sectors also supports growth momentum, helping to offset headwinds from high 
energy prices and rates
•
Inflation has picked up in most EMs. Elevated energy prices and rising food prices 
have already led a number of EM central banks to hike their policy rates. More will 
follow over the next 6 months
Fixed Income and Credit
•
Yields likely rangebound over coming months
•
Prefer intermediate maturities
•
Neutral on credit
Foreign Exchange
•
Renewed US growth outperformance to support the dollar, while the euro faces 
temporary pressures given the French budget crisis
•
FX interventions will be the SNB’s preferred policy instrument, yet they are not 
needed currently
Global Equities
•
Preference for Swiss and US equities, utilities and staples
•
Bonds: Slight underweight
•
Equities: Slight overweight

BJSS House View:
Positioning as approved by Investment Committee
Source: Bank J. Safra Sarasin, 03.11.2026. For information purposes only. Any projections constitute the authors’ judgement at the time of writing. Forecasts are the expectations of Economic & Strategy Research and are 
subject to change without notice. Past performance is not indicative of future results. 
BJSS House View as of November 2026 
Reasons for positioning
⚫
O/W Equities: Remain constructive for equities markets in 2H26. The 
earnings outlook remains positive, macro indicators are robust, fiscal 
stimulus across the globe (USA, Germany, Japan) is supportive, and equity 
trends are intact. Elevated inflation and hawkish central banks are the key 
risks for equities. 
⚫
U/W Fixed Income: Limited return potential mostly based on carry, credit still 
supported and thus high yield and emerging market bonds at neutral.
⚫
Neutral Alternatives: Neutral commodities: linked to global growth and key 
beneficiary of AI capex cycle. Neutral gold, likely to continue to benefit from 
US policy uncertainty, EM central bank buying. But headwinds from rising 
rates and a stronger US Dollar. Private markets: U/W private credit: still 
struggling from uncertainties around recent credit defaults and potentially 
higher rates. On the other hand infrastructure should benefit from AI capex 
buildout, thus O/W
⚫
FX views: USD supported by cyclical momentum in the US. EUR faces 
headwinds as long as Fed maintains its hawkish bias. CHF headwinds to 
fade once SNB embarks on hiking cycle, fiscal concerns a latent supporting 
factor and upside risk

Fixed Income: Yields likely rangebound over coming months
Source: LSEG, Bank J. Safra Sarasin, 03.11.2026. For information purposes only. Any projections constitute the authors’ judgement at the time of writing. Forecasts are the expectations of Economic & Strategy Research 
and are subject to change without notice. Past performance is not indicative of future results. 
• The current AI-related capex boom in the US and elevated inflation
rates risk higher policy rate expectations, particularly in the US.
Europe, on the other hand, seems to be more than appropriately
priced. So far, high real rates have only had a limited impact on the
global economy. Long-term real rates are highest in close to 20y.
The normalisation of term premiums looks well advanced.
• We expect long-term yields to move largely sideways from here
over the coming months. That said, financial conditions are
tightening and could increasingly prepare the ground for somewhat
lower yields in 2027.
• Consequently, carry should be the main driver for fixed income
returns over the next few months. We continue to recommend 5- to
7-year maturities, which still present the best risk-/return trade-off.
• Credit spreads have widened a bit from the lows, but they remain
historically tight. We expect more widening over coming quarters
as higher real rates tighten financial conditions further, though not
to the degree that would justify a structural underweight.
