Our view
AI investment boom has now entered its fourth year. 
Although discussions around various challenges have 
intensified recently—for example, over the past weekend, 
large AI companies commented on the potential security 
threats that future AI development could pose to human 
and called for globally coordinated regulation to ensure 
safe AI development, potentially including a slower pace 
of further advances—we do not believe this necessarily 
means that investment in AI computing capacity will slow 
significantly. This is mainly because the global adoption of 
AI in daily life continues to accelerate. At the same time, 
appropriate regulation should support AI’s sustainable long-
term development and help sustain investment in AI-related 
capital expenditure.
The extension of AI-capex spending and the increasing 
importance of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) in high-end 
AI computing have driven a powerful upcycle in dynamic 
random-access memory (DRAM). Strong demand for HBM 
has tightened supply, supported pricing and encouraged 
memory suppliers to prioritize advanced products, while 
the broader recovery in conventional DRAM has further 
strengthened industry fundamentals. These developments 
have also intensified market interest in the global memory 
sector, particularly following CXMT’s IPO in August.

In our view, this is a timely opportunity to examine 
more closely the fundamental competitive dynamics of the 
global memory market and assess the potential of China’s 
domestic vendors. While leading global suppliers retain 
significant advantages in advanced process technology, 
manufacturing yields and customer relationships, Chinese 
companies could continue to make progress in mainstream 
DRAM and NAND, supported by domestic demand and 
supply-chain localization. The pace of their development will 
depend on their ability to execute technologically, secure 
equipment, qualify products with customers, and navigate 
the evolving export-control environment.
What’s the current global competitive landscape for 
memory?
Based on the latest information, the global memory industry 
is increasingly shaped by the divergence between AI-related 
memory demand and traditional end markets. The robust 
demand from AI server system has driven the growth of 
all three major memory chip types. HBM DRAM supplies 
the extremely high bandwidth needed by GPUs or AI 
accelerators to process data rapidly, while double data rate 
(DDR) DRAM provides the system’s main working memory 
for CPUs and general-purpose tasks. NAND flash offers high-
capacity, non-volatile storage for datasets and applications, 
allowing data to be retained when the system is powered 
off.
HBM has become the fastest-growing and most strategically 
important segment, driven by data-center investment. 
Conventional server DRAM is also benefiting from data-
center demand, although smartphone and consumer-
electronics demand remains comparatively subdued. 
Nevertheless, as the intensity of AI demand continues to 
increase, we expect the overall global memory market to 
maintain rapid growth over the next few years. We expect 
global DRAM demand to see 30% CAGR in the period 
of 2025-27, from 14% during 2022-24, mainly driven by 
strong 66% CAGR in server DRAM demand.

Strong demand growth is prompting major memory 
manufacturers to actively expand capacity. Memory has 
traditionally been a relatively easy semiconductor segment 
in which to add capacity. Although equipment production 
and delivery take time, we expect global memory capacity to 
grow by 15-22% annually from 2026 to 2028. Nonetheless, 
even at this pace of capacity expansion, strong AI-driven 
demand is expected to leave a significant, double-digit gap 
in global memory supply-demand balance, measured in 
million gigabits, through 2027.
The memory cycle is increasingly bifurcated by product. 
HBM remains the tightest segment, supported by rapid 
AI-accelerator growth, rising memory content per system, 
and multi-year supply commitments. High-capacity server 
DRAM is also benefiting from AI and cloud-infrastructure 
investment, as both accelerated and general-purpose 
servers require more memory. Together, HBM and server 
DRAM are driving industry capacity allocation and pricing, 
while enterprise demand provides additional support, albeit 
with greater dependence on deployment schedules and 
customer budgets.

By contrast, conventional computer (PC), smartphone and 
consumer electronics demand is recovering only gradually, 
with modest unit growth despite rising DRAM content from 
AI PCs and premium handsets. Nonetheless, conventional 
DDR and LPDDR (low power DDR) are nevertheless facing a 
tightening supply outlook as suppliers prioritize HBM, server 
DRAM and other higher-value products, leaving less wafer 
capacity and investment for commodity memory.
As a result, commodity DRAM pricing could rise even 
without a broad-based recovery in downstream demand, 
while PC, smartphone, automotive, and industrial customers 
may respond through lower memory configurations, 
delayed 
product 
launches, 
tighter 
inventories, 
or 
margin pressure. AI-related memory remains structurally 
undersupplied, whereas commodity DRAM is tightening 
mainly because of supply constraints and product-mix shifts.

The global memory market remains highly segmented by 
product, with current global incumbents maintaining clear 
leadership in HBM and advanced/server DRAM, as well as 
broad capabilities across DRAM and NAND. Chinese new 
joiners are gaining importance through CXMT’s progress 
in commodity DRAM and LPDDR and YMTC’s expanding 
NAND presence. However, both remain some distance from 
competing in frontier AI memory, particularly HBM. The key 
implication is that China may provide additional supply in 
selected mainstream memory markets, whereas advanced 
AI-memory capacity remains concentrated among global 
incumbents.

Despite delays in technology development and constraints 
on equipment access, we believe CXMT could still capture 
part of the current AI-driven memory upcycle. The ongoing 
pricing uptrend extends beyond HBM to conventional DDR, 
where supply has tightened as leading DRAM suppliers 
reallocate capacity toward HBM. From the forecast of UBS 
Research, we see a 137% ASP CAGR for DDR versus 
43% for HBM over 2025-27. CXMT is also at an early 
stage of HBM ramp-up and plans to expand HBM capacity, 
which could provide further exposure to AI-related memory 
growth.

While China’s memory industry accelerates its general 
development and expands its global influence, we could say 
each company is pursuing a different strategic development 
focus.
CXMT is China's leading DRAM producer and is expanding 
both its output and product portfolio. Its initial focus has 
been on more mature and mainstream DRAM applications, 
while it continues to improve its process technology and 
develop products for smartphone, PC, consumer electronics, 
and server markets. CXMT is also focusing on developing 
domestic HBM alternatives, which are essential for domestic 
AI accelerators. Progress will depend not only on memory-
cell technology, but also on advanced packaging and 
collaboration with domestic AI-chip companies. HBM is 
considerably more complex than conventional DRAM, and 
China’s HBM ecosystem remains at an earlier stage of 
development than those of the leading global suppliers.
As to NAND flash, YMTC is China's leading NAND 
manufacturer and has developed increasingly advanced 
3D NAND products. Its progress is supported by 
China’s large domestic markets for smartphone, PC, 
and data centers, as well as local customers' efforts to 
diversify their supply chains. However, advanced NAND 
production remains constrained by limited access to leading-
edge equipment, manufacturing yields, and technology-
development challenges.
Government funding, local subsidies and the strategic 
objective of reducing reliance on imported semiconductors 
still provide the strongest long-term support for China’s 
domestic memory sector. Since the mid-2010s, Chinese 
companies have demonstrated strong capacity-expansion 
capabilities and a significant ability to disrupt pricing 
across various other product areas, such as electric vehicles 
and power-management semiconductors. However, the 
gap between Chinese companies and global leaders in 
memory capacity and technological advancement remains 
substantial. Therefore, we believe their impact on global 
supply-demand dynamics and pricing will remain relatively 
limited during the current 2025-27/28 cycle.

Why and how should we position in China’s memory 
sector at current stage?
Despite CXMT’s mid-double-digit forward P/E valuation, 
which is still relatively high versus global memory leaders, 
we believe its long-term strategic potential warrants 
attention. As a key domestic DRAM manufacturer supported 
by the Chinese government, CXMT is positioned to 
expand capacity, improve yields, and reduce unit costs 
while serving China’s large smartphone, PC, consumer-
electronics, automotive, and industrial markets. Its focus 
on mainstream LPDDR and DDR products, together with 
growing demand from customers seeking to reduce reliance 
on overseas suppliers, provides a foundation for sustained 
market-share gains.
CXMT could broaden its capabilities into advanced 
server memory and HBM, although progress will depend 
on process execution, equipment access, packaging 
technology, yields and customer qualification. Investors 
should therefore view CXMT as a long-term strategic 
growth investment rather than a near-term HBM competitor, 
while monitoring valuation, capital intensity, utilization and 
potential industry oversupply. Its ability to balance capacity 
expansion with margins and cash flow will be critical to 
converting government support and domestic demand into 
durable shareholder value.
For YMTC, which is expected to list later this year, its upside 
potential is in its ability to strengthen its position as China’s 
leading domestic NAND supplier and capture growing 
demand for smartphones, PC, consumer electronics, and 
AI infrastructure. Further improvements in production 
scale and yields could enhance cost competitiveness 
and support market share gains, while supply-chain 
localization may accelerate customer adoption. Enterprise 
SSD expansion and selective overseas growth provide 
additional opportunities, although investors should weigh 
these against geopolitical risks, development challenges and 
execution risks.

Equity selection: An assessment relative to a benchmark
Equity selections in Equity Preferences lists (EPLs) are assessments made relative to a sector/industry, country/regional or 
thematic benchmark. The chosen benchmark is disclosed on the front page of each EPL. It is also used to measure the 
performance of the EPL owner(s). Including a stock in the EPL constitutes neither a view on its expected, standalone absolute 
performance nor a price target. Rather, EPLs are meant to support the UBS House View, with the stocks included in them 
selected for their superior risk/return profiles.
Our selection is based on an assessment of the company's fundamental outlook and valuation, the risks owning the stock 
entails, including material sustainability risks and the diversification benefits it provides in an investment portfolio, among 
many other factors. UBS GWM CIO‘s selection methodology enables wealth management clients to invest in a specific 
investment theme or focus on a sector/industry or country/region.
The lead strategist is responsible for the CIO view on a stock, while the EPL owner(s) is/are responsible for the stock selection 
within an EPL. Stocks can be selected for multiple EPLs. For consistency's sake, a stock can only be selected as either Most 
Preferred or Least Preferred, not both simultaneously. As EPL benchmarks differ, stocks do not need to be included on every 
list to which they could theoretically be added.
