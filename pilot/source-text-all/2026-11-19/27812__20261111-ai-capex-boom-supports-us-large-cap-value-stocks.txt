ROBECO BP US LARGE CAP EQUITIES STRATEGY 
AI capex boom supports US 
large-cap value stocks 
 Large-cap equities strategy invests in four of the Magnificent Seven 
 AI capex cycle is fueling the largest infrastructure boom since the railroads 
 Value stocks in tech, financials and energy all poised to benefit from the cycle 
 
They’re among the biggest and best-known companies in the world, and have accounted for an outsize share 
of stock market returns in recent years. The ‘Magnificent Seven’ are synonymous for many investors with 
growth companies, typically trading at high multiples that reflect equally high expectations about future 
profits. 
 
But after a challenging market in July for a broad array of tech names, some of these industry titans have since 
come into scope for value investors such as the Robeco BP US Large Cap Equities strategy. Their market multiples 
now put them within the remit of the firm’s ‘Three Circles’, which seeks stocks that have a fair value, excellent 
fundamentals, and good business momentum. 
 
Nvidia, a leading manufacturer of the microchips that are powering the artificial intelligence boom, and Meta, the 
owner of Facebook, Instagram, and lately a potentially blockbuster AI-powered pocket tool, Muse, have both found 
their way into the portfolio this year. The strategy also owns Microsoft and Amazon, for a total of four of the 
Magnificent Seven – but not Apple, Tesla, or Alphabet, the owner of Google.1 
“ The hyper-scalers – some of the very largest, highest-quality companies in the 
world – have become attractive from a valuation perspective for different reasons 
Everything with chips  
“If you had told me three years ago that I would one day have Nvidia in the portfolio, I would have said ‘you're 
crazy’,” says David Cohen, Portfolio Manager of the Robeco BP US Large Cap Equities strategy. “But then we 
started to see some high-quality names with growth characteristics start to meet our value criteria.” 
 
“Several hyper-scale companies have increasingly aligned with our Three Circles for different reasons, exhibiting 
characteristics that fit our valuation, quality, and momentum screening criteria.”

“Now, when you look at the valuations, I can buy Nvidia2 at 15.5 times earnings, which is basically in line with the 
large-cap median – except that the company is growing earnings by over 200% a year.3 And it has very high 
returns on equity. It’s a discounted multiple because there's market skepticism about the sustainability of their 
profitability and the length of the profit cycle.” 
Biggest boom since the railroads 
Cohen says this profit cycle is expected to last, due to the sheer scale of the capital expenditure (capex) boom that 
is being led by AI. Aside from semiconductors, this capex cycle is fueling the building of vast numbers of data 
centers, as well as infrastructure non-tech sectors such as renewable energy and healthcare. 
 
“We have now transitioned into the largest capex cycle in US history, which has been highly positive for large caps, 
since you need the scale,” Cohen says. “AI expenditures as a percentage of GDP are north of 3%; the railroad 
buildout peaked at 1.5%. And then we have a buildout in power infrastructure, transmission lines, substations, civil 
infrastructure, bridges, roads and water.”

“So we have this massive amount of value cyclicals with strong accelerating demand, backed by good underlying 
fundamentals. That has led this transition from growth to value; the Russell 1000 Growth Index4 is up 7% this year 
while the Value Index is up 21% in the US.”  
Mineral game changers  
Reshoring – the practice of relocating essential industries at home rather than basing them overseas – is also 
aiding the boom. “Critical mineral deposits that had become dependent on China are being developed within the 
US,” Cohen says.

“All of this infrastructure investment requires raw materials – copper, steel, cement, concrete building aggregates, 
and energy. It requires industrial components, industrial machinery, and semiconductors, which are the widgets of 
the 21st century.”  
 
“Then there has been reshoring in the critical vertically integrated industries like pharmaceuticals and 
semiconductor fabrication plants. These are not short-cycle type events. These are investment regimes that last a 
decade-plus.”  
The new Muse 
While the focus has been on large-scale industrial buildouts, consumer and social platforms such as Meta5 have 
also come into play with the strategy, again boosted by the AI revolution.

“They’re among the most innovative companies in the world,” Cohen says. “They're creating innovative consumer 
products such as eyeglasses and other devices that integrate with their AI models, along with consumer agent 
products.”  
 
“The new big thing is Muse, a consumer agent platform that’s effectively like having a personal assistant in your 
pocket. It can go book reservations for you, cancel your subscriptions, find savings for you and manage your 
expenditures.” 
 
“It has been downloaded over five million times in three weeks; ChatGPT, which was the fastest ramp-up for any 
product in history, was 2.3 million. So this is growing at double that rate.” 
Automating the banks 
Financial services are another growth area where AI can bring long-term efficiencies and returns, Cohen believes. 
“We’ve seen AI being infused into organizations and consumer applications at the enterprise level. We've seen it 
revolutionize coding, and now the next phase of this is going to target automating operational functions that exist 
in the back office of information businesses.  
 
“Banks and insurance companies have massive operational layers; client onboarding, client servicing, credit card 
charge matching, claims processing, and so on. A great proportion of them are going to be automated through AI.” 
 
“In the US there are 4,000 banks, the vast majority of which are regional banks, and only a handful of very large 
banks. The very large banks spend USD 1-2 billion a year on IT and so they're able to automate this stuff, whereas 
these regional banks don't have the same ability to innovate and to automate their back offices.”  
The large-cap sweet spot 
“So we think there's a benefit to scale that's going to result in profitability and market share being taken by the 
larger caps. The benefits of scale due to technology are ever more present in the current world, and again the 
sweet spot here is actually on the value side in large cap.”
