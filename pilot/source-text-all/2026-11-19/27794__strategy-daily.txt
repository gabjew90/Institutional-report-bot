USTs remain supported this morning, extending yesterday’s risk-off bull-steepening, although the rally 
remains fairly modest considering the weakness in equities. 10Y UST yields are still above 5.10%. The DXY 
index is off the highs, trading just below 103.50. The equity/rates beta remains the more interesting part of the 
story for us. The S&P fell 1.9% yesterday, Nasdaq -2.5% and semis -5.3%, with the sell-off extending into Asia 
after the US tightened restrictions around access to advanced AI chips. So far, USTs are responding, but not 
aggressively, consistent with a market still reluctant to fade the hawkish Fed repricing. Today’s Fed speak and 
retail sales data are the key macro catalysts. Consensus looks for a sharp slowdown from September, and 
we see some downside risk. A soft number alongside another leg lower in equities would probably give the belly 
more room to rally. Oil is the other beta we are watching. Brent fell more than 3% on Monday and is now below 
$92pb as the narrative around a potential Q4 supply surplus outweigh continuing Iran/Hormuz risks. JD Vance 
also pushed back against the idea of a prolonged US war, although tanker incidents continue and reports of Iranian 
contingency plans targeting US officials underline that geopolitical tail risks have not disappeared. Lower oil should 
remain supportive for duration at the margin.  
EGBs are starting better bid, while EUR/USD bounced off Monday’s lows overnight and is trading around 
1.10. ECB speakers are plentiful today, including Makhlouf and Lane. We would watch whether they validate the 
extent of tightening now embedded in the curve (~65bp priced), although with Brent now below $92, the oil move 
should provide some counterweight to recent hawkish repricing if sustained. The ZEW survey and Schatz supply 
(€5bn) are also worth watching, but directionally the bigger impulse probably still comes from USTs, oil and equities 
rather than domestic data. On another note, Knot is reportedly expected to drop out of the ECB race this week, 
further boosting De Cos’ chances. We would read that as slightly dovish at the margin, but he is a consensus-
builder rather than an obvious policy driver, and the other two key Executive Board appointments will be just as 
important for the rates direction. 
This morning’s labour data give a mixed read on slack, but overall probably validate rather than resolve 
the BoE’s concerns around the slack/wage-growth dynamic. The September private earnings ex-bonus came 
in broadly as expected at 3.1% 3M/YoY, with the wage data still showing no clear deceleration. Unemployment 
unexpectedly fell to 4.6% vs 4.7% expected, which on its own points to slightly less slack. The counter-signal came 
from October payrolls, which fell a much larger-than-expected 34k versus +10k consensus, pointing instead 
towards some renewed loosening in the labour market. But monthly payrolls are volatile and frequently revised, so 
it feels too early to put too much weight on that downside surprise. GBP/USD struggled for direction after the 
numbers and is trading below 1.32. For rates, the payroll miss can give front-end SONIA some room to rally 
and makes December a closer call. But with wages still sticky and unemployment moving lower, we don’t 
think today’s data are enough to write December completely off. Tomorrow’s inflation print is another 
important test, particularly services inflation, and should give us a cleaner read on whether the slack/wage story is 
actually translating into less persistent domestic inflation pressure. 
Asian equities extended Monday’s risk-off tone. In Japan, moves in JGBs were quite contained, with the curve 
twist-steepening into tomorrow’s 20Y JGB auction, while USD/JPY fell below 159.40.
