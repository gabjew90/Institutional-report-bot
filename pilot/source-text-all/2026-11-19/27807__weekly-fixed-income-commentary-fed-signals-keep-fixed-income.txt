Market recap
Rates rose sharply after October U.S. CPI came in hotter than expected and U.S. 
Federal Reserve commentary turned more hawkish. Governor Waller suggested a 
50 basis point hike should be considered in December. Equities were volatile but 
finished higher, rebounding from the prior week’s AI-led selloff, though a leadership 
change at a major semiconductor firm weighed on the complex late in the week.
Fixed income losses were broad and nearly all rate-driven. Taxable municipals and 
agency MBS fell hardest given their duration, while the Bloomberg Aggregate U.S. 
Bond Index and Treasuries also declined sharply. Floating-rate assets were the 
exception. Senior loans and CLO tranches posted gains, insulated from the selloff, 
and convertibles rose with equities. Credit spreads tightened across investment 
grade, high yield and emerging markets. 
Outlook
Markets now fully price in a December Fed rate hike and additional hikes in early 
2027, a more aggressive path than our forecast. We continue to expect one more 
hike by year-end, followed by an extended hold through 2027. This view depends 
on the energy shock and its pass-through to core inflation. FOMC minutes are 
due this week.
Credit fundamentals remain sound, reinforced by spreads tightening through 
the selloff. We continue to favor high-quality income and, in municipals, longer 
duration over Treasuries. We forecast the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.95% for year-
end 2026 and 4.75% for the end of 2027, and the 2-year at 4.70% this year and 
4.50% next year.

U.S. Treasuries 
Yields rose across the curve, erasing several weeks of gains, as hot inflation 
data and hawkish Federal Reserve commentary raised expectations for further 
tightening. Short maturities moved most, flattening the yield curve.  
The 2-year rose 33 bps to 4.84%, 
the 5-year 32 bps to 4.96%, the 10-
year 28 bps to 5.14%, the 20-year 
22 bps to 5.52% and the 30-year 
20 bps to 5.47%. 
Tax-exempt municipals
Municipals fell with the broader market but outperformed Treasuries, as 
muni yields rose less than the Treasury curve. Yields remain near levels that 
have historically attracted buyers, and we continue to favor longer duration in 
municipals over Treasuries. 
The Bloomberg Municipal Index 
returned -0.78%.
Taxable municipals
Taxable municipals were the weakest sector, as their long maturity profile 
left them most exposed to rising yields. Higher absolute yields should attract 
crossover buyers, and we view the backup as a potential opportunity to earn 
attractive income.
The Bloomberg Municipal Taxable 
Index returned -1.96% with 
spreads at 57 bps. 
 
Investment grade corporates
Investment grade had a constructive week, with spreads tightening broadly 
across ratings and sectors even as rates sold off. Expectations for lighter supply 
into year-end supported sentiment, though fund inflows slowed in a U.S. 
holiday-shortened week. 
The Bloomberg U.S. Corporate 
Bond Index returned -1.35%, with 
spreads at 86 bps.

U.S. high yield corporates
High yield held up relatively well, declining only modestly as tighter spreads 
cushioned the move in rates. Sentiment softened after the inflation print, 
with earnings and company-specific news driving much of the activity. New 
issuance was light.
The Bloomberg High Yield 2% 
Issuer Capped Index returned 
-0.26%, with spreads at 316 bps.
Preferred securities
Preferreds declined as rising yields weighed on prices, though spreads 
tightened. Retail-oriented par preferreds continued to lag, extending a difficult 
year, while bank and European issues held up better. A small amount of new 
hybrid supply came to market.
The ICE Preferred Index returned 
-0.92% with spreads at 185 bps.
Senior loans
Loans were one of the few sectors to post a gain, as their floating-rate structure 
insulated them from the selloff in fixed-rate markets. Buyers were active 
throughout the week, fund flows stayed positive and new issuance was light. 
The S&P Leveraged Loan 
Index returned +0.21%, with 
spreads at 493 bps.
Securitized credit
 Agency mortgages underperformed as their yield premium over Treasuries 
widened toward a one-year high and mortgage rates rose with Treasuries. 
Securitized credit held up better. Asset-backed issuance was well received, 
particularly consumer deals, and collateralized loan obligations stayed firm as 
investors favored floating-rate assets ahead of likely further hikes.
The Bloomberg MBS Index returned 
-1.84%, with spreads at 36 bps. 
The Bloomberg CMBS Index 
returned -1.04%, with spreads 
at 71 bps. The Bloomberg ABS 
Index returned -0.73%, with 
spreads at 49 bps.
Global emerging markets
Emerging markets debt fell with global rates, though hard currency spreads 
tightened and corporates outperformed sovereigns. Local currency markets 
lagged as the dollar strengthened. Investor outflows accelerated, while new 
issuance met solid demand. 
The Bloomberg Global EM 
Index returned -0.78%, with 
spreads at 182 bps.

Investing involves risk; principal loss is possible. Debt or fixed income securities are subject to market 
risk, credit risk, interest rate risk, call risk, derivatives risk, dollar roll transaction risk and income risk.

instruments in a company’s capital structure and therefore are subject to greater credit risk. Foreign 
investments involve additional risks, including currency fluctuation, political and economic instability, 
lack of liquidity and differing legal and accounting standards. These risks may be magnified in emerging 
markets. Asset-backed and mortgage-backed securities are subject to additional risks such as prepayment 
risk, liquidity risk, default risk and adverse economic developments. The value of convertible securities 
may decline in response to such factors as rising interest rates and fluctuations in the market price of 
the underlying securities. Senior loans are subject to loan settlement risk due to the lack of established 
settlement standards or remedies for failure to settle. These investments are subject to credit risk and 
potentially limited liquidity, as well as interest rate risk, currency risk, prepayment and extension risk, 
and inflation risk. Any investment in collateralized loan obligations or other structured vehicles involves 
significant risks not associated with more conventional investment alternatives.

before choosing an investment style or manager.
