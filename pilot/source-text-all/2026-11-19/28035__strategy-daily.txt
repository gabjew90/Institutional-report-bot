USTs remain caught between resilient US data and increasingly fragile risk sentiment. Stronger October 
retail sales initially pushed the front end lower yesterday, with 2Y UST yields briefly reaching 4.85%, while 
Musalem’s warning that “several” further hikes may be needed added to the bear-flattening move. But the renewed 
equity sell-off ultimately dominated, pulling 2Y yields back below 4.80% and 10Y below 5.10%. That FTQ bid has 
somewhat extended in Asian hours as the post-Nvidia bounce in S&P futures quickly faded. The DXY index held 
onto Tuesday’s gains, trading around 103.68. Today’s focus will likely remain on equities and oil. S&P futures 
are searching for a floor, with Nasdaq futures struggling to hold the initial Nvidia-led bounce, another leg lower 
should keep a bid under duration. Meanwhile, Brent has slipped back below $92pb despite another escalation in 
the US-Iran conflict. Bahrain said it intercepted fresh Iranian attacks overnight, following yesterday’s US strike on 
an Iranian missile site at Qeshm, while disruption through Hormuz continues. Large API builds in crude and 
gasoline are nevertheless keeping the bearish supply story in control for now. Away from equities and oil, 
housing data, $16bn of 20Y UST supply and plenty of Fed speak are on the calendar, but the FOMC minutes 
are the main macro event. With December already fully priced and around 80bp of tightening priced through Sep-
27, we would need a clearly hawkish set of minutes to materially extend the front-end sell-off.  
EGBs should retain a modest FTQ bid this morning, with Bunds taking their cue from lower UST yields 
and the renewed softness in equities. German yields ended yesterday only marginally lower, with 10s around 
0.5bp down, after the early rally was largely unwound by stronger US data, Musalem’s hawkish comments and the 
rebound in oil. In France, Le Pen’s attempt to frame a lower retirement age alongside a funded private 
savings scheme was surprisingly well received, taking the 10Y OAT-Bund spread from around 157bp to 
152.5bp and below. We would be cautious about extrapolating too much from that move, however, given the 
longer-term fiscal implications still point in the opposite direction. EUR/USD is back to recent lows, below 1.0980, 
consistent with the firmer dollar and still-wide US-EUR rate differential. For today, softer oil and weak equities 
should keep duration supported, while Lagarde, the ECB Financial Stability Review and final euro-area CPI 
provide the local focus.  
There is little evidence that higher energy costs are broadening into underlying domestic inflation. The 
October headline CPI rose to 3.6% YoY, with the acceleration largely reflecting energy, while core was unchanged 
at 2.8%. More importantly for the MPC, services CPI missed slightly and remained at 3.7%, vs expectations for an 
acceleration. RPI was the only modest upside surprise at 4.2%. Following yesterday’s evidence of no clear 
wage growth acceleration, today’s data can buy the BoE some time if it wants to proceed more gradually 
with further hikes than the SONIA curve currently prices, particularly if the bearish pressure on oil persists. 
We therefore expect the strip to find a bid this morning. GBP/USD is also back around recent lows, trading 
near 1.3175, after slipping following the CPI release. Bailey speaks this morning, but unless he pushes back 
against a more gradual tightening path, we would expect the rates reaction to CPI to dominate. 
Asian equities saw an improvement in risk sentiment following better NVDA’s results, although the 
positivity started to lose steam as the session progressed. In Japan, JGBs twist-steepened after a soft 20Y 
JGB auction. Meanwhile, USD/JPY continued to trade in a tight range, currently around 159.35.
