Japan Macro & Strategy 
Japan Macro – 2027 Japan Economic Outlook: Resilient Growth and Resurgent 
Inflation Drive Faster Rate Hikes. We raise our inflation and BOJ policy rate forecasts, 
expecting resilient growth to drive faster tightening. With the government scaling 
back fuel price controls, we expect core CPI to rapidly exceed 3.5% through the 
summer of 2027. Consequently, we now forecast three 25 bps BOJ rate hikes in 
December 2026, March 2027, and June 2027, bringing the terminal rate to 2.0%. We 
expect real GDP growth of +0.9% in CY26 and +1.0% in CY27. Higher interest rates 
and inflation will likely challenge the economy. However, solid real wage growth, 
supported by an April 2027 food consumption tax cut and base pay hikes exceeding 
3%, should underpin consumption. While we expect upcoming fiscal initiatives will 
widen the FY27 primary deficit to 2.1% of GDP, the short-term economic boost and 
impact on JGB supply dynamics should be limited. Tomohiro Ota 
Japan Portfolio Strategy – 2027 Outlook: Japan’s new growth mindset. We believe 
2027 will be a transitional year for Japanese equities, with market performance 
broadening beyond the narrow concentration in AI and banks. We set a 12-month 
target of 4500 for the TOPIX, driven by moderate FY27 EPS growth of 9% rather than 
multiple expansion. We favor AI-adjacent themes with clear policy tailwinds, such as 
physical AI, energy and resource security, and cybersecurity. Amid top-down 
pressure on corporate Japan to invest in growth, we expect more event-driven 
opportunities around corporate restructuring and sector consolidation. In our sector 
allocation, we raise Energy Resources and Commercial & Wholesale Trade to 
Overweight to capture energy security themes. Conversely, we lower Banks and IT & 
Services to Equal Weight, citing potential profit-taking on financials and AI disruption 
concerns. We maintain our Overweight on Electrical Appliance & Precision, 
Machinery, and Steels & Non Ferrous Metals. Bruce Kirk

of 2.4% in 2027, above consensus, and core PCE inflation to fall substantially to 2.4% as

Global Rates 
2027 Global Rates Outlook – A Better Buffer. We expect positive total returns for global 
bonds in 2027, supported by high starting yields, fading hawkish risks and improved 
hedge value. As disinflationary evidence accumulates, we expect most central banks to 
deliver fewer hikes than markets price. We forecast the largest rallies in 10y USTs and 
Gilts, reaching 4.6% and 4.8% respectively by YE27, and a more measured decline in 10y 
Bund yields to 3.1%. We expect 10y JGBs to remain range-bound around 2.75%, as the 
BoJ likely reaches a 2% terminal rate earlier than implied. Late-cycle dynamics favor 
positioning for 5y outperformance in the US and Euro area. We expect yield curves to 
steepen in the UK and Germany, and the JGB curve to flatten. Given distinct regional 
responses to inflation shocks, we favor owning 10y TIPS on a beta versus nominals in the 
US, while preferring near-dated nominal forwards in Europe and the UK. William Marshall 
Precious Metals 
Precious Analyst – 2027 Outlook: It Is 2022 Again. We believe the 2027 setup for 
precious metals resembles 2022, as a rising Fed policy rate weighs on private investor 
demand while structural central bank buying maintains a firm bid under gold. We see 
near-term downside risk and elevated volatility as the Fed continues to hike. We expect 
continued central bank purchases to lift gold prices to $4,650/toz by end-2027. Once 
the Fed begins cutting, recovering Western ETF demand should support a stronger gold 
rally to $5,600/toz by end-2028. We expect gold to outperform silver in 2027, as Fed 
hikes pressure investment demand for both metals but only gold benefits from 
structural central bank buying. For platinum and palladium, persistent US tariff 
uncertainty should keep supply fragmented and volatility elevated. Rising EV 
penetration remains a structural auto-demand headwind, weighing more heavily on 
platinum as Europe transitions away from diesel vehicles. Lina Thomas

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
