September’s repair fades
October’s data expose September’s fragile repair: production slowed, investment 
weakened and consumption offered little offset. Tech resilience has not revived 
domestic demand, while housing depresses construction and confidence. The 
weakness extends beyond property.
Industrial production slowed to 4.8%oya from 5.5% (J.P. Morgan and consensus: 
5.3%), falling 0.3%m/m sa on our estimate. High-tech manufacturing and 
electronics grew 17.6%oya and 19.4%, but auto manufacturing slowed to 0.9%. 
Equipment manufacturing expanded, with industrial robots, integrated circuits and 
new-energy vehicles output up 26.0%oya, 21.9% and 17.1%. Tech cushions output 
without reviving domestic-demand-sensitive industries.
Investment is the clearest drag. We estimate fixed asset investment fell 16.1%oya 
in October, versus 7.2% in September, and 1.6%m/m sa. Official cumulative FAI 
declined 8.1%oya ytd (J.P. Morgan: -7.1%; consensus: -7.0%). Infrastructure and 
manufacturing investment fell 5.1%oya ytd and 2.9%, respectively, alongside a 
21.6% property-investment contraction. Manufacturing investment remained 
broadly weak despite growth in computers, communications and other electronic 
equipment (12.0%oya).
Housing reinforces the investment-consumption drag. Property investment, new-
property sales by floor space and developer funding fell 21.6%oya ytd, 13.7% and 
21.9%, respectively. Weak new sales constrain construction and confidence; 
stronger secondary-market turnover supports transactions but does less for 
developer cash flow.
Retail sales slowed to 0.4%oya from 1.1%, below our 0.7% and consensus 0.8% 
forecasts; we estimate a 0.2%m/m sa decline. Auto retail sales contracted 
21.4%oya, while communications-equipment sales growth slowed to 6.5% from 
31.1%, highlighting fading momentum in selected goods categories. Services 
retail sales outperformed goods, rising to 4.9%oya ytd versus 0.9%, but have yet 
to generate a broad spending recovery.

Weak transmission, rising downside risks
Financing has yet to become demand. In October, bank loans contracted CNY 100 billion, 
including a CNY 430 billion decline in household loans; loan growth slowed to 4.7%oya 
despite TSF growth of 7.2%. The CNY 800 billion policy-bank funding deployment still 
needs to become spending, with local matching funds and procurement constraining 
execution. Private, road, and public-facilities investment fell 11.4%oya ytd, 10.9%, and 
14.1%, respectively, consistent with limited crowding-in. Clearing arrears, completing 
projects and supporting incomes would strengthen transmission more directly than cheaper 
funding alone.
Our baseline – 5.3%q/q saar growth in both 4Q26 and 1Q27, and 4.6%y/y in 2026 – requires 
fiscal catch-up and sustained tech demand. Execution delays could weaken the transmission 
while shifting support into early 2027. October headline and core CPI of 0.7%oya and 0.8%, 
versus PPI of 5.7%, point to upstream cost pressures, not demand-led reflation; our 2026 CPI 
forecast remains 0.8%. With the next rate cut now expected in 2027, fiscal delivery remains 
decisive for near-term growth.
External resilience offers a cushion, but not a secure growth engine. October exports rose 
35.2%oya in US-dollar terms, while the surplus narrowed to US$105.3 billion. Incoming 
Democratic control of both US chambers could increase tariff-relief pressure while 
intensifying scrutiny of China, chips and other sensitive transactions. Leaders’ talks at the 
APEC summit on November 19 are the next test before the January 10 truce expiry. 
Compromises could reduce disruption, but not unwind Section 301 and Section 232 measures 
or tech controls. Risks tilt down: with housing and fiscal execution still weak, a self-
sustaining recovery requires implemented external relief and faster domestic spending.
