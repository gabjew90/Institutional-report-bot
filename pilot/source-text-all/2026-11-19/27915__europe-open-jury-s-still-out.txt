Europe Open - Jury's still out
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Nvidia shares traded slightly stronger after earnings reiterated a strong demand outlook and sales, but doubts remain on
leadership as the board confirms Jensen Huang will not return as CEO.  Mixed performance in stocks with Kospi and
Nikkei modestly higher, but the rest of the Asian complex in the red. US equity futures also drifted higher, as did USTs.
Fedspeak continues to downplay the need for a 50bp rate hike (Williams), FOMC minutes tonight and more comments
from Barr, Williams again and Kashkari will be monitored. No clear driver behind gold (-2%).
UK CPI just out comes in line with cons. estimates but services and headline lower than MPC expectations. Dec pricing
moves slightly lower but still heavily skewed towards a hike at ~17bp. Also on the docket today are ECB speakers Vujcic,
who presents the financial stability review, Kocher, and President Lagarde (14:25 GMT). RBNZ Breman speaks at 18:00
followed by Conway. South Africa CPI, Indonesia rate decision (hold) and Hungary wages are also due.

CITI DERIVS: CTA Update
In Focus‌
Equities: Mixed performance overnight with Kospi eking out slight gains (up 2%), Nikkei up 0.3% but the rest of the Asian
complex in the red. Nvidia first take from Citi Research highlights that demand remains very strong, supply constraints are
easing, and interim management is confident enough to raise long-term growth expectations. Notably, Nvidia's more selective
approach to financing AI infrastructure projects could moderate growth for some neocloud operators if funding becomes
constrained. However, skepticism remains on future leadership, as the company reiterated that it does not plan to bring back
Jensen Huang. Slight boost in US equity futures overnight, Nasdaq up 0.5%. 
Rates: AU bonds outperformed peers despite wage growth beating estimates. Q3 wage price index came at 1.1%, the
strongest quarterly gain in 3y and above 1% consensus. This was largely driven by public sector wages, but private wages
were also steady. Though our economists expect seasonal moderation in Q4, wages growth is now set to overshoot RBA
forecasts. Should tomorrow's employment data beat estimates, further tightening might be required. USTs drifted tad stronger
overnight. Fed Williams downplayed the need for a 50bp rate hike, saying that there was no need for a "dramatic" rate move.
This is similar to his colleague Hammack who preferred slow and steady interest rate hikes. While Musalem saw the need for
"several" rate hikes before Spring, he is a non voter.

FX: G10 trades in extremely tight ranges against the USD with our eTrading team confirming that interbank volumes were
~14% lower than 30d averages. While spot continues to trade heavy today despite the more positive risk sentiment, AUD
skews saw selling interests in the option markets, 3m 25d RR latest given at 1.3v Aud put. Franchise remains quiet thus far
after overnight stronger demands for risk aversion hedges in the cross JPY lower structures, as macro awaits the next trigger
of risk sentiment. Tomorrow's AUD labor print is priced at 20bp.
GBP: Oct CPI releasing at print comes in line with our expectations but services and headline weaker than MPC
expectations, coming at 3.7% YoY and 3.6% respectively. This is driving a slight knee jerk move lower in spot, but OIS pricing
still remains tilted towards a hike at 17bp, albeit lower post labor data (highs of ~20bp). We await our economists' detailed
take for more. BoE Bailey delivers a speech post data at 15:00 GMT.
Commodities: Surprising move in gold having slid nearly -2% overnight and breaking the $3800 handle. We don't see any
clear driver apart from falling oil prices and lower yields. YTD, gold is on track for its worst annual performance since 2013
(-12%). The move is being characterised as a potential capitulation.
Elsewhere‌
EUR: ECB's Vujcic presents Financial Stability Review (09:00 GMT), Kocher speaks in Vienna (14:00 GMT) and finally
President Lagarde speaks in New York (14:25 GMT). ICYMI, reports suggest de Cos is set to be the sole contender for ECB
President, with Germany is set to propose Markus Brunnermeier, an established economist currently at Princeton, as chief
economist at the ECB. My colleague Rui Ding takes a look at previous speeches and research from the candidates here.
USD: FOMC meeting minutes at 19:00 GMT precedes more Fedspeak from Barr (18:35 GMT), Williams (19:30), Kashkari at
an AI conference (21:15). Housing Starts for October and Building Permits due for October P (both 13:30 GMT). Later in the
day, monitor 20y auction (16bn, 18:00 GMT)
NZD: RBNZ Governor Breman speaks on the economy (18:00 GMT) followed by Chief Economist Conway (20:00 GMT).
Recall recent employment data suggests that the RBNZ hiking cycle is likely over. We expect the OCR to hold at 2.75%
throughout 2027.
IDR: BI Rate Decision (07:20 GMT). Our economists expect Bank Indonesia to keep the policy rate unchanged at 5.75%
(prior 5.75%). Citi's forecast path also holds the 7D reverse repo rate at 5.75% through December 2027.
HUF: Gross Wages YoY for September (07:30 GMT). Our economists forecast wage growth moved lower to 8.0% YoY from
8.4% in August.
ZAR: CPI MoM, CPI YoY and CPI Core YoY for October (all 08:00 GMT). A critical print coming just one day before the MPC
decision. CPI is likely to increase further from September driven by fuel prices, with our economists flagging the prolonged
supply-side shock as likely to show second-round effects are deepening. BBG consensus expectations have Oct CPI at
+0.7% MoM and 5.2% YoY.
CLP: GDP YoY and Current Account Balance for 3Q (both 11:30 GMT).
COP: Trade Balance for October (15:00 GMT).
MYR: Q3 GDP comes in line with expectations at 1.5% QoQ.
