from services outside traditional design and construction activity, underscoring how firms are
adapting to a prolonged period of market weakness."

WCN – GIR out with some takeaways here:  (November 16), Chiquita Canyon disclosed
that, during routine drilling to install a new well aimed at mitigating the ongoing elevated
temperature landfill event, personnel encountered a pressurized pocket within the landfill,
triggering an above-ground release of landfill gases and particulate matter. No liquids were
released. The facility promptly notified the Los Angeles County Fire Department and initiated
its emergency response plan. Site response resources expanded from ~80 workers on duty
at the time of the event to ~180 personnel dedicated to containment, cleanup, and site
stabilization. We note the following key details from subsequent updates from Chiquita
Canyon: Air monitoring has shown no exceedances of Reference Exposure Levels (RELs) for
any monitored constituents, including benzene. Management halted all well-drilling
operations across the landfill footprint effective 4 pm Monday 16 November. The drilling was
part of work being performed as required by regulatory agencies, and activities will not restart
until cleanup is complete and a full review has been conducted in coordination with
regulators. WCN stated it does not expect the situation to impact its overall ongoing efforts to
manage the elevated temperature landfill event. Site electrical circuits that were temporarily
de-energized as a precaution are undergoing staged restarts, with WCN expecting site
systems to return to normal operations as soon as possible.
