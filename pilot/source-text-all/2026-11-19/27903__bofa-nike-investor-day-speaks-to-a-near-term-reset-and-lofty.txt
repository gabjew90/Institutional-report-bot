Recovery requires patience and execution 
At Nike’s Investor Day, management outlined another year of sales and earnings declines 
in F28, an inflection in F29, and above-market growth in F30-F31. The F31 targets of 
12-14% OM and $3-3.50 EPS appear optimistic and require improved product newness 
and marketplace while delivering substantial cost savings. We retain our Underperform 
rating, as we think the multiple is too high for an uncertain recovery through another 18 
months of deteriorating fundamentals. We are lowering our F28/F29 EPS estimates by 
21%/24% to reflect increased sales and margin pressure. Our $24 PO is based on 18x 
F29E P/E (was F28E as we roll our valuation year forward past trough earnings). 
China recovery will take time 
Management expects F31 Greater China revenue to remain flat vs. F27 despite ~5% 
annual market growth. Nike is focusing on 20 cities representing ~50% of sales, 
expanding local product creation, and rebuilding its digital and physical marketplaces. 
This should improve local relevance, but the market remains extremely competitive and 
we think continued investment is needed to maintain sales at current levels.  
Applying the running playbook 
Nike’s growth plan rests primarily on amplifying five performance businesses: Running, 
Training, Global Football, Basketball, and Outdoor will drive most of the $4bn of 
incremental performance revenue through F31. Extending momentum in running across 
four additional sports is critical to achieving the outlook. Nike will focus on the Mind, 
Free, and Air platforms to offer newness. 
Sportswear needs to innovate to regain relevance 
Sportswear, Jordan and Converse comprise the remaining ~$1bn growth to Nike’s target. 
This is trickier, as Nike is currently right-sizing the businesses after rapid growth. Nike 
plans to cut its product count in half and increase new footwear models from <10% of 
revenue today to ~40% by F31. The strategy offers a more credible path than another 
cycle built on established icons, but these new platforms must still prove commercially 
successful and the elevated amount of newness increases complexity.

Price objective basis & risk 
Nike (NKE) 
Our PO of $24 is based on 18x F29E EPS, in line with the sportswear peer average. This 
reflects Nike's turnaround potential offset by limited visibility into a durable sales and 
earnings recovery. 
 
Upside risks to our PO are faster-than-expected innovation that drives full price sales, 
stronger wholesale sell-through, a recovery in China, and greater-than-expected margin 
improvement or cost savings. 
 
Downside risks to our PO are weaker-than-expected sales in China and wholesale, 
innovation being too slow or not resonating, continued pressure in larger lifestyle 
categories, a promotional retail environment, and margin or cost progress falling short 
of expectations.
