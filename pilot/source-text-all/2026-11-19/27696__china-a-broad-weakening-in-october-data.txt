October macro data weakened across the board 
China’s October activity data weakened further across the board and surprised on 
the downside by a wide margin. This points to a further weakening of domestic 
demand and larger divergence between the supply-demand indicators, amidst 
further property market strain and still quite incremental policy supports.  
Retail sales growth eased more than expected to 0.4% YoY in October from 1.1% 
in September, while the decline in YTD FAI deepened to -8.1% from 
previously -7.2%, suggesting an accelerated pace of FAI decline (-16.1% YoY in 
October vs -7.2% in September, according to our calculation). IP growth also eased 
more than expected to 4.8% YoY in October from 5.5% in September, despite 
being still quite solid and continued outpacing demand indicators. Property 
investment further declined by 21.6% YTD in October, worsening from 20.6% YTD 
in September. The earlier released data such as NBS PMIs, CPI inflation and credit 
mostly weakened and disappointed, though trade growth remained rapid with a 
large surplus. One silver lining is that surveyed unemployment rate ticked lower to 
5.1% from 5.2%.  
…despite recent easing fiscal and financial conditions  
In October, fiscal and quasi-fiscal supports have quickened, with the RMB1.3trn in 
special CGBs and RMB800bn in policy banks’ new policy financing tools fully 
issued by October, and LG issuance quota in 2026 being lifted by RMB550bn. In 
early November, the PBoC and financial regulators also rolled out a slew of policy 
measures, to ease the financial conditions, including the policy rate switch to 
overnight from 7D (effectively lowering the anchor of short-term money market 
rates by 15bp), promotion of capital market stabilisation tools as well as a 2% 
increase to the FX deposit RRR to slow the CNY gain. These show a positive policy 
intention to arrest the further growth downside and stabilise markets; however, they 
remain very incremental and targeted. While we think the potential fiscal easing 
could in theory show more positive impact in stabilising investment in Q4, the 
progress has been slow with limited evidence in data so far. The top policymakers 
pledged to improve policy execution in the 5th Plenum of the 20th CPC in late 
October, which should help speed up the funding allocation to project construction. 
On the other hand, hurdles could also rise from Beijing’s requirement to avoid 
overinvestment and excessive competition as well as upcoming government 
reshuffling heading into the 21st Party Congress in October 2027.

AI and trade to turn less growth supportive into 2027 
So far in 2026, the global AI capex boom and China’s export product 
competitiveness amidst the energy shock have helped kept China’s manufacturing 
production & export activities strong. However, the positive spillover to sectors 
outside of technology and the green economy, as well as to the labour market and 
household income & wealth growth, has been muted. Meanwhile, property market 
strain has continued nationwide, despite some selected home price stabilisation in 
tier 1 cities. The regulatory tightening of presales rules would further restrain 
property developers’ funding conditions, despite some supportive measures to 
encourage household mortgage borrowings. 
Looking ahead in 2027, we could see more headwinds externally, making net 
exports less growth-supportive. The global AI capex cycle is set to slow, resulting 
in slower demand growth for AI-related infrastructure and hardware exports from 
Asia and China. Meanwhile, there could be renewed trade tensions and further 
tightening of export controls from the US after the Democrats’ sweep of the US 
midterm elections. US President Donald Trump could become more constrained in 
his domestic policies after the US midterms, but he continues enjoying executive 
power in his trade policies. In this regard, the Section 301 investigation on 
manufacturing overcapacity would deserve some attention as the investigation 
could conclude any time. The Nvidia shakeup has revived political momentum in 
the US for stricter export controls on advanced chips to China, raising the risks of 
barriers for China’s further integration in the AI and technology supply chain.  
Watch trade and policy & reform signals 
In 2027, we think it is still more likely for the Chinese government to maintain the 
same growth target of 4.5-5.0%, instead of lowering it again to around 4.5%. That 
means greater policy efforts will be necessary to boost domestic demand more 
materially, given less supportive trade. However, markets including us are not too 
optimistic on any meaningful scale up of fiscal expansion in 2027 or a shift of focus 
on addressing the demand side weakness. We will watch for updated macro policy 
signals from the December Politburo meeting as well as the Central Economic 
Work conference in December. Macro policy and reform measures to improve the 
prospect of households’ income growth and balance sheet as well as strengthen 
local governments’ fiscal expenditure capability would be needed.   
On US-China front, Presidents Xi Jinping and Donald Trump will meet on the 
sidelines of the APEC (18-19 November in Shenzhen) and potentially the G20 
meeting (14-15 December in Miami), where they are expected to discuss a further 
extension of the Busan trade truce. The truce includes suspensions for both US 
export controls (affiliate rule) and Chinese export controls on rare earths. If 
Congress or the Trump administration move to impose more restrictive measures 
on advanced chips, this could undermine the current managed strategic stability in 
US-China relations, with rising probability of China retaliation of its own export 
controls.

IP growth slowed on autos, but still-solid on tech 
IP growth (real) declined to 4.8% YoY in October from 5.5% YoY in September, 
below market consensus of 5.3%. By sector, IP growth in mining, manufacturing, 
and utilities decelerated to 0.7%, 5.4% and 4.4%, respectively, in October, from 
1.3%, 6.0% and 5.6% in September. 
By industry, the downside surprise was mainly led by a sharp growth slowdown in 
auto manufacturing, to 0.9% YoY from 6.7%, likely reflecting a combination of base 
effects and negative impact from the upcoming EU import quota restrictions on 
Chinese EVs. Elsewhere, IP growth in tech-related industries remained robust. IP 
growth in high-tech manufacturing accelerated to 17.6% YoY from 15.3%, 
providing a key buffer to the headline figure. Within the segment, computer, 
communication & electronics equipment manufacturing rose to 19.4% from 16.4%, 
while that of electric equipment held steady at 8.1%. At the product level, IC 
production growth picked up to 21.9% from 18.4%. 
On the services front, growth in services production slowed to 4.6% YoY in October 
from 4.8% in September.

Property sector further worsened 
The contraction in new home sales deepened in 10M26 across both value and 
volume terms, falling by 14.1% and 14.2% YoY YTD, respectively, compared to 
declines of 13.7% and 13.8% in 9M26. Meanwhile, developers’ funding sources 
fell by 21.9% YoY YTD, widening from a 21.6% decline in the prior period. Across 
construction activity, YTD contractions in property completions widened to 27.9% 
from 26.6%, whereas declines in new starts narrowed slightly to 24.9% from 
25.4%. On a monthly basis, the decline of October new home sales widened 
slightly, though the contraction in sales volume moderated. Monthly property FAI 
also slid further, contracting by 34.2% YoY (vs 27.2% in September), while the 
decline in developers’ funding sources widened to 28.0% from 25.8% in 
September. 
On the front of property prices, new home price deflation accelerated slightly in 
October, with 70 major cities fell by 0.2% MoM, widening from a 0.1% decline in 
the prior month. Average price inflation in Tier-1 cities held steady at 0.1% MoM, 
though momentum moderated in Guangzhou and Shenzhen, where gains 
decelerated to 0.0% and 0.1% from 0.2% and 0.3%, respectively. In YoY terms, 
however, the decline in new home prices narrowed to -2.7% from -3.0% in 
September. Meanwhile, existing home prices declined by 0.2% MoM in October, 
easing from the 0.3% monthly drop recorded over the preceding five months, while 
the YoY decline narrowed to -4.3% from -4.7%.

Still-strong trade growth with YTD trade surplus exceeding USD1trn 
October trade growth continued at a rapid pace, with the trade balance narrower 
but still sizable. Export growth edged down to 35.2% YoY in October from 36.3% 
in September, slightly below market expectation. Import growth accelerated 
sharply to 41.6% from 31.0% in September, surprising on the upside. Trade 
balance narrowed to USD105.3bn from USD133.5bn. In 10M26, export and import 
growth quickened to 22.6% and 29.0% YoY respectively, from 5.5% and -0.2% in 
2025. The trade surplus recorded USD1,051bn in 10M26, with the full year trade 
surplus on track to match the near USD1.2trn recorded in 2025.  
Export resilience remained highly sector-specific. Tech exports continued to be the 
primary driver, supported by resilient global AI hardware demand and tech price 
inflation. High-tech export growth accelerated to 66.6% from 54.0%. Specifically, 
export growth for ICs and ADP machines rose to 137.1% and 114.8% from 107.9% 
and 92.5%, respectively, with their contribution to overall export growth increasing 
to 7.5ppt and 6.1ppt from 6.3ppt and 5.1ppt. The pick-up in export value growth 
continued to be largely driven by price inflation. IC export price inflation, for 
instance, remained exceptionally elevated at 143.0% in October vs 133.7% in 
September, while IC export volume declined 2.4%, reversing the 1.0% growth 
recorded in September. Refined petroleum product exports were also notable amid 
heightened geopolitical uncertainty, with export growth remaining elevated at 
80.3% (contributing 0.8ppt to headline) though moderating from 125.1%, 
comprising export price inflation of 57.3% (vs 61.5%) and export volume growth of 
14.6% (vs 39.3%). Auto export growth remained strong at 37.6% in October vs 
62.0% in September, contributing 1.8ppt. By trading partner, China’s export growth 
to the US, EU and ASEAN remained robust at 32.9%, 24.3% and 46.3%, compared 
to 39.6%, 25.3% and 52.3% respectively. Export growth to Korea and Taiwan 
stayed exceptionally high at 92.4% and 61.8%, compared to 68.2% and 68.9% 
respectively. 
The inflation-driven headline effect was more pronounced on the import side. For 
tech imports, IC and ADP imports grew 78.3% and 261.0% respectively, compared 
to 74.7% and 296.5% previously, contributing 13.6ppt and 8.0ppt to the headline. 
However, IC import volume growth remained modest at 0.1% (vs 1.7%), while IC 
import price inflation rose to 78.2% (vs 71.8%). Another driver for the October 
import headline was energy import inflation amid ongoing geopolitical 
uncertainties. Crude oil import growth jumped to 41.0% from 3.3%, driven 
predominantly by import price inflation, which rose sharply to 62.6% from 31.5%, 
while the import volume growth remained sluggish, contracting 13.3% vs a decline 
of 21.5% previously. Import growth from the US increased to 35.0% from 20.7%, 
while that from the EU contracted 4.2%, reversing growth of 0.7% previously. 
Import growth from Korea, Taiwan, Japan and ASEAN all accelerated, rising to 
110.2%, 41.1%, 26.5% and 42.0% from 94.3%, 34.2%, 15.4% and 36.2%, 
respectively.

Widened PPI-CPI inflation gap in October 
China’s headline CPI inflation eased a bit more than expected to 0.7% YoY in 
October from 0.9% in September, as core inflation eased and food deflation 
deepened despite higher fuel prices. Core inflation edged down to 0.8% YoY from 
1.0% previously, mainly on much lowered inflation boost from gold inflation, as gold 
jewellery inflation slowed sharply by 18.0ppt to 4.5% YoY, with its contribution to 
headline reduced to 0.02ppt. Base effects may have also dragged, as the National 
Day holiday in 2025 was eight days long, one day longer than usual. Tourism 
services inflation eased to 3.0% from 3.5% previously. Meanwhile, food deflation 
widened to -2.9% YoY from -1.7%, dragging headline by 0.49ppt. On the other 
hand, energy inflation climbed to 9.9% from 7.3%, with gasoline inflation 
accelerating by 5.8ppt to 22.4% YoY (adding roughly 0.63ppt). Inflation of 
technology-related goods – including data storage devices (+45.4%), tablets 
(+29.3%), computers (+23.0%) and mobile phones (+16.7%), remained rapid, 
collectively contributed 0.26ppt. 
Meanwhile, China’s PPI inflation accelerated to 5.7% YoY in October from 5.0% in 
September, with the MoM reading remaining rapid at 0.7% (vs 1.2% in September). 
This is driven primarily by sharp price increases in upstream mining and raw 
materials, across the coal, oil, fuel processing and chemical sectors. By product 
breakdown, PPI inflation for producer goods rose notably to 7.4% YoY from 6.5%, 
while consumer goods deflation narrowed slightly to 1.6% YoY from 1.8% in the 
prior month. Within producer goods, inflation accelerated broadly in October where 
mining, raw materials and manufacturing PPI inflation rose to 27.9%, 11.6% and 
3.5% YoY, respectively from 25.4%, 9.4% and 3.4% in September. By industry, 
PPI inflation acceleration was led by upstream sectors, with coal mining, oil & gas 
extraction, fuel processing and chemical manufacturing recording sharp YoY 
increases, while deflationary pressures persisted across downstream segments, 
including beverages, pharmaceuticals, non-metallic mineral products, automobiles 
and electricity & heat supply.  
Overall, the set of inflation data suggest that inflation remains driven by imported 
energy and tech-related raw material prices; meanwhile, domestic demand 
remained sluggish weighed by continued property sector strain. Household 
consumption demand growth stayed weak on cautious income outlook and 
negative wealth effect. The muted core CPI inflation and widening PPI-CPI inflation 
gap suggest further stress on corporate earnings on mid- to downstream 
corporates on the back of limited price pass through from upstream inflation of raw 
materials to finished goods.

Credit growth hovered at a relatively low level in October  
New aggregate financing (AF) came in at RMB1,151bn in October, missing market 
consensus of RMB1,448bn. RMB loans declined by RMB100bn, against 
RMB220bn increase a year ago. Outstanding AF growth slightly picked up to 7.2% 
YoY from 7.1% in September, whereas outstanding RMB loan growth eased further 
to 4.7% from 4.8%. Meanwhile, M2 growth moderated to 7.1% YoY from 7.2%. 
The net decline in RMB loans in October was attributed to both household and 
corporate loans reducing their RMB borrowings. Household loans dropped by 
RMB430bn in October, with RMB270bn of the decline in short-term loans mainly 
for consumption credit and credit cards, while deleveraging in mortgage assets 
continued as medium- to long-term (MLT) household loans fell by RMB156bn, 
signalling still-limited support from the mortgage subsidy policy introduced in late 
September. New corporate loans posted a net drop of RMB160bn, vs the 
RMB350bn expansion in a year ago. Corporate MLT loans rose by RMB160bn, 
improving from an increase of RMB30bn in the prior-year period, but still at a 
relatively low level. 
New AF increased to RMB1,143 bn in October, higher than RMB818bn in October 
2025. While new loans dropped sharply (to -RMB585bn vs -RMB15bn), net 
government bond issuance rose notably to RMB1,184bn (vs RMB485bn), as fiscal 
easing efforts have finally started catching up after lagging in previous months.
