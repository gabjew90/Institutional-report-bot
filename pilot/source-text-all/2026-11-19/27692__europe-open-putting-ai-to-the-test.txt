Europe Open - Putting AI to the test
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Tech rout deepened overnight in anticipation of Nvidia earnings after the US cash close. While Kospi was worst
performer, US equity futures downside momentum is more modest, Nasdaq futures flat at print. USTs benefit from risk off
sentiment. Some members in RBA minutes continued to flag that inflation risks were skewed to the upside, Feb holds the
highest weight (7bp) for a potential hike. FX largely rangebound, oil prices drifted lower. USS Theodore has reached the
Gulf, making it the third US aircraft carrier in the region. 
Ahead, eyes on more Fedspeak in the form of Musalem, Waller, Hammack and Williams. Retail sales due at 13:30 GMT,
Germany ZEW prints before that. At print, UK labor market data shows resilience with u/e rate dropping lower and wages
strong. In EM, Hungary rate decision due (hold). It's a local holiday in Czech Republic.

CitiFX Flow & Positioning: Pickup in USD Buying
In Focus‌
Rates: Slight strength in USTs overnight with mixed performance elsewhere. Duration once again oscillated within a tight
range, with weakness across equities and pessimism on AI pushing USTs higher. Flows remained light, with better selling in
the short end and the long end. Volume rebounded to around 0.8x recent averages, with yesterday’s dip being an exception
due to technical issues. 
RBA minutes overnight showed that the majority of the board voted against raising interest rates due to concerns about the
economy being on a verge of a sharp downturn. Members who voted to raise rates argued that inflation risks were still
skewed to the upside, judging that persistent inflation and rising forward-looking price pressures made the risk of policy being
too loose greater than the risk of overtightening. OIS pricing still shows limited chances of near-term hikes but Feb still holds
the strongest weight (7bp priced). 
Equities: Screens are red across the board with Kospi lagging peers, down -2.5% but this time China stocks also weaker.
Our Asia desk flags internets/AI tanked on concern the new curb damages future AI training ability, with Minimax (-2.72%),
Z.AI (-3.5%) and Alibaba (-4.94%) all trading weaker. All eyes on Nvidia earnings after the US cash close (~21:30 GMT).

Before that, watch out for Home Depot, Disney. WSJ reports that Nvidia's board is not considering reinstating Jensen Huang
as CEO, despite mounting calls from some investors and employees for his return. New: Equity Markets Positioning Model -
Positioning Improves, but Conviction Remains Uneven
FX: Our eTrading team confirms that it was an overall quiet session today with interbank volumes at 24% lower as compared
to the last 30d averages. We saw leveraged JPY, EUR, AUD and CAD buying vs USD, NZD, CHF and SGD selling with Real
Money USD and CHF buying vs JPY, EUR, GBP and AUD selling. While equity weakness persists, implied vols remain
broadly sidelined and short term FX realized vols have not demonstrated outperformance to the higher implied vol base in the
risk aversion environment. Our options desk continue to favor owning Aud downside vols, 3m 25d RR at 1.35v Aud put. 
New positioning report highlights that hedge funds returned to USD buying last week with strong net inflows versus a wide
range of G10 and EM currencies. Our Positioning Indicator (CFPI) for the dollar remains relatively muted at +2.5 for leveraged
and +2.0 for investors overall, out of a maximum of +10. This suggests that there is plenty of scope for more buying by
investors but given the gains for USD and long positioning, our quant framework is bearish. Dig deeper.
Commodities: Slight weakness in oil prices overnight, Brent trades at $91.87. USS Theodore, US' third aircraft carrier has
arrived in the Persian Gulf, Centcom announced, the third such vessel in the Middle East. This gives the US administration
more military options should they choose to re-escalate strikes with Iran. US President Trump is taking part of the ASEAN
summit today, greeting Japan, Australia and Indian leaders at 10:20 GMT. He then heads to the APEC summit where his
trilateral with Putin and Xi is the highlight on Thursday. Iran is an expected topic but not Ukraine according to reports. Check
out: DeskTalk - Trump's Asia schedule
Elsewhere‌
GBP: Oct labor report out at print. Strong data, especially an unexpected pickup in average wages, will reinforce the case for
a Dec hike, but Wed inflation will be more important. Our economists expect wages to moderate at 3.6% 3m/YoY, with ex
wages at 3.6%, private earnings at 3%. U/e rate should remain unchanged. The UK also publishes new productivity data for
Q3.
EUR: Germany ZEW Survey for November (10:00 GMT). Bloomberg consensus expects expectations broadly unchanged at
28.3, current situation to remain deeply negative at -35.3. ECB's Lane speaks at Harvard (18:30 GMT).
USD: Fed's Musalem speaks at Banco Central de la República (12:30 GMT), Waller on Payments (14:10 GMT), Hammack at
Brookings Institution (14:30) and Williams speaking in a fireside chat (19:25). Retail Sales for October (13:30 GMT) should
decelerate to 0.2% MoM vs 0.8% with core (ex-auto, gas) at 0.3% and control group at 0.4% according to consensus. ADP
weekly employment change (13:15 GMT) and Oct IP (14:15) should be overlooked.
HKD: Unemployment Rate 3Mths for October (08:30 GMT). Our economists forecast the 3MMA rate at 3.9%, unchanged
from the prior 3.90%.
HUF: Central Bank Rate Decision (13:00 GMT). Our economists expect the Monetary Council to keep the base rate
unchanged at 5.5% (prior 5.50%), with rate cuts off the table for now as long as energy prices stay elevated.
COP: GDP QoQ for Q3 (15:00 GMT). Bloomberg consensus expects a 0.9% YoY print vs 3.5% prior.
