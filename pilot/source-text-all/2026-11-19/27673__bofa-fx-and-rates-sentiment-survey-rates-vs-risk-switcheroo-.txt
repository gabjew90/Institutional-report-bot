FX and Rates Sentiment Survey 
Rates vs risk switcheroo 
     
   
 
 
Key takeaways 
• Respondents seem concerned about consensus longs in risk, but we think they should 
worry about consensus longs in rates 
• Periphery duration extensions are at odds with asymmetric skew on OAT-Bund spread 
expectations 
• EM: After the sharp correction in October, EM investor positioning has started to 
recover across both FX and rates 
It’s what you know for sure that just ain’t so 
Respondents appear convinced that “long risk” is the most consensus trade for 2027 
(see Exhibit 1). However, the highest conviction view for almost 40% of respondents is 
to be long rates (Exhibit 7), which is also mirrored in decent duration extensions in USD 
(Exhibit 35), EUR (Exhibit 38), and GBP (Exhibit 41), bringing the global duration position 
close to an all-time high (Exhibit 28).  
In our view, investors may be at risk of underestimating quite how consensus their own 
concern about risk exposures are and therefore how crowded long duration hedges have 
become. Last week’s price action speaks to crowded rate longs.

Gallic shrugs on OAT-Bund spreads 
Respondents extended duration positions in the periphery at the margin (Exhibit 2). At 
the same time, most expect the OAT-Bund spread to peak at 175-200 bp with risks

especially if spreads tighten into year-end on a successful budget adoption.

EM: trying to recover 
After the sharp correction in October, EM investor positioning has started to recover 
across both FX and rates. The improvement has been sharpest in rates, despite a 
continued deterioration in sentiment (Exhibit 4). Investors remain cautious, with cash 
positions continuing to increase. However, they have largely closed their earlier EM 
underweights, with positioning improving across asset classes. Positioning is now 
neutral in EM HC debt, while investors have shifted from UW to OW in EM vs HY debt, 
with an even stronger shift in EM local currency debt (Exhibit 5).

A guide to the BofA Global Investor Survey 
Our survey was conducted between 6 November 2026 and 12 November 2026. A total of 
41 global fixed income fund managers responded to the survey. Responses came from the 
UK, Continental Europe, APAC and the US. 
Our monthly survey has asked the same two questions since its May 1989 start date: 
“Relative to your own benchmark, 1) how is your portfolio structured in terms of 
currency exposure, and 2) how is your portfolio structured in terms of duration 
exposure?”. In December 2011, we added two questions:  1) “How would you describe 
your view on the following currencies, and 2) how would you describe your view on the 
following bond markets?”. Regarding “exposure”, there are five responses from which to 
choose: Heavily Overweight, Moderately Overweight, Neutral, Moderately Underweight, 
and Heavily Underweight, while for “view”, the equivalent responses are Very Bullish, 
Bullish, Neutral, Bearish, and Very Bearish. In both cases, responses are weighted to 
create a Bull-Bear index from -100 to 100, with zero representing neutral. 
1 The formula used to calculate the Bull-Bear Index (B/B) is as follows:  
B/B (exposure) = (% Heavy Overweight * 1.0) + (% Moderately Overweight * 0.5) + (% Neutral * 0) - (% Moderately 
Underweight * 0.5) - (% Heavily Underweight * 1.0).  
B/B (view) = (% Very Bullish * 1.0) + (% Bullish * 0.5) + (% Neutral * 0) - (% Bearish * 0.5) - (%Very Bearish * 1.0)
