TODAY’S INSIGHTS
Financial Markets
•		It was a sea of red for global share markets. In the US, the 
tech-driven sell-off continued at pace, unaided by reports 
of further technical disruptions at the NSA and Apple. The 
S&P 500 (–1.5%), Dow Jones (–1.9%) and NASDAQ (–1.3%) 
all moved lower. All eyes will be on Nvidia’s earnings report 
and any related announcements about its CEO succession 
plan, due shortly after we go to press. 
•	Stocks were hardly better across the pond, with the Euro 
Stoxx 50 (–2.1%) and London’s FTSE 100 (–1.8%) both 
posting back-to-back declines. Across Asia, the tech-led 
sell-off weighed more heavily on Tokyo (–1.3%) and Seoul 
(–2.6%) though Chinese stocks also moved lower, both the 
Hang Send (–1.0%) and CSI 300 (–0.1%). The ASX 200 
meanwhile dropped 1.8% with broad-based declines; and 
following the action overnight, futures markets are pointing 
to another weak open.
•		Treasuries benefitted from souring risk sentiment, driving a 
bull steepening in yields. The 2Y fell 5bps to 4.78%, the 10Y 
dropped 3bps to 5.10%, while the 30Y inched 1bp lower to 
5.58%. This comes as Fedspeakers continued to support the 
case for rapid imminent tightening, which also appears to be 
the growing consensus among markets – an FOMC rate hike 
is still fully priced in for December, and an immediate follow-
up in January is still seen as more likely than not. Yields 
were little changed elsewhere, with the 10Y Bund and JGB 
both inching 1bp lower, while the 10Y Gilt held steady. 
•		ACGBs didn’t budge during local trading, although both 3Y 
and 10Y yields inched 2bps lower during futures trading 
overnight. The RBA minutes revealed that the Board 
members who dissented to November’s rate hike “expressed 
their concern that the economy might be near the point of 
entering a materially sharper downturn and pointed to the 
costs in terms of unemployment” – while not new information, 
it is consistent with current market pricing, which has no hikes 
or cuts fully priced in through to late-2027.
•		The USD traded in mixed fashion overnight as risk assets 
struggled, the DXY initially pushing to a fresh cycle high 
just under 103.8, before easing back to around 103.5, down 
marginally from yesterday’s close. Moves were contained 
across individual crosses, with the Euro and Yen both up 
0.1% versus the greenback, while the Sterling gained 0.2%. 
The Aussie dollar dipped 0.1% to around USD0.6730, a 
tentative stabilising following the consistent downtrend 
since early November.
•		Oil prices rose edged slightly higher overnight amid a flare-
up in tensions in the Middle East conflict, including attacks 
on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, another targeted attack 
on US military equipment in Bahrain, and the US striking 
multiple locations on Iran’s Qeshm Island in ‘self-defense’. 
Brent is up 0.3% to US$92.7/bbl. Metals continued to 
trade lower amid weaker risk sentiment, this time with LME 
copper slipped only slightly (–0.2% to US$13,226/mt) while 
LME aluminium fell more sharply (–2.3% to US2,934/mt). 
Gold prices were broadly steady.
International Data
In the US, retail sales beat market expectations in October, the 
headline measure by just 0.1ppt at 0.2%, but the control group 
by a more notable 0.3ppts at 0.6%. In part, these gains are 
the result of inflation, but underlying growth in consumption 
volumes remain robust. Meanwhile, industrial production 
fell 0.4% in October and business inventories rose 0.5% in 
September, building on August’s 0.4% gain. Growth in import 
prices also lifted to 1.1% in October, or 0.8% after excluding 
petroleum. The NAHB housing market index also fell from 
29 to 25 in November, the lowest level since 2011, marking 
incredibly weak homebuilder confidence.
The FOMC’s Musalem kept the focus on inflation overnight, 
noting concern over the persistence of price risks and calling for 
“several” rate increases before spring 2027 to engineer a circa 
1ppt increase in the real policy rate, which presumably also 
includes a deceleration in inflation over the period. Financial 
conditions were characterised as supportive of activity, and the 
labour market as balanced and near full employment.  
 In the Euro Area, the ZEW survey of investor expectations 
rose from 18.5 to 26.5 in November, an above average reading 
compared to the full history of the survey. The UK’s ILO 
unemployment rate also edged down from 4.7% to 4.6% 
in September as employment grew solidly, 155k over the 
3 months to September. Average weekly earnings growth 
decelerated at the margin, from 3.8%yr to 3.6%yr, around 
average versus history.
