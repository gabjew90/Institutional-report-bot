Yesterday, the S&P 500 declined for a fourth straight session, matching its longest losing streak since June and bringing its MTD loss to 
3.4%. Yet weakness has been concentrated in Energy (-9.7%) and Technology (-9.4%), with six of 11 sectors positive MTD. Underlying 
breadth remains encouraging, as 65% of index constituents trade above their 50-day moving averages, versus 23% at the start of 
October. Despite weakness in the headline index, improving breadth remains an encouraging sign for the broader market.
Wednesday, November 18, 2026
The Russell 2000’s 50-DMA is on pace to cross below its 200-DMA today, forming a ‘death cross,’ a bearish technical signal of weakening 
momentum. However, over the past 20 years, this signal has occurred 13 times, with the index gaining an average of 10.4% over the 
following 12 months and posting positive returns 77% of the time. While history suggests a potential buying opportunity for long-term 
investors, we favor large caps over small caps given their stronger fundamentals, including EPS revisions and sector allocations.

Import prices rose a faster-than-expected 1.1% MoM in October, partly driven by a 3.6% jump in fuel prices that could reverse in 
November following the recent decline in oil prices. More notable was the 0.8% MoM rise in nonfuel import prices. Excluding food and 
fuel, prices climbed 0.9%, the fastest pace in six months. Echoing last week’s CPI and PPI reports, the data suggest price pressures are 
broadening beyond energy, reinforcing our expectation for a 25 bp Fed rate hike on December 9.

After lagging for much of 2026 as investors favored technology leaders, Indian equities have emerged as standout performers amid the

erasing its YTD losses. International funds have taken notice: Bloomberg estimates they poured nearly $10b into Indian equities this 
month, surpassing the previous record set in Nov. 2000. With growth and earnings prospects improving, we remain positive on India.
