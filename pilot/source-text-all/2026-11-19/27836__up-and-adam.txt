Tuesday, November 17, 2026
The Grinch may have stolen Christmas presents, but this year, soaring travel costs are stealing some Whos’ holiday plans. As Jim 
Carrey’s How the Grinch Stole Christmas turns 26 today, AAA expects 81.5M Americans to travel for Thanksgiving, down 0.1% after three 
record years. Road and air travel are slipping amid record seasonal gasoline prices and a 17% YoY surge in average domestic airfares to 
~$800. Still, travel remaining near record highs suggests consumers continue to prioritize leisure spending.

The consumer takes center stage this week, starting with October’s retail sales report. Headline sales are expected to rise 0.4%, 
moderating from September’s 0.8% gain, with control group sales, a key GDP input, also up 0.4%. While these figures suggest continued 
strength in nominal terms, inflation is eroding some of the strength in real terms. With October’s CPI rising 0.4% MoM, expected sales 
growth would translate into flat inflation-adjusted spending, highlighting how rising prices continue to squeeze purchasing power.

Empire State Manufacturing Survey Points To Moderating Growth—Philly And Kansas City On Deck
Chart of the Day: Home for the Wholidays
After reaching a nearly five-year high of 25.5 in October, the Empire State Manufacturing Index eased to 7.8 in November but remained 
in expansion territory. New orders and shipments also moderated but stayed positive, while employment rose at its fastest pace since 
June 2022. Encouragingly, the prices paid gauge fell to its second-lowest level since the US-Iran conflict began. Thursday’s Philly and 
Kansas City surveys will offer further insight into manufacturing momentum.
For the first time since early September, AAA’s national average retail diesel price has fallen below $6.00/gallon, providing some relief 
to trucking firms and freight railroads in the Industrials sector, where we remain overweight, as well as Northeast homeowners who 
rely on heating oil. Still, diesel prices remain 59% above year-ago levels, far outpacing gasoline’s 24% increase. A return to sub-$4.00 
diesel would likely require an end to refinery disruptions in the Persian Gulf and Russia, which could take considerable time.
