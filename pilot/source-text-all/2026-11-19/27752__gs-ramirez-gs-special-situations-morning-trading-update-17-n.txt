Topical Situations: DASH/CART/T CN/ BWIN
 
According to several media outlets the DOJ is set to take the leading role in the review of DoorDash Inc.’s $17
billion Instacart purchase. The news may come as a modest surprise given the FTC’s history of reviewing
grocery deals including its 2024 challenge of the Kroger / Albertsons tie-up. Investors will likely view the
development as a small positive for the deal fueled further by comments made yesterday by senior DOJ officials
that the department is open to behavioral remedies rather than requiring asset sales. In other news, last night
Quebecor's CEO indicated it was planning to meet with Telus next Monday, and BWIN will host its shareholder
vote on January 6th, 2027. 
 
Merger Arb/Event
 
• DASH/CART – DOJ to Take Lead In Review Of DoorDash-InstaCart Merger (link). The Justice
Department will take the lead on reviewing DoorDash Inc.’s $17 billion Instacart purchase for potential
antitrust concerns, according to US officials. While the Federal Trade Commission normally reviews food and
retail deals, the Justice Department is taking jurisdiction over the DoorDash review, said the officials, who
asked not to be named discussing an internal matter.
 
• DOJ Open to Behavioral Remedies in Merger Reviews, Antitrust Official Says (link). The US Justice
Department is open to approving mergers based on promises about how companies will operate rather than
requiring them to sell assets, a senior official said Monday at a conference, pointing to the agency’s decision
this month to clear Fox Corp.’s $22 billion acquisition of Roku Inc. DOJ has been focused on “how
behavioral commitments or remedies serve” as “a mitigating factor” for competition concerns, especially with
deals in industries where asset sales may be more difficult to arrange, said Charlie Beller, deputy assistant
attorney general for antitrust. At the US Federal Trade Commission, which also reviews merger deals,
behavioral remedies remain “disfavored and rare,” but regulators sometimes have reasons to accept them
rather than pursue lawsuits, David Shaw, the agency’s No. 2 competition official, said at the same
conference Monday.
 
• T CN - Quebecor Makes $21.96 Billion Bid for Telus in Canadian Telecom Deal (link). Quebecor has
proposed a combination with Telus worth 31.31 billion Canadian dollars (US$21.96 billion), potentially tying
up two of the biggest Canadian telecommunications companies. The proposal represents a total
consideration of about C$19.45 a share, representing a roughly 60% premium to Telus’s closing share price

on the Toronto Stock Exchange on Friday, according to Quebecor. Quebecor Chief Executive Pierre-Karl
Peladeau said in an interview it was time to make Telus shareholders aware of his pursuit, adding he first
approached the telecom rival to discuss a deal roughly a year ago. Peladeau said he has a tentative meeting
scheduled with Telus next week to discuss a potential tieup. Under the proposal, Telus shareholders would
receive C$6.35 in cash, as well as 0.17 Class B shares of Quebecor for each Telus share. In its statement,
Quebecor said it intends to collaborate closely with the government and regulatory authorities, “and, in this
spirit, want to inform them of its approach to Telus.” The company added it is prepared to make
commitments to authorities regarding pricing and employment to secure approval of a potential merger with
Telus.
 
• BWIN – Definitive Proxy Filed (link). The Companies filed their definitive proxy overnight and BWIN will
host its shareholder vote on January 6th. The HSR waiting period expired on November 4th, 2026. Closing
guidance remains “in the first quarter of 2027.”
