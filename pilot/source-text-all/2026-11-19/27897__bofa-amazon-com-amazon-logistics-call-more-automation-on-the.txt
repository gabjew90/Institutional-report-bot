Amazon expected to accelerate capacity growth in 2027 
We hosted Marc Wulfraat of MWPVL for a presentation on Amazon Logistics.  Key 
initiatives and issues included Gen14 fulfillment centers (“FCs”), retail capex, ASCS, dark 
stores, drones, and fuel/labor costs.  MWPVL estimates that the network is getting closer 
to full capacity and expects an acceleration in capacity additions in 2027, with Amazon 
expected to add ~91mn gross sq. ft. in 2027, up 38% from ~66mn in 2026.  Automation 
is a key initiative, and new Gen 14 fulfilment centers and a pilot for fully automated 
delivery stations are expected in 2028.  Early ASCS feedback is positive, with rates 20-
25% below UPS/FDX, though MWPVL sees little competitive B2B impact.  MWPVL sees 
materially higher barriers to disrupting B2B, reverse logistics, and national LTL. 
Retail capex increasing, still small vs. AWS 
MWPVL estimates ~$28bn of spend on new supply chain facilities over 2026-2027 
(excludes maintenance/retrofits), which could be up $10bn on an annualized basis.  
Spend will be focused on small-sortable robotic fulfilment centers, inbound cross-dock, 
and delivery station facilities.  While relatively small versus our estimates for $240bn in 
2027 AWS capex, both Leo capitalization and higher retail spend could be modest capex 
growth drivers next year.   
More automated Gen14 centers coming in 2028  
MWPVL expects new Gen14 automated fulfillment centers in 2028, with 7 sites pending. 
Gen14 centers will focus on smaller sortable items and be more compact at 2 floors and 
1.0-1.4mn sqft vs. 4-6 floors and 2.3-4.0mn sqft for Gen11/Gen12 facilities.  Average 
headcount will be lower at roughly 1,000 vs. ~2,430 for Gen11 and ~1,310 for Gen12, 
which already uses robots to pick 65% of SKUs.  Gen14 is part of a broader effort to 
automate processes, with the goal of moving the same volume with a tenth of the labor. 
B2B & LTL barriers remain despite growing 3PL ambitions 
Mr. Wulfraat sees Amazon broadening ASCS toward an end-to-end 3PL offering, though 
near-term disruption remains concentrated in B2C parcel.  Amazon Logistics is already 
the largest U.S. parcel carrier and expects to move ~8.0bn packages in 2026, up from 
6.67bn in 2025 (and 6bn in 2024), while Amazon-related USPS/UPS volumes are 
expected to decline ~800 mil/~525 mil packages, respectively.  B2C shippers are testing 
Amazon primarily in major metros, with 2–5 day (delayed) service and rates ~20–25% 
below UPS/FDX, largely reflecting lower accessorials.  MWPVL expects gradual share loss 
for USPS/UPS/FDX, particularly as Amazon expands from 592 delivery stations toward 
~950 by 2030.  Nevertheless, higher value B2B and reverse logistics remain harder to 
penetrate given Amazon’s limited pickup capabilities and greater commercial-delivery 
complexity.  We (and Mr. Wulfraat) believe risks to large national less-than-truckload 
(“LTL”) carriers remains relatively insulated.  Mr. Wulfraat estimates Amazon has less 
than 30 LTL cross docks today vs. ~300 needed for a scaled national network.  We 
maintain our Buy rating as we continue to see Amazon retail as a key beneficiary of AI 
and robotics-driven automation, which will improve delivery efficiencies and speeds, 
driving further share gains vs Online retail.                           Continues on the next page…

Labor inflation bigger impact than fuel for delivery costs 
MWPVL estimates a $1/hr average wage increase can increase cost per package 
delivered by ~3.4%, while a $1 increase in y/y gas costs adds just ~1.3% (fuel prices 
grew from $3.25/gal to $4.34/gal in past year).  Despite higher labor and fuel costs over 
time, MWPVL estimates that shipping cost per unit is down 16% since 4Q’21, supported 
by Amazon’s inbound and regionalization efforts.  MWPVL expects less delivery cost 
improvement ahead vs recent years, though automation remains a key lever. 
Automating delivery stations with Project Tetromino 
Per MWPVL, Amazon could test “fully automated” last-mile delivery stations in 2028, the 
final stop before packages reach drivers.  MWPVL expects a pilot in 2028, five sites in 
2029, and 10 more in 2030.  Mr. Wulfraat expects rollout to start at the largest of 
Amazon’s 592 US delivery stations, and sees potential for sorting rates to increase 2.5x 
those of existing stations (read our note on Tetromino).  Amazon builds much of its own 
automation infrastructure (though many robotic arms come from outside vendors), and 
is adding robot manufacturing capacity in Austin, TX.  
Sizing Amazon Grocery 
While Amazon has indicated grocery and essentials are half of all units, on a food-only 
basis, MWPVL estimates Amazon and WFM 2025 food volumes at ~$59bn (18% of 
WMT’s ~$321bn).  Mr. Wulfraat views opening stores as a key lever toward driving a 
profitable grocery business and highlighted potential for integration of AutoStore 
Holdings (cube-storage technology) at future Amazon grocery superstores.  
Maintain Buy on Amazon 
We continue to see Amazon retail as a key beneficiary of AI and robotics-driven 
automation, which will improve delivery efficiencies and speeds, driving further share 
gains vs Online retail. 
 
 
 
 
Price objective basis & risk 
Amazon.com (AMZN) 
Our $317 PO is based on our SOTP analysis that values AWS at 8x 2027 sales, 1P Retail 
at 1.1x, 3P Retail at 2.5x, and Advertising at 5.0x. Our 2027 sales multiples compare to 
7.0x for SaaS comps, 1.3x for Retail comps, 2.4x for Marketplace comps, and 3.7x for 
Online Media comps. Our PO implies 3.6x blended 2027 P/Sales, 13x '27 EBITDA, and 
30x '27 EPS. 
 
Downside risks to our price objective are increasing competition from Cloud providers 
and big box retailers including Walmart, increasing AWS infrastructure costs that will 
pressure FCF, and Agentic AI usage that could pressure direct traffic and high-margin ad 
revenues. The stock has been subject to heavy volatility in the past, based on margin 
trends, and this volatility could increase due to economic uncertainty.
