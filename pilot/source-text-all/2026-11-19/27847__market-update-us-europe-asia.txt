U.S. Markets Recap 
US -1.92% | Nasdaq -2.49% | Dow -1.41%
Synopsis

US equities finished lower in Monday trading, ending near worst levels. Equal­weight (RSP) was down but topped cap­weighted index by 
90 bp, extending last week's relative outperformance. AI trade was broadly weaker with semis, memory, neoclouds all selling off sharply. 
Big tech was down with three of the Mag 7 off 3%+. Other underperformers included E&Cs, multis, A&D, machinery, moneycenter banks 
(tech disruptions), PE, IBs, retailers, auto suppliers, and chemicals. Relative outperformers included airlines, rails, managed care, hospitals, 
P&C insurers, exchanges, payments, restaurants, hotels, dollar stores, staples retailers, HPCs, and food/beverage. Treasuries were mostly 
firmer with the curve steepening; yields were down 4­5 bp at the short end. Dollar index was up 0.2%, now back near pre­Liberation Day 
levels. Gold finished down 0.9%. Silver was down 4.6%. Bitcoin futures were down 5.7%, though came off worst levels. WTI crude settled 
off 3.2%, near session lows.  

Another day of broad pressure on the AI trade; SOX off ~9% over the past three sessions. NVDA leadership transition still a high­profile 
overhang and while company expected to deliver another beat and raise tomorrow, results unlikely to dampen concerns surrounding broader

Synopsis

European equity markets generally ended the session lower, with shares closing around the worst levels of the day. Pan­European indices 
initially traded around unchanged, before moving modestly higher and becoming range­bound. Shares extended gains and peaked around 
Wall Street's open, then saw a sharp pull­back, extending declines and turning negative during the afternoon. Denmark +1.2% and Greece, 
Switzerland both +0.6% led outperformers and remained among the less than a handful of country benchmarks up on the day at the close. 
Netherlands (0.6%) joins the DAX in leading underperformers. Despite reports of new tanker incidents in the Strait of Hormuz, oil prices 
fell as traders focused on a fresh warning from the International Energy Agency that global demand could shrink in 2026.

Asian Markets Recap 
Japan -2.42% | Hong Kong 0.49% | China -0.88%
Synopsis

Asia equities ended mostly lower Monday. There were more sharp declines in South Korea's and Taiwan's tech stocks, while the Nikkei and 
Topix both ended lower. Mainland China stocks finished down, Hong Kong ended a little higher while India is also advancing. Australia and 
Southeast Asia were lower. US futures point to a soft open despite early gains, Europe markets a little higher in opening trades. US dollar 
remains at elevated levels to push more pressure on the AUD; the yen and yuan are flat, rupiah at fresh record low. Treasury and JGBs 
mixed. Crude oil futures flat. Precious metals lower. Cryptocurrencies back under pressure following weekend's slide.  

IDP Education (IEL.AU) has rejected a third Blackstone buyout offer, saying the offer was not in the best interests of its shareholders as it 
substantially undervalues the firm. New World Development (17.HK) has proposed a $3.2B heavily discounted rights issue to recapitalize 
the company. Hindalco (500440.IN ) owned Novelis is to sell two China­based units for $265M. SK Hynix (000660.KS) is to spend around 
$18B to support smaller local chip suppliers.  

Asia equities opened the week with some steep losses in the technology chain ahead of what is expected to be a volatile week for tech stocks 
with Nvidia results and Anthropic's potential listing on the schedule. Equities had opened brighter Monday on reports Nvidia's ousted CEO 
Huang was eying a return to the company before underlying doubts over the sector's high debt resurfaced. Among the individual stocks, 
Samsung Electronics (005930.KS) lost 4.5%, SK Hynix (000660.KS) almost 8.0%, and TSMC (2330.TT) 5.7%, while there also were sub­
stantial declines at Kioxia (285A.JP) and Advantest (6857.JP). Elsewhere in the markets, oil prices inched higher again on weekend reports 
of a resumption of airstrikes on Bahrain to create another headwind for Asia equities.  
Macro

China economic activity data for October disappointed; industrial output and retail sales growth both faded from September while invest­
ment contracted by more than expected. House prices also slid from September despite measures taken then to support lower income 
families. Japan's economy contracted 0.1% in Q3 as consumption remained in the doldrums on inflation and poor weather. Thailand's Q3 
GDP grew 2.9% y/y but missed expectations as government spending contracted.
