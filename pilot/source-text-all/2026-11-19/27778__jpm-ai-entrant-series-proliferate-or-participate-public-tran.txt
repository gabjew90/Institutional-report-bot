In this third report of our AI Entrant Series, we analyze Tyler Technologies (TYL) 
and Samsara (IOT) in the context of public transportation, where these two 
otherwise different companies share some overlap. Beyond this, in typical fashion 
with this series, we examine AI-native solutions seeking to compete within the 
same space and analyze whether there is room for proliferation of TYL and/or IOT. 
Uniquely in this case, rather than constantly hearing about the same one or two 
AI-native competitors in our investor discussions, in our research we’ve 
encountered multiple smaller AI-native solutions to highlight, suggesting a more 
fragmented market – however, we think OpenGov and C3.ai have the most 
potential read-through to both Tyler and Samsara. In contrast to much of our series 
so far, we think the most credible threat to Tyler and Samsara may be each other 
at this stage, with much of the large-scale industry-deployed AI actually being 
developed by these two companies and smaller AI-native solutions not gaining 
notable traction, in our view.
•
Where Tyler and Samsara overlap. While Tyler and Samsara are often 
framed as “back-office GovTech” vs. “front-line IoT,” their overlap is 
increasingly real because public transportation is exactly where software-of-
record and real-world operations collide. In K-12 (school bus) and broader 
municipal fleets, buyers are trying to connect routing, safety, maintenance, 
compliance, and parent/citizen communication into a single operational loop 
– meaning telematics data is no longer “nice to have,” and ERP/work-order 
workflows are no longer “someone else’s problem.” As a result, the 
battleground isn’t whether an AI-native entrant can displace either incumbent 
outright, but whether Tyler can extend down-market into operational 
telemetry/safety workflows or Samsara can extend up-market into the 
government workflow layer that determines how work actually gets scheduled, 
funded, and executed.
•
Highlighting OpenGov and C3.ai. We highlight OpenGov and C3.ai because 
they are among the few “AI-native” platforms with credible adjacency into the 
overlap zone between Tyler’s system-of-record footprint and Samsara’s 
sensor-driven operational data. OpenGov is more likely to show up in near-
term municipal ERP/workflow consolidation decisions (and therefore reads 
more directly to Tyler), while C3.ai is more of a horizontal agentic/data 
unification layer that could theoretically sit on top of both Tyler and Samsara 
rather than replace them. Importantly, neither appears to have the same 
embedded distribution/installed-base gravity in state & local government as 
Tyler, nor the same hardware-linked data capture surface area as Samsara – 
keeping their competitive impact more incremental today.  
•
AI-native competitor impacts on Tyler and Samsara are seemingly 
minimal at this stage…implying the strongest competitive threat actually 
exists between the two. In our view, most smaller AI-native entrants remain 
point-solution providers (often strong demos, limited enterprise-scale 
procurement wins), and they face structural frictions in this market: long sales 
cycles, integrations into legacy systems, data governance constraints, and

Overlap
Tyler has been the legacy provider, but Samsara may be disrupting that. Samsara 
and Tyler share a significant and growing overlap in the public sector transportation 
market, particularly within the K-12 school bus segment. While Tyler has long been the 
dominant “system of record” for school districts, Samsara has aggressively entered the 
space as a “best-in-class” premium telematics and AI safety provider, recently winning 
contracts with major national operators like First Student.
The two names overlap within physical asset management. The primary intersection 
between Samsara and Tyler occurs when a city needs to manage its physical assets – 
like roads, trash trucks, and utility lines – alongside the software that schedules the 
repair and billing for those assets. Outside of this, Tyler traditionally dominates the 
“back-office” software that handles work orders and resident requests, and Samsara 
leads the “front-line” hardware for vehicle tracking and road monitoring – expanding 
into workflow automation and maintenance-oriented modules (e.g., digitizing 
inspection-to-repair loops, dispatch-to-work-order handoffs, and asset-maintenance 
workflows) and tightening integrations that let telematics events trigger back-office 
actions, bringing the two platforms closer to the same operational “middle layer.”
Recent Samsara deals in the space question how undisputed Tyler’s dominance 
may be. Samsara is aggressively targeting the “gov muni” vertical, often using 
competitive pricing concessions to win long-term business. Recent customer wins and 
expansions include: 
•
Top-5 U.S. City Expansion: Announced last month, one of the five largest U.S. 
cities expanded its Samsara deployment from a single division to a multi-department 
rollout spanning fire, parks, public works, and transit. This includes the deployment 
of “Ground Intelligence” across 7,600 lane miles to monitor road infrastructure. 
•
State-Level Wins: Samsara was recently selected as the connected operations 
platform for the State of California, establishing pre-approved contracts for fleet 
maintenance. The company also secured a major win with the State of New York in 
late 2025.
•
School Transportation: Last year, Samsara signed First Student, the largest yellow 
school bus operator in North America, providing AI-powered safety and telematics. 
This aligns with Samsara’s broader push into pupil transportation, a sector Tyler 
Technologies serves through its own transportation and routing solutions. 
Samsara offers compelling safety features that could translate well to the public 
sector... Launched in May 2026, Samsara’s Public Sector AI Suite includes Ground 
Intelligence for detecting road conditions (such as potholes and broken guardrails), 
Waste Intelligence for service verification, and Ridership Management for school 
districts. Additionally, Samsara recently launched the AI Safety Coach and Agent 
Studio, which allow public sector teams to automate manual tasks like maintenance 
workflows and back-office dispatch. Tyler similarly focuses on agentic AI layers 
tailored to government needs, including priority-based budgeting and document 
automation.

Overlapping Product Areas
The primary overlap between Tyler and Samsara exists within public works and 
infrastructure.
•
Infrastructure Monitoring: Samsara’s Ground Intelligence uses vehicle cameras 
and AI to detect potholes and road damage in real time. Tyler competes here with its 
Planning, Regulatory, and Maintenance suite, which municipalities use to log 
infrastructure issues and manage long-term repair projects.
•
Waste & Utility Operations: Samsara recently launched Waste Intelligence, which 
uses AI to verify trash pickups and detect overfilled bins via its vehicle cameras. 
Tyler handles the corresponding “system of record” for these activities through its 
utility billing and civic services platforms.
•
Public Safety Fleets: Both companies target police and emergency services. 
Samsara provides the AI Dash Cams and telematics for the vehicles, while Tyler 
provides the Computer Aided Dispatch (CAD) and records management software 
that those vehicles rely on for their missions. 
AI Competitors
Despite some degree of overlap between Tyler and Samsara, each has its own unique set 
of AI-native competitors. However, specifically within the two companies’ overlap, 
OpenGov and C3.ai are the primary competitors seeking to bridge the gap between the 
Tyler-relevant “back-office” software handling work orders and resident requests and 
the more Samsara-relevant “front-line” hardware for vehicle tracking and road 
monitoring. 
OpenGov: The Integrated Workflow Challenger. OpenGov is the most direct AI-
native threat to both companies, because it has built a “Public Service Platform” that 
intentionally combines the back-office ERP functions (Tyler) with physical asset 
management (Samsara).
•
Overview. OpenGov is a technology company that provides an AI-powered public 
service platform specifically designed for local and state governments. The 
company’s unified ecosystem connects critical government functions, including 
accounting (ERP), budgeting, asset management, permitting, and procurement, 
serving more than 2,000 agencies across the U.S. OpenGov overlaps significantly 
with Tyler, as both are major players in the GovTech ecosystem focused on 
modernizing antiquated public sector systems. 
•
Tyler impact: OpenGov is winning head-to-head bids for citywide ERP and 
financial software, offering a more modern, cloud-native experience than Tyler’s 
legacy systems. While Tyler has a broader legacy footprint across 15,000

Samsara vs. Tyler Insights
