Two Quick Things: (1) Was with our Utilities/Power analyst Carly Davenport yesterday meeting with
investors – let me know if want to connect on some of the key takeaways / debates. IPPs, Regulated
Utilities, BTM, navigating the evolving regulatory backdrop … all extremely topical in conversations. (2)
Carly and I hosted a call with Peter Lake (Former Director of Power for White House National Energy
Dominance Council) earlier this week – we spent time on Texas, PJM, permitting, and key timelines /
dates – let me know if want to catch up on the back of it.
Running through some of the key debates on the desk / inbounds over the last couple of days on the power
complex below – let us know what sticks out to you. High level – there is still little urgency to play
offense in the power complex – ie higher beta / torque names (BTM, fuel cells, nuclear) aren’t coming up
in conversations like they were earlier in the fall. Instead - number of asks for ‘low beta, high quality,
fortress balance sheet, low capital requirements’ ways to play power recently from the dedicated
community. Important to note - we don’t think there’s a strong view that the power trade has lost all of its
steam / has no more room to run – specialists are still hanging around the hoop on certain subsectors (more
on that below) - with a number of folks looking at the price action and highlighting a 'shoot first then aim'
response.
IPPs: You can see it in the price action, but the headline here is ‘ERCOT is not where I want to be right
now, if I own anything in the group, I will own PJM exposure / who has shown positive momentum on the
contracting front'. I would think the short pitch on the group is really hard to press at these levels, but then
again, the lack of offense overall is what matters most. There’s broad based frustration from both
specialists and generalists given the general view that installed capacity in the ground is worth more now
vs even a few months (weeks?) prior. Keep an eye out for December 10th and 17th for Batch Zero updates
in ERCOT.
Regulated Utilities: We are seemingly past the tape of Utilities being a bond proxy trade – and with the
10Y peaking back in early October – we are back to discussing idio plays in the Utes complex. My
conversations with the dedicated community have generally highlighted the same thing– still less reason to
lean into TX/OH/GA/IL exposed names for now and increasing willingness to own LA/Carolinas/MO.
BTM: The sense of euphoria that was tangible back in September has largely dissipated. Think we are
getting past the point of running through the ‘doomsday scenarios’ – ie assume whatever you want for
base business at mid cycle multiple, give full credit for capex and put 0 on the multiple for power. For
those still focused on the theme – there’s still preference for contracts that are inked / high degree of
confidence in contract execution before YE.

Midstream: Would think Midstream has seen an inflow of capital over the last year+ on ‘low beta ways to
play power and gas volumetric growth’. Not getting questions on 'who can execute over time / where the
backlog step ups are going to be most noticeable'– but really are around if we see a meaningful re-rate in
the stocks or if we ‘just trade back towards the top end of historical bands’. Which name generalists are
focused on: WMB. Which names specialists are focused on: WMB, DTM.
One chart for the road … from Vincent Lin and the GS Prime Desk this morning … Factor exposure to
Medium-Term Momentum is on track to decline for a 6th straight month to the lowest level since Dec ‘22
(20th percentile 5-year). At the same time, factor exposure to Value has risen sharply to 5-year highs.
