• Low-TMB tumors:  He discussed the INT Ph1 adjuvant PDAC poster 
presented at ESMO (LINK) as evidence that individualized INT can induce 
neoantigen-specific immune responses even in an immunologically 
challenging, low-TMB tumor. Patients received up to nine doses, with 
expansion of peripheral T cells following vaccination and responses across 
both CD4 helper and CD8 killer cells. He favors a broad, polyclonal response 
as a potential way to limit immune escape (calling the ability to encode up to 
34 neoantigens notable), while acknowledging that responses to a smaller 
number of high-quality antigens could be sufficient. These findings support 
biological feasibility but require further investment and clinical endpoint 
data to definitively establish efficacy.
• RCC: Despite RCC not typically being a TMB-high tumor, he sees a strong 
rationale for INT given established sensitivity to I/O, including many of those 
also used in melanoma. He discussed VHL-related biology and highlighted 
insertions/deletions as a potential source of immunogenic neoantigens, 
noting that non-driver mutations could be more attractive targets than the 
principal driver itself. His ~60–70% POS reflects the possibility that certain 
RCC subgroups may not benefit. He would not necessarily be discouraged if 
the trial does not meet its efficacy threshold at the first interim analysis, as 
benefits of I/O agents can emerge over time, and an effective Keytruda 
backbone could make an early incremental benefit harder to demonstrate in 
his view.
• NSCLC:  The expert was more somewhat cautious on lung given the range of 
disease subtypes, underlying mutations, and clinical aggressiveness (ascribing 
a ~50% POS here). While some smoking-associated tumors have high TMB, 
driver-mutated tumors can be less responsive to conventional I/O. He raised 
the possibility that INT could harness mutations in these tumors as 
therapeutic targets, although this remains a hypothesis to establish clinically.
• Early-stage lung cancer: Our KOL sees a rationale for INT monotherapy in 
stage 1 lung cancer, particularly as screening increases the number of 
patients diagnosed. However, high cure rates following surgery leave a 
smaller absolute benefit to demonstrate and imply slower recurrence-event 
accrual. He viewed the favorable tolerability of INT as particularly important 
in this population and considered monotherapy attractive, with 
immunogenicity data potentially providing an earlier indication of activity.
• MIBC:  Our KOL sees a rationale for evaluating INT in bladder cancer, 
although he noted this is outside his primary expertise and viewed it as a 
harder setting. Much of the recent progress has come from ADC's. He would 
look for evidence of immunogenicity, including T-cell expansion and clonality, 
to build confidence in the approach before extrapolating clinical benefit.
• Other tumors where it would be interesting to evaluate INT: The expert 
highlighted liver cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, glioblastoma, and gynecologic 
malignancies as potential areas of interest given substantial unmet need. He 
was more cautious on CRC, citing heterogeneous tumor biology, an 
immunosuppressive environment, prior vaccine challenges, and the 
additional immune suppression associated with liver metastases.

Model Changes and Catalyst Calendar  
Model changes: We raise INT POS for melanoma to 100% from 90% following the Ph3 
data. We  also raise the peak penetration for melanoma and NSCLC to 40% from ~30% 
prior. We also break out our INT estimates for other tumor types (i.e., RCC and PDAC). 
Based on the KOL commentary, we raise the POS for RCC to 60% from 50%, and lower 
the POS for bladder cancer to 50% from 60%. As a result, we now model INT WW 2040 
unadjusted sales of $32bn from $24bn prior. 
While we still lack detail on potential INT pricing, MRNA mgmt expressed confidence in 
attractive long-term margins for the product (LINK). MRNA noted that the accounting 
treatment of revenue recognition with partner MRK is still being worked out, but the 
company expects to receive half of profits plus a markup on COGS, given its 
manufacturing role, which in total would equate to about 60%. Hence, we raise our profit 
share to 55% from 50% to reflect these economics.
We also update COGS of the COVID franchise to 20% from 25-30% prior, in-line with 
2026 COGS.  Lastly, we update the model for the 10-Q. 
As a result of these changes, our PT goes to $200 from $95. We derive our price target 
from a DCF analysis using a 10% discount rate and 6% terminal growth (vs. 4% prior) 
given our view that it will be very challenging to replicate/genericize INT  as it’s essentially 
a personalized vaccine that uses an algorithm to design and which the companies will 
continue to iterate upon. Our new price target implies an EV/peak rev multiple of 4x on 
the INT franchise, which is a premium to historical average of 3x, but deserved in our view 
given the above point.
