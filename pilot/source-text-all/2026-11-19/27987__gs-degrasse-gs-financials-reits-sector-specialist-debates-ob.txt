GS Financials & REITs Sector Specialist: DEBATES/Observations .. Cyber
Risk .. I-Bank Boutiques .. Consumer Finance .. Size Factor
November 17th 2026; All references to "we/us/our" refer to the views and observations of the desk. 
 
Good evening .. Delving into a handful of Debates across the sector that stand out to us w/ some
desk observations .. 
 
Cyber Risk in Financials … No real investor consensus or conviction in
how this plays out across the sector
 
Conversations here have, for obvious reasons, notably picked up – but it’s important to note that leading
industry figures have been talking about this for quite some time – look no further than Jamie Dimon’s
shareholder letter back in April, where he wrote “Cyber risk. I have to mention this because it remains one
of our biggest risks, and this is probably true for many other major industries and corporations. AI will almost
surely make this risk worse. We invest significantly to protect ourselves and stay vigilant” (LINK to letter)
 
In asking investors how they see a theme of ‘greater cyber risk’ playing out across Financials, responses
were incredibly mixed (though amount of input/inbounds was high) .. A couple groups of stocks stood out
– but whether the theme was bullish or bearish was quite mixed/debated .. The ‘easiest’ answer people
came to was ‘derisk/sell’ – though one interesting angle we heard was to sell businesses that are asset
custodians (banks, wealth managers, brokers, digital asset platforms etc) while more capital light,
transaction heavy businesses (ie Investment Banks/Boutiques, Alts) may be ‘cleaner’ to own
 
Standout stocks/themes on cyber where we got mixed inbounds on
• Identify Verification .. Some people pointed to those with identity verification businesses (credit bureaus .. fraud
VAS at card networks) with TRU just today talking up its identity business – while others skeptical on if these
businesses are large enough to be investible ..
• Bank Tech (ie JKHY FIS FISV & other vendors) could be bullish (mid sized banks banks rely on vendors for
capabilities, this could catalyze a new source of demand & allow providers to push price) or bearish (existing
capabilities are not where they need to be and we need a investment cycle – and in meantime there is
disintermediation risk from potential new & more AI native competitors if need for adoption is urgent .. we haven’t
heard much of any specific competitors fwiw)
• Bank Consolidation – Bank M&A bulls argue it’s a push for banks to consolidate & scale as expense budgets at
regional & mid sized banks may be unable to deal with this on their own .. on the other hand, bears say there’s
been several reasons for scale for a long time – and there hasn’t been much of a resulting acceleration so it’s
difficult to call this the catalyst …

• Cyber Insurance .. People are debating whether 1) these business lines are large enough as a % of premiums to
actually be investible, and 2) if so, is it too early to go long (ie, do we need a large loss event for the market to
harden?) ..
 
Consumer Finance – Sentiment continues to sour
 
What got us to where we are (largely pre November): Sentiment has been souring for most of
this year despite delinquencies at the major card lenders continuing to look solid (October Delinquencies
came relatively in-line with expectations . similar to the case the past 12 months .. chart below .. LINK to
recent note by Nash/GIR w/ bunch of data) & continued room for performance (card DQs at 3.78% vs mid-
3% 2015-2019 average), with credit fears fueled by (per feedback) 1) implications of higher oil prices, 2) a
handful of negative soundbites on subprime (ie UPST, CACC) & too lesser extent (viewed as idiosyncratic)
ongoing focus in student lending (SLM), and 3) some signs of worsening 2026 vintages (personal lending,
auto) in a handful of pockets (though it’s far too early to be conclusive on 2026 ..)
 
 
The acceleration in negativity in recent weeks (see below for GS Prime’s L/S ratio on Consumer
Finance … h/t Erin Tolar)  is coming alongside investor fears (per conversations) that we could see a
changing regulatory backdrop, one in which the economics of the business get reassessed (whether it’s late
fees, rate caps, etc) .. For now – investors have been more narrow/targeted in expressing caution in the card
lenders specifically (ie COF SYF BFH .. Nash/GIR attempts to size the impacts across a range of scenarios
in a recent note - LINK) – though there is an ongoing debate around whether other more shorter duration
liquidity products (personal lending, payday lending, etc) & other forms of non-card lending (ie buy now pay

later) may also get scrutinized (ie .. all forms of credit availability start showing up on an implied APR screen)
..

Boutique Investment Banks .. DEBATED
 
What got us to where we are: This group seemed almost universally disliked into 3Q earnings amidst
the first leg higher in interest rates, under the view that 1) third party data & buyside checks on M&A
pipelines & backlogs were soft, 2) the rise in interest rates would prohibit deal activity from forming, 3) the
above two dynamics are indicative we have moved from mid-to-late cycle (which likely means multiple
derating), and 4) competitive dynamics are pressuring margins across both comp & non comp
 
The group is +~10% the past month, led by EVR (+15%), LAZ (+10%) and restructurers (HLI PJT +9%) . in
part because 1) there’s been a uptick in deal announcements the past couple of weeks, defying bearish
views on rates constraining activity (particularly since sponsors have upticked), 2) earnings were better than
feared (third party data checks may be becoming less informative of rev at print? ..)
 
Feedback / Views from here ..
 
BULL CASE: In the past couple of weeks, there’s been a more vocalized bull case on the group – arguing
that there’s a narrowing window over the next two years ahead of a potentially more onerous antitrust
backdrop, and corporates are more incentivized to ‘stretch the math’ and lean in on transformational M&A,
this of course benefits 1) large cap, and 2) strategic (which, is already leading YTD) – which point to PJT
EVR PWP ..
 

BEARS .. argue we are late cycle– and if you look back at this group in 2018-2019, the multiples derated,
indicating any better earnings revisions from a potential M&A wave would be ‘looked through’ as late inning
behavior and eat into multiples that aren’t yet appetizing enough
 
Yaro/GIR in a recent note (of which these charts are coming from .. LINK) believe’ s we’re Mid cycle, and
he’s comparing the volume growth we’ve seen since start of 2024 and compares it to prior cycles .. just
where we are in the cycle remains quite debated (And for this group, if does matter whether this is 2017-
2018 .. or 2018-2019 ..) though recent flow (defensive posturing) and selling out of financials is indicative of
the market raising their late-cycle probability …
 
 
If we are in late cycle – restructuring stands out as the beneficiary amongst this group (PJT, HLI, MC) ..
which, could make PJT a interesting long here given their leading exposure in all of 1) large cap M&A, 2)
strategic M&A, and 3) Restructuring
 

 
‘Large cap’ Size factor starting to perk up …
 
One of the more impactful but perhaps not talked much about stories this year has been the
underperformance in the ‘Size’ factor in Financials (see below for Long large cap, short small cap Financials
.. GSFISIZE) .. The reasons for the prior underperformance are not abundantly clear to us (one view that we
found interesting was large caps had ‘everything to lose, and little to gain’ with rippling AI fears to stable &
quality business models earlier in the year) – but the fact is this trade has started to reverse since
September (h/t Mitola) .. I’ll be keeping an eye on BLK here, a stock where fundamental investors have been
pounding the table on (GS Positioning score at 3Q earnings = 8/10), still trades at a discount vs its medium
term history despite several beat & raises (& the unfolding of a real margin improvement story)
