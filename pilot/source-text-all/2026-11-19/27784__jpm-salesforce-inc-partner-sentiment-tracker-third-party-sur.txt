This report summarizes the key feedback emerging from third-party channel 
checks conducted with Salesforce partners and customers. The report is 
intended to provide an aggregate view of the feedback across these checks 
rather than a detailed account of any individual conversation. Overall, the 
feedback suggests that disruption risks to Salesforce’s core systems remain 
limited. However, the company still needs to navigate several real-world 
hurdles to drive broader Agentforce adoption and monetization. The primary 
challenges include: 1) enterprise data readiness, which is slowing AI 
deployments but, conversely, supporting demand for Data 360 in more 
complex use cases; 2) demonstrating broader and repeatable ROI beyond 
service-oriented implementations; and 3) customers seeking seat reductions 
while simultaneously managing incremental AI and data spending 
commitments, potentially creating a timing gap between pressure on seat-
based revenue and the ramp in consumption-based revenue. 
Across 10 different partner and customer conversations, we believe the key 
themes were:
AI demand is real, but the monetization curve appears more gradual than 
customer interest would suggest. Agentforce is moving into production and 
successful deployments are generating follow-on opportunities, but the pace of 
expansion depends heavily on customer readiness. Many enterprises are still 
working through data quality, process design, governance, and security 
requirements before moving from isolated workflows to broader automation.
The transition to AI introduces both an incremental revenue opportunity and 
a more complex commercial model. Flex Credits lower the cost of 
experimentation, while Agentforce and Data 360 can support contract expansion 
and help offset pressure on traditional seats. However, uncertain consumption, 
limited production histories, and difficulties establishing repeatable ROI are 
extending commercial discussions and may create a lag between seat reductions 
and scaled AI revenue.
The partner opportunity is shifting toward specialized, higher-value work 
rather than expanding uniformly. Partners with strong AI, data, integration, and 
change-management capabilities are benefiting from larger and more complex 
projects, while broader consultancies are reporting flatter Salesforce activity. At 
the same time, AI-assisted development is compressing routine implementation 
work, while Salesforce Professional Services is competing more directly for 
implementation and managed-services opportunities.
Salesforce is strengthening its position as the data and workflow layer beneath 
enterprise AI, but customers are resisting a closed architecture. Data 360, 
MuleSoft, and Informatica together are helping broaden the platform’s ability to 
support cross-system agents, while Slack, Microsoft Teams, and custom interfaces

The core CRM franchise remains durable, but the competition for incremental 
spending is broadening. Embedded data, customized workflows, and high switching costs

the partners, is not widespread replacement of Salesforce, but the possibility that Microsoft,

Agentforce Engagement Is Broad, but Scaled Production 
Adoption Remains Limited
Agentforce is moving beyond experimentation, but production maturity remains 
uneven and narrower than headline deployment figures imply. Selected enterprise 
integrators report that 30%-60% of clients or delivered projects have reached production, 
although the underlying cohorts and definitions differ, while other checks indicate that many 
live agents have limited daily usage or remain confined to isolated workflows. Overall, scaled 
adoption remains concentrated among customers with defined use cases, dedicated AI 
budgets and stronger data foundations.
The Vice President at an IT Services Firm said 30%-35% of clients were live, with another 
10%-15% in the POC stage, while the Director at a Global Digital Consultancy said fewer 
than a dozen of approximately four dozen agents that had been built remained in active daily 
use. The disparity suggests that deployment activity is improving, but sustained usage and 
repeatable expansion across workflows remain more important measures of 
commercialization than the number of agents initially launched.
Data and Organizational Readiness Are the Primary Barriers to 
Agentforce Scaling
Data quality, process readiness and organizational governance remain greater 
constraints on Agentforce scaling than the underlying AI models. Fragmented records, 
unclear ownership and poorly documented workflows delay deployments, while security 
reviews and limited access constrain autonomous execution. These barriers slow near-term 
monetization but expand demand for data modernization, integration, governance and 
change-management services.
The Lead at a Global IT Services Firm estimated that 80%-90% of projects require substantial 
data cleanup, while the Executive at a Business Management Consulting Firm characterized 
the challenge as 30% technology and 70% people and process. Both estimates are specific 
to the partners’ experiences rather than market-wide measures, but point to the same 
conclusion: data foundations and operating models must improve before production 
adoption can scale.
Consumption Pricing Supports Experimentation, but ROI and 
Cost Predictability Create Friction
Consumption pricing lowers the initial commitment required to test Agentforce, but 
cost uncertainty and limited ROI proof are creating friction as customers move into 
production. Enterprises lack sufficient usage history to forecast credit and data 
consumption, while measurable returns remain concentrated in selected service workflows.

The Managing Director and Chief AI Architect at a Global IT Services Firm viewed Flex 
Credits constructively because spending scales with usage, while the Vice President at an IT 
Services Firm said CFOs remain uncomfortable replacing predictable seat costs with 
uncapped consumption. Selected service deployments have delivered 20%-35% contact

checks suggest that broad enterprise ROI is still difficult to establish from across

Growth Remains Positive but Highly Dispersed as Contract Mix 
Shifts Toward AI and Data
Salesforce partner growth remains positive but highly dispersed, with AI-ready 
integrators benefiting from Agentforce, Data 360 and complex implementation work 
while broader consultancies report flatter activity. Core renewals remain durable, 
although customers are increasingly seeking fewer traditional seats and reallocating 
spending toward AI, data and consumption. This is supporting contract value in selected 
renewals, but creates a timing gap between immediate seat pressure and the subsequent ramp 
in consumption revenue.
The Vice President at an IT Services Firm reported 25% y/y growth, with deal closures up 
approximately 30% and renewal spending up 10%-15%, while the Director at a Global 
Digital Consultancy described Salesforce-only revenue as flat. Separately, the Director at a 
Global IT Services Firm said approximately one-quarter of renewals involved requests for 
5%-10% seat reductions. Agentforce and Data 360 attach could still support 5%-10% net 
TCV expansion in those deals, although consumption revenue may take 12-18 months to 
ramp.
Headless Adoption Expands Salesforce’s Back-End Role but 
Raises Interface and Seat Risks
Headless adoption through Slack, Microsoft Teams and custom interfaces can expand 
access to Salesforce workflows and reinforce its position as a governed back-end system 
of record. However, it may weaken the relationship between workflow usage, Salesforce’s 
own interface and dedicated CRM seats. The longer-term opportunity therefore depends on 
Salesforce capturing sufficient data, workflow and consumption economics when another 
application owns the user interaction.
The Managing Director and Chief AI Architect at a Global IT Services Firm described Slack 
as an increasingly important interface for initiating agentic workflows, while the Director at 
a Global Digital Consultancy said headless integrations through Slack and Teams were 
gaining more traction than some direct Agentforce deployments. The checks do not establish 
broad seat cannibalization, but indicate that underlying Salesforce usage and dedicated seat 
requirements may become less directly linked.

external, unstructured and real-time information, while Informatica strengthens

The Vice President at an IT Services Firm said simple agents can operate using core

Partner Economics Face Pressure as Salesforce Services 
Competition and AI Reshapes Delivery
AI is increasing demand for specialized data, architecture and change-management 
skills, but partner economics face pressure from Salesforce Professional Services and 
faster AI-assisted delivery. AI-assisted delivery is reducing the effort required for 
conventional implementations such as routine coding and configuration, while production-
grade deployments are creating more complex demand across data integration, governance 
and post-deployment support. The mix therefore appears to be shifting from traditional 
billable-hour implementations toward higher-value architecture, fixed-price projects and 
recurring managed services.
The Director at a Global Digital Consultancy said the firm had lost implementation and 
managed-services work to Salesforce Professional Services. At the same time, the Managing 
Director and Chief AI Architect at a Global IT Services Firm reported that advanced agentic 
and headless engagements could reach 8,000-15,000 hours, compared to 600-2,000 hours for 
conventional implementations. The services opportunity is therefore shifting rather than 
necessarily contracting, although partner outcomes will vary by project complexity and 
commercial model.
Core CRM Remains Defensible, but Competition Is Intensifying 
Around Adjacent Workloads
Salesforce’s core CRM installed base remains highly defensible given embedded data, 
custom workflows and substantial switching costs, with little evidence of broad 
displacement among complex enterprise customers. Competitive pressure is instead 
concentrated in greenfield CRM, workflow orchestration, enterprise data and employee 
interfaces. The principal risk is therefore not wholesale migration, but leakage of incremental 
workloads and spending to Microsoft, HubSpot, ServiceNow and independent data 
platforms.
The Managing Director and Chief AI Architect at a Global IT Services Firm said Salesforce 
remains strongly positioned in brownfield accounts but faces more open competition in 
greenfield evaluations. The Vice President at an IT Services Firm identified Microsoft as the 
principal TCO threat in Microsoft-standardized environments, HubSpot in simpler mid-
market deployments and ServiceNow in overlapping workflow use cases. Salesforce retains 
the core system-of-record advantage, but surrounding AI, data and engagement spending is 
becoming more contested.
