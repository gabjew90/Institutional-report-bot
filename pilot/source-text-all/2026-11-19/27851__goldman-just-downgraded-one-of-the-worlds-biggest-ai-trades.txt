We warned about Taiwan. What comes next?
We warned about Taiwan's vulnerability in late October (here), when the market was still defying the
carnage in Korea and the broader semiconductor complex. Crowded positioning, stretched
divergences and extraordinary AI expectations had created the conditions for a violent catch-down.
We highlighted the December EWT 95 put as a cheap way to position for the reversal. That bearish call
has worked exceptionally well.
Now Goldman is downgrading Taiwan, cutting its TAIEX target by 13% despite forecasting another year
of extraordinary earnings growth. Foreign investors have dumped almost $90 billion of Taiwanese
equities this year, domestic leverage remains elevated, and the AI growth cycle is showing signs of
deceleration.
The first leg of the Taiwan reversal has played out. The bigger question is whether the reset has gone
far enough, or whether one of the world's most concentrated AI trades has more downside ahead.
The Taiwan put that delivered
In late October, we flagged Taiwan as the next potential AI domino, with positioning at the 91st
percentile of its five-year range and a growing divergence versus Korea and the semiconductor
complex.
Our preferred expression was the December EWT 95 put. The catch-down subsequently materialized,
and the downside convexity delivered.
The question now is whether the reversal has run its course, or whether Taiwan is merely taking a
breather before the next leg lower.

Goldman cuts Taiwan
Goldman has lowered Taiwan from Overweight to Market Weight and slashed its TAIEX target from
57,500 to 50,000.
Importantly, this is not an outright bearish call. The new end-2027 target still implies close to 20%
upside, supported by forecasts for 32% EPS growth in 2027 and 25% in 2028.
The catch? Goldman is simultaneously lowering its target forward P/E to 14x. Taiwan can deliver
spectacular earnings growth and still struggle to deliver equally spectacular equity returns. The
multiple is becoming the problem.

The AI boom is still booming. Stocks aren't.
One of the more interesting divergences in Goldman's macro research is the growing disconnect
between Taiwan's technology exports and its equity market.
The export machine continues to deliver exceptional growth, but equities have become increasingly
sensitive to positioning, valuations and the broader semiconductor cycle.
This is an important change in market behavior. When stocks stop responding positively to
extraordinary fundamentals, it often means expectations have already done much of the heavy lifting.
The AI boom hasn't broken. The market's willingness to keep paying up for it is being tested.

Peak growth, rising dependence
Goldman expects Taiwan's GDP growth to slow from 13% in 2026 to 6.3% in 2027, with tech's direct
contribution falling from 9.3 to 4.8 percentage points.
Yet tech's share of total growth is expected to rise from 72% to 77%.
Taiwan's economy is slowing, but its dependence on AI is actually increasing. Not an ideal combination
for a market already priced for extraordinary growth.

The $89 billion exodus
Foreign investors have sold approximately $89 billion of Taiwanese equities YTD, including $18.6
billion over the past month and another $2.9 billion in the latest week, according to Goldman.
Yet the domestic bid remains powerful. Taiwan has attracted roughly $80 billion in ETF inflows this
year, while brokerage cash has reached a record $148 billion.
This is a remarkable tug-of-war. Foreign capital continues to exit, while domestic liquidity absorbs
much of the selling pressure.
The risk is not simply that foreigners keep selling. It is what happens if the domestic bid starts losing
momentum.

The positioning paradox
Despite the enormous foreign selling, Taiwan is not universally underowned.
Goldman estimates that hedge-fund exposure remains at the 85th percentile of the past five years. Yet
active institutional managers tell a very different story.
Asia ex-Japan active funds are 530bp underweight Taiwan relative to benchmark, while global
emerging-market active funds are 425bp underweight. Taiwan Tech Hardware & Semis is also the
largest sector underweight.
This creates an unusually complicated positioning structure. Hedge funds remain relatively exposed,
traditional active managers are defensively positioned, and domestic investors continue providing
liquidity.
Taiwan is still vulnerable to deleveraging, but the substantial active-manager underweights also create
fuel for a squeeze. The next move may not be nearly as straightforward as the original bearish setup.

The leverage problem
Taiwan's domestic liquidity machine is powerful, but increasingly leveraged. Goldman highlights record
leveraged-ETF exposure and elevated margin balances, creating a potentially reflexive market
structure.
The same leverage that supported the rally can amplify the next selloff. When prices fall, leveraged
ETF rebalancing and margin-related selling can turn weakness into more weakness.
GS
Asia's AI concentration problem
Taiwan now represents 27% of MSCI Asia Pacific ex-Japan, with approximately 85% AI-related exposure,
according to Goldman. Implied volatility is already above 30%.
This has implications well beyond Taiwan. Regional investors looking for diversification are increasingly
exposed to the same AI factor through their benchmark allocations.
Buying Asia is increasingly another way of buying AI. That works until the market decides it wants less
of both.

Taiwan: The next move
Our bearish Taiwan call played out beautifully, but the setup is now more complicated. Goldman is
cutting exposure, earnings momentum is slowing, and foreign selling remains relentless. Yet active
managers are already heavily underweight, while domestic liquidity continues to provide support.
Technically, Taiwan is back at the massive long-term trendline, trading at make-or-break support. The
index remains below its 200-day moving average, and the longer it stays there, the greater the risk of
another leg lower.
The easy money on the bearish side may be behind us, but until Taiwan reclaims its 200-day and
repairs the technical damage, the burden of proof remains firmly with the bulls.
