Last evening’s tech earnings beat is having very limited effect on broader risk appetite. 
The dollar is somewhat firmer against several crosses with weakness led by sterling, the 
euro, MXN and rand while CAD is flat. N.A. sovereign yields are little changed so far with 
most of the movement in bonds occurring in the UK (CPI, see below) and Australia 
(ongoing minutes, wages, see below). Gold is getting walloped this morning and oil prices 
are a few dimes lower. Stocks are wavering with the Nasdaq outperforming on the heels of 
last evening’s solid earnings from Nvidia with lighter spillover into S&P futures, but 
equities elsewhere are on the backfoot. Turns out, it’s not just about tech.  
US—STALE MINUTES AND HOUSING DATA 
Minutes to the October 27th–28th FOMC meeting are stale before they even arrive this 
afternoon (2pmET). They don’t talk less in the Warsh era, for one thing (chart 1). A massive 
download of Fed-speak has hit markets from across all officials except the Chair. Fresh 
warm core CPI and a solid nonfarm payrolls report paired with Fed-speak has raised 
pricing for the December meeting from about 20bps to approaching 30bps now. 
The US also refreshes some housing data including starts during October (8:30amET) and 
pending home sales for the same month (10amET). TIC flows in September (4pmET) will 
round out the day.

UK CPI DROVE OVERREACTION IN GILTS 
UK CPI inflation met expectations while services inflation stabilized. That combination 
caught positioning off guard and may have driven an overreaction toward lower front-end 
yields as the 2-year gilt richened by about 6bps. The reaction took a while to kick in as 
markets digested the details. 
CPI was up 0.5% m/m NSA, matching consensus, and enough to add a tenth to the year-
over-year rate at 3.6%, also matching consensus. Core CPI was unchanged and on consensus 
at 2.8% y/y. The month-over-month core CPI change was around average when comparing 
like months of October since it’s seasonally unadjusted data (chart 2). CPI services was 
unchanged at 3.7% y/y (3.8% consensus) and has been roughly moving sideways since 
Spring. 
CHILE’S STRUGGLING ECONOMY 
Chilean GDP fell 1.8% q/q SA nonannualized which was greater than the expected drop of -1.4%. 
The prior quarter was revised up a tick to 0.1% q/q SA. The economy has been treading water for 
an extended period (chart 3). The weakness in Q3 was fairly widespread as output fell in mining (-
12.9% q/q SA) because of reduced copper output (-11.9%), while agriculture fell -1.8% and was 
matched by construction. Financial services retreated 1.1%, manufacturing fell by -0.5% and the 
restaurant/hotel business continues to retreat. 
Key will be how BCCh trades off persistent upside to CPI inflation readings—such as 
October’s 4.5% y/y rate from 4.1% the prior month) against a weakening economy and 
prospects for emergent slack to create future disinflation. Markets are pricing the central bank 
to have about a four-in-ten chance at a hike on December 15th and about a full hike priced for 
the January 26th meeting.  
AUSSIE YIELDS CONTINUE TO DROP DESPITE WAGE FIGURES 
Despite solid wage figures, short-term Australian yields fell overnight with the 2s yield down 
another 5bps. That’s an extension of the trade that began after RBA minutes arrived on 
Monday night (ET). Those minutes revealed widening disagreement on the state of the 
economy and forward-looking risks as three dissenters downplayed upside risks to inflation in 
favour of downside risks to the economy and “expressed their concern that the economy 
might be near the point of entering a material sharper downturn and pointed to the costs in 
terms of unemployment.”  
Cue tonight’s jobs data for October (7:30pmET) that have posted declines in two of the past 
three months and a continued rise in unemployment. The unemployment rate of 4.7% is 1.3 
ppts higher than the unsustainable trough in 2022.

BANK INDONESIA SURPRISES—AGAIN! 
BI sided with the smaller share of consensus (16 out of 38 forecasters) who expected a 25bps rate hike overnight. Governor Damayanti 
positioned the hike as pre-emptive against inflation risk and rupiah softness. The currency has depreciated by about 4% since September 
and the bank views this as creating stability risks in domestic markets alongside inflation risk. 
GOLD OUTMUSCLED CPI IN DRIVING A WEAKER RAND 
The South African rand is among the worst performers this morning despite warmer than expected South African core CPI. The culprit is 
probably the plunging price of gold that is off by about US$86 this morning to about $3760/oz. Gold is now more than $1600 lower than 
the peak earlier this year and about $900 lower than where it was as recently as late August. 
CPI came and went and was ignored against that backdrop as SARB is widely expected to hold tomorrow while expressing some concern 
about gold prices and hence a driver of its economy. CPI was up 0.7% m/m NSA and 5.2% y/y (4.5% prior), both of which matched 
consensus. Core inflation was up 0.3% m/m (0.2% consensus) and 4.2% y/y (4.0% prior, 4.1% consensus). The combination of falling gold 
prices and firmer than expected inflation present a bit of a quandary for the central bank that will be closely scrutinized tomorrow.
