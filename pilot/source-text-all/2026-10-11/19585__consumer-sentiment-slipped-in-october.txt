Consumer Sentiment Slipped in October
Headlines 
 
Consumer sentiment slid to 46.3 in the preliminary October print from 48.1. 
 
 
The decline in sentiment was entirely due to a sharp drop in the current 
conditions index. 
 
 
One year ahead inflation expectations edged up to 4.7% in October. 
 
 
Five-year ahead inflation expectations ticked up to 3.5% in October, the 
highest since May.  
 
 
The long stretch of depressed consumer sentiment reflects a highly 
uncertain geopolitical backdrop, the recent surge in long term interest 
rates and elevated gas and energy prices.  
Details
Higher gas prices and surging long-term interest 
rates are weighing on consumer confidence. 
Consumer sentiment fell to 46.3 in October in the 
preliminary print according to the University of 
Michigan (Figure 1). This was below PNC’s forecast of 
47.8 and the third straight drop. Sentiment is down 
1.8% month-on-month and is 13.6% lower than a year 
ago.  
 
The decline in sentiment was due to a decline of 6.2 
points in the current conditions index to an all-time 
low of 44.7 (Figure 2), implying that households' 
assessment of their present financial and economic 
situation deteriorated. The future expectations 
index rose 1.0 point to 47.3.  
 
Sentiment was mixed across the political spectrum 
with increases among Republicans (+6.8 points to 
84.0) and Democrats (+1.7 points to 37.3 but still low). 
Sentiment fell 8.8 points among Independents.  
 
There was a drop in sentiment in all stock ownership 
categories, with sentiment falling the most for the 
bottom (-3.5 points) and top (-3.6 points) terciles.  
Sentiment for the middle income tercile fell a more 
modest 1.3 points.  
 
The elusive end to the conflict in the  Middle East and 
the increase in energy prices pushed inflation 
expectations higher. One year ahead inflation 
expectations edged up to 4.7% from 4.6% in 
September (Figure 3). That is the second straight 
increase and the highest since May 2026.

views as crucial to achieving price stability – ticked 
up to 3.5% from 3.4%. Despite the slight rise, long-

In a special question, only 31% of consumers expect 
to continue spending as usual in the next 12 months

Conversely, 54% said they would cut back spending

The long stretch of depressed consumer sentiment

the recent surge in long term interest rates and 
elevated gas and energy prices. Consequently, 
sentiment is expected to remain depressed in the
