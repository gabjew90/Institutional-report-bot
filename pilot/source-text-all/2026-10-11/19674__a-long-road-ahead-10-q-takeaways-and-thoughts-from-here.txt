1Q EPS Feedback: Is This the Final Cut?  
NKE's EPS reset was deeper than expected (we were at $1.44 ahead of the print), 
and the key investor question is whether this marks the final estimate cut. While 
FY27 guidance may provide a near-term floor for earnings revisions, we continue 
to see a turnaround that remains in the middle innings, with Sportswear, Jordan, 
and China still under pressure. Looking ahead to Investor Day, we believe 
investors need a more credible path to revenue inflection and a clear margin 
bridge to restore EPS power above $2 over the next five years. 
Guidance May Put a Floor on EPS, But the Revenue Debate Remains 
The debate has intensified following the quarter. Bulls argue that FY27’s formal 
EPS guidance puts a floor on estimate revisions in the near-term, creating a more 
favorable setup if management can execute. Bears counter that Jordan remains 
early in its turnaround, Sportswear trends continue to deteriorate, and the 
innovation pipeline is unlikely to offset ongoing marketplace inventory actions 
over the medium term.  
Units and ASPs Remain Pressured Despite the World Cup; Regional SSS 
Disclosures Were Removed 
The underlying operating data continues to support the latter view. Despite a 
quarter that should have benefitted from major football-related demand drivers, 
footwear and apparel units declined -5% and -2%, respectively (vs. -4% and -8% in 
4Q), while footwear ASPs decelerated sequentially to -1% YOY from flat in 4Q. 
Furthermore, inventory levels remain elevated relative to sales trends, with EMEA 
and APLA posting their fourth and fifth consecutive quarters of negative sales to 
inventory spreads, respectively. We similarly view the removal of regional SSS 
disclosures as an incremental negative. 
Nearly 2/3 of the Business Is in Transition & Could Take Longer to Recover 
For our part, while we acknowledge that FY27 guidance may establish a near-
term floor for estimates, we remain cautious on the longer-term path. Core Nike 
Sportswear represented roughly 50% of 1Q27 revenue, and when combined 
with Jordan, nearly two-thirds of the business is still undergoing portfolio 
repositioning. While management has largely stabilized the Performance 
segment, it took more than two years for that business to return to growth (~$16B 
in annual revenues, or ~34% of total). We suspect Sportswear and Jordan could 
require a longer recovery period, particularly given elevated competitive intensity

Lowering Estimates and PT; Hold 
With numerous headwinds and considerable uncertainty ahead, we are lowering 
our estimates across the board. Our FY28 and FY29 EPS estimates sit 17% and 
18% below Street expectations, respectively. We forecast revenue down LSD% in 
FY28 before returning to modest growth in FY29, as gains in Performance are 
offset by continued marketplace management actions within Jordan and 
Sportswear.  
Our $33 PT (prior $37) is premised on 26x our FY27E EPS, which stands four turns 
below NKE's three-year average blended forward P/E multiple to reflect the 
uncertain earnings outlook. Our PT decreases alongside our lower EPS estimate. 
Upside risks include a faster return to growth in China, less GPM compression than 
expected, and a NKE Direct revenue inflection. Downside risks include stalling 
growth in NA, signs of elevated wholesale inventory, and elasticity impacts from 
tariff-driven price increases.
