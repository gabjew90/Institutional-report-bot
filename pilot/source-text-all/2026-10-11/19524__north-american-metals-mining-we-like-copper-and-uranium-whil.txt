Rating Change 
   
 
 
Reduce ratings on certain gold producers (KGC & AGI) 
BofA Commodities' latest forecast update (see: Metals Strategist 30-Sept-26) points to 
continued resilience for copper, and uranium, and headwinds to gold (as well as other 
metals). BofA raised 2026E and long-term copper prices by 3% and 20%, respectively; 
while uranium prices are forecast to rise by over 40% to $130 per pound (/lb) in 2027E. 
Freeport McMoRan (FCX) is our preferred name in copper. Cameco (CCJ) is our preferred 
name in uranium. The team now sees gold prices falling 6% in Q4’26 vs. Q3’26. We 
reduce ratings on gold producers, Alamos Gold (AGI) and Kinross Gold (KGC) to Neutral 
and Underperform, respectively. Consider Buy-rated gold producers with material copper 
exposure: Barrick Mining (B), Eldorado Gold (EGO), and Newmont (NEM). Agnico Eagle 
Mines (AEM) is our top gold pick for superior execution, and leading long-term growth.  
Copper remains our preferred base metals exposure 
Copper remains our preferred base-metals exposure given supply constraints and long-
term electrification demand. BofA increased 2026E and long-term (2031E) copper 
forecasts by 3.0% and 20% to US$13,278 per tonne (/t), or $6.02/lb and $13,578/t, or 
$6.16/lb (12,000/t, or $5.44/lb in real terms), calling 2027E to $15,250/t, or $6.92/lb. We 
raise price objectives (POs) for key copper names: FCX, +9%; Hudbay Minerals (HBM), 
+8%; and Lundin Mining, (LUN), +7%. We continue to favour FCX given its leading 
copper leverage, unfolding operational turnaround, and solid copper production growth. 
Uranium remains structurally attractive, CCJ our top pick 
While we reduced our 2026E uranium price by 6.6% to US$97/lb, the 2027E forecast 
remains unchanged at US$130/lb (+45% vs. spot prices). The uranium market continues 
to benefit from supply discipline, and growing nuclear energy demand. Cameco (CCJ) is 
our top pick given its dual exposure to uranium prices and the nuclear build-out via its 
49% interest in Westinghouse. PO maintained at C$190 (US$133) per share. 
Gold to face near-term headwinds: rating downgrades 
While BofA modestly increased its 2026E forecasts for gold (+0.7%), rising US real rates 
and a strengthening USD are near-term headwinds that could persist into next year. As 
noted, we reduce our ratings on AGI and KGC to Neutral and Underperform, and lower 
our POs for AGI (-5%) to $36 (C$51) per share, and KGC (-23%) to $24.75 per share. 
Both companies are facing operational challenges. While we see upside to our POs, its 
less than for peers. Agnico Eagle Mines (AEM) remains our top precious metals for 2026. 
Upside surprise on US steel demand driving higher HRC 
U.S. hot-rolled coil (HRC) prices continue to rise on stronger demand (government 
programs (i.e. border wall), and datacenters). Lead times are extended and inventories 
tight. Yet, rising domestic supply and imports could pressure prices. We remain selective, 
favoring Commercial Metals (CMC) for its strong backlog and free cash flow inflection, 
and Nucor (NUE), for its diversified product portfolio and strong capital return potential.

 Latest views on commodities 
BofA Commodities updated its forecasts with a notable increase in 2026E and long-term 
(2031E) copper prices, as well as increases in 2026E prices for zinc, platinum group 
metals (PGMs) and silver, while there were reductions in 2026E prices for lead, and 
nickel. Gold prices are marked higher in 2026E by 0.7%, however, they are expected to 
decline by 6% in Q4’26 vs. Q3’26.  
Though hostilities in the Middle East have eased, the team believes it will take time for 
uncertainty to fully dissipate. In addition, the risk of tighter U.S. monetary policy and a 
stronger USD remains a headwind. As such, the backdrop for mined commodities is 
expected to remain challenging through the winter, with scope for a recovery thereafter. 
On a nominal 2026E average-price basis relative to spot, key 2026E commodity price 
increases include: U.S. hot-rolled coil steel (HRC) (+12.8%), palladium (+5.0%), zinc 
(+4.2%), platinum (+3.8%), met coal (+3.2%), and copper (+3.0%). By contrast, 2026E 
prices for uranium (-6.6%), nickel (-5.4%), and lead (-1.8%) were reduced.

Price objective (PO) changes 
Copper: higher commodity assumptions support valuation increases 
Supported by higher commodity assumptions, we increase POs for FCX (+8%), LUN 
(+7%), HBM (+7-8%). However, we reduce the POs for Ivanhoe Mines (IVN), by 7% and 
10%, and First Quantum Minerals (FM), by 11% and 12%.  
We increase FCX’s 2026E EV/EBITDA multiple to 11.0x (from 10.5x) and 2027E 
EV/EBITDA multiple to 10.5x (from 10.0x) as Grasberg turnaround continues to progress, 
reducing operational risk and improving confidence in the asset's long-term cash flow 
profile. Conversely, we lower our IVN target P/NAV multiple to 1.00x (from 1.15x), 
reflecting the execution and operational work that remains at both Kamoa-Kakula and 
Platreef before the assets can demonstrate sustained operating performance and fully 
realize their long-term value potential.  
For FM, we also update our Cobre Panamá modeling assumptions, pushing-out our 
assumption for run-rate production from the end of 2027 to mid-2028 and adopting a 
more conservative ownership split of 60% to the Panamanian government and 40% to 
FM (from 46%/54% previously). We factor in approximately 5% of cost inflation for 
2027 vs. 2026 as part of our model updates.  
Precious metals: mixed PO changes reflect company-specific adjustments 
While BofA modestly increased its 2026E forecasts for gold (+0.7%), silver (+1.0%) and 
platinum (+3.8%), PO changes across our precious-metals coverage were driven largely 
by company-specific considerations, and our decision to factor-in relevant cost inflation 
into our 2027 estimates.   
In term of valuation updates, we increase IAMGOLD's (IAG’s) target P/NAV multiple to 
1.35x (from 1.25x) as the company continues to de-risk its portfolio through greater 
exposure to Canadian operations and reduced reliance on West African jurisdictions; and

Uranium: lower near-term forecasts, unchanged long-term outlook 
While BofA Commodities reduced its 2026E uranium forecast by 6.6% to US$97.4/lb, 
the 2027E forecast remains unchanged at US$129.8/lb, reflecting continued confidence 
in the long-term uranium outlook. BofA continues to see support from growing nuclear 
energy demand, and ongoing supply discipline.. Valuation multiples and POs remain 
unchanged across our uranium coverage, with Cameco (CCJ/CCO) maintaining its 
C$190/share (US$133/share) PO. Centrus Energy (LEU) was the only uranium-related 
name to see a PO reduction (-10%), to reflect the rising complexity of its substantial 
U.S. enrichment capacity build-out. We continue to view uranium as one of the most 
attractive long-term commodity themes. 
Steel: lifting HRC price forecasts & POs, remain selective on equities  
U.S. HRC prices continue to rise on stronger demand (government programs (i.e. border 
wall), and datacenters). Lead times are extended and inventories tight. Yet, rising 
domestic supply and imports could pressure prices. Domestic supply is set to increase 
following the restart of the 2.7mtpa Gary Works #14 blast furnace in September and the 
commissioning of Nucor’s 3mtpa West Virginia mill by year-end. Meanwhile, import

Downgrade Alamos Gold (AGI) to Neutral 
We downgrade Alamos Gold (AGI) to Neutral as we believe the near-term catalyst path is 
unsupportive. The key focus is Young-Davidson, where the June seismic event disrupted

discounted valuation, AGI offers limited downside protection, with 2027E FCF yield of 
just ~4% versus >10% for the sector. As a result, we expect the shares to remain range-
bound until greater clarity emerges with the February 2027 guidance update.

 Headwinds from Iran war 
Updates to price forecasts 
As the third quarter has come to an end, we are updating price forecasts. Looking into 
Exhibit 47, forecast increases for 2026 stick out for many raw materials. Why? We had 
reduced prices on expectations that the Iran war would put pressure on the mined 
commodities. This had to some extent happened, but prices have in the end been 
resilient, driven by a confluence of factors, including: 1) the global economy and also 
metals demand held up despite the energy shock, 2) tight supply has been a persistent 
issue for most raw materials 3) inventory levels are low and stockpiling initiatives, e.g. 
for copper, emptied the physical markets of metal units. 
That said, as long as the Iran war lingers we believe the upside to the base metals is 
limited, so we remain cautious into year end. At the same time though, we believe that 
falling aluminium, copper and nickel supply should give support to prices. Copper 
availability ex-US is also constrained by the popularity of warehouse financing deals, so 
we leave our bullish 2027 forecast unchanged. Meanwhile, we expect zinc to move into 
surplus and periodic exports from China should help ease the tightness.

The recent increase in oil prices, elevated inflation and a hawkish Fed are all near-term 
headwinds for the precious metals. While we keep our bullish gold forecast for 2027, 
the macro backdrop is concerning and a push higher of oil prices is the biggest near-
term risk. Indeed, the yellow metal could fall towards $3,750/oz in 4Q26. If oil rallied to 
$150/bbl, gold could average just $3,500/oz next year. With the loss of demand from 
solar panel manufacturers, silver will likely behave more like a precious metal and less 
like an industrial metal, so it should follow gold’s trajectory. As to the platinum-group 
metals, we have platinum in deficit, so see support, but palladium is set to be 
oversupplied on demand headwinds from auto manufacturers. 
In the ferrous and bulk commodities, European and US steel prices have rallied 
sharply, up to the point where lower cost producers could see an incentive to increase 
shipments, even with tariffs. Meanwhile, the coking coal market has flipped into deficit 
on production headwinds in China and we expect deficits both this year and next, which 
should ultimately support prices just below $250/t. 
Finally, lithium rallied after China’s government curtailed production last year, but 
production increases ex-lepidolite have helped loosen the domestic market. While we 
don’t factor in a restart of CATL’s mining operations, we still expect a small surplus next 
year, making it hard for prices to rally again. As to cobalt, the government of the DRC 
has reduced oversupplies, and exports have fallen sharply. As a result, prices have traded 
well above recent lows; still, there is a risk that car manufacturers will continue to 
engineer out cobalt given the recent volatility and uncertainty, so another push higher in 
cobalt quotations may not be effective and is not our base case.

Weakness in electricity FAI looks to be temporary 
While the slowdown in grid spending was well flagged after the post-COVID spending 
spree, the magnitude of the deceleration is still surprising given that, as recently as 
January, State Grid Corporation of China outlined plans to invest approximately RMB 4 
trillion (US$550-575bn) during China's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030), an increase of 
about 40% versus 2021-2025. Looking through the plans, the main investment priorities 
were not necessarily generation capacity, but the transmission, distribution and ancillary 
services themselves: 
1. 
Ultra High Voltage (UHV) transmission: Expansion of west-to-east and north-to-
south power transmission corridors. Cross-provincial transmission capacity targeted 
to increase by more than 30% from end-2025 levels. 
2. 
Renewable integration: Connection of roughly 200 GW of new wind and solar 
capacity annually within State Grid's service territory. Support for desert and Gobi 
renewable power bases.  
3. 
Energy storage: Expansion of pumped hydro and battery storage to improve grid 
flexibility and renewable absorption. Under the broader decarbonisation blueprint, 
State Grid targets over 120 GW of pumped hydro in operation or construction by 
2030 and integration of 140 GW of new energy storage during 2026-2030.  
4. 
Distribution networks and smart grids: Upgrades to urban, rural and remote 
distribution networks. Greater deployment of digitalisation, AI and microgrid 
technologies. 
‘Six Networks’ to drive RMB25tn investment in 15th FYP 
Taking a step back, our colleagues in economics research have argued that China’s next 
investment cycle is unlikely to resemble previous infrastructure booms, which were 
driven by property development and local government borrowing. Instead, policymakers 
are pivoting toward the “Six Networks” strategy, encompassing energy, computing, 
communications, logistics, water systems and urban infrastructure (see China Viewpoint: 
Demystify China’s investment downturn: placing hope on ‘Six Networks’). Put differently, 
China’s definition of infrastructure is evolving toward sectors that combine strategic 
importance with genuine economic need. 
The direct consequence is a shift away from steel and cement-intensive investment 
toward more technology-driven capital expenditure. Investment is likely to become 
increasingly concentrated in a narrower group of high value-added and strategically 
important industries rather than being spread across entire supply chains. While this 
transition changes the composition of demand, it should remain supportive for copper 
and aluminium consumption given their critical role in the electrification of the economy. 
Digging deeper, our colleagues estimate that new investment under the Six Networks 
framework could reach RMB25tn over the next five years.

Agnico Eagle Mines (AEM) 
Our $233 per share price objective is based on Agnico Eagle trading at 2.00x times our 
estimated NAV (current net debt position plus a mining asset DCF utilizing a 5% WACC). 
Historically, North American gold producers have traded between 1 and 2 times NAV, 
with 1.75-2.0 times being accorded to the organically growth-oriented gold producers 
such as AEM. 
 
Risks of this price objective not being attained are commodity price weakness, the 
inability to secure financing for expansion or development projects, unforeseen 
operating problems, and political risk in the countries the company operates in. Upside 
risks are commodity price strength, better-than-forecast operating performance and 
favorable changes in the political environment in the regions where it operates. 
Alamos Gold (YAGI / AGI) 
Our price objective for Alamos Gold (AGI/YAGI) is US$36.00/C$51.00, based on the stock 
trading at 1.00x our estimated net asset value (NAV) and a 1.42 CADUSD FX rate. 
Historically, North American precious metal producers have traded between 1x and 3x 
NAV but more recently between 1x and 2x, with unhedged, growth-oriented producers 
occupying the upper end of the range and Junior and Mid-tier producers trading 
historically at a discount to the senior producers. 
 
Upside risks to our PO are a higher-than-forecast gold price, a lower-than-forecast 
CADUSD FX rate, exploration success at Island, Mulatos and Young-Davidson, better-
than-expected synergies at Magino, advantageous changes to the regulatory and/or tax 
regimes in the jurisdictions in which they operate, and the potential for AGI to be 
acquired at a premium valuation. 
 
Downside risks are a lower-than-forecast gold price and higher-than-forecast CADUSD 
FX rate, risks of the Argonaut transaction not receiving regulatory approval, operational 
challenges with the Magino mine, risks associated with undertaken M&A (such as with 
respect to integration of assets), and unforeseen operating and development issues at 
other assets.
