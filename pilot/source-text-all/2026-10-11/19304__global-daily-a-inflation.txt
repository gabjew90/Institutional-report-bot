Market comment 
US Treasury yields fell across the curve yesterday, with the 2-year yield declining 2.3bp from 
Monday’s close and the 10-year yield dropping 4.5bp to 5.26%. The move weighed on the USD, 
the third-worst-performing G10 currency of the day, but the DXY Index remains near its recent 
high of 102. Brent crude had a volatile session, falling almost $3 after the New York open as 
increased Russian crude flows eased price pressures, before retracing to $101/bbl as the Strait of 
Hormuz is “still a warzone,” according to US Energy Secretary Chris Wright. Meanwhile, US 
equities continued to rise, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 both breaking new highs. Across the 
Atlantic, 10-year French yields retraced their late-week gains to 4.75%. 
Meanwhile, investor pricing for ECB hikes has fallen since the beginning of October, from four 
hikes by the end of 2027 to 2.9 hikes—a decline of more than 100bp—implying an ECB deposit 
rate of 3.16%. Rabobank is forecasting one 25bp hike to 2.75% in December, before slowly easing 
policy in 2027 and 2028. 
Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s experiment to pull back on forward guidance has been evolving in 
interesting ways since his appearance at Jackson Hole and the most recent Fed meeting on 
September 16. Yesterday featured five Fed speakers. Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle 
Bowman spoke on regulation and mentioned that the Fed is considering raising bank asset 
thresholds in the near future. San Francisco’s Mary Daly (an alternate voting member) noted that 
the coinciding forces of AI, tariffs, and energy may indicate the need for additional hikes. Her 
comments were later echoed by Schmid, who also opined on the persistence of inflationary 
pressures and said that the Fed is fighting not only to control inflation but also to maintain its 
credibility. 
Warsh’s proposal to incorporate the deflationary effects of AI into the Fed’s forecasts also seems 
to have been delayed, as Fed members are currently more concerned with its short-term 
inflationary effects. These are not contradictory schools of thought on AI: the short-term costs of 
adoption may ultimately be priced out, while long-term productivity gains could drive disinflation 
within the next few years, according to the Fed’s Lisa Cook. For now, however, AI appears to be 
inflationary, particularly with respect to computer software and accessories, which have bucked 
the deflationary trend for the first time since 1998, with prices continuing to rise since the 
beginning of 2026. Rabobank is forecasting one more Fed hike at the December 2026 FOMC 
meeting, followed by a hold through 2027. 
In Canada, the separatist Parti Québécois won around 30 percent of the vote in Monday’s 
provincial election, gaining 59 of 127 seats—just shy of a majority but enough to form a minority 
government. Farther west, Alberta will vote on its own independence (or at least, the process to 
start considering independence) from Canada on October 19. 
Day ahead 
Today brings Australian consumer inflation expectations for October. 
In the US, the FOMC meeting minutes and the New York Fed’s 1-year inflation expectations are 
due, along with consumer credit data for August.
