SEPTEMBER 2026 MARKET COMMENTARY  |  1
The bond market came into sharp focus in September as yields marched higher around the world, with 
investors reacting to renewed pressure in energy prices and a shift toward tighter monetary policy. The 
Federal Open Market Committee raised its policy rate by 25 basis points to a range of 3.75%-4.00%—the 
central bank’s first hike since 2023—noting the move “removed a dose of accommodation.” In response, 
yields increased across the curve with the 10-year Treasury yield climbing 53 basis points for the month, 
while the 10-year German Bund and 10-year Japanese government bond yields also pushed higher, 
rising 25 and 13 basis points, respectively.
The higher-rate backdrop proved to be a headwind across fixed-income returns, driving a 2.6% decline 
in the Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate Bond Index, while long-duration indexes underperformed with the 
Bloomberg Long Treasury Index losing 5.0%. In credit, lower-quality securities also came under pressure, 
with option-adjusted spreads on the Bloomberg U.S. High Yield Index widening 50 basis points in 
September and returns falling 2.5% during the same period.
Despite the challenging rate environment, equities took the higher yields in their stride. The S&P 500 
Index fell a modest 0.3% with growth stocks remaining resilient as the Russell 1000 Growth Index added 
2.2%; its value counterpart lost 3.1%. Other rate-sensitive areas also underperformed, with the Russell 
2000 Index down 5.3% and the NAREIT Composite Index in the red with losses of 6.0% in September. 
Outside the U.S., equities were pressured by the tighter policy backdrop and a stronger U.S. dollar, with 
the MSCI EAFE Index falling 3.1%, and the MSCI Emerging Markets Index declining 0.6%.
As of 9/30/2026, Source: S&P, Russell, MSCI, JPM, Bloomberg, Factset

Commodities remained a bright spot as tensions between the U.S. and Iran persisted alongside concerns 
around supply disruptions; spot WTI crude oil gained 3.8%, while the broader Bloomberg Commodity 
Index added 0.6%.
We encourage investors to utilize the portfolio risk budget. We continue to recommend an overweight to 
U.S. stocks within public equity allocations, given the resilient macroeconomic backdrop and earnings 
strength across the U.S. market. With recent pressure in rates, we advocate investors hold high-quality, 
liquid assets, and suggest maintaining safe-haven fixed-income securities at strategic target levels to 
support portfolio liquidity.
