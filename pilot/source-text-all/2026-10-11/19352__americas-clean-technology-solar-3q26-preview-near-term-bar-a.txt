Heading into 3Q26 earnings, we believe investor expectations are relatively subdued, 
if not outright bearish, given ongoing macro headwinds (e.g. interest rates, cost 
inflation from Section 232, equipment bans) that create perceived risk to near-term 
trends in bookings and demand that, YTD, have largely been favorable – particularly 
in utility-scale solar. To that end, we think this low bar sets up well for risk-reward in 
larger-cap names like NXT and FSLR, where potential upside to more muted 
bookings expectations exists, in our view, and amidst a pullback that has both stocks 
trading near valuation lows on the year (at ~15x and ~8x FY2 P/E, respectively). 
Within smaller-caps, SHLS appears best positioned to beat expectations given 
healthy demand trends, margin recovery and growing new product/vertical leverage 
with the company’s plan to introduce new long-term financial targets as part of 3Q 
earnings also poised to provide an idiosyncratic catalyst this quarter. Within this 
report, we preview results by company, revise estimates where appropriate, and 
recap the key trends, installation activity, and investor sentiment shaping earnings 
season. 
3Q26 Earnings Preview 
 
 
Solar stock performance and investor sentiment 
Over the past three months, our solar coverage (down 29%) has underperformed the 
broader market (R2K down 6%). This underperformance was relatively broad based 
across our coverage group due to market challenges in both the resi and utility-scale 
markets. In particular, the resi market is being negatively impacted by rising interest 
rates and some weaker than expected demand trends. Separately, the utility-scale 
solar market is facing headwinds related to demand concerns from the recent 
Section 232 outcome on polysilicon, which is expected to raise the price of solar, the 
FCC ban on new foreign inverters, rising interest rates, and a general momentum 
unwind related to the AI/data center trade. Separately, in terms of positioning, days 
to cover short interest has decreased slightly (down 6%) across the solar group, 
which we attribute to the strong underperformance.

Key solar themes in 3Q26 
 
 
(+/–) Section 232 provides greater policy clarity, though focus shifts to potential 
demand implications particularly for utility-scale solar. The Section 232 outcome 
removed a key policy overhang for the U.S. solar supply chain, establishing a minimum 
import price of $0.38/W for modules and an additional 15% tariff on downstream 
polysilicon derivatives beginning December 4, 2026. In aggregate, we estimate the 
measures imply an all-in module price of approximately $0.44/W, excluding AD/CVD 
and other tariffs, compared with market pricing of approximately $0.30/W prior to the 
announcement. While this represents a meaningful increase in module pricing, we 
previously estimated the impact on total solar project capex at approximately 10%, 
given modules account for roughly 30% of project costs. We continue to expect strong 
power demand and solar’s relative cost competitiveness to support utility-scale 
deployments, with developers managing higher costs through procurement adjustments 
and contract repricing rather than broadly reducing activity. However, with the policy 
outcome now known, investor focus is shifting toward the actual downstream response, 
including whether developers are delaying projects, revisiting return thresholds, or 
altering procurement ahead of implementation. We believe the greatest potential 
exposure remains among projects with uncontracted imported-module supply or tighter 
return requirements, and company commentary on bookings, project timing, customer 
negotiations, and module procurement will be important in assessing whether the 
Section 232 impact remains manageable. 
(+/-) Midterm elections provides potential for policy and/or sentiment shifts. We 
anticipate growing attention on the upcoming midterm elections on November 3, 2026 
as it relates to policy support as well as general sentiment towards renewables, 
particularly solar. Importantly, as of 10/2, Polymarket odds currently favor Democrats to 
take control of the House by 90% as well as 63% to control the Senate, and there 
remains a focus on whether there could be revisions to existing policies such as 
extensions to investment tax credits (ITCs) and 45X manufacturing tax credits. We see 
potential for the extension of the 45X tax credits, which are set to start phasing down in 
2030, given the continued push to support domestic manufacturing capabilities, which 
we believe without these credits these new facilities would likely cease operations given 
lower operating costs in other regions of the world. Additionally, while there is discussion 
(link) of solar ITCs potentially getting extended, especially since these credits have been 
set to expire in the past but were subsequently extended, this appears more at risk. 
(+/–) Upstream pricing firmed in 3Q26 while downstream pricing fell. The sequential 
trend reversed this quarter. In 2Q26, polysilicon fell 28% and wafer fell 23% QoQ while 
modules were up at +4%. In 3Q26, polysilicon rose 3% and wafer rose 7%, while cell 
pricing declined 13% and module pricing declined 5%. Cost relief is no longer passing 
down the value chain, which leaves integrated manufacturers absorbing higher input 
costs against weaker realized ASPs. The YoY data supports this view, with polysilicon 
down 14% and wafer down 17% against cell up 34% and module up 26%, indicating 
downstream pricing still sits well above year ago levels. We view upstream firmness as a 
function of supply discipline rather than demand recovery, and would not read it as a 
signal of returning downstream pricing power absent inventory normalization and 
sustained curtailment.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
