Brazil is breaking out
Brazilian equities are surging, ETF inflows are accelerating and the election has added fuel to an
already powerful rally.
But the bigger story may be interest rates.
With exceptionally high real yields and a potentially accelerating easing cycle, the upside could extend
well beyond the headline index.
The question is no longer whether Brazil has momentum. It's how best to trade it.
The breakout
EWZ has broken out of a massive trading range and continues to push higher, reaching closing levels
not seen in years. The short-term setup is difficult to assess after such a strong move, but the breakout
is significant and momentum remains firmly to the upside.

The ultimate rates trade
As we pointed out earlier this week, Brazil has the highest real rates in the world.
It also has the strongest negative correlation between equities and local 5-year yields across emerging
markets.
That is a powerful combination.
If the expected easing cycle accelerates, Brazilian equities could have considerably more upside than
the recent rally suggests.
GS

Three times the upside
GS highlights the extraordinary sensitivity of Brazilian domestic equities to falling rates.
Across previous easing cycles, its rate-sensitive basket delivered a median return of 39%, compared
with just 15% for the Ibovespa.
The broader domestic basket returned a median 25%.
The message is clear: when Brazilian rates fall, the biggest opportunities have historically been found
beyond the headline index.
TME/GS
The re-rating trade
Brazilian equity valuations have historically moved closely with domestic rates.
As yields fall, multiples expand.
GS highlights that the rate-sensitive basket has already responded to the latest decline in yields, but
valuations could have further room to re-rate if the easing cycle continues.
The key is that lower rates could support equities through both improving domestic conditions and
higher valuation multiples.
GS
Not all Brazil exposure is created equal
GS points out that roughly one-third of the major Brazilian equity indices and ETFs is tied to
commodities. That means global commodity prices and other macro factors can overwhelm the
domestic rates story.
If the thesis is specifically Brazilian rates fall and domestic growth recovers, GS argues that rate-
sensitive equities and domestically oriented baskets may offer more targeted exposure than the
headline index.
Use options
EWZ implied volatility has fallen sharply following the first event, but we see limited room for further
compression over the coming weeks. Rather than chasing the rally outright, we prefer call spreads to
position for additional upside while keeping risk defined.
