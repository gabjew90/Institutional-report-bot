Implications & Trade View
FY1Q27 credit implicationS: Our core cautious thesis remains intact: Oracle is still early in 
a capital-intensive buildout. Positives include completion of the $20bn ATM, ongoing 
capacity execution and encouraging renewal trends, alongside further evidence that new 
RPO is being structured through prepayments, BYOH and similar arrangements requiring 
little incremental Oracle cash. However, any use of prepayment cash to bridge spend on 
existing backlog bears monitoring, and even assuming continued prepayment benefits, we 
still estimate ~$45bn of bond funding needs through FY28, with gross adjusted leverage 
remaining above Moody’s downgrade threshold. Meanwhile, Oracle appears increasingly 
locked into both the capex and revenue-per-watt economics of much of the build despite a 
higher YTD cost of capital.
Trade view: We reiterate our prior view and spread targets. Broader AI/IG supply 
indigestion drove much of the QTD widening across ORCL and peers, which we view for 
ORCL as more macro than idiosyncratic. While that could argue for adjusting our targets 
wider, we think the market’s enthusiasm around customer prepayments – and our 
awareness of their role as a substitute for the creative debt-like chip financing we had 
previously pencilled in – provides an offset. We therefore maintain our ~200bp/~250bp 
targets for the 35s/55s (with 36s/56s 10bp wide), still implying modest widening, and 
would use the post-print tightening to lighten exposure. The recent CHTR new issue, now 
trading around ~220bp/~250bp for the new 36s/56s, provides a useful reference for 
where large lower-BBB capital structures can clear. We also reiterate ~200bp or slightly 
wider as a reasonable 5Y CDS level, with additional risk from construction-related hedge 
demand or greater bondholder hedging on ratings concerns, though we acknowledge CDS 
remains in something of a no-man’s-land from a comps perspective (see Exhibit 4  later).
Key Developments
FY1Q featured solid operating execution, but capex a notable surprise: Revenue 
reached $19.3bn (+30% YoY) and cloud revenue $11.6bn (+61%  CC), both modestly ahead 
of expectations. Management outlined at least $90bn of FY27 revenue and guided 
FY2Q27 growth to +30-34% CC versus ~31% expects. Cash capex, however, was $28.5bn 
versus expectations closer to ~$20bn, while FCF deteriorated sequentially to roughly -
$5bn despite customer prepayments. Management reiterated FY27 gross capex of $90-
95bn and net cash capex of not more than $70bn.
$20bn ATM removes overhang, but was previously announced: We had hoped to see at 
least one-third of the program completed this quarter, making full completion a positive 
surprise. However, the ATM was announced in February as part of Oracle's broader 
financing plan to aid pricing of a large bond issuance, so its completion removes an 
execution overhang rather than providing new funding relief. We also continue to believe 
additional equity issuance beyond the ATM may ultimately be needed to support the 
medium-term funding plan, consistent with our prior work.

New RPO is becoming a funding bridge: RPO increased another ~$26bn sequentially on a 
net basis, with management saying the vast majority of new contracts use prepayments, 
BYOH or similar structures requiring no incremental Oracle cash. Because this new RPO 
may not drive capex or revenue until FY28+, upfront customer cash may arrive well before 
the associated spend (and does not appear to be held in restricted cash based on current 
disclosures). Put simply, Customer B’s prepayment today could help fund GPUs for 
Customer A’s previously booked RPO as related shells are delivered over the next year or 
so. That temporarily improves liquidity for the existing backlog, although Oracle has not 
disclosed direct matching of these cash flows. The trade-off is that capital needs are 
deferred –  not eliminated – until cash flow inflects; Oracle may still need to fund 
Customer B’s chips later, creating a rollover-like sensitivity if  legacy RPO counterparty risk 
rises and/or if future prepayments slow. We think this dynamic supports an argument for 
deferred revenue to at least be qualitatively assessed as debt. That said, the FY27 guide 
implies only another ~$10-15bn of prepayments after FY1Q, likely on a lumpy cadence, so 
this neither fully solves the near-term funding need (acting more as a substitute for our 
previously-hypothesized creative chip financing plug in the overall funding toolkit) nor 
creates rollover risk across the full capex base. Also we plan to watch the restricted cash 
balance for any evidence of ring-fencing, and also the total cash balance for any softer 
company-imposed earmarking.

Encouraging pricing and renewal trends, yet competition intensifying: Management 
indicated  all GPU capacity up for renewal in FY1Q was renewed or resold at a ~20% 
premium  to prior contracts,  alongside broader GPU utilization at 97.9% in the quarter. 
Those are constructive signals, though we would not extrapolate the 20% premium across 
Oracle's largely contracted backlog. Specifically, revenue-per-watt upside has become a 
bigger focus across the space, particularly following SPCX contract disclosures and 
neocloud commentary. For Oracle, however, much of the revenue tied to legacy RPO 
appears locked in under multi-year contracts, likely limiting near-term  upside even as spot 
and renewal pricing improve. At the same time, competition for leased compute is 
broadening, as recent commentary and actions from SpaceX and Meta suggest (and as 
S&P called out in its July downgrade note). Greater NVIDIA ecosystem support could 
benefit Oracle, but may also make it easier for unrated and HY neoclouds to access capital 
and capacity, further increasing competitive pressure.
Margin framework may hold, but the hurdle rate has moved: On the June call, 
management said that where future input costs are uncertain, contracts can include 
floating-price mechanisms designed to pass through higher costs and protect margins. On 
the September call, management reiterated that higher component costs should not 
change its prior gross-margin framework, while still expecting FY27 gross margins to step 
down as infrastructure ramps. Separately, we call out Oracle’s cost of capital has risen 
materially since the fall, both idiosyncratic- and rate-driven, potentially reducing returns 
versus those originally underwritten even if operating economics remain intact.
10-Q and Analyst Day up next: The 10-Q, expected tomorrow, should provide updated 
disclosure on uncommenced leases, lease capitalization, unpaid capex, and other 
infrastructure obligations. At the late-October Analyst Day, we will focus on capex for 
FY28 and beyond, Oracle- versus externally-funded growth,  revenue-per-watt economics, 
contract duration/repricing, long-term gross margins, and the overall build-out size vs. the 
prior 10GW guide. Management also declined to provide a timeline for consolidated FCF 
turning positive, leaving that a key watch item; our prior work contemplated negative FCF 
through FY28 before a FY29 inflection.
Broader credit backdrop a consideration: IG supply is up sharply YTD, and AI summer 
spread widening affected even higher-quality issuers despite debt market access. We 
remain early in the AI financing cycle, and Oracle's potential cash funding needs leave its 
bonds exposed to both broader IG supply pressure and a growing pool of AI infrastructure 
issuers, demonstrated by SPCX's emergence this summer.
