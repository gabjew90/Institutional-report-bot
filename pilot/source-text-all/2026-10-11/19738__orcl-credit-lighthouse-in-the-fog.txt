ORCL Credit: Lighthouse in the 
Fog
The site in question: The Wisconsin "Lighthouse" project is one of two Vantage sites 
under development to be leased by ORCL. Contracted in September 2025, this 
campus is planned to comprise of four data center buildings totaling ~902MW 
critical IT load.
What's new: An infrastructure analytics firm stated in a release that there is a 
material risk that meaningful load is not available until mid-2028 for this site. That 
contrasts with ORCL's June guidance for initial delivery beginning in CY2H27 and its 
September call characterization of the project as "very much on track and going 
well" (and updates in a blog), although the timing remains broadly consistent with 
Vantage's stated 2028 full-campus completion target. The third-party report brings 
renewed attention to an already-public PSC process. We are responding  given 1) 
the complexity of tracking these large ORCL-tied sites and the broader investor 
focus now re-emerging around them, and 2) additional thoughts to share on the 
ORCL credit implications of hypothetical delays at Lighthouse in WI, Jupiter in 
NM, or elsewhere.
See our initial response regarding the Jupiter news last week: TMT Credit, Clean Tech 
Energy, Corporate/Securitized Credit Strategy: Project Jupiter Drifts Off Orbit – The 
Gravity of Force Majeure (25 Sep 2026)
Considerations for ORCL credit for any hypothetical project delay: We would be 
careful to not just view slower potential delivery, in the instance of any site, solely 
through the lens of near-term capex relief. While a possible delay could push 
associated GPU and infrastructure spending to the right, which could be helpful 
from a technical supply lens, we share a few offsetting considerations:
1. Revenue and cash-flow realization would be deferred, extending the period 
before confirmation that ORCL's substantial investment and contracted 
backlog are translating well into meaningful financial results.
2. Delays could make the eventual balance-sheet and cash-flow impact more 
lumpy. If multiple halls are construction-ready by the time power is secured, 
ORCL could face more concentration  in GPU capex (i.e., cash needs) and 
lease liabilities (i.e., balance-sheet obligations).
3. Just as there is limited visibility into the underlying lease and financing 
documents, there is also limited visibility into the contractual terms 
supporting ORCL's RPO. It remains unclear whether customers have 
specified compute delivery timelines or what remedies may apply for delays.
4. We consider whether any execution-risk overhang may spread to customer 
behavior and in turn pressure prepayments, an important potential future 
funding source for ORCL, either by affecting new RPO signings or 
customers' willingness to re-contract.

Power access again the key issue here, not construction: Similar to Project Jupiter, 
shell construction appears to be progressing for Lighthouse ( Exhibit 1 ), but timing 
increasingly hinges on power availability. ORCL (or a subsidiary) appears to be the 
direct contracted party for power at both sites, though the solutions differ with 
Jupiter relying on on-site generation. Lighthouse requires Wisconsin PSC approval of 
ATC transmission infrastructure to serve the campus from the grid via We Energies, 
subsidiary of WEC. The PSC had been reviewing ATC's application, but ultimately 
revoked its completeness determination,  prompting a recent refiling on a new 
docket.  (Separately, WEC has noted that ORCL’s S&P downgrade triggered 
additional collateral, with the current rating already requiring full credit support.)

Refresher on Stargate sites, construction loans and considerations: Lighthouse 
and Shackelford are being funded through the ~$38bn financing first reported in 
September 2025, part of ~$65bn of shorter-dated loans for ORCL-tied sites outside 
the better-known $14bn RDMICH bond ( Exhibit 2 ). As with Jupiter, limited visibility 
into the financing and lease documents makes it difficult to assess how delay 
consequences, if any, would be allocated among developers, ORCL, lenders, and 
other counterparties. We continue to consider whether greater focus on project 
delays could spill into ORCL CDS hedging, and whether execution concerns – 
alongside higher rates and a growing DC construction debt pool – could complicate 
the eventual refinancing of Abilene debt into longer-dated paper as the project 
nears completion (6 of 8 buildings delivered).
