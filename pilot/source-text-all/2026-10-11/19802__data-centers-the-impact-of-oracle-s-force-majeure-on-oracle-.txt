The announcement earlier today (Link) that Oracle had sent a“force majeure” notice to the
builders of its Project Jupiter future datacenter in Doña Ana County New Mexico drove
ORCL down by ~4% - we believe this to be an overreaction. Bloom’s fuel cells are to power
this project, BE was down ~3% on the announcement. We see the equipment relationship
as staying intact, with nothing in the contract changing. Oracle remains committed to
Bloom’s fuel cells for their buildout.
Without seeing the terms of the contract we don’t know what the effect will be of the
“Force Majeure” note, but we believe this is about covering the contingencies and
keeping all options open including delaying payments for the datacenter or payments to
subcontractors but also to potentially give Oracle the chance to cancel the contract if they
so desire at any point up through, we believe, the contracted go-live date.
Data center tenants always have provisions in their contracts for remedies or cancellations
based on delivery dates, service level agreements, etc., and hyperscalers are the most
sophisticated tenants around. Based on our research and conversations with industry
experts (Link), we understand that the hyperscalers, including Oracle, have signed contracts
for more data center capacity (not hardware) than they need, intentionally buffering their
leased footprints to account for these delays and cancellations. This is particularly true as
they have expanded their landlord base beyond the standard, high-credibility developers.
Impact on Oracle: While initial reports were that the datacenter was expected to go-live
in Q4 CY26 (Link) expectations have been for Oracle’s FY27 or later for the initial go-live
of workloads at the facility with more being added over time. But more recently, we have
heard the planned go live was in FY28 and in 4 phases. Management in fact discussed on
earnings that “In terms of construction, data center is definitely on track… neither of these
sites will have any impact into our FY '27 revenue or earnings guidance.” In other words,
delays would not have an effect till late CY27 or early CY28 and only partially since capacity
is expected to ramp over time.
What is also interesting about the street’s reaction is that the stock’s valuation effectively
excludes any revenue / profits from Oracle’s OCI AI business and at times the stock is
effectively modeling in a negative value for this revenue (Link). Therefore, if these data
centers and the related revenue and costs were to disappear Oracle’s stock should go up.
Impact on Bloom: The ~2.5 GW associated with Project Jupiter is the largest deployment
in Bloom’s disclosed pipeline against its backlog. Commercially, nothing in the contract has
been canceled or repriced and Oracle’s acknowledgment of Project Jupiter remaining on
planned schedule suggests the company’s intent to proceed.
For the broader data center market: We think this serves as a reminder that all data
centers and data center contracts are not equal. It’s why within our Communications
Infrastructure team we continue to prefer top-tier developers (DLR, EQIX) and probability
weight the market pipeline with our builder credibility analysis.

DETAILS
The announcement earlier today (Link) that Oracle had sent a“force majeure” notice to the builders of its Project Jupiter future
datacenter in Doña Ana County New Mexico drove ORCL down by ~4% - we believe this to be an overreaction. It also negatively
impacted Bloom Energy, whose fuel cells are expected to power this project, BE was down ~3% on the announcement. We
believe both reactions as over-reactions.
As discussed below the Doña Ana County New Mexico (Project Jupiter) datacenter was not expected to be servicing customers
until late CY28, even though Phase 1, likely infrastructure and some building shells, were expected to be completed in CY27.
Our estimates have four facilities of 225MW each, with one being handed off to customers in late 2028 and the other three
rolling through 2029. While the recent government driven delays that drove the “force majeure” notice are unfortunately Oracle,
similar to other hyperscale and large scale compute providers, built delays into their multi-year plans. As discussed below we do
not believe that either the delays or the announcement of the “force majeure” notice impact our Oracle thesis.
On the Bloom Energy side we see the equipment relationship as staying intact, with nothing in the contract changing. Oracle
remains committed to Bloom’s fuel cells for their build out.
WHAT EXACTLY HAPPENED?
Project Jupiter is a four-building campus in Sunland Park, New Mexico - roughly 1,400 acres outside El Paso. The first of the
four buildings is estimated to energize at the end of 2027, with the next three rolling through 2028. OpenAI is the primary
Oracle customer for the site.
Power is key to deliver all the compute and this power was expected to be delivered via Bloom Energy fuel cells which would
be fueled with natural gas. The Green Chile Project pipeline (Energy Transfer's 17-mile line linking the Transwestern system
to the site), which would supply the natural gas has slipped from an August 2026 in-service date to February 2027, after
the New Mexico State Land Office repeatedly denied right-of-way permits across state trust land, following opposition from
local officials and environmental groups over climate and resource concerns. Bloom needs that gas supply in order to start
commissioning at the site.
WHAT DOES THIS BROADLY MEAN FOR DATACENTER CONSTRUCTION
Oracle put the datacenter builder on notice of a “force majeure” event. Without seeing the terms of the contract we simply don’t
know what the effect will be of the “force majeure” notice, but these types of clauses are common in a wide range of contracts
and in most cases they excuse a party from the contractual obligations when an even outside their control occurs. That said, we
believe this is all about covering the contingencies and keeping all options open including delaying payments for the datacenter
or payments to subcontractors, including on the power / cooling side, but also to potentially give Oracle the chance to cancel
the contract if they so desire at any point up through, we believe, the contracted go-live date. Again, Oracle is keeping their
options open and delaying payment if the facility misses its CY28 target to go-live.
It also, we believe, places the government agencies and politicians on notice. Recently we have seen an upswing in political
pressure to delay datacenters construction. While we are not discussing the merits or lack of merits of delaying these
datacenter constructions projections this puts everyone on notice that Oracle could in theory walk away from the datacenter
and thus jeopardize the taxes that would be raised, the jobs that would be created etc.
Data center tenants always have provisions in their contracts for remedies or cancellations based on delivery dates, service level
agreements, etc., and hyperscalers are the most sophisticated tenants around.
In addition, based on our research and conversations with industry experts (Link), we understand that the hyperscalers,
including Oracle, have signed contracts for more data center capacity (not hardware) than they need, intentionally buffering
their leased footprints to account for these delays and cancellations. This is particularly true as they have expanded their
landlord base beyond the standard, high-credibility developers.

IMPACT ON ORACLE
To understand what this could mean for Oracle we consider 5 items:
• Oracle has likely contracted for more datacenter capacity than they need to meet all their contractual obligations and can
add more contracted capacity if need be, especially to meet requirements that are 2 years away.
• Project Jupiter was not, we understand, expected to be meeting customer demands until Q4 CY28 and management has
repeatedly said it does not impact FY27 revenue or guidance,
• While initial reports were that the datacenter was expected to go-live in Q4 CY26 (Link) expectations have been
for Oracle’s FY27 or later for the initial go-live of workloads at the facility with more being added over time. In fact,
management discussed on earnings that “In terms of construction, data center is definitely on track… neither of these
sites will have any impact into our FY '27 revenue or earnings guidance.” In other words any delays would not have an
effect till late CY27 or early CY28 and only partially since capacity is expected to ramp over time.

have signed contracts for more data center capacity (not hardware) than they need, intentionally buffering their leased

beyond the standard, high-credibility developers.
• Oracle is noticing on “Force Majeure” to give the company, we believe, the option to not simply delay payments, but
potentially to give Oracle the option to walk away from the facility if they so desire if CY28 go-live is not meet. Note we
do not expect Oracle to walk away, rather this likely limits what they may have to pay to the datacenter contractor or even
subcontractors.
• The notice may also impact Oracle’s own contract with their New Mexico customer and since the event was “force
majeure” and outside of Oracle’s control it may open up flexibility in that contractual commitment.
• Lastly, Oracle’s stock has been beaten down from its highs as investors have had concerns about the Oracle OCI AI
datacenter contracts and specifically to single customer concentration. In other words the downside risk here is extremely
low and the potential that delays in payment could decrease the negative FCF and allow Oracle time to drive more cash from
the business and decrease the needs to raise additional capital.
