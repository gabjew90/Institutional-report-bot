Will rising French bond spreads disrupt the equity rally? 
 
French sovereign bond spreads have surged amid rising global bond yields and political noise: the spread of 
French relative to German 10-year bond yields – the OAT-Bund spread – has widened sharply over the past month, from 
around 85bps to 140bps, leaving it at the highest level since the 2011/12 Euro area crisis. The widening has come 
against the backdrop of a sharp increase in global bond yields, with the US 10-year bond yield rising by 55bps over the 
same period, Higher yields imply growing pressure on countries with weaker fiscal positions and lower growth, like 
France. Our rates strategists highlight increased supply concerns and incremental political noise as additional 
idiosyncratic factors driving France’s spread widening (see: European Viewpoint, Oct 1), which has been more extreme 
than that for other fiscally challenged European sovereigns, like Italy.  
How much higher can bond yields go? Our global economists argue that the path of least resistance for rates 
remains higher, given the growth boost from the AI investment surge (see: Global Economic Weekly, Oct 2). Our rates 
strategists agree, recommending a short position in US 2-year Treasury notes (see: Global Rates Weekly, Oct 2), as the 
Fed adjusts its policy to an economy that is no longer simply resilient in the face of exogenous shocks but is 
increasingly delivering boom-like growth data (see: European Equity Strategy, Oct 2). Our rates strategists argue that 
upward pressure on bond yields will likely stop only if: (a) rising rates start weighing on growth momentum; (b) higher 
borrowing costs lead to financial stress; or (c) there are clear signs of inflation cooling. While some signs of financial 
stress have emerged, in European sovereign debt markets and to some degree in the US credit market (where CCC-
rated spreads have risen to 1,200bps, the highest level since 2022), our rates strategists argue these, for now, are too 
localized and small-scale to stop the hawkish Fed re-pricing.  
How much further will French bond spreads widen? For as long as the global rates sell-off continues, it will likely 
put upward pressure on French sovereign spreads. This raises the question of the potential backstops that could help to 
alleviate this pressure. Our rates strategists note that this could include a reduction in the weighted-average maturity 
of issuance, which they argue is already happening to some degree, increased domestic and external purchases, and 
increased incentives to pass the budget swiftly. ECB action is another important potential backstop, likely first coming 
through a shift in communication before any forceful intervention (such as the use of the Transmission Protection 
Instrument). Overall, our rates strategists expect the OAT-Bund spread to remain in a range of 100-150bps, with a swift 
move towards the upper end of this range increasing the likelihood that backstops will be triggered, which should help 
to cap the upside (see: European Viewpoint, Oct 1). 
We remain negative on European equities after the 5% pull-back over the past two months: in absolute terms, 
European equities have remained largely unfazed by the increase in French political and fiscal risk. The Stoxx 600 has 
declined by 5% from its all-time high of 660 in August, but this is mostly due to a 50bps increase in the US 10-year real 
bond yield – i.e. the discount rate for global equities – over the same period, which is worth a 6.5% drag on the index. 
The European equity risk premium, on the other hand, which should reflect any deterioration in investors’ risk 
perceptions, has widened by only 10bps from its recent 20-year low, equivalent to a 1.5% drag. The remaining gap to 
the index’s actual performance is explained by continued upward revisions to EPS expectations. That is to say, while the 
rising rates pressure has led to rising risk premia in the vulnerable segments in credit and sovereign debt markets, the 
risk premium priced into equities has remained close to recent lows, supported by strong global growth and the 
earnings upgrades fuelled by the AI investment boom, with the only drag on the index coming from a rising risk-free

discount rate. What is more, our rates strategists’ base-case expectations of the French OAT spread staying in a 100-
150bp range, helped by the potential activation of backstops, would imply little scope for additional contagion from a 
widening OAT spread into the equity market.  
That said, we nonetheless remain negative on European equities, with a further projected 7% downside for the Stoxx 
600 to 580 by Q2. To a degree that we have rarely seen over the past two decades, the outlook for the market is 
dependent on a single variable, namely whether the extraordinary AI investment boom will prove sustainable. For as 
long as hyperscaler capex continues rising, it will prolong the current environment of strong growth and rising EPS. Yet, 
we see this foundation of the current market strength as fragile, given that: (a) all large-scale capex cycles over the past 
200 years (such as the railroad boom of the 1870s and the dot.com boom of the 1990s) have ultimately ended in a bust; 
(b) the main reason for these busts typically was excess competition in the area on which the investment was focused – 
and the competition level at the model layer of the AI ecosystem has risen to unexpectedly intense levels, given the 
presence of Chinese open-weight models, leading to token price deflation, which threatens lower-than-expected 
profitability for the frontier labs and the AI ecosystem as a whole; (c) the rise in bond yields in combination with the 
debt-financed nature of much AI investment spending means that the cost of financing in the AI space is rising sharply; 
(d) the risk of insufficient electricity supply to connect the new data centres and rising political opposition to the data 
centre build-out pose additional risks; (e) the current run-rate of AI capex, at $1tn per year, equivalent to 3% of US GDP, 
is already higher relative to the size of the economy than the average level reached during any previous investment 
cycle (though in individual years in past cycles, it has risen as high as 5-7% of GDP); and (f) the market is already priced 
close to perfection, with margin expectations at all-time highs and the equity risk premium close to a 20-year low, 
suggesting that even small disturbances to the supportive macro environment could force a bearish re-pricing of these 
rosy expectations. Any disappointment in the AI capex cycle will likely help to ease the upward pressure on bond yields, 
but for as long as the capex boom endures, it will likely continue to put upward pressure on rates. This, in turn, should 
cap the upside for the equities even in a scenario in which the supportive growth backdrop continues for longer than 
expected. 
We lift European equities relative to global equities from underweight to neutral – and French equities from 
marketweight to overweight: European equities have underperformed global equities by 4% over the past month – 
the sharpest bout of underperformance since early Q2. The OAT-Bund spread widening is likely to have been an 
exacerbating factor, offsetting the support to Europe’s relative performance from rising bond yields (given its relatively 
more value-oriented skew compared to global equities overall). Yet, European equity underperformance was in any case 
likely, given the degree of recent European growth underperformance compared to the US, with the US composite PMI 
new orders index jumping by 3 points in September to 58.2, the highest since 2018 (ex-Covid), clearly outpacing the 
0.8-point improvement seen in the Euro area. Following the recent underperformance, our projections imply a broadly 
balanced outlook for Europe’s relative performance over the coming months, leading us to lift our stance on European 
equities relative to global equities from underweight to neutral.  
Among European country indices, French equities have unsurprisingly suffered the most over the past month, with a 7% 
pullback in absolute terms and just over 3% underperformance versus the European market. The move brings French 
equity underperformance to over 20% since the spring of 2024, i.e. just ahead of that year’s snap parliamentary 
election. This has left France’s 12-month forward P/E relative to the market at -5%, the largest discount since late 
2016. Given this discount and with the French OAT spread close to the upper end of our rates strategists’ target range, 
we lift France from marketweight to overweight, making this one of the few cyclical overweights in our otherwise 
defensive portfolio.

We remain underweight European cyclicals versus defensives – and lower software from overweight to 
marketweight: European cyclicals have underperformed defensives by around 2.5% during the period of OAT-Bund 
spread widening over the past month, despite significant support from tech (with semis and tech hardware 
outperforming by 9% and 5%, respectively). This is due to a relatively high sensitivity to French dynamics within 
cyclicals. For example, consumer durables (i.e. luxury) have more than 50% of their market cap listed in France, far 
above the average of around 14% among other sectors. Meanwhile, autos, capital goods and construction all have highly 
negative correlation to OAT-Bund spreads (i.e., tend to underperform when spreads rise).  
European banks and diversified financials have been the other clear underperformers over the past month (-5% each), 
despite having below-average market-cap exposure to France, little historical correlation with OAT-Bund spreads 
(particularly in the case of banks, although less so for diversified financials) and in general tending to benefit from a 
higher rates environment. This likely reflects the sector’s particular sensitivity to concerns about Euro area sovereign 
stress, given that the sector was the focal point of the negative dynamics during the Euro area sovereign debt crisis 
“doom-loop” in which weaker growth led to deteriorating sovereign debt conditions, which means weaker bank balance 
sheets and declining credit growth, which fed back into weaker growth. We remain underweight banks, given the 
sector’s sensitivity to rising risk premia and our expectations that European equity risk premia have scope to rise from 
the current historically low levels. Among other cyclicals, we remain underweight capital goods and marketweight on 
autos and construction. We are overweight luxury, however, as this cyclical sector is, amid a global boom, already 
discounting a sharper growth slowdown than we expect in our base case. 
In the tech space, software, along with semis and tech hardware, has been a strong outperformer over the past month 
(+6%). We lower the sector from overweight to marketweight, given that, after outperforming by more than 30% from 
the decade-low in its price relative reached over the summer, it no longer offers meaningful upside relative to the 
trajectory implied by our macro projections. We remain underweight semis, given our concerns around potential 
disappointment for the AI infrastructure buildout.  
Defensive performance has been mixed over the past month, with food & beverages and telecoms underperforming 
marginally, while pharma has outperformed by 4%, helped by a sharp rally in the USD trade-weighted index, which tends 
to support the sector, given its high US revenue exposure. Our sector positioning remains pro-defensive, with our 
preferred overweights being food & beverages and pharma.
