 
 
8 October 2026 
◼ Floors in US–China relations and economy… upside ahead 
◼ Fiscal policy moving from drag to driver 
◼ RMB rally to continue 
It has been a busy few weeks for Chinese policymakers, with a US trip followed by stimulus 
announcements. Political action puts a floor under both US–China relations and the economy, 
while setting up improvement over the next six months. TSL continues to see a bottom in China 
macro and re-acceleration under way in activity. As for geopolitics, the prospect of a “grand 
bargain” is still in play. The RMB stands to benefit. We keep 2026 GDP forecast at 4.8%. 
The leaders of China and the United States have never met more often in a twelve-month 
period than in 2026. APEC in November and the G20 in December will bring the total number of 
bilateral interactions this year to an unprecedented four. The relationship between Trump and Xi 
is increasingly solid, but the difficulty is for their underlings to translate that improvement into 
concrete deliverables. 
Earlier this year we noted a “Beijing Accord” was possible, based on investment, trade, FX and 
perhaps even US Treasury holdings and Taiwan. Washington and Beijing are aligned on a 
surprising number of issues; on currencies, for instance, China wants a stronger RMB and the US 
a weaker dollar (details here). The Iran conflict has limited the policy bandwidth for negotiations, 
and the recently announced two-month delay (6 and 12 months were discussed) to tariff 
imposition is concerning. However, the US$30bn export tariff carve out, upcoming summits and 
comments by Treasury Secretary Bessent suggest that a longer truce is still possible.

Beef, beans, batteries and Boeings are the most visible components to any prospective 
agreement. Sensitive areas; FX, treasuries and Taiwan are being discussed but outcomes will 
have limited visibility, barring CNY which will continue to appreciate. Tracking Chinese battery and 
rare earth exports gives an indication of deal progress. The current slump in sales is a warning 
sign, as both sides search for leverage. 
Steady US–China trade relations are critical for re-accelerating economic activity. We expect 
exports to continue to provide strong support to growth. Meanwhile the missing aspect, domestic 
demand, is poised for revival.  
Policy began to flip in July and has now fully shifted. A speech by Minister of Finance Lan Fo’an 
is the clearest “go” signal for investment. Local governments had already begun to issue bonds 
(up 60% mom in August). With a still substantial share of the annual quota, ~45% remaining, 
funds for infrastructure are available. With the political intent clear, that money is entering the real 
economy and helping to stabilize domestic activity. The fiscal drag on growth this year has been 
enormous and will now start to flip positive. (Chart 2) 
The MoF is also helping on property, announcing mortgage rate subsidies late last month. 
First-home buyers will receive a 1 ppt annual subsidy, for up to 5 years, on loans up to Rmb1m. 
The policy applies to homes ≤120 sqm and ≤Rmb1.5m; the scheme started on 1 October and will 
initially last one year. Crucially the central government bears 90% of the cost of the subsidy. The 
policy will only slightly support home-buying (rates have never been a factor in the sales slump) 
and provide a modest boost for consumption (chart 3). More importantly, it signals the potential 
for greater easing for property. 
The PBoC has stepped up, cutting the PSL rate by 25bps and expanding the re-lending quota 
by RMB 200bn. Both moves will feed into the investment push. The flagship “six networks” 
infrastructure programme is heavily weighted to tech sectors that benefit from policy lending. 
Stimulus measures are encouraging but no game-changers. The objective is both cyclical (hit 
2026 GDP targets, as highlighted by Xi) and – more encouragingly – structural (property 
subsidies). There is potential for additional easing this quarter – front loading of bond issuance, 
for instance – if domestic demand fails to improve. US–China relations have also reached a 
stable floor; the downside is limited, with a “grand deal” still possible. A stronger RMB is the 
clearest market implication.
