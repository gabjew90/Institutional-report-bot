Thought of the day
US stocks retreated on Thursday after reaching record highs earlier this week, 
as concerns over persistent inflation and elevated government bond yields 
weighed on investor sentiment.
While US President Donald Trump said on Thursday that the US will not launch 
an attack on Iran before the midterm elections, and that the two sides are 
having "productive" talks, Brent crude oil remained above USD 103/bbl on 
Friday morning amid supply concerns. A developing hurricane in the Gulf 
of Mexico has led oil producers in the region to shut in more than 510,000 
barrels per day of crude output. With the region also home to several major 
refineries, still elevated oil prices are adding to inflation angst.
Meanwhile, strong auctions of both 10-year and 30-year US Treasury notes 
this week helped alleviate some of the upward pressure in Treasury yields, 
but reports that several major technology companies were seeking to raise 
billions of dollars in debt have kept them at elevated levels.
Markets may have to climb a renewed wall of worry to set fresh records, 
but waiting for these concerns to fade could mean missing further gains. 
We believe investors should focus less on finding a perfect entry point and 
more on maintaining exposure while managing concentration and timing 
risks through a disciplined portfolio approach.
A strong market can improve the way investors take risk. Strong 
performance need not prompt an all-or-nothing choice between chasing the 
rally and moving to cash. It can instead provide an opportunity to review 
allocations, trim positions that have become overly dominant, and redirect 
capital toward a wider range of sectors and regions. This allows investors to 
participate in further gains while reducing reliance on the narrow group of 
holdings that has delivered the strongest recent returns.

is to distinguish between liquidity needed for near-term spending and capital

Risk should be managed through portfolio design rather than 
market forecasts. Record highs can coexist with genuine risks, including 
elevated valuations, geopolitical disruption, and higher yields. Investors 
do not need to predict which concern will trigger the next pullback to 
prepare for it. Diversified core equity exposure, periodic rebalancing, and 
capital preservation strategies where appropriate can make it easier to 
remain invested while keeping potential drawdowns aligned with individual 
objectives and risk tolerance.
So, investors should not interpret persistent market worries as a sign that the 
rally is over. We believe the more durable approach is to remain positioned 
for long-term gains through a well-diversified portfolio. Investors willing and 
able to tolerate the associated risks can also consider broad commodities

return.
Caught our attention
Euro sell-off looks overdone to us. The euro caught a breather at the 
end of the week, with the EURUSD up 0.1% to 1.1225 on Friday morning 
at the time of writing. Still, the currency pair has fallen nearly 4% over the 
past seven weeks. Stronger-than-expected US economic data, higher global 
yields, concerns over European energy costs, and French fiscal risks have all 
contributed.
Our view: Our analysis suggests the magnitude of the currency move has 
exceeded what the underlying fundamentals alone would imply. Positioning 
and options indicators suggest that the US dollar has become increasingly 
overbought while the euro looks oversold. Additionally, European economic 
releases have generally been more resilient than expected, while broader 
energy conditions remain considerably more favorable than during the 
2021-22 energy crisis. Historically, EURUSD has displayed relatively limited 
sensitivity to widening French-German sovereign spreads, with such episodes 
tending to be relatively short-lived. While it does not mean EURUSD has 
necessarily reached its low, we think several negative factors are already 
reflected in positioning and pricing, and we would avoid chasing the US dollar 
higher from here. We see an attractive opportunity to re-enter long EURUSD 
positions and continue to expect a recovery toward 1.16 over the coming 
months.
Fed signals patience on hikes. Further interest-rate increases will likely 
be needed to bring inflation back toward the Federal Reserve’s 2% target, 
but policymakers have “some flexibility” over when to act, according to Fed 
Governor Christopher Waller. The hikes “do not need to come at consecutive 
meetings,” although they should be delivered within “an acceptable period 
of time,” he said. Investors have recently scaled back expectations for another 
increase at the Fed’s October meeting and are instead pricing the next move 
for December. Waller said he was “not greatly concerned” that tighter policy 
would cause a damaging slowdown, given evidence that economic activity is 
strengthening. In the coming week, investor attention will shift to the release 
of the September consumer price index release and the start of the US third

Our view: The US economic backdrop remains solid enough to allow the Fed 
to focus on ensuring price stability without worrying about an excessive hit 
to growth. Waller’s emphasis on timing flexibility is consistent with our base 
case that the Fed will raise rates again in December, while remaining patient 
as it assesses incoming data. While additional tightening could generate 
bouts of volatility, we believe the equity market can cope with moderately 
higher rates when they are accompanied by resilient economic activity rather 
than recessionary conditions. A gradual and well-communicated policy path 
should give companies and investors time to adjust. We therefore continue to 
see scope for equities to perform, particularly amid strong earnings growth.
AI demand. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co (TSMC) reported a 
51% rise in quarterly revenue on Thursday, ahead of its full earnings release 
scheduled next week. This came after Samsung reported an almost nine-fold 
jump in operating profit for the three months to September, while AI server 
assembler Hon Hai Precision Industry also beat its quarterly sales estimates. 
Earlier this week, AMD chief executive Lisa Su said she expects chip demand 
to remain sharply higher over the next few years, while Marvel raised its fiscal 
2028 revenue forecast and provided an upbeat long-term earnings outlook.
Our view: Without commenting on single securities, we think the latest 
set of data points to continued overall sector demand for AI compute 
and infrastructure, which should underpin further investments. We expect 
global AI-related capex to grow over 33% to USD 1.2tr in 2027, 
providing a powerful earnings tailwind for part of the AI ecosystem. We 
continue to recommend a diversified exposure to tech, favoring high-
quality semiconductor and hardware beneficiaries of AI spending, mega-cap 
platforms, and defensive tech names.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
