OpenAI (TY Sean) – Colour on Rev run rate: Anthropic reports revenue closer to gross customer spend
through cloud partners, with the partner share recorded as a cost, while OpenAI reports closer to net
revenue, mainly reflecting its own share. Some investors grossed up OpenAI’s figures to compare the two
companies on a like-for-like basis, producing estimates of c.$40bn in August and c.$70bn more recently,
whereas OpenAI’s own investor update used the more conservative method and reported c.$50bn at the
end of September. The distinction had been disclosed for some time, including in press reports, but it was
not universally understood or applied, which explains why the clarification prompted a material one-day
reaction. The most exposed listed names are Microsoft, OpenAI’s largest partner and investor; Oracle, a
key compute provider; Nvidia, its primary GPU supplier; and CoreWeave. Broadcom, AMD and other
capacity-related companies have secondary exposure.

Tesco (Buy / PT GBP5.55 from GBP5.15/ 15% Upside): The results reinforce our view on the structural
advantages scaled grocers possess, combining price and quality (Finest 1H UK sales +9%, +26.6% yo2y),
leveraging digital/online (online 1H UK sales +8.4%, Whoosh +37%), and utilising alternative revenue
streams (retail media and marketplace). Pairing this with a healthy balance sheet (1H27 net debt/EBITDA
2.0x, below the target range of 2.3x-2.8x) and market-leading ROCE (1H27 ROCE 15.2%) drives EBIT
growth (FY27E +5%) and returns to shareholders (buyback £950mn from £750mn) that elevate EPS
(FY27E +10.5%).
