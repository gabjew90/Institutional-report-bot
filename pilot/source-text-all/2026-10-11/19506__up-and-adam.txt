After touching a five-week low of ~$88/barrel earlier in the week, WTI crude rose to ~$91 yesterday, as Iran vowed to crack down on 
what it calls ‘smuggling’. This refers to the covert use of shuttle tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, which has enabled a partial recovery in 
Persian Gulf oil exports. Iran’s threat isn’t just rhetoric: its military continues periodic strikes on ships in the Gulf. Ultimately, the path to 
our WTI forecast of $65 in 2027 hinges on a US-Iran diplomatic off-ramp. On deck next week: IEA’s production data for September.

MSCI China is down ~20% vs. one year ago in USD terms, while the S&P 500 has gained ~16%. Looking beyond the last 12 months, the 
performance ratio between the two benchmarks has widened to an all-time high. China’s equity weakness reflects manufacturing 
overcapacity and persistent headwinds in the real estate sector. Despite China’s underperformance, we see better opportunities in 
Asia’s other emerging markets, notably South Korea and Taiwan, which benefit from higher tech weightings and less political risk.

A Not-So-Happy Anniversary Highlights The Importance Of Staying Invested
Source: FactSet
Chart of the Day: US and Chinese Equities Diverge
Today marks 19 years since the S&P 500's peak before the Great Financial Crisis. From that high on October 9, 2007, the index fell 57% 
before bottoming on March 9, 2009. Yet despite enduring two additional bear markets since then, driven by COVID and the 
Russia/Ukraine war, the S&P 500 has gained ~1,050% over the past 19 years, or 8.8% annualized, exceeding its long-term average return 
of 8.3% since 1950. This is a powerful reminder that staying invested through periods of volatility can pay off over the long run.
Oracle, Broadcom, and SpaceX are reportedly in early talks with lenders about potential jumbo financing deals exceeding $30B. So far 
this year, USD borrowing by hyperscalers and other tech-related companies has climbed to ~$300B, up from less than $20B in 2024. 
While these deals have not yet been finalized, the discussions highlight the vast capital demands of AI infrastructure buildouts. Despite 
the surge in borrowing, broader credit spreads remain well contained, although strains are beginning to emerge among select issuers.
