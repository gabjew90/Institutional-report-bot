Fed minutes
08 October 2026
Message in line with projections
The minutes from the September 15-16 meeting showed that all members supported the rate hike
and that most assessed that another increase would likely be appropriate by year-end. While this is
hawkish it is also fully in line with the signals in Fed members own rate projections that were released
at the meeting and less hawkish than market pricing at the meeting and thereafter. Financial
conditions were seen as supportive, but a few members wanted to prepare for the risks of market
dysfunction.
Key points
All members supported the rate hike, most believed another would be appropriate by year-
end
Divergent views on policy stance and reasons for tightening
Financial conditions were seen as supportive because of equities and credit spreads
A few wanted to prepare for risks for treasury market dysfunction
Increased concerns for inflation but strong growth and improved labour market also had an
impact on the decision
No surprises vs projections and somewhat outdated
 
Divergent views on policy stance and reasons for tightening
While all members supported the rate hike, members were divided about why a higher policy rate was
needed. Many saw it as an insurance against persistently high inflation, while a number thought it was
necessary given their outlook. Presumably these are the same members who dissented in favour of a rate
increase already at the July meeting. The minutes also reveal that members still have differing opinions
about the degree of restrictiveness of current policy. “Several” stated that they did not see the current
rate (before the September hike) as restrictive or only mildly restrictive. This suggests that other
members are less sure about the degree of restrictiveness, which is in line with the view that a higher
policy rate would be an insurance rather than motivated by their outlook.
Supportive financial conditions, a few wanted to prepare for Treasury market dysfunction
The higher bond yields were thought to have been driven by stronger economic data, increased
expectations for AI-related borrowing and geopolitics (i.e. energy prices). Interestingly, financial
conditions were still seen as supportive by many participants due to rising equity prices and narrow credit
spreads. At the same time, a few members expressed concerns about yields at the September meeting.

Thus, “regarding balance sheet policy, a few participants observed that Treasury markets had been
functioning smoothly but noted the importance of planning for market stress.” These members suggested
“strengthening the Federal Reserve’s strategy, communications, and tools for addressing market
dysfunction, should it occur, while limiting the Federal Reserve’s footprint in the Treasury market”.
Upside risks to inflation
The discussion showed that members had become more concerned about inflation, which is not surprising
given the decision to hike and comments from Fed members both before and after the decision. Inflation
had, according to minutes, remained elevated and members had not seen sufficient progress on lowering
inflation in recent months. Ongoing geopolitical developments, which had pushed up prices for crude oil
and refined fuel products, and surging AI-related investments were seen as contributing to inflation
pressures. The minutes also highlight elevated core services prices excluding rents and elevated core
goods prices, with the latter now pushed higher by the AI build-out while the impact of tariffs had faded.
The possibility of further tariff increases was seen as an upside risk. Some members thought companies
had become more successful in passing through higher costs to consumers. Some members thought risks
to inflation had become more skewed to the upside in recent months. Many believed that the longer
energy prices remained elevated, the larger the risks for spreading to other prices.
Larger than expected effect of new methods on core PCE, warnings about seasonal pattern
Members also discussed the effects of the upcoming new methods for PCE; these were expected by the
Fed staff to lower core PCE by 0.2%-points in August (less than the actual decline of nearly 0.4%-points).
A few participants “observed that the 3-month change measure of core PCE inflation showed a material
decline since the start of the year but cautioned that this measure is more volatile than the 12-month
change measure and has shown a strong tendency to understate inflation in the second half of the year
compared with the first half”. This could imply that members will hesitate to draw strong conclusions from
the downside surprise in August and the fact that the 3-month change is now in line with the target.
Strong growth and improved labour market also had an impact on the decision
In the discussion on monetary policy “participants generally emphasized that inflation remained elevated
while the labor market appeared to be near full employment, with some signs of strengthening, and that
economic activity was expanding at a solid pace. Almost all participants assessed that, while inflation risks
were tilted to the upside, risks to the labor market had diminished and were now broadly balanced.”
No surprises vs projections and somewhat outdated
All-in-all, we do not see minutes as surprising given the projections and other communication before and
after the September decision. The outlook for rates is more cautious than market pricing at the time of the
decision and after the meeting, but in line with the projections which indicated one or two more hikes.
Strong growth data and an improved labour market appear to have played a role for the decision; it was
not only about high inflation, and according to the minutes, future decision would depend on incoming
information. It is also interesting to note the emphasis that members put on risky asset prices (equities and
credit spreads) when judging financial conditions, which at least at the time of the meeting seemed to
have outweighed the effects from rising yields in the view of the Fed.
