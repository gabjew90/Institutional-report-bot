U.S. Markets Recap
US -0.22% | Nasdaq -0.22% | Dow -0.66%
Synopsis

US equities were lower Wednesday but ended just off best levels, following Tuesday's mostly higher finish that saw both the S&P 500 and
Nasdaq hit new all-time highs. Small caps came under outsized pressure again (Russell 2000 -1.31%) while defensive pockets held up
better. The AI trade was mixed, with semiconductors weaker but memory firmer after erasing earlier declines; software was down and Big
Tech mixed, with SPCX-US and META-US faring worst. Other laggards included precious metals, industrial metals, homebuilders, invest-
ment banks, banks, private equity, building materials, airlines, engineering and construction, and ag machinery. Outperformers included
energy (refiners), staples, toys (MAT-US), pharma, managed care, payments and telecom. Treasuries were mixed with curve steepening,
reversing earlier weakness, and short-end yields finished down 1-3 bp. The dollar index rose 0.4%, gold fell 1.1%, silver fell 2.1%, bitcoin
futures fell 2.7% and WTI crude settled down 1.3%, near session lows. The session found late support as the rate backdrop stabilized,
aided by lower crude and a well-received 10Y auction, although broader overhangs persist, including retrenchment in foreign Treasury
demand, refinancing walls and growing external funding needs for AI. The biggest single-stock focus was SpaceX (SPCX-US), reportedly

Macro

Rates stabilize after strong 10-year auction: Treasuries came under pressure earlier in the session with the renewed backup in global
bond yields, and stabilization in Wednesday afternoon trading was in focus. The AI capex crowding-out narrative was also in play earlier.
Yield stabilization initially seemed related to the oil pullback, while the strong $39B 10-year note auction was a more pronounced driver:
it stopped through by 1.7 bp, and Citi noted the 2.77 bid/cover ratio was in the 100th percentile of the last 12 auctions, with indirect
participation in the 91st percentile. Initial claims and wholesale inventories are on the calendar for Thursday, Fed's Waller is scheduled
to speak in Turkey, Treasury will sell $22B of 30-year bonds, and preliminary University of Michigan consumer sentiment for October caps
off the week on Friday.

September FOMC minutes flag lack of progress on inflation: The minutes held no major surprises. All 19 Fed officials supported the
September rate hike, and almost all participants viewed inflation risks as tilted to the upside and the labor market as broadly balanced.
A majority saw another hike as appropriate before year-end, consistent with the SEP projection for one additional 25 bp increase, and
officials generally said they had not seen sufficient progress on inflation in recent months. Many viewed financial conditions as supportive
of growth despite higher long-term Treasury yields, and some flagged the AI buildout as a potential medium-term inflation risk. The release
was seen as somewhat stale following softer recent inflation prints, notably August core PCE, and October hike odds remain little changed
at around 20%. The September NY Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations showed one-year inflation expectations up 0.3 pp to 3.9%, the
highest since May-23, with the three-year horizon up 0.1 pp to 3.3% and the five-year unchanged at 3.0%. Job-finding prospects rose 0.7
pp to 46.1% and job-loss expectations fell 0.3 pp to 13.5%, the lowest since Dec-24, while perceptions and expectations for households'
financial situations deteriorated and perceived credit access worsened.

Bond volatility spreads to riskier credit: There are more signs of sovereign bond volatility spilling over to riskier pockets of the corporate
credit market. JP Morgan noted the value of loans trading as deeply distressed (below 60 cents on the dollar) is at its highest since March
2020, and distressed leveraged loans below 80 cents on the dollar are up almost 90% in the past 12 months (Bloomberg). Borrowing
costs for junk bond issuers have risen to 17%, the highest since May-2020 (FT), with the CCC-Treasury spread recently hitting its highest
since the March 2023 banking crisis. The press continues to highlight equity market resilience to rising sovereign yields, reflecting the
outsized influence of AI hardware stocks considered more immune to higher rates, while narrow breadth exposes concentration risks if
the AI trade falters, given underperformance of rate-sensitive sectors (S&P Equal Weight down 3.4% over the past month versus the S&P
500's 1.3% gain and the Nasdaq's 4.1% rise).

Iran tanker attacks and Yemen fighting complicate diplomacy: Iran is stepping up attacks on tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz
(Bloomberg), following recent coverage of a rebound in Middle East crude volumes to pre-war levels via restored East-West pipeline flows
and Strait transit recovery. Reuters reported that tanker attacks last week reached the highest level of any week since the start of the
Iran war, and an Iranian IRGC official said 'illegal routes' in Hormuz will soon be blocked. Maritime security officials and shipping execu-
tives said it is too early to determine whether the attacks will deter vessels, and Brent traded back above $100/bl in Asia. On diplomacy,
VP Vance said Iran must make a meaningful reduction in enrichment capacity to satisfy US demands and confirmed US talks with Presi-
dent Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Araghchi, though he noted uncertainty over their decision-making authority; Secretary of State
Rubio said Iran passed up multiple opportunities for a nuclear pact, and Tehran insists the US meet its own demands before agreeing to
reopen Hormuz. The Atlantic reported the White House has asked the Pentagon to develop strike options against Iran that could be
exercised before the midterms. In Yemen, fighting appears to be intensifying after Saudi-backed government forces recaptured areas
near Bab el-Mandeb from the Houthis, who retaliated with missile strikes on Yemen's international airport (Reuters). The Trump admin-
istration is hesitant to join a Saudi-led offensive amid concerns over depleted US munitions stockpiles and regional escalation risks
(Bloomberg), as Saudi leadership braces for Houthi fighting to persist at least into 2027, and Syria is considering giving Saudi Arabia
military assistance (MEE).

G7 oil reserve release in focus as IEA meets, EU pushes back: The IEA governing board will reportedly meet informally to discuss the G7's
planned 100M-barrel crude and diesel reserve release, ahead of a formal Oct. 14-15 meeting to finalize the product mix and country
contributions (Reuters). EU countries are reportedly resisting releases beyond the 400M barrels agreed in March, raising questions over
how much of the latest plan is incremental supply (Bloomberg), and France suggested roughly half the release could consist of diesel.
UBS estimated a full 100M-barrel release spread evenly over four months would provide ~800K bpd of temporary supply, helping offset
reduced refined-product exports from the Middle East, Russia and China; the added release should help contain near-term price spikes
but will not resolve underlying tightness and could leave markets more vulnerable to future disruptions. Elsewhere in energy, a super-
tanker was chartered for $76M from the US Gulf Coast to China, a 10x increase over pre-war rates (CNBC); Hurricane Isaias is expected

EU Market Recap
Germany -1.3% | France -1.2% | UK -0.8%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished lower Wednesday, near worst levels, following a slight positive performance on Tuesday, though stocks
had closed well off best levels. Cyclicals such as banks, tech and miners led the selloff, while telecom, healthcare, retail and energy
outperformed. Banks were the worst performing sector as backing-up European bond yields and renewed concerns over France's fiscal
position weighed on financials, with HSBC (HSBA-GB) in the spotlight on reported plans for sweeping job cuts in an AI push. Technology
also lagged, easing from record highs as higher bond yields pressured growth stocks, and BE Semiconductor (BESI-NL) was a notable
decliner after UBS downgraded it to sell. Autos & Parts were under pressure amid mixed developments: reports that the EU is considering
a temporary cap on imports of Chinese hybrid vehicles were a positive takeaway, but downbeat updates from Porsche (P911-DE) and
Mercedes-Benz (MBG-DE) weighed, with Porsche outlining plans to restore margins to 10-15% through 9K job cuts, lower costs and
premium pricing for top models, and Mercedes-Benz Q3 car sales falling 8% y/y amid weakening China demand. Oil & Gas was a notable
gainer on higher crude prices, with Shell (SHEL-GB) up after a strong Q3 operational update, upstream output boosted by a one-month
ARX contribution, and Repsol (REP-ES) higher after its Q3 trading statement. Chemicals were another laggard. Retail was one of the top
performers on a strong update from Clas Ohlson (CLAS.B-SE), and Reckitt Benckiser (RKT-GB) rose after an upgrade to buy at Goldman
Sachs, with Health Care and Telecom also outperforming amid inflows into defensives out of cyclicals. The euro remained the big FX story,
with the dollar index up 0.5%; gold fell 1.4%, silver fell 2.6%, Bitcoin futures fell 3.2% and WTI crude rose 0.1%. Eurozone yields stabilized
by late afternoon following a big backup through most of the day, and the France 10-year yield peaked at its highest level since 2002
before easing through the European afternoon session. Separately, tensions in Ukraine and Russia picked up, with both sides striking
each other's energy facilities.
Macro

French bond rout deepens, fuelling debate over ECB intervention: French debt crisis fears continued to rattle European bond markets
following Tuesday's brief reprieve, with France's 10-year yield rising 14bps to ~4.89% and the 2-year yield up ~6bps to 3.61%. The move
was driven by anxiety over the country's deteriorating public finances, economic slowdown and political uncertainty ahead of the 2027
presidential election, with protests this week and far-right candidate Le Pen's counter-budget proposal involving steep spending cuts
catching headlines. Le Pen presented a five-year fiscal path raising the net savings target from €125B to €140B by 2032 versus 2026,
promising a primary balance within 18 months, a deficit under 3% by 2030, debt down from 121% to 112% of GDP, a constitutional
'golden rule' by referendum and at least €30B of net tax cuts (Politico). ING argued the largest claimed savings are the least credible,
scoring a new VAT mechanism at €18B a year against a total VAT shortfall of only €9-15B, and noting that pension savings of €15B sit
against independent estimates that a lower retirement age alone costs €25-35B a year; OFCE economist Plane said ~€118B of €136B
sits 'outside the lines'. Finance Minister Lescure said the government is prepared to use special constitutional powers to circumvent
parliament and pass billions in spending cuts if 2027 budget talks stall, with red lines at a maximum 5% of GDP deficit and no growth-
damaging changes, and media reports say France is considering boosting issuance of shorter-term debt. Bank of France Governor Moulin,
also an ECB member, played down the urgency of ECB intervention, saying conditions were not met, though he called the situation for
French bonds complicated and serious (Bloomberg). Investment firms broadly see ECB intervention as unlikely and view the selloff as
primarily a French fiscal and political problem; some suggest a further ECB rate hike could be taken off the table, as tighter financial
conditions from higher French yields do some of the ECB's tightening work. DWS said that if stress worsens the ECB sequence could be
a pause in hikes, then greater flexibility around QT/reinvestment flows and then the TPI if contagion spreads, while Nuveen argued the
stress is still largely country-specific.

EU weighs hybrid-car import cap as markets price a harder China stance: Markets are pricing a tougher EU trade stance on China after
reports that the EU Commission is preparing safeguard measures to cap Chinese hybrid-car imports, most likely through a time-limited
tariff-rate quota (Bloomberg). Trade commissioner Sefcovic flies to Beijing for talks with Commerce Minister Wang Wentao (Guardian),
and the plan can still change with those talks. BEV imports from China already face steep anti-subsidy duties and growth has slowed,
while hybrids do not, so volume has shifted there: Chinese-made hybrids account for ~25%-30% of European hybrid sales and Chinese
brands took a record near-12% of all new-car sales in August. Brussels had asked Beijing to hold Chinese hybrids to ~15% of the EU
market (FT), and Beijing rejected a voluntary cap as a WTO violation. The press noted that if the cap sticks without a sharp Chinese
response it could encourage similar measures in other sectors, with chemicals already flagged by France and Germany; Beijing has used
brandy, pork and dairy as leverage and has been slow to approve rare-earth export licences.

German industrial output jumps 2% in August, most since March 2025: German industrial production rose 2.0% in August versus -1.2%
in July and 0.5% expected (DeStatis), the strongest monthly gain since March 2025, and output was 2.3% higher than August 2025, the
first positive year-over-year print since December 2025. Production from June to August was 0.4% above the prior three months
(Bloomberg). Construction drove the rebound, rising 9.3% on the month, and machinery and equipment production increased 5.3%;
excluding energy and construction, output still rose 0.6%, led by capital goods (+1.2%) and consumer goods (+0.6%), while intermediate
goods fell 0.2%, energy production slipped 0.4% and auto output dropped 5.4%, which the industry association partly attributed to factory
holidays clustered more heavily in August this year. Analysts suggest the figures reinforce signs of a cyclical recovery supported by public
infrastructure and defense spending, even after factory orders slumped more than 10% in August, though higher energy costs and the
Iran war remain risks. Manufacturers still hold a record backlog of about nine months of work, and leading institutes have more than
doubled their 2026 growth forecast to 1.3%.


UK house prices stall as mortgage rates reprice higher; Healey eyes bill rebate: British house prices were unchanged in September, Lloyds
said, weaker than the 0.1% monthly rise and 0.2% annual gain in Reuters' poll, after an August 0.3% fall that was the first annual decline
since 2023. The BoE has not moved rates since the Iran war began in late February, but lenders' funding costs have risen, markets price
over 90% chance of a 25 bp hike in November plus three further rises in 2027, and five-year fixed mortgage rates are back above 6% for
the first time in three years. Lloyds said new buyer enquiries were the strongest since February, but Stonebridge data show purchase
applications fell 18% in the third quarter, and transactions look more likely to pause than prices to reaccelerate with the Budget still
ahead. Chancellor Healey is preparing a Budget package of over £1B to cushion poorer households after forecasts that the January energy
price cap could rise by as much as £442 because of the Iran war, which could wipe out the earlier VAT cut on electricity (Guardian). The
most likely measure is a taxpayer-funded top-up of ~£100 to the existing £150 Warm Home Discount for households on certain benefits,
and Healey is expected to reject Energy Secretary Fahnbulleh's call to strip levies off bills altogether. With an extra £4.7B of defence
spending and a thinner buffer from higher borrowing costs, the package points to tax rises elsewhere.

Spain's November snap election seen unlikely to derail growth: Spanish markets largely shrugged off PM Sanchez's call for a 29-Nov
election after parliament rejected housing decrees. The People's Party leads the polls, so a change of government is seen as probable,
but Capital Economics says that should not alter the economic path; analysts expect Spain to keep outperforming and the debt ratio to
keep falling, bonds have not sold off like France or Italy, and the IBEX 35 is trading higher. JPMorgan reads polls as pointing to a PP-Vox
absolute majority with only a modest growth effect, as spending restraint, tax cuts and higher defence spending would be offset by a
tougher line on immigration that could slow labour-force growth, and Eurasia Group puts 70% probability on a centre-right majority (CNBC).
For banks, Citi said the announcement itself is not an earnings event (Expansion), though investors may watch the state's CABK-ES stake,
the bank levy and consolidation, as a PP government free of Catalan separatists could be seen as more open to deals after the failed
BBVA-ES bid for SAB-ES.

Synopsis

Asian equities ended lower Wednesday, falling almost everywhere although most boards were off their troughs by the close. The Kospi (-
1.98%) lost the most as SK Hynix fell sharply, while Singapore, Japan's two benchmarks and the Hang Seng also had noticeable declines.

are still higher week to date. The US dollar rallied, with the yen, AUD and NZD all weaker. Treasury yields were higher across tenors, JGBs
were mixed, and sovereign bond yields resumed their upward trajectories. Crude prices were higher, with Brent back above $100/bl,
precious metals were all lower, base metals were mixed and cryptocurrencies were lower. There was no significant newsflow to change

spreading through the economy. Governor Malhotra said policymakers chose to 'reinforce inflation credibility' before risks become en-

wage growth slowed to 1.5%, matching expectations, from a revised 2.0%. Overtime pay accelerated for a third straight month and total
hours worked rose for the first time in four months. JCER consensus looks for core CPI to pick up progressively to 3.1% in 1Q27, and Tokyo

measures.
