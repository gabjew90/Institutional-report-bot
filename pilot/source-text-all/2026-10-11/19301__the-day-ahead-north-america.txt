Market Monitor 
The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq ended lower as 
crude prices surged on a spike in Middle East 
tensions and a cut in US output that fed 
inflation and rate-hike fears, while 
semiconductor stocks slumped."The market's 
going sideways in anticipation of third-quarter 
earnings season, which begins next week, and 
then there's the ongoing conflict in Iran that's 
pushing oil prices higher," said Terry Sandven, 
chief equity strategist at US Bank Wealth 
Management in Minneapolis, Minnesota. 
"What's equally telling is that year-to-date, the 
path of least resistance for equities has been 
up." The S&P 500 lost 0.47% to end at 
7,765.36, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 
1.25% to 27,193.34. The Dow Jones 
Industrial Average inched up 0.10% to 
51,231.64. 
Treasuries advanced, with most yields across the curve falling for just the second time this week, after solid demand at a 30-
year bond auction suggested that investors remained willing buyers of long-dated government debt despite the recent selloff. 
Analysts said the strong 30-year auction was a big factor for the rally below the expected rate at the bid deadline, which meant 
investors bought the bond without demanding higher compensation. Jay Hatfield, chief executive officer at Infrastructure Capital 
Management, thinks the bond market has probably seen the peak in 10-year Treasury yields.  "The peak was probably the 
5.30% level, maybe even lower and we believe we have already seen peak pessimism about Fed rate increases," Hatfield said.  
Benchmark 10-year notes rose 12/32, yielding 5.2268%. Two-year notes edged up 1/32 with a yield of 4.7493%. Meanwhile, 
the Treasury Department sold $22 billion in 30-year bonds at a high yield of 5.618%. The bid-to-cover ratio was 2.54. 
The dollar retreated as a rise in euro zone bond yields stalled, while expectations for the path of interest rates from the Federal 
Reserve this year remained largely intact. "Most other currencies seem to be inversely correlated with their monetary policy 
expectations, but that was because everything was so dominated by the dollar and by the Fed, and that has continued over the 
last couple of weeks," said Christoph Schon, head of multi-asset class investment decision research at SimCorp in London. 
"The interesting bit is that now we also have this other factor affecting the euro, which is the political crisis in France and also in 
Spain now ... so this is kind of a brief period in which it's not about interest rate differentials or monetary policy, this is kind of a 
really specific euro crisis that in this case affects the euro." The euro was up 0.19% at $1.1216. Against the Japanese yen, the 
dollar declined 0.13% to 157.86 yen. The dollar index inched 0.14 % lower to 102.10. 
Oil prices jumped, then pared gains after President Donald Trump said the US will not launch an attack on Iran before midterm 
congressional elections in November. At their session highs, both contracts were up more than $5 a barrel, with Brent at its 
highest since September 29 on worries about potential imminent US strikes on Iran and a supply loss in the US Gulf of Mexico 
from Hurricane Isaias. Then, Trump said Washington was having productive discussions with Iran. The Tasnim news agency 
reported that Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Tehran is reviewing the US response to its proposal under which the Strait of 
Hormuz could be reopened within seven days. Brent crude futures were up 3.61% at $103.82 a barrel. US West Texas 
Intermediate futures rose 3.18% to $91.09 per barrel. 
Gold prices rose as investors weighed the Federal Reserve's uncertain interest-rate path against growing concerns over high 
levels of US debt. "The elevated yields seem to underscore a broadening worry about high levels of indebtedness. If debts are 
rising, then gold as the antithesis to fiat currencies and government assets is likely to be favored," said Nitesh Shah, commodity 
strategist at WisdomTree. "The minutes underscore the fact that there's no real predetermined path here, and that keeps the 
(gold) market a little bit more volatile," Shah added. Spot gold added 0.58% to $4134.47 per ounce. US gold futures edged up 
0.41% to $4157.80 per ounce.

TOP NEWS 
US freezes Microsoft, Adobe, Infosys green-card 
applications over alleged fraud 
The Trump administration froze some green-card filings 
by Microsoft, Adobe and major IT outsourcers and opened 
probes into nine elite universities, widening a visa 
crackdown it says targets fraud and American job losses. 
The twin actions mark the broadest strike yet against 
skilled-worker immigration programs, potentially blocking 
a common path to permanent residency for thousands of 
foreign tech workers while putting some of the country's 
most prominent research universities on notice. The 
moves come even as the administration cultivates closer 
ties with the same tech leaders whose companies are 
now in its crosshairs. "Our message to Microsoft is: You're 
a great American company, but you've got to hire great 
American workers," Vice President JD Vance said. Labor 
Secretary Keith Sonderling said the suspension would 
also cover Cognizant, Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, 
Wipro, HCL Technologies and Capgemini.  
 
US bank investors to focus on impact of higher rates, deals outlook in Q3 earnings 
Investors will scrutinize US quarterly bank earnings next week for signs that a recent spike in Treasury yields may curb 
dealmaking and lending growth and raise funding costs across the industry. Third-quarter earnings are expected to rise up to 
20% from last year for the largest banks, with investment banking and trading revenue significantly higher than in the previous 
period and no signs of deterioration in the credit portfolio. Higher interest rates slowed capital markets activity late in the quarter. 
Investors are focused on whether higher rates will also drive up deposit costs and weaken credit quality. Cheryl Pate, senior 
portfolio manager at Angel Oak Capital Advisors, thinks investors will look for guidance on credit growth and deposit costs. "We 
don't expect any big deposit flows with customers looking for higher yields or any drastic rise in deposit costs," Pate said.  
 
PepsiCo slashes forecast, deepens cost cuts as North America recovery drags 
PepsiCo warned that growth and margin recovery in its key North American market was taking longer than planned and said it 
would pursue additional cost cuts to offset sluggish demand for its snacks and beverages. Faced with high input costs, inflation 
hit to demand and the growing threat of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, the company is pursuing record productivity savings in its 
turnaround efforts launched after activist investor Elliott Investment Management took a roughly $4 billion stake a year ago. 
PepsiCo's third-quarter revenue exceeded market expectations, and the company adjusted its 2026 organic revenue forecast to 
about 3% from a prior view of 2% to 4% but flagged that improving growth and core operating margin in North America were 
taking longer than planned. "We expect North America's core operating margin performance to remain under pressure in the 
fourth quarter," said CFO Steve Schmitt. 
 
Devon sheds Eagle Ford assets for $4.2 billion to deepen Permian bet 
Devon Energy will sell its Eagle Ford assets to Crescent Energy for $4.2 billion, trimming its portfolio to sharpen focus on its 
core operations in the Permian Basin following the $58 billion merger with Coterra Energy. The Devon-Coterra deal helped 
create a large-cap producer with a top position in the Permian Basin, and activist investors had urged the board to pursue asset 
sales, improve capital allocation and revamp executive pay to boost shareholder value. "Selling a relatively mature asset into a 
strong commodity price environment improves our go-forward capital efficiency and allows us to accelerate share buybacks, 
strengthen our balance sheet and increase long-term value for shareholders," Devon Energy CEO Clay Gaspar said.

Starbucks has explored Chipotle takeover, FT reports 
Starbucks has explored a takeover of Chipotle Mexican Grill, the Financial Times reported, in a move that would reunite Chief 
Executive Brian Niccol with the burrito chain he led before joining the coffee giant two years ago. The potential deal comes as 
restaurant chains wrestle with uncertain demand from inflation-weary consumers and rising operating costs, prompting 
companies to look for new avenues of growth while facing pressure to improve profitability. Starbucks has worked with advisers 
in recent months on a takeover proposal for Chipotle, the FT reported, citing people familiar with the matter. Analysts said any 
acquisition would likely be a costly undertaking for Starbucks, which is still investing heavily in its turnaround efforts. "A deal 
could require heavy borrowing or issuing shares," said Lale Akoner, global market strategist at eToro. "Without a compelling 
financial case, investors may view the deal as an expensive distraction." 
 
Amazon launches first Android tablets, raising prices and incorporating Alexa 
Amazon unveiled a slate of higher-end tablets that for the first time feature its Alexa+ voice assistant and run on Google's 
Android operating system, a bet that consumers will pay more for premium devices after years of focusing on the budget end of 
the market. Known as Alexa Tablets, the line features several different versions with starting prices from $230 to $500; all are 
made with aluminum cases, a switch from Fire tablets’ plastic. The new tablets will cost as much as $550, a big bump from 
Amazon’s more recent top-of-the-line Fire HD 10 tablet which cost upwards of $180, though the company often discounted 
them. The product revamp marks a new strategy under devices chief Panos Panay to move upscale and away from the 
inexpensive gadgets Amazon sold at or near cost to hook customers into using Prime Video, Kindle, music apps and the main e-
commerce website. 
 
Venture Global breached LNG supply deal with Galp, tribunal rules 
Venture Global said on Thursday an arbitration tribunal found its Calcasieu Pass unit breached an LNG supply agreement with 
Portugal's Galp, marking the second setback for the firm over cargo deliveries from the project. BP is the only other company to 
have prevailed in arbitration proceedings against Venture Global, as the US exporter dealt with a string of disputes with long-
term LNG buyers. Venture Global faces potential damages of billions of dollars from the disputes. The company has argued the 
spot sales were permitted, but two arbitration panels have now disagreed. The amount of money Venture Global will have to pay 
to Galp will be determined in a separate damages hearing expected in 2027 or 2028, with any final award not exceeding $170 
million under the seller aggregate cap agreement, Venture Global said in its regulatory filing. 
 
Judge weighs CNN, MS NOW, Politico bid to keep White House access 
A federal judge heard arguments during a hearing in Washington over a bid by CNN, MS NOW and Politico to extend their 
protection against being banned from the White House by US President Donald Trump. US District Judge Timothy Kelly 
temporarily blocked the ban on September 24, finding it likely violated the outlets' right to fair treatment under the US 
Constitution's Fifth Amendment. An attorney for the outlets told Kelly that the bans would intimidate reporters and dissuade them 
from publishing stories that might reflect poorly on Trump. "Here we have the unusual situation where the president himself has 
said he wants to chill speech. He wants to coerce better coverage," attorney Theodore Boutrous told the judge. 
 
Alphabet's Waymo secures $5 billion term loan to accelerate expansion 
Alphabet's self-driving unit Waymo said it has closed a $5 billion term loan, marking the company's first debt financing as it looks 
to expand its autonomous ride-hailing service across the US and the world. PIMCO, Blackstone and Sixth Street participated as 
lead syndicated lenders, while Capital Group, Loomis Sayles and T. Rowe Price were significant lenders, Waymo said. Goldman 
Sachs served as the sole lead bookrunner, it added. The company closed a $16 billion equity raise earlier this year at a 
valuation of $126 billion.  
 
Apple names insider Steve Smith as new M&A chief, memo shows 
Apple appointed insider Steve Smith as head of mergers and acquisitions, while moving Adrian Perica to its services division, 
according to a memo seen by Reuters. Perica, a more than 17-year veteran of Apple's M&A and corporate development 
function, also took oversight of iCloud, Fitness+ and News, among other businesses, in 2023, the memo written by services 
chief Eddy Cue said. Perica will now dedicate his efforts entirely to the services leadership team and report to Cue, the memo 
said "We have big plans for services and I'm thrilled to be getting more of Adrian's time," Cue wrote in the memo Smith will 
report to Chief Financial Officer Kevan Parekh, the memo said

Insight and Analysis 
AI pricing lawsuit against McDonald's raises new antitrust questions 
A federal antitrust lawsuit against McDonald's this week claims the fast-food giant is using AI-powered tools to fix menu prices, 
marking the latest case to allege companies are relying on new technologies to collude and harm consumers. Plaintiffs have 
sued companies in industries ranging from hotels and real estate to health insurance, and now, hamburgers. The claims are 
pending in class action lawsuits around the country, with mixed outcomes so far. Courts are still grappling with how to apply 
decades-old antitrust laws to AI and other modern software tools that can help companies instantly share and adjust pricing 
decisions. 
 
REUTERS OPEN INTEREST-Five charts sounding the alarm for stretched US markets: McGeever 
US stocks and bonds currently appear highly stretched on several key measures, suggesting investors should tread with 
extreme caution. The big question is whether we may be at, or near, an inflection point. Wall Street's record-breaking highs 
mask historic levels of concentration and narrow breadth. Treasury yields are now so high that, depending on your perspective, 
they either represent a generational buying opportunity or are about to send the equity rally screeching into reverse. Meanwhile, 
the spiking “term premium” could slam both stocks and bonds. 
 
COLUMN-How AT&T is using AI to reduce its reliance on law firms 
Any law firm associates worried about what AI will do to the future of their work may not want to talk to AT&T General Counsel 
David McAtee. Over the course of this year, McAtee has stopped paying law firms to do many assignments traditionally done by 
junior lawyers, instead sending the work to a team of AT&T lawyers using artificial intelligence. All that document review 
associates used to do? Now done in-house. For many matters, the same is true for legal research, witness interviews and first 
drafts of documents, McAtee said.

FED COMMENTS 
Fed's Waller: More hikes needed, but there is 'flexibility' about the pace 
US Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said that additional rate hikes will likely be needed to lower inflation to the 
Fed's 2% target but added there was "flexibility" about the pace of increases and left the door open for a pause at the Fed's

LABOUR MARKET VIEW 
US jobless claims hover near 57-year low for 
fourth consecutive week 
The number of Americans filing new claims for 
unemployment benefits fell last week, pointing 
to continued labor market stability despite a 
sharp slowdown in job growth in September. 
Initial claims for state unemployment benefits 
slipped 2,000 to a seasonally adjusted 197,000 
for the week ended October 3, the Labor 
Department said. The four-week moving 
average of claims, viewed as a better gauge of 
labor market trends as it irons out week-to-week 
volatility, fell 2,500 to 198,000 last week, the 
lowest level since early October 2022. The 
claims report showed the number of people 
receiving unemployment benefits after an initial 
week of aid, a proxy for hiring, increased 17,000 
to a seasonally adjusted 1.716 million during the 
week ended September 26. The so-called 
continued claims had dropped to a 3-1/2-year 
low in the prior week.
