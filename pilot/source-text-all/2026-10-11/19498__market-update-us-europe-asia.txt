U.S. Markets Recap
US -0.47% | Nasdaq -1.25% | Dow +0.10%
Synopsis

US equities closed mostly lower Thursday, though breadth was positive: the equal-weight S&P outperformed the official index by over 100
bp, and the small-cap Russell 2000 (+0.03%) was also a relative outperformer. The AI trade led the downside as semis and memory sold
off sharply, and Big Tech was also mostly lower, with SPCX-US and NVDA-US faring worst. Underperformers included airlines, China tech,
E&Cs, building materials, machinery, networking/comms, IBs, pharma and life sciences. Staples and energy were the notable outper-
formers, while telecom, insurance, chemicals, homebuilders, retail, entertainment, payments and exchanges also rallied. Treasuries were
firmer across the curve, with yields down 5-6 bp at the long end. The dollar index was down 0.1%, gold was up 0.4%, silver ended down
1.4% and bitcoin futures were down 2%. WTI crude settled up 3.6%, off session highs after Trump said he will not attack Iran before the
midterms. The AI trade was pressured by an FT report that OpenAI recently told investors annualized revenue was approaching $50B in
September, below the $70B figure reported in the press; other AI themes were the high earnings bar, infrastructure buildout (AVGO-US
and ORCL-US following SPCX-US yesterday) and the ramp in personal agent competition at GOOGL-US. Rates once again reversed upward

Macro

AI trade pressured by OpenAI revenue headline and financing scrutiny: The AI trade was under further pressure on Thursday afternoon.
Initial weakness was partly chalked up to the high-bar dynamic around earnings, but the demand signal was dented by an FT report that
OpenAI told investors annualized revenue was approaching $50B at the end of September, $20B below the widely reported $70B figure;
the discrepancy is a function of methodology difference with Anthropic. Focus fell on the adverse headline timing amid a recent ramp in
infrastructure buildout funding scrutiny, frontier model price cuts, continued proliferation of open-weight models, increased enterprise
use of in-house AI tools, upcoming hyperscaler earnings and an Anthropic IPO expected in the middle of November. AI infrastructure
financing also drew attention: SpaceX is looking to raise $40B in a financing deal led by Apollo to purchase Nvidia chips, Broadcom is
arranging $50B+ for OpenAI's custom chip buildout (after already launching $60B of debt financing to help fund the Anthropic buildout),
and Oracle is in talks with Apollo and Goldman to arrange funding for a big chip purchase. The structural shift is a key theme given the
magnitude of the buildout and the lack of balance sheet capacity at the frontier labs, with traditional cloud providers first funding through
internal cash flows, then moving to debt markets, while private credit now plays a key role.

Iran and the Strait of Hormuz keep oil bid: Oil came off session highs after President Trump said he will not attack Iran before the 3-Nov
midterms, adding that talks with Tehran have been "productive" (AP). The comments followed Wednesday's report that Trump was weigh-
ing pre-election strikes, and the Iran-US backdrop has still turned more hawkish this week amid escalation across multiple regional fronts.
Axios reported that US officials said the Pentagon has instructed CENTCOM to conclude preparations for resumption of major combat
operations in Iran; an Israeli official said the chances of conflict before the midterms are not high but the odds of it happening after the
elections increase significantly, with a campaign involving massive bombing of energy, infrastructure and nuclear targets. Iran has
stepped up attacks on tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz, extending its reach deep inside the Persian Gulf for the first time in about
a month (Bloomberg); attacks last week were the highest since the war began and transits have fallen to a more than two-month low, with
attacks driving huge increases in freight costs. Separately, fighting in Yemen is intensifying after Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabian airports
resulted in fatalities, prompting a retaliatory assault by the Saudi-led coalition. Brent traded over $104/bl in Asian hours.

Fed commentary leans hawkish; claims remain subdued: St. Louis Fed's Musalem (voter) said additional monetary policy firming is re-
quired to return inflation to the 2% target and that rates should rise further in the next six to nine months, while keeping an open mind on
the upcoming October FOMC meeting. This followed Fed Governor Waller, who said additional rate hikes are likely needed but that the
Fed has some flexibility on the pace of tightening and that hikes do not have to come at consecutive meetings. Markets currently price an
18% chance of an October hike and 24 bp of hikes through year-end, a fresh post-September FOMC meeting low; Fed Fund Futures during
the Asian session indicated a 70% chance of the Fed hiking again in December. Treasury's $22B 30-year bond sale tailed 0.1 bp, though
sub-metrics were stronger, following a well-received 10-year note auction Wednesday. Initial claims came in at 197K vs 200K consensus
and a prior week's upwardly revised 199K (was 197K), now under 200K for a fourth straight week; the four-week moving average was
198K, down 2,500. Continuing claims came in at 1716K vs 1710K consensus and a prior 1699K (was 1701K). Wholesale inventories for
August came in a bit below consensus. The data align with the September FOMC minutes, where participants judged labor-market condi-
tions to be stable and close to maximum employment. NY Fed research said Trump tariffs caused significant inflation on everyday con-
sumer goods. Preliminary University of Michigan consumer sentiment for October is due Friday.

Busy week of M&A headlines continues: Monday saw $35B+ of deals announced, including Schneider's acquisition of PTC, CH Robinson's
purchase of RXO, Cenovus's deal for Athabasca Oil and Uber for ezCater. On Tuesday, McKesson and CD&R agreed to acquire Option Care
Health for $5.8B. The big development Thursday was an FT report that Starbucks has explored a takeover of Chipotle, which would rank
as the biggest ever deal in the restaurant industry and create a combined company with nearly $50B in sales last year. Semafor, which
reported last week that Chipotle had engaged bankers to fend off a potential takeover, noted that the theory of a tie-up between Starbucks
and Chipotle has continued to come up.

Higher rates weigh on small caps: Upward pressure on rates is an outsized headwind for small caps given exposure to floating and short-
term debt and refinancing, and the dynamic has played out in both lagging earnings revisions breadth and lagging performance. Morgan
Stanley noted that improving earnings revisions breadth is being led by the higher-quality S&P 500 (25%) versus the lower-quality Russell
2000 (7%). Goldman Sachs pointed out that the Russell 2000 underperformed the Nasdaq 100 for a fourth straight session on Wednesday
and 17 times in the last 20 sessions. BTIG noted the Russell 2000 has underperformed the Nasdaq 100 by 16% since late July, bringing
the ratio's 14-day RSI to 12.77, the 8th lowest on record. However, some firms have also discussed unwind potential on rate stabilization
given crowded Nasdaq 100 longs and Russell 2000 shorts.

EU Market Recap
Germany -1.2% | France -0.5% | UK -0.2%
Synopsis

European equities closed lower Thursday, off earlier highs in a fairly volatile session. Banks, healthcare, autos and construction were the
biggest decliners, while energy, media, utilities, food/beverage and financial services were the notable gainers. European government
bonds stabilized after the earlier backup, with yields broadly little changed: the French 10-year yield was up around 1bp, Italian and
Spanish 10-year yields were up around 1bp and 0.5 bp respectively, the German 10-year yield was broadly flat, and the UK 10-year was
up around 3bps. Attention in Europe remained on the recent backup in bond yields, with French yields the main focal point. Stabilization
on Wednesday was pinned on a media report that France is considering increasing issuance of shorter-term debt to underpin investor
demand. UK borrowing costs also hit a fresh 28-year high in the prior session, keying off global bond moves. Scrutiny on fiscal policy came
as IMF chief Georgieva called on governments across big economies to tighten belts and said governments could not rely on rapid eco-
nomic growth to lift the burden of debt. On the macro data front, the latest KPMG/REC UK jobs report showed firms seeking more staff
for the first time in two years, and the RICS survey saw the UK housing market weaken in September. Other data on the agenda included
German trade data and the BoE credit conditions survey. September ECB minutes showed the decision to hike rates was robust across
all of its scenarios and highlighted the importance in coming months of gauging the strength of indirect effects, while other ECB comments
focused on risks emanating from the energy price shock. BoE officials Pill and Greene emphasized the need to focus on keeping inflation
in check.
Macro

Sector moves: Banks were the weakest European sector, falling over 2% and hitting a more than three-month low as Eurozone bond yields
climbed back towards recent peaks, with peripheral debt spreads widening again. A combination of higher energy prices, renewed inflation
fears and rising sovereign yields raised concerns about growth and credit stress, while investors continued to sell debt issued by heavily
indebted countries such as France and Italy. Health Care was also one of the biggest laggards, with argenx (ARGX.BR-US) a standout drag
after discontinuing a Sjögren's disease trial following an analysis showing the treatment was unlikely to meet its primary endpoint;
Bavarian Nordic (BAVA-DK) gained after raising its FY26 revenue and EBITDA-margin guidance on stronger rabies-vaccine demand,
particularly in the US. Oil & Gas was the top-performing sector, and Utilities were boosted by higher energy prices, another huge round of
AI buildout financing and defensive inflows. Food & Beverage was also higher on a flight into defensives and upbeat corporate updates:
Südzucker (SZU-DE) gained after H1 operating EBITDA rose 60%, helped by sharply higher ethanol prices at CropEnergies and cost-cutting,
and Tesco (TSCO-GB) rose after reporting a 6.5% increase in H1 adjusted operating profit to £1.78B and raising the floor of its FY guidance
to £3.15B-£3.30B from £3.00B-£3.30B, with an 8% increase in online sales and a larger £950M buyback also supporting sentiment.

French fiscal and political risks intensify pressure on European bonds: French fiscal and political concerns remained the biggest drag on
European bond markets. Finance Minister Lescure signalled that France may favour shorter-term debt issuance as borrowing costs rise,
while insisting the government continues to find buyers for its bonds (Reuters, Bloomberg). Bank of France's Moulin (ECB member) argued
that geopolitical shocks are pushing up borrowing costs globally, potentially providing some cover for France's higher yields (Bloomberg).
Political pressure remains elevated, with PM Lecornu announcing the release of 10M barrels of strategic diesel reserves to curb fuel
prices and offering further support to farmers and the education sector as protests persist (Politico), while far right leader Le Pen is
seeking to reassert control over National Rally, sidelining protege Bardella after proposing a counter budget with big spending cuts
(Bloomberg). Debate over a direct ECB intervention in French bonds is heating up (Bloomberg), though investment firms broadly see
intervention as unlikely, viewing the selloff as primarily a French fiscal and political problem, and the Bank of France chief also denied
any need for ECB help. Some see a further ECB rate hike being taken off the table, with tighter financial conditions from higher French
yields doing some of the ECB's tightening work. DWS said that if stress worsens the ECB sequence could be pause hikes, then greater
flexibility around QT/reinvestment flows, then the TPI if contagion spreads; Nuveen argued the stress is still largely country-specific, with
limited evidence of contagion into wider European rates. The FT noted some investors are beginning to bottom-fish in Italy and corporate
bonds on the view that contagion fears are overdone, Reuters highlighted rotation toward German, Dutch and Swiss government bonds,
and Bloomberg noted that 38% of French high-grade corporate bonds now trade at lower yields than equivalent sovereign debt.

ECB minutes and BoE officials both stress inflation risk: September ECB minutes showed all members judged that risks to the inflation
outlook were tilted to the upside and supported chief economist Lane's proposal to raise all three rates by 25 bps. The minutes highlighted
that the energy shock was more persistent than previously anticipated, that the inflation outlook had deteriorated and that core inflation
is expected to remain above 2% for the entire projection horizon. Since the September meeting, rate hike expectations have been pared
back, with markets pricing ~30% chance of an October hike and ~50% for December; Lagarde and Lane have both cited a tightening in
financial conditions, and there are thoughts the ECB will want to avoid accelerating the backup in market rates, having hiked twice already
since the Iran conflict started. At the BoE, chief economist Pill stressed the importance of resolute policy against a backdrop of rising
bond yields (Reuters), and MPC member Greene said it was dangerous to rely on bond yields to do the job of taming inflation (Reuters).
Governor Bailey said monetary policy requires unwavering commitment to its policy aims, and Bailey, Lombardelli, Breeden and Ramsden
have warned the BoE may need to act if inflation pressures strengthen further. Market expectations for a November hike remain at ~90%
with a 50% chance of a follow-up hike in December.

German data: exports disappoint while industrial output jumps: German exports fell 0.8% m/m versus consensus for a 0.6% gain and a
prior 0.8% drop, while imports increased 0.9%, weaker than the 2.6% expected and following July's 5.5% drop revised from 5.7%. The
trade surplus narrowed to €19.5B versus €19B expected and a prior €21.1B revised from €21.3B (Destatis). Takeaways highlighted that
summer export weakness dented the resilience narrative, with low water levels on the Rhine possibly contributing. The setback followed

Asian Markets
Japan -1.42% | Hong Kong -1.43% | China -0.79%
Synopsis

Asian equities ended lower Thursday across the region and are now lower week to date. Singapore saw steep losses for a second consec-
utive day, South Korea and Taiwan also posted some steep declines, Shenzhen led losses in mainland China as trading resumed, and
Hong Kong stocks also fell. Australia, Japan and India were all lower. The MSCI Asia Pacific ex Japan index was down around 2% on the
day and down 0.7% for the week as Samsung Electronics (005930-KR) slid after results, Hong Kong's growth stocks underperformed and
Singapore's banks slipped for a second consecutive day following several negative broker notes. Japan stocks fell sharply, led by machin-
ery makers and trading houses on reignited inflation and borrowing cost worries, while Kioxia (285A-JP) was a bright spot on free float
ratio news. The US dollar was flat, the AUD was weaker with little movement elsewhere in currencies, Treasury yields were higher across
tenors while JGB yields were lower, and cryptocurrencies were down. Regional yields were either inching higher or hovering near multi-
year highs, with little regional newsflow to counter the greater trend. Australia's inflation expectations rose to their highest since June
and above the RBA's target by some distance. Japan and South Korea current account surpluses for August both rose as exports remained
robust. Japan Exchange Group (8697-JP) said Kioxia's (285A-JP) freefloat ratio would rise to 50% from 15% after a review. Samsung
Electronics (005930-KR) posted a near nine-fold increase in Q3 operating profit y/y, but it and revenue for the period fell short of expec-
tations. A consortium led by Kepco (015760-KR) has won the rights to maintain its Westinghouse stake post IPO, but the US government
will maintain a golden share. SK Hynix's (000660-KR) flash memory unit Solidigm (0W7WXX-E) has chosen three lead banks for its US-
based IPO next year that could value the group at around $10B. LG Energy (373220-KR) Q3 earnings beat estimates on ESS demand and
US incentives. TSM-US posted better-than-expected September revenue growth of nearly 55%, driving a top-line beat for Q3.
Macro

Samsung operating profit surges, AI chip demand drives memory sales: Samsung Electronics (005930-KR) Q3 preliminary operating profit
came in at KRW107.40T, up almost nine-fold from a year earlier but below the consensus of KRW109.181T. Revenue of KRW195.00T
also fell short of the consensus KRW204.050T. The company will release its full Q3 presentation on 29-Oct, which will feature a divisional
breakdown. According to Bloomberg consensus, the semiconductor unit was forecast to record operating profit of KRW110T, with HBM a
key driver amid estimates that shipments grew 50% q/q in Q3 and HBM4 expected to become a more significant contributor to revenue
going forward (Reuters). DRAM pricing tailwinds continue, with Counterpoint upgrading its Q3 price growth forecast to 10-20% from 5-
10% as customers pull orders forward, though volumes are likely constrained by limited inventory. The consumer electronics division is
seen impacted by tighter margins due to higher memory costs, and the foundry unit is seen recording another loss, reflecting high fixed
costs and low utilization rates.

BOJ regional report underlines corporate resilience: The BOJ Sakura Report for October showed seven out of nine regions left their eco-
nomic assessment unchanged, while the remaining two were upgraded marginally, matching the best outcome since Jan-25. The branch
managers meeting summary saw mostly positive corporate feedback in all key areas, with industrial production and exports primarily
driven by the AI infrastructure value chain. Most respondents noted headwinds from Middle East effects were easing, and domestic supply
chain disruptions caused by the Kumamoto earthquake were said to be temporary. Capex plans continue to see some cutbacks and delays
amid higher construction costs and labor shortages, though most maintained a strong willingness to invest, again citing AI infrastructure
demand and productivity enhancements such as digitization. Private consumption saw temporary drags from bad weather but demand
has maintained robustness on the whole, supported by the recovery in real incomes. Price-setting behavior indicated broad progress in
cost passthrough, with some firms increasing the frequency of price adjustments, while others observed an entrenched propensity to
save and have responded by limiting price hikes and focusing on low-price offerings.

Major index revamps under way in Topix and Hang Seng Tech: The TSE formally announced its second-stage Topix index overhaul, gradu-
ally phasing out some 40% of stocks to leave the constituents list at 986 (Nikkei). The reform process has been widely telegraphed and
companies need to meet liquidity requirements to remain in the index. International investors broadly see the move as constructive,
alleviating a key barrier from the sheer number of components in index replication, while noting there is still room for more consolidation
compared with the S&P 500 and STOXX 600 (Nikkei). Hang Seng Indexes also announced an expansion of the Hang Seng Tech Index to
50 names from 30, with an announcement coming 20-Nov and taking effect 7-Dec (Bloomberg). Selection criteria are based on market
value, turnover and sales growth, the candidate list expands from the main board to the Composite LargeCap and MidCap Index (The
Standard), and the requirement that candidates fit into five specific industries was removed. Individual stock weighting remains un-
changed at 8% for individual constituents and 4% for foreign companies (10% aggregate).

China and EU begin critical trade talks as Brussels mulls tougher measures: Chinese commerce minister Wang Wentao received EU trade
commissioner Maros Sefcovic in Beijing as Brussels set an October deadline to reset trade ties (SCMP). FT sources said Beijing has
rejected the EU's proposal to voluntarily limit exports of hybrid vehicles to the bloc to about 15%, compared with more than a third today,
or face retaliatory measures. The European Commission is now discussing temporary safeguard trade defense measures that could be
applied without evidence of dumping, aiming to agree a number Beijing can accept privately without retaliating. SCMP added that Brussels
hopes China will agree to moderate its exports in a single sector, in a so-called "proof of concept" arrangement that could be extended to
more sectors, by the end of Sefcovic's visit on Friday. Beijing is also seen as no longer rejecting outright that there are problems in its
trade relations with Europe, as it hopes to retain broader access to the European market amid growing economic reliance on exports. This
came after France and Germany jointly called for new measures to tackle market-distorting practices aimed at China (Bloomberg), while
China responded that it also has tools ready if trade tensions escalate (Bloomberg).
