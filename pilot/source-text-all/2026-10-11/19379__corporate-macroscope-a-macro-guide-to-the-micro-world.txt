Key takeaways
The defining feature of today’s macro environment is a rapidly intensifying 
competition for capital. Governments are borrowing more to fund defence, 
energy 
security 
and 
infrastructure, 
while 
companies 
are 
committing 
unprecedented sums to AI, data centres and digital capacity. This simultaneous 
demand has repriced capital across markets. Thirty-year government yields in 
Germany and Japan were close to zero as recently as 2022; today, rates are 
elevated, equity risk premia are compressed and financing is more expensive. 
For corporate leaders, the cost and availability of capital are again central to 
investment, funding and strategic decisions.
Financial conditions are tightening across the curve, and sovereign risks 
have risen. Ten-year yields remain high, but two-year rates have also sold off 
as markets reassess the timing and scale of monetary easing. In Europe, the 
German 10-year yield has risen about 35bp since early August, while the French 
yield has increased roughly 100bp to 4.9%. Sovereign spreads have widened 
materially and credit spreads are showing early signs of pressure. Both the level 
and speed of these moves matter: a rapid repricing can disrupt issuance 
windows, raise refinancing costs and complicate decisions on leverage, 
acquisitions and investment. We estimate each 10bp rise in 10-year German 
yields takes about 1% from STOXX Europe, and each 10bp rise in sovereign 
spreads also takes an additional 1% from index valuations, with Banks being the 
most sensitive to spread widening. 
Resilient growth is supporting profits. Euro-area activity has held up better 
than expected, earnings revisions remain positive and consensus forecasts 
about 22% year-on-year EPS growth in the second half of 2026, or 15% 
excluding Commodities. That said, EPS growth is likely to slow in 2027, mainly 
because Energy earnings growth will likely detract from index earnings rather 
than adding as it has done this year, but also because higher costs will become 
trickier for companies to digest. That said, we still expect 5% EPS growth in 
2027 and there are upside risks to this given strong structural tailwinds in 
Industrials, Financials and Technology. Growth is still supportive, but the hurdle 
rate has risen sharply. 
.
Focus chart: German 10Y Bund and EUR IG yields have risen and are now 
at or above the STOXX 600 dividend yield
Source: Datastream, FactSet, Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research
The AI cycle is creating a historic investment opportunity and an equally 
significant funding requirement. Capital expenditure among North American 
AA-rated issuers grew 65% year on year in the second quarter, the tenth 
consecutive quarter above 35%. AI-related borrowers account for about one-
quarter of US investment-grade gross supply and 44% of US convertible 
issuance, which has reached $135bn this year. The opportunity extends beyond 
technology into power, utilities, industrial equipment and infrastructure. 
Europe’s cash generation and healthy private-sector balance sheets offer 
a valuable counterweight to tighter finance. The private economy rests on 
three relatively strong pillars: consumers with healthy balance sheets, well-
capitalised financial institutions and corporates with leverage around or below 
historical averages. Europe also offers a free cash flow yield of roughly 5.5%, 
versus 3.5% for the US and about 2% for the largest US companies. Buybacks, 
limited equity issuance and rising M&A mean in effect equity supply is shrinking 
in Europe. At the same time, we see continued modest buying of European 
equity by global investors (less so domestic investors) as they seek to diversify 
concentrated positioned in the US and elsewhere.
