August Trade Deficit Rises to the Largest 
Gap Outside the Early-2025 Trade Surge 
Headlines 
 
The nominal U.S. goods and services trade deficit jumped 14% in August from 
July to $105.6 billion. 
 
Imports rose more than exports on the month, resulting in a much larger trade 
deficit in August.  
 
As tariffs have retreated from their peak levels earlier this year, goods imports 
have rebounded sharply, supported by rapid growth in AI-related capital 
spending. Meanwhile, total goods exports rose 2% to $205.7 billion in August, 
ending a three-month streak of declines. 
 
The services trade surplus was essentially flat for the month.  
 
Going forward, AI-related capital expenditure should continue to support 
strong goods imports in the near term.
Details
The seasonally adjusted U.S. nominal goods and 
services trade deficit widened 14% in August to 
$105.6 billion from $92.8 billion in July (Figure 1), 
according to the U.S. Census Bureau. Imports 
increased more rapidly than exports, resulting in a 
significantly larger trade gap. August's deficit was 
the largest on record outside the early-2025 period, 
when imports were front-loaded ahead of tariffs 
and 
subsequent 
trade-war 
disruptions. 
Total 
imports rose 4% in August while exports rose a 
modest 1%. The trade deficit was more than 77% 
larger from a year earlier, as exports increased 11% 
and imports climbed a stronger 22%. 
After smoothing out monthly volatility, the three-
month moving average trade deficit increased 12% 
to $89.9 billion in August, marking the fifth 
consecutive rise. 
The trade deficit surged to a record $133 billion in 
March 2025 as businesses accelerated imports 
ahead of anticipated tariff increases by the Trump 
administration. The deficit then narrowed sharply, 
reaching just $37 billion in October as higher tariffs 
curtailed import demand. However, as businesses 
and consumers have adjusted to the new tariff 
environment, the trade deficit has more than 
doubled from its October low. 
As tariffs have retreated from their peak levels 
earlier this year (Figure 2), goods imports have 
rebounded sharply, supported by rapid growth in AI-
related capital spending. Total goods imports 
increased 5.3% to $342.2 billion in August. Nearly 
every major import category posted gains, with 
consumer goods the lone exception, declining 0.8%.

17%, driven by a 28% increase in crude oil imports 
and a 197% jump in nonmonetary gold imports. 
AI-related investment remained a key driver of 
import growth. Semiconductor imports rose 18% in 
August and were up 84% year-to-date compared 
with the same period last year. Consumer goods 
imports declined modestly, primarily due to lower 
pharmaceutical preparations imports. Even so, 
consumer goods imports remained more than 3% 
above year-ago levels, although growth lagged well 
behind capital goods imports (up 60%) and industrial 
supplies and materials imports (up 28%). 
Total goods exports rose 2% to $205.7 billion in 
August, ending a three-month decline. Industrial 
supplies and materials (up 9%) and capital goods (up 
2%) led export gains, while consumer goods exports 
fell more than 9%. As with imports, fluctuations in 
commodity prices boosted exports of both gold and 
crude oil. Further increases in soybean and crude oil 
prices could continue to support nominal export 
values in coming months, although the recent 
decline in gold prices may provide some offset. 
On a year-over-year basis, goods exports remained 
more than 15% above year-earlier levels, with strong 
shipments of energy products, metals, chemicals, 
crude oil, computers, semiconductors, and other 
business equipment. Elevated demand for these 
products, combined with ongoing geopolitical 
tensions in the Middle East, has continued to support 
export growth. 
The services trade surplus edged up less than 0.1% 
to $31.0 billion in August. Financial services exports 
and travel services exports (inbound travel to the 
United States) both declined on the month.  
Services imports were essentially unchanged in 
August. The largest category, outbound travel 
spending by U.S. residents, declined 0.7% on the 
month. Services trade continues to expand relative 
to a year ago because it remains largely insulated 
from tariffs and is supported by ongoing business 
activity. Both inbound and outbound travel services, 
however, have softened from year-earlier levels 
after peaking earlier in 2026. 
Looking 
ahead, 
strong 
AI-related 
capital 
expenditures should continue to support goods 
imports in the near term. At the same time, a weaker 
U.S. dollar in August and expectations for slower 
consumer spending growth in late 2026 are likely to 
restrain growth in consumer goods imports. Notably, 
consumer goods imports remain near a three-year 
low despite a modest rebound in recent months. 
Meanwhile, continued uncertainty surrounding the 
U.S.-Iran 
conflict 
and 
broader 
geopolitical 
developments are expected to generate ongoing 
volatility in commodity markets, particularly for 
crude oil and related products. As a result, exports 
of nominal goods are likely to remain highly volatile 
in the months ahead.
