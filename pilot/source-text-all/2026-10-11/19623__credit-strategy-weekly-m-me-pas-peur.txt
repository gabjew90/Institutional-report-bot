Credit Strategy Weekly 
Même pas peur 
Credit recovers but sovereigns remain an issue. The credit markets have regained some of 
the ground lost previously, but they remain close to the widest levels seen last week. For now, 
credit fundamentals are still solid, in our view (and we believe the 3Q results confirm this), and 
the recent turmoil added to the ongoing hostilities in the Middle East and high oil prices should 
see companies remain cautious when it comes to spending. But volatility in the sovereign bond 
market has yet to subside and if it continues or deteriorates, that could spell more pain for the 
credit markets. We stated previously that if sovereign bonds rally or stabilise or even widen 
gently, we believe credit will rally. Judging by the very recent reaction late this week, the credit 
markets may even be able to absorb some sovereign bond volatility. Nevertheless, we prefer 
to remain tactically cautious until we have clear evidence that the worst is behind us.  
Fortunately, bonds are rising at the time of writing and the US announcement of no more 
attacks until the mid-terms should help risk appetite gain the upper hand again, for now. 
Valuations are becoming very attractive. As mentioned above, we believe that corporate 
fundamentals are likely to remain solid for now. In addition, credit spreads are now more 
attractive than they’ve been at any point this year. Credit yields are the most attractive they’ve 
been in years, and we suspect that the asset class is poised to rally strongly if overall market 
sentiment doesn’t deteriorate sharply again. 
New issue markets also fall victim to the sovereign bond turmoil. The new issue markets 
were open, but activity has fallen sharply over the past fortnight as sovereign bond volatility 
has increased. This time, we didn’t see any financial deals emerge, and most activity came from 
the IG space. In total, €7.23bn was priced (excluding Friday’s deals), which pushed up the year-
to-date tally to €722.1bn, still some €20bn higher than a year ago. If the volatility in the 
sovereign bond market calms down, we expect to see strong new issue volumes, even allowing 
for blackout periods.

Macro view 
All about sovereign bonds again. The sovereign bond market 
was again the main attraction this week, as yields remained 
volatile although the moves were less pronounced than a week 
ago. There was some positive news, including Trump’s 
announcement that the US would not launch attacks on Iran prior 
to the midterm elections. This provided some relief to the oil 
markets, although prices remain high, nonetheless.  Next up will 
be the 3Q earnings season results, starting with the US money 
centres next week, as well as some economic data releases, 
including CPI data in the eurozone, the US and China. Still, we 
believe that the key factor for the credit markets will remain the 
volatility in sovereign bond markets.  In our view, if they improve, 
stabilise or even just sell off in an orderly manner, then credit 
spreads will recover.      
 
 
 
 
 
 
Cash 
A passenger with a reckless driver. The credit markets 
performed better this week as spreads were much more resilient 
than last week, even though the sovereign bond market turmoil 
continued. Credit spreads in the euro market widened sharply in 
the second half of last week as sovereigns whipsawed 
aggressively, but this time the different credit indices we follow 
were much more stable. Credit spreads were largely unchanged 
on Monday, then rallied strongly on Tuesday, with the iBoxx 
Corporates index posting its best session of the year. Later, credit 
spreads moved in relatively narrow ranges despite the volatility in 
the sovereign bond market. As we stated earlier, we believe credit 
fundamentals remain solid and that spreads should rally if 
sovereigns stabilise or improve in the coming days. Credit is even 
likely to improve if sovereigns just sell off at a slow pace. And 
looking at this week’s performance, credit is likely to widen only if 
volatility in the sovereign bond market increases further.

followed the overall market tone, rallying strongly on Tuesday,

New issue 
markets 
Overall market volatility hampers new issue activity. The 
sharp swings in the sovereign bond market didn’t fully close the 
new issue markets but they did hamper activity as only €7.2bn 
was priced over the week. Moreover, most of the bonds came 
from the IG space, with a few HY deals testing the market as well.

sovereign bond market stabilises. We should see more IG deals at 
first, likely from some higher-rated corporates coming with 
disciplined transactions in a typical price discovery process. But 
we need that overall market stability first.

Strategy Scorecard 
Regular readers will be familiar with our SG Credit Scorecard, a tool we have used for more than 
a decade to help us forecast the direction of credit spreads.  
For readers who are not familiar with the Scorecard, we construct it in three stages. First, we 
group indicators into various categories. Macroeconomic fundamentals, for example, 
comprises GDP and inflation numbers, but also indicators such as monetary policy and fiscal 
policy. Second, we compare our forecasts for these indicators over the next six to 12 months with 
the consensus view. This gives us an impact, which can range from a maximum positive of +3 to 
a maximum negative of -3. These are shown in the table as +++ and ---; a neutral reading is =. 
Finally, we assign a weight to the indicator. This ranges from 1 (relatively unimportant) to 3 
(relatively important), shown in the table as a number of boxes. The weights depend on our view 
of where we are in the credit cycle. The scorecard for each indicator is the impact multiplied by 
the weight, so it can range from +9 to -9. The average level of macroeconomic fundamentals is 
therefore the average of the two sub-indicators, GDP and inflation and policy; the average level 
of the whole scorecard is the average of the five indicators (macroeconomic fundamentals, 
corporate fundamentals, investor demand, supply, and valuations).

The week ahead 
Here’s looking at sovereigns: Next week we’ll have several economic data releases, and the 
very early stages of the 3Q earnings season. However, we suspect the key input for the credit 
markets will remain sovereign bond volatility and, indirectly, the war in the Middle East and oil 
prices. As we’ve argued a few times already, we believe that the credit markets need sovereign 
bonds to stabilise, even if yields remain at high levels, before there will be a more sustainable 
spread tightening. 
Inflation prints among other economic data to watch: Next week will bring the final 
reading of September CPI data in the eurozone, which is unlikely to move markets much, unless 
it differs significantly from expectations. But we’ll have the inflation prints in the US and China, 
which could move sovereign bonds. There will also be other economic data releases, which 
could attest to the strength of the economies and impact the market expectations of central-
bank rate trajectories.

CPI data 
The rise in inflation looks set to continue across the major economic blocs. Next week will bring 
the final eurozone inflation data for September, and the market expects the numbers to confirm 
the earlier print. At the same time, the market expects headline CPI in the US to rise to 3.6% yoy, 
up from the previous 3.4% reading. The consensus is for the core measure to rise to 2.5% from a 
previous 2.4% reading. And in China, the market expects the CPI figure to rise to 1.0% from a 
previous 0.8% reading.

Market review and outlook 
Market review 
Sovereign bond volatility remains the focus of the markets:  The situation in the Middle 
East remains fragile and unstable and oil prices are still volatile. Futures contracts show Brent 
crude around $100 per barrel, but the cash market is much higher, around $120. WTI contracts 
are lower, and the discrepancy between futures and cash is much smaller, but prices are still 
hovering around $90. And, it seems clear that prices are unlikely to improve much until there is 
an agreement in the Middle East.  
Oil cash prices remain high 
 
Source: Bloomberg, SG Cross Asset Research/Credit 
Sovereign bonds are still the main victims of the high oil price, overall inflationary pressures and 
elevated debt levels. Not only did yields rise but they were also very volatile throughout the week, 
and the yield differential between the 10y OAT and the 10y Bund rose above 150bp at one point. 
In our view, credit will remain volatile and under pressure until sovereign bonds find some 
stability, even if that is at higher overall yield levels.
