USTs enter today’s session having weathered another test of the bear-steepening trend, with 10Y UST 
yields briefly trading up to 5.36% and 30Y reaching 5.73% before a strong 10Y auction helped stabilise 
sentiment. The broader backdrop remains challenging. Brent has marched back above $103pb this morning as 
Middle East tensions continue to escalate, with reports of further attacks on Saudi infrastructure and a tanker strike 
deep inside the Persian Gulf keeping energy markets firmly on edge. The key risk for rates remains that higher oil 
continues feeding through to inflation expectations and term premium rather than growth concerns. The focus 
today turns to jobless claims, the $22bn 30Y auction and a heavy slate of Fed speakers including Waller, 
Kashkari and Musalem. Yesterday’s FOMC minutes broadly reinforced the idea that another hike remains on the 
table this year, but with little urgency to move in October. Markets still price around one hike by year-end, with the 
front-end remaining remarkably resilient given the move higher in long-end yields. We continue to think the story 
is less about the Fed and more about supply, term premium and inflation uncertainty. A sustained break above 
5.35% in 10Y would leave little resistance before the 5.50% area, while 2s10s remains close to the summer highs 
near 55bp. In FX, the DXY index continues to trade in a tight range around 102, with higher yields and higher oil 
largely offsetting concerns around growth and risk sentiment. 
The focus in Europe remains firmly on France. Yesterday’s widening saw the 10Y OAT-Bund spread push back 
towards 140bp, retracing much of the tightening seen earlier in the week and reminding investors that fiscal 
concerns remain unresolved. The 2027 budget process is now formally underway, with debates in the National 
Assembly beginning next week and key votes scheduled for 20 October, 27 October and 17 November. The 
government’s stated aim is still to pass the budget through parliamentary votes rather than relying on Article 49.3, 
though markets will increasingly focus on whether that remains realistic as negotiations evolve. For rates, the key 
question is whether France remains a local story or starts to spill over more broadly across EGBs. Spain 
is likely to be closely watched after outperforming yesterday. ECB communication will be key too. So far, 
European diplomats haven’t shown any big signs of concern. Bunds continue to benefit from safe-haven demand, 
but I wonder if duration can sustain a bid with energy prices back on the rise. EUR/USD has stabilised overnight 
around 1.12 after Wednesday’s decline.
The bear-steepening theme has also been evident in the GBP rates space. 10Y Gilt yields briefly approached 
5.50% yesterday while 30Y yields traded through 6.00%, reaching fresh highs not seen since the late 1990s before 
recovering into the close. The move was driven largely by global factors, but the UK continues to look vulnerable 
given elevated supply expectations and growing questions around fiscal headroom. The FT estimates that the 
Iran-related energy shock may have already reduced the Chancellor’s available headroom by roughly £12bn 
through higher borrowing costs and inflation, further complicating the Budget outlook. We continue to see the long 
end as the key pressure point, with volatility likely to remain elevated into the Budget. In relative terms, the 10Y 
Gilt-Bund spread remains close to 200bp and will be difficult to compress meaningfully while fiscal uncertainty 
dominates the narrative. Today’s focus will also be on comments from Bailey, Pill, Greene and Lombardelli 
today, with Bailey and Lombardelli as the key ones given their potential to shift towards a hike in Nov. 
GBP/USD weakened again overnight and is now back around 1.32. 
Negative session in Asian equities following the rise in energy prices. In Japan, JGBs bull-flattened, helped 
by a well-digested 30Y JGB auction. USD/JPY remained within recent ranges, trading around 158.15 overnight.
