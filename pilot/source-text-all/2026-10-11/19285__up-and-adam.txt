Wednesday, October 7, 2026
Propelled by the AI megatrend, the Tech sector now accounts for more than 40% of the S&P 500’s market cap, up from 26% at the start 
of the bull market in October 2022 and 34% at year-end 2025. Importantly, Tech’s outperformance has been driven by earnings growth 
rather than multiple expansion. Consensus forecasts call for the sector to generate 38% of S&P 500 earnings in 2027, up from 22% at the 
start of the bull market. With Tech earnings continuing to outpace the broader market, we remain constructive on the sector.

Although it gets less attention than housing, vehicle affordability is also a challenge for many Americans. The average price of a new 
auto reached $50,089 in August, up 1.9% YoY, according to Kelley BlueBook. With auto loan rates at multi-year highs, Edmunds reports 
that one-fourth of new vehicle loans now carry terms of 84 months or longer. Some consumers are coping by keeping their car longer, 
with Cox Automotive estimating the average vehicle on the road is 13 years old, a trend that risks higher maintenance and repair costs. 
With its first record high since August 13 and its 28th of the year, the S&P 500 is now up 14.2% YTD. Beneath the strong headline 
performance, however, market breadth remains narrow. Technology was the only sector to also reach a record high yesterday, while 
every other sector sits at least 3% below its peak and the equal-weight S&P 500 is 4.6% off its high. While rising bond yields have kept 
market leadership narrow over the past three months, we expect participation to broaden beyond Tech as yields stabilize.
