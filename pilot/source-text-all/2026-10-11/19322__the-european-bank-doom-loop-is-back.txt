new problem to the mix.
This may not be the dip to rush into.

France is the new periphery
One of the more remarkable European macro charts right now.
Italian and Spanish 10-year spreads versus France have collapsed, with both now trading through zero.
The European sovereign problem is increasingly becoming a France problem.
And it may not be over. TS Lombard sees another round of widening if the budget negotiations fail to
improve, with 200bp potentially in play if things really unravel.
TS Lombard
The sovereign-bank loop
French 10-year yields now trade roughly 23bp above Italy. Think about that.
The market has effectively redrawn Europe's core/periphery map, with France moving from core
anchor to problem child.
For banks, this matters. Wider sovereign spreads mean pressure on bond books, tighter financial
conditions and potentially higher funding costs, just as European bank equities are starting to break
technically.
The old doom loop doesn't need to return.
Sovereign stress simply needs to persist long enough for banks to start pricing it.

Protection is interesting
BofA's excellent hedge idea from last week remains valid:
SX7E downside screens better than several other cross-asset tail hedges when current hedge costs are
compared with historical drawdowns. Bank put skew is elevated versus the rest of Europe, but that
actually makes put spreads relatively attractive.
Their Dec-26 30D–20D put spread offers a >4x maximum payout.
If you think the rates problem eventually becomes a growth problem, banks offer an interesting place
to express it.
