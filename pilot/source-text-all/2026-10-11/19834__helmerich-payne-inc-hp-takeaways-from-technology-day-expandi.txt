Takeaways from Management Meeting and Technology Day 
 
 
Point 1. Technology and automation deployment enables increased market share and 
margin growth 
We visited the company’s FlexRig Machinery Center and Rig 918 in Tulsa, OK, where we 
were given a tour and demonstration of the robotics and technology HP has developed 
as the company focuses on continued rig automation. With the expansion of their 
FlexRobotics operations with Exxon to nine systems operating by late 2027, we see 
potential market share growth for HP over the long term as the company uses 
automation expertise to win incremental rig awards with customers. HP noted that the 
use of FlexRobotics drives down the planned cycle time by 20.5 days, and the rig and 
system can be installed within 4 days when deploying for a customer. With about one 
third of HP’s fleet running with Tier 1 automation, we see the opportunity for increased 
commercialization of FlexRobotics on HP’s rigs for customers to achieve enhanced 
results. We believe the utilization of performance contracts with customers as well as 
the increased deployment of FlexRobotics should aid in margin expansion. 
Point 2. Updates on North America point to a constructive 4FQ result 
Management noted continued commercial conversations with customers, both public 
and private, as operators focus on budgeting decisions for 2027. With HP’s operational 
4FQ update of expectations for North America margins and activity to be at the high 
end of the guidance ranges, we believe the company is well set up heading into the end 
of the year. HP currently has ~151 rigs operating in North America and has the ability to 
add an incremental 9-10 rigs at low additional capital spend, though is looking for 
improving terms. We see the current dynamics of private E&Ps adding activity continuing 
over the next several months as commodity prices stay elevated. HP noted the potential 
for additional rigs to be allocated to geothermal customers such as Fervo as well as the 
opportunity for additional rigs to be used in HP’s international exposures, potentially 
tightening rig supply in US land. 
Point 3. International regions such as Argentina and Middle East present long-term 
growth opportunities 
We believe HP’s international regions pose an out-sized area of growth for the company, 
as opportunities in Argentina, Australia, the Middle East, and other regions support 
long-term margins. HP noted their increasing rig count in Argentina, where long-term 
contracts with strong margins are supporting the International business towards the 
high-end of the 4FQ26 margin guide. As HP ramps to 15 rigs in the country over the near 
term, the company sees the potential for an increasing position in the country, as they 
are working with multiple operators, and sees supportive trends over the long term. 
Given the long-term nature of international contracts, we are constructive on HP’s focus 
around increasing their international presence. In the Middle East, we are mindful of 
some variability in the international segment throughout FY2027, particularly in Saudi 
Arabia as operators there look to potential short-term suspensions. With this said, we 
view HP’s footprint in region as a value driver over the long term. Looking ahead, we 
monitor for incremental color on pricing, mobilization costs and the long-term activity 
outlook of the international business.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
