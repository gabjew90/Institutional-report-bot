 
"Stuck": Silver Bears Have Never Been This 
Comfortable; That's The Problem... 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, OCT 09, 2026 - 07:25 PM 
For most of 2026, silver has traded like gold with a caffeine problem: up more on the way up, 
down more on the way down. This month it has found a third gear: down when its own bull case 
goes up. 
That, in a sentence, is the conclusion of the latest note from Goldman's Robert Quinn, the 
desk's resident tracker of who is buying and selling Comex metals, titled with admirable 
economy "Silver Futures: Stuck" (available to pro subs). With silver down another 1.8% to 
$59.22 on Thursday morning, even as gold edged up to $4,122, Quinn lays out why the metal 
can't get out of its own way: 
"Commitment of Traders encapsulating flows towards the end of September exhibited a large 
bearish turn in speculative positioning. Through early October, barometers for certain structural 
demand drivers improved. However macroeconomic headwinds also proved formidable. Thus in 
the end, the Silver price remained stuck with negative flows arguably persisting." (emphasis 
ours) 
In other words, the AI and solar story that was supposed to make silver the "industrial gold" is 
working, and nobody cares, because the dollar and real yields keep going up. 

 
 
Below we walk through the four charts behind Quinn's call, and why a market this short, this 
hedged and this bored has historically been a better buy than a sell. 
The Biggest Speculative Dump Since February 
Start with the flows. In the week to September 29, Quinn writes, "Managed Money, Other, and 
Non-Reportable net sold a combined $1.6bn" of silver futures, the largest weekly amount since 
February. And it wasn't just tired longs heading for the exit: the selling split evenly between 
$800mm of liquidation and $800mm of brand-new shorts. Goldman's COT review from the same 
CFTC data shows Managed Money net length 29.5k lots shorter than a year ago. 
The chart below puts that in context. Speculative net length in silver has roughly halved from 
around $24bn at the January peak to about $12bn, and the price has done the same, from a 
blow-off high above $115 in late January to under $60 today. Diversification at its finest, if you 
were long both. 

 
 
Regular readers will recall that this is a round trip with a punchline. In August, Goldman's desk 
noted clients were buying 3-month $90/oz silver digitals (Aug 21), a bet that now needs a 52% 
rally in about six weeks to pay off: 
The Bull Case Rallied. Silver Didn't. 
Here is the part that makes this note more than a positioning recap. Quinn tracks two equity 
baskets as real-time "barometers" for silver's structural demand: Goldman's Data Center basket 
(a proxy for the AI buildout) and its Solar basket (photovoltaics, one of silver's biggest industrial 
uses). Both have "maintained positive correlation with Managed Money gross long changes 
during the past 6 months." 
From September 29 to October 7, the Data Center basket rose 4.2% and Solar rose 2.4%. On 
past form, that should have pulled specs back into silver. It didn't, because over the same 
stretch "US real rates edged higher and the Dollar increased (0.8%)", and, as Quinn notes, 
"historically, stronger Dollar and real rate environments coincided with Managed Money gross 
long unwinds." Result: silver fell 1.4%. 

 
 
The dollar is clearly winning the tug-of-war. Goldman's chart of Managed Money gross longs 
against an inverted DXY shows the two moving almost tick for tick since August, with gross 
longs sliding from roughly $6.9bn in early September to about $5bn as the dollar index pushed 
to 102.5, its highest level of the past six months. 
 

 
This is the mirror image of mid-September, when Quinn's prior note ("Silver Futures: Hawkish 
Enough?") showed silver dropping 4.7% as the Data Center basket "plunged -6.9% as industry 
leaders questioned the pace of AI expansion." Back then the AI story was the problem. Now the 
AI story is fine, and silver still can't rally. Which tells you who is in charge: the Fed and the dollar, 
full stop. 
Everyone Is Hedged, Every Trend-Follower Is Short 
The options market has gone from euphoria to fear in record time. Quinn notes that 
"discretionary sentiment soured", with normalized 25 delta put-call skew richening "to the top 2 
year decile". Put differently, investors are paying more for downside protection in silver, relative 
to upside, than at almost any point in two years. Meanwhile, 3-month implied vol has collapsed 
to around 33, from above 100 at the January peak, and is now the lowest in about a year. 
 
Translation: nobody expects silver to move much, and the few who do expect it to move lower. 
That is a combination that tends to precede the opposite.

We've seen this movie before, and recently. On August 6, Quinn noted that Managed Money net 
length sat at a 3% two-year rank and that short-term momentum had just flipped, "forcing the 
start of CTA short unwinds." We flagged it that day(Aug 6). From July 28 to September 8, per 
Quinn's own tally, the December contract jumped 15.2% as Managed Money bought $1.8bn, 
mostly new longs. CTAs are now shorter than they were then. 
The One Pillar That Could Crack: King Dollar 
So what gets silver unstuck? Quinn points to the same place everyone else is looking, with an 
important caveat: 
"While acknowledging uncertainty over Middle East developments, note GS FX Research 
believes one pillar of Silver resistance, namely Dollar upside, could stall. Given stretched 
positioning on several pairs and recent Fed communication, the team is cautious on near-term 
Dollar prospects." (emphasis ours) 
Goldman's FX team (Stuart Jenkins, Michael Cahill and colleagues) spelled this out in 
Tuesday's "US Outperformance and the Dollar", crediting the September dollar rally largely to 
US equity outperformance. The trade-weighted dollar just set new year-to-date highs, "but after 
a sharp move, stretched Dollar positioning in several major pairs, and an emphasis on a patient 
approach to policy tightening from recent Fed communication, we are cautious on the prospects 
for continued near-term Dollar strength." 

 
Now for the caveat, which hit the tape earlier on Thursday morning. Brent jumped more than 5% 
to above $105 on a report that the White House asked the Pentagon to draw up strike options 
against Iran, sending 10-year yields toward 5.3% and lifting the dollar again. Wednesday's 
FOMC minutes didn't help either: "most" officials thought another hike would likely be 
"appropriate by year end", and Goldman's economists still expect it in December. That is the 
"macroeconomic headwind" Quinn is talking about, and it isn't going anywhere before the 
midterms, unless the next Iran headline is the Monday de-escalation that reverses by Friday's 
close. 
Meanwhile, In The Physical Market... 
Paper silver is stuck. Physical silver is a different story. Goldman's commodity strategists (Lina 
Thomas and Daan Struyven) warned last month that tariff fears pulled so much metal into the 
US that "we expect much of the metal pulled into the US to remain trapped there, leaving 
available ex-US inventories tight and creating scope for a repeat of the 2025H2/2026H1 
volatility should investor demand recover." The cracks in the New York-London arbitrage are 
already showing: 
And while ETFs were buying gold and selling silver, per Bloomberg, Goldman's materials desk 
had a simple observation from Asia on Thursday: "Overnight China bought the dip." The desk 
calls the "China physical bid" key (India is absent), puts positioning at just "2-3/10", and asks the 
obvious question: "if you thnk rates have stopped going down why wouldn't you buy precious?" 
(typo theirs). That echoes the PBOC, which added gold again in September, and which, as we 
noted last month, may be buying twice what it reports. China's central bank doesn't buy silver, of 
course. But at a gold/silver ratio of about 70 (by our napkin math, from $4,122 gold and $59 
silver), up from 66 in early September, the cheaper metal keeps getting cheaper. 
Bottom Line 
Quinn's note is careful not to make a call. But read together, his four charts describe a market 
that has sold, shorted and hedged nearly all it can: 
●​ Specs: $1.6bn dumped in one week, half of it new shorts, net length roughly half of 
January's. 
●​ CTAs: about -$1.4bn net, the shortest in at least a year. 
●​ Options: put skew in the top 2-year decile, implied vol near one-year lows. 

 
●​ Fundamentals: AI and solar demand barometers rising; ex-US inventories tight per 
Goldman research. 
"While acknowledging uncertainty over Middle East developments, note GS FX Research 
believes one pillar of Silver resistance, namely Dollar upside, could stall." 
We'd go further. The last time Quinn found silver positioning this washed out, in late July, the 
metal ripped 15% in six weeks. The bears now have the dollar, real yields and an Iran 
escalation on their side, so one more flush toward the mid-$50s is entirely possible if Brent 
keeps climbing. But with CTAs max short, skew at extremes and Goldman's own FX team 
calling time on the dollar rally, the asymmetry is to the upside: silver doesn't need good news to 
get unstuck, just the absence of more bad news. And with the 30Y at 5.67% and the Fed openly 
debating how many more hikes the economy can take, the bad news for bonds may soon 
become good news for anything that can't be printed. 
We'll check back after Friday's COT data shows whether the late-September dump was 
capitulation, or just the start.
