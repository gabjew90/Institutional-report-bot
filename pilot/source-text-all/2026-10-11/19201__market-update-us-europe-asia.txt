U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.58% | Nasdaq +0.45% | Dow +0.49%
Synopsis

US equities were mostly higher in Tuesday trading as stocks extended Monday's gains. The Nasdaq and S&P 500 both set fresh record
highs, though the small-cap Russell 2000 finished lower at -0.59%. S&P 500 breadth was the best since 4-Aug. The AI trade was mixed,
with semis little changed, memory lower and software rallying again; big tech was mostly higher, and AMZN-US fared best. Other outper-
formers included airlines, cruise lines, space, QSRs, networking/comms equipment, building materials, homebuilders, home improve-
ment, apparel, E&Cs and machinery, while laggards included biotech, medical devices, trucking, China tech, energy, media and A&D.
Treasuries were firmer with a bull-steepening curve and yields down 1-3 bp. The dollar index was down 0.3%, gold finished up 0.7%, silver
was up 0.5%, bitcoin futures were down 0.2% and WTI crude finished fractionally higher in choppy trading. It was a very quiet session,
with better breadth again a major theme following the recent scrutiny of market internals, helped to some extent by global rate stabiliza-
tion. The broader compute and capex demand narrative was supported by MRVL-US guidance, while the AMD-US CEO talked up a strong
demand backdrop; capacity (CEG-US) and fundraising headlines were also busy, and M&A stayed in focus after a busy Monday with both

fairly upbeat, particularly at the index level with the tailwind from AI, and Q3 is expected to mark a third straight quarter of 25%+ earnings

and preliminary University of Michigan consumer sentiment for October caps off the week on Friday.
Macro

Middle East crude flows rebound, but Hormuz diplomacy is stalled: Little is new on Middle East diplomacy, with the biggest focus remaining
on oil flows. The Saudi Energy Minister said the East-West Pipeline was up to 5.8M bpd as of Tuesday as volumes continue to rebound
after the mid-September shutdown, and Bloomberg sources put the pipeline at more than 80% of capacity, up from about 50% earlier in
the week, with Saudi crude exports at 6.1M bpd in September from 3.4M in August. Yemeni forces retook the coast around the Bab el-
Mandeb Strait up to the city of Mocha on Monday, loosening the Houthis' grip on the Red Sea oil route (Reuters), although the Houthi
military spokesman denied that Saudi-backed forces have advanced and Pakistan denied a Reuters report that it has deployed 30K-40K
troops in Saudi Arabia. Kuwait is pumping ~75% of pre-war levels at 2M bpd, up from 1M bpd after Iran blocked the Strait of Hormuz, and
Iraq is hiring additional tankers, with the daily rate on the benchmark Saudi Arabia to China route at $1.29M, up from a $30K average in
the prior four years (Bloomberg). Iran has increased the pace of attacks on tankers in the Strait in recent days as flows recover, and it has
reiterated that the US must meet its seven demands before it agrees to reopen the Strait, while the White House is weighing whether to
restart combat operations, prompting a retaliation warning from Iran (Reuters). OPEC+ agreed over the weekend to keep November output
unchanged, though shipping disruptions limit the group's ability to influence the market, and the Saudi Aramco head said oil market
turmoil could last for years (Reuters).

Futures price a resolution the physical market has not delivered: Front-month Brent is trading just below $100/bbl, yet SEB noted that
the price Europe actually pays is far higher, with physical Oseberg and Forties averaging $142.50 and $140.20 last week. The effect of
the G7 pledge to release 100M barrels has largely faded. Hormuz flows have held near 17.5M bpd since mid-Sep, ~98% of pre-war, but
products are not: JPMorgan has them at 58% of pre-war (WorldPorts), and Saudi Aramco's Amin Nasser calls the stock cushion "scarily
thin." Refined products, notably diesel, have not seen a similar snap-back as Middle East refineries remain down following missile strikes
and other war-related outages. Goldman Sachs estimated global diesel and jet-fuel crack spreads will average $40/barrel next year, more
than double the typical level, with negative global refining capacity growth of -300K bpd this year as 2M bpd of Middle East refining
capacity remains offline. UBS's base case is a return toward the June memorandum by year-end, with uneven normalisation through H1
2027, a Brent range of $80-$135 against about $98 now, and $150 if Hormuz breaks again, while Goldman puts full normalisation only
in H2 2027 with oil then near $80. EIA forecasts US heating-oil bills to rise 21% this winter versus last year and expects US retail diesel
to stay above $6/gallon in October before declining to about $4.50/gallon in 2027. On gas, Goldman Sachs said Middle East LNG is
coming back online but not quickly enough to remove winter upside risks, with Dutch TTF seen ending 2026 at €70/MWh, and Hormuz
LNG crossings recovering to 21% of pre-war levels in Sep versus 4% in Aug.

Marvell and AMD advance on bullish multi-year AI revenue outlooks: As part of its Investor Day, Marvell Technology raised its fiscal 2028
revenue forecast to $20B, surpassing analyst expectations of ~$18.2B, which had already been raised from $16.5B in August (Reuters).
It also projects FY31 revenue of between $70B and $90B, significantly exceeding the $46.85B consensus, and sees a ~$400B AI market
by 2030. The company's recent agreement with Google could generate up to $120B in revenue through FY33, contingent on performance
milestones. AMD also rallied after CEO Lisa Su said the company has increased chip supply this year and will substantially increase output
in 2027 to address sustained demand for AI chips (Reuters). She said there is very high demand for AI chips in the next several years,
and AMD is building out advanced wafer capacity on a 3-5 year planning horizon to ensure sufficient production capacity for high-perfor-
mance CPUs and GPUs. Separately, French startup Mistral unveiled its Large 4 open-weight AI model to challenge US and Chinese com-
petitors, and Nvidia-backed Lambda is raising $4B at a $14B valuation in its last funding round before an IPO.

September FOMC minutes are expected to carry a hawkish bias: Minutes to the September FOMC meeting are due Wednesday (7-Oct, 14
ET). BofA said the release is likely to reflect a hawkish bias, including discussion of how much additional tightening may be needed.
Deutsche Bank noted that recent Fedspeak (Williams and Jefferson suggesting an October skip) is aligned with the SEP projection for one
more 25 bp hike this year, and that the neutral-rate debate is also in focus given the higher long-run dot estimate. However, the release
is seen as somewhat stale following softer inflation prints in recent weeks, notably August core PCE, which helped push down year-end
hike pricing to 24 bp from 34 bp post-meeting and a late-September peak near 40 bp. Elsewhere on US policy and data, the US trade
deficit jumped above $100B in August amid record imports (Reuters), US supply chain pressures increased modestly in September ac-
cording to the NY Fed (Reuters), Treasury Secretary Bessent faces market skepticism over his plan to reduce the federal deficit through
economic growth (Bloomberg), and US and India trade talks reached an impasse as it is getting harder to make further compromise
(Bloomberg).

EU Market Recap
Germany +0.77% | France +0.40% | UK +0.42%
Synopsis

European equities finished higher, but well-off best levels, after a mostly higher close on Monday. Healthcare, banks and media were the
top performers, while energy was the main laggard, followed by telecom. Easing OAT-Bund spreads (-10bps on the French 10-year) and a
calmer sovereign bond backdrop supported European equities, as did slightly lower oil prices. The rally in Eurozone bonds was driven by
fiscal discipline hopes stemming from France's 2027 budget being formally submitted today and the Le Pen budget proposal, and by the
French finance minister saying there is no need for ECB support or intervention for the moment. That said, market debate is heating up
about the ECB's potential to step in to support France amid surging borrowing costs, though the bar is seen as high and fiscal consolida-
tion by the government is seen as preferable first, though contagion risk and further yield surges could force the central bank to act.
Regarding the 2026 budget, the government is likely to use Art 49.3 to pass it without a majority, which would expose it to a no-confidence
vote. The euro bounced after hitting a near 17-month low on contagion fears, even as Spain's snap November election adds political
uncertainty. On the data front, German factory orders declined 10.6% in Aug versus a 1% drop expected, and Eurozone retail sales missed
expectations at 0.8% y/y versus 1% consensus. In the UK, reports suggest Burnham may push the UK defence-spending pathway to Oct-
2027, while UK Construction PMI beat at 46.1 versus 44.8 expected. Across assets, the dollar index was down 0.3%, gold was up 0.8%,
silver up 0.4%, bitcoin futures up 1.0% and WTI crude down 1.7%. On the corporate front, GMAB-DK's blood-cancer combination with
AbbVie's cut the risk of progression or death by 51% versus R-CHOP, INF-GB is to acquire B2B events firm Clarion for £2.24B, and French
banking group BPCE has acquired a 7% stake in SAB-ES. APAM-NL reiterated Q3 EBITDA below Q2's €110M and slightly above Q1's
€90M, versus €113M consensus, and WIE-AT cut 2026 operating EBITDA guidance to €640-650M from €700M after a weak Q3.
Macro

Healthcare, banks, media and food/beverage lead; energy lags: Health Care was the standout sector, led by Genmab (GMAB-DK) after its
combination treatment with AbbVie (ABBV) delivered statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in progression-free
survival for DLBCL patients. AstraZeneca (AZN-GB) was also up after its CEO said the company has what it needs to grow beyond 2030
without a major acquisition, Sanofi (SAN-FR) benefited from a positive currency outlook update, and Recordati (REC-IT) rose after the
takeover offer from CVC and Groupe Bruxelles Lambert was increased to €53/share. Banks were also stronger amid the stabilization in
European bond markets, with Eurozone banks driving the upside and Banco de Sabadell (SAB-ES) gaining after BPCE acquired around 7%
and said it intends to remain a long-term shareholder. Media outperformed with M&A supportive, as Informa (INF-GB) surged after
announcing the £2.24B acquisition of Clarion alongside plans to separate Taylor & Francis, and Food & Beverage benefited from Domino's
Pizza Group (DOM.LN) trading higher on a Buy initiation by Berenberg. Oil & Gas was the clear sector laggard amid the retreat in crude
prices, though OMV (OMV-AT) bucked the trend after RBC upgraded it to Sector Perform. Industrial Goods & Services were weaker relative
to the broader market even as corporate news leaned broadly positive: Alstom (ALO-FR) rose after saying FX should be slightly positive for
H1 FY26/27 sales, Fujitec and Kone (KNEBV-FI) benefited from reports of potential interest in TK Elevator's European operations, Andritz
(ANDR-AT) rose after targeting €10B revenue and a 10% comparable EBITA margin by 2029, and Clarkson (CKN-GB) surged after very
strong August/September trading and raised expectations for FY underlying PBT to at least £135M.

French bonds rally as Le Pen pledges big spending cuts amid surging borrowing costs: The 10-year French-German spread tightened 3bp
to 133bp versus a recent peak near 160bp as France formally submitted its 2027 budget and far-right presidential candidate Le Pen was
expected to outline plans to cut government spending by €25B a year, with a 2027 shadow budget intended to demonstrate fiscal
credibility (Bloomberg, FT, Reuters). Le Pen seeks a deficit of 3.7% of GDP in 2027, falling to 2.2% by 2032, against the government's 5%
target for 2027, though press noted she continues to back fuel-tax cuts and retirement as early as 60, and wants the ECB to intervene to
reduce debt costs even though it is prohibited from financing governments. Finance minister Lescure said France is "really not there" in
terms of needing ECB help, argued current stress is very different from the sovereign debt crisis, and insisted the 5% 2027 deficit target
is "non-negotiable." Politico noted the ECB's Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI) could act as a backstop but France would need to
meet criteria around sound and sustainable fiscal and economic policies; Goldman Sachs said TPI activation would likely require
significantly more intense and broad-based sovereign stress, TS Lombard argued the ECB would be reluctant to intervene absent a clear
fiscal or political emergency, and former ECB board member Smaghi argued the ECB should put QT on hold until more stable conditions
return. TS Lombard sees a further sharp widening in OAT-Bund spreads, potentially above 200bp, alongside broader contagion to Italy,
as a motivator for ECB action.

France's fiscal strain and the euro: Natixis sees the draft 2027 budget exposed to ~0.5% slippage and forecasts a 5.5% deficit, expects
the government to enact the budget by executive ordinance, and sees a 3% deficit as unlikely before 2032. Goldman Sachs cut France
2027 growth to 0.6% with 2026 at 0.4%, but sees domestic government debt at only 2% of French banks' assets and expects budget
debates and the 2027 race to keep the risk premium high without a systemic crisis or earnings shock; it prefers the international CAC 40
to domestic France stocks. Morgan Stanley suggested the selloff in French banks (ACA-FR, BNP-FR, GLE-FR) may be a buy. More broadly,
JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs pin the resilience of European equities on earnings, with Goldman finding YTD earnings revisions explain
~70% of relative sector moves and consensus H2 earnings growth at 22%, or 15% excluding energy, while both flag rates and French risk
as the shared caveat. EUR/USD tested $1.12, near a 17-month low, on French fiscal stress, with the OAT-Bund premium near the widest
since the 2010-12 debt crisis and the euro weakening against the whole G10 (Reuters). Commonwealth Bank of Australia sees the euro
under $1.10, UniCredit agrees a retest is likely, and Danske has already hit its 12-month target of 1.12, while Crédit Agricole rejects a
2022-style collapse and has 1.13 by Dec; short positioning means French relief can squeeze the pair, while further spread widening can
extend the drop.


ECB sees no second-round effects as German orders plunge on big-ticket payback: ECB policymakers hold the line, suggesting the Middle
East energy shock has not yet spread into wages or broader prices, even after Eurozone inflation hit a three-year high. Bank of Finland
governor Rehn said such effects can emerge gradually and unnoticed, chief economist Lane said the ECB has not seen very strong second-
round effects so far (ANSA), and Bundesbank president Nagel said there are no clear signs of feed-through into price and wage setting.
The ECB has hiked twice since the Iran war began, and markets price ~20% odds of an October move and 80% for December. German
factory orders declined 10.6% in Aug versus a 1% decline expected and +3.2% in Jul, the steepest drop since Jan, almost entirely a reversal
in other transport equipment, where orders dropped 61.5%; excluding large-scale orders, demand was down only 0.1%. Capital-goods
orders fell 15.3%, intermediate goods 2.6% and consumer goods 7.3%, and domestic orders slumped 17.3% against a 5.4% decline
abroad (DeStatis). Europe's Sep S&P Sector PMI showed output expanding in 16 of 19 monitored sectors, the most since Mar-2023 and
up from 13 in Aug, with 15 sectors reporting higher new orders, the most since Apr-2022; Technology Equipment was strongest, all 19
sectors saw above-average input cost inflation, and 10 of 19 sectors added staff.

UK defence-spending timetable slips; Spain heads to a November election: UK PM Burnham is reportedly set to push the spending review,
including the defence-funding pathway, from spring 2027 to Oct-2027, alongside next year's Budget (Telegraph, London Times). That
removes a near-term fiscal event but extends uncertainty on the biggest unfunded commitment, the route to 3% of GDP on defence by
2030 and the NATO target of 3.5% by 2035; the Treasury denied the speculation, and the OBR puts the extra cost of the interim 3% goal
at £17.3B. Tory leader Badenoch used the report to attack Burnham and launch a Conservative Iron Dome-style missile-defence plan. In
Spain, markets largely shrugged off PM Sánchez's call for a 29-Nov election after parliament rejected housing decrees. The People's
Party leads the polls, Capital Economics says a change of government should not alter the economic path, JPMorgan reads the polls as
pointing to a PP-Vox absolute majority with only a modest growth effect, and Eurasia Group puts 70% probability on a centre-right majority
(CNBC). Bonds have not sold off like France or Italy and the IBEX 35 is trading higher.

Synopsis

Asia markets ended broadly higher Tuesday. Japan's Nikkei and Taiwan's Taiex gained again, but the Kospi fell 0.89% despite a surge in
its software security names. The Topix, Hang Seng, India and much of Southeast Asia were all higher, and mainland China was closed for
a holiday. Long-dated sovereign bond yields generally eased back from recent highs, and Asia equity investors shrugged off the bond
market jitters and recent concerns over regional inflation while looking ahead to Q3 earnings season, which begins in earnest next week.
Early signs indicate technology is again set to lead, given monthly revenue beats from Hon Hai (2317-TW) and Nanya Tech (2408-TW) late
Monday and an upbeat operating profit forecast from Tokyo Electron (8035-JP) today. The US dollar was flat with little movement of note
in Asia forex, JGB yields were largely higher, precious metals were mixed, industrial metals found support and cryptocurrencies were
down. In other developments, Tokyo's 10Y JGB auction drew firmer than expected demand but yields remained near 30-year highs. Aus-
tralia consumer sentiment fell for a second consecutive month on higher interest rates and fuel prices, and India's September composite
PMI was revised lower to 55.2 from 56.6 in August and a preliminary 55.8. On the corporate front, New World Development (17-HK)
launched an exchange offer at 7.375% for around $991M of outstanding dollar loans in three bond tranches. Huawei (05L112-E) and
Qualcomm reached a multi-year licensing agreement for Qualcomm to use Huawei's LogicFolding chipmaking technique, and LG Elec-
tronics (066570-KR) secured a long-term deal to supply cooling solutions for several AI data centers in the US.

Moonshot set to lead a new wave of AI listings in Hong Kong: Bloomberg sources said China's Moonshot AI (0YW033-E) has closed its
final round of private fundraising at a valuation of around $50B and is targeting a Hong Kong IPO to raise up to $5B in Q1-27, with early-
look meetings to gauge investor appetite possibly as early as this month, riding on the buzz since the launch of Kimi K3 in July. Its planned
IPO is seen as one of the most anticipated deals in Hong Kong, where an AI-fueled boom pushed fundraising to record highs in the summer
(Bloomberg). It is unclear whether valuation or timeline could be affected by tighter AI regulatory oversight in China, as TheInformation
earlier reported Beijing opened a probe into DeepSeek and Moonshot after Anthropic said the Chinese AI labs had been routing sensitive
data through Claude, and OpenAI last week accused Moonshot of wide-scale effort to extract data from GPT models in so-called adver-
sarial distillation. Moonshot's previous round this summer valued it at $31.5B, and its ARR is expected to rise to $2B from the current
$1B. Separately, Bloomberg reported Kuaishou (1024-HK)'s AI spinoff Kling AI has picked several investment banks for a Hong Kong IPO
in 2027 that could raise at least $1B. AI stocks advanced sharply in Hong Kong, with Z.AI (2513-HK) leading the Hang Seng Tech index
after AWS said Z.AI's GLM 5.3 is now available on Amazon Bedrock. Bloomberg also reported DeepSeek (0YFMC1-E) is near securing at
least CNY80B ($12B) in its latest funding round with commitments from CATL (3750-HK) and Tencent (700-HK), well past its CNY50B
target ahead of a planned IPO early next year.

Kioxia shares weighed down by latent losses on retail margin trades: Nikkei discussed Kioxia's (285A-JP) depressed share price against
the backdrop of large unrealized losses on retail margin trades entered at the June peak and even during the subsequent slide in July.
These positions are due to be closed out around year-end, posing a headwind for near-term supply-demand conditions while limiting
upside. Most margin trades are of the 'standardized' variety that expire within six months. TSE data shows the book value of margin
positions on Kioxia totaled JPY848.7B ($5.4B) as of Friday, with estimated valuation losses of JPY48.3B, the standout among Nikkei index
constituents and dwarfing the next largest, Tokyo Electron (8035-JP), at JPY8.6B. The average entry price of JPY20,467 per share com-
pares with Friday's close of JPY19,300, a loss of nearly 6%. Institutional investors are deterred by an outsized margin ratio of 18.87 on
Friday, well above the TSE Prime Market average of 7.96, and daily fluctuations in retail long margin positions indicate retail investors
selling into strength and buying on dips, explaining the recent rangebound price action.

BOJ may signal underlying inflation has reached 2%, but there were no hints in Ueda's speech: Reuters, citing three sources, reported the
BOJ may signal in its next Outlook Report this month that underlying inflation has roughly reached its 2% target, highlighting readiness to
raise rates again in coming months. Any such announcement would largely be symbolic, but it would reinforce dominant market expecta-
tions of a December hike as well as the recent shift to a quarterly interval. Sources also noted that on the heels of the last move in
September, many in the BOJ are cautious about a back-to-back hike this month and prefer to gauge more data on the cumulative effects
of prior increases, though recent data such as Tokyo CPI and the BOJ Tankan are likely heightening conviction that underlying inflation
has more or less reached 2%. After the market close, Governor Ueda's speech only repeated the guidance section in the September policy
statement that, with underlying inflation approaching 2% and financial conditions accommodative, the Bank will continue to raise the
policy rate while considering the timing and pace of adjustment, and he reiterated the emphasis on preventing an inflation overshoot.
Separately, the BOJ output gap came in at +0.55% in Q2 following +0.36% in the previous quarter, the eighth straight quarter in positive
territory and the highest since 4Q25, with potential growth little changed at +0.81% in H1.

in policy: the speech contained five references to markets compared with one or two in prior speeches, yet it still lacked specifics on
funding sources, including for the consumption tax cut, strategic growth investments and defense spending, as well as the administra-

stands as the main topic of debate, and opposition parties scrutinized the lack of funding substance while pushing alternative proposals
focused on cash payouts and other tax relief (Nikkei). Hedge funds are rebuilding bearish bets on the Japanese yen (Bloomberg).
