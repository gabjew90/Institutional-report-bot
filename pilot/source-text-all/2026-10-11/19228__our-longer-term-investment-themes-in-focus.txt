Executive summary
The table below summarizes our top five rankings. 
According to our Longer-Term Investment (LTI) themes 
model, these themes currently offer the most attractive entry 
points in our universe. We also highlight themes with a less 
favorable ranking in the current environment, but advise 
short-term-oriented investors to stick with our top five.
Additionally, we emphasize that our current ranking does 
not alter our conviction in the LTI themes not discussed 
in detail in this report, as we continue to believe they 
are well positioned to capitalize on structural trends over 
an extended time horizon. We recommend diversifying by 
investing across multiple themes to mitigate drawdown risk.

Update on Middle East-exposed 
themes
Our only theme with significant exposure to the Middle East 
at the present is Identifying the next frontier, and this reports 
provides an update in light of the ongoing geopolitical 
developments.
Emerging and frontier markets came under pressure early 
this year following the outbreak of military conflict in the 
Middle East and the subsequent sharp rise in global energy 
prices, but have since resumed their upward trend as the 
global growth outlook has remained solid. In the near term, 
heightened geopolitical risks and disruptions to energy flows 
from the Middle East continue to pose risks to some of the 
equity markets in our theme.
While some individual frontier and emerging markets may 
experience heightened volatility in the near term, over 
the medium term, we continue to view emerging and 
frontier economies as attractive investment opportunities, 
and believe a broad and geographically diversified basket 
offers an appealing way for investors to gain exposure to 
these markets.
Persistent concerns about concentrated USD-denominated 
holdings in global portfolios make emerging markets 
valuable for geographical diversification. Robust global 
growth, supported by loose fiscal policy in major economies, 
should further underpin these markets, which are generally 
more sensitive to downturns than their developed market 
peers.
To identify which developing economies are likely to become 
future growth leaders, we considered both demographics 
and productivity. We then screen for markets that more 
effectively translate GDP growth into earnings growth, 
recognizing that rapid economic expansion does not always 
result in strong equity market performance.
Approach to defining theme 
preferences
To identify attractive entry points for LTI themes, we utilize 
a quantitative model combined with qualitative inputs from 
our thematic analysts. While the quantitative model relies 
on fundamental indicators such as valuation, momentum 
signals, and quality factors, we also incorporate qualitative 
factors including alignment with the CIO House View, key 
risks, and potential new catalysts.
We also apply this approach to themes that rank lower 
at present. Individual LTI themes often have very specific 
exposure to certain end-markets and industries that are 
dependent on the developments within an economic 
cycle. Economic dynamics will therefore likely influence the 
performance of a theme in the short term, meaning that 
some themes may outperform and others underperform 
within certain periods of the cycle.
Although we continue to view the structural investment 
case for these themes as valid, our quantitative model 
indicates that, on a 6- to 12-month horizon, these lower-
ranked themes fall into the 3rd or 4th quartile of our 
quantitative model—specifically, ranked 13th or lower out 
of 23 themes. In addition to this quantitative assessment, we 
further enhance our analysis by integrating analyst feedback 
on upcoming industry and thematic catalysts and consider 
the absence of positive near-term catalysts identified by 
our analysts, ensuring a comprehensive and forward-looking 
evaluation.
For the other LTI themes not discussed in detail here, we 
want to reaffirm their viability. A brief overview of all CIO LTI 
themes and their drivers can be found in the appendix.
Current theme preferences
Our model-generated ranking, applied to our Longer-
Term Investment theme universe, currently identifies the 
following top five themes: Automation and robotics, 
Enabling technologies, Energy efficiency, Diversity and 
Equality, and Aging in comfort.
We have also identified two themes with particularly weak 
model rankings and a lack of short-term positive catalysts:
Genetic therapies and Healthtech. In our view, investors with 
a shorter-term investment horizon should consider one of 
our top five themes instead.
All investment decisions carry risks, including the possibility 
of partial or total loss. Past performance does not guarantee 
future results.
Our top five themes
Automation and robotics
Investment case: When most people think of automation, 
they picture industrial robots, but the value chain goes 
beyond just factory automation, and also includes process 
automation, with industrial software as a key driver. 
Humanoids are expected to play a growing role in the 
future to address labor shortages and boosting productivity. 
Emerging markets offer strong growth potential, as they lag 
developed countries in robotics adoption and productivity. 
Aging populations make automation equipment attractive 
in both emerging and developed markets. The shift 
toward digital manufacturing, driven by industrial software 
and AI, is increasing automation investment. Recent 
US tariffs have again highlighted global supply chain 
vulnerabilities, prompting companies to reshore production, 
further supporting automation and robotics.

Why invest now: Automation and Robotics retained 
its position at the top of our theme ranking, supported 
by strong momentum and a favorable quality score. 
Manufacturing leading indicators remain constructive. In 
the US, the ISM Manufacturing Index stayed at high levels, 
while the Eurozone manufacturing PMI improved further. 
Both indicators remained comfortably above the 50 
threshold, signaling ongoing expansion. As highlighted in 
the September edition of the UBS Equity Compass (see US 
section), historical cycles suggest that once the ISM enters 
expansion territory, it typically remains there for almost 
three years. This indicates that the current cyclical recovery 
may still have substantial room to run. This reinforces 
the view we expressed in our August Automation and 
Robotics report that the theme is supported by both a 
near-term cyclical recovery and durable secular growth 
drivers, creating above-average earnings potential for 
companies exposed to the space. In that report, we 
highlighted that year-over-year growth in global robot 
installations had already turned positive in 2025 based 
on preliminary data from the International Federation of 
Robotics (IFR) (confirmed in the final data published in 
September). Looking ahead, our view is unchanged, we 
consider the topic an attractive investment theme.

Enabling technologies
Investment case: As generative AI helps speed up the 
convergence of technologies, we identify five enabling 
technologies—artificial intelligence, augmented reality/
virtual reality, big data, blockchain, and other moonshot 
technologies. We believe these technologies, excluding 
blockchain, are set to transform many industries. We expect 
the other four technologies to grow by an average of 35% 
annually, from USD 287bn in 2022 to USD 3.2tr in 2030. In 
our view, AI is likely to drive the largest incremental growth, 
accounting for 90% of the absolute dollar increase across 
these markets. We continue to view AI as one of the most 
profound technological shifts of the decade. Investors, in 
our view, will be best rewarded by focusing on software and 
semiconductor companies that have superior pricing power 
and high barriers to entry. Specifically, we now forecast 
global AI capex spending to reach USD 900 billion this year 
and grow to USD 1.2tr in 2027, implying a 57% CAGR 
between 2025 and 2027. Furthermore, we have revised 
our broader AI total addressable market to USD 3.1tr by 
2030, up from our previous estimate of USD 2.6tr. This new 
projection suggests a 30% CAGR between 2025 and 2030.
Why invest now: The Enabling technologies theme 
remains among our top five themes owing to its particularly 
strong momentum and its alignment to our House View. The 
theme is geared toward the IT sector, which is characterized 
by positive earnings revisions and robust results.
Achille Monnet, Xueqiong Huang
Energy efficiency
Investment case: Energy efficiency is driven by binding 
regulation, rising energy costs, and rapid advances in 
enabling technologies such as AI and automation, making 
upgrades and smarter operations increasingly scalable. The 
market is growing strongly, with global investment reaching 
about USD 800bn in 2025 and many segments showing 
robust growth. In addition, the International Energy Agency 
(IEA) estimates that an additional USD 1.3tr per year is 
needed to meet climate targets,implying strong long-term 
demand. Energy efficiency is essential for achieving the 
UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), as it not only 
drives major reductions in fossil fuel use and greenhouse gas 
emissions, but also delivers cost savings, boosts industrial 
productivity, and improves public health, making it a 
cornerstone of SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy) and 
SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure).
Why invest now: The theme remains in our top five this 
month, showing strong momentum in our model and strong 
exposure to the quality factor. The Middle East crisis has once 
again brought energy security to the forefront for countries 
as well as corporations. Improving energy efficiency reduces 
economic vulnerability and helps companies and consumers 
lower costs. This is especially important given that oil still 
accounts for nearly 91% of final energy demand in the 
transport sector. Like the Automation and robotics topic, this 
theme also benefits from an improvement in manufacturing 
leading indicators and a cyclical recovery in the industrials 
sector.
Alexander Stiehler, Nathaniel Gabriel (US), Achille Monnet
Diversity and Equality
Investment case: We expect the global regulatory 
framework to slowly improve disclosures and move toward 
greater parity, although this is unlikely to be the case for 
US-domiciled companies near term. Regardless of regulatory 
shifts in some regions, several studies show that improving 
diversity could close the wealth gap within societies, which, 
if realized, could boost GDP over the next decade. We 
believe companies that promote diversity and equality 
throughout their value chains and processes can outperform 
in the long term. Increasing regulation, added stakeholder 
scrutiny (proposals related to social matters represent a 
growing share of shareholder agendas and cover a wide 
range of topics from civil rights audits to pay inequality), 
growing evidence of the benefits of diversity for companies, 
the demonstrable economic benefits of a more inclusive 
society, and broader societal changes are the main drivers 
of this theme. Recently, there have been legal challenges to 
affirmative action and to explicit diversity initiatives in some 
US states, which may pose a short-term risk to our theme 
as US companies balance the benefits of investing in their 
workforce with potential legal risks. However, we remain 
confident in the long-term drivers.
Why invest now: The Diversity and Equality theme remains 
in our top five this month owing to its favorable valuation 
and quality score. The theme is well diversified across sectors

Aging in comfort
Investment case: As the world population is aging more 
rapidly because of falling birth rates and extended life spans, 
a growing share of the population is living in retirement for 
ever-longer periods of time, a development that presents 
specific market opportunities. Sustaining a high quality of 
life for as long as possible is likely to be the central demand 
motive for those entering their proverbial golden years. 
We see opportunities in three different areas. 1) Wealth 
planning: Prudent financial planning can enable individuals 
to fund a comfortable retirement. It offers a solution to 
the problem of looming age-related fiscal cliffs facing 
governments. 2) Staying healthy: Consumer companies with 
a focus both on healthy nutrition and on the personal care 
needs of aging consumers should help people to live better. 
The elderly should also benefit from the innovations we 
see in healthtech and medical devices. 3) Housing in old 
age: Demand for housing is changing as life expectancy 
increases, albeit often alongside chronic health conditions. 
These shifting housing needs should be supportive for home 
health care services, homebuilders, and retirement homes.
Why invest now: The Aging in comfort theme remains in 
our top five this month owing particularly to its favorable 
valuation. Our theme is geared toward financials, which 
we believe are still benefiting from appealing valuations 
and solid fundamentals. Meanwhile, the other sub-themes 
like health care remain among our favorite globally and 
add some downside protection in the current highly volatile 
environment.

Genetic therapies and Healthtech—a tactical step 
back.
The Genetic therapies and Healthtech themes currently 
rank low in our quantitative model, and from a bottom-
up perspective, our analysts do not identify any positive 
short-term catalysts that could meaningfully improve these 
rankings (see details below).
The Genetic therapies theme currently ranks low in our 
quantitative model, particularly in the valuation and quality 
categories, and its momentum is less attractive compared 
to other LTI themes in our coverage (see table at the 
end of the report). From a bottom-up perspective, our 
analysts do not identify any positive short-term catalysts that 
could meaningfully improve this ranking (see details below). 
Genetic therapy companies and the broader biotechnology 
sector have faced severe constraints on capital availability, 
especially for small and midsized biotechnology companies. 
While we are seeing some positive signs in terms of 
capital availability, we believe visibility into meaningful 
product commercialization and revenue growth remains 
limited. The unwinding of the biotech funding bubble that 
peaked in 2021 remains a challenge for many gene therapy 
companies. Hence, we still maintain a cautious stance on 
the near- to medium-term outlook of the genetic therapy 
theme, and would recommend switching to one of our top-
ranked themes.
The Healthtech theme currently ranks low in our quantitative 
model, particularly in the quality and momentum categories, 
and the theme is less attractive, in our view, than other 
LTI themes in our coverage (see table at the end of 
the report). Following a rally, particularly in the tech 
segment of the theme, the quantitative signal appears less 
favorable. From a fundamental perspective, we believe the 
theme lacks strong positive drivers at this stage. Investor 
enthusiasm in 2020-21 led to substantial capital inflows 
into healthtech, with valuations often based on expectations 
for digital disruption rather than proven business models. 
As financial conditions remain tight, many companies are 
under pressure to demonstrate profitability and scalability 
but have struggled to translate user growth into sustainable 
economics. Adoption of digital health solutions has also 
been slower than expected owing to reimbursement 
challenges and regulatory hurdles. This has contributed to 
longer commercialization timelines and a more challenging 
environment for sector valuations. We note the potential for 
artificial intelligence (AI) applications to drive value within 
parts of the health care ecosystem, particularly in diagnostics 
and care delivery/medical cost control. We view access to 
and control of key patient data as critical for monetization 
of AI within health care, and we do not yet see scalable, 
data-driven applications emerging as significant growth 
drivers within investable vehicles. We expect this dynamic to 
improve as patient data and AI application coalesce longer 
term. For investors who want to keep exposure to the health 
care sector, we have our Longevity TRIO topic. The Longevity
theme is primarily made up of large-cap, self-funding, cash-
flow-generating companies that are not reliant on capital 
markets, in addition to benefiting from other structural 
growth drivers defined in our report.
While we continue to believe that Genetic therapies and 
Healthtech will play important roles in shaping the future 
within the health care landscape, we are positioning more 
conservatively in the near term, at least until funding 
conditions stabilize and execution risks diminish.
Diana Na, Eric Potoker (US)

Appendix
Strong drivers support our themes
LTI themes, like sub-asset classes, group together securities 
which have prices reflecting future fundamentals, among 
other price-influencing factors. Our LTI framework identifies 
urbanization, population growth, and aging as fundamental 
drivers that create long-term value and support our 
conviction in a theme's longevity. The following statistics 
illustrate the significance of these trends:
• Population growth: According to the UN, the world's 
population will rise from 8.1 billion in 2024 to about 9.7 
billion in 2050. At the end of the century, the world's 
population could reach more than 10 billion. Most of this 
growth will occur in low- and middle-income countries. 
(Source: UN World Population Prospects 2024).
• Urbanization: In 2018, the UN estimated that 55% of 
the world's population lived in urban areas, compared 
with just 30% in 1950. By 2050, this number is expected 
to rise to 68%, with nearly 90% of this growth coming 
from Asia and Africa. (Source: UN World Urbanization 
Prospects 2018).
• Aging: In 2024, one in 10 people in the world was aged 
65 or older. By 2050, this ratio is expected to be one in six, 
with developed countries in particular feeling the impact 
of aging. In Europe and North America, more than one 
in four people are projected to be 65 years or older by 
2050. (Source: UN World Population Prospects 2024).
We believe our themes can deliver above-average earnings 
growth over a full economic cycle, and may contain small- 
and mid-cap companies not represented in well-known 
benchmarks, therefore offering opportunities beyond a 
traditional index orientation.
Approach/methodology
To define preferences, we apply a methodology that is both 
quantitative and qualitative, with stock picking deliberately 
excluded from our framework given our preferred diversified 
approach to theme investing—although stock picking could 
in theory provide an additional source of alpha.
The ranking of the themes is conducted on a relative basis, 
so when we discuss an attractive valuation, momentum, or 
quality factor, we mean this relative to our other themes 
(note that in Table 2, we show our universe of themes). 
Further details for each factor appear in the appendix of this 
publication.

Our quantitative investment theme ranking applies select 
style/factor components that can exert a significant 
influence on performance. The model looks at stocks within 
a theme in an equally weighted way and is calibrated to 
minimize turnover while still seeking to deliver solid excess 
returns.
The main model factors applied to the theme's reference list 
of stocks are as follows:
• Valuation: We rely chiefly on one-year trailing free cash 
flow (FCF) yields. However, we also consider the one-year 
trailing earnings yield and the one-year trailing price to 
book.
Note: To assess valuation, we use the published reference 
lists as a basis, which can be found in the respective 
thematic publications.
• Momentum: The percentile rank of the combined 12-
month price and three-month earnings revision score.
• Quality: Any improvement in key ratios such as 
profitability, financial health, or efficiency, basically 
observing changes rather than absolute levels of quality.
By applying a qualitative overlay, we can look at factors 
such as CIO House View consistency and vulnerability to 
identify key global risks, as well as review theme-specific 
risks published in the Risk Radar publication series. We 
also discuss upcoming catalysts such as new regulations 
or theme-specific events, and consider key macroeconomic 
trends. These quantitative and qualitative factors aim to 
help investors understand why we prefer some investment 
themes over the shorter term and deem them suitable for 
increasing thematic investments.
In addition, by tracking ranking changes within our 
framework, we monitor shifts in the themes' relative 
attractiveness from one period to the next. These rankings 
form the basis of our tactical preferences for long-term 
theme investing, although we maintain a high conviction 
on all our themes over the long run. A diversified approach 
to themes is recommended, as is a long-term investment 
horizon.

The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”

Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives
