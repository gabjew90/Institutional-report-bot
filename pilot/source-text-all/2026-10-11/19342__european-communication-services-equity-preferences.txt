Equity preferences
Communication services can be divided into telecommunication and media, and
with both we have a clear focus on quality and attractive dividend yields. Telecoms
are defensive due to their resilience in recessions. But structural drivers have
different effects depending on regulation and competition in their respective
markets. For instance, fiber can be seen as a tailwind for Deutsche Telekom but
not as supportive for Telefonica. As such, stock-picking is crucial
Sector view: Fair valuations and free cash flow should begin to improve as
capital expenditures slow. European telecom markets remain highly competitive,
but this could change under a new competition commissioner. From a valuation
perspective, the communications services sector trades at a 25% premium on
dividend yield to its 15-year average, offering a 3.5% forward dividend yield for
2026. However, much of the sector’s performance is driven by Deutsche
Telekom, which, in addition to its 3.7% dividend yield for 2026, also offers a
significant buyback. As a result, we do not view the valuation as excessive. We
expect the sector to benefit as fiber capex declines with the completion of
rollouts in more geographies (Spain, Italy, Norway, and the UK), which should
support healthy free cash flow growth and, in turn, underpin dividend yield
growth. Given the index’s concentration in higher-quality markets—namely the
US and Germany through Deutsche Telekom—consensus expectations are for
healthy EPS growth of 14% in 2026.
Our Top Picks: Deutsche Telekom, Telenor, Universal Music Group

Positioning and key trends
The telecommunications market moves slowly. Attractive regions—characterized
by light regulation, limited competitive intensity, healthy economies, and favorable
demographics—tend to stay attractive, while improvement for challenging
markets remains difficult. For this reason, we focus on high-quality regions such as
the US, Germany, and the Nordics, where incumbent market shares are relatively
stable and competitive positioning is clearer.
In the US, T-Mobile (of which Deutsche owns more than 51%) maintains the best
5G wireless network, along with the lowest pricing and smallest market share.
This combination should permit strong, profitable, above-market growth for
multiple years. Moreover, competitors carry substantial debt, and the regulatory
environment remains relatively relaxed, so we expect continued rational
competition.
Germany is a broadly stable market and offers an attractive fiber transition setup,
with Deutsche overseeing close to 70% of project build-outs. While Germany
telco competition has increased, we do not expect this to derail Germany’s market
quality or growth profile.
The Nordics have long been among Europe’s most rational telecom markets,
supported by a favorable regulatory environment and a collective “more-for-
more” pricing strategy. Several Nordic countries, such as Finland, have only three
main operators (versus four in most European markets), allowing operators to
achieve sufficient scale to invest in network infrastructure and preserve rational
pricing. In contrast, unattractive markets often harbor a sub-scale disruptor willing
to erode unit economics with promotions. Recent competitive dynamics in the
Nordics have become more volatile, with intensifying competitive pressures
weighing on sentiment. This has created some dislocation in Nordic telecom
stocks, but we view the weakness as an attractive medium-term entry opportunity
for patient investors.
We find Universal Music Group (UMG) appealing, as we believe the market is
overly concerned about UMG’s competitive positioning against streaming
platforms. Indeed, we find the relationship relatively symbiotic, with one firm
capturing a larger share of the incremental benefit in any given year. 2024 and
2025 benefited the streaming platforms more than the labels; however, with new
wholesale contractual pricing implemented, we see a more normal balance going
forward. Overall, we find music undermonetized and underpenetrated, and see
UMG as a structurally high-quality way for investors to get exposure to music.
UMG’s business model benefits from its large catalog of music rights, which
creates defensiveness. The uniqueness and high-quality nature of these assets,
combined with global streaming penetration remaining low, means monetization
has a long runway ahead. We believe UMG can capitalize on these trends over
time, retaining strong negotiating leverage with distribution platforms.
We also added several EU classifieds stocks, namely SMG and Scout24, as we view
market concerns over AI disintermediation as overblown and see this as an
attractive entry point for long-term investors. Classifieds are network effect
businesses and are ultimately driven by the consumer demand side of the network
rather than the supply side. With regard to genAI, the market is concerned that
well-distributed chatbots may shift consumer habits structurally.
We see real-estate-exposed classifieds as more defensive, as real estate often
represents the biggest single purchase for consumers and thus consumers demand
an exhaustive search, which chatbots cannot offer. We would also note that both
SMG and Scout24 are underpenetrated, underpriced, and have strong market
shares. Furthermore, both are tech-savvy, with Scout24 already integrating with
ChatGPT. For that reason, we expect continued robust fundamental performance
and for the underlying EPS performance to ultimately drive the stock.

Latest quarterly earnings summary
The last quarter brought a more volatile backdrop for European communication
services, with sector trends broadly consistent across most markets but shifting in
the Nordics. The Nordics and parts of central Europe saw an uptick in competitive
intensity, while southern Europe continued to face pricing pressure and elevated
churn. Although the volatility in the Nordics has been disappointing, operating
results remain robust, and we view the recent dislocation as an attractive
opportunity to add exposure to some of the higher-quality markets within
European telecoms. Deutsche Telekom remained a key highlight, with solid results
driven by its US business and resilient operations in Germany, despite increased
competition. Management emphasized network quality and customer retention,
supporting stable market share and robust free cash flow.
Across the sector, infrastructure demand stayed steady, B2B activity improved
slightly, and promotional activity in consumer markets was manageable. Most
operators delivered revenues in line with expectations, though some noted a
softer macro backdrop. CPI-linked pricing should continue to support growth, but
regulatory developments and M&A activity remain areas to watch. Deutsche
Telekom remains a preferred name due to its diversified earnings, strong cash flow,
and defensive positioning. We will keep monitoring competitive trends, especially
in Germany, as the year ends.
Within the classifieds segment, results remain strong with little visible impact from
generative AI. We continue to view the platforms as well-hedged to AI risks given
their homogenous inventory, suboptimal experience through chatbots, limited
revenue scale, and thus less desire to chase these markets from AI labs and strong
consumer habits. We see Scout24 and Swiss Marketplace Group as leading on AI
integration and well placed in multiple evolving scenarios. Given the sticky, high-
quality, high-margin nature of incremental growth, we find current levels attractive
for medium-term investors. AI volatility would come in the form of greater opex
investments or referral fees to AI platforms.
Ad-hoc changes
For 2026, we have transitioned from an equal-weighted benchmark to the MSCI
Europe Communication Services benchmark, adjusting on an ongoing basis. This
change better reflects the opportunity cost for investors. As a result, our portfolio
weights will change significantly and will be actively managed going forward.
Sustainability considerations
Customer privacy, access and affordability, and energy consumption are
fundamental for communications to reduce potential reputational and financial
risks. Employee engagement, and diversity and inclusion (D&I), are relevant for the
media sector as consumers and audiences become increasingly concerned with
diversity in media production and within companies’ employee profiles. We believe
our selection is underpinned by leaders in these trends.

Why it is Most Preferred
Deutsche Telekom continues to execute well operationally, with
strong and accelerating growth across its key markets. The market is
undervaluing its non-US assets, in our view, with the ex-US business
continuing to trade cheaply relative to peers. We view Deutsche as a
best-in-class operator in the telco sector with still attractive
valuations.
Company profile
Deutsche Telekom (DT) has two main units, Germany and the US,
which account for consolidated 25% and 65% of group EBITDA,
respectively. In Germany, DT is the incumbent operator in both fixed
and mobile. In the US, DT owns >50% of T-Mobile US. DT also has a
substantial footprint in the rest of Europe in markets such as Austria,
Greece, Hungary and Poland. DT owns a 12% stake in BT. T-Mobile
US completed its merger with Sprint in Q1 2020.
What drives our opinion
With its focus on structurally healthy markets, investing in leading
fiber & 5G networks, and strategic reviews of select non-core
activities (e.g., towers), Deutsche Telekom (DT) plans to spur growth.
T-Mobile US should remain the group’s growth driver with expected
healthy annual EBITDA growth, thanks to investments, customer
growth, and cost reductions. The European and domestic German
businesses should deliver lower but stable growth rates, thanks to
mobile and broadband services. Given the strength of its core
markets, namely the US and Germany, we view Deutsche Telekom as
a best-in-class telco company. The main risks are stronger competition
and regulatory changes, in our view. With 1&1’s decision to switch
wholesale partner from Telefonica Deutschland to Vodafone, there is
an increased risk of retaliation from Telefonica Deutschland, which
could lead to more promotional or M&A activity in Germany in the
near term. Over the longer term, we see the risk of a misallocation of
capital, as Deutsche Telekom should accrue significant free cash flow
in the upcoming years and future opportunities may not be as
attractive as past ones. Other risks would include rising electricity
costs, labor cost inflation, greater competition or a macroeconomic
slowdown.

Why it is Most Preferred
Given its quality, we think Elisa should trade at least in line with the
sector on a dividend yield basis, though it has historically traded at a
30% premium. We now see an attractive entry point.
Company profile
Elisa is the largest mobile operator in Finland with c43% market
share (ahead of Telia with c30% and DNA with c27%). The company
operates a fixed broadband and TV business as well as integrated
telecom services in Estonia. Elisa has also expanded into international
software services (ISS) and made a series of bolt-on acquisitions, with
ISS now accounting for c5% of revenues.
What drives our opinion
Finland is one of the more attractive markets in Telecoms as it is a
three-player mobile and fixed broadband market, with a balanced
and stable market share among them. Therefore, competition is
generally rational, price increases are regular, and promotional
periods tend to be short-lived. Finland is also a mobile-centric market,
with high prices per gigabyte. Elisa, thus, remains among the most
defensive telco’s, with stable healthy growth of revenues, earnings,
and dividends. As a result, Elisa has consistently earned returns on
capital in excess of the cost of capital. Also, Elisa share are currently
trading in line with the sector, while they have historically traded near
a 30% premium. We view this relative valuation as attractive. The
dividend is well covered and we see limited balance sheet risk.
However, while the mobile market is healthy, we do note some recent
promotional activity on sims that could create some near-term
volatility by keeping churn higher for longer. If the promotional
period continues for long, we could see risks to the mobile market,
which is Elisa’s largest standalone segment. Elisa could also relatively
underperform as it is less exposed to potential incremental M&As
compared to its peers. Other risks would include rising electricity
costs, labor cost inflation, greater competition and a macroeconomic
slowdown.

There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
We like the exposure to quality assets within the telecommunications
industry and appreciate Inwit’s organic execution.
Company profile
Inwit is Italy’s largest mobile tower infrastructure operator with just
below 50% market share of sites. Originally a Telecom Italia company,
it merged operations in Italy in 2020 with Vodafone. Inwit has some
of the most favourable master service agreements in the industry
with its two anchor tenants Telecom Italia and Vodafone, thanks to
high levels of CPI pass-through and consolidation protection. Inwit
aims to own c20% of land under its sites by 2026. Key shareholders
include private equity funds Ardian and KKR.
What drives our opinion
Tower companies offer a defensive, fixed-income annuity-like revenue
stream with inflation hedges. Inwit in particular has a good position
in Italy, underpinned by relationships via its two anchor operators
(Telecom Italia and Vodafone). Inwit’s key characteristic is it has about
95% gearing to the consumer price index (CPI), with no caps on the
upside and a 0% floor thanks to its two anchors tenants being
subject to 100% CPI pass-through. This provides visibility on futures
returns. Risks for Inwit include a consolidation of the Italian market by
one of the mobile network operators. Also, contract renegotiations
with its anchor tenants could materially affect Inwit’s future growth.

Why it is Most Preferred
We see recent M&A activity in Orange’s core French market as
supportive of improved unit economics, churn, and return on capital.
Starting from a low point, we are increasingly convinced the
underlying French telco market is inflecting positively. Orange offers
improving end-market dynamics, inflecting growth, capex
optimization, and an attractive valuation relative to the sector.
Second-quarter results were healthily ahead of expectations,
demonstrating the early returns to market repair even before the
completion of M&A. We use the recent volatility in French domestic
stocks as an opportunity ad exposure.
Company profile
Orange is the French telecom incumbent, with a leading position in
both the fixed (c40% broadband subscriber market share) and mobile
segments (c30% mobile subscriber market share). The French unit
represents roughly half of the company’s EBITDAaL and OpFCF.
Orange has operations across Europe, Africa and the Middle East. The
French state (directly or with Bpifrance) owns a c20% stake and
controls c27% of the voting rights. Company employees own a c5%
stake and control c9% of the voting rights.
What drives our opinion
Orange’s core telco markets stand to improve after significant in-
market consolidation activity in France. We see the market inflecting,
with more rational levels of competition intensity and synergies. We
see the most immediate benefit in churn and network optimizations
rather than through pure price increases in the near term. Over the
medium term, we would expect some benefit from pricing power in
Orange’s core markets. Given our view of improving market
dynamics, above-sector-average top-line and FCF growth, and
stronger medium-term visibility, we believe Orange should trade at
least in line with the its sector peers, despite its current discount.
Moreover, early signs of improvement in the French market prior to
the full consolidation of M&A has, in our view, derisked near-term
guidance. Combined with the recent volatility in French domestic
stocks, we believe Orange is tactically attractive with a visible path
towards rerating. We expect the medium-term improvement in
France to be increasingly reflected in Orange’s multiple over the next
few years and would note that telco investors typically adjust slowly
to improved market conditions, leaving much of the upside currently
underpriced in Orange’s stock, in our view. Key risks to our thesis
would be limited improvements in the competitive dynamics in both
France and Spain, changes in regulatory policy, FX/macro, sentiment
around unit economics, and pressure from Satellites or consumer AI
agents lowering switching costs for Orange customers.

Why it is Most Preferred
We view the broader sell-off in the classifieds industry as unjustified

Company profile
Scout24 is an innovative digital company and operates ImmoScout24,

What drives our opinion
Scout24 benefits from a strong market presence and high barriers to

rising profitability. The average penetration of Scout24’s platforms
within its core real estate vertical remains low relative to European
peers, providing a substantial runway for long-term revenue growth,
in our view. Moreover, Scout24’s unique three-sided marketplace—
serving landlords, real estate agents, and consumers—offers
additional defensiveness and unique growth opportunities by
balancing volume and pricing growth across the network. This
structure has made the business more resilient to the business cycle in
the past. We therefore see good prospects for above-average growth,

form of additional operating and capital expenditures on technology,
which could result in more stagnant margins. However, we believe
this is already reflected in the current valuation. Recent acquisitions in

centered on favorable market trends in Germany and Austria, and
raise questions about capital allocation going forward. While we view
the size of these acquisitions as limited—with upside optionality given
the Spanish assets’ low operating performance and margins—they
could dilute group margins more than expected.

Why it is Most Preferred
We favor Spotify for its diversification value, as we see it as a

Company profile
Spotify is a digital music, podcast, and video streaming service
launched in 2008. Spotify offers two tiers: 1) A premium service that
provides subscribers with unlimited online and offline access to its
catalog of tracks for a monthly fee. 2) An ad-supported service that
provides users with limited on-demand online access to the catalog
for no fee. With over 500m MAUs and over 200m subscribers,
Spotify is among the most popular streaming media platforms.
Spotify makes substantially all of its revenue through its premium
service, with the rest through advertising.
What drives our opinion
We believe Spotify is structurally well-positioned in global audio
streaming, with a proven ability to scale its freemium model across
both developed and emerging markets. Its expanding content
ecosystem, spanning music, podcasts, audiobooks, and increasingly
video, drives deeper user engagement and unlocks multiple
monetization levers, in our view. We think Spotify’s investments in
creator tools, automation, and advertising infrastructure are
enhancing platform utility and reinforcing its competitive edge. These
initiatives, combined with a disciplined approach to product
innovation, position the company to capture long-term value from
shifting consumer behavior and the broader transition to digital
media consumption, in our view. We are encouraged by
management’s consistent execution and ability to balance growth
with profitability. Spotify is demonstrating operating leverage through
margin expansion and cost discipline, even as it invests in new
formats and global reach. With strong free cash flow generation and
a clear path to sustained earnings growth, we think the company
offers a compelling mix of scale, innovation, and resilient growth. Key
risks include slower-than-expected user growth, pricing constraints,
macroeconomic headwinds, and intensifying competition from large
tech platforms or AI native platforms.

Why it is Most Preferred
We see Sunrise as an attractive dividend opportunity in the Swiss

Company profile
Sunrise is the number two player in the Swiss telecoms market with
c30% market share in the mobile, broadband and TV segments. Its
strategy is to stabilise its main brand (Sunrise), and grow via flanker
brands and in B2B.
What drives our opinion
After being carved out of Liberty Global, Sunrise started trading as a
standalone entity on the Swiss stock exchange in November 2024.
The stock offers a high dividend yield, which is more than double that
of the Swiss market average. Coupled with stable capex plans, its
dividend is well covered by strong and steady free cash flow
generation. With currently high amortizations and depreciations
(D&A) only gradually falling, the company is set to report solid net
profits from 2027 onward. The high D&A is not cash relevant and
thus no cause for concern to us. We expect Sunrise to see stable to
low revenue growth over the coming years, with small market share
gains. The Swiss market has a benign regulatory climate, market
structure, and demographics; however, it remains rather promotional.
Headwinds from repricing of former UPC customers are now fading
away. As a result of the large UPC acquisition in 2020, Sunrise has a
high net-debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 4.5x currently. Aside from paying an
attractive dividend, the group intends to use its strong cash flows to
gradually lower its debt leverage. With its debt at a fixed cost of
2.8% and a weighted average life of five years, we see the balance
sheet as manageable. Risks include governance due to its dual-class
shareholding structure. Other important corporate risks include
economic trends and policies, competition from satellite mobile
direct-to-device services, cybersecurity, politics, reputation,
operational execution, and product liabilities.

services as underpriced and underpenetrated. We see UMG as
offering high-quality, defensive exposure to music
Company profile
Universal Music Group (UMG) is the world’s leading music company.
It is the number one recorded music business with over 3 million
recordings, the number one music merchandising business with over
250 artists/brands, the number one music-based visual entertainment
business with over 4 trillion minutes of cumulative watch time, and
the number two music publisher with some 4 million owned and
adminstered titles. In 2024, based on IFPI data, UMG had a 30%
share of the global recorded music market.
What drives our opinion
Music labels have become attractive with the advent of paid music
streaming. Indeed, growth through paid streaming has offset the
piracy-led structural decline in the music industry’s revenues in the
1990s and early 2000s. Today, paid streaming penetration remains in
the high-single-digit range globally, with leading countries such as
those in Scandinavia closer to 55-60%. Furthermore, the price
consumers pay for music is much lower today, at around USD 10 per
user per month for an all-you-can-eat buffet of music, compared to
about USD 15 for one CD. For these two reasons, we see a long
runway of subscriber and pricing growth, and UMG is well positioned
to capture its fair share of this, in our view. Music streaming is unique
in that the backlog of music is pivotal to listeners. Unlike film or TV,
consumers regularly re-listen to their favorite songs; and for that
reason, UMG is well positioned. Its backlog is irreplaceable, in our
view. Risks to our view include continued headwinds to streaming
growth; business mix that could delay operating leverage, as lower-
margin businesses such as physical sales have seen growth in recent
years; and subscribers’ willingness to pay for music streaming may be
lower than what the market expects over the longer term. Adverse FX
risks could also impact operating performance.
