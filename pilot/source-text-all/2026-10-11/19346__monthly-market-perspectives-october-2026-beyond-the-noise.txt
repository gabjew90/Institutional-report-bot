We remain constructive on equities. While the past month brought 
renewed volatility, with escalating tensions between the US and Iran 
pushing oil prices back above USD 100 per barrel and reigniting concerns 
over the Strait of Hormuz, the key challenge for markets came from a 
sharp repricing of Fed expectations following Jackson Hole. This pushed 
the 10-year Treasury yield to its highest level since 2007.

Even so, the macroeconomic backdrop remains supportive. Labor markets 
continue to show resilience, corporate earnings momentum remains 
strong, and AI-related capital expenditure continues to provide an 
important tailwind for growth.

We maintain our overweight position in emerging market (EM) equities. 
Tighter financial conditions and a stronger US dollar have recently weighed 
on sentiment, but North Asian technology exporters continue to benefit 
from robust AI-related hardware spending, while monetary easing in parts 
of Latin America provides additional support. The recent pullback has also 
resulted in more balanced positioning, while continued earnings growth 
leaves valuations attractive.


USD: The US dollar is benefiting from higher US rate expectations, sticky 
inflation, and safe-haven demand driven by geopolitical uncertainty. 
However, the recent strength appears cyclical rather than the start of a 
new long-term dollar bull market. Over the medium term, softer US growth, 
narrowing interest rate differentials, and ongoing fiscal imbalances are 
likely to limit further upside.

EUR: The euro’s outlook has improved as ECB policy expectations remain 
relatively hawkish, supported by resilient euro-area growth and ongoing 
fiscal stimulus. With positioning no longer stretched, investors have room 
to rebuild euro exposure if relative growth and yield dynamics continue to 
strengthen. As a result, EUR/USD has renewed upside potential toward 
the 1.15 to 1.20 range, while EUR/CHF could advance toward 0.95.

CHF: The Swiss franc continues to benefit from safe-haven demand, 
Switzerland’s strong external position, and elevated geopolitical 
uncertainty. However, a significant yield disadvantage versus the US and 
euro area, and the SNB’s reluctance to tolerate excessive franc strength 
should limit further appreciation.

Gold prices faced a tactical pullback in the first half of September. After last 
month’s strong momentum driven by soft data prints, recent US 
macroeconomic releases and surging energy costs have revived inflation 
worries, shifting market expectations toward a more hawkish Federal


Rising Treasury yields and a firmer US dollar have introduced short-term



remains intact, supported by expanding fiscal deficits, elevated 
government spending and persistent geopolitical tensions.

Public

Commodities in general: In 2026, commodities have once again 
demonstrated their role as effective inflation hedge, particularly when 
supply shocks are a primary driver of rising prices. They also provide 
protection against uncertainty linked to geopolitical tensions, reinforcing 
their diversification benefits. However, with risks expected to ease ahead 
of the US midterm elections and oil supply gradually coming back online, a 
neutral stance on commodities appears justified. 

Crude oil: Brent crude climbed towards USD 110 per barrel in mid-
September as tensions between the United States and Iran increasingly 
resembled a modern-day tanker war. While Iran retains the ability to 
disrupt flows through the Strait of Hormuz, we doubt that the current 
escalation can continue indefinitely. Global oil markets are gradually 
becoming less dependent on the Strait, while the conflict is weighing 
heavily on Iran’s economy. At the same time, political pressure is building 
on the US administration as higher energy prices lift inflation expectations 
and criticism of another potential “forever war” grows. Taken together, we 
believe both sides will ultimately be pushed back towards negotiations, 
limiting the medium-term upside for oil prices.

Alternative strategies: In times of high economic and political uncertainty, 
we like investments with decorrelated returns, which can currently be 
found at insurance-linked securities (ILS).
