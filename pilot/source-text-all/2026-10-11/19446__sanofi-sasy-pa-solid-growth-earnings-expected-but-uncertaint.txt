While we believe Sanofi management have ticked off one of the 
major overhangs in investor minds by adding assets into the 
collaboration with Regeneron and helping to repair this important 
relationship, Sanofi shares sit lower than before the deal was 
announced. Part of that is market dynamics, in our view, but also 
part of this relates to uncertainty among investors as to the strategic 
direction of the company, and concerns over how Sanofi looks to 
grow in the future (i.e. the mix of organic vs. inorganic and M&A vs. 
licensing as well as size of deals). We expect that many investors will 
remain on the sidelines until they have greater clarity over the 
strategic direction, despite the punitive multiple the stock is trading 
at (7.3x 2027E EPS - being a 45% discount to the sector). Execution 
could be a lever, helping to generate cash to deliver on the strategic 
vision - however, much of our c.5% earnings upside beyond 2026 is 
driven by Dupixent which is a double-edged sword given it would 
increase the cash in the company, but also increase the depth of the 
post 2031 patent cliff. For the quarter we are broadly in line with 
Vara consensus on sales (4% ahead on Dupixent but -4% below on 
Vaccines), but c.5% below on operating profit, as consensus does 
not fully take into account the step up we anticipate in the Other 
Operating Expense, as the Regeneron R&D reimbursement rolls off. 
We remain Neutral rated on Sanofi and while our target price now 
implies double-digit upside, we feel more clarity on the strategic 
direction is needed before we can become more positive.

After updating for FX, company commentary, prescription trends and read across, we 
are in line with Vara consensus for 3Q26 sales, with our Dupixent forecasts 4% ahead, 
Altuviiio forecasts in line and Ayvakit forecasts 5% below. On Vaccines we are 4% below 
consensus, in line with company expectations for a low to mid teens sales decline 
anticipated in 3Q26, driven by our lower Influenza and Beyfortus estimates (-11% and 
-4% below consensus respectively) offset by our higher meningitis/travel and endemic 
vaccine estimates (+9% above consensus). On both Business Operating income and 
Business EPS we are c.-5% below consensus driven by our higher estimates for Other 
operating expense (+19%/+c.300mn vs. consensus, likely due to consensus not factoring 
in the end of Regeneron development cost reimbursement), offset partly by our lower 
R&D and SG&A estimates. 
Key factors to consider into the quarter:  
Dupixent: IQVIA prescription trends indicate c.5% absolute US TRx QoQ growth in 
n
3Q26 considering the impacts of public holidays over July and a stablisation 
following slowdown seen across key derma related biologics. We forecast c.25% CER 
growth in 3Q26 in US and c.16-17% CER growth ex-US. Of note 2Q26 sales were 
boosted by favourable gross-to-net and 340B adjustments, with the 340B benefit 
likely continue in 3Q26, but the GTN dynamics could wash out. We forecast €5.2bn in 
sales for 3Q26, +4% above pre 3Q Vara consensus. 
Beyfortus: We forecast €652mn for 3Q26, -4% below pre 3Q Vara consensus as it 
n
begins to face competitive pressure from Merck’s Enflonsia. Aide memoire highlights

n
the quarter, in line with pre 3Q consensus estimates 
Ayvakit: We forecast €205mn in 3Q26 and are -5% below pre 3Q consensus. Aide 
n
memoire notes that higher government rebates due to Blueprint becoming part of 
Sanofi will continue to have a negative impact on US sales in 2026.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
