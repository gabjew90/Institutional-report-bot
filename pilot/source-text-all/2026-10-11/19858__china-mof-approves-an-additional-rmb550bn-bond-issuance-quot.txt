China: MOF approves an additional RMB550bn bond issuance quota to 
ensure full-year growth target is reached
11 October 2026 | 1:49PM HKT
Bottom line: 
Following the 28 September State Council meeting and credit easing measures 
announced on 29 September, the Ministry of Finance (MOF) recently approved an 
additional RMB550bn local government bond issuance quota using those quotas 
carried over from previous years, in line with our expectations. We expect 
government bond issuance and the deployment of proceeds to accelerate in the 
coming weeks, alongside faster implementation of the RMB800bn New Policy-Based 
Financial Instrument. The incremental policy measures introduced since 
end-September point to targeted, measured easing underway—mainly through 
fiscal and credit channels—rather than broad-based, significant stimulus. They 
appear intended to reverse earlier tightening and secure this year’s “4.5–5.0%” 
growth target, rather than to launch a sustained easing cycle, in our view.  
Main points: 
1. Recent policy efforts on fiscal easing: Following the 28 September State Council 
meeting and credit easing measures announced on 29 September, the Ministry of 
Finance (MOF) recently approved an additional RMB550bn local government bond 
issuance quota using those quotas carried over from previous years. This move is in 
line with our expectations, echoing Premier Li Qiang’s recent call to “strengthen 
counter-cyclical policy adjustment” and “strive to achieve the full-year economic and 
social development targets”. The RMB550bn is equivalent to 4.6% of the RMB11.9tn 
government bond new issuance quota approved in this year’s budget report and 
0.4% of nominal GDP, by our estimates. According to the MOF announcement, the 
additional quota comprises RMB300bn of local government general bonds (LGGB) to 
strengthen local government finances and RMB250bn of local government special 
bonds (LGSB) prioritizing regions with ready-to-go projects for Q4, major economic 
powerhouse provinces, and “Six Networks” projects (“六张网”项目).1 At a symposium 
with private enterprises on 10 October, NDRC Head Zheng Shanjie pledged to 
accelerate the launch of major investment projects planned under the “15th 
Five-Year Plan” and the “Six Networks” initiative. We expect government bond 
issuance and the deployment of proceeds to accelerate in the coming weeks (Exhibit 
1), alongside faster implementation of the RMB800bn New Policy-Based Financial 
Instrument (NPBFI). Although the PBOC’s Pledged Supplementary Lending (PSL) and 
policy bank bond net issuance—two key funding sources for NPBFI—contracted in

2. Available fiscal capacity for Q4: Following recent policy developments, we estimate 
that roughly RMB5tn in fiscal capacity remains available for the remainder of this year. 
This includes RMB3.1tn in unused government bond issuance quota as of 
end-September approved in the budget (equivalent to 26% of the full-year quota), plus 
the recently approved RMB550bn extra-budget local government bond issuance quota, 
the RMB800bn NPBFI now starting to be deployed (up from RMB500bn last year), and 
the RMB550bn year-on-year increase in outstanding fiscal deposits (as of August, the 
latest data available). After recent fiscal easing measures, we estimate that unused 
government bond issuance quota carried over from previous years will decline from 
RMB1.8tn at end-2025 to RMB1.2tn at end-2026. This remaining quota comprises 
RMB0.6tn in central government bonds and RMB0.6tn in local government bonds, 
providing a potential fiscal buffer for future years. 
3. Macro and policy implications: The incremental policy measures introduced since 
end-September point to targeted, measured easing underway—mainly through fiscal 
and credit channels—rather than broad-based, significant stimulus. They appear 
intended to reverse earlier tightening and ensure this year’s “4.5–5.0%” growth target is 
reached (Exhibit 3), rather than to launch a sustained easing cycle, in our view. Pressure 
on local government finances from off-budget sources—particularly land sales and 
LGFV financing—is likely to persist. Assuming the announced fiscal funding is deployed 
by year-end, we expect China’s augmented fiscal deficit (AFD) to widen modestly from 
its recent trough, easing the growth drag from earlier fiscal tightening in the coming 
months. However, tighter tax collection and weak incentives for local officials to launch 
large projects amid intensified anti-corruption efforts and local leadership reshuffles 
may continue to constrain policy transmission. Given this year’s delayed easing, our 2026 
AFD forecast of 11.5% of GDP (vs. 11.0% in 2025) remains subject to downside risk, 
implying that fiscal policy could still tighten on a year-on-year basis. Recent policy 
communications pointed to continued priority on high-tech sectors and investment over 
consumption, in our view.
