•
How The Current Bull Market Compares With History | While the current bull market celebrates its fourth birthday next week, the
average bull market lasts about five and a half years. Yet there has been nothing average about this one. The S&P 500 has gained ~117% on
a price return basis, surpassing the historical average of ~90% and ranking as the third-strongest bull market since 1945. Along the way, it
has produced 124 record highs, more than double the average (~50) of the seven prior bull markets that lasted at least four years. Despite
those outsized gains, volatility has remained below average, with just two corrections of 10% or more (vs. an average of ~5 corrections in
prior bull markets). More importantly, the rally has been fueled by improving fundamentals. Next twelve-month (NTM) earnings have
climbed ~90% since the bull market began, the second-strongest EPS growth at this stage behind only the 2002 bull market, while
valuations have expanded just 20%. Put simply, earnings growth, not multiple expansion, has been the primary engine of returns.
•
All Sectors Have Participated, But Technology Leads The Way | While all eleven S&P 500 sectors have delivered positive returns during
the current bull market, Technology has been the undisputed leader. From a price-only perspective (excludes dividends) the tech sector
has surged 260%, outpacing the next-best performer, Communication Services (+195%), by ~65%, while no other sector has topped a 100%
gain. Powerful secular growth drivers, led by the AI megatrend, have fueled much of this outperformance. As a result, Technology now
represents more than 40% of the S&P 500's market capitalization, up from 26% when the bull market began and 34% at the end of 2025.
Importantly, consensus estimates project the sector will generate ~38% of S&P 500 earnings by 2027, versus 22% at the start of the bull
market, reinforcing our constructive outlook. Just as notably, earnings growth has largely kept pace with share prices, helping valuations
remain attractive over time. After peaking at a 31.1x NTM P/E in October 2025, the highest level since May 2002, the sector's valuation has
normalized to its 30-year average of 21.5x.
•
History Remains On The Bull’s Side | While the average bull market lasts about five and a half years, history suggests this cycle may still
have room to run. Bull markets that make it into a fifth year have generally continued to generate positive returns, and the last one to reach
four years but not five ended in 1957, nearly 70 years ago. Historically, the fifth year of a bull market has delivered average returns of ~13%
and has been positive nearly 90% of the time. The post-midterm election backdrop also provides a potential tailwind. Since 1942, the S&P
500 has returned an average of ~15% in the 12 months following a midterm election and has finished higher after all 21 midterm elections.
While history never guarantees future results, the historical backdrop remains encouraging.
•
Strong Fundamentals Point To Further Upside | Consistent with history, our bull market checklist points to continued gains. The US
economy continues to show limited recession risk, while earnings growth remains exceptionally strong for this stage of the cycle, with
consensus forecasting 15% S&P 500 earnings growth in 2027. AI spending trends remain a powerful tailwind, with Tech earnings expected
to increase 41% in 2027, while earnings growth continues to broaden across the rest of the market with eight of 11 sectors expected to post
positive EPS growth. This strength should be on display during the 3Q26 earnings season, which begins next week. Meanwhile, valuations
have become less concerning as earnings growth has driven the S&P 500's forward P/E ratio below its prior 10-year average. The two
biggest risks remain higher interest rates and elevated investor optimism, with equity allocations at their highest level since December
2017. Still, sentiment can stay elevated for long periods, and both the economy and earnings have proven resilient in the face of higher
rates. As a result, we believe strong earnings growth and attractive valuations will be the key driver of market performance.
Bottom Line | As the current bull market enters year five, history and fundamentals continue to point in a favorable direction. With economic
growth holding up, earnings remaining strong, and AI-driven investment trends intact, we expect the S&P 500 to climb to 8,450 over the next
12 months (~9% upside from current levels).

and sports legends like Pelé to cultural icons such as Gandhi, John Lennon, and Eleanor
Roosevelt. Speaking of birthdays, financial markets have a birthday worth celebrating, too.

year, a milestone reached only seven other times since 1949. Despite a steady stream of
concerns, including tariffs, Middle East tensions, and rising interest rates, the bull market
has remained remarkably resilient, climbing higher on the back of strong economic growth
and solid earnings growth. Importantly, history suggests bull markets that reach year five
have often delivered favorable returns. Below, we compare the current bull market with
past cycles, highlight the key leaders behind the market’s advance, and assess whether the
rally still has room to run.

OCTOBER 9, 2026
Economy
•
The ISM Services PMI was slightly weaker than expected in September at 54.9 but remained firmly in expansion, with the Employment
subindex returning to expansionary territory. The Prices subindex increased to 74, highlighting persistent price pressures.
•
Fueled by record imports, the trade deficit in August reached $105.6B—the widest since March 2025, when imports were boosted by
pre-tariff stockpiling. The trade deficit could weigh on third-quarter real GDP growth despite continued tariff efforts to curb imports.
•
October’s Michigan Sentiment declined more than expected to 46.3 and reached its second lowest level on record, driven by a sharp
decline in ‘current conditions’. One- and five-year inflation expectations rose for a 2nd month in a row to 4.7% and 3.5%, respectively.
•
Focus of the Week: Next week is inflation week, beginning with the all-important CPI report on Wednesday. We expect headline and
core CPI to have risen 0.45% and 0.2% MoM, respectively. On Thursday, markets expect headline and core PPI to have risen by 0.45%
and 0.25% MoM, respectively, during September. Finally, September Export and Import price indices will be released on Friday.
Equity
•
The 3Q26 earnings season is set to kick off next week, with consensus estimates pointing to another strong quarter for corporate
fundamentals. The S&P 500 is expected to deliver 29% year-over-year EPS growth in 3Q26, marking the third consecutive quarter of at
least 25% earnings growth for the index, a feat not achieved since 2021. In addition, earnings revisions continue to signal underlying
strength, with 62% of companies issuing positive 3Q26 EPS guidance thus far, the highest share since 2021. While higher rates will likely
keep volatility and dispersion elevated near-term, robust earnings underpin our long-term constructive view on equities.
•
From a sector perspective, AI beneficiaries continue to be the primary driver of index earnings growth, with the Technology and
Communication Services sectors expected to deliver year-over-year EPS growth of 63% and 53%, respectively. However, excluding
these AI-related beneficiaries, S&P 500 earnings are still projected to grow a healthy 15%, well above the long-term average of 8%.
Much of this resilience can be attributed to strong margin execution despite concerns surrounding higher energy prices and other
potential cost pressures. In fact, net profit margins are expected to improve relative to levels at the onset of the Middle East conflict
across eight of the eleven S&P 500 sectors – with the overall S&P 500 net margin expected to remain elevated at 16%.
•
Focus of the Week: Next week, markets will be focused on earnings results from the major US banks, as investors assess how the
recent rise in interest rates is affecting net interest income, loan growth, and overall profitability.
Fixed Income
•
Treasury yields appear to be stabilizing after a relentless seven-month rise that has pushed 2-year and 10-year yields more than 100 bps
higher. Notably, nearly half of that increase occurred in September alone. With investor sentiment towards bonds nearly universally
bearish, this week’s Treasury auctions offered an important test of demand with yields at multi-decade highs. Strong bid-to-cover
ratios, particularly for the longer-dated maturities, suggest investors are willing to lock in today’s elevated yields. We agree, current
yield levels offer a compelling opportunity for long-term investors.
•
This week’s FOMC minutes confirmed there was broad support for September’s rate hike. However, a softer jobs report, downward
revisions to core PCE, and recent comments from Fed officials suggest policymakers are in no rush to tighten further. While Governor
Waller downplayed the recent improvement in the 3-month annualized inflation trend, which is running near the Fed’s 2.0% target, he
also shared the Fed can afford to wait. October rate hike odds remain at just 19% ahead of the October 27-28 meeting.
•
Focus of the Week: CPI and PPI with Fed Chair Warsh set to appear at the IMF/World Bank meetings in Bangkok late Thursday night.
Washington Policy
•
The Iran conflict remains a key geopolitical risk as Congress departs until after the midterms, with the current situation remaining
fragile. President Trump also said the US would not resume strikes before the November 3 elections, citing ongoing discussions, even
as reports suggest US Central Command has been directed to prepare for renewed offensive operations.
•
The administration also took a targeted step on diesel affordability, temporarily allowing tax-exempt dyed diesel (normally restricted
to off-road uses) to be used on highways through year-end while directing Treasury to pursue deferral of the associated federal diesel
excise tax. With the federal diesel tax at 24.4 cents per gallon, the measure could provide near-term savings for truckers and farmers,
although the underlying tax liability has not been permanently eliminated.
International
•
As the latest case study of post-COVID political swings, Brazil may be poised for a new president. In the October 25 run-off, challenger
Flavio Bolsonaro (from the right) could unseat incumbent Lula da Silva (from the left), just as Lula unseated the current candidate’s
father in 2022. This week’s gains in Brazilian stocks (which set an all-time high), bonds, and currency indicate that investors see
advantages from a prospective Bolsonaro presidency, though we doubt the durability of this rally. As we explained prior to round one
of voting, its congress remains splintered across many parties, potentially impeding Bolsonaro’s promises to cut the budget deficit.
•
Meanwhile, Spain will hold a snap general election on November 29. After eight years of socialist control, polls indicate a firm lead for
the conservative opposition. Spain’s parliamentary system means that the multi-party coalition with a majority in parliament will
name the next prime minister and cabinet. The new government will need to tackle unaffordable housing—the issue that prompted
the snap election. Another pressing issue is unemployment: at 10.0%, it is the highest among the euro zone’s larger economies. The
labor market has been in a slow recovery ever since Spain was near the center of the European debt crisis in 2011/12.
