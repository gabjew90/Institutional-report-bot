Unemployment Shows A Steady Labor Market
Global Central Bank Tightening Returns
•
September’s payrolls report was weaker than expected with the economy adding just 
29k jobs while revisions subtracted 60k jobs from the prior two months. Three-month 
average job growth slowed to 51k, but the unemployment rate (4.2%) has held broadly steady 
for over two years, reinforcing the low-hire, low-fire employment backdrop. 
•
PCE inflation was revised lower in July and increased less than consensus expectations in 
August, even when accounting for methodology changes. On a YoY basis, headline (3.4%) 
and core PCE (3.0%) stayed unchanged from July to August. Although the lower readings are 
encouraging, with inflation still above 3% the data is unlikely to alter the Fed’s tightening bias.    
•
US business activity surged in September, with the S&P Composite PMI rising to 58.4—
the strongest expansion since July 2021—as services (58.7) and manufacturing (56.7) output 
accelerated. Stronger demand also drove the fastest hiring growth in over four years, but 
supply-chain strain and higher fuel costs intensified price pressures. 
•
September marked the first time ever the Fed, ECB, and BOJ all raised rates in the same 
month, underscoring growing global inflation concerns. Over the last six months, 45% of 
global central banks have tightened policy in response to renewed inflation pressures driven 
largely by higher energy prices due to the conflict in the Middle East. 
•
The US economy enters Q4 on solid footing. Consumer spending is stronger than sentiment 
data would suggest, supported by a healthy labor market and rising household wealth. 
Meanwhile, AI capital investment remains a powerful tailwind, underpinning our 2.3% 2026 
growth forecast. The expansion is set to continue into 2027, with growth moderating to 2.2%. 
•
The fuel price shock and AI-related capital investment have kept inflation elevated, but 
we expect cost pressures to subside. As fuel prices moderate, this should support cooling 
inflation across the economy’s goods-related sectors. Alongside AI-driven productivity gains, 
PCE inflation should continue decelerating through next year and beyond. 
•
The labor market remains healthy as job gains have stabilized at a lower equilibrium 
level. We expect payroll growth to average ~70k/month, keeping unemployment stable near 
4.2% through 2027. While AI may reshape some roles, it should primarily boost productivity 
and shift employment toward new opportunities rather than drive broad-based job losses.
•
While the US economic cycle is maturing, it continues to show strength and is poised to 
outpace its developed market peers. Weaker domestic demand, less exposure to AI-driven 
capex, and low productivity growth remain headwinds to growth overseas. Within EM, we 
expect EM Asia to outperform Latam.

Headline Index Strength Masks Weak Breadth
Stock Performance Is Not Uniform
•
The S&P 500 largely shrugged off the climb in Treasury yields, rising 2.3% QoQ and 
ending the period just 1.9% below its mid-August record high. AI beneficiaries continued 
to drive returns, and the rally was increasingly concentrated, with only 21% constituents 
trading above their 50-day moving average, down from 70% earlier in the quarter.
•
Gains in Energy and Tech helped offset weakness in rate-sensitive sectors, such as Real 
Estate and Utilities, while a flatter yield curve pressured Financials. Notably, roughly 40% of 
S&P 500 stocks are currently in a bear market (down 20% or more), which indicates an equity 
landscape that is far weaker than the headline index level would suggest.
•
Small caps bore the brunt of higher rates in Q3, with the Russell 2000 falling 7.2% and 
posting its widest underperformance vs. large caps (-9.5%) since 1Q20. With floating-rate 
debt comprising 54% of small-cap debt vs. 22% for large caps, smaller firms were more 
exposed to the hawkish Fed repricing and 74 bps increase in the 2-year Treasury yield QoQ.  
•
Japanese equities gained 6.1% in Q3, supported by a stronger yen, exposure to AI and 
semiconductor demand, improving manufacturing activity, and still accommodative financial 
conditions. In contrast, European equities fell 2.6%, lagging amid limited AI exposure, 
French fiscal policy challenges, and a modest currency headwind. 
•
The US bull market enters its 5th year later in October with supportive tailwinds. 
Corporate earnings have been unusually strong for this stage of the cycle and should remain 
supportive as earnings season reemerges. The historical track record suggests that fifth-year 
bull markets and the 12 months after midterm elections typically produce positive returns. 
•
Despite Fed rate hikes, Treasury yields at multi-decade highs, and geopolitical 
turbulence, the earnings outlook should help offset near-term obstacles. We expect the 
S&P 500 to climb to 8,450 ($385 EPS/~22x P/E) over the next 12 months. Robust earnings 
growth and improved valuations should provide a favorable backdrop for equities.
•
Tech, Industrials, and Consumer Discretionary are our preferred sectors. Strong earnings 
and moderating valuations support Tech, while infrastructure, power generation, and 
defense spending favor Industrials. Consumer Discretionary should benefit from job gains 
and rising incomes, and expectations have come down after a period of underperformance.
•
The US remains our preferred destination for equity allocations, supported by stronger 
growth, superior earnings momentum, and AI leadership. Abroad, we favor EM Asia, one of 
the key beneficiaries of AI investment, and Japan for its solid earnings and tech exposure. 
Meanwhile, LatAm stocks could face headwinds from lower oil prices next year.

•
The Fed unanimously raised its policy rate by 25 bps to 3.75%–4.00%, its first hike in 
three years, following Chair Warsh’s hawkish Jackson Hole speech. The updated dot plot 
signaled one additional hike by year-end and steady rates through 2027, a path consistent 
with a mid-cycle adjustment rather than the start of a prolonged tightening cycle.
•
Higher oil prices, resilient growth, and a hawkish repricing of Fed expectations drove the 
10-year Treasury yield up 85 bps to 5.29%, tying its largest QoQ increase since 2016. The 
rise in yields has been a worldwide phenomenon, with G7 sovereign yields reaching multi-
decade highs. For example, Japan’s 10-year climbed to its highest level (3.08%) since 1996.
•
Dispersion was the dominant theme in credit. IG spreads widened 6 bps QoQ but remained 
historically tight, trading between 73-82 bps. In contrast, CCC-rated spreads widened 237 bps 
to 2023 highs as elevated rates strained weaker issuers. Still, IG lagged (-3.9% QoQ) as its 
longer-duration profile left it more exposed to rising yields. 
•
Munis experienced their worst quarter since 1981 (-6.3%), trailing Treasuries by 3.3%, 
the widest margin since 1Q20. Rising rates, record issuance, and fund outflows fueled the 
sell-off, while the sector’s longer-duration profile amplified the impact of higher yields. The 
10-year AAA muni-to-Treasury ratio climbed to ~80%, its highest level since April 2025. 
•
We expect the Fed to deliver one more hike this year and then hold steady, as compared 
to the nearly four rate hikes over the next 12 months that are currently priced in. Absent 
a meaningful deterioration in the labor market, policymakers are likely to remain patient as 
long as inflation continues to improve. 
•
As inflation moderates and the Fed tightens less than the market expects, the 10-year 
Treasury yield should decline to 4.50%-4.75% over the next year. A prolonged period 
above 5% requires worsening inflation, accelerating growth, and/or a prolonged hiking cycle. 
Continued demand for US Treasuries should also help limit further upside in yields. 
•
After years of depressed bond yields, bonds have once again become a source of return. 
Today’s higher starting yields provide attractive income, a cushion against rising rates, and 
compelling total return potential if economic growth were to slow. More importantly, 
investors can earn generous yields without taking excessive credit risk. 
•
At the sector level, we continue to favor higher quality, preferring Treasuries, short-
duration corporates, and longer-dated munis where relative value has improved. Credit 
spreads remain tight, but historically attractive all-in yields, along with healthy growth and 
solid balance sheets continue to favor high-quality sectors. Spreads are too thin in high yield.

Commodity Index Climbs To Highest Level Since 2012
Supply Disruptions Propel Oil Price Bounce
•
Broad-based gains lifted the Bloomberg Commodity Index 15% in Q3 to its highest level 
in 14 years. Supply constraints drove key input prices to new records. Copper was boosted 
by mine and smelter bottlenecks, while refining disruptions in the Persian Gulf and Russia 
sent diesel to all-time highs—the latest reminder of the oil market’s global nature.
•
WTI crude oil prices surged 30% in Q3 to ~$90/bbl as escalation of the US-Iran conflict 
tightened global supply. After a brief rebound in June/July, traffic via the Strait of Hormuz 
once again declined. Meanwhile, a Houthi attack against Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline 
temporarily reduced supply and accelerated the drawdown of global inventories. 
•
Gold (+3.7% QoQ) and silver (+1.1% QoQ) managed modest gains in Q3 despite rising 
yields around the world. Strong ETF inflows and continued central bank buying gave a boost 
to gold while silver benefited from a tight physical market. However, all precious metals 
remain down sharply from their record highs set in January.
•
Rising real rates and hawkish Fed expectations lifted the dollar index 0.3% QoQ, as 
September’s gains overcame weakness in July and August. Among G10 currencies, the yen 
posted the strongest gain vs. the dollar, up 3.4% QoQ, supported by joint US-Japan 
intervention earlier in the quarter and expectations for further BOJ tightening.
•
The path to a durable US-Iran agreement remains difficult despite renewed diplomacy, 
with both sides prepared for a prolonged standoff. As a result, energy commodities are  
vulnerable to renewed disruptions and higher for longer prices. Still, we expect the broader 
impact on the US economy and financial assets to remain manageable. 
•
Amid curtailed supply, demand destruction is playing a key role in balancing the oil 
market. A portion of this year’s decline in global oil demand could become permanent. 
Longer term, higher prices should encourage more supply, supporting our view that WTI will 
retreat toward $65 over the course of 2027.
•
The backdrop of higher global yields remains a headwind for non-income-producing 
assets, highlighting the role of sentiment in precious metal markets. Industrial metals, by 
contrast, continue to benefit from growing structural demand tied to AI infrastructure, the 
electrification theme, and defense spending. 
•
The USD’s recent gains following the Fed’s hawkish pivot has likely run its course. With 
markets pricing in further hikes from the Fed, ECB, and BOJ, rate differentials should become 
less favorable for the USD. We expect the euro to remain in a 1.10-1.20 range over the coming 
year, while yen weakness is capped due to the threat of further intervention.

EUROPE | The MSCI Europe Index captures large and mid cap representation across 15 Developed Markets (DM) countries in Europe. With 428 constituents, the index covers approximately 85% of the free float-
adjusted market capitalization across the European Developed Markets equity universe. The STOXX Europe 600 (often called the STOXX 600) is a major stock index tracking the 600 largest publicly traded 
companies across 17 European countries.
MSCI EAFE | The MSCI EAFE (Europe, Australasia, and Far East) is a free float-adjusted market capitalization index that is designed to measure developed market equity performance, excluding the United States 
& Canada. The EAFE consists of the country indices of 22 developed nations. 
SOUTH KOREA | The MSCI Korea Index is a stock market benchmark that measures large and mid-cap equity performance, covers about 85% of the South Korean market, and includes 77 constituent companies.
CHINA | The National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) of China Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) is an official monthly economic indicator that measures the health of the country's manufacturing and non-
manufacturing sectors. It is scored from 0 to 100, where 50 is the dividing line: a reading above 50 means the sector is growing, and a reading below 50 means it is shrinking.
MSCI EM | The MSCI Emerging Markets Index captures large and mid cap representation across 25 Emerging Markets (EM) countries. With 1,420 constituents, the index covers approximately 85% of the free float-
adjusted market capitalization in each country.
CITIGROUP ECONOMIC SURPRISE INDEX | Citigroup Economic Surprise Index represents the sum of the difference between official economic results and forecasts. With a sum over 0, its economic performance 
generally beats market expectations. With a sum below 0, its economic conditions are generally worse than expected.
US TRADE POLICY UNCERTAINTY INDEX | The US Trade Policy Uncertainty (TPU) Index is a monthly index that measures how often trade policy and uncertainty terms appear in major newspapers.
PCE INDEX | Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) Index: The PCE price index looks at U.S. inflation by measuring changes in the cost of living for households. It tracks the prices of a basket of goods and 
services, each with different weightings, to reflect how much a typical household spends every month.
ISM MANUFACTURING INDEX | The ISM Manufacturing Index, also known as the purchasing managers' index (PMI), is a monthly indicator of U.S. economic activity based on a survey of purchasing managers at 
more than 300 manufacturing firms.
CPI | The Consumer Price Index (CPI) is a measure of the average change over time in the prices paid by urban consumers for a market basket of consumer goods and services.
EMPLOYMENT COST INDEX | The Employment Cost Index (ECI) measures the change in the hourly labor cost to employers over time.
MOVE INDEX | The MOVE index, or Merrill Lynch Option Volatility Estimate Index, is a gauge of interest rate volatility in the Treasury market.
THE DOW JONES INDUSTRIAL AVERAGE | The Dow Jones Industrial Average, Dow Jones, or simply the Dow, is a stock market index of 30 prominent companies listed on stock exchanges in the United States.
THE RUSSELL 2000 | The Russell 2000 Index is a small-cap U.S. stock market index that makes up the smallest 2,000 stocks in the Russell Index.
THE MSCI EUROPE EX UK | The MSCI Europe ex UK Index captures large and mid cap representation across 14 Developed Markets (DM) countries in Europe excluding UK.
MSCI CHINA INDEX | The MSCI China Index captures large and mid cap companies and covers about 85% of the China equity universe. 
BLOOMBERG PRECIOUS METALS SUBINDEX | The Bloomberg Precious Metals Subindex is a commodity group subindex of the Bloomberg Commodity Index (BCOM) that reflects the returns of gold and silver 
futures contracts.
BLOOMBERG COMMODITY INDEX | The Bloomberg Commodity Index is a broad, diversified benchmark tracking commodity markets through futures contracts, designed for liquid, investable exposure, providing 
inflation protection and portfolio diversification by covering major sectors like energy, metals, agriculture, and livestock, with weights based on liquidity and production, and subject to sector/commodity caps.
