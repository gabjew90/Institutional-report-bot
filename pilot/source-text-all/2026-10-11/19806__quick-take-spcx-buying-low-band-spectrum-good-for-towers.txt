Quick Take: SPCX buying low-band spectrum, good for towers

Grain Management and SpaceX have reached a definitive agreement for Grain’s 800MHZ
spectrum portfolio. We flagged this as a very likely outcome, but it comes roughly a month
before the auction was scheduled to close. Our preliminary read: SPCX is keeping the
terrestrial network build option very much alive. Towers are appropriately trading up after
hours.
The distinction matters: buying spectrum is not a commitment to build a nationwide
network. But this is the spectrum we thought SpaceX needed to make that option more
credible—and less expensive.
On its Q2 earnings call, SpaceX reiterated plans to launch its Mobile business by late
2027. Today’s V1 service is relatively small. The coming V2 constellation—with four times
the receiver size, higher beam density, and a lower altitude than the Starlink broadband
constellation—is a significant step up. Given the investment involved, terrestrial operators
need to take this seriously. There is little doubt SpaceX intends to move forward.
The mobile opportunity is large in the US alone, and SpaceX is already working with MNOs
in more than 30 countries. But to make the economics work, the business must be much
more than dead-zone coverage. The choices remain build, buy, or partner. We still think
partner—i.e., an MVNO—is the right answer and the most likely outcome. SpaceX, however,
keeps putting build on the table.
We have modeled a greenfield terrestrial network in depth, and the conclusion was clear:
SPCX needed low-band spectrum. Even a little low-band cuts the required site count by
~30%, materially improving network efficiency and lowering build costs. Grain’s portfolio
was the only obvious low-band option.
Our estimated greenfield build cost remains $50–$130B including spectrum, or ~$15–
$80B excluding it. That implies ~30k–120k macro sites—unevenly distributed across the
TowerCos, but positive for all. These are build scenarios, not our base case.
For towers, this is good news on two fronts. It chips away at the “towers are obsolete
in a D2D world” narrative, with which we have long vehemently disagreed. And it lends
credibility to a potential new buyer of tower capacity, whether the eventual build is large-
scale or more selective.
Tower multiples are deeply depressed, and the stocks are looking for a catalyst against
a rising-rate backdrop. We still favor a partnership outcome for SpaceX Mobile. But
Grain makes a terrestrial build more credible—and that could put some wind back in the
TowerCos’ sails.
