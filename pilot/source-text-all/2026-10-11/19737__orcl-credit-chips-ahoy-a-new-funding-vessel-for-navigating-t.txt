earnings reaction.

For ORCL specifically, a chip-lease SPV has seemed logical given our views that its 
IG rating could serve a purpose in obtaining financing off of a hypothetical 
commitment (now for chips, not just shells), its USD IG index debt footprint should 
not expand significantly more toward that of large financial issuers, and natural debt 
paydown in the out years could be prudent to incorporate into the complex.
Until recently, however, management appeared content to rely on potential future 
customer prepayments as the extra tool in the funding toolkit. What may have 
changed is not only cost of capital for parent bonds (now at 7.5% for ORCL 10y), but 
also the growing likelihood of a broader wave of creative chip financing – i.e., 
shorter dated, amortizing paper backed by a chip lease or compute contract. Since 
our August NVDA credit initiation, we've had extensive investor discussions on this, 
a recurring theme being uncertainty over the market's capacity to absorb the sheer 
scale of forthcoming GPU/XPU financing needs, with potential scaling beyond 
bank lending and private credit into public markets.
In this context, top of mind – given semiconductor management commentary and a 
June TPU deal – are potential additional structures designed to help unrated and 
high-yield issuers meet upfront capex needs, aided by some form of residual-value 
support or high-quality offtake (or pricing floor support). But it is also possible that 
IG corporates – for upfront capex or even in sale-leaseback format – may see 
value in establishing an early position before creative chip financing possibly 
evolves into a large, crowded asset class.
For ORCL, from a ratings and accounting perspective, we would view a chip-lease 
SPV to be debt-like, with the lease recognized on-balance-sheet at commencement 
and incorporated into adjusted leverage assessments at the parent level. We are 
also watching how already limited lease disclosures could evolve with any 
introduction of 5Y chip operating leases.
If the reported deal indeed plays out, we would appreciate management's efforts to 
diversify funding sources – which may also demonstrate willingness to explore 
other currencies for unsecured and even hybrids. It's possible such a chip lease deal, 
paired with other options, could reduce the need for a CY1H bond deal, or at least 
limit near-term equity issuance or a reliance on prepays. But we suspect any bond-
market reaction to this headline – which appears to be a fair move wider to start – 
will end up being driven less by ORCL-specific supply implications and more by 
investor indigestion around the prospective scale of the broader chip-financing 
wave. For ORCL as an IG 'tenant', we wonder whether its growing stock of leases 
could drive incremental CDS hedging activity. Conversely, any form of NVDA 
support in a hypothetical announced deal – though not currently our base case – 
would be constructive for ORCL.
