 
AI Winners/Losers: US Versus Europe 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, OCT 09, 2026 - 08:15 PM 
AI is causing divergences everywhere, winners and losers, whether it be a K-shaped economy 
or cross-country growth divergence. 
The AI CapEx boom is raising the cost of capital everywhere, through spillover effects, crowding 
out and cross-border issuance... but the benefits are not being shared equally. 
As Goldman Sachs macro trader, Rikin Shah, explains in the brief note below (full note available 
here for pro subs), the US is the clear winner, while Europe is not, and France is evidence that 
some countries will struggle in a higher rate world.  
AI winners vs losers is not just an equity theme, but a macro one too.  
The AI capex boom is US driven, but it is raising the global cost of capital. Higher rates are 
easier to manage for countries with higher nominal growth, but will put pressure on those 
without it. It boils down to a simple “r-g” equation. The US has a high starting point of debt and a 
wide deficit, but nominal growth remains high.  
 

 
Meanwhile France comes into the crosshairs for the wrong reasons in a higher rate world.  
The starting point for debt is high, it has a wide deficit that is not consolidating, but nominal 
growth is lower; “r-g” in France is currently approximately 0, but the marginal cost of financing is 
~250bp higher than the effective interest rate on debt outstanding. Therefore, as debt rolls over 
and needs to be refinanced at current higher rates, “r-g” deteriorates and the debt trajectory will 
worsen. 
Growth is key, and this ultimately becomes a question of which countries or jurisdictions are 
seeing the growth benefit of the capex boom, or just paying the price of it via the higher cost of 
capital. 
In reality the difference in impact on Europe and the US is much bigger than the chart above 
suggests as this only models the productivity gains, and doesn't factor in the potential FCI 
easing contribution if US stocks continue to lead the way. 
What’s clear is that Europe will be a laggard in terms of seeing the growth benefits from AI. 
In addition, further episodes of sovereign spread widening can trigger further FCI tightening in 
Europe, creating another headwind to growth, while energy remains a bigger drag than in the 
US. 
Fiscal has clearly been the supporting factor in Europe for growth this year, but that will begin to 
fade next year. 
 
The US clearly stands out as one of the AI winners; Europe does not and will likely continue to 
struggle in a high rate world, particularly as the fiscal impulse fades.  

 
 
Both the rates and FX markets have started to price this divergence.. whilst this could be tricky 
to chase at current levels, the theme is not going away.
