Erwan Rambourg is back and re-initiating luxury with nine stock calls and a clear message: the sector is
nearing a turning point. After three years of post-COVID normalization driven by pricing excesses, weak
innovation and brand-specific execution issues, Erwan sees Q3 remaining subdued before growth begins
to improve in Q4 and reaccelerates into 2027. His core view is that luxury can return to a more normal
mid-single-digit growth algorithm as product cycles improve, creative reboots gain traction and the US
remains a key source of demand. Please let me know if you would like a call with Erwan to discuss the
sector or any of the stock calls in more detail.
Richemont (purest exposure to compounding jewelry growth), LVMH (leather goods to rebound next
year), Moncler (superior growth with a tangible US opportunity), and Prada (growth at a reasonable price)
with Buy ratings. We initiate on Kering, Burberry and Brunello Cucinelli at Neutral and remain Neutral
rated on Zegna. We initiate on Hermès (change in growth algorithm) and Swatch (relatively limited scale
may influence the pace of margin recovery) with Sell ratings.
BUY Richemont: Purest exposure to compounding jewellery growth, with Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels
providing the strongest listed exposure to the structural growth of branded jewellery; Erwan expects this to
drive sector-leading growth and earnings resilience, yet the shares trade on just 21x FY27 P/E and offer
30% upside to his CHF225 price target.
BUY LVMH: Leather goods growth is set to rebound next year; often viewed as the luxury sector proxy
given its leadership position across categories, Erwan sees a recovery in Fashion & Leather driving a
broader earnings inflection, while the stock trades on only 16x FY27 P/E despite owning many of the
sector's highest-quality assets and offers 32% upside to €500.
BUY Moncler: Superior growth profile with a tangible US opportunity, where continued customer
recruitment and growing brand awareness should support above-sector growth for years to come; despite
this, the shares trade on 16x FY27 P/E and Erwan sees 43% upside to his €62 target.
BUY Prada: Growth at a reasonable price, combining one of the strongest growth profiles in luxury with
one of the lowest valuations in the sector; Erwan sees continued momentum at both Prada and Miu Miu,
yet the shares trade on just 12x FY27 P/E and offer 39% upside to HK$52.
NEUTRAL Kering: The relaunch plan remains unproven, with the key debate centred on whether Gucci
can sustainably rebuild desirability and regain market share; while Erwan sees 15% upside to €250, he
believes investors still require evidence that the turnaround can translate into a durable recovery.

NEUTRAL Burberry: Overweight Britishness and underweight high-end consumers, leaving the company
more exposed to aspirational demand than most luxury peers; despite encouraging turnaround progress
and 23% upside to Erwan's 1,250p target, he sees a more balanced risk-reward profile.
NEUTRAL Brunello Cucinelli: Steady growth comes at a price, with one of the highest-quality and most
consistent growth profiles in luxury already reflected in a premium valuation; despite 18% upside to €93,
the shares trade on 28x FY27 P/E, limiting scope for further re-rating.
NEUTRAL Zegna: A high-quality luxury asset with a resilient customer base and strong execution at the
core brand, but one where much of the quality is already reflected in the share price; Erwan sees 11%
upside to $14 but remains Neutral on valuation grounds.
SELL Hermès: A slowing growth algorithm, where industry-leading growth remains intact but is
moderating from the double-digit levels investors have historically paid a premium for; despite only 4%
upside to Erwan's €1,350 target, the shares continue to trade on roughly 26x FY27 P/E.
SELL Swatch: Limited scale could slow margin recovery, with exposure to a structurally more challenged
segment of luxury and a longer path back to historical profitability; Erwan's CHF174 target implies 6%
downside as earnings recovery remains uncertain.
Erwan’s "7 Cs" Framework
At the heart of the report is Erwan’s “Seven Cs” framework, a structured approach to evaluating which
luxury brands are best positioned to sustain growth, defend market share and compound value over time.
Clout: Scale creates advantages in marketing, distribution, pricing and customer acquisition.
Categories: The right category exposure can be more important than company-specific execution.
Consumer Calibre: Wealthier customers provide more resilient demand through the cycle.
Creativity: Product innovation remains essential to sustaining relevance and growth.
Consistency: Sustained execution is critical in a slower-growth luxury environment.
Codes & Icons: Enduring products and brand symbols drive loyalty, pricing power and recruitment.
Corporate Considerations: Governance and capital allocation often differentiate long-term winners from
losers.
