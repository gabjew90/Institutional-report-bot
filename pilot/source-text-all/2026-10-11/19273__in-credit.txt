Move it 
Bond markets are moving to a beat that equity markets have yet to hear. The 
MOVE Index has surged while the VIX remains near its lows – a divergence 
that has historically preceded broader risk repricing. Read on for a breakdown 
of fixed income news across sectors and regions. 
 
Chart of the Week  
Steven Nelson,  
Client Portfolio Manager, Investment Grade 
 
Since the start of 2025, the MOVE Index and the VIX – measures of implied volatility in US 
Treasuries and equities, respectively – have moved in tandem through several market shocks. 
Both reached cycle highs after ‘Liberation Day’ in April 2025 and spiked again in March 2026 
when the Strait of Hormuz closed, before falling through the summer as markets stabilised. 
That correlation appears to have broken down. The VIX remains near its lows, signalling calm in 
equity markets, while the MOVE Index points to unease in bond markets as US rates rise and 
European sovereigns come under scrutiny. 
The divergence is significant. When the VIX and MOVE have decoupled in this way, it has 
generally reflected not a discrete risk-off shock that markets can price and move beyond, but 
deeper uncertainty about the path of rates. The question is whether the VIX signals genuine 
calm or is simply the last canary yet to sing.

Macro/government bonds 
Simon Roberts 
Product Specialist, Global Rates 
 
US Treasury yields faced further upward pressure last week amid concerns about inflation, 
fiscal deterioration and tighter monetary policy. The two-year yield rose around 3bps and the 
30-year yield rose 13bps, extending the steepening of the US yield curve. In Germany, by 
contrast, two-year yields fell around 20bps as European bond investors moved out of French 
and other peripheral eurozone debt and into the perceived safety of German Bunds. 
September’s non-farm payrolls report interrupted the rise in US Treasury yields. Payrolls 
increased by a weaker-than-expected 29,000, while gains for the previous two months were 
revised down by 60,000. The unemployment rate also edged up to 4.2%. Markets immediately 
repriced rate-hike expectations, with swaps indicating that the probability of an October increase 
had fallen from 29% to 20%. 
In Europe, markets doubted the French government’s ability to pass a budget aimed at reducing 
the country’s deficit to 5% through reforms and spending cuts. Political consensus on lower 
spending remains elusive as France approaches the 2027 presidential election. Stress was 
clearest in the 10-year French-German government bond spread, which reached an intraday 
high of 152bps on Friday – its widest level since the 2011 eurozone debt crisis – before partially

debt has also weighed on Italian and Spanish bonds, raising fears of contagion across the

Liability driven investments (LDI)  
Jan Willemsen,  
Client Portfolio Manager LDI, Continental Europe 
 
Long-dated euro rates remained volatile over the past fortnight, although flight-to-quality 
demand provided some relief earlier this week as the French sovereign crisis intensified. The 
30-year swap rate fell 9bps to 3.46% and the 50-year rate to 3.10%, leaving the 30s50s swap 
spread almost unchanged and inverted by 36bps. Despite Brent crude trading above $100, 
inflation expectations have eased. Ten-year inflation swaps have moved down towards 2.2%, 
indicating scepticism that energy-driven price pressures will persist. Real yields have continued 
to rise: nominal long-end swap rates have increased by more than inflation compensation, 
implying a 10bps-15bps rise in real yields over the past two weeks and extending the trend 
seen since the start of the year. 
French 30-year OAT spreads over Bunds fell 9bps to 151bps early this week, providing some 
relief after reaching 160bps at the end of last week. However, France’s fiscal deficit remains 
high and political fragmentation ahead of the 2027 election limits the credibility of near-term 
consolidation. The France-Germany spread has exceeded its 2012 crisis level and is now 
comparable with levels last seen in 1991. Contagion has also affected other European 
sovereigns: Spanish, Italian and European Union 30-year spreads, at 79bps, 134bps and 80bps 
respectively, are all 10-20bps wider against Germany than a month ago. Hedge fund activity, 
particularly in French, Spanish and Italian debt, has amplified volatility. The European Central 
Bank’s (ECB) Transmission Protection Instrument remains available as a firewall, but it is more 
likely to support peripheral markets if contagion spreads than French OATs directly, given that 
the programme is not intended to address self-inflicted fiscal challenges. 
For LDI portfolios managed against a swap benchmark, this environment highlights the 
importance of proactive country-risk management and duration flexibility. A shorter-duration 
position relative to the benchmark reduces spread-risk exposure, while diversification across 
sovereign and supranational, sovereign and agency issuers remains essential, despite the 
impact of contagion. With the next phase of Dutch WTP transition flows expected in the coming 
quarter, close monitoring of the curve is also important because these structural flows could 
materially affect long-dated supply dynamics. 
 
Investment grade credit 
Steven Nelson,  
Client Portfolio Manager, Investment Grade 
 
Credit spreads widened sharply last week. Euro investment grade (IG) was the notable 
underperformer, widening 16bps to 100bps amid French sovereign contagion, weakness in 
European banks and the global rates sell-off. Global IG widened 7bps to 89bps, US IG 4bps to 
85bps and sterling IG 6bps to 91bps. The iTraxx Main widened 3.8bps to 64.6bps and CDX IG 
2.2bps to 60.3bps, with the European index underperforming its US counterpart in line with 
moves in cash bonds. 
European primary credit issuance slowed to around €14.2 billion as government bond volatility 
created a stop-start market. Deutsche Telekom priced a €1.9 billion deal, upsized from €1.5 
billion, while media conglomerate Bertelsmann issued €1.25 billion. In the US, Paramount 
Skydance’s $41 billion multi-tranche, M&A-linked deal to fund its acquisition of Warner Bros. 
Discovery dominated activity. It was the largest corporate bond sale by a public company, while

the $30 billion IG tranche was itself the largest IG corporate bond deal on record. Most tranches 
subsequently traded wider, weighing on demand for other issues and leaving total weekly 
issuance at around $33.6 billion. 
Corporate credit news was broad-based. In artificial intelligence (AI), Amazon is exploring a 
transaction that would move around $8 billion of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips off its balance 
sheet through a special-purpose vehicle funded by external investors – its first large off-
balance-sheet financing structure. Broadcom is reportedly arranging $60 billion of AI chip 
financing for Anthropic and other companies. Some market participants view the structure as 
vendor financing, adding to concerns about leverage embedded in AI infrastructure 
commitments. In financials, European subordinated bank spreads widened materially as bank 
equities fell, although senior debt spreads were more resilient. In mining, China’s State 
Administration for Market Regulation is pressing Anglo American to guarantee a steady copper 
supply as a condition for approving its $54 billion merger with Teck Resources – the only 
remaining jurisdiction yet to clear the deal. In the UK, retailer Tesco is reportedly among the 
bidders for Majestic Wine Group, which owner Fortress has put up for sale. 
 
US high yield credit and leveraged loans 
Chris Jorel, 
Client Portfolio Manager, US High Yield 
 
Rates volatility continued to weigh on the high yield (HY) market last week. A weak start gave 
way to firmer levels on Friday, but spreads widened 12bps to 325bps and yields rose above 8%, 
leaving the ICE BofA US HY Constrained Index down 0.65% over the week. September’s 
record gross issuance of $52 billion included Paramount Skydance’s $12.3 billion US 
dollar/euro offering to help fund its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, the fourth-largest 
transaction in HY history. Risk aversion and dispersion persisted, with BB, B, and CCC spreads 
widening 35bps, 44bps and 65bps, respectively, as lower-quality credit bore the brunt of the 
selling. 
Leveraged loans also faced turbulence as broader rates volatility ended several weeks of 
relative calm. Spreads widened by around 10bps-12bps, while the S&P UBS Leveraged Loan 
Index fell $0.33 to $94.31. Technical conditions remain supportive: September recorded the 
highest net new loan supply since 2021, but demand still exceeded supply on JP Morgan’s 
measure. Almost $1 billion flowed into loans last week, indicating that investors continue to 
favour the asset class even as volatility rises elsewhere in credit. 
 
European high yield credit  
Angelina Chueh,  
Client Portfolio Manager, European High Yield 
 
September was challenging for European HY, which returned -1.85% in euro-hedged terms as 
spreads widened and underlying government bond yields rose. Decompression was 
pronounced: CCCs underperformed BBs and Bs, while distressed credit returned -3.4%. Single 
Bs modestly outperformed BBs. Credit spreads widened 39bps to 311bps and yields rose 
60bps to almost 7%. Weekly primary issuance slowed to €3.9 billion across six issuers, but 
monthly issuance remained strong at €20.7 billion. This took Q3 2026 issuance to €28 billion – 
the second-highest Q3 total on record – and year-to-date corporate issuance to €104.5 billion. 
BB issuance rose to 67% of the Q3 total from 57% in Q2 2026, while B issuance fell to 33% and 
there was no CCC issuance. Fund flows deteriorated in the final week of September, with €411 
million of outflows from exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and managed accounts. Despite 
September outflows of €1.1 billion, Q3 2026 recorded net inflows of €1.2 billion, taking year-to-
date inflows to €3.8 billion.

In ratings news, Moody’s downgraded Innomotics to B3 from B2. The move appeared to be a 
catch-up adjustment because the weaker-than-expected outlook has been known since early 
March, while recent results showed relatively positive order intake and a sequential recovery in 
earnings. Moody’s also downgraded Rekeep to CCC from B3, citing weaker-than-expected 
operating performance, execution challenges and uncertainty over the timing of a recovery. 
In sector news, real estate remained in focus amid further reports about the €25 billion 
separation of Alecta and Heimstaden, as well as concerns about the potential expropriation of 
property in Berlin. The latter was subsequently regarded as unlikely due to the number of 
preconditions required. 
 
Structured credit  
Kinsey Wessels, 
Client Portfolio Analyst, Fixed Income 
 
Structured credit was generally weaker over the week. Agency mortgage-backed securities 
(MBS) continued to bear the brunt of recent volatility, with current-coupon spreads widening by 
around 15bps over the month. The Bloomberg US MBS Index returned -79bps last week, while 
15-year MBS outperformed 30-year securities as the curve bull-steepened. Mortgage 
applications fell 6% week-on-week as 30-year mortgage rates rose 18bps to their highest level 
since November; refinancing applications fell 8.7%. The Federal Housing Finance Agency 
House Price Index rose 0.3% month-on-month and 2.6% year-on-year, but regional 
performance diverged. The Mid-Atlantic region led with a 1.5% rise, reversing a 10bps decline 
in June, while the Mountain region fell 80bps. 
Commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) secondary trading was strong last week at 
around three times the weekly average, concentrated mainly in short-duration conduit paper. 
Benchmark conduit spreads widened modestly across the capital structure. New issuance 
remained focused on single-asset single-borrower (SASB) transactions, with six deals pricing 
for $3.7 billion, taking year-to-date volume to around 20% above last year’s pace. Asset-backed 
securities (ABS) benchmark spreads widened by 2bps-5bps. The spread between AAA and 
below-AAA tranches widened around 2bps to 96bps, still well inside the two-year average of 
116bps, while the AAA-A ABS-to-corporate spread tightened 5bps to 27bps versus a two-year 
average of 39bps. This underlined ABS’s relative value despite the broader widening. Ten new 
deals priced for $5.2 billion, lifting year-to-date issuance by 8.8% year-on-year, with momentum 
expected to continue. 
  
Asian credit  
Justin Ong, 
Research Analyst, Asian Fixed Income 
 
The JACI lost 40bps over the week as spread losses of 70bps outweighed a 31bps gain from 
lower rates. JACI investment grade (IG) and high yield (HY) recorded weekly losses of 37bps 
and 58bps, respectively. 
The People’s Bank of China (PBoC) cut the one-year Pledged Supplementary Lending (PSL) 
rate by 25bps to 1.5% and added the ‘Six Networks’ to the list of eligible sectors. The PSL is the 
PBoC’s collateralised facility providing low-cost funding to three policy banks – the China 
Development Bank, the Agricultural Development Bank of China and the Export-Import Bank of 
China – which may only lend the proceeds to designated sectors. The Six Networks are 
priorities in China’s fifth Five-Year Plan for 2026-2030: water networks, new-type power grids, 
computing-power networks, next-generation communications, urban underground pipelines and 
logistics. The PSL’s focus has evolved from shantytown redevelopment in 2014 to the Three 
Major Projects in 2023 – affordable housing, urban-village redevelopment and dual-use public 
infrastructure – and now the Six Networks.

In China, the PBoC, Ministry of Finance and National Financial Regulatory Administration 
announced a mortgage interest subsidy. Although the subsidy is below buyers’ expectations, it 
could provide incremental support to developers focused on lower-tier cities because the caps 
target lower-priced homes. Eligible buyers will receive an annual subsidy of one percentage 
point for up to five years on new commercial mortgages, excluding refinancing, for first homes 
of no more than 120 square metres and CNY1.5 million. The subsidy applies to principal of up 
to CNY1 million and could reduce cumulative interest payments by almost CNY50,000. 
According to CREIS, qualifying properties accounted for 33.1% of new-home transactions and 
55.2% of resale transactions across 40 major cities in the first eight months of 2026. 
For New World Development (NWD), the termination of the 11 SKIES lease is positive despite a 
HK$2.3 billion termination cost and HK$14.7 billion of impairment losses. It removes a key 
overhang that had reportedly stalled talks with external investors. Controlling shareholder, 
CTFE, which owns 45.2% of NWD, enhanced the agreement by granting the Hong Kong Airport 
Authority an option to acquire 750 million of its existing NWD shares. 
S&P upgraded GMR Hyderabad International Airport’s issuer and senior secured note ratings to 
BBB- from BB, while revising the outlook to stable from positive. It cited an improving regulatory 
framework, higher tariffs in the new control period and stronger financial performance. S&P also 
upgraded Delhi International Airport to BB+ with a positive outlook. 
 
Emerging markets 
Omotoke Joseph, 
Product Specialist, Emerging Market Debt 
 
 
Emerging market debt (EMD) weakened over the week amid a deteriorating macroeconomic 
backdrop. EM sovereigns returned -0.78%, ending the week 10bps wider, while corporates 
returned -0.42% finishing 12bps wider. Local markets underperformed both asset classes, 
returning -0.81% over the period. 
Geopolitical risks intensified over the week as US-Iran negotiations yielded little progress. 
Exports through the Strait of Hormuz remained below pre-conflict levels, while concerns grew 
over potential Iranian retaliation targeting shipping or regional energy infrastructure. Reports 
also suggested the US is reinforcing its military presence in the region. Elsewhere, the Russia-
Ukraine conflict showed little sign of easing, with Moscow reportedly expanding both hybrid and 
kinetic activity.   
In Brazil, right-wing candidate Flávio Bolsonaro secured the largest share of votes in the first 
round of the presidential election, receiving 47% versus the incumbent Lula’s 45%, 
outperforming opinion polls. Markets reacted positively to the result, with Brazilian assets 
outperforming and opening 1% higher on the news.  
Romania remained under pressure as another prime ministerial nominee, Siegfried Mureșan, 
failed to secure a parliamentary confidence vote, deepening the political impasse and raising 
concerns over a potential downgrade to high yield by S&P. Those fears eased on Friday after 
S&P affirmed Romania’s BBB- rating with a negative outlook, citing progress towards 2026 
fiscal consolidation despite the absence of a new government. Although bonds recovered 
modestly following the review, Romanian 10-year bonds still finished approximately 0.77% 
lower on the week. 
Elsewhere, Moody’s revised Angola’s outlook to positive from stable while affirming its B3 
rating. The agency cited an ‘emerging track record of greater macroeconomic stability through 
periods of both high and lower oil prices’, despite a larger fiscal deficit than anticipated in the 
first half of 2026. Market reaction was relatively muted, but the outlook revision is an 
encouraging signal as Angola approaches an electoral cycle in December.
