Sovereign Spreads and the Euro - Contagion is Key 
 
 
An acceleration in pressures on European sovereign credit has dragged the Euro 
sharply lower over the past week. Risks to European fiscal trajectories have been a 
growing focus in recent months, with energy prices and government borrowing costs 
rising, and a super-cycle of European elections still to come. Until recently though, 
the Euro had weathered these rising fiscal premia relatively well. We argue that the 
scale of the recent widening in sovereign spreads, and, in particular, the contagion of 
the OAT-led move across other EGB markets are key to explaining the clear sudden 
imprint on the currency. 
The underperformance in OATs has been a steadily building theme across the past 
few years, but for much of that period, EGBs in aggregate have seen a progressive 
narrowing in their spreads to Bunds (Exhibit 1). We think that overall narrowing is

Relative to that steady progress though, it is evident from price action over the past

France has been front-and-centre of recent market pressures and fiscal concerns, but in 
general, we think the hurdle for fiscal or political premia in any single member state to 
drive outcomes in the Euro is high. The FX impact from country-specific premia is diluted 
down across the currency union, and even then, we find evidence that their contribution 
to aggregate GDP-weighted sovereign spreads is less impactful for the Euro than the 
contribution from broad-based pressures. 
We regress 10-year GDP-weighted spreads on the first principal component of a range 
of country-specific spreads to extract a common component and an idiosyncratic 
component. A decomposition of this year’s moves in aggregated spreads suggests 
idiosyncratic drivers have explained the majority of the variance in recent months, but 
the sharp move over the past week has represented pressures that are more 
broad-based (Exhibit 3). Over the past five years, EUR/USD in particular has shown 
greater sensitivity to shifts in aggregate spreads when they are underpinned by common 
moves rather than idiosyncratic ones (Exhibit 4). We think this historical result goes some 
way to explaining why, alongside the outright scale of the spread moves, the recent 
escalation from idiosyncratic to broad-based price action in spreads has hit the 
threshold for a more significant move in the Euro. 
We find similar results when using longer samples that include the European debt crisis 
of the early 2010s, albeit with smaller betas and less differentiation between common 
and idiosyncratic components.

Looking ahead, while near-term risks of continued stress and contagion remain high, we 
think current credit-based pressures on the Euro are likely nearing their limit, for a 
number of reasons. 
First, fiscal fundamentals in France remain challenging, but our rates strategists argue 
the widening in OATs has outstripped what can be explained by shifts in fundamentals, 
and the presidential election is over six months away. Meanwhile, fiscal trajectories in 
most other European markets are more favourable, arguing for the risks ahead still being 
at least partially concentrated in France, and so less problematic for the Euro in our view. 
Second, technical factors and positioning unwinds likely played an important role in the 
contagion in price action over the past week, but the steady narrowing in aggregate 
spreads heading into this year (as per Exhibit 1) argues for having entered the energy 
shock on firmer footing. And third, the broader European backdrop should help stem the 
risks of contagion and Euro depreciation. Growth and activity have been resilient, 
European banks are better capitalised and better equipped to deal with sovereign bond 
stress now than in previous episodes, and, should it become necessary, the ECB is 
well-equipped with anti-fragmentation tools. 
Put together, our bias is that the Euro ultimately recovers some of its recent losses on 
key crosses in the coming weeks as acute credit pressures reduce. This includes on 
EUR/USD, where the range-break lower has likely exacerbated the move, but where, 
separate to the developments in the Euro, we view the extent of recent broad Dollar 
strength as likely to fade in the near term. A more durable shift in ECB hike pricing may 
dampen the Euro’s recovery though, and over longer horizons we think the outlook on 
G10 crosses remains heavily divided – we expect continued Euro downside versus 
high-carry energy exporters AUD and NOK, but argue the outlook is brighter versus CHF 
and GBP.
