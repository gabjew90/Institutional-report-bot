SEPTEMBER 2026 MARKET COMMENTARY
2
▪September was in large part defined by the global rise in 
government bond yields as bonds sold off into the end of the 
month. Yields across developed markets hit multi-decade highs, 
with long-dated treasuries hitting levels last seen in 2007. 
Catalysing this move was a spike in oil prices, sent higher by 
renewed tensions in the Middle East which suggested supply will 
remain constricted. With the price of crude hovering above 
$100/bbl, investors increasingly expect higher inflation to prove 
persistent. This has depressed the price of longer-dated 
government bonds as investors expect that inflation will eat away 
at the value of the coupons, while the prospect of central banks 
raising rates to combat inflation promises higher yielding bonds 
on the horizon.
▪Most developed market central banks started off on such a course 
in September, with hawkishness across the board. While the 
Bank of England held steady, the Bank of Japan, European 
Central Bank and the Federal Reserve all raised their benchmark 
rates by 25bps. While the ECB and BOJ were largely expected, 
as recently as August, markets were divided on the path of the 
Fed. Moreover, it represents the first hike in over 3 years for the 
central bank and a clear signal that the recent period of monetary 
policy divergence is over.
▪Structural effects also added to the pressure on yields. The AI-
driven capex boom is adding large quantities of supply to credit 
markets, pushing up the yields governments must offer to remain 
competitive. These effects have added to longstanding concerns 
over the rampant budget deficits many countries are running, with 
debt levels rising despite there being little risk of an imminent 
recession. The lack of political will to rein in spending has left 
investors unconvinced that this situation will be quickly remedied.
▪Equity markets however were largely resilient despite the multiple 
headwinds. Most headlines were slightly down or flat, but 
continued gains in tech and AI names continued to keep headline 
levels elevated. This came despite renewed concerns about AI 
safety, with several prominent figures from frontier labs 
expressing concern about existential risks of the technology and 
calling for greater regulation.
▪In commodity markets gold continued to slide as fears of higher 
inflation depressed the outlook for the non-yielding metal. Oil 
gained, following periodic flare ups in the Strait of Hormuz. 
Furthermore, developments on the other side of the Arabian 
peninsula put the erstwhile workaround through the Bab el-
Mandeb Strait into focus for oil traders.
▪Looking into October the rise in yields likely has further to run, 
with none of the drivers likely to abate in the short term. Central 
banks will similarly stay committed to their hawkish path as 
continuing conflict in the Persian Gulf keeps oil prices elevated.
Source: BNP Paribas, Bloomberg. 
Global equities in September proved resilient in the face of a sharp rise in global yields. This rise was triggered by higher energy prices and a 
shift in consensus towards the belief that interest rates will rise again, bringing other structural pressures into sharper focus. Developed market 
central banks were hawkish across the board in the face of persistent inflation pressures and strong economic growth. AI-related equities faced 
some volatility after safety concerns were thrust to the fore but managed to post a gain across the month.
