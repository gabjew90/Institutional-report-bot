ETF Market Pulse 
Week 5 – September 2026: Tanker Mania, Cannabis Slump and a Yield-Driven Market 
 
 
Weekly ETF Returns: Last week’s ETF performance was marked by strong dispersion 
across asset classes, driven primarily by a sharp rise in global bond yields. Japanese 
equities and US Growth outperformed, supported by continued AI and semiconductor 
momentum, a weaker yen, and strong technology earnings. In contrast, Gold, European 
equities, EM bonds, and US Value lagged as higher real rates, a stronger dollar, and 
growing fiscal concerns in Europe weighed on performance.  
 
 
From Tanker Mania to Cannabis Slump: Among top performers, the Breakwave 
Tanker Shipping ETFsurged 32.7%, taking its YTD return to +4,431.6% as of October 2. 
The move was driven by a sharp spike in tanker freight rates rather than oil prices 
themselves: the ETF holds tanker-freight futures, predominantly linked to Middle East–
Asia routes, and the attacks and disruption around the Strait of Hormuz sent shipping 
costs sharply higher. Cannabis ETFs were the clear losers, with MSOS (-13.4%), 
Roundhill Cannabis (-12.4%) and Amplify Seymour Cannabis (-11.5%) all sharply lower. 
The sell-off was highly specific rather than simply risk-off: a procedural delay by the DEA 
in the cannabis rescheduling process pushed the expected decision into 2027, removing 
an important near-term catalyst for US cannabis stocks.  
 
 
ETF flows pointed to selective risk-taking rather than a broad risk-on environment. 
While equity ETFs captured three quarters of net inflows and fixed-income ETFs 
attracted strong demand relative to assets, investors favored targeted exposures such 
as energy, value stocks, long-duration government bonds, and short-maturity 
municipal bonds. At the same time, outflows from regional banks, high-yield credit, 
European equities, technology, and utilities reflected continued caution toward rate-
sensitive sectors and concerns over higher-for-longer interest rates. 
 
 
ETF product launches remained strong last week, with more than 70 new ETFs and 
ETPs launched globally, highlighting three dominant industry trends: the continued 
expansion of active ETFs, growing demand for income-enhancement strategies, and 
rising interest in AI-, technology- and geopolitics-related themes. Active management 
remained the leading area of innovation across equity and fixed income, while covered-
call and option-income products continued to gain traction. Thematic launches 
focused on AI, robotics, defense and strategic resources, underscoring the industry's 
emphasis on active management, income generation and long-term structural growth 
themes.

Insight on ETF Primary Market Flows: Oil & Gas and Value 
Lead Equity Flows; Duration and US Munies Lead Bonds 
Last week, the market showed a mixed picture. Equity markets remained resilient, supported 
by renewed optimism around AI and softer-than-expected US inflation, which reduced 
expectations of an imminent Fed rate hike. At the same time, the bond market remained under 
pressure, with US Treasury yields reaching multi-year highs amid concerns over inflation, oil 
prices, fiscal deficits and the resilience of the economy. The ETF flow picture therefore suggests 
selective risk-taking rather than an indiscriminate risk-on positioning. 
Equity ETFs captured around three quarters of total net inflows, reflecting a still supportive risk 
appetite, while fixed-income ETFs recorded the strongest gathering relative to their beginning-
of-week AUM. This was not, however, a broad-based risk-on move: investors favored specific 
equity factors and themes, alongside high-quality and long-duration government bonds.

The strongest relative inflows were concentrated in Nasdaq US Smart Oil & Gas, MSCI World Value 
Advanced Select, Bloomberg Euro Government 30 Year Term and the S&P 0–7 Year National AMT-
Free Municipal Bond Index. The first reflects continued investor interest in energy and oil & gas 
exposure amid elevated oil prices and persistent geopolitical supply risks. The World Value 
exposure is consistent with a rotation towards cheaper, cash-generative parts of the market as 
valuation concerns around growth remain elevated. The strong demand for Euro government 30-
year exposure is more defensive and potentially reflects investors positioning for eventual rate 
relief after the sharp rise in long-end yields. Finally, the short-maturity US municipal bond 
exposure offers a combination of relatively attractive yields, low duration and tax efficiency, 
making it particularly suited to a cautious fixed-income environment. 
The largest relative outflows, meanwhile, came from S&P Regional Banks Select Industry, 
Bloomberg High Yield Very Liquid, MSCI Europe ex UK, S&P World Information Technology 
Weighted & Screened and MSCI USA IMI Utilities 25/50. Regional banks and high-yield credit both 
suffered from the market’s sensitivity to higher-for-longer rates and tighter financial conditions. 
The outflow from Europe ex UK is consistent with weaker European sentiment amid fiscal and 
political concerns and the rise in European government bond yields. The withdrawal from global 
information technology appears more like profit-taking after the strong AI-led rally, rather than a 
broad rejection of technology. Finally, the weakness in US utilities may reflect their bond-proxy 
characteristics, making them less attractive as long-term yields rise, despite their traditionally 
defensive profile.

New Products of the Week: Active, Income & Themes Drive 
Product Innovation 
More than 70 ETFs and ETPs launched globally last week, highlighting three key trends: continued 
active ETF expansion, growing demand for income-enhancement strategies, and strong investor 
appetite for AI-, technology- and geopolitics-related themes. 
Active ETFs remained the dominant launch category, particularly in Canada, the US and Europe. 
Canada led activity with a broad range of active equity, fixed income and multifactor strategies, 
while the US saw further expansion in active equity, EM and credit ETFs. In Europe, Invesco and 
Goldman Sachs continued to build out enhanced-equity UCITS ETF ranges. 
Income generation remains a major growth area. Covered-call, option-income and buffer ETFs 
accounted for a substantial share of launches across North America and Europe. The trend 
reflects persistent investor demand for higher distributions and issuers' efforts to monetize 
elevated option volatility. 
Thematic innovation was concentrated in Asia, especially Hong Kong, where issuers launched a 
series of ETFs focused on AI, robotics, future technologies, healthcare innovation and strategic 
resources. Defense, critical minerals and supply-chain resilience also continued to attract 
product development globally. 
Fixed income innovation remained robust, spanning core aggregate bond exposure, short-
duration strategies, securitized credit, multi-sector income, municipal bonds and emerging-
market debt. Most new fixed income launches were actively managed, reinforcing active ETFs' 
growing dominance in bond markets.
