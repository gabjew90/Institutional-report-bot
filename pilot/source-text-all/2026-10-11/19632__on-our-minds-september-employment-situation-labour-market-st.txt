September Employment Situation: Labour Market Stability 
 
September payrolls growth fell short of expectations and prior months were revised 
lower, but the underlying labour market remained stable. The three-month average 
payrolls gain stayed in line with breakeven hiring paces, while the rise in unemployment 
reflected stronger labour supply rather than weakening labour demand. With wage 
growth easing and unemployment dynamics remaining consistent with a range-bound 
jobless rate around 4.1%, this report should be a non-factor for the October FOMC 
meeting. 
 
Nonfarm payrolls (NFP) grew +29k in September - well below both the consensus expectation of 
+90k and the SG forecast of +120k. This was down from the NFP increase in August, which was 
revised down from +162k initially to +133k. Furthermore, the net revision of payrolls over the past 
2 months equalled -60k. Consequently, the three-month average payrolls change was 51k, like in 
August (after revisions), which is line the range estimates of the breakeven payrolls growth rate 
needed to keep the three-month average unemployment rate constant (left-hand side chart 
below). Would this persist, we should expect the unemployment rate to remain stable around 
4.1%-4.2%. Private payrolls growth saw a similar dynamic as overall NFP growth, as these 
increased by 46k in September (consensus: 81k, SG: 100k), down from 89k in August, which was 
revised down from 127k initially.

Looking into more sectoral details behind the NFP increase, payrolls growth in the goods producing 
sector (especially in manufacturing) eased in September (it went down from +34k to +18k in 
September), and private-sector services jobs growth also slowed down to +28K from +55k in August. 
Healthcare saw slower jobs growth rate in September, at 23k vs 35.1k in Augus, whereas temp 
agencies shed jobs at a -10.9k pace in September. More sectors than in August shed jobs, with the 
temp agency sector shedding the most, but most sectors continued to add jobs on a three-month 
basis (the corresponding diffusion index of three-month average job gains read 57% in September) 
– a good sign going forward.

to stay put at 4.1%. In 3-digit

The labour force participation 
rate unexpectedly went up 
again in September from 
61.6% to 61.8%, after it had 
increased from 61.4% to 61.6% in August. Although the population grew by +139k in September, the 
labour force increased by 485k, and of this increase 406k was due to growth in employment. All of 
this suggests that the increase in the labour participation rate was due to people moving back into 
the labour market. 
Given the higher labour force participation rate the slight increase in the 3-digit unemployment rate 
mentioned earlier was therefore not surprising. Indeed, looking at the reasons for unemployment 
in the household survey the total +78k increase in the number unemployed was due to a +268K 
increase in the number of new entrants and reentrants onto the labour market and decrease of -
218k of those who gave job losses or voluntary leaving their job as a reason for unemployment.

Overall, a weaker-than-expected jobs report in terms of payrolls with downward revisions to June
