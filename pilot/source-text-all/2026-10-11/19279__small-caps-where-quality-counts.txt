Small caps: Where quality counts
Factory Daily letter · 07 Oct 2026 · 12 min read
With small caps already deeply corrected and financing conditions still tight, we screen the small cap universe for quality at a reasonable price
Written by
Trading Strategy, Pictet Trading & Sales

US equities continue to be led higher by AI‑driven mega‑cap Technology, with the Nasdaq 100 at new all‑time highs and the Nasdaq 100/S&P 500 ratio near record levels. Under the surface, however, breadth has weakened sharply: the S&P 500 Equal
Weight and Russell 2000 have absorbed a meaningful correction, and much of the small‑cap universe is already in bear‑market territory.
Small caps have already absorbed a significant adjustment, while earnings revisions remain constructive – a disconnect that suggests recent weakness has been driven more by tighter financial conditions than by deteriorating fundamentals. Should
yields stabilise and market breadth improve, any next leg higher could be characterised by somewhat broader participation rather than continued dependence on a narrow group of mega‑cap leaders. 
In a “higher for longer” rate backdrop, a sustained, broad‑based small‑cap leadership phase still looks challenging, but the combination of advanced internal damage, resilient earnings revisions and a visible quality premium suggests scope for
selective opportunities. So, following yesterday’s Technical View (again here), today we look at the current macro and market backdrop for US small caps and present our screening of the S&P Small Cap 600 for PTS scores that reflect balance‑sheet
strength and profitability at relatively attractive valuations – profiles that may be better placed to participate if tactical broadening emerges, while retaining resilience should financing conditions remain tight.
Top‑line performance masks underlying fragility
As illustrated below, the S&P 500 remains close to record highs and above its rising moving averages, but participation has weakened abruptly beneath the surface. Only 24.3 per cent of constituents trade above their 50‑day SMA, versus 42.1 per cent
above their 200‑day SMA, leaving a 17.9‑percentage‑point gap between short‑ and medium‑term breadth.
The speed of this divergence is the key signal: the 50‑day measure has dropped below 30 per cent, showing that selling pressure has spread rapidly across the median stock, while the 200‑day measure has eased more gradually and still sits above 40 per
cent, indicating a less damaged primary trend.
Previous comparable episodes on the chart were followed by recoveries in short‑term participation, making the current set‑up consistent with tactical oversold conditions. However, the signal remains conditional: a recovery above 30 per cent would
indicate initial stabilisation, while a move through 50 per cent would provide stronger evidence of broadening. By contrast, a break in 200‑day breadth below 40 per cent would suggest that the correction is becoming more structural:

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 06/10/2026.
Small caps have already absorbed a meaningful correction
The S&P 500 is only about 0.3 per cent below its all‑time high. By contrast, the Russell 2000 has fallen almost 10 per cent from its recent peak to its local low and remains roughly 7.2 per cent below its record level. The drawdown gap reflects how the
recent adjustment has been concentrated outside the index heavyweights: larger, self‑financing businesses have helped support the headline index, while smaller companies have absorbed more of the pressure from rising yields and tighter funding
conditions.
This reset arguably changes the relative asymmetry. Small caps may no longer require a powerful economic acceleration to rebound; if financing pressures stabilise and domestic earnings remain resilient, lower positioning and reduced expectations
could be enough to support a period of tactical catch‑up performance.
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 06/10/2026.
Much of the Russell is now in bear market territory, with breadth measures signalling a more selective recovery

Furthermore, the index‑level decline conceals significantly deeper damage across individual companies. As illustrated in the table below, 68.1 per cent of Russell 2000 constituents are more than 20 per cent below their individual highs, while 32.7 per
cent have lost over half their value.
The latest change is mixed but informative. The share more than 20 per cent below their highs has fallen by 1.1 percentage points, hinting at early stabilisation across the broader universe, while the proportion down more than 50 per cent has risen by
0.5 points, signalling continued deterioration among the weakest names.
This distribution argues against indiscriminate small‑cap exposure: any initial recovery is more likely to be led by profitable businesses with stronger balance sheets, while highly leveraged or structurally challenged companies may continue to lag.
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 06/10/2026.
Small caps: breadth washout points to a mature correction
The breadth backdrop in small caps now appears quite advanced. As established above, the share of Russell 2000 constituents above their 50‑day moving average has dropped back towards 30 per cent, and this is a level that has, as illustrated below,
often coincided with selling‑pressure exhaustion and subsequent stabilisation. Moreover, the deterioration has occurred while the index itself remains near its longer‑term trend and well above major lows, implying that the average stock has
weakened far more than the benchmark. Accrodingly, and without implying an immediate rebound, these conditions look more consistent with a late‑stage correction than the start of a new bear market:
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 06/10/2026.
Small-cap breadth washouts have historically coincided with improved medium‑term returns
The chart below highlights episodes since 2001 when the share of Russell 2000 constituents above their 50‑day moving average fell below 30 per cent while the index remained within 10 per cent of its all‑time high. Historically, these signals have
tended to appear after the bulk of the correction has played out, as the market begins to build a recovery rather than when the downturn is just starting: on average, the Russell 2000 delivered about 3.0 per cent after 75 trading days, 5.7 per cent after
200 days and 8.7 per cent after 250 days following such breadth washouts.

However, the recovery path has been considerably less stable than in the S&P 500. Only around 56–57 per cent of observations were positive after 75–100 trading days, underlining the tendency for small caps to experience prolonged volatility before a
more durable advance emerges. While constructive, the historical message is not immediately bullish: extreme participation weakness has been associated with positive medium‑term returns, but the adjustment process has tended to be slower and
more uneven than in large‑cap indices.
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 06/10/2026.
Sector composition has been a headwind, but it's not the whole story
The Russell 2000 has much lower exposure to Information Technology and Communication Services than the S&P 500, and higher weights in Healthcare, Financials, Industrials and Real Estate – a mix that has been a clear disadvantage as market
leadership has rotated back towards mega‑cap Technology.
Stock‑level data, however, are more nuanced. On average, Russell 2000 constituents have outperformed their large‑cap sector peers in Financials, Consumer Discretionary, Healthcare, Technology and Industrials, with the main relative weakness
concentrated in Energy, Materials, Staples and Utilities.
Overall, the evidence points to a combination of limited mega‑cap Tech exposure and a less favourable sector mix, rather than uniformly weaker company fundamentals.

A question of timing? Small-cap leadership reversed as financing conditions tightened
The Russell 2000 remains ahead of the S&P 500 year to date, but relative momentum has reversed sharply. Over the past three months, the Russell 2000 has declined 5.5 per cent, while the S&P 500 has gained 3.2 per cent and the Nasdaq 100 has
advanced 5.0 per cent. The timing is consistent with a financing shock rather than a broad collapse in domestic demand: smaller companies are typically more reliant on bank lending, floating‑rate debt and external refinancing, whereas mega‑cap

Technology benefits from stronger balance sheets and internal cash generation. Recent market commentary has also linked small‑cap weakness to rising yields and a renewed preference for self‑financing mega‑caps.
Underperformance on its own does not guarantee mean reversion. A more durable recovery would likely require stabilising real yields, contained credit spreads and resilient earnings revisions. Until those conditions emerge, higher‑quality small caps
should remain better positioned than the broad Russell 2000.
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy. Data as of 06/10/2026. Past performance is not indicative of future results. For informational purposes only. Not investment advice.
Within the small‑cap universe, the cost of capital is driving a quality premium
The S&P Small Cap 600 has been outperforming the Russell 2000 as Treasury yields have risen. This distinction matters because the S&P 600 applies profitability requirements, whereas the Russell 2000 includes a larger share of unprofitable, more
leveraged and externally financed companies. Accordingly, the S&P 600/Russell 2000 ratio can serve as an imperfect but useful proxy for the quality premium within small caps.
As illustrated below, in a chart that plots this ratio (in black) against the US 10‑year yield (in blue), higher bond yields since 2022 have broadly coincided with stronger relative performance from profitable small companies, suggesting that investors
have been rewarding balance‑sheet resilience rather than abandoning the asset class altogether. A stabilisation in yields would ease this financing headwind, but a genuine broadening would require more than a rebound in quality: the strongest
confirmation would be a stabilisation in the broader Russell 2000 relative to the S&P 600, indicating that investors are becoming willing to move gradually down the quality spectrum.
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 06/10/2026.
Earnings revisions provide fundamental support for quality small caps
As illustrated by the red line in the chart below, the S&P Small Cap 600 earnings‑revision ratio has returned to positive territory and remains close to its strongest level since 2021, indicating that analyst upgrades continue to outnumber downgrades.
At the same time, the index’s rolling 12‑month return (in beige) has eased from its recent peak.
This divergence suggests that higher discount rates and refinancing concerns have recently been outweighing any improving profit expectations, making the price weakness look more like a valuation and financial‑conditions shock than a broad
earnings downgrade cycle.
This disconnect also points to potential catch‑up value, even if is not yet a sufficient timing signal. The revision ratio has stabilised rather than continued to accelerate, and the rolling return, while moderating, remains positive. A rebound would gain
stronger fundamental backing should revisions stay resilient while yields and credit conditions stabilise.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 06/10/2026.
Technicals reveal early signs of selling exhaustion near long-term support
As illustrated by the technical chart below, the Russell 2000 has now retreated towards its rising 200‑day moving average near 2,785, which converges with the former breakout zone, placing the index at an important technical inflection point. The
extended sequence of negative Heikin‑Ashi candles confirms persistent selling pressure, but the smaller recent candle bodies suggest that downside momentum is starting to ease. Historically, similar patterns on the chart have preceded periods of
consolidation or recovery around the 200‑day average.
The set‑up remains preliminary. Confirmation would require a positive Heikin‑Ashi reversal, improving small‑cap breadth and a recovery above the latest short‑term high. A sustained break below the rising 200‑day average would, from a technical

Relative weakness has reached an oversold support zone
The Russell 2000/S&P 500 ratio has reversed sharply after failing to sustain its earlier breakout. Near 0.91, the ratio is testing the 0.8986–0.9021 support zone, where Fibonacci retracements converge with the rising trendline from the 2025 relative low.
The daily RSI has fallen to around 26.8, indicating deeply oversold momentum. This increases the probability of a tactical rebound, but no bullish divergence or confirmed reversal pattern has yet developed.
Holding the support zone would preserve the possibility of a higher relative low above the 0.8620 trough. An initial recovery towards 0.95 would indicate that the current underperformance is moderating, while a move back towards 1.00–1.01 would
be needed to restore the earlier relative‑breakout thesis.
A break below 0.8986 would weaken the tactical case and increase the risk of a full retracement towards the 2025 relative low.

Screening for the small yet mighty
As discussed, US small caps may be approaching a more favourable risk‑reward profile, but history also indicates that recoveries following breadth washouts tend to be selective before they become broad‑based. Past patterns suggest that investors
have tended to favour profitability, balance‑sheet strength and earnings resilience, while penalising companies with greater financing needs.
To align this macro and market‑structure view with a more granular focus, we present below the results of our screen of the S&P Small Cap 600 universe for companies that combine stronger quality characteristics with relatively attractive valuations.
The screen retains only stocks with a PTS Quality score above 55, a Value score above 50 and a Global score above 50, helping to identify businesses that pair financial robustness with reasonable valuations and supportive overall fundamentals.
The resulting list represents a higher‑quality subset of the small‑cap universe, which may be better placed to benefit from stabilising financial conditions while offering some protection if volatility remains elevated. Historically, quality has tended to
lead in the early stages of a recovery, before participation broadens to more cyclical and lower‑quality segments.

The Equity quantitative grades
Our equity quant process is one by which we rank and score the stocks within our equity universe according to various short-term (i.e.
EPS growth) long-term (i.e. quality) and positioning (i.e. technical) characteristics, and on relative and/or historical basis as
appropriate. The various scores make up a stock’s total or ‘global' grade’:Growth Grade: The Growth Grade is a proprietary formula
made up of earnings revision momentum, past earnings growth, earnings stability, and current and long-term earnings growth.  A
grade above 55 is considered bullish on a 3-month basis, bearish below 45 and neutral between 55 and 45.
EPS Grade: The EPS Grade is a proprietary formula consisting of current and forward EPS growth, change and surprise data.  An EPS grade
above 60 or below 40 is considered predictive for future out/under performance.Sales Grade: The Sales Grade is a proprietary formula made
up of current and next year’s sales momentum, past sales growth, sales stability, and current and long-term sales growth. A grade above 55 is
considered bullish on a 3-month basis, bearish below 45, and neutral between 55 and 45.
Value Grade: The Value Grade is a proprietary formula made up of estimated P/E, P/B, P/S and P/CF ratios. 40% of the grade is based on
historical values and 60% on current market data.  A grade above 55 suggests a stock is cheap, below 45 expensive, and neutral between 55
and 45.
Quality Grade: The Quality Grade is a proprietary formula that focuses on the balance sheet (i.e. change in accruals, change in free cash flows
and profitability). A grade above 55 suggests a healthy balance sheet.Credit Grade: The Credit Grade focusses on the passive side of the
balance sheet.  It is divided into three sub-components to assess both short and long-term solvency.  A grade above 55 suggests a strong
capital structure, while a grade below 45 suggests a weak one.
Money Flow Grade: The Money Flow Grade is a proprietary formula that gives the accumulation/distribution based on the volume flows of a
stock. A grade above 55 indicates good money flow and a grade below 45 suggests weak money flow.Smart Sentiment Grade: The Smart
Sentiment grade is a contrarian indicator based on investor positioning measures such as the days-to-cover ratio, the put-call ratio, and the
short-interest ratio.  A weak grade suggests ‘too much’ optimism.
Relative Strength (RS) Grade: The RS grade measures the price momentum of a stock over its 1-year price performance.
Global Grade: The Global Grade is a weighted average of the Growth, EPS Sales, Value, Quality, Credit, Money Flow and Smart Sentiment
Grades.
Trend Score
A normalized composite of 10 underlying indicators that look at price relative to key moving averages, moving average trajectories and

The Exposure Optimisation Model
The PTS Exposure Optimisation Model (EOM) is a proprietary model designed to give tactical signals on the prevailing short-term
regional equity market regime. The model is applied to each market on a mutually-exclusive basis, with output expressed by way of
exposure scores (US; Europe; Japan and EM); in each case an average of underlying factors scores with dynamic weighting applied
according to the prevailing structural market volatility regime. Exposure score ratings are: very bullish (80-100%); bullish (60-80%);
neutral (40-60%); bearish (20-40%); and very bearish (20-0%). Factors are as follows:
Regime: A combination of technical indicators, moving averages and a Constant Proportion Portfolio Insurance (CPPI) strategy, the regime
score is designed to reflect short-term market momentum in the region;
Valuation: Built around the forward price-to-sales ratio, the dynamic valuation score is based on a calculation that is re-set on bull/bear
market regime changes and calibrated to facilitate regional comparison;
Economics: The economics score draws on real economy activity data (i.e. heavy truck sales and shipping traffic), economic signals from
financial markets (i.e. yield-curves) and medium-term economic indicators (i.e. PMIs and unemployment data); 
Flows: The flows score draws upon both active and passive weekly EPFR fund flows data giving us an indication of risk appetite going into the
week ahead; 
Market Sentiment: The market sentiment score is built around classic regional market risk indices (i.e. VIX; ARMS) as well as soft survey data
(i.e. U.of Michigan Consumer Sentiment). The scoring system is applied in a similar fashion at both regional and market level, while at sector
level we refer only to the Regime, Flows and Valuation factors.

Relative Strength Index (RSI): A momentum oscillator that measures the speed and change of price movements. It ranges from 0 to 100 and
is typically used to identify overbought or oversold conditions in a market. An RSI above 70 suggests that a stock may be overbought, while an
RSI below 30 indicates that it may be oversold.
The Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD): A trend-following momentum indicator that shows the relationship between two
moving averages. It is calculated by subtracting the 26-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) from the 12- period EMA. The result is the
MACD line. A nine-day EMA of the MACD, called the signal line, is then plotted on top of the MACD line, which can function as a trigger for
buy or sell signals. When the MACD crosses above the signal line, it is typically considered a bullish signal, and when it crosses below, it is
considered bearish.
Ichimoku Cloud Technical Analysis: Ichimoku Cloud is a popular technical analysis tool used to identify potential trends and
support/resistance levels in financial markets. It consists of several components, including the Kumo (cloud), the Tenkan-sen (conversion
line), the Kijun-sen (base line), and Senkou Span (leading span).
