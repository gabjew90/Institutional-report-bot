Alibaba Group (BABA): Alibaba Cloud APSARA - Key Takeaways; 
Powering & Scaling for the Agentic era
22 September 2026 | 4:48PM HKT
We highlight key takeaways from the Alibaba Cloud APSARA Conference (“云栖大会”) 
held in Hangzhou, across Chairman and CEO keynote speeches that focused on 
powering and scaling towards Machine Intelligence/Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI) 
for the agentic AI era, an analyst meeting with Group Chairman and management, 
and sessions hosted by respective AI Cloud & Compute and AI Labs & Applications 
management/business heads. We are encouraged by Alibaba’s progress across its 
unique AI full-stack, namely AI infrastructure (targeting to reach 20GW in total data 
center power consumption by 2032, to be powered by Alibaba Cloud & partners, 
with its custom silicon T-head to contribute meaningfully to compute), AI models 
(with Qwen’s roadmap in sustaining amongst the top-tier, a multi-modal approach, 
Qwen4 family coming soon and Qwen4.5/5 to scale towards next 5-10T parameter 
sizes) and AI applications/ecosystems (focusing on Harness x Context x Model for 
the agentic era, with new QwenWork functionalities and ‘Enterprise Context’, 
connecting AI workforce agents with DingTalk for productivity and expanded 
capabilities). 
With these, we maintain our above-street (vs. Visible Alpha Consensus Data) Alibaba 
Cloud revenue growth expectations of 53%/55%/55% year-on-year for 
Sep-2026/Dec-2026/Mar-2027 quarters. We anticipate investor focus around 
capex/funding sources in powering the 20GW power consumption target by 2032, 
which we estimate will be manageable, from a combination of asset heavy and asset 
light formats, 1) capex, funded by stable eCommerce EBITA, growing cloud profits, 
cash on balance sheet and future funding, 2) partnerships with data 
centers/telcos/others and 3) rental, esp. for overseas AI compute. 
Reconciling power consumption/capex/cloud growth: We estimate 20GW by 
calendar year 2032 will imply 1-2GW increase p.a. over the next few years, from 
c.5-6GW in end-2025 (GSe), translating to Rmb200-300bn annual capex for Alibaba 
over the next few years, assuming c.US$20-25bn capex per GW attributable to 
Alibaba from a T-head custom silicon dominated approach and mixture of asset 
heavy, asset light and rental (opex) compute. We believe its custom silicon T-head 
creates unique US$/GW advantages vs. domestic and overseas peers, where T-Head 
could contribute to half of Alibaba Cloud compute medium-term, from c.10% today. 
Meanwhile, the company continues to expect US$100bn external AI cloud revenue 
for calendar year 2030 and medium-term 20% EBITA margin, depending on the 
evolving capex-to-revenue conversion in the medium-term and internal/external 
revenue mix. We are currently forecasting Rmb209/243/260bn capex for

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
