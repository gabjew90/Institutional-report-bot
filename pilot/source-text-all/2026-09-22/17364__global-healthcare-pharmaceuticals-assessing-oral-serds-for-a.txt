Introducing Our Adjuvant Oral SERD Market Model 
Driven by our updated view on market segmentation and competition, we update our 
global oral SERDs market model for the upfront adjuvant (i.e., immediately after 
surgery/initial treatment) and switch adjuvant (i.e., after ~2-5 years of standard ET 
therapy) settings, which we estimate have a combined peak sales potential of ~$27bn 
across Roche’s giredestrant, AZN’s Etcamah, LLY’s Inluriyo, and Menarini’s Orserdu.

and coming down to $173k/161k in 2030/2035. We assess the commercial opportunity

While traditional endocrine therapy has been effective, drug resistance is a key 
n
problem, where ESR1 mutations make the estrogen receptor activated 
irrespective of estrogen binding, rendering traditional endocrine therapies 
ineffective. Selective estrogen receptor degraders (SERDs) are a class of drugs that 
directly bind to the estrogen receptor and promote degradation, vs. AI that work by 
lowering estrogen levels, allowing them to be effective even with ESR1 mutations.  
The SERD landscape has evolved from a single, poorly bioavailable injectable to 
n
a highly competitive class of potent oral agents. The first SERD on the market was 
AZN’s Faslodex/fulvestrant, which was approved for use in the 2L metastatic HR+ 
breast cancer setting post endocrine therapy by the FDA in 2002. The approval was 
based on Ph3 trials studying the drug as monotherapy vs. aromatase inhibitors in 
breast cancer patients with disease progression post endocrine therapy. The 
investigator-led PADA-1 trial demonstrated that, among patients receiving first-line 
aromatase inhibitor plus palbociclib, an early switch from the aromatase inhibitor to 
fulvestrant while continuing palbocicliub upon detection of ESR1 mutation- and 
before disease progression- significantly improved median PFS vs. continuting 
aromatase inhibitor plus palbociclib, at 11.9 months vs. 5.7 months. However, 
fulvestrant has to be administered intramuscularly due to its poor oral bioavailability 
and PK profile, which has been a key limitation to its uptake. 
Next-gen oral SERDS, where ROP, AZN and LLY have emerged as key players in 
n
large cap pharma, aim to offer a more convenient dosing, broader systemic 
exposure, and stronger estrogen receptor inhibition.  The two current oral SERDs 
on the market, Menarini’s Orserdu (approved in January 2023) and LLY’s Inluriyo 
(approved in September 2025) are only approved in the 2L metastatic settings. 
AZN’s Etcamah/camizesctrant was recently granted accelerated FDA approval in 
combination with a CDK4/6 inhibitor in the metastatic switch setting, despite a 
negative ODAC vote.  Our KOL diligence points to reservations on uptake per both 
clinical and logisitical questions.  In terms of commercial uptake, Menarini’s Orserdu 
was launched in 1Q23 and has reached quarterly revenue of ~$166mn/quarter per 
IQVIA data, although the trend seems flat over the past few quarters post LLY’s

Menarini’s elacestrant (Orserdu) 
Menarini’s (private) elacestrant was the first oral SERD on the market with FDA approval 
in 2023 for use in the 2L+ setting (prior chemo allowed) for HR-positive, HER2-negative, 
ESR1-mutated advanced breast cancer patients with disease progression following

publicly available, Orserdu has reached quarterly revenue of ~$166mn/quarter in 2Q26 
since its 1Q23 launch per IQVIA data, although the trend seems flat over the past few 
quarters post LLY’s Inluriyo launch.  
The second Phase 3 trial, ELEGANT, is ongoing and testing elacestrant vs. tamoxifen or 
aromatase inhibitors in the adjuvant setting. This Phase 3 ELEGANT trial is designed for 
adults who have already completed at least 24 months but not more than 60 months of 
adjuvant endocrine therapy (AIs or tamoxifen) with or without a CDK4/6 inhibitor, with 
the efficacy primary endpoint Invasive Breast Cancer-Free Survival (IBCFS) and 
secondary endpoints Distant Relapse-Free Survival (DRFS), OS, and IDFS. The trial is 
expected to enroll ~ 4,220 patients, with a primary completion date of August 2029.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
