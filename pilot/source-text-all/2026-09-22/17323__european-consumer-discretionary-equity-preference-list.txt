Positioning and key trends
We see a rebound in luxury spending in 2026 after two years of adjustment,
driven by newness, higher volumes, and a resilient high-end consumer. The
recovery is still in its early stages and could take time. Second quarter results
continued to show improvement in underlying trends for most luxury companies,
despite being slower than expected. The US market continues to support the
sector, while the recovery in China is still lacking momentum.
In the current market environment, we prefer hard luxury (jewelry) over soft luxury
(leather goods). The jewelry sector has undergone several structural changes in
recent years, supporting more sustained, multi-year demand. Unlike leather goods,
jewelry has seen only modest price increases, narrowing the price gap and making
it more accessible. Brands have shifted their product mix toward contemporary,
recognizable designs and expanded offerings at entry and mid-price points,
making jewelry more appealing as an everyday accessory—especially for self-

adidas AG
DE000A1EWWW0
Most Preferred
Weight: 9.5%
Added on 2025-10-01
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
Given its recent derating, we think Adidas valuations are now
attractive and we see upside given its appealing growth profile,
supported by athleisure trends.
Company profile
adidas AG is the second-largest sportswear brand globally after Nike.
The brand is present across both lifestyle and performance product
categories. Footwear is the largest contributor to the business (>50%
of sales), followed by apparel and accessories. Its products are
distributed globally with wholesale, making ~60% of its sales. GBL
owns ~8% of the share capital.
What drives our opinion
Adidas continues to deliver solid progress on its strategic plan. The
momentum remains strong for the terrace sneaker segment. China
will be key to the equity story. Adidas sales have suffered a strong
decline in China over the last couple of years, but sales now seemed
to have stabilized. Adidas is now looking to regain market share in
China in 2026 by leveraging its strong momentum in the Terrace
offerings. Despite its recent rerating, we think Adidas’s valuation (P/E)
vs. peers remains attractive given the prospect of a stronger top line
and better a EBIT margin. Risks: Weaker consumer demand in
adidas’s major markets of North America, EMEA, or Greater China;
momentum of the Terrace line fading faster than expected; Adidas
failing to gain traction in its performance offering; prolonged supply
chain disruptions or an escalating trade war, adding costs and
weighing on margins; geopolitical developments hurting consumer
sentiment in China, leading to a boycott like the one in 2021 and
adverse FX moves.

Company
Amadeus
ES0109067019
Most Preferred
Weight: 10.0%
Added on 2025-10-24
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
We continue to see a long runway for Amadeus in its core markets
and believe the global distribution system (GDS) will retain its
importance. We believe the company has shown its execution in
NDC, which goes a long way to address the bear argument of
notably disintermediation risk for Amadeus. As the company
continues to mature its NDC offering, we believe the market will
reward the improving competitive positioning and visibility.
Company profile
Amadeus is a leading travel ticket global distribution system (GDS)
with 50% share and is also a leading provider of outsourced
passenger-related IT solutions for airlines with 2bn passengers
boarded in 2023 (46% of all passengers flown). The GDS industry is
dominated by three global players (Amadeus, Travelport, Sabre), but
faces substitution/disintermediation as passengers increasingly go
direct rather than use agents. Air IT should grow by adding new
airlines and the cross-selling of new modules, and there is an
emerging opportunity in hospitality IT. Amadeus IPOed in 2010 at
EUR11 a share.
What drives our opinion
Amadeus is built on three pillars: air ticket distribution, airline IT
systems, and hospitality booking and distribution. Its position in its
two core markets—air distribution and airline IT—has been
strengthening, supported by competitors’ indebtedness and reduced
capacity to reinvest. As a result, Amadeus has gained market share, a
trend we expect to continue over the next several years. We believe
competitive disintermediation risks from so-called New Distribution
Capabilities (NDCs) are overstated, as the lack of standardization and
the complexity of integrating NDCs with the long tail of online travel
agents (OTAs) make Amadeus a natural partner for airlines. Amadeus
has recently become more vocal in targeting NDCs, with tangible
customer success stories. The aggregator role provides natural
defensiveness, and we believe Amadeus can address competitive
threats as it has in the past. We also see a long runway in airline IT,
where Amadeus holds a dominant market share, with further gains
likely from the displacement of in-house solutions and adoption by
lower-budget airlines. Hospitality IT remains ripe for growth, given the
market’s fragmentation and low levels of digitalization. Amadeus has
one of the highest free cash flow yields in our software coverage, and
we rate it as an above-average quality software business, given its
competitive positioning. Valuations (P/E) remain undemanding versus
historical norms. Key risks include faster or stronger disintermediation
from direct connects between airlines and OTAs, more rapid adoption
of NDCs before Amadeus can establish partnerships (potentially
enabling new aggregators), a slower-than-expected recovery in
corporate travel, and adverse FX movements.

Compagnie Financiere Richemont SA
CH0210483332
Most Preferred
Weight: 11.0%
Added on 2023-10-06
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
The strong growth and profitability of Cartier should help Richemont
to recover after the share price underperformance. We think
valuation (P/E) are appealing at current levels.
Company profile
Richemont is the world’s largest hard luxury player, dominant in the
jewellery category (>50% of sales). It derives most of its profits
(>90% of EBIT) from its Jewellery Maisons division (Cartier, VCA and
Buccellati brands), with the remainder attributable to Specialist
Watchmakers, and other smaller brands. The group is controlled by
the Rupert family.
What drives our opinion
Richemont offers high barriers to entry, good pricing power, and
superior free cash-flow generation. Within Jewellery Maisons, the
jewellery category represented by Cartier, Van Cleef and, to a much
lesser extent, Buccellati, we see strong long-term revenue growth
prospects, elevated and resilient profitability, and increased
polarization between brands, which should favor strong, established
brands with longstanding heritage. Jewellery Maisons represents the
largest part of the group profitability and is key to the equity story.
The group has a greater ability to increase prices than peers to its less
aggressive pricing policy in recent years. The profitability of its
specialist watchmakers segment is gradually increasing, despite
structural headwinds. The luxury watch business will likely continue
to see volatility, but Richemont owns among the most well known
brands (Piaget, IWC, Jaeger-LeCoultre). We also like the group’s
strong balance sheet and see the dividend rising over time. The latest
results show the strong momentum of JM (up 14%) and especially
Cartier driven by US and European consumers. Richemont is trading
at a 20-year relative lows vs. peers (P/E). Key risks for Richemont: 1)
external influence on consumption, such as terrorism and epidemics,
2) drastic political changes, 3) pricing pressure, 4) brand value, 5)
management execution and successor issues, 6) perception in equity
markets about the long-term growth story, 7) foreign exchange rates,
8) the high cyclicality of the watches business and 9) Trade war and
tariffs increase.

Compass Group

Most Preferred
Weight: 13.0%
Added on 2025-10-01
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
Compass Group remains a high quality company which we like in the
current volatile environment.
Company profile
Compass was reintroduced to the UK market in 2001 after being de-
merged from Granada Compass. It operates in two main business
areas: contract catering (c85% of sales) and multi-services (c15%). Its
activities can be divided into a number of segments: business &
industry, education, sports & leisure, healthcare as well as defence,
offshore & remote. North America accounts for c65% of group sales
while Europe is close to 25%, with RoW c10%. Over the last decade,
the company has been highly successful in driving margins and
revenues.
What drives our opinion
We believe Compass can continue to outperform the market, as we
expect it will see mid-single-digit sales growth in the medium term.
We expect to see more outsourcing wins in education/ healthcare,
and Compass’s track record of consistent market share wins bodes
well given a weakening competitive environment. In the short term,
inflation remains a margin risk for Compass. However, the company
has a good track record of mitigating the impact of inflation. It can
pass on higher prices directly to consumers or through contracts to
companies, and as Compass has done it in the past, it can mitigate
the impact through menu changes and cost cutting. There could be a
delay in implementing measures and Compass may see a negative
impact in the short term, but we are not concerned over the medium
term. Compass is not cheap on a P/E basis compared to peers, but
quality comes with a price and given the expected margin expansion,
we still see upside to current valuations. Compass Group’s risks
include a deterioration in employment levels in the US and Europe.
Wage and food inflation are also risks to profits. Its inability to pass
on inflation as expected could weigh on margins, and negative
currency translation should weaken the US dollar versus the British
pound.

Why it is Most Preferred
H&M’s top-line has improved on the back of a better offering,
stronger momentum compared to direct peers, and the
implementation of a range of initiatives to restructure the business.
Company profile
H&M is a leading global retailer of apparel, footwear, homeware and
cosmetics operating globally through stores and online. It has grown
rapidly since inception in 1947, with a strong track record until 2008-
09. Since then, slower growth in core markets has been compounded
by heavy investments, which has not been compensated for by
stronger LFL sales growth. EBIT margins, inventories and cash flows
have all been under pressure. The founding Persson family owns
c.65% of equity as of 2025.
What drives our opinion
H&M brand’s momentum has improved compared with peers, which
should translate into sales acceleration, supported by strong
marketing initiatives. We remain confident that H&M is implementing
a range of initiatives to drive a medium-term structural turnaround.
These efforts include optimizing sourcing strategies, leveraging
technology, and increasing the focus on higher-fashion content to
appeal to a wider range of price points and limit competition. In the
current context we would expect that H&M can sustain its GM of
about 50% despite heavy promotional activities. The turnaround is
helping H&M toward a return to a 10% EBIT margin thanks to
operational leverage, which should help the rerating of the stock.
Moreover, H&M has heavily invested in its online platform and
omnichannel capabilities, creating a seamless shopping experience. Its
online sales growth sales been strong, reflecting the shift in consumer
behavior toward e-commerce. H&M is trading at a discount (P/E)
compared to history. If H&M successfully executes on a faster
recovery, getting to 10% EBIT margin in FY25e, we see valuation
upside for the stock. Risks: Levels of consumer spending, fashion
performance, FX, management and competitive action, failure in the
turnaround. Specific ESG risk factors include the corporate culture
and the consumer shift towards circular fashion.

Added on 2023-05-26

Why it is Most Preferred
Hermès has a defensive profile compared to peers, which we like in
the current macroeconomic environment. Its valuation is expensive,
but justified by the impressive operational performance and sales
growth, in our view.
Company profile
Hermès is a French luxury brand established in 1837 and to date
controlled by the founding family. It is best known for its strong
heritage in leather goods manufacturing, with demand for its iconic
handbags exceeding supply. The company operates seven divisions:
leather goods & saddlery, ready-to-wear & accessories, silk & textiles,
other Hermès métiers, perfumes & cosmetics, watches, and others. It
distributes most of its products via its own retail channel (>90% of
sales).
What drives our opinion
Hermès operates within the high-end price points of the market, with
a significant portion of its business dedicated to the most profitable
product category, namely leather goods and silks. This allows the
company to enjoy the highest margins among peers. In light of its
high-quality products, Hermès has demonstrated greater resilience
during periods of economic turbulence when compared to its
competitors. This can be attributed to the widespread appeal of its
evergreen products, such as the Kelly and Birkin bags. Hermès
reported weaker-than-expected 2Q26 earnings, with no sign of
recovery in China. Despite this, Hermès remains one of the highest-
quality luxury companies. Given these attributes, the company
commands a premium relative to the sector average on P/E.
Competition from other luxury brands and an overall slowdown in
the global economy and discretionary spending are key risks for
Hermès. Any deterioration in Hermès’ ability to maintain its brand
image would cause a slowdown in demand and be a risk for the
stock. Volatile raw material costs or supply chain disruption could also
have an adverse impact on company profitability. A trade war or
higher tariffs and also adverse FX moves are other risks.

Most Preferred
Weight: 12.0%
Added on 2019-01-02
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
Inditex is a successful omnichannel retailer with predominantly local
sourcing/production. It can adjust its collections quickly in response to
the latest trends and is less dependent on long lead times. We
therefore expect the stock to outperform.
Company profile
Founded in 1963, Inditex operates seven retailing formats with more
than 7,000 stores. Zara is the main format, accounting for c70% of
EBIT, and offers fast fashion and accessories across a broad customer
spectrum. Other formats, such as Massimo Dutti, Pull & Bear, Bershka
and Stradivarius, target different age groups and price points.
Proximity manufacturing accounts for c60% of sales, allowing quick
response to fashion trends. Exposure to Spain has fallen to c17% of
sales as growth in international markets has accelerated.
What drives our opinion
Recent years have been a challenging test to Inditex’s dominance,
with the rise of pure online players, a global pandemic, the exit of
Russia, and consumer pressures. ITX has successfully risen to the
challenges, after a change in growth strategy. It is investing heavily in
its store network expansion and focuses on improving sales-per-
square meter. The company’s focus on high-growth regions in Asia
and other emerging markets has been key. We believe this increased
revenue base should also support margin improvements, given the
scalability of the business. Recent results confirmed Inditex’s fast-
paced, near-shored business model and store rationalization strategy
have brought the group in a league of its own. In addition, we don’t
expect the ongoing channel shift to online selling to cannibalize the
company’s store sales significantly, since its market share outside of
Iberia is modest. We think Inditex should trade at a higher P/E vs.
peers given its growth strategy. In our view, the key risk is a
slowdown in economic growth, which would negatively impact
consumer confidence, while other risks include the customer
response to product ranges, the availability of suitable real estate,
management, competitive action, and adverse FX moves.

Why it is Most Preferred
Kering is undergoing an important restructuring and we believe we
could see upside to the equity story in the medium to longer term as
valuations are looking attractive.
Company profile
Kering is a French luxury goods conglomerate controlled by the
Pinault family. The group has a dominant soft luxury goods exposure,
with its portfolio centred on Gucci as its biggest brand (>50% of
EBIT), and Saint Laurent as well as Bottega Veneta also contributing
significantly.
What drives our opinion
Kering has recently undergone management changes, with a
renewed focus on restructuring the business. A swift recovery is
unlikely, as the company continues to face significant challenges from
the broader slowdown in the luxury sector, especially in China. Its
flagship brands, Gucci and Yves Saint Laurent, remain under pressure,
though low market expectations leave room for positive surprises.
2Q26 was better than expected, driven by better-than-expected sales
at Gucci. Looking ahead, there is longer-term potential for Kering’s
equity story. The group has weathered multiple cycles in the past, and
the new management is set to unveil a strategic plan next year, which
could be seen as a catalyst for a new upward cycle. In the meantime,
we expect further cost-saving measures across various brands and
possible portfolio rationalization. Valuations (P/E) are now slightly
below long term average. Risks to our view: 1) a better-than-expected
turnaround, the 2) end of trade uncertainty, 3) a rebound in lower
and medium end-consumer demand, 4) better-than-expected
economic growth, and 5) FX moves

Why it is Most Preferred
LVMH continuously demonstrates the resilience of its brand. Its
diversified portfolio has led to stable growth, and management
discipline has resulted in better execution compared to peers. We
believe these qualities are not reflected in the current share price,
offering an attractive risk-return profile. We see China as an
important upside risk for the luxury sector, should demand in 2026
improve.
Company profile
LVMH is the world’s largest luxury goods conglomerate centred on its
megabrand, Louis Vuitton, contributing >50% of its recurring EBIT.
The group is present across multiple product categories via its five
divisions: Fashion & Leather Goods, Wines & Spirits, Watches &
Jewellery, Perfumes & Cosmetics, and Selective Retailing. The business
has been historically highly acquisitive, with the most significant
recent additions being Tiffany in 2021, Christian Dior Couture in
2017, Rimowa in 2016, Loro Piana in 2013 and Bulgari in 2011. The
Arnault family remains LVMH’s majority shareholder.
What drives our opinion
LVMH has a defensive portfolio and strong brands: Louis Vuitton and
Sephora are global leaders in their core product segments, for
example. With clear control over distribution channels and sustained
innovation, the management team has made the right strategic
decisions in the past, in our view. LVMH’s 2Q26 results were slightly
above consensus with an acceleration at Dior. Leather goods was a
slight miss while other divisions beat consensus. LVMH remains one
of the highest-quality luxury companies, in our view, and the
medium- to long-term drivers remain appealing with a 9% CAGR
growth of the top line over the last 20 years. The valuation looks
appealing (P/E) compared to the five-year average given LVMH’s
strong portfolio of brands and best-in-class execution. Key risks to
LVMH include: 1) external influence on consumption, such as
terrorism and epidemics; 2) drastic political changes; 3) pricing
pressure; 4) brand value; 5) management execution and successor
issues; 6) perception in equity market about their long- term growth;
7) foreign exchange rates; and 8) intensifying trade wars and tariffs.

Added on 2025-10-01
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
Despite the recent re-rating, we like Prosus’ growth profile supported
by its acquisition strategy and its push into global food delivery.
Company profile
Prosus is a global consumer internet group and one of the largest
tech investors in the world. The group is focused on building
businesses in the online classifieds, food delivery, payments and
fintech, and education technology sectors in markets including India
and Brazil. Through its ventures team, Prosus invests in areas such as
health, logistics, blockchain and social commerce.
What drives our opinion
We favor Prosus for its strong investment track-record on fast
growing internet-related services like entertainment, e-commerce and
fintech. We believe Tencent, a key asset that makes up the bulk of
Prosus’s valuation, will continue to thrive in China’s fast-growing
internet market, serving as the main distribution platform for services
from games to e-commerce and online payments. It is difficult for
other players to replicate Tencent’s user scale, infrastructure and
ecosystem, in our view, as the company has been funded by
significant income from gaming and will further provide capital for
aggressive mobile expansion. Tencent’s WeChat has become a
leading social network and mobile-gaming distribution platform in
China and, in our view, can be monetized through advertising and e-
commerce. Its acquisition of Just Eat should lead to the creation of a
global food delivery powerhouse alongside the already established
iFood business. Key risks include further regulatory tightening on its
investments and tighter-than-expected competition across key new
economy industries that Prosus has invested in, heightened trade
tensions between the US and China, macro (e.g., FX, GDP).

Equity selection systems
Equity selections in Equity Preferences lists (EPLs) are assessments made relative to a sector/industry, country/regional or
thematic benchmark. The chosen benchmark is disclosed on the front page of each EPL. It is also used to measure the
performance of the EPL owner(s). Including a stock in the EPL constitutes neither a view on its expected, standalone absolute
performance nor a price target. Rather, EPLs are meant to support the UBS House View, with the stocks included in them
selected for their superior risk/return profiles.
Our selection is based on an assessment of the company’s fundamental outlook and valuation, the risks owning the stock
entails, including material sustainability risks and the diversification benefits it provides in an investment portfolio, among
many other factors. UBS GWM CIO‘s selection methodology enables wealth management clients to invest in a specific
investment theme or focus on a sector/industry or country/region.
The lead strategist is responsible for the CIO view on a stock, while the EPL owner(s) is/are responsible for the stock selection
within an EPL. Stocks can be selected for multiple EPLs. For consistency’s sake, a stock can only be selected as either Most
Preferred or Least Preferred, not both simultaneously. As EPL benchmarks differ, stocks do not need to be included on every
list to which they could theoretically be added.
