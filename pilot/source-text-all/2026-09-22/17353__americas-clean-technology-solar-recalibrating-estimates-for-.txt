PM Summary: Resi weakness weighing on valuations and estimates, but 
continue to see future growth potential 
 
 
Leading indicators pointing to weaker resi recovery than anticipated amid rising 
interest rates. We are incrementally more cautious on the near-term outlook for the 
resi solar sector as a result of weaker than expected leading indicators for 
installations as well as the recent increase in interest rates. In particular, resi solar 
installations in 2Q26 declined by 21% yoy, and origination trends in 3Q26 were down 
30% yoy. At the same time, on September 16, the Federal Reserve raised  benchmark 
interest rates by 25bps to 3.75%-4.00%, marking the first interest rate increase in 
three years, while also signaling for another rate hike later this year. While we 
continue to view affordability and rising utility rates as supportive of resi solar 
deployments, given the sensitivity of interest rates to solar returns, we see the 
increase in rates as a headwind to future growth. Additionally, we believe there are 
risks to consensus expectations across select resi solar companies based on market 
fundamentals. Lastly, while the resi solar group has outperformed the utility-scale 
segment year to date, we see potential risks to valuations as a result of sentiment, 
higher interest rates, and consensus revisions.  As a result, we take a tempered view 
on the near-term outlook for stocks exposed to resi solar, while continuing to favor 
utility-scale equities.

Residential solar demand remains weak 
 
 
Residential solar demand remains under pressure. While US residential solar 
installations were down 21% yoy in 2Q26, we believe underlying demand conditions 
remain weaker than reported installation data would suggest. In particular, a portion of 
2Q activity continued to benefit from residual 25D pull forward installations, which 
supported 1Q26 volumes of ~1,056MWdc before demand stepped down to 786MWdc 
in 2Q26E, or -27% yoy (Exhibit 5). We note that 2Q, which reached the lowest quarterly 
demand levels since 2020 and ~57% below peak levels in 3Q23, is expected to represent 
the trough quarter for the year, with expectations for sequential improvements through 
the balance of the year.  
However, leading indicators upstream of final interconnections, including sales 
originations and permit issuance, tracked closer to -30% yoy during the quarter. Monthly 
permits — which lead installations by roughly one to two quarters — peaked in 2025, 
rolled over sharply into early 2026 (Exhibit 4), and have remained roughly flat to slightly 
down through July and early August. As a result, we believe there are risks to the pace of 
the resi solar recovery off the 2Q26 trough, with data pointing to a below seasonal uplift 
in 3Q26. We note that Ohm Analytics currently anticipates qoq growth of ~7% and 
~11% in 3Q26 and 4Q26, respectively. As a result, we believe current channel data 
support a more cautious outlook for residential solar volumes and lower estimates 
across our residential solar exposure. 
The proximate cause is financing, which matters for how the weakness persists. TPO 
capacity has tightened and become highly concentrated as RUN has exited the affiliate 
channel and several other providers are underwriting more conservatively, leaving 
Lightreach at ~40% of the TPO market, or ~80% excluding RUN. Additionally, prepaid 
leases (PPL) were expected to reach ~10% of the resi market by 4Q26, but growth 
beyond that is gated by limited ABS appetite for the product, and Ohm expect PPL share 
to hold flat at ~10% through 2027. With capital availability, not demand, setting the 
ceiling, a higher rate backdrop is more likely to extend the trough than further 
exacerbate it, however, in our view; in other words, the bottom may be in for longer.

Revising 1Q27 revenue... Despite the typical qoq seasonal decline in 1Q27, we see the 
potential for better than seasonal trends as a result of continued traction from prepaid 
leases, ramping sales of its 5th generation battery, and some uplift from commercial 
batteries and potentially bi-directional chargers. As a result, we lower our 1Q27 forecast 
to $240mn from $269mn, which implies flat qoq core revenue growth.  
... as 2027 resets. Given this lower starting point for 2027 amid a year which we 
anticipate more typical seasonal trends, we believe it is prudent to recalibrate our 
forecasts for the year. Importantly, ENPH received ~$255mn of safe harbor orders under 
the 5% capital cost test as opposed to the physical work test method, which we view as 
mostly pull forward from 2027 since customers need to take delivery of the physical 
equipment within 105 days. These orders boosted 2026 revenue, which we estimate at 
$1.2bn, and are not expected to repeat in 2027. As a result, we are lowering our 2027 
revenue forecast by 14% to $1.1bn from $1.3bn, representing an ~$85mn yoy decline in 
revenue. However, when excluding the safe harbor revenue uplift in 2026, our 2027 
revenue forecast implies 19% yoy growth.  
Future growth drivers. Despite the lower 2027 revenue, we continue to watch for 
ENPH’s execution across several potential ~$100mn growth opportunities. This includes 
battery storage deployments in Europe, particularly in the Netherlands, continued 
traction within the C&I solar and storage markets, and executing on its bi-directional 
charging strategy. Separately, the company continues to advance its solid-state 
transformer product, which aims for first commercial deployments as early as 4Q27, 
with revenue ramping in 2028. As a result, we see the opportunity for ENPH to grow 
revenue above industry solar growth, and we now anticipate 2028 revenue of $1.2bn, or 
growth of 14% yoy.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring
