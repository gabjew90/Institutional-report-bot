South Korea plans a US$120 bn investment in 8 US nuclear power plants; 
Japan and South Korea help rebuild US nuclear power industry 
Based on South Korean media reports, the US-South Korea nuclear power 
investment agreement is set to be signed on September 18, which is a 
crucial step towards the realization of the direct US$200 bn investment 
commitment to the US by South Korea from 2025. The US has requested 
US$120 bn be invested in the construction of 8 US large nuclear reactors. 
Regarding the model adoption of these 8 nuclear power units, the two sides 
are currently negotiating a “2+4+4” plan. South Korea will lead the 
construction of 2 self-developed APR1400 units, while Westinghouse Electric 
will lead the construction of 2 AP1000 units, with the remaining 4 units to 
be confirmed later. Meanwhile, in mid-August, the US made its utmost 
efforts to propel Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) to take a stake in 
Westinghouse, trying to resolve their long-standing intellectual property 
lawsuit in terms of nuclear power technology source through an equity 
structure change, and make up for weakness in the US manufacturing 
industry by leveraging South Korea’s engineering delivery capability. 
Combined with Japan’s previous commitment under the US-Japan economic 
and trade framework to invest up to US$100 bn to support Westinghouse’s 
AP1000 construction and Hitachi’s SMR planning, the US is now leveraging 
tariffs and domestic supply chain restructuring to rebuild the Western 
nuclear power industry alliance centered on American technical standards, 
on the back of the capital and heavy industrial bases of both Japan and 
South Korea. 
August long-term contract prices close to US$100/lb; upcoming peak season 
for nuclear power purchases 
According to TradeTech, long-term uranium contract prices rose 1.6% 
MoM/18% YoY to US$97/lb in August, setting a new record (vs the peak of 
US$95/lb in 2007), which marked long-term contract price hikes for 12 
consecutive months. In August, long-term uranium contracting volume 
surpassed 1 Mlbs. (Continued on Page 2)

Despite a decline in nuclear power plants’ contracting volume due to seasonality, Western nuclear 
power plants are running out of fuel inventories, forcing nuclear power plants in the US and 
Europe to return to the long-term contract market, which indicates the official kickoff of a 
contracting cycle for American nuclear power plants. In August, uranium spot prices rose 3% 
MoM/18% YoY to US$89.5/lb, gradually rebounding towards long-term contract prices, while spot 
trading volume of uranium rose 28% YoY to 3.2 Mlbs, with a MoM decline due to a seasonal effect. 
90% of nuclear fuel transactions for uranium producers and nuclear power plants were priced 
based on long-term contracts. That is, despite spot prices remaining low, the record-high long-
term contract prices can significantly improve existing miners’ profitability. Combined with the 
upcoming peak season for nuclear fuel from 4Q26F (the US nuclear power fiscal year ends in 
September, with budget updates scheduled for October, and thus the period from the fourth 
quarter of the current year to the first quarter of the next year is generally a peak season for 
nuclear power purchases), we expect a narrower gap between spot prices and long-term contract 
prices going forward. 
 
Kickoff of a construction boom for Western nuclear power plants; uranium prices likely to reach 
US$120/lb thanks to new uranium supply 
To meet demand for nuclear power generation, uranium prices need to rise to US$120/lb, so as to 
match supply with demand. The current long-term uranium contracting volume remains lower 
than uranium demand for electricity generation, indicating still falling fuel inventories at US 
nuclear power plants. Given rapidly increasing power demand in the US, along with policy-driven 
construction acceleration of large nuclear power plants and SMRs, the nuclear energy sector is 
likely to remain a beneficiary. Attention should be paid to SPUT (U-U.CN), a physical uranium trust 
fund with risk exposure to uranium prices and without any mining risk. For uranium producers, 
we suggest focusing on CGN Mining (1164.HK; BUY), and Kazatomprom (KAP.LI), which has a low 
valuation and the lowest production costs. For nuclear power construction, BWXT (BWXT.US), which 
produces core components for nuclear-powered submarine reactors and commercial reactors, and 
Solstice (SOLS.US), the only nuclear fuel conversion facility in the US, merit attention.
