Single Stocks. Macro remains the focus vs micro and at the time of writing this – WTI is -3% with TTF
-6% … Highlighting a handful of topical single stocks on the desk over the last couple of days / this
weekend. Feedback welcome…
| • | HAL: The stock is +17% YTD – and that’s in the context of oil +65%, OIH +38% and XLE +41%.
NAM pricing increase expectations aren’t high near term – and we think this is one of the main reasons for
relative underperformance vs the rest of the group. HAL has been taking market share in the ME from
peers over the last 6 months, gaining more traction in LATAM (this AM highlighting MOU to support VZ)
and the stock is cheap – 7.2x EBITDA vs SLB at 9x and BKR at ~9.7x. Inbounds have generally been
more cautious over the last couple of weeks, though do think there are some interesting bull cases from
here. Would think if we are in a de-escalation tape – International theme can gain some traction again. | | • |
SEI: The stock is +30% over the last month – and is one of the best performing BTM names YTD. Feels
like no secret at this point most specialists are bullish on the broader BTM theme into YE (midterms cited
as a near term headwind, but acceleration of power announcements broadly expected on go forward).
Debate from here is not on quality or path to additional contracts, but mostly on relative upside / levels
(low – mid $70s and important level). Bulls continue to highlight upside to Street numbers, execution
track record, and camping out in ‘quality’ for now. | | • | EQT & EXE: We mentioned this last week – but
sentiment on the US natural gas complex remains muted and doesn’t screen as that surprising to us given
the tape (more focus on oil/TTF/products, production beats, milder weather, shoulder season). Both EQT
and EXE trading at the low end / below the low end of historical trading ranges. Most asked question on
the desk – what gets the stocks working again from here? Even with oil flushing this morning, the stocks
are underperforming vs large cap oil and Majors (HH is -130 bps at the time of writing this). 3Q
expectations have been leaning more cautious, specialists are leaning negative and generalist interest is
muted. | | • | VG: Near term sentiment on TTF remains more bullish (see GS takeaways from Gastech in
Thailand last week), though the stocks have been underwhelming. QatarEnergy headlines a couple of
weeks back screen bullish VG relative to peers and there was more frustration on lack of px response.
Bears making the case that even if TTF moves to +100 EUR/mwh in the winter, demand destruction will
be the focus. | | • | XOM: Has been one of the worst performing Majors over the last month. Would think
Refining tailwinds broadly are positive for EPS revisions, though Qatar downtime + Joliet headlines +
more recent Singapore weakness are what bears are highlighting. Headline last week on Venezuela didn’t

EU Desk – Commodity Views. Update from our commods desk out this weekend (). Good mix of both
push and pull highlighted in the note (continued attacks in ME/Russia vs path to de-escalation / broader
diplomatic efforts). Including a couple of snippets below…
| • | EU GAS: GS trading desk remains constructive given reduced Middle East LNG supply, upcoming
winter weather risk and against a backdrop of seasonally low European inventory. There were some
further bullish developments over the past week with (1) an outage cutting 35% of production at Norway’s
largest field, serving as a reminder of the fragility of European supply and (2) Germany's Economy
Minister looking at ways to boost gas storage levels which has helped to drive strength in term structure.
We attribute this week’s softer price action to length liquidation with positioning still at ~65% of recent
highs. However, feedback from physical market participants at the Bangkok Gastech conference was
pessimistic on the chances of the near-term end to the Middle East conflict with supply impact becoming
more extended. | | • | Refined Products: There are some signs of caution for bulls with (1) US pushing
Russia/Ukraine to cease attacks on energy infrastructure, (2) Macron calling for talks on further SPR
releases, and (3) further evidence of price driven demand destruction (4) some recovery in Middle East
exports with September exports at ~400kbd vs. ~150kbd in prior 3m and ~800kbd pre-conflict. There have
been some headlines about potential for the US to curtail diesel exports (~1.6mbd US exports vs. ~112mb
US inventory) , however, we think this unlikely given it would not solve medium term global supply
issues and is expected to get pushback from US refining industry. | | • | Gold: Gold performed reasonably
well on the week considering the context of the Fed coming in more hawkish on dots (2 this year) and no
cut next year. It’s back above where we went into the Fed on Wednesday and we think it is noteworthy that
China, who are very long, held onto length. Skew has traded lower on the week as market removes tail
risk, although we have not seen substantial liquidation of positions. | | • | Copper: Copper rallied following
broader risk recovery post-Fed, alongside with micro fundamentals keeping LME in a post trade deficit,
specifically: (1) US imports remain elevated at ~200k (African swing units still going), (2) Chinese
upstream inventory remains under pressure at -46% YoY, (3) Finished product inventory also low at -12%
YoY, (4) Scrap production weak at -7% YoY in August , improved from -20% YoY in July, but still
boosting cathode demand which explains the apparent vs. end outperformance, and (5) SHFE / LME Arb:
Oct at -$50 with $85 CIF is impressive with >$14k Copper. We struggle to see Beijing allowing significant
exports even if the tolling arb opens given current Critical Mineral focus, which removes a major release
valve. |
