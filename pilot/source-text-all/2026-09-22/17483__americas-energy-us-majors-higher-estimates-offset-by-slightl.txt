In this note, we update estimates for ExxonMobil (XOM), Chevron Corp (CVX), and 
ConocoPhillips (COP) to reflect higher commodity prices and refining margins. We 
raise our Brent oil price estimate to $80/b in 2027, while maintaining $75/b in 2028 
with a constant $5/b Brent-WTI spread. We also raise our crack spread estimates, 
with NYH (Brent) 5:3:2 increasing from roughly $26/b in 2028 to $29/b, to reflect 
tighter refining fundamentals, including low inventory levels, global supply 
disruptions, resilient demand, and widening crude differentials. Among U.S. Majors, 
we continue to highlight Buy-ratings on CVX and COP, with 15% and 17% total return 
potential from current levels, respectively. Please see here for additional details on 
our recent roundtable event with CVX.

ExxonMobil (XOM) 
Our View. We expect shares to continue to benefit from greater refining exposure 
n
and positive estimate revisions into the quarter, where management sees elevated 
margins driven partly by lower Chinese exports and product supply disruptions in 
the Middle East and between Russia and Ukraine, amid some evidence of demand 
destruction. We also highlight strong operations across advantaged assets, including 
Guyana, where the company is accelerating cost and capital recovery and evaluating 
a ninth FPSO, and the Permian Basin, where it is developing stackable technologies 
to improve productivity. As the company invests in frontier exploration, we look for 
commentary on potential Venezuela activity. Management also continues to 
advance major global export projects in a tight supply backdrop, expecting to 
increase ownership in Papua New Guinea LNG, while targeting final investment 
decisions for both Mozambique LNG and Papua New Guinea LNG later this year. 
With this said, relative valuation keeps us on the sidelines, as shares trade at a 6% 
free cash flow yield in 2028 at $75/b Brent, compared to an average 8% for CVX and 
COP. Investors also note greater relative Middle East exposure, where management 
expects a full 3Q26 closure of the Strait of Hormuz to reduce regional production by 
~750 kbd relative to 2025.

refining margins, and adjustments to production and pricing realizations. Key risks 
relate to commodity prices, refining margins, and operational execution.

ConocoPhillips (COP) 
Our View. We see a positive outlook for shares as a series of major growth projects, 
n
including NFE, NFS, Port Arthur, and Willow, generates a robust free cash flow 
inflection through 2030, with incremental high-margin upside from low-cost 
inventory across the Permian, Eagle Ford, and Bakken basins. We expect the 
combination of these growth sources, alongside ~$1 bn in cost reductions, to deliver 
a ~20-25% free cash flow per share CAGR through the end of the decade and 
support shareholder returns, at around ~45% of cash flow from operations. More 
recently, management also reported early completion of a $5 bn asset disposition 
target, additional commercial LNG offtake, and new low-cost opportunities in the 
Middle East. In a challenging geopolitical environment, however, we highlight 
stronger exposure to OECD regions, including Alaska, Canada, and the Lower 48. 
Ahead of first oil at Willow in early 2029, investors focus on the upcoming winter 
construction season and summer sea lift of processing modules. While the stock 
remains widely owned among investors, some still express concerns around 
long-term capital spend. Middle East operations also draw some investor attention, 
though we note management remains confident in Qatar LNG projects, with 
potential delays on the order of months.
