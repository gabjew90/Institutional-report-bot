Ruling Modestly Negative, But Preferred 
Rollout Likely More than Offsets Impact
Zillow lost its request for preliminary injunction in the lawsuit against Midwest Real 
Estate Data (MRED) and Compass. For context, Zillow filed a lawsuit against MRED 
and Compass in May 2026 alleging the defendants "erected barriers to information 
that harm or threaten harm to sellers, buyers, and competitors by hiding real estate 
listings behind a velvet rope in a Private Listing Network (PLN)." As a first step in the 
process, it had requested the Court to grant a preliminary injunction, which the 
Court denied, and dissolved the temporary restraining order that allowed Zillow to 
access MRED's listings. In addition, the Court granted MRED's motion to compel 
arbitration; pending the outcome, Zillow's claims against MRED are stayed. 
Moreover, Zillow's claims against Compass are also stayed pending the arbitration 
ruling. While the final outcome of the arbitration could take time, we think there are 
long-term implications for Zillow and Compass arising from the ruling that could 
also potentially change the way buyers find homes for sale nationwide.
In our view, there are multiple reasons for investors to assume the ruling is 
negative for Zillow. 1) Compass has already cut off the direct listings from its owned 
and operated realtors on Zillow nationwide, and it is doubling down on pursuing 
Private Exclusives within its 3PM (or three-phased marketing) strategy. Compass 
has significant market share in the larger markets, and particularly among the 
higher-priced homes. If Compass continues to disallow its owned & operated 
agents to post directly on Zillow, and MRED continues to cut off Zillow because of 
its Listings Standards policy, then Zillow may lose access to a substantial number 
of listings in the larger markets, including in Chicago. 2) As adoption for private 
listings increases, we think more and more potential home buyers may prefer to 
visit the websites of Compass and its partners frequently, or exclusively, depending 
on the market and the price range since Compass -- most likely -- may have more 
listings than Zillow in select markets and for higher priced properties. 3) Post the 
ruling, MRED likely will have limited incentive to let Zillow access its listings, unless 
Zillow conforms to MRED's rules regarding non-exclusion of listings that had been 
privately, and exclusively, listed on Compass before becoming public. To the extent 
MRED expands geographically, or other MLSs similarly enforce exclusion-related 
rules, Zillow could lose its feed in more markets, which could also impact traffic. 
Zillow announced (link) it remains "committed to fighting for a fair and transparent 
housing market." At the same time, it is quite possible Zillow may let its Listings 
Standards Rule lapse, in which case, it will get access to all the listings when they 
become available publicly in Stage 3 of the 3PM strategy. 4) Some other 
brokerages, such as Douglas Elliman and Howard Hanna, are also pursuing a

Quantifying the longer-term impact
We looked at FY25 financials, as an example, to quantify the potential impact to 
Zillow from Private Exclusives.
In Scenario A, below, we assume Compass and a limited number of premium-
segment brokerages adopt a 3PM strategy. However, given their focus on premium 
homes which represent ~HSD % of total number of homes, we think the impact 
may be disproportionately larger in terms of the value of the homes. In its lawsuit, 
Zillow noted that MRED's private listings represented 18% of home sales within 
Chicagoland in 2024, six years after the initial rollout. To the extent that some of the 
homes are sold during the Private Exclusive phase itself, that would reduce the 
number of listings available on Zillow, which would limit the total amount of 
commission dollars agents could potentially earn from Zillow listings, and 
consequently, the amount of marketing dollars they may spend on Zillow.
Depending on the adoption of the strategy both among brokerages and sellers, the 
impact to Zillow's TAM could be in the low-SD to low-DD vicinity, in our view. This 
takes into account both the adoption, as well as the potential that a fraction of the 
homes could be sold during the exclusive period itself, and thus, may not even be 
listed on Zillow at all. We estimate the likely revenue loss in the low-to-high SD % 
of total revenue, which is modest, but given high decremental margins, the 
impact to Adj EBITDA could be significantly larger.

Another factor that could come into play would be fragmentation of the traffic. 
Zillow currently is the site with the most comprehensive listings in real estate online, 
that includes both for-sale and rental properties. Consequently, it is not surprising 
it has >70% market share of visits in the real-estate category and its brand is virtually 
synonymous with real-estate, in the same/similar vein as Google is to Search. 
However, depending on the adoption of Private Exclusives, potential buyers will 
need to visit individual brokerage sites -- in addition to Zillow, or instead of Zillow 
(because the local brokers will have access to all the local listings in any case) -- 
which suggests that Zillow may not, any more, be the most definitive and most 
comprehensive source of all real estate listings. This fragmentation of online 
traffic could reduce Zillow's attractiveness to agents as a marketing channel, 
which, in turn, could adversely affect Zillow's take rates even in Preferred Markets.
If we assume -- in steady state -- that Zillow's take rate could be 0.5-2.0bps above 
FY25 reported levels, or at 50-65% of the levels we assumed in the above analysis, 
we see the potential for marginally higher revenue and Adj EBITDA. Net-net, while 
Zillow may have lost in the first stage of the lawsuit, we think the overall impact 
to financials in the longer term may be more modest than feared.

indexing the contracted cash flows to inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates - these are common in 
emerging markets.  The index fixings may - by construction - lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables 
they are intended to track. The choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating 
coupon rates (i.e., coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons.
