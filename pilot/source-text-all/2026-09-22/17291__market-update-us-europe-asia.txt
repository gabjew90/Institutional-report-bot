U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.17% | Nasdaq +0.39% | Dow -0.18%
Synopsis

US equities finished mixed on Friday, ending off their worst levels, but breadth was negative and the equal-weight S&P trailed the cap-
weighted index by nearly 70 bp. The AI trade was firmer, with memory and semis among the leaders, while big tech was mixed. Laggards
included industrial metals, chemicals, food, homebuilders, media and rails; outperformers included E&Cs, machinery, biotech, hotels,
airlines and China tech. The market struggled for direction in a fairly quiet session after Thursday's gains, and the backup in Treasury
yields was the main focus as yields reversed their post-FOMC gains. Treasuries were weaker with some curve flattening and yields up 8-
9 bp at the short end; there was not much specific behind the move, which came despite the oil selloff. The dollar index was down 0.1%,
gold finished up 0.6%, silver was up 1.5%, Bitcoin futures rose 5.9% to near best levels, and WTI crude (October) finished down 1.6%.
Other areas of focus included depressed sentiment, an expected slowdown in FY27 EPS growth, narrow breadth, with the five largest
companies accounting for a record 27% of NTM S&P 500 earnings according to BofA, falling global oil inventories, the second-largest
triple witching on record with ~$7T of US options notional value expiring, and Goldman Sachs' view that CTAs will be sellers in all scenarios

Macro

Middle East diplomacy gains some traction: Axios reported on Thursday that Trump is considering whether to resume major combat op-
erations against Iran to bring the conflict to a close, though the comments come ahead of a planned meeting with leaders from six Gulf
countries at the UN General Assembly to determine the next phase of the war. The administration also granted visas to top Iranian officials
to attend the Assembly. China's influence was seen as a further positive after Beijing privately asked Iran to have the Houthis curb military
activity in the Red Sea, following an appeal from Saudi Arabia, going further than its prior public statements. Fresh Saudi-Houthi strikes
and Red Sea displacement kept volatility elevated, but Saudi Arabia expects partial restoration of the damaged East-West pipeline ca-
pacity within days, and Trump said the US is "hopefully toward the end" of the war with Iran, although Tehran has not confirmed that
negotiations have resumed.

Breadth deteriorates to fresh lows: The S&P 500 cumulative advance/decline over the past 21 trading days fell to -1409 on Friday, the
lowest since 30-Mar (-1464 in the trading month that followed the start of the Iran war). Just 27.8% of S&P 500 companies are above
their 50-day moving average, the lowest since 2-Apr, while 48% are now below their 200-day, under 50% for the first time since 6-Apr. At
the same time, 28 S&P 500 companies made fresh 52-week highs, tied with 10-Sep for the most since 22-Jun. On shorter-term trends,
27% of S&P 500 companies marked four-week lows, including 55% of consumer discretionary names, 48% of utilities and 43% of com-
munication services, against just 4% of tech. Chips (SOX) are underperforming software (IGV) by over 35% over the past 63 days, the
biggest underperformance in at least 10 years, and momentum (MTUM) trails the S&P 500 by 11.1% over the same window, near the
lowest (early September's 11.3%) since Mar-23. All figures are as of the 14 ET hour Friday.

2027 earnings growth draws scrutiny: Goldman Sachs strategists argued that concerns over an earnings bubble are unfounded given a
strong economic outlook and the ongoing AI boom, though they said companies are "over-earning" at the current EPS growth pace and
expect slower profit growth rather than a collapse. Goldman projects an 11% earnings increase next year and forecasts the S&P 500 will
rally 14% to 8,700. That remains below Street consensus, with FactSet's latest Earnings Insight showing analysts projecting FY27 EPS
growth of 15.1%. BofA strategists said positioning is too bullish given expected 2027 profit moderation, noting the 10-15% EPS consensus
implies ISM Manufacturing remains above 53; the index has held above 53 since May, and the prior 53 level was in Aug-22.

Week in review, the bearish case: The AI trade came under pressure on Monday after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for pacing of
frontier capability growth, citing concerns about recursive self-improvement and the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident, a call endorsed by
Altman, Musk and Hassabis; the SOX fell nearly 6% and Goldman Sachs' AI vs AI at Risk basket fell nearly 10%, its worst day since
DeepSeek. Oil rose another ~5.5% over the first two days of the week on the closure of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline (~4% of global
oil supply), the broader Houthi narrative and the postponement of a planned GCC-Iran meeting on a temporary shipping lane through the
Strait of Hormuz. FOMC takeaways were hawkish, with unanimous support for the hike, 16 of 18 dots showing at least one more hike this
year, 2027 dots mostly holding at the new higher level and a 19 bp increase in the long-run dot, alongside Warsh's framing of the hike as
"removing a dose of accommodation." Goldman Sachs noted the S&P 500 has fallen an average of -2% over the first three months of the
last seven tightening cycles. BofA and Goldman Sachs disappointed at the Barclays Global Financial Conference, higher gas prices were
cited as a continued headwind for the lower-income consumer, the September Empire manufacturing index fell to 7.6 from 20.6 against
a 15.0 consensus, and Wells Fargo flagged 5-10% downside risk.

Week in review, the bullish case: Pushback against frontier-model pacing centred on the argument that slower capability growth does not
mean lower AI capex in a compute-constrained environment, with semi demand increasingly driven by inference and agentic use cases.
The S&P 500 posted its fourth straight 1%+ rally on a Thursday following an FOMC meeting, with the hawkish Fed spun as a positive for
credibility. The oil rally lost momentum later in the week on better Saudi supply headlines, including reports the kingdom aims to return
about half of the East-West pipeline's capacity within days. August core retail sales rose 1.4% after a 0.4% contraction in July, ahead of
the +0.5% consensus. Consumer resilience was the prominent theme at the Goldman Sachs Global Consumer and Retail Conference,
large-cap bank managements at Barclays were broadly upbeat on 2H26 and 2027, and companies across industries, particularly airlines,
said solid demand, pricing, fuel surcharges and capacity discipline were cushioning higher fuel costs. Sentiment offered a contrarian
signal: the AAII bull-bear spread deteriorated to -24.5% in the week ended 16-Sep from -1.3%, the weakest since late-April/early-May
2025, and Goldman Sachs' sentiment indicator ended last week at -0.5 standard deviations, the lowest since March.

EU Market Recap
Germany -1.6% | France -1.5% | UK -1.4%
Synopsis

European equities finished lower on Friday, near worst levels, leaving the Stoxx 600 little changed on the week after positive sessions on
Wednesday and Thursday. Healthcare and tech were the notable gainers: Redcare Pharmacy and Sandoz led healthcare, with some focus
on Novo Nordisk's $1.4B deal with Orbis and its upcoming investor day, while Infineon and VAT Group rose on upgrades and ASML was
higher after Morgan Stanley reiterated Overweight, highlighting upside risk to 2026/27 estimates. Telecom, food and beverage and retail
were the big laggards. Orange fell on a Morgan Stanley downgrade, Airtel Africa was the biggest telecom decliner after reports its Airtel
Money unit is considering a smaller London IPO, and Elisa fell after JPMorgan cut its target and reiterated Underweight, citing competitive
risks in Finland. Nestlé weighed on food and beverage after Russia placed its local business under temporary external administration,
while in retail LPP rose after Poland's largest fashion retailer reported a 64% increase in Q2 net profit. In rates, the UK 10-year was around
5.3%, off Monday's 19-year high of 5.42%, and the Bund was steady at 3.52%. The regional calendar was light, leaving policy and fiscal
sequencing in focus: German PPI and UK retail sales both beat forecasts, the ECB's Consumer Expectations Survey showed households
lifting inflation views, brokers now broadly see the BoE hiking in November and economists see the ECB delivering its last hike in Decem-
ber. Chancellor Healey is expected to urge the EU to ensure Britain is included in its "Made in Europe" industrial policy.
Macro

Eurozone inflation pressures build: The ECB's August Consumer Expectations Survey showed inflation expectations rising at every horizon,
a signal favouring the hawks. One-year expectations rose to 3.0% from 2.9%, the policy-sensitive three-year gauge to 2.9% from 2.7% and
five-year to 2.5% from 2.4%, while perceived inflation over the past year was unchanged at 3.5% and expected growth over the next year
stable at −1.2%. Households appear to have re-priced the energy shock while forecasters still assume it fades: ECB September staff
projections see headline inflation at 3.0% in 2026, 2.5% in 2027 and 2.1% in 2028, peaking at 3.6% in Q4 2026, and President Lagarde
said today that second-round effects are still absent. German August producer prices rose 4.6% y/y against 4.1% expected and 1.1%
m/m, driven by energy at +8.3% y/y; excluding energy, prices rose 3.1% y/y and only 0.2% m/m. Metals rose 14.8% and basic chemicals
11.2% while food prices fell 4.3%, a split that supports ECB officials inclined to look through the energy shock and underlines the squeeze
on German industry. ECB rhetoric leaned more hawkish over the week, with officials flagging the need for gradual rate hikes.

UK data, gilts and the October Budget: August retail sales rose 0.5% m/m against a 0.2% decline expected and a 0.5% decline in July, and
were up 2.4% y/y against 1.9% expected. Clothing, food and department stores drove the beat, helped by hot weather and the World Cup,
while fuel volumes fell further on higher pump prices. The beat may not prove durable, given job cuts and an expected 25% energy-price
jump in January that could push inflation above 4%. The BoE held this week but signalled more hikes, and its decision to slow the pace of
QT offered some support to gilts. PM Burnham has shifted to a tougher tone on the October Budget, citing deteriorating market conditions
and sharply higher energy prices, and estimates of the reduction in fiscal headroom range between £10B-£12B from the prior £23.6B,
with some economists predicting it could fall as low as £5B. Barclays called UK equities unloved but not a bad place to hide, with high oil
and thin technology exposure supporting the FTSE 100 as a lower-beta diversifier, though its 5.5% dividend yield (including buybacks)
faces competition from a 10-year gilt above 5%.

France's 2027 budget: PM Lecornu previewed the 2027 budget ahead of the 30-Sep Council of Ministers and the Assembly debate from
12-Oct, warning that without spending cuts the 2027 deficit would reach 6.5%. He raised the savings target from Bercy's €30B to €54B
to make a 5% deficit attainable after extra defence spending; analysts see 5% as still a very large deficit for ~1% growth, with Oddo
suggesting 0.5% growth is more likely, and this year's aim has already slipped to 5.5%. Identified measures look thin, including a rolled-
over corporate-tax surcharge on large firms, frozen ministry envelopes except defence, a civil-service pay-index freeze (~€2B) and under
€6B from pensioners. Lecornu pledged not to use Article 49.3 if the left does not obstruct.

Merz under pressure ahead of state election: Chancellor Merz faces pressure as Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania votes on Sunday, with
AfD poised to come first again after winning Saxony-Anhalt on 6-Sep and some thoughts that the CDU could fall below the 5% threshold
required to enter parliament. State premiers issued a joint statement calling for unity amid speculation about replacing Merz as party
chairman and possibly as chancellor, and Merz cancelled next week's UN trip. Eurointelligence expects him to remain in position for now,
since removing him as head of government would require a constructive vote of no confidence with an absolute majority for an alternative
candidate.

Russian hybrid threats and rearmament: President Macron called party leaders and presidential candidates to discuss Russian hybrid
threats and the Middle East energy shock, while Polish PM Tusk suggested Russia is intensifying attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure this
autumn and winter and could use drones and missiles against NATO supply lines to test whether the alliance would invoke Article 5.
Bernstein doubted Europe can reach its 3.5% of GDP defence target by 2035, with a base case of 2.6% this year and 3.2% in 2035;
Germany is the main market at a 20% share, and equipment is seen rising to 38% of budgets by 2035, an addressable market growing at
a ~12% CAGR to 2030.

Synopsis

Asian equities advanced on Friday. South Korea and Taiwan outperformed on a memory and semiconductor rally, Greater China was
higher with strength in the tech-focused STAR 50 and ChiNext indices as well as the Hang Seng Tech, and Japan's Nikkei gained as its
tech majors posted sharp gains. Southeast Asia was mostly lower and India traded higher. The AI trade regained its footing with another
memory rally, as Samsung Electronics (005930-KR) and SK Hynix (000660-KR) posted weekly gains, and risk sentiment was aided by a
pullback in yields and stabilisation in oil. Dollar-yen moved sharply higher following the BOJ hike and mixed signals from Governor Ueda's
press briefing, both offshore and onshore yuan broke above the 6.70 handle to four-year highs, crude fell for a third day, gold and silver
gained, and Bitcoin rose past $77,900. Reuters sources said CXMT (688825-CN) is preparing to enter the flash memory chip market to
move beyond its core DRAM business. BYD (1211-HK) issued a recall of more than 183K cars in China over brake-pedal safety risks, and
its CEO may join President Xi's delegation to Washington. South Korean President Lee said Seoul will not send troops that would involve
the country in a foreign conflict, amid pressure from President Trump.
Macro

BOJ hikes to 1.25%, yen swings: The BOJ raised its policy rate by 25 bp to 1.25%, the highest since 1995, as widely expected, in a 7-2
vote with reflationists Asada and Sato dissenting on the grounds that questionable growth momentum did not warrant a move. The state-
ment cited inflation effects from oil and the Middle East, AI demand and a weak yen, reaffirmed the risk of underlying inflation overshooting
2%, judged financial conditions still accommodative given low real rates, and kept guidance signalling further hikes. Governor Ueda said
the policy phase has shifted from guiding inflation up to the 2% target to stabilisation against upside risks, and would not rule anything
out when asked about back-to-back or 50 bp hikes. The yen nonetheless weakened after the decision, as confirmation of the dissents
left an impression the tone was less hawkish than hoped; markets price roughly quarterly hikes to a terminal rate of 1.75% by March
2027, and the dissents were also read as reflecting PM Takaichi's opposition to tightening. Nikkei then reported that the BOJ conducted
a rate check, a move often preceding official intervention, sparking a yen rally. Japan spent a record ¥15.4T on intervention in the month
through 26-Aug, and Treasury Secretary Bessent earlier this week defended US yen intervention, noting a stronger yen means Japan does
not need to sell US assets.

Japan CPI benign ahead of the decision: Nationwide core CPI rose 1.7% y/y in August, just below consensus and July's 1.8%, slowing for
the first time in four months as renewed energy subsidies increased the net drag to 0.62 ppt from 0.22. Inflation excluding fresh food and
energy was steady at 1.9% against 2.0% consensus. Rice prices posted the biggest sequential drop since September 1994 on reports
that a bumper new harvest caused wholesale prices to tumble, and a slower increase in mobile phone fees also weighed. Policy implica-
tions were limited, with BOJ-adjusted core inflation excluding institutional factors at least 2% since October 2024.

RBA's Bullock warns upside inflation risks are materialising: In opening remarks before a House committee, Governor Bullock said upside
inflation risks appear to be materialising amid global cost pressures from the Middle East conflict, the AI boom and extreme weather, with
the sharp rise in oil adding directly to inflation and many firms passing on higher costs. She said the board will debate whether preceding
rate hikes are sufficient to return inflation to target in a reasonable time. Markets are pricing a ~90% chance of a September hike following
her remarks, and some economists see a further hike in November.

Xi heads to Washington: President Xi will visit the White House next week, his first trip to the US since 2023, with bilateral talks on
Thursday 24-Sep followed by a state dinner to which Trump has invited AI figures including OpenAI's Sam Altman and Nvidia's Jensen
Huang. Treasury Secretary Bessent and He Lifeng meet this weekend to lay the groundwork. AI is expected to be a major topic given
Beijing's criticism of recent calls for pacing model development, and trade will be central, with broad expectations that the meeting will
extend the US-China truce that expires in November. The White House is expected to delay any new tariff announcements over alleged
overcapacity until after the summit, and traders are watching for clues on AI competition and the yuan.

A quarter of listed Chinese companies post H1 losses: Wind data cited by Nikkei showed a quarter of China-listed companies logged net
losses in H1, even as aggregate net profit of ~5,400 non-financial mainland-listed companies rose 22% to CNY1.9T ($283B), the first
double-digit growth in five years. The CXMT (688825-CN) parent's CNY77.6B profit, against a year-ago net loss of CNY2.3B, accounted
for around a quarter of the improvement, and Cambricon Technologies more than doubled its profits, while property, automakers and
food companies lagged. Net losses were reported by 1,411 firms, the highest count on record for the period at 26% of the total, with China
Vanke (2202-HK) posting the largest loss of CNY14.9B and 60 of 104 property names in the red.
