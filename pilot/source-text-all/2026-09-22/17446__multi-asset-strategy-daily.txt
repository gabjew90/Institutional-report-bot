Open Mic Week Meets Geopolitics. The focus now shifts away from data and back towards policymakers, with 
Fed speakers finally emerging from blackout into a market that is pricing roughly 33bp through year-end. The more 
immediate challenge remains energy. While crude is softer overnight following reports of constructive US-China 
discussions ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting and a quieter Asian session, the broader Middle East backdrop 
remains tricky. Houthis continue to expand operations against Saudi-backed forces, attacks on Riyadh reinforce 
the regional escalation risk, and both Washington and Tehran continue to signal little willingness to compromise 
on key conditions for ending the conflict. Meanwhile, tanker shortages and elevated shipping costs through Hormuz 
suggest that, even if crude stabilises, energy-related inflation pressures may persist for longer than policymakers 
would like. Macro-wise, besides Fed commentary, we will be attentive to PMIs and UST supply. All eyes 
remain focused on that 5% level in 10Y UST yields. So far, it has held, but we suspect that, until oil materially 
turns lower or activity data softens meaningfully, rallies are likely to remain tactical rather than structural. FX-wise, 
the DXY rebounded from Friday's lows and is trading around 100.35. For now, elevated energy prices and a still-
hawkish Fed continue to provide support. 
France Becomes the Story Again. The market has spent much of the summer focusing on energy, but France 
has now become a story in its own. The move through 100bp in OAT-Bund spreads last Friday suggests that 
investors are increasingly treating France as a fiscal and political problem rather than a temporary volatility event. 
Valuation is becoming attractive, but attractive valuations are rarely enough when investors cannot identify a 
catalyst to stop the widening. The cherry on top was the downgrade from AA- to A+ from Scope. This week's 
focus shifts to two political developments+ ECB. First, whether RN continues to harden its stance ahead of 
budget negotiations. Second, whether Germany's regional election fallout develops into a broader challenge to 
Merz's authority. Our base case remains that Merz survives, but the leadership meetings this week will be closely 
watched by markets looking for signs of further political instability at the core of Europe. From an ECB perspective, 
policymakers are becoming more open to further tightening should energy prices remain elevated, but we still think 
current terminal-rate pricing looks too high relative to the medium-term growth outlook although the hawkish 
momentum is hard to fade. The October ECB meeting is priced around 55-45% probability in favour of a hike and 
we wonder whether this week’s PMIs will continue to follow the Q2/summer rebound narrative that is allowing 
terminal rates to keep rising. EUR/USD weakened modestly overnight and is trading around 1.1475.  
Higher for Longer, But How Much Higher? Our question for GBP rates is whether markets are getting ahead of 
themselves. SONIA repricing has been aggressive, front-end yields have adjusted sharply (100bp priced until 
Jul27) and the curve continues to flatten following the BOE's QT announcement. Like elsewhere, the debate is 
increasingly shifting from whether additional hikes are possible to whether the market can realistically sustain 
current terminal-rate expectations into 2027. This week is relatively light on data, leaving PMIs and BOE 
speakers as the main focus. We suspect officials will continue emphasising inflation risks, but the bigger 
challenge may be convincing markets that a prolonged period of highly restrictive policy can coexist with below-
trend growth (we don’t think so). GBP/USD is trading slightly off recent highs around 1.3377.  
Risk-on start in Asian equities, with tech stocks, slightly lower energy prices and positive comments from 
Bessent around the US-China talks driving the move. It was an otherwise quiet session, with Japanese 
markets closed. USD/JPY remained around 157 after the rate check conducted last Friday.
