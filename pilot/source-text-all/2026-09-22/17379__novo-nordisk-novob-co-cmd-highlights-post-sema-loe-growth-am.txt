At its CMD (21st Sept), the key message that Novo was trying to 
convey to investors was that by 2035, after the key patent expiration 
for semaglutide, the company will be larger than it expects to be in 
2026, driven by at least 5 new multi-blockbuster launches, and it will 
fight to make the semaglutide LoE curve as shallow as it can. For the 
pipeline contribution, in 2035, we include risk-adjusted sales of 
DKK150bn (across CagriSema, cagrilintide, zenagamtide, 
efruxifermin, coramitug, denecimig [mim8] and etavopivat), but do 
not yet carry any sales for other assets such as UBT251, ASCL5i, oral 
amylin 355 or zaltenibart, which have either recently moved into 
Phase 2 or Novo highlighted could be accelerated in terms of 
development timelines. Regarding the semaglutide erosion curve, 
manufacturing and COGS remain a key lever for Novo, particularly 
given the experience in some markets already facing generics where 
volumes have increased significantly given the lower price point. 
Novo’s strategy here is to defend its volume leadership position, 
while trying to switch the franchise over to the oral version in order 
to further enhance the supply/ manufacturing moat. However, while 
some new informative data was shown at the event (REDEFINE 9 and 
REIMAGINE 5), we think it is unlikely the market will significantly 
change its view around the commercial prospects for Novo’s 
pipeline following the CMD, and further analogue data is required 
from broader semaglutide LoE countries for the market to start to 
think the erosion will be shallower. Furthermore, many investors

Key takeaways from the CMD 
 
 
BD likely focused on bolt-ons: Novo intends to pursue BD, with the focus primarily 
n
on bolt-on transactions. Novo looks to focus on the core company’s core areas 
(diabetes, obesity) as well as the highlighted adjacent areas (blood & endocrine, liver, 
and cardiovascular) while also exploring consumer Rx opportunities (although it 
seems this could largely be driven by the pipeline, with some opportunistic BD). Key 
criteria for BD/M&A are: 1) is there a strategic rationale aligned to Novo’s strategy/ 
outlook?, 2) is there sound scientific rationale (new MoA, novel targets supported by 
the science)?, and 3) is the deal financially attractive? In terms of the commercial 
stage of acquisition targets, management said in the Q&A session that they would 
not rule out acquiring assets already on the market, but the focus is on bolt-on 
acquisitions.  
Semaglutide LoE trajectory: Novo noted that many markets in IO are currently 
n
experiencing generic competition and in a number of these markets, as the price 
decreases, this drives a significant volume unlock. Therefore, Novo intends to 
compete for semaglutide volumes across markets, both before and after 
semaglutide’s LoE. In addition, as noted previously, patents on the oral formulation 
extend to the mid-2030s and Novo noted that it is continuously improving the SNAC 
oral formulation technology, which could further extend patent protection. 
Therefore, switching to the Wegovy pill as well as new formulations (Wegovy pill 
utilises 3rd generation SNAC vs. 9th generation that is in the clinic), could help to 
protect new formulations of the Wegovy pill. 
Commentary on oral launch for US and ex-US - continued leadership with new 
n
SNAC tech: Regarding the Wegovy pill in the US, management highlighted that 
prescriptions have now reached 7mn with c.90% through the self-pay channel, and 
c.30% of Wegovy pill TRx are for the 9mg and 25mg dose. Overall, the Wegovy 
franchise now has c.55% share of NBRx in the US anti-obesity medication market 
(per Novo data), and a c.80% share of the oral anti-obesity medication market. Novo 
highlighted that the profile of Wegovy pill users in the US is 70% female, average age 
of 50 years old, and an average BMI of 35. However, Novo highlighted that it still sees 
growth potential for the Wegovy franchise, with <10% of obese patients in the US 
currently being treated with anti-obesity medication. Novo continues to focus on 
driving Wegovy pill uptake and expansion through all 3 channels: retail, tele-health 
(with management noting potential for signing a 10th telehealth partner in the US 
imminently) and Novocare (Novocare 2.0 to offer an improved and lower cost 
version of the end-to-end experience currently offered to Wegovy patients). 
Furthermore, in terms of retaining its leadership in the oral AOM market in the long 
term, Novo intends to further innovate the SNAC portion of the Wegovy pill design, 
with potential focus on ultra-stable peptides and intestinal delivery to develop the 
product further. In terms of the launch of the Wegovy pill in International markets, 
the UK launch was highlighted as a key success, although the market benefitted from 
well-developed telehealth infrastructure. Novo highlighted that despite the launch 
of Foundayo in the UK in 7-8 weeks after Wegovy pill launched, Wegovy pill TRx 
growth was higher in Weeks 7-10 vs Weeks 1-7. In terms of evaluating subsequent

launches, management plans to have launched Wegovy HD in >30 markets by y/e 
2026, and Ozempic 2.0mg in >20 markets within the same time frame. 
US pricing/environment: Novo expects to see pricing step down for semaglutide 
n
post the LoE, but management said they also see room for innovative pricing across 
the portfolio due to market segmentation and different population sizes, while 
noting that pricing in the broader market continues to come down. For Ozempic, 
management said they expect 10-15% price degradation from 2Q26, with the YTD 
price decrease so far closer to 10% than 15%. Looking to 2027, management thinks 
a similar price decrease makes sense given the IRA impact and MFN pricing for 2027. 
Novo intends to double down on oral leadership while extending oral peptide 
n
technology beyond obesity/ diabetes: Novo highlighted several assets within the 
pipeline which could be part of the targeted >10 product launches by 2030. In terms 
of the timing and outlook for the pipeline overall, management highlighted that 
while several of their pipeline assets are in early clinical trial stages, there is scope for 
timelines to be accelerated.

Valuation and risks 
 
 
We are Neutral rated on Novo Nordisk. We derive our price target from a 50:50 blend 
of DCF and EV/EBITDA approaches. Our bottom-up DCF analysis suggests a valuation of 
DKK 278 per share. We use a WACC of 7% and a TGR of -2%. On a multiples basis, we 
believe Novo Nordisk should trade on 13x P/E on our 2027 EPS estimate, implying DKK 
289 per share. As a result, our 12-month price target is DKK285 per share. Our ADR PT is 
set with reference to the Danish line, translated at the current FX rate, leading to an ADR 
12-month PT of $44. 
Key risks to our view and price target include: Upside risks - (1) faster-than-expected 
Wegovy pill sales ramp post launch, and (2) higher-than-expected peak CagriSema 
sales. Downside risks - (1) clinical risks if CagriSema and/or amycretin development were 
to be unsuccessful; (2) slower-than-expected scale-up in manufacturing for 
Wegovy/Ozempic; (3) stronger-than-anticipated competitor obesity data, particularly 
oral small molecule GLP-1 based assets; and (4) deeper and sustained price pressure.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
