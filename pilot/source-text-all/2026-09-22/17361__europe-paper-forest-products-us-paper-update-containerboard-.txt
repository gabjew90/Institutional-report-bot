Europe Paper & Forest Products: US Paper Update: Containerboard Up 
$70/t; Boxboard Up $60/t in September
21 September 2026 | 12:50PM BST
As per RISI’s recent release on Friday (September 18), US containerboard and 
boxboard prices increased m/m in September. This is particularly relevant for the US 
Containerboard producers, as there have been investor concerns in recent weeks 
that the announced price increases could fail to come through. We will continue to 
track the demand fundamentals for this market, but if demand remains strong, we 
wouldn’t rule out that an additional portion of the announced hikes is implemented 
in the coming months. 
Containerboard: Price hikes partly implemented. US containerboard prices rose in 
September as mills implemented $70/st increases for Kraftliner and Testliner, 
marking the third hike of 2026, culminating in an increase of $170/st YTD. Investors 
we recently spoke with had been expecting a $40-50/st increase, while concerns 
over whether any price increase would be implemented had been growing following 
articles suggesting some resistance from independent box makers to the recent hikes 
announced (here). We have been more constructive than investors (here), given the 
still strong comments on demand and tight supply. We believe the current price 
increase should be well-received by investors and should reassure on the strength of 
the US Containerboard market (especially given the weak performance of the US 
packaging stocks in the past weeks). 
Boxboard pricing increased. URB was the only grade to achieve a price increase, 
rising $60/st m/m as mills remained fully booked through October, limiting 
availability for new orders, with tight supply and reports of higher transaction prices 
from both buyers and sellers supporting the increase. In contrast, announced price 
increases for CRB ($50/st) and CUK ($60/st) were not implemented - but we believe 
this could be a timing issue with the data collection (as happened earlier this year). 
The data for October should provide us with a clearer picture on the price increases. 
We highlight SW as the name under our coverage with the highest exposure to 
North America (c.60% of EBITDA), and we estimate containerboard/box sales 
represent c.70% of the company’s earnings in the region. We maintain our Buy rating 
on SW and our relative preference for downstream names under our coverage, which 
offer a compelling bottom-up story vs. the upstream names that are more exposed 
to pulp prices. We expect a positive market reaction to SW shares on the back of the 
price increase, especially considering the stock’s recent underperformance.

North America Paper Update 
 
 
US Containerboard 
Containerboard prices increased m/m in September. As expected, price increases 
announced by most of the main mills were implemented, with Kraftliner and Testliner 
prices increasing by $70/st, marking the third rise of the year, $170 higher than the price 
at the start of 2026. Mill backlogs remained unchanged at 5-6 weeks in September - 
implying the market remains strong.  
RISI reports that while containerboard supply was described as tight by buyers and 
sellers, there was some resistance to the overall price increase this month in the US. 
Some buyers reported either delaying or holding off on paying or charging the increase 
this month.  
 
US Boxboard  
Higher prices implemented in URB. URB was the only grade to see price increases this 
month, with prices rising $60/st m/m. According to URB producers, mills remained fully 
booked, with several booked through October, limiting availability for new orders. Tight 
supply conditions, together with reports of higher transaction prices from both buyers 
and sellers, supported the increase. 
Flat SBS, CRB and CUK prices. A $50/st price increase announced for Coated Recycled 
Boxboard (CRB) for September purchases was not implemented as buyers gave mixed 
reports on the increase announced by producers. RISI reports that CRB is still 
oversupplied which has made it difficult for the announced price increase to go through. 
Coated Unbleached Kraft (CUK) producers had also announced a $60/st increase for 
early September, which was not implemented as buyers welcomed the possibility of a 
third domestic supplier in a market now led by two large producers. Solid Bleached 
Sulfate (SBS) prices remained flat with some buyers seeking another $60/st price 
increase, which, if implemented, will show up in October.

Key risks 
The key downside risks to our thesis and price target include: 
US tariffs not leading to the higher margins we anticipate. Although margins 
n
expanded in the steel sector after tariffs were implemented, if tariffs don’t lead to 
higher profitability than what was seen for the pulp & paper industry in the past, this 
could pose a risk to our estimates, as we forecast higher paper prices in the US in the 
coming years. 
Worse-than-anticipated packaging demand. After a year of global uncertainty in 
n
2025, we assume a gradual normalization in consumer confidence with more clarity 
on tariff implementation expected through the next year or so. That said, in the 
event of lower consumer confidence owing to the ongoing conflict in the Middle 
East, it could translate into lower packaging demand and pose downside risk to our 
estimates. 
Further significant volume adjustments in North America. After the 
n
implementation of Smurfit Kappa’s approach to Westrock’s legacy business in North 
America, SW identified several loss-making facilities and box contracts. We note that 
it already exited several of these contracts in 3Q25 and 4Q25, but in the event that 
there is higher-than-expected volume correction, this would lead to downside risk to 
our estimates. 
Higher-than-expected capex to achieve desired profitability. One of the main 
n
investor concerns going into the merger was that the capex required to revamp 
Westrock’s US assets would be quite meaningful. Based on SW’s current capex 
guidance for 2026 and the company’s medium-term plan, we do not see the need 
for higher levels ahead. That said, in case the company is not able to extract the 
value it expects from the assets, there could be an increase in capex beyond our 
expectations. 
Value-dilutive M&A. We expect SW’s focus to be on the integration of the Smurfit 
n
and Westrock assets in the near term, as well as capacity rationalization, with 
potential for small bolt-on acquisitions in Latin America where the company sees the 
most room for growth. Any value-dilutive M&A would be a risk to our view.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
