IAEA Raises Nuclear Outlook For Sixth 
Consecutive Year – The Weekly Blueprint 
  
The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) released its annual nuclear 
power outlook, once again raising its expectations for global nuclear 
capacity. This marks the sixth consecutive year the IAEA has revised its 
projections higher, reflecting growing electricity demand, energy security 
concerns and renewed government support for nuclear generation. Under 
the IAEA’s high case, global nuclear capacity is expected to reach 1,045 GW 
by 2050, up from 992 GW projected last year, before reaching 1,284 GW by 
2060, or roughly 3.4x the 377 GW operating at the end of 2025. Even under 
the low case, capacity is expected to nearly double by 2060. The continued 
upward revisions reinforce our view that the current nuclear investment cycle 
is increasingly structural rather than episodic. 
One of the more notable changes in this year’s outlook is the growing role 
expected for SMRs. Under the high-growth case, the IAEA estimates SMRs 
could represent ~28% of all new nuclear capacity added through 2060, up 
from 24% in last year’s forecast. The outlook is particularly notable in North 
America, where ~60% of new nuclear capacity could come from SMRs, 
considerably higher than the expected global mix. This suggests SMRs are 
increasingly moving from a niche component of the longer-term nuclear 
outlook toward a meaningful part of expected capacity additions, particularly 
in markets looking to add incremental baseload generation while potentially 
reducing the upfront capital and execution challenges associated with 
traditional large-scale reactors.  
Achieving the IAEA’s outlook will require much more than new construction. 
The existing global reactor fleet is aging, with two-thirds of operating reactors 
now more than 30 years old and 46% more than 40 years old. Even in the 
high-growth scenario, approximately one-third of existing nuclear capacity is 
expected to retire by 2060, increasing the amount of new capacity required 
simply to offset retirements. Lifetime extensions and refurbishment programs 
therefore remain an important component of the nuclear investment cycle 
alongside new large-scale reactors and SMRs. 
The scale of the buildout remains substantial relative to current activity. In 
2025, seven reactors totaling 2.8 GW were retired, while three new reactors 
totaling 3.0 GW were connected to the grid. Construction began on another 
12 reactors representing 14.3 GW of capacity. At the same time, nuclear 
generation increased 1.1% during the year but still failed to keep pace with 
2.7% growth in overall global electricity generation, resulting in nuclear’s 
share of generation declining to 8.4% from 8.7%. 
Overall, we see the latest IAEA outlook as another supportive data point for 
the nuclear investment cycle. The opportunity is also becoming broader: 
continued investment will be required across new builds, SMRs, 
refurbishment and life extensions to meet growing electricity demand while 
replacing an aging installed base.

BYD Announces NCIB 
BYD announced that the Toronto Stock Exchange has approved its Notice of Intention to 
carry out an NCIB. The company noted that the NCIB provides BYD with another capital 
allocation option to support shareholder value while continuing to pursue its core corporate 
pipeline of disciplined strategic acquisitions and industry consolidation.  
As of September 2, 2026, BYD had 27,836,435 shares issued and outstanding. Of this 
amount, 27,793,526 shares constitute the public float calculated in accordance with the rules 
of the TSX.  BYD may purchase for cancellation up to a maximum of 2,779,352 shares, or 
~10.0% of BYD's public float. Purchases will be made on the open market through the TSX,

Construction Commentary 
Microsoft To Triple Data Center Capacity By 2032 
As reported by Bloomberg, Microsoft intends to more than triple its global data center 
capacity, aiming for over 38GW by 2032 compared to ~12GW currently. This expansion 
excludes rented capacity from neocloud providers and is expected to require substantial 
capital investment, with the company’s most recent fiscal year capital expenditure reaching 
US$145B. Microsoft is accelerating construction, with new facilities like the East US 3 cluster 
in Atlanta, to ease bottlenecks in key hubs such as Virginia. We believe this signals sustained 
momentum in data center construction over the near to medium term, which will continue to 
drive demand for power and grid-related infrastructure. Recall, in February 2025, WSP and 
Microsoft announced a seven-year partnership whereby WSP designated Microsoft its 
preferred partner for digital and AI transformation services, and Microsoft will look to WSP as 
a preferred partner for engineering and science consultancy. We view this as a positive 
development for WSP. 
Government Of Canada Streamlines Project Assessments 
To streamline the review process for major projects, including pipelines, new international 
power lines, designated interprovincial power lines, and certain offshore renewable energy 
projects, the federal government has announced amendments to the Physical Activities 
Regulations under the Impact Assessment Act (IAA). With these changes, interprovincial and 
international pipelines, international and designated interprovincial power lines, and offshore 
renewable energy projects not regulated by offshore energy regulators will now be assessed 
by the Canada Energy Regulator (CER) instead of an integrated review panel. In-situ oil 
sands extraction facilities and fossil fuel-fired power generating facilities are being removed 
from the Physical Activities Regulations to ensure federal impact assessments remain 
focused on major projects, while these facilities will still undergo provincial and federal 
assessment processes. 
According to the government, these amendments will establish a more efficient, predictable,  
and transparent regulatory framework for major natural resource and infrastructure 
developments, helping to attract investment and accelerate nation-building projects. 
DMI Essentially Flat On Weaker Data Center Activity  
The Dodge Momentum Index (DMI), published by Dodge Construction Network (Dodge), 
declined 0.4% in August to 282.0, from the downwardly revised July reading of 283.0, with 
commercial planning down 3.0%, while institutional was up 4.9%. Within commercial, data 
centers, retail stores and warehouses saw a slowdown in activity, while office buildings, 
hotels, and parking garages picked up. For institutional, growth was seen in education, 
recreational, and public buildings, while healthcare slightly pulled back after consecutive 
growth in the last four quarters. According to Dodge’s Director of Economic Research, Sarah

U.S. Construction Material Prices Up M/M In August 
According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Producer Price Index data, construction 
input prices increased 1.2% M/M in August, with non-residential input prices also up 1.2%. 
On a Y/Y basis, construction input costs increased 8.9%, with non-residential prices up 8.8%. 
Two out of three energy subcategories saw price increases: crude petroleum increased 5.2% 
and unprocessed energy materials increased 1.5%, while natural gas declined 11.6%. ABC 
Chief Economist Anirban Basu noted that August’s material price increase was broad-based, 
with iron and steel, softwood lumber, switchgear, copper wire and cable, and several metal 
derivative products now up more than 10% Y/Y. Although contractors remain optimistic about 
margins, persistent increases in input costs – particularly given recent trade tensions with 
Canada and oil prices above US$100 per barrel – are expected to put pressure on 
profitability in the coming months. 
Nuclear Commentary 
CSMC Unveils Three New Nuclear Reactor Platforms 
The Canadian Strategic Missions Corporation (CSMC) unveiled three advanced nuclear 
reactor platforms designed to address Canada's energy challenges: Arctic sovereignty, 
defence readiness, scientific research, and future lunar exploration. The launch of POLAR-1, 
JAY-1, and ORCA-1 is expected to establish distinct development pathways for applications 
ranging from remote communities and military operations to space infrastructure, research, 
testing, and workforce development. The announcement marks the next phase of CSMC's 
reactor development program as its technologies mature toward deployment in increasingly 
specialized operating environments. While each platform serves a unique mission, all three 
leverage common capabilities in reactor design, systems integration, advanced 
manufacturing, testing, and deployment planning. 
• 
POLAR-1 is a transportable megawatt-scale microreactor being developed to provide 
reliable power and heat for Arctic communities, defence installations, remote industrial 
projects, and critical infrastructure. CSMC is targeting first criticality of POLAR-1 by 2028, 
with fleet-scale deployment envisioned from 2030 onward. 
• 
JAY-1 is a compact space nuclear reactor designed to provide long-duration power for 
lunar bases, scientific payloads, communications infrastructure, resource development 
activities, and future space industry. 
• 
ORCA-1 is a next-generation research reactor platform designed to support nuclear 
research, technology validation, operator training, and workforce development.  
Heavy Equipment Commentary 
Manheim Index Up In August Y/Y 
The Manheim Used Vehicle Index was down in August from July to 208.2, indicating lower 
wholesale used vehicle prices. The index is down 0.9% M/M but up 0.4% Y/Y. While the 
index has fallen out of the ~210-215 range seen thus far in 2026, it continues to show growth 
on a Y/Y basis, something it has done every month this year so far.

Motor Vehicle Insurance CPI Y/Y Growth Remains Negative 
The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' motor vehicle insurance CPI for the U.S. city average 
came in at 848.231 in August, down from 856.080 last month and 894.075 a year ago. The 
index declined 5.1% Y/Y, compared with a 4.5% Y/Y decline last month and a 4.7% Y/Y 
increase a year ago. The 20-year average Y/Y increase is 5.2%. Given the pressure elevated 
insurance premiums have had on claims volumes, we view the continued normalization in 
insurance CPI as a tailwind for claims volumes and, in turn, BYD's SSSG. The last reading 
below August's 848.231 was recorded in June 2024 at 843.579, highlighting the extent of the 
recent moderation in insurance costs.
