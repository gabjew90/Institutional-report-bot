Higher rates mostly not an issue for IPPs
Sentiment meter. We hold our sentiment meter flat at a "2" after a quiet-ish end to the
summer in IPP-land. Fall should be busy. FERC is set to rule on RBP by 9/29 and IRAS
by 10/12. Our sense is FERC will largely approve the RBP with minimal modifications
given the alignment with the White House/PJM Governors Statement of Principles.
We'll be watching the order to see if FERC better clarifies whether "protecting
existing deals" includes the IPP's hyperscaler offtake agreements (TLN/AMZN and
VST/META). It's then a tight turnaround to PJM kicking off the central procurement
on 9/30. It's possible the start of the RBP catalyzes bilateral agreements. On IRAS,
stakeholder comments were largely oppositional – IPPs, hyperscalers, and even some
T&D utilities pushed back on the legality and jurisdictional issues. Hard to know where
FERC will land here, but we wouldn't be surprised if there are significant modifications
or even potentially outright rejection. Over in Texas, ERCOT informed loads of their
initial load classification on 9/3. NRG disclosed its initial 1.2 GW data center deal
was included as studied load – not surprising given the 2029 COD. VST's Comanche/
AMZN appears to be base load. We got updates from Friday's PUCT meeting (link)
and expect more at tomorrow's ERCOT meeting (link). For now, conditional Batch
Zero load is split 66 GW base and 125 GW studied, ramping through 2032.
10-year treasuries are approaching 5%, how do IPPs trade in rising rates?
Fundamentally, we see the group as well-positioned. These companies are all
generating strong FCF. Both CEG and VST are investment grade and taking on very
little net new debt, while employing buybacks. TLN is also de-levering and has zero
growth capex right now. NRG is a bit of an exception as their leverage is currently
elevated (~4x) and they are planning for growth capex that requires incremental
debt. SEI is also currently carrying elevated leverage (~5x), but their growth plan is
fully-funded and they quickly de-lever with rapid EBITDA growth over the next ~2
years. EROC is a recent IPO that has no debt and $0.6B in net cash – rates aren't an
issue. The last time the 10-year approached 5% was this time 3 years ago. The IPPs
outperformed both into and out of the early-October peak in rates, as the AI trade
started taking hold. Before that, you have to look pre-GFC for 5% on the 10-year.
None of the IPPs, except NRG, existed then, so there isn't much history to work with.
Drilling down into debt exposures. We looked at this last week for regulated utilities
(note) and now take a look for IPPs. We break out the total rate-exposed debt –
variable rate debt (less any interest rate swaps and excluding undrawn revolvers) plus
upcoming maturities. We then show the EPS/FCF impact of a 1% move in rates. NRG
and TLN have the highest percentage of variable rate debt. TLN has zero maturities
this decade though. VST has very little variable debt, but a few near-term maturities.
CEG has a lot of interest rate swap protection and no near-term maturities. SEI would
need to see rates move much higher for their modest floating rate debt exposure to
move. Overall, NRG is likely most sensitive to higher interest rates, but it is still very
manageable overall.

Interest Rate Sensitivities
As the 10-year Treasury yield nears 5%, we have gotten more inbounds on the utilities/IPPs exposures to rates.
High-level, we do not see significant interest rate risk for the IPPs. They have fairly limited growth capex that would
require chunky debt issuance (ex-NRG with their 5.4 GW new build plan) and we see limited refi/maturity risk on their
outstanding debt.
In the tables below we breakout – fixed rate debt, variable rate debt, interest rate swaps/hedges (that reduce exposures),
and upcoming maturities. We then look at what a 1% move in rates on exposed debt would do to financial outlooks. We
use EPS for CEG and NRG, who guide to the metric. We use FCF for SEI, TLN, and VST. NRG and TLN have the highest
portion of variable rate debt, but TLN has zero maturities this decade. VST has the most near-term maturities. NRG and
TLN likely de-lever nearer-term. Longer-term, NRG has the most growth capex and TLN has zero growth capex.
SEI has higher current leverage and is investing to grow, but only the $500M fixed-to-floating loan in their 50/50
Stateline JV with xAI is exposed to rate shifts. Further, this debt is already at a ~10% rate that would only move higher if
SOFR crossed above 4.31%, when it currently sits at ~3.6%. NRG's earnings are the most sensitive to moves in rates on
their outstanding debt. Unsurprisingly, CEG and VST (the two IG-rated IPPs) both have the least variable debt and are
the least sensitive to moves in rates. CEG has no near-term maturities and interest rate swaps/hedges offsetting almost
all of their exposure. Just last week, VST issued $1.5B of junior subs at 7.0-7.25% to replace $2.0B in preferreds that
were about to step up from 7-8% to ~11-12% rates. Otherwise, VST has modest near-term maturities.

Events Calendar / Regulatory Watch 
9/29, 10/12 – FERC order on PJM’s RBP and IRAS/ C&M filings (ER26-3380, ER26-3515). This originated 
with a PJM Board Chair letter (link) in mid-August that implemented a critical issue fast-path process for large 
load additions (link). The goal was to address the development of reliability-focused solutions to ensure 
resource adequacy while connecting large loads. The original proposal looked at adjustments to the load 
utilized in capacity auctions, if such load is not capacity backed. We covered our initial thoughts in the 
Fleishman Daily on 8/12/25 and then further in our PJM/CEG visit takeaways note (link). PJM’s non-capacity 
backed load proposal (NCBL) was subsequently revised (slides) to be voluntary and similar to demand 
response, rather than mandatory. PJM is also focused on improving the accuracy of load forecasting and 
setting up an expedited interconnection process for sponsored generation. Stakeholder feedback was largely 
oppositional, and alternative stakeholder proposals were filed in October, with additional meetings held in 
early November. PJM then hosted a vote on 11/19, with all proposals failing. PJM was planning to review 
stakeholder proposals, comments, and the vote results to develop their own proposal, originally planned by 
YE25. PJM pushed this to January to be after the 2027/2028 auction results on 12/17. As part of their co-
location order on 12/18, however, FERC required PJM to file within 30-days (by 1/19) with a summary of 
proposals made in the CIFP process.  
On 1/15/26, Bloomberg reported that the White House and PJM state governors were going to urge PJM to 
run an emergency procurement auction, with the hyperscalers bidding on 15-year contracts for new 
generation (see our take – report link). We got more details the next day, with a Fact Sheet and a Statement of 
Principles from the White House. The White House / Governors’ proposal is one-time reliability backstop 
auction (RBA) for new build and includes an extension of the collar mechanism at current prices for two more 
auctions. Later in the day, PJM submitted their own proposal (letter), which appears very similar to what 
seemed to be the most agreed upon items: 1) improving load forecasting; 2) voluntary bring your own 
generation (BYOG); 3) curtailment if not BYOG; 4) holistic PJM market review; 5) consideration of the collar 
extension; 6) immediate initiation of an RBA. PJM began the discussion on the proposal for a RBA at their 
Members Committee meeting on 1/22, then held a formal stakeholder process on the implementation and 
submitted a supplemental filing on 2/23 – we discussed our in the Fleishman Daily on 2/24.  
PJM took a 5-week pause from the stakeholder process to develop their own proposal, which they shared on 
4/10 (see our takes –  report). The initial proposal was for a 6-month period of bilateral contracting, followed 
by a central procurement targeting a total 15 GW of new capacity (though highly subject to changes by the 
EDCs). PJM then ran a brief stakeholder process in May featuring initial presentations from 17 stakeholders 
ahead of a planned MC vote on 5/27. On 5/19, however, PJM issued a letter that pushed out their timeline of 
proposal, officially combining the reliability backstop procurement (RBP) critical issue fast path with the 
pending Connect and Manage (C&M) process and moving up the central procurement to September 2026. 
Rather than the previously scheduled MC vote on 5/27, PJM held a stakeholder meeting, presenting their 
updated proposal (see our takes in the May 25 Power Trip – link). Ahead of their Members Committee vote on 
6/30, PJM issued a new proposal that effectively backed off C&M (see our takes in the Fleishman Daily on 
6/22). As expected, though, all C&M proposals subsequently failed to pass the Members Committee vote on 
6/30. For the RBP, only the Data Center Coalition/Joint EDC proposal passed (PJM’s own proposal failed),

12/10 – ERCOT to file Batch Zero eligibility report. ERCOT kicked off a stakeholder engagement process on 
reviewing large loads in batches back in early-2026. They presented their draft framework back on 2/3 (link), 
proposing that large loads be reviewed in batches every six months, rather than the current process of 
individualized review by transmission service providers. We discussed some of our initial views in the 
Fleishman Daily on 2/4. ERCOT presented an update to the PUCT at their 2/20 Open meeting, followed by a 
series of workshops finalizing their proposal. ERCOT Board unanimously approved the proposal for Batch 
Zero on 6/2. The PUCT approved ERCOT’s Batch Zero proposal at their 6/18 Open Meeting. ERCOT was 
planning to notify large loads of their classification (“base load” or “studied load”) by 8/7, but this timeline 
shifted with TX Governor Abbott’s letter on 8/4 calling for a data center verification process. ERCOT 
subsequently paused the Batch Zero process in response. We got a firmer timeline from ERCOT at PUCT’s 
meeting on 8/20. ERCOT communicated conditional load classifications (subcategories within base and 
studied load in Batch Zero) on 9/3, now seeing 66 GW of base load. We got more details on the breakdown of 
Batch Zero load in ERCOT’s Board meeting on 9/15 (link). ERCOT will now begin to send out Request for 
Informations to large loads for the data center verification. We should get the final Batch Zero eligibility 
verification report from ERCOT by December 10 ahead of the PUCT meeting on December 17. The final 
timeline to conclude the Batch Zero transmission studies remain to be seen (expect more information in ~a 
month). 
Recall that ERCOT had presented 205 GW total eligible for inclusion in Batch Zero (65 GW base, 25 as either 
base/allocated (TBD) and 114 GW as allocated/studied load). Under the original timeline, large loads would 
have had until 8/31 to make any ERCOT-required modifications ahead of ERCOT putting the numbers out on 
9/2. ERCOT was planning to put out their final report including the MW allocation for studied load by 4/9/27.

or reform the tariff provisions that govern large interconnections. The RTOs all requested and received up to 
90-day abeyances ahead of the 8/17 deadline to either justify current tariffs or file tariff changes (by 11/15). 
11/16 – PJM and Transmission Owners compliance filings on FERC order in co-location paper hearing 
(EL25-49, ER26-1088). The original FERC order on this was back in December, which allowed for more 
optionality for co-location. See our note – link. FERC found PJM’s existing tariff unjust/unreasonable on co-
located load arrangements and laid out 3 new options for PJM’s revised tariff to be submitted within 60 days. 
Initial briefs as part of a paper hearing were submitted 2/23/26 and response briefs were due 3/25/26. We 
included our thoughts on response briefs in the Fleishman Daily on 3/27. Both VST and CEG filed significant 
complaints on PJM’s paper hearing, arguing that PJM’s proposal would delay the start of new transmission 
services until 6/1/2029 and effectively create a 3-5 year moratorium on co-located load. PJM submitted its 
reply brief on 4/24, reiterating the proposed in-start date of 6/1/2029. We are now waiting for a FERC action 
in the paper hearing. As part of the December order, PJM also needed to submit several compliance filings 
with proposed tariff edits. PJM submitted its initial tariff reviews on 1/20 (link). On 4/16, FERC issued an order 
on PJM’s first compliance filing. FERC accepted part of the filing but sided with a complaint submitted by CEG 
and rejected PJM’s proposal to replace “point of interconnection” with “point of change in ownership” in the 
definition of co-located load. PJM submitted an updated tariff with an updated definition on 5/18. FERC issued 
an order on 6/18, which accepted in part and rejected in part PJM’s compliance order. In the ongoing paper 
hearing, FERC also directed PJM and Transmission Owners to submit further compliance filings within 60 days

10/15 – State comments on long-term capacity auction reform (AD26-7). PJM kicked off the conversation 
on long-term market reforms with their May 2026 White Paper, laying out three potential paths for reform: 1) 
establishing mandatory hedging requirements for the LSEs; 2) creating different products with varying 
reliability standards for different loads; 3) gradually shifting economics from capacity payments to energy and 
ancillary services payments (see our takes link). The initial stakeholder comments were submitted 8/31, and 
we will get the states’ comments by 10/15. PJM is targeting making a decision on market reform by YE 2026, 
ahead of initiating the 2030/2031 capacity auction. 
11/18, 12/2 – Final RBP procurement target and zonal allocations posted; Final RBP results posted. PJM 
filed their proposal for the reliability backstop mechanism at FERC on 7/31. Pending approval, PJM is planning 
to kick off the central procurement on 9/30, will sell offers from generation due by 10/21. The initial 
procurement target will be set at the shortfall from the 2028/2029 capacity auction, which will then be 
refined downwards for any signed bilateral deals. Evidence of bilateral deals that will offset the RBP 
procurement target is also due on 10/21. PJM will then post the final RBP procurement target and zonal 
allocations on 11/18 and the RBP results will be posted on 12/2. 
Pending – VST/Cogentrix acquisition (EC26-63). VST announced their $4.0B acquisition of Cogentrix from 
Quantum Energy Partners on 1/5/26, with 5.5 GW across PJM, New England and ERCOT (see our thoughts – 
link). VST filed with FERC on 2/6 (see filing – link), and FERC approved the acquisition on 8/5. At the state-
level, VST also already received approvals from New Hampshire and Texas. Remaining approvals are DoJ and 
New Jersey. Notably, VST’s FERC application did not include any planned divestitures, after we initially 
thought there might be some required in ISO-NE. The acquisition is expected to close in late 2026. 
September 2026 – FERC deadline for on PJM proposal on governance updates (AD26-7). FERC Chair 
Swett called for a Technical Conference at PJM’s Annual Meeting in May (link), commenting that PJM may be 
“too large to function”. The technical conference was held on 7/23 and featured a lot of nuanced discussion on 
PJM’s governance and stakeholder process. The FERC commissioners were generally optimistic that PJM’s 
problems can be solved, and gave PJM a September deadline for proposed solutions or FERC will use the “full 
extent of its legal authority to intervene.” PJM put out a term sheet in mid-August with their key principles 
(link), proposing to give states more power – through "jump ball" 205 filing rights, giving OPSI equal footing in 
stakeholder processes to any other PJM Member, and allowing state participation in the PJM Board 
Nominating Committee 
12/22 – PJM final auction results posted for 2029/2030 auction. The auction for the 2029/2030 delivery 
year will be run in December 2026. As of now, this will be the final auction with the collar mechanism in 
place. Of course, there’s a lot of moving pieces that need to happen prior to then. The RBP central

cleared at the cap ($325/MW-day). The auction cleared 6.8 GW short of its reliability requirement and

the print – link. This was in-line with expectations for the price to hit the cap – see our preview (link).

Pending – White House/Japan $66B investment in 18.7 GW of new gas gen. The White House and Japan 
have announced a $66B investment in three gas plants totaling 8.7 GW – including 9.2 GW in Portsmouth, 
OH (first announced in February), 5.2 GW in East TX and 4.3 GW in Southwest PA (both announced 3/20/26). 
These are early-stage projects that have yet to hit most major milestones and our sense is that these 
agreements came together quickly. The OH plants will reportedly serve a 10 GW data center (end-customer 
not yet announced). Turbines are reportedly already secured, with the first turbines delivered with a year and 
the rest by end of the decade. The TX/PA plants will be developed and operated by NEE for a fee but owned 
by the U.S. and Japanese governments. The TX plant is the data center hub with Comstock that NEE had 
previewed at their December 2025 Analyst Day, and already has land secured but no reports of equipment or 
offtake yet. We’ve yet to see many details on the PA project. We wrote on our refreshed views of PJM and 
ERCOT supply/demand set-up (see our note – link). 
Pending – PUCT Due Diligence on TEF Plants (56896). This has been ongoing since spring 2025, the 
original timeline being loan agreements by summer 2025, and disbursements by YE25 (this was a soft 
deadline). Thus far, we've had had 10 plants drop out of the TEF – LS Power, EmberClear, Frontier, CEG, 
WattBridge, ENGIE, Howard Midstream Energy / Javelina, and Aegle Power CCGT. We've saw 11 plants fill in 
as replacements – NRG's Cedar Bayou CCGT and Greens Bayou peaker, VST's Permian Power II peaker, 
Rockland Power's New Gulf, 2 plants from Invenergy, 2 plants from Nightpeak Energy, and EMPower’s plant. 
Nightpeak has since withdrawn both of its projects. Reasons cited for the dropouts have included equipment 
availability, permit uncertainty, and terms of the loan agreements. In June, Rayburn Coop’s Rayburn Energy 
Station II became the 7th plant to close on a TEF loan. Loans have been awarded to 5.0 GWs – NRG’s TH 
Wharton, Cedar Bayou and Greens Bayou 6, Rayburn Coop’s Rayburn Energy Station II, Kerrville’s Rock 
Island, Calpine’s Freestone/Pin Oak, CPV’s Basin Ranch and VST’s Permian Power peakers. Two of these 
projects (NRG’s TH Wharton and Calpine’s Freestone/Pin Oak peaker) have come online. There are currently 4 
applications pending in the TEF program totaling 1.9 GWs, all of which have received extensions from the 
YE25 deadline for loan disbursements.

submitted a request to allow data centers on 870 acres near its 1.5 GW Montour site. The Montour Planning 
Commission voted to not recommend the rezoning to the Board of Commissioners on 11/19/25, with

Ongoing – New Jersey Legislative Session. A nuclear bill (A4881) was introduced in the New Jersey 
legislation on 5/14, unanimously passed both the Senate and Assembly floor on 6/30, and was then signed by

provide financial support for nuclear through a contract for differences with a mechanism called Reliable 
Capacity Certificates (RCC). Back on 2/24, as natural gas procurement bill (A4491) was introduced that would

allowed to participate in either, but even so it’s possible this is derisked enough for Power to participate. 
Finally, another bill was introduced in May that was aimed at streamlining permitting pathways for natural 
gas plant modernization (A4882). These bills are sitting with in committee. 
On the affordability front in New Jersey, this year’s BGS auction resulted in only negligible summer bill 
changes. We got results on 2/12 – PSEG/PEG bills were down $3/month, ACE/EXC bills were flat, and 
JCPL/FE bills were up $2/month (they still have the lowest overall bills). So no one’s bill will move more than 
2% and this is before any offsets from one-time ZEC refunds and RGGI/SBC refunds Governor Sherrill has 
pointed to. Last year, the BGS auction showed electric bills jumping 17-20% in June when PJM capacity 
auction results took effect – this drove heightened political sensitivity.  
 
Last year featured a noisy gubernatorial election campaign that culminated on 11/4 when Mikie Sherrill (D) 
won. As promised, on day one she put forth two Executive Orders. The first to prevent electric bill increases, 
while using state programs like RGGI, ZECs, SBC as a potential offset. It also required a study within 180 days 
on how to achieve long-term stabilization of electric bills. The second to open solicitations for new utility-
scale and distributed solar/batteries to help with near-term supply, plus nuclear studies to help long-term 
supply. Gas was only mentioned in terms of existing uprates, not new build. The new legislative session runs 
the length of 2026.
