Product cracks need to stay elevated to ration both end-user demand and 
restocking demand. We expect 2026 to be another year of negative net capacity 
additions in ex-China refining markets as potential ~0.3mb/d capacity additions is 
more than offset by ~0.6mb/d capacity closures (Exhibit 1). This tight capacity 
backdrop, combined with the historical utilization context, highlights why product 
cracks likely need to stay elevated for longer: the refining system cannot 
accommodate a full demand rebound in 2027 and meaningful restocking while 
keeping refinery utilization at normal, sustainable levels (Exhibit 2). If 2027 product 
demand were to recover to 1% above 2025 levels while the market also attempts to 
rebuild half of the 2026E product inventory draw, the required refinery runs would 
push utilization into unprecedented territory, which we do not view as operationally 
realistic. In practice, the adjustment mechanism is therefore likely to be price-led: 
high cracks need to suppress both end-user demand and inventory restocking 
demand enough to bring refinery utilization back toward a feasible range. Using the 
highest utilization level observed over the past five years as a guide for feasible 
operating limits, we estimate that a potential combination of 2027 demand at 1% 
below 2025 levels and no inventory restocking in 2027 would be needed to bring the 
refining utilization back toward that range (Exhibit 3). 
To address investor inquiries, we run our refining supply-demand framework 
with a monthly path across 3 scenarios. Scenario 1 assumes global refinery runs 
back to normal levels by March 2027, followed by the resolution of Middle East 
refinery outages by June 2027 and Russian disruptions by December 2027, paired 
with a robust 2.9 mb/d recovery in global oil demand in 2027. Scenario 2 models a 
prolonged disruption, delaying the normalization of global refinery runs to October 
2027. Under this scenario, Middle East and Russian refinery outages remain elevated 
at 5.0 mb/d above seasonal norms through the remainder of 2026 and 2027, paired 
with a sluggish global demand growth of 0.5 mb/d. Scenario 3 mirrors the refinery 
runs and outage normalization timeline as Scenario 1, but assumes a more modest 
global oil demand growth of 1.5mb/d. Across all 3 scenarios, we assume refinery 
utilization of the operating fleet returns to the highest 3-month average seen over 
the past 5 years post refinery runs normalization (Exhibit 4-Exhibit 5). We note total

Raising Singapore GRM forecasts to reflect a longer and more tighter refining cycle: 
We raise our 2026E/27E/28E Gross Refining Margin (GRM) forecasts to 
US$33.1/22.8/14.0 per bbl from US$32.5/13.9/11.2 per bbl (more led by diesel and jet 
fuel cracks) as we delay our refinery runs path, with a more gradual recovery through 
2027. This leaves the global refining system operating at elevated utilization rates for 
longer, requiring strong products cracks both to keep refinery runs high for available 
operating capacity and to sustain demand destruction through 2027. We raise our 
2026E/27E/28E EBITDA estimates by an average of 27%/24%/17% for our Asia refiners 
mainly reflecting the higher refining cracks forecasts and stronger lubricant oil margins, 
partly offset by higher crude premiums and logistics costs (freight/insurance), as well as 
marking to market of lower chemical spreads. Net, we raise 12m TPs for our Asia refiners 
by +24% on average. 
Diesel leverage meets capex completion: We remain Buy rated on S-Oil and Thai Oil 
for their strong exposure to diesel and compelling FCF yields. S-Oil is particularly 
attractive following the completion of its capex cycle, with 50-60% of products 
exported and significant leverage to elevated diesel margins. Even with limited 
profitability on domestic sales due to price caps, and broadly higher crude premiums 
and freight costs, we forecast 23% FCF yield in 2027E and ~40% of its market cap 
generated in cash over 2 years. Importantly we believe the best quarter is still ahead, 
with 3Q set to benefit from stronger cracks. We estimate 2H OP to rise 46% sequentially, 
placing our estimates 93% above Bloomberg consensus. 
Feedstock risks manageable; higher crude cost could support higher cracks: 
Feedstock availability remains a key investor concern for S-Oil given the company’s 
heavy reliance on Middle Eastern crude (>90% of crude intake), especially with the 
recent closure of the Saudi East-West pipeline due to escalation of attacks in the 
Hormuz and Red Sea. There will be potential near-term impact on access to Saudi crude, 
though impact on crude availability may be limited. Potential alternative sourcing 
methods include sourcing via Fujairah port, ship-to-ship transfers from Ras Tanura, 
more spot allocations, or drawing down domestic Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR). 
Thai Oil has maintained a more diverse crude sourcing strategy. That said, with refining 
supply-demand looking structurally tight through 2028, we expect higher crude costs to 
be a pass through into stronger product cracks rather than absorbed entirely by refiners. 
Valuations yet to reflect peak cycle returns: Both S-Oil and TOP shares are implying 
discounted EV/EBITDA valuations, offering compelling entry points despite a 
strengthening refining cycle. For S-Oil, P/B multiple remains near historical average at

Europe refining: highlighting Repsol, Neste, Helleniq with exposure to middle distillates strength 
 
 
European refining remains one of the clearest beneficiaries of the current oil market 
dislocation, as product markets have tightened far more than crude markets. 
Refinery outages and disruptions across the Middle East and Russia have pushed 
European benchmark refining margins above $40/bbl, versus a historical mid-cycle level 
of c.$7/bbl, with diesel accounting for over 40% of the increase in global product prices. 
We continue to believe middle distillates will remain structurally tight through 2027 
given low inventories, limited global refining capacity additions and a persistent 
geopolitical risk premium. European refiners coverage is particularly well positioned, 
with c.45-50% of product yields exposed to diesel and jet fuel. We have therefore 
recently raised 2026/27 earnings estimates across the sector (note 1, note 2), while 
remaining below current forward-implied margins. 
Among the Integrated Oils, we continue to see Repsol as one of the most attractive 
ways to gain exposure to stronger refining economics. Repsol has the highest refining 
sensitivity within our European Oils coverage, with operating cash flow sensitivity of 
c.4.6% for every $1/bbl move in refining margins. At the same time, the company 
combines this downstream leverage with industry-leading shareholder returns, which we 
estimate at 10.5% in 2026 and 11.4% in 2027, materially above the European integrated 
oil average. We model €2bn annual buybacks in both 2026 and 2027 and see further 
upside should the company move toward the upper end of its distribution framework, 
while its balance sheet is expected to strengthen to a net cash position by end-2027. 
We have also upgraded Neste to Buy as the company offers exposure to both 
conventional refining strength and improving renewable fuels fundamentals. Higher 
diesel prices support not only the Oil Products segment but also the Renewable 
Products business by improving renewable diesel economics through term sales 
contracts pricing structure (fossil diesel price plus green premium). In addition, we see 
supportive policy developments across Europe, including RED III implementation and 
anti-dumping measures, helping underpin renewable fuels demand. With the Rotterdam 
expansion nearing completion, capex set to fall sharply and renewable volumes 
expected to recover from 2027, we forecast a significant free cash flow inflection, 
continued deleveraging and a return to more meaningful shareholder distributions. 
Within pure-play refiners, the Greek names remain among the most leveraged to 
the middle-distillate theme. Helleniq Energy and Motor Oil derive roughly half of 
product output from diesel and jet fuel and therefore stand to benefit from 
stronger-for-longer middle-distillate margins. We raised our 2026/27 EBITDA forecasts 
materially for both companies and expect significantly higher cash generation and 
shareholder distributions as a result. We maintain a Buy rating on Helleniq Energy, where 
higher earnings and lower net debt support further upside, while Motor Oil remains 
Neutral despite substantial earnings upgrades, reflecting a more balanced risk-reward 
following the recent share price performance. A potential windfall tax remains a risk for 
the Greek refining sector, although the Greek government has so far provided no 
indication that such measures are under consideration.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
