Caris Life Sciences Inc. (CAI): CMS Releases Preliminary 2027 CLFS 
Rates; Positive Revision for CAI
22 September 2026 | 11:10AM EDT
Yesterday, CMS released its Preliminary Calendar Year 2027 Medicare Clinical 
Laboratory Fee Schedule Payment Rates. The preliminary CY27 PAMA fee schedule 
is incrementally positive for CAI, in our view, as CMS maintained reimbursement 
for MI Cancer Seek (tissue) at $8,455 while increasing reimbursement for Caris 
Assure (blood) to $5,250 from the current $3,649 (+44%). This should provide a 
tailwind to blood-based profiling revenue and ASPs beginning in 2027, while 
preserving the favorable economics of the company’s core tissue franchise. Given 
management’s recent commentary that blood volume growth is accelerating 
following the sales force reorganization and dedicated commercial focus on liquid 
biopsy, the higher Medicare rate could further support mix-driven revenue upside as 
adoption scales. 
We estimate the preliminary fee schedule update could represent approximately 
$15–20mn of annualized revenue upside based on our model. Overall, we view the 
update as another step in reducing reimbursement risk around Caris Assure and 
reinforcing confidence in the company’s ability to drive a shift to the faster-growing 
blood-based portfolio. 
Valuation and Risks 
 
 
We are Buy rated on CAI. Our 12-month price target is $35. Our valuation 
methodology uses a target 7x Q5-Q8 EV/Sales. Key downside risks include: 1) 
Competitive Risks as Caris faces material competition in the therapy selection space 
in addition to upcoming product launches through its MCED, MRD, and other assays, 
2) Trial risk with potential execution in a broader population as its first MCED trial 
was case controlled. This risk can lead to decreased performance of its screening 
tests and therefore impact how it is perceived by the market, 3) Execution Risks in 
receiving FDA approval and therefore CMS reimbursement remains a risk for future 
tests, and CAI has multiple upcoming submissions and launches that could pose 
challenges in addition to growing its pipeline of new products, and 4) CAI’s 
multi-channel approach to the MCED roll out (rather than a full sales force) could 
hinder the company’s ability to penetrate the market.
