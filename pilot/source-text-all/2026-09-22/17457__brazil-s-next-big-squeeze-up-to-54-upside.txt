Brazil’s double trigger
Brazil is already the best-performing EM market over the past month, but the setup is no longer simply
about global beta or commodities.
Election odds have started driving the marginal move, while valuations continue to reflect something
much closer to deterioration than reform.
Commodities support the earnings. Politics can unlock the multiple.
A sustained close above the huge $39 area would open the next phase of the EWZ squeeze.

Politics takes the wheel
Global factors normally do most of the heavy lifting in Brazil. Elections are the exception.
Since July, MSCI Brazil has risen alongside Flavio Bolsonaro’s election odds. More importantly, the
market’s sensitivity to those odds is accelerating. In September, MSCI Brazil gained an average 0.72%
on days his odds improved, versus just 0.08% in July and 0.10% in August.
Brazil has stopped trading like pure global beta. Local politics is increasingly setting the price.

The multiple has not followed
The rally has started, but the rerating has not.
Brazil trades at just 8.6x forward earnings, placing it between JPM’s “deterioration” and “improvement”
regimes, and still considerably closer to deterioration.
A move into the improvement regime implies 28% upside. A full structural fix implies 54%.
The market has begun pricing a different election outcome. It has not yet priced a different economic
regime.

The rates unlock
The election matters because it changes the path for Brazilian rates.
JPM estimates a government victory could push yields 90bp higher, while an opposition victory could
produce a 160bp rally. Over the past decade, every 50bp decline in government yields has generated
approximately 2% upside in MSCI Brazil.
That gives a mechanical range of roughly 3.6% downside under the bearish scenario against 6.4%
upside under the bullish scenario, before accounting for any broader fiscal rerating.
The larger opportunity requires credible policy, a reform agenda and a path toward lower neutral
rates. During the 2016–2020 improvement cycle, Brazil’s two-year yield fell from almost 17% to 4.7%,
equities gained 130% and the forward multiple reached 11.4x.
Using the more conservative historical relationship, JPM estimates 21% upside if yields fall 375bp. Its
more bullish full-cycle framework implies 41%.
The election can unlock the initial move. The rates cycle determines its size.

The commodity floor
Brazil does not need to rely on politics alone.
EWZ retains a powerful connection to the commodity complex. Firm commodities support earnings,
the current account and the real, while falling domestic yields create the valuation rerating.
One engine supports the E. The other can expand the P/E.

Vol got there first
EWZ volatility has exploded as the election moved from a distant political risk to the market’s
dominant local catalyst. The move confirms the regime shift we have been highlighting, but it also
changes the trade.
The original opportunity was to own cheap convexity ahead of a binary catalyst. That window has
largely closed. With vol now elevated, bullish investors should favor structures that sell some of the
newly expensive optionality, such as call spreads or collars, or simply own EWZ rather than chase
naked calls after the repricing.
The distinction matters: while the options market has priced the event, the underlying equity market
still trades at just 8.6x forward earnings, much closer to JPM’s deterioration regime than its
improvement regime.
The options market has caught up. The equity multiple may not have.
