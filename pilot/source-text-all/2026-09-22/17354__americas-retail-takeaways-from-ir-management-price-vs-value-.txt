Americas Retail: Takeaways from IR/management; Price vs. Value 
perception analysis; Commentary on efficiencies from AI
21 September 2026 | 6:00AM EDT
In this note, we discuss the key takeaways from meetings with the IR/management 
teams of Academy Sports & Outdoors (ASO), National Vision Holdings (EYE), and RH. 
We also analyze HundredX trends on value vs. price perceptions for our coverage 
universe, following the 2026 GS Global Retail Conference (Day 1 and Day 2 
takeaways). Finally, we provide commentary from our covered companies on where 
they see efficiencies from AI. 
Takeaways from IR/management meetings 
 
 
ASO 
The World Cup performed in-line with expectations. The company called out 
strength in areas such as jerseys and tees. That said, the event was not completely 
additive; given the World Cup was at the peak of Father’s Day, some customers who 
may have purchased other items for Father’s Day likely bought World Cup products 
instead. Academy was also up against Oklahoma City winning the championship last 
year, which dampened the effect a bit, and the company did not have the Knicks 
championship win in its footprint. Moreover, management explained that many 
customers wanted World Cup jerseys and there was only a finite amount, which 
capped the upside. On the other hand, within sporting goods, the company is seeing 
a World Cup effect with soccer gear sales running +DD in 2Q. Looking ahead, 
management expects this trend to continue through the remainder of the year and 
into next year. 
Academy is not as impacted by current industry-wide promotions. Within the 
sporting goods industry, management explained that while select peers are citing a 
slowdown in lifestyle footwear, it is largely impacting mall-based chains and items 
that are not a key part of Academy’s assortment. In the styles Academy carries, there 
is not an excess of supply. Management also highlighted that Academy benefits from 
its diverse assortment; the company has a large outdoor business, and within 
footwear, the company sells cleats and performance running, along with categories 
such as work boots and seasonal items. In FY25, footwear was 20% of Academy’s 
sales. 
The company expects gross margins to be about flat y/y in 2H. Tailwinds include 
shrink and a lower overall tariff rate, while the company expects to reinvest in price,

EYE 
Ticket growth and traffic outlook. Management noted that one of the most common 
investor questions is whether average ticket growth can remain durable as the company 
laps strong comparisons. Unlike traditional retail categories, the company emphasized 
that optical is a highly consultative business, where ticket growth is driven primarily by 
product mix rather than price. As associates gain experience selling more advanced 
products, such as premium progressive lenses, management believes their ability to 
drive higher-value transactions should improve over time. On traffic, management 
reiterated that declines in lower-value transactions are an intentional part of its 
customer mix evolution and do not change its long-term strategy. While traffic could 
improve if macro conditions stabilize, management noted that lower-income customer 
pressure has tracked closely with higher fuel prices. As a result, the company is focused 
on driving growth with higher-value customers through store segmentation and their 
product premiumization strategy. Management also noted that they plan to help 
quantify customer evolution mix in the future.   
Smart glasses and competitive positioning. Management views the planned launch of 
AI glasses by other industry participants as a positive for the category, noting that 
increased consumer awareness, advertising, and product innovation should help drive 
broader adoption of smart glasses. Management highlighted that Meta glasses is 
currently one of the fastest-turning categories in its assortment. Notably, the company is 
positioning itself as a destination for smart glasses, and believes its 1,200 points of 
distribution will enable it to have relationships with many vendors. Compared to other 
competitors such as Best Buy’s Meta Labs, management believes its integrated optical 
model provides a meaningful advantage, as customers can complete eye exams, apply 
vision insurance, obtain prescriptions, and purchase smart glasses through a single 
channel. Management also highlighted future opportunities from its unified commerce 
platform, which could allow the company to leverage prescription data and customer 
relationships to drive repeat purchases.  
While we are encouraged by management’s initiatives to drive revenue and profitability, 
we think investors would likely want to see sustainable +MSD% comps as National Vision 
laps harder compares in order to become more constructive. Additionally, we remain 
cautious on National Vision’s commentary on the deferral behavior of the value-seeking 
consumer, given it extends across all income demographics and the macro environment 
remains uncertain. We estimate 3Q26 comp of +3.8% (vs. Factset consensus of +3.4%) 
and EPS of $0.16 (vs. consensus of $0.17).

RH 
Margin outlook in the 2H. Management indicated that there have been no meaningful 
changes to its 2H margin outlook beyond the impacts from higher freight costs and the 
IEEPA tariff refund. The company also clarified that the incremental $50 million cost 
headwind reflects an annualized impact relative to prior guidance rather than a 
single-quarter effect. While management did not provide a quarterly breakdown, it 
noted that freight, fuel surcharges, home delivery costs, and vendor cost increases flow 
through inventory costs over time, causing the impact to be recognized gradually rather 
than all at once. 
Sourcebook cadence. For the 2H, RH is planning to release one sourcebook each in 3Q 
and 4Q. Management clarified that the recent sourcebook release (~500 pages) is a mix 
of both the RH Modern and RH Interior books. This sourcebook has been distributed to 
all customers, with management believing it reflects the best of both product 
collections. The 4Q sourcebook will be for RH Estates, and management noted that the 
business will lap no sourcebook as the company omitted the RH Modern sourcebook in 
4Q25.  
Following 2Q, we are encouraged by management’s commentary that the Estates 
collection is seeing strong demand and is attracting incremental customers into the 
galleries. We were also pleased by management’s guidance that FY27 should see a 150 
bp international expense drag (an improvement from 340 bps in FY26). That said, we 
remain Neutral on the name, as the Estates rollout is not yet complete. We view 3Q as an 
important proof point to assess whether the collection continues to resonate with 
consumers and whether additional promotions are needed to support demand in other 
categories. We also acknowledge that the housing environment might create a more 
challenging environment for RH as interest rates increase. For 3Q, we estimate revenue 
growth of +5.4% (vs. consensus of +5.7%), and EPS of $0.91 (vs. consensus of $1.07).  
Analyzing price and value perception 
 
 
We supplement our work with data from HundredX, a mission-based data and insights 
company that takes an innovative approach to monitoring consumer perceptions and 
gathering consumer feedback to understand trends across 80+ industries and 3,000+ 
brands. HundredX analyzes collective opinions of everyday customers and evaluates 
how their priorities influence purchasing decisions and attitudes toward businesses and 
brands. 
Value vs. price perception remains a topical theme following the 2026 GS Global Retail 
Conference (Day 1 and Day 2 takeaways), as macroeconomic pressures and shifting 
consumer priorities drive shoppers to become increasingly selective. In Auto Parts, ORLY 
holds the most favorable pricing perception, while AAP has experienced a sharp decline 
recently; notably, both AAP and ORLY are seeing a deterioration in their overall value 
perception. In Broadlines, WMT historically maintains the strongest pricing perception 
and COST leads the sector in overall value, whereas TGT consistently ranks lowest on 
both metrics. Within Home Improvement and Furnishing, TSCO has faced a steep decline 
in price perception since January 2026. Meanwhile, BOBS continues to be more 
favorably than RH and WSM in both price and value. In the Discounters, DLTR is 
traditionally viewed as the most favorable in pricing, whereas both DG and OLLI have

Valuation and risks 
 
 
ASO 
We are Buy rated on ASO with a 12-month price target of $63 based on our 
downside/base/upside case relative P/E multiples of 35%/40%/45%. 
Downside risks 
Potential for Nike to limit product distributions to ASO: If Nike were to begin 
n
considering ASO an undifferentiated retail partner, it could limit or stop product 
distributions to ASO. Without Nike product, ASO could lose customers and market 
share, driving downside to its financial results and the stock price. 
Weaker omnichannel/digital capabilities vs. competitors: Though ASO has 
n
taken/is taking steps to improve its digital presence and omnichannel capabilities, if 
ASO is unable to catch up with competitors in this regard, it could cause it to lose 
customers and market share, which could hurt its financial results over time. In 
addition, as ASO attempts to catch up with competitors in this area, it could result in 
increased operating expenses, harming ASO’s financial results.

Commentary by companies on seeing efficiencies from AI
ASO
Academy is testing agentic-based search on its website to replace traditional search functionality, with preliminary results showing good 
performance despite still representing a small portion of traffic. The company is exploring AI automation for rote tasks including item setup with 
proper attributes and field configuration, and accounts payable processes, with the goal of freeing employee time for higher-value functions 
rather than reducing headcount. Additionally, the company is testing AI-powered business intelligence dashboards that consolidate weekend 
and weekly performance data with recommendations, allowing employees to spend time on qualitative decision-making rather than manual 
data compilation. Management expects AI to enable better localization of assortments on a store-by-store basis.
BOBS
Management is executing a targeted growth and margin expansion strategy. Key drivers include broadened yet curated customer targeting, 
supply chain optimization (aligning inventory, packaging, and capacity across distribution centers to reduce costs), and enhanced corporate 
agility via data analytics and financial automation. Management noted that these initiatives are already yielding early traction, positioning the 
firm for significant operational upside.
DKS
Management is accelerating AI integration to drive operational efficiencies and enhance omnichannel conversion. By leveraging proprietary first-
party data from its ScoreCard loyalty program and digitizing in-store expertise, the firm recently launched Coach by DICK'S, an AI-powered 
conversational assistant embedded in its mobile app. Management noted that this initiative is designed to reduce employee friction while 
delivering hyper-personalized product and training guidance to consumers.
GPC
Management is prioritizing initiatives designed to reduce friction for both customers and employees. Key focus areas include optimizing 
inventory management, improving overall operational efficiency, and driving salesforce productivity. 
HD
Management noted that the goal is to translate AI-enabled operational speed into higher sales and compounding market share gains.
LOW
Management expects maturing AI investments to drive continued productivity gains across IT, inventory planning, and payroll management. 
Furthermore, the company is leveraging agentic AI tools to reduce customer friction and equip associates with real-time, highly technical 
product knowledge. The company anticipates these AI capabilities will meaningfully lower training costs while delivering immediate, accurate 
responses to complex customer inquiries.
WMT
Management highlighted the deployment of ambient automation during the recent Back-to-School season to optimize inventory flow. By 
constructing layered pallets that move directly from delivery trucks to seasonal store shelves, the company reduced handling friction and 
improved in-store execution. 
WSM
Management highlighted that AI is already generating meaningful top-line contributions, with revenue acceleration expected through 2027. The 
company is executing AI-driven cost savings across three core areas: technology, customer care, and supply chain. Substantial engineering 
efficiencies are being reinvested to drive higher output rather than flowing to the bottom line. In customer service, management noted that the 
deployment of AI agents to manage the majority of chat volume is delivering superior efficiency at a lower cost, with further savings anticipated 
next year. Additionally, management is actively data-mining its supply chain via AI to identify and unlock future operational opportunities.

Potential for a pullback in consumer demand for sporting goods: Given the 
n
strong consumer demand for the sporting goods and outdoors categories 
throughout the pandemic, there is a possibility that much of this demand proves to 
be pull-forward, resulting in a dip in spending within the category going forward. If

EYE 
We are Neutral rated on EYE. Our 12-month price target is $23 based on our 
downside/base/upside case EV/EBITDA multiples of 8.0x/9.0x/10.0x.  
Upside risks include better-than-expected demand for eye care and eyeglasses 
following softer trends in recent years; a meaningful acceleration in trade down trends; if 
margins recover faster than expected; and the potential for a multiple re-rating if 
sentiment improves following better-than-expected top line results and a clear pathway 
to margin recovery. 
Downside risks include a slower-than-expected top line recovery as lower-income and 
uninsured customers defer demand, or if certain consumers exit the market altogether; 
less-than-expected trade down; continued exam capacity headwinds; margin 
headwinds due to higher wages; increased competition; and the risk that certain costs 
remain elevated. 
RH 
We are Neutral rated on RH with a 12-month price target of $164 based on our 
downside/base/upside case relative P/E multiples of 60%/65%/70%.   
Upside risks: International galleries ramp faster than expected, driving sales growth and 
margin expansion; RH continues to launch brand collections that resonate with the 
consumer; a meaningful decline in interest rates and/or home prices, leading to an 
improvement in the existing housing market; RH gains market share in the high net worth 
households; RH increases its FCF through extended sale-leasebacks and pays down 
debt. 
Downside risks: International galleries ramp slower than expected and continue to 
require large expenses, leading to margin headwinds; interest rates and home prices

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
