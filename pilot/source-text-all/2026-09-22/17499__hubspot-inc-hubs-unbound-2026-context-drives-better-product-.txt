Investor Day Takeaways 
Context is the moat: HubSpot views proprietary business context as the key 
1.
layer that converts AI intelligence into measurable outcomes. HubSpot’s view is 
that foundation models will continue to improve and become increasingly accessible, 
shifting differentiation away from model intelligence and toward the context 
available inside business applications. HubSpot introduced Growth Context Graph — 
which brings together business, team, and customer context, allowing agents to 
understand company-specific workflows, customer relationships, organizational 
processes, permissions, governance, and commercial data. The company showed 
data suggesting customers with rich context achieved materially better outcomes 
and that bad context made outcomes worse than no context, reinforcing 
management’s view that application software remains strategically important in an 
AI-driven world because it houses the context that agents need to act effectively. 
This framework increasingly underpins HubSpot’s positioning as the system where 
customer data, workflow logic, and execution reside, rather than simply a CRM 
database.

Rather than helping users complete individual tasks, Breeze can now build

Headless strategy expands the ways customers interact with HubSpot while 
3.
preserving monetization through context and workflow execution. HubSpot 
believes that software consumption will increasingly happen through external AI 
interfaces, agents, and conversational surfaces. Management outlined a three-layer 
API strategy consisting of Data APIs, Context APIs, and Work APIs. Rather than 
simply exposing CRM records, HubSpot intends to monetize the higher value layers 
that provide intelligence and execute work. Context APIs return insights generated 
from HubSpot’s Growth Context Graph, while Work APIs allow customers and third 
parties to invoke HubSpot agents and workflows directly. The company believes 
future interactions will occur across multiple surfaces, but that the underlying 
context and workflow execution remain anchored within HubSpot. Management 
also emphasized that monetization scales through both HubSpot agents and 
customer-built agents consuming credits, creating a framework where usage growth 
is tied not only to users but also to AI-generated work performed across the 
platform.  
Broader frontier-model strategy reinforces HubSpot’s open ecosystem 
4.

major frontier-model ecosystems and announced a deeper integration with OpenAI 
through ChatGPT Ads. The company also introduced an AI Growth Bundle that 
combines HubSpot Starter, HubSpot credits, a ChatGPT Business seat, and 
advertising incentives tied to ChatGPT Ads. Management framed these partnerships 
as extensions of its broader strategy to meet customers wherever they choose to 
interact with software while ensuring HubSpot remains the source of context, 
customer intelligence, and workflow execution. The company disclosed that it is now 
the number one CRM connector on both ChatGPT and Claude. More broadly, 
management’s message was that increasing adoption of external AI interfaces 
should expand engagement with HubSpot rather than disintermediate it, provided 
HubSpot continues to own the customer context and execution layers.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.

return potential, price target and associated time horizon are stated in each report adding or reiterating an Investment List membership.
