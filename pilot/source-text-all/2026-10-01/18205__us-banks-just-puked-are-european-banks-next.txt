The great outperformer
Eurozone banks have been monsters this year.
SX7E is up 20.2% YTD, outperforming the broader European market by roughly 12 percentage points.
Even as the SXXP and SX5E pulled back over the past five weeks, banks largely refused to participate.
Strong earnings explain part of it. So does positioning: banks remain the third most-owned sector in
Europe according to BofA's Fund Manager Survey.
Great when everything works. Less great when expectations start moving the other way.

The curve says something different
Banks normally dislike curve flattening, particularly when it reflects central banks being forced to
tighten into a weakening growth backdrop.
That relationship worked earlier this year. But recently it has broken.
The EUR 2s10s curve has flattened again while SX7E has barely blinked.
Either the curve is too pessimistic, or the banks haven't caught up yet.

The correlation has flipped
The dislocation is now showing up in correlation.
The relationship between EZ banks and long-end EUR rates has recently turned negative, an unusual
setup given banks' historically positive sensitivity to rates.
BofA thinks that creates an interesting hedge: if growth becomes the dominant concern, banks and
yields could suddenly fall together as markets simultaneously price weaker earnings and less ECB
tightening. The rates shock eventually becomes a growth shock.

Protection is interesting
The obvious problem with hedging something that has already become a market favorite is usually
price.
Not necessarily here.
SX7E downside screens better than several other cross-asset tail hedges when current hedge costs are
compared with historical drawdowns. Bank put skew is elevated versus the rest of Europe, but that
actually makes put spreads relatively attractive.
Their Dec-26 30D–20D put spread offers a >4x maximum payout.
If you think the rates problem eventually becomes a growth problem, banks offer an interesting place
to express it.

What if US banks aren't the anomaly?
US banks have puked while their European counterparts have barely moved, opening a huge
transatlantic divergence.
The obvious trade is to fade the US move. But BofA's work raises the opposite possibility: what if EZ
banks are simply late?
European curves are flattening, growth expectations are deteriorating and SX7E remains one of the
year's great outperformers. The gap may close from the European side.
Mean reversion doesn't tell you which leg does the reverting.
