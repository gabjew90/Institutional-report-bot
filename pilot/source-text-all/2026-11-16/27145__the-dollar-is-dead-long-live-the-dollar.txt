King dollar is back
The dollar bears keep waiting for the reversal, but the greenback keeps pushing higher. DXY has
broken out, reclaimed key long-term levels, and is tracking both US economic outperformance and
rate differentials. Meanwhile, the euro remains stuck in reverse, speculative dollar longs are building,
and foreign capital continues to favor US assets. The path of least resistance remains higher, but there
are risks worth watching. The dollar bull is alive and well. What could eventually kill it?
Long live the king
The dollar remains firmly bid, with the latest breakout pushing DXY to its highest levels since April
2025. Momentum is strong, the 200-day MA is sloping higher, and despite the recent surge, RSI
remains well below overbought territory. Little meaningful resistance stands in the way until the major
trendline around 105. The path of least resistance remains higher.

The longer-term view
The major dollar trendline in place since 2011 has held once again. Notably, DXY has now reclaimed

similar to what we saw in late 2021.

Soggy euro
We continue to struggle to see why so many remain bullish on the euro. The soggy currency trades
well below its 200-day MA, which has been sloping lower for months. Our initial downside target has
been the 1.10 area, but momentum remains poor and Europe has no shortage of problems. Perhaps a
pause around 1.10, but then what? Credit where credit is due: JPM's FX strategy has nailed the dollar
strength over the past few weeks.

The euro is not playing ball
The euro continues to struggle despite several potentially supportive developments. EUR has failed to
benefit from sharply lower energy prices or the ECB's more hawkish-than-expected policy update.
French fiscal risks remain a persistent headwind. If the euro cannot rally on good news, what exactly

Dollar longs are building
MUFG's latest positioning snapshot shows leveraged funds had increased USD longs ahead of the
midterm elections, reaching their highest level since early August. The biggest dollar longs are against
EUR and CAD, while euro shorts have reached their most extreme levels since March 2020. The
speculative community has been moving back into the dollar trade. The risk is that positioning
eventually becomes too crowded.
MUFG
The dollar's $1.5 trillion tailwind
The dollar continues to enjoy enormous support from foreign demand for US assets. MUFG highlights a
record $1.47 trillion of foreign purchases of US equities and corporate bonds in the year to August,
almost double the previous peak before the GFC. With FX hedge ratios still low, these inflows have
provided a powerful source of dollar demand. US exceptionalism has not just been an equity story. It
has also been a dollar story.

MUFG
The $1.5 trillion reversal risk
The dollar's biggest tailwind could eventually become its biggest headwind. MUFG warns that a
sustained reversal in US equity outperformance could undermine the record foreign capital flows
supporting USD. With FX hedge ratios still low, investors could respond by reducing US exposure or
increasing dollar hedges. The dollar doesn't necessarily need an exodus from US assets to weaken. A
shift in hedging behavior could be enough.
For now, the trend belongs to the bulls. The risk is that the next dollar reversal starts in US
equities, not FX.
