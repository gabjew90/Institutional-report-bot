Post mid-terms hangover for the US bonds market 
MARKET LINES 
► 
By stating that the United States no longer needed an agreement with Iran, President D. Trump 
has reduced the potential for energy prices to fall, further weakening a bond market already 
under pressure since the release of US inflation data for October and last Friday’s nonfarm 
payrolls report. Penalised as well by Ny Fed John Williams hawkish comments (inflation is far too 
high and the Fed has work to do), the 2-year US Treasury yield fell by nearly 14bp during the 
session, returning to 4.88%, while the increase over the week reached nearly 45bp (+13bp for the 
10-year US Treasury yield). The simultaneous rises in TTF gas prices (up nearly €6.5 to €94.69) and 
Brent crude (up $3.3 to $95.9) had a more limited impact in Europe. French yields rose by 7bp in 
the 10-year segment, widening the 10-year OAT-Bund spread by 3.8bp during the session and 
bringing it back to around 151bp. Despite increasingly light primary-market issuance, the bond 
market more broadly is likely to remain fragile over the next few sessions, particularly if the US 
PPI due today also comes in well above consensus expectations (consensus forecast: +0.4% 
month on month in October). 
Rates 
► Yields rose again after Donald Trump’s comments reignited expectations of monetary tightening. 
The 10-year Bund rose 3 bp to 3.35%, with yield curves bear steepening. Markets are now pricing 
in 73 bp of ECB rate hikes through the end of 2027.  
► In the United States, the rise in yields was more pronounced, with the 2-year UST up 11 bp to 4.85%.

2027, including 27 bp as early as December 2026.
