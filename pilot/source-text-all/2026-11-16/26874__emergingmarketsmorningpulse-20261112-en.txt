 
 
Emerging Markets Morning Pulse 
FI Sales Trading Desk View | 12 November 2026 | Page 1 of 2 
EM credit experienced a subdued session yesterday as the US Veterans’ 
Day holiday and absence of Treasury cash trading significantly reduced li-
quidity and client activity across regions. LATAM markets were largely in-
active, with only limited private banking demand for short-dated oil names 
amid exceptionally low volumes. Brazilian sovereigns and corporates saw 
virtually no trading, while BEEFBZ remained under pressure ahead of its 
3Q26 earnings release after the close. BRASKEM remained in focus fol-
lowing reports that creditors had presented two restructuring proposals. 
MENA credit was similarly quiet, with minimal secondary market activity, 
while primary market attention shifted to upcoming transactions from 
Boubyan Bank, which announced an AT1 mandate with fair value esti-
mated around 7.50%, and Al Rayan Bank, which mandated its inaugural 5-
year Sukuk. Turkish credit traded quietly with limited client flows, concen-
trated primarily in corporates, while financials remained subdued. CEE 
credit continued to show resilience despite pressure on EUR rates, with 
EUR-denominated bonds remaining supported. The new € NOVALJ perpet-
ual attracted two-way interest and closed +0.20 to +0.40pts above reoffer 
levels. 
 
*** Rates *** 
  US Treasuries edged higher this morning as renewed concerns sur-
rounding Nvidia’s leadership triggered safe-haven demand, partially off-
setting expectations of tighter Federal Reserve policy. The 10-year Treas-
ury yield declined -1bp to 4.98%, while the 30-year yield fell as much as -
3bps to 5.32%. Cash Treasury trading was closed on Wednesday for Veter-
ans’ Day, although futures weakened following hotter-than-expected US 
inflation data and hawkish Fed commentary. Markets are now fully pricing 
a 25bp rate hike in December, with a small probability of a 50bp increase. 
Germany’s 10-year Bund yield was unchanged at 3.32%, while UK 10-year 
yields held steady at 5.22%. In Asia, Japanese 10-year yields declined -
2bps to 2.77%, while Australian yields fell -1bp to 5.03%. 
 
*** Equities *** 
  Global equities reversed some of Wednesday’s strong gains as the 
leadership crisis at Nvidia weighed on technology stocks and broader AI 
sentiment. S&P 500 futures declined -0.4% and Nasdaq 100 futures fell -
0.9%, after the underlying indices rallied +1.3% and +2.3%, respectively, 
on Wednesday. Nvidia shares plunged as much as -10% in after-hours 
trading amid uncertainty surrounding CEO Jensen Huang, overshadowing 
renewed AI optimism following CoreWeave’s +25% surge and strong indus-
try developments. Asian markets traded mostly lower, with the MSCI Asia 
Pacific Index declining -0.5%, Hong Kong’s Hang Seng falling -0.9% and 
Japan’s Topix gaining +0.3%. European equities reached record highs on 
Wednesday, while Euro Stoxx 50 futures advanced +0.3% this morning. 
 
*** FX *** 
  The US dollar remained firm after the Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index 
gained +0.4% on Wednesday, reaching its highest level in 18 months, sup-
ported by stronger-than-expected inflation and increasingly hawkish Fed 
expectations. The index was little changed this morning. EUR/USD traded 
at 1.1080 after declining -0.7% yesterday, while GBP/USD fell -0.5% to 
1.3274. USD/JPY hovered near the closely watched 160 level, trading at 
159.70 after the yen weakened -0.7% on Wednesday. The offshore yuan 
remained stable at 6.6673 per dollar, while Indonesia’s rupiah fell to a 
record low amid dollar strength and concerns over a potential exclusion of 
Indonesian equities from MSCI indexes. 
 
*** Oil *** 
  Oil prices retreated this morning as rising Middle Eastern production 
and subdued US-Iran hostilities weighed on the market. WTI declined -
1.5% to $85.24 per barrel, reversing Wednesday’s +0.4% gain, while Brent 
traded near $91 per barrel after rising +1.4% in the previous session.
