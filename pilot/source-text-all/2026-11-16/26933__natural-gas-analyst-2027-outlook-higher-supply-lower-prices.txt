Mideast LNG Supply Adaptation to Soften European Gas Balances 
Persian Gulf LNG exports have increased sharply over the past several weeks, averaging 
33% of pre-war levels in October and more than 60% thus far this month (above our 
35% Nov base case), showing that Mideast energy supply adaptation has continued 
(Exhibit 1). In particular, Qatar has increased the usage of its larger Q-Flex and Q-Max 
tankers, allowing it to move more LNG for a given number of crossings through the Strait 
of Hormuz (SoH). 
To be clear, LNG supply adaptation is still below that of oil, whose Persian Gulf exports 
remain in an approximate 80%-100% range of 2025 levels, given that LNG is more 
flammable than crude oil and LNG ship-to-ship transfers are more technically 
challenging. 
 
With this month’s Mideast LNG supply beating our expectations, we are comfortable 
maintaining our base case assumption that Persian Gulf LNG exports will reach their 
operating max (with Qatari loadings for now capped at 83% of pre-war levels owing to 
infrastructure damage during the war) in 1Q27. Further, we have lowered the supply risk 
premium we embed in our TTF forecast. 
Specifically, our 65/45 EUR/MWh 1Q27/2027 TTF forecasts embed a subjective 30% 
probability (down from 45% prev.) that we face a low-winter-Mideast LNG supply 
scenario1 (worth 17 EUR to 1Q27 prices), and a small cold-weather risk component

Risks to Our 2027 TTF Forecasts remain Skewed to The Upside 
Importantly, risks to our bearish 2027 TTF forecasts remain skewed to the upside. On 
the supply side, a still relatively high number of tanker attacks near Hormuz (Exhibit 13 
in the Appendix) and headlines this past week suggesting a potential slowdown in 
diplomatic efforts to solve the conflict suggest that the further recovery in Mideast LNG 
supply we expect is far from certain. 
Should Persian Gulf LNG exports average near 35% of pre-war levels in 2027, we 
estimate that 2027 TTF prices would likely need to average 104 EUR/MWh, well above 
our 45 EUR base case, to discourage enough LNG demand outside of Europe to help 
manage NW European gas inventories through the year (Exhibit 2). 
In contrast, should Mideast LNG supply increase in line with our base case, along with 
market confidence that this would be sustainable, driving market risk premium to 
dissipate, we estimate TTF can balance NW European gas markets by averaging 2027 at 
32 EUR/MWh. Assuming average NW European winter temperatures, current 2027 
forward prices suggest the market is pricing a 58% probability that Mideast LNG supply 
is constrained throughout 2027. 
 
Winter weather also remains a source of risk. A 1 std. deviation from average in winter 
temperatures (an average 0.7C deviation through Nov-Mar) across NW Europe can swing 
regional gas demand to the tune of 12% of storage capacity. We estimate that a 1 std. 
deviation colder than average winter in NW Europe could lift our 65 EUR/MWh base case 
1Q27 TTF forecast to 106 EUR (Exhibit 3). If combined with our Low-2027 supply 
scenario, we estimate 1Q27 TTF prices would rise to 147 EUR/MWh. In contrast, we 
estimate that a 1 std. deviation warmer than average winter in NW Europe could 
pressure 1Q27 TTF to 41 EUR/MWh, assuming our base case Rising-Winter ME supply 
scenario.

How Can TTF Prices Decline So Significantly in One Year? 
While our base case sequential decline in TTF prices from more than 90 EUR/MWh 
currently to the low 30 EURs/MWh in late 2027 might at first glance seem excessive, 
sharp rallies and sell-offs are not uncommon in natural gas markets and are driven by 
what the market needs to price at the margin in order to appropriately manage its gas 
inventory levels.  
This year NW Europe has faced the risk of a supply deficit through the winter, requiring 
TTF to price high enough to discourage LNG demand elsewhere. That’s an effective way 
to leave more LNG supply available to be delivered into Europe, but it is expensive. 
Going forward, because we assume that LNG supply adaptation in the Middle East will 
continue, we expect NW European gas balances to face a much more comfortable 
supply outlook in 2027 without having to compete with Asia or other buyers for LNG. 
Hence, we expect that 2027 TTF will not price demand destruction at the margin. 
Instead, we estimate that all that European natural gas prices will need to do to 
guarantee a comfortable storage path heading into the 2027-28 winter is to make sure 
that a potential increase in gas demand for power in 2027 (as gas becomes cheaper 
relative to coal over the course of the year) is not too much to tighten inventories. From 
a price perspective, this can be done at a much lower level vs current TTF prices, with 
hard coal currently trading near 43 EUR/MWh (in gas-equivalent terms) in Europe 
(Exhibit 14, Appendix), and likely to come down further as risk premia unwind next year. 
Long-Dated Balance Softening Continues 
This winter’s tightening risks notwithstanding, longer-term European gas and global LNG 
balances look increasingly soft, with Venture Global recently bringing forward the start 
date for their 29 mtpa (3.9 Bcf/d) CP2 project, leaving 2028 global LNG balances 
particularly oversupplied.

US Softer in 2027, But Tightening from 2028 
We maintain our view that, after going through most of the last 18 years oversupplied, 
having to often price as low as PRB coal to balance, US natural gas markets will transition 
towards a more balanced (and at times, tight) state in the coming years, as demand 
growth picks up. A tightening or loosening of the balance going forward will in our view 
be largely driven by the mismatched timing of US supply and demand growth (Exhibit 
15, Appendix), creating a “push and pull” on prices, likely in a $2.50-$5.00/mmBtu 
range. 
We still see 2027 oversupplied, driven by associated gas production growth from the 
Permian basin, as incremental gas takeaway infrastructure is added. We hence maintain 
our $2.75/mmBtu Summer 2027 Henry Hub forecast. 
However, we see higher power and LNG export demand for gas bringing forward our 
expected tightening of the US gas balance. Accordingly, we have raised our 2028/29/30 
Henry Hub forecasts to $4.00/$4.50/$4.50/mmBtu from $3.05/$3.80/$3.70, well 
above current forwards near $3.60.
