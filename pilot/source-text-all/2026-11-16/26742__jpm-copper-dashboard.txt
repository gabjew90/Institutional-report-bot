Copper prices have remained range-bound for the past three months, trading 
between ~$14,000-14,800/t ($6.35-6.70/lb). The ongoing threat of US tariffs 
lingers, but market sentiment has tilted towards ‘cautious uncertainty’ as 
highlighted by Greg’s LME week feedback (here). Meanwhile, the faltering AI 
trade and fears around deceleration of AI capex, have not been reflected in global/
US PMI readings which remain strong at 52.6/54.9. The US inventory pull has 
continued since our last update, leading to physical tightness in the RoW, 
compounded by weak mine production (particularly from Chile), and evident 
through the spike in China cathode premia. Nevertheless, the cautious tone has 
seen LME speculative positioning fall materially, and forward pricing below spot 
through next year. Key takeaways: (1) Global visible copper inventories have 
ticked higher to 1.1Mt from 975kt in mid-August, as US located stocks continue 
to move higher; ~90% of the inventory is now within the US. SHFE + China 
bonded inventories remain tight, down to 60kt vs +400kt in late March. (2) Global 
copper production is down 1.3% YTD through August (Chile mined production 
down 15% YoY in September). However, global apparent demand is also down 
~2% YTD. (3) High-frequency indicators are mixed – China cathode premia are 
up sharply, TC/RCs remain deeply negative, while LME canceled have fallen since 
August. We are OW on CSC/BHP/RIO/GLC/SFR in Australia. FCX is the key OW 
in the US. In EMEA, we have OW Antofagasta and UW Lundin Mining. 
We recently launched our inaugural Global Copper Supply Tracker. The 
September quarter was soft, with output down 1.3% or 43kt and around 180kt 
below the June 2025 quarter high, but we expect an aggressive ~150kt rebound into 
the December 2026 quarter as Chile recovers from recent rain impacts. The listed-
company dataset covers 25 companies, more than 100 mines and 27 countries, 
representing about 54% of world copper supply, and points to sequential quarterly 
gains through 2027 and into 2028. We forecast covered output growth of 987kt in 
2027 and another 1,089kt in 2028 after a 261kt decline in 2026, with Grasberg and 
Collahuasi the largest 2027 contributors and Cobre Panama driving 2028 growth 
assuming a mid-2027 restart. At the global level, Greg Shearer’s supply/demand 
model shows mine growth of 1.43Mt in 2027 and 1.57Mt in 2028, with a modest 
surplus over the coming years and copper price risk linked to tariff outcomes.
Greg Shearer, Head of J.P. Morgan’s precious and base metal strategy, notes that 
the copper market is shaped by a contrast between acute physical tightness in 
China and weaker investor sentiment amid uncertainty over US data-centre 
investment and copper tariffs. Smelter maintenance, constrained scrap supply and 
low Chinese social inventories have lifted premiums and cushioned prices,

Carry Trade Examples
The substantial build in US copper inventories over the past year was driven by a carry 
trade, whereby traders purchased physical copper, shipped it to the US and sold 
COMEX futures typically 3-12 months forward, locking in a premium that more than 
offset financing, storage and freight costs. With the COMEX premium to LME now 
largely eliminated, the economics supporting further inventory accumulation have 
largely disappeared, meaning new inflows into COMEX warehouses should slow. 
However, inventory drawdowns are unlikely to occur immediately, as existing positions 
remain financed and hedged. Meaningful destocking would likely require offshore 
markets to offer superior returns, such as a sustained LME premium to COMEX or 
stronger physical premiums in China and Europe, incentivising metal to be withdrawn 
from US warehouses and returned to the global market.
