Strong quarterly results from a neocloud computing firm reignited

After the US close, a significant risk emerged for Asian markets.
Reports surfaced that the chief executive of the world’s leading
AI chipmaker is under investigation over alleged ties to a Chinese
intelligence operative. A separate report then emerged that
the same executive reportedly had an additional undisclosed
relationship with a company vice president. The company’s shares
fell over 8% in after-hours trading. The development adds a
layer of uncertainty heading into the Asian open, particularly for
technology-heavy markets in Taiwan, South Korea, and Japan. In
early Asia hours, the S&P e-mini futures are down 0.5% while the
Nasdaq 100 e-mini futures are down 0.9%.
Tensions in the Strait of Hormuz escalated overnight. Iran’s
IRGC Navy claimed two additional oil tankers struck mines while
attempting to transit the waterway, with both vessels reportedly
on fire. The US and Iran have been waging a battle over control
of the strait, with both sides striking islands in the waterway in
recent days. Iran’s natural gas deficit has surged to 530 million
cubic meters per day, raising the prospect of forced supply cuts
to households and industry. Separately, a major LNG customer
terminated a long-term supply contract with Qatar’s state energy
company, citing the inability to deliver amid the ongoing conflict,
the first such cancellation since the war began. A Japanese refiner
indicated it plans to boost Middle Eastern crude purchases once
the strait reopens, underscoring the pent-up demand building
across Asia.
The DJIA, the S&P500 and the Nasdaq Composite Index rose 0.5%,
1.3% and 1.6% respectively. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index
surged 5.7%. The Euro Stoxx 50 rose 1.0%. The Dollar Index climbed
0.7% to 102.90. EUR-USD fell 70 pips to around 1.1090, a new 52-
week low and the weakest level since May 2025.
US Treasury markets were closed for the Veterans Day public
holiday. The German 10Y yield fell was little changed at 3.32%. The
UK 10Y yield was unchanged at 5.22%.
Brent crude rose 1.2% to USD92.63, as the latest Hormuz mine
incidents provided a floor to prices despite the broader November
slide of approximately 17%. Gold fell 1.1% to USD3,988, weighed by
the stronger dollar and rising rate-hike expectations.
There were no major US data releases overnight.

Economic Briefing: Germany Recovery Monitor – Update
The German economy has grown quite strongly again in recent quarters, although it has been
burdened by the Iran war and the resulting sharp rise in energy prices. We regularly look at indicators
for the individual sectors of the German economy and check them for indications of whether the
German economy will continue to grow. This is supported by the fact that sentiment indicators have
recently risen further or are at least at quite high levels. In terms of "hard" figures, there has recently
been positive news from manufacturing in particular. Residential construction and retail in particular
remain weak points. However, they are unlikely to prevent the German economy from growing in the
fourth quarter as well. Please click here for more details.
India: October CPI inflation likely to exceed 6% yoy
We will get CPI inflation for October today. It is expected to climb further and could well exceed 6%
you compared to 5.5% in September. This will be driven by elevated cost pressures stemming from
high oil prices, strong domestic demand pressures, and most pertinently, an unfavourable base of
comparison from last year. Inflation slipped to just 0.3% last October due to a drop in food prices
and indirect tax cuts. More worryingly for RBI, this low base effect will persist into November and
December, which is likely to keep inflation elevated above 6.0% throughout Q4 2026.
In the October review, RBI projected inflation at 6% in Q4 2026, and for it stay firm for the next two
quarters, at 5.7% in Q1 2027 and 5.6% in Q2 2027. These projections sit at the upper end of RBI's
2-6% target range, well above the mid-point of 4%. Core inflation, which excludes food and energy, is
expected to stay sticky and even drift higher towards the upper end of the 4.5-5% range in Q4 2026
compared to 4.6% in September 2026.
The implications are that RBI has no choice but to hike again in December, by another 25bp to 5.50%.
RBI hiked for the first time in October since February 2023. It lowered rates by 125bp in 2025. The
OIS market is fully pricing in a 25bp hike in December. It is also pricing in a total hike of 88bp by
February 2027, suggesting the possibility of a 50bp hike in February 2027.
For USD-INR, the hawkish RBI tone and lower oil prices have pushed it below the 94-97 range of the
past six months. It fell 0.3% to 93.70 yesterday. A key factor for INR will be oil prices. A further slide
in oil prices could see USD-INR trade in a new range of 92-94.
