Gold Strategy: Narrowing the Call 
2027 Outlook and 2028 Rollout 
The scenarios we published in late 2025 remain unchanged through 2027 even as we roll out our 
2028 view. Importantly, however, we are narrowing our 2027 call from our mid-to-high scenario 
range to just our middle scenario for 2027 ($4,225/oz on average) and push our range call into 
2028 (middle-to-high scenario range of $4,123-4,587/oz). We maintain conviction even with gold 
on its back foot, with trend-following CTAs reaching max short this week for the first time since 
2022 amid systematic selling. There is pain out there, but anecdotally, the investor sentiment
remains positive on gold, even as higher interest rate expectations and broader volatility weigh 
on gold. Safe-haven appeal should help in the long run with strong investor appetite, and when 
combined with ongoing central bank interest, we think gold can manage gains even amid
opposing forces and uncertainty. Given where gold is currently trading, we must highlight that 
our low scenario is a floor in our view. We now favor our middle scenario in 2027 and push our 
mid-to-high scenario call into our newly published 2028 numbers. 
• 
Narrowing our call: We are tightening our 2027 price call from a mid-to-high scenario range 
to squarely our middle scenario of $4,225/oz (average), while leaving all scenarios unchanged 
since late 2025. We are also rolling out 2028 with a middle-to-high scenario range call of 
$4,123–$4,587/oz — reflecting greater confidence in the macro underpinnings in 2027 even 
as near-term headwinds persist, and accounting for a more benign environment in 2028. 
• 
Near-term macro pressure is real but manageable: Gold has retreated from a post-election 
high above $4,100/oz to well below $4,000/oz, driven by Federal Reserve expectations,
inflation (with October CPI core prices running above consensus), 10-year Treasury yields 
spiking back above 5.0%, and the dollar at its highest since April 2025. We view our low of 
~$3,757/oz (Q4 2026) and $3,661/oz (2027) as a well-supported floor. 
• 
ETP and investor demand resilient: Gold ETP inflows recovered to 211t in Q3 2026, more than 
offsetting Q2 outflows, with YTD net inflows of 225t. We forecast full-year inflows of 317t in 
2026, 209t in 2027, and 218t in 2028 — each year roughly as constructive as 2021–2023 was 
destructive. Anecdotally, our fundamental investor conversations reveal no wholesale selling 
despite CTA selling, and more stable prices below $4,000/oz should attract renewed interest. 
• 
Central bank buying re-accelerated: Central banks purchased 321t net in Q3 2026, up 42% y/y 
and pushing YTD demand 6% above last year; China (63t) and Poland (40t) led the quarter. We 
have raised our official sector demand forecasts to 870t (2026), 801t (2027), and 709t (2028) 
— well above the 2020–2025 average of ~795t/year — underpinned by de-dollarization, 
diversification, and a trend of gold repatriation from US vaults. 
• 
Consumer markets softening but structurally evolving: Elevated prices weighed on consumer 
volumes across China and India, with Chinese bar and coin demand decelerating from 211t in 
Q1 to 81t in Q3 2026. However, the 'investorization' of gold, i.e., growing access to digital and 
investment-grade products, is broadening the buyer base, offsetting consumer weakness. 
• 
Fundamentals versus Macro: Supply set to crest 3,700t in 2027–2028 and scrap (targeting 
>1,500t in 2027–2028, up from ~1,400t in 2025–2026) will push total supply a bit higher. 
However, investor demand and official sector flows remain the primary price drivers, 
alongside uncertainty, including elevated congressional/White House tensions, persistent 
tariff and trade policy risks, and geopolitical premiums.

Uncertainty, Macro, and Opposing Forces 
Uncertainty is a key driver of gold prices largely because investors view it as a store of value, 
and a means to preserve value during risky or volatile times. This inverse or lack of correlation 
with key asset classes adds to this narrative, but uncertainty remains a fickle driver, subject to 
sentiment, headline fatigue, and other factors. Looking ahead into 2027, we highlighted in our 
"Midterm to Midpoint?" note that we expect elevated uncertainty to at least get us to our 
middle scenario outlook if not higher, and we also identified the potential gold-relevant macro 
implications of a Democratic sweep. On the uncertainty side, we think that elevated 
congressional investigations at odds with the White House could introduce uncertainty. 
Notably, we are already seeing headlines pointing to this risk given the White House's response 
to potential congressional investigations. Moreover, we see the administration as left with 
using executive action to achieve policy goals, likely in areas that have already been sources of 
uncertainty (foreign policy, tariffs and trade, etc.). We have viewed Trump's net approval 
rating to be a useful proxy for uncertainty, or at least the sentiment around it (with the current 
correlation in his second term to date at -75% and declining). However, we think its efficacy 
will decline at the current net approval rating and below given the floor in a two-party system. 
In terms of potential macro implications, how gold investors view Democrats as potentially 
constraining further deficit spending matters for the debasement trade, and potentially their 
view on pressure on yields. 
This brings us to the macro headwinds gold is currently facing and how it layers into our view. 
Gold has come under meaningful pressure, retreating from a post-election high above 
$4,100/oz to below the psychological $4,000 level. The primary driver has been a reassertion 
of Federal Reserve hawkishness, itself driven by hotter-than-expected October CPI data (core 
prices rose 0.3% m/m, above consensus), reinforced expectations that the Fed is not done 
tightening, and a shifting oil outlook. Some have flagged the possibility of a 50-basis point rate 
hike in December; it seems markets are now fully pricing a December hike, with the 10-year 
Treasury yield spiking back above 5.0% and the dollar touching its highest level since April 
2025. Persistent inflation appears to be entrenching more tightening expectations in the eyes 
of gold investors, but still, we continue to view our low scenario, approximately $3,757/oz in 
Q4 2026 and $3,661/oz in 2027, as a meaningful floor, underpinned by sustained central bank 
demand, risk premiums, and investor appetite.

ETPs and Investor Appetite 
Investor demand was strong overall, with ETP flows recovering to 211t in Q3, more than 
offsetting Q2's outflows and just 6% lower y/y. The pattern strengthened over the quarter, 
and so far in Q4 holdings have mostly stabilized in November after coming off a peak in 
October. While sentiment matters and rate expectation headwinds will weigh on gold, we 
think that, as forward price expectations for 2027 and 2028 solidify and uncertainty unfolds, 
holdings can stabilize. Anecdotally, nothing about our conversations with investors would lead 
us to believe there is wholesale selling of gold in the offing. There was some hesitancy about 
layering in at high prices, but as prices are below the $4,000/oz and should eventually stabilize 
(in our view), we see inflows returning and helping to provide a tailwind to prices. Q3's inflows 
are responsible for most of the YTD increase (225t), and while the US (driven by fiscal, 
volatility, and FX concerns) and Europe (driven by inflation and fiscal concerns) led in Q3, Asia 
edges out both in YTD terms, with Chinese equity weakness and general appetite driving YTD 
flows. We suspect accumulation will continue to move east over the long run, but globally 
there is scope for ETP demand to hold on. We forecast gold ETP inflows of 317t overall in 2026, 
209t next year, and are rolling out our 2028 expectations at 218t. Each of these years is roughly 
as positive as the 2021-2023 period was negative. We also think that higher approved gold 
allocations in recent years will remain sticky, and that the universe of investors having any gold 
allocations at all will slowly increase. The ETP strength is in contrast to bar and coin demand, 
which was 19% lower y/y in Q3, and now at a four year low of 268t in Q3 according to the WGC 
— with China one of the few key markets to grow amid falling government bond yields and 
weak Chinese equity markets. Even with 1053t YTD, our full year forecast is still not far off 
from 2025 levels. However, we do think that interest will recover in 2027 and 2028, for 1685t 
and 1644t respectively, for similar reasons as ETP demand should be strong.

central banks remain in multi-year buying streaks, and we still think that de-dollarization,

purchases, especially if prices remain calm (closer to our middle scenario than our high). We 
also view the repatriation of gold holdings into respective national vaults and out of US vaults 
as not only a symptom of the times, but a sign that central banks are in it for the long haul. In 
September, the Netherlands transferred 86t out of the US to Canada and London, and France 
now has all of its gold in Paris, after repatriating 129 tons from US vaults this year. Germany 
and Turkey also repatriated gold holdings too in prior years, and countries like Russia hold gold 
domestically as well, but arguably for differing and more specific reasons. We may also 
eventually see some gold move towards Asia, with Hong Kong and Singapore (whose monetary 
authority has launched a Central Bank Gold Vaulting service) as potential locations; but 
regardless, London will remain the center of the global gold market. Overall, while repatriation 
patterns may continue, the undercurrent of interest in gold among central banks is near 
universal, and we are reminded of WGC's Central Bank survey earlier this year that indicated 
as much. Thus, we have upped our official sector demand number for 2026 to 870t, 2027 to 
801t, and roll out 2028 at 709t — mostly around but decelerating through the 2020-2025 
average of ~795t per year. This represents a healthy undercurrent for gold prices overall and 
reinforces the permission structure for investor accumulation, institutional, and retail alike.

Key Market Check: China and India 
We always try to take a closer look at both China and India given they remain the world's 
largest consumer markets. In both China and India, quarterly gold demand this year was mostly 
weaker versus last year, but India saw an acceleration from 66t in Q1 to 100t in Q3, while China 
decelerated to a similar extent — albeit its trough was in Q2. Strong prices largely squeezed 
volumes, as did the VAT burden, but Chinese demand in value terms held up. Meanwhile, in 
India, seasonal drivers, below average monsoon rainfall reducing rural appetite, and general 
affordability held volumes back. WGC data points to Chinese bar and coin demand 
decelerating from a very strong Q1 (211t) to 110t in Q2 and 81t in Q3. India meanwhile 
decelerated y/y for the most part, with just 50-63t of demand per quarter YTD. While there 
was some bargain hunting mid-summer, high prices were a heavy weight on demand, as was

Macro versus Fundamentals 
On the supply side, 2027 and 2028 should surpass our forecasted 2026 levels and crest the 
3700t level in our data both years, and we have layered in a more normalized level of scrap 
volumes in 2027 and 2028 of above 1500t, versus 2025-2026 levels of closer to 1400t, mostly 
as we expect a less volatile price environment, even with uncertainty factoring in at various 
points. Perhaps the biggest headwind to scrap supply, which should rise more with higher 
prices, is that existing holders anticipate still-higher prices. In 2027 and 2028, our favoring of 
our middle scenario prices and less wide price outcomes than we did previously helps support 
our scrap numbers. With prices still high, we do not expect jewelry demand to recover to pre-
2025 levels, even if we have slight improvements in both 2027 and 2028 in our numbers. 
Overall, the biggest drivers of prices, investor demand, and official sector demand remain the 
swing factors and more influential line items in our balance, even if they fall short of macro 
and uncertainty together. 
To that end we are staying the course on our forecasts through 2027 and newly rolling out our 
2028 view. At the same time, we are narrowing our call from our mid-to-high scenario range 
to squarely our middle scenario in 2027 ($4,225/oz on average) and pushing our range call into 
2028 (middle-to-high scenario range of $4,123-4,587/oz). We maintain this conviction even 
with gold on its back foot at the moment. There is pain out there, but anecdotally, the investor 
base remains positive, and its safe haven appeal should help in the long run with strong 
investor appetite. When coupled with ongoing central bank interest, we think gold can manage 
gains even if it is stuck between opposing forces and uncertainty.
