Global Metals & Mining: Iron Ore; cutting our 2027 forecast to 
~US$90/t, CMRG value leakage could reach US$10bn
14 November 2026 | 12:08AM AEDT
The iron ore price has continued to de-rate, recently dropping below US$90/t for the 
first time since 2024. The decline reflects a shift to more of a balanced market driven 
by lower Chinese and global steel production (down -3%/-2% YoY to end of 
September), improving seaborne supply from the large Australian and Brazilian 
miners, new supply from the Simandou mine in West Africa, and ongoing dynamics 
from China Mineral Resources Group (CMRG). We see the iron ore market remaining 
challenging over 2027 and downgrade our 61% Fe benchmark forecast to US$92/t 
(from US$96/t). We expect CMRG’s influence over price to continue getting stronger 
going forward, and for the value leakage from the major Australian iron ore 
producers to China steel mills to increase potentially up to US$10bn. 
Iron ore price outlook 
Price outlook for Nov/Dec 26: We lower our 4Q26 61% Fe forecast to US$93/t 
n
and see downside risk to US$85-90/t over the next few months due to ongoing 
weak Chinese steel production and low mills margins and profitability (weak steel 
prices and high coking coal prices), high iron ore port inventories (~160Mt) and 
expectations of strong seaborne supply into year-end. We see any seasonal 
bounce from China steel mill restocking in December leading up to Chinese new 
Year (CNY) in early February as likely being short-lived. Although our recent 
updated iron ore cost curve showing that marginal cost (90th percentile) has 
increased over 20% over the past two years to ~US$95/t, and recent trade data 
showing that high cost producers in Brazil, India and other regions are starting to 
curtail production, freight rates have started to decline with the falling oil price, 
leading to a drop in the cost curve. CMRG’s current negotiations with FMG could 
be a swing factor, as our recent discussions with iron ore traders point to 
10-15Mt of FMG’s lower-grade 56-57% Fe Super Special Fines (SSF) product 
being ring-fenced at Chinese ports (see Exhibit 35), that could come to market if 
a settlement is reached.  
2027 price outlook appears challenging: Looking into 2027, we forecast higher 
n
production from Simandou (currently shipping at ~23Mtpa, full ramp-up to 
>100Mtpa expected during 2028 - see Exhibit 23), and a further 2-3% decline in 
Chinese steel production, but only modest growth from Aus/Brazil shipments. 
We lower our 2027 forecast to US$92/t (from US$96/t) and believe there is 
downside risk to VA consensus currently at ~US$96/t. Looking at the downside

China Minerals Resource Group (CMRG) negotiations & price discounts  
China Minerals Resource Group (CMRG) negotiations: CMRG is continuing its drive 
n
to consolidate seaborne iron ore procurement, mostly with the major Australian iron 
ore miners, and shift price discovery onshore. Its centralized iron ore procurement 
now covers more than half of domestic steel mills, with CMRG acting as a 
custodian/agent and/or negotiator for volumes under long-term contracts (LTC). 
Negotiations with Australian iron ore miners are occurring yearly with CMRG pushing 
for a RMB component in price setting and discovery through Price Reporting 
Agencies PRAs such as Beijing Iron Ore Trading Centre Corporation or COREX, 
Mysteel, Argus, and Fastmarkets (Metal Bulletin), in addition to the Platts Index. 
Price discounts: Based on our assessment, we have calculated the effective 
n
discounts agreed between CMRG and the iron ore miners. BHP settled with CMRG in 
April, for a 1.8-2.2% discount/agent fee to some Indices for some products, but we 
think the effective discount is ~2.7%, Anglo settled in August for Minas Rio and 
Kumba products we think for ~2.3%, and RIO was reported to have settled in 
October for multiple years to May 2028 at a 1.75% discount for some products but 
retains marketing rights and some premiums for the flagship Pilbara Blend Fines 
(PBF), for an effective discount of ~2.1%. FMG and Hancock are negotiating now, 
noting FMG’s negotiations started back in June. FMG has stated that CMRG-related 
unsold inventory was ~4Mt at end of Sep, and FMG expects a further ~4-5Mt build in 
October, and if negotiations drag out that they may have to curtail production (we 
think the higher cost Cloud Break mine is most likely). Our discussions with traders 
suggest that CMRG is targeting around a 3% discount on the lower grade SSF and 
Fortune Fines products in addition to the current ~20% Value-in-Use (ViU) 
discounts, and before any other rebates and adjustments.  
Estimating CMRG value leakage from Australia to China: For the listed miners 
n
(BHP, RIO, AAL, FMG & MIN), our analysis suggests the agreements could cost 
shareholders ~US$1-1.1bn pa in EBITDA, or US$8-9bn over the next decade based 
on US$85-90/t 61% Fe IODEX.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
