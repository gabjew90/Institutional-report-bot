Global rates strategy: hawkish realities 
USD rates markets remain the principal pace setter for the global duration outlook, 
caught between opposing cross-currents over the past two weeks, with these 
forces operating on distinctly different time horizons.  
On the medium-term horizon, dovish forces are gaining traction, driven by 
questions around cycle sustainability stemming from anticipated tighter oversight 
of AI development and data centre construction, compounded by a sharp energy 
price decline on expectations of an accelerated Iran war resolution. This materially 
reduces the probability of the belly repricing significantly higher from current 
expansionary short-term levels, compressing real yields and positioning the belly 
for outperformance. This dynamic has reverberated across the entire G10 rates 
complex, establishing a positive duration backdrop (Figure 1). 
Fig 1. G10 2-5-10Y flies (bp)

Yet, the embarked momentum of the US economy cannot be ignored after the 
NFP and CPI prints and should keep upside pressure intact on the front end of 
the curve – which brings us to the 27bp of implied tightening for December. The 
idea of speeding up the pace of tightening as some form of insurance has been 
floated by Fed Governor Christopher Waller, arguing a 50bp hike would be 
warranted in case of an October inflation print “similar to the past two monthly 
readings – with core CPI inflation around 0.3% or even higher”, which is exactly 
what happened. Other FOMC members echoed the idea but with a faster pace of 
tightening starting in December rather than an outsized move in the next meeting 
(with the obvious caveat that most hawkish voices of the FOMC will rotate out of 
the voting members of the regional Federal Reserves).  
We remain sceptical of the possibility of a 50bp hike in December, especially if the 
stock market remains jittery, but very comfortable with the 43bp of implied 
tightening for the January 2027 meeting.  
Looking ahead, the AI trade is likely to remain the dominant driver of USD rates, 
and it should remain a short-term tailwind for inflation and real growth expectations 
through wealth effects, consumption and AI capex. True, the first two aspects can 
deteriorate significantly should the selloff in technology stocks accelerate, but the 
bar remains high with the S&P500 still up 11% YTD.  Any restrictions on data 
centre construction would mostly affect 2028 capital expenditure while the Q127 
debt ceiling debate is unlikely to lead to a resolution of the deficit once it is resolved 
despite the political gridlock.

EUR rates strategy: focus shifting 
The big picture 
We maintain our cautious long EUR duration positioning, underpinned by the 
energy price recovery. Our conviction remains that next year's core inflation 
dynamics will constrain ECB accommodation, limiting scope for meaningful 
easing despite below-trend growth prospects. Current economic resilience further 
reinforces this view, albeit the market is increasingly shifting its focus towards 
financial conditions. Conversely, front-end repricing lower remains feasible as 
energy normalises, though rising debt service burdens offset fiscal tailwinds and 
cap upside in the belly of the curve. Second-rounds effect will also be under 
scrutiny in the months to come. All in all, the front end looks well-anchored at 
prevailing levels. This backdrop continues to favour lower rate volatility and 
extended duration exposure. 
We persist in viewing the belly as a relative outperformer, supported by a steeper 
5-30Y IRS curve and a lower belly in the 2-5-10Y fly. OATs retain tightening 
potential should Middle East tensions ease, though budgetary pressures in 
Belgium and France as well as Spanish electoral dynamics temper enthusiasm 
for further compression in the front end. 
EGB spreads continue to grind tighter within this constructive setup. Short-
end rate rallies face structural headwinds given the current political and fiscal 
cross-currents. Belgian and French budget dynamics, combined with Spanish 
electoral uncertainty, limit the upside for peripheral spreads. Front-end spreads, 
however, stand to benefit from Schatz ASW normalisation and improved technical 
positioning.

It takes one 
French promises 
EUR rates and EGB spreads have consolidated near recent lows following the 
Democratic midterm electoral sweep, as markets reassess potential shifts in 
Middle East policy and their downstream effects on commodity markets. Within 
this environment, OATs outperformed most EGBs, despite idiosyncratic headlines 
not being particularly supportive last week following Fitch’s outlook cut to negative 
and little to no progress on France’s budget adoption.  
On the budget side, the LFI party led by Jean-Luc Mélenchon officially published 
its election programme, formalising the budget plans that were initially presented 
in mid-October. The party is currently polling between 15-20% for the first round 
and our economists suggest that the full implementation could deteriorate the 
annual public balance by roughly EUR50-60bn (c.1.5-2.0% of GDP) in the first 
year. This would potentially push the deficit near 8% of GDP on a structural basis 
by 2028. For comparison purposes, the proposed fiscal consolidation amounts to 
c.EUR25bn, while the RN plan pencils in c.EUR72bn (see page 7 of this 
publication for more details).

Despite rising first-round polling and improved odds for Jean-Luc Mélenchon in 
the presidential election, OAT valuations have remained unresponsive: a notable 
finding given the candidate’s market-unfriendly policy platform. This suggests two 
key dynamics: (1) OAT performance is currently driven primarily by broader beta

domestic political developments; and (2) while first-round polling has 
strengthened for Mélenchon, second-round matchup polls remain weak, limiting

correlation between Mélenchon's polling trajectory and OAT valuations.

Appendix 1: Positioning & momentum 
Here we highlight a few of our positioning and momentum indices. You can find 
them, updated daily, in this dashboard at Red Mount Analytics. 
Our open-interest positioning indicator focuses on the relationship between 
daily changes in price and open interest in order to determine the gauge in bullish 
and bearish exposure over a given period of time. 
Within this bullish/bearish framework, there are four possible positioning 
indications: 
⚫ 
Outright longs: price and OI increases (bullish exposure) 
⚫ 
Outright shorts: price decreases, OI increases (bearish exposure) 
⚫ 
Reducing longs: price and OI decreases (bearish exposure) 
⚫ 
Reducing shorts: price increases, OI decreases (bullish exposure) 
To get the magnitude of the positioning indications, we use the correlation of the 
daily changes of open interest and futures prices. More precisely, we use the 
weighted average price/open interest of the two future contracts being traded to 
limit the impact from futures contracts rolling over. The correlation has the 
advantage of keeping the index scaled between -1 and +1.
