PPI Inflation Moderated in October 2026, 
Up 6.5% Versus One Year Ago 
Headlines 
 
Final demand producer prices rose 0.3% for the month in October 2026. 
 
 
Goods PPI decelerated to 0.5% in October 2026. 
 
 
Services PPI stabilized in October 2026 at a 0.2% monthly gain. 
 
 
Energy PPI posted a modest monthly decline at -0.1%. 
Details
The Final Demand Producer Price Index (PPI) gained 
0.3% in October 2026 versus September. On a year-
over-year basis, this result translates to a 6.5% rise in 
prices received by producers (see Figure 1), up 
marginally from September’s 6.3% pace. Producers’ 
prices in both the Goods and Services categories 
settled toward 2026 trends after spiking in 
September. Energy PPI was down for the month in 
October 2026. Some further easing in Energy PPI can 
be expected in the months ahead as oil prices have 
backed down from the $100 per barrel range. 
 
Goods PPI for October 2026 was up by 0.5% versus 
the month prior. Although producers’ Goods prices 
are now 10.2% higher than one year ago (see Figure 
2), October’s 0.5% gain is below the 0.8% monthly 
average endured from January through August, 
before the sharp spike in September. With the Fed 
now in full monetary tightening mode—responding 
to persistent consumer price inflation with stronger-
than-expected job creation providing cover—
higher interest rates bring the potential to slow 
consumer spending on big-ticket purchases that 
households would normally finance. Whether this 
development calms overall consumer spending or 
simply shifts it more heavily toward services remains 
to be seen. 
 
Services PPI saw a similar downshift in October 
2026, coming in at a 0.2% gain for the month. This 
compares with a 0.4% average monthly growth 
from January through August. Gains in service 
providers’ prices now sit at a 5.0% plateau with 
September and October gains matching one 
another. This is the strongest sustained year-over-
year inflation rate experienced by Services PPI since 
early 2025 (January 2025 = 5.4%; February 2025 = 
5.9%). 
 
As previously noted, shifting consumer spending 
trends face the complication of rising borrowing 
costs in the way of interest rates. Q3 2026 personal 
consumption expenditures, per the Bureau of 
Economic Analysis’ National Accounts report (GDP), 
saw the Services and Nondurable Goods categories 
post a second consecutive quarter of acceleration 
while Durable Goods backed down from a growth 
spike in the second quarter. Healthy job creation 
and the upcoming holiday spending season are 
likely to keep households’ wallets open. The resulting 
demand pressure on services and less expensive but

Energy PPI declined slightly in October 2026, falling 
by 0.1% for the month. Despite this respite after 
September’s 10.0% monthly jump, year-ago Energy 
PPI is still up by 36.8%. The follow-on effects of higher 
producer energy costs were also evident in the 
October PPI report’s Transportation & Warehousing 
category. Final demand for Transportation & 
Warehousing Services was up another 2.0% versus 
September alone and continues the energy cost 
pass-through that has been in place since March of 
this year. The domino effect of higher energy costs 
and the resulting higher transportation costs 
threatens to push price tags on retail shelves higher 
in the months ahead. 
 
The October PPI report supports the case for 
additional monetary policy tightening from the Fed. 
PNC projects two more 25 basis point Fed Funds rate 
hikes through early 2027. The continued elevated 
year-ago pace of producers’ cost pressures in 
October provides data that supports future Fed rate 
hike moves. Higher prices may also contribute to 
slower growth in consumer demand, reducing some 
of the inflationary pressure that the Fed is 
attempting to address with higher interest rates.
