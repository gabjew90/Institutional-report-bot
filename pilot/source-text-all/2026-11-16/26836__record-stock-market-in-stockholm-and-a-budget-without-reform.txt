Record stock market in Stockholm and a budget without reforms
Global key stories
Risk appetite returned yesterday after CoreWeave’s report eased concerns about AI investment following
this week’s slump in Nvidia. Nasdaq rose 1.6% and the S&P 500 gained 1.3%. Futures, however, point to a
lower open today. The Stockholm stock exchange rose for a third straight day, up 1.0% yesterday to a new
record high. It is now up 17% since the start of the year, 7 percentage points more than in an average year.
Oil rose to around USD 95 per barrel yesterday before falling back overnight, and gold, which now trades
more like a risk asset, fell below USD 4,000 per ounce.
The AI pendulum swung back. CoreWeave, which rents out computing power for AI, raised its full-year
guidance and expects to reach adjusted profitability in the first half of 2028, easing concerns about the
company’s debt. At the same time, Anthropic is reported to be considering a higher valuation for its IPO
after record demand. This also calmed a volatile market that has become an important macro issue
because of its financial and economic impact.
Prisoner swap but no breakthrough in Abu Dhabi. Two days of talks have brought no breakthrough on
the big issues of territory, security guarantees and a ceasefire. The parties did agree in principle on a
larger prisoner exchange, whose scope is to be finalised today. The ball is now in Moscow’s court, where
US envoys Witkoff and Kushner travel today to meet Putin.
Strong UK GDP expected for the third quarter. Consensus expects 0.7% q/q, which would show that the
economy has so far weathered the energy shock and that underlying growth is stronger than expected. A
significant part of this year’s growth is estimated to come from AI, but mainly from building and developing
the technology rather than from its spread through the economy. Whether it brings broader effects
remains to be seen. A strong outcome would be in line with our relatively optimistic forecast of 1.4% for
2026. That is needed to ease pressure on public finances ahead of 2027, a much tougher year in which the
economy turns down and major budget issues must be settled.
General strike in Spain ahead of the snap election. According to the unions, millions of workers walked
out yesterday in the country’s first general strike in 14 years, with the housing crisis as the main issue. The
same issue triggered the snap election on 29 November. The Socialists trail in the polls, and the People’s
Party together with the right-wing populist Vox could win a majority of their own. The economy is strong,
however, and we do not expect the election to affect growth significantly.
Norwegian oil investment at 08:00. In the previous survey, plans for 2027 were lifted sharply to NOK
227bn, mainly after three new development plans were submitted, but the level is still just over 17%
below this year’s. The peak appears to have passed, and investment is expected to decline next year as
few new projects are in the pipeline.

Global rates pull Swedish rates higher. The Swedish 2-year yield has risen 14bp over the past two days,
driven mainly by the repricing towards a hawkish Fed but also by the Riksbank minutes. The scope for a
