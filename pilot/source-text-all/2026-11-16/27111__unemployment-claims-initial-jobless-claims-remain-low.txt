Initial Jobless Claims Remain Low 
Headlines 
 
Initial jobless claims remained at or below 200,000 for the ninth week in a 
row. 
 
 
Continuing claims fell by 20,000 to 1.697 million in the week ending 
October 31 and continue to decline from a year ago.  
 
 
The insured unemployment rate was unchanged at 1.1% in the week 
ending October 31 for the tenth straight week. 
 
 
The percent of the unemployed pool who have been unemployed for 27 
weeks or longer rose to 27.6% in October – the highest since December 
2021. 
 
 
The combination of a strong increase in nonfarm payrolls in October and 
a hotter-than-expected CPI print last month all but ensures the Federal 
Open Market Committee (FOMC) will raise the fed funds rate by 25-basis-
points in December.  
Details
First-time jobless claims remained at or below 
200,000 for the ninth consecutive week in the week 
ending November 7 (Figure 1). The print of 200,000 
was close to PNC’s forecast of 195,000 and down 
from 191,000 in the prior week. Initial claims are 
down 28,000 from the same week one year earlier.  
 
The trend of low unemployment insurance claims 
indicates that layoffs remain limited, and employers 
continue to retain workers. 
 
The four-week moving average of initial claims in 
the week ending November 7 was unchanged and 
has held below 200,000 for six weeks in a row. The 
four-week moving average is down 31,750 from the 
same week in 2025. 
 
Continuing claims, or the number of people who 
continue to receive jobless benefits after filing an 
initial claim, fell by 20,000 to 1.697 million in the week 
ending October 31 (Figure 2). Continuing claims are 
down 249,000 from the same week a year ago.  
 
The four-week moving average of continuing claims 
for the week ending October 31 was unchanged but 
is 12.5% below the level in the same week last year.
