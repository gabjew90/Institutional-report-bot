2027 INVESTMENT THEMES
2027 GLOBAL ECONOMIC SCENARIOS
High and volatile inflation. Geopolitical tensions, trade 
frictions, and AI-related supply pressures continue to fuel high 
and volatile inflation, making it a hallmark of the current macro 
regime. Our base case for the conflict in the Middle East is a 
"prolonged quagmire" with crude oil at $90-120 per barrel. 
Meanwhile, executive actions may lift average U.S. tariff rates 
from about 10% toward 15%. With global inflation already 
elevated, central banks see these developments as flashing amber 
for inflation expectations.
The new globalization. For now, we see the AI buildout as a 
global demand story rather than a productivity story. Hyperscaler 
capex could add up to one percentage point to 2026 U.S. real 
GDP growth and is consistent with our base case for the U.S. 
economy. U.S. demand is outstripping domestic production, so 
imports are filling the gap. This creates a new flavor of 
globalization, in which suppliers such as Germany, the UK, 
Taiwan, and South Korea gain from requisite exports. Meanwhile, 
an AI productivity boost—the potential “Goldilocks” outcome 
with vast economic ramifications—has yet to materialize.
Unchecked fiscal pressures. Sovereign issuance is competing 
with heavy corporate AI-related issuance for a finite pool of global 
savings. Yet fiscal consolidation remains limited amid higher debt-
servicing costs as well as rising defense and infrastructure needs. 
France is Europe's weakest link, with spreads over Bunds above 
those for Italy and Greece. While U.S. deficits (6-8% of GDP) 
exceed France's (5-6%), solid U.S. growth is supporting Treasury 
absorption. That said, an economic slowdown, higher post-
midterm spending, or a debt-ceiling standoff could trigger another 
repricing. Markets will increasingly favor stronger fiscal positions.
Central banks and tighter policy. The key takeaway from our 
macro backdrop is one of a converging hiking cycle across major 
global central banks. China is an exception as it continues to battle 
the bursting of its property bubble and associated weak domestic 
demand. China aside, we see the current rate hiking cycle as being 
relatively moderate, reflecting the comparatively smaller energy 
shock as well as the capex boom. Inflation and policy rate risks are 
tilted to the upside, opening the possibility that central banks could 
hike until something breaks.

INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS BY ASSET CLASS
PUBLIC FIXED INCOM
YIELDS ARE BACK TO “NORMA
PRIORITIZE SELECTIVE CARRY
• Yields are again a meaningful source of total return, even if rates have not peaked.
• Favor short-to-intermediate fixed income maturities, short-maturity high yield, and high-quality 
securitized credit, including AAA CLOs.
• Expanding credit-spread dispersion from historically tight levels emphasizes the value of active 
security selection.
PUBLIC EQUITIES
FOLLOW THE BOTTLENECKS
IN THE AI BUILDOUT.
• AI demand remains durable, but leadership can shift as the buildout evolves.
• Focus on semiconductors, Asian supply chains, memory, cloud, power equipment, grid technology, 
and select industrials.
• As monetization expands, distinguish operating fundamentals from market sentiment.
PRIVATE CREDIT
STRUCTURAL EXPANSION,
WITH DISCIPLINE.
• ABF, infrastructure debt, middle-market lending, and secondaries are expanding the opportunity set.
• Illiquidity and complexity premia remain attractive in several sub-markets relative to public credit.
• Rising dispersion and hyperscaler financing needs make underwriting and manager selection critical.
PRIVATE REAL ESTATE
USE THE EARLY-CYCLE 
EXTENSION.
• Higher rates are extending the early phase of the real estate recovery, while tight vacancies and 
limited supply continue to support income growth.
• The next six to 12 months may offer compelling entry points for credit, equity, and refinancing 
approaches.
• Favor income-driven credit, data centers, defensive retail, and long-horizon value creation.
