Five Investment Themes for 2027
As we look forward to 2027, we believe investors should be more 
discerning and shift their focus from broad AI-related exposure toward 
asset classes, sectors and companies that stand to benefit most from 
this transformational technology. AI is becoming embedded within our 
economic infrastructure, and we believe the investment case is moving 
from buildout to proof of return—from funding chips and data centers to 
demonstrating adoption, productivity gains and returns on capital. At the 
same time, capital is getting more expensive and issuance is increasing. 
Together, these shifts should increase dispersion across markets, 
making selectivity and agility essential across the whole portfolio.
We think the following five themes will shape markets in 2027:
1. The Golden Age of Productivity—AI will drive productivity gains 
across economies
2. Higher Capital Costs—Capital is getting more expensive and the 
need for it remains high
3. Earnings Durability—Not all companies will thrive amid 
tech disruption 
4. Resilient Infrastructure—The AI buildout may stress the global 
economy’s infrastructure 
5. Faster Disruption, Faster Markets—Investors will need agility 
and disciplined diversification as companies transform and 
markets reprice

The Golden Age of Productivity
AI will drive productivity gains across economies
The AI buildout played out as the dominant trade of 2026: buying chips and 
building data centers lifted earnings, multiples and portfolios together. 
AI has now been validated by companies, policymakers, regulators and 
central banks as a lasting economic force—not a narrow technology theme. 
Our view is fundamentally optimistic: AI should amplify human capability, 
support growth and accelerate innovation, rather than merely displace labor. 
As with any transformational technology, there are risks that need to be 
considered, both in the fundamental technology itself and its impact within 
the financial markets.
We believe 2027 will bring an acceleration of the transition from capital 
spending to utilization and measurable productivity. As that shift takes 
hold, AI’s long-term impact should become increasingly disinflationary. 
Experimentation is giving way to implementation, implementation is 
revealing efficiencies, and those efficiencies are beginning to translate into 
productivity gains.
AI’s staying power doesn’t guarantee that every company in the ecosystem 
will succeed. In 2027, we expect the broad market to recognize what rigorous 
analysis shows: some companies turn investment into durable growth, others 
spend to keep pace, and distinguishing between the two can help drive 
investor outcomes.
Risks to Watch
• Revenue generation fails to keep pace with capital spending.
• Weakening hyperscaler earnings prompt a broader repricing of the 
AI ecosystem.
• Power, capital, resource or political constraints slow the buildout and 
delay broad adoption.
Investment Opportunities 
• Public equities: Consider companies that show tangible returns on AI capex, 
preserve margins and grow earnings outside AI hardware (i.e., the “adopters” 
more than the “enablers”). 
• Public fixed income: Durable cash-flow coverage and resilient capital 
structures should separate quality borrowers from overleveraged ones.
• Private markets: Well-run private equity firms are expected to provide portfolio 
companies with AI resources and expertise to create genuine value, with the 
biggest impact, in our view, in the middle market.

Higher Capital Costs
Capital is getting more expensive and the need for it 
remains high 
Heading into the new year, the central risk, in our view, is not simply another 
50- to 75-basis-point increase in rates—we believe the economy is strong 
enough to withstand a few rate hikes. More worrisome is the enormous 
debt burden on the horizon. Along with a massive AI infrastructure buildout, 
governments around the world continue to run large deficits. Headwinds 
may emerge as borrowers refinance and tighter financial conditions affect 
investment, hiring and demand. Stronger corporate and household balance 
sheets may mitigate this effect, but they cannot shield every borrower or 
asset from higher financing costs.
The consequences will diverge across economies. The Fed has an inflation 
challenge, while the ECB has a growth problem, compounded by Europe’s 
lower AI investment relative to the U.S. Longer-term pressures are mounting 
as well: Aging populations, low birth rates and rising debt-service costs 
are placing additional strain on welfare states, particularly those where 
productivity growth and fiscal flexibility are limited.
Risks to Watch
• Inflation could re-accelerate and force central banks to hike more than the 
markets expect.
• The long end of the yield curve becomes unanchored as demand continues to 
surge and central bank confidence wanes. 
• Tightening in Europe breaks something; funding costs continue to strain social 
safety nets in France or the U.K.
Investment Opportunities 
• Public equities: Higher long-end rates and the start of a rate-hike cycle, even 
a modest one, are reasons for caution, particularly in industries with significant 
rate and/or energy-price sensitivity and thin margins. 
• Public fixed income: As markets debate how restrictive policy rates need to 
become, we believe it’s prudent to revisit credit positioning and add duration 
opportunistically. In rates, we favor shorter-dated bonds.
• Private markets: In private real estate, elevated interest rates may continue 
driving attractive entry valuations, while strengthening supply-demand 
fundamentals could create a favorable backdrop for commercial real estate 
investment. Within private credit, rising interest rates will likely result in increased 
challenges for weaker borrowers.

Earnings Durability
Not all companies will thrive amid tech disruption 
In 2027, we see the investment case moving from funding the AI buildout to 
proving that spending can generate durable earnings and returns on capital. 
Some formerly asset-light companies have become highly capital-intensive 
and, in certain cases, free-cash-flow negative. The essential test is whether 
incremental return on invested capital exceeds the weighted average cost 
of capital. 
That test is becoming more demanding, as earnings growth appears to be 
slowing from exceptional levels. The market continues to expect strong 
earnings, yet we see recent growth rates as unlikely to persist. As AI capex 
moves through financial statements, we expect the gap between EBITDA 
and net earnings will be felt—earnings will be pressured by growing 
depreciation expense, while continued capital spending weighs on 
free cash flow.
We expect pockets of earnings strength beyond AI hardware, particularly 
among resource providers and infrastructure, and potentially industrials and 
defense. We do not believe that slower aggregate earnings growth implies 
an earnings collapse, and we expect attractive opportunities to emerge as 
company and sector dispersion widens, and balance-sheet strength, capital 
discipline and execution are rewarded.
Risks to Watch
• Investors and companies are beginning to question the return-on-investment 
assumptions of AI-related capital spending. 
• Higher rates slow economic activity in interest-rate-sensitive sectors. 
Investment Opportunities 
• Public equities: We favor companies with defensible competitive moats and 
sturdy balance sheets spanning technology, cyclicals and health care.
• Public fixed income: In credit, we prefer issuers with reasonable leverage, 
durable business models and experienced management teams. Higher interest 
rates are expected to pose a challenge, but we believe financial and certain 
industrial companies are well positioned to navigate this market.
• Private markets: We prefer private equity firms that seek to generate returns 
by growing earnings through strategic and operational improvements, not 
through financial engineering. This approach can mitigate valuation risk. Within 
private credit, we believe investors should focus on managers who prioritize 
capital preservation over capital deployment.

Resilient Infrastructure
The AI buildout may stress the global 
economy’s infrastructure 
Infrastructure resilience has moved from a defensive concern to a sustained 
capital-formation priority. The need to expand and modernize physical and 
digital capacity—from power generation and grids to chip fabrication, data 
centers and networks—should create a multiyear investment opportunity. It 
also could establish an inflationary floor under resources, power and critical 
materials as demand runs ahead of supply.
We do not believe this is a case for broad regional exposure: selectivity should 
favor providers with cost advantages, strategic positioning and the ability 
to deliver scarce capacity. Lower-cost resource providers in the emerging 
economies of the Americas and strategically important digital suppliers in 
Asia are positioned as sellers, while the U.S. and Europe remain large buyers of 
resources, hardware and manufacturing capability. 
Politics can interrupt even powerful demand trends. Social and political 
resistance may delay data center construction and other infrastructure 
projects, increase costs or redirect capital. The need for strategic capacity is 
rising, but the ability to build and protect it will vary considerably by jurisdiction. 
Risks to Watch
• The increased probability of a consequential cyber disruption to financial, power, 
water, transportation or digital infrastructure.
• Political backlash that slows data center approvals faster than demand adjusts.
Investment Opportunities 
• Public equities: Consider positions in infrastructure, industrials and defense that 
offer earnings strength tied to resilience spending. Data center exposure now carries 
political risk that sits outside fundamentals.
• Public fixed income: High levels of hyperscaler issuance can provide value and 
yield pick-up along the yield curve. Select AI opportunities are expected to be 
available across credit asset classes, but close attention to fundamentals will be 
critical given likely dispersion in borrower quality.
• Private markets: The flexible and long-term nature of private infrastructure is well 
suited to the AI buildout and gives investors access to investments not available 
in the public markets, but manager and investment selection will continue to be 
paramount as we expect pockets of over-exuberance.

Faster Disruption, Faster Markets
Investors will need agility and disciplined diversification 
as companies transform and markets reprice 
The AI investment theme to date has made portfolio construction look 
relatively simple. For the first nine months of 2026, results hinged on owning a 
narrow set of semiconductor and memory names, and before that, only seven 
to 10 mega-cap tech stocks drove the market. We expect that concentration to 
unwind in 2027, with meaningful intra-sector and intra-industry rotation, volatile 
factor swings, and high-velocity changes in correlation. 
Navigating this environment requires understanding the biases and bets 
embedded in underlying portfolios. Nimbleness will be a necessity: in equities, 
rotation and dispersion are accelerating, and in fixed income, higher yields 
could introduce a cushion against volatility that should reinforce the benefits of 
old-school diversification.
In private markets, large backlogs and constrained distribution are expected 
to continue to create opportunities for liquidity providers. More than 30,000 
private-equity-owned companies are reportedly waiting to exit, against a little 
over a year of dry powder in the secondary market, and vintages four years and 
older make up roughly 70% of U.S. private equity net asset value. We believe 
investors should expand their toolkits beyond secondaries into co-investments, 
minority equity investments and hybrid capital, and favor middle-market value 
creation over mega-buyouts dependent on IPO exits.
Risks to Watch
• Correlations stay high and concentrated AI leadership persists longer than 
expected. Markets lack dispersion.
• Private-market underwriting proves too optimistic as valuations recalibrate, 
exits and distributions remain slow, and refinancing assumptions prove unrealistic.
Investment Opportunities 
• Public equities: Selectivity across portfolios can offer a defense against 
benchmark concentration and rapid, sometimes painful, rotation.
• Public fixed income: Higher yields can provide greater cushion against volatility, 
reinforcing bonds’ traditional role as diversifiers. 
• Private markets: Many of the companies leading in AI innovation can only be 
accessed through private markets, especially as companies stay private for longer 
periods. Market volatility has resulted in a lack of realization in private equity 
portfolios creating opportunities in secondaries, co-investments, hybrid capital 
and minority equity providers driven by investors demanding liquidity.
• Commodities and alternatives: Commodities, hedged strategies and other 
public- and private-market diversifiers may help in periods of higher volatility.
