Coming out of the 3Q earnings cycle, Business & Information Services fundamentals 
remained resilient, but the outlook became more varied as demand momentum, 
investment requirements and visibility into 2027 diverged across companies. Info 
Services organic revenue growth slowed to 6.8% y/y in CY3Q from 8.8% in CY2Q, 
partly offset by Business Services growth improving modestly to 3.5% from 3.2%, 
with recurring revenue streams and pricing power providing a source of durability. AI 
outcomes diverged in the quarter, as proprietary data and embedded workflows

Companies generally outperformed consensus in 3Q 
 
 
Companies generally outperformed consensus on margins and EPS, while revenue 
results were more mixed in 3Q. Despite macro uncertainty, uneven transaction activity 
and softer discretionary demand, Business & Info Services companies delivered resilient 
earnings relative to expectations, supported by recurring revenue, pricing, favorable mix 
and expense discipline. Across our 29-company coverage, excluding VSTS, which has yet 
to report, 17 companies surpassed consensus on revenue, 21 outperformed on EBITDA 
margins and 22 beat on EPS. Revenue missed consensus by 0.5% on average, though 
results were broadly in line excluding CLVT, where the completed LS&H divestiture 
affected comparability with consensus, while EBITDA margins exceeded consensus by 
0.7% and EPS beat by 3.6%. The largest revenue upside reflected improving staffing 
trends at KFY, installation mix and pricing at ADT, stronger Conferences and Consulting 
performance at IT and durable subscription or volume-led growth at MSCI, CTAS and 
ULS. In contrast, the largest revenue shortfalls reflected weaker leveraged finance 
issuance at MCO, continued AI-search pressure at PXED, hardware and 
cryptocurrency-related transaction weakness at NATL and softer digital lead generation 
at ROL. The largest margin upside reflected cost reductions and expense discipline at 
CSGP, IT and PBI, favorable revenue mix and operating leverage at FICO and productivity 
initiatives and tariff refunds at NATL, while the largest shortfalls reflected weaker 
issuance mix at MCO, higher technology and G&A investment at ADT, weaker operating 
leverage and Development volatility at BV and increased product and growth 
investment at MH and FDS.

Several key themes emerged from the 3Q earnings cycle 
 
 
Companies reported a mixture of positive and negative trends in the quarter, with many 
citing evolving AI competitive landscape concerns and ongoing uncertainty in the macro 
environment, balanced by internal initiatives to drive outperformance. Below, we 
identify a number of key themes that emerged from the 3Q earnings cycle within 
Business & Info Services. 
AI debate becoming more differentiated across Info Services. The potential 
n
impact of AI remained top-of-mind for investors in CY3Q, but results increasingly 
distinguished between AI as a monetization opportunity, a productivity lever and a 
direct threat to customer acquisition or existing workflows. Companies with 
proprietary data and embedded, decision-critical workflows generally 
demonstrated stronger positioning, with MSCI highlighting faster product 
development and expense efficiencies, VRSK discussing data licensing, pricing and 
potential MCP monetization, EFX executing against its AI productivity program and 
SPGI reporting favorable renewal pricing and rapid API usage growth. TRI also 
reported continued adoption of Gen AI-enabled products, though higher AI and 
growth investment reduced its near-term margin outlook. In contrast, AI-related 
disruption became more tangible at ROL and PXED, as LLM-driven search pressured 
residential lead generation at ROL and weakened PXED’s B2C starts, pushing its 
expected revenue recovery into F2028. Earnings reactions among other companies 
perceived as more exposed remained driven primarily by company-specific 
execution, with CLVT benefiting from improving recurring revenue, product traction 
and leading indicators, while slower Insights contract value recovery outweighed IT’s 
headline beat and guidance raise, despite the company reporting no material 
evidence of AI-related disruption. We believe proprietary data, workflow 
entrenchment, regulatory barriers and cost of error remain important moats for 
VRSK, EFX, MCO, TRU, FICO, MSCI, SPGI and TRI, while IT, CLVT and FDS remain more 
exposed to disruption across selected research, analytics and interface-driven 
functions. Within Business Services, AI risk remains more structural than immediate, 
with RHI delivering improving staffing trends despite longer-term uncertainty 
around white-collar labor demand, while HRB could face greater competition as DIY 
platforms add more sophisticated AI-enabled tax support. 
Macro sensitivity contributed to more varied outcomes. Uneven labor demand, 
n
elevated interest rates and subdued mortgage activity remained important 
differentiators during the 3Q earnings cycle, though company-specific mix and 
execution also shaped results. More defensive revenue models generally delivered 
healthy results, including VRSK, where durable subscription growth returned organic 
revenue growth to its medium-term range, and IRM, where recurring storage revenue 
and growth across data centers, ALM and digital solutions supported the multiyear 
outlook. Companies with more cyclical exposure produced a wider range of 
outcomes, with RHI benefiting from Talent Solutions returning to growth, while 
MAN’s broadening demand was offset by unfavorable mix and limited EPS 
conversion. Credit-related results diverged as EFX’s diversified growth and margin 
execution mitigated mortgage weakness, TRU faced both mortgage and margin

contributor in 2027, as well, with IRM projecting 25%+ organic growth from higher

enterprise customers. We expect RIM storage to preserve durable cash generation,

Moody’s (MCO, Buy-rated, $573 12M PT, $88bn market cap): We believe MCO will

though we expect near-term issuance to remain volatile due to higher Treasury yields,

contributing more volume while higher-yielding leveraged-finance activity is 
moderating. In Moody’s Analytics, increasing AI usage, paying-customer adoption and

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
