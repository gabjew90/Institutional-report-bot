 
This week in Business & Information Services, we published our post-3Q sector 
overview, which highlighted resilient underlying fundamentals but a widening 
dispersion in 2027 growth visibility, AI investment requirements and cyclical 
exposure. Investor feedback was most constructive on companies with proprietary 
data, embedded workflows and identifiable monetization paths. Specifically, interest 
has reemerged in IRM around data-center leasing, rising memory prices and the US 
Treasury contract ramp, and TRI’s ability to sustain high-single-digit organic growth 
despite increased AI investment remaining a key focus. Investors also continued to 
view VRSK and MSCI favorably, and debated whether AI-related financing can sustain 
ratings growth at SPGI and MCO beyond 2027. Earnings this week reinforced a more 
selective tone. HRB’s F1Q results and reiterated outlook did little to change investor 
questions on whether its consultative service and Second Look initiatives can reverse 
assisted-tax share pressure. BV’s F4Q miss similarly had investors focused on 
Development volatility despite improving Landscape Maintenance trends, and KLC’s 
weak 3Q underscored the execution required to restore enrollment and occupancy. 
At FDS’s investor day, feedback centered on whether modest growth targets and 
ambitious margin goals adequately offset increasing AI competition and its 
dependence on third-party data. Separately, investors this week remained focused 
on FICO mortgage share risk, BCO’s ability to sustain DRS/AMS growth through the 
NATL acquisition, ADT’s go-shop process, and potential regulatory hurdles for 
CTAS/UNF.

KinderCare (KLC, Neutral): Weak 3Q reflecting core enrollment pressure and limited 
turnaround visibility. KinderCare reported weak 3Q results, with revenue, EBITDA 
margins and EPS missing our estimates and consensus and the full year guide cut across 
the board following soft back-to-school enrollment. Revenue declined 1.7% y/y, 
enrollments decreased 6% y/y and same-center occupancy fell 320 bps y/y to 63.8%. 
KLC’s path to improved earnings now depends on converting higher inquiry volumes into 
sustained enrollment growth, since lower utilization continues to drive meaningful 
fixed-cost deleverage across the core ECE portfolio. Recent new enrollment trends are 
encouraging, with the y/y enrollment deficit narrowing by ~200 bps since 
mid-September, though total enrollment remains below prior-year levels and the 
company has not provided sufficient operating KPIs to establish the durability of the 
improvement. Portfolio optimization should improve the quality of the retained fleet 
through ~85 planned closures, a ~150 bps occupancy benefit and an estimated $10mn 
annualized EBITDA benefit, though $15-17mn of 2026 termination costs, $8-10mn of 
early-2027 costs and ~$10mn of dead rent will delay the cash flow payoff. Champions, 
Crème and KinderCare for Employers provide growth diversification, but these 
businesses remain insufficient to offset near-term pressure in the core center base. We 
see KLC taking necessary corrective actions, though uncertainty around the pace of 
enrollment recovery, occupancy normalization and lease resolution supports a balanced 
risk/reward profile. Link to our 11/13 note here. 
BrightView (BV, Sell): F4Q miss and Development volatility overshadow Land 
momentum. BrightView reported weak F4Q results, with revenue, EBITDA margins and 
EPS underperforming our estimates and consensus and the F2027 guide coming below 
Street expectations. Development revenue declined sharply and more than offset 
accelerating Landscape Maintenance growth, with residential weakness, delayed 
commercial projects and potential data center delays limiting our confidence in the 
timing of a recovery. The ~$800mn backlog could support flat Development revenue 
based on historical conversion, but we believe uncertain project timing and potential 
slippage into F2028 reduce visibility. In contrast, Landscape Maintenance should sustain 
3-4% growth as positive net new sales, improving seller productivity and customer 
retention of 84.5% strengthen the recurring contract book. The company characterized 
its Land outlook as somewhat conservative, though ancillary demand and the balance 
between pricing, scope and retention remain sources of variability, in our view. F2027 
margin expansion should benefit from >25% flow-through on incremental Landscape 
Maintenance revenue and continued technology, fleet and procurement efficiencies, 
though ~60 bps of the guided 110 bps improvement reflects the benefit from lapping 
the F2026 self-insurance adjustment and fuel remains a risk as BV avoids surcharges and

FactSet (FDS, Sell): Investor day outlines AI strategy with modest growth and 
ambitious margin targets. FactSet hosted an investor day in NYC where it introduced 
new medium-term targets and discussed its AI strategy, evolving commercial model and 
capital allocation priorities. Through F2029, FDS is targeting a ~6% organic revenue 
CAGR, adjusted operating margins above 36%, average FCF conversion above 90% and 
~10% annual adjusted EPS growth. Growth should benefit from deeper client 
penetration, adoption of connected data, advanced analytics and intelligent workflows, 
and greater use of enterprise agreements and consumption-based pricing. Early AI 
traction includes 10%+ of F2026 net new ASV, ~650 MCP clients and faster ASV growth 
and higher Workstation engagement among AI adopters. We view the ~6% growth 
target as modest given these initiatives and the margin target as ambitious given FDS 
has required elevated product development and compensation investment to sustain 
mid-single-digit growth, contributing to margin contraction in recent years. Identified 
gross savings of $100mn+ and internal AI productivity could support margin expansion, 
though continued investment in AI, compute, content and private markets may constrain 
conversion. We believe near-term MCP monetization may benefit from client 
experimentation, but vendor consolidation creates longer-term competitive risk for FDS 
given its reliance on third-party data that is neither exclusive nor created internally. Link 
to our 11/10 note here. 
H&R Block (HRB, Sell): F1Q strategic progress, with assisted share improvement still 
to prove. H&R Block’s F1Q represents one of its seasonally lightest quarters, with the 
company generating only ~5% of full-year revenue during the tax off-season. HRB’s 
reiterated F2027 guidance leaves its near-term earnings outlook largely unchanged, 
making the key investment debate whether it can deliver assisted volume growth and 
convert elevated strategic spending into durable earnings improvement. The expansion 
of its consultative client experience from 5 offices to ~650 could improve conversion, 
retention and tax pro productivity. At this early stage, the consultative model remains 
unproven across HRB’s broader network, and HRB has not quantified its potential 
conversion, retention, productivity or margin contribution. Second Look also offers a 
tangible retention opportunity, with participating new clients returning at a rate more 
than 600 bps higher. That said, its incrementality, unit economics and durability remain 
unclear as HRB scales its dedicated team. Constructive off-season filing trends and 
continued Wave subscription growth provide some support, though neither is large 
enough to materially alter the outlook for HRB’s core US assisted business. We believe 
HRB’s efforts to broaden its value proposition through a more consultative client 
experience and Second Look are unlikely over the near-term to offset continued assisted 
competitive and market share pressure in a low-growth industry, pointing to valuation 
downside risk. Link to our 11/9 note here.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
