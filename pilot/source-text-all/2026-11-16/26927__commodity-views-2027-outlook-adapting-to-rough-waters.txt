2027 Outlook: Adapting to Rough Waters  
 
 
We believe that shifts in market sentiment and in near-term Mideast escalation risks, 
rather than in physical fundamentals, have driven the November selloff in energy and 
copper prices.1 We estimate that the risk premium in Brent spot prices has dropped from 
$27 on the Friday before the midterms to only a few dollars over the last few days 
(Exhibit 1). In our view, this reduction in risk premia increases the hedging and 
diversification value of commodities by offering a more attractive entry point.  
 
Exhibit 2 presents our top commodity hedging and trading ideas for 2027.   
While Mideast diplomatic efforts are now top of mind, the recent pickup in tanker 
attacks near Hormuz and challenges regarding reaching a lasting diplomatic 
breakthrough imply downside risk to our Persian Gulf commodity supply base case, 
which we expect to rise further in the coming quarters. As a result and given winter 
weather risks, we recommend targeted energy hedges against geopolitical escalation 
with asymmetric upside: (1) long European summer 2027 gasoline futures, and (2) call 
spreads on TTF European natural gas for 2027Q1 (Exhibit 2).  
Our top trading recommendations express our highest conviction structural views, 
where we see stronger commodity demand meeting persistent supply constraints, like 
with gold and copper. While the recent AI-related equity volatility has reduced copper 
investor length, we expect positioning to at least partly recover as the US midterms 
results are more likely to delay rather than derail US data center capacity growth.

Base Case: Adapting to Rough Waters, But at a Cost  
Energy prices in 2026 have run well above prewar expectations. But given how long the 
Iran conflict has actually lasted, they have risen less than we and consensus expected 
when it began. The main reason for this surprise is a remarkable adaptation of both 
commodity demand and supply. 
On the demand side, energy destocking and fuel switching, especially in China, helped 
the global economy come through the period of tightest supply, March-May, relatively 
intact. 
Supply adaptation then gathered pace, with Persian Gulf exports of crude oil recovering 
to near 2025 levels in September, and the global oil market likely returned to a surplus in 
October. We expect continued adaptation through ship-to-ship transfers and pipeline 
additions to lift Mideast oil output further, to an average of 1.1mb/d above 2025 levels 
in 2027. 
Supply has adapted less well for other commodities than crude oil (Exhibit 3), for two 
reasons: 
Other fuels such as refined oil products and LNG are more flammable than crude oil 
n
and thus harder to transfer from ship to ship; 
Production capacity isn’t fully repaired from strikes for oil refining, aluminium 
n
smelting, and natural gas liquefaction.  
While Persian Gulf flows of refined oil products, LNG, and aluminum feedstock gradually 
rise further in our base case, this path can be volatile depending on shipping safety and 
the state of energy infrastructure (Exhibit 3).  
 
This supply adaptation has also come at a cost.

Tactical Case for Commodity Diversification 
Tactically, three factors favor long commodities positions as a portfolio hedge: upside 
risk to energy prices, the high sensitivity of bond and equity prices to energy prices, and 
upside risks to agricultural prices. 
Factor 1: Upside Risk to Energy Prices 
Our base case is that Mideast energy supply keeps adapting, and risk premia have fallen 
sharply this month. Even so, we remain concerned that renewed geopolitical 
escalation—such as stepped-up shipping attacks or damage to energy infrastructure—
and, for European natural gas, a cold winter, could push energy prices significantly 
higher: 
Brent might rally over $130 in early 2027 and average around $120 in 2027 (red line, 
n
Exhibit 5) if 2027 average Gulf output remains 2.5mb/d below its 2025 average level. 
Refined product prices would climb further in this scenario, with US wholesale diesel 
prices reaching new highs of $230 around year-end if renewed escalation were to 
reduce refining output by an extra 3mb/d (Exhibit 19).  
TTF might rally near 150 EUR/MWh in 2027Q1 if Persian Gulf LNG exports average 
n
25-30% of pre-war levels this winter and Europe’s winter turns out 1 std. dev. colder 
(0.7°C) than average (Exhibit 6). If Gulf LNG exports stall at around 35% of pre-war 
levels through end-2027, we estimate that TTF prices would average 104 EUR/MWh 
in 2027 (grey line, Exhibit 20).

Factor 2: High Sensitivity of Bond and Equity Prices to Energy Prices 
Since the Iran war began, equity prices—and especially bond prices—have become 
unusually sensitive to energy prices. 
Since the war began, daily changes in Brent prices have generally shown strong 
correlations with changes in both the S&P 500 and 10-year US Treasury yields (Exhibit 
7).3 Without this year’s rise in energy prices, yields would likely have risen less and 
equities gained more, as strong earnings growth has outweighed lower valuation 
multiples. These correlations have recently softened as macro markets have shifted 
focus to downside risks to AI spending and GDP growth, but would likely strengthen 
again in a renewed energy supply shock. 
3
This increased sensitivity of asset prices to energy prices likely reflects not only the size and uncertainty of the 
energy supply shock but also resilient global GDP growth and five years of above-target inflation, which have 
prompted central banks to respond strongly to the inflationary impact of higher energy prices.

Targeted Energy Hedges 
We favor targeted energy hedges against potential renewed conflict escalation and 
supply disruptions with asymmetric upside:  
Long summer 2027 European gasoline. We still recommend long summer European 
n
gasoline (EBOB Jun27) for two reasons. First, refiners switching output from gasoline 
to diesel tightens gasoline markets (Exhibit 12). Second, relatively low gasoline 
margins leave room for sharp price upside if the Mideast and Russia-Ukraine 
conflicts continue to constrain refining output or if more energy infrastructure were 
to be damaged. The recent selloff in gasoline offers an attractive entry point for 
EBOB gasoline Jun27 futures, with the total expected return of 20% (if held until 
expiration) in our base-case and a 62% return in the case of renewed geopolitical 
escalation (Exhibit 20, right).4 
Call spreads on 2027Q1 European natural gas. In addition to facing winter supply 
n
risk, European natural gas markets face weather risk, as colder-than-average winter 
temperatures can send heating demand sharply higher. Given currently low 
European inventories, should these tightening risks combine, we would see sharp 
upside to TTF prices, especially following the recent risk premium liquidation. Hence, 
with the outlook for European winter weather still highly uncertain, we favor hedging 
a tight European winter via 2027Q1 TTF call spreads.

Structural Case for Commodity Diversification in US-China Power Race  
Reason 1: Insurance Against Structurally Heightened Supply Disruption Risk 
Commodities can insure portfolios against negative supply shocks, which tend to raise 
inflation, slow GDP growth, and weigh on equity and bond prices. Because the source 
and timing of disruptions are inherently unpredictable, a broad commodity basket 
(excluding precious metals) offers the most robust protection. 
We think that commodities’ insurance value has increased because geopolitical 
fragmentation and high commodity supply concentration have structurally raised 
commodity disruption risk (Exhibit 13). The 2022 Russian natural gas shock, last year’s 
Chinese rare earths shock and this year’s Hormuz cross-commodity shock are symptoms 
of the same shift: geopolitical fragmentation is increasingly colliding with concentrated 
commodity supply chains. With few countries controlling critical supply and greater 
willingness/incentives to use that control as geopolitical leverage, the risk of disruption 
has structurally increased. 
 
Reason 2: Upward Price Pressure in Commodities Where Rising Demand Meets Supply 
Constraints 
Commodities for which rising demand meets persistent supply constraints face 
structural upward price pressure, especially copper and gold. 
Copper. We expect the LME copper price to rise further from just under $10,000/t last 
year to $15,600/t by Dec27, +13% from spot. Solid demand growth related to 
electrification, energy security, quasi-strategic stockpiling, data centers and AI supports 
the long-term copper price trend, while mine supply is constrained. 
Over the past year, US and China policies aimed at securing domestic supply chains have 
intensified US-China competition for refined copper, sharply reduced ex-US copper 
stocks to seasonal lows (Exhibit 14, left), and added to price pressure beyond the 
underlying structural supply constraints.

In the US, uncertainty over potential import tariffs has driven copper imports into and 
stockpiling into the US, diverting refined metal away from the ex-US market where LME 
refined copper prices are set. In China, tighter scrap-invoicing rules are constraining 
domestic scrap supply, pushing semi-fabricators, which make rods, wires and tubes from 
either copper cathode or scrap, to rely more heavily on refined copper. 
We expect this US tariff ambiguity (which allows the US to build up large copper 
inventories without imposing tariffs and without major government spending on copper) 
and China scrap-to-cathode substitution to extend well into next year, leaving the ex-US 
market in deficit and raising prices again in 2027. That said, we see near-term downside 
risk to our price forecast from a potential reduction in US tariff ambiguity and from a 
lackluster recovery in AI-related investor length. 
Aluminium, by contrast, does not face the same structural supply constraints as copper. 
Bauxite, the key input, is relatively abundant, and high aluminium smelter margins are 
incentivizing supply, particularly through a wave of China-backed smelting capacity in 
Indonesia. While Mideast disruptions have kept the physical market tight, we expect the 
Indonesia supply boom to push aluminium into surplus and reduce the aluminium price 
to $2,600 by Dec27 (Exhibit 14, right).  
Given structurally tighter supply constraints for copper and our forecast that Indonesia 
primary aluminum output surges to around 4% of global output by end-2027 (vs. 1% in 
end-2025), we reiterate our recommendation to go long Dec27 LME copper and short 
Dec27 LME aluminium. This relative value trade should also be more immune to 
potential macro-driven reductions in positioning (e.g., turn in AI cycle, more Fed hikes) 
than our long Dec27 LME copper recommendation.
