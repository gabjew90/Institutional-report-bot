The S&P 500® Index is a free-ﬂoat market capitalization-weighted index of 500 of the largest U.S. companies. The S&P 500 Equal Weight Index 
(EWI) is the equal-weight version of S&P 500 Index and includes the same constituents, but each company in the EWI is allocated a ﬁxed 
weight—or 0.2%—of the index total at each quarterly rebalance. These indices are calculated on a total return basis with dividends reinvested. 
These indices are unmanaged, their returns do not reﬂect any fees, expenses, or sales charges, and they are not available for direct 
investment. Past performance is no guarantee of future results.

S&P 500® Monthly Excess Return
• The average S&P 500 stock (equal weight) returned +5.0% in October while 
the index itself (cap weight) fell -0.9%, a gap of about 5.9 points. The gap was 
the widest since 2009 and the second largest since 1990.
• Cap weight outperformed equal weight by roughly 6 to 12 percentage points 
annually from 2023 through 2025. One month is not a trend, but similar 
rotations in 1999 to 2002 and 2009 came as market leadership broadened.
