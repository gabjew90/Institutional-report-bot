U.S. Markets Recap 
US +1.31% | Nasdaq +1.60% | Dow +0.49%
Synopsis

US equities finished higher on Wednesday, ending a bit off best levels. S&P breadth was positive, though the equal-weight S&P trailed the 
cap-weighted index by about 50 bp. The AI trade led the market, with neoclouds the standouts; SOX posted its third strong gain in four 
sessions and climbed about 17% from last Thursday's sharp drop. NVDA stabilized after recent declines while MSFT, AMZN and META 
were weaker. The rally followed well-received CoreWeave earnings, positive headlines on Anthropic's coming IPO, a strong acceleration in 
OpenAI's Q3 revenue growth and continued positioning support. The dollar index rose 0.7%, gold fell 0.7%, silver gained 1.5% and WTI 
settled up 0.6%, off best levels. 

The bond market was closed for Veterans Day after a big backup in Treasury yields on Tuesday, which followed hotter-than-expected CPI 
and hawkish Fed comments. It was otherwise a very quiet session with no data or Fedspeak. Thursday brings jobless claims, a $25B 30-year 
bond auction and several Fed speakers, while Friday's PPI has taken outsized importance, with headline expected up 0.4% m/m after a 1.3% 
gain in September and core up 0.3% after 0.7%.


Outperformers included networking/communications, tech hardware, software, A&D, investment banks, exchanges and energy, while man-
aged care, regional banks, airlines, retail/apparel and homebuilders lagged. CoreWeave rose 24.7% after a beat-and-raise with a $132.5B 
backlog and reassurance on funding. Rumble gained 16.6% on strong Quake AI performance, with GPU capacity nearly sold out, and Mer-

Macro

CoreWeave beat and raise eases funding concerns around the AI trade
CoreWeave beat and raised. Takeaways highlighted management's focus on addressing concerns surrounding funding. Growth can be fi-
nanced largely via customer prepayments (about $9B in prepayments and deposits from signed contracts in the next three months alone), 
asset-level debt, vendor financing and cash flow rather than significant parent-level capital raises. The company will not issue parent-level 
unsecured debt through 2027 and sees parent-level capital needs below $5B through 2027. Fundamental takeaways were also upbeat, with 
a $132.5B backlog as demand runs well ahead of supply and pricing on new contracts rises materially. About 300K GPUs roll off in the 
2027-28 period, with renewals potentially generating nearly $10B with no additional capex. On regulatory risk, the existing backlog is 
largely already permitted or under construction. 

Anthropic IPO demand may lift valuation to $1.5T; OpenAI Q3 revenue up 50%
Anthropic's bankers are reportedly targeting a $1.5T valuation for the upcoming IPO, up from yesterday's initial $1.4T target following 
strong investor demand (FT). The IPO has drawn multiple times the orders for shares available (Bloomberg), and a higher price would lift 
the amount raised to over $100B. Some backers are offering part of their IPO allocation ahead of listing at a premium, including Jones-
Trading marketing a block of Anthropic shares worth up to $500M at a 3-5% premium over the final IPO price. OpenAI reported $10B in 
quarterly revenue for the period ending September, a 50% increase from the prior quarter, pushing ARR to $50B, as the company works to 
secure funding at a $1.25T valuation, down from the $1.5T investors had initially sought (Reuters). 

Hot CPI and hawkish Fed comments lift pricing for a December rate hike
Treasury yields recorded their steepest daily rise since April 2025 on Tuesday, attributed in large part to hotter-than-expected US CPI. Fed 
speakers added to the pressure: Waller mentioned the possibility of a 50 bp move, Logan suggested quicker rate hikes may be needed, 
Goolsbee called the CPI report disturbing and Bowman said there is still work to do on getting inflation down to target. Markets now price 
about an 85% chance of a December Fed rate hike and a greater likelihood of three more in 2027, compared with two before the CPI data. 
October PPI is due Friday morning, alongside a busy Fedspeak calendar that includes NY Fed President Williams after the close on Thursday. 

White House opts for economic pressure on Iran; return to fighting seen unlikely
Axios reported that the Trump administration is adopting a third-way strategy of economically strangling the Iranian regime, including a 
new naval blockade operation dubbed Iron Gate. Secretary of State Rubio confirmed the US will maintain indefinite economic pressure to 
degrade Iran's ability to fund military and proxy operations, while saying major combat operations are no longer needed and a diplomatic 
deal is unlikely anytime soon (NY Times). Trump repeated that renewed strikes are quite unlikely but has not ruled out the option if nuclear 
talks stall. Iran's security chief Rezaee repeated that the US must meet Iran's seven preconditions, after which Iran would agree to reopen 
the Strait of Hormuz within two days, and said none of Iran's enriched nuclear material would leave the country. Al Arabiya reported a 
preliminary agreement allowing Iran to down blend enriched uranium to 3.67%, with IAEA inspectors allowed in and the release of $6-9B 
of frozen Iranian assets.

European Markets Recap 
Germany +1.2% | France +0.7% | UK +0.8%
Synopsis

European equity markets closed higher on Wednesday, with cyclicals leading and defensives lagging. Core government bonds were mixed: 
German Bund yields rose 0.8bp while French OATs eased 0.5bp, narrowing the OAT-Bund spread by about 1.3bp to 146.8bp. Technology 
was among the best performers on renewed enthusiasm for AI infrastructure, which boosted European chip stocks. 

The macro calendar was quiet, with final German CPI and Italian industrial production on the agenda. Official ECB comments leaned 
hawkish: Lane said the outlook hinges on second-round inflation effects, while Escriva said the market repricing of fiscal risks in France 
should be seen as a positive development. Bundesbank chief Nagel signalled an upward revision to German growth forecasts next month, 
and NIESR said the BoE may need to hike rates to 5% next year. Zelensky said Europe must act fast on who will represent it in peace talks 
with Russia, and UK PM Burnham faces cabinet divisions over EU policy. 

Energy, tech and basic resources outperformed, while food/beverage, healthcare and insurance lagged. Siemens Energy rose 10.7% on a Q4 
EPS beat and a buyback expanded to up to €9B through FY30, FLSmidth rose 5.6% on beats across Q3 EBITA, orders and backlog with 
margin guidance raised, and RWE gained 2.2% after 9M EBITDA and EBIT beat consensus. Voestalpine fell 5.9% after missing Q2 estimates 
and lowering the upper end of FY EBITDA guidance to €1.70B from €1.85B. Alcon fell 4.4% despite a Q3 core EPS beat, as narrowed FY 
guidance came in below consensus, and CSG dropped 20.3% on a Q3 revenue miss and a cut to FY operating EBIT margin guidance. 
Macro

Bundesbank's Nagel signals upward revision to German growth outlook
Bundesbank chief Nagel signaled an upward revision to German growth forecasts next month (Bloomberg), saying he is seeing encouraging 
signs of improvement in the latest economic data and projections as the economy rebounds faster than expected a few months ago. The 
government recently upgraded its 2026 forecast to 1.3%, and the German chamber of commerce (DIHK) and five leading economic institutes 
have also improved forecasts, though growth next year is forecast at a relatively weak 1%. Nagel said the economy has regained cyclical 
momentum and that defense and infrastructure spending has been a positive factor, mitigating some of the impact from the Iran war. He 
warned that stronger public spending cannot solve structural problems, pointed out that potential growth of 0.3%-0.4% is too weak, and 
named labor supply, the energy transition and the business environment as areas of reform. 

ECB's Lane backs a December hike if data does not shift
ECB chief economist Lane told Bloomberg he backs a December rate hike if data does not shift in the weeks ahead. He said inflation will 
probably be a little bit above 4% by year-end and sees food and core prices accelerating in early 2027, fitting sell-side expectations that the 
bar for not hiking in December is high. Lane urged investors to look beyond energy prices to wider macro developments. Dovish Governing 
Council members are concerned about tightening too much into a slowdown, but growth has been surprisingly resilient despite the October 
PMI easing to a four-month low of 51.7. S&P Global said the economy is still trending at a quarterly growth rate of about 0.3%, encouraging 
hawkish policymakers that it can absorb another hike without undermining output. 

NIESR criticizes BoE inflation response and sees a case for higher rates
Think tank NIESR criticized the BoE's response to inflation and warned it risks higher long rates. It is more concerned about inflation 
persistence than in July and now expects peak inflation at 4.4% in February, below the BoE's recent forecast of 4.9%, because second-round 
effects are now more likely: three quarters of pay awards are settled early in the new year, households expect inflation of 4.2% and about 
90% of private-sector employees work in industries where median pay exceeds 4%. Markets price Bank Rate reaching 4.75% next year, 
which NIESR believes is closer to what is needed; on that path inflation returns to target in early 2029, about 18 months earlier than the 
BoE's central forecast, at a cost of 0.4% of GDP growth in both 2028 and 2029. Sell-side economists expect another BoE hike in February, 
though deputy governor Lombardelli has said December is a live meeting (Guardian). 

European natural gas prices resume their climb
Front-month Dutch TTF rose for a second consecutive session after dropping sharply from an early-November peak near €104/MWh to its 
lowest level since mid-October near €79/MWh on Monday. The fluctuations reflect uncertainty over the US-Iran conflict and the region's 
supply balance into the winter heating season, with imports lagging last year's levels and winter stockpiles unusually low. Poland's Orlen 
ended a long-term LNG contract with QatarEnergy over supply disruption (Reuters). According to European Commission analysis, Europe's 
energy import bill has risen by over €127B since the start of the Iran conflict amid a doubling in gas prices this year (Bloomberg). Weather 
models project a broadly mild winter, and sell-side analysts forecast an average winter gas price between €70/MWh and €90/MWh. 

Costa reassures Merz on EU budget as Ukraine funding gap remains open
Euronews cited a letter from European Council President Costa seeking to reassure German Chancellor Merz on discussions over Ukraine 
funding. Merz wanted to link an EU budget deal with financing for Ukraine by all member states but faced pushback from Slovakia, the 
Czech Republic, Hungary and Bulgaria, which want to opt out of any future loan to Ukraine; Merz said it mattered for European security 
and threatened to veto the EU's 2028-2034 long-term budget. The EU has promised to cover two-thirds of Ukraine's €70B gap, approxi-
mately €45B, and a new round of joint debt is the most likely option, according to diplomats and officials, though some members may be

Asian Markets Recap 
Japan +1.31% | Hong Kong -1.06% | China -0.46%
Synopsis

Asian equities ended mixed on Wednesday. Japan, South Korea and Taiwan all rose again, while Hong Kong and Shanghai fell, with the 
Hang Seng at the bottom of its trading range. Australia's ASX ended higher, the Topix held onto gains and Singapore also rose. India stocks 
were notably higher, led by banks. JGB and regional sovereign yields were all higher, jumping from recent lows. The Australian dollar 
bounced off months-long lows, crude futures came off overnight lows and precious metals bounced from the overnight selloff. Base metals 
were mixed. 

Regional data was supportive. South Korean unemployment sank again to a two-year low after the economy added another 139K jobs, and 
Japan's Reuters Tankan showed manufacturer sentiment rising again on demand for semiconductors. Australian home mortgage lending 
fell 5.3% q/q, with first-home demand falling by more, underlining the impact of recent RBA rate hikes. The PBOC is reported to have 
surveyed fixed income investors on a possible income tax on CGBs as a way of increasing yields. 

Honda (-4.5%) booked a further $3B in EV-related costs for Q2 and its operating income missed estimates, though it raised its full-year 
outlook. SoftBank posted a $7B loss following a $3.7B write-down of US data center projects. Alibaba's chip design business T-Head filed 
for a Hong Kong IPO, with size and structure not yet finalized. TSMC is to invest $500M each into Anthropic and OpenAI. IDP Education 
received an improved bid from Blackstone valuing it at around A$834M ($571M). In Korea, Shift Up (-14.5%) and Krafton (-9.6%) fell on 
Q3 operating profit misses. 
Macro

BOJ seen on track for a third rate hike this year despite market volatility
A Nikkei editorial discussed market attention on how the BOJ will be affected by the latest stock market volatility as board members mull 
a third rate hike in a year for the first time since 1989. A decision has yet to be sealed despite Friday's market rout, the third-largest absolute 
drop in the Nikkei on record, with more than a month until the next MPM. OIS pricing for a December hike fell to 52% last Friday from 
79% though has since recovered to 76% on Tuesday. Comments from former BOJ officials remained sanguine and still see a December move 
as the likely scenario, underpinned by the BOJ's assessment that underlying inflation is now generally consistent with the 2% target. NLI 
Research Institute suggested US data center regulations following the Democrat victory in the US midterm elections won't become clear 
until at least next summer, and that stock market volatility will remain high in the meantime. The article recalled that the third hike in 
December 1989 came after a delay when then-Prime Minister Hashimoto called for a complete revocation. 

BOJ October Summary of Opinions remains mostly hawkish
The Summary of Opinions for the October meeting was largely hawkish on policy. Several comments advocated more rate hikes based on 
the declaration that underlying inflation had reached the 2% target, and one member suggested the MPC should proceed without becoming 
overly cautious, consistent with a recent speech by Koeda. A few sought to bring the policy rate to the neutral level, with a couple using 
phrasing such as soon and at an early stage, and one suggested the neutral rate is considerably above 1%. The inflation section skewed 
attention to whether underlying inflation will be anchored. Views generally conveyed a wait-and-see approach centred on wage growth, 
bringing the 2027 shunto talks back into focus. Not much was revealed on discussion of ETF sales; attention turns to the minutes for more 
details. 

China weighs tax on interest income from government bonds
Bloomberg sources noted China is in talks with market participants about a possible tax on interest income from government bonds, which 
firms and individuals are currently exempt from. A PBOC survey asked lenders how a 25% tax would affect their demand for central and 
local government securities; it is unclear from the survey whether a levy will be introduced. One estimate indicated a 25% tax could imply 
a 52 bp increase in the 10Y CGB yield to maintain the same after-tax return. Banks are the largest holders, with roughly CNY100T ($15T) 
of central and local government securities. The idea comes as the China 10Y yield trades near a record low, fanning concern that depressed 
rates will entrench economic pessimism. Reuters sources also noted China banks sharply raising dollar deposit rates to curb yuan strength, 
with the currency near its highest since May 2022. 

SoftBank reports Q2 net loss as AI-related impairments weigh
SoftBank reported a Q2 net loss of JPY1.115T ($7.1B), its biggest since 3Q24, with weakness accentuated by comparison with a blowout 
performance a year earlier. Vision Funds logged a net loss of JPY81.6B versus a year-ago JPY2.4T as investment gains collapsed to JPY85.4B 
from JPY2.8T. Advanced procurement costs related to US AI data center projects resulted in an H1 impairment of about JPY600B (Nikkei), 
with more coming from the planned Ohio facility buildout and higher costs at subsidiary SB Energy. The AI compute business including 
Arm saw H1 net losses deepen to JPY322B from JPY275B. CFO Goto noted the latest OpenAI funding round had not been factored in and 
could provide significant investment gains in Q3 (Nikkei). NAV fell JPY12.5T to JPY59.9T in September versus June, and LTV rose 2.9 ppt 
to 15.9%; both metrics have deteriorated further since September.
