1) Chime Prime: The premium membership tier driving significant 
product attach 
Chime Prime has grown significantly over the last three quarters, exceeding mgmt’s 
expectations and supporting the company’s updated guidance revision for the 
remainder of the year. A key driver has been product bundling, where consumers gain 
access to 5% cash back in select categories and 4-5% interest on savings (for accounts

customers access to products like Instant loans and higher limits across MyPay, further

members also have 2x the ARPAM compared to the average non-Prime member and

2) Chime Enterprise: Future longer-term customer acquisition pipeline 
Mgmt remains positive on the opportunity to drive customer acquisition through its 
enterprise channel, highlighting the significant customer acquisition cost benefits and 
the high quality nature of these direct deposit relationships. Management noted that 
2026 strategy was around planting the seeds by adding new partners, while 2027 will be 
about execution of the strategy, with mgmt pointing to further sales leadership 
additions to drive the enterprise adoption strategy forward. The sales motion around 
enterprise builds off the capabilities that came from the acquisition of Salt Labs. The 
company also noted they believe they are in an advantageous competitive position given 
the no-fee proposition (vs other EWA competitors that charge consumer fees). 
Management continued to emphasize that enterprise is a $0 CAC channel and that the 
company has several partnerships in the pipeline. Management indicated that it expects 
to see meaningful net adds contribution from the channel over the longer-term (noting 
they are still being prudent with their expectations in the near term given the long sales 
cycles and ramp times), with the company emphasizing the quality of the new customer 
base and the opportunity to cross-sell the CHYM financial suite. Major enterprise wins 
this year include Allied Financial, Michael’s, and the City of Charleston. 
3) Chime Invest: Rounding out the financial product offering  
Management indicated as part of its move up-market that it intends to address the 
needs of members that want investing products included with the broader relationship - 
this is part of the bundling strategy that allows customers to use more of the products 
that the Company has to offer. The offering includes both self directed investing, as well 
as a managed portfolio / Robo offering for all members, although fees are waived for 
Chime Prime members (10bps advisory fees for Plus members, 25bps for Standard 
members). As the company continues to round out their product offering, management 
believes it will manifest in both stronger retention and higher engagement.

Instant Loans: Continued traction with strong product market fit 
Mgmt remains positive on the progress scaling instant loans, which has recently 
accelerated due to the bundling of instant loans with Chime Prime. MyPay addresses 
two-week payroll cycle liquidity, while Instant Loans addresses discrete 
three-to-six-month liquidity needs with loans being more episodic in nature. Mgmt 
noted that 3Q saw strong instant loans attach rates (~20%), fueled partially by the push 
of Chime Prime, with the company noting they believe there’s an opportunity to drive 
further adoption across their base, while maintaining a prudent approach to 
underwriting. Despite the outsized growth, management noted they have intentionally 
paced origination volumes to calibrate underwriting algorithms across seasoned 
cohorts, which they believe will help the company manage risk exposure over time (the 
company also noted that repeat borrowers have ~50% lower annualized loss rates 
compared to first-time borrowers). Mgmt reiterated that the trajectory of losses in the 
near term will likely be higher, both because of the increase in first time borrowers, 
which carry higher loss rates relative to more seasoned customers, but also over time as 
a result of increasing duration beyond the predominantly 3-month duration profile they 
are running currently. Mgmt noted that they expect loss rates to improve with successive 
cohorts as customers season, while higher loss rates for longer duration loans will also 
come with commensurately higher revenue rates.  
 
Unsecured Credit: Early innings, but opportunity to expand the TAM 
Mgmt also discussed the opportunity with unsecured cards, which they expect to roll 
out in 2027, and where they believe they can address the growing demand from higher 
income customers. The company noted they will be taking a very prudent approach 
rolling the product out, with intentions of limiting the amount of risk the company takes 
as the product scales. They believe the card can serve as an additional customer 
acquisition engine and enable the company to cross-sell direct deposit and further 
expand into the higher income brackets. Finally, the company reiterated their belief of 
being in an advantaged position from a credit risk perspective as a result of their direct 
deposit relationship, and noted that they will follow the same approach of tying access 
to new credit products to primary account behavior.

Mgmt remains positive on the Stride bank deal opportunity 
 
 
Stride transaction refresher 
On 9/8, CHYM announced it had reached a definitive agreement to acquire Stride Bank, 
a 7-year nationally chartered bank partner, for $590mn (~1.5x 2Q26 Stride TBV). The 
transaction will be fully cash funded and is expected to close in 1H27, pending 
necessary approvals from the OCC and the Fed (and customary closing conditions). The 
Company expects ~$100mn of targeted after-tax net synergies, in addition to the 
~$100mm annualized earnings related to the Stride Bank standalone business. On net 
synergies, the Company expects to realize synergies from 1) the reduction in funding 
costs, now that it can hold deposits on its balance sheet, 2) the elimination of bank 
partner fees that are charged by Chime’s current partner banks (i.e. Stride) on its loan 
products, 3) an unlock to other states for its lending business, and 4) some reduction in 
OpEx as a result of the combined entity, somewhat offset by dis-synergies related to 
income from loan products held off balance sheet. 
The latest from management 
Management noted that it has worked hard to help policymakers appreciate the low fee, 
consumer-friendly nature of the product. Management indicated its intention to be 
payments led and tech forward and noted that the bank purchase is not a strategic shift 
towards more capital-intensive lending. Mgmt also sees other less tangible benefits in 
areas such as customer acquisition as a result of the perception of increased safety and 
security that results from being a regulated bank. Management indicated that its 
high-velocity balance sheet and spend-centric business model allows higher returns on 
allocated capital and will allow CHYM to remain Durbin-exempt for the foreseeable 
future. On a pro forma basis, mgmt sized total assets to be in the $6.5bn range (inclusive 
of Chime Financial, Stride Bank, and the Chime associated assets at The Bancorp bank), 
but noted that this includes $1.6bn in non-core assets (examples include agricultural 
and industrial community lending) that will likely be in runoff mode over time. In Exhibit

Sketching out the Durbin Interchange cap potential impact 
Chime has been clear about its intention to stay below the $10bn Durbin threshold. 
However, investors often ask about the imapct from losing the Durbin exemption. In the 
below chart we estimate that revenue impact from the loss of the Durbin exemption 
would modestly ease to ~13% by 2028 as debit interchange becomes a smaller portion 
of the overall revenue base. The earnings impact would be significantly greater given the 
high incremental margins on lost payments revenue and the size of CHYM’s earnings 
base. Although mgmt noted they expect to have continued growth runway below the 
$10bn asset threshold, the company believes if and when they decide to make the jump

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
