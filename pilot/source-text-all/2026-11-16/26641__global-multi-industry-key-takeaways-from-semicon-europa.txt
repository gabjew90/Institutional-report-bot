Global Multi-Industry: Key Takeaways from SEMICON Europa
12 November 2026 | 1:11PM GMT
We visited the VAT Group and Ebara booths and met with company 
representatives at SEMICON Europa in Munich on 11 Nov. We also had 
unscheduled meetings with Pfeiffer Vacuum, Inficon and Edwards Vacuum (VT 
division of Atlas Copco). Our key takeaways are: (1) The semiconductor 
equipment industry is seeing very strong demand, and there are no signs of 
customer hesitation or deceleration in order momentum. (2) The steep capacity 
ramp-up has put pressure on the industry supply chain, but several players think 
they have diversified their production base or Tier-1 suppliers, having learned 
from the semiconductor shortage during Covid. (3) Pricing power is strong for 
players with dominant market share despite the consolidated end-customer 
base of equipment OEMs and foundries, while sub-fab equipment 
manufacturers are seeing more competition vs. cleanroom equipment. (4) 
Services continue to be a focus for most companies to offset the volatility of the 
WFE cycle and support more resilient margins through the cycle. Digitalization 
and software integration are key to minimizing foundry downtime by 
orchestrating the different services needed. (5) Most players have 
operations/plants and/or sell directly to Chinese foundries and see increasing 
competition in the sub-fab equipment space, but have not yet seen Chinese 
players outside their local markets. More details below:

Company-specific takeaways 
 
 
VAT Group  
VAT sees no signs of customer demand slowing down, and backlog visibility is 
n
increasing. Customer planning windows have extended from 2 quarters to 3, with 
some even discussing deliveries for September 2027, and lead time is now 3-4 
months. Order inflow is currently at a similar level to 2Q26, but VAT could see a good 
quarter if it further accelerates towards year-end with normal seasonality (CHF 
20-30mn pre-buy). The Chinese business remains strong, with growth expectations 
now at LDD vs. M-HSD at the start of this year; the region is also margin accretive 
due to the mix of new customers and limited pricing pressure. With backlog 
visibility increasing, VAT is likely to provide quantitative revenue guidance for 
2027E with its FY26 results in March and believes it is feasible and even 
conservative to achieve revenues 15-20% above the top end of the range 
previously stated at the 2025 CMD (previous 2027 sales guidance was 
CHF1.5-1.7bn). 
The capacity ramp-up remains on track, with the factory output run-rate 
n
already at CHF450mn. VAT has hired ~1,000 employees YTD to support expansion 
plans, with most of the new workforce in Malaysia, alongside 200-300 in Switzerland 
and 100 in Romania. VAT is preparing machines and setting up assembly stations to 
open its Malaysia 1B factory in 1Q27. This would bring VAT’s total capacity to 
CHF2.5bn, with ~CHF1bn in Switzerland, ~CHF600mn each in 1A and 1B in Malaysia 
(although 1A is slightly lower than 1B), and CHF200-300mn in Romania. However, 
management flags normal seasonality in 1Q27, driven by lower Malaysia sales due to 
the Chinese New Year effect. 
Margin progression should continue into 2027E, driven by operating leverage 
n
despite ramp-up costs, while VAT has been able to pass through the cost 
inflation seen this year and put through about 10% pricing, as it has >70% 
market share in leading-edge processes. VAT guided for 32-33% EBITDA margins 
for FY2026 (vs. Visible Alpha Consensus Data at 33.5%), with 2H expected at >35% 
margins (consensus at 36.1%). The Global Service segment is also a key contributor, 
in addition to operating leverage from the Valves segment, as ~30% of the division is 
consumables, which are directly linked to fab utilization and typically require 
replacement every 6 months. VAT highlighted that it could see an acceleration of 
field services growth next year, as retrofit/upgrades (30% of services) are currently 
being delayed by customers as they want to keep production lines running as much 
as possible. Beyond 2027/28E, there could be an uptick in field services as more of 
the installed base comes out of warranty and moves into service contracts, which 
could lead to a return to historical EBITDA margins (>50% vs. 45.6% in FY2025).

sales by 2029. VAT recently added gas inlet valves to its systems solution offering

Ebara Group 
Ebara continues to see strong demand, with its backlog now extending to ~12

dry pumps, as they are technologically easier to develop and sit within the sub-fab.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
