We preview RE+, the industry’s largest N.A. solar/storage trade show, being held 
November 16th-19th in Las Vegas, Nevada, where we will host meetings with

investor focus to center on the demand outlook into 2027/2028, including recent 
bookings trends across utility-scale solar and the trajectory of resi demand, while 
policy is also likely to feature prominently following the recent midterm elections in 
the US. In utility-scale, we believe investors will be particularly focused on project 
timing and contracting activity amid continued uncertainty around Section 232 
impacts and higher interest rates, while in resi we expect the key debate to center on 
the pace of demand recovery and adoption of newer products. We also expect new 
product opportunities, including SSTs, storage and other solutions addressing 
growing power demand, to receive increased attention at the show. While solar 
stocks have underperformed the broader market YTD, the group has seen some 
improvement over the past month, particularly following the midterm elections. 
Looking ahead we continue to prefer Buy-rated NXT (on CL) and FSLR within 
utility-scale and ENPH within residential. 
Ahead of the event, we recap the solar industry YTD, highlight the key themes we 
expect to hear at RE+, and outline our top questions for management. We also 
preview NXT’s upcoming Capital Markets Day and areas where we see potential for 
incremental detail, including updated long-term financial targets and greater

Macro/Policy outlook 
With RE+ following the recent US midterms, we expect investors will be particularly 
focused on potential implications for solar policy and tax incentives, particularly 45X, 
and the potential for greater policy visibility into 2027. As discussed in our recent 
midterm note, we view 45X as the key sector-specific policy lever, particularly for US 
solar manufacturers, while the existing 45Y/48E framework remains important for solar 
project timing and economics. Beyond the election, we expect a focus on how the recent 
Section 232 outcome as well as higher interest rates could impact module ASPs, project 
economics, and overall demand. We will look for greater clarity on the magnitude and 
timing of polysilicon-driven price increases, as well as the extent to which higher costs 
can be passed through. 
New Products 
We expect a greater focus on new products across both utility-scale and resi 
applications at this year’s RE+, with solid-state transformers (SSTs) likely to be a key area 
of discussion given the push toward 800 VDC power architectures for data centers. RE+ 
will feature an 800 VDC Data Center Demonstration at its Grid Edge Theater, bringing 
together 13 industry participants, including ABB, Direct Energy Partners and ENPH, 
alongside several show-floor sessions focused on DC power and SSTs. Beyond SSTs, we 
expect investors to focus on a broader set of new product offerings across our coverage, 
including SEDG’s recently introduced Nexis platform, ENPH’s G5 battery, and SHLS’ 
AirLink and battery storage solutions. Collectively, we see these products expanding the 
conversation at RE+ beyond underlying solar demand toward how companies can 
address growing power, storage and grid-infrastructure needs.

Expect positive revisions to long term guidance. On November 12, 2025, NXT held a 
CMD where it outlined a FY2030 revenue target of $4.8bn-$5.6bn, implying a ~12% 
CAGR from FY2025-FY2030. We note that at the midpoint this includes ~$3.6bn from 
trackers, $0.9bn from foundations/frames, $0.5bn from electrical/EBOS, and $250mn 
from software. Additionally, non-tracker revenue was expected to increase to ~1/3 of 
total revenue, from ~13% in FY2026. Since then, the company has closed on the 
acquisitions of Apex Power (closed 7/30/26), Prevalon Energy (closed 7/20/26), and 
Zimmermann PV-Steel Group (closed after F2Q27). While NXT does not expect Apexto 
be a material revenue driver over the near-term, we do forecast $115mn in revenue in 
FY2029. Additionally, Prevalon was at a ~$333mn revenue run rate at the time of its 
acquisition, and we are forecasting revenue of $408mn in FY2029. Lastly, Zimmerman 
was at a $343mn revenue run rate when it was acquired. Combined, we estimate that 
these acquisitions can contribute over $1bn in revenue in FY2030.  
Separately, the company had communicated as early as its F1Q27 earnings call it was 
already trending above its initial FY2030 targets. As a result, we see meaningful upside 
to NXT’s FY2030 revenue targets, with additional upside to adj EBITDA and FCF 
generation. In particularly, we believe management could increase its revenue and adj. 
EBITDA outlook to $6.2bn-$7.0bn and $1.4bn-$1.6bn, respectively, which would also 
capture consensus views at the midpoint, as well as FCF to $1.0bn-$1.2bn.  
 
Market outlook and general demand environment. We also anticipate that NXT will 
provide some incremental color on the overall demand environment, including thoughts 
on F2028, which it did not provide on its F2Q27 earnings call. This time period is of 
particular importance given general market concerns that there could be a steep drop in 
demand as a result of contract delays or restructuring following the Section 232 
outcome on polysilicon that is expected to meaningfully increase the price of solar 
panels as well as the impact from higher interest rates. 
Potential for improved segmenting clarity and/or disclosure. Given the amount of 
acquisitions that NXT has closed over the past two years, with its largest deals 
completed in the past year, we believe management is also likely to provide improved 
segmenting across its business. Currently, the company does not break out revenue by 
segment, but does provide directional views on the revenue contribution from new 
products. Looking ahead, we could see the company break out revenue for battery 
storage, core trackers, and then other revenue (structural/eBOS/power 
conversion/software). We believe this would provide enhanced comparability to analyze 
the performance of NXT’s underlying business segments, including margin disparities.

normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
