Five key themes  
 
 
The non-M&A advisory outlook remains constructive, notably in restructuring. There was an incrementally positive 
1.
tone at 3Q26 EPS from management teams on restructuring activity potentially growing from here, as well as a still 
constructive secondaries growth backdrop. We estimate that secondaries + restructuring advisory reached 18% of 2025 
total revenue across the IBanks (Exhibit 2). In restructuring, elevated activity levels appear set to either remain at these 
levels or grow from here, driven by: 1) mounting debt maturity walls in 2028/29; 2) higher rates, with the forward curve 
now pricing in ~3 rate hikes by the end of 2027 vs. 2 cuts as of the beginning of the year, and a number of sponsor 
portfolio companies which are highly levered and yet to refinance; 3) a more challenging financing market, with emerging 
financial distress among certain frontier AI companies; 4) increasing bankruptcy filings; and 5) a bifurcated impact from 
technological disruption from AI, e.g., in software. We observe that restructuring revenue has a 40% positive R-squared 
to the absolute level of Fed funds (Exhibit 3, Exhibit 4). We forecast 2026E restructuring revenue for the group to remain 
roughly flat after a robust 2025, and then rise 6% in 2027E YoY. On secondaries, we see structural tailwinds, including an 
increasing number of GPs and LPs viewing secondaries as a tool, as well as cyclical factors, with the current PE backdrop 
(most recently due to higher rates) continuing to limit traditional M&A and ECM exits for long-held positions, prompting 
PE to resort more frequently to alternative capital return methods, such as continuation funds. Notably, while 
secondaries capital raised remains elevated, 2026 annualized funds raised fell ~30% YoY (Exhibit 9). A recent Secondaries 
Investor survey indicates that 85%/70% of those surveyed see valuation gaps/asset competition as top reasons 
impacting the ability to deploy capital (Exhibit 12). We see pressure from LPs for GPs to return capital continuing to 
mount, given increasingly aged funds, with our analysis suggesting that 59% of private equity (PE) unrealized value is 5+ 
years aged vs. 50% historically (Exhibit 10, Exhibit 11). Best positioned: EVR, PJT (Exhibit 77). 
M&A remains mid-cycle, with growth continuing to be driven by strategic/large-cap M&A, while sponsor/mid-cap 
2.
M&A growth is more muted. We remain constructive on M&A industry growth continuing. Most leading indicators with 
strong correlations to M&A remain healthy; using these, we forecast 17% NTM industry M&A volume growth (Exhibit 15). 
Further, M&A growth in historic cycles suggests that we remain mid-cycle - M&A industry volumes have risen ~85% thus 
far this cycle, leaving ~70-80pp of remaining potential growth (Exhibit 13). Moreover, M&A excluding AI infrastructure 
deal volumes indicates growth this cycle of only ~75%, leaving a larger ~80-90pp of remaining growth in the cycle. Best 
positioned: EVR, PJT.

results, ahead of a potentially more onerous antitrust backdrop in a subsequent administration. YTD announced 
strategic/large-cap M&A volumes are up ~40% YoY, and up ~90%/220% from the recent cyclical trough (in

transactions have substantial debt financing components. Notwithstanding higher rates, we expect measured 
growth in sponsor M&A to continue, driven by: 1) mounting LP pressure on GPs to return capital; and 2) the fact 
that structural PE M&A drivers remain, namely large $s of aged, deployed capital that needs to be returned, and 
large quantities of uncommitted capital that need to be deployed. Finally, 3) sponsor M&A volumes have been 
muted so far this cycle. YTD announced sponsor/mid-cap M&A volumes are -5%/+15% YoY and up ~70%/55% 
from the recent cyclical trough on an annualized basis (Exhibit 31, Exhibit 32). YTD annualized levels remain 
~30%/20% below all-time historical annual peaks. Further, sponsor/mid-cap volumes are at 1.9%/4.5% of global 
equal weight market-cap, which compare to 2.6%/7.4% on average over the past 16 years - in particular, small + 
mid-cap market cap-weighted M&A volumes remain near the low over the past 16 years (Exhibit 35, Exhibit 36).

3.
reported advisory revenue: As they migrate to non-M&A revenue, 3Q26 Dealogic M&A fees captured only ~50% of 
aggregate 3Q26 advisory revenue across the IBanks, down from >80% a decade ago, and vs. a 56% long-term average 
(Exhibit 38). This compares to ~95% of Dealogic data on advisory revenue for bulge brackets, for which a great % of 
activity is traditional M&A (and the % has not changed materially in a decade). IBanks’ non-M&A advisory revenue2 has

95% in 1Q22 (Exhibit 39).

Modest operating leverage continues, with negative structural comp leverage, and recently moderating non-comp 
4.
spend growth: We see a still challenged operating leverage backdrop across the group, as comp ratios remain above 
historical levels, and non-comp spend growth continues, although has begun to moderate more recently, potentially in 
reaction to investor concern post 2Q26 EPS. We see 2026E pre-tax margins rising only ~10bps YoY, and running ~100bps 
below the 2017-19 average, despite much more scaled IBanks (Exhibit 41, Exhibit 42, Exhibit 47). We see greater 
operating leverage in 2027E/28E, with 180bps/90bps of YoY margin expansion. While this would take 2027E/28E pre-tax 
margins to above the 2017-19 average, they would still be well below prior cyclical peaks, with 2026E/27E/28E 
9.1pp/6.8pp/5.7pp below the 2021 peak. We continue to be constructive on mid-cap skewed IBanks around margin 
sustainability, given more variable compensation structures, with less reliance on large fixed comp guarantees and 
deferred stock vs. in large-cap M&A. Best positioned: HLI, PIPR. Further detail on comp and non-comp leverage trends: 
Structural negative comp leverage across the group, given the elevated competition and cost of hiring senior 
a.
bankers. We model 80bps/90bps/80bps of comp leverage in 2026E/27E/28E (Exhibit 45), with average comp 
ratios of 63.4%/62.3%/61.5% that are 290bps/180bps/110bps above 2017-19 averages, and 
490bps/390bps/310bps above 2021 peaks (Exhibit 47). We see comp leverage driven by fixed compensation 
accounting for 35% of total revenue in 2026E, declining to 31% of total revenue in 2027E, and 30% of revenue in 
2028E (Exhibit 49). 
Slight moderation in the pace of non-comp expense growth, as 3Q26 non-comp expenses came in 3% below 
b.
the Street, potentially in part as IBanks look to limit expense growth and increase focus on ROI. However, 
non-comp $ growth has risen notably over the course of 2026 - we currently forecast 2026E non-comp $ growth 
YoY of 10%, up from only 8% at the beginning of 2026, and 5% at the beginning of 2025. These higher non-comp 
costs appear to be driven by a combination of greater AI-related and data subscription expenses, and higher travel 
costs. We decrease our median 2027E/28E non-comp ratios by 10bps each (Exhibit 42).

n
diversified its business over the years to a greater degree than peers, which should allow it to perform in a wider range of 
market backdrops, and make it more of an all-weather firm. The company has diversified into a few areas: 1) secondaries 
advisory contributed 14% of 2025 revenue for EVR vs. only 5% for the group (Exhibit 56). 2) EVR’s sponsor business has 
reached scale, now middle of the group in terms of % of total firmwide M&A revenue according to Dealogic, with the 
company highlighting at 3Q26 EPS that it now has 60 sponsor coverage bankers on the platform. We believe that EVR’s 
investments have also supported better growth than peers, as evidenced by better publicly available Dealogic data, with 
its YTD completions up by 26% YoY (from an already elevated base) vs. +9% on average across the group (Exhibit 53). 
Looking forward, the same is true of its backlogs, which are up 195% since 1Q23 (Exhibit 52). Finally, the company guided 
to strength continuing going forward, advisory outlook, despite delivering strong YTD advisory fees (up 42% YoY), leading 
to our 2026E/27E/28E EPS estimates being 3%/2%/5% above the Street. Overall, we model an 8% 2026E-28E revenue 
CAGR, after 2 consecutive years of ~30% YoY revenue growth 3. 
LCLN (Buy, $30.50 12-month PT, 15% upside): LCLN offers strong top-line and earnings growth trajectory over the 
n
medium term, driven by: 1) its smaller scale supporting more rapid growth; 2) market share gains through organic 
investment; and 3) inorganic expansion upside potential. We forecast a 2026E-28E revenue CAGR of 13% vs. 12% for 
peers (Exhibit 57). We forecast organic growth catalyzed by continued senior banker hiring, with a 7% 2026E-28E MD 
CAGR (Exhibit 58). The company’s investments in IBanking Advisory and in Valuations and opinions continue to diversify 
the business beyond M&A and reduce procyclicality, with M&A representing ~2/3 of 2025 revenue, down from 82% in 
2021 (Exhibit 59). The company has a structurally growing Valuations and opinions business, which benefits from 
continued alternative asset management AUM growth, as well as increasing frequency of valuations across the industry. 
Further, the company offers clients access to its aggregated proprietary valuation data, which represents a differentiated 
client acquisition strategy (Exhibit 60). LCLN’s IBanking advisory revenue benefits from a constructive M&A market 
backdrop, and leverage to areas of the M&A market we are increasingly more constructive on, namely: 1) the steady 
growth area of mid-cap M&A; and 2) long-term structural sponsor M&A growth. Strong top-line growth supports a 20% 
normalized net income CAGR, better than peers at 15%. (Exhibit 61).

Valuation and new vs. old 
 
 
Our investment bank stocks increased 13% on average since the day before first 3Q26 IBank EPS print (10/21/2026),4 
outperforming the S&P 500 by 16pp, driven entirely by multiple rerating, on: 1) a 6% median earnings beat, driven by 
higher revenue and lower expenses than expected; and 2) investor fears around being late cycle abating, with 
sentiment shifting conclusively back to being that we are mid-cycle, on IBanks’ constructive tone around both the 
M&A outlook in 4Q26 and into 2027, as well as constructive non-M&A advisory commentary, particularly in 
restructuring. As we appear to be mid-cycle already, we see limited rerating potential from here. The group trades at 
~16.0x/15.5x GSe/consensus NTM P/E, or ~55th %ile P/E multiples over the past decade (Exhibit 73).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted 
are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized 
ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary 
depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. 
Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based 
on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a 
stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future 
compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the 
potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of 
the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be 
discussed in research.
