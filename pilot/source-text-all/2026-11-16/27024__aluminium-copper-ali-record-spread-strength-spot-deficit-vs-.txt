Aluminium Mkt Summary:
Prompt aluminium spreads trade to record backwardation (Nov / Dec $100b), pricing a spot deficit from
legacy Middle Eastern supply disruptions, exacerbated by positioning and Chinese tightness, whilst back-
end remains (broadly) anchored on forward surplus expectations. Desk view is continued spread strength
is required to attract supply into the LME system, either from Middle East or India (as majority of
Indonesia metal remains non-deliverable). Deferred aluminium should remain under pressure on
Indonesian supply growth. As always, the path matters.
1. Chinese Inventory: Continues to draw; down another 30k MT vs Monday to -13% y/y (social + bond +
billet). Salient point is the deficit caused from ME disruptions was met by China, through higher billet +
semis exports, and lower ingot imports (which resulted in an ingot draw), in addition to ~1.5mn MT of
ingot held off-warrant by banks and Trade Houses ex China. With China and Europe now de-stocked
spreads are doing the work to solve the spot deficit.

1. SHFE / LME arb: The challenge for LME at this juncture is China (locally) needs metal which explains
the SHFE spread inversion (Nov / Dec ¥350b) and (almost) open import ARB for Russian metal last
month. Struggle to see spreads easing when China and LME are both competing for metal.
1. Premiums: We think spreads will rally to incentivise deliveries (without meaning to state the obvious)
putting significant downward pressure on premiums – hence we like being short all regional premiums.
We see the path as
2. MJP: We think more fresh metal will be heading to Asia for the LME backstop; in a falling MJP market
(spot last ~$220/mt), with Nov / Jan at 100b, think merchants will be looking to offer LME + $100/mt in
order to forego backwardation – issue is there is only ~28k MT of off-warrant (deliverable) metal in Asia
per LME. The unknown is how much (non-deliverable) Indonesian metal can be swapped, but this will
likely take 1-2 months to hit the exchange
3. Middle East: We think cargos will be priced to Asia despite the spot EPD / MJP premium differential
given higher freight and longer transit time (for Asia vs Europe).
4. Europe: The debate is the extent to which there is enough metal via Asian LME / Indonesia / Middle
East to feed the spot deficit, or whether some combination of i) higher spreads, and / or ii) lower European
premiums are needed to solve the deficit.
x26 / f27 negative carry (mostly) erodes MJP premium with a $100/mt LME incentive

1. Positioning - Spreads: Whilst we view the physical tightness as genuine, we think the spread strength
has been exacerbated by a) spec z6/z7 shorts; sold into Q2 strength looking to take back against prompt
contangos, b) Trade House shorts vs non-deliverable Indonesian units, and c) large systematic shorts, we
think out of Jan27. Noteworthy that our index rolls (Dec / Mar) were well absorbed even at large
backwardations.
1. Positioning - Vol / Flat Price: Despite short aluminium being the conviction trade at LME week we
don’t think positioning is elevated; in the vol space we’ve seen some 1x2 q1 put spread buying (which is
probably decaying nicely here, but isn’t a conviction position), but we’re not seeing ~30% delta 3M put
buying.
