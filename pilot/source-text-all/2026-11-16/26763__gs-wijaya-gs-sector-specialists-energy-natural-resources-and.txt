Adam Wijaya  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Energy, Natural Resources & Utilities Sector Specialist, FICC & Equities

Large Cap Oil. While crude has felt like a ‘race to the bottom’ – the E&Ps for the most part have held in
relative to the move in crude over last 1m (WTI -9% with Large Caps -4% on average). Medium term
views on oil remain more cautious – Hormuz flows have recovered, there’s supply growth / upward
revisions to balances for the next couple of years (LATAM, CAD more in focus on the desk) and we are
getting less questions about the right level of risk premium that should be applied to oil / products.
Running through three of the more topical names below – feedback welcome…
• DVN – easily the most debated stock on the desk post 3Q print. The question is really ‘is all the news out
there and how much downside from here?’ Specialists that have defended the stock for the better of the
year are largely highlighting (A) the strategic review is done – so there should be less focus on debating
asset sale bogeys / the remaining kit, (B) the buyback is ramping and that’s a hard narrative to fight and
(C) positioning actually appears more favorable now – ie it’s not as well owned like it has been and would
think with a crude flush this one can hold in.
• OXY – less questions around rate of change / new debt target / how the ops are running – but more
around ‘if we get a crude flush over the next few weeks – how much could the stock underperform vs the
rest of the group?’ Some questions around EOR discussions on the call (ie have seen more focus recently)
– but would just say EOR capabilities have been part of the OXY story for some time now and given most
mgmt teams in E&P have talked about increasing recoveries, proppants, etc – not a huge shocker to me
this is more topical.
• COP – couple of downgrades away over the last few days. Most of the pushback largely around relative
performance (best performing Large Cap and US Major) and too early to play for a FCF inflection that is a
couple of years away. Buybacks expected to trend higher in 4Q (known) with the stock trading at a high
single digit FCF yield on GS numbers. Still think there’s a preference for Large Cap and quality in this
tape – but get the sense the level of offense being played here has slowed.
Uranium. Have started to see a slight tick up in investor inbounds on uranium over the last week or so.
Most of the questions revolve around (1) positioning – how light it is in the space, (2) commodity vs
equity – would you rather own ‘direct’ U3O8 vs miners vs supply chain players, (3) does the commodity
move (spot and term) really even matter at this point and (4) what will it take for the narrative to
disconnect from the broader DC story. We have been clear – the fundamental story for uranium here really
has nothing to do with data centers / AI / power infra. Earnings really aren’t the driver for these stories and
some of the dedicated guys have been hitting F9 on their models to see where ‘P/NAV has been trading
relative to the last cycle’. Have seen nothing but constructive headlines on the uprate front and nuclear
largely being viewed as having bipartisan support is less of a concern vs some of the other topics on power

/ infra buildout. Interestingly – this was flagged yesterday: CME Pursues New Physical Uranium Contract
as Prices Climb…
