Inflation and Fed minutes in the limelight 
Inflation and growth momentum remain in focus. In the US, Fed minutes and key activity data 
will provide further clues about the monetary policy outlook and whether strong growth 
momentum has continued into Q4. In the euro area, inflation data and business surveys will 
be closely watched for signs of energy-related price pressures and the impact of political 
uncertainty. In the Nordics, inflation expectations will be in focus, particularly in Norway, 
alongside Swedish unemployment data and ongoing government negotiations. 
  
• 
US: Fed minutes will be in focus for insights into the decision to leave rates unchanged at the 
October meeting and members’ views on the policy path ahead. Retail sales and industrial 
production will provide clues as to whether the strong growth momentum has continued into 
Q4. We will also get regional November sentiment surveys and housing market data. On 
Monday, Fed Chair Warsh will deliver opening remarks at the Federal Reserve’s productivity 
and jobs task force conference in San Francisco. Several other Fed members are also 
scheduled to speak during the week. 
 
• 
Euro area: We will scrutinise the final October HICP figures for further details on inflation 
developments. German PPI will also be of interest for signs of price pressures stemming from 
the war in the Middle East. France’s INSEE business survey may reflect the current political 
turmoil. Finally, the EU–UK summit will be closely watched, as well as other EU meetings. 
• 
Sweden: Money market inflation expectations and unemployment are the only indicators this 
week. Government negotiations will continue and the Moderate leader Ulf Kristersson will 
report to the speaker of parliament on Tuesday. 
 
• 
Norway: The upcoming Q4 Inflation Expectations Survey (Thu) will be important, especially 
business leaders’ inflation expectations as they lead domestic inflation. The Committee has 
previously highlighted elevated inflation expectation as a risk for inflation becoming 
entrenched. Social partners’ wage growth estimates will also be closely watched.

The outlook for short-term expectations is mixed. Low actual

• 
Retail sales increased strongly in September, but largely due to 
rising energy prices. Underlying spending, excluding volatile 
auto sales and gasoline prices, was muted.  
• 
Spending has been surprisingly strong so far in 2026, 
supported by large tax refunds following last year’s tax cuts 
and positive wealth effects from rising equity prices. We think 
the support from tax refunds has now faded, while equity 
markets have become more volatile recently. We expect 
spending to moderate in the coming months and in 2027. 
• 
Indicators point to a continued increase in spending in October. 
However, declining auto sales and falling gasoline prices should 
weigh on total retail sales.

BOE revised the CPI forecast sharply higher in November, 
predicting that headline inflation will approach 5% in the 
middle of 2027.  Indirect effects from energy prices are the 
main driver and prices on core goods and food will rise sharply 
already in the beginning of 2027 according to the Bank’s

•

near-term indicators, neither in the UK nor internationally, 
support the sharp increase in inflation. SEB’s 2027 forecast is 
slightly above consensus. 
• 
BOE predicts headline inflation to rise to 3.7% y/y in October 
and core inflation to rise to 2.9% y/y. 
 
US: FOMC meeting minutes 
Wednesday 18, 20:00 
• 
Minutes from the October 27-28 FOMC meeting. 
• 
After raising rates by 25 bp at its September meeting, the Fed 
kept its policy rate unchanged, citing the need for more 
information before deciding on its next move. The decision was 
in line with comments from several Fed members ahead of the 
meeting, who had called for a pause in rate hikes.  
• 
Two members, Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed 
President Beth Hammack, dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike.   
• 
Fed Chair Warsh struck a dovish tone at the press conference. 
He did not repeat earlier warnings about inflation, instead 
highlighting that the Fed had made progress through the 
tightening of financial conditions since September.  
• 
The minutes will be closely watched for discussions about the 
decision to leave rates unchanged, how much further tightening 
may be needed, the balance between supply- and demand-side 
drivers of inflation, and how restrictive the current monetary 
policy stance and financial conditions are.

• 
CPI expectations declined across most groups in the Q3 survey, 
and long-term inflation expectations among economists and 
social partners are close to pre-pandemic levels.  
• 
Business leaders remain the outlier, with inflation expectations 
still elevated. Norges Bank pays particular attention to this 
group because their role as price-setting firms may provide an 
indication of future domestic inflation. 
• 
Average wage growth for 2026 was revised down to 4.3%, 
just below Norges Bank’s estimate and the wage norm of 4.4%. 
Meanwhile, growth in average quarterly earnings eased 
somewhat in Q3, pointing to softer near-term wage pressure.  
• 
In 2027, average wage growth was revised down to 3.8%, 
below Norges Bank’s 4.0% projection. Nevertheless, slower 
wage growth should help ease domestic cost pressures and 
support further disinflation next year.
