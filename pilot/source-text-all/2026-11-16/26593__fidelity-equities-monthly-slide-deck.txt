AI demand persists, but physical bottlenecks could stretch the investment cycle
AI wobble about deployment, not demand
Trading desk 
update 
October marked a broadening in market leadership as crowded AI and mega-cap positions corrected, while 
defensive, value and previously lagging areas gained ground. The next phase is likely to reward companies with 
resilient earnings, disciplined capital allocation and clear returns on investment, rather than exposure to structural 
growth themes alone.
The UK discount is 
becoming hard to 
ignore
UK equities offer diversification from concentrated global markets, with broadening leadership and greater 
exposure to asset-backed businesses creating opportunities for stock pickers. Attractive valuations, improving 
sentiment and strong shareholder returns strengthen the case for revisiting underweight UK equity allocations.
Market review 
– US, Europe
US equities declined in October as investors rotated out of AI-linked growth stocks into value and defensive 
sectors. The S&P 500 fell, while the Russell 1000 Value Index advanced, as a broad technology sell-off weighed 
on sentiment. European equities advanced in October, outperforming US and global markets as positive earnings 
and strength in energy and defensive sectors offset late-month volatility. 
Market review 
– Japan, UK
Japanese equities weakened in October as concerns over the sustainability and financing of AI investment 
triggered profit-taking in crowded technology stocks. Higher global bond yields and renewed inflation concerns 
added to risk aversion. UK equities rose in October, with the FTSE All Share gaining 1.1% as resilient activity and 
corporate earnings supported sentiment. 
Market review 
– EM, APxJ 
EM equities fell sharply in October, underperforming developed markets as risk aversion equities fell 0.9% in 
September. Middle East tensions added to inflation and policy concerns, while AI and semiconductor stocks 
provided support. APxJ equities fell 6.9% in October as concerns over the sustainability and returns of AI 
investment triggered a broad technology sell-off.

(FY26)
