We update following Zealand’s 3Q26 earnings report where the 
key update was a modest reduction of FY26 operating expense 
guidance with the mid point of the range now 5% lower than 
previously. During the quarter Zealand concluded its $200mn share 
buyback program and has a cash position of DKK 13.5bn, with a net 
cash inflow of $400mn from Roche expected in 4Q26 ($575mn 
Phase 3 milestone partly offset by a $175mn combination 
contribution payment). Overall, the earnings update was uneventful 
with financials of limited importance to the equity story at present. 
In the context of the pipeline for which timelines remain on track 
with the petrelintide monotherapy Phase 3 trial initiated with 
partner Roche and Phase 2 combination with enicepatide expected 
to be initiated in 4Q26. Whilst the obesity pipeline is the key investor 
focus and basis for our positive view on the stock, the near-term 
catalyst is likely to be European approval of GLP-2 analog 
glepaglutide for short bowel syndrome (SBS) following its CHMP 
recommendation. Zealand management continue to target a 
partnership deal for the drug’s commercialization and we believe 
European approval could remove a gating factor ahead of Phase 3 
data for US approval. We continue to view glepaglutide as 
differentiated relative to standard of care in SBS and believe the 
opportunity is underappreciated. 
Pipeline updates: Partner Boehringer Ingelheim is moving forward 
with global regulatory submissions in 2027 (as announced this 
week). BI is broadening the franchise with a new Phase 3 trial in type 
2 diabetes and three planned Phase 3b studies 
(SYNCHRONIZE-START, SYNCHRONIZE-HERA, and ELEVATE-LIVER) 
implementing flexible, patient centered dose escalation. For 
petrelintide, Zealand and partner Roche have initiated the global 
registrational Phase 3 ZUPREME program (comprising ZUPREME-3, 
ZUPREME-4, and ZUPREME-5) evaluating petrelintide monotherapy 
and initiation of Phase 2 ZYNERGY combination trial of petrelintide 
with dual GLP-1/GIP agonist CT388 remains on track for 4Q26.

Key takeaways from the call 
 
 
Survodutide’s MASH profile could broaden its differentiation in obesity as well 
n
as support a standalone liver opportunity. Management sees positive MASH data 
as additional reason to prescribe survodutide in obesity, noting its effects on liver fat 
and relatively limited muscle mass loss. The Phase 3 LIVERAGE interim analysis is 
expected in 2027, while newer trials incorporate more flexible dose escalation to 
potentially improve tolerability compared to the original SYNCHRONIZE program. 
Potential differentiation for petrelintide could extend beyond weight loss with 
n
bone mass preservation. Management highlighted bone mass preservation as 
relevant during weight loss and indicated that early amylin class data have increased 
interest in this potential benefit. They also confirmed interest in long acting amylins 
as part of its earlier stage obesity pipeline. Management highlighted that amylins are 
not insulin secreting, potentially explaining the step-down from 10.7% weight loss in 
ZUPREME-1 to 9.2% at an earlier 28 week timepoint in ZUPREME-2. Management 
views this as encouraging but noted that confirmation is required in the Phase 3 T2D 
and non-T2D populations.  
Partnerships remain a key focus across pipeline assets. 1) For glepaglutide, 
n
following the positive CHMP opinion and continued EASE-5 recruitment, 
management highlighted significant partner interest and noted the benefit of having 
a global partner ahead of a US launch. Management sees the existing short bowel 
syndrome market at >$1bn, with potential for glepaglutide to expand the market 
given its clinical profile and twice-weekly administration; and 2) For ZP9830, 
management expressed interest in early partnership following Phase 1b psoriasis 
data for broader and faster development across the autoimmune opportunity. 
 Management sees positioning for petrelintide monotherapy as a first choice 
n
treatment and the combination aimed at patients requiring the highest weight 
loss. Management continues to position petrelintide monotherapy around double 
digit weight loss with placebo like tolerability, while the petrelintide/CT-388 
combination could address patients requiring greater weight loss or with established 
obesity related comorbidities. Management did not provide a weight loss target or 
timing for ZYNERGY data.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
