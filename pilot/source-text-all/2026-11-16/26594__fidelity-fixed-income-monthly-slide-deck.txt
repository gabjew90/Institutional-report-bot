Midterms sharpen the focus on fiscal risk

Fixed Income Monthly
2
Executive summary
 
October highlighted the difficult balance between resilient growth, sticky inflation and tighter financial conditions, while the Democratic midterm victory added a 
new layer of uncertainty around the US fiscal, trade and regulatory outlook. In contrast to equities, fixed income markets have reacted little so far, but the scale of 
the Democrat gains could prove more consequential over time, particularly through next year's budget and debt-ceiling negotiations and potential implications for 
data-centre investment.
Government bonds rallied strongly after September's sell-off, with the US 10-year Treasury yield falling around 35bps to 4.94%. But the move reflected safe-haven 
demand amid weakness in technology and concerns around AI investment more than a decisive improvement in the inflation outlook.
The US economy remains resilient, with private demand holding up and little evidence yet of a slowdown sufficient to return inflation sustainably to target. Recent 
PCE revisions do little to alter that picture, while higher energy prices add further inflation risk. At the same time, tentative signs of consumer weakness need 
confirmation from official data, with some uncertainty over the extent to which World Cup-related effects have distorted recent activity.
Markets may therefore be underestimating the scope for further Fed tightening. The Fed remained on hold in October, but persistent inflation and firm demand 
leave our December hike call intact and could ultimately require more tightening than currently priced. The midterms do not materially alter that near-term view, 
although they increase medium-term fiscal uncertainty and could complicate the policy backdrop further.
We have trimmed long-duration exposure, particularly in Europe, while retaining a modest long rates bias. AI-related equity weakness has tightened financial 
conditions and restored some diversification benefits to government bonds, but yields now appear closer to levels justified by the macro backdrop. The key 
question is whether weakness in risk assets broadens into the economy or resilient activity and inflation again push yields higher.
Europe faces its own difficult mix. Higher energy prices, euro weakness and persistent inflation keep pressure on the ECB, while French fiscal and political 
concerns have driven substantial sovereign spread widening. We have therefore reduced European duration and favour greater differentiation across countries 
rather than treating euro area rates as a homogeneous exposure.
Credit is finally showing strong dispersion after impressive resilience during the rates sell off. As a result, we have taken action on the portfolios both in terms of 
credit beta and selection in credit quality. 
Overall, we favour selective risk-taking while awaiting greater clarity on growth, inflation and policy. Duration retains value as protection against a sharper 
slowdown, while wider credit dispersion is creating opportunities for active investors without requiring a broad increase in portfolio risk.
Marion Le Morhedec 
Global CIO, Fixed Income

Strategy summary
We remain modestly long duration overall, but have taken some profits following October's rates rally. We move to a neutral overall stance on Global IG, as sound corporate fundamentals 
and attractive all-in yields are balanced by tight valuations, growing AI-related supply and increased regional dispersion. While we are constructive on local duration and EM FX in the 
longer-term, we move to a cautious stance given near-term given headwinds including persistent dollar strength.
7
Macro and unconstrained
We remain modestly long duration overall, but have taken some profits following October's rates rally. We continue to favour Australian and UK government bonds, whilst maintaining 
selective exposure to German Bunds and underweight positions in France and Italy. We remain long US inflation, as the rise in energy and refinery costs, combined with the risks of 
renewed food inflation, could feed through into broader prices. We have reduced exposure across selected EM rates and FX while retaining a long US dollar bias. We also closed our 
remaining yen and Australian dollar shorts, and remain cautious on credit given current valuations.
8
Investment grade credit
We move to a neutral overall stance on Global IG, as sound corporate fundamentals and attractive all-in yields are balanced by tight valuations, growing AI-related supply and increased 
regional dispersion. We favour cross-market relative-value opportunities and security selection over broad credit exposure. Selective opportunities have emerged in European financials 
and corporate hybrids following recent spread widening. We remain cautious on Euro IG, favouring selective government-spread exposure over broad corporate credit risk. We maintain a 
balanced outlook on Sterling IG and remain selective in US and Asia IG.
9
High yield
We maintain a slightly negative stance on US HY, as tight valuations and rising financing needs outweigh support from higher income, solid earnings and controlled leverage. We maintain 
a slightly negative stance on European HY, as rising rates, a difficult macro backdrop and a heavy new-issue pipeline outweigh support from higher yields, subdued defaults and earnings. 
We maintain a neutral stance on Asian HY, where attractive carry, improving valuations and generally healthy credit fundamentals are balanced by Treasury volatility and the need for 
selectivity.
10
Emerging markets
We maintain a neutral stance in hard currency sovereigns and corporates, focusing on selective opportunities rather than broad beta exposure amid uncertainty and richer valuations. While 
we are constructive on local duration and EM FX in the longer-term, we move to a cautious stance given near-term given headwinds including persistent dollar strength.
