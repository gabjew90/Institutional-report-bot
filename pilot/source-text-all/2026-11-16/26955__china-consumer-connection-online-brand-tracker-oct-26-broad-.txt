We summarize the Oct updates from our Online Brand Tracker. Key highlights: 
1. Category performance: In Oct, most sectors we track saw muted growth - 
across Tmall/Taobao/JD combined, Pet Foods/Dairy led at +12%/+3% yoy 
growth, while Beauty/Sportswear were flat yoy. On the other hand, 
Supplements/Small kitchen appliances/Women’s clothing/IMF/Beer/White 
goods/Sports shoes lagged at 3%/6%/11%/14%/19%/21%/24% yoy declines. 
2. Domestic vs. MNC brands:  
In cosmetics, we note overall growth in Oct remained muted while select MNC 
and local names remained solid, echoing the trend from Day 1 rankings. Local 
players continued to record divergent performance, favoring local leaders: 
Maogeping led growth despite more disciplined pricing, followed by Forest Cabin 
with both continuing to benefit from growing prestige momentum. Proya Group saw 
stable growth at 7% yoy, driven by the recovery of the Proya core brand and 
sustained multi-brand momentum with Flowerknows/Insbaha/Off & Relax at DD% 
growth. Other domestic names recorded declines. MNCs posted softer Tmall yoy 
growth, though we suggest looking at Oct + Nov combined; leading western 
MNCs still outperformed based on our channel checks: Amore led growth off an 
easy comparison base. Estee Lauder Group saw 4% yoy growth, thanks to premium 
brands including the Estee Lauder brand/La Mer. L’Oreal Group grew 2% as strong 
performance of HR/Skinceuticals was offset by declines in mass brands. Other 
Japan/Korean MNCs including Shiseido, Kose, LG H&H were under pressure. Within 
our cosmetics monthly tracker, we also included Douyin growth & platform 
discount comparisons where MNC/leading local brands outperformed on Douyin, 
supported by greater leverage of top-tier KOLs and higher discounts.

outperform, while Lululemon and MAIA Active gained momentum, but Descente, 
Arc’teryx and Kolon weakened. ON slowed to broadly flat, while Asics and Onitsuka

Pet food 
In Oct, overall pet food online GMV saw 20% yoy growth, accelerating from 11% yoy in 
3Q26. Douyin continued to be the core driver at 40% yoy while Taobao/Tmall saw 16% 
yoy, both accelerating vs. 3Q26. JD saw a narrower decline at 1% vs. 10% in 3Q26. China 
Pet Foods led growth among covered names while other local brands/MNC names 
accelerated in Oct. Among covered local brands, China Pet Foods continues to sustain 
strong momentum, with 29% yoy growth in Oct driven by Wanpy at 45% growth. On the 
other hand, Gambol saw greater pressure into Oct with 26% decline yoy, dragged by 
47% decline for Fregate due to persisting pressure from negative news. Petpal remained 
weak at 7% yoy decline in Oct. Meanwhile, other local brands saw growth acceleration, 
averaging 28% in Oct vs. 22% in 3Q26, led by Rosy Fresh/Nourse at 85%/61% yoy. MNC 
names also recorded strong performance across the board with Royal Canin leading at 
40% in Oct, while other players saw DD% growth as well. 
More details within our pet food monthly online tracker. 
 
Sporting Goods 
Looking at Tmall, JD and Douyin combined, October readings pointed to greater 
divergence in brand performance, with softer momentum across several brands. Within 
mass sports, Anta returned to growth and FILA stabilized following a decline in 
September. In contrast, Nike remained under pressure, while Li Ning, Puma and Xtep 
softened. Performance across other outdoor brands was mixed - Salomon continued to 
outperform, while Lululemon and MAIA Active gained momentum, but Descente, 
Arc’teryx and Kolon weakened. ON slowed to broadly flat, while Asics and Onitsuka Tiger 
turned negative following growth in September. Saucony remained weak with sales 
continuing to decline. We note that the timing of Singles’ Day promotions vary across 
brands and therefore recommend assessing October and November sales together to 
better gauge underlying momentum. We also flag that brands have been executing 
omni-channel strategies in both online (e.g., emerging channels like Dewu) and offline, 
suggesting that the sales data collected by data vendors may not reconcile with actual 
growth.

Appendix: Tracker details and data source 
 
 
Online sales data is from Moojing, a source that tracks data through web crawler 
technology on the Alibaba/JD platforms. It includes the Tmall, Taobao and JD platforms 
through its industry data function, and provides store sales data for selective brands 
that we closely monitor through its store monitor function. This data set includes a wide 
range of consumer product categories such as baby products, supplements, cosmetics, 
sportswear/shoes, luxury items, home appliances, alcohol, and F&B. 
The brand/store/industry monitor captures all the relevant SKUs sold on the Alibaba/JD 
platforms, and the sales value data is calculated with sales volume and price shown on 
the platforms. However, there could be differences between actual numbers and 
estimations from Moojing. 
We also note that online penetration has been increasing over the past several years, 
and could account for up to 30%-35% of overall sales (diapers, infant formula, etc.) and 
an average of 10%-15% for most categories based on Euromonitor data. Hence, we 
believe online sales trends will be an increasingly important indicator for overall sales 
trends in the Consumer industry. In addition, the online market share gain/loss for each 
brand is also a reflection of the company’s specific strategy and key SKU performance. In 
our brand tracker, we highlight overall category growth as well as the performance of 
top players/brands and market share trends.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
