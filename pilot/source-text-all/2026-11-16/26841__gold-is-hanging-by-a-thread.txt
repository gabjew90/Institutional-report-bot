Gold's make-or-break moment
Gold is back testing the major trendline in place since March 2024. Momentum remains ugly, with
prices trading well below the 200-day MA, which has now turned lower (no bueno). A close below the
$3,900 area would leave little meaningful support until $3,400. This is a big technical test, with
potentially significant downside if support breaks.

Critical long-term levels
Gold's weekly chart shows the 100-week MA coming in right around current levels. The uber-long-term
200-week MA sits all the way down near $3,000. A critical test of long-term support, with a massive gap
to the next major moving average.

Gold vs. the dollar
The inverse relationship between gold and the dollar remains firmly intact. The latest leg higher in DXY
is weighing heavily on gold. Forget about a meaningful gold bounce as long as the dollar stays well bid.

The positioning reset

The latest CFTC report shows managed money gold longs running at relatively subdued levels
compared with the past two years. The speculative long liquidation has already been substantial,
raising the question of how much additional selling remains.

China's gold buyers turn sellers
Goldman's McGeoch flags a shift in Chinese speculative flows, with investors now selling gold after
previously buying the metal against copper shorts. US rate hike concerns are part of the story, but
lingering worries about Chinese demand appear to be the bigger driver. Onshore feedback suggests
little improvement before Q2 2027, absent meaningful policy stimulus. The Chinese bid is fading,
and the winter outlook is looking increasingly fragile.
Where are the buyers?
Gold remains high on investors' long-term wish lists, but Goldman reports little urgency to buy. ETF
demand has pulled back, open interest has declined approximately 6%, and the commodity desk is
seeing limited client re-engagement. Two significant attempts to rebuild options exposure earlier this
year ultimately failed as rates and oil moved against the metal. Investors still want to own gold. They
just don't want to own it today.
Gold's volatility warning
Gold volatility is spiking as prices sell off further, reversing the metal's usual volatility dynamics.
Investors are getting nervous and paying up for downside protection, driving vols higher.

The crowded short
The bearish gold trade is becoming increasingly crowded. Goldman's commodity desk says its CTA
model is close to maximum short exposure, with the pain trade now likely higher. The trend may still
favor the bears, but positioning is becoming a dangerous reason to press the short.
