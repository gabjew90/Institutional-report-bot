Existing Home Sales Tumbled in October; 
Higher Mortgage Rates Remain a Headwind 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Headlines 
 Existing home sales dropped 5.5% in October, the largest decline since 
January. Sales were down 8.8% from the same time last year. 
 
 With sales down and inventories up, the inventory-to-sales ratio rose to 
5.1 months in October and is now above its pre-pandemic level. 
 
 Annual house price growth slowed to 0.4% from 1.5% in September, with 
the largest annual increase in the undersupplied Northeast. 
 
 Looking ahead, PNC expects elevated mortgage rates to continue 
weighing on existing home sales in the near term. 
 
Details
Total existing home sales fell 5.5% to a seasonally 
adjusted annual rate of 3.75 million units in October, 
according to the National Association of Realtors 
(NAR). This was below PNC’s forecast for a decline 
to 3.88 million. 
 
Elevated mortgage rates continue to weigh on 
housing demand. The average 30-year fixed 
mortgage rate was 7.33% in October, according to 
Freddie Mac, up from 6.86% last month and 6.25% a 
year ago. 
 
Regionally, existing home sales declined in the West 
(9.7%), South (6.5%) and Midwest (3.2%), while sales 
were unchanged in the Northeast. Compared with a 
year ago, sales declined in all four regions, led by a 
14.5% drop in the West. Sales were down 8.2% in the 
Midwest, 8.0% in the South, and a more modest 4.1% 
in the Northeast. 
 
Existing single-family home sales dropped 5.8% from 
September, while condo and co-op sales fell 2.9%. 
On a year-over-year basis, both categories posted 
declines, with condo and co-op sales down a 
steeper 10.5%. 
 
The inventory of existing homes available for sale 
increased about 1.3% from a month earlier to 1.59 
million units (not seasonally adjusted). Inventory has 
trended higher over the past few years and has 
risen sharply since early 2026, although it remains 
low by historical standards.

With sales dropping and inventory increasing in 
October, the supply of existing homes on the market, 
measured by months' supply at the current sales 
pace, rose to 5.1 months. The inventory-to-sales ratio 
has risen substantially from its record low of 1.6 
months in early 2022 and now stands above its pre-
pandemic level. 
 
The median existing-home price increased 0.4% 
year-on-year to $416,700 in October (not seasonally 
adjusted). Although price growth slowed from the 
1.5% increase in September, home prices have risen 
annually for 40 consecutive months. 
 
The Northeast, where housing supply remains 
particularly constrained, posted the strongest home 
price appreciation among the regions, with the 
median sales price up 3.2% from a year earlier. The 
Midwest followed with a 1.5% increase. Limited new 
home construction continues to support price 
growth in both regions. In September, the Northeast 
and the Midwest accounted for just 9.3% and 12.9%, 
respectively, of total U.S. housing starts. By contrast, 
the median home price slipped 0.4% in the South 
and was unchanged in the West. 
 
Housing affordability has improved somewhat over 
the past three years as mortgage rates retreated 
from their peak of 7.6% in late 2023 and household 
incomes 
continued 
to increase. 
Nevertheless, 
affordability remains near historical lows in 2026. 
Home prices continue to rise, income growth has 
moderated, and mortgage rates have moved 
noticeably higher this year. 
 
Looking ahead, PNC expects elevated mortgage 
rates to continue weighing on existing home sales in 
the near term. The average 30-year fixed mortgage 
rate climbed to 7.33% in October, the highest since 
November 2023. Meanwhile, labor market softening 
has contributed to slower income growth, while 
inflation has accelerated. Lower household savings, 
slower wage growth, and higher inflation are likely 
to constrain households' ability to accumulate down 
payments, limiting support for home purchases in 
the near term.
