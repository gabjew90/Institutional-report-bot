Europe Open - Unplugged
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Asia stock sentiment skews net weaker as Nvidia jitters loom and Microsoft application outage not helping. US equity
futures fatigued to end the week, with SPX e-minis trading close to 7600 support. Overnight bonds played catch up to
bearish winds with USD and UST drifting otherwise. USDJPY knee-jerk move once again - volumes are elevated but
suggests jitters instead of action. Finmin Katayama warned of sharp, one-sided moves, but no further change in
guidance. 160 remains key resistance. 
US PPI the main event of the day where our economists are currently expecting final demand at 0.3% MoM. Details
which matter for PCE will be closely watched. Heavy Fedspeak calendar with Goolsbee, Collins, Logan, Collins again,
Bowman and Daly. In EM, watch out for Poland GDP and Brazil retail sales.

In Focus‌
Equities: Mixed performance overnight but general trend is weaker, HK/China stocks lead losses but Taiex holding gains
(+1.35%). Microsoft Outlook outage continues, while Nvidia jitters persist, both are not helping tech sentiment. US equity
futures tad weaker, but weakness has stalled as SPX e-minis traded towards 7600. This is the key level to watch into the
close this week. Our European equity trading strategist still recommends CGDOSQUB Index, a basket of stocks that won't
benefit from further AI pain especially as they can't withstand broader economic pain. Dig deeper.
Rates: JGB/AU/NZ bonds caught up on bearish moves but UST moves largely calm. Directionless session with duration
oscilatting sideways, our Tokyo desk saw better buying in the short end and intermediates, while volumes ran steadily at 0.8x-
1x recent averages throughout the session. US PPI in focus after stronger CPI - our economists expect Final Demand a 0.3%
MoM vs 1.3% prior with ex Food, Energy and Trade at 0.3% MoM (vs 0.5% prior). Core PCE is currently tracking at 0.33%
MoM but we are closely watching details within PPI such as airfares, medical services and portfolio fees. 30y auction due

later. Heavy Fedspeak schedule starting with Goolsbee (13:30), Collins at 14:10 GMT followed by Logan (14:40), Bowman
(15:50) and Daly at 17:00. 
FX: USDJPY saw another slight knee-jerk move as it hovered above 160, with our eTrading desk seeing 2-3x volumes above
30d average in the last hour. Finmin Katayama was on the wires overnight saying that "we're currently seeing sharp, one-
sided moves in the FX market...we'll continue to work closely with the US Treasury and respond appropriately". 160 appears
as the clear line in the sand. G10 pairs otherwise mostly flat but EURUSD did make fresh YTD lows at 1.1048.  ICYMI, FX
Strategy re-entered short EURNOK given the risk of re-escalation. While we didn't get to their preferred sell levels of 10.70,
they see current levels enough, especially as the third US aircraft carrier, USS Roosevelt, enters the region. 
Commodities: Gold took center stage on Thu but has modestly recovered overnight, trading at $3900 at print. This remains
the key support level where a more decisive break could drive a deeper correction. However, we would need to see
meaningful geopolitical re-escalation and still expect a gradual return of physical demand, limiting further losses. Oil prices
drift slightly higher. New Oil Monitor highlights that if a US-Iran deal is not reached in the coming week or two, Iran's
incentives increasingly shift toward breaking and/or circumventing the “Iron Gate” blockade rather than waiting for a better
negotiating environment.
Elsewhere‌
EUR: ECB's Simkus and Schnabel are speaking at print, with Zigman expected to follow at 08:30 GMT. Eurozone GDP SA
YoY for Q3 (second estimate), Trade Balance SA for September are due at 10:00 GMT. On Thu, Le Point reported that French
PM Macron and German leader Merz agreed that de Cos should succeed Lagarde as ECB President. These reports were
later denied by the German government. We continue to keep a close eye. 
RON: Q3 GDP printed at 2.1% QoQ, sharply higher than Citi and consensus forecasts at 0.5% and stronger than flat prior.
We await our economists' detailed take. 
PLN: GDP SA QoQ at 08:30 GMT for Q3 (preliminary). Our economists forecast growth in Q3 moderated to 3.5% YoY (down
from 4.1% in Q2), though risks remain skewed to the upside. This puts the Q3 growth slightly below the full-year forecast of
3.6%, supported by strong H1 2026.
BRL: Retail Sales YoY at 12:00 GMT for September. Our economists forecast retail sales, shrinking by 0.4% QoQ in 3Q26.
Assets underperformed Thu on single stock weakness and fiscal developments. President Lula is reportedly eyeing additional
budget measures to improve fiscal results to around 80bn instead ($14.2bn).
