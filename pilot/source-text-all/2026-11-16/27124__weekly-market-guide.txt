Weekly Market Guide
Waves of Rotation Keep the Market Choppy: The dominant theme for equities this year has been aggressive sector rotation,
contributing to continued choppiness across equities. While the S&P 500 remains near its highs, nearly one-third of its
constituents are in bear market territory, highlighting the significant bifurcation beneath the surface. Leadership has repeatedly
shifted between the AI complex and the broader market, creating considerable volatility beneath an index that has largely moved
sideways.
The post-election reaction has intensified these rotations, with consumer-oriented sectors, particularly Consumer Discretionary,
emerging as some of the biggest beneficiaries, while the AI and data center ecosystem has absorbed the brunt of the selling
pressure. The unwind in large-cap growth has coincided with improving relative performance in value-oriented areas, while
small-cap stocks have demonstrated notable resilience. Importantly, we are not seeing evidence of broad-based capital flight
from equities. Instead, capital is rotating aggressively between themes and sectors, suggesting that underlying risk appetite
remains intact despite the volatility.
Avoid Knee-Jerk Reactions to the Election: Political events often trigger emotional market reactions, and this election has
been no exception. However, long-term fundamentals rarely change overnight. While potential policy shifts may influence the
timing and pace of investment, the underlying drivers of economic growth, corporate earnings, and innovation remain far
more important than election headlines. The debate surrounding AI infrastructure and data center development is a prime
example. Concerns over permitting, regulation, and project approvals have weighed heavily on sentiment post-midterms, yet the
structural demand remains firmly intact. In fact, a more measured pace of development could ultimately extend the investment
cycle, mitigate the risk of overbuilding, and improve free cash flow generation for hyperscalers. With substantial projects already
approved or under construction, we believe the long-term AI infrastructure investment thesis remains intact despite
heightened political scrutiny.
Semiconductors have borne the brunt of the post-election correction, with selling pressure particularly acute among AI and data
center beneficiaries. Despite the severity of the drawdown, earnings visibility over the next one to two years remains strong,
supported by healthy backlogs and sustained demand for AI-related infrastructure. Meanwhile, sentiment appears increasingly
stretched, with semiconductors trading near one standard deviation below their historical average distance from the 200-day
moving average.

WEEKLY MARKET GUIDE
 
Technical Backdrop Improving, but Not Yet an All-Clear Signal: The S&P 500 successfully held above its 200-day moving average again last week. The subsequent rebound has been encouraging,
particularly following last Thursday's broad distribution day. However, we would stop short of declaring an all-clear signal. While market breadth has improved, upside participation has yet to
demonstrate the type of breadth thrust typically associated with a clearing event. Importantly, despite the recent weakness, RSI never reached oversold territory, and the index has continued
to hold above its 200-day moving average. Taken together, these conditions suggest the underlying bullish trend remains intact, even as the market continues to work through near-
term volatility and leadership rotation.
Lower Oil Prices and Interest Rates Provide Relief: Prospects for a quicker resolution to the conflict with Iran have helped push energy prices lower, providing an additional tailwind for equities.
Declining oil prices have eased inflation concerns and contributed to some relief in longer-term Treasury yields. This combination has improved the backdrop for interest-rate-sensitive and
consumer-oriented businesses, helping support the recent rotation toward cyclical and value-oriented segments of the market.
Bottom Line: The overarching backdrop remains constructive. The economy continues to demonstrate resilience, corporate earnings remain healthy, and forward earnings expectations continue
to support a favorable long-term outlook. While political uncertainty may warrant somewhat higher risk premiums and create near-term volatility, it does not fundamentally alter the broader
earnings story. For now, the market remains defined by waves of rotation rather than broad-based weakness. Leadership has shifted away from Technology and AI in recent weeks, but history
suggests leadership cycles rarely move in a straight line. As a result, diversification, balance, and a focus on quality remain critical. Rather than reacting emotionally to election-driven headlines,
investors should remain focused on fundamentals and be prepared to take advantage of opportunities created by periods of excessive pessimism and market dislocation.

Index Definitions
The S&P 500 is an unmanaged index of 500 widely held stocks that is generally considered representative of the U.S. stock market.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA) is a price-weighted average of 30 significant stocks traded on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and the NASDAQ.
The NASDAQ Composite is a stock market index of the common stocks and similar securities listed on the NASDAQ stock market.
The MSCI World All Cap Index captures large, mid, small and micro-cap representation across 23 Developed Markets (DM) countries. With 11,732 constituents, the index is
comprehensive, covering approximately 99% of the free float-adjusted market capitalization in each country.
The MSCI EAFE (Europe, Australasia, and Far East) is a free float-adjusted market capitalization index that is designed to measure developed market equity performance,
excluding the United States & Canada. The EAFE consists of the country indices of 21 developed nations.
The MSCI Emerging Markets Index is designed to measure equity market performance in 23 emerging market countries. The index's three largest industries are materials,
energy, and banks.
The Russell 2000 index is an index measuring the performance of approximately 2,000 smallest-cap American companies in the Russell 3000 Index, which is made up of 3,000
of the largest U.S. stocks.
The NYSE Alerian MLP is the leading gauge of energy infrastructure Master Limited Partnerships (MLPs). The capped, float-adjusted, capitalization-weighted index, whose
constituents earn the majority of their cash flow from midstream activities involving energy commodities, is disseminated real-time on a price-return basis (AMZ) and on a
total-return basis (AMZX).
The Barclays Intermediate Government/Credit Bond index measures the performance of U.S. Dollar denominated U.S. Treasuries, government-related and investment grade
U.S. corporate securities that have a remaining maturity of greater than one year and less than ten years.

The Euro Stoxx 50 Index is a market capitalization weighted stock index of 50 large, blue-chip European companies operating within Eurozone nations. Components are
selected from the Euro STOXX Index which includes large-, mid- and small-cap stocks in the Eurozone.
The China CSI 300 is a capitalization-weighted stock market index designed to replicate the performance of top 300 stocks traded in the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock
exchanges. It had a sub-indexes CSI 100 Index and CSI 200 Index.
The S&P 500 Futures is a capitalization-weighted index of 500 stocks. The index is designed to measure performance of the broad domestic economy through changes in the
aggregate market value of 500 stocks representing all major industries.
The DJIA Futures is a stock market index futures contract traded on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange`s Globex electronic trading platform. Dow Futures is based off the Dow
30 stock index.
The Nasdaq 100 Futures is a modified capitalization-weighted index of the 100 largest and most active non-financial domestic and international companies listed on the
NASDAQ.
Europe: DAX (Deutscher Aktienindex (German stock index)) is a blue chip stock market index consisting of the 40 major German companies trading on the Frankfurt Stock
Exchange.
Asia: Nikkei is short for Japan's Nikkei 225 Stock Average, the leading and most-respected index of Japanese stocks. It is a price-weighted index composed of Japan's top 225
blue-chip companies traded on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.
