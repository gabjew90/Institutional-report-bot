 
Silver Specs Have Hardly Ever Been This Short: Is 
The Squeeze Finally Here? 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, NOV 11, 2026 - 09:05 PM 
Silver bears have had a great autumn. This week they finally got a scare. 
When we last checked in on Goldman's Robert Quinn's running silver diary, the metal was "Still 
Stuck" at $57.48, specs had dumped $2.2 billion in three weeks, and we argued that the 
positioning setup "tends to end with a violent squeeze." Well, the squeeze took its sweet time: 
silver kept puking all the way into the midterms, briefly testing $50 in what our friends at The 
Market Ear dubbed a "Silver Bloodbath: From FOMO To Full-Blown Panic" (Oct 30). That is a 
cool 55%+ below the $121.67 record set on January 29. 
And then the election happened. In his latest note, titled "Silver Futures: Surviving the Hot CPI" 
(available to pro subs), Quinn sums up the past five weeks: 
"A month of selling entering the US midterm elections left Silver speculative positioning near a 
multiyear min. With the outcome boosting China Solar stocks and lowering the Dollar, Silver 
jumped ~6% over November 3rd - 9th. For at least one cohort, long buying potentially returned. 
The option surface certainly reflected a retracement from peak fear. At the moment, Silver has 
even survived a hot CPI report." 
Translation: the specs got about as short as they have been in two years, and the market has 
started making them pay for it. 
 

 
 
 "At the moment" is doing a lot of work in that last sentence, however, so let's take a closer look.

The Midterm Silver Lining: Beijing's Solar Stocks 
Then came November 3. The Democratic sweep, Quinn writes, "implied more favorable trade 
policies," and he reminds us that "the Trump administration targeted China Solar companies via 
tariffs." The result: "GS's China Solar basket surged 7.1%, overshadowing a drop in Data Center 
stocks - another less important structural demand indicator." Silver ripped roughly 6% over 
November 3-9, with the dollar's dip adding fuel. 
Recall that Quinn tracks two equity baskets as real-time proxies for silver's industrial demand: 
Solar (photovoltaics, one of silver's biggest end uses) and Data Centers (the AI buildout). Three 
weeks ago both were getting carted out on stretchers and dragging silver with them. This time 
they split, and silver followed the one that went up. And yes, the same election that sent China 
Solar flying is the one that sent data centers lower: as we noted in yesterday's market wrap, the 
political turn against data centers is now a very real headwind for the AI trade. Silver, it seems, 
would rather have Chinese solar panels than American server farms. 
 

 
Which brings us to the most interesting chart in the note. Quinn writes that "for at least one 
cohort, long buying potentially returned," pointing out that over the past six months "changes in 
Managed Money gross longs and China Solar were positively correlated" (chart source GS 
GBM, CFTC): 
 
The chart is striking: Managed Money gross longs have slid from roughly $12.5 billion at the 
December 2025 peak to around $4 billion as of November 3, with China Solar tracing a 
near-identical path lower from the low-70s to the high-30s (approximate). The post-election pop 
in the solar basket (back to the mid-40s) came after the COT cutoff, which is why Quinn hedges 
with "potentially." If the correlation holds, though, Friday's COT data should show hedge funds 
buying back some length. That's the tell to watch. 
Options Traders Stop Panicking (A Little) 
The options market, which three weeks ago was panic-bidding puts, has calmed down too. Per 
Quinn, "Silver 3 month normalized 25 delta put-call skew cheapened from the highest levels 
since Covid." 
Recall that in his last note, skew had hit a 98% one-year rank. It then kept going, and as 
Goldman's chart shows (chart source GS GSM as of November 10th), normalized skew spiked 
 

 
to roughly +4.5 in late October, the richest reading on the two-year chart and higher even than 
the July spike, before easing back toward zero (approximate): 
 
Meanwhile, 3-month at-the-money implied vol sits near 38-39, a far cry from the 100+ seen 
during January's parabolic melt-up. Put differently, the market is no longer paying up for crash 
protection, but it isn't paying up for upside either. That is the definition of a "retracement from 
peak fear": a little less fear, which is still quite a lot of fear. 
A Hot CPI, A Hawkish Fed... And A Bounce? 
Which brings us to yesterday. October CPI came in hot across the board, with headline 0.4% 
MoM vs 0.3% expected and core 0.3% vs 0.2%; and just 1 of 70 Wall Street forecasts was 
higher: 
Add in Waller openly floating a 50bps hike and Logan going full hawk, and the result was what 
one bond veteran described to us as "total f**king carnage": the 2Y yield spiked 20bps, its 
biggest daily jump since Liberation Day, and the dollar ripped to the top of its 21-month range, 
whacking gold back toward $4,000. Overnight, Goldman's economists threw in the towel too: 
This is exactly the kind of day that broke silver back in October, when the last hot CPI print sent 
the dollar flying and silver plunging 6% in a day. This time? "While Silver fell 2.8% on November 
 

 
10th, it rebounded ~3% in early November 11th trading." In other words, silver gave back one 
day and took it right back the next. When a metal can shake off a hot CPI, a 20bps rip in 2Y 
yields and a dollar at multi-month highs within 24 hours, that tells you most of the forced sellers 
are already gone. 
Not everyone has gotten the memo, of course. Bloomberg reports this morning that ETFs were 
buying gold and platinum but selling silver, while the dollar was gaining across the G-10 as 
hawkish Fed bets build (and have continued to do so this morning). Rate-insensitive silver is 
not; dollar-insensitive even less so. 
Too Short To Stay Stuck 
Time for the honest bookkeeping we promised. On October 21 we said we were sticking with 
the contrarian side "with a shorter fuse." The fuse turned out to be longer than we hoped: silver 
slid as much as ~13% more (to roughly $50) before the election finally lit it. But what Quinn 
describes now is, if anything, an even more lopsided setup than three weeks ago: spec net 
length in the 2nd percentile of the past two years, $2.6 billion of fresh shorts added in a month, 
and skew coming off its highest levels since Covid as panic hedges get monetized. 
The risks are obvious: a Fed that is now pricing three more hikes, a dollar at the top of its range, 
and an AI trade (and with it, silver's data center bid) under political assault. But silver no longer 
needs everything to go right. It just needs the dollar to stop going up and the solar trade to keep 
working, which, with Washington about to soften its stance on Chinese solar, looks far more 
likely than it did a week ago. We'll go a step further than Quinn's "at the moment": silver's ability 
to shake off a hot CPI is the first real sign that the bears have run out of ammunition, and the 
most crowded short in metals is about to get a lot less comfortable. 
Then again, silver bears have been comfortable for so long, they may need Friday's COT data 
to tell them otherwise. We'll check back then.
