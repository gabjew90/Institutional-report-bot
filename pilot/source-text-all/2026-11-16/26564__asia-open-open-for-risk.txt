Asia Open - Open for risk
By Preston Pagli
Despite thin holiday liquidity and the closure of US cash Treasury markets for Veterans Day, US equities staged a strong
rebound led by the AI complex, following Tuesday's modest pullback. NDX rose more than 2% on positive demand
signals following CoreWeave's beat-and-raise earnings and stronger OpenAI quarterly revenue. In FX, the dollar
remained well supported by Tuesday's hotter-than-expected CPI print, with DXY reaching its highest level since May
2025. USDJPY climbed just shy of 160, where intervention concerns typically emerge, while EURUSD fell to an 18-
month low. In commodities, crude retraced from intra-day highs following a de-escalatory WSJ report on diplomatic
Middle East efforts, while gold weakness continued, slipping below $4,000 once again. Looking ahead, liquidity will
return to Japan PPI at 23:50 GMT, a speech from the BoJ's Asada at 01:30 GMT, followed by a 5y JGB auction at 03:35
GMT.

In Focus‌
Equities: Following a modest pullback on Tuesday, headline US equity indices rebounded, led by the AI complex on a series
of supportive developments including CoreWeave's firm "beat-and-raise" earnings report, easing fears around AI demand,
and moving CoreWeave's bond yields below 15%. In addition, OpenAI generated $10bn in Q3 revenue (+50% q/q vs 17%
prior), while TSMC called chip demand “insatiable”. Despite light participation, vols remained firm against limited upside
demand. Flows were mixed with some tail hedge unwinds. Our markets trading strategy desk remains constructive on the
broader equity market as earnings and technicals remain constructive. For SPX, CTAs will look to flip back long SPX above
$7,676. 
FX: The dollar index hit its highest level since May 2025 following Tuesday's hotter than expected CPI report and mixed
headlines out of the Middle East. EURUSD dropped 0.70% to its lowest level since May 2025 at 1.108. The Aussie dollar
showed some resilience despite declining -0.25%, with our G10 FX Strategy desk remaining bearish AUD as headwinds
mount. The desk suggests the dovish RBA shift should open the way for further downside, as AUDUSD has broken below
0.69 with a move toward 0.6660 in play. Our strategists also look to fade any bounces in EURNOK. Volumes on the eFX
trading desk were 5% below average in light of the holiday in the US though USDJPY and USDTHB activity surged. Net
inflows were skewed toward CZK, GBP, NOK, and JPY. Outflows led in MXN. 

USDJPY hit its highest level since September at 159.82 with intervention risks top of mind. Yet with the yen stronger on a
trade-weighted basis than during July's intervention period and a potentially less sensitive US political backdrop, our trader
believes the risk of official action is not as acute as current levels may suggest.
Rates: US cash Treasury markets were closed Wednesday in observance of Veterans Day with the read through for
Thursday's open being the futures market where Treasury futures fell a few ticks, continuing Tuesday's post-CPI move. OIS
prices a 61% chance of a hike in January. 
Commodities: Crude oil rebounded after a sharp multi-day decline with Brent back to $92, though a WSJ report detailing that
US VP Vance is open to face-face talks with Iranian hardliner Rezaei took some air out of the upward move. Saudi Arabia
notified OPEC crude oil production surged 26% to one of the highest monthly levels in years. The precious metals complex
retreated further with gold back below $4,000 for the first time since last week.
Elsewhere‌
PLN was the weakest performer across EMEA with no concrete driver despite a lack of liquidity with both the US and Poland
on holiday. Our trader looks to tactically fade the move in EURPLN with a stop above the years highs at 4.4375. The MPC's
Maslowska said she would wait for a rate hike until January. 
BRL (+0.5%) rebounded following Tuesday's unwind as optimism arises surrounding the Governments fiscal adjustments for
2027 which would save 0.5% of GDP, moving to 1% and 1.25% in subsequent years. The fiscal package was sent to
congress late Wednesday with spending growth capped at 2% and a spending freeze even within the fiscal target. 
Ahead:
JPY: BoJ Board Member Asada speaks in Nagasaki at 01:30 GMT, followed by a 5-year bond sale at 03:35 GMT.
GBP: GDP QoQ at 07:00 GMT for Q3 (preliminary), alongside Industrial Production MoM and Industrial Production YoY for
September. Citi Economics expects Q3 growth printing at a resilient 0.6% QoQ. BoE Chief Economics Pill recently stated that
stalling growth could see a rate hold or even a cut, making downside surprises more pivotal.
EUR: ECB publishes its Economic Bulletin at 09:00 GMT.
RON: CPI MoM at 07:00 GMT for October, followed by the NBRO rate decision later in the day. Our economists expect YoY
inflation to rise to 6.9% in October, up from 6.6% in September. The NBR should keep the policy rate unchanged as inflation
remains elevated.
TRY: Turkey Central Bank publishes its Inflation Report at 07:30 GMT.
INR: CPI YoY at 10:30 GMT for October. Our economists look for headline inflation to advance to 5.9%YoY in October from
5.5%YoY in September.
