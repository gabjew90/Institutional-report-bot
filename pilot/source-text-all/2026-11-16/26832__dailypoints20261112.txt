The tech trade and Fed-speak are the main considerations as markets come back from 
Remembrance Day.  
US stock futures are broadly lower and led by the Nasdaq’s nearly 1% dip as markets await 
the fate of Nvidia’s Jensen Huang and possible implications for Trump’s coming visit to 
China next week given evidence of spying. European cash markets are broadly higher after 
a weak Asian session. Short-term US Treasury yields are higher by about 3bps as ongoing 
Fed-speak continues to be digested. Markets continue to price a few basis points over a 
quarter-point hike on December 9th although the only Fed-speaker who so far seems to 
support upsizing is Governor Waller (see below). The effect is continuing to spill into 
Canada with pricing for the December move on the fence at 13bps.  
Oil prices are little changed this morning. The dollar is mixed but the Mexican peso slid 
after Trump issued this social media threat to double unspecified tariffs against Mexico if 
it does not curtail caravans.  
MORE FED-SPEAK — LITTLE SUPPORT FOR UPSIZING 
More Fed-speak arrives today to further inform size and pace of future rate changes 
including the December 9th decision. We’ll hear from Cleveland’s Hammack (9amET), 
Governor Barr (10amET), Chicago’s Goolsbee (11:15amET), San Fran’s Daly (1:10pmET), 
KC’s Schmid (1:30pmET) and NY Fed President Williams (6:30pmET).

SOLID, BUT LAGGING UK READINGS 
The UK economy put in a decent performance in Q3 and the way the quarter ended but sterling and gilts largely ignored it as stale. GDP 
was up 0.7% q/q SA nonannualized, matching consensus. Growth drivers included consumer spending (0.6%), exports (1.6%) and 
investment (1.7%), while surging imports (1.8%) dented growth as a leakage effect from traditional GDP accounting. 
The quarter ended on a high note. GDP was up 0.2% m/m (0% consensus) with some help from downward revisions to August (0% instead 
of 0.1%). Industrial output was up 0.3% m/m, services output expanded 0.2%, construction was up 0.8%, but the trade deficit widened.
