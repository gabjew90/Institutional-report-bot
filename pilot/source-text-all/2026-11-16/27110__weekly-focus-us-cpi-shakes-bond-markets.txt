Macro Research 
 
   
A 22bp intra-day rise in US 2-year yields is not something you see every day. 
But Tuesday’s US CPI report coupled with hawkish comments from the Fed 
triggered a sharp sell-off in bond markets sending yields higher and EUR/USD 
to the lowest level in more than a year below 1.11. The CPI report showed core 
CPI rising 0.3% m/m for the third month in a row, and the annual increase 
moved from 2.6% y/y to 2.9% y/y challenging the Fed’s mission to get inflation 
back to 2%. Prior to the release Fed governor Christopher Waller said that the 
Fed “should consider a 50-basis-point hike [in December] if October CPI data 
matched the previous two monthly readings”. Following the report, Chicago 
Fed’s Golsbee called the CPI print “borderline disturbing”. Markets now price a 
13% probability of a ‘jumbo hike’ at the 9 December meeting. We look for a 
25bp hike followed by another 25bp hike in March. 
You would think equity markets would struggle in a week with such a sharp 
rise in yields. However, equities are currently driven by AI sentiment and 
following a sharp sell-off after the mid-term elections, AI sentiment 
recovered this week. Apart from being technically oversold, equities got help 
from a strong earnings report from CoreWeave, the neocloud company that 
recently caused jitters, and positive news on Anthropic’s IPO coming up next 
week, which is oversubscribed according to the company.  
Oil prices fluctuated this week first moving below USD90 per barrel before 
recovering to USD96 per barrel on Friday. US President Donald Trump said on 
Sunday it is ‘highly unlikely’ he will order more strikes on Iran and data showed 
OPEC production has increased back close to pre-war levels. However, Iran 
halted two oil supertankers on Thursday and Trump stated Washington no 
longer needs a deal with Iran, which put new upward pressure on oil prices.  
French government bond spreads to Germany tightened further this week  
to 153p after hitting 178bp on 22 October. The situation remains fragile, 
though. We had little data out of the Euro zone this week.  
In China, export data showed more strength with a 35.2% y/y increase in 
October, lifted by a 15% rise in export prices due to the leap in memory chip 
prices this year. The jump in chip prices feed into electronics prices globally.  
Next week the main event for markets will be the Anthropic IPO on 
Wednesday. Given the anxiety over AI lately, the IPO and how it trades in the 
coming weeks will be a litmus test of AI sentiment going into year-end. On the 
data front focus turns to US retail sales and Philly Fed, as well as German ZEW 
for November. Some indicators (such as metal price momentum) point to a 
roll-over in the global manufacturing cycle and the ZEW often gives a good 
indication of the direction for manufacturing. On the geopolitical front, China 
is hosting the annual APEC Summit in Shenzhen where Trump, Chinese 
president Xi Jinping and Russian leader Vladimir Putin are set to meet.

Market Movers Scandinavia 
 
 
• In Denmark, Friday will be an important day in terms of data releases. 
Firstly, Danish Q3 national accounts are released. Following an impressive 
GDP run with an average of 1% quarterly growth through the recent 6 
quarters, we expect GDP has come to a standstill. Industrial production has 
been close to unchanged in Q3 as a whole, and indicators suggest service 
sector production has not been strong either. Our own spending monitor 
suggests private spending increased by a solid 1.5% in Q3, but GDP growth 
is measured from the production side in the flash estimate. 
Also on Friday, we receive consumer confidence data for November. In 
October, confidence fell to a very low level of -17.2, as uncertainty and 
higher energy prices appeared to weigh on sentiment. While petrol and 
diesel prices have eased in November, electricity prices have remained 
elevated since the start of the month, partly reflecting less favourable 
weather conditions. Energy prices may therefore continue to weigh on 
confidence in November. Friday also brings the business sentiment 
indicator. 
 
• In Sweden, next week’s data highlight will be the LFS labour market release 
on Friday. Highly volatile monthly unemployment prints have been edging 
somewhat lower from recent highs near 9%. The more stable SPES 
unemployment measure has already marched lower for over a year, 
confirmed by the most recent print. Going forward, we expect LFS 
unemployment to follow its body double on its way down, along with higher 
employment and labour market participation. 
On Monday, Money market player’s inflation expectations will be released 
from Origo. 
 
 
 
• In Norway, the Norges Bank's increased focus on the importance of 
inflation expectations for actual inflation makes the Expectations Survey 
more important. In the previous round, the overall picture was that 
respondents expected lower price and wage growth than previously. But 
perhaps more important was that CEOs, who NB believes have the greatest 
impact on actual price developments, raised their price expectations both 
1 and 2 years ahead. These figures will therefore be central to NB's 
assessments of whether price expectations are on the rise or fall. In 
addition, we will keep an eye on wage expectations among labor market 
organizations, which in the previous round were below NB's estimates for 
both 2026 and 2027.

Scandi Update 
Denmark – No clear signs of second-round price effects 
In Denmark, CPI inflation came in at 2.2% in October. Higher energy prices 
continued to push inflation up, partly offset by lower food prices. Electricity 
prices rose sharply by 24.1% m/m, reflecting both higher market prices and 
the introduction of winter tariffs. Core inflation fell to 1.7% from 1.9% in 
September, with no clear signs of second-round effects so far. 
Total exports rose 4.4% m/m in September, led by a 6.1% increase in goods 
exports. Danish export growth remains strong and is largely driven by large 
companies, as documented by Statistics Denmark. One driver is the global 
buildout of AI data centres, particularly in the US. Goods exports to the US rose 
21.5% m/m in September, underlining the strong momentum. 
The current account surplus widened by DKK 4.8bn in September, mainly 
driven by a sharp increase in exports of goods that do not cross the Danish 
border. Over the course of the year, the services balance has also improved 
markedly, shifting from a deficit at the start of the year to a surplus, largely 
driven by sea transport. 
 
 
Sweden – Volatile holiday prices behind weak inflation  
Minutes from the Rikbank’s latest rate meeting revealed a slightly more 
hawkish state of mind among governors than was expected. There was broad 
support for the November hike, seen unanimously as a natural step towards 
rate normalization. Governors were less explicit and remain more divided on 
the next moves. However, governor Thedéen leans towards two hikes early 
next year to 2.50%, in line with our forecast. Read more here Riksbank Minutes 
– November 2026, 10 November.  
Final inflation data confirmed the downside surprise in October, with CPI at 
0.91% y/y and CPIF at 1.13% y/y. Details pointed to low package holiday prices 
as the single driver behind the deviation for services. Nevertheless, goods 
prices were soft across the board. Meanwhile, household consumption came 
in strong at an annual growth of 3.3% y/y in October. Private sector production 
printed weaker for September, with annual growth at 1.1%. While new orders 
were more bullish, some spots of weakness in manufacturing are visible

Norway – a mixed bag of news 
Core inflation dropped from 3.0% to 2.7% in October. As expected, the drop in 
annual core inflation mainly stems from lower imported inflation which 
seems to be due to base effects. In addition, domestic goods inflation was 
lower than expected whereas rent was a bit higher. Inflation seems to be 
trending downwards roughly as expected, but domestic (cost-driven) inflation 
remains too high, and will keep monetary policy restrictive for a period. 
Inflation is exactly in line with Norges Bank’s estimate from the September 
MPR and will in isolation be neutral ahead of the December meeting. 
According to the Q4 oil investment survey, oil companies expect investments 
at NOK 265.5bn in 2026 and NOK 236.6bn in 2027, signalling -5% nominal 
growth in 2026 and a -3% in 2027.  Importantly, to get the real figures (what 
Norges Bank projects) we need to adjust for a deflator which is tricky, but has 
been around 1% recently. If so, real growth in 2026 should be around -5.8% 
(NB: -3.5%) and 2027 around -4.5% (NB: -4.0%). On margin a bit lower for 2026, 
in line for 2027 but the figures nevertheless confirm our expectations that oil 
investments are about to turn into a headwind for the economy. 
The number of vacant positions increased from 87k to 89.5k in Q3, taking the 
vacancy rate from 2.7% to 2.8%. The figures illustrate that demand for labour 
remains strong, partly driven by the public sector, and that the labour market 
remains a bit tighter than expected.
