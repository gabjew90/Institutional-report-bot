Out of bonds, into stocks 
Bond funds reported outflows, while flows improved into stocks this past week ending 
on November 11. 
Inflows slowed for US HG bond funds and ETFs, with a +$1.92bn inflow down from a 
+$7.70bn inflow in the prior week. Since IG corporates account for about a third of the 
high grade universe AUM, that translates into ~$0.6bn demand for IG. That was driven 
by while flows turning negative for HG Funds (to -$4.43bn, the biggest outflow since 
April 2025, from +$1.59bn), since flows were steady into HG ETFs (to +$6.35bn from 
+$6.11bn). Inflows moderated for both short-term HG (to +$0.96bn from +$1.29bn) and 
ex. short-term (to +$0.96bn from +$6.40bn). 
Into loans and stocks 
Inflows accelerated for equities this past week (to +$51.63bn, the biggest inflow in 8 
weeks, from +$29.54bn), while inflows slowed for HY led by outflows from HY Funds (to 
+$0.61bn from +$1.55bn). Inflows were steady but moderate for loans (to +$0.11bn 
from +$0.13bn), while inflows slowed for munis (to +$0.41bn from +$1.61bn). Outflows 
grew for global EM bonds (to -$1.00bn, the biggest outflow since April 2026, from -
$0.47bn). Finally, money markets reported a -$18.39bn outflow this past week, following 
a +$94.46bn inflow a week earlier.

Appendix: defining high grade 
We define our high grade flows metric as a combination of “bond” and “corporate bond” 
fixed income funds and ETFs domiciled in the US (and investing in any currency, 
although the majority is invested in USD). We also exclude funds primarily focused on 
Government, HY, EM and MBS from the sample. The “bond” category refers to the broad 
high grade market, which includes Treasuries, mortgages and agencies in addition to 
corporate bonds. This category accounts for the majority of AUM and flows. On the 
other hand, the dedicated corporate bond funds and ETFs are much smaller in terms of 
assets under management. We also include the “total return” bond category in our 
tracking of high grade flows. Finally, note that “short-term” maturity refers to duration 
of 0 to 4 years. On average, IG corporate bonds account for about ~32% of the total 
AUM of our high grade bond fund / ETF universe.

Daily foreign demand tracker

for USD IG corporate bonds. Negative values for “net dealer buy” denote net buying by 
foreign investors (i.e. US dealers taking down inventory in favor of foreign affiliates).

Asian demand) while Exhibit 24 and Exhibit 25 show net dealer-to-affiliates volumes 
between 8am and noon (biased toward European demand). Exhibit 26 shows the 
relationship between foreign buying and change in treasury yields. A drop in yield 
typically leads to lower foreign demand, though not always.
