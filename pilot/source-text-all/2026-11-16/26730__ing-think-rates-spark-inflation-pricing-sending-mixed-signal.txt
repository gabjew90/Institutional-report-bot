Rates Spark: Inflation pricing sends
mixed signals
Markets are fearful of near-term inflation risks, reinforced by this week's
US CPI numbers, yet the structural outlook looks benign. We think
markets risk underestimating the possibility that mounting fiscal
pressures leave central banks having to tolerate higher inflation
Benign long-term inflation pricing seems to contrast with near-term inflation fears
Long-term inflation pricing is not reflecting upside risks
Markets are anxious about near-term inflation surprises but seem to view structural inflation
very benignly, a risk we see as possibly underpriced. Despite this week’s US inflation surprise
and still-high energy prices, long-term market measures of inflation expectations remain low
by historical standards. The 5Y5Y EUR inflation swap is just 2.07%, only slightly above the ECB’s
target. Before the global financial crisis, this number was often closer to 2.4%. For the US, the
difference is even bigger.
We have seen lower inflation swap rates since 2008, when inflation failed to return to 2%, but
we argue this should be seen as quite an exceptional period. Current growth dynamics remain
healthy and central banks have plenty of room to ease policy if needed. Structural growth
challenges remain, but so does the increased risk of supply shocks amid global tensions. Low
unemployment rates also mean wages can quickly add to price pressures.

One could even argue that central banks might be forced to tolerate higher inflation in the
future. High government debt piles are hard to sustain if policy rates are kept too high, which
can trigger financial stability concerns. The volatility around France is a test in the making and
a similar parallel should be drawn with the US. In an environment of deteriorating fiscal
conditions, central banks might be more comfortable leaning on the dovish side than risking a
sovereign debt crisis. So, whilst our baseline sees quite favourable inflation dynamics going
forward, we do think the risk is tilted to the upside.
