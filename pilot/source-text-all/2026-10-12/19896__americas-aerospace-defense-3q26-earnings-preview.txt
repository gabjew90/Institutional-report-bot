Our 3Q26 A&D earnings preview provides our current thoughts on each sub-market 
and single stock ideas into the results period. We like the set up for aerospace 
aftermarket as concerns are priced into stocks, but we expect strong results in 3Q 
and positive commentary regarding 2027, while many of the companies have 
specific bottom up positive drivers. Aero OE has visible growth, but it also keeps 
facing risk of extension in the pace. Business jet is solid, but growth is slower than 
other parts of aerospace. In defense we expect debate to continue around 
medium-term growth pace and margins and for the market to determine there are 
risks. Defense tech has exposure to growth priorities, and some valuations have 
become very attractive, though it is hard to take a view on the bottom in the current 
de-rate. Government IT valuations have moved lower, though we think the demand 
pace will prove still slow in this results and outlook period. Into the earnings season 
we like HII, WWD, TDG, ARXS, AVEX. We are cautious on LMT, BAH, MRCY.   
Aerospace OE: Boeing has averaged ~41/month deliveries of 737 MAX this summer 
(41 in August, 39 in July, 42 in June) with the plan to stabilize at 47/month taking 
longer than anticipated. 787 production has stabilized at 8/month, but the engine 
delivery performance needed to go to 10/month has not been achieved. The OEMs 
continue to march output higher, supply chain is improving, demand is quite strong, 
but it is not easy business.  
Aerospace aftermarket: We expect aftermarket to remain strong through the 
medium-term. Airlines are not making major changes to the fleet despite the fuel 
price as they have been able to price and they are short new aircraft. There is also 
substantial pent up demand in aircraft maintenance. Pricing is currently stronger 
than its historical average which itself is consistently above inflation. Many of the 
companies in the vertical have unique bottom up drivers. The set up for the vertical 
is somewhat easier into this results period because the stocks price in more 
negativity than normal.  
Business jet: Market fundamentals remain solid and supply-demand remains tight. 
The industry has recently proven relatively insulated from broader demand 
headwinds, such as disruptions in the Middle East and higher fuel prices. Business jet 
operations have been increasing in the summer months yoy and deliveries have 
improved August YTD versus last year.

Defense hardware: Defense stocks have been confusing to investors as they de-rate 
while revenues grow and the geopolitics are complex. We think defense large-cap

authorization, in large part simply due to difficult baseline compares, and their 
valuations are historically highly correlated to authorization because it is the most 
leading indicator. Traditional defense companies are also facing more margin questions 
today than they have in the past, as the Pentagon looks to better balance risk sharing, 
and that has made its way into contract terms. We still see pockets of growth and margin 
expansion within that backdrop, like Navy.   
Defense Tech: We continue to believe Defense Tech can buck the trend of growth and 
margin questions within defense, because the companies are tethered to the highest 
priority growth verticals in the budget, and are purpose built to operate under more 
commercial terms vs needing a major shift in order to do it. There have been some 
significant de-ratings in the space which we think creates opportunity for certain names,

Government IT & Services: There has been a substantial growth slowdown and

satellites, lunar missions) while investments in larger rocket programs continue. Satellite 
operators have seen increased demand given ongoing geopolitical conflicts, which we 
expect to be reiterated in 3Q. 
EVTOL. Newly unveiled defense variants provide substantial upside potential in the

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
