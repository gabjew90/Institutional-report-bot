US Rates Weekly
Pension plan for 20s?
CITI'S TAKE 
We remain neutral on duration despite this week’s strong 10y auction, 
finding little evidence that two consecutive strong 10y auctions mark an end 
of the current mini-buyers’ strike. Ahead of next week’s CPI report, we 
remain positioned for the FOMC to keep rates unchanged at the October 
meeting. Treasury’s dealer survey, released next Friday, could hint at a 
possible reduction in long-end auction sizes. Treasury Secretary Bessent 
may point to poor pension fund Treasury demand, perhaps caused by 
increased long-end corporate bond issuance, as a reason to reduce and/or 
cancel the 20y bond. We position for this for via 10s20s spreads steepeners.
Short-end — In line with our view, funding markets continue to be well supported 
here. For example, SOFR has not fixed above IORB in more than a month now and 
we think that can continue for the next couple of weeks. We highlight potential 
repo stress drivers towards the end of October but remain constructive around 
year-end funding conditions.
Inflation — We expect a decline in MoM core CPI inflation to 0.2% in September 
with risks of a steeper decline. 5y TIPS auction this month could be stronger than 
usual due to increased demand from ETFs.
Vol — EUR upper-left vol can underperform US given that the fragmentation risk 
could lead to reduction in ECB rate hike pricing relative to the Fed. SOFR futures 
convexity adjustments are low vs implied vol. We recommend shorting EUR 6m1y 
straddles against long Blues convexity to position for Fed/ECB divergence. 3y10y 
vol has started to cheapen vs 15y15y-10y10y rate curve and it can normalize further, 
in our view.

Overview: Pension plan for 20s?
Still challenging to venture past the front-end
Prior to Jackson Hole, we expected Chair Warsh would not open Pandora’s box with 
a September rate hike due to the risk of a UST buyers’ strike. Indeed, uncertainty 
around his reaction function and the path of core PCE had already increased front-
end risk premium. However, Chair Warsh pushed on ahead with a hike, and so far, 
our concerns are playing out with term premium going above the post-2025 tariff 
range (Figure 4). We moved neutral after that hawkish September FOMC and tried 
to catch the falling knife of the 20y bond on our Treasury “put” thesis (see US Rates 
Weekly - Only hawks outside the window). We reiterated our neutral views last 
week, even with the growing fragmentation risk in Europe, as we did not expect the 
move to spill over materially into US rates (see US Rates Weekly - OAT of the blue). 
The fact that Bunds could not rally this week, while the OAT-Bund and BTP-Bund 
spread widened, supports our neutral view from here. 
Our duration conviction sits in the front end, where we remain positioned for the 
FOMC to keep Fed funds unchanged at the October meeting. We plan to hold this 
position into the October meeting even though the market-implied probability 
remains relatively low entering next Wednesday’s CPI. Our economists expect a 
0.18% MoM core CPI print and see the August wireless services spike as a one-off. 
In our view, even a 0.3% MoM core CPI print would not justify an October hike. 
After the October meeting, we think a January FOMC receive position provides the 
next-best value (either outright or via Dec/Jan FOMC flatteners). The revised core 
PCE data retains the residual seasonality patterns from the pre-revised dataset, 
with November and December prints typically soft (Figure 5 and Figure 6). This 
likely sets up the Fed to skip hiking in January, assuming a hike in December, thus 
solidifying a quarterly cadence for the possible rate hiking cycle.

Short-end: Repo holding steady  
Repo rates have stayed relatively contained over the past week (Figure 16), in line 
with our view (see US Rates Weekly - OAT of the blue). We have been arguing that 
reserves have been in a lightly abundant regime over the past few months, as the 
increase in T-bill supply from July and August did not pressure repo rates higher 
(see Figure 17). However, we could see some light upwards pressure in repo rates 
towards the end of October. First, T-bill settlements, which have now turned 
positive, should continue growing over the coming weeks (see Figure 18). For 
example, private T-bill outstanding will increase by around $350bn through the 
end of November, and we expect it to be over $6.9tn by the end of November. 
Second, the TGA balance will temporarily be over $1tn during the last week of 
October, in line with Treasury’s guidance, which in turn will temporarily drain 
reserves from the system (see Figure 19). Together, these two factors could push 
SOFR fixes lightly above IORB.

Next Wednesday the Fed will publish their RMP schedule for the upcoming month

Front end-swap spreads into 2027
As we discussed last week, we think the debt ceiling dynamic will keep funding 
markets flush with liquidity through most of 2027 (see US Rates Weekly - OAT of 
the blue). This in turn should keep term repo bounded through most of the year, 
and we think that will be generally supportive of front-end spreads. But what can 
we expect as we near the resolution of the debt ceiling?  As we show in Figure 20, 
front-end spreads tend to cheapen during debt ceiling episodes, particularly as the 
resolution approaches. The green segments show the six months before the 
resolution and the yellow segments show the six months after the debt ceiling 
resolution. Here we look at 1y maturity, original 2y issue, USTs to define our 1y 
spreads time series (not 1y T-bills which tend to trade richer). Presumably the 
cheapening occurs due to some expectation of increased T-bill supply once the 
ceiling is resolved. Note however that 2021 was an outlier due to the elevated post-
pandemic liquidity environment, therefore liquidity conditions also play a clear role 
here.

Vol: Diverging vols on Fed vs ECB
EUR upper-left vol can underperform US given that the fragmentation risk could 
lead to reduction in ECB rate hike pricing relative to the Fed. SOFR futures 
convexity adjustments are low vs implied vol. We recommend shorting EUR 6m1y 
straddles against long Blues convexity to position for Fed/ECB divergence. 3y10y 
vol has started to cheapen vs 15y15y-10y10y rate curve and it can normalize 
further. Note: Futures trading involves substantial risk of loss.
Short EUR upper-left vol vs US 
We have been arguing since the September hike that the pricing of an extended Fed 
tightening cycle would increase the risk of an eventual policy mistake and should 
cause the front-end of the rate curve (2s5s) and the SOFR futures curve to bear 
flatten. However, the market’s expectation for US growth has been more robust 
than we thought, and the SOFR futures curve has bear steepened since then. 
Instead of the US, risk of policy mistake has started to appear in Europe where the 
growing fragmentation risk, as reflected by the extreme widening in OAT-Bund 
yield spread, has led to a reduction in ECB rate hike probability. Our EUR rates 
strategists believe that ending ECB hikes would be the right first response to the 
contagion risks (European Rates Weekly - What to do about OATs?). 
Given the differing tail risks between a Fed that is still tilted towards hiking 
indefinitely and an ECB that is likely forced to pause, we expect their upper-left 
vols to also diverge with EUR swaption vol underperforming the US. For most of 
this year, both US and EUR 6m1y vols have moved directionally and in-line with the 
expected change in the underlying rates, proxied by the absolute spread between 
6m1y fwd rate and the spot 1y rate (Figure 32). This relationship makes intuitive 
sense, and points to the potential cheapening in EUR 6m1y vol if the market prices 
out the ECB hikes and compresses the fwd-spot rate spread to nearly zero. Notice 
that even at the current EUR fwd-spot rate spread, EUR 6m1y vol (solid blue 
triangle) already appears to be 6bp vols too higher based on the regression over the 
past 6 months.

Similarly in the front-end, convexity adjustments for SOFR Blues (Figure 36) and 
Golds packs currently look low relative to their model fair values, which are strongly 
tied to front-end implied rates vols. Essentially this means that investors who are 
looking to be long front-end vol can find better risk/reward by being long SOFR 
futures convexity adjustments.
As a reminder, convexity adjustments represent the differences in rates between 
the SOFR futures and the maturity-matched Forward Rate Agreements (FRA). The 
higher rate in the SFR contract (which has no convexity) can be viewed as the 
compensation for the positive convexity benefit that is found in the corresponding 
FRA (from a long position’s perspective). The trade-off between SFR’s convexity-
adjusted favorable rate and the FRA’s positively convex profile is analogous to the 
trade-off between the upfront premium and the long gamma profile of an option. 
In both instances, the PnLs when held to maturity are dependent on the magnitude 
of the realized rate moves relative to the initial expected breakevens. Large rate 
moves in volatile markets would help the long convexity/long gamma positions in 
overcoming the breakevens that are represented by the convexity 
adjustments/option premiums.
