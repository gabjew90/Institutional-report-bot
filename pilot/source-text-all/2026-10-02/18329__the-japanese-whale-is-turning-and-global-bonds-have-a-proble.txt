The Japanese bid is disappearing
The global bond market may be losing one of its most dependable buyers.
Japanese money is starting to come home just as the global long end is already under serious
pressure. That matters everywhere, but nowhere may be more exposed than Europe.
The JGB is back
People have talked about Japanese repatriation forever, but unattractive domestic yields kept
Japanese institutions abroad.
Now JGB yields exceed those available on hedged DM government bonds, while the long end has
become relatively more stable. TS Lombard expects a much more active BoJ, with another hike in
December as its base case and October still live.
TS Lombard
Japan is actually outperforming
JGBs have outperformed OATs on a total-return basis over the past three months for the first time since
2024. And during September's global bond pain, Japanese 10y/30y yields moved substantially less
than the US/Europe.

TS Lombard
The whale is starting to move
The benchmark is 25% domestic bonds, but GPIF was already at 27% at the end of March and is
permitted to move as high as 31%. TS Lombard thinks a major allocation change could occur well
before the scheduled 2030 review.
That's potentially huge.
Another problem for the long end
The Fed has become less threatening at the front end, but the 30-year is still around 5.6%. MOVE has
exploded. Real yields/term premium have repriced. Long duration simply isn't getting the relief that
easier Fed expectations would normally imply.
Japanese repatriation obviously isn't responsible for the entire move. But the timing couldn't be worse.
Just as global duration is struggling to find an equilibrium, one of its most dependable structural
buyers is becoming less dependable.
France is in the firing line
Japan owns plenty of Treasuries, but TS Lombard argues Europe may actually be more exposed at the
margin. And within Europe, France stands out.
French bonds are Japan's second-largest foreign bond holding after USTs and its largest individual
European sovereign exposure. 
TS Lombard
Europe really doesn't need this
OATs already under pressure. French spreads elevated. V2X waking up. iTraxx Main showing much
more stress than the equity index. EUR deeply oversold.
Europe already has plenty of reasons for investors to demand more compensation. Losing part of the
Japanese bid would simply add another one.
TS Lombard even suspects that some of the rapid recent OAT selling may already have been driven by
Japanese portfolio rebalancing, although that's their interpretation rather than something they
establish conclusively.

Who buys the bonds?
The question isn't whether France, Europe or the US can find buyers. They obviously can.
The question is at what price, and who becomes the marginal buyer?
Japanese capital has historically been relatively stable, long-duration money. As pension funds/central
banks retreat and hedge funds and insurers increasingly become marginal long-end buyers, losing
another source of sticky demand could mean structurally greater volatility.
The bonds will find a buyer. The interesting question is how high yields have to go to find one.
