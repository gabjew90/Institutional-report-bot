Memory S/D outlook: tightness confirmed through CY28, no line of sight 
to balance. MU guided memory S/D to be tighter in CY27/28 more so than in 
CY26, an extension of the cycle horizon and in-line with JPMe. Management 
guided industry DRAM/NAND bit shipments at low-20%/mid-20% through 
CY27/28, with the industry remaining supply constrained. For DRAM, 
management elaborated on the limited line of sight of when the S-D will return 
to balance as the demand growth far outpaces that of supply growth amidst 
significant cleanroom shortages. On HBM, management guided HBM bit 
shipments to grow faster than conventional DRAM through CY28, in-line with 
JPMe (HBM bit demand growth : +63% vs. non-HBM server : +37%). 
•
SCA coverage extended to 2031. MU disclosed that 26 SCAs were signed 
covering : 1) >35% of revenue through 2030 (conservative pricing assumption, 
as per management); 2) both DRAM and NAND (management guided DRAM 
mix slightly lower than NAND); 3) across all customers including AI 
hyperscalers, mobile, PC, auto; 4) pre-payment at US$32bn (up from US$22bn 
in the May-Q) and RPO at US$150bn; and 5) 75% of the estimated revenue 
having a defined pricing framework, with the remaining 25% negotiated based 
on market prices. Taking into account the SCA horizon extension, growing 
RPO/prepayment mix, and 75% bit output locked-in by contracts for 27E, we 
see little doubt about the robust memory demand profile. On the other hand, we 
believe MU’s >35% SCA revenue coverage through 2030 is conservative 
compared to Asian memory makers with LTA coverage accounting for well 
above 50%+ of memory capacity. We believe the divergence may arise from 
strategic decisions (diverging priorities across contract duration, profitabiltiy 
(i.e. pricing), prepayment mix, contingent terms, etc.). Overall, we walk away 
with a more constructive impression of the memory fundamental view.

to raise FY27 capex vs. previous plan, driven predominately by an increase in 
construction capex to accelerate cleanroom availability in late-CY28 (see figure 12), in 
addition to some pull-forward of WFE to optimize output from existing cleanrooms.

already baked-in to our GMM supply equations and see limited changes to our tight S/D 
outlook until 28E (JPMe: +25% CAGR in CY27-28E). Management retained the 
disciplined capex tone, citing historical low capital intensity vs. historical mid-30% 
framework being outdated (JPMe: DRAM capital intensity <mid-10% and NAND at 
<10% through FY26-28E). On technology migration, managmeent indicated 1-gamma 
and G9 DRAM nodes (278L NAND) accounts for the highest mix with the next-gen node 
beginning volume productions in the 2HCY27, a two generation node ahead of Chinese 
competitors (in-line with our view - report). We believe MU’s capex and supply 
commentaries were in-line with our industry view given limited bit output per

Shareholder returns update. Similar to Asian memory makers, shareholder return 
policy update was another key FAQ from investors towards MU, in our view. MU has 
reiterated its intent to return excess cash to investors via share buybacks, especially after 
9 December 2026 (the second anniversary of CHIPS agreement). Management clarifeid 
that it will likely reach its target cash level by end-1QFY27, hinting that the incremental 
free cash flow generated through 2QFY27-4QFY27 could be returned to shareholders 
after lifting the current authorization of share repurchases which stand at US$2.2bn. As 
we have discussed in the memory bull-bear market debate report, we believe proactive 
shareholder returns are a pre-requisite for memory stocks to re-rate and is an indirect 
evidence of a higher for longer memory upcycle. We expect Asian memory makers to 
provide updates on their shareholder returns at their Oct-end earnings conferences (i.e. 
SEC hinting stronger shareholder returns via. lifting the FCF pool or SKH disclosing an 
additional share buyback following the W40trn in August - report).

Micron's 4QFY26 results flash and 1QFY27 outlook. MU (OW, covered by JPM's 
Harlan Sur) reported a strong Aug-Q print: sales of US$54.2bn (consensus: $51.5bn) and 
EPS of US$33 (consensus: US$32) - report from Harlan. DRAM bit shipment rose MSD
% Q/Q broadly in-line with Asian memory makers (JPMe: SEC/SKH +5%/+10%) and 
DRAM ASP rose high-teens% Q/Q (broadly in-line with SEC/SKH of +17%/+20%). 
NAND bit shipments rose ~10% and ASP rose +30% Q/Q tracking higher than Asian 
memory makers (SEC/SKH 3Q26E B/G at +8%/+3% and ASP +12%/+12%). MU’s 
Nov-Q guidance was stronger at ~US$61.5bn, GPM of 86.25% and EPS of US$38.2 (vs. 
consensus of US$56bn/87%/US$36). 
•
Share price outlook. Memory share prices have gradually recovered in the past 1M 
(+10% vs. SOX: +12%) and reacted positively after MU’s strong earnings print (SEC/
SKH/Kioxia/NYT: +3%/+3%/+6%/flat), in our view. As Asian makers head into 3Q26 
earnings seasons, we believe MU’s solid fundamental outlook (i.e. memory S/D 
tightening in 27/28, order discussions extended into 2031, 75% of the 27E bit output 
locked-in by contracts) should be largely echoed by memory makers with shareholder 
return update being the key monitoring point. We are positive on all Asia memory maker 
names and in the short term prefer SKH over SEC upon superior shareholder return policy 
and higher TSR yield.
