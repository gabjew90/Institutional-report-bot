Bottom Line: With the completion of the Amazon (AMZN) 
volume drawdown and its respective cost take-out, UPS (CL; 
BUY) should begin to see a more consistent profit growth 
inflection. We believe the market is not yet contemplating that ex 
the Amazon transition drag, UPS should see a structurally leaner, 
highly automated, and higher-yielding Domestic network - and 
remains one of only three fully integrated time definite parcel 
carriers that can operate on a global network scale. 
Over the course of the difficult AMZN drawdown, UPS shares traded 
down -29% (from $134 to $94), significantly lagging FedEx (FDX), 
which gained 30%. This performance gap has pushed UPS to a 
trough valuation of 12.0x NTM EPS, representing an unusual 
discount to FDX. At what could be a critical inflection point, we 
highlight a pathway to earnings growth, margin expansion, and 
multiple re-rating.

group at large since June likely contemplate heightened inflationary 
risks from energy costs and potential impact on consumer demand 
resiliency - something that could yet adversely affect the all 
important peak season quarter; however from recent channel 
checks it would appear that as of now the demand environment 
remains stable and GS economists suggest continued economic 
resilience (see our recent economists’ note).

stock.

International Step-Up: We will be lapping global de minimis in September which set 
n
up international with favorable comparables (4Q25 International ADV was -5% YoY); 
UPS noted post 2Q that International is already seeing strong volume growth 
resumption on its most profitable trade lane of China to US (~20% YoY). The 
expectation is that 4Q26 should see positive international export volume 
growth which in turn should lead to sharp sequential margin uptick. 
Beyond the near term, UPS sees an opportunity in international markets that are 
60% SMB and 70% B2B - both of which tend to be higher yielding and higher 
margins. Now that the AMZN drawdown is complete in the US, it is important to 
recognize that UPS can now redirect capex dollars to International if it chooses to 
invest in higher premium parcel capacity and capability globally. As the world 
moves from a China only supply chain to a China +1 or China +2 strategy - UPS is 
one of three globally integrated supply chain providers that can execute on time 
definite supply chain needs - which should lead to greater secular growth 
opportunity than the market may be appreciating. Basically, inventory could be 
sourced in multiple countries, with an increase in supply chain complexity leading to 
additional freight moves as components need to move towards a final assembly 
location.  
UPS recently reiterated this capability and desire to invest globally by announcing 
their latest operating model through standardizing operations across geographies 
led by Chief Global Operations Officer, Nando Cessarone, who recently guided UPS 
Domestic through its glidedown of AMZN by taking out cost and improving 
operations through automation.  
2) Pathway Does Exist To Improve Profitability: 
We believe the opportunity is there for UPS to improve its EBIT growth and margin 
from recent year’s pressured scenario (Exhibit 5-Exhibit 6) - with our current 
estimated EBIT CAGR thru 2029 pegged at ~11%.  
We put forth three scenarios ranged around UPS’ targeted goal set at RPP>CPP 
spread of 50-100bp incorporating the low, middle and high end of their base pricing 
growth target of 250-350bps YoY, adjusted for fuel and keeping mix neutral for RPP, 
which could be conservative as mix improves from higher quality revenue targets 
(note our current estimate is closer to lower end of this range). Under this scenario 
range, UPS can demonstrate stronger operating profit torque than the market 
appreciates relative to its current valuation (details below on method and valuation 
thoughts).  The ranged EBIT CAGR, by our calculation is approximately 11-14% 
(12.5% at midpoint). 
Partly driving their EBIT leverage is what we have already discussed, an improved 
mix of higher yielding revenue. Driving the other side of EBIT leverage is an 
improved fixed and semi-variable cost base. As UPS drew down AMZN volumes, 
they underwent their Network Reconfiguration and Efficiency Reimagined - taking 
out fixed, semi-variable, and variable costs - largely through closing inefficient, 
outdated buildings, and diverting volume to automated buildings. UPS now has 
68.5% of its US volume flowing through automated facilities, to which cost per piece is 
28% lower than a non-automated building.

UPS closed 93 buildings in 2025 and 45 in 1H26, anticipating total closures of ~150 over 
2025 and 2026. While we are two years away from a new Teamster contract, we should 
recognize the meaningful headcount reduction and related cost-out over the last few 
years.  
In aggregate, the UPS cost reduction efforts took out $3.5b in 2025 and an anticipated 
$3bn in 2026; with the reductions likely to contribute to the favorable RPP versus CPP 
spread outlook. Putting it all together - UPS targets anticipates 250-350bps of base 
pricing moving forward, and with a lower structural cost, expects a domestic RPP vs 
CPP spread of 50-100bps.

3) Discounted and Attractive Valuation - Coupled with Strong Dividend Yield: 
We believe UPS could re-rate higher should it execute on the above revenue and 
profit driver plan. UPS is currently trading at 12.0 X NTM EPS, or less than its -1SD of 
13.5x and a 2-turn discount to FDX (Exhibit 12). 
With the Amazon drawdown complete, and the potential for improved Domestic margin 
and overall company earnings - we think at the very least UPS should trade closer to its 
long term average PE at roughly 15x to 15.5x once the company begins to deliver on its 
stated objectives.  The path to improved valuation could come as soon as 2H26 
(assuming that high gasoline prices does not adversely impact consumer resiliency and 
the expectation for a solid peak season - which is the biggest near term worry in our 
view).  The three 2H valuation catalysts are: 1) YOY positive volume growth on an 
ex-Amazon basis, 2) 2H Domestic margin that is up versus last year, and 3) step up 
in 4Q International margin. 
Longer term, there is an argument to be potentially made that there should not be 
much discrepancy versus the FDX multiple as the companies essentially do the same 
thing.  FDX 2026-2029 EPS CAGR is about 13% (using 2026 guidance midpoint of 
$17.50 to 2029 target of $25) - which compares to our UPS scenario range (50-100 
bp RPP v CPP spread with 250-350 base pricing) of 11.5%-15% - or essentially the 
same at the midpoint. 
The earnings growth runway is clear - but we fully understand that proof is in the 
pudding - especially given UPS has fallen short of expectations in the recent past. 
That said, should UPS begin to see operational torque kick in and deliver a more 
consistent EPS flow - we do think the valuation gap and how we set our price target 
versus FDX could narrow over time - which when combined with a nearly 7% 
dividend yield - offers strong upside opportunity.  As it is our current price target  is 
$132 per share - or 40% upside from here - before taking into account the dividend 
yield which we have covered by FCF this year. 
 
UPS is also particularly appealing on a risk adjusted return basis. On a risk adjusted 
return, UPS offers the most meaningful return in our coverage driven by our price target 
return of 41% and strong dividend yield of 6.6% at 1.5x.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
