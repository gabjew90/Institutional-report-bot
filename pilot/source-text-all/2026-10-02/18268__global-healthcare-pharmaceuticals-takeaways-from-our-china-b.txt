anticipate an increasing contribution towards novel innovation over time with

High-Level Takeaways 
New policies continue to support biopharma innovation, although market 
n
development and resource allocation remain a debate amongst some of the 
more advanced commercial companies. Sanofi noted that the majority of the 
China biopharma market is out-of-pocket or government funded with only 6-7% 
of the market funded through commercial health insurance. While the insurance 
market is growing, it is taking time to penetrate, and Sanofi believes that 
increasing access and rewarding innovation through the insurance market is key 
to sustainability of the domestic industry as well as attracting further global 
investment. On resource allocation, Innovent noted that in order to potentially 
stop inefficient resource allocation in the industry, the government could 
increase the bar for approvals of any additional, undifferentiated GLP-1 assets, 
in a similar way that in 2020, raised the bar for approval of PD-1/L1 drugs by 
mandating that the control arm needs to be a commonly recognized standard of 
care. Separately, China recently unveiled a 5-year plan aimed at increasing the 
global competitiveness of its biopharma industry and targeting 25% global share 
of first-in-class drugs.  As one downstream effect, Sanofi noted this could serve 
to further bid up the escalating value of China-based deals as competition from 
potential local acquirers also increases.

Obesity/Diabetes KOL session (Physician from Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital) 
Doctors in China appear to have less influence over obesity treatment decisions 
n
than diabetes treatment decisions, with obesity therapy selection more driven 
by online platforms, promotion, and brand value. For diabetes, the KOL noted 
that patients are highly price sensitive. Since tirzepatide was included in the NRDL, it 
has gained meaningful share from mazdutide, which remains in the self-pay channel. 
For obesity, prescriptions are often obtained through online platforms, with many 
patients not seeing physicians in traditional clinic settings. As a result, patients tend 
to make their own treatment decisions, making brand recognition, promotional 
activity, and perceived product value more important drivers of uptake. 
The KOL estimated that only approximately 10% of patients discontinue 
n
weight-loss therapy due to muscle loss. The KOL noted that muscle loss has 
become a more recent focus for clinicians, particularly with broader use of GLP-1 
and incretin-based therapies, but diagnosis remains challenging because it is hard to 
identify visually and functional testing takes time. Clinicians also tend to prioritize 
more serious safety concerns, such as pancreatitis and cancer-related risks. Patients 
rarely report muscle loss directly; when they present with symptoms such as fatigue, 
clinicians may recommend dose reduction and dietary changes if symptoms are 
mild. As a result, discontinuation due to muscle loss appears limited to a small subset 
of patients. 
The KOL believes the obesity market could split roughly 50/50 between oral and 
n
injectable therapies if prices are comparable. He sees oral therapies gaining 
meaningful use due to convenience, needle-free administration, and no need for 
cold storage. However, he expects injectables to retain approximately half of the 
market because of their stronger efficacy, particularly among patients seeking 
greater weight loss.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
