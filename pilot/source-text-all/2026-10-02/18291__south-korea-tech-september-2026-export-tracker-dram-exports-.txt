South Korea Tech: September 2026 export tracker: DRAM exports reach 
another all-time high at US$33bn (+552% yoy)
1 October 2026 | 10:13PM SGT
According to the MOTIE (Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy) and the TRASS 
(Trade Statistics Service), exports for the major tech products we track (memory, 
OLED, Li-ion batteries, and MLCC) showed an overall positive trend except for in 
display products (-10% yoy). Export value for Li-ion batteries (+1% yoy), MLCC 
(+22% yoy), and WFE equipment (+148% yoy) all achieved positive growth rates for 
September, with memory exports delivering growth of +359% yoy, marking 8 
consecutive months of 200%+ yoy growth. WFE equipment import again showed 
strong growth of +65% yoy. There were 2.5 fewer working days compared to 
September 2025 (21.5 days in September 2026 vs. 24 days in September 2025). Key 
points are: 1) DRAM export value growth reached +552% yoy in September, 2) 
MLCC exports increased by +22% yoy, and 3) YTD Hynix epoxide resins import 
+4% yoy, while SEC plastic film import +61% yoy. 
Memory: 8 consecutive months of 200%+ yoy growth with DRAM exports +552% 
yoy 
Memory exports showed strong growth of +359% yoy and +32% mom in September, 
extending 200%+ yoy growth trend to 8 consecutive periods. DRAM exports reached 
an all-time high with +552% yoy and +41% mom growth for the same period. Both 
NAND and SSD export also showed strong numbers (NAND chip exports: +365% yoy, 
SSD exports: +531% yoy). MOTIE attributed the positive results to solid memory 
demand from data center build-out and proliferation of agentic AI. For the full 
quarter 3Q26, memory exports rose +312% yoy, implying likely strong fundamentals 
for the Korean memory companies during the quarter. For SEC, we expect memory 
revenue to increase +428% yoy with DRAM/NAND growth at +447%/387% yoy 
respectively in 3Q26. For Hynix, we expect total revenue to increase +305% yoy with 
DRAM/NAND growth at +291%/392% yoy respectively for the same period.  
HBM: YTD Hynix epoxide resins import +4% yoy; SEC plastic film import +61% yoy

proxies to gauge HBM shipment scales for Hynix and SEC, given their strong

have replaced the previous Icheon and Cheongju epoxide resin dataset with the sum 
of epoxide resins imported to Icheon and North Chungcheong Province. Similarly, for

In August, import value of epoxide resins from Japan to Icheon/North Chungcheong 
increased by +19% yoy and import value of plastic film from Japan to

Display: Weak yoy results for both OLED and LCD 
Display exports decreased by 10% yoy in September, with notable decline of OLED 
exports at -11% yoy compared to -2% yoy of LCD. MOTIE noted that pricing pressure 
from mobile OLED downstream companies and delay of new set device release resulted 
in negative growth. For the full quarter 3Q26, display exports decreased by -4% yoy 
(OLED: -6% yoy, LCD: +4% yoy). For Korean display names, we expect 3Q26 revenue for 
LG Display and Samsung Display to both decline by 2% yoy.  
MLCC: Continues 5 consecutive months of double-digit yoy growth 
MLCC exports continued to see a positive trend, with double-digit yoy growth 
continuing for 5 consecutive months. For the full quarter 3Q26, MLCC exports increased 
by +20% yoy and +20% qoq. For Samsung Electro-Mechanics (SEMCO), we expect MLCC 
revenue to grow by +44% yoy in 3Q26 mainly led by continued strength in AI server/auto 
MLCCs. 
Li-ion batteries: continues positive yoy growth 
Li-ion battery export increased by +1% yoy in September, continuing the positive yoy 
trend after the strong rebound in May (+53% yoy). MOTIE commented that ESS market 
expansion and increase in mineral price contributed to the positive result. For the full 
quarter 3Q26, Li-ion battery exports increased by +4% yoy, continuing on its positive 
growth after ending 10 consecutive quarters of yoy decline in 1Q26. 
WFE: Strong yoy growth trend continues for exports and imports in September 
Semi WFE exports and import showed yoy growth of + 148% and +65% in September 
respectively, for which we believe the strong import growth comes from the increasing 
capex of the memory suppliers in an effort to upgrade technology and expand capacity. 
For the full quarter 3Q26, export and import showed +114%/+92% yoy growth 
respectively, signaling higher demand for WFE.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
