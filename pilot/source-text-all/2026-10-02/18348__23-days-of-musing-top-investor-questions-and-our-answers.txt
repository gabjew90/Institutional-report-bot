1. How and When Could Meta Monetize Muse Directly? First, we expect Muse 
subscription revenue to be quite small for a while as Meta focuses on scaling a user 
base, engagement and commercial behavior well before monetization. To this point, 
we believe initial "take rates" on the connection agreements are de minimis. But over 
the long-term as the agentic behavior scales (think late '27/'28 at the earliest) we 
would expect Meta to monetize through a real-time bidding auction market 
(businesses paying for transactions/acquisitions) with businesses across the 
economy bidding based on return on investment and paying Meta for completed 
transactions. The $30tn of consumer spend to be digitized by agentic highlights the 
opportunity here. In the meantime, we do see Muse being a further source of data 
capture and profile build for Meta's core products to showcase even more targeted 
organic content to drive more engagement.

2. How Much Are Muse Serving Costs? Our sensitivity work showcases how it  
ranges from $3-$130 per user per month based on token consumption.  We have 
built a detailed cost to serve model separating the two primary compute workloads 
of GPU inference (which scales with tokens consumed) and the CPU sandbox (which 
scales with how frequently users activate Muse and how long the underlying VM 
remains provisioned). The GPU workloads consist of running Muse's underlying 
model inference (processing user prompts, reasoning through tasks, etc.) while the 
CPU workloads likely support the agent environment itself, including running the 
VM, browser, tools, code execution, etc. The mix of these costs shifts with intensity 
as the CPU sandbox expense is the larger cost driver for lower-usage users, while 
GPU inference becomes the dominant component for users consuming the full 
token allowance. Please let us know if interested in our new interactive Muse Cost 
to Serve Model fully shown in  Exhibit 2.
There are many assumptions behind this analysis that investors can flex...and while 
drawing a clean analogy to Meta's historical cost to serve is difficult (given limited 
disclosure on users across FB/Instagram/WhatsApp) we think this analysis does 
speak to the cost prohibitive barriers to entry for smaller players to scale a 
competitive product to Meta. Meta's $250bn core ad business growing 27% y/y 
(that is only getting stronger) certainly provides cushion too.  Consider too that 
META's global ARPU (total revenue divided by FOA users) is ~$70 (with North 
America multiples higher)...so the company certainly does have a history of driving 
monetization of its behavior.

3. Is Meta at Risk of Being Capacity Constrained When it Comes to Scaling Muse? 
No, we estimate Meta is acquiring the equivalent CPU capacity in '26 alone to 
serve 1bn Muse DAUs who spend 25% of their weekly token allowance.  Even this 
25% could be high but we would rather assume adoption and use cases ramp in this 
instance to show the large amount of capacity Meta already has coming on.  As 
shown, our bottom up estimates of Meta's expected CPU purchases in '26 and 
Graviton capacity acquired from Amazon earlier this year (see AMZN and META: A 
Graviton Enabled Muse Ahead?) could potentially serve as many as 1bn DAUs 
(assuming they use 25% of their weekly token allowance).  Note too this does not 
consider other CPUs they may already have in the fleet to use.

In some ways, Meta for Enterprise is also designed to fuel META's consumer 
agentic opportunity because we believe companies' underlying "Web 1.0" 
architecture needs to evolve to support agent-to-agent interactions. All 
companies from SMBs to large enterprises will need to build or adopt an agentic 
interaction layer including MCP/tool interfaces and observability to allow external 
agents to securely discover and transact with their systems. This presents a large 
opportunity for META as it builds its Meta Enterprise Platform, giving business 
customers access to its full suite of agentic tools. Muse pinging websites through 
browsers and calling human call centers isn't a high-quality, consistent or repeatable 
experience that will scale. Instead, consumer agents ultimately need a machine-
native way to interact directly with enterprise systems… and Meta is aiming to help 
solve this problem. In our view, SMBs are likely going to be easier to penetrate 
with agentic tools than large enterprises (given large enterprises will likely look 
for more cross-platform independence), but we expect Meta to work to address all 
companies...in particular given its recent hire to head Meta Enterprise. 
What about Monetization? Meta has now launched its Meta One subscription 
product in the US  and we have shown how charging anywhere from $15-$50/month 
to businesses and influencers for its latest models, content creation/business 
management tools, and higher AI limits can scale quickly given the 100mn 
businesses on Meta Platforms.  We would think of Meta for Enterprise through a 
similar lens.  
But Meta Enterprise Platform also represents an opportunity to scale an eventual 
API business...which as we have shown even assuming 100 MW of allocated API 
capacity can add 4% (~$1) to '28 EPS.  The models need to be compelling (very low 
cost, fast, compute capable, etc.) but this at least speaks to the opportunity that 
META is going after with Watermelon on the horizon.

6. After a Muse-Driven Re-rate to 21X, What are We Focused on For META Shares 
From Here?  META shares have re-rated to 21X NTM consensus EPS (from 14X 
before it settled the state AG case), driven largely off of improved sentiment 
following the release of Muse. We believe today's multiple now largely reflects 
META's position of strength, with a first mover advantage bringing a free Muse to 
market and better models on the horizon. That said, further upward multiple 
revisions will be dependent on 1) user adoption of Muse, 2) commercial behavior on 
the agent platform and 3) the competitive set of agentic products rolled out by 
GOOGL/SPCX/privates and others in the space. It is still early in the consumer 
agentic landscape, and while not our base case, we  would remind investors that 
META traded at 28X as recently as February 2025, so there is precedent for taking 
the multiple higher from here even after the recent move if un-contested 
momentum continues.  On the revision side, we are focused on more durable core 
engagement and monetization (from GPU enabled machine learning), business 
messaging/agents, or the API to drive upside.

why we expect META to focus first on building engagement and high-value commercial
