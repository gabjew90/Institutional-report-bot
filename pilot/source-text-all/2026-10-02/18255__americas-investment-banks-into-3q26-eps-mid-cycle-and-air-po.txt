We appear to be mid-M&A cycle, with a 3Q26 M&A air pocket resulting in lower top-lines; however, long-term M&A 
1.
ingredients remain in place:  YTD announced/completed M&A volumes are up ~30% YoY, on the back of robust 
AI-related dealmaking, as well as some large deals announced in 2025 that slipped into early 2026, driving a strong 1H26 
(Exhibit 9). That being said, we see a temporary slowdown in near-term M&A activity. 3Q26 IBanks Dealogic M&A 
completed fees are down ~10% YoY/QoQ (Exhibit 10). We see this temporary slowdown potentially continuing into 4Q26, 
as deals announced earlier in the year have not been fully replenished, with 3Q26 M&A announcements down ~5%/30% 
YoY/QoQ (Exhibit 8). Given the increasing odds of this air pocket persisting through year-end at least, we downgrade 
3Q26E/2026E/2027E/2028E total revenue by 4%/2%/1%/1% and we are now 2%/1%/2% below the Street on 
3Q26E/2026E/27E total revenue (Exhibit 2, Exhibit 5). That being said, we believe remain constructive on longer-term 
M&A industry growth. Most leading indicators with strong correlations to M&A remain healthy; using these, we forecast 
20% NTM industry M&A volume growth (Exhibit 15). Further, M&A growth in historic cycles suggests that we remain 
mid-cycle - M&A industry volumes have risen ~90% thus far this cycle, leaving ~65-75pp of remaining potential growth 
(Exhibit 13). Moreover, M&A excluding AI infrastructure deal volumes indicates growth this cycle of only ~80%, leaving a 
larger ~75-85pp of remaining growth in the cycle (Exhibit 14). Overall, we forecast 2026E/27E/28E YoY total advisory 
revenue (which includes non-M&A advisory) growth of 14%/16%/9% (Exhibit 16). 
Independent investment bank advisory share gains could slow going forward: US independent investment banks 
2.

investment in talent, with an 11% MD headcount CAGR over the past decade (Exhibit 18). That being said, we expect

Can strategic/large-cap M&A grow from elevated levels, and can the M&A cycle broaden out to sponsor/small & 
3.

Strategic/large-cap M&A: There appears to be an air pocket in large-cap and strategic M&A, with 3Q26 
a.
announced strategic/large-cap M&A volumes down ~5%/45% YoY (Exhibit 8, Exhibit 23). However, activity levels 
are already elevated, with YTD announced strategic/large-cap M&A volumes up ~50%/55% YoY, and up 
~90%/220% from the recent cyclical trough (in 2023-24) on an annualized basis (Exhibit 27, Exhibit 28). YTD 
strategic/large-cap annualized levels are now ~15%/40% above all-time historical annual peaks. 
Strategic/large-cap volumes are at 4.6%/2.3% of global equal weight market-cap, which compare to 6.7%/1.9% 
on average over the past 16 years - large-cap market cap-weighted M&A volumes are above the long-term average 
for the first time since 2021 (Exhibit 31, Exhibit 32). Further, the average M&A transaction deal size is up 21% YoY, 
and sponsor M&A is only ~27% of YTD announced M&A volumes, vs. 35%/32% trailing 5-yr/10-yr averages (Exhibit 
26, Exhibit 25). In our view, large, strategic M&A could remain strong, due to corporates taking advantage of a less 
aggressive US antitrust enforcement posture, with potential upside risk if strategics feel a yet greater urgency to act 
before this US presidential administration ends. 
Sponsor/mid-cap M&A: We saw sponsor/mid-cap M&A begin to recover in July-August before slowing down again 
b.
in September, with 3Q26 announced sponsor/mid-cap M&A volumes changing by ~-5%/+15% YoY. July and August 
sponsor M&A volumes were up 79%/9% YoY, outperforming strategic M&A; however, they slowed in September, 
falling 61% YoY (Exhibit 29, Exhibit 39). The key concern around the sponsor M&A recovery from here is higher 
interest rates, with the current market forward interest rates curve discounting Fed Funds/5Y UST rates 
~110bps/115bps higher at year-end 2026 than at the beginning of 2026 (Exhibit 35, Exhibit 36). Sponsor M&A has 
a stronger inverse correlation to the absolute level of short interest rates, relative to strategic M&A (Exhibit 33, 
Exhibit 34), which we attribute to the return focus of private equity firms, as well as the fact that many sponsor 
transactions have substantial debt financing components. Notwithstanding higher rates, we continue to modestly 
prefer sponsor M&A over strategic M&A, with: 1) sponsor growth driven by mounting LP pressure on GPs to return 
capital. Further, 2) structural PE M&A drivers remain, namely large $s of aged, deployed capital that needs to be 
returned, and large quantities of uncommitted capital that need to be deployed. Finally, 3) sponsor M&A volumes 
have been muted so far this cycle. YTD announced sponsor/small+mid-cap M&A volumes are up only 2%/21% YoY, 
and up ~75%/60% from the recent cyclical trough on an annualized basis (Exhibit 37, Exhibit 38. YTD annualized 
levels remain ~25%/20% below all-time historical annual peaks. Further, sponsor/small+mid-cap volumes are at 
2.0%/4.8% of global equal weight market-cap, which compare to 2.6%/7.4% on average over the past 16 years - in 
particular, small + mid-cap market cap-weighted M&A volumes remain near the low over the past 16 years (Exhibit 
41, Exhibit 42).

Preferred Buys 
 
 
EVR (Buy, $331 12-month PT, 28% upside): EVR remains a quality name across the peer group, which we expect to 
n
deliver above-average revenue growth and market share gains, as multiyear, robust investments across M&A and other 
IBanking businesses continue to compound. Overall, we model a 14% 2025-28E revenue CAGR, outpacing peer median 
growth of 10% (Exhibit 50). We believe that EVR’s M&A business skews to areas of the market that are poised to grow 
more (Exhibit 53). We see evidence of this in EVR’s stronger publicly available Dealogic data compared to IBanks peers, 
with its YTD completions up by ~25% YoY (from an already elevated base), vs. +14% on average across the group (Exhibit 
52). Further, its backlogs remain elevated, up ~180% since 1Q23, while announcements are up ~140% (Exhibit 51). 
Finally, EVR has notably diversified its business over the years to a greater degree than peers, which should allow it to 
perform in a wider range of market backdrops, and make it more of an all-weather firm (Exhibit 54). For example: 1) 
secondaries advisory contributed 14% of 2025 revenue for EVR, vs. only 5% for the group (Exhibit 55). 2) EVR’s sponsor 
business, a key investment area, has reached scale, as it is now middle of the group in terms of % of total firmwide M&A 
revenue according to Dealogic (Exhibit 53). 
HLI (Buy, $148 12-month PT, 16% upside): We remain constructive on HLI, underpinned by improving M&A trends after 
n
a challenging CY2Q26, and its through-the-cycle business mix, given best-in-class restructuring skew (ex. PJT) at 22% of 
revenue (vs. 10% for peers - Exhibit 56). We expect momentum in restructuring to persist and potentially accelerate, 
given dislocations in private credit and software, as well as elevated energy price volatility. The company’s mid-cap and 
sponsor skew also offers greater upside optionality, relative to large-cap M&A that is already strong. Finally, the company 
offers best-in-class margins, and much less relative margin downside, due to its fixed 61.5% comp ratio. We model a 24%

Our investment bank stocks declined 10% on average in 3Q26, underperforming the S&P 500 by 12pp, driven by both 
multiple derating and estimate downgrades, on investor concerns including: 1) that we are mid-M&A cycle, which

the ingredients for M&A growth remain strong for the group, with several years of M&A growth left. The group trades at

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted 
are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized 
ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary 
depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. 
Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based 
on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a 
stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future 
compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the 
potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of 
the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be 
discussed in research.

comparisons between companies in different sectors and markets.
