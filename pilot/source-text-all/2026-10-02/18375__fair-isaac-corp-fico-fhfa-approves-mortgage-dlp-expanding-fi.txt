On 10/1, FHFA Director Bill Pulte approved FICO’s Mortgage Direct 
License Program, enabling mortgage resellers to calculate FICO 
scores using bureau-provided credit report data and pay associated 
fees directly to FICO, or continue sourcing bureau-calculated scores. 
FICO has signed agreements covering ~73% of reseller volume and 
could reach ~90% if it completes negotiations with another large 
reseller, establishing a meaningful alternative to the bureaus’ score 
calculation and distribution channel. Strategically, DLP enables FICO 
to introduce performance-based pricing that could lower its upfront 
score cost to a level more competitive with VantageScore, mitigating 
potential market-share pressure, and link incremental economics 
more directly to funded loans. FICO could also avoid per-score or 
per-bureau fees, reducing potential revenue pressure from 
waterfalling and bi-merge adoption. The bureaus remain critical 
providers of underlying credit data, though FICO noted that the 
bureaus cannot use penalty fees or differential credit file pricing to 
disadvantage reseller-calculated scores. FICO has not disclosed 
F2027 pricing or made pricing commitments to the FHFA Director, 
and the FHFA has not committed to a FICO 10T implementation 
timeline, though the FHA has confirmed January 1 FICO 10T 
implementation for FHA-insured mortgages. 
Mortgage DLP receives FHFA Director approval. Overnight on 
10/1, FHFA Director Bill Pulte approved FICO’s Mortgage Direct 
License Program, which enables mortgage resellers to calculate FICO 
scores and pay the associated fees directly to FICO. Under the DLP, 
resellers can use credit report data provided by the credit bureaus 
to calculate FICO scores directly or continue sourcing scores 
calculated by the bureaus. FICO has signed DLP agreements 
covering ~73% of reseller volume and is negotiating with another 
large reseller that could increase coverage to ~90%. The approval 
establishes a meaningful alternative channel for calculating and 
distributing FICO scores.

DLP creates flexibility to introduce performance-based pricing. FICO can use the 
reseller channel to implement performance-based pricing for mortgage scores, an 
option that is not currently available through the credit bureaus. This would expand 
FICO’s pricing architecture beyond a flat or per-score fee by linking economics more 
directly to the value delivered. The performance-based model would lower FICO’s 
upfront score price to a level that is more competitive with VantageScore, mitigating 
potential market share pressure from FICO’s higher initial cost. FICO could also design 
the model without a per-score or per-bureau fee, helping mitigate potential revenue 
pressure from waterfalling and bi-merge adoption. We expect FICO to implement DLP in 
F2027. 
Bureaus would have limited ability to disadvantage the reseller channel. Resellers 
can calculate FICO scores directly using bureau-provided credit report data or continue 
obtaining bureau-calculated scores when packaging credit information for lenders. The 
bureaus remain critical to the process since they provide the underlying credit-report 
data, but DLP reduces their role as the exclusive calculation and distribution channel. 
FICO noted that the bureaus are unlikely to withhold credit-report data since doing so 
would sacrifice credit-file revenue and prevent them from fulfilling lender requests. FICO 
also stated that the bureaus cannot impose penalty fees or vary credit-file pricing based 
on where the score is calculated or whether the lender uses FICO or VantageScore, 
though the basis for this constraint and how it would be enforced remain unclear. 
Assuming the constraint holds, the bureaus would have limited ability to use their 
control of the underlying data to make the reseller channel economically unattractive. 
FICO 10T timing and F2027 pricing remain unresolved. FICO has not shared F2027 
pricing with the FHFA Director and made no related pricing commitments as part of the 
DLP approval. The FHFA also has not committed to a timeline for implementing FICO 
10T, leaving the timing of broader adoption uncertain. The FHA has confirmed a January 
1 implementation date for FICO 10T for FHA-insured mortgages.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
