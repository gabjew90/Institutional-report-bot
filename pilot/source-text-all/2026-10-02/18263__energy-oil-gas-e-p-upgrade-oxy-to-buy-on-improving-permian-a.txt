In this note, we 1) upgrade Occidental Petroleum (OXY) to Buy from Neutral, 2) 
downgrade Tourmaline Oil (TOU) to Sell from Buy and 3) update estimates across 
our US E&P coverage incorporating an updated price deck with $80/$75 per bbl 
Brent pricing in 2027/2028. 
We upgrade OXY with a raised 12-month price target to $69 given OXY’s 1) 
differentiated advanced recovery approach, 2) strong outlook for debt reduction 
and dividend growth potential, 3) self-help initiative with a target of $4.0 bn in cash 
flow improvement by 2030, and 4) attractive risk/reward skew given discounted 
valuation at current levels. Across our oil E&P coverage, we have seen strong YTD 
share price performance from our oil E&P coverage (+34%) given an elevated oil 
pricing environment, and we have seen OXY shares (+34%) trade in line with our 
broader oil E&P coverage despite what we see as structural improvements that OXY 
has driven across its business (Exhibit 1) through an incremental focus on capital 
efficiency and deleveraging under the new CEO.  
For TOU, we downgrade to Sell with a lowered 12-month price target of C$49 driven 
by 1) a challenging Western Canadian natural gas macro environment in the 
near-term despite long-term progress with LNG Canada Phase 2 FID, 2) a premium 
valuation relative to peers, and 3) delays to a sustainable FCF inflection following the 
one-year NEBC infrastructure buildout pause. We have seen the AECO forward curve 
meaningfully decline while Henry Hub pricing has remained more resilient (Exhibit 2), 
and we seek more clarity on the timing of demand catalysts in Western Canada to 
materialize. As a result, we revise our AECO differential to Henry Hub to 
US$1.50-$1.75/MMBtu (vs US$1.25-$1.50/MMBtu prior) in 2027/2028. While TOU’s 
incremental focus on medium-term cash flow optimization and shareholder returns 
is positive, our more cautious outlook on the Western Canadian gas macro 
environment tactically in 2027/2028 and the current premium valuation to peers 
drives our revised view. We see more attractive risk/reward skew elsewhere in the 
natural gas E&P complex, such as AR, which we see trading at a 15% FCF yield on

1) OXY’s differentiated advanced recovery strategy strengthens core oil 
and gas business 
Advanced recovery techniques have grown in relevance across the Oil E&P industry in 
the context of the discussion around the maturation of US oil shale basins, the 
emergence of incremental chemical treatment programs such as surfactants, and the 
early innings of exploration programs in deeper oil reservoirs. We see OXY as an 
experienced leader in advanced recovery techniques - particularly for EOR 
developments that leverage CO2 use - and highlight the >4 BBOE of resource 
opportunity for OXY across conventional and unconventional advanced recovery 
developments. Year-to-date, we have seen OXY report strong oil productivity relative to 
peers (Exhibit 4) as the company has stacked a full-suite of EOR solutions across its 
operations, and historically, this has helped OXY drive a strong track record of quarterly 
outperformance versus consensus expectations on total production (Exhibit 5). As OXY 
continues to test and scale its advanced recovery solutions across upstream operations, 
we see these developments strengthening the base production levels of OXY’s shale 
portfolio and driving various pathways for cash flow efficiency and production rates to 
surprise to the upside over time.  
For conventional operations, OXY sees a >2 BBOE resource opportunity across 1.2 mn 
acres and 100+ Mboe/d of production, primarily driven by early EOR sidetrack projects 
and conventional horizontal development. OXY has quickly adopted sidetracks as a new 
technique to build on its existing EOR infrastructure across conventional operations with 
60 sidetracks implemented year-to-date, and OXY has seen a 6 Mbo/d uplift in 2026 
stemming from the sidetrack program. OXY expects to reach 100 sidetracks by YE2026 
and the company has identified 500+ opportunities for future sidetracks. OXY also sees 
>200 MMBOE of development ready resource unlocked by conventional horizontal 
techniques. OXY notes its conventional horizontal opportunities are low-cost and have 
high oil cuts of ~80%, and the seven horizontal wells drilled year-to-date added 5 Mbo/d 
of resource. OXY has identified 450+ wells with potential resource upside from 
conventional horizontal opportunities, and the company sees additional upside through 
leveraging CO2 EOR in these developments. 
For unconventional operations, we see OXY leveraging its extensive expertise in CO2 
capabilities across future developments that unlock a combined >2 BBOE of resource. 
OXY highlighted the potential for a ~70%-140% EUR uplift in recovery as a result of key 
Advanced Recovery initiatives. OXY is working on three commercial developments, with 
each development expected to deliver ~$125 mn in cumulative free cash flow over 10 
years and OXY has identified 30 more development-ready projects the company can 
pursue over time. We also see OXY rapidly developing their subsurface capabilities, as 
the company leveraged AI to optimize completion designs that increased EUR by 15% 
compared to previous well designs. We also see OXY as a key participant in the emerging 
Barnett play given 124k acres available to target the zone at an average breakeven 
<$35/bbl WTI in core areas. From 2021-2025, OXY tested ~76 wells targeting the 
Barnett play and have reduced well costs by 40% since 2023 to ~$750/ft year-to-date in 
2026.

including the data center co-location opportunity TOU is pursuing, and we remain 
focused and hold a more cautious view on the timing of new natural gas demand ahead 
of any longer term LNG demand increase.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.

their respective areas of coverage.  The addition or removal of stocks from such Conviction Lists are managed by the Investment Review Committee or 
other designated committee in each respective region and do not represent a change in the analysts’ investment rating for such stocks.    
Total return potential represents the upside or downside differential between the current share price and the price target, including all paid or 
anticipated dividends, expected during the time horizon associated with the price target. Price targets are required for all covered stocks. The total 
return potential, price target and associated time horizon are stated in each report adding or reiterating an Investment List membership.
