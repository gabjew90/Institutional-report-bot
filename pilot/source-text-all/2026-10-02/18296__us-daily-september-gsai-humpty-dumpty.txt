September GSAI: Humpty Dumpty  
 
 
The Goldman Sachs Analyst Index (GSAI) declined by 9.1pt to 55.6 in September (Exhibit 
1). The composition was mixed-to-weak, as the shipments component declined to its 
lowest level since April 2025 (-29.3pt to 40.8) and the employment component also 
declined (-5.1pt to 52.2), while the new orders component increased (+3.8pt to 62.5). 
The exports (-6.3pt to 50.0) and inventories (-10.7pt to 49.4) components both 
declined. The orders less inventories gap increased (+14.5pt to 13.1). 
The GSAI’s materials prices (-5.8pt to 75.0), wages (-5.3pt to 68.2), and output prices 
(-12.2pt to 61.1) components all declined. 
 
Other business activity surveys were mixed but little changed on net this month. Our 
manufacturing survey tracker edged down (-0.6pt to 56.2), while our services survey 
tracker ticked up (+0.4pt to 53.2) in September. We found that some of the strength in 
the level of our survey trackers is a result of nominal bias, the effect of prices on survey 
index levels caused by respondents who think about shipments and orders in dollar 
rather than real terms. The employment components of both our manufacturing (+0.1pt 
to 52.5) and services (+1.2pt to 50.8) survey trackers increased.

In the special questions in this month’s Dallas Fed surveys, firms expected the pace of 
wage inflation to remain roughly unchanged, input price inflation to slow, and output 
price inflation to accelerate over the next year. Firms reported that wages increased by 
3.9% over the past year, below the 4.0% pace when the question was last asked in June. 
Firms expected wages to increase by 3.9% over the next year. Companies reported that 
non-labor input prices increased 4.9% over the past year, a notable acceleration from 
the 4.4% pace in the June survey. Over the next year, firms expected input price inflation 
to slow to a 4.6% pace. Firms reported that output prices increased by 3.0% over the 
past year, up from 2.9% in the June survey, and expected them to accelerate further to a 
3.5% pace over the next year.
