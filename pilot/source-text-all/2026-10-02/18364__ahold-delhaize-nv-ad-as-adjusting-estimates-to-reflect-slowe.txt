GS View: We expect Ahold Delhaize to have faced a persistently 
challenging operating environment in 3Q26E, with muted food inflation 
in Europe and weak US grocery demand constraining top-line momentum 
(see below). In Europe, food CPI has continued to decelerate, while 
declining food PPI points to further near-term pressure. In the US, lower 
SNAP participation, soft consumer trends, Cyclospora, and negative 
market volumes remain headwinds, although we adjust our estimated 
3Q26E SNAP impact to c.70bps (from c.90bps) due to phasing where cost 
sharing implementation disproportionately impacts 4Q. Looking ahead, 
SNAP cost-sharing implementation and potential additional benefit costs 
from October 2027 remain medium-term risks. The recent share-price 
decline reflects multiple de-rating amid uncertainty around US margins, 
online mix, European inflation, and management transition in our view. 
See inside for our key charts ahead of 3Q26. We maintain our Neutral 
rating and €39 price target. 
Implications: We update 3Q26E/FY26E underlying diluted EPS to 
€0.66/€2.67 (from €0.71/€2.73), reflecting refreshed FX, 
Europe/US 3Q26E LFLs (ex-fuel) of +1.1%/flat (from 
+1.5%/+0.9%), and Europe/US FY26E LFLs (ex-fuel) of 
+1.6%/+0.6% (from +1.8%/+1.0%). This is accompanied by updated 
Europe/US 3Q26E underlying operating margins of 3.7%/4.2% 
(from 4.0%/4.5%) primarily to reflect higher energy costs, and 
FY26E margins of 3.8%/4.4% (from 3.8%/4.5%). All in, these

€957m/€3,778m). We note our forecasts are on a 52-week basis. 
Full model is available on our portal.

What We’re Seeing: CPI Muted in Europe, SNAP Participation Headwinds, Multiple Compression 
 
 
European food inflation continues to slow: Food inflation releases have shown a 
consistent slowdown through the third quarter, particularly in core markets. In 3Q to 
date, the Netherlands has averaged -0.7% and Belgium +0.2% which we believe will 
pressure European top line growth despite expected positive volume impacts (Europe 
3Q26E LFL +1.1%, from +1.5%). 
 
PPI trends imply continued weakness: Prior to 2Q26 results, we noted downside risks 
to 2026 food inflation using lagged PPI as a guide. This trend has continued into 3Q26, 
with the Netherlands and the European Area food PPI still in decline. We reiterate our 
view on near-term food CPI risk but see rising energy prices and input costs potentially 
affecting 2027 to the upside.

Key risks 
 
 
Key upside risks to our view and target price: 
(1) Successful integration of recent acquisitions (e.g., Profi in Romania and Delfood in 
Belgium) may yield higher-than-expected cost synergies; 
(2) A weakening EUR vs. USD could prove a material translation FX tailwind; 
(3) An acceleration in market share gains driven by price investments in the US; 
(4) GLP-1 customer behaviour shifts into higher-margin categories faster than we 
anticipate; and 
(5) Online grocery profitability improves faster than expected as the store-pick model 
matures in the US and volumes scale in European automated warehouses. 
Key downside risks: 
(1) Higher-than-expected integration/conversion costs from acquisitions which could 
prove a margin drag; 
(2) A market environment which is such that inflation cannot be passed through to 
consumers at the levels we forecast; 
(3) A strengthening EUR vs. USD could prove a material translation FX headwind; 
(4) A continued loss of market share in the US, which could become a structural top line 
and earnings drag; 
(5) Aggressive competitor price investments in Europe requiring a similar level of 
investment by Ahold; 
(6) Any decline in customer behaviour either in terms of trading down or cutting food at 
home consumption that could be caused by SNAP headwinds or an increased uptake of 
GLP-1 type drugs; and 
(7) A faster shift of groceries online, which would be more margin-dilutive than we 
believe.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
