Philips reports Q3 26 results on Nov 3. We expect Q3 results to 
highlight a couple of key trends for Philips: (1) following subdued 
order intake growth in Q2, we expect a rebound back to MSD in Q3 
results, which would mark a return to solid trends exhibited over last 
12 months, as innovation lays the foundations for better 
momentum, (2) a modest deceleration in group organic revenue 
growth to 3.4% (vs 4.1% in Q2; 3.3% in Visible Alpha consensus) 
with a healthier balance between the divisions, (3) slight decline in 
Y/Y margin, in line with previous guidance, as the impact of cost 
inflation weighs on margin ahead of mitigation efforts beginning to 
meaningfully flow the P&L. Overall we forecast Q3 26e revenue of 
€4,439m and adjusted EBITA of €521m (+1%/-2% vs consensus). 
Investor focus on Q4 margin inflection, cost environment and 
China. Philips shares have underperformed broader European 
Medtech since Q2 results. Based on our investor discussions, 
investors concerns gravitate around a couple of key issues; (1) 
implied inflection for Q4 margin and associated execution risks, (2) 
concerns around potential impact of Centralised Procurement in 
China and (3) the impacts of cost inflation and potential implications 
for margin expansion into FY27. We frame each of those points in 
this note, and look to Q3 results and company conference call to 
provide further commentary and datapoints on those elements of 
the debate.  
Based on our estimates, Philips is trading on 12.6x NTM P/E, having 
derated on absolute and relative basis since Q2 results. In context of 
valuation and our constructive view on the momentum of Philips’ 
innovation and order book, which should support top and bottom line 
momentum into Q4 and FY27, we continue to see an attractive risk 
reward and reiterate our Buy rating.

What to expect at Q3? 
 
 
We forecast 3.4% organic growth (vs 3.3% consensus) in Q3 driving €4,439m sales (+1% 
vs consensus). GSe 11.7% margin (-30bps vs 12.1% consensus, -50bps y/y) as 
inflationary pressure persists across Philips’ cost base, with mitigation efforts scaling 
through the year; Q3 will be hit hardest by inflation while carrying less offsetting 
mitigation relative to Q4. Additionally, tariffs should not be a margin detractor in Q3, but 
are expected to turn into a modest tailwind from Q4.  We expect margin progression to 
look most challenging in certain parts of the portfolio (e.g., Precision Diagnosis) even as 
underlying order momentum continues to build.  
In Personal Health, we forecast 4.0% organic growth (vs 3.2% consensus), driving 
n
€930m Q3 26 sales (+2% vs consensus) noting the strong 10.9% comparison base 
from Q3 25 (which benefited from an easy China comp following prior-year 
destocking). We now expect that base effect to fully lap, leaving underlying 
momentum in the US and Europe as the primary growth driver. We forecast a 17.3% 
adj. EBITA margin (+20bps y/y, but -40bps vs consensus), with adj. EBITA of €161m 
-1% below consensus.  
In D&T, we forecast 3.0% organic growth, in line with consensus, and €2,169m sales 
n

improving commercial momentum in PDx ex. China. This implies a further sequential

and improvements in PD, supported by recent launches and order-intake strength 
ex. China. We forecast an 11.3% adj. EBITA margin (-50bps y/y, +20bps vs

inflation in the near term.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
