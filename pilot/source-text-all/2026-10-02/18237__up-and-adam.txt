provides useful perspective, corporate fundamentals ultimately drive markets, and they remain supportive of further equity gains.

Alongside narrowing leadership among large caps, 3Q also saw fading momentum in small caps. Russell 2000 companies tend to be 
more sensitive to interest rates, with ~54% of their debt tied to floating rates versus ~22% for the S&P 500. Against that backdrop, small 
caps trailed large caps by 9.6% during 3Q, their widest quarterly underperformance since 1Q20. We continue to favor large caps, 
supported by the S&P 500's more attractive sector mix, including greater Technology exposure, and stronger EPS revisions.

Today marks one year since the start of the 2025 partial government shutdown, which lasted a record 43 days. Amid the chronic budget 
deficits that we highlighted earlier this week, the federal debt is nearing the $41.1t statutory limit, most recently raised by $5t in July 
2025. The Bipartisan Policy Center projects the debt ceiling will be reached by mid-summer 2027, with the Treasury able to operate for 
another 6 to 9 months using cash and ‘extraordinary measures’. The new Congress elected on Nov. 3 will need to address this issue.
The bond market sell-off in 3Q was one for the history books, with the 10-year Treasury yield surging 85 bps to a 24-year high of 5.29%. 
Rising oil prices, the Fed’s hawkish pivot, and a more robust growth backdrop all contributed to the move, but the magnitude of the 
sell-off appears disproportionate to the shift in the fundamental outlook. Notably, the rise in yields ranks in the top ten of largest 
quarterly increases since 1980 and the biggest since 4Q 2016. In our view, the sell-off is getting increasingly stretched.
