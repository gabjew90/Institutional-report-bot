Accenture Plc (ACN): Better-than-expected FY27 guidance plus solid 
near-term metrics should drive stock re-rating
1 October 2026 | 12:40PM EDT
Key stock takeaways: We expect the stock to trade higher following stronger results 
and bookings, with FY27 revenue guidance that came in above the Street - set 
against very bearish investor expectations heading into the print.  The company 
delivered a solid quarter, with FY27 guidance that was just above with the Street; 
bookings growth was also solid.  In addition, gross and operating margins slightly 
beat the Street - which has been a point of contention among investors in recent 
quarters.  We believe investors had expected weaker results and guidance given the 
impact of higher macro uncertainty on corporate confidence (and IT services 
budgets) over the past several months.  Although the overall IT services environment 
remains challenging, we believe the company’s commentary on idiosyncratic growth 
drivers - as well as the stable organic growth outlook implied by FY27 guidance - will 
drive a modest re-rating for the stock.  We recognize that the investor debate on 
whether the IT services sector is structurally impacted by AI is likely to continue.  For 
the stock to further re-rate toward historical multiples, we think the company will 
need to demonstrate several quarters of organic revenue acceleration while at the 
same time showing gross margin expansion which validates positive unit economics 
for the business.  We remain Buy rated on Accenture, as we believe the company is 
among the best positioned to capture transformational enterprise AI work and is well 
positioned to capture a recovery in discretionary spending as it occurs. 
Commentary from the call: 
Demand commentary: Management continues to see a muted spending 
n
environment for IT Services, as macro uncertainty has weighed on corporate 
confidence and priorities.  However, on an idiosyncratic level, Accenture 
continues to see strong demand for large-scale transformational projects which 
is driving its growth.  Relative to the company’s FY27 guidance for 3%-6% CC 
revenue growth, the company sees the high end of the range capturing a stable 
to modestly improving demand outlook, with the low end of range assuming 
further deterioration.  Although the direct impact of the Mideast conflict has 
worsened somewhat, the indirect impact on other industries appears to have 
stabilized.  Overall, the company expects Consulting and Managed Services to 
each grow roughly in line with the company’s overall guidance for FY27.  
Management also noted an expected inorganic contribution of 2.0% - 2.5% 
entering FY27, which we believe translates to flat to 4.0% organic growth 
entering the year.

AI demand trends and AI model safety: Management continues to see AI as a clear 
n

than before. Management cited a number of client examples where the company is

Anthropic on AI model safety, with both companies each investing $1bn over five

Margins: Accenture guided FY27 operating margin guidance to 16.0% at the 
n
midpoint, with 10-30bps of margin expansion yoy, slightly above the Street 
expectations of 15.9%. Accenture beat Street expectations on 4Q gross margin 
(32.0% vs. 31.8% GS/Street) also exceeded on operating margin (15.3% vs. 15.2% 
GS/Street). Given the company’s solid gross margin expansion demonstrated in FY26 
(up 10bps yoy), we expect gross margin to be a modest tailwind in FY27, 
contributing to the ~20bps of expansion we are now modeling - driven by a greater 
proportion of fixed-price contracts and overall fixed-cost leverage. 
Read-through to our coverage: We view Accenture’s results as modestly positive for 
our IT Services coverage, as the pace and mix of revenue growth in 4Q and FY27 
guidance suggest limited near-term improvement in the broader discretionary 
environment. 
4Q revenue results above the Street, with stronger bookings, margins, and EPS: 
Accenture reported 4Q26 revenue of $18.68 bn, above the Street (Visible Alpha 
Consensus Data) at $18.04 bn and our estimate of $18.13 bn. Managed Services revenue 
was above the Street ($9.40 bn), and Consulting revenue ($9.28 bn) was also above the 
Street. Adjusted operating margin was 15.3%, slightly above the Street and our 
estimates at 15.2%. EPS from continuing operations of $3.29 was above the Street at 
$3.19 and our estimate of $3.12. Accenture reported new bookings of $22.2 bn in 4Q26, 
an increase of 5% in local currency ($9.40 bn in Consulting, up 6%), $12.77 bn in 
Managed Services (up 3%, albeit lumpy) and was well above the Street at $19.90 bn. In 
terms of vertical performance in 4Q26, Communications/Media/Technology was up 
11% in CC, Financials was up 6%, Health & Public Services was up 9%, Products was up 
4%, and Resources was up 6%. 
FY27 CC revenue guidance is above the Street, with EPS in line: For FY27, Accenture 
guided to constant currency revenue growth of 3% - 6%, or $76.4 - $78.6 bn (vs. our 
estimate of 3.0% - 4.0%; we model 3.7% CC growth or $76.2 bn vs. the Street at $76.4 
bn), and the company expects a neutral FX impact. The company noted that this outlook 
represents growth of 3% - 6% in CC.  Accenture guided for 10-30 bps of operating 
margin expansion (vs. our estimate of 10 - 30bps of expansion; we model 20 bps of 
expansion or 16.0% vs. the Street at 15.9%). Accenture also guided for FY27 adjusted 
EPS of $14.39 - $14.81, or growth of 3% - 6% (vs. our estimate of 6.0% - 8.0%; we 
model FY27 at $14.75 or ~6.9% growth vs. the Street at $14.62). The company guided 
free cash flow in the range of $11.9 - $12.7 bn, and aims to return ~$9.5 bn through 
dividends and share repurchases. For 1Q27, Accenture guided for 2% - 6% in constant 
currency revenue growth with a negative 1% FX impact, or $18.95 - 19.60 bn vs. the 
Street and our estimates at $19.4bn (roughly consistent with our preview; we model 
3.5% and a negative FX impact of .1%).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
