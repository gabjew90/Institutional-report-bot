Valuation & Risks 
 
Strong F4Q - M&Aster Class in Platformization 
We reiterate our Buy rating on PANW following a strong F4Q finish and FY27 
outlook that reinforce the company’s position as the leading cybersecurity 
consolidator at what increasingly looks like a meaningful AI-driven demand 
inflection. Results exceeded guidance across every major metric and the 
company impressively recorded nearly $1bn of net new NGS ARR, almost double 
the year-ago level, alongside record platformization activity and a second 
consecutive quarter of accelerating pro forma bookings growth. Initial FY27 
guidance was likewise solid as the respective Revenue and RPO growth outlooks 
were set ahead of Street and NGS ARR was set in line with likely room for a steady 
beat cadence through the year. While the headline growth rates are flattered by 
acquisitions, we view the breadth of performance across Network & AI Security, 
Cortex and Idira, together with the size of strategic customer commitments, as 
clear evidence that the underlying platformization engine is strengthening rather 
than being obscured by M&A.  
Just as important, we believe the quarter materially speaks to management’s 
credibility as an acquirer and integrator. CyberArk, now Idira, and Chronosphere 
are both scaling faster than their prior standalone trajectories and performing 
ahead of initial expectations. Observability ARR has more than doubled since the 
Chronosphere close to over $500mn, with XSIAM contributing to half of net new 
Chronosphere logos in the quarter, while Idira generated over 400 shared leads 
and more than 200 new-logo wins from PANW’s installed base. Integration 
synergies for CyberArk remain three to six months ahead of plan, and 
management indicated that the business is approaching PANW’s standalone 
margin profile only two quarters after close. This matters because PANW is now 
leveraging a demonstrated ability to identify, acquire and rapidly scale strategic 
assets to move faster through an unusually fluid technology cycle. The 
acquisition of Console, announced as closed yesterday, appears consistent with 
that playbook by adding AI-native product-development capabilities to Cortex 
and advancing the longer-term objective of making security operations more 
agentic, data-driven and autonomous.  
Idira is perhaps the clearest rebuttal to the bear case that CyberArk customers 
would become dislocated or churn following the acquisition. Instead, pro forma 
Idira revenue grew 21% in FY26, bookings grew faster than revenue in F4Q, 
CyberArk ACV growth accelerated to 27% from 20% in F2Q, and $5mn-plus deal 
activity increased 50% y/y. Management’s characterization of the first integration 
phase was effectively “do not break it, then accelerate it,” and the evidence so far 
suggests PANW has accomplished both. Beyond retaining momentum in

what could likely become the defining identity problem of the AI era: governing

credentials and privileges. In our view, Idira is not just holding share after the deal, 
but instead appears to be gaining relevance and distribution velocity inside

The broader AI security opportunity is similarly moving from narrative to dollars. 
Prisma AIRS reached approximately $120mn of ARR within four quarters of 
general availability, making it PANW’s fastest-scaling product, with more than 
800 customers and over 100 Agentic Endpoint Security (i.e., Koi) logos. 
Momentum is also appearing across the rest of the portfolio: software firewall AI 
traffic has increased more than fourfold since June, agentic traffic across SASE 
has risen more than ninefold over nine months, and Chronosphere is benefiting 
from the observability demands created by AI-native workloads. Together with 
recent strength across CrowdStrike and SentinelOne, we believe these data 
points suggest AI security is already at least a several-hundred-million-dollar 
market and scaling rapidly. More strategically, this innovation cycle appears to be 
accruing value differently from prior ones. Historically, new security categories 
often favored point-product disruptors, in part because incumbents were slow to 
innovate or relied on M&A that proved difficult to integrate. AI is moving too 
quickly, touching too many control points and creating too much interconnected 
data, identity and infrastructure complexity for that pattern to repeat cleanly. 
Customers appear to be favoring scaled, trusted platforms capable of combining 
frontier innovation with integrated telemetry, policy and machine-speed 
response. In other words, AI is not only necessitating the development of a novel 
security segment, it is strengthening the architectural and economic arguments 
for consolidation.  
In part a reflection of mgmt.'s impressive ability to seamlessly integrate M&A of 
various scales, PANW continues to drive impressive leverage through the model 
(i.e., NGOM expanded 40bps y/y in FY26, adj. FCF margin was 38%+ for the fourth 
consecutive year) despite absorbing the company’s two largest acquisitions, 
integration costs and the ongoing transition toward annual billing. Management 
expects continued operating leverage and M&A synergies to more than offset 
higher cloud-hosting and hardware component costs in FY27 and improving cash 
flow visibility underscored mgmt.'s confidence in reaffirming their 40% FCF 
margin target for FY28.  
We view the combination of accelerating platform adoption, better-than-
expected performance from Idira and Chronosphere, strong early traction with AI 
security and sustained cash-flow discipline as broadly supportive of our thesis. As 
one of the highest quality cyber platforms, PANW is well positioned to take 
outsized share as enterprises seek strategic partners to safely adopt, and protect 
against, AI. We therefore reiterate our Buy rating and update our DCF-derived TP 
to $430.
