Western Tungsten Re-Shoring Trade. Upside
Hinges On Sangdong Ramp. Init BUY.
Almonty offers long-dated leverage to Western tungsten supply-chain
re-shoring through Sangdong, Panasqueira, Browns Lake, and planned
downstream oxide capacity. China controls ~80% of supply, while U.S. defense
procurement restrictions begin in 2027. Phase I commercial production is the
key proof point: upside depends on a clean Sangdong ramp, Phase II timing,
and sustained Western pricing premiums. We initiate with a Buy rating and a
$26.25/sh. price target.
Our Thesis: Almonty is repositioning from a tungsten miner into a Western-aligned critical minerals
platform. Sangdong Phase I, the company's project in South Korea, gives it one of the largest high-
grade tungsten assets outside China, while Phase II could double throughput to 1.2m tpa and
supply ~40% of non-China demand. Panasqueira adds premium low-impurity concentrate, Browns
Lake adds a U.S. footprint, and the planned 4,000 tpa nano tungsten oxide facility could move the
company downstream.
Key Debates: 1/ Whether Sangdong can reach steady-state production without delays or cost
overruns; 2/ Whether tungsten prices and Western premiums remain supported by export controls,
defense procurement rules, and reshoring demand; 3/ Whether leverage, project financing, and
internal control weaknesses create dilution or refinancing risk before the asset base fully scales.
Our Takes: We view Almonty as a scarce public-market way to gain exposure to Western tungsten
supply security. Industrial demand for drills and cutting tools represent ~60% of demand, but it is
the ~12% used in military applications (armor piercing rounds, rocket nozzles, etc.) that has driven
the fly-up in prices over the last couple of years. The 21-year Global Tungsten & Powders offtake
covers ~90% of Sangdong Phase I volumes and provides revenue visibility, while SeAH’s life-of-mine
molybdenum offtake adds embedded optionality. Sangdong Phase I commercial production in
June 2026 is a key de-risking milestone, but valuation should still distinguish Phase I execution from
Phase II, downstream oxide, Browns Lake, Panasqueira L4, Los Santos tailings, and molybdenum
upside. Our model supports fair value of $26.25/sh., after taking into account estimated ASPs of
$1,300/mtu. Our project risk adjustment is equivalent to simply using an 11% discount rate. Each
100bps shift in the discount rate moves our fair value estimate $2.00/sh., and each $100/mtu shift
in the tungsten ASP forecast moves the fair value estimate $2.40/sh.
Risks: 1/ Sangdong ramp execution; 2/ Phase II starting up later in 2H27 or in 2028; 3/ Debt
and refinancing costs; 4/ Operating execution; 5/ Commodity volatility; 6/ Regulatory complexity,
particularly around permitting.

Downstream Integration & Brownfield Optionality
To enhance margins and vertically integrate, Almonty is planning a 4,000 tpa nano tungsten oxide plant

the local government and an LOI for up to $50m in project financing from KfW IPEX-Bank. South Korea

Separately, tailings reprocessing at Los Santos and the Panasqueira L4 extension use existing permits,
infrastructure, and technical expertise to generate near-term tonnes with limited underground mining
risk and minimal capital intensity, supporting core growth projects or debt reduction.
 
A Concentrated Market With Appetite For New Supply
The global tungsten market remains highly concentrated, with China controlling ~80% of worldwide
production as of 2025. This concentration creates material supply-chain vulnerabilities for Western
economies and defense infrastructure, particularly as geopolitical tensions continue to shape critical-
mineral policy and export restrictions. Against this backdrop, Sangdong positions Almonty as a primary
Western alternative to Chinese supply.
Almonty’s growth strategy centers on completing commissioning of 0.6m tpa Sangdong Phase I and
moving into Phase II development. The planned Phase II expansion is fully permitted and expected
to commence operations in 2027, doubling ore throughput to 1.2m tpa. At full capacity, Sangdong is
projected to supply ~40% of non-China tungsten demand, or ~8% of total global supply.
 
Aligned With Western Supply-Chain Initiatives
Almonty relocated its corporate headquarters to Dillon, Montana, and acquired the Gentung
Browns Lake Project, establishing a North American operational footprint aligned with U.S. defense
procurement standards and federal critical-mineral initiatives. Browns Lake targets initial output of
~140kt MTUs per year and could provide near-term domestic supply that supports government
contracts or strategic stockpile programs.

Investment Concerns
 
 
Operational & Ramp Execution Risk
Almonty faces typical underground mining risks. Unexpected rock formations, seismicity, stope
instability, flooding, and metallurgical recovery deviations. Material disruption at Sangdong or
Panasqueira could affect production volumes and contracted deliveries, potentially triggering offtake
penalties or force majeure negotiations. The transition from development to steady-state production
carries particular execution risk during commissioning, especially if ground conditions or equipment
performance delay nameplate capacity or raise sustaining capital needs. Run-of-mine processing
began in June 2026 using approximately 139,700 tonnes of stockpiled development ore, so the precise
timing of steady-state commercial concentrate grade and planned metallurgical recoveries remains
the key open question.
 
Buyer Concentration
Western tungsten markets are relatively consolidated, with a limited number of qualified processors,
creating customer concentration and negotiating leverage risk. Historically, the vast majority of
revenue from operating assets has been generated from sales to a single U.S. customer under long-
term supply agreements.
The GTP and SeAH offtakes mitigate this by locking in stable margins and cash-flow visibility for
most contracted production, and as Western governments prioritize critical-mineral security, Almonty's
position as a leading non-Chinese producer may strengthen negotiating leverage on future renewals
or expansions.
 
Commodity Price Leverage
Almonty remains unhedged to tungsten prices to preserve upside that might arise due to supply
constraints or rising Western demand. The offtake agreements provide floor-price downside
protection, but a severe or prolonged decline in ammonium paratungstate prices would pressure
uncontracted volumes, reduce cash flow, and potentially threaten project-debt covenant compliance.
Conversely, geopolitical tension, Chinese export restrictions, or faster Western defense spending could
drive pricing above contracted floor levels and create margin upside on merchant production.
 
Labor Market Risk
The technical labor pool required to build and operate underground tungsten mines is limited. Labor
shortages or wage inflation in South Korea, Portugal, and the U.S. could raise operating expenses or
delay timelines if key engineering, metallurgical, or underground trade roles remain unfilled.
 
Macro Considerations
Tungsten and molybdenum demand is undergoing a structural and policy-driven transformation that
is increasingly decoupled from traditional economic cycles. Industrial tooling remains tied to global
manufacturing activity, but demand is increasingly supported by multi-year defense re-armament
and advanced technology growth. This shift has elevated tungsten and molybdenum from cyclical
industrial inputs to strategic security assets.

Income Statement
The Sangdong tungsten mine is ramping, and 2H26 revenues will accelerate geometrically
as Sangdong contributes meaningful tungsten concentrate volumes alongside production from
Panasqueira and other assets. Revenue growth is expected to be driven primarily by increasing
tungsten output rather than price, although price remains elevated. Margins should expand as
productivity is supported by automation, modern flotation technology, and scale advantages. 2027
will likely be the first full year of normalized production. As throughput stabilizes and fixed costs are
absorbed across higher volumes, operating margins and EBITDA should improve substantially. This
operating leverage, combined with higher production rates and the absence of major commissioning
disruptions suggest EBITDA will increase >200% in 2027 and jump another 55% in 2028. The increase
in debt level after the issuance of the convertible debt will be a headwind, but EPS still likely will increase
from a 0.79 loss in 2026 to $1.40 in 2027 and 2.20 in 2028.

Company Overview
Almonty Industries Inc. is a diversified global natural resource mining and development company
focused on acquiring, exploring, developing, mining, and milling tungsten ores and related minerals.
Its strategic focus is securing supply-chain independence for Western allies by producing conflict-free
tungsten concentrate and other strategic metals.
The company operates high-grade, long-life assets in South Korea, Portugal, Spain, and the U.S.,
positioning its footprint away from higher-risk or politically restricted jurisdictions. Revenue is
generated primarily through mining, processing, and shipping high-grade tungsten concentrates,
supplemented by copper and tin by-products. In early 2026, Almonty relocated its corporate
headquarters to Dillon, Montana.

completed commissioning in March 2026, with targeted ore throughput of 640ktpa and ~2.3ktpa
of tungsten concentrate. Run-of-mine ore processing began in June 2026 using stockpiled
development ore.

Appendix: Tungsten 101
Tungsten has emerged as one of the world's most strategically critical metals, possessing the
highest melting point of any element (3,422°C), exceptional density (~19.3 g/cm³), and unmatched
hardness. Once a sleepy industrial commodity, tungsten has been thrust to the forefront of geopolitical
competition.
China controls ~83% of global supply and has enacted sweeping export restrictions, while the West
scrambles to build alternative supply chains. Prices in North America have surged ~123% quarter-
over-quarter in 2026, reaching ~$251,000/t. The market is projected to grow from $7.6bn (2026) to
$11.6b by 2035 (CAGR ~4.8%), though near-term dynamics are driven by acute supply shocks rather
than secular demand growth alone.
Applications Landscape
Understanding tungsten's demand profile begins with its end-use diversity. The metal's physical
properties make it irreplaceable across several high-value industries.

segment. Cemented carbide—tungsten carbide combined with cobalt binder—is used extensively in

Defense & Aerospace applications (~15–20% of demand) constitute the single fastest-growing

Electronics & Semiconductors (~10–15% of demand) leverage tungsten in semiconductor
fabrication, where it serves as interconnect metallization and contact plugs in CMOS logic—particularly
critical in sub-7nm nodes. AI accelerator chips (GPUs, ASICs) drive incremental demand through
expanded chip area and complexity. Additional applications include filaments for incandescent and
halogen lamps (declining), X-ray tube anodes in medical imaging (growing), and specialized quantum
computing hardware in cryogenic environments.
Energy & Industrial applications (~10% of demand) encompass heating elements in high-
temperature industrial furnaces, electrical contacts and electrodes in plasma cutting and welding
equipment, and catalysts in petroleum refining (tungsten disulfide). Emerging applications include
electrodes in hydrogen electrolyzers, a nascent but growing segment.
Other Specialty Applications include radiation shielding in medical and nuclear applications,
counterweights and ballast for aviation, motorsport, and marine use, and sports equipment such as
golf club weights and darts.
Porter's Five Forces
Threat of New Entrants (low to moderate): Tungsten mining requires massive upfront capital
($200m–$1b+ for greenfield projects), long permitting timelines (7–15 years in many jurisdictions),
and specialized metallurgical processing expertise. Processing infrastructure—particularly APT
refining and carbide manufacturing—is highly concentrated and difficult to replicate quickly. However,
Western government subsidies through initiatives like U.S. DoD strategic mineral programs and the
EU Critical Raw Materials Act are actively de-risking new entrants, lowering effective barriers for well-
positioned junior miners. While barriers remain structurally high, policy tailwinds are beginning to erode
them at the margins.
Bargaining Power of Suppliers (Very high): China controls ~83% of mined output and an even larger
share (~90%+) of downstream processing including APT, oxide, and carbide powder production. Export
licensing, production quotas, and designated export company lists give the Chinese state quasi-
monopoly pricing power. The top five producers hold ~40% of the global market, dominated by Chinese
companies. Secondary and recycled supply (~30–35% of Western consumption) provides partial
offset but cannot fully substitute primary supply. Supplier power represents the defining structural
feature of this market and the most important variable in the entire investment thesis.
Bargaining Power of Buyers (Low to Moderate): Tungsten content represents a small fraction of end-
product cost—for example, in a drill bit—reducing sensitivity to raw material price increases. No viable
material substitutes exist for most applications; engineers cannot simply design out tungsten from
cemented carbide tools or kinetic energy penetrators. Large industrial buyers have scale and long-term
supply contracts that moderately improve their negotiating position. Defense buyers (governments)
are increasingly willing to pay premium prices to secure supply, further reducing price sensitivity.

Overall, buyers have limited power due to the lack of substitutes, which structurally advantages

Threat of Substitutes (Very Low): No commercially viable substitute exists for tungsten carbide
in hard metal tooling. Titanium carbide, cubic boron nitride, and ceramics are available but inferior
in their toughness-to-hardness balance. For kinetic energy munitions, depleted uranium (DU) is the
main alternative, but regulatory, environmental, and political constraints limit DU adoption—effectively
driving substitution toward tungsten. In semiconductors, molybdenum can substitute for some
applications but with performance tradeoffs. Recycling (secondary tungsten) is the closest functional
"substitute" for primary supply but depends on scrap availability. The substitution threat is structurally
negligible; tungsten is genuinely irreplaceable in core applications.
Competitive Rivalry Among Existing Players (Moderate) : At the mining and refining level, competition
is muted by Chinese state coordination through quota management that effectively coordinates
output. Western producers compete aggressively for off-take agreements and government contracts,
but the pool of credible players is small. Downstream tool manufacturers operate in a highly
competitive environment with a fragmented global customer base. Consolidation pressure exists, and
M&A activity—with majors acquiring junior tungsten assets—is accelerating. Rivalry is moderate and
increasing at the Western producer level as governments push allies to develop supply, though this is
less relevant given the structural supply squeeze.
Supply/Demand Dynamics
Supply Side Global mine supply totals ~85,000–90,000 MT of tungsten content annually. China
accounts for ~83% of mined output and ~90%+ of APT processing capacity. Key non-China producers
include Vietnam (~7%), Russia (~3%), Austria (Wolfram Bergbau), and emerging operations in Canada,
Portugal, and South Korea.
China enacted sweeping export licensing and quota regimes in 2025–2026, reducing APT exports
by up to 70% for certain product types. Secondary supply meets ~30–35% of Western consumption
through recycling of scrap carbide and spent catalysts. Multiple Western greenfield and brownfield
projects are in development, though most are 3–7 years from meaningful production scale. A ~17%
annual supply deficit appears likely through 2028, persisting longer given project lead times. A critical
supply risk exists: the 20–30 year timeline to rebuild meaningful non-Chinese processing capacity
represents a structural vulnerability that cannot be resolved through policy alone in the near term.
Demand Side Industrial production demand is moderate, tied to the global manufacturing cycle.
Defense and aerospace demand is strongly accelerating, driven by NATO rearmament, Indo-Pacific
build-up, and the Ukraine conflict, which are driving multi-year procurement cycles. Semiconductor and
AI hardware demand is growing as advanced node chip proliferation (AI, quantum) drives incremental
consumption. EV and clean energy applications are nascent but emerging, particularly in electrolyzer
electrodes and precision machining of EV drivetrain components. Recycling penetration is slowly
increasing but remains constrained by scrap availability and collection infrastructure in non-OECD
markets. Structural demand growth is robust at ~4–5% CAGR, but the near-term demand signal
from defense is the most important marginal driver. Western governments are stockpiling strategic
tungsten reserves, creating demand that is price-inelastic and multi-year in nature.

Tungsten is one of the most capital-intensive commodity markets relative to its size. Greenfield
mine development capital costs range from $300m to over $1bn depending on ore grade, location,
and infrastructure. Tungsten ore grades are typically low (0.1–0.5% WO3), requiring large tonnage

bilateral strategic stockpile agreements are reducing investor risk in select Western projects.
Pricing
Tungsten pricing is complex because the market lacks a centralized exchange (unlike LME metals).
Prices are set through bilateral contracts and published APT benchmark quotes from Metal Bulletin/
Fastmarkets.
Primary Structural Drivers. Chinese export policy is the single most important price driver. Export
quota tightening creates immediate price spikes in ex-China markets; the 2025–2026 restriction
regime caused a ~123% price surge in North America to ~$251,000/t. The supply-demand balance
establishes structural scarcity as the baseline condition, with a projected 17% annual supply deficit
through 2028. Energy costs significantly impact pricing because high-temperature processing makes
tungsten energy-sensitive, with gas and electricity price shocks transmitting directly to production
costs.
Secondary Cyclical Drivers Industrial production cycles—including manufacturing PMI, auto
production, and mining capex—drive cemented carbide demand. Defense procurement cycles are
multiyear, budget-driven, and relatively insensitive to commodity price fluctuations. USD strength
affects purchasing power since tungsten is USD-priced; dollar appreciation reduces purchasing
power for non-USD buyers. Inventory and stockpiling cycles create demand spikes during strategic
government stockpiling and temporary oversupply during destocking.
Macro and Geopolitical Drivers Sino-Western trade relations directly move tungsten prices through
escalation or de-escalation of trade tensions. Resource nationalism in emerging producer nations
(Vietnam, Rwanda) imposing export restrictions creates additional supply uncertainty. ESG and supply
chain transparency considerations are driving Western buyers to pay premiums for conflict-free,
traceable tungsten supply.
Pricing Outlook Near-term (2026–2027) pricing is expected to remain elevated and volatile, driven
by the export restriction regime and supply deficit. Medium-term (2027–2028) prices should remain
structurally high with potential for further spikes on geopolitical catalysts. Longer-term (2029–2030),
gradual moderation is expected as Western capacity comes online, though the price floor will remain
elevated versus pre-2024 norms.
