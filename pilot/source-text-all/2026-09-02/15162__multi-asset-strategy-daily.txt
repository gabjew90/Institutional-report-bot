Energy is back in the driving seat. Brent tested $96pb overnight as fresh US strikes against Iranian targets were 
followed by retaliation against US positions and allies in the region. With Trump downplaying the prospect of a 
deal and the Strait of Hormuz still at the centre of the escalation, markets have little reason to take the geopolitical 
premium out of crude just yet. For rates, the takeaway is that energy inflation has moved back from tail risk to the 
main driver of recent price action. Regarding Fed pricing, around 17bp is now priced for September and roughly 
65bp of tightening by mid-2027.Energy price developments and data continue to dominate the broader narrative, 
particularly after Barr warned that the Fed should act decisively if inflation is not moderating sufficiently. 
Yesterday’s softer ISM and JOLTS figures still point towards cooling goods demand and a low-hire, low-fire labour 
market, but weak employment data may now be faded unless accompanied by softer inflation. The more important 
hurdle for September will be whether PPI and CPI imply core PCE is flat or accelerating. Today will be relatively 
quiet, with the ADP employment report as the highlight (and this unlikely to settle the September Fed 
meeting debate). The bias remains for higher yields and flatter curves while crude is rising. But with the market 
already pricing a sizeable hiking cycle, we may see some dip-buyers in the belly as we approach key levels (most 
2-10Y UST yields are already trading around the 2025 highs; the next hurdle would be to see the market pricing a 
100bp hiking cycle). The risk/reward in chasing the front-end sell-off is becoming less attractive, particularly if 
escalation headlines stabilise or payrolls materially disappoint. 
The ECB has an energy problem, not yet a second-round problem. EUR rates remain caught between a 
worsening energy shock and slightly better underlying inflation news. Headline CPI accelerated to 3.3%YoY, but 
core undershot at 2.4% and services slowed to 3.0%. That does not remove the case for next week’s hike, but it 
matters for what could come afterwards. Makhlouf’s comments in the FT capture the current ECB reaction function 
well: the Council must be prepared to move into restrictive territory (described as moving beyond 2.75%) if inflation 
risks shift materially higher, but there is still no clear evidence of wage second-round effects. With a September 
hike fully priced, another by February and depo approaching 3% by mid-2027, the market is already discounting a 
fairly persistent energy shock. We would not chase the sell-off at the front end from here. Energy and geopolitical 
headlines can keep EUR rates under pressure, but evidence should limit how far the ECB can follow oil.  
High-beta oil trade, but fiscal confirmation is still missing. GBP rates continue to trade as the high-beta 
expression of the energy story. Some of yesterday’s underperformance was catch-up after the UK holiday, but the 
larger move in the SONIA strip also reflects the higher sensitivity of UK inflation pricing to oil. Three hikes are now 
fully priced by June 2027, despite domestic data continuing to soften at the margin. For now, we still see 
yesterday’s move being global catch-up plus oil, rather than the start of a fresh UK repricing. BoE pricing looks 
hawkish relative to the growth backdrop. The fiscal backdrop is also moving back into focus as Parliament returns 
from the summer recess. Burnham's first statement was heavy on direction (public control, regional devolution and 
lower household costs) but light on fiscal detail. Against a backdrop of rising gilt yields, that lack of clarity is likely 
to keep term premium elevated. 
The risk-off tone continued in Asian equities, while JGBs twist-flattened following a hawkish statement from 
the BoJ’s Takata. The BoJ board member didn’t specify the timing of further rate hikes but he said that the neutral 
rate could end up higher than what markets are expecting.
