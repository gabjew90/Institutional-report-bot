Global Daily 
Market comment 
‘A History of Interest Rates’ by Homer and Sylla has sat on my desk for 25 years. It’s missing the 
negative bond yields experienced up until five years ago but makes abundantly clear that the 
long-run norm we call “geopolitics”, and natural disasters/weather, are *the* key drivers of 
structural meltdowns in bonds and currencies. That this is only now reemerging in market 
consciousness shows what a literal and ideological bubble we have just lived through. Yet it’s 
bursting. As the Australian Financial Review puts it today, ‘This is a generational global bond 
meltdown. There’s no easy way out.’ Not even saying, ‘Whatever it takes.’ 
US 10-year yields just jumped 6bp to 4.81%. Japan’s 10-year JGB yield hit 3%, the highest since 
1996, pre the Asian Crisis that turned most of Asia from current account deficit to surplus 
economies with low rates. UK PM Burnham maiden speech to the House of Commons rejected 
Brexit and Thatcherism ahead of a budget where the new Chancellor, who toppled the last PM for 
not funding a 3% of GDP defence pledge, will refuse to meet it too - as the 10-year Gilt jumped 
16bps to 5.22%. The 10-year Bund had a better day but is at 3.34% as the country faces a further 
surge in defence spending and a Saxony state election the populist AfD is expected to win. 
France’s 10-year is at 4.21% vs. 0% in late 2021, as polls point to Le Pen as the next president 
while populist left Melenchon wants to write off French debt. 
Meanwhile, the ‘geopolitics’ headlines are clear even for those who think them exogenous to 
markets rather than their current masters. 
The US launched further strikes on Iran, hitting radars being rebuilt. Iran fired at Jordan, Kuwait, 
Bahrain, and Iraq. It also hit Saudi and South Korean oil tankers in Hormuz, with Axios reporting 
the US responded by striking two Iranian tankers – apparently it will be a “tanker for tanker” war 
now. Oil, so yields, were up strongly on that news. 
The US is hoping its economic war against Iran will work without a major military escalation; 
however, Iran is looking to lean on Russia and China as a shield, according to Bloomberg. 
Tellingly, US Treasury Secretary Bessent just told Iran’s leadership, “We know where in the British 
Virgin Islands your accounts are, at these trust companies. We know the hundred million dollar 
houses you have around the world. We are going to close all that down… Either you are with us, 
or against us.” If you think he was only talking to Iran’s leadership, you need to read more history. 
Can that pressure work?    
Flexing, Putin pledged to support to Tehran in its war with the US at the Shanghai Cooperation 
Organisation (SCO) summit in Bishkek, which the West ignored as usual. The Financial Times 
claims Russia is now secretly helping Iran to develop supersonic cruise missiles. Xi is in Egypt as 
the febrile Middle East looks for security. 
With the global energy and agri sectors reeling from Russian and Ukrainian strikes, seeing 
Trump call in US refiners, Zelenskyy warned airlines that ‘Russian airspace will effectively be 
closing’ due to stepped up drone attacks - that could disrupt the global economy further. 
Germany blamed Russia for a recent drone “attack” and announced retaliatory measures, as the 
EU is mulling accelerating its €90bn loan payouts to Ukraine, which will inflame things further. 
Euronews reports, ‘As war fears grow in Europe, Sweden plans mass burials for part of its people.’ 
And you’re worried about long bond yields?

The EU is now to gather its “brightest minds” under Kallas, the EU High Representative for 
Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, who stated: “The aim is to have some of the brightest minds 
on security and defence come together and really provide out-of-the-box solutions on how we 
can be faster in fulfilling these gaps and how we can be more effective, especially now that the 
security situation in Europe is so much more real." So, it wasn’t real for the last 4½ years? This is 
not going to be cheap at a time when borrowing isn’t. 
The conflation of the Ukraine and Iran wars is awkward for the US, which would prefer not to 
be involved in the former anymore; and for Europe, which has refused to be involved in the 
latter at all. Yet they are slowly becoming one front - and perhaps more, in the worst case.  
Taiwan’s defence ministry has warned that the PLA is stepping up its preparations for a 
blockade of the island, and states it is “sharpening its plans to break such a cordon in response,” 
as Reuters quotes US officials that ‘Chinese shipper COSCO collects intel for Beijing with 
concealed equipment, showing a weaponisation of civilian logistics. 
Meanwhile, in the geoeconomics conjoined twin of geopolitics, the EU ‘wants to convince’ 
China to curb its exports as Beijing derailed the G20 consensus communiqué over language 
calling for the elimination of “non-market” policies to curb trade imbalances. If you aren’t 
dealing with them, or the currency via a new Plaza Accord, then you have no other option than: 
(1) Buy Chinese (everything); or (2) high tariffs, subsidies, and bans on transshipment; and (2a) 
alone, which is staggeringly inefficient; or (2b) as part of a larger bloc, which is less so. 
We can already seen such blocs forming, and not just via the SCO. Bessent, who intervened to 
push JPY up and JGB yields down, a move now being reversed by markets, just met with the BOJ 
Governor Ueda. If you think that was two geopolitical equals and a truly independent central bank 
speaking, then you need to do a lot more reading in general and about generals. As Reuters puts 
it, ‘Japan faces day of policy reckoning as Bessent calls time on big stimulus.’ Not PM Takaichi - 
Bessent. Of course, with higher rates Japan’s debt burden could become unmanageable, as could 
the losses for its insurance and pension funds: which would open the door to further integration 
via bailouts from the US. That would point to historic shifts in both countries and their markets. 
Pulling the other way, Canada’s press accuses Google Maps, and now Apple, of becoming “a 
tool of digital imperialism.” The solutions are to develop one’s own AI at staggering expense or 
choose the Chinese option, at a staggering cost in other fields. Europe is attempting regulation, 
rather than innovation and integration, of US AI into its economy, flagging ChatGPT, Reddit, 
and Roblox as security risks to be controlled by its digital rules by year-end. Will these firms 
comply or pay token fines? Will Trump threaten to raise tariffs again? Or will US AI be withheld 
from Europe entirely, as its own smaller-scale AI leader, Mistral, integrates Chinese models? On 
such decisions geopolitics turns, “digital imperialism” rises or falls, and so do bond yields. 
It’s unclear how this all ends up, but one thing is clear: as the New York Times argues today, ‘A 
New World Order Is Coming. We Aren’t Ready.’  
Down to earth Down Under, Aussie Q2 GDP came in better than expected at 0.4% q-o-q and 2.1% 
y-o-y. That likely nails an RBA rate hike this month even as the housing market tumbles, 
something very few Aussies would have predicted. The RBNZ hiked 25bps to 2.75% as expected, 
but dovishly, suggesting perhaps one more move by year end. In both cases, the world backdrop 
will get a say.  
Day ahead 
Today has US ADP employment data as the main highlight.
