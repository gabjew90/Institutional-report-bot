KEY SENATE RACES 
Currently, the GOP has a 53 - 47 majority in the Senate. The focus is on whether 
Democrats can flip 4 Senate seats with the most likely opportunities being in Alaska, Iowa, 
Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, and Texas. Betting markets give the GOP a 53% chance of 
holding the Senate while Democrats are favored in 4 of the aforementioned races. 


[Page 6]
  
•         ALASKA – Dems favored, 65% implied probability. This race features former Dem 
Rep. Mary Peltola vs. Republicans Dan J. Sullivan and incumbent Republican Dan S. 
Sullivan. To clarify, Alaska uses a system where the top 4 finishers advance to the general 
election. Confusion among the Dan Sullivans may split the GOP vote.  
o   US MKT INTEL – Adding some levity, there is an Eddie Murphy movie 
called The Distinguished Gentleman where Murphy’s character uses similar 
name recognition to win an election (IMDB). 
•         IOWA – GOP favored, 62% implied probability. This is an open seat with Sen. Ernst 
retiring. The race features two state Representatives in GOP Rep. Ashley Hinson and Dem 
Rep. Josh Turek. 
•         MAINE – Dems favored, 69% implied probability. Incumber GOP Sen. Collins is 
facing a tight race vs. former Maine State Senate President Troy Jackson who is replacing 
Graham Platner, winner of the Democratic Primary, who dropped out of the race following 
sexual assault allegations. 
•         NORTH CAROLINA – Dems favored, 91% implied probability. This is an open seat 
with Sen. Tillis retiring. It will see former NC Governor Roy Cooper vs. former RNC Chair 
Michael Whatley. 
•         OHIO – Dems favored, 54% implied probability. Incumbent GOP Sen. Jon Husted is 
challenged by former Dem Sen. Sherrod Brown. Husted was appointed to the seat which 
was vacated by Vice President JD Vance.   
•         TEXAS – Toss-up. This seat opens after incumbent Sen. Cornyn lost a primary to 
Trump-backed candidate and current Texas AG Ken Paxton. Paxton faces Dem state Rep. 
James Talarico.

VOTER FRAUD 
Looking at data provided by the Heritage Foundation, there are 1,561 proven cases of voter 
fraud across federal and state elections dating to 1979 (Heritage Foundation). From 1980 
through 2020, there were more than 1.25bn ballots cast in federal elections, which implies 
that the voter fraud incident rate is less than 0.0001%. Though, the actual number is likely 
materially lower once you include state-level elections. The conclusion is that voter fraud 
does not have an impact on election outcomes. WSJ reports on the commonality of 
noncitizen voting in US elections coming to the same conclusion, “Evidence suggests 
noncitizen voting is rare. In Utah, election officials reviewed over two million voters between 
2025 and 2026 and found one confirmed noncitizen registration and no instances of voting. 
In 2024, Idaho said it was removing 36 “very likely” noncitizens from its registered-voter 
rolls. Many states use the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements program to check 
citizenship with few flagged cases.” (WSJ). 
•         In 2020, Trump and Trump-affiliated entities filed 62 lawsuits challenging the 
Presidential Election outcome, many on ground of fraud. 61 of 62 suits were dismissed or 
dropped due to lack of evidence, including cases heard by Trump-appointed judges 
(American Bar Association; Campaign Legal).  
•         Fox News paid $787mm to Dominion Voting Systems to settle false claims of election 
fraud (AP); 
o   Smartmatic, another voting machine company, has outstanding litigation 
against Fox News for $2.7bn due to defamation. Smartmatic is under criminal 
investigation by the current Trump Administration, a move that the company 
claims is vindictive prosecution (NYT). 
•         US MKT INTEL – To put voter fraud in context, the probability of voter fraud occurring 
in a federal election is similar to the probability of being struck by lightning, twice.

JOYCE CHANG: TOP 10 STRATEGIC THEMES FOR 2H26 AND BEYOND (here) – This 
note was published on July 16, 2026 
•         Congressional pushback has increased as the midterm elections approach, with 
members focused on public opinion ratings. A number of Republicans in the House and 
the Senate crossed party lines to pass a War Powers Resolution demanding that Trump 
seek Congressional authorization for the war in Iran or end all military action. Trump’s 
economic ratings have dropped significantly due to the Iran conflict and the rise in gas 
prices, which remain 31% above the levels preceding the Iran conflict. Looking back at 
the 2018 midterm elections, when the economy seemed on stronger footing, 
Republicans still lost. The Senate failed to pass the SAVE America Act, and in 


[Page 17]
response, Trump refused to sign the bipartisan 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, 
allowing it to automatically become law without his signature. 
•         While the House is likely to flip to the Democrats, the margin is unlikely to be large 
and will likely fall short of a full “blue wave.” There has been a drastic reduction of 
competitive seats in House races. Republicans have been successful in redistricting 
and there is a 15-seat difference between where Republicans would have been under 
the old maps and the new maps. Current estimates point to maybe 20-30 competitive 
seats compared to 90 in past midterm election cycles 20 years ago. While all of the 435 
seats in the House are up for election, estimates for a Democratic majority range from 
220 to 230 seats. One-third of Senate seats are up for reelection, and Republicans 
remain favored to retain the Senate.

DUBRAVKO: GLOBAL EQUITY STRATEGY: 2026 MID-YEAR OUTLOOK (here) – This 
note was published on June 24, 2026 
•         On average, the sitting president’s party has lost ~27 House seats and ~3 Senate 
seats over the last 23 midterm cycles (i.e., back to 1934). For reference, the current 
Senate majority is 53 in favor of Republicans and a House majority of 218 (see Press 
Release). In terms of market implications, Congressional gridlock has been associated with 
positive S&P 500 returns since 1950 whether on an election date basis (+20% gridlock vs. 
+18% Single Party Control over 2-year Congressional term) or start date of the new 
Congress (+25% Gridlock vs. +18% Single Party Control). In the tail scenario where 
Democrats gain control of both chambers of Congress, their more extreme policy ambitions 
could still be checked by a Republican White House, Presidential veto authority and a 
Senate filibuster.  
•         Therefore, the possibility of strong Democrat-led tax, regulatory, healthcare or 
climate legislation becoming law likely remains constrained over the next two years. 
Must-pass legislation such as the government budget, debt ceiling, defense authorization 
and tax/healthcare extensions will be familiar battlegrounds in the meantime.
