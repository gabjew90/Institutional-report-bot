 
Under Pressure "From Multiple Sides": Anthropic 
Opens Talks With NYC Comptroller For IPO Pause 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SATURDAY, OCT 17, 2026 - 06:30 PM 
It has been a rough week for the biggest IPO in history. 
On Wednesday, Anthropic opened its books to Wall Street and revealed that growth is hitting the 
brakes. On Thursday, investors who sat through the pitch started talking about a valuation of 
around $1.5 trillion, roughly $500 billion below the $2 trillion that had been penciled in for 
November. And on the same day, New York City's top pension official asked the company to 
stop the IPO altogether. 
Now, according to Bloomberg, Anthropic has opened a line of communication with that official, 
NYC Comptroller Mark Levine, who told Bloomberg TV on Friday that "it does appear they are 
under pressure from multiple sides right now."He couldn't characterize the discussions, and 
Anthropic didn't respond to Bloomberg's request for comment, the kind of non-answer that tends 
to precede actual answers, or even more non-answers. 
What they also precede are IPO delays which are then blamed on "regulatory consultations" 
and most certainly not on collapsing ARRs. 

 
 
To be clear, nobody has announced a delay: Anthropic has never formally set a date (even if it 
has explicitly stated late November as the most likely IPO date), and the last reported target 
was a listing after the Nov 3 midterms. But the market isn't waiting for a press release. On 
Polymarket, the odds of an Anthropic IPO by the end of November have tumbled from 89% in 
mid-September to 56%, i.e., a coin toss. 
 

 
The Comptroller Reads The Risk Factors 
Levine's letter, dated Wednesday and addressed to CEO Dario Amodei, was first reported by 
CNBC and picked up by Reuters on Thursday. It urged Anthropic to delay the listing until it 
addresses concerns over i) safety, ii) governance and iii) the pace of development. Instead of 
"proceeding headlong" with the IPO, Levine wrote, the company should focus on the 
"unprecedented risks" it has been warning about.  
And there's the twist: Levine is using Anthropic's own words against it. According to Benzinga's 
summary of the letter, he cited the warnings about potentially catastrophic AI risk in the 
company's draft IPO filing (roughly 80 pages of disclosures), plus concerns that its proposed 
governance structure could limit shareholder accountability. The FT reported this week that in an 
early draft of the S-1, almost a third of the document was devoted to risk factors, including the 
possibility of models showing unpredictable and malicious behavior. 
He also had a ready-made quote bank. Last month, Amodei called on the industry and 
governments to slow the pace at which AI models improve, a contradiction we dissected in "Sex 
Parties, AI Doom, And Anthropic's IPO" (Sep 29), when the prospectus described a continuous 
release schedule as essential to staying at the frontier. Then, just this Tuesday, Anthropic issued 
a warning over self-replicating AI. Levine's argument, per AFP, boils down to this: "Prudence 
should not only be shown with respect to the pace of the frontier," it should also apply to the 
timing of the IPO. 
Levine also said that last month's "morally binding" AI safety accord between the Trump 
administration and the AI labs (including Anthropic) was insufficient to meet the moment, and he 
wants Anthropic to disclose "with specificity" how it will implement audits and controls, and on 
what timeline. 
Speaking on CNBC, he spelled out the investor case: once public, "new pressures on growth 
and profits" would push against the safety work, and in his view the worst outcome would be to 
go public "and then have an unprecedented safety crisis." On Bloomberg TV he added the 
security angle, warning of what a cyber breach could do to the stock should something go 
wrong after the listing. 
Incidentally, all of the above is what none other than CEO Dario Amodei would want if he knows 
that he has missed his IPO window as a result of plunging revenue growth, and instead of 
having to explain it to stunned investors reading the prospectus, he would rather have a 
politician "force" Anthropic to delay the IPO indefinitely.  

 
$7 Million Of Exposure, $325 Billion Of Leverage 
So how much skin does New York actually have in this game? According to the comptroller's 
office, NYC's pension funds have a paltry $7 million in exposure to Anthropic through private 
equity holdings. That's a rounding error for a company that just booked $16.8 billion of revenue 
in a single quarter. 
But the $7 million is the wrong number. Levine oversees more than $325 billion of pension 
assets, some of which sit in index funds, and those funds assume "the indexes will rush 
Anthropic in". Said otherwise, New York will own a lot more Anthropic whether it wants to or not, 
which is why the comptroller wants answers now, and why he says he is teaming up with other 
"like-minded public pension investors". He has also been in touch with OpenAI informally, and 
talking to the London Stock Exchange and other institutions about safety. 
Levine can't legally stop an IPO (even if Anthropic would like nothing more now that its revenue 
growth has flatlined). But "I think we do have influence and I hope we are heard," he told 
Bloomberg, adding that he doesn't know whether Anthropic will delay. Notably, a company with 
nearly $17 billion in quarterly revenue picked up the phone within days for a letter from a holder 
with $7 million at stake, which is either good corporate manners or a sign of how badly 
Anthropic needs public pensions on the order book. 
Anthropic, for its part, made a safety gesture of its own on the same day the letter went public, 
saying it would give independent evaluators like METR employee-like access to its models and 
that every AI company should do the same voluntarily (especially, we noted at the time, 
cut-price Chinese competitors). 
The Other "Sides" 
Levine's "multiple sides" comment was an understatement. Consider just the past 72 hours: 
●​ The numbers. Q3 revenue rose 45% sequentially to $16.8 billion, and Anthropic said it 
was profitable on an adjusted basis. Revenue had more than doubled in Q2, and IPO 
buyers price a company like this on the second derivative; worse, revenue over the past 
three months has flatlined: a catastrophic sign for a company that wants to be priced at 
20-30x revenue.  
●​ The valuation. Some investors who attended Wednesday's investor day discussed a 
valuation of around $1.5 trillion, according to Reuters, down from the $2 trillion 
previously expected. 

 
●​ The regulators. The FTC subpoenaed OpenAI and Anthropic on Thursday, seeking 
records related to AI safety incidents. 
●​ The tape. CoreWeave's stock and bonds tumbled on Thursday and Korea's Kospi 
crashed into a bear market, as the AI trade looks increasingly debt-funded and 
increasingly fragile, leaving the frontier models blowing in the wind.

Goldman's APAC custom baskets desk (also available to pro subs) put it in context: less than a

Put differently, investors are being asked to pay up for a growth curve that is flattening, for a 
product whose unit price is falling, while the company's own filing warns the product might go 
rogue. No wonder everyone at Anthropic is hoping to find some external scapegoat to blame for 
an IPO delay... 

 
 
The Goalposts Keep Moving 
None of this is new to regular readers. Back in August, when Polymarket gave Anthropic a 74% 
chance of listing by the end of October, we tweeted that Anthropic was racing to list before the 
cracks showed: 
Two days later, we warned that "Anthropic's $2 Trillion Bet Is That Nothing Goes Wrong." Since 
then, the timeline has done what timelines do: 
●​ Late August: listing expected in late September or early October, per Reuters. 
●​ Early September: marketing pushed to mid-October at the earliest, with the listing 
completed just before the midterms, Reuters reported. 
●​ Sep 18: the target moved to November, per the WSJ and NYT, and the end-November 
odds dropped below 50% in two days. 
●​ Oct 14: the debut is likely to be pushed until after the Nov 3 midterms, Reuters reported. 
●​ Oct 15: the comptroller of the largest US city asks for an open-ended pause. 
Polymarket's monthly market tells the rest of the story: November still leads at 55%, but the 
market now sees roughly 45% odds that the listing slips into December or later, with January 
(17%) ahead of December (13%): 

 
 
Not Everyone Is Worried 
The bulls are still out there, of course. An unnamed early backer told Fortune on Thursday that 
he expects the IPO to price at around $1.8 trillion and then trade up to 
2.4 trillion, topping

SpaceX's debut. His reasoning: "I feel like I have to own it." Because nothing says "rational price 
discovery" quite like FOMO from someone who already owns it. 
Levine himself acknowledged the other side of the argument: some people say that a delay 
could itself be a problem for the market, which has been leaning on the Anthropic IPO as the 
bellwether for the entire AI trade. As Reuters put it, a disappointing share sale could add to 
worries about an AI buildout increasingly financed with borrowed money, and Anthropic has 
committed to spending at least $518 billion over the next decade on cloud, compute and 
infrastructure. 
Bottom Line 
Levine's closing pitch on Bloomberg TV: "Much better to take a little more time right now and get 
this done." 
Anthropic now faces a choice with no good options. List in November into a weak tape, with 
growth slowing, the valuation already marked down by a quarter in investors' heads, and a 

 
public pension coalition reading the risk factors out loud. Or wait, and hand everyone (including 
OpenAI, which filed in June and has since pushed its own listing to 2027) a ready-made 
explanation for why the AI bellwether blinked. If it does delay, expect "safety" and "governance" 
to feature prominently in the press release, and the Q3 deceleration not at all. 
Our bet: the odds keep sliding, because a "growth" story with flat (or declining ARR) that also 
needs just the perfect timing for its IPO, is a very hard sell into a market already dumping 
everything with "AI" in the name.
