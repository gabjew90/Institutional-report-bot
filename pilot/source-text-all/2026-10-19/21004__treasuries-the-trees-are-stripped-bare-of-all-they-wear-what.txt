Market views
Treasury yields retraced to new highs on Wednesday following a firm CPI report, but quick-
ly reversed lower amid growing concerns over AI equity valuations, evident in the 15% fall 
in the KOSPI this week and the ongoing underperformance of the NASDAQ relative to 
other domestic equity indices (Figure 1Treasury yields traded a 30bp range this wek, ending lower.). Moreover, commentary from Fed officials this 
week ahead of the blackout period gave no indication of the need to hike at consecutive 
meetings. Importantly, Governor Bowman spoke on the sidelines of the IMF/World Bank 
meetings in Bangkok, arguing that she did not “feel any urgency for taking further action 
at this point.” These comments came prior to the CPI release, but given Chair Warsh likely 
had the CPI data, and that Bowman is politically aligned with the Chair, it’s unlikely that 
she spoke out of turn. Warsh ratified these comments on Friday, expressing optimism about 
the US economy, and saying that the economy had “broadly conformed to our expectations” 
since the September meeting.
Though the likelihood of an October hike was quashed this week, the data does not support 
a one and done. The CPI print was strong across categories, and with the PPI data in hand, 
we forecast PCE rose 0.31% over the month, lifting the over-year-ago rate from 3.0% to 
3.1% (see US CPI & TIPS: On firmer footing, Abiel Reinhart & Harry Downie, 10/14/26). 
Meanwhile, labor markets remain healthy as the four-week average of initial claims remains 
below 200k, and continuing claims declined to 1.71mn (consensus: 1.709mn), running 
counter to seasonal patterns and suggesting risks that the unemployment rate could fall in 
the coming months (see US: Continuing claims still point to lower unemployment, Abiel 
Reinhart, 10/15/26). In the wake of these developments, 2-, 5-, 10-, and 30-year yields 
declined 11bp, 17bp, 19bp, and 20bp, respectively, and the US outperformed its DM peers 
in the process (Figure 2.and the US outperform

Overall, it is impressive that Treasury yields declined so sharply from their mid-week peaks, 
even in the face of strong data over the period. This rally could reflect one of three factors, 
or some combination of the three. First, as discussed above, the move in yields is finally 
affecting equity valuations. Our equity strategy team has found that there is an inverted “U” 
relationship between 10-year yields and S&P 500 multiples, and typically this relationship 
turns negative when yields sit above 5% (see Global Equity Strategy: Implications of High-
er Rates for Equity Fundamentals, Valuation, and Positioning, Dubravko Lakos-Bujas et 
al., 9/14/26).

Finally, it could be symptomatic of a shift in Treasury debt management strategy expecta-
tions: 30-year swap spreads widened by 3-4bp as long-end yields declined and the curve 
flattened this week. Treasury released its primary dealer questionnaire today, and there was 
no explicit mention of cutting or discontinuing long-end issuance. Treasury did inquire 
about trends in structural demand for Treasuries and how it should change auction sizes to 
reflect future expected shifts in demand, which market participants could perceive as leav-
ing the door open for future cuts. We have argued for over a year now that the pace of demand 
for longer-duration assets should slow, as corporate DB funds are more than fully funded, 
their fixed income asset allocation has increased sizeably, and the expected vol of their sur-
plus is relatively low (see Before you ask...it’s not QE: 2026 Treasury demand outlook, 
11/20/25). However, in the face of a looming funding gap in 2027 and beyond, it would be 
imprudent to cut one source of funding when end-user primary demand has been strong. 
Thus, we think future auction size increases should be focused in the 2- to 10-year sector, 
while keeping long-end sizes unchanged (see US Treasury Market Daily, 8/5/26).
In aggregate, given this week’s moves, we think there are some interesting opportunities 
along the Treasury curve. We’ve been making the case in recent weeks that the front end had 
finally been flagging somewhat cheap after controlling for the shift in Fed rhetoric and rise 
in growth expectations, and a bit of that value has eroded given the rally in the last few days 
(Figure 4The front end no longer screns very cheap after controling for growth expectations and Fed rhetoric. ). Moreover, it’s interesting that the curve move has been somewhat parallel in this 
rally, as we would have expected more steepening given the front-end repricing that has 
occurred. Figure 5.but the curve apears to flat and ofers value shows that 1y1y OIS has explained close to 90% of the variation in 
10s/30s over the last 6 months, demonstrating that this curve tends to steepen 2bp for each 
10bp decline in front-end rates. The curve currently appears 3bp flat on this basis, and this 
whole decoupling has occurred this week.

has moved further underweight, the curve beta has also fallen, indicating that active core 
bond funds have reduced steepening exposure overall.

Looking ahead, if our forecasts come to fruition, we see some headwinds for the asset man-
ager community. While active core bond funds appear to be somewhat underweight corpo-
rate credit, our credit colleagues have shifted their YE spread targets tighter, as they expect 
the supply/demand balance to improve throughout Q4 against a backdrop of strong funda-
mentals (see JPM Daily Credit Strategy Update, Nathaniel Rosenbaum, 10/5/26). In the 
mortgage space, recent tightening has left spreads looking somewhat less attractive (see 
Agency MBS, US Fixed Income Markets Weekly, Nick Maciunas, 10/2/26). Meanwhile, we 
see value emerging in Treasuries at these levels, with current levels more fair versus funda-
mental drivers in both the front-end and intermediate sectors. On the other hand, our interest 
rate derivative colleagues remain soft bearish volatility, especially in longer tails, and with 
asset managers similarly positioned, this should buoy excess returns as volatility subsides 
(see Interest Rate Derivatives, US Fixed Income Markets Weekly, Ipek Ozil, 10/15/26).
