China: Q3 GDP slightly beat expectations on broad-based improvement 
in September activity
19 October 2026 | 2:35PM HKT
Bottom line: 
China’s Q3 GDP slightly beat market expectations, thanks to a broad-based 
improvement in September economic activity — especially retail sales and services 
industry output. The imbalance between strong exports and weak domestic final 
demand remained significant. Industrial production (IP) growth accelerated further 
to 5.5% yoy in September from 5.2% yoy in August, led by faster output growth in 
chemicals and non-metal product manufacturing industries. On a single-month 
basis, the year-on-year contraction in fixed asset investment (FAI) narrowed to 
-7.2% in September from -10.8% in August, thanks to more favorable weather and a 
low base. Retail sales growth rebounded to +1.1% yoy in September from +0.4% yoy 
in August, mainly led by offline goods sales and restaurant sales revenue, as the 
earlier Mid-Autumn Festival may have shifted some consumption from October into 
September. The Services Industry Output Index, reported in real terms and closely 
correlated with tertiary GDP growth, rebounded to 4.8% yoy in September from 
4.1% yoy in August. Following the stronger-than-expected Q3 GDP outturn, we 
nudge down our Q4 sequential growth forecast to 4.0% qoq annualized from 4.5% 
qoq annualized previously, but raise our 2026 full-year real GDP growth forecast 
slightly to 4.6% from 4.5%.

Main points: 
1. China’s real GDP growth rose slightly to 4.5% yoy in Q3 from 4.3% yoy in Q2, 
marginally above market consensus and our forecast (both at 4.4% yoy), taking 
year-to-date growth to 4.6% yoy in Q3 from 4.7% yoy in Q2. In sequential terms, NBS 
estimated that real GDP growth increased to 1.2% qoq sa non-annualized in Q3 from 
0.9% qoq sa non-annualized in Q2, also slightly above market consensus and our 
forecast (both at 1.0% qoq sa non-annualized). Nominal GDP growth edged up to 6.0% 
yoy in Q3 from 5.9% yoy in Q2 thanks to the improvement in real GDP growth, although 
the GDP deflator remained largely unchanged from Q2 at +1.5% yoy in Q3 based on our 
estimates. 
2. Industrial production (IP) growth rose further to 5.5% yoy in September from 5.2% 
yoy in August amid stronger export growth. Sequentially, we estimate IP gained 0.9% 
mom non-annualized in September (vs. +0.8% mom non-annualized in August; Exhibit 
1). By industry, the August-to-September acceleration in year-on-year IP growth was led 
by faster output growth in chemicals and non-metal product manufacturing industries, 
while slower output growth in electric machinery and automobile industries was a drag 
(Exhibit 2). Among major industrial products (which differ from the by-industry IP 
breakdown), crude steel and cement output growth increased to +0.1% yoy and -5.3% 
yoy, respectively, in September from -3.7% yoy and -11.7% yoy in August. Chemical fiber 
and sulfuric acid output growth rose to +0.4% yoy and -12.4% yoy, respectively, in

3. On a single-month basis, the year-on-year contraction in fixed asset investment (FAI) 
narrowed to -7.2% in September from -10.8% in August, thanks to more favorable 
weather and a low base (Exhibit 3). Sequentially, FAI gained 1.8% mom in September on 
a non-annualized basis, following a 2.1% mom increase in August, based on our 
estimates. This kept year-to-date FAI growth unchanged from August at -7.2% yoy in 
September. By sector, manufacturing and infrastructure investment growth rose to 
-1.0% yoy and -11.6% yoy, respectively, in September from -6.5% yoy and -16.8% yoy in 
August, while property investment growth weakened further to -26.2% yoy in 
September from -25.4% yoy in August despite a low base. Other (i.e., services and 
agriculture-related) investment growth increased to +3.7% yoy in September from 
+0.5% yoy in August due to base effects, as in sequential terms it showed notable 
contraction. Although we caution that occasional NBS “statistical corrections” of 
previously over-reported data may have amplified reported FAI volatility in recent 
quarters, the September improvement in FAI growth appeared broadly consistent with 
steel and cement output performance. 
4. Nominal retail sales growth rebounded to +1.1% yoy in September from +0.4% yoy in 
August, above market consensus of +0.5% yoy. The improvement was mainly led by 
offline goods sales and restaurant sales revenue, where growth rose to +1.4% yoy and 
+2.1% yoy, respectively, in September from -0.3% yoy and +1.1% yoy in August, as the 
earlier Mid-Autumn Festival may have shifted some consumption from October into 
September. In contrast, online goods sales growth dipped to -0.1% yoy in September 
from +2.0% yoy in August. In sequential terms, we estimate retail sales value gained 
0.5% mom non-annualized in September (vs. 0% mom sa non-annualized in August). 
Among major product categories, food and clothing sales growth rose to +6.7% yoy and 
+1.2% yoy, respectively in September from +4.0% yoy and -0.5% yoy in August. 
Communication appliance sales growth rose to +31.1% yoy in September from +27.3% 
yoy in August, while automobile and home appliance sales growth both weakened, to 
-21.2% yoy and +1.9% yoy, respectively, in September from -18.5% yoy and +2.3% yoy 
in August. Gasoline and other oil-product sales growth improved to -0.8% yoy in 
September from -7.0% yoy in August. Note that with the Mid-Autumn Festival falling on 
September 25-27 this year, travelers could combine the holiday with three days of 
annual leave for a 13-day break, supporting earlier departures and longer-haul trips, 
although elevated global oil prices remained a headwind. Adjusting for prices, we 
estimate gasoline and other oil-product sales volume contracted 14% yoy in September 
(vs. -8% yoy in August) amid a 13% yoy increase in domestic retail fuel prices. Separately, 
the NBS began publishing a new series this year on retail sales value for goods and 
services, which rose 2.6% yoy in January-September, with services sales growth of 5.0% 
yoy outpacing goods sales growth of 1.0% yoy.

6. Property activity remained sluggish in September (Exhibit 4). Property sales fell 20.5% 
yoy in volume (floor space) terms and 20.0% yoy in value terms in September (vs. 
-14.7%/-12.1% yoy in August). New home starts, new home completions, and floor 
space under construction declined by 25.6% yoy, 36.4% yoy, and 12.8% yoy, 
respectively, in September (vs. -31.0% yoy, -28.4% yoy, and -12.8% yoy in August). NBS 
and private-sector data indicate early signs of home-price stabilization in some large 
cities, while most lower-tier cities remain under downward pressure.  
7. Labor market conditions were broadly stable in September. The official (unadjusted) 
nationwide and 31-city unemployment rates both edged down to 5.2% in September 
from 5.3% in August. On a seasonally adjusted basis, we estimate that the nationwide 
rate was unchanged at 5.2% after rounding, while the 31-city rate inched up to 5.2% 
from 5.1% in August (Exhibit 5). The seasonally adjusted unemployment rate for migrant 
workers with local Hukou remained flat at 5.1% in September. Following the NBS 
definition change in January 2024, which excludes students in school, youth 
unemployment data have been released about three days after the broader labor 
market statistics. The latest data show that the unemployment rate for those aged 
16-24 rose to 18.9% in August from 17.9% in July, matching the prior peak registered in 
August 2025. Although the youth unemployment rate may follow seasonal patterns and 
decline in coming months, we caution that the revised definition may have understated 
labor market stress among the younger generation, given weak domestic demand and 
potential pressure from AI adoption on entry-level white-collar jobs. 
8. Incorporating the stronger-than-expected Q3 GDP outturn, we nudge down our Q4 
sequential growth forecast to 4.0% qoq annualized from 4.5% qoq annualized 
previously, while leaving subsequent quarters unchanged (Exhibit 6). After rounding, this 
raises our 2026 full-year real GDP growth forecast slightly to 4.6%, just above the lower 
bound of this year’s 4.5-5.0% growth target. Our baseline implies year-on-year real GDP 
growth will remain flat at 4.5% in Q4. We view the modest easing since end-September 
as an effort to reverse earlier tightening and secure this year’s growth target, rather than 
the start of a sustained, significant easing cycle. Given the delayed policy easing this 
year, some of the growth impulse may spill into early next year.
