Merde
France's 10-year yield is exploding higher again, pushing further into virtually uncharted territory. The
scale and persistence of the move are remarkable. Since the initial liftoff, the yield has barely traded
below its 21-day moving average. A relentless trend higher, with little sign of a meaningful reversal.

OAT-Bund
The infamous spread between French and German 10-year yields is widening sharply again. It is hard
to have much conviction about how the political and fiscal mess in France will unfold, but the latest
move in bond markets is hardly encouraging.
And it may not be over. Gentle reminder from some 10 days ago: TS Lombard sees another round of
widening if the budget negotiations fail to improve, with 200bp potentially in play if things really
unravel.

Vive la périphérie
The European sovereign problem is increasingly becoming a France problem. The OAT-BTP spread
continues marching higher, highlighting just how dramatically the market is redrawing Europe's
traditional core/periphery divide.

Problem child
French 10-year yields now trade roughly 35bp above Italy. Think about that. France, once a core
anchor of European sovereign debt, is increasingly trading like a peripheral credit.
The widening French-Italian yield spread is coinciding with a sharp selloff in European banks (SX7E).
The sovereign stress is no longer just a bond market story. Bank equities are taking notice.
The old sovereign-bank doom loop doesn't need to return. Persistent sovereign stress alone could be
enough to keep pressure on European banks.
We stick to our bearish SX7E logic outlined a few weeks ago (here).

The immunity is gone
European banks used to be immune to almost everything. That has changed abruptly. SX7E has sold
off sharply and is now testing both its major rising trendline and the 200-day moving average.
Could we see a bounce from here? Absolutely. But momentum is deteriorating, and investors are still
sitting on massive gains in the sector. There is plenty of room for further profit-taking if these key
support levels fail.

Stress
European equity volatility (V2X) is spiking today, but so far this looks more like a sovereign credit stress
event than a full-blown equity market panic. The question now is whether the stress remains contained

or starts spilling over more seriously into European equities.
