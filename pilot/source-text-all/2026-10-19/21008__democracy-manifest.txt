Market comment 
US stocks defied a strong lead from Europe and Asia on Friday to close broadly in the red. IT, 
telecommunications and the consumer discretionary sector led declines while energy, materials 
and consumer staples performed well. Oil prices surged following further reports of attacks on 
shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, with Iran claiming that two tankers carrying up to 4 million 
barrels of crude were struck by marine mines. The BBC reports UKMTO figures noting that the 
number of ships being attacked in and around the Strait hit a new high of 12 vessels in the week 
to the 11th of October. 
Rising oil prices helped to see ten-year Treasury yields close 1.5bps higher at 5.07% after a big 
drop on Thursday. Two-year yields fell by 3bps, causing a steepening of the yield curve that might 
have provided a slight tailwind to financial stocks. Higher oil prices and rising long yields were not 
helpful for the consumer discretionary sector that was perhaps still trading off the signal of a 
weaker-than-expected retail sales report released the day before, which gave the impression that 
spending on automotive fuels is cannibalising demand for other goods. 
Following market close on Friday news emerged that President Trump is enacting a waiver of 
steep Graham Act sanctions and tariffs that were due to come into effect. The Act requires the 
imposition of secondary sanctions in the form of 100% tariffs against the top five buyers of 
Russian crude oil and gas products while also placing 500% tariffs on Russian energy products 
being imported into the United States. Active Brent crude futures are down by $1.80/bbl at time 
of writing. 
Depending on how the calculation is performed, the top five buyers of Russian energy products 
are likely to include China, India, Turkey and the European Union. The imposition of 100% tariffs 
on China would certainly torpedo the truce in the trade war that was extended to January only a 
few weeks ago and would risk a response in the form of renewed export restrictions on rare earth 
minerals. 100% tariffs on the EU and Turkey would be a major strike against NATO allies at a time 
when the Trump administration wants Europe to contribute more to collective defence and Turkey 
to play ball with US interests in the Middle East. Striking India would antagonise an emerging 
great power that the USA would like to use as a geopolitical counterweight to China in the Indo-
Pacific, an investor in new US production, and a customer for US defence and nuclear energy 
exports. 
Of course, the imposition of Graham Act sanctions would also directly contradict recent efforts of 
the administration to reduce fuel prices ahead of the midterm elections. Relations between 
Moscow and Washington appear to be in an upturn following the recent controversial deal for the 
US to import Russian diesel sanctions-free over the coming months. That deal drew 
condemnation from Ukraine and several members of congress, as did Friday’s decision to waive 
the Graham Act sanctions. In a post to Truth Social on Sunday President Trump said that his 
decision was aimed at avoiding anything that will “drive, foolishly, Russia away from the 
bargaining table”.

the global economy and even the US economy”. Some might detect an implied threat that while

Rubio’s 60 days would expire after the midterm elections, which is the line in the sand that 
President Trump has repeatedly nominated when saying that the US would not engage in fresh 
strikes against Iran before that date. Once the midterms are out of the way, it looks like all bets 
are off and we can expect a dramatic ramping up in geopolitical volatility. That impression was 
seemingly confirmed last Thursday when President Trump again implied in an interview with 
Newsmax that the United States was considering striking Iran’s Pickaxe Mountain complex, where 
nuclear centrifuges from the Natanz site are thought to have been relocated.  
An IRGC spokesman said in a post on X that any attack on Iranian energy infrastructure would 
trigger a “scorched earth” response against Israel, with other Iranian sources suggesting a ‘nuclear 
for nuclear’ attack on Israeli reactors if Pickaxe Mountain were to be struck. Israeli Prime Minister 
Netanyahu – freshly endorsed for re-election at next week’s poll by Donald Trump – said that the 
Islamic Republic must be toppled and that he had directed Israel’s security agencies to pursue 
that goal, without US support if necessary. 
While the world grapples with the energy shock emanating from hot wars in Europe and the 
Middle East, French President Macron continues to grapple with political unrest at home. Tensions 
ran high over the weekend as widespread protests erupted into rioting and looting, with images 
of the burning of the historic town hall in Limoges circulating on social media and about 1000 
police officers reported injured.  
President Macron said in an address to the nation that a distinction must be drawn between the 
actions of the rioters and the legitimate concerns of protestors demonstrating against rising living 
costs and decaying public services. Marine Le Pen, the right-wing frontrunner for the upcoming 
presidential election, railed against what she called “exhausted Macronist power” saying that 
France faces a crisis of confidence and authority that now poses a threat to democracy.  
Hungarian Prime Minister Peter Magyar echoed sentiments expressed by Donald Trump blaming 
the riots on uncontrolled immigration, claiming that the scenes in France vindicated Hungary’s 
tough approach. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk – avoiding explicit mention of France -
similarly extolled the virtues of his own country’s hardline stance on immigration, saying that 
Poland would “neither repeat nor pay for” other countries’ mistakes. The Euro is this morning 
trading lower despite a broad US Dollar selloff. 
Keeping with the theme of democracy, today is an important date in the Canadian province of 
Alberta. A non-binding referendum will be held where voters are asked to indicate whether 
Alberta should remain a province of Canada or commence the constitutional process to secede. 
Voters will also be asked a series of questions about their views on control of immigration, voter 
ID, and to what degree non-citizens should have recourse to public healthcare and other social 
programs.  
Needless to say, the results of the vote will be closely watched in Ottawa, and in Washington D.C.
