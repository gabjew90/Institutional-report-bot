GS O'Connor - Lev Fin Weekend Reading + Observations: finally
movement in HY...is it indicative of a broader trend? 18 Oct 2026
KC O'Connor  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
18 Oct 2026
Growing concerns over AI infrastructure spending, hotter-than-expected inflation readings, and Brent
crude closing near its highest level since May all combined to send the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq 1.9% and
4.5% lower, respectively – marking the latter’s worst week since June. While the S&P 500 sits just 2%
from its record high, the median stock is 15% from its peak. This dynamic is symbolic of the bifurcation
present not just in equities, but in credit as well.
This bifurcation was on full display in the high-yield market, as USD HY AI-related spreads widened by
50bps, reaching a new wide of 371bps over Treasuries. By contrast, non-AI-related spreads were only
2bps wider and still remain below their March peak (h/t GIR’s Amanda Lynam).
As we enter what is sure to be another consequential week, below is a quick “around-the-horn” look at
Lev Fin to examine the current pressure points and assess whether last week’s movements are indicative of
a broader trend.
Source: iBoxx, Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research. As of October 16, 2026
HY – More Cracks
First the bad news: the HY market logged its sixth weekly loss out of the last seven as yields touched an
18-month high and spreads hit their six-month wides. Despite only $2.1bn of new issuance printing on the
week (one of the lowest weekly totals of the year), investors still felt the residual pain from the calendar –
notably from September’s two largest historic HY issues, whose tranches are now trading an average of
3.79pts below their respective OIDs.

Questions around the funding requirements of the AI capex buildout remained front and center, sending
our HY AI DC basket (GSHYAIDC) down more than 2pts on the week. This left nine bonds in the
subsector with yields north of 10%+ (and four above 14.75%) and pushed the “points travelled” metric
back to the local highs. Consequently, more than $2.2bn of AI HY trading volumes went through at GS
over the holiday-shortened week.
The good news is that investors still want to buy “the right” bonds and have the cash to do so. Consider
the following:
• Despite the recent repricing, 38.3% of the USD HY market still trades at an OAS inside 200bps – down
only slightly from 40.2% two weeks ago – and further demonstrating investors’ proclivity for regular-way
paper;
• Looking at Thursday’s trading session, 59% of the AI bonds traded by real money accounts with GS
involved them purchasing risk; and
• In October, all 10 HY deals that have priced have traded up, with the average tranche adding 1.8pts
CCCs – Still Challenged
While many positive takeaways can be still be gleaned from the broader HY market, it is much more
difficult to find a similarly optimistic mindset when it comes to triple-Cs. Yields for the lowest-rated
subset have skyrocketed to a four-year high, leaving 10% of the HY index in a danger zone.
Aggregate leverage and coverage metrics have already been stretched, but now with the incremental
pressure applied by the higher-rate environment, the ability to address near-term maturities amidst

potentially higher debt service costs – coupled with the overhanging threat of impending LMEs – looms
large.
This is one reason why GIR expects the USD HY 12-month trailing, issuer-weighted default rate to rise to
around 4% by year-end 2027, up from the 3.3% recorded at the end of September.
Loans – You’ve Got Next
The floating-rate asset class has not been immune to the broader credit weakness, as secondary prices
declined for a fifth straight week. However, loans still have a lot going for them:

• Technicals remain strong: Fund flows have been positive over the last seven weeks, and open CLO
warehouses have risen to 185;
• Focus on secondary and new money deals: The volume of $100+ loans has decreased, resulting in fewer
repricings. This has allowed investors the ability to focus on secondary and new money deals rather than
the flood of opportunistic transactions; and
• Software is back? Two new underwrites emerged in the software space for CCC Intelligent Solutions and
Proofpoint last week, showing that the thawing of the BSL and private credit’s largest sector continues
• Also interesting: In addition to CCC and Proofpoint, another pair of issuers is lining up to address their
loan maturities. In contrast to the first batch of software A&Es – which featured substantial coupon step-
ups and document changes – neither of the initial proposals features outsized pricing increases or deep
discounts – data points that will be carefully watched

TAKEAWAY
The AI buildout will continue. For 2027, our GIR team expects $300bn of global project finance issuance
across the IG and HY markets to fund data center and compute investments, in addition to $420bn of
direct issuance from the hyperscalers. After last week’s repricing in the HY space, the most asked
questions remain…What will upcoming financings look like, and what form will they take?
Last week’s $5.012bn debt package from Volta Infrastructure highlighted the role that the loan market
could play going forward. Even though the transaction flexed wider and was unrated, it saw a welcome
reception in the secondary market, trading up numerous points on the break. To date, the loan market has
accounted for less than 1% of the total universe of AI-related issuers – a stark contrast to the high-yield
market, where AI-linked deals have driven nearly 20% of gross issuance YTD.
In summation: 1) A breather in HY AI issuance is likely as investors work out updated relative value; 2)
More GPU transactions in loans are currently welcome; and 3) The HY market remains open for the right
issuers, but triple-Cs should tread carefully
