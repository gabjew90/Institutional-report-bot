3Q26 Travel Earnings Preview: Mixed Signals, 
Still Healthy Demand, AI Back in the Frame 
Our 3Q checks for ABNB, BKNG, and EXPE, our third-party data checks, and 
proprietary DBDig travel survey work continue to point to a mixed but generally 
healthy travel backdrop, with higher oil prices, elevated fares and heightened 
monetary concerns weighing on affordability and travel intent. On the softer side, 
TSA throughput declines y/y continued in 3Q (higher fuel, lower capacity, and 
higher fares) though may have bottomed in August, our proprietary DBDig travel 
survey indicates U.S. monetary concerns remain elevated off a recent peak, travel 
intent remains below February 2026 levels, and several digital engagement 
datasets point to weaker trends since 2Q earnings (except EXPE engaged web 
visits). At the same time, hotel RevPAR trends improved sequentially, alternative 
accommodation intentions remain at peak levels, web visits remain higher y/y for 
the travel stocks we cover, and supplier commentary across airlines and lodging 
operators continues to point to healthy leisure, premium, and corporate travel 
demand. The overall demand backdrop remains strong, though higher fuel 
prices and related monetary concerns weigh on the outlook. Meanwhile, Meta’s 
Muse launch has renewed investor focus on AI. We continue to view agentic 
platforms as a potential high-intent acquisition channel for OTAs, with AI 
referrals still representing only ~1% of traffic and platforms retaining 
meaningful value through payments, servicing, loyalty and disruption 
management. ABNB appears most insulated given substantial direct traffic, 
differentiated inventory and a fragmented host network largely unavailable 
elsewhere. Our proprietary DBDig survey inside our report tracks consumer 
willingness to use LLMs at each stage of the travel journey, from planning 
through payment. 
For ABNB, intra-quarter checks remain mixed, with engaged web visitor growth 
decelerating and app engagement growth modestly softer since earnings, while 
US credit / debit card spend data improved and alternative accommodation 
intentions remain supportive. We forecast 3Q revenue growth of 16.3% to 
$4.76bn, near the high end of $4.69–$4.77bn guidance, and see potential upside 
given improving US credit / debit card spend data and the company’s historical 
revenue outperformance. RNPL and the simplified fee structure continue to offer 
booking and monetization tailwinds, while hotels remain a longer-term 
opportunity as we think ABNB is refining its playbook ahead of a potentially 
broader push in 2027. Our model also assumes GBV growth of 16.3%, nights 
growth of 11.9% and an Adj. EBITDA margin of 49.6%. For 4Q, we forecast GBV 
and revenue growth of 13.4%, nights growth of 10.1% and a 29.2% Adj. EBITDA 
margin. The FXN GBV y/y growth comparison is ~300bps tougher in 3Q vs. 2Q 
then ~100bps tougher in 4Q vs. 3Q, while FXN revenue comps ease ~300bps then

Agentic platforms more likely to emerge as a new high-intent acquisition 
channel for OTAs 
While elevated fuel prices and related macro considerations around travel 
affordability have weighed on the group, investor concern has increasingly 
shifted back towards agentic commerce following the recent launch of Meta's 
Muse. The core question is whether AI agents ultimately become a new 
intermediary between consumers and travel suppliers/OTAs or simply another 
acquisition channel. We continue to view the immediate risk as manageable. 
OTAs provide value well beyond search and discovery, including functioning as 
the merchant of record, handling payments, customer service, fraud prevention, 
itinerary management, loyalty integration, and disruption management. As a 
result, we believe the most likely near-term outcome is not disintermediation 
but rather a new, growing referral layer that directs higher intent traffic toward

scaled travel platforms. Our checks show AI referral traffic remains de minimis 
relative to total traffic (~1%), though the trajectory is clearly upward and 
warrants monitoring. 
Longer term, we envision a model where agentic platforms increasingly 
monetize through referral economics or transaction-based fees, effectively 
charging OTAs and suppliers for highly qualified, higher-converting traffic. In 
that scenario, AI agents may look less like a substitute for OTAs and more like 
another performance marketing channel. Importantly, OTAs retain meaningful 
strategic leverage. Travel platforms could choose to restrict bot access to 
inventory, pricing, or booking functionality if they believe agentic interfaces are 
extracting value without appropriate compensation. In addition, loyalty 
ecosystems and direct traffic remain important offsets. Airlines have faced a 
similar dynamic for years, yet loyalty programs, rewards ecosystems, app 
engagement, and direct relationships have supported substantial direct booking 
activity despite the existence of OTAs. We believe the same considerations apply 
to OTAs, particularly for platforms with strong brand recognition, large loyalty 
bases, and high levels of direct traffic. Net-net, we continue to view AI as a 
structural watch item rather than an immediate estimate driver, though Muse's 
launch has elevated investor focus on the topic.

Monitoring the AI risk: Low but growing referral traffic, while our survey indicates 
some consumer willingness to use LLMs for trip planning but net negative for 
booking and payment 
AI-driven disintermediation remains one of the more persistent investor debates 
for the OTAs, particularly as LLMs become a more natural starting point for travel 
inspiration, comparison, and itinerary planning. Our prior work and recent data 
continue to suggest the risk is still early and uneven. AI referral traffic remains de 
minimis as a share of total traffic for the major OTAs at less than 1%. Notably, 
hotel suppliers such as Hilton, Marriott, and Hyatt have screened higher than the 
OTAs on AI traffic share as suppliers aim to diversify their distribution channels. 
At the same time, the direction of the AI-commerce model has become less 
threatening than feared, with OpenAI’s shift away from in-chat checkout and 
toward referral / third-party app flows reducing the most acute disintermediation 
tail risk by keeping the transaction with scaled, trusted travel platforms.  
More recently, Meta's Muse Agent was launched in early September in the US. 
The product was introduced as a standalone AI agent that can perform tasks 
across apps, browse the web, manage calendars, send emails, help with shopping, 
and continue working in the background. This would allow consumers to move 
beyond trip planning and recommendation into transaction execution through a 
conversational interface. 
We plan to track this debate through a combination of SimilarWeb AI referral 
traffic from ChatGPT, Perplexity, and other AI surfaces and our proprietary DBDig 
survey work. Recent survey results show meaningful and increasing (30s %) 
consumer willingness to use LLMs across travel inspiration, comparison, planning, 
booking, and payment use cases with only a ~MSD percentage point of drop off 
from beginning to end for trip booking. When netting likely respondents and 
respondents that are unlikely to engage the only area that doesn't skew more 
unlikely would be in the option comparison phase. Net-net, we would frame AI as 
a structural watch item rather than an immediate estimate driver: ABNB screens 
best given organic traffic and unique inventory that would benefit from better 
surfacing and categorization from in-house AI tools.

DBDig Survey: Monetary concern remains elevated off recent highs in US, while 
plans to stay in an alternative accommodation for US travelers remains at highs; 
European intention declines 
Deutsche Bank's DBDig team runs a monthly survey spanning 650 respondents in 
the US with select European country surveys as well (550 each) that include travel 
related questions. Highlights below: 
▪ 
US net monetary concern (concerned minus not concerned respondents) 
about being short money in the near-term remains elevated off recent 
peak in August 2026. June 2022 was the only post-pandemic reading 
well above recent levels, corresponding to oil in the 120s and core CPI 
growth running around ~6%. Monetary concern for the European 
countries we track picked up slightly in recent months (except for Italy).  
▪ 
Travel intent remains below pre-Iran war levels for US, European, and UK 
based respondents. The US rebounded in June but declined through the 
summer, with European intention also lower. 
▪ 
The number of vacations planned on average for US respondents took a 
step down in August then rebounded in September.  
▪ 
Plans to stay in a private rental / B&B reached a new peak in May 2026 
for US respondents at 18% and remained at that level in September, 
despite World Cup no longer spiking demand for certain markets.

TSA Throughput: ~3% y/y decline in 3Q on airfare CPI growth that remains 
elevated 
US airport throughput y/y declines have been worsening since the spring with 
Spirit Airlines ceasing operations in May and elevated fuel prices brought on by 
the Middle East conflict that started on February 28th. Both have put downward 
pressure on capacity and corresponding upward pressure on ticket prices. 3Q26 
was down ~3% y/y, while CPI for US airfare remains at 20%+ y/y. Given these 
dynamics and our checks, we expect an incremental shift in travel preference to 
closer destinations.

Airlines and Hotels: Broad-based demand strength continues 
Supplier commentary since 2Q earnings has generally remained supportive of 
travel demand. Across major U.S. airlines (Delta, United, American), management 
teams continue to describe broad-based leisure, premium, and corporate 
demand strength, while lodging companies including Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt, and 
IHG have highlighted positive RevPAR trends and resilient booking activity 
despite geopolitical disruption and elevated fuel prices. European carriers have 
acknowledged booking volatility associated with the Middle East conflict and 
higher fuel costs, though demand itself has remained resilient.  
Delta: Healthy consumers continue to prioritize travel 
Delta described sustained 3Q26 demand strength across leisure and business 
travel and all cabins, supported by consumers’ continued prioritization of 
experiences. Management highlighted limited resistance to higher fares, robust 
premium demand, and improving Main Cabin trends alongside broader industry 
capacity discipline. Corporate sales grew double digits across all sectors, led by 
Banking, Technology, and Energy, while the week after Labor Day delivered 
record corporate sales. Premium demand also absorbed additional supply 
without discounting, with load factors and fares both increasing. International

Macroeconomic fluctuations often account for most of the risks associated with exposures to instruments that 
promise to pay fixed or variable interest rates. For an investor who is long fixed-rate instruments (thus receiving these 
cash flows), increases in interest rates naturally lift the discount factors applied to the expected cash flows and thus 
cause a loss. The longer the maturity of a certain cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher 
will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most 
common adverse macroeconomic shocks to receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client 
segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets holding limits for different types of investors), changes in tax 
policies, currency convertibility (which may constrain currency conversion, repatriation of profits and/or liquidation of 
positions), and settlement issues related to local clearing houses are also important risk factors. The sensitivity of 
fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by indexing the contracted cash flows to 
inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates - these are common in emerging markets. The index fixings 
may - by construction - lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables they are intended to track. The 
choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating coupon rates (i.e., 
coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons. Funding in 
a currency that differs from the currency in which coupons are denominated carries FX risk. Options on swaps 
(swaptions) the risks typical to options in addition to the risks related to rates movements.
