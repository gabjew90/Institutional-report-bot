U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.51% | Nasdaq +0.48% | Dow +0.93%
Synopsis

US equities finished higher on Friday, near best levels, with the Russell 2000 up +0.07%, and the Nasdaq and S&P 500 posting good
gains for the week with tech the tailwind. Semis were among the best performers, alongside tech hardware, airlines, travel and leisure,
machinery, multis, parcel and logistics, managed care, banks, alts, builders and autos. Big tech was mixed, with MSFT-US the notable
gainer and META-US weaker after a big run-up over the week. Energy/E&Ps, cable, food, cybersecurity (a new OpenAI cyber model is
reportedly coming), software and REITs lagged. Treasuries were mostly stronger with the curve steepening and front-end yields down 7
bp, a bullish steepening that followed several days of headlines about the magnitude of the bond yield backup. The dollar index was off
0.3%, with yen strength the highlight in FX. Gold finished up 0.5%, silver up 1.3% and bitcoin futures down 0.8%, while WTI crude finished
down 2.3% and posted a second straight weekly decline. Stocks were helped by oil weakness on reports of diplomatic progress between
the US and Iran, which Iran quickly denied, in an otherwise quiet session. The AI trade outperformed as the compute-demand narrative
broadened to inference and agentic workloads. MSFT-US stood out on new Copilot capabilities, including a coming "Autopilot" personal

Macro

US–Iran talks enter technical phase: CBS News, citing Al Jazeera, reported that Iran–US negotiations have entered a "technical phase"
with an increasingly positive atmosphere, with technical experts joining after initial discussions involving US envoys Steve Witkoff and
Jared Kushner. The latest plan centres on an accelerated seven-day process to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, down from a prior 60-day
process, in exchange for the US lifting its economic blockade, easing oil export curbs and unfreezing Iranian assets. Both sides are also
evaluating joint Hormuz management between Iran and Oman, US vessel transit protocols and a pathway back to direct nuclear talks;
Qatari mediation remains active, but US officials have yet to confirm progress. Energy flows have improved: Saudi Arabia rerouted 3.6M
bpd of oil back through the Strait (up from 900K bpd in August), the East-West Pipeline is reportedly resuming flows to Red Sea coast
refineries after a mid-September drone shutdown, and Saudi oil exports in September are the highest since the start of the war, although
restoring full volumes may take weeks.

Akamai signs major Anthropic infrastructure deal: Akamai announced an $11.6B take-or-pay contract with Anthropic to provide distrib-
uted, CPU-based cloud infrastructure for Anthropic's AI workloads, with an option to expand by a further $9B for a total potential commit-
ment of $20B. The deal is capital intensive, requiring $5.5B of Akamai capex over the next two years, with revenue ramping in 2027. It
feeds the broadening of the AI compute demand narrative beyond GPUs to inference and agentic workloads that has built around Meta's
Muse, and Akamai pointed to rising demand for CPU-based infrastructure to run AI agents such as voice assistants, personal AI agents
and B2B agentic DevOps tools.

Oracle's force majeure unsettles AI financing: Following Thursday's Bloomberg report that Oracle invoked force majeure on its Project
Jupiter data center in New Mexico, the FT reported that Oracle remains liable for carry costs for up to three years if the project misses its
1H-27 operational deadline. Oracle may be able to defer rent payments to Blue Owl but must still pay a baseline equity return and a fee
covering interest to debtholders. Reuters reported that the delay has sent a chill through trillion-dollar AI infrastructure financing, already
reverberating through talks on other proposed data center financings; power is the key constraint, reflecting broader community and
regulatory backlash.

Data beat modestly; Fedspeak stays hawkish: Final September Michigan consumer sentiment was marked up 0.3 points from the prelim-
inary reading to 48.1, with current conditions and expectations also revised higher though still below August; 1Y inflation expectations
were unchanged at 4.6% and longer-term expectations at 3.4%. Preliminary August durable goods orders of 0.0% m/m beat the -0.3%
consensus, down from July's +0.9%; orders ex-transportation rose +0.3% m/m against +0.7% consensus, while core capital goods orders
rose +1.6% m/m against +0.5% consensus and core shipments rose +0.6% m/m, missing +0.8% consensus. In Fedspeak, New York's
Williams cautioned against letting shocks become permanent, Kansas City's Schmid questioned whether US debt levels were extreme
and whether AI was becoming too big to fail, and Cleveland's Hammack voiced concern about persistent above-target inflation becoming
embedded in expectations.

Week in review — yields at post-2007 highs, earnings the offset: Rates came under further upward pressure through the week on hotter
growth, weak auctions, hawkish post-FOMC Fedspeak and geopolitics: 2-year Treasury yields hit their highest level in over two years, 5-
year yields went above 5% for the first time since 2007, and 10-year yields pushed back through 5.1% and over 5.20% on Friday, a new
post-2007 high. The September flash composite PMI was the highest since July 2021, with input prices in services at their highest since
late 2022 and some of the most severe supply chain bottlenecks in near two decades outside the pandemic. Goolsbee, Barr, Williams,
Hammack and Paulson all leaned towards further tightening. Consensus estimates Q3 earnings growth nearing 30%, a third straight
quarter of 25%+ growth, which remains the most widely cited source of resilience.

EU Market Recap
Germany +0.56% | France -0.04% | UK +0.14%
Synopsis

European equity markets closed mostly higher on Friday but well off their highs, following a weaker close on Thursday, with late-afternoon
weakness tracking a softer US session and attention on fluctuations in oil prices and bond yields. Cyclicals outperformed defensives.
Financials, tech and travel were the best performers, while energy, chemicals and food and beverage were the main laggards. European
bond yields rose again following an earlier reprieve. Banks led on easier yields after the week's sharp global backup, and UBS (UBSG-CH)
gained after a Semafor report that the bank has resumed talks about combining with a foreign bank after Swiss lawmakers advanced a
proposal requiring it to raise up to $20B in capital. Financial services also rose on the improving macro backdrop despite Flow Traders
(FLOW-NL) reporting Q3 net trading income modestly below Q2. Travel and leisure performed strongly as lower crude prices boosted
airlines, with Ryanair (RYA-IE) and Lufthansa (LHA-DE) leading the carriers. Konecranes (KCR-FI) led construction and materials, trading
near the top of the STOXX 600 after launching a buyback and raising its financial targets, and Alten (ATE-FR) rose on strong H1 results.
Oil and gas was the biggest sector laggard as crude retreated, although HSBC upgraded BP (BP-GB) and TotalEnergies (TTE-FR) to Buy
from Hold; BP is reportedly weighing US shale acquisitions, including Devon Energy's Eagle Ford assets, in the $2-5B range, and To-
talEnergies' board unanimously reaffirmed its two-pillar Oil & Gas and Integrated Power strategy ahead of its investor day. HelloFresh
(HFG-DE) slumped on a profit warning, and Novo Nordisk (NOVO.B-DK) edged lower despite a licensing and collaboration agreement with
Nanexa (NANEXA-SE) covering its PharmaShell long-acting injectable technology, potentially involving up to €1.165B of payments.
Macro

BoE hike odds rise as officials turn hawkish: Energy was the dominant macro driver this week, with hawkish rate decisions from Norway
and Sweden, a somewhat less hawkish Switzerland, and the ECB keeping the door open to further hikes on the risk that higher oil and
gas prices make inflation more persistent. At the BoE, comments from Governor Bailey and deputy governors Lombardelli and Breeden
— two policymakers who recently voted to hold — raised the odds of a November hike, which is now almost fully priced, with a more than
70% chance of a February increase. Lombardelli saw little sign yet of second-round effects but warned the risk grows the longer energy
prices stay high, while Breeden said it was becoming increasingly appropriate to respond. Dovish member Dhingra said the impact of the
Iran war would become clearer over the winter but did not see the broad-based price pressure of 2022.

Schnabel exit accelerates race to succeed Lagarde: ECB Executive Board member Schnabel tendered her resignation a year earlier than
planned to take a role at the IMF and will step down on 3-Jan. With Lagarde and chief economist Lane also due to depart next year,
governments may come under pressure to decide senior positions sooner; speculation that Lagarde could leave early has grown with
news she will publish a memoir in January. Former Dutch central bank chief Knot and former Bank of Spain chief de Cos are the names
most frequently mentioned. Germany has not yet put forward a candidate despite Bundesbank governor Nagel's interest, while France is
said to be pushing for the chief economist role.

Consumer data diverge; eurozone lending steady: German GfK consumer confidence for October fell to -30.6 against consensus of -27.4
and a prior -26.8 (revised from -26.6), the lowest since May, as rising energy costs eroded purchasing power, income expectations hit
their lowest since April and willingness to save increased. UK GfK consumer confidence rose one point to -13 against consensus of -16,
a two-year high and the first three-month run of gains since summer 2024, though GfK warned momentum is fading, saving intentions
jumped and the reading follows weak S&P, BRC and CBI updates this week. ECB data showed annual growth in private sector loans rising
to 4.3% in August from 4.1%, while lending to firms eased to 4.2% from 4.4% and household lending held at 3.1%; monthly loans to
business more than halved to €11B from €21B.

UK fiscal squeeze and early-election talk: Chancellor Healey is reported to be open to smaller fiscal headroom after PM Burnham signalled
reluctance to raise taxes, with forecasts suggesting headroom may have at least halved to ~£12B and could be as low as £5B once known
spending requirements are included (FT). Treasury officials have examined higher taxes on banks, oil and gas firms and gambling, and a
capital gains tax increase; gilt investors think a buffer of ~£14B would not unsettle the market. The Telegraph reported that Downing
Street advisers are pushing for a spring election, after a YouGov poll showed Labour could fall 85 seats short of a majority, contingent on
the Middle East crisis easing and energy costs falling.

French fiscal and political risk: Far-left leader Mélenchon continues to press a proposal to convert Banque de France debt holdings into
zero-coupon perpetuals, which ECB's Lagarde and Nagel said would be illegal, yet a 5-Sep poll found 43% of voters support it. Natixis
warned that even a French push for a Europe-wide write-off could spike volatility and strain relations with Germany and the Netherlands.
The real risk is seen as a second-round run-off between Le Pen and Mélenchon, against a backdrop of Lecornu's €54B 2027 package
aimed at a 5% deficit while debt rises toward 122% of GDP and debt service reaches €91B next year.


+7.2% ATE-FR (Alten): H1 net income rose 38% to €113.8M and operating profit 27% to €158.3M on revenue of €2.11B; raised FY organic
growth outlook to 1.7-1.9% in constant currency, citing Aerospace & Defence.

12% in constant currency versus consensus of -6.8%; FY adjusted EBITDA cut to €350-370M from €375-425M.

Asian Markets
Japan +1.30% | Hong Kong -1.01% | China closed (holiday)
Synopsis

Asia equities ended mixed on Friday in a quiet day's trading amid low volumes and holidays in mainland China, South Korea and Taiwan,
rounding off a largely stronger week. Japan's two main boards posted strong gains, led again by technology stocks, and India and Singa-
pore were also higher, while Hong Kong and Australia ended lower but away from their lows and Southeast Asia was mixed, with the ASX
at -0.43%. Focus in Japan stayed on JGBs and currency markets, with 10Y yields rising to more than 30-year highs. The yen strengthened
in afternoon trade after finance minister Katayama warned on intervention, although it weakened late on to reverse Thursday's gains.
The won was also noticeably stronger and the yuan quiet, and Treasuries and JGBs were mixed. Crude oil fell sharply from its overnight
spike, with Brent a shade below $100/bl, precious metals rose and base metals were mixed. For the week, the MSCI Asia Pac ex Japan
index advanced slightly, with South Korea and Taiwan outperforming and Australia and Greater China underperforming, and the Nikkei
outperformed the Topix. In corporate news, China Life Insurance (2628-HK) said it would launch a CNY20B investment fund focused on
semiconductors, AI and biomedicine, SoftBank (9984-JP) fell after confirming details of its $11B junk bond sale, and Sumitomo Mitsui
Financial Group (8316-JP) is in talks to increase its stake in Vietnam-based VPBank, according to Reuters.
Macro

Trump–Xi summit delivers little beyond a truce extension: Treasury Secretary Bessent said he agreed with Vice Premier He Lifeng to
extend the trade truce to 10-Jan from its 10-Nov expiry, while flagging that Beijing has not fulfilled some deliverables; Xinhua cited Xi
calling the new agreement good news for the global economy, without further detail. There was no news of purchase commitments, and
Boeing is said to be losing hope of new aircraft orders beyond the 200 planes in the May deal (Reuters). Xi expressed hope that the US
will oppose Taiwan independence and handle the issue with prudence, a notable strengthening in language, though there are no signs
the administration will move from its "does not support" formulation. Both leaders signalled no inclination to curb AI competition, and a
planned CEO delegation became a point of friction over reciprocity. Further meetings are possible at APEC in Shenzhen (18-19 Nov) and
the G20 in Miami (14-15 Dec).

Tokyo steps up verbal defence of the yen: Finance Minister Katayama said President Trump raised concern about yen weakness at his
meeting with Prime Minister Takaichi, who responded that an undervalued yen was problematic, and said she will keep close contact with
Bessent; she declined to comment on BOJ policy or reports that the BOJ conducted a rate check on Sep 18-19 (Nikkei). Economic Security
Minister Kiuchi declared the Abenomics-style reflation era "is over" and that Japan is no longer in "an era of monetary easing", though he
added Japan has yet to fully escape deflation. A Nikkei QUICK article said the rhetoric and yen depreciation reinforce expectations of
further intervention this year to defend the 160 line that triggered the coordinated US–Japan operation.

PBOC reaffirms moderately loose stance: The PBOC's Q3 policy report showed few changes, describing economic and FX conditions as
generally stable against a volatile global backdrop. Members stressed continued moderately loose policy, stronger counter-cyclical ad-
justment and closer monetary-fiscal coordination, with a forward-looking, flexible and targeted approach to the intensity, pace and timing
of implementation. The bank paid more attention to the bond market, saying it would monitor long-term bond yields, and kept its FX stance
largely unchanged, aiming to prevent herding and self-fulfilling expectations to keep the yuan stable.

Japan fiscal funding remains under scrutiny: Nikkei discussed Japan's difficulty in funding fiscal expansion without deficit bond issuance,
focusing on the government's idea of bridging bonds tied to multi-year economic security outlays, projected at 1.3% of outstanding ordi-
nary bonds at FY26-end. Such bonds are typically attached to revenue sources in advance, and markets are unlikely to accept anything
less than a specific and sustainable funding source; ministry insiders have suggested a corporate tax hike, a proposal that met opposition
from the Japan Chamber of Commerce and Industry when floated for the FY27 consumption tax hike. Separately, Japan's financial regu-
lator is to increase scrutiny of AI data center financing (Bloomberg).

Regional headlines: India eased overseas investor access to commodity derivatives and widened portfolio managers' scope (Reuters).
Thailand's August exports surged on AI-related electronics and agricultural shipments, while a Thai court is set to rule on the validity of
the general election, imperilling the six-month-old government (Bloomberg). Taiwan backed plans to restart its nuclear power program,
the US and Japan discussed China's yttrium curbs ahead of the Trump–Xi meeting, and India's Akasa Air is in talks with Boeing over a
200-plane order (Bloomberg).
