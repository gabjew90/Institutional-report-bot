Second-Lowest Consumer Sentiment 
Ever in September With High Gas Prices
Headlines 
 
Consumer sentiment fell in September to its second-lowest level ever.  
A big jump in gasoline prices over the month drove the index lower. 
 
 
The drop in the expectations index over the month was larger than the 
drop in the current conditions index. 
 
 
Consumer spending continues to increase despite extremely low 
sentiment. 
 
 
Consumers expect higher inflation in the near term, but inflation 
expectations are down from the spring. 
Details
Consumer sentiment fell in September to its second-
lowest reading ever, behind only May of this year, as 
high energy prices remain a drag. Sentiment, as 
reported from a survey by the University of 
Michigan, fell to 48.1 in September from 51.7 in 
August; the all-time low was 44.8 in May. The index 
goes back to 1951. The final number for September 
was up from the preliminary reading for the month 
of 47.8. The measure is indexed to 100 in the first 
quarter of 1966. 
 
Consumer views on current and future economic 
conditions both weakened over the month. The drop 
in future conditions was larger, falling to 46.3 in 
September from 51.5 in August. This was the second-
lowest reading ever for the future conditions index, 
behind May’s 44.1. The current index was 50.9 in 
September, down from 51.9 in August. Although the 
current conditions index was historically weak in 
September, it was even lower in mid-2026 and the 
end of 2025. 
High gasoline and diesel prices are a huge drag on 
consumer sentiment in the fall of 2026. The price of 
a gallon of gasoline, after seasonal adjustment, 
jumped 5% in September from August, although this 
is slightly below the all-time high in mid-2022. 
Consumers are feeling stretched with energy prices 
up sharply entering the fall. But at least until now, 
consumers’ actions have belied their feelings. 
Consumer spending, adjusted for inflation, has 
continued to rise steadily since energy prices surged 
in the wake of the U.S. conflict with Iran and stronger 
inflation. And retail sales jumped a very strong 1.2% 
in August without adjusting for price changes, and 
were up 1.1% after taking out the boost from higher 
spending on gasoline.

pandemic even as consumer sentiment has been 
historically weak. High inflation has consumers

It is possible, however, that the most recent jump in 
energy prices could be the straw that breaks the 
camel’s back for consumers. In addition, home 
heating oil and diesel are very closely related and 
their prices move in tandem. So if high diesel prices

be facing much higher costs this season, taking an

One-year ahead inflation expectations jumped to 
4.6% in September from 4.0% in August as gasoline 
prices rose over the month. But one-year inflation 
expectations are lower than they were in April and 
May. And five-year inflation expectations are 
holding up better, rising to 3.4% in September from 
3.3% in August. Five-year expectations are also 
lower than they were in the spring.
