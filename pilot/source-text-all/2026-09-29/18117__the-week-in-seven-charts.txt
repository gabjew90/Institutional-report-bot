Chart #1
The 10-year Treasury yield just surged 
above 5.20% for the first time in 19 years
The US 10-year Treasury yield has climbed nearly 30 basis 
points in only two days, one of the sharpest bond selloffs 
in years. Yields were last at comparable levels in 2007, 
the year the US economy slipped into the recession that 
ultimately turned into the Global Financial Crisis.
There are clear drivers behind the move. September’s flash 
PMI reached a five-year high, inflationary pressures are 
building again and markets are pricing in a more hawkish 
Fed. Meanwhile, oil is hovering close to $100 and heavy 
government borrowing continues to weigh on the bond 
market.
That is why the 5.20% level matters. The 10-year Treasury 
serves as the benchmark for mortgage rates, corporate 
borrowing costs and valuations across financial markets, 
so each further rise adds pressure to the entire financial 
system.
Source: Bull Theory
Chart #2
The gap between US earnings growth 
and the rest of the world has never been 
wider
Since 1990, US corporate earnings have increased 
by almost +1,300%, compared with a +500% rise for 
companies outside the US over the same period. US 
earnings growth has therefore been 2.6 times that of 
the rest of the world. Over the same span, profit growth 
outside the US was broadly flat between 2008 and 2025. 
The US economy stands apart.
Source: The Kobeissi Letter
Chart #3
America’s debt problem is becoming a 
growth test
Gross federal debt stood at roughly 63% of GDP when 
Bill Clinton entered office. The most recent figure ex-
ceeds 122%.
Wars, tax cuts, financial crises, a pandemic and mounting 
long-term spending have all contributed. Interest costs 
are now adding to the total, with the CBO projecting net 
interest outlays of more than $1tn in 2026.
How then can the US regain control?
	
 Faster growth: raise productivity and lower the 
barriers to investment.
	
 Long-term costs: put Social Security and Medicare on 
a sustainable footing.
	
 Funding new commitments: pay for spending 
increases and tax cuts rather than borrowing to 
finance them.
Growth is the most attractive way out, and AI may help 
provide it.
The question is whether AI-driven productivity gains can 
outpace the debt and its interest costs, or whether the 
temptation to inflate the burden away will keep growing.
Source: Michel A.Arouet

Chart #4
China has spent a record amount on 
gold imports this year
China imported over 1,000 tonnes of gold in the first 
eight months of 2026, at a record cost of $158.8bn, 
already well above the $96.5bn spent over the whole 
of 2025. The demand is not limited to the central bank. 
With a weak property market, subdued equities and 
low bond yields, Chinese households and investors are 
turning to gold as a store of wealth that does not depend 
on any issuer’s promise to pay. At the same time, China’s 
holdings of US Treasuries dropped to $618bn in July, the 
lowest since 2008.
Source: FT
Chart #5
The iPhone got 6.3% more expensive 
this year. In gold terms, it actually got 
cheaper
Priced in gold, the 1 TB iPhone 18 Pro Max costs 0.38 
ounces, less than half the 0.92 ounces required for the 
first model in 2007. This year also marks the 20th edition 
of both the iPhone and the In Gold We Trust Report.
 
Source: In Gold We Trust
Chart #6
Artificial thought may be getting 
cheaper faster than any transformative 
technology in history
AI costs are declining faster than those of any previous 
transformative technology. For a given level of 
performance, prices have dropped by around 47% per 
quarter since 2023.
This pace is four times faster than DNA sequencing, 
six times faster than computing, 18 times faster than 
lithium batteries and, through 1973, 54 times faster than 
electricity.
Source: Epoch AI
