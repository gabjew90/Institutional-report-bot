Credit Expression: AI Financing Is Reshaping Credit Curves
THE VIEW
From Sarah Zappone (Credit ETF & Portfolio Trading) 26 September 2026
Hyperscaler curves have steepened another 8bp relative to the market since August, with hyperscaler
10s30s now 6bp steeper versus ticker-matched benchmarks that are 2bp flatter. The divergence is
overwhelmingly supply-driven. Hyperscalers continue aggressively issuing at the ultra-long end to fund AI
capex while many other sectors are shortening issuance profiles. Monitor relative curve steepness versus
matched IG baskets.
Preferred Expression(s):
• Long hyperscaler 10s30s steepeners


[Page 2]
Credit spreads are already near the lows, while expected hyperscaler issuance may limit further
compression. Equity upside may therefore offer a more attractive way to express a constructive risk view.

Hyperscaler credit curves have continued to steepen relative to the broader investment-grade market.

issuance profiles.

The spread differential between CDX IG 10Y and the GSUCI30Y basket has narrowed substantially since
2022. The move reflects a significant repricing in long-dated hyperscaler-related credit and is consistent
with the broader increase in AI-related financing activity highlighted in recent Goldman Sachs research.

The steepening observed in hyperscaler credit curves is also visible at the issuer level. The z-spread
differential between long-dated and shorter-dated Microsoft and Alphabet bonds has widened during 2026,
consistent with increased financing activity and issuance at the long end of the curve.
Risk Considerations
• Credit spreads may continue tightening despite already compressed levels.
• Anticipated hyperscaler issuance may prove lower than expected or be absorbed without meaningful
spread widening.
• Equity markets may underperform even if credit spreads remain stable.
• Slower growth or weaker earnings could weigh on equity upside participation.
• Changes in financing conditions, issuance trends, or risk sentiment could alter the relative attractiveness
of equities versus credit.
