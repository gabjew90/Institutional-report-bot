Trump-Xi summit: Stability first, more talks later
Chinese economy
 
 Authors: Yifan Hu, CIO Greater China and Head Macroeconomics APAC, UBS AG Hong Kong Branch; Kathy Li, CFA, Macro Strategist, UBS AG Hong 
Kong Branch
• Political signaling and high-level engagement took 
priority over detailed negotiations in the recently 
concluded Trump-Xi summit.
• Both sides downplayed strategic disputes to preserve 
stability and reduce the risk of near-term escalation.
• The summit produced a modest but practical set of 
deliverables, largely in line with market expectations. 
More substantive discussions will continue through 
working-level and senior-official negotiations.
 
(Shutterstock)
 
Chinese President Xi Jinping has concluded his 23-25 
September visit to Washington, marking his second face-to-
face meeting with President Donald Trump this year. Against 
a backdrop of unresolved disputes over trade, technology, 
critical minerals, and geopolitics, both sides emphasized 
dialogue, mutual respect and "strategic stability".
Political signaling took center stage
Leadership summits seldom focus on detailed negotiations. 
Instead, this meeting provided political backing for 
agreements developed by the two sides’ economic and 
diplomatic teams, while reaffirming the value of high-level 
communication. In this respect, President Xi’s visit to the US 
set a positive tone for bilateral relations and future talks.
The reception accorded to President Xi during his 
Washington visit was widely viewed as unprecedented in 
protocol. President Trump and First Lady Melania Trump 
personally welcomed President Xi and Madame Peng 
Liyuan on the tarmac at Joint Base Andrews, marking the 
first plane-side greeting by a sitting US president for a 
foreign leader since 1962. The arrival ceremony featured 
exceptional honors, including a 21-gun salute, military band 
performances of the national anthems, children presenting 
bouquets, and a military flyover.
The following day, the White House reinforced the 
message of respect and importance with a full State 
Arrival Ceremony, including a 479-member military honor 
formation representing all branches of the US Armed Forces, 
a Presidential Salute by the Marine Silent Drill Platoon, and 
an aerial demonstration featuring B-2 stealth bombers and 
F-22 fighter jets.
President Trump’s visibly warm body language, including 
repeated physical gestures of welcome, further projected 
an image of personal connection and Trump’s recognition 
of China's leadership as an equal. Taken together, the 
scale, visibility, and protocol-breaking nature of the welcome 
underscored the significance both sides attached to 
maintaining stable US-China relations despite continuing 
strategic competition.
For Trump, avoiding a renewed trade confrontation could 
help limit market volatility and economic uncertainty ahead 
of the US midterm elections. For Xi, a stable external 
environment is valuable as China approaches important 
domestic political milestones, including the Fifth Plenum 
expected in October and preparations for the 21st Party 
Congress next year.

Strategic differences were downplayed
The 
leaders 
avoided 
publicly 
emphasizing 
the 
most contentious issues, including advanced-technology 
restrictions, industrial policy, Taiwan, investment screening, 
critical minerals and broader geopolitical competition. 
Instead, the read-outs from Beijing and Washington 
adopted a notably positive tone, highlighting cooperation, 
continued communication and the goal of maintaining a 
constructive relationship.
This does not mean that the difficult issues were not 
discussed. Rather, both sides appeared satisfied that their 
key priorities and red lines had been communicated clearly 
to the other side, while avoiding public confrontation. 
Maintaining a positive atmosphere is particularly useful 
given the important political agendas ahead. Washington 
will be approaching the US midterm elections, while Beijing 
faces significant domestic political milestones and policy 
priorities. Neither side therefore appears eager to trigger a 
major bilateral disruption in the coming months.
Planned leader-level engagements at the APEC and G20 
summits would provide further opportunities to maintain 
momentum and reduce the risk of miscalculation. However, 
continued dialogue should not be interpreted as evidence 
of a meaningful narrowing of strategic differences.
Further discussions likely behind the scenes
Ahead of the summit, US and Chinese trade representatives 
led by US Secretary Bessent and China Vice Premier 
He Lifeng already met in the US to negotiate potential 
deliverables and narrow differences. Wire reports also 
indicated that several prominent Chinese business leaders 
such as BYD also traveled on their own to the US in 
the hope of exploring commercial agreements. However, 
no prominent Chinese business leaders joined the official 
events due to US regulatory scrutiny and potential domestic 
backlash. Still, a commitment on continuing Board of 
Investment discussions suggests business opportunities in 
non-sensitive sectors remain plausible.
The trade teams had already laid important groundwork, 
helping to raise expectations for a constructive outcome. 
The summit ultimately produced a modest but practical set 
of deliverables, mainly including a two-month extension 
of the tariff truce to 10 January 2027, reciprocal tariff 
cuts covering USD 30bn of non-sensitive goods, the 
establishment of an AI dialogue, and continued cooperation 
for the upcoming APEC and G20 meetings, among others. 
These outcomes provide breathing space rather than a 
durable settlement, with their limited scope suggesting that 
more substantive discussions will continue through working-
level and senior-official negotiations. The next phase is likely 
to remain focused on incremental progress around trade 
concessions, rare earths, tech restrictions, and commercial 
deals.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
