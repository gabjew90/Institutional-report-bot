28 September 2026 
Hormuz deadlock: bond yields climb along with oil 
MARKET LINES 
Global markets opened the new week on a defensive note on Monday. Indeed, Donald Trump's 
refusal to grant a seven-day ceasefire to Iran rules out the prospect of a quick reopening of the Strait 
of Hormuz. Oil prices pushed higher, with Brent trading above $107/bbl, as Iran held firm on its 
conditions and refused to soften its stance. The Hormuz deadlock continued to keep upward pressure 
on bond markets. The US 10-year yield held near 5.21%, its highest level in over twenty years, in 
response to growing expectations of further Fed rate hikes. On the foreign exchange front, the dollar 
strengthened against most G10 peers as investors sought haven assets amid renewed geopolitical 
uncertainty. This week, markets will remain hostage to developments in the US-Iran negotiations, 
with Iran still awaiting a formal US response through mediators. End-of-quarter rebalancing could 
also amplify volatility mid-week, while the upcoming US jobs report will add a further macroeconomic 
catalyst to an already fragile backdrop.  
Rates  
► 
The yield curve continued to steepen on Friday, with the front end showing greater resilience, 
supported by falling energy prices. Expectations of ECB tightening moderated slightly, with the 
probability of a rate hike in October falling back below 50%. The Schatz yield thus closed down 5 
bps at 3.28%.  
► 
Tensions, however, remained pronounced at the long end, with yields on bonds maturing beyond 
10 years rising again. The 5s30s EUR swap spread widened by 7 bps to -13 bps. In terms of spreads, 
the 10-year OAT-Bund spread narrowed slightly to 109 bps.  
FX  
► 
The DXY fell 0.26% to 101.02, weighed down by the pullback in oil prices, snapping a four-session 
winning streak. 
► 
The EUR/USD gained 0.15% to 1.1396, supported by dollar weakness, though the pair remains on 
track to post a third consecutive weekly decline. 
► 
The yen was the best-performing G10 currency, with USD/JPY shedding 0.92% to 157.37, 
rebounding from a three-week low after Japanese authorities reaffirmed their joint commitment 
with Washington on the stance that led to July's intervention. 
► 
The British pound closed near a three-month low against the dollar, with GBP/USD edging up a 
modest 0.16% to 1.3236.

INDUSTRY NEWS 
 
Real Estate  
► 
European Hospitality – according to Green Street, rising hospitality taxes in Europe are 
squeezing investors due to three key factors. Soaring tourist taxes (pushing total tax costs in 
Amsterdam to 37% by 2027), the transition to taxes based on a percentage of room rates and the 
devolution of taxing powers to local authorities (notably in England, Norway, Denmark, Finland, 
and Ireland). In response to these hikes, travelers may shift their demand away from major 
gateways to untaxed destinations like Munich or Warsaw. 
► 
Investors’ cautiousness – according to Deloitte, facing rising interest rates and the high cost 
of capital, commercial real estate executives are exercising unprecedented caution, which 
drives their business sentiment index down to 57.8/100 (compared to 64.9 last year). Yet, the 
sector is not collapsing, it is adapting. On one hand, 51% of market players still expect their 
revenues to grow by more than 5%. On the other hand, this pressure is pushing investors to secure 
their capital outbound by targeting three key countries the United States (28%), the United 
Kingdom (15%) and India (15%) with a clear priority on future-proof sectors like logistics and the 
digital economy. 
► 
UK Life Science – according to IPE Real Assets, Federated Hermes has put its 50% stake in 
Milton Park, a science hub near Oxford, up for sale. The other half remains owned by the 
Canadian fund CPPIB. Valued at a total of £800m (€930m), this strategic site is on the market for 
the first time in 40 years. At this stage, the sale process is just beginning and the buyer is not yet 
known. 
Energy Transition  
► 
According to Les Echos, EDF (BBB+/Baa1/BBB+) is seeking a revision of environmental 
standards to acknowledge the warming of rivers independently of its hot water discharges, 
following a scorching summer marked by massive reactor shutdowns. As a result, up to fifteen of 
its French nuclear reactors were shut down at the same time due to the consequences of the 
high temperatures. Consequently, the operator lost 10 TWh of nuclear generation, equivalent to 
12% of its reactors' electricity production between the beginning of June and the end of August, 
according to RTE. 
Others 
► 
The French Ministry of the Armed Forces is launching the ALIQUANTE project to develop a 
sovereign quantum programming platform tailored to defense needs. Handed over to a 
consortium led by Thales, Bull, and the CEA, this operating system will enable the exploitation of 
future computers from the PROQCIMA program by 2032. It will ensure the secure processing of 
classified data using shared programming tools, supported by a planned investment of €300m.
