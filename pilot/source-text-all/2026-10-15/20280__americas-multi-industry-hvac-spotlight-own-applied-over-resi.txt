PM Summary.  Over the past year, HVAC stock performance has been bifurcated as 
Applied HVAC (JCI, TT) has materially outperformed Resi HVAC (LII, CARR) with 
dispersion being very wide (JCI: +47%, LII: -33%). We expect the trend to continue. 
Three key reasons for our more favorable view of Applied HVAC vs. Resi HVAC over 
the next 12 months. First, we expect positive FY27 estimate revisions for both JCI 
(GS: +6% vs. consensus) and TT (GS: +4% vs. consensus), while our estimates for LII 
(GS: -4% vs. consensus) and CARR (GS: -3% vs. consensus) are below. Second, the 
structure of the Resi HVAC market could be changing with competition increasing 
and pricing harder to come by. Additionally, while we believe inventory levels have 
normalized post the 2024/2025 disruption from the refrigerant change, demand 
remains tepid given low housing turnover and affordability concerns. Third, we 
expect the demand for Applied HVAC to remain robust despite backlog rising ~50% 
on average this year for JCI and TT as the longer-cycle parts (e.g., power generation) 
of the AI/DC value chain are now booking orders beyond 2030.  As a result, we 
upgrade TT to Buy (from Neutral) and downgrade LII/CARR to Neutral (from Buy). 
We reiterate our Buy rating on JCI (on the Americas Conviction Buy list).

Has our outlook for Resi industry unit demand changed? As we have written, we 
expected Resi unit demand to stabilize in 2026 and for inventories to normalize, with the 
market achieving equilibrium this year (LINK). This has largely played out, and we now 
expect Resi HVAC industry shipments to grow +MSD this year. This should result in 
approximately 8.0mn shipments in 2026.  Our base case is that 2027 Resi HVAC 
shipments are slightly below normalized levels at 8.1mn units based on weaker housing 
starts (~15% of annual Resi HVAC shipments) and a replacement rate slightly below the 
prior 20-year average. This would result in units +1.8% next year, slightly below current 
consensus expectations.

Recent commentary from HVAC OEMs on Resi pricing 
Company
Date
Commentary
Carrier
9/15/2026
Dave Gitlin (CEO) noted that Carrier is recalibrating pricing expectations away from the unprecedented inflationary cycle of 
2021–2022, when manufacturers pushed through compounded ~8% price increases multiple times a year. While the era of high-
frequency, high-single-digit hikes has passed, he emphasized that structural pricing power remains firmly in place. Carrier intends to 
implement measured, disciplined price increases to offset persistent input cost inflation. 
Lennox
8/11/2026
Alok Maskara (CEO) highlighted a significant divergence in the residential HVAC value chain since the pandemic: while equipment 
manufacturers raised wholesale prices by roughly 40%, the final installed price charged to consumers surged by more than 100%. This 
sharp inflation at the consumer level has materially heightened price elasticity, particularly during non-peak shoulder periods. 
Homeowners have transitioned from pandemic-era distress purchasing - where availability trumped price and customers accepted the 
first contractor who showed up - to disciplined comparison shopping, routinely soliciting three to four competitive bids.
Crucially, he thinks consumers have become increasingly discerning regarding contractor ownership structures, actively identifying 
and pushing back against high-margin pricing models typical of private equity-backed dealer rollups. Consequently, price deflation and 
margin compression are now occurring downstream between the installer and the homeowner rather than upstream at the OEM 
factory gate. Lennox expects this downstream contractor margin normalization to remain a multi-year headwind for retail installers as 
consumer price sensitivity resets the channel's unit economics.

Trane Technologies (TT): Best in class operator with accelerating growth; upgrade to Buy 
 
 
We upgrade TT to Buy from Neutral with a $565 12-month price target, representing 
~18% upside. The bottom line is that we see upside to 2027/2028 EPS (GS: 
+3.7%/+5.5% vs. consensus) as we believe organic growth is likely to meaningfully 
accelerate on the back of orders >+20% for three consecutive quarters (applied >100% 
for four consecutive quarters) and a robust backlog going into next year. We view TT as a 
best in class HVAC OEM with a strong track record of execution and raising guidance, 
while strong FCF generation and balance sheet flexibility provides additional EPS upside 
potential. With shares having de-rated ~10% over the last 6 months and now trading at 
~1 turn discount to its three-year average, we view risk/reward as compelling. Our 
12-month price target of $565 is based on 21.0x Q5-Q8 EV/EBITDA and implies a 4.0% 
FCF yield. 
Investment thesis 
1. Applied HVAC to drive upside to current estimates. We expect Applied Commercial 
HVAC, led by data centers, to be the largest source of upside to TT’s estimates over the 
next several years. Americas Commercial HVAC bookings increased ~40% in 1Q26 and 
50% in 2Q26, while Applied bookings increased >160% and 130%, respectively. 2Q26 
marked the fourth consecutive quarter of Applied bookings growth >100%. This has 
driven enterprise backlog to $12.1bn, including ~$6bn scheduled for 2027 and beyond, 
providing strong visibility into future revenue conversion. While data centers are an 
important driver, TT is seeing broad-based Commercial HVAC demand across other 
verticals. Further, we think the magnitude and duration of Applied demand will provide 
meaningful upside to current revenue estimates. All in, we expect TT to deliver DD+ 
organic growth in 2027 (GS: 11.6% vs. consensus: +HSD), with Applied backlog 
conversion providing upside to current estimates. 
2. Strong execution supports continued outperformance, while incremental 
margins could surprise to the upside. TT has established a strong track record of 
market outgrowth and execution, providing confidence that the company will continue 
to outperform going forward. This performance has continued despite volatility across 
Residential, Transport and geopolitical headwinds. Additionally, we believe there is the 
potential for incremental margins to surprise to the upside (ahead of management’s 
25%+ target) in FY27 for the following reasons: 1) track record of incremental margins 
averaging closer to 30% over the last 4 years; 2) Americas incremental margins 
improving after averaging 17% the last 3 quarters due to Resi HVAC bottoming (~60% 
Resi HVAC deleverage in 4Q25) and capacity additions/investments in Applied. We 
expect this to improve in 3Q26 and sustain thereafter; 3) EMEA margins (GS 2026: 
15.8%, -130bps) should improve materially next year after a year of intentional 
investments and headwinds from the Middle East (~15% of EMEA sales) dissipating. The 
Middle East impacted 2Q26 EBIT by $30mn. 
3. Strong balance sheet and solid FCF generation provides EPS upside. TT’s strong 
FCF profile and under-levered balance sheet provides significant capital deployment 
optionality. As of 2Q26, net leverage is ~0.7x, and we estimate ~$9bn of FCF generation 
in 2027/2028.  Even with ~$1.2bn/~$1.7bn in buybacks baked in for 2027/2028, we 
estimate that TT will be net cash by 2028, and therefore has > $10bn in balance sheet 
optionality to still remain investment grade.

Overview of Trane Technologies (TT) 
 
 
TT is a global climate company that provides commercial and residential HVAC 
equipment, controls and services, as well as transport refrigeration solutions, primarily 
through its Trane and Thermo King brands. TT generated ~$21.3bn of revenue in 2025, 
with ~81% from the Americas, ~13% from EMEA and ~6% from Asia Pacific. Equipment 
represents ~66% of revenue, with services representing the remaining ~34%. TT became 
a standalone climate company in 2020 following the separation of the legacy Ingersoll 
Rand Industrial business. 
Commercial HVAC: TT provides heating, cooling and ventilation equipment, 
n
controls and services for commercial buildings and industrial applications. Products 
include chillers, air handlers, rooftop and unitary systems, heat pumps, variable 
refrigerant flow systems and building management systems. TT also provides 
installation, repair and maintenance, rental and energy management services. The 
portfolio includes data center HVAC systems, facility controls, liquid cooling 
solutions and data center services, as well as smart and AI-enabled services. 
Residential HVAC: TT provides residential heating and cooling solutions primarily in 
n
the Americas, including air conditioners, furnaces, heat pumps, ductless systems, 
thermostats and controls, indoor air quality products and home automation. 
Products are sold through branch sales offices, distributors and dealers. 
Transport refrigeration: Through Thermo King, TT provides temperature-controlled 
n
transport and cold-chain solutions across truck, trailer, rail, bus and container 
applications. Products include diesel, electric and hybrid refrigeration systems, 
auxiliary power units, telematics and portable and stationary cold-storage solutions, 
supported by aftermarket parts and services.

LII: Down to Neutral as Resi HVAC expectations too high 
 
 
Investment thesis. We downgrade LII to Neutral ($390 PT, ~10% upside) from Buy as 
Resi HVAC (HCS segment) expectations for next year appear too high given the demand 
weakness LII is experiencing in Resi new construction as well as competitive pressure 
intensifying, resulting in downward pricing pressure. While the stock has de-rated 
considerably over the last 6 months and is trading well below historical norms, we see 
risk/reward as more balanced given Resi has now rebounded and LII appears to have lost 
share. Since being added to the Buy List on October 9, 2023, LII shares are -8% vs. XLI 
+65% and S&P +80%. When we upgraded LII to Buy, the crux of our thesis was that Resi 
HVAC volumes were about to accelerate, refrigerant-related pricing would support 
growth and Commercial growth/margins would surprise to the upside. The Commercial 
thesis has played out better than expected, with BCS organic sales averaging ~11% over 
the last 4 years, supported by share gains in emergency replacement and national 
accounts, while margins are also up ~200bps since 2023. The Resi HVAC thesis also 
played out for the ensuing 20 months until 2H25, which turned out to be a much more 
severe downturn than we anticipated. In 2026, Resi HVAC industry volumes have been 
better than expected but LII growth has disappointed owing to weaker Resi new 
construction. While we now believe that the Resi HVAC market has bottomed, we no 
longer expect the recovery to be V-shaped and are beginning to see early signs of 
intensifying competition at a time when pricing is likely getting more difficult given 
declining consumer affordability. As such, we see earnings risk for next year (GS 2027E 
EPS -3% vs. consensus) and downgrade LII to Neutral.  
Valuation. We update our FY26/FY27/FY28 EPS estimates to $23.15/$24.95/$26.70 
(from $23.50/$26.00/$28.40), which is -1%/-3%/-5% vs. FactSet consensus, driven by a 
more muted growth and earnings conversion outlook. Our 12-month price target moves 
to $390 from $547 as we apply an 11.5x Q5-Q8 EV/EBITDA multiple (vs. 15.5x 
previously) given slower EPS growth and limited catalysts.  
Key risks. 
Upside: (1) Stronger Residential replacement recovery. Faster conversion of deferred 
repairs into replacement demand could drive higher HCS growth; (2) Sustained BCS 
share gains. Continued strength in emergency replacement, national accounts and 
service could support higher growth and margins; (3) Better returns from growth 
initiatives. Distribution investments, JVs and bolt-on M&A could drive stronger 
share-of-wallet and earnings growth. 
Downside: (1) Residential affordability remains pressured. Higher installed costs 
could keep repair versus replacement elevated and weigh on HCS volumes; (2) 
Sustained pricing competition. Aggressive bidding could pressure share if LII maintains 
margin discipline; (3) Growth initiatives under-deliver. Slower returns from 
acquisitions, JVs or distribution investments could limit long-term growth and margin 
expansion.

CARR: Weaker incremental margins limit upside; down to Neutral 
 
 
Investment thesis. We downgrade CARR to Neutral ($61 PT, ~10% upside) from Buy, as 
we see risk to CARR’s incremental margins next year given negative mix from a growing 
DC business as well as the fact that CARR will be ramping capacity rapidly in tandem. As 
a result, we see risk/reward as more balanced especially as valuation does not screen 
inexpensive relative to history. Since being added to the Buy List on 12/12/24, CARR 
shares are -25% vs. XLI +22% and S&P +29%. The crux of our call was that its 
transformation into a focused HVAC OEM positioned the company to benefit from 
secular growth in Commercial HVAC, alongside a cyclical recovery in Residential, Europe 
and Transport. Our thesis remains intact from a growth perspective, but mixed 
execution since adding to the Buy list and limited visibility on margin conversion now 
gives us pause. Specifically, we see risk to CARR’s ability to deliver on the 35%+ 
incremental margins that consensus is modeling for 2027 given stubbornly high inflation, 
the capacity ramp, and negative mix from the DC business, which will likely constitute 
the vast majority of incremental revenues next year. As such, we see earnings risk for 
next year (GS 2027 EPS -3% vs. consensus) and downgrade CARR to Neutral. 
Valuation. We maintain our FY26/FY27/FY28 EPS of $2.85/$3.20/$3.60. Our 12-month 
price target moves to $61 (from $74) as we lower our Q5-Q8 EV/EBITDA multiple to 
14.0x (from 16.0x prior). We lower our target multiple to reflect limited visibility on 
margins and risk to earnings flow through. Our 14.0x multiple is in line with the 
company’s historical average. 
Key risks. 
Upside: (1) Stronger data center growth. Backlog, pipeline and capacity additions 
could support faster growth than we currently model; (2) Faster short-cycle recovery. 
Continued improvement in Americas Residential and Light Commercial and Europe 
could drive upside to organic growth and EPS; (3) Better margin execution. Stronger 
pricing, productivity and faster improvement in Europe could drive higher incrementals. 
Downside: (1) Data center execution. The significant production ramp increases 
supply-chain and delivery risk; (2) Data center slow down. Project delays, weaker 
customer capex or slower deliveries could pressure growth; (3) Price/cost and mix 
pressure. Higher input costs, tariffs and unfavorable mix could limit margin expansion if 
pricing and productivity do not fully offset the headwinds; (4) Weaker end markets. A 
renewed slowdown in Americas Residential and Light Commercial, Europe or China could 
pressure organic growth and earnings.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring
