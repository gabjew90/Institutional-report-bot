| GS FX Research Notes: CPI Prep |
| 14 October 2026 | 5:01AM EDT | Research | Economics |
| Inflation data remain key for the Fed outlook, and CPI releases in turn should be an important input to
Dollar performance. But in practice, initial broad Dollar reactions to recent releases have been muted, with
an average magnitude of 13bps in the thirty minutes post-release across 2026 prints so far (first chart
below). This includes last month’s release where the Dollar failed to rally despite an ~8bps upside surprise
to the consensus mean on core CPI. This low FX release vol is more notable still in the context of each of
the past three CPI releases having come with front-meeting Fed pricing being close to 50-50 between a
hike and a hold. We have shown in the past that FX responds most clearly to rate differentials at the 2-3
year point, and in this sense the readthrough to front-meeting expectations isn’t necessarily the most
impactful for the Dollar. But we think it is useful context that this time around the market is pricing a
much more ‘well-defined’ series of upcoming Fed decisions, with a high implied probability mass of a
hold in October and a hike in December (second chart below)—helped by recent Fed commentary. In light
of this recent pattern of muted Dollar responses to CPI data and slightly firmer near-term policy guidance,
we think the hurdle for a high FX vol outcome on today’s release remains relatively high. Our economists
estimate a 24bp M/M core print for today’s release, consistent with a slightly softer 20bps on core PCE—
which would be 6bps below the average so far this year. Past the September data, they expect a softer
series of core inflation readings into year-end, and continue to see dovish risks to Fed pricing next year
(more so than for almost any other G10 central bank through to end-2027—third chart below). Shifting
rate differentials have been a key contributor to the Dollar's gains over the past month, but mostly due to
moves abroad while Fed pricing is now aligned with our economists' forecasts for the next few meetings,
so should be a more neutral factor in the near term. Charts of the Day: Recent CPI releases have delivered
fairly limited initial broad Dollar reactions. Markets go into today’s data with ‘better-defined’ expectations
for the next two FOMC decisions than ahead of the prior three CPI releases. Our economists’ modal
expectations point to meaningful downside risks to the Fed hike pricing through to the end of next year,
more so than for almost any other G10 central bank (after the BoE). |
