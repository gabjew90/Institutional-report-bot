Existing Home Sales Rose in September, but 
Higher Mortgage Rates Remain a Headwind 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Headlines 
 Existing home sales rose 1.5% in September. Sales were down 2.2% from 
the same time last year. 
 
 Inventories jumped to the highest level since late 2019. The inventory-to-
sales ratio held steady at 4.8 months in September and is now above its 
pre-pandemic level. 
 
 Annual house price growth ran at 1.3%, with the largest annual increase 
in the undersupplied Northeast. 
 
 Looking ahead, PNC expects elevated mortgage rates to continue 
weighing on existing home sales in the near term. 
 
Details
Total existing home sales rose 1.5% to a seasonally 
adjusted annualized rate of 3.99 million units in 
September, up from 3.93 million units in August 
(revised down from 3.98 million), according to the 
National Association of Realtors (NAR). The increase 
partially reversed the declines over the previous 
three months. Despite the monthly rebound, 
elevated mortgage rates continued to weigh on 
housing demand. Sales were down 3.4% from 
February and 2.2% from a year earlier. The average 
30-year fixed mortgage rate averaged about 7.0% 
in September, according to Freddie Mac, roughly 
100 basis points higher than the 6.05% average in 
February. 
 
Existing home sales have hovered near 4 million units 
in recent years, well below the pre-pandemic pace 
of roughly 5 million. Sales fell sharply in early 2022 as 
mortgage rates surged from about 3% to nearly 7%, 
remained below 4.5 million throughout 2023, and 
reached a near 14-year low of 3.90 million units in 
September 2024. 
 
Regionally, existing home sales declined 2.1% in the 
Northeast in September, while sales increased 2.8% 
in both the South and West. Sales were unchanged 
in the Midwest. Compared with a year earlier, sales 
declined in all four regions, led by a 5.2% drop in the 
West. Sales were down 4.1% in the Northeast, 2.1% in 
the Midwest, and 0.5% in the South.

Existing single-family home sales rose 2.0% from 
August, while condo and co-op sales fell 2.8%. On a 
year-over-year 
basis, 
both 
categories 
posted 
declines, with condo and co-op sales down a 
steeper 5.4%. 
 
The inventory of existing homes available for sale 
increased about 2% from a month earlier to 1.60 
million units (not seasonally adjusted), the highest 
level since late 2019. Inventory has trended higher 
over the past few years and has risen sharply since 
early 2026, although it remains low by historical 
standards. 
 
With both sales and inventory increasing in 
September, the supply of existing homes on the 
market, measured by months' supply at the current 
sales pace, held steady at 4.8 months. The inventory-
to-sales ratio has risen substantially from its record 
low of 1.6 months in early 2022 and now stands 
above its pre-pandemic level. 
 
The median existing home price increased 1.3% from 
a year earlier to $417,500 in September (not 
seasonally adjusted). Although price growth slowed 
from the 1.6% increase in August, home price 
appreciation has strengthened from near-flat 
readings early in 2026. 
 
The Northeast, where housing supply remains 
particularly constrained, posted the strongest home 
price appreciation among the regions, with the 
median sales price up about 5% from a year earlier. 
The Midwest followed with a 3% increase. Limited 
new home construction continues to support price 
growth in both regions. In August, the Northeast and 
Midwest 
accounted 
for 
just 
8% 
and 
16%, 
respectively, of total U.S. housing starts. By contrast, 
home price growth remained subdued in the better-
supplied South and West, where median sales prices 
were up only 0.2% and down 0.3%, respectively, 
from a year earlier. 
 
Housing affordability has improved somewhat over 
the past three years as mortgage rates retreated 
from their peak of 7.6% in late 2023 and household 
incomes 
continued 
to increase. 
Nevertheless, 
affordability remains near historical lows in 2026. 
Home prices continue to rise, income growth has 
moderated, and mortgage rates have moved 
noticeably higher this year. 
 
Looking ahead, PNC expects elevated mortgage 
rates to continue weighing on existing home sales in 
the near term. The average 30-year fixed mortgage 
rate rose to nearly 7.4% in October, close to its 
highest level in three years. Meanwhile, labor 
market softening has contributed to slower income 
growth, while inflation has accelerated. Lower 
household savings, slower wage growth, and higher 
inflation are likely to constrain households' ability to 
accumulate down payments, limiting support for 
home purchases in the near term.
