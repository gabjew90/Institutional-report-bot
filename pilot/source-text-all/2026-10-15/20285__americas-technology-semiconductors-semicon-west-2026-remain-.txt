Americas Technology: Semiconductors: SEMICON West 2026 - Remain 
bullish on group as industry visibility continues to extend
14 October 2026 | 4:01PM EDT
We attended the SEMICON West Conference in San Francisco, California on October 
13-14 and met with Applied Materials, Lam Research, Teradyne, Camtek, Onto 
Innovation, MKS Instruments, and a variety of other companies across the industry. 
Overall, we came away from the event with an incrementally positive view on WFE 
growth for 2027 and 2028 - consistent with our recent WFE outlook. We highlight 
key industry takeaways, along with company-specific takeaways below: 
WFE trends: Although companies avoided giving quantitative updates on WFE 
expectations, there was agreement that 2027 is set to be a very strong year, 
supported by 8-10 new wafer fab openings globally across a variety of product 
types. Our current estimates call for 2027 WFE to grow ~45% Y/Y to ~$218 bn, 
driven by DRAM, leading-edge foundry/logic, and NAND. 
Leading-edge logic: As AI datacenter buildouts continue to scale, leading-edge logic 
is positioned to continue growing robustly into next year. We expect leading-edge 
foundry to underpin this growth rate as we forecast TSMC’s CapEx to be $85 bn in 
2026 - and we also see the opportunity for incremental growth to be driven by 
customers such as Intel and Terafab. 
DRAM: We expect DRAM spending momentum to continue given increased 
competition in HBM and a growing need for more CapEx-intensive solutions with 
more required wafer capacity expansions. We expect Applied and Lam to benefit 
from continued industry tightness as they are over-indexed to memory. Our checks 
suggest memory makers continue to prioritize DRAM supply additions over NAND. 
NAND: We believe supplier utilization rates remain near peak levels, and expect 
NAND WFE to accelerate in 2027. However, this spending remains almost entirely 
driven by upgrades rather than greenfield capacity expansion, which we would not 
expect to begin until late 2027 at the earliest. 
Test: Given increasing levels of chip complexity, innovative solutions are necessary to 
shorten time to market for customers. However, test times continue to move higher 
for the latest generations of GPUs and accelerators, which we see as a significant 
driver of ATE growth over the next few years - along with new markets such as 
optical networking. 
Back-end inspection and metrology: Industry participants remain very 
constructive on the demand outlook heading into 2027, driven by a number of 
factors including CoWoS, HBM, and the early ramp of longer-term demand drivers.

Key Takeaways from Company Meetings 
 
 
Key Takeaways from Applied Materials Meeting 
Participants: Kevin Moraes, VP, Strategy and Marketing; Sree Kesapragada, VP, 
Advanced Packaging; Mike Sullivan, Head of Investor Relations 
Investor questions mainly focused on clarifications tied to the company’s investor event 
and financial targets. On revenue and capital intensity, management noted that 
semiconductor industry pricing is a key variable that it cannot control - but has high 
confidence in its framework tied to increasing WFE intensity per node - and these 
increases have already been programmatically locked in by customers today. On 
Applied’s target of 200bps of market share gain, management noted the complex set of 
mix effects involved in the calculation, but noted the substantial impact from China 
export control exclusions. Relative to the company’s newly articulated gross margin 
target of 54% by 2030, Applied believes there is the potential for upside to this target - 
but pointed out that the vast majority of its costs are variable in nature. On its optical 
tool portfolio, Applied believes that it can leverage its expertise and market position in 
e-beam technology to expand into this adjacent $9bn market. On AGS, management has 
high confidence in its outlook for mid-teens long term revenue growth - but noted that 
its services attach rate is higher on its newer tools relative to its installed fleet. 
Key Takeaways from Lam Research Meeting 
Participants: Ram Ganesh - VP of Investor Relations 
Consistent with prior commentary, Lam expects 8-10 new fab projects to come online in 
2027 - and currently expects product growth to be driven by DRAM, followed by 
advanced foundry/logic, followed by NAND flash. The company has not specified a 
timeframe for achieving its new long-term gross margin target of ~55%. However, we 
expect this to be achieved over several years, and see it tied more to product mix 
(particularly with new products such as Akara) and overseas operational manufacturing 
efficiencies rather than like-for-like pricing. Management remains constructive on the 
company’s competitive positioning in Logic, DRAM, and advanced packaging - 
particularly as etch and deposition intensity continues to grow with a variety of new 
device structures coming into the mix - including hard masks, surface prep, TSVs, and 
other materials engineering steps. 
Key Takeaways from Teradyne Meeting 
Participants: Roy Chorev - VP & GM of Compute Test Division; Amy McAndrews - VP of

SEMICON West. Teradyne remains committed to an open ecosystem of probe card

Regarding its XPU business, Teradyne continues to expect to gain merchant GPU share in 
the long run - and noted that Rubin test times are nearly 2X Blackwell given the near 
doubling in transistor count. Teradyne is also pursuing the tester opportunity at Intel, 
which is focused on increasing its exposure to external vendors (currently using an

Key Takeaways from MKS Instruments Meeting

Valuation/Risks 
 
 
AMAT (Buy, on CL): Our 12-month price target of $670 is based on a 32X P/E multiple 
applied to our normalized EPS estimate of $21.00. Key downside risks include: (1) 
incremental export restrictions; (2) local China-based supplier share gains. 
LRCX (Buy): Our price target is $380, which is based on a 35X multiple applied to our 
normalized EPS forecast of $10.75. Key downside risks: (1) softer DRAM/HBM demand; 
(2) delays in planned clean room and fab expansions; (3) lower-than-expected content 
gains from advanced packaging and 3D architectures. 
TER (Buy): Our 12-month price target is $465, which is based on a 37X P/E multiple 
applied to our normalized EPS forecast of $12.50. Key downside risks: (1) delays in the 
revenue ramp with a merchant GPU customer, (2) significant quarterly volatility due to 
the lumpy nature of large AI-related infrastructure projects, (3) slower-than-expected 
recovery in Mobile. 
ONTO (Buy): Our12-month price target is $400, which is based on 32X our normalized 
EPS estimate of $12.50. Our target P/E is in line with peer multiples, and it is on the 
higher end of ONTO’s historical median, given recent momentum around HPC. Key 
upside/downside risks: (1) additional volume purchase agreements and accelerated 
customer adoption of Dragonfly G5; (2) shift towards OSATs or domestic Chinese 
manufacturers; (3) competitor launching a rival tool. 
CAMT (Neutral): Our 12-m price target of $160 is based on a 38X P/E multiple applied 
to our normalized EPS of $4.20. Key upside/downside risks: (1) notable push-outs in 
chiplet architectures; (2) failure to pass on higher pricing; (3) better-than-expected 
growth in HPC-driven revenue in the medium-term. 
MKSI (Sell): Our 12-month price target is $270, which is based on a 20X P/E multiple 
applied to our normalized EPS of $13.50. Key upside risks: (1) rebound in the NAND 
market from an equipment ordering perspective; (2) greater realization of synergies 
from the Atotech acquisition; (3) any material pickup in momentum for the company’s 
Semiconductor business due to broader WFE trends.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
