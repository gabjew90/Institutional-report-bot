Progressive Corp. (PGR): First Take: CATs/PYD Drive Sep EPS Beat, 
Though Below Expectation Revenue/PIF Growth and Attritional Loss 
14 October 2026 | 9:31AM EDT
PGR September operating EPS of $1.40 modestly beat Visible Alpha Consensus 
Data/GSe of $1.27/$1.16. The beat vs. GSe was driven by a 2pp beat on the Cat ratio 
(1.3% vs. 3.3% GSe) and a 1pp beat on the PYD ratio (2.0pp favorable vs. 0.8pp GSe), 
while attritional margins missed our expectations by 1.7pp (90.6% vs. 88.9%). After 
allocating PYD by premium mix, the attritional combined ratio miss appears to be 
driven by a 2pp miss on the Personal Auto attritional combined ratio, as both the 
attritional loss ratio (70.7% vs. 69.3% GSe) and the expense ratio (20.9% vs. 20.2% 
GSe) came in weaker than we expected in the Personal Auto segment. NPW growth 
of +2% fell 3pp below our +5% expectation (and ~+5.5% growth in July/August) due 
to a 3pp miss in Personal Auto NPW growth (+1.2% vs. +4.4% GSe). The weaker 
Personal Auto growth was driven by weaker than expected growth in PIF (-27k 
sequentially vs. +85k GSe and +~+150k in Aug/Sep) and ~40bps weaker than 
expected Y/Y Premium-Per-Policy growth of -4.2% (vs. -3.8% GSe). Outside of 
Personal Auto, PGR continued to deliver mid-single digit growth in Commercial 
Auto/Home and margins were slightly favorable to our expectation in both lines. PGR 
modestly increased buybacks to ~$200mn in the quarter (vs.~$150mn August QTD), 
but the company continues to retain significant excess capital relative to its stated 
maximum premium-leverage level. While monthly results can be volatile, we 
expect investors will react negatively to weaker growth and margin results in 
Personal Auto, particularly as this is a change of pace from stronger growth 
results over the past 2 months. We will look to PGR’s upcoming earnings call on 
Nov. 3rd for details surrounding the growth outlook. 
Personal Auto: The combined ratio of 90.0% beat our 91.5% estimate by 1.5pp, 
driven by a 1.7pp beat on the Cat ratio, while attritional margins likely missed our 
expectation by ~2pp depending on the allocation of PYD by segment. NPW growth 
of 1.2% missed our 4.4% growth expectation, driven by misses on PIF growth (+7.4% 
Y/Y growth vs. +7.8% GSe) and average NPW-Per-Policy growth (-4.2% Y/Y vs. -3.8% 
GSe). On a sequential basis, PIF declined by 27k (vs. +85k GSe and +~150k in each of 
the past 2 months) with the miss driven primarily by Direct PIF which declined by 31k 
in the quarter (80k miss vs. GSE of +50k) while Agency missed our expectation by 
30k (+4k vs. +34 GSe). Y/Y growth in Premium-Per-Policy declined by 1pp during the 
quarter, driven by a 0.8pp sequential decline in Agency Y/Y Premium-Per-Policy 
growth to -5.0% and a 1.2pp decline in Direct Premium-Per-Policy growth to -3.6%. 
Despite the slowing growth in September, the company maintained its elevated 
Direct Expense Ratio at 22.2% which was 1pp above GSe of 21.2%.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank

across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
