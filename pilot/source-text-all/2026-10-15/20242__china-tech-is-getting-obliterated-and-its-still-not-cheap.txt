China's tech meltdown is getting serious
China's tech reversal is becoming increasingly brutal.
ChiNext and STAR 50 have broken key technical levels, with oversold readings reaching extremes not
seen in years. Meanwhile, the divergence with Nasdaq has become enormous.
But this isn't just a technical correction.
Goldman highlights extraordinary semiconductor valuation premiums despite significantly weaker
profitability, while China's AI trade is unwinding and software fundamentals are deteriorating.
The big question: Is this becoming an oversold buying opportunity, or are Chinese tech valuations still
facing a much bigger reality check?
The ChiNext bear
ChiNext, China's Nasdaq-style growth index, continues to crash. The index is trading well below its 200-
day moving average, the death cross has been in place since mid-September, and we are now seeing
the most oversold levels since the Liberation Day chaos.
Note the longer term trend line comes in slightly lower.

Fallen STAR
China's STAR 50 index, the epicenter of the country's technological innovation, has crashed as well.
The index is trading well below both its 50- and 200-day moving averages, while RSI has plunged to its
most oversold levels on record.
Unlike ChiNext, which covers broader growth stocks, STAR 50 is more concentrated in hard tech,
particularly semiconductors and China's push for technological self-sufficiency.

The tech gap
The gap between Nasdaq and ChiNext has become enormous. Chinese tech was on fire just a few
months ago. Today, the reversal is spectacular, while US tech continues to trade in an entirely different
universe.
Two tech markets, two very different realities. The question is whether this divergence can persist.

China's semiconductor valuation problem
China's hard-tech selloff is becoming increasingly uncomfortable, but valuations remain anything but
cheap.
Goldman Sachs highlights an extraordinary disconnect: Chinese semiconductor stocks trade at a 237%
forward P/E premium to their global peers.
The push for technological self-sufficiency and strong government support have helped justify
elevated multiples, but GS warns that overpaying for growth and policy support remains a key risk.
The question is whether China's semiconductor stocks have corrected enough to reflect
those expectations.
GS
Paying more for less?
The Chinese semiconductor valuation premium looks even more interesting when compared with
profitability.
Goldman Sachs estimates that Chinese semiconductor companies will generate net margins of 16% in
2026, versus 36% for their global competitors. Return on equity is estimated at 24% versus 39%, while
R&D spending relative to sales also trails.
China's technological ambitions remain enormous, but the profitability gap is very real.
The technicals are screaming oversold. The fundamentals are not necessarily screaming cheap.
GS
The China AI unwind
China's AI trade has already suffered a significant reversal.
According to Goldman Sachs, Chinese AI exporters surged 126% between early 2025 and June 2026,
before losing 23% from their recent peak.
Meanwhile, domestically focused AI companies trade at 37x 2027 earnings versus 26x for exporters,
despite considerably weaker earnings revisions. Domestic AI earnings estimates have been revised
down 8.1%, compared with upward revisions of 22.1% for exporters.
The AI trade may be correcting, but some of the most expensive pockets still have plenty of
expectations embedded in their valuations.

China's software slowdown
China's tech weakness isn't limited to stock prices.
According to Goldman Sachs, Chinese software industry revenue growth slowed to just 6.5% year-over-
year in August, compared with 15% a year earlier.
Growth for the first eight months of 2026 came in at 8.8%, down from 12.6% during the comparable
period last year.
Profitability isn't looking much better. August net income grew just 1% year-over-year, while industry
net margins remained stuck at 11.1%.
Meanwhile, enterprise software spending remains under pressure, with budgets increasingly shifting
toward AI foundational models and customized AI projects.
China's AI ambitions remain enormous, but the broader software industry is struggling to deliver the
growth and profits.
GS
Oversold doesn't mean cheap
After such a brutal reversal, the risk of a sharp countertrend bounce is clearly rising. But with
semiconductor valuations still elevated, profitability lagging global peers and software momentum
deteriorating, the fundamental backdrop offers considerably less comfort.
A bounce is one thing. A sustainable recovery is another.
