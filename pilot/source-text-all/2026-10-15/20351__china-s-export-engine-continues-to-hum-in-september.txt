Exports stronger than execrated again in September
China's exports grew 36.3% year-on-year in September, up from 25.0%, far stronger than
forecasts (market: 25.7%, ING: 24.8%). It was the highest growth since 2021, when data were
heavily affected by base effects from pandemic-related shutdowns in 2020. The 2026 data is
considerably more impressive, building on the solid growth seen in 2025. In the first three
quarters of the year, exports rose 21.3% YoY, totalling $3.37tn. 
By product, this year's strength has been concentrated in the tech and transportation sectors.

In the first three quarters, hi-tech product exports rose 45.6% YoY. Semiconductor exports rose
108.1% YoY, while exports of automatic data processing machines rose 54.6%. In
transportation, auto exports rose 54.3% YoY, while ship exports rose 29.9%.
Higher prices also boosted the value of rare earth (46.6%) and refined fuel (37.6%) exports,
even as volumes fell year-on-year. 
By export destination, exports to the US have continued to recover as the trade truce holds,
surging 39.6% YoY in September and bringing year-to-date export growth to 9.8%. An
extension of the trade truce through January 2027, combined with $30bn in tariff cuts on
products such as toys, Christmas decorations, and kitchenware, should keep the recovery
trajectory intact for the rest of the year. Exports to ASEAN, China's largest export market,
surged 52.3% YoY in September, bringing the year-to-date growth to 28.7%. Exports to the EU
also surged 25.3% YoY in September, hitting a 7-month high and bringing the year-to-date
export growth up to 16.4%. Finally, exports to Korea also hit 68.2% YoY in September, the
highest level since 2021, bringing year-to-date growth to 39.1%.
China's exports have evolved significantly over the past decade, both by product breakdown
and by destination. As China moves up the value-added ladder, its fastest-growing exports
increasingly include higher-margin goods, while low-end manufacturing is moving elsewhere
in the region. Rising trade barriers have sharply reduced the US share of China's total exports
from 19.2% in 2018 to 10.3% through the first three quarters of 2026.

Tech products continue to drive import surge
Import growth accelerated to 31.0% YoY in September, up from 28.2% in August and well
above forecasts (market: 25.0%; ING: 21.7%). This brought first-three-quarter import growth to
$2.43tn, up 27.5% YoY. Compared with early-year expectations, imports have been the biggest
surprise this year. 
Technology products remained the main driver of import growth, suggesting China continues
to spend in the ongoing tech race. High-tech product imports rose 63.9% YoY in September,
bringing year-to-date growth to 48.4%. Imports of automatic data processing machines and
parts surged 296.5% YoY, up from 209.1% in August, lifting year-to-date growth to 124.8%.
Semiconductor imports also remained strong, rising 74.7% YoY in September, bringing year-to-
date growth to 63.5%.
However, strength outside technology remained less convincing. On the crude oil front, China
imported 37.1m tonnes in September, with volumes down -21.5% YoY during the month and
-15.3% in the first nine months of the year, though higher prices led import values to rise a
modest 0.3% YoY year-to-date. Auto imports also fell -38.1% YoY in September, a deeper
contraction than the -32.4% level recorded in August, as Chinese auto sales continue to fall
after years of consumption front-loading driven by trade-in policies. We’re also seeing what

Trade surplus likely to break records again this year
China's trade surplus reached $133.5bn in September, comfortably above expectations, as the
export beat was larger than the import beat (market: 117.8bn, ING: 118.3bn). The September
trade surplus marked a 20-month high.
In the first three quarters of the year, China's trade surplus is now $938.1bn, about 7.8% higher
than the comparable period in 2025. The surge in imports at the start of the year shrank the
trade surplus early on. Yet the subsequent pickup in exports in recent months has set China up
to build on last year's record-breaking $1.2tn trade surplus this year.
Successful trade talks with the US and EU over the past month are likely to keep the
commercial environment relatively stable through the end of the year. But uncertainties may
hang over next year's outlook. As China's growth looks increasingly imbalanced, a slowdown in
the tech capex cycle or new tariff shocks are the key risks to monitor moving forward for the
year ahead.
