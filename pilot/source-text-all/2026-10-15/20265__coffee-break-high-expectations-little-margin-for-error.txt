THE CONTEXT 
 
The 3Q26 earnings season begins this week, with US banks kicking off the reporting cycle. Expectations are elevated. 
Consensus forecasts point to S&P 500 earnings growth of around 30% yoy, while European companies are expected 
to deliver roughly 20% earnings growth in 2H26. In Europe, we focus on the second half rather than 3Q alone, given 
that only around half of the STOXX Europe 600 report quarterly. Technology remains the dominant earnings driver, 
fuelled by strong AI investment and accelerating cloud monetisation. Yet the backdrop has become more challenging. 
Rising UST yields have increased the hurdle for equities and reduced investors’ tolerance for disappointment. Put 
simply, markets are now priced for delivery. The key question this earnings season is not whether profits will grow, 
but whether they will grow fast enough to meet the market’s high expectations.

THE DATA 
By the end of October, firms representing roughly 70% of S&P 500 market capitalisation will 
have reported results, with most hyperscale technology companies scheduled to report in the 
final week of the month. Consensus expects S&P 500 EPS growth of approximately 27% in 3Q, 
compared with around 33% in 2Q. While this implies a moderation in growth, earnings 
momentum remains exceptionally strong by historical standards. AI beneficiaries are expected 
to account for more than half of total index earnings growth, while hyperscaler capital 
expenditure is projected to roughly double yoy. The earnings picture remains highly 
concentrated. Information technology and energy are expected to contribute almost 80% of 
aggregate earnings growth, while the top ten contributors alone are forecast to generate more 
than two-thirds of total S&P 500 profit growth. Nvidia and Micron remain among the most 
significant drivers. At the company level, underlying fundamentals continue to appear 
constructive. Although consensus expects earnings growth for the median S&P 500 company 
to slow from 14% in 2Q to 9% in 3Q, economic activity has remained resilient and upward 
earnings revisions have continued to outnumber downward revisions. Margin expansion may 
become more difficult as input costs remain elevated and the USD provided less of a tailwind 
for large parts of 3Q than in previous quarters. Even so, analysts expect most companies to 
once again exceed consensus forecasts. AI monetisation will remain a key theme. Cloud 
revenue growth among hyperscalers is expected to accelerate further, from 48% in 2Q to 
approximately 55% in 3Q, providing additional evidence that the enormous wave of AI-related 
investment is translating into tangible revenue growth and productivity gains. 
OUR VIEW 
We remain constructive on equities heading into the final quarter of 2026. The key issue is not 
whether earnings will grow, but whether companies can clear a bar that has been set 
increasingly high by investors. On balance, we believe they can. Economic momentum remains 
supportive, earnings revisions have held up well and the AI investment cycle continues to 
generate powerful profit growth, particularly among technology companies and beneficiaries 
of AI infrastructure investment. Recent evidence of accelerating cloud monetisation also 
suggests that the return on AI investments is becoming increasingly visible. Importantly, the 
valuation backdrop has improved. The S&P 500’s 12M forward P/E ratio has declined from 
around 23 at the start of the year to roughly 19 today, bringing valuations back in line with 
long-term averages. This leaves equities in a healthier position to absorb higher bond yields 
than they were earlier this year. The main risk is not weak earnings, but earnings that fail to 
live up to elevated expectations. This risk is amplified by the narrow group of companies 
currently driving both profit growth and market performance. As a result, investors are likely to 
remain highly selective. Nevertheless, if earnings broadly meet expectations and management 
teams continue to demonstrate progress on AI monetisation, productivity gains and underlying 
demand trends, we believe the combination of solid profit growth and more balanced 
valuations can support further equity market gains into year-end. 
OTHER THINGS TO NOTE 
Focus on US CPI 
We expect energy to have fuelled a 0.6% mom increase in headline CPI, half of which was 
probably driven by the price of petrol at the pump, which rose by a further 7% mom. The 
core CPI gauge that excludes food and energy is likely to have been relatively well-behaved, 
rising by 0.2% mom. The yearly rate for headline inflation likely rose to 3.7% from 3.4%, 
while that for core inflation remained at 2.4%.
