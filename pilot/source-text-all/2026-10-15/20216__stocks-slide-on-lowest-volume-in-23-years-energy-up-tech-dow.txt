Energy 
Oil prices are up around 2% from Friday's close at two week highs with a series of Middle East 
attacks outweighing optimism about Trump’s Russian diesel and energy-ceasefire 
announcements (which did prompt a brief dip during EU hours that was swiftly bought). 
 
A lot went down over the last 72 hours:  
●​ Riyadh airport hit (Saudi) - Houthis struck King Khalid International on Saturday, killing 
12 and injuring 300+ (other reports say 13). Saudi authorities also reported one death 
and injuries from a second attack on the airport Sunday evening. This came after earlier 
hits on the airport during the week. Trump said Washington was considering joining 
Saudi strikes on the Houthis. Saudi officials reported more strikes on Monday. 
●​ Tankers struck (Hormuz / Gulf of Oman) - Three tankers were attacked near Oman and 
Hormuz over the weekend, and US forces struck a cargo ship accused of breaking the 
blockade on Iranian ports. This followed an IRGC warning on Friday of 'definitive' 
punishment for vessels anywhere in the region that it hadn’t cleared through Hormuz. At 

 
least 17 tanker attacks have been reported since Sept 28 stalling any hoped for recovery 
in Gulf exports, which had (allegedly) reached 81% of prewar levels in September. 
●​ Russia–Ukraine 'energy ceasefire' and diesel deal - On Friday, Trump announced eased 
sanctions on Russian diesel exports to bring fuel prices down, angering Kiev (saying 
Russia would supply more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the US and international 
markets). Saturday saw Trump call for Ukraine to replace Zelensky and then Sunday, 
Trump posted that an energy ceasefire was "effective immediately". A White House 
official said it covers both sides halting refinery attacks, with a deal on power plants and 
grids possibly to follow. Z said the announcement was news to him. On Monday Russia 
declined to say whether any deal exists. Caveat: Trump made a similar announcement in 
mid-September, and both sides kept striking. 
●​ Russia–Ukraine strikes - Russia launched 116 drones overnight Oct 10–11, causing 
power outages in Kyiv city and region. A high-voltage line in Kiev was damaged. Hours 
after Trump's announcement, Russia hit Kharkiv's CHP-5 thermal plant at least twice, 
starting a fire. Zelensky said on Monday that Russian refineries were not burning 
overnight, but at midnight a Russian jet-powered Shahed hit a Ukrainian gas production 
facility (Z alleges 200+ Russian strikes on Ukrainian power infrastructure and 17 on gas 
and oil sites this week). While no major refinery hits were reported since the truce 
announcement, a drone strike hit Russia's Sochi Airport overnight Oct 10–11 and plus 
Russian data centers continue to be hit.  
And the relatively small rise in oil prices shocked many (though some suggested that market

and 'more of the same' between Saudis and Yemen).

Rates 
With credit markets closed (Columbus Day), futures (low liquidity) were our only guide to where 
bond traders might be moving. 
Higher oil prices, meant higher yields and 10Y Futs signaled only a 2bps rise or so... 

 
 
But, markets were a little more active at the short-end with hawkish comments from Cleveland 
Fed President Beth Hammack, who said she would prefer that the central bank make small 
policy adjustments quickly to rein in inflation rather than wait and risk a bigger course change 
later on. 
"With each passing month, persistently elevated inflation raises the risks of an inflationary 
mindset becoming entrenched in the economy," she said. 
Since the labor market is stable and with signs of strength elsewhere in the economy, now is the 
right time to take on a more restrictive monetary policy stance, Hammack said. 
"Trimming the sails through frequent smaller adjustments reduces the risks of being blown far 
off course, of an inflationary mindset taking hold, and of taking longer to reach the destination," 
Hammack said. 
That pushed SOFR futs to price in a small rise in rate-hike odds (Dec odds back to their recent 
highs)... 

 
 
Before we leave bond-land, we would note (as we detailed earlier) some chatter about 
Bessent's next Treasury put: specifically, Deutsche Bank strategist George Saravelos recently 
returned from visiting clients in New York reporting “mumblings of a coming suspension in 
20-year Treasury issuance.” A Citigroup counterpart is advising to position for the 20-year to

Where Have All The Volumes Gone? 
On Friday, volume in the S&P 500 ETF (SPY) was just 22.6 million shares, the lowest since 
12/24/19 which was a half-day. 
If we only look at full trading days, it was the lowest volume day since 11/25/05, and if we look at 
only days that weren't bordering stock-market holidays, it was the lowest since 10/11/04, which 
coincidentally was also Columbus Day. 
Today ended around 18m shares which is the lowest non-holiday volume since 2003 (the two 
lower days were Thanksgiving 2024 and Thanksgiving 2025). 
 
So the question is, why is volume so low, and does it mean anything? BTIG's chief technical 
market strategist, Jonathan Krinsky, offers the following details (full note available here for pro 
subs) 
Why is Volume so Low? 
The easy answer is the calendar. 
We are in the lull between major macro data/catalysts and earnings season. 
There hasn't been any major new macro news to drive things, and investors are content to see 
the banks start reporting later this week before making any new decisions. 
Broad Volume Not as Low.  

 
While there is often a focus on SPY volume, a more broad-based look at NYSE Composite 
Volume shows a less extreme read, with Friday's volume below its 20-day average, but above 
levels seen in August. 
What Does it Mean? 
It's hard to find anything useful from one-day extremes, especially on the low-volume side. 
Typically, a surge in volume after a decline in any asset is useful for capitulation and looking for 
a contrarian take. We saw that in Software in April, and Semis in late July. 
If we take a 5-DMA of SPY volume it finished today just below 30m. 
 
Since 2005, there have only been three other times when the 5-DMA was below 35m.  
●​ The first was early December 2024. SPY would fall 5% into mid-January, before rallying 
to make a marginal new high. 
●​ The second came after that new high, in mid-February '25. This was the peak before the 
liberation day decline where SPY fell -21%.  
●​ The third was recently in late August '26. SPY saw a modest -2.9% pullback into 
mid-Sep. 
No Volume, No Volatility.  

 
Not only are volumes abnormally low, but so is index level volatility. The SPX hasn't had a -1% 
down day in 52 trading days, tying the streak ending in July '24. If no -1% day tomorrow, it will 
be the longest streak since Jan. 2020 (74 days). 
 
There hasn't been an 80%+ downside volume day on the NYSE in nearly a year, the longest 
streak since at least 1996 by a huge margin.  
Bottom Line. 
The extreme lack of SPY volume is unusual. The lack of index-level volatility has gone on longer 
than we, and many others, would have thought. 
Surging volume and volatility tend to be an 'event'. A lack of volume and volatility are more 
difficult to use as a timing tool. 
That said, each of the prior three low-volume periods (Dec. '24, Feb. '25, Aug. '26) all saw SPY 
weaken in short-order. 
Our sense is this time will be no different, and should expect both to pick up as we enter the 
heart of earnings season.
