DB CoTD: The High Cost of Cheap Intelligence 
 
 
There is an exciting potential path to superintelligence, but getting there will likely be bumpy and dependent 
on a few things falling into place. 
It depends on scaling – throwing more and more compute at the problem – continuing to deliver better and 
better AI, and eventually reaching a tipping point where AI improves itself at an ever more rapid pace, so-
called recursive self-improvement. 
A key factor will be whether the cost of AI continues to plummet at its current breakneck pace, given that 
even the most enthusiastic estimates suggest we are perhaps three orders of magnitude, or 1,000 times, 
away from the kind of computing power necessary to achieve artificial general intelligence (AGI), the human-
level general intelligence seen as a precursor to superior-in-every-field superintelligence. 
The good news is that today’s Chart of the Day shows how AI has become cheaper faster than any other 
transformative technology in history, according to Epoch AI. The cost of achieving a given level of AI 
performance has tumbled about 47 percent a quarter, or 13 times a year, since 2023. That is four times faster 
than DNA sequencing, six times faster than compute, and 18 times faster than lithium batteries, as Epoch 
points out. 

The chart comes from Adrian Cox’s new report written in collaboration with the renowned AI author and 
commentator Azeem Azhar, founder of Exponential View, for the Deutsche Bank Research Institute. “AI 
boom or doom: what will superintelligence feel like and when will we get there?” looks at the pathways to AGI 
and beyond and what it could mean for investors, enterprises and the economy. Click here to read and 
share. 
The question now is whether the pace can be sustained. The past three years have seen historic levels of 
investment exceeding the megaprojects of the past, with estimates suggesting that the US and Chinese 
hyperscalers and neoclouds are set to invest almost $1 trillion this year on AI-related capex. That’s almost 
twice as much as last year, and is set be exceeded again in each of the next few years. 
For that to be justified, the enterprises that are paying up for more and more compute need to be seeing ever 
more bang from their buck for their current AI projects, whether that’s greater employee productivity, more 
efficient workflows or new products and services. 
As Adrian and Azeem’s report shows, even before we reach the considerable theoretical and practical 
hurdles to AGI and superintelligence, the fundamental question is how it will be paid for. 
We live in fascinating times and how this resolves will determine all macro and micro in the years ahead.
