Live entertainment and sports
US equities
 
 Author: Sunny Mehra, Equity Strategist, Consumer Discretionary & Consumer Staples, CIO Americas, UBS Financial Services Inc. (UBS FS)
Live entertainment and sports have emerged as 
standout beneficiaries of the consumer shift toward 
experiences, with spending in both categories continuing 
to outpace overall consumption and now exceeding 
USD 100bn annually domestically. Demand remains 
supported by powerful structural tailwinds, including 
premiumization, 
sponsorship 
growth, 
media-rights 
monetization, destination-event travel, and the growing 
preference among younger consumers for shared, 
memorable experiences over material goods.
 
 
US spending on live entertainment and sports remains 
structurally 
robust, 
reflecting 
consumers’ 
continued 
preference for experiences that are scarce, social, and 
memorable. Over the past several years, spending has 
steadily shifted from goods toward services, with live 
entertainment and spectator sports among the fastest-
growing categories. At the same time, operators have 
expanded monetization opportunities through premium 
seating and hospitality offerings, dynamic pricing strategies, 
sponsorships, and merchandise sales.
Consumer spending on both live entertainment and 
spectator sports continues to grow faster than overall 
personal consumption. As a result, the live entertainment 
ecosystem, including concerts, theater, comedy shows, 
and festivals, has become one of the most resilient and 
attractive segments within the broader consumer economy. 
Combined spending on live entertainment and spectator 
sports in the US now exceeds USD 100bn.
Ticket demand, sponsorship dollars, media-rights values, 
and franchise valuations have all set records over the past 
18 months.

The live entertainment industry is gaining from a durable 
consumer shift toward experiences, the globalization of 
music, improved artist discovery via social media, the unique 
and irreplaceable value of live events, rising sponsorship 
revenue, pricing power, and longer touring cycles.
Spending on live entertainment (excluding sports) has 
roughly doubled since 2015. According to the Bureau of 
Economic Analysis (BEA), spending on Live Entertainment 
now exceeds USD 60bn on an annualized basis, while 
spending on spectator sports exceeds USD 50bn. Spending 
has outpaced overall personal consumption expenditures 
(PCE) over the past decade. Growth rates have been 
impressive and show no signs of slowing.

The growth in the past several quarters has been strong as 
well, with faster growth than overall consumption. In 2Q26, 
growth for live entertainment was up a remarkable 15% 
year over year and over 20% y/y for spectator sports, easily 
outpacing PCE growth of around 6%.

The post-pandemic rebound in live entertainment has 
been both swift and durable. In 2022, concert attendance 
surpassed pre-pandemic levels by 24%, with 121 million 
fans attending more than 43,000 events promoted by Live 
Nation alone. Momentum has continued since then. As 
the dominant player in the industry, Live Nation provides 
perhaps the clearest lens into the sector's trajectory, with 
global revenue rising from USD 16.7bn in 2022 to USD 
25.2bn in 2025. Its domestic-only revenue has almost 
doubled since before COVID-19, reaching 2025 revenue of 
USD 14.3bn versus 2019 revenue of USD 7.6bn.
The broader market remains on a strong growth path. 
US live music ticket sales are expected to generate 
approximately USD 20bn billion of revenue in 2026 
according to Mordor Intelligence, while the global live 
music market is projected to grow from roughly USD 
39bn in 2025 to USD 62bn in 2034 according to Custom 
Market Insights. Growth forecasts remain constructive 
across industry observers, with most forecasters projecting 
US live music revenue to expand at a 6-9% CAGR through 
at least 2031.
Importantly, the consumer base driving demand for live 
entertainment has characteristics that support the sector's 
cyclical resilience. Spending and attendance skew toward 
younger and more affluent consumers, demographics that 
have historically demonstrated greater spending power 
and demand durability. Moreover, a significant portion 
of industry spending is generated by frequent attendees, 
consumers who attend four or more events annually and 
who themselves tend to be younger and higher-income.
Younger consumers, particularly Gen Z and Millennials, 
are increasingly prioritizing concerts and other live events 
as a means of social connection, shared experiences, and 
participation in fan communities. This shift is evident in 
attendance trends: The percentage of Gen Z consumers 
attending at least two concerts annually increased from 
29% in 1Q24 to 37% in 1Q26, according to Luminate 
Intelligence. Spending patterns have strengthened as well, 
with Gen Z live-event attendees spending an average of USD 
101 per month on live music in 1Q26, surpassing Millennials, 
historically the industry's highest-spending demographic. 
As younger consumers continue to favor experiences and 
personal enrichment over material purchases, they are 
becoming an increasingly important driver of long-term 
industry growth.
The supply side of the industry also benefits from powerful 
structural support. As the music business has transitioned 
from physical album sales to streaming-based consumption, 
artists have become increasingly dependent on touring 
and live performances as a primary source of income. The 
decline of CD sales and the economics of streaming have 
shifted monetization toward live events, reinforcing both the 
importance of tours and consumers' willingness to pay for 
them.

This dynamic is reflected in ticket pricing trends. According 
to Pollstar, the top 100 North American tours generated 
USD 1.92bn in gross revenue through mid-year 2026, 
compared with USD 1.55bn during the same period in 
2019. While total gross revenue increased 24%, tickets sold 
declined 6.6%, implying that growth was driven entirely by 
pricing. Average ticket prices rose approximately 33% over 
the period, underscoring the industry's substantial pricing 
power and consumers' continued willingness to absorb 
higher costs for premium live experiences.

Premiumization and destination experiences
Growth in live entertainment is increasingly driven 
by spending intensity rather than attendance growth. 
Consumers are allocating a larger share of discretionary 
budgets to a smaller number of high-priority experiences, 
benefiting marquee concerts, rivalry games, playoffs, 
championship events, and other culturally relevant 
moments. As a result, pricing power is increasingly 
concentrated at the top end of the market, raising the 
importance of premium content, brand equity, and scarcity.
This is evident in the financials of leading operators, where 
revenue growth has generally outpaced attendance growth. 
The revenue opportunity now extends well beyond the 
ticket itself to VIP experiences, premium seating, food and 
beverage, parking, merchandise, sponsorships, and resale 
economics. As a result, operators can continue to grow 
revenue even in a more mature attendance environment.
Premiumization has been a major driver of ticket price 
inflation, particularly for marquee events such as major 
concert tours, the NBA Finals, and the Super Bowl. Dynamic 
pricing, hospitality packages, early-access programs, and 
bundled offerings allow operators to better segment 
demand and capture consumer surplus. The trend is 
supported by the growing purchasing power of higher-
income US consumers, who have shown an increasing 
willingness to spend on unique experiences.
The strongest demand has emerged around bucket-list 
events. These are cultural moments that feel unique, 
memorable, and difficult to replicate, creating a sense of 
urgency and communal participation. Taylor Swift's Eras 
Tour, the FIFA World Cup, and major theatrical releases such 
as The Odyssey in IMAX are recent examples. In sports, 
championship runs by local teams can have a similar effect, 
as evidenced by record-setting ticket prices for New York 
Knicks home games during the 2026 NBA Finals.
A related trend is the rise of the destination event, where 
the ticket becomes just one component of a larger travel 
experience. Fans often pair major events with spending 
on airfare, lodging, restaurants, transportation, retail, and 
entertainment, amplifying the economic impact for host 
cities. This dynamic is particularly relevant for playoff games, 
festivals, concert tours, and major international sporting 
events.
Taylor Swift's Eras Tour demonstrated the power of this 
effect. Visit Cincy estimated roughly USD 48mn of economic 
activity associated with the tour's Cincinnati stop, while the 
Federal Reserve's June 2023 Beige Book highlighted Taylor 
Swift concerts as a key factor behind Philadelphia's strongest 
hotel revenue month since the pandemic.
Major sporting events have produced similar outcomes. 
During the World Cup, Visa reported nearly 20% year-over-
year growth in cross-border transaction volume across host 
cities. Bank of America Institute found that card spending 
in US host cities increased roughly 5% y/y during the 
tournament, with spending by non-local visitors rising more 
than 17%. Restaurants and bars were among the largest 
beneficiaries, underscoring how destination events generate 
economic value well beyond the venue itself.
Broadway and live comedy
Even beyond the one-off marquee type events, overall 
pricing for live entertainment has steadily increased. The 
average paid admission into a Broadway show has steadily 
risen over the past decade and is now over USD 130. The 
total Gross for Broadway shows is up 39% from a decade 
ago and at all-time highs.

Live comedy has emerged as a fast-growing segment 
within the broader live entertainment industry, offering 
customers an increasingly compelling alternative to 
traditional concerts and sporting events. The industry has 
evolved from a predominantly club-based format into a 
large-scale live entertainment category, with comedians 
filling theaters, amphitheaters, and arenas.
Industry activity has accelerated with annual comedy 
performances exceeding 6,000 events in each of 2023, 
2024, and 2025 (using Pollstar’s chart years mid-November 
to mid-November), compared to approximately 3,000 
events annually during the three years preceding COVID-19. 
Attendance trends have followed a similar trajectory, with 
annual ticket sales averaging 12.8 million over this post-
pandemic period versus just over 7 million in the three years 
prior. Nate Bargatze’s "Big Dumb Eyes" tour grossed over 
USD 77mn in 2025 alone and set the record for the most 
tickets sold for a comedy tour as it continued this year.
Beyond live performances, comedians have increasingly 
leveraged digital distribution channels to expand audience 
reach and enhance monetization opportunities. High-
profile comedy specials distributed through streaming 
platforms such as Netflix, coupled with short-form content 
across social media platforms, have broadened consumer 
engagement and strengthened artist discoverability. This 
growing intersection of digital media and live entertainment 
creates a virtuous cycle in which online exposure drives 
ticket demand, while live performances generate content 
that further expands audience reach.

Venue investment
Operators are continuing to invest heavily in amphitheaters, 
clubs, theaters, and destination venues, upgrading 
sight 
lines, 
concessions, 
hospitality 
offerings, 
and 
sponsorship inventory to enhance the fan experience 
and drive incremental revenue. Live Nation’s 2025 capital 
expenditures surpassed USD 1bn, more than double 2023 
levels. While these investments can boost attendance, 
per-capita spending, and venue utilization, they also 
increase fixed-cost exposure, making booking quality, local 
permitting, and construction execution more critical to 
returns.
Las Vegas’ Sphere has emerged as one of the most ambitious 
venue developments in live entertainment, combining large-
scale concerts with immersive media experiences. Built at 
a cost of more than USD 2bn, the venue generated USD 
781mn of revenue in 2025, supported by programming 
such as Eagles' residencies and its 4D presentation of
The Wizard of Oz. Momentum has remained strong, 
with first-half 2026 revenue rising 48% y/y. Encouraged 
by its performance, Sphere Entertainment is evaluating 
opportunities to replicate the concept in additional markets.
Culture
Several broader cultural shifts are also contributing to this 
trend. The rise of streaming has led to content saturation 
and media fragmentation, meaning audiences are no longer 
watching the same shows and movies at the same time. 
Algorithmic personalization and the growth of niche online 
communities have further widened the range of cultural 
experiences, creating a landscape that is richer and more 
diverse but less universally shared. As a result, there are 
fewer common cultural touchstones that bring large groups 
of people together.

Against that backdrop, many people are increasingly seeking 
shared experiences. Particularly in a period marked by 
social and political polarization, there is strong appeal in 
participating in events that create a sense of collective 
connection, whether that is cheering alongside others at a 
major concert or World Cup match, or gathering in theaters 
for highly anticipated films such as The Odyssey. Nostalgia 
may also be playing a role, as people gravitate toward 
experiences that feel communal and familiar. More broadly, 
growing fatigue with screens and the pervasive role of 
technology in everyday life may be encouraging consumers 
to seek out real-world, in-person experiences.
We have seen this in the recent stock price performance 
among the media companies. Netflix has dramatically 
underperformed IMAX over the past year.

Conclusion
The appeal of live, immersive experiences extends well 
beyond attendance itself. Increasingly, these events function 
as social currency, providing younger consumers with visible 
signals of status, taste, and cultural relevance that can 
be amplified across social media. The value proposition 
is therefore both experiential and expressive, helping to 
reinforce demand even amid broader shifts in consumer 
behavior.
The category is also relatively insulated from many of 
the disruptive forces associated with the AI cycle. Unlike 
digital content, live sports and entertainment derive their 
value from scarcity, authenticity, and shared real-time 
participation, making them inherently difficult to replicate 
or displace. This disruption-resistant profile is attractive 
from an investment perspective and may offer a useful 
counterbalance to portfolios with significant exposure to AI-
related themes.
As we highlighted in our Sports report, substantial private 
capital is flowing into the sports ecosystem, reflecting 
growing investor conviction in the asset class despite a 
limited set of public market opportunities. As a result, 
private and alternative investments remain the most direct 
way to gain exposure to many of the sector's strongest 
secular growth drivers.
More broadly, we remain constructive on the long-
term outlook for US live entertainment and sports. 
We expect the category to continue outpacing overall 
household spending over the medium term, supported 
by pricing power, premiumization, sponsorship growth, 
media monetization, and related travel expenditures. In 
an increasingly fragmented and digitally distracted world, 
consumers continue to place a premium on experiences 
that offer immediacy, emotional engagement, and a sense 
of communal participation. As spending continues to shift 
from goods toward services, and from possessions toward 
experiences, live entertainment and sports remain well 
positioned to benefit from these durable consumer trends.
