October 2026
U.S. midterm elections are rapidly approaching,  
with the latest polls pointing to a divided government, 
with Democrats gaining control of at least one house  
of Congress. 
So, what does this mean for investors? Depending on 
the outcome, market performance may be shaped by 
shifts in policy, sector-specific opportunities, and the 
governing landscape.
Scenarios for Congressional Control
A unified Democratic Congress, especially with larger margins of victory, could 
produce more aggressive investigations and funding constraints on the Trump 
administration agenda, creating more market uncertainty. Legislation to limit 
executive actions like tariffs or advance Democratic priorities will face frequent 
Trump vetoes, though this could elevate risks of debt-ceiling and budget 
showdowns as leverage for compromise. A split Congress with Republicans 
holding the Senate would likely see a more constrained legislative environment– 
generally positive for markets in limiting change, even as budget shutdown  
risks persist.
A Republican sweep would likely be read initially as supportive for risk assets, 
reinforcing President Trump’s agenda on deregulation, fossil fuels, and the 
artificial intelligence (AI) buildout. However, a Republican Congress could add 
the most pressure on bond markets, with greater fiscal supply risk from Trump’s 
spending and tax priorities and limited prospects for meaningful budget cuts.
In our view, defense, energy, healthcare, and crypto sectors could see the biggest 
shift in outcomes. While a Democratic Congress is unlikely to cut defense 
spending, any supplemental funds and increases regarding the Iran conflict 
will face much greater scrutiny. A Republican sweep would reinforce fossil fuel 
development and power infrastructure expansion tied to AI, while a Democratic 
Congress could restore some support to solar and wind power.

Referendum on AI
This year marks the first election shaped by the AI backlash, or “techlash,” with 
polls showing strong public concern over both the data center buildout and 
the potential disruption from rapidly improving AI models. Given AI’s centrality 
across markets, any policy efforts to slow it could have significant impact. 
While AI concerns feature prominently in key Senate races, opposition from 
President Trump is likely to limit major federal regulation–making state-level 
action the key area to watch.

Democrats have signaled plans to expand oversight of AI development and 
deployment, with a new House Select Committee. With no federal guidelines 
or legislation and a lack of votes for federal “pre-emption” legislation, states 
may take the lead instead–similar to the historical role of California in driving 
national fuel efficiency standards.
Fiscal Pressures to Persist
With long-end yields under persistent pressure, fiscal policy will be key to watch 
in 2027. Despite market concerns, we see little chance of fiscal consolidation 
after the midterms, while at least one government shutdown, potentially paired 
with another debt-ceiling standoff, is likely. 
The U.S. fiscal deficit is set to stay near or above 6% of GDP in 2027 and 2028, 
with mandatory spending and interest accounting for almost 75% of federal 
outlays.1 Based on past experience, divided government tends to produce 
modest fiscal deterioration rather than consolidation, as the two parties 
eventually compromise on competing priorities. 
Midterms have often catalyzed budget and debt standoffs between the 
executive branch and Congress, as evidenced by past shutdowns and standoffs.

The current continuing resolution expires December 11, 2026, requiring a  
further stopgap into the new year, and providing a last opportunity for the 
outgoing Republican Congress to raise the debt ceiling and pursue spending 
priorities unilaterally. 2 
If debt ceiling action is deferred to 2027, Democrats could seek concessions 
such as extending ACA subsidies or preserving clean energy incentives.
For investors, fiscal standoffs could add pressure and volatility to long-end 
yields. A government shutdown may also revive data disruptions for key series 
(delayed Consumer Price Index data, payrolls, etc.), just as investors and the 
Federal Reserve try to gauge where the economy sits in the cycle.
America-First Trade Policies: Reshoring a Long-Term Theme
Trump administration tariffs look set to persist, with Section 301 putting a firmer 
floor under tariff levels, alongside continued support for U.S. manufacturing 
reshoring through tax incentives and industrial policy.3  
Democratic legislation in the new Congress may signal the party’s stance 
on any future tariff adjustments. Democrats appear inclined to protect key 
manufacturing sectors while reducing tariffs on certain consumer goods.
Against this backdrop, reshoring should remain a durable investment theme 
regardless of the midterm outcome. Advanced manufacturing areas such 
as semiconductors, AI-linked infrastructure, pharmaceuticals, aerospace, 
machinery, and grid equipment are showing strong momentum, with a broader 
range of U.S. industrials enjoying sustained tailwinds.
