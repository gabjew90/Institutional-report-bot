It could spin on a dime after US CPI, but for now, global markets are in a generally good 
place to start the trading day. Stocks are up from about ¼% in N.A. futures to as much as 
¾% across Europe. Sovereign bond yields are under mild downward pressure. FX land is 
little changed relative to the USD. Oil prices are up by around 1%. 
Today’s three main focal points include a) Macklem comments overnight, b) US CPI, and 
c) US bank earnings. We might also start to get very preliminary advance polling turnout 
(not results) from Alberta’s week-long referenda. 
NEW RATES FORECASTS 
We have issued fresh rates forecasts for Canada and the US. They are shown in the 
accompanying table and charts 1–2 and represent the Scotiabank Economics house view. 
BoC: We’re forecasting a hike this quarter as soon as the October 28th decision or in 
December followed by 75bps of additional increases totalling 100bps. The terminal rate is 
projected to be 3.25% which is 25bps higher than previously forecast but as previously 
guided in presentations and client meetings. That would still leave policy barely within or 
slightly outside of the nominal neutral rate range with a real policy rate that turns 
meaningfully positive. Greater upside growth potential has been moderated by further 
forecast policy tightening. 
FOMC: We’re forecasting two more rate hikes to a fed funds target rate of 4.5% including 
one more increase likely in December followed by one more hike in 2027Q1 and then flat 
at the terminal rate over the rest of 2027. Call it reinsurance on the Fed’s insurance hikes 
because raising the policy rate by just 25-50bps would accomplish very little in terms of 
the lagging effects on growth and inflation. We view 4.5% as meaningfully restrictive and a 
policy overshoot that we take back as soon as late next year or starting in 2028.

BOC’S MACKLEM DIDN’T SAY MUCH 
BoC Governor Macklem’s comments during his short fireside chat at the IIF meeting in Bangkok a few hours ago were kind of a dud. 
He said nothing about monetary policy. Nothing about the job market. He just commented on stretched equity valuations (see my weekly 
for various measures) and that every central bank is worried about the Middle East conflict and price pass through. A full transcript is 
unavailable but a video link is to be made available. 
Macklem speaks again on Friday. Today was a more open Q&A. Friday’s appearance is supposed to be more about AI. On that issue, 
Canada is seeing explosive investments in data centers now with about 300 of them which is about 6% of the total number in the US and 
slightly below what would be proportionate in terms of relative market sizes (). It’s going on nationwide, but Alberta is likely to lead going 
forward. 
 
Macklem’s appearance will disappoint anyone expecting any updated remarks since the hawkish September 2nd press conference and his 
September 21st presser when he sounded hawkish on inflation, dovish on Q4 GDP growth and skipped right over Q3 resilience.  
What is that new information? Canadian core inflation measures were quite warm on September 14th. Or consider GDP that’s tracking 2% in 
Q3 after the August 0.2% m/m ‘flash’ estimate last month. Hours worked were up sharply in Q3 as more hours substituted for fewer bodies. 
Note that the BoC released a short note on demographics and curtailed labour supply the day before the job numbers. Or note the 
resumption of energy market tensions since the last decision, including higher WTI and especially higher Brent that affects the east coast 
projects. Macklem won’t reveal the results this week, but he will probably also have in his head the outcome of the latest Q3 business and 
consumer surveys that will probably follow the CFIB measure of inflation expectations higher. He’s a tad cagey so the balance he strikes 
between all of these readings isn’t certain but if I were him, I wouldn’t spend two seconds on the jobs reading as argued in three separate 
publications and would instead focus instead on GDP, closing slack, inflation tracking and inflation risk.

core respectively. See my weekly for more of an explanation of the drivers. Key may be whether core services inflation repeats (chart 3).

CHINA TRADE MUSHROOMED 
China’s trade figures blossomed in September (chart 5). Exports were up 36% y/y in dollar 
terms (consensus 26%) and 30% in yuan terms. Imports were up 31% y/y (consensus 25%) and 
25% in yuan terms. The surplus with the US increased and has been reversing the narrowing 
pattern that unfolded during the early part of Trump 2.0 (chart 6). 
The PBOC continues to fail to achieve its inflation goal with CPI landing at 0.9% y/y in 
September and core CPI at just 1%. This is one central bank that couldn’t care less about its 
inflation target as it is serially too high relative to reality (chart 7).
