MIDDLE EAST WEEKLY TRACKER 
 
The Houthis’ attacks on Saudi push any 
type of peace deal even further 
Conflict update:  
Talks stalled while the war spilled onto Saudi soil. On 8 October, Trump 
said the US would not attack Iran before the 3 November midterms, 
citing productive discussions. Iran kept up the pressure at sea, UKMTO 
recorded 12 attacks on commercial vessels around Hormuz between 5 
and 11 October. On 10 October, Saudi Arabia shut Riyadh’s King Khalid 
airport after repeated Houthi strikes. Trump said the US may join strikes 
on the Houthis but has not decided, having turned down two 
Saudi requests for US strikes last month. On 11 October, Iran’s security 
chief Rezaei called the US reply to Tehran’s conditions insufficient and 
said that Iran is no longer responding until the US clarifies. No 
agreement has been announced. 
Oil: 
Brent futures jumped 3% to USD104/bbl on 8 October as Iran stepped 
up tanker attacks, then rose to around USD107/bbl on 12 October after 
weekend attacks on Saudi airports and a hurricane shut down a 
quarter of US Gulf of Mexico output. 
CDS:  
Bahrain’s CDS rose higher and its bond spread over US Treasuries 
widened to 365bp in the week to 12 October.  
Stock/other markets:  
GCC equities fell, led by Saudi Arabia and Qatar. Saudi Arabia’s main 
stock index dropped over 6% in the past month as the war moved onto 
its capital. 
Investment flows:  
 
Foreign investors sold a net USD419mn of Saudi and Dubai stocks in 
the week to 11 October.  
Real economy:   
The war has spilled over into Saudi Arabia’s services sector. Riyadh’s 
King Khalid airport, the kingdom’s second busiest gateway, has been 
shut since 10 October, hitting business travel, tourism and air cargo.
