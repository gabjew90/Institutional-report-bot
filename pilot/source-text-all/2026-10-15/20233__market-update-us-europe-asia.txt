U.S. Markets Recap
US -0.02% | Nasdaq -0.15% | Dow -0.35%
Synopsis

US equities finished mostly lower in Tuesday trading, though the downside was very limited and S&P gainers outnumbered decliners.
Semiconductors and memory were mostly weaker again, with the SOX down five straight sessions, the longest streak since July. Other
laggards included banks, private equity, networking and communications, multi-industry names, pharma and biotech (JNJ-US), airlines,
aerospace and defense, department stores, quantum computing and space. Big tech was mostly higher, with META-US having another
strong day, and other outperformers included energy, P&C insurers, payments, chemicals, homebuilders and housing-related retail, auto
suppliers, casual diners, telecom and media. Treasuries were mixed with some curve flattening, and long-end yields were off 2-4 bp. The
dollar index was down 0.4%, gold finished down 0.3%, silver was off 1.3%, bitcoin futures were up 0.4% and WTI crude settled up 0.4%,
reversing morning weakness. It was another rangebound day for the S&P, with a range of about 28 points. Pressure across the AI complex
was broad, particularly on semicap and memory names, and sentiment remained fragile, with analyst comment focused on debt and
equity issuance alongside longstanding worries such as IPO delays, China competition and monetization challenges. The market also

Macro

Another day of pressure on the AI trade: Semiconductors and memory came under continued pressure, following Monday's fourth straight
decline for the SOX, the worst streak since July. There was little new in the headlines, but comments from AVGO-US CEO Hock Tan on
funding constraints and investors' extreme expectations (Reuters) exacerbated longstanding sector worries, including open-source and
open-weight model competition, the flood of debt and equity issuance, the parade of new frontier models, lingering security concerns,
monetization struggles and the scramble for compute capacity. On Monday the Intel and Broadcom CEOs also warned that financing and
cost of capital may emerge as a significant bottleneck on the AI boom (CNBC), against a backdrop of rising AI-related debt issuance,
widening CDS spreads and higher cost of capital.

Fed's Williams expects one more rate hike this year, sees no case for further tightening in 2027: Speaking to Bloomberg, New York Fed
President Williams said he continues to anticipate one additional rate hike in 2026 and sees no current reason for further tightening
thereafter, with inflation still showing signs of slowing significantly toward the 2% target next year, though policy remains data dependent.
Governor Waller said higher Treasury yields are helping to cool demand in interest-sensitive sectors, doing a bit of the work of rate hikes,
and Richmond's Barkin said taking measures to address inflation is reasonable. The market is pricing about a 22% chance of an October
hike and 27 bp of tightening through year-end, near the lower end of the post-September FOMC range. On data, September NFIB small-
business optimism dipped to 97.9 from prior month's 98.7, with owners cautious amid rising costs, higher rates and persistent hiring
challenges. The ADP weekly private-payroll estimate came in at 24.75K jobs/wk for four weeks through 26-Sep, a slight deceleration from
the prior week. September existing home sales came in at 3.990M, ahead of the 3.960M consensus and up from August's downwardly
revised 3.930M (from 3.980M), the first monthly gain after three consecutive declines, though August and September were the two weak-
est months of the year; the median existing-home price was $417,500, up 1.3% y/y. September core CPI is due Wednesday at 8:30 ET,
with consensus at 0.2% m/m (from 0.3% in August) and 2.5% y/y. Goldman Sachs sees rebounds in used vehicles and airfares, BofA
expects shelter and lodging to offset broader disinflation, and JPMorgan is a dovish outlier at +0.18% m/m; BofA noted an in-line print is
unlikely to shift market pricing toward further tightening, while a hotter print could revive hawkish pushback at the October meeting.

Banks mixed post-earnings as constructive NII, expense outlooks offset some headline misses: Moneycenter banks and investment banks
kicked off Q3 earnings season with performance divergences. Goldman and Citi had standout prints, in contrast with top- and bottom-
line misses at JPMorgan and lighter Wells Fargo revenues, but guidance leaned constructive, with favorable revisions to NII (notably JPM),
lending margins (notably C) and expense trajectories (C, JPM). Equity trading was a bright spot for Goldman and JPMorgan, helping offset
widespread FICC softness, and Citigroup's fixed-income traders reported their best Q3 in at least a decade (Bloomberg). Consumer health
remained solid across credit metrics and card spend, although CFO commentary highlighted borrowing caution across middle-market
corporates facing persistent input cost inflation. Financing of AI infrastructure emerged as a key theme: Goldman and Citi noted stronger
activity in public and private debt for tech issuers, while Wells and JPMorgan flagged institutional risk limits around power constraints
and potential capital crowding-out. Goldman Sachs CEO Solomon dismissed rumors of an imminent leadership transition (Bloomberg).

Iran reportedly prepping for renewed conflict as diplomatic efforts stall: Progress on Iran-US diplomacy remains elusive after an overnight
report said Iran is preparing for potential renewed US strikes following the midterm elections. Satellite imagery shows efforts to strengthen
nuclear sites, some Iranian officials have raised the possibility of land incursions into neighboring countries along with attacks on Saudi
oil facilities, the UAE and regional data centers and internet cables, and Tehran has stepped up attacks on commercial vessels around
Hormuz, with at least three ships hit over the weekend. Kpler data showed confirmed Hormuz flows plunging to 2M bpd on Saturday from
22.8M on Thursday, while seven-day flows averaged 10.5M bpd, about 38% below the prewar baseline. Saudi-Houthi tensions escalated
further on Tuesday after Saudi Arabia ordered civilians to evacuate a key oil facility in Yemen's capital ahead of a military operation, with
initial reports of an explosion in the area; the Houthis declared Saudi Aramco headquarters and facilities legitimate targets, following
deadly Houthi strikes on Riyadh's airport over the weekend. Four tankers were struck Saturday and more than a dozen supertankers
reportedly turned around near the Hormuz entrance, though there is no clear evidence that broader oil loadings are slowing (Bloomberg).
A UK intelligence chief warned that Iran is shifting overseas operations toward harder targets such as military facilities. Brent climbed
back above $106/barrel (Bloomberg), Saudi Arabia told OPEC its oil output has recovered, with production jumping by a third last month
(Reuters), and Guyana's crude oil exports are set to surpass 1M bpd for the first time (Bloomberg).

BofA fund manager survey and earnings season: BofA's October Global Fund Manager Survey showed investors remain broadly bullish,
with cash levels at 3.9% and the Bull & Bear Indicator at 8.1, both in contrarian "sell signal" territory. The broader sentiment gauge slipped
to its least bullish since Jun-26, though expectations for an economic "boom" surged to a net 47%, the highest since Feb-22, and global
inflation expectations dropped to the lowest since Jan-25. Investors trimmed US equity exposure, led by banks and consumer discretion-
ary (most underweight since Jul-08), while technology allocation is the highest since Aug-20 with 37% of managers overweight; long
semiconductors remained the most crowded trade, and a disorderly rise in bond yields was again cited as the primary tail risk. Respond-
ents expecting AI hyperscalers to cut capex more than doubled m/m to 30%. According to FactSet, S&P 500 earnings growth is expected
at +29.6% y/y in Q3, better than the +26.7% expected at the start of the quarter, which would mark the eighth straight quarter of double-

Synopsis

European equity markets pared session gains to finish little lower on Tuesday, following positive performance on Monday. Technology,
industrials and miners were the biggest laggards, while chemicals, media, insurance and real estate were the main gainers. Europe held
up better through most of the day on a rate reprieve and stabilization, although tech was under pressure and the broader market later
tracked weakness in US equities. European government bonds were little changed, erasing earlier gains, with 10-year yields edging higher
across the board: French OATs up 0.2bp to 4.838%, Bunds up 0.2bp to 3.465% and UK 10-year Gilt yields up 0.4bp to 5.450%. The dollar
index was down 0.4%, gold was flat, silver was off 1.3%, bitcoin futures were up 0.9% and WTI crude was up 0.4%, reversing morning
weakness. In macro data, German September inflation was higher on surging energy costs, while French business uncertainty jumped in
September on concerns about fuel prices and a deterioration of the economy. The IMF cautioned the UK to continue fiscal consolidation
and avoid unleashing cost-of-living support measures, the latest consumer surveys showed UK shoppers remain cautious as fuel costs
squeeze household budgets, and BoE's Greene said interest rates should be higher as UK growth and jobs data show policy is not restric-
tive enough.
Macro

Tech, miners, luxury the big laggards; chemicals, health and insurance gain: Technology was the biggest laggard, tracking the global chip
and memory selloff, with European semiconductors sliding and broader concerns about the sustainability of AI investment also weighing;
Nokia (NOKIA-FI) traded lower after agreeing to sell its Enterprise Campus Edge business to private equity group Aurelius. Basic Resources
also traded lower as copper and aluminum prices retreated on growing concerns about massive AI buildout spending. Oil & Gas was higher
but still underperformed the broader market amid lower crude prices, the prospect of additional Russian diesel supplies and ongoing
Middle East conflict risk, with BP (BP-GB) higher after an operational update highlighting near-full refinery utilization, high capture rates
and significantly higher gas trading, although it warned impairments may reach $4.5B. Luxury stocks drove Personal & Household Goods
lower, with LVMH (MC-FR) lower after Q3 organic growth of 1% missed expectations, while Autos & Parts posted a modest gain following
an EU-China hybrid car import deal and Stellantis (STLAM-IT) reported Q3 shipments down to 1.3M as a North American slump offset
European EV growth. Chemicals were the top gainer, led by Givaudan (GIVN-CH) after Q3 sales and LFL growth comfortably beat
expectations, with Fragrance & Beauty growth of 13.5% particularly strong and analysts seeing scope for upgrades to FY growth and
margin forecasts. Health Care also advanced, with Roche (ROP-CH) higher after an agreement with Rona Therapeutics to develop an
obesity treatment for up to $1.16B, and Saniona (SANION-SE) rallied on positive preclinical findings for its potential rapid-acting
antidepressant SAN2465. Insurance benefited from defensive inflows and easing bond yields alongside Real Estate, with Swiss Re (SREN-
CH) higher despite a Vontobel downgrade to hold that flagged a softening property and casualty reinsurance cycle, potential margin
compression and higher combined ratios after a share price advance of nearly 30% since June. Utilities were stronger alongside other
defensive sectors such as Food & Beverage on easing bond yields.

French bonds stabilize but fiscal concerns keep spreads elevated: European government bonds have stabilized to a fair degree this week,
a process that began in the latter half of last week, even as concerns about French debt remain elevated amid deteriorating public
finances, political gridlock and fragmentation before the 2027 presidential election, and economic weakness (Fitch Ratings). The French
10-year yield reached its highest since 2002 at 4.9% in early October and the OAT-Bund spread exceeded 140 bps for the first time since
late 2011, but both have stabilized since, with the 10-year French yield down 6.8 bps to ~4.831% versus 4.899% at the previous session
close; easing oil prices after Trump's Russian diesel deal are seen as driving some of the reprieve. Stabilization also followed the formal
budget submission and fiscal commitments, alongside signaling from the finance ministry of a plan to skew auctions to shorter-term debt.
Goldman Sachs said the European bond selloff has become more structural, with stronger economic data and higher energy prices lifting
estimates of the neutral interest rate, and identified lower oil prices, a dovish ECB shift or a credible post-election fiscal plan as potential
catalysts for spread stabilization, although each faces significant obstacles; the firm remains neutral on French rates, citing improved
relative value but considerable uncertainty, with fiscal clarity unlikely before the 2027 election. Morningstar noted French spreads remain
vulnerable to further widening and that the European government bond market will continue to struggle in Q4, according to fund managers
and strategists it surveyed, with France the main driving force behind moves.

Central banks and UK fiscal policy: Focus this week remains on volatility in the bond market, with a lot of central bank commentary to
digest as the annual IMF and World Bank meetings run through the week. Recent comments suggest the ECB is increasingly likely to leave
policy on hold in October given the recent tightening in financial conditions, while the BoE seems prepared to implement a precautionary
rate hike in November with inflation picking up and energy prices still elevated. It is a relatively light week for Eurozone data, with some
attention on Eurozone industrial production (Thursday) and final Eurozone CPI (Friday), where analysts anticipate a soft reading after
weakness in German hard data. In the UK, BRC retail sales (Tuesday) give the latest update on September consumer spending and monthly
activity data (Thursday) are forecast to show a slowdown in August GDP after an upside surprise in July. Ahead of the 28-Oct Budget,
weekend press said Chancellor Healey is due to unveil time-limited support for energy costs this winter that will not affect the fiscal target
because most will expire before the main budget rule bites in 2029-30, and that he has ruled out lowering the mansion house tax to
£1.5M, while some reports highlight nervousness within the Labour Party that the Budget may lack ambition. Healey also rejected calls
by senior Labour figures to raise capital gains tax to the same level as income tax (FT), easing concerns that such a move would damage
Britain's attractiveness to investors and entrepreneurs; capital gains tax is currently 24% for higher-rate taxpayers against up to 45% for
income tax. Private-client advisers expect narrower measures rather than full alignment, and the Treasury is examining targeted reliefs

European equity optimism turns selective as growth hopes fade and France hits record underweight: The Bank of America Fund Manager
Survey suggested European fund managers are more constructive on the near-term equity outlook, with a net 44% seeing equities higher
over the next one to three months versus 39% previously and a net 94% expecting Europe to avoid recession, up from 86%; a net 41%
expect cyclicals to outperform defensives, the highest reading since Feb. Over a 12-month horizon, conviction weakened: expected equity
upside fell to 4.5%, the lowest since Jan-2025, net expectations of stronger European growth halved to 22%, and a net 44% expect a
higher ECB deposit rate in 12 months. Global investors are moving the other way, with a net 23% planning to underweight Europe in global
allocation, the most negative reading since Sep-2022. Within Europe, positioning is highly selective: Spain is the most favored market at
a net 28% overweight, the highest since May-2018, while France reached a record net 56% underweight, with 63% citing fiscal
sustainability and the 2027 election cycle as the largest political risk. The LVMH print is seen as negative for soft-luxury: its Fashion &
Leather Goods division fell 5% on a ~15% drop in Chinese customer spending amid a tax crackdown, weaker sentiment and some brand-
specific issues at Louis Vuitton (CNBC). Deutsche Bank said the miss, even against soft expectations, points to further low-to-mid-single-
digit EPS downgrades for H2 and 2027, though at a near-record-low valuation of ~15x calendar-2027 earnings the stock offers
asymmetric upside if the slowdown proves cyclical rather than structural. Read-across is seen as negative for KER-FR, RMS-FR and BRBY-
GB, with CFR-CH relatively less affected, while jewellery names are seen as a relative offset and Wines & Spirits performance offers a
modest positive read-across for cognac-exposed names.

Europe's gas storage filling faster than feared, but winter tightness still looms: Europe faces the prospect of a double energy crisis this
winter, with benchmark gas prices roughly tripled YTD (Eurointelligence) and underground storage ~15% below the five-year average. RBC
said the two decisive variables are winter weather and Qatari LNG volumes, with a base case of just under 20% full by end-March versus
last winter's 28%. Citi offered a more constructive counterpoint, noting pan-European storage reached 73.1% by 7-Oct versus
expectations of not reaching 70% before winter, and maintains a Q4 TTF base case of €56/MWh, more than 30% below the current ~€84
level; with Qatar shipping less than a quarter of prewar LNG volumes, however, Europe must still compete globally for cargoes. On Russia-
Ukraine, there is no diplomatic progress and the energy ceasefire shows little sign of holding, with Zelensky saying Russia struck at least
six Ukrainian energy targets yesterday (BBC). Separately, an EU anti-dumping investigation into PVC, cellulose ethers and salicylic acid
(Argus, Ineos) opens a possible 2027 catalyst window for the chemicals sector, with Jefferies noting European tyre stocks rose 34% during
the investigation period in the tyre precedent; provisional measures are typically expected around Apr-May and a final decision by Nov.
ING noted the Iran war hit European chemicals less severely than feared as Asian competitors faced feedstock and energy disruptions,
but analysts broadly expect that respite to fade once Gulf supplies to Asia normalize. PVC is the largest of the three cases, with ECR-ES
and ORBIA-MX among listed producers with European exposure and BAYN-DE the most relevant name in cellulose ethers and
acetylsalicylic acid.

Synopsis

Asian markets pared early losses to finish mixed. The Kospi and Nikkei 225 still ended sharply lower but well off their respective troughs,
the Taiex was also down by around 2% and Shenzhen finished lower, while Shanghai, Hong Kong, India and Singapore gained and Aus-
tralia's ASX was back at the flatline. The US dollar was lower, the yen rallied and the won strengthened on hawkish comments from South
Korea's President. JGB yields edged higher, precious metals were higher, base metals pared some of their overnight losses and crypto-
currencies were higher. Technology stocks came under pressure again on Tuesday. The selloff began in Seoul, where Samsung Electronics
(005930-KR) and SK Hynix (000660-KR) both gapped lower from the open, while Kioxia (285A-JP) fell sharply in Japan on reports late
Monday that China's CXMT (688825-CN) was near to perfecting a server-memory product that could challenge its South Korean rivals.
Stocks rallied in the afternoon. In regional developments, Japan PM Takaichi said Tokyo will continue to communicate with the US to
maintain stable forex, and BOJ governor Ueda said inflation is very close to the 2% target and it was important to keep it there. Australia
business sentiment fell again in September just as RBA meeting minutes revealed members indicated further rate hikes were possible if
inflation persisted, and late Monday India inflation rose again to reinforce the RBI's hawkish tone last week. Omron (6645-JP) is to buy
US-based industrial PC firm Onlogic (0TC2LL-E-US) and its parent company Interlogic for $675M. NTT's (9432-JP) data unit is said to be

its Europe-based production to comply with new EU local content rules. Shanghai International Port Group (600018-CN) is in talks to buy
an LME-approved warehouse firm, H&M Metal Warehousing. Data center operator AirTrunk (0G9J9F-E) is to delay its IPO in Singapore
until next year amid market volatility and higher interest rates. Air New Zealand (AIR-NZ) has applied to various regulators to extend its
codeshare partnership with Virgin Australia (VGN-AU) into a broader reciprocal relationship.
Macro

Korean equities continue to face heavy selling pressure: The memory selloff continued, dragging the Kospi to its lowest since late July
and extending its week-to-date decline to 10%. The selloff triggered a downside sidecar after the index fell 5%, and was again driven
principally by Samsung Electronics (005930-KR) and SK Hynix (000660-KR), both of which hit two-month lows. The competitive threat
from China remained an early-week focus after CXMT (688825-CN) claimed progress on a '4F square' design that can enable higher
memory storage density. The role of foreign selling was also a discussion point, with the Kospi's global outflow streak extending to six
days; South Korean equities have experienced the largest foreign outflows among major Asian benchmarks this year, according to Bloom-
berg, and local media highlighted how the Korean won's appreciation to a two-year high is playing an increasingly prominent role in
encouraging foreign investor selling.

Risks tied to the AI boom: A Bloomberg piece discussed systemic risks from the surge in debt-fueled AI investment. It noted an increasingly
concentrated market, both at the index level (market dominance of US big tech and Asian semis and memory) and in foreign investors'
rapidly increasing exposure to US AI stocks. Strategists also highlighted Asia's vulnerability to a sudden downturn in AI capex, given the
amount of foreign capital funding the boom.

IMF leaves global growth projections little changed: The IMF's October World Economic Outlook revised up the 2026 global GDP growth
forecast to 3.1% from 3.0% in July, with 2027 unchanged at 3.4%. Among major Asian economies, the China outlook was shaved 0.1 ppt
in each year to 4.5% in 2026 (the bottom of the government target range) and 4.0% in 2027, Japan was revised up 0.1 ppt in 2026 to
0.7% with 2027 unchanged at 0.7%, and South Korea was upgraded more notably to 3.3% in 2026 and 2.8% in 2027 from July estimates
of 2.6% and 2.5% respectively. The executive summary highlighted crosswinds from two main themes, the Middle East war and AI invest-
ment, each leading to some winners and losers. Risks are still seen skewed to the downside, noting the potential ramifications of no
improvement in the Middle East situation as well as an AI investment correction, although AI risks are more symmetric. The IMF stressed
that rebuilding fiscal space is increasingly urgent and endorsed only temporary and targeted cost of living relief measures; in contrast, its
prior hawkish monetary policy prescription shifted to one more tailored to local circumstances, versus an emphasis on price regulation in
July.

BOJ price survey implications still hawkish though little evidence of ramp-up: The BOJ opinion survey for September showed the combined
proportion of respondents expecting price increases over one year at 88.6% versus 90.4% in June, and the five-year projection at 86.5%
versus 86.1%. Median expectations of increases remained steady at +10.0% in one year and +5.0% in five years, with adaptive formation
(recency bias) remaining the dominant factor. Optimism toward Japan's growth potential is waning and now virtually evenly split between
those who see higher than current actual growth and those who see lower, and a mild majority continue to say household circumstances
are worsening. Income and spending outlook DIs remain soft; a clear majority are spending more, though largely on non-discretionary
items, with reported cutbacks to dining, apparel and travel. Factors influencing future spending remain dominated by price, well ahead of
incomes, and there are no major concerns regarding the employment outlook, consistent with labor market tightness. Market rate hike
expectations for October have diminished after the two dissenters at the September meeting and a lackluster BOJ Tankan survey, and the
Summary of Opinions for the September MPM and press reports generally underlined the high bar facing a back-to-back hike.

RBA ready to tighten further if needed with upside inflation risks materializing: RBA minutes showed September's rate hike was supported
by three main considerations: upside inflation risks materialized, responding now rather than later best balanced inflation and employ-
ment objectives, and the possibility that the current cash rate is not as restrictive as previous two given a potentially higher global neutral
rate. Brief consideration was given to leaving the cash rate on hold, based on the possibility that downside risks to growth and employment
prove larger than expected. The decision to hike reflected a conclusion that excess demand was greater than previously assessed and an
