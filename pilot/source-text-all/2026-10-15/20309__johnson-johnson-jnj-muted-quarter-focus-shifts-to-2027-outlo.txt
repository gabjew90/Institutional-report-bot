Johnson & Johnson (JNJ): Muted Quarter; Focus Shifts to 2027 Outlook
14 October 2026 | 6:00AM EDT
On the back of a mixed set of 3Q26 results, shares of JNJ traded off -6% (vs. DRG 
-2%) with investor feedback pointing to questions around key workhorse products 
that have been propelling the Innovative Medicines segment (Tremfya beat was of a 
lower magnitude vs. recent quarters; Darzalex came in below) alongside an ongoing 
deceleration in the MedTech segment. On Tremfya, we continue to see scope for 
upside and again raise our estimates to reflect ongoing trends, though on Darzalex 
we slightly moderate our FY26/27 projections to reflect OUS pricing headwinds that 
the company said could have an impact through the comparative period next year.  
While in prior quarters Darzalex and Tremfya contributed significantly to the 
outperformance, we believe the mid-stage portfolio and new launches will now 
increasingly be watched for upward sales revisions, with particular focus on Icotyde 
where management reiterated their expectation of a $10bn+ opportunity (vs. VA 
consensus peak sales of $8.3bn) — we slightly raise our FY27 Icotyde estimates to 
reflect management commentary on the launch. For the MedTech segment, we 
moderate our 2026 organic revenue growth estimate to 5% (from 5.8% prior), driven 
primarily by weaker OUS performance balanced by US strength and a tailwind from 
the additional selling week. Our FY26 enterprise-level revenue forecasts of $101.5bn 
(vs. current Cons $101.4bn) remain a touch above company guidance (of $101.0- 
$101.4bn) given the extra week in 4Q26, which management clarified as a 400bp 
tailwind for the quarter and 100bps for the full year.  
We see the selloff in JNJ shares as overdone, presenting a buying opportunity in one 
of the HC sector’s premier mega-cap secular growth compounders where we remain 
constructive on numerous new product cycles unfolding across the Innovative 
Medicines business. That said, with the 2026 beat/raise narrative now better 
reflected/understood, and current 4Q26 VA Consensus revenue estimate of $26.5bn 
near the top-end of the implied 4Q guidance of $26.2bn to $26.6bn, we believe 
shares could be rangebound pending clarity on the 2027 outlook (expected at the 
company’s Enterprise Business Review Day on Dec. 8th).  While management 
continues to frame underlying 2027 growth as accelerating over 2026 (excluding the 
extra week), our investor conversations point to questions on the magnitude and 
components of that acceleration given multiple moving parts. VA Consensus 
reported topline growth of ~7% in 2027 (vs. 7.4% at midpoint guided this year) 
seems to embed some level of underlying fundamental acceleration, given reported 
topline growth in 2027 will face an LOE impact, an FX headwind as well as ~ 100bps 
impact from the extra week in 2026.  We will look for more color when we host the JNJ 
management team (CEO, Head of IM, and Oncology leadership) at the upcoming ESMO

Notable Highlights from the Earnings Call 
Tremfya. JNJ maintained a bullish outlook for Tremfya, driven by the IBD launch, 
n

Icotyde. While not providing a sales breakout (as expected, but noting they will do 
n

over 20k patients have initiated therapy through nearly 9k prescribers (up from vs.

achieved broad commercial access, now covered across all national commercial 
plans. Approximately 60% of patients were systemic-naive (consistent with prior), 
20% switched from orals, and 20% switched from other treatments (mostly 
biologics). Indication expansion is underway with Ph3 data in psoriatic arthritis

is large enough for both products to succeed commercially CARTITUDE-5 and

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
