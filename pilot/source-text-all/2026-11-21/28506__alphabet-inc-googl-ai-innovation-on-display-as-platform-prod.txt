Gemini can now execute multi-step tasks including making phone calls, booking 
appointments, analyzing bills, managing calendars, and completing shopping 
workflows. Importantly, the product ties together Gmail, Calendar, Maps, Password 
Manager, Shopping Graph, Google Pay/Wallet, and third-party services, highlighting 
Google’s advantage of having users’ email, calendar, files, photos, passwords, and 
payment credentials already within its ecosystem in terms of distribution and utility 
as a personal assistant. 
Agentic commerce is emerging as an increasingly tangible component of that 
n
consumer-agent strategy - Gemini’s growing commerce-related capabilities 
(product research, checkout through Google Pay, Mastercard & Visa with American 
Express coming soon, ability to retain card rewards & protections, etc.) and 
integration of Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) further links discovery to 
conversion. We see this as strategically important to ensure Google (mainly across 
Search & Shopping) continues to play a role across the entire purchase funnel as 
consumer behavior migrates from queries/links toward agents completing tasks. 
With Gemini 4 Neon, Google is highlighting its strategy of delivering 
n
frontier-level intelligence at materially improving economics and speed - Neon 
is positioned between Hydrogen (for fast/everyday execution) and Carbon (for more 
difficult/long-horizon problems). Priced at $3 per 1mm input tokens and $15 per 
1mm output tokens with a 1mm token context window, Google has cited a few stats 
to quantify the cost/performance of Neon: a) performance comparable to or 
exceeding Carbon at 3x the speed and 60% of the cost (based on Glean evaluations); 
& b) at its default high thinking level, a 32% lower cost per task than Carbon and 
~50-80% lower cost per task than leading competing frontier models on selected 
benchmarks. 
The cost/performance advantage of Neon is particularly important for 
n
high-volume agentic use cases - Neon emphasizes coding, analytical reasoning, 
spreadsheets/finance, scientific workflows, cybersecurity, and multi-step 
autonomous execution, including orchestrating parallel Hydrogen sub-agents in 
Antigravity. Benchmark results are particularly strong across agentic enterprise 
workloads, including #1 on Zapier’s AutomationBench, Legal Research Bench and 
Tax Agent Bench and #2 globally on Agent Arena. That combination of intelligence, 
latency and cost should help broaden economically viable use cases as AI moves 
from occasional queries toward sustained agentic workloads. 
Genie 4 extends Alphabet’s frontier work beyond language/multi-modal 
n
models toward interactive world simulation - The new Project Genie experience 
lets users generate and enter interactive environments in-browser and then modify 
them through natural-language prompts in real time. Google is pairing its world 
models with Gemini and a new Project Genie agent that orchestrates multimodal 
models to help create environments, music and experiences conversationally. Access 
begins with eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers, alongside a community gallery for 
publishing/remixing worlds. 
In total, these three announcements reinforce a broader shift in Google’s AI strategy 
(one that we first outlined in August – link) from “raw intelligence” (i.e. standalone

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

and represent investment recommendations focused on the size of the total return potential and/or the likelihood of the realization of the return across 
their respective areas of coverage.  The addition or removal of stocks from such Conviction Lists are managed by the Investment Review Committee or 
other designated committee in each respective region and do not represent a change in the analysts’ investment rating for such stocks.    
Total return potential represents the upside or downside differential between the current share price and the price target, including all paid or 
anticipated dividends, expected during the time horizon associated with the price target. Price targets are required for all covered stocks. The total 
return potential, price target and associated time horizon are stated in each report adding or reiterating an Investment List membership.
