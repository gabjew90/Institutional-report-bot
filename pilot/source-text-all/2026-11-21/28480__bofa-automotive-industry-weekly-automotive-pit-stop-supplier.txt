Industry Overview 
   
 
 
Takeaways from Mobility Global outlook lunch 
We hosted Mike Wall at Mobility Global to discuss the current and long-term outlook for 
autos. US sales are forecast flat YoY in 2027 at 16.1mm units, though Mobility Global 
sees risk to the downside as the consumer looks stretched with loan payments 
approaching $800/month. In North America, model launches accelerate to ~30 in 2028, 
~60 in 2029 and ~48 in 2030, well above the ~40 peak in 2006, as programs have 
repeatedly been pushed out amid EV strategy reset but there is risk of delays, and 
extension to current programs. On trade, a renewed long-term USMCA deal looks 
unlikely for at least another year. Mobility Global also assumes existing tariffs largely 
remain in place, with limited political appetite even under a different administration for 
a rollback. The outlook in China remains challenging, with Mobility global recently cutting 
production forecasts in the region by 500K units in 2026, and 1.6mm units in 2027 as 
domestic sales decline 21.5% this year. While Chinese OEM entry into the US looks 
unlikely over the next few years, Chinese OEM and supply-chain presence in Mexico is 
already growing rapidly. EU import cap for Chinese hybrids in 2027 is likely to accelerate 
Chinese OEMs shift to local production in Europe, where they could represent 24% of 
capacity by 2033. Component cost inflation was also a focus, with DRAM supply and 
price pressure broadening to PCBs and legacy-node chips, adding $350-400 of cost per 
vehicle in 2027.  
Key learnings from large supplier conference 
We attended the MEMA Annual Conference, a large conference for the supplier 
community, and thought the overall tone was relatively cautious, with concerns around 
rising cost inflation & competition from Chinese brands, and pricing pressure for 
suppliers with operations in the region. The timing around the entrance of Chinese 
vehicles into the U.S. was debated, most agreed it will not happen near term but 
eventual entrance seems inevitable and share gains could be rapid given lower price 
points, and better contented vehicles.  On AVs, NHTSA is updating safety standards for 
vehicles designed without human drivers and working with developers on AV standards, 
targeting a proposal within a year.  
Tesla Texas robotaxi fleet now larger than Waymo  
We estimate Tesla's robotaxi fleet reached 1,507 vehicles as of 11/19, up 204 WoW 
(+16%) and 649 (+76%) since 10/23 (per registrations with the Texas DMV). That moved 
it ahead of Waymo's 1,328 for the first time. Cybercab drove the weekly gain, adding 
200 units to reach 773 vs. just 4 added Model Ys, which lifted Cybercab to now be the 
majority of the fleet at 51.3%. Cybercab units have more than doubled from 351 since 
10/23. NHTSA safety data for TSLA through 10/15 shows 43 minor incidents, still with 
no significant injuries or fatalities. Beyond the robotaxi network, Tesla priced a personal-
use Cybercab at $29,990 with a $299 monthly Autonomy Subscription, though Musk said 
the fee is too high and should be closer to the ~$115 FSD and connectivity cost on other 
models. We think personal use is still in very early innings with limited ODDs, with 
robotaxi the primary near-term focus for Cybercab.

October US light vehicle sales fell -0.8% YoY on a selling day 
adjusted basis to a SAAR of 15.5mm, a step down from 16.0mm in 
September. The result was below our mid-month estimate of 
15.9mm and Bloomberg consensus at 15.8mm. October brings YTD 
SAAR to 16.0mm, still below the 16.3mm level in FY25. 
We think the sequential decline may reflect persistently elevated 
retail gas prices and a recent reacceleration in inflation against an 
already constrained affordability backdrop. October lapped an 
easier compare given the EV pull-forward into September 2025, 
making the YoY decline more notable. Still, demand has held up 
YTD as wealthier consumers are making up a growing share of new 
vehicle buyers, and an aging vehicle fleet is adding pent-up 
replacement demand.

(+16k) and offset by Stellantis (-22k). On an inventory days basis, 
Ford (83DS) and Stellantis (99DS) remain elevated relative to GM

(+35% YoY). Fluids & lubricants, plastic and plastic composites, and steel drove the increase,

pointed to fuel surcharges, electronics (mainly DRAM), resin and aluminum as their main cost 
pressures.

Price objective basis & risk 
Adient Plc (ADNT) 
Our price objective of $17 for Adient (ADNT) is based on an EV/EBITDA multiple of 
roughly 3.1x on our 2028E estimates, which is slightly below its historical average of 
roughly 4.0x. The company is currently experiencing pressures on production volumes, 
which are partially mitigated with cost cutting actions. However, challenging market 
environment in Europe, combined with the risk of contract extensions that could prolong 
the unprofitable metal contracts, weigh on stock valuation. 
 
Upside risks: 1) ADNT successfully wins more profitable business, expands its backlog 
and reaccelerates its top line growth, 2) better than expected operating leverage from 
growth in global auto production, 3) significant progress made on restructuring 
programs and cost rationalization actions, 4) material market share gains in China 
market, 5) wind-down of non-recurring items boosts ongoing cash flow power. 
 
Downside risks: 1) inability to win sufficient and profitable new business, expand the 
backlog, and reaccelerate top line growth, 2) inability to complete restructuring 
programs or cost rationalization initiatives, 3) decline in new vehicle production, 4) 
inability to consistently execute, 5) raw material cost inflation, 6) inability to handle OEM 
price downs, 7) failure to maintain a lean cost structure, 8) new program/platform launch 
risk. 
Aptiv PLC (APTV) 
Our PO of $65 for APTV is based on an EV/EBITDA multiple of roughly 7.8x our CY2027 
estimates which reflects our updated forecasts post the spin-off of Versigent. This 
multiple is a premium to the traditional automotive suppliers, which trade in ranges of 
3x to 6x. We believe a valuation premium to the group is warranted, as APTV is exposed 
to the part of the car where automakers are investing in content/R&D which should 
enable both growth over market, and higher margins vs. more commoditized segments 
(e.g. interiors).
