GS Wang - Sales Trader Commentary: Squeezing the Shorts,
Fading the Rallies; Navigating TAIEX Chaos Before Heading to the
Vice City | GSTW 12 Nov 2026
Robert Wang  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  AEJ Sales Trading, Taipei
Thu 12 Nov 2026, 2:15am ET
Post the historical open on 11/6/2026, we saw a jaw-dropping 14% climb from day-low last Friday. Finally
took a breather (and a big one too) today; admittedly, I was writing a different narrative, but last hour fade in
Taiwan has forced me to rewrite this (>1k points drop and picked up market turnover). So, where do we go
from here? Check out below for my observation and investor feedback.
Key Investor Feedback and Takeaway
•  Sentiment. Sentiment is still cautious among foreigners; a lot of HFs faced serious short squeeze from this
rally. Yet, many are still reluctant to cover all their short positions given the new short sell restrictions. and
hence held on to the pain trades. Interestingly, market still exhaust short sell quota almost every day.
Additionally, there is little conviction for a full-on rally and also low willingness to chase around here
given year-end. Overall, HFs have de-grossed much, one of the most aggressive de-gross per GS prime
book data. Key focus will be whether LOs are willing to participate and to what extent will they add back
the positions. Only then, we will likely see a more sustainable rally further from here. Bottom line: are we
out of the woods? Not yet. Cautious optimism is perhaps the most positive thing I can say for now.
•  Positioning. Foreigners have net sold for 10 straight sessions, totaling around US$25.4bn. Most outflows
are in TSMC but worth noting that we finally saw TSMC net buy for the first time since 2 weeks
yesterday. On the other hand, local funds have been good size net buyers to absorb the foreign outflows,
mainly from retail's mutual fund subscription. Just regurgitating some of my berg blurbs from the past few
days, we still see lots of short interest in crowded long sectors, most in ABF/CCL (i.e. NY Plastics)
followed by CPO names; and most smid cap outperformance was driven by short covering. The overly
sold names saw little attention and minimum desire to bottom fish (i.e. Delta, Chroma, KYEC). On a
brighter note, we also picked up some real money inflows into TSMC this week, but I don't know whether
it's enough to turn the tide. The more convicted long right now for foreigners is still ASE in my opinion.
•  Retail. This is perhaps the most important factor now in Taiwan. Coupled with the sentiment boost from
National Stabilization Fund, many retail decided to hold instead of cutting loss and even bought into the
dip. For those retail that were facing margin call during this correction, some were eventually saved by the
market rally. Cleaner positioning for sure, though perhaps not clean enough to fuel another 10% climb in
index. Per my local investor feedback, the smart pundits actually many have missed out on this rally (of
course they've also de-grossed much ahead). Margin loan balance and maintenance ratio are two key data
that I will follow closely in the coming weeks.
•  Fundamentals. Fundamentals are still good with most company reporting record high quarterly earnings.
Monthly revenue also grew MoM. But stock trading is all about forward looking. People are anticipating
AI capex growth to slow down or even plateau in the coming 2 years. Quanta (2382 TT) earnings call

yesterday validated this narrative - still high growth in 2027 but unlikely to see another triple-digits rapid
growth like 2026. Earnings outlooks probably matter less than positioning and macro backdrop. With that,
we will conclude most of our 3Q26 earnings this week with Hon Hai (2317 TT) as the main finale.
•  Liquidity Event: MSCI just published their quarterly review, and NYPCB (8046 TT) and TUC (6274 TT)
will be added into the index, while Lotes (3533 TT), Chailease (5871 TT), Wan Hai (2615 TT), an E-Ink
(8069 TT) will be removed. Kinsus (3189 TT) did not get added due to its disposition status. See below
and this report for our flows estimate.
•  Risk. Risk reward level here is not attractive, especially when the rebound came so aggressively.
Momentum to sustain the rally is not constructive, so investors should be cautious of more volatility and
further unwind (if there's anything to be unwound). Jensen and Nvidia's headlines are certainly not
helping, though I did see some funny memes on the social media. Credit is another risk here; neoclouds
top line is +ve but let's not forget at what cost are these companies carving those revenue from. Otherwise,
market seems to process the macro prints well, with now expecting a rate hike in Dec'26 and Mar'27. Last
but not least, cross-strait tension can be a focus next during Trump-Xi meeting at the APEC Summit next
week. I know the Taiwanese has been (probably will be) indifferent and just another BAU, but anything
assertively negative comment on Taiwan might fret the macro traders, such as Xi's comment just now.
•  My View. Not a BEAR so far. I do not think the market is broken, but I acknowledge the downside risk
has risen again and enlarged as I respect the technicals. That said, I am still excited about next week, even
though we have fewer fundamental catalysts now. In the US, Nvidia is set to report next week. A lighter
book might fare better in today's environment. To end on a fun personal note, GTA VI will finally come
out after 13 years of waiting. So if you can't find me on Bloomberg, try looking for me in the Vice City.
