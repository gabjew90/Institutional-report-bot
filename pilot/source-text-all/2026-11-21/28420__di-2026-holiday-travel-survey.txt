Methodology 
An independent online research panel was used to survey 4,272 Americans, representative by age, income, 
gender, and geographical region. Of these, 2,019 respondents who are planning to travel between 
Thanksgiving and mid-January qualified as holiday travelers. The survey has a margin of error of ±2 
percentage points for the entire sample. It was fielded from Sept. 28 to Oct. 2, 2026.
Definitions 
Generations
Baby boomers are those born between 1946 and 1964, Generation X born between 1965 and 1979, 
millennials born between 1980 and 1996, and Generation Z born between 1997 and 2012.
Laptop luggers and disconnectors
Among respondents, those who plan to work during their longest trip of the season are considered laptop 
luggers. Those who do not plan to work are disconnectors.
Marquee trip
The longest trip that travelers plan to take over the holiday period.

Executive 
summary
Even as financial challenges have strained several 
consumer categories over the last few years, demand for 
travel has largely proven resilient. Travel intent 
fluctuated over recent holiday seasons, but one constant 
held at the highest income levels: Surveyed Americans 
earning US$200K and above consistently maintained 
their intent to travel and increased their budgets for 
three years running. 
Until now. Fewer Americans at that highest income level 
plan to travel than in any holiday season since 2022, 
leading a pullback across income levels. And those 
higher-income Americans are specifically planning to take 
fewer vacations with paid lodging, versus trips to stay 
with family or friends. 
Airfares and hotel rates play a significant role. One in 
four high earners staying home cite travel prices as a 
reason. And several other indicators point to financial 
factors affecting travel intent and budgets across income 
groups and generations.
Still, nearly half of surveyed Americans plan to travel 
between Thanksgiving and mid-January. Unlike most 
other consumer categories, travel is time-bound. School 
calendars limit openings for family trips. Most paid time 
off is “use it or lose it.” The prospect of missing those 
windows may help explain why travel has remained a 
priority. When schedules allow, many remain 
determined to take trips, whether to explore, relax, or 
reconnect. 
The flexibility and breadth of options also help to enable 
travel. Adjust your expectations for luxurious lodging, 
and now the trip fits your budget. Choose an 
international destination with a favorable exchange rate, 
and your dollar stretches to allow for upgraded lodging 
or more in-destination activities. The data indicates 
travelers are indeed making these adjustments. And 
while those with income of $200K or more are spending 
slightly less than in 2025, more travelers at lower income 
levels report that they are expanding budgets to account 
for higher prices.
As holiday travelers build their trips, the share planning 
to book direct is down slightly compared to 2025. 
Elevated cost concerns may help explain an uptick in the 
use of aggregated platforms that allow for cross-brand 
pricing comparisons. Increasingly, gen AI platforms are 
part of the process, and while adoption growth has 
slowed slightly, uptake continues to gain across 
generations.
Bigger budgets and thoughtful adjustments aren’t the 
only indicators of Americans’ commitment to holiday 
travel. A bigger share is planning international trips. 
Intent to stay in hotels, take cruises, and book in-
destination activities are all steady or up slightly. The 
upcoming holiday travel rush may be a little quieter 
overall, but the Americans still planning to get away are 
determined to reach their destinations with dollars left 
over for celebrating the season.

Key takeaways
Travel incidence is down 
across ages and income 
levels
Intent to travel this holiday season is 
at a post-pandemic low, with 47% 
(down 7 percentage points YoY) 
planning trips and 27% planning full 
vacations with paid lodging (down 
5 pp YoY). When cost concerns are a 
significant factor in travel incidence, 
travelers at lower income levels 
usually drop out while higher earners 
travel at similar levels. But in 2026, 
lower incidence reaches across the 
income spectrum—and Americans 
earning $200K and above exhibit the 
biggest declines.
Among those traveling, many are 
expanding budgets to accommodate 
higher prices. This willingness to pay 
the price translates to slight upticks in 
the share of travelers planning to use 
most travel products. This effect is 
seen in hotels, cruises, and even 
international flights.
Many high-income 
respondents feel financial 
pressure, take a step back
Cost is at the heart of the slowdown in 
travel among surveyed Americans 
with incomes of $200K or more. But 
it’s not the most often cited reason for 
staying home. More than a third at 
this income level say they plan to 
travel at a later date instead. 
For higher-income holiday travelers, 
signs do not point to austere trips. 
Compared to 2025, more plan to get 
away for a week or more. 
Participation in in-destination 
activities is similar. And while slightly 
fewer say they’re booking business 
class fares, the share booking 
upgraded economy tickets is up. More 
plan to travel internationally, with a 
greater number visiting Asia and 
Africa, perhaps prioritizing 
destinations where their dollars might 
go further. 
Gen AI tools are becoming tech 
for all ages
Compared to social media apps and 
video content, generative AI platforms 
are used more broadly across 
generations for travel discovery and 
planning. Social and video usage show 
a classic new-tech adoption pattern, 
with younger generations leading and a 
steep downward slope among older 
generations. 
In the case of gen AI, it’s millennial 
respondents, not Gen Z, who use it 
most. And while boomers still use social 
video in trip planning at about one-
eighth the rate of millennials, they use 
gen AI about half as much. And they 
follow through on recommendations at 
a similar rate to younger generations. 
Nearly half of boomers who have 
searched for in-destination activities 
with GenAI say they did an activity the 
tools recommeded. As gen AI travel 
tools evolve, there is an opportunity for 
brands to reach all generations of 
travelers. 
Travelers find ways to make 
the trip fit
Travel is complex, personal, and, 
perhaps most importantly, flexible. 
This season’s travelers seem 
determined to keep holiday plans 
within reach, whether by expanding 
budgets or making selective 
concessions to account for higher 
prices.
While some are opting to stay home 
and wait out the busy holiday travel 
season, others are making trade-
offs—giving up a day or two or a 
higher-end hotel—to make the trip fit 
their budgets.
For the 17% downgrading their trips, 
shorter duration, lower in-destination 
spend, and budget lodging are the 
most common. A 6 percentage point 
shift among flyers from first or 
business class toward upgraded 
economy illustrates how travelers 
can trim costs while preserving some 
comfort.

Holiday travel 
incidence and 
spend
Fewer Americans plan to travel for the holidays 
this year, bringing travel incidence to a post-
pandemic low. Cost concerns are prompting 
even those in the highest income brackets to 
skip or streamline holiday trips, while lower-
income households intend to increase their 
travel budgets to keep pace with prices. Still, 
many high-income Americans opting out of 
travel this season are planning to travel during 
a less busy time.
