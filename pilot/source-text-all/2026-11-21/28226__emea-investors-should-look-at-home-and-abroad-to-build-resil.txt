Thought of the day
For much of the recent market cycle, investors have looked to the US and 
Asia for growth, led by an increasingly concentrated group of companies 
connected to artificial intelligence. In our Year Ahead 2027: Sustaining 
resilience publication (here), we highlight why we expect AI investment to 
remain an important earnings driver in 2027. But elevated expectations, 
questions over monetization, and the concentration of AI exposure in major 
equity indexes mean that for EMEA investors, resilience may now come from 
combining selected opportunities closer to home with return sources from 
further afield.
This does not mean adopting a narrow home bias, especially when we expect 
2027 to be a year with an unusually wide range of possible outcomes for 
economies, geopolitics, and markets.
Rather, it means making greater use of assets where the region offers 
distinctive opportunities, while diversifying exposure to return drivers that 
are less readily available in local public markets. The resulting cross-asset 
strategy for 2027: use selected home-region assets to participate in Europe’s 
broadening earnings and income opportunities, while reaching globally for 
diversification through commodities, emerging market bonds and currencies, 
and alternative investments.
Use today’s bond yields to rebalance, add income, and build resilience.
We view fixed income as Attractive and favor an intermediate average 
duration, generally through maturities of around two to 10 years. Quality 
bonds can provide attractive income and diversification if equity volatility

Within European credit, we favor medium-maturity bonds from quality A- 
and BBB-rated issuers, including selected utilities, infrastructure beneficiaries, 
telecoms, energy companies, and banks. Investors should limit exposure

Prefer Eurozone equities, but broaden the drivers of growth. The 
Eurozone is our preferred equity region. We expect earnings to grow by 15% 
in 2027, matching our US forecast but from a lower starting valuation and 
with less dependence on a small group of AI-linked companies. Investment 
in defense, infrastructure, automation, power networks, and electrification 
should support European industrials, while health care can provide a less 
cyclical earnings stream. We also favor banks, information technology, 
utilities, and Germany. Utilities can offer exposure to rising electricity demand 
while providing some portfolio ballast if growth disappoints. Investors should 
remain selective in consumer sectors, where weak Chinese demand continues 
to pose a risk.
Look beyond oil within commodities. We expect broad commodities to 
benefit from structural investment in power, electrification, data centers, and 
supply chain resilience, but the case is not based on a single commodity 
cycle. Each segment contributes a different potential return driver: Energy can 
help protect against geopolitical disruption, supply constraints, and renewed 
inflation pressure; industrial metals provide exposure to investment in power 
networks, electrification, data centers, and AI infrastructure; agriculture 
introduces exposure to extreme weather, harvests, inventories, and crop 
cycles, which differ from the forces driving conventional financial markets; 
and gold can diversify against fiscal, monetary, and geopolitical uncertainty.
This distinction is important for Middle Eastern investors. Adding a 
conventional commodity index that remains dominated by oil and gas may 
reinforce an existing economic exposure rather than diversify it. An actively 
managed allocation spanning industrial metals, agriculture, precious metals, 
and energy can adjust with shifting market leadership.
Use alternatives to access return sources that may be less 
available in public markets. Alternatives can complement regional public-
market holdings with differentiated income, growth, and relative-value 
opportunities. We favor hedge fund strategies able to exploit greater 
market dispersion, including low-net and market-neutral equity, merger 
arbitrage, fixed income relative value, discretionary macro, and multi-strategy 
approaches.
Within private markets, we favor operationally focused private equity 
managers, middle-market and complex transactions, and secondaries. 
Investors can diversify beyond US technology into European and global 
health care, energy, infrastructure, industrials, and materials. Core and 
core-plus infrastructure can add visible, sometimes inflation-linked cash 
flows through operational assets in power networks, digital infrastructure, 
transport, logistics, energy security, and supply-chain resilience.
Alternatives can involve long lockups, uncertain capital calls and distributions, 
leverage, high fees, limited transparency, and subjective valuations. 
Investors should diversify across managers, strategies, regions, vintages, and 
investment years, while pacing commitments around liquidity needs.

We also see wider opportunities to manage currencies more proactively. 
Sterling is our preferred G10 carry opportunity, supported by resilient UK 
activity and its yield advantage over the euro and Swiss franc, although fiscal 
credibility remains an important risk. We also see opportunities in diversified 
emerging market carry, including the South African rand, Mexican peso,

positioning aligns with where investors live, earn, spend, and hold assets.
Caught our attention
Fed minutes confirm more hikes ahead. Minutes from the Federal 
Reserve’s 27-28 October meeting showed that almost all officials expected 
further interest rate increases would likely be appropriate to return inflation 
to the 2% target. Two participants said a move larger than 25 basis points 
could be warranted in December if inflation data proved unfavorable. Officials 
described price pressures as broad-based and identified AI-related demand 
as a material inflation risk, while also discussing financial vulnerabilities 
from rapidly growing AI debt, complex financing structures, elevated asset 
valuations, and hedge fund leverage. Separately, San Francisco Fed President 
Mary Daly on Wednesday called for a measured approach to raising rates, 
while Governor Michael Barr said higher borrowing costs would likely 
be appropriate. Earlier this week, New York Fed President John Williams 
dismissed the suggestion the Fed needs to consider an outsized rate hike.
Our view: The minutes and latest policymaker comments are consistent with 
our view that the US central bank is likely to raise rates in December and again 
in March 2027. While recent inflation data increases the risk of consecutive 
moves, further hikes are largely priced in by markets. We also believe the 
cumulative impact of rate increases so far and tighter financial conditions is 
likely to limit the need for a prolonged hiking cycle. We rate fixed income 
as Attractive, as elevated starting yields provide appealing portfolio income 
as well as a cushion if yields rise further. In a scenario where economic and 
equity market risks intensify, quality bonds would likely rally, helping insulate 
portfolios.
Copper’s declines. Copper prices fell across London, New York, and 
Shanghai on Wednesday amid concerns about AI’s trajectory of growth. The 
benchmark three-month LME copper fell 5.7% to USD 12,471 per metric 
tonne, the lowest level since April. At over 10% below its peak, copper’s 
recent consolidation came amid AI-related volatility, concerns about the Fed’s 
tightening weighing on demand, and growing skepticism over the Trump’s 
administration’s potential tariffs on refined copper.
Our view: We think the sharp decline reflects a combination of weaker 
sentiment and rising exchange inventories rather than a deterioration 
in copper’s underlying fundamentals. Mine-supply growth continues to 
disappoint, tight concentrate availability is reflected in deeply negative 
treatment charges, and demand from electrification and power-grid 
investment remains robust. We remain constructive on copper, and view 
periods of weakness as opportunities to add exposure as part of a broader 
commodity allocation that is managed actively. We maintain our price 
forecast of USD 15,500/mt by mid-2027.
US health insurers fall on policy concerns. US managed care shares sold 
off this week after the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) 
proposed new measures to strengthen oversight of Medicare Advantage risk 
adjustment and other federal health programs. The S&P 500 Managed Health

Our view: The headlines remind investors that health insurers face rising policy

view the sell-off as driven more by a reassessment of previously benign 
policy expectations and investor positioning than by a material change in 
near-term fundamentals. Managed care valuations appear reasonable in a 
historical context, supported by relatively strong earnings visibility, although 
policy headlines are likely to generate greater volatility as the 2028 election

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
In line: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
