This report is a compilation of analyst takeaways from Day 2 of the RBC Capital Markets Global
Technology, Internet, Media and Telecommunications Conference, which took place on November 18,
2026, in New York. See below for our most notable takeaways from the day and the body of the report
for more comprehensive takeaways on each presenting name.
Day 2 Most Notable Takeaways
• Cloudflare, Inc. (NET): NET remains our favorite LT idea as we came away from our management
meeting incrementally positive about our bullish thesis. We continue to view the company as a tier 1
AI beneficiary as acceleration is being driven by growth engines firing on all cylinders, and we expect
momentum to continue building with the rapid proliferation of agentic traffic via multiple avenues of
monetization. Workers traction continues to build momentum, with the developer base crossing 10M
(we see no signs of slowing net adds), and the company is also seeing tailwinds from rising agentic
web traffic and security-driven tailwinds. Management sounded bullish that Act 4 could be bigger
than their other acts as ~30% of the Internet sits behind the Cloudflare network as they added 5%
of the Internet this past quarter. Broadly, we believe the S-curves of growth remain numerous and
also think the company is working on Act 5 back in the lab. And they are doing all of this with GAAP
profitability expected in Q4/26.
• Datadog, Inc. (DDOG): In our view, Datadog remains a tier 1 AI winner as we view weakness post Q3
earnings as a buying opportunity. Management believes Datadog is in the early innings of a durable
growth cycle driven by enterprise AI adoption and cloud modernization, with the early renewal wave
and large customer headwind masking underlying core growth in the low-to-mid 30s. Observability is
positioned as a foundational layer regardless of how the AI landscape evolves, with multiple emerging
vectors including training/GPU monitoring, Bits AI, and security that could layer on incremental
growth.
• F5, Inc. (FFIV): We thought management sounded positive on the company's opportunity beyond the
Systems refresh and feel good about the company's FY/27 outlook as overall growth should benefit
from cybersecurity upside, accelerated enterprise AI adoption and digital sovereignty. We think the
selloff post Q4 results is overdone as we see an attractive risk/reward due to what we think is an
upward bias to consensus revenue and profitability expectations.
• IBM (IBM): We came away from the keynote with more comfort on IBM's positioning and innovation
in a landscape that continues to evolve at a rapid pace. Key themes were around their view of
the benefits of an open-source model, their container architecture, Lightwell, M&A playbook, AI
momentum and quantum.
• Google read-through (GOOGL): We hosted panel discussions on the evolving digital advertising
landscape and AI-driven search surfaces. While panelists acknowledged AI Mode monetization is
lagging, the consensus view was that Google is putting user adoption ahead of monetization, and
that the fix will likely come from better measurement and attribution as AI Mode inventory is folded
into existing campaign types. On chatbot ads, early underperformance was viewed as an ad product
problem rather than a user intent problem, with natively embedded sponsored recommendations
seen as the eventual direction as the integration layer matures. While agents spreading spend across
more channels could be a modest headwind at the margin, we came away constructive on Google's
ability to monetize AI surfaces over time.

• Celestica (CLS): Celestica has strong visibility into FY27. FY27 guidance for $40B in revenue (+90% Y/
Y) includes only awarded programs, and customer demand exceeds that level. Celestica's end-to-end
design capabilities, spanning network switches to full AI racks, are helping it win programs earlier and
secure more strategic engagements with hyperscalers and frontier AI labs. While FY27 growth requires
significant working capital and capex investments, management anticipates a return to positive FCF in
FY28. Given Celestica's downward valuation re-rating and strong growth visibility, we see compelling
risk-reward on the stock.
• Nasdaq, Inc. (NDAQ): CFO highlighted that Generative AI and always-on markets have created a
self-reinforcing flywheel driving broad-based demand across all business segments. Verafin and
Regulatory Technology are benefiting from heightened demand as financial crime and regulatory
complexity continue to grow across an increasingly interconnected global market, while Capital
Markets Technology is gaining momentum from the modernization of trading infrastructure.
Separately, the Index business continues to enjoy durable demand, underpinned by the long-term
secular tailwinds of passive investing and AUM growth.
• Axon Enterprise (AXON): Axon left us with greater confidence in the company's ability to sustain 25%
+ revenue growth, meet its 2028 targets, and expand margins through multiple levers, all supported
by its connected ecosystem and a growing software/AI mix.
• TD SYNNEX Corporation (SNX): We believe SNX is well positioned to grow across both Distribution
and Hyve. Distribution is benefiting from continued execution and above-market growth, while Hyve's
ramping programs should turn cash generative and its margins should stabilize as the AI server
program laps. Rising enterprise AI factory and storage demand gives both businesses a long-term
growth runway.
• Pegasystems (PEGA): Overall, we walk away incrementally positive on pipeline strength, particularly
new logo momentum within the pipeline, and on the GTM changes, which match how clients now
want to see value before they buy. That said, we remain cautious on a meaningful reacceleration in
ACV growth as management noted close rates and 2027 budgets remain hard to predict.
• Flywire Corporation (FLYW): Key takeaways included 1) the non-big 4 markets, which account for high-
teens mix of revenue, are growing at 30%; 2) stablecoin vs. fiat payment rail spreads the firm captures
are comparable; 3) the travel vertical remains a strong growth vector, with potential upcoming
international expansion in hospitality and avenues for growth in experiential; and 4) although gross
margins have been declining, it is a function of mix, rather than pricing, which is reflected in their adj.
EBITDA margin expansion and growing free cash flow.
• NCR Voyix Corporation (VYX): Key takeaways included 1) VYX’s evolution to a SaaS provider focused
on retail and restaurants; 2) the acceleration of their cloud-native VCP with 35 total customers having
signed upgrade/new agreements; 3) the payments business, which was historically 100% restaurant
attach, is now beginning to capture retail; and 4) AI is now being deployed to speed up product
development times, and more recently, for customer service call handling.
• PicS (PICS): In one on one conversations with management, our key takeaways were that 1)
management views credit quality as not deteriorating, but is currently reflective of the aging in the
credit portfolio; 2) following the change in its unsecured credit write-off policy, management expects
NPLs, which were the most significant metric to investors in 3Q26 results, to normalize at ~9% in 2Q27;
3) the company's operational growth and additional features in private payroll loans should enable
an improvement in first payment defaults moving forward; and 4) given the lower-than-expected
earnings contribution of the KEV acquisition, management expects KEV to be accretive to consolidated
earnings in FY27.

Aaru (Private company)
Ned Koh, Co-Founder and President, presented.
Key takeaway: We walked away with a better understanding of Aaru’s vision and ongoing momentum in its business. We
appreciated the conversation as we continue to look for AI-native businesses that are using LLMs to unlock fundamentally new
use cases, such as simulating decision-making behavior to redefine the marketplace research space like Aaru is addressing.
Founding vision and the gaps in decision-making. Management noted that Aaru operates in the market research space, born from
a deep interest of understanding why people do what they do and the tangible outcomes that can come from predicting behavior.
The founding insight was that traditional decision-making suffers from three core gaps: 1) delays across information gathering;
2) distortion, where respondents lie or can't quantify their own behavior; and 3) reach, where the most impactful populations
are often the hardest to access. To address this, Aaru built a simulation platform that generates populations of AI respondents,
enabling more accurate behavioral predictions than speaking to people directly.
Uncovering real behavior by focusing on outcomes. Rather than relying on what consumers say, Aaru focuses on what they
do. Management views traditional survey data as a "massive primitive", acknowledging how fallible self-reported responses are.
Instead, the platform ingests outcome-based data like credit card transactions, voting results, and other behavioral signals. One of
Aaru's biggest business lines in the U.S. is utilities, where it works with companies like Con Edison to understand tenants who can't
pay and why. Management argued that in every business, the individuals that matter most often exist outside the populations
you can directly speak to.
Scale and the New York advantage. Aaru is two-year old New York-based company with a Series A valuing the company around
$1B in late 2025, according to TechCrunch. Eight months ago the team was 12 people, with plans to grow to over 650 in the next
four years, supported by a record-breaking hiring grant from New York State. Management believes being outside San Francisco
has been a significant advantage, both in understanding the real problems businesses need solved and in accessing talent. On
GTM, management emphasized that building good technology alone doesn't sell, and that proximity to customers in New York has
helped Aaru go directly to decision-makers rather than the market research gatekeepers who traditionally hold purse strings but
may not be accountable for outcomes. Management acknowledged that confidence is the key challenge in this space, so Aaru's
go-to-market approach has been to ask prospects to provide a known outcome, the demographic they studied, and the plan they
had, and then Aaru delivers a more accurate result than the prospect achieved on their own.
The simulation engine under the hood. Aaru generates highly specific populations that clients may have never spoken to,
whether that's the U.S. general population or F500 CEOs. The platform uses a spread of language models and diffusion models
on demographic distributions, relying exclusively on behavioral studies, credit card data, voting results, and similar outcome-
based inputs rather than asking people anything. The core architecture combines diffusion models with proprietary energy-based
models (EBMs). Management described LLMs as the gas and Aaru's statistical engine as the car: LLMs power the inference and
data gathering, but the statistical engine is what turns the wheels.
Could agentic commerce be understated? When asked how the business will change as agent behavior becomes more prevalent,
management said it is a game of prioritization at this stage. Aaru believes timelines on agentic commerce being widely adopted
by the public are vastly overstated, a product of the San Francisco echo chamber. Management noted that people underestimate
how much consumers enjoy shopping and doing it themselves.
Platform expansion and the selection layer. Management sees Aaru's moats rooted in the discipline of chasing absolute accuracy
above all else, which it believes opens expansive platform potential. The analogy offered was selling picks and shovels versus
mining gold, and the TAM for selling decision-making is incalculable, according to management. Looking forward, Aaru has
reached significant utility and breadth, with the next step being piping insights directly into execution workflows, for example
fully automating how a brand buys ads. Management frames modern businesses as three parts: idea generation, selection, and
execution. As LLMs proliferate, there will be an exponential increase in apps and ideas created, and complete commoditization
of both generation and execution. That abundance makes selection more valuable, and Aaru believes it is the best-positioned at

Cisco Systems, Inc. (NASDAQ: CSCO; Not covered)
Sami Badri, Senior Vice President, Head of Investor Relations & Strategic Finance, presented.
Key takeaway: Product order growth was top of mind and management reiterated that it can only contemplate orders received in
a quarter and expected to ship within 90 days, which means net orders are understated given there are some orders with delivery
expectations over 90 days that are only included in backlog. From a demand perspective, consistent with broader themes out
of the conference, management believes cybersecurity spend is poised to see a significant budget increase within enterprises
heading into CY/27.
Product order growth understates actual demand. To classify an order into the Q1/27 product order growth rate of 21%, the
order must be received in the quarter and ship within 90 days. Orders with ship dates beyond that 90-day window flow into
backlog, which is not publicly reported. Once a backlog order comes within the 90-day shippable window, it can then be recognized
in product bookings growth. This dynamic is driven by hyperscaler customers, whose orders don't always ship immediately,
unlike enterprise customers which are typically book-and-ship. Management emphasized that reported order growth numbers
understate actual net order activity. This is distinct from the supply chain period roughly three years ago.
Compares get more difficult, but management noted cycle fundamentals remain intact. Management acknowledged the
business does not move in a straight line but pushed back on the idea that tougher compares signal a slowdown. Hyperscaler
demand continues to grow, campus activity is increasing, and sovereign infrastructure is an added tailwind.
Hyperscaler order windows generally cap at 12 months. The preferred maximum window for hyperscaler orders is approximately
12 months, though exceptions exist. Management noted that when orders do extend beyond 12 months, they have tended to pull
in faster than originally expected. The extended timelines reflect customers aligning shipping schedules with data center readiness.
AI infrastructure is the primary driver of y/y change in the aggregate business. The biggest factor distorting y/y comparisons is
the booking profile of AI infrastructure, which was a much smaller contributor to revenue at the same time last year and is now
a meaningful portion. Hyperscaler activity carries different seasonality than the enterprise business, which still generally follows
corporate seasonality patterns. Everything outside of AI ultimately harmonizes back to that traditional cadence. On concentration
risk in AI infrastructure, management noted the business is highly distributed across products and customers, with multiple levels
of diversification, making it unlikely that a single customer could meaningfully shift a quarter.
Scale-up products not yet announced; scale-across momentum continues. Management confirmed no scale-up product has been
announced and said it would be surprising to generate scale-up revenue dollars in FY/27 given the need to announce, test, and
ramp. TSMC capacity is also a gating factor, as volume allocation must be requested and justified, and TSMC requires confidence
that a program is well-funded and durable. On wafer planning for Silicon One, management indicated TSMC approval processes
are lengthy and that a scale-up chip announcement would likely precede deeper allocation conversations.
Enterprise campus pricing stable; no lower-tier SKUs introduced. Pricing was not a factor in Q1/27 results in terms of lower-price
campus SKUs. Management has increased prices on products and is managing both pricing and channel distribution as levers but
has not announced lower-tier SKUs entering the mix.
Security growth inflecting, driven by firewalls and SSE. Underlying trends are now improving: firewall product orders have grown
30%+ for three consecutive quarters following a full refresh of firewall SKUs across all tiers over the past three years. More frontier
model cyber capability-related product orders are starting to come in as organizations acknowledge they are underinvested,
though this is not yet widespread. In Q1/27, security came in at 7% growth versus an initial expectation of low-single-digit growth
accelerating through the year, with upside driven by Cisco security core, specifically SSE and firewalls. Management expects

Gross margin confidence supported by visibility and mix dynamics. Management expressed confidence in gross margin targets,

Lumentum acquisition unlikely to change competitive landscape. Management does not expect significant competitive shifts

Revenue linearity increasing with hyperscaler mix. As hyperscaler business becomes a larger share, revenue has become more

automation and reduced need. For organizations to be secure and modernized, management believes they will need to request
larger budgets to fund resilience and security upgrades.
Cloudflare, Inc. (NYSE: NET)

Our view: Cloudflare delivered strong results highlighted by 31% revenue growth that again accelerated vs. 28% q/q. Additional
highlights include accelerating RPO and DBNRR, momentum in GTM, large customer success, Pool of Funds, and Workers. Overall, it
was another clean/impressive qtr that continues to give us confidence on ST estimates and the LT opportunity. CJ Desai announced
his departure for a CEO role, and although disappointing, this is understandable. Maintain Outperform rating.
Key takeaway. NET remains our favorite LT idea as we came away from our management meeting incrementally positive about our
bullish thesis. We continue to view the company as a tier 1 AI beneficiary as acceleration is being driven by growth engines firing on
all cylinders, and we expect momentum to continue building with the rapid proliferation of agentic traffic via multiple avenues of
monetization. Workers traction continues to build momentum, with the developer base crossing 10M (we see no signs of slowing
net adds), and the company is also seeing tailwinds from rising agentic web traffic and security-driven tailwinds. Management
sounded bullish that Act 4 could be bigger than their other acts as ~30% of the Internet sits behind the Cloudflare network as
they added 5% of the Internet this past quarter. Broadly, we believe the S-curves of growth remain numerous and also think the
company is working on Act 5 back in the lab. And they are doing all of this with GAAP profitability expected in Q4/26. We rate
Cloudflare Outperform.
Building blocks for broad-based momentum. Management framed the recent acceleration as the result of three compounding

generations.
Hyperscaler networking leadership likely to continue through 1.6T and CPO. Celestica's leadership in data center switches stems
from investments made a decade ago in anticipation of hyperscalers disaggregating hardware from software. The company has
secured ten 1.6T program wins (six scale-out, four scale-up) and a CPO (co-packaged optics) switch win with a hyperscaler. Growing
product complexity, particularly in optics, is raising barriers to entry. Networking pricing and margins have remained consistent
across product generations and accretive to the corporate average.
Increasing HPS mix is driving margin expansion. Celestica is selective, pursuing strategic, long-duration programs aligned with
its design strengths. Operating leverage and the increasing mix of high-margin HPS have driven significant operating margin
expansion. Given strong returns on its HPS design capabilities, Celestica plans to invest over $300MM in R&D in FY27. This includes
expanding its engineering team from 1,600 to over 2,000 and its design centres from seven to nine. In the near term, however, gross
margins will depend on program mix (compute carries lower margins than networking) and may fluctuate as specific programs
ramp.
FCF to turn positive in FY28. Due to working capital requirements from strong revenue growth, as well as capex, Celestica
anticipates negative FCF in FY27. Management emphasized that working capital intensity has not changed structurally and that
the current build reflects the timing of program ramps rather than a lasting shift. Capex is tied to awarded programs with
attractive payback periods (e.g., YTD ROI exceeds 50%). Celestica has also stress-tested capex investments against downside
volume scenarios. As capex intensity moderates and margins expand on higher FY28 volumes, Celestica expects FCF to turn positive
in FY28.
