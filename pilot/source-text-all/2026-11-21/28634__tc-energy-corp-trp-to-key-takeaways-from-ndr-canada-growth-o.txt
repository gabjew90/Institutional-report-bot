TC Energy Corp. (TRP.TO): Key Takeaways from NDR: Canada Growth 
Opportunity Accelerating, Complementing US Gas Growth - Buy
19 November 2026 | 11:37PM EST
We hosted TC Energy (TRP/TRP.TO, Buy) for an NDR to Chicago and Dallas. 
Joining us this week (11/18-19/26) were Sean O’Donnell, CFO; David Brast, President 
US Gas Pipelines; and other members of the finance and IR teams. Key takeaways: 1) 
the Canada power opportunity is material, with potential near-term catalysts; 2) 
NGTL negotiations are progressing and could drive meaningful growth over time; 3) 
the US gas pipeline growth outlook remains robust; 4) there are multiple paths for 
TRP to convert the backlog into sanctioned growth, with the high-end of the recently 
guided 5-7% EBITDA CAGR to 2031 appearing increasingly likely; and 5) the ramp In 
Bruce Power cash distributions early next decade provides considerable funding 
flexibility. See inside for more detail. Overall we are impressed by the material 
turnaround in the TRP story over the past three years, and reiterate our Buy rating. 
Key Takeaways 
 
 
Key takeaways from our NDR with TRP include: 
The Canada power opportunity is material, with potential near-term 
n
catalysts. TRP disclosed a new C$15b power opportunity in their projects in 
origination backlog with the recent 3Q26 earnings release. While this was largely 
new to the market, the company has been working on the opportunity since last 
summer - with the C$15b representing a specific, bottom-up (though unrisked) 
list of potential projects (as opposed to a top-down macro-driven projection). 
The focus is entirely, for now, in Canada - across multiple provinces - as TRP looks 
to leverage its meaningful incumbent position in gas pipelines, land, community 
and indigenous relations, supply chain, and government relations; this compares 
to a more crowded and competitive US market. We note Canada also screens 
well, from our perspective, on a relative basis vs. the US following the elections - 
though management stressed these potential projects were already in flight. 
Data centers are the core focus, with TRP targeting initial deals (say, 300 MW) as 
proofs-of-concept in the immediate term (1-2 by the 4Q26 earnings release) - 
and ramping into multiple larger deployments over time. TRP plans to own and 
operate the power and affiliated gas pipeline laterals, offering hyperscalers (with 
whom conversations are already progressing) a fully-wrapped land and power 
package anchored by a take-or-pay PPA. Management expects to partner with

NGTL negotiations are progressing and could drive meaningful growth over 
n

system, which dominates the WCSB gas market. Recall, the system is governed by a 
decades-old regulatory structure that offers a consistent, though relatively low, 
cost-of-service return model for TRP. Specifically, there is limited ability for different 
rate structures on the system, despite a very broad geographic reach and diverse set 
of customers: in effect, highly-profitable and well-funded oil sands producers and 
thinner-margin sub-scale dry gas producers have the same terms, despite very 
different priorities / different willingness to pay. TRP is currently working through a 
potential new negotiated settlement on top of the current structure (all >300 
customers would need to agree), which it is calling the “new investment framework” 
and would follow its recent “multi-year growth plan”.  Customer interest over the 
summer suggested a range of C$6b of capital spend over 5 years to C$20b over 10 
years. TRP is evaluating these asks while pushing for higher returns on its side, as 
NGTL returns continue to screen lower than US gas projects (see below) or the 
potential Canada power set mentioned above. While ongoing, we saw the recent 
announcement of the potential Alberta Growth Connector project (which would 
effectively directly connect gas supply on the northwest side of the system to oil 
sands demand in the northeast via a northern line, effectively completing a loop) as 
a signal from TRP that it remains committed to growth in the region. We believe the 
project could move forward at C$3-4b, with targeted returns being slightly higher 
(100-200bps) above the current NGTL structure and in-service coming later this 
decade if negotiations can be completed in the near-term (1H27).  While it would be 
modestly disappointing for medium-term growth upside if this process were to fail, 
we see longer-term upside potential in that scenario for 2029+ (when the overall 
NGTL rate settlement expires) if this friction with shippers can drive a wholesale 
restructuring of the regulated structure.  
The US gas pipeline growth outlook remains robust. Management noted US gas 
n
sanctioning has stepped up meaningfully, from ~US$1b/year historically to ~US$2b 
in 2025 and ~US$4b in 2026, with a goal of a higher level in 2027. Gas demand 
overall continues to inflect: interconnect requests on TRP’s US gas pipeline systems 
reached ~190 through 3Q26, vs. 140 in 2025 and 85 in 2024. TRP noted there had 
been no meaningful shift in their outlook following the elections, with data center 
developers having already started refining their strategies around community 
engagement, etc, and with new regulations generally appearing manageable. 
Management also stressed they see coal-to-gas switching as a key secular driver of 
demand, with 26 coal plants near their assets set to retire by 2030. Permitting

There are multiple paths for TRP to convert the backlog into sanctioned growth, 
n
with the high-end of the recently guided 5-7% EBITDA CAGR to 2031 appearing 
increasingly likely. Recall, with 3Q26 results, TRP updated its sanctioned and 
pending approval project backlog to C$30b (24/6 split) and announced a C$40b set 
of projects in origination. TRP also extended its 5-7% comparable EBITDA CAGR to 
2026-2031 (from 2025-2028 prior). The lower-end (5%) is already effectively 
reached, with TRP needing to execute on just the currently sanctioned and pending 
approval bucket. To move higher, TRP would need C$6b of projects from the 
origination bucket to hit the midpoint and C$12b to hit the high-end. This 
incremental spending would largely fall in 2029-31 and amount to a shift from C$6b 
spend/yr to C$8b (midpoint) or C$10b (high-end). The power opportunity and/or 
NGTL upside could fill much of this required wedge, in addition to strong incremental 
US gas pipeline wins - suggesting, to us, that the high-end could be very achievable. 
Note the new range implies ~C$15.92b of 2031 EBITDA from the midpoint of the 
2026 guide, versus current GSe of C$15.51b.  
The ramp in Bruce Power cash distributions early next decade provides 
n
considerable funding flexibility. Management appears to see the high-end of their 
targets (7% CAGR to 2031 with spend ramping to C$10b/yr) as a relatively optimal 
outcome - both because it would allow overall growth to be strong but generally 
ratable and as it would allow for comfortable self-funding out of 
internally-generated cash flow. Management was also - definitively - clear that they 
continue to see 4.75x leverage (on their calculation) as an inviolable ceiling, and that 
equity issuance is off the table. In that context, conversations focused on funding 
plans in upside growth scenarios - the most likely being if TRP is successful in 
converting on the Canada power opportunity and ongoing NGTL negotiations. 
Management noted three levers. The first was potential base business 
outperformance, as AI optimization / cost savings have started to become 
meaningful on the US gas pipeline side and should ramp further - with Columbia the 
next focus (in 2027), which given its size and complexity, could be material in our 
view. The second was potential asset sales, with ~6 assets seen as contenders

gaps in ~2029-31. Management highlighted private credit (insurance capital) as an 
increasingly liquid and available capital pool, which they would use to bridge that

repayment, and the reason behind the idea of this funding being temporary, is Bruce 
Power. The ongoing MCR program there wraps early next decade, driving C$2-3b of 
ramping cash flow back to TRP. Notably, we see this repayment flexibility staying 
intact even if TRP moves forward with the Bruce C project. Recall, Bruce C (up to 
~4.8 GW) remains in early pre-development, with management continuing to work 
through feasibility, early development, and financing arrangements. Management 
noted Bruce C would ramp as the MCR program winds down in 2031. Long-lead 
items would need to be ordered ahead of construction in 2032+, implying a need to 
FID around 2030. Next steps include commercial terms and technology selection, 
with ~C$300m of government funding secured to date. Hypothetical cost could be 
~C$60b gross, requiring ~C$20b of equity, half of which would be TRP’s share. That 
implies roughly C$1b/year of equity over ~10 years, which we view as manageable 
within the broader capital plan. We continue to see an early-2030s FID, or late 2029 
at the earliest, consistent with commentary from the Bruce CEO at our March 
nuclear symposium. 
Valuation and Risks 
 
 
We have a Buy rating with a 12-month price target of C$102 for TRP.TO and US$73 
for TRP based on a 14.75x multiple on our 2027 EBITDA estimate. 
Downside risks include: 1) Softer than expected execution on gas and/or power 
projects, 2) higher regulatory hurdles for natural gas pipeline and nuclear projects, 3) 
slower than expected deleveraging, 4) higher than expected capex requirements and 
lower return profiles on FID’d projects, and 5) counterparty concentration risks on 
Mexico transmission assets.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
