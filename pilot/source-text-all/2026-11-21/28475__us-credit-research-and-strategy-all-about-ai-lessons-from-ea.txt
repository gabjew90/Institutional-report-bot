All about AI: Lessons from earnings
calls (November 9-13)
Results within our AI Scorecard framework were little
changed last week and remain slightly below last quarter's
levels. Fewer issuers screen with AI tailwind exposure (33%
vs. 38% in 2Q26) or as realizing meaningful benefits (17% vs.
21%), though credible investing remains higher (35% vs.
30%).
AI commentary on earnings calls is widespread, but uneven in substance. While AI is often
presented as a clear positive, the nature, credibility, and materiality of its effects vary widely
across issuers. This series is designed to separate meaningful AI effects from earnings call noise.
As shown in Figure 15, issuers are evaluated across two dimensions: AI exposure and adoption.
Using the same universe as our Corporate Earnings Scorecard, each company receives a
qualitative score from 1 to 5, supported by analyst commentary and earnings call excerpts.
The share of issuers screening with tailwind exposure has moved below the 2Q26 level as
more issuers have reported (Figure 1).  The exposure mix was little changed last week, with
each bucket within a percentage point of prior week's level. 33% of issuers screen as AI tailwind
beneficiaries, compared with 38% across the full 2Q26 reporting period. Neutral exposure
accounts for 62%, versus 57% in 2Q26, while 4% screen as facing AI headwinds, down from 5%.
Credible AI investing remains above 2Q26 levels, though the share of issuers realizing
meaningful benefits has fallen (Figure 2). Adoption scores were unchanged last week. 35% of
issuers are classified as credible AI investors, up from 30% in 2Q26, while 17% screen as credibly
investing in AI and realizing meaningful benefits, down from 2Q26's 21%. The share with
minimal or no AI investment plans is 47%, roughly in line with 48% in 2Q26.
