Rates Spark: ECB reserve rethink would
bring funding pressure closer
Longer rates remain sensitive to jittery tech sentiment, while front-ends
face hawkish central bank commentary. A potential ECB move to double
minimum reserves in December would cut excess liquidity by €175bn
and save the ECB €4.4bn a year – but bring pressure on money markets
closer
The ECB looks closer to lifting the minimum reserve requirement (MRR) for banks than previously thought
US dynamics are in the driving seat. The Treasury curve flattened as the front end felt pressure
from hawkish Fed comments and FOMC minutes, with a couple of Fed officials hinting that a
hike of more than 25bp in December could be appropriate.
Away from the short end, Treasuries eye ongoing weakness in equity markets, where solid
earnings by Nvidia were not enough to lift the overall gloom.
While 10y US Treasury yields test their way back to 5.05%, European rates look more range-
bound with fewer domestic drivers to work with. But the ECB commentary is also leaning
hawkish – Vujcic commented that price pressures beyond energy markets would emerge "for
sure" next year, not ruling out more hikes after a likely one in December.

ECB reportedly close to doubling the minimum reserve for
banks
The ECB looks closer to lifting the minimum reserve requirement (MRR) for banks than
previously thought. According to a Reuters report, the ECB will discuss the move next week and
make a decision at the December meeting. The majority of board members are said to be
supportive of the move.
Raising the amount of reserves banks have to keep at the ECB without remuneration from 1%
to 2% of the deposit and short-term liability base would directly save the ECB a bill of €4.4bn a
year at current rates – this does indeed appear to be the primary motivation, reducing the
costs of the ECB's past bond-buying programmes.   
It is not the first time the ECB has mulled this change. It refrained from implementing the
change largely because it is a relatively blunt tool with potentially uneven impact, as excess
reserves are not distributed evenly. We think this reasoning should still hold, but the ECB might
feel encouraged by intermediation in the short-term funding markets running relatively
smoothly, also across borders. But at €2.1tr, overall excess reserve levels are already moving
into a range where the ECB had previously estimated they would no longer be considered
“abundant”, and doubling the MRR would immediately shave off another €175bn.
Short-term funding rates would be closer to the point where they could feel increased upward
pressure. Some banks might find the ECB liquidity operations more appealing – maybe also
part of the ECB's intention. To temper the disruption, the ECB could pair the decision with the
announcement of structural (longer-term) liquidity operations, which have been part of the
new operational framework.
Thursday’s events and market view
Broader risk sentiment likely remains more relevant for longer rates than data
releases. From the US, we will get the initial jobless claims, which have remained fairly
robust so far. The front end will listen to continued Fed commentary, with Hammack,
Jefferson, Goolsbee and Barkin speaking today. In the eurozone, the ECB’s Kocher and
Panetta are scheduled to speak with little other data of note.
Primary markets will see Spain auctioning a new 7y bond and reopening 5y and 10y
lines (€5.5bn in total). France will tap bonds out to eight years as well as inflation-linked
bonds (€13.75bn in total). The US auctions 10y TIPS (US$19bn).
