Our view: WMT delivered solid 3Q results, but investors were largely
already there. We're encouraged by the consumer response to price
investment (highest level of market share gains in 3 yrs) and continued
momentum in advertising, though weigh that against higher fuel costs
near-term. We maintain our 4Q Walmart US comp estimate of +3.1%,
though slightly trim our cc net sales estimate from +6.0% to +5.8% (driven
by FX) and adj. EPS estimate from $0.81 to $0.80 (driven by higher fuel). For
FY'27/FY'28, we now model Walmart US comp sales +3.2%/+3.3% (+3.2%/
+3.0% prior), cc net sales +5.2%/+4.5% (+5.3%/+4.4% prior), and adj. EPS
$2.91/$3.23 ($2.92/$3.23 prior). Our PT remains unchanged at $131 based
on ~40x our revised FY'28 adj. EPS estimate of $3.23.
Key points:
Positives: 1) Walmart US sequential improvement - Walmart US comp
grew +2.9%, up from +2.6% in 2Q. This result was driven by grocery
+MSD (consistent q/q / LFL food inflation +1.4%), general merchandise
+LSD (consistent q/q / LFL inflation +0.3%) and health and wellness -LSD
(consistent q/q / incl. ~8pt MFP headwind). Importantly, mgmt said they're
seeing the highest level of share gains they've seen in 3 years, which we
view as evidence that tariff-related price investment resonated with the
consumer. The team also spoke to a higher retention of share gains over
the past few years, which should help alleviate concerns that tariff refund
investments were essentially bought sales that at risk of disappearing
when rollbacks end. It's also worth noting that, mgmt shared that 4Q
is off to a strong start and rollbacks have already slowed from 11,200
in 2Q to 7,700 in 3Q. 2) Advertising a meaningful contributor to GM -
Walmart US gross margin expanded ~50 bps y/y to 28.1% in 3Q. Mgmt
shared that ~75 of the y/y improvement was driven by advertising with
the majority of the remainder coming from the mix impact of MFP. We
note that Walmart U.S. advertising grew +37% y/y, the 7th consecutive qtr
of 30%+ growth. The team remains confident in the opportunity here and
specifically noted that they haven't even scratched the surface on Vizio/
Vibe.co. 3) E-commerce near breakeven ex advertising - WMT said that
its e-commerce business is approaching break-even excluding advertising,
reflecting densification (e-comm penetration ~25%) and ongoing cost
efficiencies improving incremental margins. Mgmt shared that a key
enabler of further improvement is the forward deployment of marketplace
inventory in stores (currently piloting in a couple hundreds stores ahead

~68-70% of stores) freeing up space in store backrooms.Negatives: 1)

Valuation
Our $131 price target is based on applying a 40x multiple to
our CY’27E EPS estimate of $3.23. While this multiple screens
expensive relative to history, the company has changed its
business model. With more profit deriving from advertising
and membership income, we’d argue that COST and AMZN
(both of which have historically traded at 30x+) are better
comps. Our price target supports our Outperform rating.
Upside scenario
In our upside scenario of $143, we assume that consumer
demand for general merchandise bounces back more quickly
than expected, and/or the profit opportunity from retail
media is larger than currently assumed. In this scenario, we
apply a 42x multiple to our upside CY’27 EPS estimate of $3.41.
Downside scenario
In our downside scenario of $104, we assume that consumer
demand for general merchandise continues to remain soft
while food disinflation/deflation weighs on grocery sales and
tariffs dull profitability. Our downside target is based on
applying a 35x multiple to our downside CY’27 EPS estimate
of $2.97.
Investment summary
In late 2013, Walmart management made the tough decision
that sustained reinvestment was necessary to re-establish
competitive positioning. While this was a painful exercise
(as margins re-based lower), the company emerged stronger
and now has a much more sustainable top-line outlook,
in our view. We also believe the company is pivoting
investment spend away from the operating expense line
(wages + price) and toward capex (automation). This paired
with continued cost discipline and alternative profit streams
(Walmart Connect + Marketplace) should allow for both
operating margin expansion and a steady improvement in
ROIC. Our price target supports an Outperform rating.
Risks to rating and price target
Many general business/economic conditions could impact
results. The general health of the economy and the
consumer could pressure Walmart’s results. While ~55% of
the company’s sales stem from grocery items, it offers a
wide assortment of more discretionary items as well. As
such, we believe that changes in consumer sentiment could
affect the company’s performance (e.g., an increase in fuel
prices could negatively impact consumer spending). Lower
consumer spending levels and job losses could adversely
impact Walmart’s results.
Intensifying competition in the digital battleground. Growing
consumer demand for online services has resulted in
increased competition from existing competitors with digital
operations as well as online-only players like Amazon further
intensifying competition. In addition, online investments may
also continue to negatively impact profitability.
Margin degradation from inflation. Inflationary pressures
have impacted the entire supply chain. If Walmart is unable to
mitigate these cost headwinds, shares will likely come under
pressure.

Key sustainability questions
This section is intended to highlight key sustainability discussion points relevant to this company, as well as our views on the outlook. Both the
questions we highlight and our responses will evolve over time as the dialogue between management, analysts and investors continues to
advance. We welcome any feedback on the topics.
Our view
What are the most material
sustainability issues facing this
company?
While Walmart has historically seen pressure to increase worker pay, its recent
actions in this area have largely alleviated these issues, in our view. Since 2015,
Walmart has raised minimum starting wages by more than 50% to $11/hr (while
average hourly wage will soon exceed $15.25/hr), provided expanded benefits, and
offered new training programs to employees.
Does the company integrate
sustainability considerations into its
strategy?
Walmart’s mission is to help people live better by saving them money. To achieve this
in the long run, the company states that it must focus on sustainability as a key part
of its strategy. From a management perspective, Kathleen McLaughlin is Walmart’s
Chief Sustainability Officer and is responsible for Walmart’s sustainability efforts.
The company reports along United Nations SDGs, SASB, and GRI guidelines.
What is diversity like at the board/
management level?
Walmart has a diverse board, in our view, with women comprising 27% of the board.
Further, in FY'25 43% of management promotions went to people of color and 43%
to women (in the US).
What is the company’s strategy to
engage in climate action?
We believe Walmart is making solid progress toward reducing its impact on the
environment. The company estimates that 49% of its operations are powered
by renewable energy; it targets 100% by 2035. Additionally, Walmart established
Project Gigaton, an industry-wide initiative to avoid one billion metric tons of
emissions by 2030. As of its latest report, the project has seen over 2,300 suppliers
contribute to avoiding more than 136 million metric tons of CO2 emissions in total.
How is the company providing
economic opportunity for
employees?
In 2015, Walmart launched an effort to strengthen jobs and advancement
opportunities for its employees. The program’s initiatives include increasing
minimum starting pay (up more than 50% from 2015), expanding benefits (including
expanded parental leave), and training programs (more than 279,000 associates
were trained through Walmart Academy in the U.S. in 2025 alone). Further, the last
five years have seen Walmart and the Walmart Foundation contribute more than
$130 million to programs that support retail employees gaining new skills.
How is the company encouraging
product quality and safety?
We believe that Walmart provides important health care offerings for many
consumers, with more than 5,100 retail pharmacies, 3,500 vision centers, and 470
hearing centers across Walmart and Sam’s Club. Additionally, the company employs
more than 16,000 pharmacists and nearly 33,000 pharmacy technicians. Further,
Walmart requires its medium and large private brand suppliers to achieve stringent
food safety standards.

Company description
Walmart opened its first location in 1962 in Arkansas and is now the largest retailer on the planet, serving nearly 260 million
customers per week through its more than 10,500 stores across more than 25 countries and e-commerce websites in 11 countries.
The company’s long-term strategy has revolved around offering customers the lowest prices on a wide range of goods and
enabling “one-stop shopping” through the development and expansion of its Supercenter format (~150,000 sq. ft. or larger), where
consumers can find everything from fresh grocery items to consumer electronics and apparel. At the end of 2023, Walmart’s
U.S. segment consisted of ~4,600 stores, including Supercenters, Discount Stores, and Neighborhood Markets and accounted for
~69% of its overall sales. The company opened its first Sam’s Club, a membership-only warehouse club named for its founder, Sam
Walton, in 1983. Sam’s Clubs accounted for ~13% of overall sales in 2023 and ended the year with 599 locations. Walmart began
its international expansion in Mexico in 1991 and now operates ~5,300 stores in more than 18 countries outside the US, with
numerous store formats in the retail, wholesale, and other (i.e., drug and convenience store) categories, as well as e-commerce
websites in 19 countries. In 2023, the International segment accounted for ~18% of overall revenues.

Walmart Inc.
Valuation
Our $131 price target is based on applying a 40x multiple to our CY’27E EPS estimate of $3.23. While this multiple screens expensive
relative to history, the company has changed its business model. With more profit deriving from advertising and membership
income, we’d argue that COST and AMZN (both of which have historically traded at 30x+) are better comps. Our price target
supports our Outperform rating.
Risks to rating and price target
Many general business/economic conditions could impact results. The general health of the economy and the consumer could
pressure Walmart’s results. While ~55% of the company’s sales stem from grocery items, it offers a wide assortment of more
discretionary items as well. As such, we believe that changes in consumer sentiment could affect the company’s performance
(e.g., an increase in fuel prices could negatively impact consumer spending). Lower consumer spending levels and job losses could
adversely impact Walmart’s results.
Intensifying competition in the digital battleground. Growing consumer demand for online services has resulted in increased
competition from existing competitors with digital operations as well as online-only players like Amazon further intensifying
competition. In addition, online investments may also continue to negatively impact profitability.
Margin degradation from inflation. Inflationary pressures have impacted the entire supply chain. If Walmart is unable to mitigate
these cost headwinds, shares will likely come under pressure.
