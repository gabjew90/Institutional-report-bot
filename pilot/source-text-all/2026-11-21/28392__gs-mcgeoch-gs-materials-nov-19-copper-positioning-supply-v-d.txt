BHP - Copper SA day on Nov 23, the Phase 1 expansion to ~500ktpa. Phase 1 is already in our base case forecasts,
and forms ~1/3 of BHP's long run 3-4% Cu Eq production growth target. What we are watching for 
1. 1. The capital budget: last estimate for Phase 1 was US$10-11.6bn vs. our modelled ~US$11bn. We think capex
will likely peak at ~US$3bn p.a. in the early 2030s. 
2. 2. Operating costs: we think mining costs at Olympic Dam & Carrapateena can be reduced by ~40% with block
caving and flexibility of the 2-stage smelter. 
3. 3. Project returns: we calculate average returns (IRR) of ~20% for the Phase 1 block caves and smelter capex
at our long run ~US$5.2/lb (real $) copper price (note: ~15% IRR at consensus prices). 
4. 4. Smelter expansion & design: the expansion will include the installation of a new primary Flash Smelting
Furnace (FSF) to produce copper matte, changing the existing direct blast furnace (DBF) to a flash converting
furnace (FCF) + acid plant & refinery expansion. 
5. 5. Block cave design and risks: Around 2/3rds of ore mined at Olympic Dam is from the Southern Mining Area
(SMA) and the mine is currently operating at ~11Mtpa (ore). We think the block cave will be deeper and offset to
the south-west to the current sub level open stoping operation. Based on standard industry geotech
measurements & data, Olympic Dam is deemed very strong to extremely strong/highly competent. 
6. 6. Uranium: becomes increasingly refractory with depth in the SMA. We therefore expect BHP would need to
treat the flotation tailings in an autoclave to improve uranium recoveries and boost production. 
7. 7. Phase 2 details (NOT in GS base case): Is a further expansion realistic? Includes a proposed development of
a ~10-12Mtpa block cave at the nearby Oak Dam/Ankata deposit (deep and has complex geology) with first
production around 2038, and a 2nd block cave at Olympic Dam "Deeps".
