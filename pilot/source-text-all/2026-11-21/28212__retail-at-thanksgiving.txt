Holiday resilience is already the base case

As Thanksgiving approaches (and with it Black Friday and Cyber Monday), the US shopping season has arrived. The
National Retail Federation expects another resilient US holiday season, albeit short of a consumer boom. They
forecast November–December retail sales at a record $1.013tn in 2026, up 4.2% versus 3.1% growth in 2025. After
accounting for inflation, roughly half of that projected increase would represent real growth, suggesting underlying
demand remains supportive but considerably softer than the headline increase.
Beneath the headline, however, the consumer cushion is thinning. Real disposable income fell 0.3% month-on-
month in September, real consumption was broadly flat and the saving rate declined to 3.8%. Households are still
spending, but with less room for manoeuvre and greater sensitivity to prices, promotions and employment.
For equities, resilient holiday demand is increasingly the base case rather than a catalyst. With aggregate spending
already expected to reach record levels, the key distinction is likely to be at the company level: which retailers can
convert demand into traffic, market-share gains and margins. That leaves a stronger case for selectivity within Retail
than for broad sector exposure into the holiday season.
Today, we look beyond the holiday narrative, assessing Thanksgiving seasonality, consumer confidence, Retail
performance, earnings trends and technical indicators before turning to the growing divergence between price and
earnings expectations and concluding with a screen of US Retail names where positive EPS revisions have yet to
translate into stronger share-price performance.
Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 18/11/2026.
Retail has historically lost momentum after Thanksgiving
A strong holiday season has not necessarily translated into stronger relative performance for US retailers around
Thanksgiving. 

As illustrated by the red line in the chart below, the equal-weighted Retail index tends to improve relative to its
broader market peer ahead of the event, before losing momentum as the peak shopping period begins. Average
relative performance then falls to a trough of around −2.3% by the end of January, while the share of positive
observations remains below 50% across much of the post-Thanksgiving period.
The historical pattern therefore points to stronger relative performance in anticipation of Thanksgiving than in the
weeks immediately following it.

US consumer confidence remains subdued, with signals diverging
Beyond seasonal patterns, the consumer backdrop remains fragile. Both major US confidence surveys sit well below
their late-2024 levels, although they are telling somewhat different stories. The Conference Board Expectations
Index, which focuses on business conditions, employment and income, rebounded to 72.6 in October from 65.7 in
September and has been broadly range-bound through much of 2026. Michigan sentiment, which takes a broader
view of household finances and buying conditions, has been weaker, falling for three consecutive months to 48.0 in
October from 55.2 in July.
Taken together, the picture looks subdued rather than outright deteriorating. However, households remain ever-
sensitive to inflation and rising borrowing costs, leaving employment and income trends important for
consumption as the holiday season gets underway.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 18/11/2026.
US Retail has rebounded, but relative weakness remains
Despite subdued consumer confidence and the prospect of higher borrowing costs, US Retail equities recently
recovered some ground after several months of relative underperformance. This is illustrated below: the XRT/S&P
500 equal-weight ratio rebounded from a September low of 0.387 to around 0.420 in early November, reversing part
of the summer decline.
More recently, however, that momentum has faded, the ratio slipping back to 0.409 by 17 November. The broader
trend therefore remains weak, even as the September lows have held so far. 
Combined with a still-subdued consumer backdrop and an uncertain path for borrowing costs, the recent rebound
looks more like an improvement in relative momentum than a clear reversal of the broader trend.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 18/11/2026.
Moreover, technical momentum has cooled after the recent rebound
The recent recovery in US Retail has also been reflected in momentum indicators. The 14-day Relative Strength Index
(RSI) rose from below 40 in September to around 70 in early November, briefly reaching overbought territory as
Retail rebounded from its September lows.
Momentum has since cooled, with the RSI falling back to 48.2 by 17 November. This puts the indicator back in
neutral territory and suggests that the strong momentum behind the early-November rebound has faded, and
without yet pointing to an oversold condition:

Retail leadership has fragmented beneath the surface
The Retail recovery is no longer a simple consumer trade-down story. Mid-Market led over six months, gaining
35.6%, with Discount Retail also strong at +28.8%. More recently, leadership has shifted toward Premium Value, up
15.5% over three months versus +10.2% for Mid-Market.
Off-Price has moved sharply in the other direction, falling 16.6% over three months and 9.1% over six months. That
leaves the subgroup 44.7 percentage points behind Mid-Market over the six-month period. The dispersion within
Value is therefore more revealing than a simple Value-versus-Mid-Market comparison.
The question is whether the Off-Price weakness reflects deteriorating fundamentals or an expectations reset.
Premium Value has gained momentum more recently, while Mid-Market and Discount Retail retain the strongest
six-month performance. Off-Price now stands out as the clearest dislocation, leaving comps, transaction growth,
merchandise margins and guidance important in determining whether the fundamental picture is also turning.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 18/11/2026.
Good results are no longer enough
Turning to Q3 reporting, Retail earnings remain resilient, but investors appear to be becoming more selective about
what they reward. 
Across the S&P Retail Select Industry, 56% of reported companies have beaten expectations and 55% have seen a
positive price reaction, even as the aggregate earnings surprise remains negative at −2.1%.
Beneath the headline, dispersion is wide. Consumer Staples Distribution & Retail has delivered +7.7% blended
earnings growth and an +8.3% aggregate surprise, with 57% of companies beating estimates. Yet only 38% have seen a
positive price reaction. Specialty Retail has been better received, with +7.7% earnings growth, +2.8% surprises and
positive reactions from 58% of companies.
The message heading into the holiday season is that a headline beat may not be enough. Traffic, margins, inventory
and guidance are increasingly important in distinguishing which results the market rewards.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 18/11/2026.
US retailers' Q3 earnings growth remains uneven
The latest earnings season confirms a mixed picture for US retailers. With 42 of 74 companies having reported, the
S&P Retail Select Industry shows blended EPS growth of 2.4%, compared with 2.2% among companies that have
already published results. Beneath the headline, dispersion remains significant, with Carvana (+97.7%), Target
(+48.8%) and Costco (+15.0%) posting strong earnings growth, while Coupang (−649.7%) and Tractor Supply (−13.7%)
recorded declines.
The divergence highlights an uneven earnings recovery across the sector. While several retailers continue to deliver
solid growth, others remain under pressure, reinforcing the importance of company-specific fundamentals rather
than broad sector trends. With more than 40% of companies yet to report, the overall earnings picture remains
incomplete.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 18/11/2026.
US retailers' Q3 sales growth paints a stronger picture
The top-line picture looks healthier than earnings. With 57% of companies having reported, the S&P Retail Select
Industry Index is tracking blended sales growth of 7.9%, compared with 10.0% for companies that have already
reported. Growth remains broadly positive across the largest retailers, led by Carvana (+42.4%), Murphy USA
(+34.8%) and CarMax (+19.5%), while Amazon (+12.1%) and Costco (+11.1%) have also delivered double-digit growth.
The gap with earnings is important. Demand remains relatively resilient, but stronger sales are not translating
evenly into earnings, putting more focus on margins and operating leverage. Heading into the holiday season, the
ability to convert top-line growth into profitability is likely to matter as much as the sales numbers themselves,
particularly as market reactions to results remain selective.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 18/11/2026.
Retail's price recovery has run ahead of earnings revisions
Forward-looking earnings revisions tell a less supportive story than recent price performance. After peaking at
around 42% in June, the Earnings Revision Ratio (ERR), which measures the balance between upward and
downward estimate revisions, has steadily deteriorated. The ratio fell from 21.7% in early October to −7.0% by 13
November, meaning downward revisions now outnumber upward revisions. This while Share prices have moved in
the opposite direction. The sector's 1-year return recovered from −5.0% in early October to +13.8% by mid-November,
even as earnings revisions continued to weaken. The divergence suggests that the recent Retail rebound has yet to be
matched by an improvement in earnings expectations, reinforcing the more cautious momentum picture
highlighted earlier.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 18/11/2026.
US Retail: momentum may have faded, but the medium-term uptrend remains intact
As we’ve discussed, the recent recovery in Retail equities has yet to find support in earnings revisions, while
technical momentum has also cooled. From a technical standpoint, however, the sector benchmark ETF (XRT)
nevertheless remains in a medium-term uptrend, despite another failed break above its previous highs. The latest
rally stalled around the 76.4% Fibonacci retracement at 93.33, with the rejection below 94.93 marking a third
unsuccessful test of this resistance area.
Momentum has normalized from overbought levels, but XRT remains above its rising 50-day SMA at 86.14 and 200-
day SMA at 85.71. The broader technical structure therefore remains constructive despite the recent loss of
momentum.
The 88.29–85.71 range now represents an important support zone. A pullback that holds this area and forms a higher
low would reinforce the medium-term uptrend, while a sustained break below the 200-day SMA would weaken the
recovery and bring 81.65 into focus. On the upside, a confirmed break above 94.93 would challenge the recent
sequence of failed breakouts, with 104.89 representing the next major resistance level.

The pullback may matter more than the holiday narrative
The 2026 holiday season should remain supportive for US Retail, but resilient spending is already widely expected.
Thanksgiving has historically provided an inconsistent seasonal signal, while XRT's recent rebound has yet to be
matched by improving earnings revisions. Technically, momentum has also cooled after another failed test of
resistance. The 88–86 area therefore remains important: a pullback that holds this support zone and establishes a
higher low would reinforce the medium-term recovery, while a sustained break below the 200-day SMA would
weaken the technical picture.
Beneath the index, dispersion is increasingly important. Mid-Market has led over six months, Premium Value has
gained momentum more recently, and Off-Price has moved sharply in the opposite direction. TJX, Ross Stores and
Burlington have all sold off, raising the question of whether the weakness reflects deteriorating fundamentals or an
expectations reset.
That distinction is clearest at the company level. Ross shows a notable divergence between earnings revisions and
share-price performance, while TJX combines a significant derating with positive estimates. Burlington's deeper sell-
off presents a less clear-cut picture, leaving greater weight on subsequent earnings, margins and guidance in
assessing whether fundamentals support a stabilisation.

The Equity quantitative grades
Our equity quant process is one by which we rank and score
the stocks within our equity universe according to various
short-term (i.e. EPS growth) long-term (i.e. quality) and
positioning (i.e. technical) characteristics, and on relative
and/or historical basis as appropriate. The various scores
make up a stock’s total or ‘global' grade’:Growth Grade: The
Growth Grade is a proprietary formula made up of earnings
revision momentum, past earnings growth, earnings stability,
and current and long-term earnings growth.  A grade above 55
is considered bullish on a 3-month basis, bearish below 45 and
neutral between 55 and 45.
EPS Grade: The EPS Grade is a proprietary formula consisting of
current and forward EPS growth, change and surprise data.  An
EPS grade above 60 or below 40 is considered predictive for
future out/under performance.Sales Grade: The Sales Grade is a
proprietary formula made up of current and next year’s sales
momentum, past sales growth, sales stability, and current and
long-term sales growth. A grade above 55 is considered bullish on
a 3-month basis, bearish below 45, and neutral between 55 and
45.
Value Grade: The Value Grade is a proprietary formula made up
of estimated P/E, P/B, P/S and P/CF ratios. 40% of the grade is
based on historical values and 60% on current market data.  A
grade above 55 suggests a stock is cheap, below 45 expensive,
and neutral between 55 and 45.
Quality Grade: The Quality Grade is a proprietary formula that
focuses on the balance sheet (i.e. change in accruals, change in
free cash flows and profitability). A grade above 55 suggests a
healthy balance sheet.Credit Grade: The Credit Grade focusses
on the passive side of the balance sheet.  It is divided into three
sub-components to assess both short and long-term solvency.  A
grade above 55 suggests a strong capital structure, while a grade
below 45 suggests a weak one.
Money Flow Grade: The Money Flow Grade is a proprietary
formula that gives the accumulation/distribution based on the
volume flows of a stock. A grade above 55 indicates good money
flow and a grade below 45 suggests weak money flow.Smart
Sentiment Grade: The Smart Sentiment grade is a contrarian
indicator based on investor positioning measures such as the
days-to-cover ratio, the put-call ratio, and the short-interest
ratio.  A weak grade suggests ‘too much’ optimism.

Relative Strength (RS) Grade: The RS grade measures the price
momentum of a stock over its 1-year price performance.
Global Grade: The Global Grade is a weighted average of the
Growth, EPS Sales, Value, Quality, Credit, Money Flow and Smart
Sentiment Grades.

The Exposure Optimisation Model
The PTS Exposure Optimisation Model (EOM) is a proprietary
model designed to give tactical signals on the prevailing
short-term regional equity market regime. The model is
applied to each market on a mutually-exclusive basis, with
output expressed by way of exposure scores (US; Europe;
Japan and EM); in each case an average of underlying factors
scores with dynamic weighting applied according to the
prevailing structural market volatility regime. Exposure score
ratings are: very bullish (80-100%); bullish (60-80%); neutral
(40-60%); bearish (20-40%); and very bearish (20-0%). Factors
are as follows:
Regime: A combination of technical indicators, moving averages
and a Constant Proportion Portfolio Insurance (CPPI) strategy,
the regime score is designed to reflect short-term market
momentum in the region;
Valuation: Built around the forward price-to-sales ratio, the
dynamic valuation score is based on a calculation that is re-set on
bull/bear market regime changes and calibrated to facilitate
regional comparison;
Economics: The economics score draws on real economy activity
data (i.e. heavy truck sales and shipping traffic), economic signals
from financial markets (i.e. yield-curves) and medium-term
economic indicators (i.e. PMIs and unemployment data); 
Flows: The flows score draws upon both active and passive
weekly EPFR fund flows data giving us an indication of risk
appetite going into the week ahead; 
Market Sentiment: The market sentiment score is built around
classic regional market risk indices (i.e. VIX; ARMS) as well as soft
survey data (i.e. U.of Michigan Consumer Sentiment). The scoring
system is applied in a similar fashion at both regional and market
level, while at sector level we refer only to the Regime, Flows and
Valuation factors.

Technical Analysis
The technical analysis used in this presentation combines
traditional technical tools: graphical analysis (trend lines,
support lines, continuation and reversal patterns) which
determines the tendency, mathematical indicators (moving
averages, RSI, MACD) used as numeric filters and Elliot wave
theory which allows us to build a scenario with target levels
and invalidation points. We also occasionally refer to
Ichimoku Cloud Technical Analysis. 
Elliott Wave Theory: According to Elliott Wave Theory, markets
move in impulse waves – with five sub-waves (numbered 1-5 or I-
V) following the direction of the main trend, followed by three
corrective sub-waves (A-B-C) (example below).  These waves
follow a set of specific rules and are linked to each other by target
and retracement ratios based on the Fibonacci sequence, and the
characteristics of each wave form an integral part of the
reflection of the mass psychology it embodies.
Relative Strength Index (RSI): A momentum oscillator that
measures the speed and change of price movements. It ranges
from 0 to 100 and is typically used to identify overbought or
oversold conditions in a market. An RSI above 70 suggests that a
stock may be overbought, while an RSI below 30 indicates that it
may be oversold.
The Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD): A
trend-following momentum indicator that shows the relationship
between two moving averages. It is calculated by subtracting the
26-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) from the 12- period
EMA. The result is the MACD line. A nine-day EMA of the MACD,
called the signal line, is then plotted on top of the MACD line,
which can function as a trigger for buy or sell signals. When the
MACD crosses above the signal line, it is typically considered a
bullish signal, and when it crosses below, it is considered bearish.

Ichimoku Cloud Technical Analysis: Ichimoku Cloud is a popular
technical analysis tool used to identify potential trends and
support/resistance levels in financial markets. It consists of
several components, including the Kumo (cloud), the Tenkan-sen
(conversion line), the Kijun-sen (base line), and Senkou Span
(leading span).
