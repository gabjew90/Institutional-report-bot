has increasingly focused on applying this framework with greater precision by category, 
country, channel, customer, and consumer segment, particularly in businesses where its 
performance has lagged. Mgmt highlighted several examples of near-term interventions

innovation, sharper communication, and more holistic retailer partnerships have

should support both consumer trade-up and household penetration over time.

Innovation remains a key driver of trial, household penetration, and stronger value 
delivery. Mgmt highlighted innovation as a key enabler of superiority and category 
growth, with the company increasingly focused on being more selective in strengthening 
its core, while also creating bigger opportunities to drive greater trial and household 
penetration. In that vein, mgmt discussed recent innovation across the portfolio, 
including Tide Evo, Tide Boosted, Pampers Prestige, Downy Intense, Gillette Labs Body, 
Braun Nevo, Oral-B iO1, and Crest Active BioCleanser, which illustrate PG’s efforts to pair 
product performance with more differentiated form factors, clearer consumer 
communication, and stronger in-store and digital execution. Importantly, mgmt stressed 
that these expanded capabilities should create more and better innovation 
opportunities at the beginning of the funnel, but should not result in greater portfolio 
complexity or fragmented innovation at the end of the funnel. Rather, mgmt reiterated 
its focus on a stronger core and bigger more, with new ideas needing to be sufficiently 
large, scalable, and relevant to earn investment behind them. PG also continues to stress 
that superiority does not simply mean premiumization, but instead requires delivering a 
compelling value equation across all pricing tiers in which it competes. This remains 
particularly important in the U.S., where the consumer remains pressured and 
competitive activity has intensified, and we believe the company’s ability to improve its 
value-tier offerings without compromising its broader premiumization strategy will 
remain a key determinant of sustained market share gains ahead. 
Productivity initiatives to drive continued gross margin support and fuel 
reinvestments. CPG companies are once again facing meaningful cost pressure, with PG 
expecting ~$1.4bn of after-tax headwinds from commodities, energy, and 
transportation costs in FY27, along with a ~$50mn after-tax FX headwind. As a result, 
mgmt announced a new productivity target of up to $10bn from FY27-FY30, which 
includes accelerated savings across the P&L, up to $1.8bn of annual COGS savings, and 
assumes roughly flat headcount and media spending as the business returns to algo 
growth. Mgmt noted that this target is consistent with the company’s current 
productivity run-rate, and emphasized that the primary objective of these savings is to 
fund reinvestment behind superiority. In mgmt’s view, this reinvestment should support 
growth ahead of the market, which should then generate the P&L leverage necessary to 
drive margin expansion. Indeed, PG delivered a record $2.8bn of productivity across 
COGS and SG&A in FY26, while productivity, tariff recoveries, and pricing helped support 
FQ1 gross margins despite pressure from product reinvestment, commodity costs, and 
unfavorable mix. We believe revenue growth management, portfolio optimization, 
disciplined overhead spending, and productivity will remain key levers to mitigate a 
higher-for-longer inflationary backdrop, while preserving PG’s ability to reinvest behind 
innovation, value, media, and retail execution. 
Supply Chain 3.0’s end-to-end optimization approach via data, analytics, and AI 
remains a key productivity driver. Mgmt highlighted the progress it has made with 
Supply Chain 3.0, wherein it has partnered with retailers to solve for end-to-end supply 
chain optimization in order to drive improved capacity planning, agility, supply chain 
resilience, and service levels. Notably, PG’s approach is not limited to its own cost 
structure, but allows it to identify and simplify costs across its retailer and distributor 
network. Mgmt views its investments toward digitization, automation, data and 
analytics, and AI as key enablers in this regard, including through improved consumer 
insights, consumption data for better planning, manufacturing data for greater 
efficiency, media optimization, and resource allocation. Mgmt highlighted its plans to

highlighted India, Mexico, Brazil, and the Philippines as examples where improved 
portfolio choices, innovation, digital execution, and more focused go-to-market 
strategies are driving stronger growth, shares, and profitability.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2

factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
