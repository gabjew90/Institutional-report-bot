Digital Advertising 
 
The digital advertising landscape remained broadly healthy in Q3’26, with resilient 
advertiser demand, healthy pricing and continued adoption of AI/ML-driven 
performance tools supporting growth. Direct-response/lower-funnel advertising 
remained a key source of strength as advertisers continued to prioritize measurable 
returns, while scaled platforms with differentiated data, automation and measurement 
capabilities remained best positioned to capture incremental budgets. This dynamic was 
evident across GOOGL and META, while RDDT continued to highlight consistently strong 
core advertising demand and significant runway to increase ad load and advertiser 
adoption. At the same time, Q3 results reinforced increasing dispersion in 
company-specific outcomes beyond the broader advertising backdrop, specifically 
around user growth, traffic acquisition, geographic exposure and platform execution. In 
particular, RDDT highlighted emerging traffic headwinds from changes to Google Search 
while PINS pointed to durable reacceleration in UCAN alongside some pressure in 
Europe. Overall, we continue to view the underlying digital advertising environment as 
constructive, with advertising demand remaining healthy, but with performance 
increasingly dictated by each platform’s ability to drive engagement, demonstrate 
advertiser ROI and reduce dependence on external traffic sources. 
Q3 also reinforced AI’s evolution from a productivity/automation tool into an 
increasingly important driver of advertising performance, product innovation and the 
broader competitive landscape. Platforms continue to integrate AI across campaign 
creation, targeting, bidding, measurement and conversion optimization, with GOOGL’s 
Performance Max, META’s Advantage+, PINS’ Performance+ and other emerging AI/ML 
systems helping automate more of the advertising workflow and improve advertiser 
outcomes. At the same time, GOOGL is increasingly integrating Gemini-powered 
experiences across Search and advertising while META expands its AI strategy across 
consumer agents, business agents and its broader Family of Apps ecosystem. As such, 
both companies continue to increase investment levels across related 
infrastructure/compute, talent, and model development. While investors generally view 
these investments as strategic, questions remain around the timing and magnitude of 
returns, the impact of rising depreciation expense on future earnings power, and 
whether current AI monetization trends can ultimately justify elevated capital intensity 
levels.

constructive, though performance across the group remained somewhat 
company-specific, with the quarter reinforcing several of the key themes that have

and the growing importance of AI and agentic commerce. Key themes in the quarter 
included: a) customer acquisition, engagement and frequency remain central to the

leverage remains an important earnings driver, supported by fulfillment efficiencies, 
marketing optimization and technology investments; & c) AI is increasingly moving from

the consumer relationship in an agentic world which in our view has potentially 
meaningful implications for customer acquisition costs, advertising monetization, 
conversion, merchant relationships and ultimately the long-term economics of online 
marketplaces.

Online Travel 
 
The Online Travel sector (BKNG, ABNB, EXPE, YOU, TRIP) demonstrated a resilient 
demand backdrop and solid execution during Q3, with results across the larger travel 
platforms generally exceeding expectations despite ongoing macro uncertainty and 
geopolitical volatility. Key themes during earnings included: a) a global travel demand 
environment that remained broadly stable, albeit with some pockets of international 
softness; b) continued platform expansion and product innovation, including BKNG’s 
Connected Trip and alternative accommodations initiatives, EXPE’s B2B growth and 
improving consumer execution, and ABNB’s expansion across hotels, services, and 
partnerships; c) increasing focus on AI as both a consumer-facing product opportunity 
and a driver of internal efficiencies, with companies highlighting applications across 
discovery, personalization, customer service and engineering productivity; d) continued 
emphasis on strengthening direct customer relationships through loyalty, 
personalization and broader product offerings; & e) companies continuing to reiterate 
commitments to returning capital to shareholders given strong FCF performance. 
We expect investor debates to continue to center around: 1) the normalized growth 
algorithm for online travel, including the durability of recent demand trends and the 
extent to which company-specific product/platform initiatives can drive growth above 
underlying industry levels; 2) market share dynamics across geographies & segments 
(hotel vs. alternative accommodations, consumer vs. B2B, experiences, etc); 3) the

magnitude of future shareholder returns and the extent to which continued capital

Streaming Media  
 
In streaming media, NFLX and SPOT’s Q3’26 earnings results reflected a generally 
healthy operating environment, though underlying trends varied across key areas of the 
businesses: a) continued healthy underlying user growth and engagement trends, with 
SPOT reporting strong premium subscriber growth across an increasingly broad set of 
geographies, while NFLX highlighted opportunities to improve U.S. engagement and 
content performance; b) continued focus on monetization, with SPOT progressing 
toward double-digit advertising growth alongside opportunities across pricing, add-ons 
and subscription products, while NFLX’s advertising business faced near-term 
headwinds from product development and third-party integrations, despite a favorable 
longer-term growth outlook; & c) both platforms continuing to demonstrate underlying 
operating leverage and margin expansion potential, while balancing profitability with 
incremental investments in content, product development and AI-driven personalization 
and discovery. 
For WBTN, Q3’26 results reflected a mixed but improving operating backdrop, 
supported by: a) healthy user growth and monetization trends in Korea, alongside 
continued strength in Advertising; b) ongoing weakness in Japan, partially offsetting 
strength in other regions; & c) continued investment in AI-driven product initiatives, IP 
adaptations and new distribution opportunities to support a return to double-digit 
revenue growth. Lastly, STUB’s Q3’26 results highlighted continued healthy demand 
across the live events landscape, with: a) solid GMS growth and market share 
positioning; b) improving operating efficiency and margin leverage, particularly across 
marketing efficiencies; & c) continued balance sheet improvement and FCF generation 
supporting greater capital allocation flexibility over time.  
Near term, we expect investor debates to remain focused on the durability of user 
growth and engagement trends across streaming and digital media, particularly as 
platforms balance pricing and monetization initiatives with user retention. Key areas of 
focus include the pace of advertising revenue growth, geographic subscriber trends, the 
durability of live events demand, and the impact of content, AI/product investments, 
and marketing efficiencies on revenue growth and margin trajectories into 2027. Longer 
term, industry debates increasingly center on the impact of AI-driven personalization 
and content discovery on platform differentiation, and the ability to translate scale into 
sustained operating leverage and FCF generation, supporting capital allocation 
flexibility.

Online Education 
 
The online education landscape showed encouraging signs of operating momentum in 
Q3’26, with both DUOL and COUR increasingly positioning AI as an opportunity to 
improve product experiences, expand learner engagement and drive greater operating 
efficiency. For DUOL, Q3 provided further evidence that its strategic pivot toward 
prioritizing long-term user growth and teaching efficacy is beginning to translate into 
improved monetization, with DAU growth accelerating to +29% YoY and bookings 
growth accelerating ~400bps QoQ to +12% YoY. Management attributed the improved 
user trajectory to initiatives implemented over the prior 6-9 months, while stronger 
bookings growth provided an early indication that recent improvements in engagement 
are beginning to flow through to monetization. Looking ahead, we see the key debate 
shifting toward the durability of these trends and DUOL’s ability to translate sustained 
user/engagement growth into stronger bookings over time through subscription 
expansion, in-app purchases, advertising and broader adoption of AI-enabled premium 
offerings. 
At COUR, focus remains around building a broader skills-development platform 
following the Udemy transaction, with management increasingly framing rapid changes 
in AI and the labor market as expanding the need for continuous upskilling and reskilling. 
The company believes that as information becomes increasingly commoditized by AI, 
the value of learning platforms shifts toward personalized learning pathways, 
demonstrated skills and verified capabilities that help individuals and enterprises adapt 
to changing job requirements. Against this backdrop, COUR continues to integrate AI 
across its learning experience through products such as personalized guidance, Role Play 
and Course Builder, while expanding role-based offerings such as Skills Tracks. At the 
same time, we expect execution following the Udemy combination to remain an 
important element of the equity story, with the combined platform focused on 
improving its revenue mix, realizing cost synergies and driving structural margin 
expansion.

Online Marketplaces 
 
In the Online Marketplaces sector, Q3’26 results underscored increasingly divergent 
operating trajectories, while the key strategic priorities remained broadly consistent 
across the group. Key themes included: 1) the transition from traditional search-led 
customer acquisition toward AI-native discovery, distribution and workflow integration, 
as search-related traffic pressure remained a headwind [UPWK/FVRR], with companies 
launching & advancing MCP/LLM integrations to embed their marketplaces within AI 
tools [UPWK/FVRR/ANGI]; 2) a continued shift toward higher-value, more complex and 
enterprise activity, partly offsetting pressure in lower-value or more transactional 
cohorts, with enterprise and higher-value offerings generally delivering DD% growth, led 
by increased adoption and wallet-share expansion across the group; 3) continued 
product and marketplace enhancements aimed at improving matching, conversion and 
monetization, with companies advancing AI-enabled matching and search, supporting 
improved marketplace efficiency and customer outcomes [XMTR/ZIP]; & 4) a continued 
focus on profitability and free-cash-flow generation, albeit with differing forward 
margin trajectories, as operating leverage and incremental cost actions supported 
improving profitability [XMTR/ZIP/ANGI], while mix shifts [UPWK] as well as 
marketplace headwinds and continued transformation investment [FVRR], weighed on 
the forward margin outlook. 
Going forward, we expect investor debates to center on: 1) whether AI-native discovery, 
integrations and agentic workflows can scale sufficiently to offset pressure on legacy 
search traffic and lower-value marketplace activity; 2) the pace at which higher-value 
and enterprise offerings can broaden the companies’ growth algorithms; & 3) the 
durability of margin & free-cash-flow generation as companies balance operating 
leverage and restructuring benefits against platform investments, marketing spend and 
evolving revenue mix.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
