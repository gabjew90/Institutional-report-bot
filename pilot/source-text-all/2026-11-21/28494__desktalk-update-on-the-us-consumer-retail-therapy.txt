DeskTalk - Update on the US consumer: Retail therapy
By Varshi Karamsetty
Strong Walmart earnings reinforce US consumer resilience especially after Oct retail sales, and offer a constructive signal
heading into holiday season. That said, beneath the headline strength, there are some interesting differences in spending
behavior across income groups.
We defer to the experts for the read on Walmart numbers - first take from our consumer spec salesperson is that guides were
"dead inline". With Walmart in "investor apathy" bucket in our latest crowding monitor, and extreme valuations (40x+), this
print is unlikely to get investors off the sidelines. 
What's more interesting to us is that share gains in the latest quarter was led by upper-income shoppers with annual incomes
over $100,000. Walmart is historically been associated with budget-conscious customers, so the shift suggests that wealthier
households are also becoming more selective. Management added that shoppers are "feeling some pressure from higher fuel
prices, but overall, spending remains reasonably strong". 
It's a slightly different nuance to what we flagged last week - DeskTalk - The US consumer splits in two, where spending data
pointed to an increasingly K-shaped economy. Walmart's data reiterates that consumer is spending, but higher income
households are becoming more value conscious while lower income consumers remain exposed to higher fuel, transportation
and essential costs. 
Another interesting tidbit - for all the cyber scares this month, Walmart CFO Rainey said that shoppers have been stocking up
on propane, bottled water and camping gear. "Preparedness shopping" has abated somewhat with Rainey adding that it tends
to be a full-forward of future demand. Put together, consumer staples appear to be well-placed, especially as we head into the
holiday season. 
Citi credit card data is improving as of last week, but our economists still warn that spending risks remain tilted to the
downside over the medium term. Restaurant prices were unexpectedly strong in October CPI, rising 0.5%MoM, suggesting
that real restaurant spending could fall in October.
