Next up is the Anthropic IPO, which may also influence 
sentiment around frontier AI revenues and profitability. We 
believe that at least part of the recent rally in US 10-year 
yields was due to equity sellers. 
Both oil and equities have helped the fall in Treasury 10-
year yields since early October’s 5.3%. At just under 4.9%, 
almost one half of the summer sell-off has been reversed.
These levels look fair for now, as levels currently balance 
50bp of expected future Federal Reserve policy interest 
rate hikes and still-elevated headline and core PCE inflation 
with increasingly plausible predictions by most macro 
forecasters of ongoing inflation declines in 2027. With 
headline GDP growth expected to remain firm, near-term 
prospects for range-bound Treasuries, credit spreads and 
the dollar are boring but reasonable, in our view.
The US Congressional mid-term elections demonstrated that 
American voters do not like $4 per gallon gasoline and $6 dollar per 
gallon diesel prices. Exit poll interviews named other significant 
issues that contributed to the Blue Wave (ICE conduct, Trump 
family enrichment, data centers) but inflation and affordability 
topped the list. These are linked in obvious ways to the war with 
Iran, in our view.
Iran and AI remain the key factors driving market sentiment. First, 
Iran. The Brent nearby futures price has dropped $20 to $93 per 
barrel since Halloween. Earlier, markets may have feared an 
upsurge in violence once the midterms were over (the Eurasia 
Group base case), but sentiment has shifted to look for an earlier 
end to the conflict. This may not be as formal as the stillborn 
Memorandum of Understanding, but rumors of new deals have re-
emerged. Separately, even the current standoff may allow 
additional oil price declines. In our view, high oil prices have 
encouraged substitution via accelerated electrification, greater 
supplies of non-Gulf oil production, and even a revival of Mideast 
production that is reaching markets by various means. OPEC has 
just reported that October production was 88%-90% of prewar 
output, with the biggest monthly increases coming from Saudi 
Arabia and Kuwait. Stable or falling oil prices would be good news 
for both headline inflation and the bond markets. The current 
Brent price is only 3% above levels seen last May. The oil impulse 
to inflation is fading.
After a Wednesday post-electoral relief rally, equity investors 
apparently looked at incoming Democratic governors in Texas, 
Georgia, and Ohio, where nearly 50% of data centers reside, and 
sold off the semiconductor names. Semi stocks have since 
bounced, but a datacenter slowdown would alter the profits 
landscape, easing cash flow worries for the hyper-scalers while 
directly slowing chip demand.
