USTs start the session with a modest bid after yesterday’s bull-flattening move, helped by the softer 
Aussie employment figures. The DXY saw two-way price action in Asia and trades around 103.80 after testing 
104. We still see equities and oil as the key drivers of duration for today. The overnight digestion of 
Anthropic’s IPO has been relatively smooth and S&P/Nasdaq futures are back in the green. If that risk-on tone 
holds into NY, it should put a cap on how far the duration rally can extend from here. Meanwhile, Brent is holding 
around $92.5pb despite reports that Trump is considering a new round of strikes on Iran. Further escalation 
that pushes Brent back towards $100pb would quickly revive the bearish-duration trade. The front-end remains 
much stickier. The message from the FOMC minutes was clearly hawkish, with almost all participants expecting 
further tightening and a couple seeing scope for 50bp in December, but that was largely consistent with recent Fed 
speak and did little to shift December pricing. With Hammack, Jefferson, Goolsbee, Barr, Barkin and Paulson 
speaking today, alongside claims and Philly Fed, there is plenty of scope for that message to be reinforced. 
For now, 5% remains the key level in 10Y UST yields. I would be reluctant to chase a duration rally below there, 
particularly if equities and oil prices hold up, and still prefer selling strength ahead of next week’s 2s/5s/7s supply. 
Bunds start close to yesterday’s levels, with 10Y yields around 3.37% after another fairly wide 3.35-3.40% 
Wednesday range. Today should again be more about global duration, equities and supply than the European 
data, with French and Spanish auctions likely to keep some pressure on EGBs through the morning. EUR/USD 
found a floor overnight and trades around 1.0975. The more interesting rates story remains the reported ECB 
discussion around lifting the MRR from 1% to 2%. At current reserve bases, another 1% would lock up roughly 
€175bn, but against just over €2tn of excess liquidity this is still unlikely to create any meaningful funding squeeze. 
For markets, I would therefore see this primarily through the plumbing rather than the policy-rate channel: positive 
EURUSD XCCY basis at the very front end and some widening risk to €STR-Euribor basis, but on the latter the 
beta should remain relatively small while excess liquidity is abundant. The key point is that this becomes 
increasingly non-linear as liquidity falls. Lagarde and Elderson are the ECB speakers to watch today, as well 
as whether the OAT supply allows the latest tightening in OAT spreads to hold. 
Today’s focus shifts to BoE speak, with Dhingra and Lombardelli both on the calendar. The tension remains 
the same one we have highlighted in the past. Upside inflation risks are building and wage growth remains sticky, 
but there is no clear evidence of second-round effects. We still think the BoE can afford to wait based on the latest 
round of data. Inflation is expected to pick up into 2027, but the relatively looser labour market vs where we were 
in the 2022-23 shock makes the case for aggressive tightening less straightforward. Meanwhile, 10Y Gilt yields 
rejected the 5.30% resistance on Wednesday, helped by the softer services CPI, with 5.20% as the next level to 
watch if the bid extends. GBP/USD has rebounded and trades around 1.3150 after testing 1.3125. The next 
domestic test will be tomorrow’s retail sales and public finances data.  
Mixed session for Asian equities as the initial Anthropic optimism faded later in the session and bonds 
reacted to the softer Aussie employment data. In Japan, JGBs bull-flattened in what looked like a catch-up
move to USTs and thanks to stronger 25Y+ Rinban results. Meanwhile, USD/JPY rose towards 159.75.
