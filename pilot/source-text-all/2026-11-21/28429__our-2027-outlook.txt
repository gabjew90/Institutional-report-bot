Our 2027 outlook 
 
Our expectations for a weaker dollar this year did not play out. The AI boom has been the big factor – it has 
pushed US real rates higher, attracted massive equity inflows, prompted renewed inflation pressure and a 
hawkish Fed. Still, the dollar could have been materially stronger given the oil shock plus the rise in French 
political risk. Robust global growth, large US twin deficits, and an easing in fixed income inflows have played 
a part, while strength in CNY has also dented dollar appreciation pressure. On balance, though, the "AI 
dollar" is alive and well. 
We are revising our dollar forecasts up for 2027 to acknowledge this exceptionalism. Our forecasts exhibit a 
"higher for longer" dollar scenario that is very unusual by historical standards of mean-reversion. We see 
some dollar strength into 1H27, and then modest weakness in 2H27 that would still leave the dollar very 
elevated. Supporting dollar strength are a return to the top in US rankings for both yield and growth, courtesy 
of the AI boom, as well as persistent equity inflows. The comeback of the dollar as a high-yielder and a sharp 
compression in rate spreads against EM FX is a critical factor in supporting USD strength, even in a positive 
global growth and risk environment, as remains our base case for next year. 
Why do we not have a much higher dollar forecast? There are a few negatives on our mind. First and 
foremost is the dollar's increasing reliance on the AI capex boom. Recent equity market weakness in the tech 
sector has, for now, failed to translate into a lower USD as Fed pricing remains elevated. We have high 
conviction that a more pronounced downgrade to capex expectations would push down US real rates and 
weaken equity inflows, undermining the two key pillars of USD support. We are doubtful a rotation from 
equities to bonds would be sufficient to support the USD in such a scenario, with rising evidence of rotation 
away from US Treasuries to foreign bond markets. Beyond that, we see hedge ratios on USD assets as biased 
upward given the decreasing diversification value of holding the USD unhedged, while downside risks to 
energy prices could also help weaken the dollar. We maintain a negative dollar bias further out on the horizon 
to acknowledge these downside risks and the dollar's large twin deficit position. 
From a regional perspective, the euro is likely to be weighed down by French political uncertainty, and fairly 
aggressive ECB pricing doesn't help either. Yet we think the balance of risks will improve as 2027 progresses. 
French risk premium is already substantial, the election is more likely to reduce than exacerbate political 
fragmentation, and Europe's underlying macro backdrop remains surprisingly resilient. Strong portfolio 
inflows are offsetting the weaker current account, while fiscal spending and corporate health may lift the 
neutral rate from historic lows. 
Asia FX still has plenty of scope for outperformance, which is increasingly important to the USD outlook. The 
AI boom is generating huge regional trade surpluses that are recycled into US assets, reinforcing the “chip 
dollar” cycle but also creating greater circularity and vulnerability to an AI re-rating. We see increasing scope 
for that recycling to weaken in 2027. In Japan, higher domestic yields, BoJ tightening and a policy push to 
mobilise savings at home could encourage capital repatriation, supporting a move in USD/JPY into the 140s. 
In China, capital controls, geopolitical constraints on US investment and greater exporter conversion of 
record trade surpluses should support continued low-vol CNH appreciation. More broadly, any shift in North 

Asian savings away from US assets would remove an increasingly important source of structural USD 
support. 
Elsewhere, after a strong year, there is much less low-hanging fruit in EM carry. Compressed rate spreads, 
heavy positioning and supply and fiscal risks make us more cautious on CEEMEA and LatAm, although 
frontiers and markets benefiting from strong terms of trade and attractive valuations still offer selective 
upside.
