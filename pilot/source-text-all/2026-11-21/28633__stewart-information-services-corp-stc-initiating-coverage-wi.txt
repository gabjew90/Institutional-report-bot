1) Focused on driving share expansion in the coming years 
 
 
Of the four largest title insurers in the US, STC is the smallest player with ~11% total 
premium share as of 1H26 versus ~32% at FNF, ~26% at FAF, and ~14% at ORI. 
Following share losses coming out of the financial crisis, STC has undergone a 
transformation of its strategy, which has led to market share improving from ~10% 
in 2019 to ~11% as of 1H26. STC has pursued an organic and M&A-led strategy 
across its three title segments: 1) Direct, 2) Agency and 3) Commercial. Its strategy 
is focused on expanding share on an MSA basis in the Direct business (often through 
acquisition), improving share in agency through reintroducing the company to 
agents or gaining shelf space with clients, and expanding share in commercial 
through improved balance sheet capacity (increasing capital surplus) along with 
targeted M&A.  
From here, STC has laid out a handful of market share goals that it expects to reach 
in 2028+, including: 1) ~15% share in its direct agency business, 2) 15-20% share in 
the national commercial business, which is within the direct business, and 3) 15%+ 
share in its agency business. With mortgage rates remaining elevated at ~7%+, the 
pace of existing home sales (EHS, to which the residential business is most levered) 
is likely to remain tepid, but we think STC’s title revenue growth can be ~2% in 2027 
(vs. EHS growth of -0.430% and VA cons. of ~3%, which does not seem to reflect the 
updated mortgage market) as it takes market share; into 2028, with Fannie Mae EHS 
growth forecasts expecting an improvement to ~5% YoY, we see STC growing 
revenues by ~7% YoY (vs. consensus of 2.5% YoY). While we don’t model STC 
reaching its market share goals (we estimate ~13% share in 2028 in direct and ~13% 
in agency), in part owing to not assuming further large inorganic activity, as we look 
at market expectations it doesn’t seem that the Street is factoring in STC reaching 
its goals either (we estimate market is pricing in 13% share in direct and 12% in 
agency). So if STC can deliver on its goals by 2028+, it could represent meaningful 
upside potential to shares.

3) Commercial should see continued growth, but we are cautious given energy / data center exposure  
 
 
Growth in commercial title revenue has been a meaningful tailwind for STC, 
particularly as direct residential revenue growth slowed and the commercial 
segment saw improving tailwinds from the energy segment (in part due to the IRA 
and other renewable energy infrastructure initiatives) as well as the build out of 
data centers as a result of AI CAPEX spending. Additionally, STC has been able to 
expand its share in the commercial business through expanded balance sheet 
capacity and leveraging of its existing reputation for its ability to underwrite larger 
/ more complex commercial transactions. While the contribution to total growth 
has slowed from ~65% in 2024 to 18% in 2026E, we estimate that with direct title 
residential revenue growth likely to be slower next year, the contribution from 
commercial growth will step back up to ~70% of growth in 2027E. On a mix basis, 
this implies commercial revenue as a % of title revenue remaining at 16% in 2027 
and moving to ~17% in 2028 vs. the 16% we expect in 2026.  
That said, we are somewhat cautious on the pacing of data center and energy 
growth from here given a) concerns in the market over AI CAPEX return on 
investment and potential for CAPEX spending to decelerate and b) with Democrats 
back in control of Congress, news reports point to different legislative actions that 
would target reductions in energy costs borne by consumers as a result of the AI 
build out, calls for data center moratoriums, and a general negativity towards the 
further development of data centers. While STC has not directly quantified the 
benefit it has seen from the data center build out, it has seen meaningful tailwinds 
to its commercial fee per file (from $12.2k in 2023 to $17.8k in 2026E), with the 
strength partially attributable to the data center build out. So while the commercial 
business should see tailwinds assuming that activity levels remain elevated, if 
commercial revenue growth is slower than we expect, it would be a headwind to 
top-line growth, particularly if residential growth is more muted.

In early 2024, the FHFA announced a title insurance pilot program focused on reducing 
homeowner costs in a refinance transaction. Specifically, the program waives the 
lender’s title insurance requirement for eligible loans with Fannie and is focused on 
refinance transactions given they represent a lower risk population as the title remains 
with the same borrower in a refinance transaction. Within the pilot, Fannie is continuing 
to require that titles are free and clear of liens or encumbrances but doesn’t require a 
purchase or make-whole payment for certain title reps and warranties if the lenders 
don’t obtain title insurance. Instead, an automated title review made by Fannie will 
assess the title risk and, if deemed low risk, lenders don’t need to provide an 
independent title verification and instead pay a fee to the agencies.  The FHFA Director 
has been an advocate of this program (and reducing housing costs in general) and made 
a handful of posts on social media that have weighed on shares of title insurers.

Appendix B: Real Estate Solutions 
 
 
Outside of the title insurance business, STC has driven meaningful growth in its real 
estate solutions segment through the acquisition of different services businesses that 
largely help lenders with the valuation, appraisal, and servicing of real estate. Within the 
segment, STC runs a handful of businesses focused on 1) credit and verification services 
(Informative Research), 2) investment data (Propstream), 3) appraisal (Stewart Valuation 
Services), and 4) property preservation (MCS).  With a number of acquisitions flowing 
through the segment, STC has seen revenues increase from ~$40mn in 2019 to a 
~$785mn run-rate in 3Q26 (Exhibit 56). With the revenue growth of the business tied to 
the housing cycle, we think organic growth is likely to remain relatively stable YoY in 
2027, though STC should benefit from the run-rate benefit of the acquisitions it has 
made in 2026, which should drive low-double-digits revenue growth into 2027 (Exhibit 
58). For margins, the Real Estate Solutions business has typically generated a pre-tax 
margin ahead of the title business, with margins averaging in the low-double digits since 
2022, and while the business YTD has seen some margin pressure due to delayed or fall 
out transactions, STC expects the pre-tax margin to expand to 13-14% in 2027 as some 
of the fallout costs come out.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
