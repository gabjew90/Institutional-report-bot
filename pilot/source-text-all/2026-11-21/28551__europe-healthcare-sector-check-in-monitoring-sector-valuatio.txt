Following 3Q26 reporting for our European BioPharma coverage, we update our 
earnings tracker which suggests continued robust earnings momentum with 
60% revenue/86% earnings beats across the sector compared to 64%/91% in 
2Q26 and 40%/70% in 1Q26, respectively. Notably, GSK continues to demonstrate 
strong earnings execution, having met or beat both consensus revenue and earnings 
at every report since 1Q24. Whilst our tracker represents a lookback analysis, more 
importantly for forward momentum we note positive consensus revenue revisions 
(+2%) and upward EPS revisions of 3% across the sector broadly post 3Q26 earnings, 
and our forecasts suggest the most upside risk vs consensus for UCB and Sanofi on 
the forward.  An improving FX outlook for 2026 was a notable trend continued in 
3Q26 earnings; specifically, Galderma, GSK, Novo Nordisk, Roche and Sanofi revised 
FX guidance favourably. 
Clinical readout execution has moderated as the year has progressed. Innovation 
momentum remains central to our investment framework and whilst execution has 
been solid over the past few years, more recently clinical readouts have disappointed 
with Phase 3 success rate across the sector trending lower YTD compared to 2025 
(60% vs 71% in FY25). Our analysis of key Phase 3 readouts implies a 60% rate in 
2026 YTD which compares to 82% at 1Q26 and 75% at 2Q26. However, we note 
divergence between R&D success and stock performance at the company level. The 
sector’s long-term earnings are largely dependent on the sector’s ability to offset 
patent expirations with innovation, we continue to favour those companies seeing 
innovation momentum and expect a continued relative valuation divergence 
between innovation leaders and laggards. Whilst standardised metrics for measuring 
pipeline health/R&D productivity are a perpetual source of debate within the sector, 
noting companies highlighting their own metrics of success, our analysis of Phase 3 
delivery year to date as well as the forward outlook support our view that 
AstraZeneca remains the innovation leader in the sector based on: 1) the absolute 
number of positive pipeline readouts/approvals; 2) the success rate of key Phase 3 
readouts/approvals with >$500mn commercial potential; as well as 3) the number of 
first/best-in-class/disease assets. Within our coverage, among companies with 
more than one Phase 3 readout, Argenx, AstraZeneca and Roche have delivered 
above the sector-average YTD success rate.

Monitoring earnings momentum 
 
 
Across the sector, earnings delivery was strong in 3Q26, continuing an ongoing trend. 
Since 1Q25, companies have beaten both consensus revenue and earnings expectations 
on c.49% of occasions where both estimates were available. In particular, we highlight 
GSK for the longest-running streak of earnings beats vs consensus expectations since 
1Q24. At 3Q26 specifically, 60% of reporting companies beat revenue expectations and 
86% beat bottom-line expectations, compared with 64%/91% at 2Q26 and 27%/73% 
at 4Q25, respectively (Exhibit 6).  
The 2026 FX outlook has steadily improved since 1Q26, with this trend continuing 
through 3Q26 earnings; specifically, Galderma, GSK, Novo Nordisk, Roche and Sanofi 
revised FX guidance favourably (Roche, GSK, Sanofi and Novo Nordisk guide to an 
average impact of -1.5% on revenue (vs -2%/-3% at 2Q/1Q26, respectively) and -3% on 
earnings (vs -4% in 2Q/1Q26) for 2026). Relatively modest revenue headwinds translate 
into larger earnings effects at GSK, Roche and UCB, with Roche’s 3% turnover drag 
implying a 5% impact on core operating profit and EPS. Conversely, Novartis, Galderma, 
AstraZeneca and Sandoz guidance suggest FX tailwinds, with positive revenue impacts 
ranging from 1-2% alongside neutral to modest positive impacts on core earnings (see 
Exhibit 7). 
Whilst our earnings tracker represents a lookback analysis, more important on a forward 
basis is subsequent revisions. Following 3Q26 earnings, consensus revenue revisions 
have been positive across the group for FY26 averaging +2%, and more pronounced at 
the earnings level with an aggregate EPS revision of +3%. At the stock level, Novo has 
experienced material upgrades of +7% and +12% to revenue and EPS. UCB and Argenx 
have experienced positive revisions post guidance upgrade and 3Q26 earnings, 
respectively. Conversely, Novartis has seen the largest negative EPS revision of -5%, 
despite a +1%/+4% increase in revenue/operating profit estimates. Our forecasts 
suggest the greatest upside risk vs consensus for UCB and Sanofi.

M&A developments  
 
 
M&A/BD within Pharma remains critical for backfilling pipelines and managing through 
LoE impact. On the global level, deal activity in 2026 is expected to be backed by the 
substantial balance sheet capacity of large cap pharma companies, which we estimate 
at approximately $600bn (based on a standardised leverage formula of 3x EBITDA). A 
notable convergence of interests is observed in therapeutic areas such as 
cardiometabolic disease, immunology and hematology (see here). Within EU Pharma, 
Novartis has been the most active YTD in absolute terms, with seven deals announced, 
and GSK leads the coverage in terms of deal value, with a cumulative reported deal value 
of c.$15.8bn. The largest deal within the sector from an upfront perspective is GSK’s 
acquisition of Nuvalent with an upfront value of $10.6bn. 
AI-integrated deals and partnerships remain an important theme, spanning discovery 
platforms, drug delivery and diagnostics. AstraZeneca has been particularly active, 
entering a collaboration with insitro centred on its virtual human platform for causal 
disease biology discovery, alongside its $4.7bn collaboration with CSPC Pharmaceuticals 
covering AI-enabled peptide capabilities and enterprise cloud compute alliance with 
Oracle. Novartis partnered with Orionis Biosciences for its Allo-Glue platform and 
AI-driven discovery engine, while Novo’s collaboration with Vivtex combines oral 
biologics delivery technologies with AI tools for obesity and type 2 diabetes. These 
transactions build on the trend of AI-integrated partnerships/collaborations we have 
discussed in previous research. From a therapy area perspective, Oncology and 
Immunology (30%/27% of the deals, respectively) continue to be the areas of key focus 
in 2026, followed by neurology (9% of the total deals). 
Beyond the headline areas, additional themes that stand out include increasing access to 
China originated innovation, particularly through licensing transactions, continued 
investment in ADCs, T-cell engagers, targeted protein degraders and RNA-based 
therapeutics and a greater willingness to acquire earlier-stage assets and enabling 
platforms rather than only late-stage, de-risked assets.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
