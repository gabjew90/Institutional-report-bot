We hosted nearly 100 companies and 500 attendees at our fifth annual Global 
Space & Defense Tech Summit this week in NYC. Investor interest was the highest 
we have ever seen and in this report, we provide a recap of key themes and 
takeaways across launch, direct-to-device, satellites, orbital data centers, and 
defense tech (space, drones, propulsion) .
Launch & In-Orbit Transportation
Our Launch panel featured Hirefly, Impulse SpacHTHT
Rocket Lab. Panelists 
highlighted an intensifying launch shortage that is creating anxiety among 
customers. SpaceX is essentially phasing out Falcon 9 in 2029, creating a massive 
supply gap the panel believes cannot be replaced by alternatives including multiple 
rockets currently in development (Neutron, Eclipse, Nova, Terran R, etc...). It's also 
unclear if New Glenn can deliver material incremental capacity given its already 
very full manifest for Amazon LEO and US government. And while some new 
rockets may be flying by the end-of-the-decade, the history of launch suggests it 
takes multiple years to ramp up cadence. In particular, some panelists commented 
there could be up to a 100 F9 equivalent launch deficit by 2030 if Starship capacity 
is dedicated exclusively to internal needs (Starlink, Starmind).
Regarding orbital data centers, Rocket Lab has had discussions with hyperscalers 
about collaborating. Impulse questioned the economics for those who cannot 
match Starship's future launch costs ($200-400/kg). Firefly highlighted 
opportunities in space-based supercomputing, building upon capabilities from its 
Scitec acquisition last year.
D2D Connectivity
Our D2D panel featured AST SpaceMobile, Elveo, and Viasat. The D2D opportunity 
is increasingly shifting from proving the technology to executing at scale, with 
spectrum, satellite technology, launch access, and MNO partnerships among the 
key enablers for success. Interoperability will also be important, as widespread 
adoption requires satellite connectivity to integrate with terrestrial networks. 
Against that backdrop, the panel viewed the formation of MNO-led D2D JVs in the 
US and Europe positively, as greater coordination and aggregation of demand 
could support broader adoption and more attractive economics. Longer term, 
panelists noted that the infrastructure being developed for D2D could also address 
a broader set of advanced MSS opportunities, including government and sovereign 
applications, as well as Enterprise IoT.

Access to launch remains a key near-term execution challenge, although the panel

European Space Renaissance
Our European renaissance panel featured OHB, PLD, Seraphim, and SWISSto12T
European governments are facing a heightened sense of urgency to rebuild their 
regional space capabilities, with Seraphim noting that reconstructing the space 
ecosystem from its historically low baseline will require massive capital. This shift 
is accelerated by pressing geopolitical realities, notably the conflict in T
T
which have forced governments to recognize the necessity of regional technology 
control. In response, both the German government and the European Space 
Agency (ESA) are actively urging regional players to invest in domestic space 
infrastructure and establish direct ownership of key space assets. To support this 
transition, countries are simplifying procurement processes while governments 
increasingly demonstrate a willingness to award large-scale contracts to start-ups. 
Worth mentioning: Germany is targeting €35bn spend on space-related defense 
projects by 2030, and OHB believes that these benefits are just starting to flow 
through. SWISSto12 highlighted a growing appetite among small-and-medium-
sized European nations for sovereign constellations.
A central concern remains Europe's acute launch shortage and its dependency on 
US capacity. PLD Space pointed out that Europe has relied heavily on Falcon 9 in the 
post Soyuz era. This launch gap may be pushing European operators to vertically

Convergence of Space & Defense
Our Convergence of Space & Defense discussion included a fireside session with 
the Space Development Agency (SDA) and panel comprised of HawkEye360T
H

results, pointing to bipartisan support, programs in the base budget, and limited

payloads. Looking forward, value is shifting to data and compute. SDA’s priorities

chain faster. With Tranche 2 in production and Tranche 3 in critical design review, 
Tranche 4 adds sensor accuracy, edge compute, faster processing, and extended

Drone Revolution
The Drone Revolution panel included Neros, NextVision, OndasH and Unusual 
Machines. The panel agreed that the drone revolution is increasingly an 
industrialization and supply-chain story, not just a technology story. T
 has 
shown that competitive advantage depends on the ability to produce, replenish, 
iterate, and sustain unmanned systems at scale, thus requiring the industrial base 
to adapt. Panelists emphasized stable, modular product designs, disciplined 
manufacturing, and selective vertical integration in strategically important or 
supply-constrained areas such as interceptors, seekers, and autonomy. Although 
the domestic industrial base is approaching the capacity needed to meet current 
DoW demand, the panel agreed it remains unprepared for another large-scale 
contingency.
T
 represented ~85% of the U.S. drone supply chain as recently as two years 
ago, according to one panelist, and material gaps remain across thermal and optical 
sensors, memory, and other critical components despite efforts by domestic 
suppliers to scale. Counter-UAS is another meaningful opportunity, already 
representing ~50% of Unusual Machine’s business, but the panel agreed that this 
will require further adaptability of the industrial base and layered solutions. 
Traditional defense primes were viewed as complementary rather than competitive

Next-Gen Propulsion
The Next-Gen Propulsion panel includedHFirehawk Aerospace and RocketdyneT
The participants agreed that rising demand across munitions, solid rocket motors, 
and space is making scale and schedule as important as technology. Firehawk’s 
automated thermoplastic propellant process is designed to reduce production 
cycles from months to days, lower labor requirements, and identify batch failures 
within hours, supporting faster inventory replenishment. The company believes it 
could produce ~400,000 units annually at scale, broadly consistent with its view of 
potential demand. Space demand was also characterized as strong and growing 
across the DoW, defense primes, and commercial customers such as Amazon. 
However, capital, demand visibility, and supply-chain readiness remain key 
constraints on capacity expansion, increasing the importance of multi-year 
agreements. Strong domestic and international demand should support multiple 
suppliers, but the winners will be those capable of delivering affordable mass at 
speed without sacrificing reliability or quality.

companies demonstrated distinct sensor advantages: BlackSky utilizes electro-
optical imaging for high-frequency, human-interpretable contextual monitoring, 
T

weather-agnostic imaging. The panelists reported healthy demand driven by 
geopolitical conflicts. BlackSky highlighted significant growth driven by the 
transition of its Gen-3 capabilities from pilot programs to long-term contracts, as 
well as strong sovereign demand supported by a $3bn sovereign pipeline. T
T
is also experiencing rising international demand, largely driven by geopolitical

proprietary high-definition database and real time AI processing to build precise 
models for object detection and classification. Management also aspires to create 
a data-use “flywheel” where customers can build models and query data in real-
time, leveraging BlackSky’s feeds and AI agents, rather than simply purchasing raw 
data. For T
