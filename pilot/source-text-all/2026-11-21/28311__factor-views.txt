In brief
•	 Factors posted a positive third quarter, on average, despite a significant 
reversal within equity markets and unexpected macro developments in 
geopolitics and monetary policy.
•	 Equity value and equity quality performed well globally, offsetting weakness 
from the equity momentum factor.
•	 Macro factors gained, strongest across fixed income trend and fixed 
income, equity and currency alpha factors.
•	 We maintain our positive outlook for factors overall. Equity value and 
equity quality are now historically inexpensive, while macro factors offer 
heightened opportunities. However, we recommend diversification 
among and across factors more than ever, given the potential for market 
disruption and elevated factor volatility.
Overview
The third quarter saw stark divergences across and within asset classes, yet 
factors performed well in aggregate, reaffirming the importance of diversification. 
Global equity markets were positive at the broad index level. Equity factors, 
however, had a dramatic reversal as equity momentum saw a sharp drawdown, 
following one of the strongest multi-year runs, and exceptional performance 
in the first half of 2026.1  In contrast, equity quality and equity value’s strong 
performance was a sufficient offset for equity factors in aggregate to post a
positive Q3 (Exhibit 1).

Factors in focus
Equity momentum: An exceptional run meets an
abrupt reversal
The third quarter of 2026 provided a stark reminder 
that even persistent market trends—such as equity 
momentum’s strong run—can reverse sharply, as 
during July’s sharp drawdown when investors first began 
reassessing the artificial intelligence (AI)-driven rally. 
Equity momentum: The factor’s decline2 came after 
a period of the strongest multi-year performance 
since the dot-com bubble. The equity momentum 
factor had, however, entered Q3 looking vulnerable 
(as we highlighted in last quarter’s Factor Views) due 
to elevated factor volatility, growing dispersion and 
increasingly crowded positioning.
While momentum recovered toward the end of the 
quarter and dispersion levels normalized, historically, 
dispersion levels often don’t just return to normal but 
tighten significantly, reducing opportunities for the 
momentum factor. We recommend investors reduce 
any momentum overweights they may hold without 
abandoning the factor—and we continue to expect 
momentum to offer important diversification relative
to the value and quality factors.

Equity value rebounded in Q3 after a challenging Q2, 
delivering positive returns across all major regions, 
with strength especially in emerging markets with the 
second strongest quarter in 30 years, and in developed 
Asia Pacific ex-Japan (where it recorded its fourth
best quarter).
These valuation spreads provide an important starting 
point for expected long-term returns. Historically, 
periods of significant valuation dislocation have often 
been followed by stronger performance from less 
expensive companies as market expectations normalize. 
However, the current environment is potentially more 
complicated. AI has introduced uncertainty around 
the future profitability of a broad range of companies, 
creating questions about whether some inexpensive 
businesses today are genuinely attractive investments 
or, rather, facing structural disruption. 
Equity quality posted a strong quarter after an 
exceptionally challenging recent stretch, delivering 
positive returns across all major markets except Europe 
and the UK, where quality was modestly negative. In 
some ways, the quality factor’s recovery was the inverse 
of the momentum unwind this quarter. Following the 
Liberation Day equity market lows in early 2025, we’ve 
noted that market returns seemed driven by AI-related 
exuberance rather than company fundamentals. In July’s 
stark reversal, which largely persisted, fundamentals 
reemerged as a key driver of stock price returns.

From a valuation perspective, the quality factor remains 
one standard deviation inexpensive globally, relative to 
its long-term history, and even more inexpensive within 
U.S. markets (Exhibit 4).
The equity quality factor’s inexpensiveness in the U.S. is 
intriguing considering current broad macroeconomic 
uncertainty. By the end of 3Q 2026, U.S. consumer 
confidence had reached multi-decade lows. 
Historically, we have observed an inverse relationship 
between the quality factor’s valuation and consumer 
confidence: Low U.S. consumer confidence about 
future economic growth tends to correlate with higher 
valuations for relatively high quality stocks.
But that relationship has recently been challenged. 
Equity quality declined even as enthusiasm and 
confidence have waned about future growth and tech 
disruption. 
There also remains an attractive level of fundamental 
dispersion (the difference in fundamentals, such as 
ROE) between high and low quality businesses globally. 
That dispersion likely signifies an opportunity to favor 
high quality over low quality stocks. We therefore 
retain a positive longer-term view on quality factor. 
Attractive starting valuations, substantial fundamental 
dispersion and renewed investor attention to company 
fundamentals provide a supportive backdrop. 
Macro factors: A very strong quarter, with gains across 
both trend and macro alpha factors
Macro factors posted an exceptional quarter, mostly 
driven by gains from positioning across fixed income 
markets but also bolstered by performance across 
equity and currency markets.
Trend factors exhibited above-average performance, 
led by fixed income trend factors and continuing a 
generally strong period since the tariff unrest of mid-
2025. In fixed income trend factors, short positioning 
across global developed bond markets profited 
significantly from an escalating rise in yields, driven 
more by rising real yields than inflation expectations, 
as markets priced in more hawkish central bank policy 
and increased bond issuance. 
Equity trend factors, on the other hand, posted a 
negative Q3, hurt most by long trend positioning in 
equity indexes with higher interest rate sensitivity (such 
as the S&P 400 and S&P 600) and/or by exposure to 
stock-level momentum reversals (the Nasdaq 100 or 
MSCI Emerging Markets Index). In commodities, trend 
positioning detracted in 11 of 17 markets; losses were 
highest in agricultural commodities (such as corn, live

cattle, feeder cattle, sugar and wheat) amid disrupted 
Russian and Ukrainian grain exports, the reopening 
of the U.S. border to Mexican cattle and as El Niño

Macro alpha factors posted the best quarter in our data 
set (dating to the early 2000s), recovering losses earlier 
in 2026, led by strength in government bond market 
alpha factors benefiting from the rise in yields. Equity 
index and currency market alpha factors also rose. 
Commodities were a weak spot.
Relative value factors within government bond markets 
were additive as valuation and technical signals drove 
gains through short positioning in German Bunds and 
U.S. Treasuries, more than offsetting losses from longs in 
UK Gilts and Japanese Government Bonds (early in Q3). 
Relative value factors also performed well within 
FX markets, driven by contribution from carry and 
valuation signals which led to short positioning in Swiss 
francs and long positioning in Japanese yen, among 
others, and within equity markets where sector-, 
region- and style-based alpha factors all profited.
Our aggregate outlook on macro factors is unchanged 
at neutral, with opportunity should a reflationary regime 
continue, but at risk if markets were to shift toward 
pricing in concerns about economic growth. 
Trend factors moved significantly shorter in duration 
over the quarter, nearing extreme levels: The average 
signal moved from -0.2 (on a scale of -1 to 1) at the 
beginning of the quarter to -0.8 by quarter end. The 
moves were led by strength in (short) signals across 
U.S. Treasuries and European bonds.
Trend positioning weakened across equity markets, 
with the average signal moving from +0.5 to +0.3 and 
signals moving short across certain markets; however, 
it rebounded across commodity markets, where the 
average signal moved from neutral to +0.2 as strength 
in energy, industrial metals and certain agricultural 
commodities offset weakness in livestock, precious 
metals and other agricultural commodities. 
Macro alpha factors: Our outlook is neutral. Strong 
opportunities in some factors (G10 FX relative value 
and directional equity and rates factors) are offset by a 
weak opportunity set in other factors.  
Concluding remarks
We continue to see generally attractive prospects for 
equity factors, led by attractive valuations for value and 
quality; however, we see elevated risks for momentum. 
We favor diversification, not any outsized positions. 
We rate most macro factors neutral, and again favor 
diversification.
We believe the global expansion remains intact and 
supportive of a pro-risk posture across traditional 
assets, and continue to see factors as an attractive 
opportunity for diversifying sources of return.

Glossary
•	 Equity value: long/short global developed stocks 
based on book-to-price, earnings yield, dividend 
yield, cash flow yield; sector and region neutral
•	 Equity quality: long/short global developed stocks 
based on financial risk, profitability and earnings 
quality; sector and regional neutral
•	 Equity momentum: long/short global developed 
stocks, based on price change and earnings 
revisions; sector and region neutral
•	 Macro trend: short-, medium- and long-term trend 
signals across equity fixed income and commodity 
markets 
•	 Macro alpha: directional and relative value models 
across equity, fixed income, and currency markets, 
based on fundamental, valuation, technical and 
risk signals
