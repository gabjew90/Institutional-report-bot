Intuit Inc.
AI Headwinds Compound, FY27 Revenue Guidance
Lowered
Our view: Intuit reported F1Q upside, with total revenue, Non-GAAP
Operating Margin, and Non-GAAP EPS all above consensus. However, lower
Credit Karma guidance on AI-driven discovery shifts and higher rates,
along with a soft F2Q guide, sent shares down ~(9%) AMC. The Mailchimp
divestiture sharpens focus, and the proceeds will go to buybacks. Despite
near-term headwinds in Credit Karma, we continue to believe the AI
opportunity set outweighs the risks longer term. We maintain Outperform
rating and revise our PT to $330 from $385.
Key points:
• We maintain our Outperform rating but revise our PT to $330 (from
$385) on INTU following F1Q results. Revenue was $4,354M vs. cons. of
$4,307M; Non-GAAP EPS (incl. SBC) was $2.91, vs. cons. of $2.47.
• Our key takeaways from earnings include:
○Decent Q1 beat. F1Q27 results showed upside with revenue (+12.1%
YoY vs. cons. 11% YoY), Non-GAAP Operating Margin (incl. SBC) (24.0%
vs. cons. 21.1%), and Non-GAAP EPS (incl. SBC) ($2.91 vs. cons. $2.47)
all above consensus.
○Mailchimp divested. Intuit agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons
for ~$3.68B ($3.31B cash, $368M BSP stock), with close expected in
early CY27. Management notes the sale sharpens its focus on building
AI-native lead-to-cash capabilities inside QuickBooks. All cash proceeds
are expected to be allocated to incremental buybacks in FY27. We view
the exit favorably as we believe it removes a persistent drag on growth
and management distraction.
○AI headwinds in Credit Karma. CK revenue grew 9% YoY, below
expectations. Higher interest rates hurt demand, and consumers
shifted to AI-powered discovery faster than expected, which weighed
on engagement. Management highlighted that SEO has historically
driven less than 1% of CK traffic, and intends to apply the same
GEO playbook that is seeing success in TurboTax and QuickBooks. The
AI impact was described as limited to CK, and the 4-8% three-year
Consumer CAGR target was reiterated.
○Guidance lowered on tempered CK expectations. F2Q revenue
guidance came in below consensus, with revenue growth of +6-7%
YoY and Non-GAAP EPS of $3.17-$3.22. FY27 revenue was lowered to
+8-9% YoY (from +9-10%), driven entirely by CK, which is now +3-7%
(from +11-13%); that takes Consumer to +2-4% (from +4-6%). FY27 GBS
revenue (+13-14%), non-GAAP operating income ($8,063-$8,145M),
and non-GAAP EPS ($22.88-$23.12) guidances were reiterated, with
lower SBC offsetting CK.
• Stepping back, while Q1 results were decent, updated Credit Karma
guidance will likely weigh on the stock. However, we believe INTU can
be a net beneficiary of AI over time. Shares trade at ~9x EV/CY27E FCF
while our PT implies ~10.7x.

Investment summary
We like Intuit shares for four primary reasons:
1. We like Intuit’s successful transition to a subscription
model. Today, 80%+ of revenue comes from recurring
subscriptions. This has resulted in greater platform
expansion, with platform revenue representing 60% of
total revenue in FY26, up from 53% in FY20.
2. We like Intuit’s market leadership position in tax and
accounting software, with $2T+ invoices managed, 18M
total U.S. workers paid annually via QB payroll, and $105B
in consumer tax refunds annually. We believe as tax codes
and regulations become increasingly complex, this can be
a tailwind for Intuit.
3. We see a major opportunity for Intuit in GenAI, especially
because tax preparation and SMB accounting are very
services-heavy industries and Intuit has an opportunity to
help customers automate a significant portion of the low-
hanging fruit and generate real cost savings. We expect this
to translate to real revenue for Intuit, although we caution
that it could take longer for theory to become the reality
that investors expect today.
4. Finally, Intuit continues to expand margins, and we
estimate potential to see sustainable 40%+ non-GAAP
operating margins and 30%+ GAAP operating margins over
the next several years.
Risks to rating and price target
Investment risks include: 1) macro sensitivity, with Intuit’s
business dependent on small business and consumer growth;
2) execution risk with recent acquisitions, most notably Credit
Karma and Mailchimp; 3) legislation risk, particularly if US tax
codes become more simplified; 4) Intuit’s GenAI strategy may
not pan out as expected; and 5) competition, including from
large software vendors (e.g. Oracle NetSuite, Sage Software)
and smaller upstarts (e.g., Freshworks, Gusto).

Key fundamental questions
Our view
Can Intuit maintain steady revenue
growth in a challenging macro
environment and accelerate revenue
growth in the future?
While we do have concerns about Intuit’s growth in the near term due to the
challenging macro environment, we have been impressed by the company’s ability
to execute. We think that Intuit can maintain steady growth from its market
leadership position in consumer tax and small business accounting, and potentially
accelerate growth in the future with Assisted offerings, Business Tax, GenAI, and
international TAM expansion.
Can Intuit continue to expand
operating margins?
Despite pressure from online revenue ramping (which depresses gross margins),
Intuit has managed to expand operating margins over the past several years.
We see room for continued margin expansion opportunities from operational
efficiencies, subscription momentum driving higher ARPCs, and improved cross-
selling increasing sales efficiency.
Can Intuit monetize GenAI?
In our view, Intuit can monetize GenAI through: 1) easier customer onboarding
experiences from natively embedding AI across the portfolio (i.e., increasing the top-
of-funnel conversion); 2) increasing average revenue per customer through exposing
customers to higher-priced expert (Live) offerings; 3) potential future GenAI product
SKUs (“do-it-for-you” products); and 4) increasing overall customer retention across
the platform from the implementation of free product enhancements. We think the
largest opportunity for monetization of Intuit Assist comes from ease of customer
introduction to (and enablement of) Intuit’s virtual expert network.
Should Intuit divest Credit Karma and
Mailchimp?
We believe management should conduct a strategic review of both assets and
consider a sale. In our view, both assets are highly macro-sensitive and not well
integrated with Intuit’s core products.

Company description
Intuit is a leading provider of financial technology solutions, including TurboTax, Credit Karma, QuickBooks, and Mailchimp,
founded in 1984 and headquartered in Mountain View, CA. Intuit provides a variety of financial management, compliance, and
marketing services to individuals, small businesses, and accounting professionals. Intuit solutions attempt to facilitate more
efficient tax preparation and filing, money management, customer engagement, expedite payment processes, manage payroll,
access capital, and help ensure accurate bookkeeping. Intuit’s customers are primarily consumers and small businesses. Intuit
counts 9M small business customers, 1M self-employed customers, and 90M consumer customers.
