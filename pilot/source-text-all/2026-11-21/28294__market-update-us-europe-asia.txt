U.S. Markets Recap 
US -1.03% | Nasdaq -0.74% | Dow -1.64%
Synopsis

US equities closed lower on Tuesday, though stocks ended off their worst levels, with the Dow down 1.64%, the S&P 500 down 1.03%, the 
Nasdaq down 0.74% and the Russell 2000 down 1.49%. The decline extended Monday's selloff, with S&P decliners outnumbering gainers 
by nearly 6:1, the third­worst breadth reading year to date, and all sectors lower; the S&P 500 also fell below its 200­day moving average 
for the first time since early April. The AI trade failed to bounce, with semiconductors, memory and neoclouds largely lower and Nvidia 
down again, as tighter export controls and AI buildout funding concerns weighed. Treasuries firmed, with yields 2­4 bp lower. 

October retail sales matched headline estimates, but ex­autos and control­group sales beat and the prior month was revised higher. Indus­
trial production unexpectedly declined, import and export prices came in notably higher than expected, and NAHB homebuilder sentiment 
printed at a 14­year low. Fedspeak stayed hawkish, with St. Louis Fed's Musalem saying the economy could require several rate hikes before 
the spring.

Energy, casual diners, telecom and aerospace and defense outperformed, while software, homebuilders, managed care, banks and payments 
lagged. Bill Holdings rose 12.6% on a report that TPG is nearing a take­private deal, and Energizer gained 12.6% on a fiscal Q4 beat. 
UnitedHealth fell 5.4% after CMS proposed a rule to address fraud across Medicare, Medicaid, CHIP and the Marketplace, and Nike fell

Macro

October retail sales in line, though core and control-group sales beat
October retail sales rose 0.2% m/m, in line with consensus, slowing from September's 0.8% gain. Annual growth reached 6.8%, strongest 
since Oct­22. Ex­autos rose 0.6% vs +0.4% consensus (September revised higher), while control­group sales increased 0.6% vs +0.3% con­
sensus. Miscellaneous retailers (+4.7%), online (+1.2%) and gasoline (+1.5%) led gains, while autos (­1.6%) and furniture (­2.3%) were the 
biggest decliners. Analyst reactions were broadly constructive, with Goldman highlighting upward revisions and accelerating real core sales 
growth, though Citi flagged uneven demand. Attention now turns to key retail earnings this week including Walmart, Target, TJX, Lowe's 
and Ross Stores. 

Williams sees no need for aggressive action; Musalem backs several hikes
A busy day of Fedspeak included New York's Williams, who said he does not think current conditions warrant aggressive policy action, 
though inflation remains too elevated. Williams also said the economy is seeing some normalization of financial conditions and that AI does 
not currently pose financial stability risks. Cleveland's Hammack (voter) advocated strict principles for Treasury market interventions, 
including separating asset purchases for liquidity from those intended to stimulate the economy (Bloomberg). St. Louis Fed's Musalem 
(non­voter) advocated multiple hikes to combat persistent demand and supply­side inflation pressures, saying the economy needs a 2­2.5% 
real policy rate by spring to get to the 2% target. Governor Waller spoke on payments, and Governor Cook will not attend a White House 
hearing on a mortgage fraud case, which her lawyers called a facade (NY Times). 

October FOMC minutes due Wednesday after a hawkish hold
Minutes to the 28­Oct FOMC meeting are out Wednesday (18­Nov, 14:00 ET). The meeting ended with a hawkish hold at 3.75%­4.00%, 
with two dissents for a 25 bp hike, solidifying bets on a December hike. However, sell­side previews noted the minutes are somewhat stale 
given firmer economic data since the decision, notably hot CPI and an NFP beat, alongside a hawkish Fedspeak ramp, including Governor 
Waller flagging the potential for a 50 bp December hike if inflation ran hot. BofA expects the minutes to reveal a deeper committee divide 
than the two formal dissents suggested, and its team recently added a third 25 bp hike to its forecast. Goldman Sachs said the focus will be 
on the assumptions underlying participants' economic outlooks and the balance of risks at the time of the meeting, and it has also added a 
third 25 bp hike (in Mar­27) to its rate path. 

Huang willing to return at Nvidia as board rules it out; Q3 results due
Former Nvidia CEO Huang said Tuesday he would accept a board request to return as CEO if deemed in the best interest of shareholders 
and US interests (Reuters). However, the board reiterated that a return is not under consideration and established a board­level committee 
to oversee national security and export controls. Huang also agreed to appear before the House Foreign Affairs Committee on 1­Dec, while 
Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter will appear on 2­Dec (Reuters). Nvidia reports Q3 results on 17­Nov after the close, and the Street is 
widely expecting another beat and raise. The bigger question is current FY28 guidance for ~70% revenue growth, with export restrictions 
and the funding of the AI infrastructure buildout expected to be key areas of focus on the call. 

S&P 500 breaks below 200-dma amid sharp defensive factor rotation
The S&P 500 fell below its 200­dma on Tuesday for the first time since early April, with the market also pacing for one of the worst breadth 
days YTD. Shorter­term momentum continues to fade, as the 10­day cumulative advance/decline sits below ­400 (down from the recent 21­
Oct peak of +921). The recent selloff has marked sizable factor rotations, with low volatility (SPLV) and quality (QUAL) outperforming high 
beta (SPHB) by over 460 bp and 280 bp, respectively, over the past five days. However, BofA noted that while some AI­related themes 
(photonics, data centers, AI compute) rank among the worst performers over the past month, AI groups remain top­ranked on its Triple 
Momentum screen, suggesting underlying earnings momentum remains intact. Mizuho characterized the recent factor selloff as an indis­
criminate buyers strike driven by systematic degrossing, even as token economics and compute demand remain sound.

European Markets Recap 
Germany -2% | France -1.6% | UK -1.8%
Synopsis

European equities closed sharply lower on Tuesday, near the worst levels of the day, with the DAX down 2%, the FTSE 100 down 1.8% and 
the CAC down 1.6%. Technology, miners, luxury and autos were the biggest decliners, while energy and travel and leisure were the best 
relative performers. Tech extended its multi­day sell­off on concerns about AI investment returns and elevated valuations, and miners fell 
as copper was hit by weak China growth data. European government bonds were broadly firmer, with French OATs outperforming Bunds 
as 10­year yields fell 2bps against a 1bp decline in German yields. 

German ZEW for November beat expectations at 35.6 against 28.1 prior. UK unemployment beat expectations at 4.6%, though regular pay 
growth slowed to 3.5% against 3.6% expected. Spain's de Cos emerged as front­runner to succeed Lagarde as ECB president after Knot 
withdrew, and Le Pen confirmed she would end new French financial aid to Ukraine if elected. 

Luxury fell on softer China demand, with Moncler down 4.5% after Morgan Stanley cut it to underweight. Stellantis fell 1.6% after an Oddo 
downgrade to underperform. Deutsche Bank fell 6% on press reports about compliance, Russian­business expansion and Epstein­linked 
payments, which the bank said are largely historical. Dassault Systemes fell 4.9% despite raising its 2029 targets, and Nokia fell 3.8% after 
Nvidia cut its stake by 56% in Q3. Avio rose 14.9% on ICEYE's all­cash tender offer at €41.00 per share, and Evonik rose 4.2% on reports 
BASF is preparing a new takeover bid. 
Macro

German ZEW sentiment beats, highest reading since February
Germany's ZEW economic sentiment for November beat, at 35.6 versus 28.1 prior and 29.0 expected, the highest reading since February 
(Bloomberg, Reuters). The current­conditions gauge also improved more than expected, to ­33.2 from ­35.9, and eurozone sentiment 
climbed to 26.5. ZEW President Wambach said the economy remains on a recovery track, with rising manufacturing orders and lower oil 
prices supportive. He also said government spending has so far been the mainstay of the rebound, but concerns about public­debt sustain­
ability are rising. The print marks a resumption after the October dip, which had been linked to energy costs and a softer consumption 
outlook, though persistent headwinds remain, including elevated inflation, the prospect of further ECB tightening, and a recovery still 
heavily reliant on fiscal support. 

UK labour market mixed, unlikely to shift BoE plans
UK labour market data for November painted a mixed picture (ONS), with lagging figures stronger and more recent signals weaker. Unem­
ployment was the lowest since Apr­2025, at 4.6% in the three months to Sep, yet payrolled employees declined 34K in October and vacancies 
posted their largest quarterly decline in over a year and the lowest level outside the pandemic since 2014 (Reuters). Regular pay growth 
slowed to 3.5% from 3.7%, but private­sector pay (the BoE's preferred measure) held at 3.1% as expected. For the BoE, the data neither 
confirms nor refutes Chief Economist Pill's hawkish case (Guardian), and the release is unlikely to deter a December hike. A clear majority 
of economists nonetheless expect a hold in December and only one further hike, most likely in Feb­2027 (Reuters). 

De Cos likely to succeed Lagarde as ECB president as Knot exits race
Spain's de Cos has emerged as the clear front­runner to succeed Lagarde as ECB president. Reports that France and Germany aligned behind 
the former Bank of Spain governor and current BIS general manager compelled former Dutch central bank chief Knot to withdraw from the 
race (De Volkskrant, Bloomberg). A formal decision is expected at the 26­27 November EU summit. The appointment is part of a broader 
package covering two other Executive Board vacancies, Schnabel's markets portfolio (Jan) and Lane's chief economist role (May), with the 
process held up by a German coalition dispute between Chancellor Merz and Finance Minister Klingbeil over the German candidate to 
replace Schnabel (Eurointelligence). Most observers expect little near­term change in monetary policy direction under either candidate. 

Le Pen's pension plan returns retirement age to 60-62 via capitalisation pillar
Le Pen detailed National Rally's pension reform, aiming to reassure investors regarding fiscal discipline (LeParisien, FranceInfo). The legal 
age would return to 60­62, with 40­42 years of contributions required for a full pension and a maximum age of 67, and Macron's suspended 
reform raising the age toward 64 would be abolished from 1­Jan 2028, with implementation phased over five years. The main innovation is 
a mandatory capitalisation pillar: existing contributions would be reallocated to generate a minimum €12B annual flow into a Caisse des 
Dépôts­managed fund targeting 4­6% returns, applying to workers under 52. Tanguy put the gross cost at €18.6B and the net cost at €10.1B, 
while still claiming €15­20B in eventual annual savings as part of a €140B consolidation target by 2032. The OAT­Bund spread narrowed 
3 bps to 152 bps following the announcement. 

French election campaign intensifies as Le Pen sets out Ukraine stance
The French election campaign is under way, with Attal, Le Pen, Mélenchon, Glucksmann and Retailleau debating poverty, wages, housing 
and pensions (LesEchos). Le Pen confirmed she would end new financial aid to Ukraine if elected while honouring existing commitments. 
Eurointelligence suggested freezing aid would depend on securing a National Rally majority or navigating cohabitation, and that Le Pen 
would be more likely to block new EU loans and plans to cap France's net EU contribution at €5B, from current levels of €10­11B. Goldman 
Sachs still sees Le Pen as the clear favourite, though a parliamentary majority is uncertain, and expects French bonds to underperform EU

Asian Markets Recap 
Japan -1.34% | Hong Kong -1.03% | China -0.50%
Synopsis

Asian equities closed lower on Tuesday, with Japan's Nikkei down 1.34%, the Hang Seng down 1.03% and the Shanghai Composite down 
0.50%. South Korea, Taiwan and the Nikkei 225 again saw steep declines, with the Topix underperforming, and Australia fell sharply as 
commodity prices sank. Greater China boards were all lower, led by IT and internet stocks, while India rose and Singapore was lower. The 
AI trade was weaker, though losses were mild relative to the overnight selloff. 

RBA minutes showed the board debated raising or holding the cash rate in November before hiking, and Governor Bullock said inflation 
risks remain skewed to the upside. New Zealand one­year inflation expectations neared the upper end of the RBNZ's 1­3% target range, 
reinforcing expectations of a December rate hike. Economists called China's October activity data bearish, while Japan's Q3 GDP was softer 
than expected but attributed to special factors, with no major setback to December BOJ rate hike calls. 

China pure­play model developers reliant on Nvidia hardware were among the decliners, with Z.AI and MiniMax down more than 25% 
month to date, while SMIC, Hua Hong and Cambricon outperformed. KE Holdings rose 8.2% and XPeng 4.6% after Q3 results beat esti­
mates, and Pigeon rose 3% on a $373M deal to buy Eleeo Brands. Shanghai Fosun Pharmaceutical fell 9.2% as its AR1001 phase III trial 
met its primary endpoint in the monotherapy population but not the overall population. Elite Material fell 6.7% after denying a media report 
that Nvidia would account for 25­30% of FY26 revenue, and Dentsu fell 3.1% on FY guidance below estimates. 
Macro

US to add restrictions on China's remote access to AI chips
Bloomberg cited a Trump administration announcement signaling restrictions on China's remote access to advanced AI chips. An interim 
rule issued by the Commerce Department would require non­US data center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and 
Brazil to obtain a US government license before exporting advanced AI chips from US manufacturers to customers in those countries. The 
article noted Southeast Asia has emerged as a data center hub with demand from Chinese firms such as Alibaba and Bytedance helping to 
drive the infrastructure buildout, with Malaysia and Thailand the hot spots and Singapore suspected of being used as a destination for 
servers that were rerouted to mainland China. The Commerce Department noted advanced cloud computing has made it increasingly pos­
sible to access US AI computing systems remotely, and the new measure is intended to align with existing rules on physical access to the 
technology. 

US chip export crackdown creates ripple effects for China AI stocks
Intensified US efforts to restrict offshore access to chips and computing power are drawing discussion about ramifications for China's AI 
firms. China pure­play model developers that are heavily reliant on Nvidia hardware and its CUDA software system are among the high­
profile decliners, with Z.AI and MiniMax down more than 25% month to date. Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance, which have a large physical 
footprint across Southeast Asia, also face potential disruption to AI training and cloud operations from the new Commerce Department 
order curbing shipments of advanced chips to Southeast Asian countries. China­listed semiconductor firms have outperformed, with SMIC, 
Hua Hong and Cambricon among a handful of Asia AI stocks trading higher month to date. They are seen as potential beneficiaries of forced 
domestic chip adoption, though production constraints and technological lag have fed skepticism about their ability to displace Nvidia in 
China. 

Inflation concerns drove RBA rate hike, Bullock says risks skewed to upside
RBA minutes showed the board debated whether to hold or hike the cash rate in November. The decision to increase was informed by upside 
risks to inflation materializing, the risk that recent data signaled a more inflationary environment than previously assessed, and whether 
financial conditions were insufficiently restrictive. Those voting to hold pointed to 100 bp of previous tightening still playing out, significant 
weight placed on potential downside risks to the growth outlook, and whether inflation risks were judged to be more balanced than assessed.

Economists bearish on October China activity data
Economist takeaways for October activity data were bearish. While the K­shaped dynamic persists, the divergence is widening to a point 
where growth drivers are not enough to outweigh the drags. Softer retail growth was seen as payback for government subsidies, and an 
earlier than usual Mid­Autumn Festival may have brought forward some demand to September from October. Much of the attention went 
to lagging fixed asset investment, with some sense that expected support from the fiscal pipeline was delayed. Global AI demand has yet to 
permeate the domestic economy against the backdrop of a growing overhang from US tech restrictions, and JPMorgan pointed out that 
policy financing support has not translated to demand and fiscal delivery is key. Q4 GDP implications were limited, with existing forecasts 
retained, and 2026 is seen just reaching the growth target range of 4.5­5.0%, with forecasts for 2027 pointing to another downshift to the 
4.0­4.5% range.
