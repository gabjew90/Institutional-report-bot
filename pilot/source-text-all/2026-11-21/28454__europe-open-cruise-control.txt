Europe Open - Cruise control
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
A record tech IPO helped boost Nasdaq futures higher by 2% overnight, but failed to drive Asia sentiment, with Taiex
leading losses and China/HK stocks weighed by growing US-China curbs and weaker earnings. AU bonds sharply rallied
after weaker jobs data, with u/e rate climbing 0.2pp and employment plunging - cash rate is expected to remain
unchanged until Q3 2027 but sticky inflation still keeps risks of one more hike. USTs catch the bullish wave, meanwhile
USD mostly steady with G10 pairs trading in tight ranges (barring AUD). Oil prices drift, some headlines from Trump
overnight but nothing meaningful from the trilateral with Xi/Putin as we go to print. 
Lots of central bank speakers ahead with BoE dove Dhingra speaking at 12:15 GMT, Lagarde speaking at 13:30 is
unlikely to be market relevant. Fed Hammack, Goolsbee, Barr, Barkin and Paulson feature later in the session. US
second tier data includes Philly Fed business outlook, weekly jobless claims. SARB rate decision due (hawkish hold),
Banxico minutes also on the cards.

In Focus‌
Equities: A record tech IPO points to a stronger open for European equities with Nasdaq futures also up 1.6%, but Asia
stocks were less impressed. Sentiment remains weak, with Taiex leading losses overnight, down -4%, likely jitters amid
APEC summit takeaways. Meanwhile weaker Tencent earnings is driving Hang Seng weaker. Management warned that US
curves curbs on Chinese access to overseas data centers should slow short-run AI model training. This is weighing further on
Chinese model names (Minimax down -9%, Z.AI -6%). 
Rates: AU bonds sharply rallied after unemployment rate jumped 0.2pp to 4.9% with employment declining by -43.5k (Citi;
35k, consensus; 20k). Though our economists warned of downside risks and seasonal noise, the underlying trend clearly
shows a looser labor market. We think that the cash rate will be kept at 4.85% until Q3 2027. Risk of another hike exists, but
the bar is higher and requires stronger inflation beats. USTs follow the bullish mode set by AU but momentum stalled into the
London open. Flow wise, our traders saw buying from fast money accounts in the 5y and 30y sectors. Oct FOMC last night
showed that most officials expected further rate hikes despite the decision to pause, making a 25bp rate hike in Dec highly

likely and a consecutive hike in Jan also plausible. Our economists still don't think officials will pursue aggressive hikes if
sentiment deteriorates materially and financial conditions tighten substantially.
Not much information from other central bank speak overnight - BoJ Ueda said that they were watching the impact of global
central bank tightening on the Japanese economy, data is within expectations but wage hikes are spreading. This leaves the
door open for another rate hike next month, in line with market expectations. RBNZ Governor Breman warned of two sided
risks to inflation, and that economic recovery remains uneven. Some indicators of economic activity have been stronger than
expected, particularly in export-focused parts of the economy, Breman said.
FX: AUDUSD plunged as low as 0.6662 before recovering to 0.67 at print, with eTrading confirming that volumes in the Asia
session were x2.1 higher for the pair. G10 pairs trade steady otherwise, with USDJPY hovering at 159.6. AUDJPY lower
debate grows amid weaker AU data, we attempted to break the 106 handle which remains our first target. Selling EURJPY
rallies still is appropriate - we are watching a weekly close below 175 which is the 2024 high and 100w MA. Our options desk
like owning convexity as we approach 160 in USDJPY..
Commodities: Trump/Xi/Putin bilateral taking place as we print, so we are closely monitoring headlines. So far, Trump said
that he would not be asking Xi or Putin for help on Iran. He added that he wanted a lasting deal with Iran - MOU delayed
things, but it wasn't a mistake. Oil prices drifted overnight, while gold recovered above $3700. 
Elsewhere‌
GBP: BoE's Dhingra speaks in Oslo (12:15 GMT). Comments post jobs/CPI data will be interesting but expect a dovish lean
reflecting her historical stance. Oct CPI failed to solidify a Dec hike (but still heavily skewed at 20bp). Our economists thought
there was minimal evidence of second round effects, despite acknowledgment that the hiking cycle has further to run. 
EUR: ECB President Lagarde speaks on Financial Literacy (13:30 GMT) so will unlikely be market relevant.
USD: Lots of second tier data in the form of Philly Fed Business Outlook for November and weekly jobless claims (both at
13:30 GMT), with Kansas Fed Manuf. Activity at 16:00 GMT. Fed’s Hammack and Goolsbee feature at 13:50 and 17:30 GMT.
Barr Speaks at an AI conference at 19:15 GMT); Fed's Barkin in Fireside Chat (21:00 GMT); Fed's Paulson Speaks at Money
Marketeers (23:30 GMT).
ZAR: Bad day on Wed following gold moves, declining more than -2%. A hawkish hold is expected by SARB at 13:00, but a
25bp hike would help stabilize sentiment, with our strategists seeing scope for an additional move in January depending on oil
prices and diesel spreads.
MXN: Central Bank Monetary Policy Minutes (15:00 GMT). Following a 3-2 split decision to cut the policy rate by 25bps to
6.25%, the board justified the move based on continued disinflation, limited expected exchange-rate pass-through amid
prevailing slack, the ascense of demand pressures, and a still restrictive monetary stance. When similar "extended period"
language was used, the policy rate remained unchanged for almost a year.
UYU: Monetary Policy Rate Decision. Our economists expect inflation shock risks, Middle East tensions, El Nino, and USD
strength to be enough to project gradual adjustments following Copom's rate hike to 6.0%
MYR: Oct CPI printed softer at 1.9% YoY vs 2% consensus and 1.8% prior.
