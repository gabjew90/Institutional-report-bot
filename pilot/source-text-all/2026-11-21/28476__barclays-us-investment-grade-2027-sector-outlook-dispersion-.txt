Sector dispersion with or without AI-related issuers has increased materially in the past

still relatively compressed, we see more attractive relative value at shorter maturities.

Dispersion is back!
US investment grade sector spread dispersion has increased sharply following a prolonged
period of compression (Figure 1). Measured as the standard deviation of IG sector spreads
relative to overall index OAS, dispersion has risen 29% from its September 8 trough of 0.26 to
0.33 as of November 16, well above its last two-year average of 0.26 and also higher than its
long-term historical average of 0.30. However, note that dispersion spiked to 0.37 on October 23
amid a broader risk-off session in global credit markets, as AI-related equity weakness coincided
with rising oil prices and escalating geopolitical tensions.
Dispersion is lower excluding AI-related credit, but it also spiked recently (Figure 2). While
both the overall IG and ex-AI measures rose sharply in October, the gap between the two
widened recently, with overall dispersion reaching a higher peak and remaining above the ex-AI
measure through mid-November. So AI-related credits have contributed meaningfully to the
recent pickup in sector differentiation, but the increase clearly goes beyond just direct AI
exposure.
 
 
AI's growing footprint is contributing to rising credit
dispersion
Spread dispersion across AI-related issuers has increased sharply over the past year,
highlighting growing differentiation within the AI credit universe (Figure 3). Overall spread
standard deviation rose from 24bp in October 2025 to 67bp in November 2026, an increase of
43bp, or nearly 180%. The trend is also evident across the middle half of issuers, where
dispersion increased from 33bp to 61bp. Excluding Oracle and data centers, standard deviation
rose from 23bp to 41bp, suggesting that the increase extends beyond these issuers, although
they contribute meaningfully to the overall measure. While dispersion has moderated from its
October 23 peak of 71bp, it remains well above year-ago levels, pointing to greater
differentiation across AI-related credit.

AI-related credit has driven the greatest spread differentiation within AA-rated bonds,
pushing the broader AA index closer to A-rated spread levels (Figure 4). AA spreads stood at
66bp as of November 16, compared with 49bp excluding AI-related bonds, a 17bp difference
that far exceeds the 1bp gap in A-rated credit and 7bp in BBB. This has compressed the AA/A
spread differential to just 7bp in the broader index, compared with 23bp excluding AI, despite
the higher credit quality of AA-rated bonds.
 
 
The AI ecosystem's growing footprint has increased its importance to overall index spread
risk and dispersion (Figure 5). In 2026, the market weight of hyperscalers rose 1.7pp, from
2.9% to 4.6%, while the AI ecosystem gained 2.0pp, from 6.0% to 8.0%, with both reaching
historical highs. In contrast, the Big 6's market weight declined modestly, from 9.3% to 9.0%,
although it remains the largest of the three groups.
The surge is more pronounced when measured by duration times spread (Figure
6). Hyperscalers' share doubled from 4.8% to 9.6%, while the AI ecosystem's share rose 6.3pp,
from 7.8% to 14.1%, with both reaching historical highs. Meanwhile, the Big 6's DTS share
increased only marginally, from 6.7% to 7.0%. As a result, the AI ecosystem now accounts for the
largest share of DTS among the three groups, despite ranking second by market weight, with its
lead over the Big 6 widening from 1.1pp to 7.1pp. Hyperscalers have also overtaken the Big 6 in
DTS share.

BBB/A decompression extends beyond AI
BBB/A spread compression has partially reversed in recent weeks (Figure 7). The BBB/A
ratio, measured as the weighted OAS of BBB rated bonds relative to A rated bonds, has
rebounded from the lows of 1.50 in September to 1.58 in November. Despite the recent
recovery, the ratio remains below its average over January 2024-November 2026, as well as the
highs of 1.67 reached in January 2026 and 1.69 in October 2025. A similar trend is evident when
excluding AI-related issuers, with the ex-AI ratio rising 3.72% from its September low of 1.49, to
1.55. We highlight the most compressed sectors at the 30y point in Figure 9.
Excluding AI-related bonds, BBB/A spread ratios show meaningful dispersion across the
maturity curve, with spread compression most pronounced at the 3y and 30y tenors
(Figure 8). Current ratios at these tenors sit at just the 24th and 15th percentiles of their five-
year ranges, respectively, well below the median and the 56th and 45th percentiles
corresponding to their historical averages. In contrast, the 5y ratio is above the median and
remains broadly in line with its historical average at the 60th percentile, while the 10y ratio is
relatively elevated at the 71st percentile, versus its 53rd percentile historical average. This
divergence highlights uneven BBB/A spread compression across maturities, with the greatest
compression at the short and long ends of the curve.

Starting OAS did not explain returns in 2026
Sector performance has also varied considerably, with starting OAS explaining almost
none of the cross-sectional variation in year-to-date excess returns (Figure 10). Sectors
with similar starting spreads have delivered widely different excess returns, underscoring the
substantial dispersion in performance across the IG market.
Against this backdrop, we reassess relative valuations across sectors, using current spread
differentials and our sector scorecard to identify potential opportunities.

Sector scorecard
Combining many of these metrics into one table helps us identify which sectors might be more
vulnerable to headwinds in the coming quarters. We aggregate sector scores based on the
below fundamental and valuation metrics, with the highest percentile rank suggesting more
favorable positioning.
Valuation/technical metrics included in the scorecard:
• Estimated EBITDA growth and EBITDA margin change on aggregate;
• Aggregate net leverage changes from 4Q19;
• Percent of the subsector on watch negative at any ratings agency;
• Sector/index spread ratio z-score: sector-level valuation relative to its historical norm,
expressed in standard deviations; higher values indicate relatively wider spreads and lower
values indicate relatively tighter spreads;
• Current sector/index spread ratio and percent in range (3y): sector-level valuation relative to
the index, with historical context; and
• Spread per duration relative to the index: spread compensation per unit of duration for each
sector relative to the index.

Sector commentary
Upgrades
Peter Troisi, peter.troisi@barclays.com, BCI, US
BDCs (Underweight to Overweight)
We upgrade our rating on the BDC sector to Overweight from Underweight. BDCs have
outperformed the IG corporate index by over 100bp this year in excess returns, despite very high
spread volatility, with peak-to-trough spread movement of about 100bp (160-260bp). The
outperformance despite the sector’s beta is a testament to its high spread carry and significant
premium to the IG corporate index, which averaged 115bp this year. At current spreads of about
+190bp and an OAD of less than 3, the BDC sector could widen by 66bp and still outperform on
an excess returns basis, given our outlook for IG spreads next year.
It is difficult to see BDC spreads being more volatile in 2027 than they were this year. Indeed,
sector fundamentals have deteriorated, and that trend is likely to persist next year. However, we
think that outcome, albeit negative, would be less surprising to investors relative to this year.
We believe risks in the BDC sector are better understood by credit investors and will likely
increase more gradually than they did (real or perceived) this year. We think credit investors are
more than compensated for that fundamental trajectory, even if it is at best mixed, given the
excess spread in the BDC sector at current levels.
Fundamentally, we expect non-accruals in the BDC sector to continue to increase as credit
quality normalizes and portfolios season. Higher rates are likely to pressure borrowers in the
private credit market, reducing their resilience to stress. We expect PIK rates in the BDC sector
to remain elevated, albeit at relatively stable levels. The outlook for net flows in the BDC sector
remains negative, with subdued inflows and an uncertain outlook for the normalization of

Our sector rating is relative to the IG market, but given how wide BDCs trade, there are more

Another risk to our Overweight sector rating is that secular concerns about AI risks

borrowers, some of which we expect to be disrupted by AI. That said, this risk is better
understood and likely to be more in focus as the year progresses, given that software maturity
walls at BDCs will inflect higher in 2028 and thereafter.
Life Insurance (Underweight to Market Weight)
We upgrade our rating on the life insurance sector to Market Weight from Underweight. The
sector materially underperformed IG corporates earlier this year, given concerns about its 
growing exposure to private credit investments (see here, here, and here). As the market’s focus
on private credit has subsided overall, this has allowed the higher carry in the life insurance
sector (+123bp OAS) to support better excess returns. At current levels, the spread basis
between the life index and IG corporates is 32bp, a few basis points wider than at the start of the
year. We are less concerned about the beta that life spreads will have to BDCs and overall
private credit sentiment over the next six months.
Sector fundamentals are relatively stable, anchored by the higher-for-longer interest rate
environment, which we expect to remain supportive of robust levels of annuity production
(Figure 15). Leverage and capital levels are stable and healthy. Balance sheets remain
somewhat reliant on reinsurance from less transparent entities, but we think associated risks
are being actively managed, including with strengthened oversight in Bermuda and more
regulatory scrutiny of other offshore justifications.

Pharmaceuticals (Maintain Market Weight)
The sector is trading tight to the sector framework, but we believe it is more likely to perform in
line with the market than to lag. It remains among the most defensive sectors and has
performed well during periods of heightened macro volatility.
M&A, which was expected to drive significant supply this year, has been underwhelming, and
issuance that has come to market has largely avoided the long end. Pharmaceutical companies
have continued to pursue M&A but have been less willing to undertake “mega” transactions,
increasingly focusing on growing pipelines through domestic and international partnerships.
The lack of long-end issuance also reduces incentives to sell existing pharmaceutical long
bonds, helping keep spreads steady rather than widening.
More recently, pharmaceutical companies have partnered with strategic investors/private
equity, with the latter assuming some R&D risk. This reduces the cash cost of research in
exchange for sharing the economics, including some of the sales upside, with private equity
partners. For 2027, we expect the industry to continue this strategy given the higher rate
environment and elevated political scrutiny of pharmaceutical pricing.

Health Insurance (Maintain Overweight)
For the sector, we expect medical cost trends to remain elevated but stabilize, allowing
companies to price in a way that supports margin growth. We expect insurers to focus on
benefit redesign and pricing to preserve margins and absorb elevated costs.
Further margin expansion will depend on a favorable 2027 reimbursement backdrop, with the
early MA rate notice (expected in late January/early February) likely to be a key focus for
investors. Healthy reimbursement guidance could help narrow the gap with medical costs.
Companies are also likely to remain disciplined on membership, as reflected in fewer plan
choices available to customers. Medicaid rate adequacy and acuity will remain headwinds for
some in the sector, with work requirements adding further pressure. Reduced ACA subsidies are
also likely to create earnings quality and adverse selection challenges, but we expect insurers to
continue promoting cost-sharing plans that shift a greater proportion of the burden to
customers. The sector also benefits from being purely domestic, which softens the effects of
geopolitical uncertainties.

E&Ps (Maintain Underweight)
