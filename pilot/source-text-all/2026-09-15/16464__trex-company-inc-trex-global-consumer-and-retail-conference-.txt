Trex Company Inc. (TREX): Global Consumer and Retail Conference 
2026 — Key Takeaways
14 September 2026 | 5:01PM EDT
 
Presenters: We hosted Trex’s CFO Prith Gandhi, and COO Zach Lauer at the 33rd 
Annual Global Retailing Conference. 
Bottom Line: The speakers reiterated their second half outlook, which reflects 
steady end-market demand given the strength in contractor backlogs along with 
company-specific initiatives to drive market outperformance. 
Key Takeaways 
 
 
Near-Term Demand Remains Favorable: Despite persistent macro headwinds, 
n
Trex noted the green shoots in demand this spring held through the summer. This 
is led by the success of targeted promotions and marketing of entry-level 
products that highlight the value proposition over wood, resulting in growth after 
several years of weakness. The lock-in effect of historically low-rate mortgages 
during the pandemic is also leading homeowners to make investments, with a 
focus on outdoor living given its lower-cost alternative vs larger interior projects. 
2030 Strategic Objectives on Track: Trex reaffirmed the long-term target of 
n
$2bn in revenue by 2030, vs the $1.215-1.250bn 2026 guide, with ~$500mn of 
the growth from organic initiatives and the balance from close-to-core M&A. 
This includes the ramp of Little Rock, which can support $1.8-2.0bn in core 
product over time. Despite near-term headwinds from Little Rock depreciation 
and less profitable railing growth, rising capacity utilization should allow for 
greater operating leverage, supporting margin expansion in the coming years. 
Productivity Supports Operational Efficiency: Trex continues to leverage 
n
advanced technology and material science to optimize manufacturing including 
automation of manual steps, machine learning and generative AI. In addition, the 
recycling supply chain is highly insulated from volatility in commodity prices. We 
highlight state-of-the-art capabilities at Little Rock to process polyethylene and 
next-generation waste streams that will also contribute to profitability over time.

Railing Primed for Growth and Margin Expansion: This category is continuing to 
n
deliver double-digit growth, in line with the stated objective. The company’s leading 
share at 7% reflects rail’s highly fragmented nature, leaving ample room for Trex to 
double segment sales from 2023-28. Growth partially reflects its comprehensive 
portfolio and higher attachment rates as well as strength in core decking sales. From 
a margin perspective, management is focused on vertical integration to narrow the 
gap vs decking with key initiatives including in-sourcing outer shell manufacturing 
and the production of raw materials, optimizing material formulations, and pursuing 
targeted acquisitions.  
Realignment of Distribution Progressing as Planned: The company stated the 
n
national partnership with Specialty Building Products, complemented by select 
regional distributors, will simplify the channel model and accelerate market 
penetration. More specifically, the strategy is designed for faster contractor 
pull-through and share gains from smaller, tertiary brands, which represent an 
estimated $100+mn opportunity across decking and railing over time. 
FCF Outlook Supports Capital Deployment: As peak investment of the Little Rock 
n
campus winds down, capex is expected to decline to $100-$120mn in 2026 and 
further normalize to 5-6% of sales in the coming years. This, alongside working 
capital improvements, will drive a sizable increase in FCF supporting share 
repurchases and opportunistic M&A. Management is focused on bolt-on deals that 
can: 1) enhance vertical integration, 2) expand the outdoor living portfolio, or 3) 
extend its reach into the building envelope. That said, any potential acquisitions will 
be weighed against the return from share repurchases in the current environment. 
Valuation/Risks 
 
 
We are Buy rated on TREX with a 12-month price target of $61 which is based on 17.0x 
our Q5-8 adj. EBITDA forecast. Downside risks include: (1) decline in consumer 
confidence or other weakness limiting the willingness to spend on R&R projects, 
especially larger-ticket items, (2) delays or the inability to deliver on strategic initiatives, 
(3) increase in raw material costs and the inability to pass them through, and (4) loss of 
key customer or distributor could impact sales.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
