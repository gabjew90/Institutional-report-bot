Consumer Sentiment Fell More Than 
Expected in September
Headlines 
 
Consumer sentiment slid to 47.8 from 51.7 in the preliminary September 
print. 
 
 
The drop in sentiment was broad based with the expectations index and 
the current conditions index slipping. 
 
 
One year ahead inflation expectations surged to 4.6% in September. 
 
 
Five-year ahead inflation expectations ticked up to 3.4% in September.  
 
 
Low levels of consumer sentiment reflect a high level of uncertainty 
regarding trade policy and the Middle East conflict which remains 
unresolved and the persistent rise in gas prices.  
 
Details
The disconnect between the resilient U.S. economy, 
rebounding job growth and consumer confidence 
continued in September. Consumer sentiment fell to 
47.8 in the preliminary print according to the 
University of Michigan (Figure 1). This was well below 
PNC’s forecast of 51.2 and the second consecutive 
drop. Sentiment is down 7.5% month-on-month and 
is 13.2% lower than a year ago.  
 
The decline in sentiment was broad based with the 
future expectations index falling 5.7 points to 45.8 
(Figure 2) and the current conditions index dropping 
a much more modest 1.0 point to 50.9.  
 
Sentiment was mixed across the political spectrum 
with declines among Republicans (-8.6 points to the 
lowest since November 2024) and Democrats (-5.0 
points to 35.2). Sentiment rose 0.5 points among 
Independents. The sharp drop in sentiment among 
Republicans could be related to the ongoing conflict 
in the Middle East and the subsequent rise in gas 
prices that is further eroding affordability.  
 
There was a slight improvement in sentiment in all 
stock ownership categories with sentiment in each 
tercile increasing by less than a point. Sentiment fell 
for those who do not own stock. 
 
The resumption of hostilities in the Middle East and 
the increase in energy prices pushed inflation 
expectations higher. One year ahead inflation 
expectations surged to 4.6% from 4.0% in August 
(Figure 2). Inflation expectations are well above the 
pre-conflict level of 3.4% in February and could head

Long-term inflation expectations – which the FOMC 
views as crucial to achieving price stability – ticked 
up to 3.4% after holding steady at 3.3% for three 
straight months (Figure 3). Despite the slight rise, 
long-term inflation expectations are well below the 
3.9% reading in May. 
 
Low levels of consumer sentiment reflect a high 
level of uncertainty regarding trade policy and the 
Middle East conflict which remains unresolved and 
the continuous rise in gas prices with a gallon of 
regular unleaded hitting $4.30/gallon on September 
11, the highest since late May. As a result, sentiment 
is expected to remain depressed in the near term 
until there is a resolution to the Middle East conflict.
