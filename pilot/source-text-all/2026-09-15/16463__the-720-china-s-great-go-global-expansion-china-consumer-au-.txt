In Focus | China’s great Go Global expansion 
China in Transition – China’s great Go Global expansion. 
China’s nearly 40% share of global markets may be unsurprising after a decade of 
domestic substitution. But with a sharp rise to an 18% share for Chinese players in 
ex-China markets, competition is now moving from peripheral towards core market 
segments of the leading global incumbents. We collaborated globally across 36 
analysts using a framework of classic competitive analysis between new entrants and 
incumbents, encompassing 11 sectors and 40 companies, to ask what the learnings 
from past global expansions are, and how they help us think about the decade 
ahead. From peripheral to core, we expect a more uncertain path ahead: Chinese 
companies’ market shares to rise further to 31% by 2035E, with revenue growth of 
3.6x, favoring the “latecomers”. We see a harder path and slower pace, as the 
competitive ground shifts toward core segments, where the moat of the incumbents 
is widest. Nonetheless, markets have yet to price in China’s global growth 
opportunity, with a number of sectors trading at less than 1.0x sales ex China. Trina 
Chen et al 
China Consumer | Cost Inflation Risks 
China Restaurants – Revisiting cost inflation risk from rising oil and El Niño. We see 
limited correlation between raw material cost indices and margin performance for 
most of our covered restaurant companies, despite rising oil prices and potential 
Super El Niño impacts on sugar and coffee beans. We attribute this margin resilience 
to diversified product structures, multi-month price lock-ups, and strong corporate 
cost controls that allow companies to adjust menus flexibly. Within our coverage, we 
continue to prefer Buy-rated Yum China and Guming, both on the Conviction List, 
given their strong track records in managing costs and diversified raw material mixes. 
We also highlight Buy-rated Luckin Coffee, where we expect a margin turnaround 
starting in the second half of 2026 as coffee bean prices become a tailwind and 
delivery mix improves. Michelle Cheng

Themes in Play | US rate hike, AU Gold sector, Solar Industries 
US Macro – A Hike Without a Signal?. We add a 25bp rate hike at this week’s September 
FOMC meeting to our forecast, driven by market pricing nearing 90% following the 
August CPI report rather than a strong economic case. We also raise our terminal rate 
forecast to 3.25-3.5% from 3-3.25% and shift our expectation for two 2027 cuts to 
September and December from June and December. We believe the inflation overshoot 
is driven by fading one-time tariff effects, with core PCE improving to a 2.5% pace and 
the broader economy showing no signs of overheating. Consequently, we expect the 
FOMC to make minimal statement changes without signaling further hikes, and we 
project a narrow 10-8 majority in the dots showing just one hike this year. David Mericle

Asia Macro | China TSF 
China – Weaker-than-expected credit extension. We note that August money and credit 
data missed market expectations, driven primarily by slow bank loan extension and 
weaker government bond issuance. Total social financing flows came in at RMB 1,660bn, 
well below our RMB 2,000bn forecast, while new RMB loans of RMB 60bn significantly 
lagged consensus expectations of RMB 404bn. The detailed breakdown indicates that 
both household and corporate loan demand remained weak, with household loans 
declining by RMB 203bn during the month. Meanwhile, M1 growth edged up to 4.1% 
year-over-year, whereas M2 growth moderated to 7.5%, largely reflecting slower 
deposit growth among non-bank financial institutions. Xinquan Chen

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
