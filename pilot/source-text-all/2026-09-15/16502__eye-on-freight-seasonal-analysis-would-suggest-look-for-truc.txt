Eye on Freight: Seasonal Analysis Would Suggest: Look For Truckload 
Spot Rate and Share Price Re-acceleration To Close Out Year
15 September 2026 | 5:01AM EDT
Bottom Line: Following a significant increase in spot trucking rates over the first half 
of 2026 (1H26), trucking stocks broadly outperformed both the wider market and 
other transportation subsectors. Through August, trucking equities achieved a 
year-to-date (YTD) return of 31.5%, compared to 21.0% for transports 
(excluding trucking) and 12.0% for the S&P 500. However, recent seasonal 
declines in spot trucking rates have likely contributed significantly to trucking 
equities pulling back from their mid-year highs, with the group declining by an 
average of 16.0% from their June/July peaks. 
In response to recent investor inquiries regarding this downward rate trajectory 
and the corresponding drawdown in share prices, we highlight the historical 
strength in truckload equities that typically re-emerges in October and 
November (Exhibit 10). This equity performance pattern aligns with our 
historical analysis, detailed below, which demonstrates that trucking spot rates 
generally re-accelerate through September and gain further momentum during 
the peak November and December holiday freight and shopping season. By 
placing these short-term sequential rate patterns into historical context, we look to 
ameliorate near-term rate concerns and highlight the supportive seasonal trends one 
could typically expect through the remainder of the year - something that we think 
could re-invigorate the truckload shares in the coming months. 
Key Takeaways And Analysis: Rate Recovery Typical Through Year-End 
1. Although August’s spot trucking rate (excluding fuel surcharges) declined by 
~-18% month-over-month (MoM) tracking below the historical seasonal 
average—the pullback was directionally in line with typical seasonal patterns, 
which usually see August rates decline by ~-7% MoM (Exhibit 1). Following 
exceptionally strong, above-seasonal sequential performance from April through 
July (where truck spot rates outperformed normal seasonality by an average of 
~600bps during Q2 and early Q3), a more pronounced seasonal drop-off in August is 
not entirely unexpected. Furthermore, sequential rate gains in January and February 
were also strong relative to history, significantly outperforming the typical early-year 
seasonal decline by ~900bps. Given this generally robust year-to-date spot 
trucking rate per mile performance, we view the recent August drop-off as more 
of a seasonal adjustment rather than a change in the upward trajectory pattern, 
which we expect to re-emerge as we move deeper into the year.

To evaluate spot rate seasonality trends within a year, we also employ a 
n
min-max normalization methodology. This approach scales monthly trucking spot 
rates from 0.0 to 1.0 within each calendar year. Under this framework, a value of 0.0 
represents the absolute rate floor (the annual low) for a given year, while a value of 
1.0 represents the rate ceiling (the annual peak). Consequently, as the normalized 
rate approaches 1.0, it indicates increasing strength in rates relative to other months 
of the same year. 
Our historical analysis (based on 2014-2019 average) reveals that August 
typically represents a seasonal ‘lower ebb’ in any given year in the spot market. 
Following these August doldrums, historical average for normalized rates 
demonstrates a clear upward trajectory through September, and then again in 
November and December with rates approaching the upper end of the 0.0 to 1.0 
range as the peak holiday shipping season takes hold.

Aside from just looking at normal seasonal patterns, we also note that 
fundamentals especially around supply should be supportive of the re-emergnece 
in upward rate trajectory. The tighter driver/capacity situation today (note: BLS 
official TL truck driver count is down -6% vs COVID highs) could lead to 
above-normal spot rate increases through 2H26—particularly into Nov/Dec—should 
underlying consumer demand remain at least stable or should retail inventories 
accelerate into peak holiday shopping season. We do note that the CASS freight index 
(a volume proxy for trucking) inflected positively YoY in August (+2.1%) after a

pre-and-post-COVID (2007-2025) performance illustrates that October and November 
are usually better stock-price performance months for Truckload shares. 
While we recognize that the past does not predict the future, we thought it worthwhile 
to note how transport shares, specifically TL stocks, trade as we move through the 
second half, especially in light of how 1) truck rates seasonally perform, 2) continued 
tight driver supply, and 3) potential for demand greenshoots for peak season and 
industrial recovery. For TL stocks, the post-COVID (2020-2025) median price 
performance in October is slightly down -1.5% before improving ~+9% in November.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
