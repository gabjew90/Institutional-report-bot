supply as Russian and Middle Eastern refining capacity declined and refiners diverted scarce blending components toward higher-value jet
and diesel production. That helps explain why distillate and jet cracks have remained elevated. For airlines, truckers and rails, the implication

Treat the diesel and jet crack as a sustained level this cycle, not a spread that automatically closes when crude falls.

TRUCKING
Spot truckload rates rose across every equipment type in week 36 — and essentially all of it was fuel Truckload spot rates rose
across all equipment types in the week of August 30 to September 5, with broker-to-carrier van rates up roughly 3 cents per mile and reefer
and flatbed up 4 cents. Fuel accounted for more than half the van increase and all of the flatbed increase; flatbed linehaul was unchanged.
The move therefore says more about cost recovery than carrier pricing power. A higher fuel-inclusive headline rate may also delay shipper
acceptance of contract increases, pushing realized linehaul pricing further right.
This week's spot-rate gain was fuel recovery, not linehaul pricing power, and should not move the house realized-price call.
Source: DAT truckload spot market data (week of Aug 30 - Sep 5, 2026) via AJOT and Commercial Carrier Journal
JOC: shrinking capacity is lifting rates, but the data still do not show a freight recovery. The Journal of Commerce reported that
shrinking carrier capacity is directing freight to fewer motor carriers and lifting rates, even though the underlying data do not show a genuine
freight recovery. That supports the structural-supply case for price but not a volume rebound. In the models, tonnage and load counts should
remain restrained while price does the work; a supply-led rate cycle also lacks the volume leverage of a demand-led upturn.
The rate recovery remains supply-led: hold volume flat and let price — not freight demand — drive the model.
Source: Journal of Commerce

RAILROADS
Notices of anticipated responsive applications are in (Exhibit 4). Three Class I railroads and eleven short lines trailed proposed merger
conditions on September 8–10; formal requests are due November 18. Three carriers seek relief at Kansas City and only CN has negotiated
for it — its package already sits inside UP's amended application under a July 22 MOU covering St. Louis–Kansas City rights on both routes,
a Neff Yard lease with a purchase option, overhead rights Tuscola to East St. Louis, and NS's interests in the Kansas City Terminal Railway
and the Terminal Railroad Association of St. Louis, for 99 years. CSX now seeks 50% divestiture and dispatching control of the same UP
line; CPKC seeks access on the grounds that the CN remedy is insufficient. BNSF seeks 824 route miles of overhead rights from Chicago to
Bethlehem — rights that serve no shipper and convert an interline division into a single-line haul — while CSX seeks the same destination by
a different route. On the Gulf, BNSF seeks a region-wide neutral terminal railroad from Corpus Christi to the Mississippi, a remedy the Board
refused on similar facts at Houston in 1998, while CPKC seeks new operating rights over UP in Louisiana and a short line proposes itself as
neutral operator at New Orleans. CPKC's package is the only one weighted toward preservation, protecting existing Texas and Chicago–
Detroit rights. BNSF and CPKC share a public coalition that excludes CSX, but the asks compete rather than combine — the opposition is
aligned against the transaction and not toward any shared remedy, which suggests individual commercial positioning rather than a common
competitive harm.
The asks compete, revive remedies the Board has already refused, or move margin between carriers without adding shipper choice. The Board
can grant selectively without endangering approval, and we see nothing here costly enough to change the investment case — the live risk
remains timing, not concessions.
Source: Canadian National Railway statement via MT Newswires; CN Investors
US rail traffic up 13.8% for the week ending September 5 — read the base, not the trend. AAR reported total US rail traffic up 13.8%
year-over-year for the week ending September 5, with carloads up 8.9% and intermodal up 18.0%; year-to-date traffic is up 3.6%. The gap
between the weekly headline, the year-to-date trend and the prior week's 4.1% increase points to an easy comparison rather than a new
demand inflection.
The more useful signal is mix: intermodal continues to outgrow carloads, while coal was the only major commodity group to decline.
Source: Association of American Railroads
Rails finish bottom of the table for a third week as the 10-year reaches 4.96%. NSC, CP, UNP and CSX all lagged the S&P 500 during
a week when the ten-year Treasury yield reached 4.96%. This was the third consecutive week of rail underperformance alongside a higher
curve, even though the merger docket produced potentially constructive news through CN's condition filing. The cleaner explanation is
duration rather than a deterioration in weekly operating data. Rail valuation and entry points are therefore increasingly tied to the rates
outlook.
Rails are trading as long-duration assets: the entry point is now as dependent on rates as on operating fundamentals.
Source: Yahoo Finance daily closes; MT Newswires
