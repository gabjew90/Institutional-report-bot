• US HPC - Beauty Tracker: Sephora Bestseller Trends Across Key Markets Report In this note, UBS
analyze Sephora bestseller rankings across makeup, skincare, haircare and fragrance in four strategically
important markets: the US, UK, France (Sephora’s largest market in Continental Europe) and Brazil (its largest
market in South America). The analysis highlights continued strength for LVMH, L’Oréal and ELF, with LVMH
leading overall representation in the US, UK and France, while L’Oréal dominates Brazil and remains
particularly strong in haircare. Private and founder-led brands account for around half of bestsellers across the
US, UK and France, with rhode particularly strong in makeup and skincare. K-Beauty continues to gain
traction, especially in skincare, with France showing the highest penetration, while Brazil remains more
skewed towards established beauty houses and professional haircare.
• ‘US Consumer Staples - The Weekly Tab: Running Up The Score Report The XLP once again
outperformed the S&P 500 this week, marking the seventh consecutive week of relative outperformance
(+240bps), albeit declining ~270bps on an absolute basis. In fact, the XLP now trades at a ~15% premium to
the S&P 500, which would mark the widest relative valuation gap since 12/22. Although our conversations
would suggest sentiment has improved on fundamentals to a degree, it does not mirror the magnitude of
outperformance observed over the last several weeks making the path forward far more difficult to call. A
framework anchored on risk/reward has struggled to gain footing with investors over the past year but our
discussions would suggest that is beginning to shift with many of the view that it is harder to argue for further
multiple expansion for many of the more crowded longs in the group. UBS do not get a sense that investors
are significantly moving out on the risk curve, but UBS are fielding more questions on companies that have
historically been viewed as higher quality and offer some degree of multiple expansion. The group remains
sensitive to broader market dynamics (as evidenced by the performance yesterday), but with valuation near
peak levels the path forward remains difficult and will likely hinge on estimate revisions. Although UBS think
downside risk to numbers looks limited, UBS think the magnitude of upside priced in at current levels looks
also unlikely as well.

SECTOR THOUGHTS:
The consumer sectors both underperformed this week (SXQP -3.2%; SX3P -3.3%) as a broader market
de-risking and persistently high yields weighed on Consumer, with neither Discretionary nor Staples offering
much protection. The key has been in Staples, if the sector if it has run too far given fundamentals haven’t
materially changed. Similar to last earnings season, the outperformance in Staples has been driven more by
rotation out of AI/growth than by improving fundamentals, prompting debate around taking profits. In
Sportswear, ADS (S) in focus with the HF’s this week due to Nike investor day & JD Sports trading update,
the read-through from both was it is looking increasingly challenging for European sportswear. The feedback
on JD Sports (N) was all negative on the day with most having questions around demand heading into the
crucial holiday season. In Luxury, HF’s & LOs agreed Richemont (B) results was punished too much on the
day of results and instead would continue to own as one of the best names in the sector right now. some
interest on EssilorLuxottica (B) post Dutch Data Protection Authority (AP) advises consumers against buying
AI camera glasses but most agree the headline risk looks worse than the likely impact due to EssilorLuxottica’s
camera-free model and other changes already addresses privacy concerns, in line with EDPB guidance.
Ferrari (B) also seeing some interest this week from LOs with most agreeing underlying business is solid and
it remains a good hiding place in Luxury.

• ‘Indonesia Consumer Sector - 2027 minimum wages: A meaningful step-up to c.10% Report As of 23
November 2026, 30 of Indonesia’s 38 provinces have announced their 2027 minimum wages, with
mean/median increases of 9.9%/7.6%, a meaningful step-up from the c.5.8% average increase in 2026. The
stronger increase reflects a more supportive wage-setting framework, driven by both a higher alpha factor and
greater consideration of Kebutuhan Hidup Layak (KHL, or decent living needs). Under Government Regulation
(PP) No. 29 of 2025, the wage setting framework retains the formula Minimum wage (t+1) = Minimum wage (t)
× [1 + inflation + (economic growth × a)], while the permissible alpha range rises from 0.5–0.9 for 2026 to
0.5–1.0 for 2027. As alpha represents the share of provincial economic growth reflected in workers’ wages, the
higher ceiling allows a greater proportion of economic growth to feed through to minimum wages. The
framework also places greater emphasis on KHL alongside regional economic and labour-market conditions,
helping to explain the unusually large increases in selected provinces, notably Bali (+42%) and West Java
(+29%), which UBS view more as one-off catch-up towards living cost benchmarks than a structural
acceleration in underlying wage inflation.

• ‘BJ's Wholesale Club Holdings, Inc. (UBS Research Rec Buy) – Money Well Spent? Report BJ’s 3Q
results has centered on whether the company over-invested in its near-term pricing structure after benefiting
from a one-time legal settlement and a sale-leaseback. UBS do see some validity to that concern. The
incremental investment helped its 3Q comp and creates a much tougher comparison. But UBS think it doesn’t
give enough credit to a strategic rationale that UBS think makes sense. BJ’s leaned into its value proposition at
an opportune time, with consumers increasingly pressured by higher living expenses and diminished
household savings. Its 42nd Anniversary sale helped generate 200k+ membership sign-ups in October alone
(equivalent to more than 2% of its total membership base). BJ’s has already incurred the cost of acquiring and

positions it well to retain them, even as its price gaps normalize. Meanwhile, the company’s two-year,
category-by-category SKU reduction initiative should improve its assortment consistency and quality. These
changes can help support its comp over time. UBS slightly reduce our price target to $130 from $132 and
maintain our Buy rating.
