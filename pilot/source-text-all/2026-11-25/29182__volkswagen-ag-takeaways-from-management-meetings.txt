Volkswagen AG
Takeaways from Management Meetings
Our view: We hosted investor meetings with Andreas Buchta and Christian
Rottler from VW Group IR in Canada. Headcount reduction could drive
better underlying EBIT in '27, but shares might remain under pressure until
we get clarity on restructuring cash charges. China is still challenging, but
VW is faring well against exports into Europe. Finally, a US/Mexico trade
deal could provide upside to numbers.
Key points:
2027 Underlying EBIT Could be Higher y/y. We model 4.7% underlying total
EBIT margins for '27, up from 4% in '26. Drivers include lower overhead
cuts (in the billions of Euros given €11B is expected by 2030), thanks to '26
actions, lower tariffs at Audi, the US tariff deal coming from the Georgia
plant investment, Cariad/Battery improvements, and better Traton. These
are offset by higher raw material costs (in the hundreds of millions of Euros
level), lost Everllence EBIT, and EV mix headwinds.
German plant rationalization on track. In Sept, VW's Supervisory Board
approved a plan to end current models once they expire at 4 plants in the
early 2030s. 1) Zwikau produces the VW ID.4, ID.5, Cupra Born, Audi Q4
e-tron - goes to VW's Slovakia plant. 2) Emden makes the VW ID.4, ID.7,
ID.Tiguan will go to VW's Czech Repub plant. 3) Hanover has the VW ID/
Buzz, the vans program will go to VW's Slovakia plant. 4) Nickarsulm makes
the Audi A5, A6, A8, whose successor will go to Porsche's Leipzig plant. A
key overhang on shares is the timing and magnitude of cash restructuring
costs related to headcount reduction. The company has ample liquidity to
absorb this thanks in part to asset dispositions like Everllence.
Expanding production capacity in the US. Currently, VW Group produces
~600K vehicles in North America. Its Tennessee plant makes the VW Atlas
(~150K vehicle capacity in '25) and stopped making the VW ID.4 in April '26.
Its Puebla Mexico plant is its biggest outside of Germany and makes the
VW Jetta, Taos, and Tiguan (~350K/year capacity). Its last plant is also in
Mexico, in San Jose, and produces the Audi Q5 (~150K/year). A US/Mexico
trade deal could yield upside to estimates. 2 new facilities could add 400K/
year by the early 2030s. The Scout South Carolina plant is targeting initial
production in '27 (200k/year capacity) with more than 80% of orders being
E-REV. Its Georgia Audi plant will make the Q7, Q8, and Q9 in the early '30s.
Outside of vehicles, VW makes engines at its Silao, Mexico plant. Further,
it plans to make ~1M batteries/year by 2029 in Ontario, Canada.

Valuation
We derive our €115 price target by applying a 1.0x multiple
(trough multiple) to our 2027E Industrial EBITDA. We then
subtract Industrial Net Cash (includes the ~€7.4B in proceeds
associated with the sale of Everllence) with a 70% discount,
Finco (@ .75x Book Value), and Underfunded Pension
Liabilities. We then take 41% (share cap of pref shares) of the
total equity value and divide by the total number of preferred
shares (206M) to arrive at our €113 per share value (ex-
China). For the China Joint Venture, we assign a €2 per share
contribution, applying a 3.0x P/E multiple to our 2027E EPS
estimate. This brings our total PT to €115 (€113 + €2), which
supports our Outperform rating.
Upside scenario
We derive our €149 upside value by applying a 1.5x (historical
midcycle) multiple to our 2027E Industrial EBITDA. We then
subtract Industrial Net Cash (40% discount with ~€7.4B from
the sale of Everllence), Finco (@ .75x Book Value), and the
Underfunded Pension Liabilities to arrive at our €147 per share
value (ex-China). For the China Joint Venture, we assign a €2
per share contribution, applying a 3.0x P/E multiple to our
2027E EPS estimate. This brings us to our €149 (€147 + €2)
upside scenario.
Downside scenario
We derive our €40 downside value by applying a 0.5x
historical discount to trough multiple to our 2027E downside
Industrial EBITDA (applies a 20% discount). We then subtract
Industrial Net Cash (70% discount), Finco (75% Book Value),
and Underfunded Pension Liabilities to arrive at our Equity
Value. We do not give credit for VW's China JV.
Investment summary
VW has historically been well positioned in China and
emerging markets. We expect VW to continue to benefit
from global motorization trends and not compromise on
profitability with its ICE vehicles, especially given strong
margins. Despite a challenging price environment and new
BEV domestic players taking market share, we think VW's 'in
China, for China' initiative is a step in the right direction.
We believe VW's exposure to US tariffs could be limited
relative to other German OEMs. First, VW's North American
production is heavily weighted toward Mexico, and we think
the effective tariff rate could be lower when considering
USMCA exemptions. VW is also planning to offer “massive”
investments in the US, which could involve localizing
production for Audi and increasing exports out of the US, in
hopes of reducing the tariff burden below 15%.
VW is well positioned on EVs. Despite our contention that
the mass-market players are not as well positioned on EVs vs.
the premium players, we believe VW’s aggressive campaign to
fully electrify its portfolio is the correct approach in the long
term.

Key fundamental questions
Our view
Is VW well positioned in China and
emerging markets?
Volkswagen has struggled in China, with BEV deliveries lagging both the market's
32% penetration rate and domestic competitors, while JV equity income has
declined sharply in recent years (€2.6B in 2023 to ~€1B forecasted for 2025).
However, we remain optimistic about the long term as VW ramps up cost-cutting
efforts and its "in China, for China" strategy, which includes 50 new model launches
by 2030 (over 20 NEVs) and innovative partnerships with local suppliers like
XPeng and Horizon. While the competitive environment remains intense, we see
substantial upside in VW's ability to scale its BEV portfolio, leverage its strong
premium brand in higher-end segments, and benefit from an eventual stabilization
of pricing pressures in the region.
How is VW positioned in the global
electrification race?
Better than its mass-market European peers. VW is vertically integrated which
should help it absorb higher costs longer term.
What is VW's goal on battery
capacity?
VW will have six cell-making facilities in Europe, each capable of producing 40gwh
(for 240gwh in total), which could support 4M BEVs, well above its 2.5M 2030 target
in Europe assuming a 60kwh battery size. For its similar-sized 2.5M vehicle target in
2030 in China, VW has secured 150gwh through 2025 with partners.

Key sustainability questions
This section is intended to highlight key sustainability discussion points relevant to this company, as well as our views on the outlook. Both the
questions we highlight and our responses will evolve over time as the dialogue between management, analysts and investors continues to

Our view
What are the most material
sustainability issues facing this
company?
The most material sustainability issue faced by Volkswagen is the compliance with
emissions standards, which can be significantly challenging and require significant
investment. Water availability is a concern, as it can lead to added costs and
drastically affect the supply chain. Governance issues include the fact that the
company’s new CEO will also be at the helm of Porsche AG.
Does the company integrate
sustainability considerations into its
strategy?
The group has a specific code of conduct in place that defines sustainable and
responsible business behaviour under environmental, social, and ethical aspects.
The group also regularly monitors battery raw materials suppliers for adherence to
the sustainability criteria.
What is diversity like at the board/
management level?
VW’s 11-member board of directors has 11 members (2 female, 9 male). The
company has a supervisory board in place that is responsible for monitoring
management and approving important corporate decisions; it also appoints the
Board members. The supervisory board has 20 members (7 female, 13 male).

Valuation
We derive our €115 price target by applying a 1.0x multiple (trough multiple) to our 2027E Industrial EBITDA. We then subtract
Industrial Net Cash (includes the ~€7.4B in proceeds associated with the sale of Everllence) with a 70% discount, Finco (@ .75x
Book Value), and Underfunded Pension Liabilities. We then take 41% (share cap of pref shares) of the total equity value and divide
by the total number of preferred shares (206M) to arrive at our €113 per share value (ex-China). For the China Joint Venture, we
assign a €2 per share contribution, applying a 3.0x P/E multiple to our 2027E EPS estimate. This brings our total PT to €115 (€113
+ €2), which supports our Outperform rating.
Risks to rating and price target
1. China competition intensifies
2. Corporate governance restricts action
3. Weak consumer EV demand
4. Battery prices do not fall and profitability is impaired
