 
We update our US online sports betting (OSB) and iGaming GGR trackers, 
drawing on operator-level data disclosed by state gaming commissions across 31 US 
states. This note provides insights into the state-level and national trends on market 
share dynamics, market size evolution, promotional intensity, win margins, and 
parlays as well as insights into customer perceptions of US OSB/iGaming brands 
using data from HundredX. We highlight read-across for the most topical debates in 
our gaming coverage such as: the growth and market share trends of the top players; 
the impact of competition from smaller operators such as Fanatics; the implications 
for the OSB industry from growth in prediction markets; trends in promotional 
intensity and implications for the US profitability J-curve; how existing states are 
maturing and implications for the long-term TAM; and the path to structurally higher 
gross win margins (hold rates). 
Summary of the latest trends 
This month, our trackers cover insights on October from 16 states that have 
reported so far: 
OSB handle growth remained soft in October, tracking flat yoy (vs. c.+1% in Sept). 
Consistent with Flutter’s commentary on unfavourable early-4Q sports results, 
October hold is slightly below its LTM average, though improved sequentially after a 
weak September. On a relative basis, FanDuel’s yoy handle growth appears broadly 
similar to DKNG’s, while Fanatics continues to outperform (off a lower base). 
Promotional intensity showed usual seasonality with a sequential decline into 
October, and in states that have reported data so far promos as a % of handle is 
tracking down slightly yoy. In iGaming, GGR growth re-accelerated slightly to c.+15% 
yoy in October vs. c.+12% in September.

How our US state-level OSB & iGaming trackers work 
 
 
Our trackers and why they are useful 
The OSB and iGaming trackers presented in this note collate and analyse publicly 
available data from state gaming commissions across 31 US states on operator-level 
handle, GGR, and NGR. This data provides high-frequency insights into market share, 
monthly revenue trends, promotional intensity, and win margins at the state level for 
online sports betting and iGaming within a rapidly growing market that generated GGR 
of c.$26bn in 2025, with a path to >$40bn by 2028E on our forecasts. This data provides 
an indication of intra-quarter trends by operator in the US as well as insights into the 
most topical debates for our gaming coverage such as: the differential growth and 
market share trends of the top players; the impact of competition from smaller 
operators like Fanatics; the implications for the OSB industry from growth in prediction 
markets; trends in promotional intensity and implications for the US profitability 
J-curve; how existing states are maturing and implications for the long-term TAM; and 
the path to structurally higher gross win margins (hold rates). 
Additionally, our trackers incorporate insights on customer perceptions using data from 
HundredX, reflecting relative Net Promoter Score (NPS) and Net Purchase Intent (NPI) 
trends across a number of US OSB/iGaming brands. 
Definitions and assumptions  
Gross Gaming Revenue (GGR): GGR is defined as the total wagers placed by a 
n
player less winnings. 
For any given month, the data disclosed by the respective state gaming commission is based 
on taxable revenue and considers the entire wager as GGR for an event that will take place 
in the subsequent month, inflating gross win margins slightly (because it implies 100% 
margin on open bets). 
Net Gaming Revenue (NGR): Defined as GGR less bonuses/free bets offered by 
n
operators. 
For some states (e.g. PA and CT), there is a cap on the bonuses that can be deducted to 
arrive at the taxable NGR, with bonusing above the threshold not being reported, leading 
to NGR from these states not being reflective of the exact bonusing in the month (especially 
in periods of heavier promotional activity, such as around the start of the NFL season). 
NGR vs. GGR: NGR remains the real revenue metric that matters to a company’s 
n
financials in the Online Sports Betting and iGaming industry, rather than the GGR 
covered primarily in this report. We focus on GGR because of the availability of 
disclosure from state gaming commissions. We explore the differences between NGR 
and GGR data below. Market shares and growth trends based on GGR quoted in this 
report may or may not be representative of the market share on an NGR basis given 
the impact of bonuses/promotions, among other things, which also vary significantly 
between states, operators, and over time. 
State reporting, joint licenses, and market share estimation: While we analyse 
n
OSB/iGaming data for 31 US states, this includes operator-level OSB market share 
data for only 23 states for GGR and 8 states for NGR. Hence, market share and other

In this report, we develop a quantitative framework around the three interlocking questions that, in our view, will 
determine the net impact of prediction markets on Flutter’s US earnings outlook and equity story: (1) What is the 
incremental EBITDA opportunity from FanDuel Predicts? (2) What competitive risk do prediction markets pose for

Entain: Improved risk/reward driven by Online and BetMGM momentum; upgrade to Buy (26 June 2025) 
We view the risk/reward as increasingly skewed to the upside on Entain, and upgrade the stock to Buy from Neutral. 
Our thesis is predicated on improved Online momentum and a clearer route for BetMGM’s profitability inflection to 
be reflected in Entain’s share price. We highlight that over recent months: (1) Entain has delivered on key elements 
of the Online turnaround and improved its growth; (2) the strategic pivot at BetMGM and recent trading 
momentum have increased our confidence in the transformation of BetMGM’s profitability and capacity for cash 
returns to the parents; and (3) certain overhangs have been resolved. We believe progress on these topics unlocks 
the path for a further re-rating of the shares, which currently trade at a significant discount to peers and history, 
and underappreciate the value of BetMGM.

explore the underlying economics of cohort monetisation and growth, new customer acquisition in existing and 
new states, and promotional intensity. This framework adds to our conviction in the US compounding at a higher 
rate than Visible Alpha Consensus Data expects and why we believe there are further US upgrades to come.

Customer perception insights from HundredX 
 
 
We analyse data from HundredX, a mission-based data and insights company that takes 
an innovative approach to monitoring consumer perceptions and gathering consumer 
feedback to understand trends across 80+ industries and 3,000+ brands. HundredX 
analyses collective opinions of everyday customers and evaluates how their priorities 
influence purchasing decisions and attitudes toward businesses and brands. 
The following charts analyse Net Purchasing Intent (NPI) and Net Promoter Score (NPS) 
across key brands present in the US online sports betting and iGaming market. 
Definitions: 
Net Promoter Score (NPS) - Widely used measurement that asks respondents, 
n
“Would you recommend to a friend?” on a scale of 0-10. The responses allow 
individual customers to be sorted into promoters, passives, and detractors as well as 
the computation of a company-level NPS through aggregation of respondents. 
Promoters: respondents to the NPS question who choose 9 or 10. Passives: 
respondents to the NPS question who choose 7 or 8. Detractors: respondents to the 
NPS question who choose 0-6. NPS standardized measure for ‘Overall 
Recommendations’ is on a scale of -100 to 100. 
Net Purchase Intent (NPI) - Metric indicates consumer(s)’s future spend, purchase 
n
or usage intent for an industry or a company/brand. In some cases, can be known as 
“Future Customer Loyalty”. A positive number indicates industry or company/brand 
growth, a negative number indicates industry or company/brand decline. If a 
company/brand has higher (i.e. more positive) PI vs. industry, this can indicate 
market share gain (and vice versa). Calculation represents percent of respondents 
who expect to use less over the next 12 months.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
