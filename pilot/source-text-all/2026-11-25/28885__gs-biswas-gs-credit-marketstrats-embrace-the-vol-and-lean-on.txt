I could think of only one phrase to describe last week – VOLATILITY. No asset class was spared in this
storm (Friday seeing some of the largest moves in recent memory) – the above chart illustrates the
magnitude of realized volatility investors are having to navigate through on a X-asset basis (there have
been few periods historically with higher realized volatility than current levels). This week’s price action
clearly demonstrated the value of having uncertainty hedge – as I was advocating in my notes over the last
two Sundays
In my view, the X-asset vol/options market was getting (very) complacent and underpricing the macro-
economic uncertainties in play – in fact, owning vol convexity was one of my conviction views over the
past 2 weeks. And it didn’t take long for the vol market to wake up to reality. Graphic 2 demonstrates that
awakening.
/ Credit this week – was relatively well behaved compared to equities and rates

+3.375bps of move in iTraxx Main on Friday can’t be categorized as a benign move but in the context of
the move in equities and rates, credit was one of the good boys out there. Below table demonstrates that
point – last week iTraxx (and CDX) indices widened just above half of the SX5E (and SPX) implied
widening using the historical credit/equity beta.
In fact looking at the flows in our European PT channel, it definitely didn’t feel panicky from the credit
investor community [EUR IG - 1.1x client sells vs 1x client buys, EUR HY – 1.7x client buys vs 1x client
sells imbalance over the last week]. Our sub-financial cash trader (Marin Velez) summarized it nicely –
“Equities weakness dragged our spreads wider with flows skewed to better sellers. However everything
was very orderly with no one really hitting back bids and RMs still adding risk on specific places”.
/ Negative correlation benefits and resilience of credit technicals
This is something I alluded to in my note 2 Sundays back (rates/credit spread correlation had been turning
negative (Graphic 3), making rates/duration an automatic stabilizer for credit total returns (= has been the
key difference for credit compared the spread widening episode from mid-August to end of September
(when both rates and spreads widened). This was in clear demonstration last week where both USD IG
and EUR IG bond indices had +ve total returns (rates rallied more than the magnitude of IG spread
widening) – so if you are an IG total return investor, you probably spent a happy weekend thinking about
what Christmas gifts to buy for your kids/wife/parents. In fact, DM credit total returns haven’t been too
bad over the past 1-month (Graphic 4 which shows the rolling 1m total returns across USD and EUR
credit).
Consequently credit fund flow picture has been holding reasonably well with inflows last week into EUR
IG / EUR HY / USD IG (some outflows from USD HY) as per Lipper data. This is providing technical
support to credit (Graphic 5) amidst this period of heightened macro and X-asset volatility.
/ OK - thanks for the above but how to position from here
This is clearly a tricky environment to have high conviction – especially going into the last stretch of what
has been a difficult year for many investors. I have been running with a framework of expecting a squeeze
in credit spreads into the end of the year - the week before last week was a step in the right direction for
that view, only for last week’s spread widening to reverse all of that. But I continue to view the current set-
up for credit as one where it’s unlikely (in my view) to realize a sustained move wider in spreads. Credit
spread valuations are back again to historical median levels (Graphic 1), credit investors aren’t over-risked
(judging from anecdotes and trader commentaries), credit fund flows are holding well (helped by OK total
returns) and December is the month of lowest issuance from a seasonality perspective (reducing the risk of
investors having to sell in the secondary to make way for primary). So these are what I will consider:
Consider some creative structures via iTraxx options to get long risk – structures which are flat premium
and cap the downside exposure. One of my favorite expressions to get long in credit has been buying
iTraxx receivers funding by selling iTraxx payer spreads. For example, investors can buy a Jan expiry 60
strike receiver vs sell a 70-80 payer spread in iTraxx Main (and get net premium paid). Investor has +ve

MtM if spreads move tighter while capped downside into spread move wider (Graphic 6 shows the payout
profile of that structure) – reach out on Monday morning for refreshed pricing.
Keep sourcing carry via defensive longs like iTraxx Main 5y5y fwd – I continue to see this as an
underappreciated long within credit. Use the carry from these longs to fund SPX/NDX gamma/vol.
Long risk SX7E (European bank equities) vs short risk European Snr Fins CDS: After having seen a
healthy rally in the first 2 weeks of November, SX7E sold off by 7% last week. In my view, European
bank stocks screen as reasonable source of upside exposure (both in terms of fundamentals and valuations)
– I would recommend being long SX5E and pairing it vs owning protection via European SnrFins CDS
(Graphic 7 shows the decent entry point on this trade). iTraxx SnrFin index skew (traded spread minus
NAV spread) screens wide (Graphic 8) – shorting the single names instead of the index allows investors to
set cheaper credit shorts vs SX7E longs.
