A more discerning market
Equities entered the final months of 
2026 on a more selective footing. 
October saw investors scrutinise the 
assumptions behind some of the year’s 
strongest performers, particularly in AI. 
Rather than questioning AI’s potential, 
markets appear focused on whether 
today’s exceptional investment can 
translate into tomorrow’s earnings. We strongly believe 
markets are being impatient: pricing a payoff years away 
requires staying the course. 
This is reshaping the market. Areas outside tech are 
attracting greater attention, while some AI beneficiaries 
have come under pressure. Headline moves increasingly 
conceal wide differences.
For investors, this may be a healthy development. Strong 
themes such as AI remain important, but valuations, earnings 
delivery and capital discipline are likely to matter more. With 
bond yields elevated and geopolitical uncertainty ongoing, 
the environment is rewarding selectivity: identifying not just 
where growth is occurring, but which companies are best 
placed to turn it into sustainable returns.
Michael Heldmann
CIO Equity
Global Equity Compass | What to watch
1 A higher hurdle for equities
Elevated government-bond yields are 
raising the return required from equities. 
With some valuations stretched, 
companies may need to demonstrate 
stronger earnings growth, reliable 
cash generation, and disciplined use of 
capital to justify valuations. This could 
increase dispersion between businesses 
that deliver and those relying on 
optimistic expectations.
2 From spending to returns
After several years of exceptional 
investment in chips, data centres and 
computing infrastructure, investors are 
now focusing on which companies can 
turn AI adoption into productivity gains 
and profits. This could further broaden 
opportunities beyond the companies 
building the infrastructure to businesses 
using AI to improve operations and 
strengthen positioning.
3 Putting ambition to work
Europe’s drive for autonomy is 
moving from commitment towards 
implementation. Investment in defence, 
energy, semiconductors, and industrial 
capacity could become visible in order 
books and earnings. Opportunities 
may begin to extend beyond the 
obvious beneficiaries to the suppliers, 
tech providers, and industrials enabling 
Europe’s ambitions.

Market review – style lens
•	 Stocks benefiting from strong market trends 
(“Momentum”) experienced a reversal in October, 
falling sharply after September’s strong rebound. 
The decline suggests investors rotated towards 
previously overlooked areas of the market, as 
greater dispersion created opportunities beyond 
established winners.
•	 “Quality” also struggled in October, after holding up 
relatively well through much of the year. Companies 
characterised by resilient earnings, strong balance 
sheets and dependable cash flows lost ground as 
investors showed greater willingness to embrace 
cheaper and more cyclical areas of the market.
•	 Attractively valued stocks (“Value”) were the 
clear beneficiaries of October’s rotation, reversing 
September’s weakness. As investors increasingly 
recognised opportunities among companies trading 
at more modest valuations, market participation 
broadened beyond expensive growth-oriented 
stocks and attention shifted towards a wider range 
of sectors and businesses.

Before the Fed starts hiking, markets tend to expect too 
little. The chart compares what money markets priced in 
on the eve of each of the last five hiking cycles with the 
tightening the Fed delivered over the following 12 months. 
In four of five cycles, markets fell short: by 165bp in 1994-
95, 78bp in 1999-2000, 16bp in 2004-06 and 296bp in 
2022-23. The average shortfall stands at 109bp.
For equity investors, the implication is that the starting level 
of rate expectations has been an unreliable anchor for 
valuations. When markets underprice tightening, the repricing 
of discount rates comes later and faster, putting pressure on 
long-duration and richly valued stocks. Investors should thus 
not assume that today’s priced-in path is the ceiling. Balance 
sheet strength, pricing power and earnings resilience become 
more important if rates rise further than expected.
