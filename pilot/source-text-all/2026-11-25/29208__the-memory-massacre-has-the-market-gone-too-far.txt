Memory: Pricing in disaster?
Memory stocks have been absolutely crushed. Positioning has collapsed, technicals are deeply
oversold, and the market is already discounting a brutal downturn in 2028.
But GS's latest numbers suggest some extraordinary pessimism is already priced in. Has the selling
gone too far?
The memory liquidation
Memory stocks have been among the most heavily sold names across North Asia. According to GS
trading desk data, memory led the selling across much of the region last week, contributing to an
eighth consecutive week of tech selling. Asia fundamental L/S net leverage has fallen to record lows.

The technical damage across memory has been massive. Samsung, Hynix and Kioxia have all been

Pricing in the worst?
This is where things get interesting. According to GS, current valuations for Samsung and Hynix appear
to be pricing in zero AI capex growth in 2028, alongside an additional 40% decline in memory ASPs.
That is considerably more bearish than the bank's base case. The market is already discounting a
substantial deterioration in the memory cycle, even as 2027 supply/demand remains tight.
GS
Strong 2027, weaker 2028
GS's Giuni Lee still expects a meaningful memory shortage in 2027, but the outlook for 2028 has
deteriorated. DRAM undersupply is expected to narrow from 5.1% in 2027 to just 1.2% in 2028, while
NAND undersupply falls from 2.2% to 0.3%.
The pricing revisions are substantial. GS now sees DRAM prices rising 32% in 2027 before falling 19%
in 2028. NAND prices are expected to rise 20% before declining 21%.

GS
The HBM explosion
Despite the more cautious conventional memory outlook, GS has raised its HBM market forecasts
substantially. The bank now sees HBM TAM reaching $60bn in 2026, $200bn in 2027 and $237bn in
2028, driven mainly by higher ASP assumptions.
That is more than 3x growth in the HBM market next year.
GS
The 50% collapse scenario
GS has stress-tested what happens if both DRAM and NAND ASPs collapse 50% year-on-year in 2028.
Under that scenario, Samsung's operating profit would fall 54% below the bank's base case. Yet the
company would still generate a 35% operating margin and trade at just 4.7x earnings and 1.0x book
value on downside-case estimates.
The contract cushion
One of the more interesting parts of the GS analysis concerns long-term agreements. According to the
bank, conventional DRAM prices would need to fall 32–54% below its forecasts over the next two years
before customers have a meaningful economic incentive to default on their LTAs.
That provides some protection against a sharp deterioration in spot pricing, although it does not
eliminate the risk of weaker demand or renegotiations.
GS

The cash return machine
Despite the deteriorating 2028 outlook, GS expects enormous shareholder distributions across
memory.
The bank forecasts Samsung shareholder returns of ₩187tn in 2027 and ₩195tn in 2028, while Hynix
is expected to return ₩135tn and ₩138tn.
Kioxia stands out, with GS highlighting a valuation of approximately 1.5x FY2028 earnings and a
potential shareholder return yield exceeding 30%.
GS
Stocks bottom before earnings
An important reminder from the previous memory cycle. During the 2017–2019 downturn, Hynix
shares troughed well ahead of earnings.
Memory stocks tend to anticipate the cycle rather than wait for earnings revisions to finish. With GS
already forecasting substantial ASP declines in 2028, the timing of the equity bottom may prove very
different from the timing of the earnings bottom.
