stabilise

nearly 9% lower, equity futures are marked 2% higher today. Equity
corrections have tended to be short, sharp affairs since a major rally
started in 2022, and investors will be hoping the worst is over. The
challenge will be another hot US inflation print this week. USD is set to
hold gains
Equity markets are stabilising after a torrid week, but the dollar can hold onto its gains
USD: Hawkish Fed undercurrent still a dollar support
After several support measures from authorities across Asia, local equity markets are
stabilising. This has been helped in part by seeing US equity futures marked 2% higher today.
Notably, Friday's US session did not see complete capitulation in equity markets, with
defensive sectors like consumer staples posting net gains on the day. However, with a 34%
weight in the S&P 500, the 7% fall in the IT sector did most of the damage. Since the current
major rally in US equities started in 2022, corrections have tended to be short, sharp
affairs. The S&P 500 has now corrected over 12% from its high last month, and some investors
may be considering buying the dip.

What would help stabilise equities would be a less aggressive Federal Reserve. Over the
weekend, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent chose his words carefully in saying that "many
people believe that they [the Fed] may be making a policy mistake if they
overtighten." However, Wednesday's release of the core PCE data for October looks set to
come in hot again and maintain solid support for a tightening cycle into 2027. That is why it is
probably a little too early to expect much downside for the dollar yet.
Any reversal in the likes of copper or precious metals may suggest that some of the dollar
buying pressure has peaked. Until then, however, we expect the defensive currencies of the
dollar, yen and Swiss franc to stay in demand. 
For today, US S&P PMI business confidence data is expected to come in on the strong side, and
we do not see the need for DXY to correct much below the 103.35/50 area.

CEE: Reasons to remain bearish reasons still in place
This week will be relatively quiet in the CEE region, with attention focused on data from Poland.
Today, figures for industrial production, wages, and PPI in Poland for October will be released,
followed by retail sales data tomorrow. In the Czech Republic, consumer confidence data is
due on Tuesday, and S&P is scheduled to publish a rating review on Friday after the markets
close. Last Friday, Moody’s upgraded its outlook from negative to stable, and we might see a
similar move from S&P this week.
Meanwhile, in the Czech Republic, Czech National Bank Deputy Governor Eva Zamrazilova and
Bank Board member Jakub Seidler characterised the recent interest rate hike as a pre-emptive
measure against the spillover of energy prices into the broader economy, though they offered
little forward guidance regarding the next meeting.
The CEE market has come under significant pressure from global trends, compounded by
dovish signals from most central banks in the region. Essentially, all regional currencies tested
new local lows on Friday. Following sharp moves over the last two trading sessions and a rally
in rates, we might see some relief. However, the global backdrop remains largely unchanged,
and in our view, the reasons to remain bearish on CEE currencies persist.
Frantisek Taborsky

EUR/USD while US tech stocks tanked. Nearly 30bp was priced out of the 2027 Fed tightening
cycle last week, but the dollar held its gains pretty well. We felt this was due to the market
already being relatively long dollars and a lot of option strike congestion in the 1.0850/0950

This week, EUR/USD has to face another high US October inflation reading on Wednesday, and
on Thursday, EU leaders should reach a decision on the next European Central Bank President.
According to Polymarket, the contest is over, with Pablo Hernández de Cos given a 96% chance
of getting the job. We doubt the euro needs to soften on this much-anticipated outcome and
would expect the Chief Economist position to go to a candidate from northern Europe as a
counterweight.
For today, look out for the flash PMIs for November. Apart from French business confidence
remaining in contraction territory, the rest of the region's confidence is expected to hold up
pretty well.
We do not see a strong reason for this EUR/USD correction to go much further than 1.1030 in
the short term. But we are moving closer to seasonal dollar weakness, and any close above
1.11 this week would provide much relief to an otherwise weak EUR/USD story.

CHF: SNB turning a little more hawkish
Over the weekend, Swiss National Bank Vice President Antoine Martin warned that inflation
was picking up. He was probably referring to the 1.5% year-on-year reading seen in October.
But his remarks do serve as a reminder about whether the SNB can keep rates at zero during a
global inflation crisis. Money markets price unchanged SNB policy into year-end, but two hikes
are priced in by next September. 
We doubt the SNB is ready to hike in December, but we do think it will grow increasingly
tolerant of Swiss franc strength, where the franc plays an important role in monetary
conditions for a small open economy.
We see downside risks in EUR/CHF over the coming quarters and could see a retest of 0.9125
over the coming weeks.
Chris Turner
