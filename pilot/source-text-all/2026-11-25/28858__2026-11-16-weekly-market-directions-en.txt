With US bond yields rising this year, government finances 
have become an important topic for capital markets. High 
debt levels, interest payments on debt, and elevated fiscal 
deficits — the excess of expenditure over revenue — are 
some of the key yardsticks markets are tracking. US interest 
payments as a share of government revenues reached an all-
time high of close to 20%, as shown in the chart. In fact, 
interest payments have been one of the main drivers of the 
increase in expenditure for the Federal government in the 
fiscal year ended 30 September. Looking ahead, this expense 
is likely to increase further.
We think this complicated fiscal backdrop, along with 
high inflation, will create more uncertainty in bond 
markets. The latest October CPI came in above expectations 
at 0.4% month on month, and confirms our view that the Fed 
will raise rates in the near term.

Global equities rose on the back of positive corporate earnings in the US 
and Japan. Renewed optimism in the AI sector also helped lift sentiment, 
despite an abrupt leadership change at a leading technology company. 
European markets also reached new highs. In fixed income, bond yields 
rose due to elevated US inflation and expectations of rate hikes by central 
banks. As a result, gold prices fell. Gold is a non-yielding asset, and higher 
interest rates reduce the appeal of the yellow metal.

UK growth firms at the end of Q3
GDP grew 0.2% in September after a flat August, beating 
expectations for no growth. Output expanded 0.7% in 
the third quarter, in line with forecasts. Services 
contributed most to quarterly growth, while construction 
contracted. Industrial production and manufacturing 
both exceeded expectations in September. The breadth 
of the monthly gains suggests the economy ended the 
quarter on a firmer footing, even as elevated inflation 
continued to weigh on household purchasing power.
Persistent inflation raises the stakes for December
US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October from 
September, above expectations. Core prices rose 0.3%, 
beating forecasts for a third straight month and 
signaling persistent price pressures. Grocery prices 
rebounded, while expensive diesel threatened to keep 
distribution costs high. The data strengthened 
expectations of a Fed rate rise in the near term.
Food prices shape the inflation outlook in India 
Consumer price inflation rose to 6.09% yoy in October, 
the highest in two years, and slightly above 
expectations, breaching the upper limit of the Reserve 
Bank of India's tolerance band. Food prices were the 
main driver, rising nearly 9% yoy. The acceleration in 
food inflation was evident in both rural and urban 
areas, adding to pressure on household budgets and 
keeping food costs central to the near-term outlook. 
Core Prices keep rising to, reaching 4.7% from 4.5%. 
The RBI is likely to hike rates again.
