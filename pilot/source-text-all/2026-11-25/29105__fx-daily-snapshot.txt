USD: Deepening equity market sell-off in focus during holiday shortened week 
The main focus in financial markets at the start of this week is the deepening correction 
lower for global equities which has been led by AI-related investments. The sharp sell-
off in US equity markets on Friday has spilled over into Asian equity markets overnight 
although they have staged a rebound heading into the close. At the worst point today 
the South Korean Kospi equity index had fallen by around -5.8% but it has since 
trimmed its losses to around -3.3%. It follows the heavy sell in US tech stocks on Friday 
when the Nasdaq composite equity index by a similar amount of -5.3%, and extended 
the decline from last month’s record high to -18.4% as it moved closer to bear market 
territory. The sell-off in AI related/tech stocks has also started to spillover more into 
broader equity market weakness with the S&P500 equity index closing sharply lower 
by -3.7% on Friday and extending the decline from last month’s high to -11.9%.

haven demand has helped global government bond markets to rebound. The yield on

In the foreign exchange market, price action over the past week shows that 
performance has been mainly driven by risk off trading conditions. The traditional safe 
haven currencies of the yen and Swiss franc have outperformed to a lesser extent the 
more liquid euro. In contrast, the high beta G10 currencies have underperformed such 
as the Australian dollar, Swedish krona, and Norwegian krone. We expect the 
performance of G10 currencies to remain tightly liked to global investor risk sentiment 
in the week ahead. The US dollar strengthened last week amidst more risk-off trading 
with the dollar index hitting a fresh year-to-date high just below the 104.00-level 
although it has lost some upward momentum late last week. In our latest FX Weekly, 
we recommended a new short USD/CHF trade idea to reflect the risk that the US dollar 
could correct lower if Fed rate hike expectations are pared back further in response to 
the sharp tightening in financial conditions and lower commodity prices.        
 
Commodity FX: Spillovers for FX market from reversal in commodity prices 
The worst-performing G10 currencies over the past week have been the commodity-
related NOK and AUD. However, not all G10 commodity currencies have sold off as 
sharply, with the CAD and NZD holding up better. At the other end of the spectrum, 
EM commodity currencies have been hit even harder, with the BRL, COP and ZAR  all 
recording outsized sell-offs this week. Last week’s price action extends the bearish 
trend for commodity currencies that has been in place throughout this month. 
The sell-off in commodity currencies is being driven primarily by the deepening 
correction in commodity prices. After peaking in mid-September, Bloomberg’s 
Commodity Price Index has since fallen sharply by around 14%, breaking below 
support at its 200-day moving average for the first time since the summer of last year, 
when the recent upswing in commodity prices began. The index peaked at just above 
the 140 level, similar to the peak reached during the previous upswing in commodity 
prices between 2020 and 2022. On that occasion, commodity prices subsequently lost 
around a third of their value over the following two years. This suggests there is still 
scope for commodity currencies to correct further in the year ahead if commodity prices 
continue to fall. Looking more closely at FX performance since commodity prices 
began rising last summer, the AUD, COP, NOK and ZAR have been among the best 
performers and are therefore more exposed to downside risks as commodity prices 
reverse course. 
It marks a reversal from earlier this year, when the Fed’s inflation-fighting credibility 
was being questioned ahead of President Trump’s decision to nominate Kevin Warsh 
as the new Fed Chair in March. The popularity of USD debasement trades peaked just 
before the US-Iran conflict triggered another energy price shock, which has since 
encouraged a stronger USD. Precious metals were viewed as key bellwethers of the 
popularity of the USD debasement trade, with gold and silver prices surging by around 
70% and 230%, respectively, between August 2025 and January 2026 to record highs.

Those bull runs have now almost completely reversed, with gold and silver falling back 
towards USD3,500/ounce and USD40/ounce, respectively. This highlights how 
precious metals have led the broader reversal in commodity prices currently under 
way. The ZAR has had the strongest correlation with the gold price and has come 
under more intense selling pressure over the past couple of weeks. USD/ZAR has 
consequently risen back to levels last seen in August 2025, just above 17.500, 
mirroring the reversal in gold. 
The correlation between gold prices and US real yields has clearly strengthened this 
year. USD debasement trades have unwound as threats to the Fed’s monetary policy 
independence have eased. Kevin Warsh has so far proved more hawkish than 
expected since taking over at the Fed, while the Democrats’ victory in the US midterm 
elections and their control of Congress have further reduced concerns over Fed 
independence. Together, these developments have dampened fears that monetary 
policy could be kept too loose to bring inflation back under control. The resulting 
reduction in the US policy risk premium priced into the USD has likely contributed to 
its recent strength, while putting greater downward pressure on commodity currencies.
