Key trends in apparel retailing: +ve NXT; ITX 9M
preview
In fashion retailing we have seen accelerated channel shift online in
mainland Europe. We see potential for the stronger chains to beat
expectations on top line and cashflow, but see potential for margin
pressure in the sector due to higher costs and promo activity. Our top picks
are NEXT and Zalando, while the valuations of H&M and ABF look fuller
at these levels. We also preview Inditex's 9M results to be reported on
December 2.
Channel shift favours stronger omnichannel/digital chains. In fashion
retailing a tough start to the Autumn season has been followed by
stronger trends as temperatures have fallen. However, consumers remain
exceptionally value-for-money focused and price and promo sensitive.
Online channel shift has accelerated recently in mainland Europe and
in this environment we expect the stronger online and omnichannel
companies to outperform eg NEXT, Zalando and Inditex.
Inditex's multi-concept expansion. We have seen Inditex looking to
internationalise its non-Zara concepts more eg in the important US
market. There Massimo Dutti, which has appeared in the Lyst brand index
since late last year, has recently opened in New York, while Bershka has
started out in Miami. Zara has reopened its Fifth Avenue flagship and has
>20 openings, renovations and relocations planned for the U.S. in 2027.
Pre-owned apparel gaining share. As exhibit 7 on page 7 shows, the global
secondhand apparel market has broadly doubled over the past 5 years to
reach just over 10% of sales. Resale makes up around 60% of the market
and around 2/3 of sales are made online. However we think the major
international fashion retailers (Zara and H&M), and online retailers such
as Zalando and ASOS are responding well to this trend.

Large chains in Spain continue to outperform independents and 
department stores 
In Inditex’s home market Spain we have seen a consistent trend of larger chains taking share 
from independents and also department stores. We attribute this to a concentration of spend 
towards larger retailers like Inditex, who have the best locations and a strong all round 
omnichannel offer.

US growth should accelerate 
Inditex has been in the US now for almost 40 years. In 1989 Zara had 69 stores in Spain and 1 
in Portugal and decided to enter New York. Sometimes the real estate market has been 
difficult, due to long-term contracts and fixed rents. But Inditex now sees the US as a very 
attractive market. Inditex’s current mindset is consistent with the view of selective growth 
rather than rapid expansion, and it wants to continue to be very precise and selective. There 
are three main reasons why we see a better outlook for Inditex in the US now than in the past: 
1) In the past only cities like New York, Boston, LA would be suitable but now due to 
demographic changes, Salt Lake City, Dallas, Houston, Miami and lots of other cities have 
become very attractive.  
2) Landlords are more open to negotiate shorter-term contracts with break clauses, which 
gives Inditex more flexibility.  
3) Inditex now has the knowledge to open physical stores with a higher degree of accuracy 
given its online presence. Eg, Charlotte opened in December 2025 – before that online sales 
were very positive, probably as Madrid has a direct flight to Charlotte. The idea is not to 
cannibalise but to foster the online presence with a physical location. In some locations where 
it has 1 store eg in Phoenix, we think Inditex is considering an upgraded store.

This approach also applies to the other formats eg Massimo Dutti opened in 2024 in Miami

are happy to take risks with Inditex. Inditex goes project by project – EUR40bn of sales is the 
consequence of making the right decision store by store in the right markets.

Focus on quality space 
What matters more than quantity is the quality of the space and this is improving for Zara, as 
a result of its retail optimisation programme.  
Eg, Zara closed several months ago two iconic flagship stores - London Bond Street (where the 
UK HQ is) and Bocconi in Rome. Inditex reopened the two stores at the end of May. The trend 
in sales for both has been very strong. The same should go for the 660 Fifth Avenue store in 
New York which has just reopened, following a 7-month renovation.  
This is the first U.S store to feature an automated system for identifying and sorting both newly 
arrived garments and items returned from fitting rooms. It spans three floors and also features 
automated kiosks for picking up online orders and processing returns and it integrates the in-
store experience with the brand’s mobile app. We expect a big sales uplift given the improved 
layout and experience. So Inditex is much more focused on the quality rather than the quantity 
of space.

Within stores Inditex has more than 15k self-checkouts now, taking 15% of the total sales in 
stores. Also Inditex can add shoes and handbags sections to many stores. ITX has been selling 
shoes for decades but has found that performance has been much better with a stockroom 
close to that space (within 4 minutes customers can have the right size). Sometimes Inditex 
allocates space 500m to 1 km away from the main store – it has 100 standalone stores with 
that option, mainly Zara Man stores. 
Massimo Dutti and other ITX concepts gaining brand heat 
Globally ITX’s share is still just c.2.5% of the global fashion market and only 0.5% in the US, 
where it is gaining share. And non-Zara formats have a limited presence in 59 markets so have 
plenty of growth potential. Inditex is now finding it easier to secure prime locations in major 
cities for non-Zara concepts eg Massimo Dutti and Bershka.  
The establishment of a global online platform for the concepts changes the risk profile of real 
estate projects and their viability. The use of client location heat maps is helping.  
There are also other factors in play. The attractiveness of the non-Zara concepts to real estate 
operators is significantly higher today than in previous years due to their relative size and 
success. Inditex has become more attractive to landlords as it is reliable and its stores draw 
traffic.  
The non-Zara concepts often compete with each other. Data flows well across the group. Zara 
represents 70% of group sales which gives it some diversification. Even within Zara, Zara Basic 
and other ranges compete for space with other segments.

As mentioned Bershka opened its first store at the Aventura Mall, Florida, in August and we

Massimo Dutti has just opened a store in SoHo New York (575 Broadway). This is its first New

Massimo Dutti has also just opened its first store in central Oslo (7 years having last been in

Interestingly Massimo Dutti has appeared in the Lyst Index of leading brands since late last

Lefties expansion 
Lefties, which used to be Zara’s outlet format, has had its own design/buy setup for the last 
15 years. It has the same business model as Zara with the same feedback loop and supply 
chain, and stock sent to stores twice a week.  
It has 8 stores in Turkey, it recently opened in Florence, it has opened in the outskirts of Paris, 
and there will be a second one soon in France. It now has two stores in the UK (at Liverpool 
One and Lakeside) and is opening soon at the Metrocentre at Gateshead. We believe Lefties’ 
margins are broadly in line with the group average. In 2027 we think Lefties will open its first 
store in Germany (in Dusseldorf). Traffic is good and the conversion rate is good. Also products 
are available online unlike at some other value retailers.

Major fashion retailers eg ITX and H&M are responding well to 
the growth of the pre-owned segment 
The global second-hand apparel market has grown broadly in line with our expectations five 
years ago, doubling in size to represent around 10% of apparel retail sales. Over the next 5 
years we expect over 40% further growth meaning that the segment should grow to around 
12-13% of clothing sold.  
More shoppers are choosing to buy second hand, in order to save money, drive circularity in 
the sector and to avoid waste. Also, younger consumers in particular are increasingly seeing 
value in their wardrobes, and purchasing decisions are often made with resale value in mind. 
The risk for the overall sector is that second-hand clothing cannibalises the sale of new fashion.  
However, the major fashion retailers are responding well to this trend, in particular the major 
international fashion retailers (Zara and H&M), and online retailers such as Zalando and ASOS. 
The key point for us is that the global fashion retail market remains very fragmented. In 
European fashion retailing we see the most valuation upside for NEXT.

Inditex business model hard to replicate 
In our view there is a general misconception that Inditex’s business model is all about one 
thing (eg being fast), whereas Inditex offers a whole array of advantages eg its model is quite 
expensive and is not easy to replicate. Inditex sees its model as strong supplier relationships 
enabling speed.  
Also, it sees stores and online working together and Inditex offers a lot of diversification in 
terms of sales and production. It sources from 50 different countries, and its supply chain 
allows Inditex to react with speed which is more important than ever, given the role of 
influencers and globalisation of fashion. Nowadays some trends only last a few weeks. 
In the last few years Inditex has lost Russian airspace (but its teams identified new routes). 
Inditex has a fully integrated model with a single inventory view with the best locations. Inditex 
has 16 DCs (15 in Spain, 1 in the Netherlands) which distribute stock to its stores. Also, the 
most expensive cost in fashion is markdowns and Inditex is very strong at minimising these.  
Inditex’s model offers a combination of different components and we think if another retailer 
doesn’t have all the elements its model would be very hard to replicate eg Inditex’s capacity 
to spot and react to trends (its feedback loop), with >700 designers, strong long-term 
relationships with suppliers, and its ability to allocate inventory efficiently to stores/online.  
Inditex 9M preview 
Inditex is due to report its 9M results on Wednesday 2nd December. Our preliminary forecast 
is for Q3 sales of EUR10.6bn (+8% in cc), with PBT of EUR2.54bn and EPS of EUR0.63 (+8% yoy). 
This is slightly below consensus of EUR0.64.  
Inditex reported sales up 9% yoy in cc between Aug 1 and Sept 7, which we think showed the 
strength of its base collections. However, September was a challenging month for the sector 
due to mild weather in many major markets, before temperatures normalized in October and 
November.  
For Q3 we model gross margin down -15bps yoy and EBIT margin down -30bps yoy as we 
expect transportation cost pressures (eg air freight and online deliveries) to remain elevated 
in H2. Partly offsetting this should be a lower rate of growth in D&A as Inditex has now passed 
through its very high period of capex in the years to Jan 2025 and Jan 2026. 
For FY28 we model gross margin slightly down due to recent EUR weakness versus the USD, 
which is likely in our view to cause some small upwards pressure on pricing, to maintain a 
broadly stable gross margin. As ever we expect the stronger retailers to negotiate hard with 
suppliers to achieve their bought-in gross margin expectations.

For the most recent trading period (November), we forecast sales in cc +7% versus a relatively 
challenging comparison of +11% yoy last year. Inditex’s LFL sales improved through the course 
of the year last year as it successfully responded to fashion trends. 
For this year we have nudged down our LFL sales forecast due to softer industry trends eg in 
Inditex’s home market Spain. However, the recent weaker EUR trend versus other currencies 
should boost sales for next year, but at the expense of gross margin on a c.6-month lag. The 
U.S. is now Inditex’s second biggest market which gives it something of a natural hedge, but 
overall Inditex is net short USD and hedges half of its net USD exposure. As such we can see 
gross and operating margin down slightly next year, particularly in the first half.

Risks to rating and price target 
We could see downside risk to estimates due to macro, fx or geopolitical challenges in key 
markets, or if the competitive environment becomes more intense. Inditex is fairly leveraged 
to the euro exchange rate versus a basket of other currencies; a stronger EUR has in the past 
caused negative translation and gross margin mix effects, due to Inditex pricing higher outside 
the Eurozone and having a relatively high proportion of costs in euros. ITX could also see softer 
reported sales trends in Americas or Asia/RoW which we think would be unhelpful for its 
valuation multiple. 
Upside scenario 
Our upside scenario of EUR71 assumes that Inditex can accelerate its normalised LFL sales 
growth from mid- to high-single-digit long term, with stronger full-price sales. Short-term 
potential upside could come from an improved LFL sales trajectory, stronger full-price sales or 
tighter cost control. 
Downside scenario 
Our downside scenario of EUR46 assumes that Inditex sees a very low-single-digit trend in 
normalised sales growth and that its operating margin falls to 17% long term. Short-term 
potential downside could come from a slowdown in sales momentum, or higher-than-
expected discounting.

JD Sports (SP): reducing FY27e-28e PBT 1-2%, raising EPS 1-2%, PT 90p to 100p 
For JD Sports we have reduced our FY27-28 PBT forecasts by 1-2% mainly due to lower EBIT 
forecasts for Europe and APAC, due to a weaker EUR trend vs the £, a slightly lower expected 
space contribution and a tough Australian consumer backdrop. 
Our EPS forecasts rise slightly due to JD buying back more shares than we expected in H2 at 
an average share price of c.85p. Our DCF based PT increases from 90p to 100p due to slightly 
lower longer-term capex and cash rental forecasts and a slightly lower share count. 
Key points on JD Sports are as follows: 
• 
JD’s thoughts on Nike comments from the CMD. Nike made a lot of comments in line with 
what JD has been saying for the last 12-18 months. They acknowledged that the 
addressable market was growing at 3-4%. Their market is bigger than JD’s; JD has talked 
to 2-3% average growth in its addressable market over time. A lot of the $5bn growth Nike 
talks about for the topline is being driven by Performance, which should be good for JD as 
most ranges that do well in Performance end up being adopted into Lifestyle in time. It is 
quite natural for Nike to go back to its roots of Sports first and then Lifestyle follows; that 
logically makes sense to us with how the typically industry works. 
Vomero is a business that has bucked the trend as Nike has built a $1bn turnover business 
in c.15 months. Equally, there are examples of products that have not worked and brands 
have been pulled back. Salomon is being driven by 1 franchise, the XT-6, which it brought 
to the market originally 3 years ago and it didn’t work. Salomon brought it back when the 
market was more willing to address that style and it is flying. JD thinks we will see some 
old silhouettes being reinvented to support sales in 2028 as Performance is rebuilt. 
Overall JD sympathises with the sort of shape Nike is guiding to i.e. short-term weakness 
then longer-term recovery. As JD said, “If it’s painful for them then it will be painful to 
some extent for us”. A recent RBC note on Nike is here: Capital Markets Day feedback. 
• 
FY28 outlook 
There are three things to flag for FY28: 
1) JD doesn’t see anything to indicate that the market will be anything other than 
muted, either in terms of consumer financial health or in terms of lifestyle product 
choices. There isn’t anything to suggest an inflection in FY28.  
2) On space, JD will be focusing on its existing estate, rather than opening new stores. 
A lot will be around restructuring. JD did a lot in Eastern Europe in Q3. It saw 85 net 
closures in Q3, including 21 stores that moved to a franchise model. As such, space 
contribution to sales has stepped down. This has been 1.9% YTD, slightly below the 
bottom end of its 2-3% target range and it expects something similar next year as 
well. 
3) Inflation has ticked up a bit again since September. JD will continue to work hard on 
structural cost savings to offset against that.

Risks to rating and price target 
Macro and competitive risk – a deeper or more prolonged downturn in consumer spend on 
sports fashion is a key risk for JD, particularly in the UK and US markets. There is the risk of 
more price competition for JD, particularly from wholesale, off-price and online competitors, 
or weaker brands engaging in higher discounting, which may impact JD's LFL sales, given its 
more full-price stance. Also, JD has material (c.38% exposure) to the US younger consumer 
which has been under pressure recently due to cost-of-living concerns. 
Fashion and inventory risk – JD Sports is exposed to fashion risk, as such its sourcing expertise 
is very important. In addition, given its sports fashion positioning, if sporting apparel and 
footwear become less popular, or the product cycle weakens, JD Sports would be at risk of 
weaker sales. JD generates over 50% of sales from Nike and adidas, so it will be somewhat 
dependent on the performance and outlook for these key brands. 
Forex risks – if the GBP were to strengthen materially versus the USD, EUR and other 
currencies, JD could see a negative translation effect and mix effect on margin. Alternatively, 
JD could face higher purchasing costs if the GBP weakens versus the USD, although this should 
be less than for other retailers as less than 10% of product is directly sourced in USD. 
Sustainability risks – JD has ranked only moderately well on our sustainability scorecard, and 
generally the sportswear retailers are perceived less well on sustainability metrics than the 
major international fashion retailers. Generally, the fashion industry has higher-than-average 
sector risk in terms of working conditions and its impact on the environment. 
Upside scenario 
Our upside scenario of 130p assumes a faster 10-year CAGR in sales of c.2pp above the current 
CAGR of c.1% and with a long-term operating margin c.1pp above our estimate of c.7.0%. For 
instance, JD could see higher-than-expected online growth or a better-than-expected margin 
in the US or the UK. It could also benefit from further consolidation by major brands in the 
European market towards major partners. 
Downside scenario 
Our downside scenario of 70p assumes a long-run CAGR in sales of closer to flat, and that its 
long-run operating margin trends down to c.6%, owing to a combination of weaker sales, 
higher-than-expected cost pressure or a more competitive environment. This could also come 
about from a structural move away from the sports fashion category or if JD suffers from a 
more competitive environment in sports fashion.

Explanation of RBC Capital Markets Equity rating system
An analyst's 'sector' is the universe of companies for which the analyst provides research coverage. Accordingly, the rating assigned
to a particular stock represents solely the analyst's view of how that stock will perform over the next 12 months relative to the
analyst's sector average.
Ratings
Outperform (O): Expected to materially outperform sector average over 12 months.
Sector Perform (SP): Returns expected to be in line with sector average over 12 months.
Underperform (U): Returns expected to be materially below sector average over 12 months.
Restricted (R): RBC policy precludes certain types of communications, including an investment recommendation, when RBC is
acting as an advisor in certain merger or other strategic transactions and in certain other circumstances.
Not Rated (NR): The rating, price targets and estimates have been removed due to applicable legal, regulatory or policy constraints
which may include when RBC Capital Markets is acting in an advisory capacity involving the company.
Risk Rating
The Speculative risk rating reflects a security's lower level of financial or operating predictability, illiquid share trading volumes,
high balance sheet leverage, or limited operating history that result in a higher expectation of financial and/or stock price volatility.
