Comments from US Federal Reserve (Fed) Governor Christopher Waller about the potential need for a 50bps rate hike at the
December Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting set a bearish tone in markets early last week. With no
incremental positive news on AI or around Iran, equities continued their moves sharply lower, with Friday particularly volatile
as the VIX index spiked above 35 for the first time since ‘Liberation Day’ in April 2025. The S&P 500 and MSCI EM are now
well below their 200-day moving averages and up just 2% and 3% YTD, respectively. Quite incredibly, the NASDAQ is now
negative YTD as at last Friday, having erased its 24% gain in just over five weeks, even as forward earnings estimates
continue to rise. AI jitters have also fed through to metals, with London Metal Exchange (LME) copper down around 17%
MTD and now below its January level at USD 12,200.
NVIDIA reinstating Jensen Huang as chief executive officer (CEO) today, with US government support, is likely to stabilise
markets and lead to a relief rally. Beyond a near-term bounce, if earnings estimates hold, current valuations represent very
attractive buying levels for technology companies and AI capex beneficiaries in particular. This is even more true in
emerging markets (EM), where the Information Technology (IT) index has never been cheaper at just 5.3x forward earnings,
a 60% discount to its long-term average. Of course, expectations are high but continued strong earnings growth in tech
remains our base case, even as growth mathematically compresses from a high base: EM IT earnings growth of 186% over
the trailing 12 months is expected to slow to around 50% annualised over the next two years. For now, there is no indication
that this or something close to it won’t be achieved. Korean export growth is still running at over 100% yoy as at November,
AI monetisation by the hyperscalers and labs continues to improve, and supply and demand dynamics around compute are
unlikely to flip for the foreseeable future.
That said, fundamental strength alone risks downplaying the extent to which the weakness in markets reflects a justified
shift in perceptions around the skew of potential outcomes. Markets are currently focused on the potential for AI capex to
disappoint in 2027 relative to high expectations, and to flatline or decline in 2028. At the same time, the outlook put forward
by the most bullish AI investors in recent years, such as Leopold Aschenbrenner at the more extreme end, looks much less
likely than it did six months ago, given a hawkish Fed and the political shift on AI within the US after the Democrats’
resounding midterms victory. That outlook called for continued rapid yoy increases in capex from 2027, rising to well over
USD 5tn a year by 2030.
Policy-driven growth concerns meant Dollar strength rotated from developed markets (DM) to EM last week. The prior week
had marked the biggest five-day outperformance of the DXY (heavily weighted against the euro and yen) versus the broad
Dollar in the last year, and that reversed sharply. This is typical price action, as the Dollar tends to act as a market-sensitive
safe haven and usually outperforms during a policy-driven tightening, while EM currencies only come under pressure once
the growth risks begin to show up in the data. In our view, the risks of a collapse in AI-driven growth are currently
overblown, with widespread technical selling and leverage unwinds exacerbating the downside price action. Once the sell-
off stabilises, the macro-negative view on AI should moderate, which should stem EM foreign exchange (EMFX) downside.
While we are likely still not very close to a Fed policy shift, falling energy prices are likely to lower inflation figures in the
coming weeks, which may support a more dovish outlook for rates than currently priced.
Geopolitics
US President Donald Trump said the US will ban companies from building data centres outside the country, threatening a
“tremendous tax” on US companies that build them in foreign nations (“we want them to build it here”). He described past
chip outsourcing as “destructive” and “horrible” and vowed to stop offshore data centre expansion. He also said he and
Chinese President Xi Jinping had agreed to set “joint guardrails” on AI as the US and China lead the global race.
Trump, Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin held their first trilateral summit on 19 November in Shenzhen, on the sidelines
of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) forum. It was more optics than substance. Their joint “peace declaration”
commits to a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and reaffirms “stable energy flows”, but it leaves out Ukraine and Iran.
Trump said Xi agreed that Hormuz must stay open, but no mechanism was announced. On trade, both sides say they are
sticking to the Busan truce, which expires on 10 January. Rare-earth exports remain constrained, however, and the US
tightened loopholes in its AI chip export controls days before the summit.
Elsewhere, Russia struck bridges and power plants in Kyiv and western Ukraine, and US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi earlier
in the month produced nothing concrete. Saudi Arabia is reportedly preparing for a prolonged campaign against the Houthis,

China:  October activity data disappointed across the board. Retail sales growth slowed to 0.4% yoy from 1.1%, below the
0.8% consensus, as gold and jewellery sales fell 28.7% and autos 21.4%, with the trade-in programme now a headwind.
Industrial production growth eased to 4.8% yoy from 5.5%, although high-tech manufacturing grew 17.6%. Fixed asset
investment fell 8.1% yoy year-to-date, with real estate investment down 21.6% and private investment down 11.4%, while
new home prices fell for a 41st straight month.
Indonesia:  MSCI appears to be softening its stance on Indonesia. Press reports said CEO Henry Fernandez told President
Prabowo on a 10 November call, brokered by Ray Dalio and attended by Danantara CEO Rosan Roeslani, that MSCI is open
to alternatives to a two-step downgrade of Indonesia’s market status and does not necessarily intend to downgrade at all.
MSCI would keep working with the authorities on reforms instead. These include removing politically affiliated figures from
the board of the Financial Services Authority, which MSCI sees as conflicted and too passive, communicating better with
investors, and setting clearer rules on the high shareholding concentration list and on the criteria and timing of LQ45
changes. Prabowo reportedly pledged stronger enforcement.
South Korea:  Exports for the first 20 days of November totalled USD 77.4bn, rising 101.5% yoy versus import growth of
39.4%, putting the trade surplus over the period at USD 27.1bn. On a per-day basis, exports were up 95.2% yoy, with chip
exports +294.4%, exports to China +145.6% and exports to the US +142.7%.
Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il said the measures announced on Sunday 22 November, which include preparing a
KRW10.76tn market stabilisation fund and relaxing daily share buyback limits for three months, will ease investor sentiment.

Separately, Korea and China concluded talks on liberalising services trade and investment under their free trade agreement
(FTA) after nearly nine years, opening Chinese markets in film, gaming, tourism, legal services and construction to Korean
firms.
Thailand:  Trump and Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul announced a “substantial conclusion” to talks on a reciprocal trade
agreement, which Trump said would be finalised in the coming weeks. Thailand will remove all tariffs on US imports, cut
non-tariff barriers on US vehicles, medical devices, pharmaceuticals, ethanol and poultry, and buy around USD 2.6bn of
additional US farm goods. Thailand will also seek a cut in the US forced-labour tariff to 10% from 12.5%. Exports to the US
rose 43.6% yoy to USD 74.9bn in January-September, a quarter of the total.

Brazil:  Vice President Geraldo Alckmin and Piauí Governor Rafael Fonteles are reportedly the frontrunners to become
finance minister, with President Lula da Silva expected to decide by the end of November. Alckmin’s fiscal track record and
political experience would be welcomed by markets ahead of a more right-leaning Congress from 2027, though appointing a
vice president who cannot be dismissed carries its own risks. Lula reiterated his commitment to fiscal balance while ruling
out cuts to social rights, and the Senate approved the government’s fiscal plan, but markets still await concrete measures to
address rigid spending such as minimum wage indexation.
Chile:  GDP contracted 1.1% yoy in Q3 and 1.8% qoq, as mining output fell 14.7% yoy and fixed investment 6.7%, while non-
mining activity grew 1.0%. Central bank Governor Rosanna Costa said the expected investment recovery may be delayed
and will be reassessed in December’s Monetary Policy Report, while warning that inflation risks remain significant as fuel
price ceilings only postpone pass-through. She attributed the peso’s slide past USD/CLP 1,000 to global factors and lower
copper prices, signalling no need for FX intervention. The weaker investment outlook undermines part of the case for
October’s pre-emptive hike.
Colombia:  GDP growth slowed sharply to 0.5% yoy in Q3 from 3.6% in Q2, below expectations, and contracted 0.8% qoq.
Government consumption fell 2.6% qoq as election-related spending ended and a spending freeze took effect, household
consumption slowed to 1.7%, and the August earthquake disrupted activity. Separately, a leaked letter from President
Abelardo de la Espriella urging ministers to deliver results and coordinate, exposed tensions within the government, coming
days after he reversed key parts of the Economic Rescue Bill against the advice of his finance team, and fuelled speculation
of a cabinet reshuffle.

Hungary:  The central bank held its base rate at 5.50%, although some Monetary Council members voted for a cut. Room
for easing has improved on a favourable market reaction to the government’s budget plans and lower energy prices since

Commentary
Egypt:  Egypt reached a staff-level agreement with the IMF on the eighth and final review of its USD 8bn Extended Fund
Facility and the third review of its resilience and sustainability programme, which should unlock USD 2.3bn once approved
by the board in December. The IMF said inflation has moderated more than expected and welcomed October’s 100bps hike,
while warning that regional escalation, high debt and large financing needs remain key risks. The government remains
committed to a primary surplus of 5% of GDP in 2026/27 and to cutting gross financing needs by 5% of GDP through
maturity extensions and liability management.
Nigeria:  Headline inflation eased to 14.06% yoy in October from 14.63% in September, down from 18.97% a year earlier.
Core inflation slowed to 10.78%, but food inflation remained elevated at 19.13% yoy despite a 0.75% monthly fall in food
prices.
Saudi Arabia:  Saudi Arabia is preparing for a prolonged military campaign against Yemen’s Houthis that could last into next
year, Reuters reported, aiming to secure battlefield gains through its Yemeni allies before negotiating. The Houthis have
captured key territory along the Red Sea coast and around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, forcing Saudi-backed forces to
regroup to defend Aden, while repeated attacks on Saudi cities and Aramco facilities have forced the postponement of
major investment, sports and tourism events.
South Africa:  Inflation rose to 5.2% yoy in October from 4.5%, driven by fuel, with petrol and diesel prices each up 11.8%
mom and fuel inflation at 42.9% yoy. Politically, Helen Zille of the Democratic Alliance (DA) conceded defeat in
Johannesburg as a coalition of the African National Congress, ActionSA, the Economic Freedom Fighters and the uMkhonto
weSizwe (MK) Party neared agreement to run the three Gauteng metros and eThekwini, leaving the DA in opposition despite
winning the most seats in several councils. The arrangement raises governance concerns and could strain the Government
of National Unity, making the market-unfriendly scenario increasingly likely.

Zambia:  The rating agency S&P upgraded Zambia’s sovereign credit rating to ‘B-’ with a stable outlook. The agency seems
to have more constructive copper assumptions than the current spot prices suggest. The upgrade is likely to hold given the
significant improvement in the country’s macro dynamics.
“The upgrade reflects our expectation that Zambia’s credit profile will continue to strengthen, primarily through robust
economic growth and narrower fiscal deficits from 2027 onward. We anticipate that copper prices will remain favourable,
supporting both economic growth and a narrower fiscal deficit and contributing to government deleveraging and improving
external dynamics. Net external indebtedness has significantly reduced, and we expect Zambia’s external liquidity position
to improve, bolstered by current account surpluses over the forecast period. Copper accounts for over 70% of total goods
exports. We forecast that prices will fall slightly from recent highs but will remain supportive.”
