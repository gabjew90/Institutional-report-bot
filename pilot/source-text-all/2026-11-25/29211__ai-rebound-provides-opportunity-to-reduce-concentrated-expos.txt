AI rebound provides opportunity to reduce

UBS House View - CIO Alert
Mark Haefele, Global Wealth Management Chief Investment Officer, UBS Switzerland AG
What happened?
US stocks rebounded on Monday from the steep sell-off last week, with the 
bounce gaining further impetus from the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as 
NVIDIA's chief executive officer. The S&P 500 closed 4.7% higher, and the 
Nasdaq 100 gained 6.9%. NVIDIA's shares rose 31.5%.
US Treasury bonds, which rallied last week as risk-off sentiment gathered 
momentum, gave back a little ground. The yields on 2-year and 10-year 
US Treasuries rose by 5bps and 1bp, respectively. Gold, which fell 9% last 
week, rebounded, gaining 3% to USD 3,636/oz.
We note that the holiday-shortened US trading week may also be 
magnifying moves, as reduced liquidity and adjustments ahead of 
Thanksgiving could amplify index swings in either direction.
Policy measures in Asia had helped steady markets before the US opened. 
Taiwan tightened short-selling restrictions, Chinese exchanges eased some 
margin-financing requirements, and South Korean authorities reiterated 
that they could deploy market-stabilization measures if needed. Taiwan’s 
equity index rose more than 2%, although South Korea’s KOSPI remained 
lower.
What do we think?
In recent weeks, markets have become more volatile as investor focus 
has shifted from the scale of capital expenditure to the returns that AI 
investment might generate. The Nasdaq fell around 10% last week, its 
largest weekly decline since March 2020. At Friday's close, the index had 
fallen 19.8% from the high reached on 6 October, putting it near to bear 
market territory, defined as a decline of 20% from a recent high. The S&P 
500 lost 8.6% last week, dropping into correction territory, defined as a 
decline of 10% from a recent high, having fallen around 11.5% since 6 
October.
We do not think the volatility is a signal that the structural AI investment 
story has broken. Cloud growth, rising usage, and continued demand for 
computing capacity remain supportive.
But we do expect greater differentiation between established platforms 
with visible monetization and strong balance sheets, and companies 
dependent on debt, scarce capacity, or continued speculative growth.

Our view on equities had already become more measured before the latest 
volatility. We keep global and US equities as Neutral, reflecting a more 
balanced risk-reward given elevated expectations, concentrated market 
leadership, and growing uncertainty around AI monetization.
This does not mean selling equities altogether. It means holding exposure 
broadly in line with a strategic allocation. In a representative balanced 
portfolio, equities may still account for around half of the allocation, with 
technology representing roughly one-third of the equity exposure.
Factors to watch before considering changes to our tactical stance 
include rates of AI investment, demand for computing capacity, financing 
conditions, and the relationship between revenues, free cash flow, and 
investment spending.
How to invest?
In a world where government policy increasingly drives economic outcomes 
and AI capacity is concentrated among a few large companies, the 
economic future cannot be forecast by understanding market forces alone. 
We therefore think it is crucial to build resilience by diversifying the sources 
of return across portfolios.
Volatility in the past week may have revealed some weaknesses in individual 
investor portfolios. Today's sharp rebound provides investors with an 
opportunity to strengthen portfolios before the next potential period of 
market instability. We think a gradual and disciplined approach to portfolio 
rebalancing is preferable to making all-or-nothing calls on where the 
market might move on any given day or week.
Manage excessive technology concentration. Investors already heavily 
exposed to US megacap technology should use periods of strength 
to reduce overlapping positions and broaden their sources of return. 
Regionally, we favor the Eurozone market, given similar earnings growth 
to the US but lower valuations and a broader sector mix. At a sector 
level, we prefer industrials and health care. Diversifying toward US equal-
weight strategies can also help investors reduce the influence of the largest 
technology companies on US equity portfolios.
Prioritize quality within AI exposure. Within AI, we recommend 
rebalancing toward established platforms with visible monetization, 
strong balance sheets, and the capacity to fund investment internally. In 
semiconductors and hardware, we prefer compute, leading foundries, and 
selected semiconductor-equipment companies, where returns depend more 
on technological leadership rather than on temporary scarcity. In contrast, 
we are more cautious about leveraged capacity providers and areas where 
earnings are particularly exposed to weaker capital spending, normalizing 
prices, or disappointing utilization.
Rebalance into fixed income. After several strong years for equities, 
some portfolios may be holding less fixed income than their strategic 
allocation recommends, creating an opportunity to rebalance. Elevated 
starting yields offer attractive income, which provides a cushion if yields rise 
further from here. We favor an intermediate average duration, generally 
through maturities of around two to 10 years. We see the most compelling 
return potential from select high yield and emerging market bonds, with 
yields in the range of 7-8%, though their higher income comes with 
greater credit risk and equity-like sensitivity. Quality bonds also offer 
attractive yields and can help insulate portfolios if volatility persists or

Add drivers of return beyond traditional assets. We keep gold as 
Attractive and forecast it to reach USD 5,000 an ounce by December 
2027. Central bank reserve diversification, sovereign debt, geopolitical 
uncertainty, and Chinese demand support its strategic role, despite pressure 
from higher US real yields, dollar strength, and liquidity-driven selling. 
Diversified commodity exposure (including copper) can also help position 
portfolios to weather inflation, geopolitics, adverse climate conditions, and 
supply risks.
We also favor adding exposure to less correlated returns via hedge funds. 
Macro, market-neutral, and fixed income relative value hedge funds 
offer differentiated returns when stocks and bonds move together, 
complementing rather than replacing the portfolio’s other sources of 
diversification.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
In line: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
