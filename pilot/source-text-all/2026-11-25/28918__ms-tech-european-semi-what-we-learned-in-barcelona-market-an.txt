Industry View
In-Line
Strong fundamentals but what can drive the shares from here? At this year's 
conference, companies across our coverage continued to strike a constructive tone 
despite the volatile market backdrop. Orders remain on track and pricing power is 
evident, with last quarter's prints showing further sales and margin progression. We 
agree that near-term fundamentals are solid, but the tape shows this is not 
translating into positive share price action  in the current environment. 
Geopolitics continues to weigh heavily on the sector, with most meetings devoting 
considerable time to risks around data centre build-out and potential export 
restrictions. Share price moves make the market's nervousness clear. We continue to 
like ASML given their unique positioning in the supply chain and think their shares 
have the best chance of outperformance from here. Overall, the week reinforced a 
clear disconnect between resilient business conditions and cautious investor 
sentiment.
Semi Cap Equipment - Balanced Tone, but Export Restrictions are the debate. 
ASML and ASMI offered the most balanced commentary, acknowledging uncertainty 
around potential US legislation and export restrictions while highlighting clear 
business strength into 2027. ASMI reiterated FY27 revenue above €5.7bn at 
constant currencies, supported by broad investment across leading-edge nodes, 
while VAT’s commentary on revenue moving towards CHF2bn reinforced confidence 
in backlog conversion. Strong near-term visibility, however, has done little to settle 
the 2028 debate. China and the proposed MATCH Act remain the principal

Analog and Optics - Data Centers, Data Centers, Data Centers. Solid orders, 
evident pricing power and confidence in the data center opportunity were the key 
topics of discussion. Infineon highlighted resilient AI DC demand (above the €4bn 
guide for FY27) and rising content per kilowatt, while STMicro reiterated that data 
center revenues should more than double in 2027 from c.$1.3bn in 2026 with 
messaging suggesting c.$2.8bn next year.  Photonics particularly stood out with 
Soitec reinforcing breadth of demand  and accelerating capacity expansion. More 
broadly, management teams appear confident that political pragmatism and 
engineering solutions to infrastructure constraints will help prevent a material 
slowdown in data center construction. Investor conviction, however, remains much 
weaker. Higher power and optical content offers a credible growth vector, but 
does not remove exposure to the pace of infrastructure spending. 
Arm - Power efficient architecture at the forefront We see power efficiency as 
one of the clearest structural themes, with Arm confident that their architecture 
becomes increasingly valuable as customers seek more compute within constrained 
power budgets. Management outlined an ambition for a 4x improvement in power 
efficiency by 2030, alongside firmer confidence in achieving c.$2bn of AGI CPU sales 
across FY27/28. Management sees ample demand, with wafer commitments 
supporting the initial ramp; delivery determines revenue conversion, while further 
upside requires additional supply. We also expect higher core counts to improve the 
economics of successive CPU generations. On the IP side, higher royalty content 
supports data center growth, while we think Arm is intent on launching a new v10 
architecture (2027?) with a  focus on AI compute and security. That said, the 
discussion also highlighted the wider gap between management conviction and 
investor caution. Arm sees build-out plans as largely intact through CY27, while 
acknowledging risk of potential delays thereafter (systemic). In  our view, the 
efficiency proposition addresses a real infrastructure constraint, but for now 
does not resolve the debate around the pace of AI investment beyond 2027.
