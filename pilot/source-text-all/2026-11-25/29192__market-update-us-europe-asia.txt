U.S. Markets Recap 
US -3.70% | Nasdaq -5.28% | Dow -1.94%
Synopsis

US equities ended lower on Friday, with all major indices logging notable weekly declines. The S&P 500 posted its seventh straight decline, 
the longest since February 2020, and the Russell 2000 fell 2.76%. The AI trade was broadly lower again, with selling across semis, memory 
and neoclouds; ANT­US fell sharply to just above its $83/sh IPO price, and META­US and NVDA­US were the biggest Mag 7 decliners. 
Strategists cited capital and regulatory constraints on AI, a peak earnings and capex growth overhang and tighter financial conditions from 
the hawkish global monetary policy tilt, which has raised concerns about a Fed policy mistake. Treasuries rallied with the curve steepening, 
yields down 9­14 bp. Gold finished down 4.6%, silver fell 7.4% and Bitcoin futures lost 5.7%, back below $60K. WTI crude rose 1.2% but 
logged a third straight weekly decline. 

On macro, Fedspeak leaned hawkish, with Waller again pitching a 50 bp move in December, though Williams saw no need yet for dramatic 
action; fed funds futures reflect about 55 bp of hikes through March, down from about 63 bp a week earlier. Final November UMich senti­
ment was 51.5 versus 51.6 consensus, and one­year inflation expectations dropped to 3.7% from the 4.3% flash reading.

Macro

Fedspeak hawkish, but expectations for December and March hikes little changed
The market wrapped up an extraordinarily dense two weeks of Fedspeak. The general tone has been hawkish, with many members high­
lighting persistent inflation and prospects for more hikes ahead, consistent with October's FOMC minutes, which said 'almost all' saw 
further hikes as appropriate. Governor Waller (voter) again pitched a 50 bp increase, though several members pushed back, including NY's 
Williams (voter), who said there was no need yet for 'dramatic action'. Chicago's Goolsbee (nonvoter) flagged AI­demand inflationary pres­
sures and said 'everything's always on the table' (Bloomberg). Philadelphia's Paulson (voter) defended members' communication blitz, 
saying a shared understanding matters more than explicit guidance (Reuters). Cook said financial conditions are too accommodative and 
that embedded inflation will need a more aggressive response from the Fed. Despite the hawkish drumbeat, fed funds futures currently 
reflect expectations for only about 55 bp in hikes through March, lower than about 63 bp last Friday. 

Final November consumer sentiment in line, inflation expectations ease
Final UMich November consumer sentiment came in at 51.5 versus 51.6 consensus and the preliminary 51.0 reading. Sentiment rose about 
7% m/m, driven by substantial post­election gains in short­ and long­run business expectations, though it remains about 9% below pre­
Iran war levels in February. The Current Economic Conditions Index improved to 49.4 from 46.7, while the Consumer Expectations Index 
climbed to 52.9 from 48.8. Year­ahead inflation expectations fell sharply to 3.7% from 4.7% in October, though they remain above Febru­
ary's pre­Iran war level of 3.4%. Long­run inflation expectations eased to 3.3% from 3.5%. Consumers still flag inflation rather than unem­
ployment as the greater concern. Personal finances improved for low­ and middle­wealth consumers amid falling gas prices, while wealthier 
consumers reported deterioration amid AI­related stock market volatility. 

Oil set for third weekly decline, though some see the selloff as overdone
Oil is on track for a third straight weekly decline, with an unwind of some geopolitical risk premium following the lack of post­midterm 
escalation, alongside recovering Middle Eastern flows, seen as the main causes. Trump told Axios he now does not expect an Iran decision 
until the new year. China's crude imports from the Persian Gulf surged 42% m/m in October to the highest level this year (SCMP). Goldman 
Sachs warned this week of a 2M bpd global crude surplus in 2027, even after accounting for 1M bpd in supply disruptions, and the IEA sees 
a return to surplus in Q4 this year. However, UBS argued the recent selloff is likely overdone, noting Middle East exports have fallen back 
in November following October's rebound. It also flagged renewed tanker attacks, stronger Chinese imports and depleted global inventory 
buffers, and sees attractive near­term upside, warning the market is still vulnerable to supply disruptions. 

Week-ended 20-Nov: AI headwinds and hawkish central banks versus depressed positioning
Bearish talking points: export controls are a new AI headwind, as Commerce issued rules requiring licenses for foreign data centers renting 
AI compute to Chinese firms; China concerns are not limited to Nvidia, with AMD hit by a Hunterbrook short report that also played into 
AI circular financing scrutiny; financial conditions tightened for the AI ecosystem as Nvidia signaled some dialing back of its 'bank of AI' 
role; and widely anticipated 'clearing events' failed to stop the repricing of the AI trade. Middle East hostilities ramped up this week, with 
reports the US is considering another round of extensive strikes, and there were more signs of housing market softness, with homebuilder 
sentiment at 14­year lows in October and the lowest level of pending home sales on record. Bullish talking points: Anthropic's IPO saw 
strong demand; Nvidia raised FY28 revenue growth guidance to about 75%; positioning is depressed, with an extended hedge fund selling 
streak in semis and CTAs having flipped to net short NDX; October core retail sales were better, and BofA spending data showed a strong 
start to holiday shopping; and 2027 strategist S&P 500 price targets cluster around 8,700, implying 20%+ upside.

European Markets Recap 
Germany -2.6% | France -1.6% | UK -1%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished lower on Friday, with the STOXX 600 registering its steepest weekly drop since March. A broad cyclical 
selloff led by tech, miners, industrials and banks was met by a flight to safety that supported defensives such as food and beverage, healthcare 
and utilities. The move followed sharp losses in Asia, led by Taiwan and South Korea, and weakness in the AI trade, with investors citing 
the risk of a Nvidia financing pullback, elevated valuations, competition pressures, ROI doubts and tightened US export controls. Bund 
yields were 10 bp lower at 3.31% and gilt yields 8 bp lower at 5.23%. 

On macro, UK October retail sales beat expectations with a 0.7% m/m rise versus 0% consensus, and GfK consumer confidence edged up 
two points to ­22. German PPI was firmer than expected, French business confidence fell to 95 versus 96 consensus, and eurozone negotiated 
wage growth edged up to 2.57% in Q3. ECB's Panetta issued a fresh inflation warning in line with expectations of a December hike. UK PM 
Burnham and EU leadership announced a deal on youth mobility, energy and food. 

Technology led the sector selloff, with ASML­NL, ASM­NL, IFX­DE, STM­FR, BESI­NL and SOI­FR sharply lower alongside AI­infrastruc­
ture industrials NOKIA­FI, SU­FR, LR­FR and PRY­IT. UBI­FR fell 23.2% as a H1 operating loss of €156M and free­cash outflow of €338M 
disappointed, WLN­FR fell 15.5% on renewed scrutiny of its 2021­23 cash­flow quality, and VK­FR fell 14.6% after Q3 EBITDA dropped 
26%. G24­DE and HEM­SE were cut to neutral at JPMorgan, and AC­FR fell after activist Parvus called for management changes. 
Macro

ECB to discuss higher bank reserve requirement as December hike looms
Reuters cited sources who said the ECB will discuss raising the minimum reserve requirement for banks at a meeting next week, in a bid to 
reduce its interest bill and withdraw some liquidity from the system. The ECB is looking at raising the share of cash that commercial lenders 
must keep in unremunerated accounts to 2% from 1% of deposits and other short­term liabilities, and most Governing Council members 
support a change now, which may raise speculation of a December announcement. Raising the level by 1% would save the ECB at least 
€4.4B a year at current interest rates, with savings potentially larger given that markets price a rise in the deposit rate from the current 
2.5% to as high as 3.25% next year. There is more than a 90% probability of a December hike and the possibility of two further increases 
next year. Separately, the ECB published Eurozone negotiated wages, which edged up to 2.57% in Q3 versus consensus 2.60% and prior 
2.44%, following Thursday's labor cost index, which moved up to 3.5% y/y in Q3 from 3.1%; pass­through from higher energy costs remains 
limited. 

UK and EU announce joint agreement worth up to £17B a year
The UK and EU announced a joint agreement worth up to £17B a year covering food and drink, carbon markets and youth mobility. Further 
discussions will take place on tuition fees and the EU's Made in Europe scheme (Guardian). Prior to the agreement, reports suggested that 
implementation was contingent on the UK lowering tuition fees for EU students. The government will bring forward legislation next week 
that replicates EU law in the UK, and details on the future relationship will be discussed over the next year. UK PM Burnham said the reset 
deal was the limit of what can be achieved in the current parliament, and press takeaways highlighted that he effectively kicked any decision 
on the future relationship into next year. Near­term focus is on Burnham's 10­year plan for the economy due in December. 

Upside surprise in UK retail sales; public finances helped by resilient economy
UK retail sales surprised on the upside in October, with a 0.7% m/m increase versus 0% consensus and a prior 0.6% fall revised down from 
a 0.4% drop. Excluding fuel, sales rose 0.7% versus a 0.1% forecast. The better outturn coincided with stabilization in consumer confidence, 
with the GfK measure edging up two points to ­22 in November on a pickup in willingness to make major purchases. UK public finance data 
showed the government borrowed less than expected in October, with net borrowing of £17.0B versus consensus £18.0B and a prior revised 
up to £22.9B from £24.5B. The deficit for the first seven months of the financial year was £111.3B, £3.3B higher than the OBR expected in 
March, though £7.3B less than the same period last year. ONS said income­related tax receipts were particularly strong, but overall debt 
continued to rise to £3.1T, equivalent to 95.8% of GDP. 

French business confidence deteriorates for a third month in a row
The French November business climate index recorded 95 versus consensus 96 and prior 97, with broad­based weakening in the survey. 
The manufacturing indicator fell one point to 104 versus consensus 102, services came in at 97 versus 99, and retail trade at 90 from 92, 
while the employment climate gained one point to 95. Takeaways pointed to ongoing expectations of weakness in growth into year­end 
following a 0.1% contraction in Q3, with investment down 0.6% for a second consecutive quarter and construction a weak point. Weaker 
growth expectations are a potential complication for the fiscal outlook, as Budget Minister Amiel said the growth slump means France will 
need to find €6B of additional savings in the proposed 2027 budget (Bloomberg).

(Axios). Trump expressed skepticism about further large­scale European financial support for Ukraine, particularly plans to help cover 
Ukraine's ~€70B financing gap for 2027. Envoy Kushner said Russia is now 'comfortable' with Article 5­like security guarantees for Ukraine, 
and Putin publicly invited European representatives to Moscow to clarify positions before any next­round format is set. At the Coalition of

Asian Markets Recap 
Japan -5.06% | Hong Kong -1.89% | China -1.22%
Synopsis

Asian equities ended sharply lower on Friday. Japan's Nikkei 225 ended 5% lower and the Topix 3.9% down, while Taiwan's Taiex closed 
more than 6% lower for a near 18% decline w/w. The Kospi ended more than 5% down even after a brief trading halt, and the Hang Seng 
fell to 19­month lows, down 5.4% on the week to enter a technical bear market. Mainland China was led lower by Shenzhen, and India again 
bucked the trend and traded higher. The selloff was led by the AI chain in South Korea, Japan and especially Taiwan, where local retail 
selling and underperformance by mid­cap tech stocks weighed despite tighter short selling rules. 

On macro, Japan's core nationwide CPI rose 2.2% y/y in October, just below the 2.3% consensus, and a Reuters poll showed 61 of 71 
economists expect a BOJ hike in December. China kept its 1Y and 5Y LPRs on hold. Australian employment fell 43.5K against a 25K gain 
expected, and markets brought the first RBA cut forward to September. 

SoftBank fell as much as 16%, and TSMC lost 4.8% on reports it is tightening Chinese customers' access to advanced manufacturing, with 
TSMC and Samsung Electronics suspending some China­bound shipments. NetEase fell 17.6% on a Q3 miss, Kuaishou 11.4% after guiding 
to Q4 pressure, Rakuten Bank 11.6% after Japan's FSA ordered a data­leak report, and Japan Tobacco 10.1% on a FY guidance cut. Alibaba 
gained 3.5% on a Q2 beat. 
Macro

Big selloff across the Asia AI trade as export curbs weigh on China tech
Significant losses across Asian tech benchmarks on Friday were led by Taiwan, with a weekly decline of 17% dragging it to its lowest level 
since April (now down 28% from its October record high). There was no support from the latest stabilization efforts after regulators tight­
ened short selling curbs on Thursday. South Korea fell to its lowest since early 2026 levels, with the selloff triggering another temporary 
trading halt, and SoftBank was the main decliner in Japan, dropping as much as 16% to its lowest since Aug­2025. Tightened US export 
controls and moves to close loopholes China exploited to access computing power from advanced US chips remain a drag on Chinese AI 
model developers. The Hong Kong tech gauge fell to its lowest since Sep­2024, and signs of fallout are emerging, with Tencent conceding 
curbs will slow model development, Alibaba flagging an impact on data center construction, and reports that TSMC and Samsung Electronics 
have halted some China­bound chip shipments. 

Japan core CPI slightly softer than expected
Core nationwide CPI rose 2.2% y/y in October, just below the 2.3% consensus and following 2.1% in the previous month. Inflation ex­fresh 
food and energy climbed 2.2%, also below the 2.3% consensus and 2.4% in the prior month. Higher energy contributions provided the main 
support, while net drags from policy effects also grew to 0.97 ppt from 0.82 in September. Electricity and gas subsidies roll off next month, 
though the ruling coalition is preparing another round in Q1, and Prime Minister Takaichi has spoken of plans to gradually phase out the 
gasoline price cap subsidy from December. The main drag on CPI was non­fresh food, with rice declines continuing to deepen as the latest 
wave of price hikes fades. Input cost pressures stemming from the Middle East are now the key variable, with higher freight rates apt to 
feed another round of price hikes from around April next year. This runs into the implementation of a consumption tax cut on food items 
scheduled for April, after which JCER consensus looks for core inflation to fall notably from a peak 3.2% in 1Q27 to 0.95% in 1Q28. 

BOJ seen keeping its quarterly rate hike cadence as Ueda stays the course
The Reuters BOJ consensus survey for November showed 61 of 71 (86%) economists anticipate a December rate hike, stronger than 69% in 
the previous month. The survey period covered November 11­18, in the aftermath of the AI selloff, and a strong majority said market 
volatility would not deter a December move. Consensus now looks for the following hike by the end of March 2027 at 60%, brought forward 
from June, taking the policy rate to 1.75%, which remains the baseline for the expected terminal rate with a 48% response. Shunto 2027 
talks are expected to deliver another 5.00% in wage hikes, just below 5.01% this year. In a semi­annual statement to the upper house, 
Governor Ueda repeated that rate hikes will continue while considering timing and pace, named the Middle East, AI demand and FX as the 
main risk factors, and emphasized a focus on containing upside inflation risk. He sees inflation expectations running at about 2%, the basis 
for declaring that underlying inflation has generally reached target. 

Weak Australian jobs data prompts dovish market reaction
Australian employment shrank another 43.5K in October, contrasting with consensus for a 25K gain and widening September's revised 
36.4K contraction. Full­time jobs fell by 2,900 while part­time employment decreased by 40,600. The unemployment rate unexpectedly 
rose to 4.9% from 4.7% (also consensus), a fresh five­year high, and the participation rate fell to 66.6% from 66.8%. The policy­sensitive 3Y 
yield slid to its lowest since early August and the Australian dollar hit an 11­month low. Markets have priced out further RBA rate hikes 
and brought forward the timing of the first rate cut to September, with minor odds of a reduction as early as May. RBA Governor Bullock 
this week noted inflation risks remain skewed to the upside, but a housing market slump and economic uncertainty could potentially offset 
some of those risks.

US, China, Russia summit high on symbolism, low on tangibles
Reuters reported that China, the US and Russia agreed on a symbolic 'peace declaration' at an unprecedented trilateral summit. While 
expressing solidarity against nuclear war, there were few tangible outcomes: the statement reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and

mention of negotiations on a trilateral nuclear arms reduction treaty. Trump noted Xi agrees on limiting nuclear weapons in principle, 
though he acknowledged challenges. Putin said in a press conference that the summit did not set ambitious goals and nobody would be 
forced to comply. The article noted mixed reactions, with some praise for Trump's willingness to engage US adversaries and others critical 
of giving autocrats a global stage, while some thought Putin gained the most from a return to the international stage.
