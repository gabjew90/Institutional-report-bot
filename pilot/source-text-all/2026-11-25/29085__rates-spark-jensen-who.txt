  RATES SPARK
Rates Spark: Jensen who?
Treasury yields paid minimal respect to the big moves in equities
through Monday. Yields, in fact, in the end, decided to edge lower,
mostly in a nod to the easing in the price of oil. That will help take
headline inflation rates off highs in December CPI releases. But the
upcoming US core PCE reading will still head in the wrong direction. That
needs to change
Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang
Bonds view the Nvidia saga as nothing more than a sideshow
The Nvidia reversal was reflected by a brief and ultimately minor tremor in Treasuries, but that
was about it.
The Treasury market effectively said - We see your 6% Nasdaq
rally, but we're not for moving.
The 10yr yield held in the mid-to-low 4.9% area, with, if anything, a tendency to test lower in
yield, which is the reverse of what would be expected from a resumed risk-on day. The
movement in the price of oil had a more significant correlation effect, with Brent managing to
dip below $90/bbl. This is clearly helpful for headline inflation rates down the line, and will

show up in the December inflation readings (reflecting November).
That said, the October personal consumption expenditure (PCE) inflation reading is due on
Wednesday, and it's not going to be pretty. The good news is the market should already be
anticipating an acceleration in core PCE inflation to 3.2% year-on-year (YoY) (up from 3.1%
YoY). The bad news is this is still a high number, and it continues to head in the wrong
direction. Also, the fall in energy prices will have a far less direct effect on core PCE. Ultimately,
it will, of course, have some effect, as, for example, airline fares can ease.
That apart, the bond market remains in a troubling environment over the coming months. We
suspect that, while the risk-on mode seen on Monday may not be repeated immediately, the
recent sell-offs in the AI space are, in fact, healthy over the medium term. They can ultimately
result in risk coming back on by the turn of the year. On top of that, fiscal narratives stretching
from Germany (defence) to France (already bloated) to the US (untamed) are all adding to a
sense of heightened risks for core bonds. The big swing factor could be inflation. But it's just
too early to declare victory on that front yet. So, we maintain a cautious bond view for the
next few months.
Tuesday's events and market views
Markets will likely remain focused on the bounce back in AI stocks. Eurozone data
includes Spanish PPIs for October, which, given the focus on second-round effects, will
be watched. Other releases include Germany's Ifo survey results. Consensus sees a
slight improvement in the business climate in November. From the US we have the
Conference Board consumer confidence index for November. A slightly higher headline
index is expected, but still weak by historical standards.
The Netherlands will auction €2bn of 3y DSLs. The US will auction $28bn of 2y FRNs and
$70bn of new 5y notes.
