Red warning sign – with a brave attempt at a rebound
Global key stories
One big red warning sign – key takeaways. The Nasdaq plunged 10% during an extremely volatile week
for equities, with 5.3% of the decline coming on Friday alone. The S&P 500 and Sweden’s OMXSPI ended
the week down 8.6% and 5.0%, respectively. Many AI companies are trading at stretched valuations, but
frankly, the potential of AI technology is extremely difficult – if not impossible – to quantify. From an
economist’s perspective, there are three reasons behind the AI sell-off: (1) signs of increasing political
intervention in AI, reflecting shifting power dynamics in Washington and Trump’s decision to penalise US
companies seeking to build data centres abroad; (2) rising short- and long-term real interest rates,
forcing a more aggressive discounting of future earnings – i.e. lower equity valuations – while also raising
funding costs; and (3) seasonal factors, as major investors seek to “close their books” and lock in strong
performance figures ahead of year-end. This increases the risk of deteriorating market liquidity, potentially
amplifying price swings. Analysts – and economists – now need to distinguish between AI valuations and
the long-term impact of these transformative technologies on growth, inflation and productivity. Anthropic
shares, which surged 17% on their market debut on Thursday, also lost ground but remain 7% above their
IPO price. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell by as much as 11 basis points on Friday to 4.95%, while
movements in the dollar and oil (Brent) were relatively modest. Thursday’s Thanksgiving holiday will
reduce market liquidity. Early on Monday, equity markets are attempting to rebound, with Nasdaq futures
currently up 2.3% (see table below for other market quotes).
A complicated world for central banks – reflections. In just over two weeks, the Fed will announce its
next rate decision. At the time of writing, markets are pricing in a 94% probability of a 25-basis-point hike
to 4.25%. It remains to be seen how the Fed – and other central banks – will navigate the new risk
environment: high inflation and still-solid GDP prospects, now set against less supportive financial
conditions (see chart below) and falling equity markets. The question is whether an equity market
correction, within reasonable limits, might even be welcomed by the ECB and the Bank of Japan, both of
which are also expected to raise their policy rates in December.
An extraordinary trilateral meeting with little substance – epilogue. Last week’s Xi-Putin-Trump
meeting was a Russian PR success, but hardly a Yalta 2.0 – despite the Kremlin’s efforts. Trump continues,
rather astonishingly, to refer to Putin as “my friend”. President Zelenskyy says: “To us, he is a murderer.” A
sign of the times, reflecting the erosion of the rules-based international order and its replacement by an
increasingly power-based one: on Friday, the UN Security Council failed – for the ninth consecutive time –
to appoint a new UN Secretary-General. A weakened and increasingly marginalised António Guterres will
leave the UN in six weeks. Conclusion: Geopolitical unpredictability must become a permanent parameter
in economic forecasts. The next major geopolitical event: the G20 summit in Florida on 14–15
December, offering Trump an excellent photo opportunity on home turf. Putin has been invited, but his
attendance remains unconfirmed. Notably, Europe will have a substantial presence this time – compared
with Shenzhen.

Nordic key stories
70 days without a Swedish government – some reflections. “No, but no thanks” was the Left Party’s
swift and clear response to the weekend’s offer from the three red-green parties. Government
negotiations (S+C+MP+V) are progressing at a snail’s pace – like a snail crawling uphill on a sand-covered
slope with the handbrake on. Frustration is growing among voters. A new TV4/Novus poll shows that 44%
favour a snap election, while 36% oppose it. The question now is: will the Centre Party switch sides and
join the right-wing “Alliance”? Keep an eye on 27 November, the deadline for parties to submit their
alternative budget proposals. This could provide an opportunity to abandon – or at least amend – the
caretaker budget if an alternative proposal secures support in parliament.
