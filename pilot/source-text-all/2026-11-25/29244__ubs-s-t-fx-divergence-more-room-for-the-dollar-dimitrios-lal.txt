• G10 FX Macro Implied Fair Value Model: Stress Testing the G10 Valuation Signals
A slower-moving calibration challenges some of the apparent cheapness in G10 FX. Extending the estimation
horizon helps distinguish cyclical dislocations from sensitivity to the model window, which is especially
important as G10 policy paths begin to diverge materially. The longer history places greater weight on stable
relationships among anchor variables. Where the baseline and longer-run specifications disagree, I would
apply a higher hurdle before implementing the tactical signal. In short, the longer window serves as a
robustness check and identifies where valuation conclusions are most specification-sensitive.
The table below compares implied spot levels under the baseline and longer-run specifications. The main
takeaway is that the longer-run model leaves more room for USD appreciation against most G10 currencies,
most notably in USDNOK (+5.1%), AUDUSD (-2.6%), and USDSEK (+2.0%).

• Equity Macro Thoughts: Framework for Evaluating Equity Stress
Over the past ten trading sessions, SPX, NDX, and RTY have fallen 8.6%, 10.4%, and 7.5%, respectively. The
scale and speed of the move warrant a structured framework for assessing equity stress. The macro narrative
currently resembles an equity-led growth scare: cyclicals have underperformed defensives by 9%, 10-year real
yields have fallen 13bp, and lower-quality credit has widened sharply, with CCC option-adjusted spreads up
188bp to nearly 1,200bp.
The framework assesses the current peak-to-trough move across three dimensions: depth, breadth, and
persistence. Persistence is not yet measurable given the recency of the selloff. The first two dimensions can
help investors calibrate whether to chase the move, fade it, or remain sidelined.
Start with depth: how exceptional is the current selloff? On a rolling one-year maximum-drawdown basis, SPX,
NDX, and RTY are down 11.6%, 19.8%, and 15.9%, respectively, placing each near the most severe decile of
the past twenty years (figure #12). Those drawdowns occurred over 34, 34, and 71 days. The corresponding
time-normalized drawdown intensities are roughly 2.0%, 3.4%, and 1.9%, ranking near the 95th, 99th, and
85th percentiles, respectively. Relative to the realized-volatility regime preceding the selloff, the moves in SPX,
NDX, and RTY are 2.5x, 2.8x, and 1.6x more extreme.
