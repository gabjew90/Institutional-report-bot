GS O'Connor - Lev Fin Weekend Reading Observations cold turkey
22 Nov 2026
KC O'Connor  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
Sun 22 Nov 2026, 9:44am ET
AI jitters boiled over last week, shooting the VIX to its highest level of 2026 and leaving many risk assets
reeling from some of their worst weekly performances since COVID. The S&P 500 finished in correction
territory after dropping 8.6%, but the real story was the Nasdaq which lost 10.4% – its worst weekly slide
since March 2020, wiping away its entire gains for the year18.
In last week's 2027 Global Credit Outlook, GIR's Amanda Lynam argued the credit cycle was becoming
"increasingly mature" and forecast modest spread widening across USD and EUR IG and HY next year
with excess returns below post-GFC medians. Credit wasted no time in getting to work on these wider
targets.
HY spreads widened 51bps to +367bps – not only the largest weekly widening of 2026, but also ~double
the prior yearly high of +27bps during the week of 9/2520. IG had a similarly challenged week, widening
10bps, but thanks to the rally in rates the asset class actually logged a return of +0.84%. This – compared
to the 0.86% loss seen from HY – was the widest gap between IG and HY weekly returns this year.
Beneath the index-level damage in HY, the week was less about single names and more about the market:
nine out of every ten HY bonds (1,753 of 1,934) fell in price, and the left tail got longer.
Four things to chew on before Thursday’s Turkey…

1. CREDIT VS. EQUITIES: slow-roasted
As mentioned above, HY cash finally moved last week, taking its daily widening streak to six consecutive
sessions – and it moved more than its equity beta said it should have. At HY’s 2026 norm of ~4.5bps of
widening per 1% move in the S&P 500, last week’s 8.6% drop implied ~39bps of widening. However, HY
cash widened 51bps, with double-Bs doing most of the catching up20.
CDX HY was different. As GS’s head of Lev Fin Trading Jake Woodson pointed out, last week’s -1.4%
return in CDX HY was just a 1.1 standard deviation move over the last 15 years, resulting in the 44th
worst weekly reading. In short, equities were in the microwave, cash was on the stovetop, and CDX was
simmering on the back burner.

2. THE LEFT TAIL: second helpings
The 5+ point movers continued with an alarming frequency last week as 65 bonds from 37 issuers fell
5+pts. This compares to 24 the week before and the prior 2026 high of 41 (from the week of 10/23)20.
This means that more than half of the year’s 488 5+ point decliners have now come since the start of
October.
However, it's no longer just a triple-C story: 45 of the 65 decliners were single-B (37) or double-B (8), vs.
18 the week before.


3. PROBLEM SECTORS: the table is getting crowded
Last week brought more agita from the Building Products and Cable spaces with the latter producing two
grace periods on the same day (Cablevision, Cable One) – a rare occurrence – but, also a reflection of how
deep and uniformly challenged select lower-rated sectors have become.
To date, this sector-specific weakness has not been a signal of broader systemic credit stress, as evidenced
by the 44.5% of the HY market which still trades with an OAS inside of +200bps, though that share fell
12.4pts last week – the largest drop of 20262,20.
Yet, more “problem” sectors are starting to appear by the day, challenging this narrative. In addition to the
violent moves seen from various HY Financials this month (GSYCN, IEP, NAVINT), investors have also
become more worried about the education and packaged foods spaces and their mixture of weaker
quarterly earnings and problematic trends (education: negative enrollment and budget uncertainty;
packaged food: supply chain inflation and GLP1 fears).
4. NEW ISSUES & SOFTWARE: saving room for dessert
Four of last week’s ten HY new issues finished below their respective issue prices – a dramatic reversal
from what we’ve seen over the last 6 weeks where all new issues have traded higher in their respective
issuance weeks26. Perhaps the most surprising stat, though, is which deal performed the best: CCC
Intelligent Solutions, whose LBO we flagged last week as the first software buyout in the BSL market
since October 202522.
The $2.85bn debt package was the standout across loans and bonds. In addition to being the best
performer on the HY side, their $2.2bn TLB cleared at S+375 @ 99.5 and was the largest reverse flex –
excluding off-the-run-business – since May21.

Thoma Bravo-backed Proofpoint also completed 1) a $735MM add-on to its 2030 TLB to refinance the
stub under its TLB due 2028 outstanding following its GS-led A&E this past summer while 2) making its
HY debut with a $1.25bn issue of 8.5% secured notes due 2031 to help finance its $6.3bn acquisition of
Varonis9.
Not all software deals sailed through the market, though, as a $400MM loan for UST Global – an IT
services firm – reportedly failed to attract sufficient investor demand, resulting in another shelved deal6.
The contrast is instructive: pure-play, scaled software (CCC Intelligent, Proofpoint) continues to attract
outsized demand even in a risk-off tape, while IT services – a lower-margin, more commoditized sub-
segment – is where investor selectivity is biting hardest.
Keep in mind: 73% of the $209bn in software loans outstanding in the Bloomberg Loan Index now trade
above 90. With the recent success in the space, more pull-to-pars and refis are a possibility (h/t Amanda
Lynam).
TAKEAWAY
Don't fill up on the bread. One week was all it took to flip HY's 2026 return negative (-0.32% YTD, from
+0.54% a week earlier) and push triple-Cs to their worst YTD mark of the year (-5.82%). The spread on
the table certainly looks more appetizing than it did a week ago – 354 HY bonds now yield 10%+ (+40 on
the week) and only 385 still trade at or above par (-83) – but cheap isn't the same as nourishing.
With GIR penciling in a 4% USD HY default rate next year and preferring to pick up subordination
premium over moving down in credit quality. Plates should be built from the top of the table, not the
bottom. Into a holiday-shortened week, selection is still King – and this Thanksgiving, so is portion
control
