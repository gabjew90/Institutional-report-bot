Key Takeaways
Investors at the conference broadly cautious on the sector: driven by rising 
competition, higher cost of growth and unproven growth in new products and 
markets.
Accelerating expansion beyond the core: Adyen (Talon.One, Orb, capital), Wise 
(SMB tools) and Kaspi (grocery) looking to boost differentiation and stickiness.
Cost of growth rising, mainly via engineer hiring; we think winners will turn 
higher spend into above-market growth, supporting valuations.
Pricing pressure rising as competition grows, but positioning still differentiates; 
we see it offset by larger addressable markets and new VAS revenue pools.
AI already drives efficiency at Wise, Adyen and Kaspi; we see it as margin support 
for now, with bigger product upside (e.g. agentic commerce) still ahead.

Key Takeaways
1- Expanding Beyond Core Payments: VAS as the Next Growth Leg
The push into value-added services was a common theme across fireside chats and group 
meetings, and we see it accelerating. Adyen is becoming a multi-product platform, with 
Talon.One (loyalty), Orb (billing) and its capital product adding revenue pools beyond 
processing. Wise is embedding itself in SMB workflows through multi-language invoicing, 
multi-currency accounts and accounting software integrations, alongside cards, Assets and 
FX Forwards. Kaspi is expanding into in-store grocery to capture more everyday spend. In 
our view, this reflects both take-rate pressure and customers’ growing demand for one-
stop solutions combining payments, software and back-office processes. VAS should also 
strengthen differentiation and increase customer stickiness.
2 - Cost of Growth: Rising, but We Think the Winners Will Convert It 
into Superior Growth
Cost of growth appears to be rising as product needs expand, mainly through hiring, 
especially engineers and in some cases marketing. Wise treats costs, including price cuts, 
as investments, holding marketing to 15–20 month paybacks and a 20% IRR hurdle, and 
signalled at its 1H FY27 results that cost growth should soften. Adyen is hiring in 
engineering and go-to-market to sustain ~20% growth, noting that higher margins would 
be easy to reach if it chose to. Kaspi continues to invest in Turkey, with losses limited 
relative to group size and recoverable as financial products launch. We think the best 
businesses will be those that turn this higher spend into growth ahead of the market and 
peers, which should support valuations.
3 - Take Rate and Competition: Pressure Rising, Offset by Larger 
Revenue Pools
More players are entering the market and putting pressure on pricing, though industry 
positioning remains a key differentiator. Wise acts as a price maker, with self-directed cuts 
funded by efficiency taking the take rate to 49bp from 52bp, and Platform pricing around 
40bp. Adyen faces processing pressure under tiered pricing, but Q3 volume grew 30%, 
and it aims to lift the take rate through loyalty, billing and fraud tools. Kaspi’s payments 
pricing should stay stable at 95bp+, while overall pricing is supported by its dominant 
fintech and marketplace position in Kazakhstan as it moves into grocery. Overall, we think 
pricing pressure should be offset by expanding addressable markets through VAS and new 
revenue pools.
4 - AI a growth enabler and cost and product support
All three companies are putting AI to work, both for efficiency and in product. At Wise, 
treasury machine learning has kept working capital flat at ~$250m despite fourfold 
volume growth, and AI agents handle ~45% of customer chats. Adyen is lifting engineering

Valuation Methodology and Risks
 Adyen NV (ADYEN.AS)  
We use a discounted cash flow valuation approach to establish a 12-month price target for 
Adyen. We think DCF is appropriate for payments businesses as much of their value sits in cash 
flows expected several years out, once scale, network effects and operating leverage have 
built up. We assume a WACC of 11.4% and terminal growth rate of 3%.
 Risks to Upside  
n Further large merchant wins, driving incremental upside
n Faster than anticipated scaling of platform product
n Higher VAS penetration
n Earlier agentic commerce traction
 Risks to Downside  
n New merchant wins being slow, penetration at existing merchants approaching a ceiling
n US/APAC expansion lags behind targets
n Online payments commoditising, leading to take-rate pressure
n Prolonged leadership transition
 Wise PLC (WISEa.L)  
We use a discounted cash flow valuation approach to establish a 12-month price target for 
Wise. We think DCF is appropriate for payments businesses as much of their value sits in cash 
flows expected several years out, once scale, network effects and operating leverage have 
built up. We use a WACC of 11.4% and terminal growth rate of 3%. We translate our USD price 
target back into GBp using an assumed USD/GBP rate of 1.32.
 Risks to Upside  
n Share gains vs. traditional banks and next gen players
n Higher volume business division sustains outsized growth
n New products gain traction
n Platform offering scales quickly
n Wise Account balance growth
 Risks to Downside  
n Regulatory risk
n Licence risk
n FX exposure risk
n Failure to comply with rules & regulations could cause customer harm, financial losses, 
reputational damage and a loss of operating licence.
