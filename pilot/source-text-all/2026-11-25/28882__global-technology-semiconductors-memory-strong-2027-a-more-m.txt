AI-driven memory demand remains strong, supporting our constructive view on 
2027 industry fundamentals. However, rising concerns over AI capex sustainability, a 
muted smartphone/PC memory demand outlook, and incrementally higher supply 
from China lead us to take a more measured view of 2028. In addition to updating 
our 2027/2028 DRAM, NAND and HBM outlook, we stress-test three key questions 
to assess how much downside is already priced into memory stocks. 
1) What is priced into memory stocks for 2028 AI capex? Current share prices of 
Samsung Electronics (SEC) and SK Hynix (Hynix) appear to price in a scenario of zero 
AI capex growth in 2028E and an additional 40% ASP decline versus our base case. 
2) How material is the LTA default risk? Conventional DRAM prices would need to 
fall more than 32-54% below our base case over the next two years before 
customers have a meaningful economic incentive to default on LTAs. Even if 
customers were to default, we believe the downside would be relatively limited 
versus a fully honored LTA scenario. 
3) What is the earnings downside from significant memory price declines? 
Assuming memory price declines similar in magnitude to recent severe downcycles, 
we estimate 29%/54%/48% downside to our 2028E base-case revenue/OP/NP for 
SEC. Even under this scenario, SEC would still generate a 35% OPM and trade at 4.7x 
P/E and 1.0x P/B on 2028E downside-case earnings. 
While we continue to expect meaningful memory undersupply in 2027, especially for 
DRAM and HBM, we now expect a smaller industry undersupply in 2028E. We 
therefore revise our 2027E/2028E conventional DRAM pricing forecast to 
+32%/-19% YoY (from +32%/-5%) and lower our NAND pricing forecast to 
+20%/-21% YoY (from +30%/-14%). We also raise our 2026E/2027E/2028E HBM 
TAM estimates to US$60bn/US$200bn/US$237bn, mainly reflecting significantly 
higher ASP assumptions. Potential memory optimization and datacenter delays 
remain key downside risks, primarily to our 2028E forecast. 
We lower our TPs to W350K (from W500K) for SEC (Buy, on APAC CL) and W2mn 
(from W3.5mn) for Hynix (Buy), mainly reflecting lower 2028E pricing and earnings 
assumptions. Despite our more measured 2028E outlook, we remain constructive on 
both stocks given their solid fundamentals, ~20% shareholder return yields and our 
view that meaningful downside risks have likely been priced in.

Analysis 1 - What is priced into memory stocks for 2028 AI capex? 
 
 
We estimate memory to account for roughly 40% of total hyperscaler capex in 2028 
Given the recent concerns over the sustainability of AI capex growth, we conduct a 
scenario analysis to assess the potential impact from hyperscaler AI capex cuts on 
memory companies’ revenue and profit, and what the shares are currently pricing in. 
GSe on hyperscaler capex for 2027E/2028E is around US$1.1tn/US$1.2-1.3tn, implying 
a slightly higher than a 10% yoy growth for 2028E. We estimate that memory to account 
for roughly 40% of this capex in 2028E, down from around 44% in 2027E, reflecting our 
expectation for memory pricing to decline in 2028E. 
2028 hyperscaler capex being flat/-10%/-20% yoy would lead to 
US$55bn/US$99bn/US$144bn less spending on memory 
We consider three 2028 hyperscaler capex scenarios relative to 2027: flat yoy, -10% yoy, 
and -20% yoy. Based on these three scenarios, we estimate a reduction in the memory 
spending pool of US$55bn/US$99bn/US$144bn, respectively. We then allocate the 
impact across memory suppliers based on their memory market share, estimating 39% 
for SEC and 30% for Hynix. This results in potential revenue reductions of 
W27tn/W48tn/W70tn for SEC and W20tn/W37tn/W54tn for Hynix across the three 
scenarios. This corresponds to a 4–10% decline in DRAM bit shipments and a 4–11% 
decline in NAND bit shipments for SEC, and a 4–9% decline in DRAM and 5–14% decline 
in NAND bit shipments for Hynix.

We incorporate the secondary impact from less capex: Lower memory pricing 
We further incorporate the potential secondary impact on memory pricing by 
stress-testing 20%/30%/40% ASP declines (additional to our base case of a ~20% yoy 
decline in 2028) on top of the volume impact from lower AI capex. This is because we 
believe the lower memory demand from the hyperscaler customers would release 
additional supply to the market, and thereby driving down the market pricing. Under the 
mildest scenario, where 2028 AI capex remains flat yoy and ASP declines by an 
additional 20%, we estimate a 23%/26% downside to SEC/Hynix 2028E OP, respectively. 
Under our most aggressive scenario of a 20% yoy decline in AI capex combined with an 
additional 40% ASP decline, 2028E OP downside increases to 45% for SEC and 48% for 
Hynix. 
Conclusion: Current share prices of SEC/Hynix already pricing in a no AI capex growth 
in 2028 with additional substantial ASP declines 
We then translate the earnings downside under each scenario into implied equity value, 
applying our TP-implied P/E multiples to the stressed earnings under each scenario. 
Relative to our current TPs for the two Korean memory suppliers, the sensitivity analysis 
implies 31–51% downside for SEC and a 28–50% downside for Hynix. Based on our 
scenario analysis, the current share prices of SEC and Hynix appear to be pricing in a 
scenario where AI capex does not grow in 2028E and also leads to an additional 40% 
ASP decline compared to our base case. 
We note that this analysis is deliberately conservative, as we assume that the reduction 
in hyperscaler memory purchases translates directly into lost bit shipments and does not 
account for memory suppliers potentially reallocating these bits to other customers or 
end markets. In practice, successful resale of excess bits could partially offset the 
volume impact, although potentially at lower ASPs. As such, we believe the actual 
revenue/earnings downside could be materially softer than suggested by the more 
severe scenarios in our analysis.

Analysis 2 - How material is the LTA default risk? 
 
 
Scenario analysis on LTA default risk  
One of the key concerns around LTAs is whether customers could walk away from 
contracts if memory prices fall sharply. While the companies have emphasized that 
sufficient safeguards and floor-price mechanisms are embedded in LTAs, investors 
remain concerned that customers could default the LTA and revert to quarterly or 
spot-like contracts once market prices fall sufficiently below contracted prices. 
We therefore assess: 1) how far DRAM prices would need to fall before customers have 
an economic incentive to default, and 2) how much downside SEC and Hynix could face if 
defaults actually occur. For this analysis, we focus on conventional DRAM pricing, rather 
than blended DRAM ASP including HBM. 
We assume that LTAs reach 65% of conventional DRAM bit shipments by 4Q28, with the 
floor price set at the 2Q26 price and the ceiling at the 3Q26 price. We also assume 
prepayments equivalent to 30% of expected contract revenue and a 50% default rate 
once the economics become more favorable for customers to walk away (as there will 
still be customers that honor the contract given product mix availability, long-term 
relationship with specific supplier, etc.).

Analysis 3 - What is the earnings downside from significant memory price declines? 
 
 
Given the market concerns on a significant memory pricing decline in 2028, we conduct 
a sensitivity analysis based on the changes to our DRAM/NAND pricing assumptions. We 
assume different rates of decline in 2028E DRAM and NAND ASPs and conduct 
sensitivity to revenue, OP, and NP under each pricing assumption.  
For SEC, our base case is a -18% yoy decline in DRAM ASP and -21% yoy decline in 
NAND ASP for 2028E. If we assume a 50% yoy decline in both DRAM and NAND ASPs for 
2028E which is a similar magnitude of decline seen during the downcycles in 2019 and 
2023, this would result in W660tn revenue, W234tn OP, and W231tn NP, which would be 
29%/54%/48% lower than our base case of W935tn/W509tn/W442tn. For Hynix, a 
50% yoy decline in both DRAM and NAND ASPs in 2028E would lower our base case 
revenue/OP/NP by 39%/55%/52%, but still at W322tn/W172tn/W146tn. 
While earnings are sensitive to memory pricing assumptions, we believe both SEC and 
Hynix would continue to generate meaningful level of absolute earnings even under a 
substantially weaker DRAM/NAND pricing scenarios.

Analysis 4 - How do memory stocks move ahead of earnings cuts? 
 
 
Given the potential of consensus earnings cuts for the memory names due to the 
concerns over AI capex cuts and datacenter delays, share price for memory names have 
already corrected substantially over the past few months. In this section we examine 
how the memory share price have moved ahead of large earnings cut during previous 
cycles. 
Share prices of memory names have generally tended to bottom few quarters before the 
earnings trough, in some cases as long as one year ahead. In other words, share prices 
started to rebound even while earnings were getting cut, especially if the share price 
correction prior to that was substantial. This suggests that memory stocks tend to 
discount the earnings cuts well in advance.  
If share prices undergo a meaningful correction well ahead of the eventual earnings 
decline, a subsequent earnings reset may have limited incremental downside for the 
stocks, as much of the deterioration could already have been priced in.

Valuation methodology: Our 12m 2027E-2028E EV/EBITDA-based SOTP target price 
for the common share is W350,000. Our 12-month target price for the preference share 
is W275,000, applying our target preferred-to-common discount of 22%, derived from

average discount to common shares. We are Buy rated on both the common and 
preference shares. 
Key downside risks: 1) major deterioration in memory supply/demand, 2) sharp 
contraction in smartphone margins, and 3) mobile OLED market share loss. 
Investment Thesis - Samsung Electronics 
Samsung Electronics (SEC) is one of the largest tech companies in the world, with the #1 
market share in several products including memory chips, OLED panels, smartphones, 
and TVs. We expect the strong earnings power led by memory to continue, supported by 
our expectation of solid memory pricing continuing and also remaining at an elevated 
level. We expect this to be helped by the long-term agreements (LTA) with customers, 
with a higher level of binding commitment likely this time compared to the past. The 
company is starting to show meaningful progress in HBM, and also considering the 
higher scope for meaningful shareholder returns, we remain constructive on the name 
and reiterate our Buy rating.

W2,700,000 on 5X P/E and 2027E-2028E P/B based target price of 1,300,000 on 1.3X

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2
