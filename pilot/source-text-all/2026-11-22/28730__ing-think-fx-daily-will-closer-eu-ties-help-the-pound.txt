FX Daily: Carry trade unwind in full
swing
The prospect of faster Fed tightening and continued dislocation in tech
stocks is sucking funds into the dollar and the yen. Popular FX carry trade
positions are being unwound across the board.  Given that a hawkish Fed
lies at the heart of the current correction in risk assets, and there
are no signs of Fed rhetoric softening anytime soon, USD can stay strong
The prospect of faster Fed tightening is starting to weigh heavily on risk assets and helping the dollar
USD: The Waller correction
After another day of losses, the US S&P 500 index is now 8.5% off its highs of the year. The
extent of this correction is not far from that seen in March this year (9.5%) and way off the
'Liberation Day' rout of 21% seen in March/April 2025. The cause of the current correction has
been the threat of a faster Federal Reserve tightening cycle, put on the table by the Fed's
Christopher Waller last week. We discussed high US real interest rates here yesterday.  And the
volatility in tech stocks may also be owing to this week's Anthropic IPO, where investors have
probably been shuffling portfolios to participate in a $100bn IPO. The fact that the Fed wants
to tighten faster because of a demand shock should not necessarily be bad news for equities
and, barring some major earning misses, the case for another 10% correction in equities is a
weak one.

Yet there seems no reason for the Fed to drop its hawkish rhetoric anytime soon. Next
Wednesday sees the release of the Fed's preferred inflation measure – the core PCE index. The
October data is expected to show the year-on-year rate rising to 3.2% with upside risks, and
confirm that the Fed will need to take action. 
For FX, that means a continued strong dollar and continued pressure on popular carry trades.
The Latam complex has had a terrible week, with the most popular carry position, the
Colombian peso, off heavily. Brazil yesterday stepped in with FX intervention, but $1bn in sales
was quickly overwhelmed. Elsewhere, the big drop in metals has left the South African rand
vulnerable, and a dovish central bank has seen an unwind of crowded positions in Hungary's
forint. We are also starting to see expectations build for rate cuts in Australia next year on the
view that the Reserve Bank of Australia has overtightened. We highlighted this as one of the
key risks in our 2027 FX Outlook. Pressure may also be building on long positions in the Turkish
lira, where implied yields are starting to rise again.
A big carry trade unwind and emerging markets sell-off remain a positive for the dollar, and to
a lesser degree, the yen. We expect the dollar to hold gains into next week and keep 104.60 as
a near-term DXY target.
The US data calendar is light today, and the only Fed speakers are the hawkish Waller
(10:15CET) and Lisa Cook, who surprisingly voted for a hike at the October FOMC meeting.

EUR: Correction in risk assets doesn't help the euro
EUR/USD continues to consolidate below 1.10. Our FX options traders suggest the low volatility
price action here is a function of a lot of FX options barriers in the 1.0850-1.0950 area.
Nonetheless, a correction in risk assets – and especially a correction in emerging market
currencies – is typically dollar-positive, and we expect EUR/USD to face continuing downside
risks. 1.0880 remains the potential for this move.
On the calendar is the release of eurozone negotiated wages for the third quarter. These are
expected at 2.6% year-on-year, way off the 4-5% levels seen in 2024-2025 that would have
worried the European Central Bank with the threat of second-round effects.
Elsewhere in Europe, the Hungarian forint is under heavy pressure. The National Bank of
Hungary's dovish tilt at this week's meeting won't have helped, but long forint positions would
always have been vulnerable to a global carry trade unwind. So far, positions in the long end of
the Hungarian local currency bond market have been untouched – this is where investors are
positioned for Hungary's euro story. Any sharp spike in these bond yields would caution a
deeper correction to the Hungary story and even more pressure on the forint. Any EUR/HUF
close above the 373.65 upside gap from April would warn of an extension to 380.

GBP: New EU-UK agreements announced
At a press conference in Brussels today, UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham and EU leaders are
expected to announce three new agreements. The first is on SPS (Sanitary and Phytosanitary
measures), which will reduce border checks on trade in food, animals and plants. The second is
on a greater alignment between the separate EU and UK Emissions Trading Schemes (ETS),
where greater recognition of each other's schemes can reduce carbon-related friction in trade
– especially important for the steel and manufacturing sectors. The third looks at the thorny
issue of mobility in the Youth Experience Scheme (YES), where up to 100,000 18-31 year-olds
can obtain work visas for up to three years. Burnham will be marketing these deals as
addressing the cost-of-living crisis and giving young Britons valuable opportunities overseas.
The deals, as always, will be subject to last-minute negotiation and serve as a reminder to the
Burnham government that 'cherry-picking' the best parts of a relationship with the EU is no
easy task. That may sway the Labour Party against any commitment to rejoin the EU in its
next election manifesto – which could potentially emerge as early as next year.
Sterling typically responds well to the prospect of closer UK ties with Europe on the view that
reversing a bad Brexit deal can generate a 1-2% larger UK economy in 5-10 years' time.
Today's agreements are fully expected, however, and may not generate any independent
sterling strength.
That said, we like sterling over the coming months on the view that Bank of England tightening
expectations prove more resilient than those of the ECB. This corrective EUR/GBP rally may
again stall in the 0.8400 area, and we are happy with a year-end forecast of 0.8300.

Latam: $1bn Brazil intervention is not enough
USD/BRL briefly corrected yesterday when the Central Bank of Brazil announced it would
conduct an auction to sell US$1 billion. Gains in the Brazilian real were only fleeting, with
investors still wary that President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has no interest in fiscal
consolidation. Back in late 2024, BCB had to increase the size of its spot USD auction to $3-5bn
to provide stability to the real. We cannot rule out USD/BRL extending to the 6.00/6.30 region
again, but imagine investors would start to find those levels as attractive again for the real and
its high real interest rates.
Elsewhere, USD/MXN is trading comfortably through 19.00. This outcome cannot have been
unexpected for Banxico after it cut rates in November. 19.60 is now the risk, assuming the
dollar stays strong.
