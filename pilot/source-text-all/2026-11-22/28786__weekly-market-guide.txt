Weekly Market Guide
Market Volatility through a big week of headlines- Hot October CPI (led to talks of a 50bp Fed hike in December and a big
bond yield bounce back), Jensen Huang’s resignation, global tech outages, FAA nationwide ground stop, and bank technical
issues. Headline volatility is contributing to market volatility, a particularly bad week for Technology.
Technology is getting piled on- The post-election data center reaction, Jensen Huang ramifications, and technical issues
have investors asking themselves “Does the combination of these events potentially increase AI regulation and reduce AI
momentum, risk-taking, and spending?” All else equal, it’s easy to think the answer is Yes/Maybe. However, even if you get the
direction right, it is easy to get the magnitude wrong. The structural forces in place are strong. Companies are more than one
person, the AI ecosystem is more than one company, and political hype can often exceed legislative realities (and economic
impacts). To be sure, it is important to have the right balance in your Tech allocations given market concentration, leverage,
and vulnerabilities. But valuation has re-rated a bunch, fundamentals are still strong, and the cycle may actually be elongated
by a slower ramp-up.
Short-term Technicals- The Nasdaq & Technology’s inability to follow-though on the initial bounce (following post-election
pullback) is indicative of some caution in the short-term technical picture. Catalysts to monitor this week include major AI
earnings and an IPO, along with President Trump/Xi (maybe Putin too) meeting at the APEC Summit.
Market rotation persists- Investors continue to rotate exposures rather than outright selling. Diversified portfolios
have largely held up (and performed) well through the underlying volatility this year. It is an important reminder that
diversification is important, not only as an offset to Tech volatility but also as an opportunity. Over 100 S&P 50 stocks
have a negative 1-year beta (meaning they have typically moved inversely to the actual index). We believe these waves of
rotation are likely to continue, and can be used opportunistically (as needed) along the way. Favor Quality over High-
Beta Momentum.
The overriding environment remains supportive of equities- The economic and earnings backdrop remain healthy. In the
short-term, we are dealing with a lot of headline volatility that can act as a headwind to market momentum. Nonetheless, the
fundamental positives outweigh the potential negatives in our view.

Performance Following Mid-term Elections
While emotions can run high around elections (often spurring market volatility), forward returns are typically positive when the dust 
settles.  There have been 5 instances since WWII where both chambers flipped to the same party in a single midterm election: 1946, 
1954, 1994, 2006, and now 2026.  These wave-midterm elections saw average 3-month, 6-month, and 12-month returns of +6.1%, 
+13.5%, and +19.5% respectively.  The latest example was a flip from Republican sweep to Democrat-led Congress in 2006 under 
President Bush- next 3-, 6-, 12-month return was +2.7%, 8.7%, and 7.1%.  Additionally, Q4 returns are typically stronger during midterm 
years (as shown on bottom right) with average returns of 6.2% vs. 4.1% in all years.

October Retail Sales
Strong October spending, along with upward revisions, 
indicates healthy consumer demand.  The Retail sales 
control group was up 0.6% m/m (vs. 0.3% estimate) and 
the prior month was revised up to 0.5% (from 0.1%).  
The lack of demand destruction in the face of high 
interest rates and oil prices is encouraging, especially if 
oil prices can continue to subside (as they have over the 
past couple of weeks).  This would provide a boost into 
year-end, as well as reduce inflationary pressures and 
Fed rate hike odds.  Holiday shopping is on deck, and 
we expect it to be solid (Back-to-School shopping is 
often a good indicator).

Index Definitions
The S&P 500 is an unmanaged index of 500 widely held stocks that is generally considered representative of the U.S. stock market.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA) is a price-weighted average of 30 significant stocks traded on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and the NASDAQ.
The NASDAQ Composite is a stock market index of the common stocks and similar securities listed on the NASDAQ stock market.
The MSCI World All Cap Index captures large, mid, small and micro-cap representation across 23 Developed Markets (DM) countries. With 11,732 constituents, the index is
comprehensive, covering approximately 99% of the free float-adjusted market capitalization in each country.
The MSCI EAFE (Europe, Australasia, and Far East) is a free float-adjusted market capitalization index that is designed to measure developed market equity performance,
excluding the United States & Canada. The EAFE consists of the country indices of 21 developed nations.
The MSCI Emerging Markets Index is designed to measure equity market performance in 23 emerging market countries. The index's three largest industries are materials,
energy, and banks.
The Russell 2000 index is an index measuring the performance of approximately 2,000 smallest-cap American companies in the Russell 3000 Index, which is made up of 3,000
of the largest U.S. stocks.
The NYSE Alerian MLP is the leading gauge of energy infrastructure Master Limited Partnerships (MLPs). The capped, float-adjusted, capitalization-weighted index, whose
constituents earn the majority of their cash flow from midstream activities involving energy commodities, is disseminated real-time on a price-return basis (AMZ) and on a
total-return basis (AMZX).
The Barclays Intermediate Government/Credit Bond index measures the performance of U.S. Dollar denominated U.S. Treasuries, government-related and investment grade
U.S. corporate securities that have a remaining maturity of greater than one year and less than ten years.

The Euro Stoxx 50 Index is a market capitalization weighted stock index of 50 large, blue-chip European companies operating within Eurozone nations. Components are
selected from the Euro STOXX Index which includes large-, mid- and small-cap stocks in the Eurozone.
The China CSI 300 is a capitalization-weighted stock market index designed to replicate the performance of top 300 stocks traded in the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock
exchanges. It had a sub-indexes CSI 100 Index and CSI 200 Index.
The S&P 500 Futures is a capitalization-weighted index of 500 stocks. The index is designed to measure performance of the broad domestic economy through changes in the
aggregate market value of 500 stocks representing all major industries.
The DJIA Futures is a stock market index futures contract traded on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange`s Globex electronic trading platform. Dow Futures is based off the Dow
30 stock index.
The Nasdaq 100 Futures is a modified capitalization-weighted index of the 100 largest and most active non-financial domestic and international companies listed on the
NASDAQ.
Europe: DAX (Deutscher Aktienindex (German stock index)) is a blue chip stock market index consisting of the 40 major German companies trading on the Frankfurt Stock
Exchange.
Asia: Nikkei is short for Japan's Nikkei 225 Stock Average, the leading and most-respected index of Japanese stocks. It is a price-weighted index composed of Japan's top 225
blue-chip companies traded on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.
