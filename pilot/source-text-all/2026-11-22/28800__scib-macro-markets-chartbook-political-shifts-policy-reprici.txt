Political and leadership races are increasingly taking centre stage across Europe, starting with the contest for the ECB presidency and two other 
key Executive Board vacancies also in play Next week, the focus shifts to Spain, where Sunday’s elections will follow a final week of campaigning 
and televised debates. Meanwhile, France’s presidential race is beginning to take shape, with the first policy proposals emerging and pension 
reform already in focus. Beyond Europe, increasingly hawkish rhetoric from FOMC members continues to support US rates and the USD, while the
Anthropic IPO and UK inflation data have also attracted market attention.

1. ECB top jobs: Getting closer to resolution
The race for the ECB’s top jobs is becoming clearer. Pablo Hernández de Cos is increasingly emerging as the frontrunner for the presidency (see lh 
chart), while Klaas Knot could be considering withdraw from the race.
Germany has nominated Markus Brunnermeier for the chief economist role, while a French candidate is expected to fill Schnabel’s Executive Board 
seat, currently responsible for market operations, market infrastructure and payments (see rh chart).
The name of the new ECB president is expected to be announced at the 17-18 December EU leaders meeting.

between Pedro Sánchez and Alberto Núñez Feijóo, Monday will see a debate among the spokespeople of the eight main political parties,

3. French elections: Fiscal proposals begin to take shape
Following Spain, France will be the next major European economy to head to the polls, with presidential elections scheduled for April. Although 
the vote is still six months away, the first major policy proposals are beginning to emerge, drawing particular attention given the continued market 
focus on France’s fiscal dynamics. This week, Marine Le Pen unveiled her proposed overhaul of the French pension system, a key policy issue for 
markets given its potential fiscal implications. As seen below, the main proposals include lowering the retirement age and a new funded pillar, with 
claimed annual savings of €15-20bn.
Markets reacted positively to the announcement, with the 10y OAT-Bund spread tightening by 9bp, although this differential remains close to 
recent highs (see rh chart). However, we remain cautious about reading too much into the move at this early stage, with six months still to go 
before the election.
Main measures under Le Pen’s pension proposal :
1. Lower retirement age to 62, or 60 for some long-career workers.
2. Reward long careers with earlier retirement based on contribution 
years.
3. Promote earlier employment through education and apprenticeship 
reforms.
4. Incentivise older workers to remain employed longer.
5. Introduce a funded pension pillar alongside the pay-as-you-go 
system.

4. FOMC hawkishness picks up
As highlighted last week, hawkish rhetoric among FOMC members has intensified in recent weeks. This week's FOMC minutes reinforced that 
trend, marking the most hawkish set of minutes in five years (see Bloomberg’s hawkish-dovish Fed minutes index in lh chart), with “almost 
all” participants seeing scope for further rate hikes. This shift in tone comes against a backdrop of continued resilience in the US economy and 
inflation data. In response, our US chief economist has revised his Fed call, now forecasting one additional rate hike in March 2027 (on top of the 
universally expected hike at the 9 December FOMC).
However, as shown in the rh chart, market pricing remains more hawkish than us, with one additional hike priced in by September 2027. Although 
significant uncertainties remain, including geopolitical developments and equity market performance, we continue to see current pricing as overly 
aggressive and believe there is scope for a correction towards a less hawkish outlook.

5. Rethinking USD bullishness: Mind the gap (or rather, the differentials)
The hawkish repricing of US rates over the past couple of weeks has supported the USD, pushing EUR/USD below 1.10 for the first time since 
the beginning of 2025. We remain structurally bullish on the USD, underpinned by the resilience of the US economy, persistent inflation, higher US 
interest rates and a still-wide US yield advantage, alongside geopolitical and European fiscal risks.
However, the latest USD rally appears increasingly stretched, particularly against the EUR. EUR/USD is trading below the levels implied by rate 
differentials (see lh chart), while positioning is already very short (see rh chart). Our medium-term USD-positive view therefore remains intact, but 
we would be cautious about chasing the rally at current levels.

6. Could the EUR weakness become important for the ECB?
As several key ECB members — and the statement from the latest policy meeting — have argued recently (see quote below), tighter financial 
conditions could ultimately limit how much the ECB needs to hike. Our version of the ECB’s FCI makes this particularly clear: financial conditions 
have tightened noticeably again, close to their September peak, with the recent move driven largely by the repricing across the EUR curve as well
as the recent equity sell-off. The key offset remains the exchange rate. The recent EUR depreciation has provided meaningful easing pressure 
and partly cushioned the tightening coming from rates. However, it has not been enough to offset the broader tightening in financial conditions, 
reinforcing our view that the ECB may not need to deliver as much tightening as currently priced by markets.
29 October ECB meeting - Press Release:
- “Financing conditions have tightened…which is 
likely to weigh on inflation and growth.”
- “renewed energy price increases have hit

7. Anthropic makes IPO history
The AI company has finally gone public at an IPO price of $83/share (for 980mn shares offered), implying total IPO proceeds of $81.3bn, wrestling 
the no.1 spot from SpaceX, which went public earlier, in June this year, selling approximately $75bn worth of shares. Like SpaceX, Anthropic shares 
will be fast-tracked to Nasdaq100 membership and could debut in the index before year-end with an approximate weight of 2%. Anthropic closed 
c17% up on its first day of trading. What remains to be seen is whether the newcomer to public markets can continue to trade above its IPO price, 
in contrast to the weakness seen in the price performance of other big names after going public. According to the pre-market price, it is currently 
up just 5% from its IPO price (rh chart).

8. UK inflation remains concentrated; US inflation is more widespread
UK inflation edged up to 3.6% in October and is set to keep rising. But the pressure was concentrated in components of the basket of less concern 
for the MPC, such as volatile Airfares and Tobacco duty, while discretionary areas suggested a lack of pricing power (full analysis here). Combined 
with this week’s softer labour market data, there is no “smoking gun” for the MPC to rush into consecutive hikes.
Looking at individual UK inflation components shows the acceleration is very concentrated around energy and is not spreading into the wider 
basket; i.e. no sign of second-round effects. Inflation pressures are more widespread across individual components – and broadening – in the US 
than in the UK. This distributional analysis reinforces our argument that the BoE does not need to tighten as aggressively as the Fed.

9. Markets remain more hawkish than our base case
December MPC pricing has proved remarkably stubborn, with c.19bp of tightening still priced in today. We think that is excessive. December is 
genuinely live, but we put the probability of a hike at slightly below 50%. There is one more inflation print before the MPC but – given the lack of 
alarm bells in that October data (see above) even a stronger reading may struggle to justify a back-to-back move absent a renewed escalation 
in the Middle East that pushes energy prices materially and sustainably higher. Further out, the market-implied terminal rate is ~4.84% in a year’s 
time (three hikes fully priced), a full 25bp higher than implied for the Fed. This remains too hawkish relative to our Bank Rate call for just one 
further hike in February, albeit with serious risks of a third later in 2027.
