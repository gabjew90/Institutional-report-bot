Editorial 
Staying diversified, adding US mega caps 
Strategy summary. AI-related concerns continue to weigh on 
equities, especially with new US restrictions on third-country 
access to advanced American semiconductor chips. Renewed 
volatility across the AI ecosystem drove the S&P500 volatility 
index (VIX) back above 25. However, two major event risks this 
week turned out to be less concerning - Nvidia’s Q3 revenue 
and earnings exceeded expectations, while Anthropic’s record 
initial public offering was priced above its indicative range.  
Our quant models are not signalling strong buy signal yet. 
However, with VIX spiking above 25 and investor positioning 
and sentiment indicators suggesting limited downside, we see 
value emerging in the US mega caps segment following its 
recent underperformance. We add an opportunistic idea on the 
segment, which we see benefitting from scale, financial 
strength and potential industry consolidation.  
Opportunistically adding US megacaps. Asian equities led 
declines this week, led by Taiwan, China and Korea, after new 
US export controls targeted access to advanced AI computing 
through overseas data centres. Licensing requirements focus 
on capacity supplied to restricted customers, with additional 
controls affecting regional hubs. These measures increase 
compliance burdens and financing uncertainty, potentially 
weighing on AI capital expenditure. We expect the market’s AI 
capex estimates for 2027 to be cut towards our own more-
conservative 
projections. 
Established 
cloud 
operators 
(hyperscalers) may be better equipped to manage customer 
checks than smaller providers. We initiate an opportunistic idea 
on US mega caps as we see a catch-up opportunity, given their 
established franchises, strong cash generation and relatively 
resilient earnings prospects (see page 7 for details). 
Diversifying beyond the AI trade. Although positive cross 
asset and sector correlations returned this week, sector 
rotations earlier this month reinforces the importance of 
maintaining exposure to different drivers of equity returns. Our 
preferred US sectors remain technology, healthcare, financials 
and materials, combining technology participation with 
opportunities beyond AI. Beyond the US, we like Euro area and 
Japanese banks, which are benefitting from elevated interest 
rates, actively managed global dividend strategies for income 
opportunities, and Indian equities due to their low correlation 
with the AI trade. 
Allure of high dividend equities. While global dividend yields 
remain historically low, attractive pockets persist. The Swiss 
equity market yields almost 4%, versus 1.6% for global equities. 
Euro area (3.6%), Australia (3.5%) and Singapore (3.4%) are 
other leading dividend paying markets. Taiwan offers almost a 
2% 
yield, 
combining 
income 
with 
structural 
growth 
opportunities. We favour active management over simply 
chasing yield, avoiding value traps. Dividend equities can 
complement bonds within a diversified portfolio, balancing 
income opportunities with broader exposure across markets. 
Increasing diversification offered by bonds. The one-month 
correlation between global stocks and bonds has turned 
negative after more than a year. This means bonds are offering 
greater diversification benefits amid the equity drawdown. 
Minutes from the Fed’s October meeting suggest policymakers 
are likely to hike rates further. However, we remain dovish vs. 
markets on the Fed’s 12-month rates outlook (we see Fed 
target rate at 4.25% by end-2027 vs. market pricing above 
4.5%) as lower oil prices and fading tariff effects lead to 
noticeable disinflation by H2 2027.  Thus, we see particular 
value in the 3-7-year maturity bucket, with a preference for BB-
to-BBB segment corporate bonds, which have resilient balance 
sheets and offer attractive yield pick-up. We see the US 10-year 
government bond offering value if the yield rises above 5.25%.  
Dollar appears overbought; potential for gold upside: The 
USD index (DXY) has jumped over 2% in the past two weeks 
after stronger-than-expected US jobs and inflation reports for 
October revived Fed rate hike bets. Nevertheless, crude oil 
prices have slumped almost 20% this month as the Republican 
party’s heavy losses in the US mid-term elections raised the 
prospects of de-escalation in the Middle East conflict. Forward 
markets are pricing US crude oil below USD 75/bbl by Q3 next 
year. Lower oil prices are likely to support disinflation next year. 
With DXY technically overbought and Fed policy expectations 
excessively hawkish, we expect a pullback in USD. Conversely, 
gold looks oversold. We see an upside for XAU/USD, despite 
revising lower our 3- and 12-month targets (see page 7).   
—  Rajat Bhattacharya

Top client questions 
  How will the new US semiconductor restrictions impact 
the technology sector?  
Our view: While compute is likely to remain a bottleneck for AI, 
greater US regulatory restrictions could weigh on AI capex.   
Rationale: The US has announced broader restrictions on overseas 
computing capacity used by Chinese and other restricted-country 
companies, extending controls beyond physical chip shipments to 
remote access through data centres. This could delay data centre 
deployments and raise financing costs, as affected operators may 
defer expansion plans until licences or replacement customers are 
secured. Lower spending could weigh on chip and hardware orders, 
bringing consensus capex estimates closer to our forecasts. While 
we expect AI capex to rise 50% to USD 1.2trn in 2027, supported 
by projects already under construction, the compound annual growth 
rate (CAGR) between 2027 and 2030 is likely to moderate to c.12%.  
We therefore favour broadening technology exposure beyond 
semiconductors through our US Information Technology (IT) 
opportunistic idea, which also includes hardware and software 
industries. Investors should also consider diversifying into our other 
preferred sectors, such as US healthcare and financials.  
—  Ryan Goh, Investment Strategist 
  How were Chinese technology giants' Q3 2026 earnings? 
Our view: Chinese tech giants’ Q3 earnings highlight growing 
demand for AI, but heavy spending is pressuring margins and 
cash flow, underscoring the need for stronger AI monetisation. 
Rationale: Heavy AI capex continues to weigh on profitability, 
compressing near-term margins and deepening negative free cash 
flows, with share dilution a risk as tech giants may have to tap into 
equity markets for the AI buildout. Cloud growth points to genuine AI 
demand, but the AI-related revenue contribution remains small 
relative to the cost base. AI monetisation remains challenging, with 
competition set to intensify. Weak consumer spending was a 
common thread contributing to revenue misses. Major players 
reiterated the need for more tangible investment returns amid 
aggressive AI investments. 
In the near term, investor sentiment remains weighed down by 
tightening US restrictions targeting China’s access to advanced 
chips, introducing downside risks to 2027 AI cloud revenue. While 
this could hamper the development of next-generation AI models, 
accelerating domestic AI chip adoption should benefit local 
semiconductor companies as China pushes for technological self-
sufficiency. We expect rising AI adoption to benefit China's IT sector 
over time, though we prefer diversifying our China equity exposure 
across preferred sectors, such as financials and healthcare. 
—  Cindy Lam, CFA, Senior Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  What are the implications of the October Fed meeting 
minutes for the rate path forward and short-dated bonds? Do 
US 10-year government bonds now offer a good re-entry point? 
Our view: October’s Fed meeting minutes maintained a 
hawkish-hold narrative. We continue to expect the Fed to raise 
rates by 25bps at its 8-9 December meeting. However, with 
short-dated US government bonds having largely priced in 
three hikes by June 2027, we see any spike above 5.25% in the 
US 10-year bond yield as an opportunistic bullish entry point.  
Rationale: The hawkish-hold tone of the Fed’s October meeting 
minutes suggests the December Fed meeting is ‘live’. The market is 
currently pricing in three hikes by year-end 2027 (vs. our 
expectation of two hikes by H1 2027 followed by a potential cut 
in H2 2027). Short-dated government bond yields – using the US 2-
year yield as a proxy – have largely priced in three hikes, and we do 
not expect the hawkish minutes to have a material impact on them.   
Should the US 10-year government bond yield spike above 5.25%, 
the near-term risk-reward would become favourable, in our opinion. 
As we expect inflation to moderate in H2 2027, we see scope for 
the Fed to pivot towards easing, and we would turn more 
constructive on short-dated government bonds. We expect the US 
2-year government bond yield to decline to 4.00-4.25% in 12 
months, contributing to yield curve steepening. 
— Ray Heung, Senior Investment Strategist 
  Is it time to add exposure to the EUR after its recent slide 
against the USD? 
Our view: Yes, we are bullish on EUR/USD and favour gradually 
adding EUR exposure in the coming weeks. Markets are pricing 
in more Fed tightening than we expect. Lower oil prices support 
the Euro area and are likely to ease the Fed’s inflation concerns 
on a forward-looking basis. 
Rationale: Lower oil prices are a double positive for EUR/USD, 
as they ease costs for Euro area households and businesses and 
temper US inflation, reducing the need for the Fed to tighten as 
much as markets expect.  
Markets are pricing in a terminal Fed funds rate close to 4.63%, down 
from 4.72% a week ago, with the yield curve now also pricing a rate-
cutting cycle after the September 2027 peak. We expect the bullish 
near-term USD momentum to linger until markets’ peak rate 
expectations converge towards our view. EUR/USD’s relative 
strength index (RSI) is below 30, and consolidation above 1.0950 
points to oversold conditions. We expect a rebound towards 1.11 
to confirm our recovery view in the next three weeks. 
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  What could support a sustained recovery in the AUD? 
Our view: We believe a sustained AUD recovery requires a 
combination of domestic resilience and fading USD momentum. 
Rationale: AUD/USD has been pressured by two key external 
headwinds: hawkish Fed repricing and weaker AI-related risk 
sentiment. A reversal in either could provide scope for recovery. 
Softer US economic data or less hawkish Fed communication could 
ease upward pressure on US government bond yields, narrow rate 
differentials and weaken the USD. At the same time, a stabilisation 
in AI and broader equity sentiment could restore demand for the 
risk-sensitive AUD, particularly if Asian equities recover. 
Domestic fundamentals remain a relative source of support. 
Still-elevated inflation should constrain the RBA’s ability to turn 
dovish quickly, while exporter and real-money demand for the AUD 
could 
cushion 
the 
downside. 
However, 
Australia’s 
rising 
unemployment raises the prospect of RBA rate cuts in H2 2027, 
potentially eroding the AUD’s carry advantage over time. With 
AUD/USD technically oversold, we see scope for a rebound from 
0.6600, with 0.6830 as the key resistance level. 
—  Iris Yuen, Investment Strategist 
  How will the improving oil supply-demand balance shape 
crude oil prices over the coming quarters? 
Our view: Improving supply-demand balances should support 
inventory rebuilding and ease oil prices over time, despite 
persistent geopolitical risks, supporting disinflation in H2 2027. 
Rationale: Crude oil’s recent retreat marks an important transition: 
expanding physical supply is finally asserting control over 
headline geopolitical noise. With non-OPEC production resilient, 
Gulf exports recovering and alternative transit routes adapting, 
markets are steadily pricing in an approaching supply surplus. 
However, high freight costs and extended voyage times keep 
significant crude oil volumes tied up at sea rather than refilling local 
storage tanks. Crucially, the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve’s 
crude inventories have fallen nearly 33% from pre-conflict levels, 
leaving a thinner buffer against renewed supply disruptions. 
For investors, improving fundamentals point to gradual price 
normalisation as trade routes adapt, despite persistent geopolitical 
volatility. We expect near-term West Texas Intermediate (WTI) oil 
to range between USD 80/bbl and USD 90/bbl. Softer energy 
prices should provide some relief to headline inflation, but are 
unlikely to materially alter the Fed’s near-term policy trajectory. 
—  Anthony Naab, Investment Strategist

Top client questions 
  Are there any tactical opportunities amidst the equity 
drawdown? 
Our view: We introduce US mega cap equities as a new 
opportunistic idea, favouring their scale and financial strength 
amid heightened volatility and potential industry consolidation. 
Rationale: We believe the mid-cycle backdrop favours large 
companies with strong cash generation, established competitive 
positions and the financial capacity to invest through periods of 
uncertainty. US mega caps’ forward P/E has fallen well below its 
two-year average and the -2x standard deviation level, offering a 
more attractive entry point into US market leaders. 
A potential reset in AI capex expectations could also accelerate 
industry consolidation, favouring established leaders with the 
balance sheets, infrastructure and customer reach to sustain 
investment and capture market share. Key risks include a sharper-
than-expected economic slowdown, weaker returns on AI 
investment, regulatory pressures, key man risks and concentration 
in a few dominant companies.  
—  Robin Xie, CFA, Senior Investment Strategist 
 
  Do you see opportunities in gold after the recent sell-off? 
Our view: We are lowering our gold price targets to USD 
4,000/oz (three months) and USD 4,500/oz (12 months). Despite 
gold’s recent drop below our revised three-month price target, 
we continue to believe the precious metal can rebound in the 
medium term. 
Rationale: Several factors have been driving down gold prices. A 
combination of stronger-than-expected US jobs, consumer 
inflation and producer inflation reports since early November has 
reinforced the case for Fed tightening. This, in turn, brought renewed 
hawkish Fed rhetoric, driving up the repricing of Fed rate-hike 
expectations. Higher rates raise the opportunity cost of holding non-
yielding gold, while also strengthening the USD – a double 
headwind for bullion. In addition, recent volatility in the equity 
markets has raised liquidity needs for leveraged investors, causing 
some of them to sell gold to cover their losses.  
However, at current levels, gold has undershot even our newly 
revised three-month price target for the precious metal. Emerging 
Market central banks remain structural buyers of gold as they 
continue to shift away from USD-denominated reserves. The US 
fiscal outlook remains dire, and with political gridlock now having 
returned to Washington, persistent fiscal deficits with no reforms will 
likely remain entrenched for the next two years at least. Confidence 
in US government finances and the USD is expected to weaken 
into 2027, and investors are likely to once again turn to gold as a 
store of value, informing our 12-month price target of USD 4,500/oz. 
—  Jonathan Liang, CFA, CIO, Fixed Income and FX
