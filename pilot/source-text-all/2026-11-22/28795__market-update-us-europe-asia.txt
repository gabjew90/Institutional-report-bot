U.S. Markets Recap 
US -2.02% | Nasdaq -2.25% | Dow -2.19%
Synopsis

US equities ended near their worst levels on Thursday, with the Dow down 2.19%, the S&P 500 down 2.02%, and the Nasdaq down 2.25%. 
S&P 500 breadth was the worst of the year to date (­423) and negative for a fourth straight session. The AI trade reversed earlier strength, 
with semiconductors, neoclouds and memory lower, and AMZN­US, SPCX­US and GOOGL­US the weakest large­cap tech names. The risk­
off move followed the ANT­US IPO, which opened almost 40% above the $83 IPO price before falling nearly 20% from the opening tick. It 
sparked a broader selloff. Other focus points were volatility near 28, higher oil on reports Trump is weighing renewed strikes on Iran, poorly 
received retail earnings, hawkish Fedspeak and rising odds of a government shutdown. Treasuries firmed with a bull steepening and yields 
down 1­4 bp, gold fell 1.5% and WTI settled up 0.5%. 

Philly Fed manufacturing came in at 6.3, well below the 20.0 consensus and October's 20.9, with new orders down for a fourth consecutive 
month. Initial claims were 203K against a 197K consensus, and continuing claims were 1763K against 1710K. Goolsbee said inflation is 
still far above target, while Jefferson spoke of the challenge of calibrating policy amid coinciding AI, energy and trade shocks.

Macro

Fed officials keep a hawkish tone on inflation and AI risks
Fed officials continued to strike a more hawkish tone. Chicago's Goolsbee (non­voter) said the labor market remains stable but inflation is 
still well above the 2% target, calling it the Fed's failure. He expressed concern that tariff­driven inflation has proven more persistent than 
expected and cannot be looked through if it continues, and warned that investment and the AI buildout risk overheating the economy, 
though he does not currently see stagflation (Reuters). Last week, Fed's Bowman (voter) estimated AI accounts for 25% of excess inflation. 
Fed's Jefferson (voter) warned that the AI investment boom is fueling near­term inflation by boosting demand and flagged risks of AI­
driven job displacement, though he offered no guidance on policy (Reuters). Fed Governor Barr (voter) noted inflation remains excessively 
high and policy adjustments are still necessary. Barkin and Paulson were set to speak after the close. 

Anthropic's record IPO underscores selective AI appetite
Anthropic priced its IPO at $83/sh, above the marketed $70­78 range, selling 980M shares versus the 825M planned and raising $81.3B, 
the biggest listing of all time and ahead of SpaceX's $75B (Bloomberg, CNBC). Today's record $107B debut ($81B IPO and $26B private 
placement) saw shares pop about 40% from the IPO price, briefly hitting a valuation above $2T before paring gains to about $95/share in 
afternoon trading (FT, NY Times). Some analysts warned that strong demand for the shares is not necessarily an all­clear for debt­heavy 
neoclouds and lower­tier AI infrastructure plays (Bloomberg). Some also flagged signs that capital moved out of megacap AI incumbents, 
with most Big Tech names reversing earlier gains and turning lower after the IPO, possibly to fund Anthropic positions after tight allocations 
left institutional buyers underweight (Bloomberg). Broader AI concerns remain despite the debut, including equity and debt issuance, ROI, 
and rising competitive and pricing pressures. 

Iran tensions elevated as Trump weighs new strikes
The NY Times reported Wednesday that Trump is weighing another round of extensive military strikes on Iran, as the White House considers 
new intelligence on Iranian capabilities and Israel warns of Iran's success in rebuilding its missile and drone arsenals and advancing chem­
ical and biological weapons programs. Internal divisions persist within the administration, with some officials questioning the credibility 
of Israel's chemical weapons intelligence and arguing the economic pressure campaign is enough (Axios). The potential re­escalation comes 
as Iran has intensified military aggression in the Strait of Hormuz following the midterm election, as Tehran's perception of waning US 
political will has left it emboldened (Washington Post). 

Jobless claims rise and Philly Fed misses consensus by a wide margin
Initial jobless claims rose to 203K versus a 197K consensus and the prior week's upwardly revised 202K (from 200K), the highest since 
early September. The 4­week moving average increased 2K to 197,750. Continuing claims climbed to 1,763K versus a 1,710K consensus 
and a prior 1,693K (revised from 1,697K), the highest since late August. November's Philly Fed Manufacturing Index declined to +6.3 from 
October's +20.9, missing the +20.0 consensus and the lowest since May. The new orders index fell to a near­zero reading for a fourth straight 
decline, while the shipments index moved higher. The employment index rose for the first time in three months, though both price indexes 
also rose. 

Nvidia approaches Arm CEO Haas in search for Huang's successor
Bloomberg, citing a source, reported that Nvidia (NVDA­US) recruiting firm Heidrick & Struggles approached Arm (ARM­US) CEO Rene 
Haas as a next step in finding a successor to Jensen Huang, having started reaching out to candidates. This follows confirmation on the 
earnings call that the firm does not intend to reinstate Huang after he expressed a willingness to return. Haas is seen as a leading candidate, 
and others mentioned include Nvidia VP Ian Buck, AMD (AMD­US) CEO Lisa Su and Apple (AAPL­US) Chief Hardware Officer Johny 
Srouji. Haas has prior history at Nvidia as general manager of computing products, yet any successor faces intense scrutiny externally and 
steps into a corporate culture shaped around Huang.

European Markets Recap 
Germany ­1% | France ­0.8% | UK ­0.9%
Synopsis

European equities finished weaker on Thursday, giving back earlier gains and following another weak session on Wednesday. The FTSE 100 
closed at a six­week low. Banks, tech and industrials were the best performers, while food and beverage, healthcare, real estate and travel 
and leisure lagged. Core European government bond yields were broadly unchanged, with the German Bund and French OAT 10­year yields 
flat and the OAT­Bund spread little changed at 153.5 bp. Spanish and Italian yields were also flat, while UK gilts underperformed, with 10­
year yields up 5 bp. The dollar index was flat, gold fell 1.4%, silver fell 1% and WTI crude rose 0.8%. 

The macro calendar was quiet. The ECB's labor cost index accelerated in Q3, reinforcing the likelihood of a December rate hike, and ECB's 
Nagel reiterated support for one. BoE Governor Bailey said inflation was likely to peak below the BoE's 4.9% forecast as energy prices ease, 
and sterling hit a one­year low as rate­hike bets eased. Swiss exports rose for a second consecutive month, widening the trade surplus. 

European tech was supported by SOI­FR, which surged 9.7% on upbeat FY27 guidance. Banks also outperformed, with CBK­DE and UCG­
IT both higher after progress toward resolving key takeover obstacles, and BAB­GB rose sharply on strong H1 results and an unchanged 
outlook. On the downside, NOVN­CH fell 3.2% after setting mid­term sales growth targets at the lower end of its previous range, and MCB­
GB slumped 19.3% after warning that FY27 adjusted profit would be 20­25% below consensus amid persistent cost pressures. 
Macro

ECB data show Eurozone labor costs picking up in Q3
ECB data showed a pickup in Eurozone labor costs in Q3 to 3.5% y/y versus a prior 3.1%, revised from 3.0%. The ECB has previously noted 
limited signs so far of second­round effects from energy price rises, as rising productivity has helped contain growth of unit labor costs. 
However, the expectation is that there will be some pass­through to wages the longer energy prices remain high and if inflation takes longer 
to ease. Recent ECB analysis showed average growth in compensation per employee was 3.3% in Q2 versus the prior 3.6%. The ECB's wage 
tracker and surveys on wage expectations show wages rising in the coming quarters before slowing toward the end of 2027, with negotiated 
wages reaching 2.8% in the first half of 2027 before declining to 2.4% in Q3. 

ECB to discuss raising bank reserve requirement next week
Reuters, citing sources, said the ECB will discuss raising the minimum reserve requirement for banks at a meeting next week in a bid to 
reduce its interest bill and withdraw some liquidity from the system. The ECB is looking at raising the share of cash that commercial lenders 
must keep in unremunerated accounts to 2% from 1% of deposits and other short­term liabilities, and most Governing Council members 
support a change now, which may raise speculation of a December announcement. Raising the level by 1% would save the ECB at least 4.4B 
euros a year at current interest rates, and savings could increase further given that markets price a rise in the deposit rate from 2.5% to as 
high as 3.25% next year. There is more than a 90% probability of a December hike and the possibility of two further increases next year. 
The Eurosystem currently pays out 52.2B euros a year in interest on just over 2T euros of excess liquidity. 

BoE's Bailey sees a lower inflation peak
BoE Governor Bailey said Wednesday he expects a lower inflation peak than forecast due to easing energy prices but still anticipates an 
acceleration in inflation through early 2027 (Bloomberg). He reiterated that the policy outlook is contingent on the Middle East conflict 
and warned that if energy prices remain elevated, persistent inflationary effects become more likely to take hold and interest rates will need 
to be higher. Reuters highlighted that, based on BoE forecasts, the UK faces its longest run without inflation dipping below 2% since the 
early 1990s. Market expectations point toward a February rate increase, though some sell­side economists still lean toward a December 
hike followed by a pause. Near­term hike expectations were pared back after yesterday's CPI data met expectations and was slightly below 
BoE estimates, gilt yields eased, and sterling fell to its lowest level in a year versus the dollar, under $1.3150. 

UniCredit and Germany clear key obstacles to Commerzbank takeover
UniCredit (UCG­IT) and the German government resolved the main political sticking points in the Italian bank's two­year pursuit of Com­
merzbank (CBK­DE), clearing the path toward an orderly transfer of control (Handelsblatt, Reuters). UniCredit CEO Orcel committed to 
keeping the combined entity's German headquarters in Frankfurt as a listed company, placing German nationals on UniCredit's board, 
expanding the Mittelstand franchise and maintaining constructive dialogue with the works council. Germany, in turn, dropped its demand 
to nominate two supervisory­board members at Commerzbank. Finance Minister Klingbeil said Berlin is now supportive, though the resid­
ual 13.3% government stake remains unresolved. Regulatory approvals from the ECB, EU Commission and Federal Reserve are already in 
place, and the debate now shifts to price, residual­stake mechanics and integration execution. 

FT: Shenzhen summit shows Europe's interests are settled without Europe
The Trump­Xi­Putin trilateral in Shenzhen produced a thin peace declaration that ignored Ukraine, yet illustrated a larger European prob­
lem (FT). ASML (ASML­NL) is the clearest example: US export controls already bar most advanced EUV machines from China, and a 
bipartisan US bill under discussion could extend restrictions to newer DUV immersion tools and support services. The Hague signalled 
opposition to further extraterritorial pressure, but ASML's reliance on US technology and manufacturing sites leaves limited room for 
manoeuvre (Politico). China still accounts for a substantial share of sales, and Beijing has linked controls to broader EU trade friction, 
including rare­earths access for European suppliers (Reuters). The same pattern appears in Ukraine financing: after a 90B euro loan, the

Asian Markets Recap 
Japan +0.71% | Hong Kong ­1.95% | China ­0.28%
Synopsis

Asian equities ended mostly lower on Thursday. Taiwan's Taiex saw the worst of the selling, while IT and internet stocks again weighed on 
Hong Kong's main index, which fell back to its twice­held, year­long support line. The Kospi and Japan's Nikkei 225 posted small gains, but 
the Topix ended flat. Australia and much of Southeast Asia fell further, while Singapore gained a little and India was again higher. The 
Kospi was held up by a marginal gain at SK Hynix and by outperformance in smaller­cap tourism and entertainment stocks. The yen weak­
ened, closing in on 160 per dollar again, and the Australian dollar lost more ground. 

Australia's unemployment rate ticked up to its highest in nearly five years, and markets now price an RBA rate cut by late next year instead 
of 2028. RBNZ Governor Breman warned that inflation remained elevated amid higher import costs and that the economic recovery was 
uneven. BOJ Governor Ueda warned about rising inflation and reiterated that the bank was still on a tightening path. 

CJ ENM (035760­KR) rose 28.1% and Hotel Shilla (008770­KR) 10.3% on reports Beijing and Seoul agreed to expand their FTA to cover 
services and financials, with China granting preferential treatment to Korea­made content. Tencent (700­HK) fell 6.5% after Q3 revenue 
and adjusted EBITDA missed estimates. Japan Tobacco (2914­JP) is to take a 1.4T yen ($8.9B) charge after writing down its Russian 
subsidiaries following Moscow's forced seizure earlier this month. Lenovo (992­HK) reported higher­than­expected Q2 revenue of $30.2B 
despite a 21.2% decline in PC shipments on higher component prices. 
Macro

Economists increasingly expect a December BOJ rate hike
The Reuters BOJ consensus survey for November showed 61 of 71 (86%) economists anticipate a December rate hike, stronger than 69% in 
the previous month. The survey period covered November 11­18, in the aftermath of the AI selloff, and a strong majority said market 
volatility would not deter a December move. Consensus now looks for the following hike by the end of March 2027 at 60%, brought forward 
from June, taking the policy rate to 1.75%. That level remains the baseline for the expected terminal rate with a 48% response, though the 
remainder is spread out a little more from 2% or higher, little changed from the October poll. Shunto 2027 talks are expected to deliver 
another 5.00% in wage hikes, just below 5.01% this year. 

Ueda says rate hikes will continue, plays down consumption tax effect
In a semi­annual statement to the upper house on currency and monetary control, Governor Ueda repeated the main points in recent policy 
statements and the Outlook Report: rate hikes will continue while the timing and pace are considered, and the main risk factors are the 
Middle East, AI demand and FX. He emphasized a focus on containing upside inflation risk, already sees inflation going clearly above 2% 
driven by wage pass­through and higher prices for crude oil and tech, and sees inflation expectations running at about 2%. Nikkei cited 
earlier remarks by Ueda in the upper house on Wednesday downplaying the consumption tax effect on inflation as temporary, to be stripped 
out of the assessment of underlying inflation. The October Outlook Report estimated the tax cut would be a 1.3 ppt drag on core CPI in 
FY27, though only in a footnote; Ueda explained this was not in the baseline forecast given the bill is still being debated in parliament, and 
that the degree of impact is highly uncertain. 

Weak Australian jobs data prompts a dovish market reaction
Australian employment shrank another 43.5K in October, against a consensus for a 25K gain and widening September's revised 36.4K 
contraction. Full­time jobs fell by 2,900 while part­time employment decreased by 40,600. The unemployment rate unexpectedly rose to 
4.9% from 4.7%, a fresh five­year high, and the participation rate fell to 66.6% from 66.8%. The reaction was dovish, with the policy­
