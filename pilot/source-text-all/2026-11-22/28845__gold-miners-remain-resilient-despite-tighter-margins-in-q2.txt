The balance between gold prices and mining costs shifted in Q2’26. After an exceptional opening quarter, lower gold 
prices coincided with another increase in costs, putting margins under pressure. Global average gold producer All-In 
Sustaining Costs (AISC) rose by 8% q/q and 22% y/y to a record US$1,930/oz. This marked the 29th consecutive year-on-
year increase, underlining the persistence of cost pressures. Higher sustaining capital expenditure, operating expenses 
and production disruption outweighed lower royalties, although strong cash generation continued to support 
investment, shareholder returns and resilient balance sheets. 
Sustaining Investment and Operational Disruption 
Sustaining capex replaced royalties as the principal driver of higher AISC in Q2’26, rising by 34% q/q and 25% y/y to 
US$441/oz, a record level according to our dataset. The increase was equivalent to around 80% of the US$139/oz q/q 
net rise in AISC, lifting sustaining capital’s share of the industry’s cost base to 23%, from 18% in Q1’26. 
However, spending increases were concentrated in a minority of operations rather than being uniform across the 
industry. Agnico Eagle’s sustaining expenditure rose by 21% q/q to US$237m, with most of the increase at Detour Lake 
and Fosterville.1 The timing of expenditure also amplified quarterly fluctuations, as deferred spending raised unit costs 
without necessarily implying a comparable increase in annual expenditure. 
Operational disruption intensified these effects at some operations. At B2Gold’s Goose mine in Canada, catch-up 
expenditure deferred from Q1’26 coincided with a fire in the crushing circuit. Restricted throughput and lower-than-
expected gold sales compounded higher sustaining expenditure, taking AISC to US$6,390/oz.2 Newmont also reported 
additional costs during downtime at Cadia, in Australia, following a seismic event in April. Lower gold and by-product 
sales, alongside weaker by-product credits, further increased reported gold costs. The mine returned to normal 
operations in mid-June, distinguishing temporary setbacks from persistent cost inflation.3

q/q and average grades improved by 1.4%. However, on an annual basis, output and grade were lower, by 4.4% and

Operating Costs and Supply-Chain Pressures 
Onsite operating expenses also continued to increase. Cash Operating Costs rose by 6% q/q and 18% y/y to 
US$1,215/oz, while Total Cash Costs increased by 4% q/q and 22% y/y to US$1,406/oz. Labour and fuel remained 
important pressures. Both Agnico Eagle and AngloGold Ashanti cited higher labour and diesel costs y/y, while Newmont 
also reported higher diesel prices.5 The supply-chain pressures discussed and anticipated in our Q1 blog continued to 
reach major producers’ cost bases.6 
Fuel contracts and hedges can limit immediate exposure, but replenishment at higher prices may prolong cost pressure. 
After quarter-end, Agnico Eagle purchased around 70% of its Nunavut diesel requirements through to the 2027 delivery 
season at prices approximately 30% above its 2026 budget, net of hedges.7 Westgold Resources’ long-term procurement 
agreements prevented supply interruptions but did not eliminate price exposure. Higher diesel prices added A$12m to 
its June-quarter costs.8 
Supply-chain constraints in Q2’26 not only affected the cost, but also the availability of essential inputs. Delays in 
obtaining consumables or mobilising equipment can restrict activity, while more expensive supplies raised expenditure 
on the work that continued. Resolute Mining, for example, reported logistics difficulties, higher fuel and reagent costs, 
and explosives supply problems at Syama in Mali.9 Energy availability also impacted costs at Evolution Mining’s Mungari 
in Australia, which reported approximately three days of grid-power outages among factors affecting AISC.10 Reliable 
procurement and energy supply therefore remained important to both costs and production. 
Meanwhile, lower gold prices provided partial support through lower royalties. Average royalty and production-tax costs 
fell by 11% q/q to US$185/oz, although they remained 53% higher y/y. Their share of AISC eased to 9.6%, from 11.6% in 
Q1’26. However, lower prices do not reverse fiscal changes: Newmont reported a full quarter of Ghana’s increased 
royalties, illustrating why relief was uneven across producers.11 Corporate general and administrative costs also fell by 
21% q/q to US$82/oz. Together, these reductions softened, but did not offset, higher operating expenses and sustaining 
capex.

Margins Retreat but Cash Generation Remains Strong 
Higher costs coincided with a 7.2% q/q decline in the average gold price to US$4,512/oz. Consequently, average AISC 
margins contracted by 16% q/q to US$2,582/oz, reversing part of the exceptional expansion seen in Q1’26. Nevertheless, 
gold prices remained 37% higher y/y and margins were up by 52%. The average margin was still the second highest in 
our dataset, leaving miners with a substantial cushion despite the quarterly squeeze. 
Strong underlying profitability continued to support cash generation, although quarterly outcomes varied. AngloGold 
Ashanti generated US$727m of free cash flow (FCF), up by 36% y/y, while Kinross generated attributable FCF of 
US$727m, up by 12% y/y.12 Elsewhere, however, the retreat from Q1’26 was more pronounced. Endeavour Mining’s FCF 
fell to US$149m from US$613m, reflecting higher tax payments, lower realised gold prices, increased operating costs 
and greater investing outflows. Even so, the company ended H2’26 with US$254m of net cash, demonstrating resilience 
despite weaker quarterly cash generation.13 
Shareholder distributions continued despite lower quarterly cash generation at some producers. OceanaGold’s FCF 
declined to US$130m from US$255m in Q1, but it returned US$78m to shareholders, including US$58m through share 
buybacks.14 Endeavour Mining paid approximately US$200m in dividends and repurchased US$42m of shares during the 
quarter. These distributions demonstrate the role of accumulated financial resources, rather than current-quarter cash 
flow alone, in supporting shareholder returns. 
Cash was also used to reduce financial risk. AngloGold Ashanti repurchased approximately US$666m principal of 
outstanding bonds in April, lowering gross debt and future interest obligations, and ended June with US$991m of net 
cash.15 Newmont, Agnico Eagle and Kinross ended June with reported net cash of US$3.4bn, US$3.3bn and US$1.9bn, 
respectively.16 These balance sheets provide flexibility to maintain investment and distributions through weaker prices or 
operational disruption. The margin contraction increased the importance of cost control without eliminating the financial 
strength built during the gold-price upswing. 
Outlook 
Q2’26 marked a retreat from exceptional margins rather than a collapse in cash generation. Gold prices remained high 
by historical standards and miners’ balance sheets remained robust. As AISC approaches US$2,000/oz, expensive fuel 
purchases, continuing supply-chain and inflationary pressures are expected to sustain cost increases. Meanwhile, the 
gold price averaged US$4,266/oz in Q3’26, down by 5.5% q/q, and stood at around US$4,040/oz at the time of writing. 
Combined with rising costs, this sets the stage for further margin contraction over the remainder of 2026. Looking 
ahead to 2027, a forecast recovery in gold prices would lift royalty costs but this should be more than offset through 
higher revenues, supporting wider margins and further strengthening miners’ financial positions.
