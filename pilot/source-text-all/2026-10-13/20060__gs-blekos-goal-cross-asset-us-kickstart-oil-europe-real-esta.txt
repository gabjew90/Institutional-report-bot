US WEEKLY KICKSTART - The outlook for corporate equity supply and demand in 2027
| • | 2026 is on track to be a record year for US equity issuance. US corporates have raised $431 billion
YTD via IPOs, follow-ons, converts, and SPACs, representing growth of 98% year/year. The total dollar
volume of US equity issuance in 2026 will likely surpass the previous high of $540 billion in 2021.
However, the number of deals and the value of issuance scaled to market cap are close to historical
averages. | | • | Following record issuance volumes in Q2, macro volatility has weighed on activity in
recent months. In addition to a handful of large deals, more than 90 US equity offerings came to market in
each of May and June. However, the monthly average declined to roughly 50 deals in Q3. Sharp rises in
interest rates have historically been associated with declining issuance volumes, and this pattern has been
repeated recently. |
OIL ANALYST - Supply Adaptation Meets Global Restocking Demand
| • | We maintain our base case that continued adaptation of Mideast supply will eventually reduce crude
oil prices, while potential renewed escalation still poses significant net upside risk. We nudge up our Brent
Dec26 price forecast to $90/bbl (vs. $85 prior) because ongoing downside risks to supply likely slow the
moderation in the timespreads risk premium—which we estimate at just over $20 today. | | • | Our 2027
average Brent forecast of $80 is unchanged and balances downward price pressure from faster-than-
expected adaptation in Gulf supply against a slower moderation in the risk premium. |
EUROPE REAL ESTATE - 3Q26 sector preview: we cut 2026E NTAs by 6%; AT1 down to Sell
| • | We downgrade Aroundtown from Neutral to Sell; we now forecast decreasing EPS over 2026-28E
mostly on higher finance costs. We forecast ICR to reach c.2.2x by 2030 (covenants <1.8x). We cut our
NTA estimates by over 40%, factoring in c.100bp yield expansion by 2028 (vs 20bp previously), implying
LTV reaching 48% (c.63% with hybrids). | | • | We see AT1's low ROCE (3.8% 27E), high cost of capital at
c.6.2%, and high leverage including hybrids as challenging. While valuation is already at c.78% discount
to 2026E NTA, the stock has already traded wider, and we see more risks should funding costs increase

further, while lower rates and/or disposals could alleviate leverage questions. Our revised 12m PT declines
by c.28% to €1.30 (from €1.80), implying c.15% downside potential (7th Oct close) vs. average upside of
15% for our coverage. |
ENERGY - Quantifying transportation costs by oil company. US and North Sea producers most
advantaged, Asian refiners the hardest hit
| • | Freight rates have surged 10-50x across all tanker classes since year start, leading to a wide divergence
in realized price and margins for E&Ps and Refiners across the globe, and a return to regional trading in a
traditionally global market. We introduce a route-by-route analysis with an evaluation of the relative
advantages of oil producers and refiners, based on their geographical location and their long-term tanker
availability. |

| • | Nordex SE has announced that it secured orders totaling 2,810 MW in the third quarter of 2026,
marking an increase of approximately 29.5% compared to 2,170 MW in the same period of the previous
year. The average sales price in euros per megawatt of capacity (ASP) EUR 0.90 million/MW in the third

last year (9M/2025: EUR 0.92 million/MW). | | • | The development is primarily driven by project scope

turbines for projects in 10 countries, with most projects coming from Turkiye, Germany, and the US. |
Terna (TRN.MI) - Tightening balance sheet may cap future growth; Sell
| • | We revisit our investment thesis for Terna. While Terna remains well-placed to benefit from structural
industry tailwinds long term, we believe the market is increasingly overlooking the constraints that may
weigh on it. In our view, rising balance sheet utilisation reduces the likelihood of meaningful capex
upgrades, while growing affordability concerns could pressure a remuneration framework that remains
relatively reliant on incentives. | | • | As a result, we expect modest earnings growth (+c.2% EPS CAGR to
2030E), making Terna's double-digit premium P/E valuation hard to justify. |
