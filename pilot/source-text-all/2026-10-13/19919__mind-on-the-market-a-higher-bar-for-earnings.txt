A higher bar for earnings 
Strong first-half results have raised the bar heading into the next reporting season. With expectations 
continuing to rise, investors are increasingly focused on the breadth and durability of earnings growth, 
not just companies' ability to beat forecasts. 
On the surface, first half results suggest broad-based strength. In the first quarter, 84.6% of S&P 500 
companies exceeded earnings expectations, followed by 85.8% in the second quarter, both well above 
the ten-year average of 76.6% (FactSet, as of 10/2/26). However, the composition of those surprises 
reveals an important nuance. The median company beat expectations by 5.8% in the second quarter, 
well below the 26.0% aggregate surprise at the index level (FactSet, as of 10/2/26). This disparity 
suggests that, although a large majority of companies exceeded expectations, the largest index 
constituents accounted for a disproportionate share of the overall outperformance. 
Sector leadership provides additional context. Communication Services and Consumer Discretionary 
delivered the largest earnings surprises in both the first and second quarters, driven in large part by 
exceptionally strong results from Amazon and Alphabet. Amazon exceeded earnings expectations by 
68.5% in Q1 and 212.5% in Q2, while Alphabet surpassed consensus by 94.3% and 215.2%, 
respectively, reflecting continued strength in cloud and AI-related businesses (FactSet, as of 10/2/26). 
Information Technology and Communication Services accounted for the majority of earnings growth in 
both quarters, while Energy also made a meaningful contribution in Q2. Technology and Communication 
Services benefited from robust AI-related spending, cloud demand, and digital infrastructure investment, 
while higher commodity prices supported Energy profitability. The strength was broad in direction but 
concentrated in magnitude: 10 of 11 sectors contributed positively to earnings growth in both Q1 and Q2 
2026, with Health Care the only sector to post negative growth. Yet Technology, Communication

Although earnings growth was concentrated in a handful of sectors, the impact extended beyond the 
companies driving the strongest results. Consensus CY 2026 EPS estimates increased from $313.72 at 
the end of February 2026 to $362.65 on October 2, 2026, a gain of 15.6%, while CY 2027 estimates rose 
from $363.54 to $419.32, or 15.3%, over the same period (FactSet, as of 10/2/26). The revision pattern 
was also unusual: forecasts for both years continued to rise, including notable upward resets during July 
and August, although the pace moderated after midyear. The persistence of these revisions suggests 
that analysts treated at least part of the recent earnings strength as a durable improvement rather than a 
temporary boost.

The steady rise in earnings expectations has important implications for the valuation debate. Higher 
forecasts provide a supportive backdrop for US equities by allowing earnings growth, rather than further 
multiple expansion, to carry a greater share of future returns. At the same time, stronger expectations 
raise the hurdle for companies heading into the next reporting season. 
The focus now shifts to third-quarter earnings, which kicks off this week with major Banks, and offers an 
important signal about the durability of recent profit growth, especially later in the month when many of 
the Mag 7 report during Oct 28-29th. According to FactSet estimates as of October 2, 2026, expected Q3 
earnings growth increased to 29.5% from 26.7% at the end of June, while expected revenue growth rose 
to 12.3% from 10.9%. If realized, Q3 2026 would be the first quarter in the available FactSet market EPS 
data1 since Q3 2008 to simultaneously complete three consecutive quarters of earnings growth above 
25% and eight consecutive quarters of double-digit growth. All 11 sectors are projected to report positive 
earnings growth, led by Energy, Information Technology, Communication Services, and Materials.
