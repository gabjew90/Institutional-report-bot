 
Next week (20-21 October), we will host our third Global Metals & Mining 
Conference in London during LME Week. Last year, the debate centred on whether 
electrification, decarbonisation, AI and Western re-industrialisation could sustain a 
period of demand growth for minerals. While some of these themes are beginning to 
play out, the focus is now shifting to supply. 
Metals are increasingly viewed through the lens of resource security, and 
governments have moved from bystanders to active participants through faster 
permitting, shared infrastructure, price floors and, more recently, direct equity 
stakes and offtake agreements. We will host senior management from the world’s 
largest diversified, copper, aluminium, rare earth and lithium producers, alongside 
traders and industry bodies, to discuss how the industry can deliver growth and 
returns in an increasingly complex environment. 
Can the industry deliver the supply the market now expects? 
Record prices and tightening physical markets for copper, aluminium and zinc have 
put growth back on the agenda. However, the project pipeline has rarely looked 
harder to execute. Deposits are deeper, higher altitude and in more remote 
jurisdictions; permitting, labour availability and productivity remain binding 
constraints; and inflation continues to pressure costs. 
While margins are expanding, miners are also spending more to sustain production. 
Weather disruptions, operational setbacks, accidents and growing risks of industrial 
action are making the path to new supply increasingly complex. With several projects 
approaching FID, including Vicuna and Olympic Dam, we expect the market to 
differentiate less on resource endowment and more on production consistency and 
the credibility of growth plans. Project quality, fiscal stability and execution 
capability may become the dividing line between the projects that proceed and 
those that do not this cycle.

Metals Security, Growth and Consolidation
 
 

With few shovel-ready greenfield projects and limited appetite to carry execution risk 
alone, asset-level partnerships and district-scale tie-ups have become an increasingly 
attractive route to scale. Los Bronces/Andina, Collahuasi/QB, Spence/Sierra Gorda and 
Pelambres/Pachon are among the opportunities in copper that we will explore during 
our fireside discussions. 
Following an eventful year, corporate M&A also remains front of mind, but perhaps more 
divisive. Executives see the strategic logic (scale, relevance), while investors continue to 
question whether transactions create genuine operational value or simply add scale. 
With RIO and GLEN having explored a combination earlier this year, the debate between 
scale and simplification will be among the most closely watched themes at the 
conference. 
How will growth be funded? 
Rather than relying solely on balance sheet capacity, the sector is increasingly 
considering infrastructure monetisation, capital recycling and alternative pools of 
capital. We estimate that the majors could unlock significant value from power, water 
and logistics infrastructure and redeploy the proceeds into higher-return opportunities. 
BHP’s Pilbara power transaction may prove the first of many, and we see scope for RIO 
to follow, but believe many miners are exploring these avenues. The question is whether 
alternative funding structures and portfolio simplification can provide an alternative 
funding playbook for delivering growth while preserving balance sheet strength and 
shareholder returns. 
Sector re-rate underway 
Sector multiples have expanded this year, with diversified miners narrowing the gap with 
base-metal peers. Operational consistency, disciplined capital allocation and 
sustainable returns may now matter as much as commodity prices in determining 
whether the sector can continue to attract a broader investor base. 
The industry is entering this growth phase with strong balance sheets, improving 
fundamentals and growing strategic importance. Yet mining continues to trade at a 
discount to the industries competing for the same capital. Whether the sector can 
convert stronger markets and renewed strategic relevance into credible growth and 
sustainable returns will be a central debate at this year’s conference.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
