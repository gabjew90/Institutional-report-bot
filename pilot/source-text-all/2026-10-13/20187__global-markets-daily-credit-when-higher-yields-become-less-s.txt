• Credit spreads have historically provided some cushion when government yields rise, particularly when
higher rates reflect stronger growth expectations. That cushion has become less reliable in recent months.
Since July, USD and EUR credit spreads have moved higher in tandem with government yields, creating a
dual headwind for unhedged credit returns.
• In this Global Markets Daily, we revisit the relationship between rates and credit spreads, focusing on
why the correlation has shifted into more positive territory and what that means for credit investors. The
spread/rates correlation is especially important for IG credit, where elevated all-in yields have been an
important technical support for demand in recent years. In USD IG, credit spreads account for less than
15% of all-in yield today, versus an average of 45% between 2019 and 2021. But the support from higher
all-in yields is less reliable when the increase in risk-free rates is accompanied by wider spreads and
elevated rates volatility.
• We think the recent spread moves reflect a combination of higher oil prices, a rapid rise in government
yields, and renewed fiscal concerns in Europe. While the spread/rates correlation should moderate if the
pace of the rates sell-off slows, we do not expect a quick return to the reliably negative relationship that
previously helped cushion credit returns. With spreads still tight and credit spread curves relatively flat, we
favor carry with controlled duration exposure. Within credit, we retain a modest preference for USD over
EUR, favor BBBs over AAs and As, and see better value in the belly of the curve than at the long end.
