USTs ended the week weaker, with the belly leading the sell-off as higher oil prices and renewed Middle 
East tensions reasserted themselves as the dominant macro driver. After an initial London-session rally, fresh 
Iran-related headlines pushed WTI back above $92/bbl, taking 5Y yields to around 5.05% and 10Y yields 
close to 5.28% at the intraday highs and flattening the curve, with 2s10s and 5s30s ending modestly flatter 
on the day. Treasuries recovered some ground into the close as crude reversed lower following reports that 
Russia could boost diesel supplies, helping WTI retreat from its highs. Swap spreads were little changed 
in subdued trading conditions, while the dollar remained supported by the combination of firmer oil prices 
and higher Treasury yields. Looking ahead, focus shifts to Wednesday's CPI report and the start of Q3 bank 
earnings, with markets continuing to price only a limited amount of additional Fed tightening this year. The 
dollar index closed at 102.21, up a slim +0.07% on Friday, reflecting a muted session with no dominant 
directional catalyst. Markets are closed today due to the Columbus Day holiday. 
EGBs finished a volatile session little changed overall as oil and geopolitical headlines remained firmly in 
the driving seat. Bunds opened stronger and initially extended gains as energy prices eased, but those moves 
gradually reversed as crude recovered through the European morning. The 10Y Bund yield briefly pushed 
up to 3.48% before retracing towards 3.45% as oil came off its highs, only to move back towards 3.49% 
following further Iran-related headlines later in the session. Despite the swings in core rates, semi-core and 
peripheral markets outperformed, with BTP-Bund and OAT-Bund spreads tightening by as much as 4-5bp 
intraday before ending the week around 1.5-2.5bp tighter. Flows were light across the board, reinforcing the 
sense that price action was being driven by macro headlines rather than positioning or data. Looking 
ahead, Monday's holiday-thinned session should leave markets focused on energy developments, IMF 
meetings and central bank speakers, with German and French bill supply the only notable issuance event. 
The euro slipped -0.10% to 1.1200, giving back a small amount of ground against the dollar as the oil-driven risk 
tone kept the currency on the back foot. 
Gilts tracked the broader oil-driven rates story, opening firmer alongside Bunds and USTs before reversing 
lower as energy prices rebounded. Selling intensified into the US open, with yields rising 4-5bp on the day 
and the curve flattening as markets continued to price the inflation implications of higher oil prices. The 10Y 
Gilt yield reached 5.44% before retracing towards 5.40% as crude briefly eased in early New York trading, 
although renewed geopolitical headlines later pushed yields back up towards 5.46%. Cross-market moves 
remained important, with the 10Y Gilt-Bund spread tightening to around 195bp before giving back part of the 
move. Away from geopolitics, domestic drivers were limited, with attention instead turning to next week's 
appearances from Breeden and Mann and Thursday's UK activity data. The DMO announced a £500mn 
programme tender of the 4.0% 2063 Gilt for Tuesday, while S&P reviewed the UK’s sovereign credit 
rating, affirming it at AA / Stable. Sterling outperformed modestly, closing at 1.3234, up +0.05%, supported 
by relative BoE hawkishness even as broader FX volatility remained subdued. 
JPY Markets are closed today due to the Sports Day holiday.
