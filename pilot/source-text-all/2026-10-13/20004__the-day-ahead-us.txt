Market Recap at 4 PM ET    
The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq ended lower, with tech heavyweights facing heat ahead of big bank earnings and inflation data 
due later in the week. Gold prices were pressured lower by a stronger dollar. Oil rose as attacks on Saudi Arabia 
overshadowed the prospect of increased supplies. 
  
 
 
 
COMING UP 
Wall Street’s earnings season gathers pace, with major banks leading a slate of third-quarter results from corporate America. 
JPMorgan is expected to report third-quarter results, with analysts forecasting higher profit. Investors are likely to focus on the 
outlook for dealmaking and trading, as well as executives’ views on the health of the economy. At the same time, Goldman 
Sachs is expected to report a higher third-quarter profit, benefiting from a rebound in mergers and acquisitions that is set to 
boost investment banking fees and a rise in equities revenue driven by market volatility amid the US-Iran war. Investors will also 
be watching for any comments on the bank's CEO succession plans. Similarly, Citigroup is expected to report strong third-
quarter profit, helped by growth in investment banking and trading income. Meanwhile, Wells Fargo is expected to report a rise 
in third-quarter profit and revenue, supported by strong net interest income. Investors will look for commentary on consumer 
spending and the impact of interest rate hikes. 
 
In healthcare, UnitedHealth Group is expected to report its third-quarter results. Investor focus will be on its medical cost 
management and the performance of its health insurance and Optum health services units. Additionally, Johnson & Johnson is 
set to report its third quarter results before markets open, the focus will be on changes in the company's annual outlook and 
comments around demand for key drugs and momentum in its medical devices business.

Market Monitor 
The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq ended lower as 
oil prices jumped after attacks on a Saudi 
airport, and investors waited for banks to kick 
off the third-quarter earnings season and for 
economic data this week. Technology was the 
biggest decliner, down 0.90% to end at 
7,314.54 with weakness most pronounced 
among 
chip 
stocks. 
The 
Philadelphia 
semiconductor index lost 2.67% to end at 
12,236.79. In coming days, investors will focus 
on corporate earnings, with major US banks 
including Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and 
JPMorgan Chase reporting early on Tuesday.  
"Looking out further in the week, we have some 
big economic reports," said Tim Ghriskey, 
senior portfolio strategist at Ingalls & Snyder in 
New York. He said investors were cautious due 
to a lack of direction from bonds. "We're also 
waiting on earnings and that's significant. The 
four large banks reporting on Tuesday can have 
a big impact," he said. Also due this week are September readings for US consumer and producer inflation data as well as retail 
sales data. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.38% to 51,850.31, while the S&P 500 was down 0.06% at 7,807.20 and 
the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.44% to 27,245.90.  
The dollar rose to an 18-month high, supported by safe-haven demand as an escalating Middle East conflict kept investors 
cautious ahead of key US inflation data later this week. Weekly data from the US markets regulator shows investors have been 
adding to their bullish positions on the dollar, which rose to around $36 billion in the week to October 7 from $8.4 billion three 
weeks ago, largely at the expense of the euro. "Markets got off to a relatively encouraging start to the week, but sentiment has 
since deteriorated as renewed geopolitical uncertainty and rising oil prices weigh on risk appetite, prompting a fresh round of US 
dollar demand," said Joel Kruger, a market strategist at LMAX Group in London. The dollar index rose 0.36% to 102.58, its 
highest since April 2025. The euro was down 0.44% at $1.1152. Against the Japanese yen, the dollar gained 0.21% to 158.65 
yen.  
Oil prices gained as attacks on Saudi Arabia overshadowed the prospect of increased supplies after US President Donald Trump 
hailed fresh Russian diesel exports and a Russia-Ukraine ceasefire on energy targets. The Iran-aligned Houthis claimed 
responsibility for a Saturday strike on Riyadh's international airport that killed 13 people and wounded more than 300. Further 
attacks happened on Sunday. In addition, more attacks on tankers sailing through the Strait of Hormuz have been reported this 
month, threatening to dampen a recovery in Middle East crude exports seen in September. "The sense of some kind of normality 
of exports from the Strait of Hormuz was shattered last week. Fresh attacks by the Houthis on Saudi Arabia added to this and 
helped to lift prices," said SEB commodities analyst Bjarne Schieldrop. Brent crude futures were up 1.51% at $106.30 a barrel. 
US West Texas Intermediate futures rose 1.73% to $93.44 per barrel. 
Gold prices eased, pressured by a firmer dollar and higher oil prices, while investors awaited US inflation data for clues on the 
Federal Reserve's interest-rate trajectory. "We're seeing some selling pressure in gold because the dollar is near a one-and-a-
half-year high," said Jim Wyckoff, a market analyst at American Gold Exchange. An escalating Middle East conflict kept investors 
wary. "I don't think (the reports) are going to show any signs of easing in inflation, and they would probably lean bearish for the 
metals market," Wyckoff added. Investors would look out for remarks from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, along with key inflation data, 
later this week to gauge future monetary policy. Spot gold fell 0.60% to $4,168.51 per ounce. US gold futures lost 0.57% to 
$4,192.40 per ounce.

TOP NEWS 
Microsoft plans huge deployment of its own AI 
chips, with an eye on saving costs  
Microsoft announced the next generation of its custom 
AI chip and said it plans to deploy potentially more 
than a million of them next year to back its internal AI 
services. The Maia 300, as the chip is called, is the 
third generation of the company's custom chips 
designed to handle AI data crunching such as coding 
and carrying out complex business tasks on a user's 
behalf. Microsoft said the Maia 300 chips will get 
about 40% more useful computing done per megawatt 
than "leading GPU-based systems," though it did not 
name the systems it used for a comparison. "It's 
basically delivering more intelligence for every watt, 
every dollar, and every byte of memory by optimizing 
across the entire system," Rani Borkar, president of 
Azure hardware systems and infrastructure at 
Microsoft, told Reuters in an interview. 
 
Starbucks says not buying Chipotle, ends takeover speculation 
Starbucks is not buying Chipotle Mexican Grill, the coffee chain said, quashing talk of a deal that could have been the largest-
ever involving restaurants but was doubted by both investors and analysts. "As a matter of policy, we don't comment on rumors 
and speculation, but we want to be clear we are not buying Chipotle," a Starbucks spokesperson said. Chipotle declined to 
comment. The statement follows a report by the Financial Times on October 8 that Starbucks had worked with advisers in recent 
months on a potential proposal to acquire Chipotle, citing people familiar with the matter. Starbucks also reiterated that its 
management was "laser focused" on "Back to Starbucks" strategy and had "strong momentum and confidence" in its long-term 
growth prospects. The company aims to share its fourth-quarter results and fiscal 2027 outlook later this month. 
 
Steris doubles down on healthcare consumables with $2.1 billion Xodus buy 
Medical equipment maker Steris will buy privately held Xodus Medical for $2.1 billion, adding surgical products designed to 
improve safety for patients and operating-room staff to its healthcare consumables business. The acquisition strengthens Steris' 
push into hospitals and laboratories, where its sterilization and consumables business is already seeing sustained demand. 
"Net-net, this is a logical tuck-in that adds additional operating-room supplies to Steris' healthcare business," said Stephens 
analyst Steven Etoch. He also said Steris may be able to lift Xodus sales by using its larger sales network and established 
relationships with hospital operating rooms. Xodus makes surgical-safety devices for hospitals worldwide and is best known for 
its Pink Pad system, which secures and stabilizes patients on the operating table to reduce the risk of slipping or pressure-
related injury during surgery. 
 
Bristol Myers radiopharma cancer drug slows rare tumor progression in study 
Bristol Myers Squibb said its experimental radiopharmaceutical cancer therapy significantly delayed disease progression in 
patients with rare, advanced gastrointestinal and pancreatic tumors that had progressed after earlier treatment. The therapy, 
RYZ101, which Bristol Myers added through its $4.1 billion takeover of RayzeBio, belongs to a closely watched class of cancer 
therapies that act like a 'cannonball' to kill cancer cells by delivering radioactive particles to tumorous tissue or tumor-associated 
cells. RBC Capital Markets analysts said the results validate Bristol Myers' radiopharmaceutical platform and may boost 
confidence in rival programs across the sector. Separately, the company said a lower dose of its experimental drug slowed the 
progression of a serious lung-scarring disease, putting it on track to be a first-of-its-kind treatment if approved. To read more,

Delta pushes back Atlanta-Riyadh service after Riyadh airport attacks 
Delta Air Lines said it would delay the launch of its planned nonstop Atlanta-Riyadh service until March 2027, amid ongoing 
geopolitical tensions. "We remain in close contact with relevant government and aviation partners, including Saudia and Riyadh 
Air, as we monitor the situation," the carrier said. The decision follows a series of flight cancellations by several airlines serving 
Saudi Arabia and neighboring countries after deadly weekend attacks on Riyadh airport by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis. Delta 
had announced the Atlanta-Riyadh service last year, months after US President Donald Trump's visit to Saudi Arabia. The 
service would have been Delta's first direct passenger route to Saudi Arabia and would have made it the only US carrier offering 
nonstop flights to the kingdom. 
 
Amazon in talks to buy AI startup Decart for around $7 billion, WSJ reports 
Amazon is in talks to acquire AI startup Decart in a deal valued at around $7 billion, the Wall Street Journal reported, citing 
people familiar with the matter. Decart's Lucy model, backed by Nvidia, can edit live video in real time, according to the 
company's website. It has also developed Oasis, a model that generates simulated environments to train and test robotics and 
autonomous-driving systems. The Journal reported that Decart was valued at nearly $4 billion in May, citing PitchBook data. A 
deal could happen soon if talks do not fall apart or another suitor emerges, the report said. Separately, the Massachusetts 
Institute of Technology has sued Amazon in Boston federal court, alleging Amazon Web Services' data security technology 
infringes two of its patents. To read more, click here  
 
Meta releases Muse Wonder AI models to challenge Anthropic and OpenAI 
Meta launched Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, calling them its most capable AI models yet, as the social media firm 
looks to compete more forcefully with OpenAI and Anthropic. The launch comes after CEO Mark Zuckerberg pledged billions of 
dollars and aggressively poached researchers over the past year to catch up in Silicon Valley's intensifying AI race. Meta said 
Muse Wonder would soon power its Muse personal AI agent, initially for subscribers, while the more powerful Muse Wonder Full 
model is available to subscribers of its AI coding tool Muse Code. "Fourteen months ago, we began an overhaul of our AI efforts, 
and in that time we've gone from rebuilding our foundations to pushing the frontier," Meta said in a blog post. 
 
IonQ to buy cybersecurity firm Elisity in up to $1.3 billion deal 
Quantum computing company IonQ said it would buy cybersecurity firm Elisity in an all-stock deal valued at up to $1.3 billion, as 
it looks to expand its quantum security business. IonQ said the acquisition would provide it with expanded channels to bring its 
quantum offerings to both enterprise and government customers, helping them deal with current threats and prepare for attacks 
from future quantum computers. The deal value includes up to $150 million in earn-outs tied to Elisity's performance, it added. 
The number of IonQ shares to be issued will be calculated at a price between $33.07 and $49.61 per share. "Q-Day is coming 
much faster than predicted," IonQ CEO Niccolo de Masi said, referring to the point when quantum computers could break widely 
used encryption. 
 
Anduril unveils interceptor missile family Steelfin to counter varied threats 
Defense tech firm Anduril unveiled its Steelfin family of configurable and scalable surface-to-air interceptor missiles designed to 
tackle diverse aerial threats, as the US looks to beef up military stockpiles amid the Middle East conflict. Steelfin's two-stage 
architecture features a first-stage booster for range, performance, and launch configurations and a second-stage terminal 
interceptor with airframe, avionics, and flight controls common across variants, Anduril said. The defense contractor said it plans 
to produce on the order of several thousand Steelfin variants a year in the short term. Anduril will begin flight testing Steelfin 
variants next year. It has already completed ballistic testing with representative air vehicles. 
 
Norway fines Microsoft's Xandr $9.6 million over Grindr data sharing 
Norway's data protection authority has fined Microsoft's adtech unit Xandr 92 million crowns ($9.61 million) for unlawfully 
relaying Grindr users' sensitive data to thousands of advertisers, it said. Xandr relayed data revealing the sexual orientation of 
users of Grindr, a dating app for LGBTQIA+ people, to companies in automated ad auctions from July 2018 to April 2020. It is 
the biggest fine the Norwegian authority has issued, and targets the advertising middlemen rather than the app itself. The 
regulator said companies that traded in people's data could not pass the blame to others. The fine was based on Xandr's 
revenue, not Microsoft's, which did not own the unit at the time. Xandr has three weeks to appeal.

Insight and Analysis 
REUTERS OPEN INTEREST-Wall Street’s AI bet is now too big to hit: McGeever 
The hurdle for continued AI-driven equity gains is strikingly high. And with cracks starting to appear in the seemingly 
impenetrable tech edifice, there are mounting signs AI risk is now far more than just a case of "bad vibes." While the S&P 500 
and Nasdaq climbed to fresh highs last week, a string of negative headlines related to AI – from a shelved initial public offering  
in Australia to reports that ChatGPT maker OpenAI's revenue is falling short of earlier estimates – suggests markets could soon 
run into some serious obstacles. Earnings releases and forecasts from the big AI players in the coming weeks may ease those 
concerns. But the bar is high – very high. 
 
GRAPHIC-Wall Street's big but narrow rally: 4 in 10 S&P stocks are down this year 
The rally that has driven the S&P 500 up 14% this year to record highs is masking the fact that more than 4 in 10 stocks in the 
index are lower in 2026, as the gains are concentrated in a few chipmakers and other AI-related heavyweights. The gap 
between the benchmark and its average member highlights how much of Wall Street's rally rests on a handful of companies as 
the US bull market nears a four-year anniversary. 
 
FOCUS-Citi seeks rapid resolution of its US regulatory burdens 
Citigroup is seeking to rapidly resolve its efforts to address regulatory demands that have long dogged the third-largest US 
bank, in order to fully re-focus on growth, according to two people familiar with the matter. The demands have lingered since 
2020, leading to costly fines and distractions for Citi. Investors and analysts are laser focused on when Citi might shake off its 
regulatory burden, which could free up resources, boost the stock and become a key achievement for Chief Executive Jane 
Fraser.

US yield surge triggers mortgage hedging, adds to bond market pressure 
The sharp rise in long-term US Treasury yields has spurred mortgage-related hedging that adds selling pressure to an already 
strained bond market, though the impact is likely smaller than in past selloffs. 
 
PROSPERITY GAP 
In the US, a wealth boom and a price shock 
collide 
The US is in the middle of a historic and costly 
technological revolution, with the artificial 
intelligence buildout driving a level of 
investment set to eclipse the previous 
telecommunications boom, as well as national 
projects like the construction of rail and 
highway networks. The transformation has 
spurred immense stock market gains that have 
boosted household wealth across the 
economy. But with investment gains 
concentrated among the already rich, some 
Americans are benefiting more than others. 
On housing in particular, the pandemic shock 
has had a long tail, and so far things have not 
gotten better under Trump. In fact, with bond 
yields rising, home mortgage rates have 
jumped more than a full percentage point in 
the past year, intensifying what was already an 
unaffordable situation. 
 
 
FED COMMENTARY 
Fed's Hammack backs restrictive policy, says better if it comes 'relatively quickly' 
Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said the US central bank should move "relatively quickly" to a restrictive monetary 
policy stance in order to control inflation she feels has become more broad-based and is starting to influence firms' price-setting 
plans.

AI DISRUPTION 
IMF says AI investment keeps global growth around 3% but brings massive disruption 
Strong investment in AI infrastructure is keeping global growth at around 3% with major benefits for Asia, but also foreshadows 
a "slow-moving tsunami" that could massively disrupt labor markets, taxation and education, International Monetary Fund 
Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said. 
 
GOING PRIVATE 
Dating app Bumble in advanced talks to go private at $1 billion valuation, source says 
Bumble is in advanced talks to be taken private by Ithaca Holdings in a deal that values the dating app operator at roughly $1 
billion, a source familiar with the matter told Reuters. Shares of the company surged 52.56% to end at $3.88. 
 
SUPPLY DEAL 
EnergyX signs binding $2 billion lithium supply deal with Trafigura 
Lithium startup EnergyX said it has signed a $2 billion binding offtake to supply commodities trader Trafigura with the battery 
metal from Chile and the US, one of the largest deals ever tied to direct lithium extraction technology.
