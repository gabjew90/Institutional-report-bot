Klarna and the Apple Upgrade Program: Incremental Volume Opportunity, Margin Still the Key Debate 
 
 
Klarna’s recently launched Apple Upgrade Program represents a meaningful incremental 
growth opportunity (please find our recent note). The program expands beyond Apple’s 
prior Citizens-backed iPhone financing offering to include Apple Watches, iPads, and 
Macs, while launching at a time when carrier financing programs are seeing less 
emphasis and device prices continue to increase. We believe these factors could support 
greater adoption of financing solutions and create a sizable opportunity for Klarna to 
participate in future Apple upgrade activity. Our analysis suggests the program could 
contribute roughly 4% of 2027 GMV, with iPhone upgrades representing the primary 
volume driver and higher-ticket iPads and Macs providing an additional tailwind. 
Importantly, the opportunity extends beyond simply replacing Apple’s former 
Citizens-backed financing relationship. We believe the combination of moderating 
carrier subsidy intensity, expansion into additional Apple product categories, and 
financing structures that reduce friction relative to traditional lending products 
broadens the addressable market. While carrier financing is likely to remain the 
dominant purchase channel, even modest share gains within the Apple ecosystem can 
support meaningful GMV growth given the size of the underlying market. As a result, we 
view evidence of accelerating Apple-related originations as a potentially important 
contributor to GMV growth over the next several quarters. 
That said, the primary investor debate remains economics rather than volume. While the 
program could support attractive transaction margins if receivables were retained on 
balance sheet, Klarna’s strategy of selling loans to funding partners transfers a 
meaningful portion of the economics to third parties. As shown below, transaction 
margins under a loan-sale structure could range from roughly 30-130bps depending on 
merchant pricing, funding costs, and credit performance, versus materially higher 
economics under a retained-loan scenario. Consequently, we believe investors will be 
focused not only on evidence that Apple volumes are ramping, but also on whether the 
program can ultimately support a path toward sustainable transaction margin 
expansion.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns
