Jeffrey Papai  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Investment Grade Credit Sales

IG credit (especially the non-TMT division) remains calm amidst the broader fixed income storm. We
have weathered a rate vol spike, start of a potential Fed hiking cycle and a continued drumbeat of
geopolitical headlines with only minor scratches to show for it. With seasonals turning broadly positive in
Q4 the current dip I think is one to buy for the short/medium term. There remain left tails lurking
(European sov debt contagion, midterms, any acceleration of Fed hikes), but the runway in credit
specifically is clear until the AI CAPEX debt creation machine turns back on in a couple months.
* Despite a series of headwinds that historically derail IG spreads and fund flows (start of hikes, rate vol,
Euro sov stress), IG credit has hung in remarkably well.
* The most notable recent development was the announcement of a wave of chip financing deals for 2027.
These alone project to be $150bn+ and will reshape IG portfolios and curve shapes in the coming year.
* The Q426 technical setup is constructive: The temporary dearth of AI issuance leading to de minimis net
supply with a positive seasonality.
* *My favorite long is bank 10yrs (GSUCIBNK) as they have their own positive local technical coming
shortly, and would continue to buy any dips we see in non-AI 30yrs (GSIG30NH).*
Consistently in the past few years dips in non-AI IG spreads have been buys with the index 6-10bps off
local/medium term tights. Combined with some headwinds potentially receding and the index yield at 6%
for just the 3rd time since the GFC I am temporarily constructive until we get closer to 2027 AI supply
(see here - also note SKYD inclusion in index at this month end will take index ~+1).

We have climbed a large wall of worry with remarkably little damage to show for it. The rate selloff
impact of total returns + rate vol spike + potential hiking cycle was supposed to slow the yield-based
demand that drove IG spreads to the tights over the past couple of years.
Instead, we have seen markedly little evidence of that. In the 6 weeks post Jackson Hole and hike(s)
became increasingly likely we saw net $12bn worth of inflows (65th percentile of 6 weeks inflows post
2020), including $5bn last week (well above the $3bn per week we had been averaging since 2026). While
mutual funds/ETFs only represent ~30% of IG holders it is among the least sticky and most prone to
outflows in periods of vol/stress, so the current resilience is notable.

And while it is a very small sample size (only 2 data points), spreads have absorbed this start of hiking
much better than in the past. In prior periods from 1mo before to 1mo after the first Fed rate hike in at
least 1yr (i.e. potential start of hiking cycles) we saw significant widening. In 2015/16 and 2022 index
level spreads went 27 and 15bps wider respectively, however so far from 8/15-10/15 we are only +1bp.
Source: Bloomberg as of October 11. Past performance is not indicative of future results

Further rate vol historically has caused widening/reduced fund flows. Despite the MOVE index hitting
>110, we did not make it more the 10bps from the local tights.
Source: Bloomberg as of October 11. Past performance is not indicative of future results
Now with rate vol starting to recede over the past week, that has historically led to a spread rally. When
the MOVE index starts a month >100 and declines at least 10%, spreads have rallied on average 10bps. If
rate vol remains elevated the move wider is only 3bps.

Investors want the yields offered by fixed income but are requiring less additional compensation than ever
to be in corporates over sovereigns.
French corps trading through OATS is the most obvious example (see Amanda Lynam's piece
"Completing our IG and HY Supply Outlook from 9/30 for a more in depth look at French Corporates),
with ~25% trading through.

But even in the US we are seeing a record low on both a % and nominal basis being charged to own
corporates over UST. The IG index’s spread as % of total yield dipped below 13% for the first time last
month. All this strikes me as a world of fixed income investors feeling increasingly comfortable in the
non-AI corporate picture, which should continue until more recession risk is added to the economy.

At least for now it appears that rate vol, total returns or Fed hikes will not materially widen spreads, which
implies the strength of underlying demand should not be ignored while the technicals are good (see
below).
*Chip Financing and Curves*
Away from SKYD the biggest headlines were related to chip financing. Just in the past week “APCHIP
2.0” began its syndicate process, FT reported on a chip deal for SPCX, and the WSJ reported on 2 more:
AVGO/OPENAI “Jalapeno” and ORCL/NVDA GPU deal. In aggregate that brings us to ~$150bn of
announced chip financing, not even accounting for the $20bn+ of “APCHIP 1.0” that still needs to fund
given delayed draw structure.
*Announced Chip Financing ~$150bn*
AVGO Anthropic TPUs $60bn (APCHIP 2.0)
AVGO OpenAI $30-50bn (Jalapeno)
SPCX Nvidia GPUs $40bn
ORCL Nvidia GPUs (no amt given)
AMZN Nvidia GPUs $8.5bn
Given this is chip financing it will generally be shorter duration, with the expectation of it being <7yr
maturity. For context, there was ~$100bn worth of 7yr and in AI related IG issuance YTD (excluding the
non-funded portion of APCHIP) representing ~10% of YTD IG issuance in =<7yr maturities.
Using what I view as relatively conservative assumptions (25% increase in 7yr and in AI related non-chip
supply in 2027 from hyperscaler CAPEX increase and shortening of duration of datacenter issuance with
rate backdrop) we get to an estimated $275bn of =<7yr AI issuance next year not including the
ORCL/NVDA GPU deal.
That would represent ~20-25% of total front-end issuance in 2027, a material uptick vs. prior years.

However, anecdotally (especially from insurance) we have seen more demand for AI front-end (ex.
QTSODY still above reoffer px still despite 5yr USTs selling off ~70bps since pricing) as the yields are
very attractive relative to generic IG and people feel more confident in the credit worthiness of frontier

technology companies in the short term (especially when one of the core arguments for lending is the

Additionally, the spread pickup from chip deals looks to be materially higher vs. generic IG front-end than
hyperscalers represent vs. generic long end. Using APCHIP spreads as the proxy the pickup vs. our 5yr

Over the course of 2027 this should cause credit curves to flatten. Short of a material outflow cycle or rate
rally I would not expect materially selling of existing IG front-end as investors are loathe to take losses on
such short duration (so the impact will take a long time to be fully felt), but I would imagine new issue
concession demanded for non-AI front-end will rise significantly as chip financing comes online.
*Seasonal Headwinds Become Tailwinds*
*1/ Temporary AI Supply Dearth*
As noted in prior note we are in a period of AI dearth that seems likely to continue for the next 1-2mo,
except for 1 expected deal. Q3/4 was supposed to be a heavy period for datacenters, but they have broadly
held off given rates. If we do not see a deal for the balance of October it will be the first 2mo stretch since
the CAPEX build started without a $3bn+ AI deal.
Unsurprisingly this has made the recent stretch the 2nd best for AI in the past year (behind the May no
supply window). The big selloffs (Oct, Feb, Jun-July) generally occur at the start of issuance cycles and as
such would enter 2027 short but still have a few months of relative strength/stability to get there.

The drag from major AI names (2-2.5bps on index level) on the index (rising due to widening and
increased % of index) should slow into the end of year before resuming on both fronts in 2027. See below
for chart of IG index spread ex AI names – IG index spreads.

*2/ Net Supply*
In our last note (link above) we discussed that net supply for Q4 is supposed to materially lessen. Since
then, our syndicate team has revised our supply estimates even lower given the rate backdrop, making
expected net supply (issuance – coupon – redemptions) to be ~$0 in Q4. Since 2014 quarters with
<$100bn of net supply have averaged a 2.5bp rally (vs. 1bp sell off for those of >$100bn), excluding 2020.

*4/ Bank Seasonals*
Given the amount of supply we have seen this year from banks to fund their portion of the AI CAPEX
build out they unsurprisingly have underperformed. It seems that the markets expectation for Q4 is
similarly very high leading to spread weakness into earnings. For the past year the peaks in our bank
basket (GSUCIBNK) spread relationship vs. the GS100 were on 10/15/25, 1/16/26, 4/15/26, 7/14/26 and
now 10/11/26 – all right at bank earnings. In 3 of the 4 prior cases we saw a material relative rally post
earnings.
