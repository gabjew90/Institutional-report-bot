The Trump diesel charade
Brent higher last week and today as fresh attacks by Iran and the Houthis shattered a sense of
temporary normality exports from the Persian Gulf. Brent crude traded in a range of $97.06 - 105.92/b
and closed the week at $104.72/b. The Brent Dec contract gained $2.47/b on the week. Fresh attacks by
Iran on ships in the Gulf and the SoH probably reduced exports of oil through the SoH last week. Exports
from the Persian Gulf had reached close to normal levels in the second half of September and into early
October. This has led to a large increase in oil on water globally which has helped to ease and reduce
November and December prices over the past month. The sense of some kind of normality of exports
from the SoH was shattered last week. Fresh attacks by the Houthis on Saudi Arabia added to this and
helped to lift prices. It is well known that the US and its allies in the Gulf are running low on defensive
rockets. Hard choices over what to defend will increasingly have to be made: Oil infrastructure, military
installations, royal palaces, civilian airports or other. The US will also have to decide how many defensive
rockets it can provide to its ME allies versus what it needs for its own defenses. Ukraine is at least not
getting any of these defensive rockets however much needed for defense against a continuous barrage of
Russian attacks on Ukraine. The front-month ICE Gasoil 10ppm (essentially ULSFO Diesel) is down 3.8%
this morning (due to Trump's diesel charade) to $1427/ton ($191/b) which is where it traded last
Thursday.
Trump's diesel charade. The site "535" now puts the probability for control of the US Congress and
Senate following the midterm elections at 99% and 61% respectively. Trump's war in the Middle East with
high or record high gasoline and diesel prices at the pump in the US is largely to blame. Trump's diesel deal
with Russia is clearly Trump trying to shift the blame from his own war in the ME to Ukraine instead. He
could of course have placed the blame on Putin and Russia for its war against Ukraine, but no, it is Ukraine
who gets the blame and not the least Zelenskyy. Physically it is of course correct that it is Ukraine who is
destroying Russian refineries to the point where 45% of Russian refineries are out of operation. The result
is that there is too little gasoline for domestic Russian consumption and no diesel, VGO and Jet fuel left
over for exports. Russia normally (pre-war 2022) exports some 1.3-1.4 mb/d of diesel products of which a
lot used to go to Europe.
Russia/Putin would happily export diesel products to the world, but it can't because Ukraine is
destroying its refineries. Russia implemented export restrictions of diesel already in July this year and the
plan was to extend that to early 2027.
The only way that Putin/Russia can start sustained diesel exports, is if Trump is able to discipline and
prevent Ukraine from attacking and destroying Russian refineries. Then Russian refineries can be
repaired, ramp up, produce more diesel and potentially export what ever is of surplus to the world. Trump
has threatened Ukraine that if it doesn't stop its attacks on Russian refineries, then there will be no more
indirect sales of weapons from the US via Europe to Ukraine and the intelligence which US currently is
providing to Ukraine will stop.
It is clear that if Russia stops its attacks on Ukraine, then Ukraine is prepared for a energy infrastructure
cease-fire.
