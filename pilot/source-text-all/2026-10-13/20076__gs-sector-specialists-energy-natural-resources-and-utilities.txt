Monday Thoughts – Macro & Micro
Natural Gas. Where are we on the state of the nat gas trade? For the last couple of months – it's evident
most of the focus has been on TTF vs Henry Hub – and for good reason (ME supply disruptions, small
albeit positive momentum on commercial contracts, freight, TTF back at highs). That said - the long
European natural gas trade is losing some steam with investors relative to other pockets of Energy – and
this likely due to (A) crowding – well known upside risks to TTF into the winter, (B) more talks around
Russia / Ukraine energy ceasefire and (C) weather forecasts (yes, it’s early) showing no real change from
norms. Aside from direct exposure to the commodity, infrastructure assets have been the most topical on
the desk – where we have seen a clear divergence in multiple re-rates over the last couple of months
between geographies (West vs East) and market beta (open capacity vs LT contract structures). On Henry
Hub – ‘wake me up when it’s October’ was the punchline of investor conversations since the summer –
and here we are – with front month at $3.15, storage data less than compelling (see below), and equities all
broadly trading towards the lower end of ranges. Crude has been stubbornly high at $106/b – reflecting
more focus on shipping / freight (great note from Michele on this here) – so with a good amount of risk
premium + freight already reflected in the price – bulls making the argument that downside risks to crude
should be net positive US nat gas for a catch up trade.

DVN. The #1 inbound on the desk over the last week and for good reason: Devon Energy Announces
Agreement to Exit the Eagle Ford for $4.2 Billion (). With the market having time to digest the proposed
asset sale – running through some of the feedback below. The stock had generally outperformed oily E&P
peers over the last few weeks ahead of the asset sale – and post conference season – investor feedback was
generally more constructive on the setup into EPS and YE. Since announcing the sale – the stock has
lagged US peers – here’s what folks are saying + asking. (1) More questions post adjustments and tax
leakage - highly debated. (2) Now that we have the first proposed asset sale announced – what’s the
timeline for next potential steps? (3) DVN calling out ‘after-tax proceeds will be used to accelerate share
repurchases and to strengthen the balance sheet through debt reduction’ – how should we think about the
split between the two? Bulls continue to point out the stock trades at a discount relative to peers (GIR has
the stock at 15% FCF yield on 2027 vs EOG at 10% and OXY at 13%) – with potential to close the
valuation gap over time. Feedback welcome.

Trading Desk Thoughts. Tom Evans out with a note this weekend with thoughts from the GS Commods
Trading desk () – Refined Products section worth taking a look at…
REFINED PRODUCTS - distillate cracks remain extremely volatile, with the front trading in a $15/bbl
range over the past week, given rapid developments in Middle East, Russia/Ukraine and global policy
responses to high prices. The scale of the premium keeps the global refining system firmly in distillate
maximisation mode. Trade? We like Summer 27 US gasoline vs. heating oil for four reasons:
| 1. | Yield switch. Distillate maximisation comes at the expense of Gasoline yields. Refiners are being paid
to make as much diesel as possible, which keeps gasoline-making units underused and high-octane
blending components scarce. US finished gasoline yields are down 1.9% YoY vs. distillate up 0.4% YoY. |
| 2. | Octane remains tight. The yield switch is tightening octane availability in its own right, as the units
that produce high-octane blendstock are run at lower rates. Two additional dynamics are worth flagging:
(a) fewer blending components are arriving from Asia — Indian alkylate exports dropped off in September
after New Delhi instructed refiners to prioritise domestic LPG; and (b) reformer feedstock is being pulled
elsewhere, with heavy naphtha, the raw material reformers run on, drawn to Venezuela to support its crude
production. Blendstocks have rallied particularly hard since mid-September. | | 3. | Policy focus is clearly
on diesel at the moment. The US export-ban threat pushed the G7 into announcing strategic stock releases
for both crude and diesel. While there has been some disappointment around the timeline and the
additionality of the release relative to the initial announcement, it should deliver gradual relief to diesel,
and the messaging leaves little doubt about where the policy focus sits. A US diesel export ban looks off
the table for now, but were it to return, it would create further upside for RBOB/Heat. Also worth noting
that Europe's gasoline SPR reserves are roughly four times smaller than its diesel reserves, reducing the
available policy levers were this to become a focus. | | 4. | Pricing is already very stretched to the
downside, which we think offers better risk-reward in the current geopolitical context - one in which
cracks and spreads are very difficult to deploy any size in due to headline volatility. |
