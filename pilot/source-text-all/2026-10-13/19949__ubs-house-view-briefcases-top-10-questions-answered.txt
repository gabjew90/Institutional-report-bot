Where are the opportunities in fixed income?
Key message
With global government bond yields at multi-decade highs, we believe 
investors should revisit fixed income exposure to find the right balance 
between yield, potential capital gains, and diversification to match their goals 
and constraints. We like quality bonds—including potential opportunities in 
longer-dated bonds—and diversified fixed income exposure.
House view
01
Major central banks have taken a hawkish turn, 
prompting another step up in global bond yields.
•
The European Central Bank and the Federal Reserve both raised rates by 25 basis 
points in September. Together with the Bank of England, policymakers indicated 
greater willingness to tighten before inflation pressures become entrenched.
•
Ongoing inflation worries, persistent fiscal concerns, and rising issuance of 
hyperscaler debt have all kept global government bonds under pressure.
02
In quality bonds, there may be opportunities to add 
interest rate risk selectively.
•
The recent rise in yields has created tactical opportunities to add some duration 
in high-quality bonds. Medium- to long-duration high-quality bonds have scope 
for price gains as we believe current market pricing of central bank tightening is 
too aggressive. These bonds can also provide valuable diversification.
•
We are more cautious on the longest-dated bonds, given fiscal sustainability 
concerns, growing AI-related debt issuance, and the potential for term 
premiums to remain elevated.
03
And we see opportunities to diversify, especially in 
emerging markets.
•
We like select exposure to more growth-sensitive and higher-yielding bond 
market segments such as emerging market credit and high yield debt.
•
Emerging market credit offers attractive income, diversification benefits, and 
increasingly resilient fundamentals.
•
In low-rate economies like Switzerland, we also see merit in equity income 
approaches and select structured strategies.
New this week
Auctions of both the 10-year and 30-year US Treasury notes 
last week were stronger than expected, helping to alleviate 
some of the upward pressure on Treasury yields.
One liner
Combining high-quality bonds with selective emerging 
market exposure can broaden sources of portfolio return.
Did you know?
•
Current elevated outright yields offer a carry cushion 
against potential further volatility that was not available 
in 2022.
•
EM sovereign and corporate bond yields stand at 
around 7.2% and 7%, respectively (JPMorgan EMBI and 
CEMBI indices). These yield levels should underpin high-
single-digit returns over the next 12 months, well above 
cash, in our view.
Investment view
We view fixed income as Attractive, with opportunities across 
regions and market segments. Emerging market bonds offer 
appealing yields and diversified return potential. In high-
quality bonds, investors can consider adding exposure in the 
medium- to long-term maturity range. Stronger investment 
grade issuers offer attractive carry across medium maturities, 
in our view, while higher-risk credit should remain relatively 
short-dated. We are more cautious on the longest maturities 
given fiscal concerns and rising AI-related issuance. Investors 
should calibrate credit and duration risks to their objectives
—for example, more income-focused investors may prefer 
shorter maturities to reduce duration risk.

How can I position for equity upside?
Key message
The S&P 500 reached a fresh record high last week, but the rally was 
concentrated in a narrow group of large technology companies. We expect 
positive returns across the US and most global markets, and recommend 
investors maintain diversified core equity exposure as they position for further 
upside.
House view
01
We believe AI remains a powerful structural investment 
opportunity.
•
Demand for compute remains robust and continues to exceed available supply.
•
Constraints in semiconductors, power, and related infrastructure are unlikely to 
ease quickly. We believe global AI-related capex is likely to rise to around USD 
1.2tr in 2027.
•
We expect cloud revenue growth—an indicator of how companies are 
monetizing AI—to continue to accelerate in the coming quarter.
02
But the concentration of market gains reinforces the 
importance of managing risk through diversification.
•
Holding a narrowly concentrated equity portfolio increases exposure to 
company-specific setbacks.
•
A robust US economy, a solid labor market, strong credit creation, and 
continued fiscal support should all support earnings outside the AI complex, 
helping the rally to broaden further.
•
Robust earnings growth outside the US also broadens the opportunity set for 
global investors.
03
We believe investors should position for equity upside 
by maintaining diversified core equity exposure.
•
Investors should ensure their core equity allocation is broadly diversified. Our 
preferred US sectors are consumer discretionary, financials, health care, industrials, 
and utilities.
•
We remain positive on Eurozone equities and favor banks, consumer discretionary, 
health care, industrials, IT, and Germany.
•
In Asia, we forecast 72% earnings growth for MSCI Asia ex-Japan this year, 
supported by the AI hardware supply chain and a recovery in cyclical sectors.
•
We think Japanese equities should also benefit from AI-related capital spending, 
cyclical improvement, and continuing corporate reforms.
New this week
TSMC reported a 51% rise in quarterly revenue, after 
Samsung flagged an almost nine-fold jump in quarterly 
operating profit. AI server assembler Hon Hai Precision 
Industry also beat its quarterly sales estimates.
One liner
Becoming dependent on a few individual stocks, particularly 
in the fast-moving tech sector, increases portfolio risks—so 
investors should diversify.
Did you know?
•
Excluding strategic holdings, almost 40% of equity 
investors on our platform with self-managed 
portfolios have more than half of their equity assets 
concentrated in just 10 stocks or fewer.
•
Investors with concentrated positions in megacap tech 
can consider using rallies to rebalance into structured 
investments that offer a degree of capital preservation.
•
In Europe, we forecast 15% earnings growth in 
both 2026 and 2027, supported by a recovery in 
manufacturing, structural investment, and operating 
leverage as revenues improve.
Investment view
We expect positive returns across the US and most global 
markets, supported by strong earnings growth. Investors 
should maintain diversified core equity exposure, reduce 
concentrated single-stock positions, and consider capital 
preservation strategies where drawdown tolerance is limited.

Should investors pay more attention to cash?
Key message
With investment opportunities abundant, inflation still elevated, and the Fed 
tightening rather than easing, investors should manage their cash holdings 
strategically. First, they should optimize returns on necessary liquidity. They 
should then establish a plan to deploy excess cash into markets, retaining 
sufficient liquidity for expected withdrawals and near-term commitments. 
Phasing strategies can help put capital to work while reducing the risk of 
mistiming markets.
House view
01
Investors should begin by actively planning how much 
cash they really need.
•
CIO recommends that investors maintain enough cash to cover 2-5 years of net 
expected portfolio withdrawals.
•
If an investor holds more than this, their excess cash may not be working 
efficiently.
•
A thoughtful cash management strategy involves segmenting liquidity into three 
categories: everyday cash, core liquidity, and investment cash. It is crucial to 
optimize diversification, liquidity, and returns within each.
02
Focusing on high fixed rates may overlook risks.
•
The Federal Reserve raised rates in September. We believe it will hike again in 
December, then hold, whereas markets expect around three hikes in the next 12 
months. A wider range of outcomes speaks to greater flexibility.
•
Investors should consider a more deliberate mix of short-term liquidity, fixed-
term deposits, and high-quality bonds across maturities for income and 
diversification.
03
Investing surplus cash systematically can overcome 
emotional pitfalls.
•
Investors may currently be loath to invest near all-time highs or fearful of a 
substantial correction. But history suggests market timing seldom pays.
•
Making a plan to phase into core portfolios when stocks decline by a certain 
threshold—or delegating the management to professionals—may help 
overcome bias and help investors focus on the constructive growth, earnings, 
and Fed rate outlook.
New this week
The 50-day moving average of the VIX index of implied 
volatility in US equities currently stands at its lowest level 
in 25 months despite ongoing concerns associated with 
geopolitics and debt sustainability. That is, equity markets 
remain confident and resilient.
One liner
In an uncertain world, and yet one where we see compelling 
investment opportunities across asset classes, we believe 
treating cash as a residual may be a mistake.
Did you know?
•
History shows that phasing excess cash into diversified 
portfolios can deliver more robust long-term outcomes. 
Over 10- and 20-year holding periods, US stocks have 
beaten cash in 97% and 100% of cases, respectively, 
between 1945 and July of this year, based on our 
analysis.
•
CIO analysis of S&P 500 price data since 1954 finds that, 
12 months following the first Fed rate hike of a cycle, 
the S&P 500 has tended to rise about 10%.
Investment view
We believe investors should optimize returns on necessary 
cash, using a variety of tools to balance returns against 
managing interest rate, credit, FX, and market risk over a 5-
year horizon. Phasing excess capital into diversified portfolios 
of stocks, bonds, commodities, and alternatives can maximize 
chances of achieving financial goals, seize current tactical 
opportunities in stocks, and reduce timing risk.

Have European sovereign yields peaked?
Key message
European government bonds have not been immune to fresh oil price and 
inflation concerns stemming from the Middle East conflict. But the region's 
debt has also experienced wider spreads and higher risk premia on fiscal 
concerns in France. We cannot rule out higher French yields amid budget 
negotiations and likely bond market pressure that forces all parties to engage 
in fiscal consolidation. But for Europe as a whole, we do expect yields to fall 
going into next year as excessive pricing of European Central Bank rate cuts 
likely moves closer to our view that inflation pressures will abate more swiftly. 
We therefore find selective opportunities in European fixed income, equities, 
and the euro.
House view
01
European government bond yields have climbed on oil 
prices and French fiscal worries.
•
Eurozone government bond yields have climbed as worries about a widening in 
the Middle East conflict have pushed up oil prices and fed inflation fears.
•
Concerns about the French budget and the nation's fiscal sustainability have 
pushed up spreads between French and Italian bonds versus Germany's toward 
multi-year highs.
02
While we cannot rule out higher yields, we still expect 
yields to fall by year end.
•
While Eurozone headline inflation rose more than expected in September, core 
inflation increased only modestly, to 2.5% from 2.4% and there is scant evidence 
that these costs are becoming embedded in wages.
•
Beyond a healthy macroeconomic backdrop for the Eurozone, the region’s 
institutional framework provides tools to limit disorderly fragmentation.
03
So, investors should diversify across both liquidity and 
fixed income tools.
•
We remain Attractive on fixed income and see the rise in European yields as 
creating selective opportunities in high-quality bonds.
•
We continue to prefer short- to medium-term maturities and stronger investment 
grade issuers, while remaining cautious on the longest maturities and keeping 
higher-risk credit relatively short-dated.
•
Investors should manage liquidity actively across both fixed income and tools 
including fixed-term deposits, diversified fixed income approaches, and select 
structured strategies.
New this week
Eurozone finance ministers and the European Central Bank 
(ECB) called on France on Thursday to pass its 2027 budget 
and restore confidence in its public finances, based on 
reporting from Reuters. "It is essential that all member states, 
and in particular those with high deficits or debts, implement 
prudent fiscal policies," EU Economic Commissioner 
Dombrovskis, who took part in the ministerial talks, told a 
news conference afterward. French 10-year government bond 
yields have risen by nearly 80 basis points since the start of 
September and now trade at their highest level since July 
2002.
One liner
While Eurozone sovereign yields could climb further, we 
expect the region's borrowing costs to fall by year end.
Did you know?
•
France faces a structural deficit, a rising debt burden, 
and a growing interest bill. Debt-service costs are 
projected to increase from around 4.3% of government 
revenue currently to more than 6% by 2030, making 
fiscal consolidation progressively harder.
•
Italy’s prospective borrowing path also deserves 
attention, with the government expecting the deficit to 
rise to 3.4% of GDP in 2027 after falling to 2.9% in 
2026.
Investment view
We remain Attractive on fixed income and equities within 
Europe. In bonds, we favor short- to medium-term maturities, 
including select agency, covered, and corporate bonds, as 
well as stronger investment grade issuers across medium 
maturities. Within European equities, we favor information 
technology, industrials, banks, Germany, and health care, 
alongside our “European leaders” and “Swiss mid-caps in 
focus” themes. We also see opportunities in the euro against 
the US dollar and Swiss franc.

What does the US-Iran conflict mean for markets?
Key message
Middle Eastern energy supplies have improved from their earlier lows, and 
we do not expect the conflict to derail the equity rally. But renewed attacks, 
depleted inventories, and stalled US-Iran negotiations leave the recovery 
vulnerable and have prompted us to raise our year-end Brent forecast by 
USD 5 to USD 100 a barrel. We remain constructive on global equities, while 
broad commodity exposure can help protect portfolios against geopolitical 
disruption and inflation risks.
House view
01
Middle Eastern energy flows have improved from their 
earlier lows.
•
Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz picked up notably in September, while Saudi 
Arabia restored flows through its East-West pipeline to at least 80% of maximum 
capacity.
•
The release of 100 million barrels from G7 emergency reserves has provided 
additional near-term relief, although rebuilding depleted inventories will take time.
•
A proposed halt to Russian and Ukrainian attacks on energy infrastructure has 
the potential to ease some pressure on global energy supplies. But details of the 
arrangement remain limited.
02
But continuing attacks and stalled diplomacy leave the 
recovery vulnerable.
•
Iran described the US response to its seven conditions for reopening the Strait 
of Hormuz as insufficient and said negotiations would not proceed until its 
conditions were met.
•
Further attacks on tankers around the Strait threaten the recovery in Middle 
Eastern crude exports seen in September.
•
A deadly Houthi strike on Riyadh airport, followed by another attack the next 
day, underscored the continuing risk of wider regional disruption.
03
So, investors should remain positioned for equity gains 
while maintaining protection against geopolitical risks.
•
We remain constructive on global equities, supported by resilient growth, strong 
earnings, and sustained AI investment.
•
Broad commodity exposure can help diversify portfolios when energy disruption 
or inflation weighs on equity and bond returns.
•
Investors should consider exposure across energy, precious metals, industrial 
metals, and agriculture rather than relying on one commodity or geopolitical 
outcome.
New this week
Iran described the US response to its proposal for reopening 
the Strait of Hormuz as insufficient, while renewed attacks 
highlighted the vulnerability of the recovery in regional energy 
flows.
One liner
Improving energy flows and resilient fundamentals support 
our constructive equity outlook, while continuing geopolitical 
risks strengthen the case for broad commodity exposure.
Did you know?
•
Global visible oil inventories fell by 95 million barrels 
in August and by 507 million barrels from February 
through August, according to the International Energy 
Agency. The spread between the first and sixth Brent 
futures contracts widened to more than USD 15 a 
barrel, another indication of tight physical supply.
•
Saudi Aramco said the conflict had removed nearly 
3 billion barrels of oil supply, while around 1 billion 
barrels had been drawn from stocks. Its chief executive 
said replenishing inventories while meeting demand 
could take up to two years, even after the Strait of 
Hormuz fully reopens.
•
Before the conflict, the Strait of Hormuz carried 
around one-fifth of the world’s oil and liquefied 
natural gas, illustrating why threats to the waterway 
can affect energy prices far beyond the region.
Investment view
We remain constructive on global equities, supported by 
resilient economic growth, strong earnings, and sustained AI 
investment. Investors should remain diversified across regions, 
sectors, and return drivers. We also favor broad commodity 
exposure to help protect portfolios against geopolitical 
disruption and inflation risks.

How will Fed rate hikes impact markets?
Key message
Strong US economic data and higher energy costs pushed Treasury yields to 
multi-decade highs and raised concerns about an extended tightening cycle 
from the Federal Reserve. We expect one further rate increase in December 
and believe resilient economic growth and robust corporate earnings should 
help equities withstand moderate policy tightening. Higher starting yields are 
also creating opportunities to add portfolio income.
House view
01
Strong growth and renewed inflation concerns have 
pushed Treasury yields higher.
•
The FOMC raised the federal funds target rate by 25 basis points in September.
•
High energy prices and strong economic data have added to inflation concerns.
•
The 10-year US Treasury yield rose to 5.34% in early October, its highest intraday 
level since 2002.
02
But we believe equity markets can withstand a 
measured tightening cycle.
•
We expect one 25-basis-point rate hike from the Fed in December. Markets are 
pricing roughly three hikes over the next 12 months.
•
We expect underlying inflation data to continue to moderate. The three-month 
annualized rate of core personal consumption expenditures (PCE) inflation fell to 
2.05% in August, its lowest level since July 2024.
•
The September labor report showed signs of cooling, reducing the pressure 
for an imminent rate hike. Nonfarm payrolls rose by 29,000, below consensus 
expectations for a 90,000 increase.
03
So, resilient earnings and higher starting yields 
continue to offer investment opportunities.
•
We forecast S&P 500 earnings growth of 25% in 2026 and 14% in 2027, which 
should help equities absorb moderately higher yields while economic growth 
remains firm.
•
We recommend positioning for further equity gains while diversifying across 
regions, sectors, and return drivers and managing concentrated single-stock 
exposure.
•
We rate fixed income as Attractive and see opportunities to add portfolio income 
through medium- to long-duration high-quality bonds and medium-maturity 
credit from stronger investment grade issuers.
New this week
Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he is "not greatly 
concerned" that higher interest rates could cause "a 
damaging slowdown in the economy." He added that he is 
more concerned about the risk that inflation expectations 
among consumers, investors, and businesses could become 
de-anchored.
One liner
We expect the Fed to raise interest rates again in December, 
but resilient growth and robust corporate earnings should 
help equities withstand a moderate hiking cycle.
Did you know?
•
US factory activity has remained in expansion 
territory for eight consecutive months. Historically, US 
manufacturing expansions have lasted nearly three 
years on average.
•
US nominal GDP was growing 6.6% year over year at 
the end of the second quarter, comprising around 2.1% 
real growth and a 4.4% increase in the broad price level. 
This can help companies grow revenues and profits in 
current-dollar terms.
•
Past US economic downturns generally did not begin 
until at least two years after the Fed started raising 
rates, although the timing varied with the scale of the 
tightening cycle.
Investment view
We expect one further 25-basis-point Fed increase in 
December and do not believe a measured tightening cycle 
will derail the US economy or equity rally. Investors should 
maintain diversified equity exposure and use the rise in yields 
to add selectively to quality bonds, while remaining cautious 
on the longest maturities and lower-quality credit.

How can I invest in transformational innovation?
Key message
We believe investors can complement diversified equity portfolios with 
targeted exposure to AI, Power and resources, and Longevity. AI remains a 
powerful driver of earnings growth and investment, while electrification and 
rising data-center demand should continue to support Power and resources. 
Clinical innovation, meanwhile, underpins long-term growth potential in 
Longevity.
House view
01
We have strong conviction in the long-term potential 
gains offered by AI.
•
We estimate that overall AI-related capex will rise to USD 1.2tr in 2027, with 
agentic AI the next growth leg.
•
The heightened volatility in semiconductor shares this year has underscored the 
risk of single-stock concentration.
•
We favor diversified and balanced exposure across the AI value chain—from select 
semiconductors and hardware to megacap tech and more defensive areas of the 
industry.
02
Structural demand and the energy transition support 
long-term investment in power and resources.
•
Opportunities to invest in the power and resources value chain span grid resilience, 
renewables, nuclear, industrial automation, and critical minerals.
•
Recent earnings point to sustained demand for electrification, grid modernization, 
data center infrastructure, automation, and clean-power capacity, with strong 
order trends and guidance supporting the outlook for equipment and 
infrastructure providers.
•
Exposure to copper and aluminum is backed by structural demand drivers, 
while utilities offer visible earnings growth, dividend support, and a constructive 
medium-term outlook.
03
Companies offering products and services to extend 
healthy lifespans should continue to gain traction.
•
We are constructive on the long-term outlook for obesity treatments as greater 
supply and lower pricing are enabling broader patient access.
•
Multiple late-stage trial results in cancer, cardio-metabolic conditions, and 
neurology in the coming months could provide further catalysts.
•
By 2030, we estimate annual revenues in the global longevity market could reach 
USD 8tr, up from USD 5.3tr in 2023.
New this week
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 4.3% last week, 
snapping a five-week rising streak, as elevated Treasury yields 
and concerns that investor expectations for frontier-model 
revenue run rates may have become too optimistic weighed 
on sentiment.
One liner
We favor a diversified approach to investing in 
transformational innovation, enabling investors to stay at the 
forefront of change, seek long-term growth, and manage 
specific risks.
Did you know?
•
With hyperscalers raising their 2026 capital expenditure 
estimates and projecting further increases in 2027, we 
now expect overall AI spending to hit USD 900bn this 
year and USD 1.2tr in 2027.
•
Data centers could account for up to 9% of total US 
electricity use by 2035 (versus around 4% currently), 
based on Bloomberg New Energy Finance forecasts.
•
For global obesity drugs (GLP-1s), we forecast 12% 
revenue growth per year on average through 2030, 
driven by rapid adoption, potential clinical benefits 
stretching beyond weight-loss treatment, expanding 
insurance coverage, and potential launches of oral 
GLP-1s.
Investment view
We believe that exposure to structural trends will remain a 
key differentiator for equity market performance over the 
long run. We see transformational innovation opportunities in 
Artificial intelligence, Power and resources, and Longevity.

What role can gold play in portfolios?
Key message
Elevated real yields and a firm US dollar have pushed gold close to its lowest 
level in two months. The precious metal is now around 25% down from its 
record high earlier this year. But central bank demand, reserve diversification, 
elevated government debt, and geopolitical uncertainty continue to support 
its strategic portfolio role. We remain constructive over the next 12 months 
and view price weakness as an opportunity to add exposure.
House view
01
Higher real yields and a firm US dollar have weighed 
on gold.
•
Gold lost around 7% in September as rising government bond yields increased 
the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset.
•
Elevated US yields and broad US dollar strength could continue to create near-
term volatility for precious metals.
•
Expectations for an October Federal Reserve rate increase have receded, 
although investors still expect a 25-basis-point increase in December.
02
But central bank buying, reserve diversification, and 
fiscal concerns continue to support gold.
•
Central bank demand remains robust, supported by efforts to reduce 
concentrated exposure to US dollar assets.
•
Elevated government debt, fiscal sustainability concerns, and questions about 
currency purchasing power continue to support demand for real assets.
•
Gold is not another institution’s liability, reinforcing its appeal as a store of value 
and an alternative reserve asset during periods of financial or geopolitical stress.
03
So, we continue to view gold as a strategic portfolio 
hedge and diversifier.
•
We forecast gold to rise toward USD 5,400 an ounce by September 2027, 
supported by central bank buying, reserve diversification, elevated government 
debt, and geopolitical uncertainty.
•
Pullbacks toward USD 4,000 an ounce can be seen as opportunities for investors 
to add exposure.
•
Investors with an affinity for gold can consider maintaining a mid-single-
digit allocation, while those with substantial holdings can diversify into other 
commodity sectors.
New this week
Gold reached a one-week high of around USD 4,194 an 
ounce on 9 October as US Treasury yields retreated from 
recent peaks, recovering from a two-month low earlier in the 
week.
One liner
Near-term rate and currency headwinds may keep gold 
volatile. But structural demand supports its role as a portfolio 
hedge and creates opportunities to add exposure during 
periods of weakness.
Did you know?
•
Gold is trading at around USD 4,172 an ounce, down 
3.3% since the start of the year and around 23% 
below its January peak, when it was up nearly 25% 
for the year.
•
Central banks have accumulated an average of around 
1,000 metric tons of gold a year over the past four 
years, around twice the average of the preceding 
decade.
•
The People’s Bank of China bought around 20 metric 
tons of gold in August, its largest monthly addition 
since October 2023 and its 22nd consecutive month 
of purchases.
Investment view
We remain constructive on gold over the next 12 months 
despite the near-term headwinds from elevated real 
yields and a firm US dollar. Central bank demand, reserve 
diversification, elevated government debt, and geopolitical 
uncertainty continue to support its strategic portfolio role. 
Investors with an affinity for gold can consider using price 
weakness to build or maintain a mid-single-digit allocation.

How to diversify with alternatives?
Key message
As oil prices swing and government bond yields trade near multi-year 
highs, we believe alternative investments have a role to play in long-term 
portfolios as both a source of differentiated returns and as potential portfolio 
diversifiers. However, investors should be willing and able to bear the unique 
risks of alternative investments, including illiquidity, strategy complexity, and 
potential lockup periods on invested capital.
House view
01
Hedge funds can be a fresh return source and portfolio 
stabilizer.
•
We continue to favor discretionary macro for its ability to navigate policy 
divergence and to diversify portfolios in periods where higher oil and refined 
product prices pressure stocks and bonds at the same time.
•
We also prefer lower net and equity market neutral strategies, alongside multi-
strategy funds. High dispersion and low correlations between stocks support alpha 
generation while diversified return sources and risk management can navigate 
market rotations.
02
Private markets still offer opportunities for 
diversification, return generation, and income.
•
In private equity, we favor value-oriented strategies, diversification into Europe 
and Asia (e.g., Japan), and secondaries, with technology, health care, energy, and 
defense offering structural growth.
•
In private debt, we continue to see merits for the asset class in a diversified 
long-term-oriented portfolio, but in the near term, the risk-return outlook has 
diminished, underscoring the need for greater selectivity and caution.
•
In real estate and infrastructure, we favor existing assets with visible income, 
resilient cash flows, and disciplined interest-rate risk management, particularly 
those benefiting from deglobalization, the energy transition, and digitalization.
03
We see numerous paths to invest in alternatives, 
subject to careful risk management.
•
Investors should carefully weigh potential investments against their needs for 
liquidity, especially in an environment where exits may be delayed or restructured 
amid uncertainty.
New this week
A 5 October article from PitchBook reported a gradual 
improvement in the exit market for US private companies. 
The number of PE-backed M&A deals to close in the third 
quarter was up nearly 6% quarter over quarter and nearly 
8% year over year. 2026 is on course to be the third-best for 
PE-backed deal volume and exit activity on record.
One liner
Including an allocation to alternatives can improve 
diversification, growth potential, and portfolio resilience 
through the market cycle.
Did you know?
In the current climate, we believe the investment 
backdrop for infrastructure is solid. Investor appetite is 
strong: PitchBook data show that trailing 12-month net 
flows into open-ended infrastructure funds increased 
30% year over year through the first quarter of 2026. 
Strong conviction in long-term secular themes, combined 
with solid year-to-date returns from listed infrastructure 
equities, supports continued optimism.
Investment view
Incorporating alternatives into a diversified portfolio may 
enhance returns, reduce volatility, and generate income 
through the market cycle. Alternatives such as hedge funds 
and private markets can provide unique return streams that 
complement traditional assets. However, investors should 
be willing and able to bear the unique risks of alternative 
investments, including illiquidity, strategy complexity, and 
potential lockup periods on invested capital.

What's next for commodities?
Key message
While shifting conditions in the Middle East continue to impact oil and refined 
product markets, we believe the investment case for broad commodity 
exposure goes well beyond energy, offering both return potential and 
inflation-hedging benefits. Industrial metals should stay supported by 
investment in AI and electrification. Agricultural commodities have potential
