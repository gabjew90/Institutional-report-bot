• Q3 reporting season is about to start. Given the increased volatility in the past 
weeks on a number of fronts, a lot is at stake. We find that investors in general 
have a constructive view on earnings, but are concerned that actual results might 
underwhelm. We believe that earnings will be a support for the market.
• Consensus expectations might appear elevated at face value, but we do not 
believe that to be the case. For S&P500, consensus Q3 EPS growth projection 
is at 24% according to IBES. Factset & Thompson Reuters are at 30%, but that 
is due to Meta's one-off tax charge adjustment. Ex-Nvidia and Micron, the 
hurdle rate falls to 15%, from 24%. If we look at median S&P500 stock, the yoy 
Q3 EPS growth forecast by consensus drops further, to 8%.
• European consensus EPS growth projection stands at 19%. Ex Energy, this 
comes down to 11%, and median is at 9%. Looking at revenue growth, the 
median for SXXP is at 7% for Q3, with S&P500 also at 7%.
• Importantly, global PMIs have accelerated during the quarter to multi-year 
highs, and are projecting a strong EPS delivery - top chart. Our lead 
indicator of earnings is staying on an uptrend, not showing an inflection yet 
- middle chart. It typically has a 2 quarters lead-lag, suggesting the next 2 
reporting seasons at least should be positive.
• AI continues to do the heavy lifting for the US. At the same time, earnings 
strength is broadening. The proportion of global equity market constituents 
that are showing accelerating earnings has climbed to near record highs. This 
should improve the odds of the upcycle being more sustainable. For Europe, 
margin expansion is doing the heavy lifting. This year European EBIT/Sales 
are projected to be at 14.4%, 250bp higher than pre-Covid, in 2019. All the 
sectors are stepping up, apart from Staples, Autos and Software.
• Weakening Euro will turn into a support for Exporters. Energy is likely to 
surprise on the upside, but Miners pose a risk, and broader China exposure could 
stay soft, as well as consumer exposure, with companies likely to highlight the 
headwind of rising energy costs. Semis are poised to deliver strong results, along 
with Industrials. Banks have derated 10%+ in the past weeks, but are on track 
to see reassuring earnings. If the reporting season coincides with a peaking in 
bond yields risk, together with stabilisation in credit spreads, then Banks should 
be set to recover. Utilities results are also likely to be positive.
• French exposure remains hostage to bond yields direction. Having said that, 
French plays have derated 15%+, in contrast to earnings which show 
resiliency, given that activity indicators such as PMIs held up so far, and CAC40 
topline is 75% non-French - bottom chart.
• Overall, we have been arguing that when the Q3 results season starts, the 
market focus will shift onto still supportive fundamentals, and move on from 
September’s volatility. Also, in our October Chartbook, out last week, we 
suggested that bond yields spike is approaching oversold territory. These 
two developments, if they gain traction, would allow equities to advance into 
year end, with likely risk-on internals. We have advised to re-enter Tech sector 
at end July, and are in particular positive on European Semis, in addition to likely 
upside in Banks, Chemicals, and Industrials.

Equity Strategy Key Calls and Drivers
Equities got hurt by the spike in bond yields, but we do not think this will persist. First, historically, the start of Fed tightening was a confirmation that the economy was strong, rather than a signal to sell. 
We expect continued robust earnings delivery, with Q3 results starting next week, EPS revisions remain in positive territory in most places. This is aided by a rebound in PMIs, even in challenged areas, 
such as Eurozone. Our lead indicator of earnings is staying on an uptrend. The spike in bond yields, the key headwind for stocks, might be getting stretched. Bonds are close to oversold territory, and we 
note Fed funds futures, which made a huge, almost 200bp, move up ytd, are not increasing anymore. Crucially, we do not see inflation expectations becoming de-anchored, the differences to 2022 
template remain big, in terms of Tech outlook and the inflation/wages/labour backdrop. Beneath the surface, we favour Tech sector. Also, in the past 3 months, during the spike in yields, market internals 
remained largely risk-on, with Cyclicals top in Europe, and all Defensives behind. Market recovery will likely see Cyclicals favoured further. French political risk will likely not go away. Having said that, 
CAC40 has already done poorly, spreads are near record wide, and CAC40 P/E relative is at record lows – a lot is in the price.
