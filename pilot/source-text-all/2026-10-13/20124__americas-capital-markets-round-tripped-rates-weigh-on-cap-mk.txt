Rising risks from higher rates on capital markets activity and re-emergence of 
terminal value debates (Agentic cash sorting/ Advisory fees for Wealth Mgrs, 
On-chain trading for Exchanges) drove material reversal in stock performance 
half-way through 3Q, erasing earlier gains and leaving Alts, Wealth Brokers, and 
Exchanges at 20-30% P/E discounts relative to history (with Alt multiples historical 
trough levels). Among the three sub-sectors, we see Exchanges/Market Structure 
stocks as most out of sync with fundamentals - solid volumes in 3Q (outside slower 
retail), easy comps into 4Q and long-term tokenization / other structural benefits not 
contemplated in current multiples (ICE and TW top picks here). For Alts, we see the 
rates-driven reversal (20% swing in stocks in 2H of the qtr) meaningfully 
overshooting the low-to-mid single digits EPS risks in 2027 from slower activity 
(though with wider dispersion among stocks - BX and CG having potentially more 
Carry/PRE risks vs consensus). That said, we don’t expect much impact to FRE and 
still see 15% avg mgmt fee CAGR across the group through 2028. Wealth firms 
should benefit from higher rates, though most will get offset by a continued drag on 
cash balances. Traditional Managers and Trust Banks seem least controversial - most 
Trad Mgrs continue to face steady to improving organic growth (outside of MF Active 
Equities), margin expansion and capital returns; Trust Banks should benefit from 
incremental NII tailwinds further boosting operating leverage prospects. Top ideas 
into 3Q include - TPG, STEP, APO among Alts ; BLK and BEN in Trad Mgrs, TW and 
ICE in Exchanges, BNY/STT in Trusts, and LPLA in Wealth.

Sector Views and Key Stock Ideas into 3Q26 EPS 
 
 
Alternative Asset Managers 
Sector View: Investor sentiment on the Alt Managers turned sharply negative, with 
n
the group reversing all of its post 2Q gains - down ~6% in 3Q and ~31% YTD against 
an S&P 500 up 13% and 7%, respectively. We see a sharp rise in yields as the primary 
driver of this dynamic, with the 10yr yields negatively correlated with the group’s P/E 
multiples. This reversal, however, has once again overshot the actual earnings risks 
from higher rates, in our view. Specifically, the group now trades at an average 17X 
NTM P/E (net of SBC) - a historical trough, running 33%/20% below 3/5-year 
averages. Moreover, on our updated estimates, the group screens at an 18% 
discount to the 1Q avg when Private Credit concerns were front and center. 
Near-term, higher rates (and more importantly a sharp change in direction, which 
could also reverse just as quickly) will result in (1) slower realization and deployment 
activity in PE and RE, driving renewed downside risks to Carry / PRE into 2H26 and 
2027 (BX and CG have most earnings risk exposure to this in 2027); (2) lower level of 
US Direct Lending deployment, weighing on 3Q/4Q credit management fee growth 
(particularly at OWL and ARES); (3) ongoing concerns about the DPI lock jam in PE 
and RE businesses weighing on fundraising sentiment; (4) some moderation in 
capital markets / transaction fees. That said, collectively, we think these headwinds 
amount to only a low-single digit impact to 2027 DE/share across the group vs >20% 
decline in share prices from recent levels. Against this, we see sustained growth and 
upside potential across several areas: (1) PE fundraising for managers with better 
track records is resilient, driving share gains for several public firms (as an example, 
TPG’s recent close of its Climate PE fund at $10bn surpassed our expectations by 
$2bn); (2) AI CapEx cycle is an underappreciated earnings lever across the group for 
both Infra funds, Private Credit and Syndication businesses; (3) Redemptions in 
Retail Private Credit funds are moderating (down 200bps qoq) and we estimate new 
requests are down ~50%, while momentum outside of Credit is healthy (flows in PE, 
Infra, Secondaries); (4) lingering DPI issues likely to drive continued supply/demand 
imbalance, benefiting secondary opportunities.  
On estimates, we are ~3% below consensus DE for the quarter, and modestly 
o
above for the out-years (+1% in 2027E, +3% in 2028E), with the dispersion 
driven almost entirely by PRE assumptions. FRE estimates remain mostly 
unchanged; we are essentially in line with consensus for 3Q (+0.1%) and 2026 
(+0.9%) but we remain ~3% above for 2027. Importantly, absolute growth 
levels remain healthy: we expect ~16% management fee CAGR from 
2026-2028E, with ARES, HLNE, and STEP at the high end (~17%-20%) while 
OWL, BX, and BAM are at the lower end (~11-12%). This translates to ~18% 
FRE/share CAGR across the group over the same period, led by STEP (~24%), 
CG (~22%), and TPG (~21%).  
Key Ideas: We highlight TPG and STEP, with compelling combinations of 
n
above-average FRE growth (21% and 24% 2025-28E FRE/share CAGRs, 
respectively), potential upside to consensus estimates (our estimates are above

Traditional Asset Managers:  
Sector view: We lean favorably into Traditional Managers into 3Q results as organic 
n
growth rates for most continue to improve (active ETFs, fixed income, Alts), equity 
markets (while more volatile) have been favorable and expense efforts should drive 
margins higher over the coming quarters. While rising rates dampened fixed income 
performance and flows in September, history shows that higher rates are typically 
followed by acceleration in flows. Valuation levels also remain relatively low (most 
are in the 9-10X NTM P/E outside of BLK and WT).  
Key Ideas: BLK and BEN are our top Buy ideas among Traditional Managers into 3Q 
n
results. We see BLK delivering industry leading organic fee growth of ~8% with 
continued expense focus supporting margin expansion into year-end. BEN’s flows 
continue to improve and growth in Alts should be supportive of the firm’s fee rate 
offsetting mix shift from growth in SMAs. Meanwhile, margins here also have a long 
runway into 2027 and 2028, keeping us MSD above consensus EPS for 2027 and 
2028. Meanwhile, we expect organic growth at both TROW and APAM to remain 
weak, creating continued downside risks to both stocks.  
Brokers & Wealth Advisors:  
Sector View: Brokers & Wealth Advisors stocks reversed recent gains as worries 
n
about AI agentic cash sorting theories resurfaced, leaving valuations at a ~16% 
discount to historical averages, with LPLA (~23% discount) and SCHW (~21%) 
screening the widest. The September Fed hike and the forward curve now price 
further hikes into 4Q26/2027 should be a clear tailwind to NII/cash sweep revenues, 
though we expect this to be mostly offset by weaker growth in cash balances. 
Organic growth remains below trend and little signs of competition for advisors 
abating, though we note an improving trajectory at LPLA recently and steady trends 
at RJF, while SF and AMP seem to face relatively lower NNA; we also think SCHW’s 
retrenchment from l/s tax aware products could weigh on NNA by an est 
50bp-100bp. We expect more aggressive recruiting/retention packages to either 
weigh on margins (most notably at AMP where reports suggested higher retention 
incentives) or keep NNA below trend at firms that are not competitive on capabilities 
or price. With respect to AI/cash sweep debates, we don’t expect a change in tone 
from management teams on strategy; however, LPL’s valuation likely strengthens the

Exchanges: 
Sector view: Exchange fundamentals remained broadly robust in 3Q, with most 
n
asset classes seeing healthy volume growth, retail being the one exception pointing 
to a q/q slowdown (mainly in US cash equities). Despite 3Q EPS upside potential (we 
are above the Street for most), follow-through into stock performance remains 
limited - mostly due to fear of unknown market structure changes on the back of 
on-chain trading evolution and introduction of new products (perps, event 
contracts, tokenized equities, etc). We continue to see risks from these potential 
developments as limited - particularly to institutional liquidity pools. Meanwhile, a 
wider retail adoptions of new wrappers could result in higher market making activity 
across all venues.  In terms of 3Q trading volumes, areas of strength included: 1) 
Energy, where macro and geopolitical set up drove elevated energy vol, clearly seen 
in ICE energy volumes up 18% y/y (15% q/q), with CME Energy also up 17% y/y (but 
down 2% q/q), with OI up 4% at ICE; and 2) Rates, where the Fed/ECB rate hike and 
pick up in yields drove elevated rate vol, with Cash Treasury ADV up 18% y/y and TW 
Swaps >=1Yr ADV up 46% y/y. Rates futures at CME were also up 12% y/y, with CME 
Treasury futures up 20% y/y while SOFR down 6% y/y. Notably, while Index Options 
activity was strong in 3Q, with SPX Options was up 25% y/y (down 3% q/q) mostly 
driven by 0DTE activity (up 27% y/y); On the other hand, US Cash Equity volumes 
were down 4% y/y amid muted retail activity (down 32% y/y per our dataset). Cash 
Credit trends were mixed this qtr, with IG up 16% y/y and HY 2% y/y, but with PT 
volumes up 40% y/y in IG and flat y/y in HY. Looking ahead, we think macro and 
geopolitical uncertainty remains supportive of Energy volumes into 4Q, while the 
move higher in Treasury yields sets up a better near-term outlook for Rates volumes.  
Key Ideas: Into 3Q EPS, we favor 1) TW, our 3Q EPS estimates are about 5% above 
n
Street, robust volumes in Rates and opportunities in Credit amid potential 
disruptions from the pending acquisition of MKTX by ICE; 2) ICE amid volume driven 
EPS upside offsetting a drag from Mortgage and discounted valuation, which we 
think fails to capture the stock’s durable opportunity in Energy trading (AI-driven 
power demand) and growth in Data businesses.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
