U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.59% | Nasdaq +0.64% | Dow +0.83%
Synopsis

US equities were higher in Friday trading as stocks bounced back after two straight down days. The AI trade faded off earlier strength with
semis lower and memory modestly higher. Big tech was mostly higher as AMZN-US and MSFT-US led to the upside while AAPL-US trailed.
Other outperformers included software, MCOs (HUM-US), biopharma (vaccines), China tech, networking/IT equipment (LITE-US), towers,
alts, moneycenter banks, payments and industrial metals. Laggards included telecom (SPCX-US spectrum), airlines (DAL-US), ag equip-
ment (USDA data), homebuilders, media, casual diners, regional banks, beverages and grocers. Breadth was better again to cap off an up
week for stocks. Treasuries were mostly weaker with curve flattening, though off the worst levels, with yields up 3 bp at the front end; Fed
rate path pricing was little changed at ~25 bp of hikes through year-end. The dollar index was up 0.1%, gold finished up 1.4%, silver was
up 0.4%, bitcoin futures were up 0.8% and WTI crude settled up 0.4% in choppy trading. Chips were down again despite a positive LITE-
US update, while widening AI-linked CDS spreads and positioning remain overhangs. Oil was choppy, with weakness tabbed to Trump
comments downplaying pre-midterm strikes on Iran, though physical flow risks remain elevated as Strait of Hormuz vessel strikes

Macro

OpenAI revenue and the AI trade: The AI trade came under pressure on Thursday on an FT report that OpenAI told investors annualized
revenue was approaching $50B at the end of September, $20B below the widely reported $70B figure. The headline seemed to drive
concerns about a dent in the demand narrative even though the discrepancy was a function of a methodology difference with Anthropic
(which includes cloud partner revenue) that has been discussed before. Sentiment shifted again on Friday on a Bloomberg report that
OpenAI has told investors it expects to reach or exceed $70B in annualized revenue by the end of the year, driven largely by the enterprise
business. OpenAI's enterprise momentum, narrowing the gap with Anthropic, also received attention earlier in the week, and the Bloom-
berg article noted OpenAI is in talks to raise $30B+ at a $1.4T valuation in its latest funding round.

Iran and the Strait of Hormuz: Trump said on Thursday that no US strike on Iran will occur before the 3-Nov midterm elections, adding
that the US and Tehran are having productive discussions (FT). This follows recent reports indicating potential strikes on Iranian energy
and nuclear infrastructure could occur ahead of voting, with the US military gearing up to deploy additional assets to the region (Axios).
Iran's escalating attacks on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz remain in focus, with at least nine ships hit last week, the highest weekly tally
since July (Washington Post). Transit volume fell from 16M barrels last Friday to 8M barrels by Monday. The US Treasury sanctioned 17
vessels, individuals and networks to disrupt Iranian crude and product exports, leaving Iran with ~20M barrels of crude on vessels outside
the blockade.

SpaceX spectrum deal pressures legacy telecoms: SpaceX agreed to acquire nationwide 800 MHz spectrum from Grain Management for
~$8B in cash (Reuters, Bloomberg). The move accelerates Starlink Mobile ambitions and pressured T-Mobile, AT&T and Verizon, which
all fell sharply in extended trading (CNBC). UBS noted the selloff seems overdone given the time needed to scale, while JPMorgan argued
telecom sentiment will be hit given a potential fourth entrant. Tower names rallied, including American Tower, SBA and Crown Castle.
Wells Fargo noted SpaceX must ultimately build a hybrid satellite/terrestrial network leveraging existing towers, adding that SpaceX par-
ticipation in the 2027 Upper C-Band auction could inflate bidding (Verizon is expected to spend ~$18B).

Fuel costs and iPhone demand weigh on corporates: Delta Air Lines fell after a Q3 adjusted EPS and revenue miss, with FY26 adjusted
EPS guidance cut to $5.10-5.60 from a prior $6.50-7.50. The airline expects to absorb $6B in incremental fuel expense this year, with
Q3 adjusted fuel costs jumping 62% y/y to $4.1B, partly offset by a ~$1B benefit from its internal refinery according to some analyst
estimates. Analysts flagged persistent fuel and non-fuel cost pressures but remained constructive on operational execution, citing robust
consumer demand and pricing power. Separately, Apple was pressured by the Nikkei report on iPhone 18 Pro/Pro Max component order
cuts of at least 15% for October, citing softer demand in the face of higher prices triggered by a surge in memory costs. The devices
launched last month at starting prices of $1,199 and $1,299, up $100 from the prior cycle, and Goldman Sachs noted Micron alone is
expected to contribute nearly 20% to S&P 500 earnings growth in Q3 (and 20%+ in Q4).

Consumer sentiment falls, inflation expectations rise: Preliminary October consumer sentiment came in at 46.3 vs 47.6 consensus and
September's final reading of 48.1. The Current Economic Conditions Index fell to 44.7 from 50.9, the lowest on record (since 1951), while
the Index of Consumer Expectations rose to 47.3 from 46.3. Year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.7% from 4.6%, well above Feb-
ruary's pre-Iran conflict level of 3.4%, and long-run expectations increased to 3.5% from 3.4%; both measures rose for a second straight
month to their highest since May. The report noted buying conditions for durables deteriorated sharply amid high prices and borrowing
costs, with weakness concentrated among lower-income consumers.

EU Market Recap
Germany +1.1% | France +0.9% | UK +1%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished higher, following weaker performance on Thursday. Miners, healthcare, financial services and retail
were the best performers, while telecom was the big laggard, with DTE-DE, VOD-GB and ORA-FR pulling back sharply on a US spectrum
deal covered in the United States section. Equity sentiment benefited from stabilization in bond markets. European bond yields were
lower across the board: UK 10-year yields led the decline, down 3.6 bp, while German, French, Italian and Spanish bonds were all broadly
flat. France remained a focal point, with reports suggesting investors have moved away from French OATs in favor of German Bunds
because of fiscal risks. The dollar index was up 0.2%, gold up 1.2%, silver up 2.7% and bitcoin futures up 1.5%. The macro data calendar
in Europe was very quiet: ahead of the open, Norwegian inflation was weaker than forecast and Swedish monthly activity beat, while
Italian industrial production missed. An adviser to UK PM Burnham said lower fiscal headroom was a better option than tax rises.
Macro

French fiscal risk and the ECB backstop: The big European bond yield backup was the main development of the week, with France leading
the selloff as fiscal concerns, political fragmentation and 2027 presidential election uncertainty weighed on sentiment. Paris pledged to
cap the deficit at 5% of GDP but threatened to bypass parliament to enforce €43B in savings, while far-right leader Le Pen's proposed
€25B annual unfunded spending cuts offered only brief relief. French 10-year yields hit their highest since 2002 and the OAT-Bund spread
moved above 140 bps, the widest since the Eurozone debt crisis, before narrowing to around 135bp by Friday as investors bought
peripheral sovereign and corporate debt and rotated toward German, Dutch and Swiss government bonds. Finance Minister Lescure
signalled France may favor shorter-term issuance, and Bloomberg noted 38% of French high-grade corporate bonds now trade at lower
yields than equivalent sovereign debt. Bloomberg sources said ECB President Lagarde told eurozone finance ministers the ECB has tools
to stabilize markets if needed, subject to clear eligibility criteria that include compliance with the EU fiscal framework, though the Bank
of France chief argued France's fiscal problems should be addressed domestically and the criteria for intervention are not yet met.
Eurozone finance ministers and the ECB told France to pass its 2027 budget. Political pressure remains elevated as PM Lecornu
announced the release of 10M barrels of strategic diesel reserves and further support for farmers and the education sector while protests
persist, and Le Pen is seeking to reassert control over National Rally, sidelining protege Bardella.

Brussels and UK fiscal pressure: European Commission finance chief Dombrovskis pushed back against proposals from Italy and Greece
for additional fiscal leeway, emphasizing fiscal prudence given bond market developments and saying the Commission could not keep
coming up with new flexibilities; Italy wants higher-than-expected inflation revenues taken into account, and Greece wants an exemption
up to a specified limit for temporary support measures. Germany's coalition also faced budget funding disputes, while Spain's snap
election call added to political risk. In the UK, BoE Governor Bailey urged Chancellor Healey to protect market credibility ahead of the 28-
Oct Budget, citing the 2022 mini-Budget as an illustration of the risks, and former BoE chief economist Haldane said public spending
must be cut to demonstrate discipline to the bond market. The UK 10Y yield hit its highest level since July 2007, above 5.5%, on Thursday,
with 20Y and 30Y yields at levels seen in 1998. Economists expect higher borrowing costs and a weaker growth outlook to have cut
around half of the £24B fiscal buffer, and the FT estimated the Iran conflict could leave a ~£12B hole in public finances. Higher taxes for
banks and a press report of £1B in energy support for lower-income households drew attention, and proposed business-rate increases
prompted pre-Budget talks with retail and hospitality leaders.

ECB and BoE rate paths: A Reuters poll found 70 of 73 economists expect the ECB to hold in October, with 64 expecting a 25 bp increase
in December; most see December as the last hike of the cycle, while a minority anticipate one more move early next year to see the deposit
rate peak at 3.00%. September ECB minutes showed full support for the 25 bp increase on a deteriorating inflation outlook, although
some officials did not think the energy shock was as persistent as expected. Lagarde and chief economist Lane have played down the
need for another near-term hike, citing recent tightening in financial conditions, and December is also preferred because the ECB will
publish updated macro forecasts. At the BoE, chief economist Pill and MPC member Greene emphasized the inflation fight and warned
against relying on bond yields to do the job of taming inflation. Bailey, Lombardelli, Breeden and Ramsden have warned the BoE may need
to act if inflation pressures strengthen, and market expectations for a November hike remain at ~90% with a 50% chance of a follow-up
hike in December.

Sector moves and UK consumer weakness: Basic Resources was the strongest-performing sector amid an improving risk backdrop and
higher precious metal prices, supported by UBS upgrades to European steelmakers citing rising EU hot-rolled coil prices. Retail advanced
even though BRC data showed total UK footfall fell 2.9% y/y in September versus a 1.7% decline previously, with shopping centre footfall
down 3.5%, retail parks down 1.7% and high streets down 3.0%; BDO data pointed to the weakest September for total discretionary
spending in seven years excluding the pandemic, with like-for-like high street sales down 0.3% against 3.1% a year ago. Financial Services
outperformed on a cyclical rebound, with Quilter (QLT-GB) in focus after Panmure Liberum raised its price target to 250p, implying 43%
upside. Technology was also higher, with the AI trade bounce cited as the driver. Telecom was the biggest underperformer: Deutsche
Telekom (DTE-DE) slid, reflecting its 54% stake in T-Mobile US, while tower names Cellnex (CLNX-ES) and Inwit (INW-IT) rose. Oil & Gas
was weaker than the broader market, with OMV (OMV-AT) also lower after its Q3 trading update, and Travel & Leisure lagged as Barclays
warned persistently high fuel prices threaten airline profitability in Q4'26 and 2027.

UK-EU alignment on Chinese trade: The Telegraph reported the UK is preparing to raise tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles as part of a
closer relationship with the EU. PM Burnham will visit German Chancellor Merz on Thursday ahead of a UK-EU summit in November, with
youth mobility and food and drink deals on the agenda; Burnham wants Brussels to allow the UK to benefit from EU defense loans and
industry subsidies, but Merz is expected to warn this will not be possible unless the UK raises tariffs on Chinese cars from 10% to match

Asian Markets
Japan -0.02% | Hong Kong +1.79% | China +0.05%
Synopsis

Asia equities ended mostly higher Friday. The Hang Seng saw the best of the gains while there were solid advances in Australia, India and
Japan's Topix. The Nikkei was flat. Mainland China benchmarks rose following a poor open, Southeast Asia was mixed, and Singapore
was flat after two sharply lower days. South Korea and Taiwan were closed for a holiday. Benchmarks opened lower but moved higher as
the oil price came down a tad and sovereign bond yields edged away from their recent highs, in an otherwise directionless week for stocks.
The US dollar was lower as the DXY index fell below 102 for a time, and the AUD was stronger with little movement of note elsewhere.
Treasury yields were mixed and JGB yields were lower across tenors. Oil futures were down sharply, although Brent was still well above
$102/bl. Precious metals rallied from recent losses, industrial metals were mixed and cryptocurrencies were higher. Mainland China
stocks rose in the afternoon session on reports regulators had advised funds and insurance companies to limit sell orders, while 'national
team' fund activity surged to its highest in weeks. Separately, the yuan rose to a four-year high after the PBOC defended its forex policy
ahead of EU trade talks. For the week, Asia equities still fell on aggregate as the MSCI Asia Pacific ex-Japan index declined around 0.8%,
with outsized falls in South Korea, Singapore and mainland China, while Taiwan, Hong Kong and Japan's Nikkei 225 all gained. In other
developments Friday, Japan household spending growth contracted for a ninth consecutive month y/y but beat estimates. Tokyo launched
a spending review as it seeks to cut government spending costs to pay for PM Takaichi's spending plans, and Indonesia's finance minister
has ordered cost-cutting measures to keep fiscal spending below the country's deficit ceiling. In corporates, Fast Retailing (9983-JP)
posted record quarterly profits as sales in western nations overtook China for the first time. Meta has banned ByteDance's TikTok from
advertising on its apps, according to Bloomberg. Xiaomi (1810-HK) reported strong orders for its newly launched SkyNomad SUV, with
shares sharply higher in Hong Kong. Firmus scrapped its $5B Australia-based IPO on weak demand but said it will instead try to list on
the Nasdaq next year.
Macro

Japan Cabinet presses ahead with consumption tax cut plan: Nikkei reported the Cabinet approved the consumption tax cut proposal,
with the tax rate on food items to be reduced to 1% from 8% for two years, followed by a cash payout to the low-income demographic. The
bill ensures the tax cuts expire after two years with no clause allowing amendments due to economic circumstances. With annual funding
requirements of JPY5T ($31.7B), the article recalled Prime Minister Takaichi's pledge to earn market credibility by securing funding out-
side of deficit bond issuance, and the administration aims to pass the legislation in the current Diet session through 12-Dec. The ruling
coalition controls three quarters of the lower house but is four seats short of a majority in the upper house, requiring support from some
opposition members. Opposition counter proposals have centered on cash payouts amid skepticism over the efficacy of a tax cut, and a
follow-up article highlighted the lack of specifics on funding, the per-person cash payout figure and eligibility determined by undefined
means testing; payouts are so far expected to run at about JPY600B per year. Opposition parties have yet to unify their stance, Komeito
has not ruled out support, and party leader Okamoto rated the bill 30 out of 100 while conceding the measures would be better than
nothing.

SoftBank looks to raise up to $100B from Gulf investors: Sources told the FT that SoftBank (9984-JP) is looking to raise up to $100B from
Gulf investors in a bid for fresh capital to fund its AI investments, with the money to be used to set up a fund to buy companies and
implement AI technology to improve operations. SoftBank's physical AI company, Roze, is expected to play a key role. The company's
balance sheet has been an ongoing focus following its fundraising spree, culminating in last month's record $11.1B junk bond raising
that sold at a yield as high as 9.75%.

China markets choppy as national team likely steps in: Chinese stocks fell for a second day on Friday morning after the Golden Week
holiday, with the CSI 300 down to its lowest since Aug-25. Tech shares were hit hardest, with the ChiNext down 2.6% in the morning to its
lowest since Nov-25 and the STAR 50 Index falling 3.8% to a six-month low. Stocks rebounded after trading resumed in the afternoon,
with sharp increases in volumes of several ETFs tracking major benchmarks fueling speculation that national teams or state-backed funds
came to the rescue. Factors behind onshore tech weakness included multi-year high global bond yields and heavy selloffs in Chinese AI
hardware names, especially in the optical chip sector, amid market chatter over a Morgan Stanley research note speculating the US FCC
could impose restrictions on Chinese products starting from the 3.2T optical module generation with a 65% US content exemption thresh-
old.

Improving JGB sentiment dampened: Nikkei published a bearish commentary on superlong JGBs, with yields still elevated as smooth
auction results have so far failed to translate into sustained buying in the secondary market, and demand appears confined to attractive
