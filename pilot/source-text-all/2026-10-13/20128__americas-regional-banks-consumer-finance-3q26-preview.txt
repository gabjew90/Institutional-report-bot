Thoughts on Regional Banks into 3Q26 EPS 
We are upbeat on the regional banks headed into 3Q26 EPS as the stocks are coming 
in on the lows (down ~12% since last reporting and 11% since 8/1, UP market by 
>1,000bps over those timeframes) given a handful of macro and micro concerns that 
are weighing on the stocks.  While there are a handful of issues, including 1) the 
impact on the economy from rate hikes (1 in September, marketing pricing in ~3 
more hikes by mid-2027) and higher LT rates (~5.20% today; peak of ~5.3% and up 
~70bps QoQ), 2) deposit competition in a higher rate environment and 3) what 
agentic AI could mean for profitability over time (deposit costs, loan pricing etc.), we 
expect earnings to be solid with continued loan growth, deposit costs rising at a 
lower level than is being priced into the stocks and 4Q outlooks to be relatively 
upbeat as banks benefit from lagged deposit re-pricing, loan growth remains solid 
and capital markets support higher fees. So while the stocks are now on the lows and 
trading at <10x 2027E, we think many of these risks will prove to be less impactful in 
the near-term and could drive a relief rally in the group.  
Key areas of focus / areas worth watching: 1) The path of deposit costs in a more 
competitive environment along with how betas will respond to rate hikes, where we 
look for most to point to the 50-55% incremental betas experienced during the 
rising rate cycle, 2) the durability of loan growth in a higher rate environment, 3) 
combining these, whether asset sensitivity will help support higher NII growth into 
4Q and 2027, 4) updated thoughts on credit where most updates were positive 
(reserve releases?) although with the move in rates we will be looking for any early 
indicators on stress, particularly areas like multifamily, 5) capital, where we estimate 
adj. CET1 ratios will fall 23bps in 3Q26 and wonder if any additional banks point to a 
pause in buybacks if capital ratios fall below their targeted levels, 6) expectations for 
capital markets, where results were solid but the move up in rates could potentially 
weigh on activity, 7) any early 2027 guides (historically have got some color at 3Q but 
nothing too formal) as some may point to levels of operating leverage and 8) the view 
on the impacts of agentic AI and whether deposit cost fears are realistic or 
overblown.

KEY: NII tracking ahead, could get greater clarity on ‘business performance’ 
 
 
The results: We expect KEY to post in-line 3Q26 results (EPS $0.49 vs. street at $0.46; 
with the beat driven by a reserve release) although it seems like the street has not yet 
updated for its recent conference update where it increased fee (+4-5% vs. +3-4% prior) 
and expense growth (+4% vs. +3-4% prior) expectations reflecting the Clearwater 
acquisition as well as pointed to improved credit trends (lower NCOs, potential for 
reserve release). We look for the focus to be on 1) its NIM expectations in 4Q and 2027 
2) expectations for commercial loan growth and 3) trajectory for capital return given a 
lower adj. CET1 in 3Q than its target (<9.5% vs. 9.5%-10%).  
The 2026 guide: We believe KEY could tighten its NII range to point to +10-11% NII 
growth for 2026 (we model +10.2% YoY for 2026; guide is +9-11%) with exit rate NII 
tracking ~180bps ahead of the street on stronger balance sheet growth (given 
commentary on 3Q loan and deposit growth) with an in-line exit NIM (3.01%). Given we 
expect deposit costs to tick up ~3bps in 3Q, we look for commentary on betas into 4Q 
(could be a source of NII upside if track slower than expected) along with its ability to 
continue to grow deposits into 4Q (pointed to $2.5bn+ of deposit growth in 3Q and 
$3bn in 2H which it appears to be tracking ahead on) which would help it pay down 
more borrowings. Lastly, with the fee and expense guide recently reiterated (ex. 
incorporating the deal), we don’t expect any changes.  
What we are watching:  
• 3Q NII / NIM: With a handful of 1x items in 2Q weighing on NIM (NPA interest accrual, 
SOFR), we think KEY should be able to see NIM expand towards ~2.96% in 3Q which 
should support NII outperformance given faster than expected loan growth. Specifically, 
flowing in the impact of SOFR reversal (+1bps; ex rate hike), NPA interest accrual (+2bps) 
puts KEY’s NIM at 2.92% for 3Q. Adding on fixed rate repricing (+4bps) along with a very 
modest benefit from hikes points to a ~2.96% NIM in 3Q. This along with faster balance 
sheet growth (AEA likely to grow $2bn guided the $ amount in 3Q) points to NII ~1.3% 
ahead of the street.  
• Path to a 3.25% NIM: KEY has previously laid out expectations to reach a ~3.25% NIM 
by 4Q27 with the expansion driven by half from business performance and half from 
fixed rate repricing. We previously estimated a $60mn benefit from fixed rate repricing 
or ~13bps of NIM which implies the remainder to come from business performance 
(loans growing at SOFR +150-200bps) funded by deposits at portfolio rates. We 
sensitize the business performance below and believe that growing loans $5.8bn at a 
~6% yield with deposits at ~2% points to a ~6bps benefit from ‘business performance’. 
While this is a touch softer than the 3.25% KEY is expecting, given concerns about the 
path to 3.25%, we think showing the moving pieces would improve investor confidence 
in reaching its goals.

growth. While our estimates come up a bit short of DD, we think there will be an 
increased focus at earnings on SYF’s ability to drive DD EPS growth into 2027. 
Specifically, we flow in the benefit from buybacks (~12% reduction shares; +$1.26 to

expense growth (-$0.29). Together, this points to $10 of EPS in 2027, which is $0.35 
below consensus, and we look for greater clarity at earnings on the drivers of EPS into 
next year.

which is partially offset by higher net servicing revenues and interest income on servicing 
deposits (higher rates). We estimate 4Q26 adj. revenue of $2.56bn and, given RKT 
typically guides conservatively, we think the guide could come in at $2.4bn - $2.6bn for 
adjusted revenues (consensus of $2.67bn though we believe estimates have not been 
updated for a softer mortgage market). On costs, with 4Q synergy pull through a touch 
slower vs. 3Q but lower variable costs, we see expenses of $2.32bn (consensus of 
$2.34bn).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
