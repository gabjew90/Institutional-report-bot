HEDGE FUNDS
Hedge fund performance was negative in October, with the asset-weighted Hedge Fund Composite 
returning -1.25%. Equity long/short (-2.64%) was the weakest master strategy, while event driven 
strategies (-0.24%) declined the least. October showed a shift in investor sentiment as concerns over 
the returns on AI spending weighed on US and Asian technology shares. Major government bond 
yields fell, while escalating Middle East tensions pushed oil prices higher.
Hedge fund 
composite
Concerns over the scale of AI spending prompted a sell-off in technology shares. Selling intensified towards month-
end as US earnings announcements raised further questions about the returns on that investment. Escalating 
Middle East tensions pushed oil prices higher, while the Federal Reserve held interest rates unchanged.
Corporate 
bonds
Equity long/short strategies fell 2.64% in October, with losses across all sub-strategies. Asia Pacific 
long/short fell the most (–5.37%). Long positions were vulnerable to broad declines in South Korean 
and Japanese equities, while market hedges may have offered only partial protection against volatility 
in AI-related positions.
Long biased strategies returned -0.57% in October. Diversified growth (+1.45%) led, while the long 
biased other sub-strategy (-6.80%) was weakest. Sharp declines in Asian technology and 
semiconductor equities challenged long books. However, UK equities provided some support; 
benefiting from energy sector strength.
Long biased
Global equities declined in October as investors questioned the returns on AI spending. Technology and 
semiconductor shares fell sharply in the US and Asia, although a majority of US stocks ended the month higher. 
Declines in bank and technology shares weighed on eurozone large-cap equities. However, UK equities 
outperformed, supported by gains in energy shares.
Government 
bonds
Government bonds gained across most major markets in October. US yields declined as the sell-off in technology 
shares increased demand for safer assets. German and UK yields also fell, while French yields rose amid political 
uncertainty and renewed concerns about government spending.
Quant strategies returned -0.75% in October. The sharp factor rotation away from tech and towards 
value and defensive sectors benefited risk premia (+0.30%), which was the only positive sub-strategy. 
However, this also weighed on systematic equities sub-strategies, most notably in quant multi-
strategy funds (-1.55%).

Equities
US investment-grade corporate bonds gained in October as falling Treasury yields supported bond prices. US high-
yield bonds also ended the month slightly higher, although returns varied by credit quality. Higher-rated issues 
held up better, while bonds issued by lower-quality borrowers underperformed.

Macro
Multi-strategy funds returned -1.47% in October. All fund-size cohorts declined. The largest funds 
(AUM> US$10bn) fell the least (-1.36%), while the smallest funds (<US$0.5bn) fell the most (-2.44%). De-
grossing drove losses, with equity teams adversely impacted by the unwind of crowded AI and 
technology trades.
The US dollar edged higher in October, giving up some of its gains after the Federal Reserve held interest rates 
steady. Political and fiscal uncertainty in France weighed on the euro, while higher oil prices supported the 
Canadian dollar. The Brazilian real fell sharply after the election result renewed concerns over public spending.
Currencies
Macro strategies returned -1.52% in October. Commodities (+0.82%) was the only positive sub-
strategy, while global macro (-2.35%) was weakest. Global macro strategies struggled as flattening 
yield curves and shifting rate expectations pressured directional positions. Emerging markets macro 
strategies were hit by Brazil’s surprise election result, however, commodity macro strategies gained 
from rising oil and power prices amid supply concerns.

Commodities
Oil prices rose sharply in October as attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and tankers in the Strait of Hormuz 
threatened supplies. Gold and silver fell as selling intensified towards month-end, with silver recording the 
steeper decline. Higher US supply estimates weighed on wheat and corn prices, while sugar gave back much of its 
mid-month rise but still ended October higher.
