The Commodities Feed: Oil extends
losses while copper gives back Nvidia
bounce
Commodity markets came under renewed pressure on Tuesday. Copper
reversed Monday’s Nvidia-driven rebound despite continued physical
tightness, while oil extended its decline on hopes of US-Iran talks, and US
natural gas fell sharply on milder weather forecasts
Macro concerns are likely to keep copper volatile in the near term
Energy – Oil falls as hopes of US-Iran talks grow
Oil prices extended their decline on Tuesday, with WTI remaining below $80/bbl as
expectations of potential US-Iran negotiations continued to unwind some of the geopolitical
risk premium. Prices fell almost 4% on Monday, with WTI touching its lowest level since August
after reports that the US was willing to engage in talks with Iran.
The prospect of negotiations has eased some immediate concerns over Middle East supply,
particularly as crude exports from the Persian Gulf have remained relatively resilient despite
continued disruption around the Strait of Hormuz. However, significant differences between
the US and Iran remain, leaving oil vulnerable to any setback in talks.

Geopolitical risks also remain elevated elsewhere. Ukraine has reported another attack on a
major Russian refinery in the Perm region, with more than 260k b/d of processing capacity.
Around 15 Russian refineries have reportedly been targeted since the start of November,
representing more than 3.6m b/d of nameplate capacity, or over half of Russia’s total refining
capacity.
Meanwhile, US natural gas prices fell more than 9% on Monday to around $3.1/MMBtu, the
biggest daily decline since February. Milder weather forecasts across key consuming regions
have weighed on heating demand expectations, while US production remains close to record
levels. The combination of softer weather-driven demand and strong supply should continue
to weigh on prices in the near term.
Metals – Copper gives back Nvidia bounce
Copper gave back Monday’s Nvidia-driven gains on Tuesday, falling more than 2% towards
$12,000/t as focus shifted back to Chinese demand and US interest rates.
The latest weakness is largely a financial market story rather than a broad deterioration in
copper fundamentals. The Chinese physical market remains tight, with elevated import
premiums and nearby spreads signalling limited availability. Some of the copper accumulated
in the US earlier this year is beginning to return to the international market, but this should
take time to materially ease tightness elsewhere.
Aluminium is experiencing similar near-term constraints, reflected in a sharp widening of the
LME cash-to-three-month spread above $400/t on Monday as exchange availability is at
historical lows. More Indonesian supply should eventually help, although much of the new
material is not yet deliverable against the LME.
Macro concerns are likely to keep copper volatile in the near term, but tight physical
availability should limit some downside.
