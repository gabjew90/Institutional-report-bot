DB CoTD: Just Another Manic (Trillion Dollar) Monday 
 
A reminder that yesterday all Deutsche Bank's economists and strategists came together to publish our 
latest World Outlook for 2027, “Great Expectations, but Risky Business”. Given the current market swings, it 
feels like an appropriate title. 
Notably, our equity strategist Binky Chadha sees the S&P 500 reaching 9,000 by end-2027, which is a 
remarkable forecast. See the full report here. 
 
Nvidia yesterday gave Binky a helping hand and at one point was on course to become the first company to 
add a trillion dollars of market cap in a day, before it ended up just the $952bn higher. For context, a trillion 
dollars is around the size of the Swiss economy ($1.14tn) and around the entire market cap of Walmart 
($1.03tn) and Berkshire Hathaway ($1.22tn). This was more than double the size of the second largest single 
company move in history which was Microsoft's $450bn back in July of this year. Outside of yesterday Nvidia 
has four other entries in the top ten which is full of the obvious huge tech names. 
All those top ten moves have occurred in the last 2 and a half years and for a sense of perspective as to how 
size has changed in the US equity market, today’s chart shows the value of the largest company in the S&P 

500 at year end each year since 1981, in both nominal and real terms. In 1981, the largest company IBM was 
worth $33.7bn. In today’s money adjusted for inflation, that’s worth $123.8bn. At the market peak at the end 
of 1999, Microsoft was worth $601bn which was actually $1.2tn in today’s money. Before the pre-GFC peaks, 
at year-end 2007, Microsoft was again the largest company but was “only” worth around $333bn at year end 
($536bn in today’s terms). In nominal terms Apple became the first trillion-dollar US company in August 
2018. 
So, these numbers and graph show how the scale of the largest names in the market has changed, with 
Apple and Nvidia now the largest two companies at around $5tn and $4tn market cap respectively, and 13 in 
total over a trillion dollars. Never in history have a handful of companies seen such daily moves and had as 
much influence on global markets. 
One thing is for certain, 2027 will not be dull. The factors that are hoped to drive markets higher are also the 
ones that pose the biggest risks. As a minimum it will be another high-octane year.
