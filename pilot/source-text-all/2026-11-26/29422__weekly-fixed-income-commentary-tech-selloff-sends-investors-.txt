FIXED INCOME WEEKLY COMMENTARY  23 NOVEMBER 2026
Key 
takeaways
•	A tech stock selloff drove 
investors into Treasuries, 
pushing the 10-year yield 
down 19 bps to 4.95%.
•	Taxable municipals led gains, 
while wider credit spreads 
weighed on high yield bonds 
and emerging markets debt.
•	We expect Fed rate hikes 
in December 2026 and 
March 2027, followed by 
a pause to assess whether 
inflation is easing.
Market recap
A selloff in technology stocks pushed the S&P 500 into correction territory and sent 
investors looking for safety in U.S. Treasuries. That demand outweighed signals 
from the U.S. Federal Reserve that rates could move higher. The 10-year Treasury 
yield fell -19 basis points (bps) to 4.95% and the Bloomberg U.S. Treasury Index 
gained 1.28%. 
Bonds that benefit more from falling yields led the gains: Taxable municipal bonds 
rose 1.57%, agency mortgage-backed securities gained 1.41% and the Bloomberg 
U.S. Aggregate Bond Index rose 1.18%. 
Credit sectors lagged as spreads widened by 10 bps to 96 for investment grade 
bonds, by 51 to 367 for high yield corporates and by 37 to 219 for emerging markets 
debt. High yield corporates fell -0.86% and emerging markets debt fell -0.65%. 
Preferred securities declined -0.50%, senior loans slipped -0.09% and tax-exempt 
municipal bonds fell -0.27%. 
Outlook
We still expect the Fed to raise rates in December 2026 and now expect another 
increase in March 2027. Strong inflation readings, steady economic growth and a 
stable job market support the case for additional hikes. After March, we expect the 
Fed to pause to see whether inflation is easing. 
Despite wider spreads, credit fundamentals remain sound. We continue to favor 
high-quality bonds with attractive overall yields. In municipals, we favor longer 
duration over Treasuries. We expect the 10-year Treasury yield to end 2026 at 
4.95% and 2027 at 4.75%. For the same time periods, we expect 2-year yields at 
4.70% and 4.50%.

U.S. Treasuries 
Treasury yields fell across the curve as the equity selloff drove safe-haven 
demand, outweighing Fed signals that rates could rise. The 5-year yield fell 
the most, while the long end saw smaller declines. The yield curve steepened 
as a result.  
The 2-year yield fell 20 bps to 4.65%, 
the 5-year fell 22 bps to 4.74%, the 
10-year fell 19 bps to 4.95% and the 
30-year fell 14 bps to 5.33%.  
Tax-exempt municipals
Tax-exempt municipals lagged the Treasury rally and posted a modest decline. 
With muni yields at 4.29%, we continue to favor taking longer-duration 
exposure in municipals rather than Treasuries.
The Bloomberg Municipal Index 
returned -0.27%.
Taxable municipals
Taxable municipal bonds led the week’s gains as their longer maturity profile 
benefited most from falling yields. Higher absolute yields should continue to 
attract crossover buyers.
The Bloomberg Municipal Taxable 
Index returned 1.57% with 
spreads at 58 bps. 
 
Investment grade corporates
Investment grade corporates posted a positive return as lower Treasury yields 
more than offset wider spreads. Equity volatility and heavy new issuance pushed 
spreads wider, though demand for new deals remained solid. 
The Bloomberg U.S. Corporate 
Bond Index returned 0.84%, with 
spreads at 96 bps.
U.S. high yield corporates
High-yield bonds fell as the equity selloff and heavy new issuance weighed on the 
market. Higher-quality bonds held up best. Lower-rated credits lagged and fund 
flows turned negative.
The Bloomberg High Yield 2% 
Issuer Capped Index returned 
-0.86%, with spreads at 367 bps.

Preferred securities
Preferred securities fell as wider spreads more than offset the benefit of lower 
rates. Retail-oriented par preferreds continued to lag, while bank issues held up 
better. New issuance was light.
The ICE Preferred Index returned 
-0.50% with spreads at 212 bps.
Senior loans
Senior loans were little changed but outperformed high-yield bonds. Their 
low duration limited the benefit of the Treasury rally. Steady demand from 
collateralized loan obligations (CLOs) and loan funds supported the market. 
Fund flows were positive, led by CLO exchange-traded funds. 
The S&P Leveraged Loan 
Index returned -0.09%, with 
spreads at 500 bps.
Securitized credit
Securitized sectors benefited from lower rates, led by agency MBS, followed 
by CMBS and ABS. ABS absorbed heavy issuance with spreads generally firm. 
CMBS spreads were little changed, but simpler, high-grade bonds remained 
easier to trade than more complex credits. Senior CLO tranches held firm, while 
performance varied more among lower-rated tranches.
The Bloomberg MBS Index returned 
1.41%, with spreads at 34 bps. The 
Bloomberg CMBS Index returned 
1.03%, with spreads at 72 bps. 
The Bloomberg ABS Index returned 
0.66%, with spreads at 52 bps.
Global emerging markets
Emerging markets debt fell despite lower Treasury yields. Hard currency 
spreads widened and higher-yielding credits underperformed. Local currency 
debt also lagged as the dollar strengthened. Retail fund outflows accelerated. 
The Bloomberg Global EM 
Index returned -0.65%, with 
spreads at 219 bps.

Investing involves risk; principal loss is possible. Debt or fixed income securities are subject to market 
risk, credit risk, interest rate risk, call risk, derivatives risk, dollar roll transaction risk and income risk.

investments involve additional risks, including currency fluctuation, political and economic instability, 
lack of liquidity and differing legal and accounting standards. These risks may be magnified in emerging 
markets. Asset-backed and mortgage-backed securities are subject to additional risks such as prepayment 
risk, liquidity risk, default risk and adverse economic developments. The value of convertible securities 
may decline in response to such factors as rising interest rates and fluctuations in the market price of 
the underlying securities. Senior loans are subject to loan settlement risk due to the lack of established

potentially limited liquidity, as well as interest rate risk, currency risk, prepayment and extension risk, 
and inflation risk. Any investment in collateralized loan obligations or other structured vehicles involves 
significant risks not associated with more conventional investment alternatives.

alternative minimum tax (AMT) and/or state and local taxes, based on the state of residence. Income from 
municipal bonds held by a portfolio could be declared taxable because of unfavorable changes in tax laws, 
adverse interpretations by the Internal Revenue Service or state tax authorities, or noncompliant conduct 
of a bond issuer. It is important to review your investment objectives, risk tolerance and liquidity needs 
before choosing an investment style or manager.
