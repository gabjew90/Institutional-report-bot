Strat Chat: Bond markets rally, but inflation
risks persist
Government bonds rallied in October as concerns around France and weaker risk
sentiment supported markets. However, resilient economic data and persistent
inflation risks have since driven a renewed sell-off, led by the US. Mike Riddell, Lead
Portfolio Manager of the Fidelity Strategic Bond Fund, outlines why we have
selectively added duration in the UK and Australia while retaining a defensive stance
on credit.

Rates: Selectively adding back duration after the sell-off
We selectively added back duration as the recent sell-off pushed yields higher, creating more attractive
opportunities in government bond markets. We increased UK duration through 10-year gilt futures as
gilts began to look relatively cheap again, particularly compared with other government bond markets.
We also added to Australian rates, taking our duration long back to around 1.5 years relative to the
benchmark. With further Reserve Bank of Australia tightening looking increasingly unlikely, we see a
strong possibility that rate cuts will be needed from the second half of 2027.
In Europe, we continue to favour German government bonds over France and Italy, albeit with a smaller
relative position. The France-Germany yield gap has widened amid continued French fiscal uncertainty.
Within emerging market local rates, we added to Paraguayan bonds after a sell-off triggered by the
collapse of a major domestic bank. We believe the macroeconomic and external backdrop remains
supportive, with no evidence of contagion to the wider financial system.
Inflation: US inflation remains our preferred exposure
We increased US inflation exposure as market expectations moved lower despite resilient growth and
continued inflation pressures. We believe medium-term pricing may still be underestimating the upside
risks.
Although agricultural commodity prices have retraced much of their earlier rise, we remain concerned
that markets may be underestimating the risk of renewed energy-driven inflation pressures.
We remain more selective in UK and European inflation, where market pricing already reflects a higher
inflation path.

Currencies: Maintaining a more defensive stance
Our currency positioning remains defensive, with a long US dollar bias and fewer emerging market
currency longs following reductions over recent months. Higher developed-market yields could continue
to weigh on emerging market currencies.
We established a short position in the South African rand as weaker terms of trade could put pressure
on the country’s fiscal balance. We remain selective despite attractive valuations in some emerging
markets.
We also closed the remaining yen short against the euro and took profits on our Australian dollar short
following its depreciation.
Credit: Valuations remain the constraint
We remain underweight developed-market credit. Although spreads have widened, valuations still offer
limited protection if tighter financial conditions feed through to risk assets. Heavy issuance from large
technology companies is also adding pressure, particularly at longer maturities, reinforcing our
preference to remain defensive.
Performance
In October 2026, the fund delivered a gross return of 2.41%, compared with 1.70% for the benchmark.
Favourable credit positioning was the main driver, while currencies and inflation also contributed.
Duration detracted as government bonds rallied.
Rates: The fund adjusted duration tactically during October, although duration and yield curve
positioning detracted from relative performance as government bond yields declined. US rates
positioning, ranging from underweight to neutral, contributed positively, while the underweight in
euro rates detracted as German Bunds rallied. Overweight Australian and Norwegian rates
contributed positively. These gains were partly offset by underweight Japanese rates and weakness
in Paraguayan local bonds.
Currencies: Currency positioning supported relative performance, with overweight positions in the
Norwegian krone and Swiss franc contributing the most. The underweight euro position also
supported absolute performance. This was partly offset by the Paraguayan guarani position
following the collapse of a major local bank, while the US dollar overweight also weighed on
returns.
Inflation: Long US inflation exposure contributed positively during October, supported by a
September US CPI reading of 3.7%, slightly above expectations of 3.6% and up from 3.4% in August.
Lower US real Treasury yields and resilient inflation expectations also supported inflation-linked
positioning.
Credit: Short positions in US and European high yield were the primary contributors during October.
US spreads widened amid market volatility, AI financing concerns and issuer-specific credit stress.
European spreads widened more significantly amid French fiscal uncertainty, elevated energy prices
and concerns over highly leveraged issuers. Our defensive credit stance proved beneficial.
