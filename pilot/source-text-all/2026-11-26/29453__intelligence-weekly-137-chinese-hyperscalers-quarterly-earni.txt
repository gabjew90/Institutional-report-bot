Alibaba: Cloud reset better than 
expected
Solid quarter, with cloud the key positive
Alibaba delivered solid quarterly results, with the main 
upside coming from cloud. The cloud segment achieved 
53% y/y growth, with external customer revenue growing 
64% y/y. More importantly, the cloud segment's adjusted 
EBITA margin reached 14.5%, above already elevated 
market expectations. We think the simultaneous strength 
in cloud growth and margin expansion is significant. It 
suggests Alibaba is beginning to demonstrate that the 
AI opportunity can translate not only into faster revenue 
growth, but also into meaningful earnings contribution.

The most important issue ahead of the quarterly result 
announcement was the potential impact of compute 
constraints on Alibaba's forward cloud trajectory.
Management now expects cloud growth of about 30-45% 
in FY28 (April 2027 to March 2028), below the previous 
expectation of 50%+, before reaccelerating to 50%+ in 
FY29 as domestic compute capacity ramps up. At first sight, 
this appears to be a meaningful downgrade. But we think 
the underlying message is much better than the headline 
suggests. Most importantly, management has not changed 
its CY2030 cloud ambition, including the goal of reaching 
around USD 100bn in external cloud revenue and a margin 
above 20%.
Our interpretation, therefore, is that the CY2027 slowdown 
is primarily supply-constrained rather than demand-
constrained. That distinction is critical. If AI demand were 
slowing structurally, the long-term Alibaba cloud thesis 
would need to be reassessed. Instead, what we see today 
is strong underlying demand confronting a temporary 
constraint in advanced compute availability. A period of 
30-45% growth in CY2027 is acceptable, in our view, 
provided domestic compute capacity subsequently allows 
growth to reaccelerate.
This is also why maintaining the CY2030 target matters. It 
signals that management regards the current disruption as a 
timing issue rather than a structural impairment of the cloud 
opportunity.
One of the more encouraging conclusions from the quarterly 
results is that the earnings consequences of slower cloud 
revenue appear considerably smaller than the headline 
revenue reset, helped by three important cushions.
First, cloud margin is expanding faster than previously 
expected. Second, Alibaba can adjust the pace and 
allocation of AI-related investment, particularly between 
infrastructure, models, and applications. And third, 
profitability outside cloud is normalizing as losses in newer 
businesses decline. As a result, we think investors should 
increasingly focus on cloud's EBITA growth rather than 
cloud's revenue growth alone. Even under a slower CY2027 
revenue trajectory, Alibaba could continue producing 
attractive cloud earnings growth if margins progress toward 
management's longer-term 20%+ target.
T-Head becomes increasingly important
The other major positive is greater clarity around 
Alibaba's proprietary silicon strategy. Investor concerns 
about advanced compute availability have been one of the 
biggest overhangs on Alibaba's AI thesis. We therefore see 
T-Head's reliance on domestic manufacturing partners as 
reassuring, as it provides a potential path to reduce Alibaba's 
dependence on constrained overseas compute.
That said, we think it's too early to draw firm conclusions. 
The investment debate is increasingly moving from AI 
demand to AI supply. The key question over the next 
12-18 months will be whether T-Head and the domestic 
semiconductor ecosystem can deliver sufficient capacity, 
yield, and performance to support Alibaba's rapidly growing 
model training and inference requirements. This is the single 
most important execution variable, in our view.
E-commerce: No growth is acceptable when the 
business remains a cash cow
Alibaba E-commerce Group's revenue declined around 5% 
y/y, while CMR also fell approximately 5%, reflecting 
continued pressure from the macro environment. However, 
its adjusted EBITA increased sharply due to low base effect.
We are relatively comfortable with the lack of near-term e-
commerce growth. In the current consumer environment, 
stability is good enough, in our view. The strategic role 
of the business within Alibaba has changed. It does not 
need to provide the group's incremental growth narrative. 
But it needs to maintain competitive position and remain a 
highly cash-generative franchise capable of funding cloud 
and AI investment. By that measure, the business continues 
to perform its role.
New initiatives: Loss narrowing remains on track
The 
other 
important 
earnings 
lever 
is 
continued 
improvement in its newer businesses.
Loss reduction remains broadly on track, providing an 
additional source of group-level earnings improvement. This 
becomes particularly relevant considering the divergence 
between Alibaba's revenue and earnings trajectories. In 
other words, Alibaba does not necessarily require strong 
group revenue acceleration to deliver earnings growth. 
Cloud margin expansion, reduced investment losses, and 
disciplined spending can all contribute to operating 
leverage. This helps explain why the reset to forward 
earnings expectations has so far been substantially smaller 
than the reset to cloud revenue expectations.
What matters from here
Following the quarterly results, we focus on four issues.
1. Cloud execution. Can Alibaba maintain cloud growth 
within the 30-45% range through the constrained 
period? And is there evidence that growth can return 
toward 50%+ as compute capacity improves?
2. Cloud profitability. The recent margin upside is 
arguably as important as revenue growth. Continued 
progress from the current mid-teens margin toward 
the 20%+ long-term target would significantly increase 
cloud's contribution to group earnings.
3. Domestic semiconductor supply. We will closely 
monitor T-Head deployment, foundry capacity, yields, and 
performance. Failure of domestic compute supply to scale

is now the biggest identifiable risk to the FY29 cloud

4. E-commerce cash generation. We are comfortable 
with limited top-line growth, but underlying profitability 
and cash generation need to remain resilient enough to 
finance Alibaba's AI investment cycle without materially 
damaging group FCF.
Overall, we come away from the quarterly results more 
constructive.
The market's key concern has shifted from AI demand 
to compute availability. The quarterly results provide some 
reassurance on both fronts: underlying cloud demand 
remains strong, profitability is improving faster than 
expected, the immediate growth outlook is better than 
expected, and management has maintained its long-
term cloud ambition. The temporary CY2027 slowdown 
therefore looks manageable.
The next phase of the thesis comes down to execution. 
If T-Head and China's domestic semiconductor supply 
chain can provide sufficient incremental compute capacity, 
there is a credible path for cloud growth to reaccelerate 
after CY2027 while margins continue expanding. If that 
happens, we think today's interruption should prove to be a 
temporary supply-driven slowdown rather than a structural 
deteoration of Alibaba's AI opportunity. Meanwhile, e-
commerce remains a substantial cash generator and 
loss reduction elsewhere will provide additional operating 
leverage.
Tencent 3QCY26 earnings review: 
Pain before payoff
Tencent's 3QCY26 results suggest that the stock is likely to 
remain in an earnings digestion phase for the next several 
quarters. The central issue is not the quality of Tencent's core 
franchises, which remain healthy, but a growing mismatch 
between the timing of AI investment and the timing of 
AI monetization. Costs and capex are rising now, while 
the earnings contribution from the next generation of AI 
products remains limited.
We therefore expect earnings revisions, free cash flow 
pressure, and uncertainty around the appropriate valuation 
multiple to constrain the shares in the near term. The pain 
may last longer than the market expects.
That said, we do not see the current investment cycle 
as evidence that Tencent's structural competitive position 
is deteriorating. Tencent still has one of the strongest 
ecosystems in China, combining Weixin distribution, 
gaming, advertising, payments, cloud, and enterprise 
relationships. In our view, these competitive advantages give 
Tencent a credible path to monetize AI over time.
Our investment thesis is straightforward: The near-term 
earnings outlook has deteriorated, but the long-term one 
has not. We would stay patient through the earnings reset 
and become more constructive as AI monetization and peak 
capex come into view.
3QCY26: 
Underlying 
businesses 
resilient, 
but 
investment burden rising
Tencent delivered 8% y/y revenue growth in 3QCY26, 
while non-IFRS net income declined 6% y/y. The divergence 
between revenue and earnings is increasingly important: 
the weakness at the bottom line is being driven less by 
deterioration of the existing franchises and more by the 
rapid increase in investment behind AI infrastructure and 
products.
Advertising grew 19% y/yt, demonstrating that Tencent 
can continue gaining wallet share despite a relatively soft 
macroeconomic environment. Cloud was even stronger, 
accelerating to around 35% y/y, versus approximately 21% 
y/y in the previous quarter. Domestic games grew 11% y/y, 
but international games declined 9% y/y.
This was not primarily a revenue problem. It was an 
investment-cycle problem. And investment-cycle problems 
can take longer to work through than the market 
anticipates.
We highlight three concerns for the next several 
quarters.
1. AI costs are arriving well ahead of AI monetization
Tencent appears willing to invest aggressively to secure its 
position in the AI era. Strategically, we believe this makes 
sense. Financially, however, the timing is less favorable.
AI-related infrastructure requires substantial upfront capex. 
Depreciation and operating expenses follow, while 
monetization ramps up much more gradually. With quarterly 
cash capex potentially remaining around CNY 65bn through 
2027, the cash flow consequences could remain significant.
This creates the central tension in our Tencent thesis: We 
are reasonably confident that Tencent can monetize AI 
eventually. However, we are much less confident about 
the timing and near-term return on the current level of 
investment.
Until that gap narrows, we see limited room for meaningful 
earnings upgrades.
2. Core growth may not be sufficient to absorb the 
investment cycle
Tencent's core businesses remain healthy, but they are no 
longer growing quickly enough to fully offset the rising AI 
cost base.

Gaming growth is normalizing. Advertising remains strong, 
but is still sensitive to the China's macroeconomic 
environment. Meanwhile, increased R&D and infrastructure 
spending is creating material pressure below the revenue 
line.
We therefore see a meaningful probability that CY2027 
becomes an earnings trough year.
Importantly, we do not need revenue to deteriorate 
significantly for earnings to disappoint. A combination of 
high-single-digit revenue growth, elevated depreciation, 
higher AI operating costs, and persistently high capex 
could be enough to keep EPS and FCF expectations under 
pressure.
That is why we think the near-term risk remains skewed 
toward further estimate normalization rather than rapid 
upgrades.
3. FCF could matter more than EPS
We increasingly prefer to look at Tencent's investment cycle 
through free cash flow rather than headline EPS.
Capex is currently exceptionally high, and compute-related 
prepayments add another layer of cash consumption. As 
long as these investments continue growing materially faster 
than operating cash flow, the market is unlikely to give 
Tencent full valuation credit for potential future AI revenues.
This is particularly important because Tencent has historically 
been a highly cash-generative business.
A temporary reduction in FCF to fund an attractive new 
opportunity is not, by itself, concerning. But there is a key 
difference between temporary investment and structurally 
higher capital intensity.
That distinction is not yet clear enough.
For us, therefore, peak capex is likely to be a more important 
stock-price catalyst than a single quarter of better-than-
expected EPS.
Long term: We remain bullish on Tencent's competitive 
position
Our near-term caution should not be confused with a 
bearish structural view.
We remain constructive precisely because Tencent possesses 
assets that are very difficult for competitors to replicate.
Weixin is more than a messaging platform
Tencent's greatest AI advantage may ultimately be 
distribution rather than the foundation model itself. Weixin 
gives Tencent direct access to a very large installed user 
base and an ecosystem spanning messaging, content, 
Mini Programs, payments, advertising, and commerce. 
This means Tencent does not necessarily need to build 
a completely separate AI destination and then acquire 
hundreds of millions of users. It can bring AI to where its 
users already are.
That distinction could become increasingly important 
as foundation-model capabilities commoditize. If model 
differentiation narrows, the competitive battleground 
shifts toward distribution, proprietary context, application 
ecosystems, and monetization. These are areas where 
Tencent is very well positioned.
AI does not need to become a standalone profit pool
We also believe the market may be asking the wrong 
question when it focuses exclusively on direct revenues from 
Tencent's AI products.
Our longer-term thesis does not require Tencent to become 
China's dominant standalone foundation-model provider. 
AI can create economic value throughout the existing 
ecosystem:
• Bbetter recommendation and conversion in advertising;
• Productivity improvements in game development;
• Stronger engagement and content creation within 
Weixin;
• Incremental enterprise cloud workloads;
• AI agents connected to Mini Programs and payments;
• Customer-service and productivity applications;
• Eventually, agent-driven transactions.
The acceleration of cloud revenue in 3QCY26 to around 
35% y/y is encouraging in this context. It is still too early to 
extrapolate this growth indefinitely, but it provides an initial 
indication that AI infrastructure investment can translate 
into incremental commercial demand.
Its competitive position could actually become more 
valuable in an AI world
There is also a broader strategic argument. If foundation 
models become increasingly comparable in capability, 
value should migrate toward companies controlling users, 
proprietary data, transactions, and distribution. Tencent has 
all four. This is why we separate our view of Tencent's 
earnings cycle from our view of its competitive position.
The earnings cycle has become more challenging. The 
competitive position has not.
If anything, Tencent's ability to combine AI with Weixin, 
games, payments, advertising, and cloud could make that 
competitive position more valuable over time.

Valuation: Attractive is not the same as a catalyst
Following the correction in the share price, valuation has 
become increasingly interesting. But we would distinguish 
between valuation support and a rerating catalyst.
A cheap stock can stay cheap while earnings estimates are 
falling. In the near term, we believe investors are likely to 
require evidence that earnings expectations have bottomed 
before assigning Tencent a materially higher multiple. For 
that reason, we would not chase valuation alone.
What would make us more bullish?
We see four indicators as particularly important over the 
next few quarters.
1. Peak capex. A stabilization or decline in quarterly cash 
capex would be the clearest signal that the most painful 
phase of the investment cycle is approaching an end.
2. AI monetization. We want to see evidence that AI 
adoption is moving beyond usage into economics: 
paid enterprise adoption, incremental cloud revenue, 
advertising uplift, and eventually transaction-related 
monetization.
3. Earnings revisions bottoming. The stock does not 
necessarily need earnings growth to reaccelerate 
immediately. It does, however, need the market to 
become confident that numbers have stopped falling.
4. Resilience of the competitive position. Advertising 
market-share gains, Weixin engagement, gaming 
durability, and cloud growth will tell us whether the core 
ecosystem remains healthy while Tencent goes through 
this investment cycle.
The ideal inflection would therefore be AI revenues 
accelerating while incremental AI spending begins to 
decelerate. That is when Tencent changes from an 
investment-cycle story back into an earnings-growth story.
Investment conclusion: Patience required
Tencent's 3QCY26 results make us more cautious about the 
next several quarters, but not less bullish on the company's 
long-term competitive position.
We believe the current AI investment cycle will continue 
weighing on margins, EPS, and FCF. There is still insufficient 
visibility on how quickly incremental AI revenue will offset 
the rapidly expanding cost base. As a result, earnings 
expectations could remain under pressure and the shares 
may lack a clear near-term rerating catalyst.
But we would not interpret this as structural deterioration.
Tencent still owns one of China's strongest digital 
ecosystems. Weixin provides unmatched distribution; 
gaming, advertising, and payments generate substantial 
monetization; cloud provides infrastructure; and Tencent has 
the balance sheet and cash-generation capacity required to 
invest through the AI cycle.
Our key concern is therefore duration, not direction.
We are cautious about how long Tencent's earnings reset 
may last, but constructive about where the company 
emerges on the other side.
This leaves us with a deliberately patient stance: Near-term 
pain, long-term competitive position intact.
We would become more constructive as earnings revisions 
bottom and capex approaches its peak. And if further share-
price weakness is driven mainly by earnings normalization 
rather than evidence of competitive deterioration, we would 
increasingly view that weakness as a long-term buying 
opportunity.
The main risk to this thesis is straightforward: AI spending 
remains structurally high but fails to generate adequate 
monetization. In that scenario, what we currently regard as 
a temporary FCF trough would instead represent a lasting 
reduction in Tencent's return on invested capital. That is the 
debate investors should focus on over the next 12 months, 
in our view.

Equities Investment Risk
Stock market returns are difficult to forecast because of fluctuations in the economy, investor psychology, geopolitical 
conditions and other important variables. UBS does and seeks to do business with companies included in its research reports. 
Investors should therefore be aware that the firm may have a conflict of interest that could affect the objectivity of this 
report. Investors should consider this report as only a single factor in making their investment decision.
CIO Equity Research Approach
Effective 1 November 2026, CIO does not rate or rank equity securities in the Most Preferred, Bellwether (in the US), 
and Least Preferred framework or publish “top picks” or similar ratings. Instead, CIO seeks to synthesize UBS Investment 
Bank (IB) Global Research issued by our affiliate and evaluate its implications from the perspective of Wealth Management 
investors highlighting relevant stock opportunities from the broad universe covered by IB Global Research as CIO Selected, 
while placing greater emphasis on portfolio-level analysis through CIO Equity Portfolios. For more information, see
Equity methodology, published on 28 October 2026. The description of “CIO Selected” as a designation rather than a 
rating does not affect the application of any legal or regulatory requirement governing investment research, investment 
recommendations or information recommending or suggesting an investment strategy where that requirement otherwise 
applies.
CIO Selected Designation and CIO Selection Lists
CIO Selected stocks are intended to support the UBS House View (see global asset class preferences definitions) and are 
chosen based on their overall risk-return profiles and other relevant considerations. The starting universe for the CIO Selected 
designation generally consists of securities covered by IB Global Research and rated Buy or Neutral. From that universe, CIO 
Equities applies its own investment judgment, considering a variety of factors such as the fundamental outlook, expected 
performance relative to an identified benchmark, market, sector and industry trends, risk and return characteristics, downside 
considerations, diversification benefits, and other relevant factors. No single factor is necessarily determinative. Rather, CIO 
considers these factors collectively and holistically in determining which securities warrant inclusion in a CIO Equity Selection 
List.
In certain circumstances, CIO may designate a security that is not covered by IB Global Research as CIO Selected where 
CIO determines that the security presents a relevant investment opportunity and applicable eligibility, reasonable-basis and 
approval requirements for such selections have been satisfied. A security will no longer be eligible for inclusion in any CIO 
Selection List if IB Global Research changes its rating to Sell. In such cases, CIO Equities will remove it from applicable 
Selection Lists within a reasonable timeframe.
• The CIO Selected designation is not a rating.
• Inclusion reflects CIO Equities' current preference within the defined universe and objective of the applicable Selected List.
• It does not create a separate Buy, Neutral, or Sell rating, does not establish a CIO Equities price target, and does not 
supersede or modify the underlying IB Global Research rating or price target.
