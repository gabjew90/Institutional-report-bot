Strong growth supports high rates 
We expect Treasury yields to stay high through H127 as the Fed completes its 
tightening cycle. The front end should lead the move, reflecting the additional rate 
hikes and a period of restrictive policy. Our house view remains that the Fed will 
deliver two additional 25bp hikes, in December and March, taking the upper bound 
of the Fed Funds rate to 4.50%. 
The labour market remains in good shape, with the downside risks that prompted 
Fed rate cuts in late 2025 having largely faded (Figure 1). October payrolls rose 
165k, comfortably above expectations, while the unemployment rate edged lower 
despite higher labour force participation. More broadly, labour market conditions 
remain characterised by a low-hire, low-fire environment. Hiring has moderated 
from the rapid pace seen earlier in the cycle, but layoffs remain limited, keeping 
unemployment broadly stable. Our economists expect the unemployment rate to 
average around 4.2% in 2027. 
In the current economic and policy environment, a strong labour market does not 
necessarily imply tighter policy. Rather, continued labour market resilience allows 
the Fed to remain focused on inflation. As long as unemployment remains low and 
labour market conditions broadly balanced, inflation is likely to remain the dominant 
driver of policy decisions, in our view.

Elevated inflation pushes 2Y towards 5.00% 
Recent inflation data have reinforced the Fed’s cautious stance. Core CPI rose 
0.3% MoM in October, while the three-month annualised core inflation rate 
accelerated to 3.75% from 3.41% (Figure 2). Despite the recent upside surprise, 
our inflation strategists expect gradual improvement over the coming year. 
Headline inflation is expected to remain in the mid-3% range through Q127 before 
easing later in the year. Core inflation is projected to remain within a 2.7-2.9% 
range through early 2027 before moderating further. 
The outlook remains consistent with a Fed that prioritises inflation risks. 
Policymakers have repeatedly emphasised that they require greater confidence 
inflation is moving sustainably towards 2% before considering policy easing. 
Against that policy backdrop, we expect the 2Y Treasury yield to rise towards 
5.00% by mid-2027. Front-end yields are likely to remain sensitive to inflation data 
and Fed communication, particularly as markets continue to debate the terminal 
policy rate. 
While markets price a more aggressive tightening cycle, we believe the Fed can 
stop at 4.50% if inflation evolves broadly in line with our forecasts. Looking further 
ahead, improving inflation should allow the policy debate to shift from additional 
tightening towards the timing of eventual rate cuts later in 2027.

10Y yield to stay high, with term premium elevated 
The long end should also remain under upward pressure. We expect the 10Y 
Treasury yield to rise to around 5.15% by H127, supported by elevated inflation, 
sizeable Treasury financing needs and continued issuance (Figure 3). Term 
premium should remain relatively elevated as investors price high Treasury supply 
and a slower return of inflation towards target (Figure 4). 
Fig 3. US rate forecast (%) 
 
Source: Crédit Agricole CIB 
Further rise in yields cannot be ruled out if inflation surprises to the upside. 
However, we believe already high outright yield levels, improving inflation, and 
potential asset allocation trades from equities to bonds should ultimately limit the 
extent of the sell-off. 
We expect Treasury yields to decline during H227 as inflation moderation allows 
the Fed to transition from tightening towards easing. The front end should lead the 
rally, producing a steeper curve. The 10Y Treasury yield is expected to move back 
to around 5.00% by late 2027 and early 2028. Additional easing in early 2028 
should support further declines in yields across the curve.

Source: Crédit Agricole CIB 
Risks abound 
AI worries front and centre. Flight-to-quality trades from AI-related sell-off can 
help Treasuries. There have been concerns that the pace of AI-related data center 
investment could slow under a more restrictive regulatory environment after the 
mid-term elections, creating a potential headwind for growth and capital spending, 
from which Treasuries can benefit. 
The recent correction in US technology stocks highlights how quickly market 
sentiment can shift when expectations become stretched. Concerns over AI-
related growth have intensified that revenue growth may be tracking below earlier 
projections, triggering sharp declines in semiconductor stocks and broader 
weakness across equity markets.

For rates, the macro and market backdrop has created tension between two 
opposing forces. On one hand, inflation data has been hotter than expected, 
prompting markets to price a more hawkish Fed path. On the other hand, the risk-
off environment has generated demand for safe-haven assets, making 10Y 
Treasury yields near 5% increasingly attractive. As a result, Treasuries caught 
between persistent inflation concerns that increase yields and flight-to-quality flows 
that lower rates. 
Fed policy. Our house view has two rate hikes by March 2027. If this forecast is 
correct, it would be positive for risk sentiment, particularly as markets have priced 
in a more hawkish path. Rate cuts in late 2027 would be even more supportive, 
though this hinges on inflation moderating. Inflation remains the key variable – if it 
declines, the Fed has room to be less hawkish. 
Geopolitics. De-escalation in either the Russia-Ukraine or US-Iran wars would 
serve all parties’ interests and would be positive for risk assets, particularly through 
lower energy prices, which can put downward pressure on front-end US rates. 
However, the outlook remains uncertain: Ukraine-Russia peace talks are active but 
appear deeply stalled, with a wide gap between Russia’s demands and Ukraine’s 
security requirements. On the US-Iran side, the June MOU has not held, and the 
Strait of Hormuz remains a flashpoint. 
Curve to flatten before steepening 
We expect the Treasury curve to continue flattening through H127 as front-end 
yields rise faster than long-end yields during the final phase of the Fed tightening 
cycle. The key driver is the move in the 2Y yield towards 5.00%. As markets price 
remaining Fed hikes, front-end rates should experience greater upward pressure 
than longer maturities. We expect the 2-10Y curve to reach its flattest level around 
mid-2027, broadly coinciding with the end of the hiking cycle (Figure 5). 
By H227, the curve should begin to stabilise as investors gain confidence that the 
Fed is done tightening. Thereafter, the curve is likely to steepen as inflation 
improves and markets begin pricing eventual policy easing.

Source: Crédit Agricole CIB, Bloomberg 
Swap spreads to widen modestly 
We expect swap spreads to widen modestly in 2027, implying that Treasury 
securities outperform swaps (Figure 6). The outlook is supported by regulatory 
tailwinds, including SLR and LCR reforms, alongside the Federal Reserve's 
commitment to maintaining ample reserves, both of which should support bank 
demand for Treasuries and balance-sheet capacity. 
Treasury supply remains an important consideration. We expect coupon auction 
sizes to remain unchanged near term before increasing in H227, primarily in the 
2-7Y sector. While additional front-end supply could weigh on spreads in shorter 
maturities, a strategy that concentrates issuance increases in the front end while 
keeping long-end auction sizes relatively stable would support long-end Treasuries 
and favour wider 10Y-30Y swap spreads. As a result, we expect the swap spread
