USD: Sharp turnaround in AI investor sentiment lifts USD to fresh highs 
The US dollar has continued to trade at stronger levels overnight after the dollar index 
rose to a fresh year-to high yesterday at 104.53. The US dollar was lifted by the 
outsized rebound in US AI-related stocks which reversed the heavy sell-off at the end 
of last week. The Nasdaq composite index closed higher on the day by around 6.5% 
and extended its rebound from the low point on Friday to 7.8%. The sharp turnaround 
in investor sentiment was triggered by the announcement from President Trump that 
Nvidia had reinstated Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang just 11 days after he 
resigned following revelations of his affairs with a suspected Chinese spy and a direct 
report. The decision to reinstate Jensen Huang was welcomed by investors in the 
company resulting in Nvidia’s share price surging higher by 31.3%, and returning to 
levels before Jensen Huang’s resignation on 12th November. The intervention from 
Presdeint Trump who dialled into an Nvidia board meeting to argue for Jensen Huang’s 
return also sends a clear signal that the President is will to get involved in the running 
of the company if required which is helping to support investor confidence as well given

The US dollar benefitted both from the improvement in investor sentiment towards AI-
related investments in the US, and to a lesser extent the pick-up in US yields. The 2-
year US Treasury yield has risen by around 4bps as market participants have become 
more confident that the Fed will press ahead with plans to continue tightening policy if 
the recent sell-off in US stocks is reversed. Once of the reason why the Fed kept rates 
on hold in October was the tightening in financial conditions particularly for AI-related 
companies, and financial conditions have since tightened further. At the same time, 
energy prices have since dropped significantly with the average price of gasoline down 
by around -14.5% since late September. While the Fed remains on course to hike 
again in December, the recent tightening of financial conditions and lower energy 
prices casts some doubt on expectations for further multiple hikes in 2027.  
New York Fed President Williams spoke again overnight providing a timely update on 
the Fed’s current policy thinking. He reiterated that the Fed still has “work to do” to 
ensure that they get inflation back to their goal in a “timely manner and on a sustained 
basis”. He said that recent data showed that the US economy “remained strong and 
that inflation has continued to run too high”. When asked he added that “I would not 
exclude moving at consecutive meetings at all if that was what was needed” and would 
be watching for stronger underlying economic strength and further gasoline price hikes 
contributing to inflation pressures. When asked about recent developments in financial 
markets, he stated that he is watching financial markets “very closely”. He added that 
“the optimism and enthusiasm in the AI sector have been big drivers of the US 
economy of late. If that were to change in a material and sustained way, I think it would 
affect my outlook”.                
 
EUR: President Macron considering proposing fiscal rule 
The euro has continued to weaken at the start of this week falling to afresh year-to-
date low at 1.0881. The pair has now fallen back into the 1.0500-1.1000 range that 
was in place between 2023 and early 2025 following the energy price shock in 2022. 
During that period the pair averaged just below the 1.0800-level. Absent another 
negative shock for euro area, we do not expect the pair to break below the current 
lower trading range. We are more optimistic that the negative impacts on the euro from 
the US-Iran conflict and heightened fiscal risks in the euro area will ease in the year 
ahead helping it to gradually rebound.  
After widening sharply in September and October, yield spreads between French and 
German government bonds have stabilized at elevated levels this month. The much 
wider spreads continue to highlight that market participants remains wary of fiscal risks 
in France ahead of next year’s President elections. It was reported yesterday that 
French President Macron is considering pursuing a so-called golden rule that would 
require the government to set out a multi-year path toward balanced public finances, 
particularly if markets remain volatile in January. In an interview with Le Figaro 
newspaper he stated “this is not an unjustified debate”. The proposal would require 
lawmakers from the National Assembly and the Senate to approve a constitutional 
change by a three-fifths majority. It sets a high hurdle to adopt the golden fiscal rule 
requiring support across the political spectrum. RN leader Le Pen reportedly told 
France Inter radio over the weekend that she backed the idea. Former Presdeint 
Nicolas Sarkozy previously attempted and failed to pass similar legislation back in 
2011 that would have established multi-year budget frameworks with spending ceilings 
and minimum revenue levels. For the euro the adoption of a golden fiscal rule is one 
potential positive catalyst.
