Markets are consolidating yesterday’s powerful snapback rally in equities. That’s hardly 
surprising, given the nearly 5% gain in the S&P500 and 6½% Nasdaq rise. Futures are 
down by almost 1% and about 1¾% for the S&P and Nasdaq respectively. Just as Asian 
equities lagged by a day when they sold off on Monday, they lagged by a day in rallying 
strongly overnight and they’ll lag tonight if today is a down session.  
Yesterday’s rally was roughly 50–50 between pre- and post-headlines on the 
reinstatement of Nvidia’s CEO. What got revealed was a combination of the value 
attached to being a national champion with explicit support from the administration over 
and above other legitimate concerns such as high valuations across the tech space, 
coming Congressional oversight, national security, internal culture that may affect the 
Board and some in senior management, and sundry other concerns about the 
intervention. 
Sovereign bonds are rallying by single digits with US Ts leading via about a 5bps drop 
across 2s through 10s, alongside smaller rallies across Europe. CGBs are rallying but 
underperforming US Ts. The dollar is mixed. Oil prices are little changed this morning. 
WILL BOC’S MACKLEM MAKE OR BREAK DECEMBER PRICING? 
Governor Macklem’s speech will be available at 12:25pmET today when the embargoed 
release to journalists ends. It’s going to be titled “Outlook for the Canadian economy and 
the challenges facing monetary policy.” There will be moderated Q&A after he begins 
speaking at about 12:40pmET and it will be followed by a press conference at 2pmET. It’s 
the last formal communication before the BoC’s pre-decision blackout begins next 
Wednesday. He will speak again on December 15th which questions what he’ll have to say 
that’s new by then.  
Markets are presently pricing about 75% odds of a hike on December 9th. If Macklem has 
something to say that would influence expectations, then now’s his last chance to do so. 
I would expect the tone to be generally hawkish. Deputy Governor Gravelle warned last 
week that “we’re seeing the spillover spread” in reference to the impact of higher 
commodity prices moving into core inflation.

US RELEASES TO BE A WARM-UP FOR TOMORROW’S ONSLAUGHT 
Pre-holiday cramming of macro reports begins today and culminates in tomorrow’s more consequential download when PCE, spending 
and incomes, GDP revisions, durable goods orders, new home sales and the Fed’s Beige Book all get released. 
US consumer confidence (10amET) will be the main release for today. ADP’s weekly private payrolls (8:15amET) and repeat sale home prices 
for September (9amET) will be accompanied by the Richmond Fed’s manufacturing index (10amET). 
LIGHT OVERNIGHT DEVELOPMENTS 
Japan added its purchasing managers’ indices to the prior day’s releases when it refreshed its own for November overnight. The composite 
slipped by 0.6 points to 51.5, signalling slower overall economic expansion toward the 50-line that divides expansion from contraction. The 
driver was weaker growth in services (50.5, 51.7 prior) as manufacturing accelerated a touch (53.7, 53.3 prior). Japan’s PMIs were generally 
consistent with evidence of slowing in the week’s earlier global PMIs except for Germany. 
 
Germany added more soft data with its IFO business confidence reading for November. It rose by 0.5 points to 90.6 as both the current 
assessment and expectations components increased. That rounds out improvements in each of the ZEW investor sentiment gauge and the 
composite PMI.
