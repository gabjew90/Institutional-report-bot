U.S. Markets Recap 
US +4.73% | Nasdaq +6.51% | Dow +3.01%
Synopsis

US equities finished sharply higher on Monday, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq posting their best sessions since 9 April 2025, the Trump 
tariff pause. Breadth was strongly positive, with gainers outnumbering decliners by more than 5:1, although the equal-weight S&P lagged 
the cap-weighted index by more than 200 bp. The rebound began as a bounce off Friday's lows led by the AI trade and accelerated on news 
that Nvidia's board had reinstated CEO Jensen Huang. Positioning, volatility and sentiment dynamics were also cited as tailwinds, though 
the credibility of the bounce remains up for debate given an already hawkish-leaning Fed. Treasuries were mixed with the curve flattening. 
The dollar index rose 0.5%, gold finished up 4.0% and WTI crude settled down 3.7%, just above $80 a barrel. 

The November flash composite PMI came in at 54.6 against a 56.1 consensus, with services at 54.7 against 56.4. The $69B 2-year note 
auction stopped through by 0.3 bp on solid foreign demand. Core PCE inflation is the highlight of a holiday-shortened week, with markets 
closed Thursday for Thanksgiving.


Semiconductors, memory and neocloud names led, with software, tech hardware and airlines also outperforming, while energy, staples, 
utilities and REITs lagged and managed care fell on CMS rule updates. Nvidia rose 31.5% and Kymera Therapeutics 30.4% after a phase 2B 
atopic dermatitis trial met all endpoints. Zscaler gained 17.7% on CrowdStrike's roughly $43.9B all-stock offer, which sent CrowdStrike 
down 8.9%, and Monro fell 11.8% after suspending its dividend. 
Macro

Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang as president and CEO
Nvidia announced that its board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and CEO, effective immediately, after discus-
sions in which the board was convinced of his candor and ability to lead. The board said it received input from senior levels of the United 
States government and is convinced the reinstatement poses no national-security risk, having previously said it was not interested in rehir-
ing him. The move comes against recent reporting of connections between Huang's mistress and China's Ministry of State Security (NY 
Times). Over the weekend Treasury Secretary Bessent did not rule out Huang's return and President Trump repeated his call for reinstate-
ment. Analysts were broadly positive: Oppenheimer upgraded Nvidia to Outperform from Perform, Melius upgraded to Buy from Hold, 
Cantor reiterated Overweight and added the stock back to its Top Picks, and B. Riley called a bottom in the SOX. Regulatory overhangs 
remain, with Goldman flagging DOJ/SEC probes and KeyBanc maintaining Sector Weight, and Huang is still scheduled to appear before 
multiple congressional committees. 

US flash PMI slows in November as new orders and prices cool
The November flash composite PMI fell to 54.6 versus 56.1 consensus and October's 55.9, the lowest since Jul-26. The manufacturing PMI 
slipped to 54.5 versus 55.0 consensus and 54.9 prior, a three-month low, while the services PMI eased to 54.7 versus 56.4 consensus and 
56.1 prior, the lowest in four months. New orders rose but growth slowed for a second straight month to its weakest since July, weighed 
down by softer services demand and declining exports. Employment growth moderated for a second month but remained the joint-third 
strongest in over four years, with manufacturing hiring particularly robust. Input-cost and selling-price inflation both cooled, helped by 
lower fuel prices, but remained elevated, while supplier delivery times lengthened to the greatest extent since Aug-22 amid global shipping 
disruptions. 

Strategists bet on Fed rate hikes amid policy-mistake concerns
The AI-driven market selloff, concerns about Nvidia scaling back its financing support and a slower data center buildout after the midterms 
are driving talk of a Fed policy mistake, even as officials reaffirm their support for further rate hikes. Waller reiterated the potential for a 
50 bp move in December. Treasury Secretary Bessent lent his voice to those arguing the Fed risks overtightening, pointing to a substantial 
tightening of financial conditions in recent months (Bloomberg). Market-implied odds of three Fed rate hikes by Mar-2027 have fallen lately 
amid the volatility, but strategists retain conviction that the Fed will stay the course in leaning against inflation and argue for curve flattener 
trades, with long-end bonds seen outperforming as markets price in the growth impact of rate hikes (Bloomberg). 

Global crude exports hit a record, but longer routes keep costs high
Nikkei cited Kpler data showing global crude oil exports reached 47.53M barrels a day in October, the highest on record going back to 2013. 
Daily throughput remains above 40M barrels in November, compared with 36.69M in May after Iran effectively blocked the Strait of Hor-
muz. Broader Middle East supplies have recovered sharply since September on growth from ports outside the strait and more ships passing 
through with identification systems turned off to avoid attacks from Iran, while exports from South America rose 24% in October versus 
pre-war levels in February, North Africa 23% and North America 7%. The article noted a significant easing in crude markets, reflecting 
expectations for talks to end the war after the Trump administration refrained from renewed attacks following the US midterm elections. 
Front-month Brent futures have declined to the lowest since August-end, but costs remain elevated because of war insurance shipping 
premiums and longer transport distances from detours around the strait. 

Copper selloff raises growth concerns, though other factors in play
Copper prices are down nearly 20% from the September peak, prompting talk that the selloff may be an indicator of slowing economic 
growth, though it could also foreshadow easing inflation pressures. There are some signs of moderating manufacturing momentum: the 
October S&P Global PMI eased month on month with new order growth the weakest since March, October ISM Manufacturing ticked higher 
but respondent sentiment deteriorated for a third straight month, and last week's November Philly Fed Index was well below consensus. 
UBS noted China construction and investment data also remain weak, but argued the selloff more likely reflects the AI trade unwind, hawk-
ish Fed repricing and dollar strength than weaker demand. Traders are shipping copper from the US to China as tariff expectations fade, 
potentially unwinding large US stockpiles (FT, Bloomberg), and UBS flagged sustained outflows as the key downside risk while remaining 
constructive longer term amid supply constraints.

European Markets Recap 
Germany +2.1% | France +1.5% | UK +1.3%
Synopsis

European equity markets closed sharply higher on Monday, near best levels, following the steepest weekly drop since March. Technology, 
industrials and miners were the best performers, with 16 of 19 sectors recovering ground, while autos and defensives lagged. ASML, ASM 
and BESI led as metals recovered. The rebound was largely attributed to contrarian signaling from positioning, volatility and sentiment 
dynamics, though its sustainability was debated. German Bund and French OAT 10-year yields were flat, the OAT-Bund spread was around 
150bps, and UK 10-year Gilt yields fell 5bps. The dollar index rose 0.6% and gold gained 4.3%. 

Flash PMIs were the focus: the eurozone composite reading missed expectations for an uptick, coming in unchanged, as manufacturing beat 
but services missed, while the UK PMI dipped on a services slowdown. ECB's Simkus said a December hike may not suffice; that hike is 
almost fully priced, while the market is split on whether the BoE moves in December or February. The EU proposed cuts to its long-term 
budget to reach an agreement by year-end. 

ArcelorMittal rose 6% on reports it is seeking a price increase of about €60/t for European hot-rolled coil, Drax gained 5.2% after saying 
2026 adjusted core profit would exceed consensus, and ABB rose 4.6% on its Ziehl-Abegg acquisition. Autos were the weakest sector after 
BNP Paribas Exane downgraded the group to underweight. Crest Nicholson fell 29.6% after guiding to an FY adjusted operating loss of £30-
40M versus about £10M previously, and Nemetschek fell 6.1% on a JPMorgan downgrade. 
Macro

Eurozone composite PMI steady at 51.7 as German strength offsets French slump
The flash Eurozone composite PMI was steady at 51.7 in November, matching October and extending modest private-sector growth for a 
fifth month, but missing the 51.9 consensus. Manufacturing output accelerated to a two-month high of 53.3 while services growth eased to 
a five-month low of 51.2. The headline masked the widest Germany-France divergence in six years (Bloomberg): Germany's composite was 
the strongest since Mar-2022 at 55.0 versus 53.3 consensus, supported by government spending on infrastructure, defence and AI-related 
investment, while France's composite declined to a six-month low of 46.1 versus 48.1 consensus on weak domestic demand, political un-
certainty and fiscal concerns (Reuters). New orders rose only slightly, employment was broadly stable and business confidence dipped to 
its lowest since May. Input and output price inflation eased but remained sharp, and surveys still signal inflation well above the ECB's 2% 
target, supporting expectations of a December hike. 

UK composite PMI slows to a two-month low as services ease
The UK composite PMI for November fell to a two-month low of 52.3 versus consensus of 52.9 and a prior reading of 52.9, with the services 
sector easing to a five-month low of 52.0 versus 53.0 forecast and 52.8 prior. The drag from services overwhelmed resilience in manufac-
turing, where the PMI eased to a two-month low of 52.7 versus 53.0 forecast, though the manufacturing output index hit a 27-month high 
of 54.2. S&P Global said sustained levels of business growth point to robust quarterly growth of 0.6%, with services providers signaling the 
weakest rise in activity since the current expansion began. Average prices charged increased at a historically strong pace despite input costs 
rising at the slowest pace in three months, and demand for labour remained cautious. 

ECB's Simkus says a December hike may fall short
ECB's Simkus (Lithuania, hawk) said a 25 bp rate hike in December may not be enough to fully contain inflation, though a follow-up hike 
is not urgent because second-round effects are building only gradually (Reuters). With inflation at 4%, markets assign an 87% probability 
to a December hike, taking the deposit rate to 2.75% from 2.50%, and discount about 60 bps of additional tightening by late 2027, with the 
path peaking near 3.10-3.11%. Simkus said further action depends a great deal on the outcome of wage negotiations; negotiated wages 
edged up to 2.57% in Q3 and labour costs rose to 3.5% y/y. VP Vujcic, Nagel and Chief Economist Lane have backed a December increase, 
Pereira warned that energy-driven price pressures are still building, and Panetta (Italy, dove) cautioned against over-zealous tightening 
(Bloomberg). 

Macron weighs enshrining a public spending golden rule in the constitution
French President Macron is considering convening Congress, a joint session of both houses of Parliament, to enshrine a golden rule on 
public spending in the constitution (Le Monde), reviving a 2011 amendment that was voted in identical terms by both houses under Presi-
dent Sarkozy but stalled ahead of the 2012 presidential election. An unnamed Macron advisor told Politico the Elysée is looking at the move 
to demonstrate the country's ability to maintain sound public finances over the long term, although opponents think he is setting a trap for 
presidential rivals who have floated their own constitutional spending limits. A constitutional reform requires a referendum or a three-
fifths majority of both chambers. A former aide to an MP said such a measure could pass by as many as 20 votes if all centrists and con-
servatives back it and National Rally abstained. Le Pen said she would wait to see details, Retailleau backed the idea while questioning its 
timing, and the left is likely to oppose. 

UK and EU announce a joint agreement worth up to £17B a year
The UK and EU announced a joint agreement worth up to £17B a year covering food and drink, carbon markets and youth mobility, with 
further discussions to take place on tuition fees and the EU's Made in Europe scheme (Guardian). Reports before the announcement sug-
gested implementation was contingent on the UK lowering tuition fees for EU students. The government will bring forward legislation next

Asian Markets Recap 
Japan closed (holiday) | Hong Kong -0.12% | China +0.15%
Synopsis

Asian equities were largely weaker on Monday, although many benchmarks recovered from their worst intraday levels as investors digested 
market-support measures after a brutal rout last week in nearly all major markets except India. South Korea's Kospi nearly halved its slump 
to close 3.3% lower, its lowest level since January. Mainland China rose, with bigger gains in Shenzhen-listed stocks, while Hong Kong 
finished marginally lower after paring early losses. Taiwan outperformed, Japan was closed for a holiday, and India fell sharply after a 
stellar run this month. Gold and silver recovered following heavy Friday declines. 

Policy support was the main driver: South Korea said it would deploy market stabilization funds immediately if conditions worsened, Taiwan 
banned short selling after the Taiex's worst week in 35 years, and China will cut its minimum margin requirement for leveraged investors 
to 80% from 100% from 30-Nov. South Korea's exports in the first 20 days of November more than doubled, and Singapore's October core 
inflation slowed to its lowest in five months. 

GS Engineering & Construction fell 12.6% and Samsung SDS also lost heavily after President Trump threatened a “tremendous” tax on US 
firms building data centers overseas. SK Hynix fell 2.5% after The Information reported SoftBank's Son had approached banks about a $1T 
buyout, which SoftBank said he was no longer considering. Shanghai SUNMI Technology jumped 48.6% on a shift in strategic focus to 
Commercial Physical AI. Want Want China fell 15.5% on H1 results and Leapmotor fell 13% as Q3 revenue missed estimates. The Hang 
Seng Tech Index will expand to 50 constituents from 7-Dec. 
Macro

Asia stock market stabilization measures broaden
Bloomberg recapped the latest policy announcements in Asia to support equities. China will lower its minimum margin ratio from 100% to 
80% effective 30-Nov, estimated to free up some CNY400B ($59.58B) in incremental funds (Xinhua), while the Shanghai and Shenzhen 
stock exchanges are waiving participant business unit usage fees, with a two-year extension of existing waivers for stock listing and bond 
transactions, and Shenzhen is looking at introducing market makers in ChiNext. South Korean officials met Sunday to discuss possible 
spillover from the US market selloff and prepare contingency plans (Bloomberg); the government will immediately begin preparations for 
a stock market stabilization fund with capital raised by financial institutions, size unspecified, though the article recalled a target of 
KRW10.7T ($7.95B) in 2020 and that funds were last deployed in 2008 (Chosun). Daily buyback limits will be eased for listed companies 
for three months. Taiwan imposed a blanket ban on short selling below the previous session closing price effective Monday (Bloomberg), 
the third move this month on short sales, and also raised the minimum margin requirement for short sales to 150% from 130% and sus-
pended applications to list new put warrants, with hedging trades among securities and futures firms exempt. The National Financial Sta-
bilization Fund disclosed it has been supporting Taiwan stocks. 

Nvidia server suppliers halt Southeast Asia shipments amid US curbs
Nikkei reported that at least two Nvidia server partners have suspended shipments to clients in Southeast Asia amid the latest US export 
controls. Suppliers in the Nvidia server supply chain are said to have been holding emergency meetings and reviewing orders amid growing 
uncertainty over the demand outlook beyond mid-2028. A third server partner noted that approvals to ship Blackwell chips to Southeast 
Asia clients were already becoming increasingly difficult before the latest controls were announced last Monday. One supplier has halted 
all shipments to the region from Friday in compliance with the restrictions and stopped taking new orders from new clients for now, another 
halted shipments after taking advice from its legal team even though the products are legally compliant, and a third continues to ship but 
will apply for a new export license for orders placed after 19-Nov. Industry sources said business plans for the current Vera Rubin and Vera 
Rubin Ultra in 2027 are mostly unchanged, but there is more uncertainty for the next-generation Feynman architecture as specifications 
and capacity plans from 2028 remain unclear. 

RBI and BOK seen tightening; Korean exports surge
