Get paid to be patient
At some point - possibly in 2027 - the balance of risks will shift from inflation to
growth, and it will pay to hold duration. In the meantime, fixed income investors
should take advantage of the carry the market is offering.

Top fixed income convictions:
If you take one message away from this fixed income outlook, it should be: don’t try to time the turning
point for duration.
Duration’s time will come. But not only is market timing a notoriously unreliable endeavour, there’s also
little incentive to take such a risk. Yields today are a lot higher than they have been over most of the
last two decades. 
As we head into 2027, therefore, investors should focus on generating high-quality carry while keeping a
watchful eye for opportunities to extend duration. The extra carry on offer compared to recent years
gives investors a cushion to absorb further volatility, which we certainly anticipate. Indeed, recent stock
market action is a reminder that market conditions can and do change quickly, and that it pays to stay
prepared.   
More yield, less hunting
Short-dated credit: less sensitive to further increases in yields, and less exposed to the AI theme.
Select emerging markets: can offer attractive carry with better fiscal dynamics than some developed

Higher for longer, but not necessarily forever
A big reason we advocate patience is that yields could still move higher from here. In the US, inflation
remains stubbornly above the Federal Reserve’s target. Economic growth has also shown resilience so
far, suggesting further interest rate hikes may be needed to bring inflation down, while fiscal pressures
and concerns about bond market supply could also keep yields elevated.
History also suggests that investors have a habit of underestimating the scale of a hiking cycle at its
outset, adding a further note of caution.
Interest rates could go higher than you think
Source: Fidelity International, Deutsche Bank, Bloomberg, St Louis Fed, November 2026.
 
However, at some point in 2027, borrowing costs may start to weigh on economic activity. Keep in mind
too that the composition of US growth has shifted markedly towards AI and data centre investment over
the last few years. In other words, capex rather than consumption is centre stage, and capital spending
is famously prone to sharp, procyclical reversals when animal spirits turn cautious.
This is why we want to look for opportunities to add duration selectively. In the meantime, exposure to
high-quality short-dated credit, which is less sensitive to further increases in yields, offers a way to
generate income for a portfolio. 
“Persistent inflation pressures from higher commodity prices and geopolitics have created uncertainty
around central bank policy,” says Durance, “reinforcing our conviction in the short-duration part of the
curve, where investors can capture attractive income with less exposure to rising yields.”
Short-dated credit also tends to be less exposed to the AI theme, given that AI borrowing has been
concentrated at the long end.

AI credit supply is raising eyebrows
One example of credit dispersion came from Meta’s bond offerings at the start of November 2026. The
fundraising included the company’s first ever sale of euro-denominated bonds, issued during a period
of heightened market volatility. Despite strong demand, Meta issued the new debt at a notable
discount.
The episode illustrates both the ongoing enthusiasm for the AI trade and rising circumspection among
investors. Market participants have noted the implications for future funding needs of AI infrastructure
spending, and what that means for bond market supply now that hyperscalers have moved beyond
financing their AI capex from free cash flow.
AI capex revisions point to sustained funding needs

On the one hand, the Meta example shows there are opportunities in the current market to earn good
yields from solid investment-grade credits. On the other, the potential scale of future AI-related issuance
suggests it would also be prudent to look at how to hedge AI exposure by investing in other areas.
Meta’s November issue notwithstanding, credit spreads remain historically tight, and investors should
be sure they’re satisfied that the compensation on offer justifies the risk being taken.

Looking for 'anti-AI'
In the infrastructure space, assets like motorways and airports look more attractive right now than AI-
related infrastructure. Leverage tends to be lower, track records are longer, and there is much less
sensitivity to economic growth.
Regional dispersion meanwhile has created relative-value opportunities. French sovereign weakness
has led to an unusual inversion. At one point in October, almost 40 per cent of French investment-grade
corporate bonds traded at lower yields than comparable government debt. 
The spillover from the volatility in French government bonds has weighed on European investment
grade, creating selected opportunities in European banks, insurers, and corporate hybrids. And, of
course, it has improved the relative value of French (as well as Italian) government bonds compared to
high-grade corporate debt. Selective government-spread exposure appears more inviting than broad
corporate credit risk. However, we still retain a cautious outlook on Europe for 2027, given ongoing
political uncertainty and the likelihood of further rates volatility.
Emerging market debt meanwhile continues to offer attractive all-in yields relative to the underlying
risks. 
“Latin America benefits from high real yields and exposure to commodities, while in Europe and Africa
several countries have made progress in reducing fiscal vulnerabilities through stronger revenue
collection and greater expenditure discipline,” says portfolio manager Philip Fielding.
The Middle East has proved resilient in the face of the conflict in Iran, while initial concerns about
supply disruptions in Asia, as oil shipments were unable to transit the Persian Gulf, have largely proved
unfounded. Higher petrol, diesel, and jet fuel prices have generated some inflationary pressures, but
central banks have generally reacted prudently, limiting the risk of inflation expectations becoming de-
anchored.  
In many cases, emerging market sovereign fundamentals compare favourably with those of developed
market peers. 
Is the script flipping for EMs vs DMs?

Source: Fidelity International, Macrobond, Federal Reserve, October 2026.
 
Economic growth is higher, public debt burdens are generally lower, populations are younger, and
companies are more conservatively financed. This creates differentiated sources of return for investors
that should be accessed via a strategic allocation rather than a tactical approach. 
Finally, the technical backdrop remains supportive for the asset class with net bond supply relatively
low, particularly when compared with substantial issuance volumes in the developed market technology
sector. 
Active investors can afford to wait 
AI-related growth, persistent inflation stoked by the Iran conflict, and an oversupply of debt driven by AI
capex and government borrowing have made 2026 a challenging year for fixed income, underlining the
importance of being patient and selective. Those same challenges have also helped make fixed
income a more attractive asset class than it has been for some time. 
In the months ahead, credit dispersion will allow for selective risk taking as investors wait for more
clarity on inflation, the economy, monetary policy, and even geopolitics. Duration, as ever, remains
valuable as a protection against a sharp slowdown, although yields can still go higher from here. In the
new year, patience will be a virtue.
