SoFi Technologies Inc. (SOFI): Communacopia + Technology 2026 — Key 
Takeaways
8 September 2026 | 3:06PM EDT
 
Presenters: Chris Lapointe, CFO 
Bottom Line: Management highlighted SoFi’s strong momentum so far in 2026, 
delivering approximately 40% YoY revenue growth and 30% adjusted EBITDA 
margins in each of the first two quarters of the year. This performance translates to a 
Rule of 40 score of approximately 70, marking the company’s 20th consecutive 
quarter exceeding the Rule of 40. Key drivers of this momentum include the 
successful execution of the “everything app” strategy (achieving a record 51% 
cross-buy rate), dynamic balance sheet management, the expansion of the 
multi-product Loan Platform Business (LPB), and the scaling of high-margin 
fee-based revenue streams. 
Key Takeaways

Long-Term Growth Targets: Mgmt expressed high confidence in achieving its 
n
30% revenue growth CAGR target through 2028. 
Target ROTCE: Mgmt aims to reach a target ROTCE of 25% to 30% over the 
n

income margin (currently at 29% over the last 12 months and 30% implied in the 
forward guide) and scaling fee-based revenues to achieve a target 1:1 
revenue-to-average-tangible-equity ratio. 
Full-Year 2026 Outlook: Despite shifting macro assumptions from two rate cuts 
n

Equity Capital: Under its current operating plan, mgmt does not see any need to 
n

and a shift toward capital-light fee-based businesses.

Robust Consumer Health: Mgmt sees the member base as remaining highly

Strong Spending Trends: Consumer spending remains robust, with SoFi reporting 
n

Interest Rate Sensitivity: If interest rates rise further, mgmt expects some pressure 
n

Credit Trends & Funding Mix 
Dynamic Balance Sheet Allocation: In Q2, SoFi originated $10.7 billion in personal 
n
loans, allocating $7.6 billion to the balance sheet and $3.1 billion to the LPB. Mgmt 
does not target a fixed split but dynamically allocates originations based on 
maximizing risk-adjusted returns. 
Deposit-Led Funding: High-quality deposits remain the foundation of SoFi’s 
n
funding stack, accounting for 93% of total funding. Over 90% of these deposits 
come from sticky, direct deposit relationships. 
LPB Multi-Product Expansion: The Loan Platform Business is expanding beyond 
n
personal loans into new asset classes, including a $3 billion funding agreement for 
SMB loans and a new partnership for close-end second mortgages. 
Customer Relationships & Brand 
Inflection in Cross-Buy: SoFi achieved a record 51% cross-buy rate in Q2, proving 
n
the efficacy of its financial services productivity loop. For the first time, SoFi added 
twice as many products as members (2.2 million products vs. 1.1 million members). 
Brand Awareness & CAC: Unaided brand awareness reached an all-time high of 11% 
n

announced Notre Dame varsity sports jersey deal. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

SoFi Plus Subscription: Surpassed 200,000 paying subscribers, generating $24 
n
million in annualized revenue. 85% of these subscribers are existing members, and 
25% have gone on to adopt an additional product.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
