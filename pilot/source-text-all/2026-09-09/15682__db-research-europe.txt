SOCIETE GENERALE  (SOGN.PA) - CMD preview: Balancing Ambition and Credibility; Tgt 
EUR81 to EUR86. Last Close EUR74.34. Buy. 
Sharath Kumar: SocGen enters its 2026 Capital Markets Day from a fundamentally different position 
than in 2023. The turnaround phase focused on rebuilding capital, restoring profitability and simplifying

capital allocation. The key investor question is whether management can set sufficiently ambitious

CREST NICHOLSON  (CRST.L) - Crest now guiding to -ve EBIT - questions over longer term 
viability; Tgt 79p to 65p. Last Close 57.8p. Hold. 
Chris Millington: Crest's unscheduled update points to the group now being loss making at the EBIT 
line, reflecting slow trading and the impact of cost inflation>price inflation. Whilst net debt guidance has 
been reduced c.£30m to reflect fire-safety recoveries and land sales, the group has yet to secure a 
covenant relaxation. Based on current market conditions persisting and considering Crest's requirement 
to run for cash, we think that PBT losses will persist into future years, and Crest could need a covenant 
relaxation through to FY28 - which is likely to come at a cost. With Crest trading on 0.2x P/NTA we think

liquidate its balance sheet, or be acquired by a rival. We reduce our target price to 65p (from 79p), 0.25x 
P/NTA, and retain a HOLD rating.

THE VIEW (FROM 35,000 FEET)  - Key topics to be addressed at our 16th Annual Aviation Forum 


[Page 8]
Michael Linenberg: On Thursday, September 10, we will be hosting 18 US, Latin American, and 
European airlines at Deutsche Bank’s 16th Annual Aviation Forum which follows two days of industry 
information sessions and aircraft lessor and manufacturer presentations on September 8 - 9. Most 
airlines have indicated that overall demand is healthy. We agree, especially for corporate travel and many 
long-haul, international markets which are also experiencing good demand strength. However, investors 
have noted the summer decline in passenger volumes per the TSA passenger data which are averaging 
down about 2% year-over-year. We think the primary culprits are moderating supply growth (system seat 
capacity is down about 1%) and higher average airfares, of which the latter is in response to higher fuel 
prices (and flight cancellations due to inclement weather have also depressed volumes in some markets).
