Methodology Notes for GS Proprietary Economic Indicators 
 
 
Financial Conditions: Our Financial Conditions Indexes are designed to gauge the 
1.
overall looseness or tightness of financial conditions across the world’s major 
economies. The GSFCIs can provide valuable information about the GDP growth 
outlook, the transmission of monetary policy to the real economy, and the 
importance of financial shocks hitting the economy. (Latest methodology notes here 
and here.) 
FCI Impulses: Our FCI impulses measure the effect of financial conditions on real 
2.
GDP growth. For details on the methodology please see here. 
Current Activity Indicator: In statistical jargon, the CAIs are the “first principal 
3.
component” of several real activity indicators, expressed in GDP-equivalent units. 
The CAIs can be interpreted as the growth signal in the main high-frequency 
indicators for each economy. At any given point, data for certain indicators may not 
be available. The CAIs therefore incorporate forecasted values for missing indicators, 
which are then replaced with actual values when they are released. (Latest 
methodology note here.) 
MAP Surprise Index: Our daily MAP surprise indices summarize the importance and 
4.
strength (relative to consensus expectations) of economic indicators worldwide. 
Across numerous countries, our surprise index’s methodology standardizes the 
criteria for indicator selection and importance, thresholds for “surprise” scores, and 
schemes for aggregation, while allowing for occasional judgmental input from local 
economists. (Latest methodology here.) 
Trimmed Core Inflation. Our trimmed core inflation measure trims the one third 
5.
most extreme price changes from the individual core inflation components.  (Latest 
methodology note here.) 
Jobs-Workers Gaps: Our jobs-workers gaps capture the difference between total 
6.
labor demand (i.e. job openings plus employment) and labor supply (i.e. the labor 
force). We forecast the jobs-workers gap by forecasting official job openings with 
country-specific models reliant on high-frequency job posting data, and by 
forecasting the unemployment rate using jobless claims and other leading 
unemployment indicators. Our latest note can be found here. 
Wage Survey Leading Indicator: Our wage survey leading indicators summarize 
7.
survey questions about current and expected wage growth from business and 
consumer surveys. Our latest note can be found here. 
Wage Trackers: Our wage trackers measure the underlying pace of wage growth 
8.
across the G10 economies. Our latest notes can be found here and here.  
Fiscal Impulses: Our fiscal impulses measure the effect of fiscal policy on real GDP 
9.
growth. For details on the methodology please see here. 
Short-Run Utilization Scores: Our short-run utilization scores are based on scores 
10.
for both the labor market and the industrial sector, each based on a number of hard 
indicators and surveys, such as, for instance, the unemployment rate or supplier 
delivery times. We then define the short-run utilization score as a weighted average
