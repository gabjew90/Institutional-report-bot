Equity preferences
The European Information Technology Equity Preference list (EPL) is a list of our
highest conviction European Information Technology stocks.
Eurozone sector preference: Attractive
Sector view: This high-quality sector benefits from AI-related demand for
semiconductor equipment, while software revenues are boosted by the
transition to the cloud. The sector should continue to benefit from accelerating
EPS growth beyond FY26 into 2027. Consensus estimates see EPS growth
accelerating from 14% in 2026 to 24% in 2027. On valuation, the sector is
trading on a forward P/E of 28.2x, versus a 15-year average of 21x. While
elevated, we don’t see this as excessive given the concentrated nature of the
index, visibility on growth drivers for the largest incumbents, accelerating
growth, and the attractive structural trends. We therefore retain our Attractive
rating on the EU information technology sector.
Our Top Picks: ASML, ASM International, SAP
Latest Changes: We have increased the portfolio weight of BE Semiconductor
Industries by 1 percentage point (ppt). We have decreased the portfolio weight of
ASML by 1ppt.

Positioning and key trends
Our IT portfolio covers semiconductor wafer equipment vendors, automotive and
industrial semiconductors, IT services, and software. Throughout the portfolio,
stock selection is underpinned by our conviction in the sustainability of competitive
advantages and valuation. We aim to invest when the margin of safety is
compelling to reduce the probability of permanent capital loss.

Within the semiconductor subsector, we are positive on leading-edge wafer
equipment incumbents ASML and ASM International. ASML is the only provider of
transistor shrink technology globally and is therefore the only company driving
traditional Moore’s law, the concept that transistor density on integrated circuits
doubles over time. We believe the stock is particularly attractive today, given the
strong capacity upcycle we expect in leading-edge logic and memory spending.
We think momentum in semiconductor equipment stocks continues to tilt to the
upside for most firms. Elsewhere in leading-edge semiconductors, we are positive
on ASM International’s positioning to capture the gate-all-around transistor
architecture shift from FinFET. We see a significant inflection in both atomic layer
deposition (ALD) and epitaxy resulting from the GAA transition, areas where ASM
International has leading product portfolios, strong entrenchment advantages, and
is well positioned to continue gaining market share. Within the automotive and
industrial semiconductor markets, while we do not anticipate a strong recovery in
2026, we believe consensus expectations have adjusted appropriately and
management guidance has bottomed or is close to bottoming. However, we see
encouraging signs, particularly within industrial end markets, as several
incumbents expect a shallow recovery through 2026. Infineon and
STMicroelectronics also boast fast-growing data center power and connectivity
businesses, which we expect to drive material upside to their through-the-cycle
growth rates. We believe this outlook is partially reflected in current valuations;
however, we see additional upside if the data center cycle extends beyond investor
expectations.
Within software and IT services, we prefer more defensive, high-quality names, as
we see attractive valuations relative to structural growth trends. We view
generative AI as an opportunity for IT services companies. Because generative AI is
not an off-the-shelf product, we believe it should support demand for the IT
services companies in our portfolio. Software stocks have derated significantly as
investors discount growing concerns over the terminal value and unit economics
of software vendors in an AI environment. We believe this indiscriminate selling
has created opportunities in certain idiosyncratic names and would focus on more
defensive companies with a greater likelihood of delivering accelerating revenue
growth. In this regard, we like SAP.
Summary of recent results
Recent results showed continued operating momentum for European
semiconductor and technology companies. Geopolitical concerns have somewhat
faded in company updates, while orders came in much stronger than expected.
Most companies in our coverage delivered results that were in line with or slightly
above expectations. ASML’s results were healthy, with strong, broad-based
demand early in the cyclical upcycle leading to upgrades to estimates. Memory
markets were a particularly strong driver, and we expect this to continue.
STMicroelectronics’ and Infineon’s results indicate the start of a cyclical recovery,
with industrial markets proving particularly resilient. Infineon also highlighted
continued strength in its AI power division. SAP’s results provided reassurance
amid concerns about AI disintermediation risk, with cloud backlog exceeding
expectations. Strength in the core business supported the stock despite no change
in incremental AI monetization expectations or AI-related risks.
Ad hoc changes
As of 2026, we are now adjusting the benchmark on an ongoing basis, moving
from an equal-weighted benchmark to the MSCI Europe Information Technology
Index, as we believe this better reflects the opportunity cost to investors. This will
also lead to significant changes in portfolio weights, which will be actively
managed going forward.

Why it is Most Preferred
We favor Adyen as it stands out from other payment providers with
its unified, single-platform architecture, the company is likely to be a

Company profile
Adyen is a modern payment processor based in the Netherlands,
primarily operating in the e-commerce channel, but also supplying
POS and omnichannel solutions. The company has become well
known for its single technology stack and purely organic growth
strategy (zero M&A in its history). It is a full-stack processor with

well as a licensed bank in the EU, the UK and the US.
What drives our opinion
Adyen is benefiting from industry disruption and merchant migration,
and we think it is well positioned to gain further market share from
traditional payment providers. Its innovative agentic commerce
strategy acts as a “universal translator” for merchants, enabling them
to retain control over transactions and shopper data. The company’s
suite of optimization modules especially the widely adopted and
monetized Protect module further enhances merchant outcomes and
drives higher payment volumes. Adyen’s robust payment volume
growth and strong traction with platform customers highlight its
ability to capture a growing addressable market in embedded finance
and software-led payments, supporting our conviction in its long-
term growth potential. Key risks include a macroeconomic downturn
hurting transaction volumes and idiosyncratic risks from high
customer concentration.

Why it is Most Preferred
We continue to see a long runway for Amadeus in its core markets
and believe the global distribution system (GDS) will retain its
importance. We believe the company has shown its execution in
NDC. We thus view Amadeus’s free cash flow yield as attractive and
compensatory for the downside risks.
Company profile
Amadeus is a leading travel ticket global distribution system (GDS)
with 50% share and is also a leading provider of outsourced
passenger-related IT solutions for airlines with 2bn passengers
boarded in 2023 (46% of all passengers flown). The GDS industry is
dominated by three global players (Amadeus, Travelport, Sabre), but
faces substitution/disintermediation as passengers increasingly go
direct rather than use agents. Air IT should grow by adding new
airlines and the cross-selling of new modules, and there is an
emerging opportunity in hospitality IT. Amadeus IPOed in 2010 at
EUR11 a share.
What drives our opinion
Amadeus is built on three pillars: air ticket distribution, airline IT
systems, and hospitality booking and distribution. Its position in its
two core markets—air distribution and airline IT—has been
strengthening, supported by competitors’ indebtedness and reduced
capacity to reinvest. As a result, Amadeus has gained market share, a
trend we expect to continue over the next several years. We believe
competitive disintermediation risks from so-called New Distribution
Capabilities (NDCs) are overstated, as the lack of standardization and
the complexity of integrating NDCs with the long tail of online travel
agents (OTAs) make Amadeus a natural partner for airlines. Amadeus
has recently become more vocal in targeting NDCs, with tangible
customer success stories. The aggregator role provides natural
defensiveness, and we believe Amadeus can address competitive
threats as it has in the past. We also see a long runway in airline IT,
where Amadeus holds a dominant market share, with further gains
likely from the displacement of in-house solutions and adoption by
lower-budget airlines. Hospitality IT remains ripe for growth, given the
market’s fragmentation and low levels of digitalization. Amadeus has
one of the highest free cash flow yields in our software coverage, and
we rate it as an above-average quality software business, given its
competitive positioning. Valuations (P/E) remain undemanding versus
historical norms. Key risks include faster or stronger disintermediation
from direct connects between airlines and OTAs, more rapid adoption
of NDCs before Amadeus can establish partnerships (potentially
enabling new aggregators), a slower-than-expected recovery in
corporate travel, and adverse FX movements.

ASM International

Most Preferred
Weight: 5.0%
Added on 2024-08-16
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
We expect strong top-line growth given the transition to gate-all-
around (GAA) transistors and ASM International’s defensive position.
Moreover, we expect growth to accelerate as the 2nm ramp drives
atomic layer deposition (ALD) and epitaxy adoption, with a significant
scale-up in capacity for 2nm and beyond over time. Over the medium
term, we see innovations such as molybdenum, which replaces some
copper and tungsten metal layers, as a driver of top-line growth. We
expect continued strength in leading-edge semicap equipment
demand, supported by robust fab investments and tight memory
capacity.
Company profile
ASM is a leading supplier of semiconductor process equipment for
wafer processing. In recent years, it brought Atomic Layer Deposition
(ALD) and Plasma Enhanced Atomic Layer Deposition (PEALD) from
R&D right through to mainstream production at advanced
manufacturers sites.
What drives our opinion
ASM International (ASM I) is a key enabler of the shift in transistor
architecture from fin field-effect transistors (FinFET) to gate-all-around
(GAA) designs. As transistor sizes continue to shrink to boost
computing capabilities, FinFET transistors are reaching their limits in
terms of performance and power efficiency. At increasingly smaller
nodes, FinFET designs encounter issues such as short-channel effects
and power leakage, which impact the reliability of leading integrated
circuits (ICs), ultimately reducing yield and increasing costs. GAA
transistors offer greater control over the channel, as the gate
surrounds it entirely, allowing for improved current flow between the
source and drain. This enables the production of faster, smaller, and
more efficient chips, supporting the continued progress of Moore’s
Law. ASM International is well positioned for this transition due to its
expertise in atomic layer deposition (ALD) and epitaxy, in our view.
Both technologies are critical and experience a significant increase in
layer intensity during the shift to GAA transistors. We believe ASM I is
likely to maintain or grow its market share over time, as its
established presence in existing fabs and broad chemistry know-how
—developed over decades—provide a strong advantage in
distribution, product offering, and partnerships. ASM International is
particularly geared toward the most advanced technology nodes, as
foundries are scaling their first generation GAA capacity. Key risks
include the possibility of a smaller 2nm node than expected, which
could limit equipment demand; competitive pressures that may erode
ASM International’s market share; and, since equipment stocks are
sensitive to the economic cycle, any macroeconomic slowdown could
weaken end-market demand and ultimately impact equipment sales.
Adverse FX risks could also impact operating performance.

Why it is Most Preferred
ASML has one of the strongest business positions in our coverage, in
our view. We think its strong leadership position on its technology
and irreplaceable supply chain make it truly defensive. We expect the
significant tightness in memory supply-demand to sustain amid the
strong structural growth of AI, and continued conversion of
traditional DRAM capacity to AI High Bandwidth Memory capacity.
Therefore, we see an attractive multi-year prospect of elevated
capacity additions. Having said that, semiconductor equipment stocks
tend to be very sensitive to macroeconomics as any deterioration in
end markets such as consumer semiconductors, memory or AI
semiconductors can lead to order push outs or cancellation of
equipment due to the lower need for capacity. We thus recommend
investors monitor consumer macroeconomic data points
Company profile
ASML is the leading supplier of lithography equipment for the
manufacture of semiconductor devices. A Philips and ASMI spin-off
established in 1984, ASML has progressively gained market share to
become the leading supplier of lithography equipment to the
semiconductor industry, with few able to compete at the leading
edge. The company’s products are critical to the ongoing advances in
semiconductor technology. ASML supplies all major semiconductor
manufacturers, including Samsung, TSMC, Intel and Hynix.
Competition primarily comes from Nikon and Canon.
What drives our opinion
ASML is a leading supplier of semiconductor lithography, a critical
part of the semiconductor manufacturing process that has been the
driving force behind continued transistor shrinkage. We believe
spending on lithography as part of semiconductor capex should at
least maintain its intensity over the medium term, which should
enable ASML to benefit from healthy topline growth. Given its strong
visibility, we view ASML’s long-term (2030) target as plausible, which
implies quite an attractive valuation for such a defensive business.
However, we note that the revenue recovery may be uneven;
therefore, investors should stay attentive to this risk. While ASML
doesn’t have the most attractive revenue growth profile in the semi-
cap landscape, we believe it has a strong gross profit positioning
through its pricing power and thus we envision healthy bottom line
EPS growth. ASML’s core moat is centered around ﻿technological
differentiation leading to a monopoly position in EUV lithology.
However, often underappreciated, ASML’s role as a systems
integrator and thus a strong supply chain partner increases its
defensive qualities. Therefore, attempting to disrupt ASML requires
exceptional execution on many hard-to-do tasks. Risks include a
structural decline in lithography spending and significant execution
issues across major customers resulting in delay in capital spending
plans. In the near term, a lack of recovery in consumer semiconductor
end-markets could also limit wafer demand. ASML will need to scale
capacity significantly over time, the complexity of which creates
execution risk, which may lead to disappointing financial results in
any given quarter. Adverse FX risks could also impact operating
performance.

Why it is Most Preferred
We see BE Semiconductors (BESI) advanced packaging products are
near multiple inflection points. The company is well positioned to
take share across CPU, GPU, Photonics and HBM advanced packaging
with its dominant hybrid bonding tool. BESI boasts a strong
technological leadership on advanced die-attach with near-term
emerging applications. 60% of BESI’s system sales are for AI
applications, making BESI one of the most leveraged to AI plays
across European IT.
Company profile
Besi is a Dutch-based company that manufactures and distributes
semiconductor assembly equipment for the global semiconductor and
electronics industries. The assembly process referred to as back end
provides the electronic interface and physical connection between a
semiconductor device or integrated chip and other electronic
components. The assembly also protects the integrated chip from the
external environment. Besi operates through three segments: die
attach systems, packaging and plating systems. It has operations in
the Netherlands, Austria, Switzerland, Malaysia and China.
What drives our opinion
BE Semiconductor is nearing an expected strong product inflection
point, with its hybrid bonding tools well positioned to take share
across leading-edge logic, memory, and photonics advanced
packaging steps. Advanced packaging has grown in importance for
foundries as “More than Moore” technologies drive the frontier of
performance. We view the wafer-capacity backdrop as very healthy,
given the lack of capacity additions post-COVID and strong demand
growth from artificial intelligence processing. Therefore, we see an
attractive cyclical and product-cycle setup for BESI, with continued
momentum in orders. Risks include a delay in the adoption of hybrid
bonding technology across its end-markets, volatility in order patterns
as technology adoption cycles can be uneven, a general slowdown in
artificial intelligence processing demand, or deterioration in the
macroeconomic environment.

Why it is Most Preferred
We believe the firm is well positioned for continued execution and
growth. We therefore view Capgemini’s valuation discount relative to
US peers as an attractive opportunity for investors. While we have a
below-benchmark position, we think Capgemini should be part of a
diversified portfolio.
Company profile
Capgemini is Europe’s largest domiciled IT services group with over
340k staff but still sub-5% share globally in a very fragmented
market. It IPOed in 1985. Since the 2007 acquisition of Kanbay,
Capgemini has built a strong offshore footprint (59%), making it cost
competitive, while investments in cloud, cyber and intelligent industry
(augmented by the 2020 Altran acquisition) have improved its growth
profile. It has a strong cash generation profile, with dividends,
buybacks and M&A all valid use cases. Aiman Ezzat has been CEO
since 2020. Competitors include Accenture, Cognizant, Infosys, TCS
and the Big Four.
What drives our opinion
We view Capgemini as a diversified way to get exposure to the
ongoing digitalization trends. As an IT services company, it is not
dependent on any given technology and thus grows alongside the
secular IT spend, which we expect to continue to outgrow GDP over
the long-term. The IT services industry is extremely fragmented with a
long tail of competitors, but at the high end of enterprises there are
few true competitors. We therefore see a long runway of growth
ahead for Capgemini and note the natural defensiveness of the
business model with regards to margins. These are operationally
intensive businesses that have deep industry and technology specific
knowledge, which create barriers to compete. Artificial intelligence
could serve as an additional tailwind as AI is not an off-the-shelf
product and requires integration with a company’s infrastructure and
dataset. However, Capgemini is also macro-sensitive and any
worsening in the IT spending environment would impact it. The
macroeconomic recovery in IT spending could be shallower and more
prolonged than we expect given the high level of uncertainty for
market participants today. AI could also pose a risk as it facilitates
transformation projects, democratizes the ability of customer to build
solutions internally and may ultimately lower Capgemini’s pricing
power. The emergence of the offshore services delivery model poses a
threat to traditional European IT services’ companies by adding to
pricing pressure. Finally, the shift to cloud delivery of software could
impact the volume and complexity of systems integration and
outsourcing relationships over the long run. Adverse FX risks could
also impact operating performance.

Why it is Most Preferred
We like Computacenter for its attractive business model with
entrenchment, higher returns on capital, a long reinvestment runway,
and an appealing valuation. We see Computacenter’s geographic
exposure as attractive given its strong position in North American
hardware and thus exposure to sizable capex investments as well as
the Germany public sector, where we expect significant infrastructure
and fiscal spending. We believe that with continued consistent
execution, it can close the substantial valuation discount relative to
peers.
Company profile
Computacenter is a value-added reseller of hardware and software,
with HP, Microsoft, Cisco and IBM among its major vendor partners. It
listed in London in 1998 at 670p under CEO Mike Norris, who
remains CEO today.
What drives our opinion
Computacenter is a value-added reseller (VAR) specialized in
hardware and software, serving the large enterprise market. We think
the quality of the business model is underappreciated, as VARs have
sticky relationships with long runways and are not dependent on any
given technology. The largest VARs typically have less than a mid-
single-digit market share in their respective markets, which
demonstrates the length of the runway and the potential for above-
market growth. Moreover, due to Computacenter’s market
segmentation on large enterprises, it does not compete with the UK’s
other large VARs, which often focus on SMEs. The company currently
holds significant cash, providing flexibility, and its management has a
history of opportunistic capital returns. We also note the company
has a history of value accretive M&A. Despite Computacenter’s ability
to outgrow the overall market, it remains sensitive to the economic
cycle. Downturns in the IT spending environment have a direct impact
on Computacenter’s business, with cost mitigations only partially able
to offset this pressure. Key risks: As it addresses the large enterprise
market, Computacenter also has a larger degree of customer
concentration relative to other VARs and is therefore more susceptible
to key client risks and generally more volatile in terms of revenue.
Adverse FX risks could also impact operating performance.

Why it is Most Preferred

Company profile
Infineon Technologies is one of the leading suppliers of

markets. Infineon was a Siemens spin-off and IPOed in 2000.
Following several disposals and closures, Infineon is now focused on
analog semiconductors (particularly power) and on markets in which
it is a number one or two provider. Infineon is now a top 3 supplier of

power semiconductors globally.
What drives our opinion
Infineon is a leading automotive semiconductor company with a
portfolio spanning power semiconductors and microcontrollers,
serving the electric vehicle (EV), advanced driver-assistance systems
(ADAS), industrial, and consumer markets. The company is also well
positioned in the AI data center power market, in our view,
supported by a broad and competitive portfolio of solutions. This has
enabled Infineon to gain significant market share in the data center
power market over the past two years, which we expect it to
maintain. We also believe the company is well positioned to exceed
its AI power revenue guidance as substantial capacity comes online to
meet growing demand. Automotive remains Infineon’s largest end
market, representing approximately 50% of sales. Given its product
and regional exposure, particularly its strong positioning in China’s EV
market, we believe the company is well placed to benefit from the
expected increase in semiconductor content per vehicle over the next
five to seven years. We expect Infineon to continue gaining market
share as a result. While the operational outlook is attractive,
expectations are elevated. This means that beating expectations
without a guidance upgrade may not be sufficient to unlock further
upside. We could also be wrong on the timing and strength of the
automotive recovery, which may prove slower than anticipated. Over
the longer term, risks include geopolitical tensions and increasing
competition from Chinese semiconductor manufacturers. The
Chinese government continues to support the development of
domestic semiconductor capabilities, including in automotive and
industrial chips, particularly in the low- and mid-range power
semiconductor segments. In addition, adverse FX movements could
negatively affect operating performance.

Why it is Most Preferred
SAP’s cloud migration cycle offers strong visibility for healthy and

Founded in 1972, SAP defined the enterprise applications market
after its early adoption of the client/server architecture in the 1990s.
SAP is the leader in many of its end markets, but they remain
fragmented. Growth comes from new customer wins, market share
gains and upgrades, with added impetus to the latter from the
planned end of support for legacy ERP at the end of 2027. This is also
driving a migration of customers to the cloud. While acquisitive in the
past, SAP has been less so since Christian Klein became CEO in April
2020. Two co-founders still own c12% of the capital.
What drives our opinion
SAP has been executing well on its transition to the cloud, and we
see continued upside as its cloud backlog growth remains healthy.
We believe the cloud transition provides several tailwinds for the
company, including new functionality for customers, cross-selling
opportunities across existing modules, and the sale of standalone
databases. Against this backdrop, we believe the S/4HANA product
cycle should significantly exceed the addressable market of the
previous product cycle. SAP is also targeting the Rule of 40, which
refers to a combined revenue growth rate and free cash flow margin
in excess of 40. We believe SAP has multiple levers to support this
execution, and we note that its margins have historically lagged those
of US peers. The main risks to our investment analysis are a slower
cloud transition than expected and an increased need for operating
expense investments to address emerging AI opportunities and risks.
SAP will also have to navigate potential pricing-model shifts if AI
leads to a significant reduction in software seats as employment
trends evolve over time, changes in competitive dynamics and pricing
power, and weaker unit economics. Other risks include a slowdown
in the overall IT spending environment, elevated valuation multiples
limiting the potential for a rerating, and a slower-than-expected cloud
transition constraining revenue reacceleration over the longer term.
We also expect top-line growth to peak around the 2027-28 period,
as customer cloud migrations should be more mature by then.
Adverse FX movements could also affect operating performance.

Why it is Most Preferred
While visibility is low, we believe STM guidance is conservative. Add
to this idiosyncratic content wins at a large smartphone customer
ramping up, and we see scope for operating performance to beat
expectations. Finally, we believe STM’s data center exposure is set to
grow meaningfully in the next few years as the company is well
positioned to serve that market. On its core business, we believe we
are at or near the trough of the industrial and automotive cycle given
the length of the downturn and low level of inventories at customers.
We also expect STM to benefit from improvements in utilization rates
through next year and for the benefits of its cost optimization
program to start adding to material annual savings by 2027.
Company profile
STMicroelectronics (STM) is one of the top 10 largest global semi
vendors by revenue. It was formed in 1987 as the combination of
SGS Microelettronica and the non-military business of Thomson
Semiconducteurs. Following several key divestments/closures (eg, ST-
Ericsson, STBs), STM still addresses a broad array of products
(analogue, digital, mixed-signal, MEMs applications) across the
comms, autos, industrial and consumer electronics markets. The
company has helped innovate a number of technologies such as 3D
sensing and silicon carbide solutions.
What drives our opinion
STMicroelectronics is a leading microcontroller (MCU) and power
semiconductor integrated device manufacturer (IDM) with significant
exposure to the automotive, industrial, and consumer end-markets.
Its main drivers are: smart mobility (advanced driving assistance
systems and the electric vehicle transition), data center optics, power
management, and Internet of Things. Its automotive and industrial
segments currently represent 70% of group revenue, with both
enjoying attractive long-term trends, in our view. Within high-end
automotive applications, STM’s end-markets have faced material
headwinds in recent quarters as manufacturing weakness in China
persists and European automotive OEMs, particularly with their EV
models, continue to struggle. Moreover, STM has significant customer
concentration with large customers. This customer mix was a material
tailwind for STM in the last cycle, but given idiosyncratic issues at
some of them today, they now represent a risk. That said, we believe
STM has largely de-risked its non-data center business guidance,
given a potential cyclical recovery, unwinding of significant
underutilization charges impacting margins, and upcoming content
gains in the smartphone market. Within the data center segment, in
the medium term, we see STM as well positioned to the ongoing
photonics adoption curve. This should contribute materially to group
growth by the end of next year. On the downside, we could be
wrong about the automotive, industrial, and electrification cycle,
which could lead to pressure on STM’s most important segment, and
the consumer end-market recovery may be more gradual than we
expect. This risk may be exacerbated by tariff-related macroeconomic
pressures, as any deterioration in end-market demand would slow the
cyclical recovery for STM. Over the longer term, STM faces some risk
from growing Chinese semiconductor capacity and geopolitical risks,
as the Chinese government is seeking to support its domestic
semiconductor industry, including automotive and industrial
semiconductors. Adverse FX risks could also impact operating
performance.
