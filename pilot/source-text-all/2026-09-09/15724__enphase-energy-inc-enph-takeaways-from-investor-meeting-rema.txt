Enphase Energy Inc. (ENPH): Takeaways from investor meeting; remain 
Buy
9 September 2026 | 12:17AM EDT
We reiterate our Buy rating on ENPH following investor meetings with Raghu Belur, 
co-founder and Chief Products Officer, and Zach Freedman, Head of Investor 
Relations, in NY where investor discussions focused on (i) progress on 
commercializing its SST product, (ii) European battery growth prospects, (iii) new 
product launches, including traction with its ongoing Propel program, and (iv) 
thoughts on the 2027 demand outlook, among other topics. We detail our full 
takeaways below. 
Implications 
Updates on SST commercialization. ENPH’s September 8 IQ SST update 
n
advances full scale rack validation, with power modules now manufactured in 
Texas. Management characterized this update as an important milestone 
following nine product generations and ~20 years of custom ASIC and power 
electronics development. ENPH expects to complete a full ~4.2MW, 13.8kV 
system demonstration at its Austin lab by late November, followed by shipment 
to a national lab for testing into 1Q27. The company plans to manufacture 
~50MW of modules in 4Q26 for demonstration and testing systems, with 
customer pilots expected in 1H27. Management also expects to demonstrate its 
34.5kV configuration within the next ~4-6 weeks, which it views as important for 
addressing larger data center and utility-scale applications. The 13.8kV system 
uses 48 power modules, while the 34.5kV configuration requires ~3x as many 
modules; management indicated the core power module remains the same 
across both configurations. Importantly, management believes hyperscalers are 
likely to tri-source SST products, and early customer feedback appears to 
suggest that ENPH remains within the top 3 manufacturers in terms of 
technological and commercial readiness. Looking ahead, ENPH anticipates first 
commercial deployments as early as 4Q27, with revenue ramping for the high 
margin products in 2028. 
European battery opportunity. Management expects European growth to be 
n
increasingly led by battery deployments, with the Netherlands remaining a key 
opportunity, while solar-only demand remains more mixed. The company 
continues to position its fifth generation battery as a meaningful step forward, 
citing ~50% higher energy density and ~40% lower cost versus the prior product. 
Management sees this as supporting a broader battery addressable market,

Importantly, ENPH continues to advance products and capabilities to better address

product. We note that management aims to secure a 40% market share within the

the 1GWh-2GWh battery storage market. Separately, ENPH discussed signing two US 
auto OEM agreements for its bidirectional charger and expects its first vehicle linked 
product launch in 4Q26, with commercial shipments beginning in early 2027. 
Management indicated each OEM program could represent more than $100mn of 
revenue over time, although vehicle compatibility and adoption will determine the 
ramp. Lastly, Propel has now reached ~350 weekly net originations, up from ~200

sell through. 
Typical seasonality and lack of safe harbor activity could weigh on 1Q27. 
n
Management discussed how the $61mn in safe harbor activity anticipated in 4Q26 
implies core sales of ~$240mn when considering consensus views of $302mn 
(FactSet). Additionally, 1Q results typically experience a 10%-20% seasonal decline, 
as opposed to the consensus forecasted growth of ~15%+ qoq, with actual growth 
likely somewhere in between. Management noted that this implied view for 1Q27 
establishes a roughly ~$1bn annualized runrate, with upside provided by the 
aforementioned growth opportunities given the current consensus view of $1.25bn 
in 2027 revenue. That said, it remains to be seen how much of this growth ENPH can 
capture during the year.  
Valuation 
Our 12-month target price of $55 is based on our target P/E multiple of 20.0X applied to 
our Q5-Q8 EPS, and then adding back ~$7 of net cash per share. 
Key Risks 
Key risks include lower-than-expected revenue growth and margins, changes in the 
competitive and technology landscape, US-China tariffs, and abnormal inventory builds 
across the channel.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
