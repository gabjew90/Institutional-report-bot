We raise our 12-month target price for Recruit HD to ¥19,400 from 
¥14,900 (20% upside) and reiterate our Buy rating. The target price 
increase is mainly driven by a change in our valuation methodology 
(from SOTP based on EV/EBITDA to SOTP based on EV/NOPAT). Our 
earnings estimates are largely unchanged, with only minor revisions 
to our assumptions reflecting discussions with the company and 
new service announcements, and our FY3/27-FY3/29 operating 
profit estimates are 7%/18%/26% above the Bloomberg consensus. 
Since the Q4 results announcement on May 15 and the Q1 results 
announcement on August 7, the share price has risen 106%/23%, 
respectively. Given the rapid pace of earnings growth and share 
price appreciation, investors have increasingly focused on the 
potential ceiling for US ARPJ and the sustainability of the high 
growth rate. However, revenue per hire and the take rate at 
Indeed (revenue per hire ÷ annual salary of the hired candidate) 
remain well below the levels of peers, and we do not believe it is 
necessary at this stage to be concerned about a ceiling on unit 
prices or a sharp deceleration in growth. Below, we discuss FAQs 
from our discussions with investors following the Q1 results, along 
with our views on each.

At the same time, we recognize that the gap in take rates between Indeed and other 
business formats is largely due to differences in fee structures. Recruitment agencies and 
executive search firms typically operate on a fully contingent fee basis, with fees only 
incurred upon a successful hire, whereas job boards generally do not use a take-rate 
model, instead charging mainly on a cost-per-click (CPC) or posting fee basis, with fees 
incurred regardless of whether a hire is made (Indeed is primarily CPC-based). It is also 
common for companies to post job ads on multiple job boards simultaneously, and 
recruitment budgets may be spread across multiple providers. In other words, we 
recognize that the take rate a job board can generally receive is, by virtue of the business 
model, lower than that of providers operating on a fully contingent fee basis(Exhibit 3). 
Some investors ask how much realistic upside there is for job board take rates. In our 
view, even based on an objective calculation from the perspective of hiring companies, 
around 2% can reasonably be assumed as a minimum level. 
Our specific calculation method is as follows. First, based on statistical surveys by the 
Japan Executive Search and Recruitment Association, we estimate the hiring success rate 
per operator for fee-charging employment placement providers at 6.5% (FY2024; 
number of regular employment placements ÷ number of regular job openings for 
fee-charging employment placement providers = 6.5%). Next, we assume that 
companies pay job boards the same aggregate amount as they pay to recruitment 
agencies and executive search firms operating on a fully contingent fee basis. In that 
case, companies would allocate spending broadly across job ads regardless of whether a 
hire is made, and we calculate the ratio of revenue received by an average job board to 
the annual salary of the hired candidate at around 2% (average take rate for the 
recruitment agency business of 30% × hiring success rate of 6.5%) (Exhibit 4). 
Based on this calculation of how hiring companies allocate spending, we think a take rate 
of around 2% can reasonably be assumed as a minimum level. However, considering that 
(1) Indeed is the world’s largest job site by visits and we think it makes a significant 
contribution to companies’ hiring activities, and (2) the visibility of Indeed’s hiring 
success rate and the value it provides is rising further with the expansion of Premium 
features, we think there is a strong possibility that the ratio of revenue Indeed can 
receive to the annual salary of hired candidates could ultimately far exceed 2%, and 
we believe there is still significant room to expand monetization. 
As a reference case at another company, Amazon launched an auction-format 
advertising business similar to the current one, known as Sponsored Products, in 2012. 
By continuously introducing new features, it has maintained a high growth rate in 
advertising revenue that exceeds GMV growth even in recent years (Exhibit 5). Similarly, 
we think Indeed can maintain a high sales growth rate over the long term if it can 
continue to enhance the performance of its AI tools and its dominance as a job platform.

 (2) Monetization has progressed rapidly, but should we be concerned about the risk 
of hiring companies churning? 
A. The retention rate for Premium is better than for Standard, and we see no need 
for excessive concern about customer churn at this point. 
As noted above, even with accelerating monetization, revenue per hire and the take rate 
at Indeed remain well below the levels of peers. In addition, the company explained in 
May 2026 that the retention rate for Premium is 20% higher than for Standard. We 
therefore do not see a need for excessive concern about customer churn due to the 
recent rise in unit prices. The company has also explained that it does not aim for simple 
price increases and would slow the pace of monetization if it judged that it was not 
providing sufficient value to customers, giving the impression that it pays close attention 
to customer satisfaction. We organize the value proposition of Indeed’s AI tools along 
the following three axes. The added value delivered by improvements in Indeed’s 
functionality is significant along all three axes, and as long as the company continues to 
enhance AI functionality and carefully monitor customer feedback, we do not see a need 
for concern about customer churn.

HR Technology segment in recognition of its strong operating profit growth outlook

Investment Thesis - Recruit Holdings 
Recruit Holdings is an internet company with three core segments: HR technology 
(matching job seekers and companies via Indeed and Glassdoor), marketing matching 
technology (providing matching platform services to connect individual users and 
companies in the fields of housing, beauty, travel, and dining, etc., and SaaS products 
centered on Air Business Tools in Japan), and staffing (providing staffing services in 
Japan, Europe, the US, and Australia). 
We are Buy rated on Recruit. At Indeed, hiring speed has accelerated due to 
higher-quality candidate recommendations from AI, creating a virtuous cycle that is also 
leading to higher job seeker satisfaction, and the results of efforts to refine AI matching 
using proprietary data from both companies and job seekers are starting to be 
confirmed in earnings. In addition, we believe Indeed has vast room for future growth in 
monetization compared to competing platforms, and that US ARPJ will continue to trend 
higher over the long term. 
Price Target Risks and Methodology - Recruit Holdings 
Our 12-month target price of ¥19,400 is based on SOTP, using FY3/29E as a base year. 
We assume target EV/NOPAT multiples of 17.4x/15.4x/10.5x for the HR 
Technology/Marketing Matching Technology/Global Staffing segments. Key downside 
risks to our target price are as follows. (1) Macro environment: Risks include possible 
changes in job market momentum due to a sharp deterioration in macro conditions, 
mainly in the US, and a resulting weaker-than-expected earnings performance in the HR 
technology business. (2) Indeed monetization: Indeed has introduced numerous 
measures to increase monetization. Risks include progress with monetization at the 
platform falling short of our assumptions. (3) Capital allocation: Equity investors appear 
to largely expect Recruit to continue with share buybacks of a certain size. Risks include 
shareholder returns falling short of market expectations.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank

across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2

departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
