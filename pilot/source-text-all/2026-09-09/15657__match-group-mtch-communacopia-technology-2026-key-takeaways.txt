Match Group (MTCH): Communacopia + Technology 2026 — Key 
Takeaways
8 September 2026 | 6:39PM EDT
 
Presenters: We hosted Match Group’s CEO Spencer Rascoff at the Communacopia + 
Technology Conference.  
Bottom Line: We highlight a few key takeaways from our conversation. 1) there 
remains a large addressable market opportunity within the online dating market and 
dating-adjacent (i.e. social) market opportunities; 2) DAU trends have continued to 
improve at Tinder, largely driven by product/platform innovation; 3) mgmt. remains 
optimistic around the growth trajectory of Hinge; & 4) mgmt. reiterated capital 
allocation priorities (anchored around organic growth investments and shareholder 
returns).  
Key Takeaways 
 
 
Market Opportunity. With ~250mm single daters globally, the company reiterated 
that there remains a large addressable market opportunity. Beyond the core 
addressable dating market, mgmt. emphasized that focus remains around scaling 
dating adjacent categories (i.e. in person events).  
Tinder. The operational turnaround at Tinder remains well underway, with progress 
being seen on the back of product/platform innovation (incl. Events) and algorithm 
improvements. On the back of product/platform innovation, mgmt. noted that DAU 
trends have continued to improve on a sequential basis from July (-2.5% YoY) to 
August (-1% YoY) - with trends also continuing to improve during the first week of 
September. 
Events. Given that Tinder is a demand aggregation platform - MTCH framed there to 
be an attractive opportunity to drive increased engagement through double dates, 
group dates & Events to drive a more social experience. Today, Events has rolled out 
to 25 cities and is expected to further scale to 75 cities. Beyond scaling into new 
addressable markets - Events are driving engagement (with mgmt. noting that ~70% 
of 18-24 year old users in Los Angeles have engaged with the Events Tab & ~50% 
have come back to the Events tab the week after to look at additional events).

Hinge. Reiterated that the app is on track to reach $1bn in revenues during FY27, while 
also maintaining an optimistic tone around the long-term user monetization opportunity 
given the customer segment (relative to Tinder). 
Product Innovation. Across the ecosystem of apps - mgmt. reiterated that focus 
remains around driving: a) a lower friction experience for users (through improving 
onboarding); b) introducing new methods to interact with other users in a lower stakes 
environment (i.e. through group dates & Events); & c) driving recommendation 
improvements.  
Profitability & Capital Allocation. The company remains focused on the balance 
between investing into organic growth opportunities (i.e. marketing, product innovation, 
etc.), shareholder returns (through share repurchases & dividends), and opportunistic 
M&A/investments (as seen with their prior investment into Sniffies).  
Valuation/Risks 
 
 
We are currently Buy rated on MTCH. Our $46, 12-month price target is based on an 
equal blend of: (1) EV/GAAP EBITDA applied to our NTM +1 estimates; and (2) a 
modified DCF using an EV/FCF-SBC multiple applied to our NTM +4 estimates 
discounted back 3 years.  
Risks to our Buy rating include evolving macro and geopolitical landscape and resulting 
headwinds, increased regulatory scrutiny (i.e., around privacy laws), 
lower-than-anticipated payer net additions, heightened user acquisition costs, 
heightened competition, decreased user retention, security breaches, and app store 
fees.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
