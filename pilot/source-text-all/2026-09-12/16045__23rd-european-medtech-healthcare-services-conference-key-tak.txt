23rd European Medtech & Healthcare Services Conference — Key 
Takeaways
11 September 2026 | 6:23AM BST
We hosted our 23rd European Medtech & Healthcare Services Conference, with 
participation from >20 companies across the sector as well as a broad range of 
investor clients (LO, HF and private equity investors). Key themes at the conference 
included the role of innovation in supporting growth and market share gains, shifting 
dynamics in China, as well as implications from rising cost inflation. Discussions also 
highlighted a broad range of company-specific risks and opportunities, spanning 
product launches, operational execution, margin progression, capital allocation and 
changing competitive landscapes. In this note, we summarise the key takeaways from 
each company session.

Ambu (not covered) 
 
 
Presenter: Henrik Skak Bender, CFO 
Bottom line: We hosted a fireside with Henrik Skak Bender, CFO of Ambu, discussing the 
reduction in FY2026 growth guidance following more difficult US Anesthesia & Patient 
Monitoring (A&PM) trading, alongside the continued growth opportunity in Endoscopy. 
Management remained confident in the long-term single-use conversion thesis, with 
innovation, commercial investment and lower tariff exposure supporting the longer-term 
growth and margin outlook. 
Key takeaways:  
A&PM weakness drove the FY2026 reset, but management views this as a 
n
temporary volume issue rather than a structural change in the business. US price 
increases led to some volume loss where contracts became non-exclusive. 
Management nevertheless believes the pricing actions were appropriate and 
expects volumes to stabilise and improve over the next 6-12 months. It reiterated its 
3-5% long-term A&PM growth target, supported by Patient Monitoring and the 
planned launch of a single-use sensor. While Ambu continues to assess the strategic 
role of A&PM as Endoscopy becomes a larger part of the group (70%+), 
management sees no need to accelerate a portfolio decision given its strong cash 
position. 
Endoscopy remains the core growth engine, with Respiratory momentum and 
n
innovation supporting the broader single-use conversion opportunity: 
Management highlighted SureSight as a good example of product innovation 
expanding the addressable market, enabling Ambu to address new procedures and 
compete for hospital equipment replacement opportunities. Respiratory continues 
to benefit from conversion from reusable to single-use products and early customer 
wins, although adoption cycles remain lengthy in critical-care settings. Ureteroscopy 
is also converting rapidly toward single-use, but Ambu acknowledged that its ramp 
has been slower than expected as customers evaluate a wider set of competing 
products. 
Management remains confident in the 12-14% EBIT margin guidance through 
n
lower tariffs and Endoscopy mix, but Ambu will continue to invest behind 
growth and differentiation. Manufacturing expansion in Mexico is reducing 
underlying tariff costs on US products, while Endoscopy should become an 
increasingly important contributor to revenue. However, management does not 
expect major reported margin expansion in FY2027 given continued investment in 
production capacity and the US commercial organisation. It also sees 
EndoIntelligence, software integration and eventually AI-enabled procedural 
support as important longer-term differentiators, although the commercial model, 
regulatory approvals and reimbursement pathway are still developing.

Carl Zeiss Meditec 
 
 
Presenter: Justus Felix Wehmer, CFO  
Bottom line: Our discussion with Carl Zeiss Meditec addressed the causes of weaker 
operational performance in recent years and focused on the steps being taken by management 
to drive better performance via the ProfitUp programme. We also discussed the outlook for 
China across Refractive, the recovery in Microsurgery, and the path towards the group’s 
medium-term growth and margin framework. Management reiterated FY26 adjusted EBIT 
margin expectations of 8-10% (more likely to be at the lower end).  
Key takeaways: 
ProfitUp is the central margin-recovery programme, but its benefits to build 
n
over several years. Management targets approximately €200m of gross 
contribution by 2028-29, or around €160m after offsetting headwinds such as 
higher ERP costs. The programme spans workforce reductions in Germany, COGS 
improvement, leaner processes and portfolio streamlining. Management stressed 
that this is the first major shift in Carl Zeiss’ operating model in the history of the 
company and in addition to the direct financial implications it noted the broader 
cultural shift towards clearer accountability and return-focused resource allocation. 
China remains strategically essential, despite VBP and a more competitive local 
n
market. China is the largest ophthalmology market globally, and Carl Zeiss Meditec 
is accelerating localisation and local manufacturing of flagship products to remain 
competitive. The forthcoming lens tender, expected to be implemented from 
December, could result in pricing reductions (management suggested 20-30%) but 
having resolved recent registration issues the new tender process will enable Carl 
Zeiss to recommence sales of certain IOLs. Regarding Refractive, management 
pointed to 3%+ YTD (which we believe implies an improvement of trends in August); 
SMILE Pro penetration continues to increase. 
Product ecosystems and workflow integration support customer retention and 
n
differentiation. The company is simplifying its platform portfolio while retaining 
mission-critical products and building integrated surgical workflows that link devices 
across the operating pathway. Management also pointed to prioritisation / focus on 
R&D where they see scope to improve R&D productivity by reducing project 
proliferation and focusing resources on faster and more commercially relevant 
programmes. 
Microsurgery is the clearest near-term growth and margin bright spot. 
n
Leading-edge products are generating strong order growth that should support 
revenue into next year, while products such as PENTERO are competing effectively in 
China. New adjacent categories, including surgical visualisation, broaden the 
addressable market.

Ion Beam Applications (not covered) 
 
 
Presenter: Catherine Vandenborre, CFO 
Bottom line: We hosted a fireside with Catherine Vandenborre, CFO of IBA, discussing the 
group’s accelerator-technology platform across proton therapy, isotope production and 
medical-device sterilisation. The discussion focused on the growing clinical support for proton 
therapy, the related potential for reimbursement evolution and services-led margin progression, 
as well as longer-term opportunities in radiopharma and Actinium-225. 
Key takeaways: 
Clinical evidence is strengthening the case for wider Proton Therapy adoption: 
n
Proton Therapy remains a niche part of radiotherapy today, but management sees 
substantial scope for broader use as clinical evidence, reimbursement and treatment 
economics evolve. IBA noted expert consensus shows that around 13% of 
radiotherapy patients could benefit from Proton Therapy, compared with current 
penetration of around 1%. The recent MD Anderson study in head-and-neck cancer 
provides further support, showing lower mortality and toxicity vs. conventional 
radiotherapy, and management noted treatment guidelines have now changed in 
this indication. IBA expects the commercial implications to build over time: hospital 
decisions typically take at least 18 months, but interest is increasing and discussions 
with US authorities on reimbursement criteria are ongoing. Further work with MD 
Anderson will focus on quantifying the potential total cost benefits associated with 
lower toxicity and fewer side effects. 
Reimbursement dynamics. Today, an estimated 0.5-1.5% of radiotherapy patients 
n
receive PT; the company estimates this could reach 13%, with the “Dutch model” 
implying up to 20%. Management noted hospital conversations typically take at least 
18 months from initiation to conclusion, and that US reimbursement criteria have 
not yet changed materially, though discussions with US payer authorities on revising 
PT reimbursement criteria are ongoing. On national-scale procurement, Spain’s 
order for 10 PT systems was described as the largest in industry history (equipment 
funded philanthropically rather than by government, with hospitals responsible for 
maintenance), alongside national-first PT projects in Brazil and Portugal. 
Radiopharma, PanTera and industrial solutions provide longer-term growth 
n
optionality: IBA has identified the theranostics market (spanning diagnostics and 
treatment) as a ~$20bn opportunity by 2030. Their RadioPharma business helps 
nuclear-medicine departments design, build and operate isotope-production 
centres, while PanTera gives IBA exposure to Actinium-225 production for cancer 
treatment. IBA contributes its equipment and technical expertise, while its partner 
provides access to the raw material required for production, creating a capital-light 
model. Industrial Solutions remains another application for IBA’s technology, 
particularly in medical-device sterilisation, although management acknowledged 
competitive pressure in this market.

Presenter: Joseph Mercer, Senior Managing Director 
Bottom line: Marwood’s fireside chat focused on the evolving US trade and 
reimbursement-policy agenda, including the outstanding Section 232 medical-device 
investigation, competitive bidding for Ostomy and Catheter products, and the longer-term 
reform of skin-substitute payment. 
Key takeaways: 
The Section 232 investigation remains unresolved, with publication of the 
n
Administration’s findings still pending. The process may be extended as officials 
continue to assess complex medical-device supply chains and negotiate wider trade 
arrangements. The Nairobi Protocol is expected to remain in place for qualifying 
products. 
Competitive bidding for Ostomy and Catheters remains uncertain and could be 
n
delayed or challenged. CMS faces substantial administrative work before adding 
these categories, creating a meaningful chance that implementation slips beyond 
2028.  
Skin-substitute reform continues to prioritise spending control and stronger 
n
clinical evidence. CMS scrutiny and auditing remain intense, while the longer-term 
opportunity for higher reimbursement depends on demonstrating that premium 
products deliver materially better healing outcomes.

Key takeaways: 
Management sees potential for Roche’s Diagnostics business to accelerate 
n
towards the top end of the mid-to-high-single-digit growth after 2027/28, as

The initial launch of AXELIOS (Roche’s sequencing solution) has exceeded 
n
management expectations, although Roche’s first-year target remains

speed, flexibility, accuracy, scalability, and cost efficiency. Initial demand appears 
encouraging, with orders for c.30 instruments from academic centers and hospitals

unchanged at 100 instruments, likely reflecting a prudent approach to the early

Management expects FDA clearance for the majority of Roche’s Wave 1 mass 
n
spectrometry tests in 2027. Roche noted that current mass spectrometry 
workflows remain mostly manual and complex, creating an opportunity for its fully

reiterated its goal of reaching CHF1bn in sales within an estimated CHF4bn mass

receive US approval in 2027. 
Roche’s CGM solution has been launched in more than 30 countries, while the US 
n
launch remains pending with no specific timeline provided. Management 
highlighted the algorithm for predicting nocturnal hypoglycaemia as a key 
differentiator, while acknowledging that the current sensor size is a disadvantage 
versus competitors. Roche pointed to the second-generation sensor as smaller, more 
comfortable, and cheaper to manufacture. On launch progress, the company noted 
broad availability across more than 30 countries, but provided no specific guidance 
on US launch timing.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
