Key takeaways 
• Maintain Buy on TTE into NY investor day on September 28, which we see as positive 
catalyst underlining TTE's quality edge: 
• Growth from integrated footprint across oil, gas, LNG and power into 2030 (and 
beyond) offsets return to mid-cycle by 2028 
• We see growing consensus FCF upside from 2026 to 2030 - revealing TTE as 
mispriced at c11/12/14% FCF yields 2026/28/30 
Maintain top pick TTE and preference for quality > beta 
The rationale behind TTE as our top Big Oil pick is unchanged: TTE's superior organic 
volume (and corresponding FCF) growth exemplifies our preference for quality > chasing 
Hormuz beta: On our estimates, TTE's FCF best withstands the inevitable drop to mid-
cycle commodity prices (which we currently assume occurs by 2028): And yet, TTE is one 
of only two stocks left in our coverage pricing in <$65/bbl Brent long-term. 
Superior growth – and FCF – into 2030 (and beyond) 
We believe TTE’s portfolio has access to growth projects driving FCF organically higher 
and its oil price breakeven lower – both via sanctioned projects as well as pre-FID 
projects extending Upstream growth well beyond TTE’s 3% CAGR for 2030 – see Exhibit 
3 and 4: We show project-by-project growth adding almost 400kboepd production post 
2030 (almost 15% of TTE’s 2030 footprint). Before TTE’s integrated Power portfolio 
adding $5bn non-declining CFFO (without requiring any replacement capex). As a result, 
we expect TTE's CMD to leave capex unchanged and extend visibility on FCF growth well 
beyond 2030 – while confirming upside to its buyback run-rate into year-end 2026: 
Cash return upside into yearend 
As we highlighted in our review of TTE’s 2Q26 results (see TotalEnergies: Next: More 
upside at September CMD), we expect TTE to step up its quarterly buyback run-rate 
from $1.5bn each in 2Q26 and 3Q26 to $2.5bn in 4Q26 (which may already be 
announced at TTE’s investor day) – just to meet the minimum 40% of TTE’s CFFO 
payout policy (see Exhibit 5). We believe the latter overrides sensitivity surrounding 
France’s political climate – already outperforming the CAC40 by >40% this year. 
Premium growth, so why still no premium yield? 
TTE’s ~8% total distribution yield this year ranks inline with most of its Big Oil peers. 
But we see superior organic growth across TTE’s oil, gas and power portfolio (as well as 
Integrated Power's FCF inflection) as best equipped to offset our assumption of 
normalising commodity prices into 2028: We see TTE at ~12% FCF yield in 2028 at our 
"mid-cycle" macro deck ($70/bbl Brent + <$6/bbl European refining margin + $9/mbtu 
TTF). At the same deck, we see TTE's peers average ~8% FCF yields.

 What to expect from TTE in NY? 
TTE’s 2030 outlook: BofA Scorecard vs. consensus 
We expect only finetuning from TTE’s 2030 outlook at last year’s investor day – 
assuming TTE will stress continuity as a strength and rare evidence of counter-cyclical 
behaviour in its peer group. In this vein, we also assume TTE will not change its medium-
term commodity deck (which is close to our – unchanged – mid-cycle assumptions from 
2028 onwards). Accelerated FCF inflection in Integrated Power will be the main change: 
Integrated Power’s 2026 FCF inflection drives key improvement vs. last year 
The main improvement in its FCF outlook is the result of TTE’s acquisition of 50% of 
EPH’s European gas-fired power generation portfolio (see our positive assessment of 
the transaction in TotalEnergies: Full power ahead): 
VA consensus offers undemanding threshold for TTE’s outlook 
• 
We expect medium-term capex guidance to remain unchanged at $14-16bn p.a. This 
is down $1bn p.a. from last year’s investor day as a result of TTE completing the 
EPH transaction – but we note consensus estimates already point to the upper end 
of this range. 
• 
We also expect 2030 oil & gas production guidance to remain unchanged at 3% 
CAGR – but we note VA consensus today still only stands at ~2% (vs our 3%). 
• 
As a result of both of the factors above (given our underlying commodity deck is 
similar), we also see upside to consensus 2030 FCF at $20bn vs. our $27bn.

turning to net cash by the end of the decade – from ~$40bn at the end of 2025.
