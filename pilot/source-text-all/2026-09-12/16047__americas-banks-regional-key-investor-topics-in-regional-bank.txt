Regional bank and consumer finance stocks have been soft over the past 1-2 months 
as investors have been concerned with increasing deposit competition and what 
could happen if we get rate hikes (banks) as well as the rebound in oil prices back 
towards $100 and the softness in retailer results and what it could mean for spend / 
borrowing (consumer finance).  However, outside of these, it feels like we are in more 
of a micro market where investors are focused on issues facing specific companies.  
While we could likely have done this for a wider range of stocks, in this report we 
attempt to tackle the main stock-specific issues that we are being asked about and 
what our views are on each.  While some conclusions end up coming out similar to 
what either consensus or investors (based on our conversations) are thinking 
(HBAN), we believe our view is more upbeat on some (TFC, KEY) and potentially a 
touch more negative on others (ALLY, COF).  See below for company-specific 
debates and our views on each: 
COF: What is the right ROTCE (and EPS) in 2028? We think it is ~20%+ (street at 
23%) and could go to ~22% depending on capital leverage, with $26.30 - $27.30 
of EPS. A key debate for the stock has been what is the level of ROTCE the combined 
firm will be able to generate, and what does it imply for earnings power. COF has 
pointed to 2023 as the year it used for planning purposes, and we estimate COF/DFS 
combined generated a 16% ROTCE and when we layer on synergies, plus normalizing 
credit gets us closer to 22.1%.  We did the same exercise for 2024 and believe it also 
points to 22.3% and took a bottom up approach from 2Q26 to 2028 and believe the 
math implies a ~21% ROTCE in our base case (~12.5% CET1; no reserve releases) in 
2028 and with capital levered to ~11% we see a ROTCE of 20%+ which implies EPS of 
$26.30 / $27.30. While some will ask about 2027, keep in mind the deal was delayed 
6 months and we won’t see the earnings power until 2H27 (vs. a full year at that 
level). With the stock trading at 8x our base case, if the market can get comfortable 
with $26+ as the right earnings power, we believe shares should have a path to 
$260+ over time.  
ALLY: With the mid-point of its NCO guide pointing to flat losses YoY in 2H, what 
do we expect for 2027 losses? What do rate hikes mean for its 2027 NIM? We see 
ALLY’s losses coming in at ~1.90% in 2026 and ~1.95% in 2027 with rates hikes 
bringing ALLY’s NIM to ~3.74% in 4Q26 with a ~3.75% NIM in 2027 as NIM

TFC: What could the potential playbook for its new CEO be? While there are a 
handful of different directions he can go, we believe the focus will likely be on: 1) 
reducing auto loan exposure, with a focus on Regional Acceptance Corp and prime 
auto, 2) accelerating investments in consumer deposit growth, 3) further investing 
in fee-based areas including TM, payments, and wealth, 4) broader investments 
that would bring TFC’s costs relative to AEA closer to peers, 5) the potential roll-out 
of a continuous improvement program to help offset gross cost growth and 6) and 
potential for a securities restructuring. While there are a lot of moving pieces, we 
estimate TFC should be able to deliver at least a ~14% ROTCE in 2027 (15% guidance)

HBAN: Is there a path to $1.90-$1.93 of EPS in 2027? Yes. But it likely requires 
slowing of cost growth, releasing reserves and potentially buying back more stock 
(though it may be constrained given its adjusted CET1 target). In our view of 
operating trends, we see HBAN exiting the year with $1.61 of annualized earnings 
power, implying ~20% growth to reach $1.90 of earnings, and with continued NII growth 
plus fee outperformance, we see earnings reaching $1.83 in 2027. Adding on a touch 
slower cost growth vs. its 4Q exit plus ~5bps of reserve releases could bring earnings to 
$1.90 and so while we think (based on conversations) the street has begun to expect a 
reset to EPS expectations, we believe the market would like to see earnings reset closer 
to $1.80 so that the story can resume being a beat and raise as it was in the past.

in 4Q26 feels achievable with ~5bps of tailwinds from fixed rate repricing, along with 
benefits from accretive balance sheet growth, reduction in short-term debt, and no 
repeat of a handful of headwinds in 2Q (NPA non-accrual interest, SOFR dynamics). 
Stepping into 2027, we see an exit NIM of ~3.20% with ~13bps of expansion from fixed 
rate repricing and ~7bps from balance sheet growth / potential rate hike impact with a 
path to a ~15%+ ROTCE even if NIM expansion is slower. With the stock having lagged 
the KRE coming out of EPS on concerns over the pace of NIM expansion, we think the 
bar has been lowered and a reiteration of NIM expectations would be positive for the 
stock.

COF: What is the right ROTCE (and EPS) in 2028? 
 
 
While there has been lots of debate around the right ROTCE for COF as it realizes the 
last portion of the DFS cost saves (along with BREX / Hopper, and higher investment 
spend), historical math points to ~20%+ and in our base case (12.5% CET1), we 
estimate a ~20%+ ROTCE in 2028 and with capital levered to an ~11% CET1 we see a 
ROTCE of ~22.0%. While our base case is below the street at ~23% with the street 
having 1) faster loan growth, 2) reserve releases, 3) a higher margin - which could be 
a touch better than our expectations, we think the ~$26.30 is likely a base case for 
2028 EPS power (including normal macro caveats) and could have some upside.   
For a handful of quarters now, the focus for COF has been consistent - what is the right 
long-term earnings power of the company. While the path to reaching the destination 
has been extended (delay of synergy timing) and has had added wrinkles (acquisitions of 
BREX and Hopper, accelerating investment spend), COF has continued to highlight it 
sees the same earnings power (ROTCE) as it did at the time of the DFS announcement. So 
while the exact level of earnings power has not been defined, and it feels like the range 
of outcomes has narrowed, the question of the right medium-term earnings power 
remains outstanding.  
To assess where ROTCE can move to in 2028, we to see where 1) the historical math 
would point to, 2) annualizing 2Q’s results, adjusting for the lower reported NIM as well 
as expected remaining cost saves ($1bn) and flow in our estimates of balance sheet 
growth, costs and provision. For the historicals, we look to take 2023 / 2024’s PF results, 
flow in synergies, and normalize for the outsized reserve build in 2023 and higher losses 
in 2024 which points to a ROTCE of ~22%. Using 2Q as a jumping off point and assuming 
no reserve releases, we estimate a ROTCE of ~20.6% at a ~12.6% CET1 ratio with a 
~22.0% ROTCE if CET1 is lowered to ~11.1%. For earnings, in our base case it implies 
$26.30 of EPS in 2028 with $27.30 in the ~11% CET1 scenario. While our base case is 
~6% lower than the streets $28.00, if the market can get comfortable with a ~$26 EPS in 
2028, we think the stock should be able to trade back to $260 (a 10x P/E).  
Bridging from standalone to today - Normalizing for credit costs in 2023 / 2024 and 
adding synergies points to a ~22% average ROTCE: 
As a jumping off point, we look to COF and DFS’s long-term ROTCE to assess the baseline 
of combined earnings power. With COF at a ~14% ROTCE over time and DFS a ~25% 
ROTCE, the combined ROTCE of the two (ex. synergies) was ~17% between 2014-2024. 
With synergies of $2bn net of tax adding ~350bps to ROTCE, the back of envelope 
historical math would point to a ~20%+ ROTCE for COF.

While COF’s earnings power commentary reflects (at the time) forward looking views, we 
look to 2023 / 2024 to assess more recent earnings power trends particularly when 
normalizing for credit costs. With outsized reserve builds in 2023 given more challenging 
macro conditions (Exhibit 4) bringing PF ROTCE to 15.6% (below LT avg) and then the 
realization of higher credit losses in 2024 (Exhibit 5) also resulting in a ROTCE below LT 
averages of ~15%, we look to normalize for credit costs in both years to pull out core 
return trends in those periods. Starting with reported results, we note a PF ROTCE in 
2023 of ~15.6% and ~15.0% in 2024. 
Next in both years, we assume a ~3.20% loss rate (in-line with our 2026E estimate) 
which we view as ‘normalized’ losses for the combined companies which is ~30bps 
higher than reported in 2023 and ~60bps lower in 2024 which points to a ~90bps ROTCE 
benefit on average. For reserves, given outsized reserve builds in 2023 and flattish 
reserves in 2024, we manage each year to provide for 1) our normalized losses, 2) 
balance sheet growth relative to the BoP reserve rate which combined points to a 
~220bps benefit to ROTCE. Lastly, we flow in expected pre-tax synergies of $2.5bn 
which points to a ~400bps benefit to ROTCE. All together, this implies a ~22.1% ROTCE in 
2023 and a ~22.3% ROTCE in 2024 with an average of ~22.2%.  Given this, we think a 
ROTCE of ~20%+ as a baseline expectation is the right starting point for returns into 
2028.

To bridge it all to medium-term earnings power, we start by annualizing 2Q’s resulting 
which pointed ~$22 of EPS power (though it includes reserve releases) and adjust for 1) 
an 8.30% NIM, 2) a $1bn of incremental cost saves. Next, we flow in our estimates of 
AEA growth (+3.5%), interchange (+6%; in-line with purchase volume), and other (+3%) 
which points to core revenues of $70.66bn which is ~2% below the street largely on 
lower NII (-1.9% vs. the street) as well as lower other. For costs, we assume COF 
continues to drive a ~39% operating efficiency ratio while marketing grows ~8% annually 
and amortization of intangibles declines which points to $36.72bn of expenses or ~2.5% 
below the street. Together, this points to core PPNR of $33.94bn which is ~1% below the 
street.   
In terms of credit, we assume a consolidated NCO rate of 3.20% which is 3bps below 
2Q’s results and 2bps below consensus while assuming a relatively stable reserve rate 
which implying a provision of $17.27bn (~1% ahead of the street). All together, this 
points to pre-tax income of $16.67bn or ~3% below the street (and ~9% higher vs. 2Q 
annualized).  
For capital, we assume two scenarios: 1) our base case, where COF manages its CET1 to 
12.5% in-line with deal targets, 2) COF brings its CET1 to ~11% closer to its LT targets. In 
the 12.5% scenario, we estimate repurchases of $9.5bn in 2027 / 2028 and in the ~11% 
scenario we estimate $14bn of repurchases in 2027 / 2028.  
Bringing it all together for earnings and ROTCE, in our base case scenario we see $26.30 
of EPS which implies a ~20.6% ROTCE. While this points to earnings ~6% below the 
street (and ROTCE ~190bps), we see this as a floor for earnings power given we do not 
assume the additional capital leverage, further reserve releases, or a higher margin. For 
the 11% scenario, we estimate $27.30 of EPS in 2028 or a ~22.0% ROTCE. So with the 
stock trading at ~8x our base case EPS and what we think is a floor for ROTCE in 2028, if 
the market can get comfortable with this as the right earnings power there should be 
meaningful upside for shares.

ALLY: What do we expect for 2027 losses? What do rate hikes mean for its 2027 NIM? 
 
 
We see ALLY’s losses coming in at ~1.90% in 2026 and ~1.95% in 2027 (consensus of 
1.79%) with rates hikes bringing ALLY’s NIM to ~3.74% in 4Q26 with a ~3.75% NIM 
in 2027 (consensus of 3.80%) as NIM expansion slows in 1H and increases 1-2bps in 
2H assuming the fed pauses.  
ALLY reported a 2Q26 retail auto NCO rate of ~1.57%, down ~-18bps YoY (vs. down 
~15bps YoY in 1Q), while DQs of ~4.80% (all-in) were down ~-8bps YoY (vs. ~-17bps YoY 
in 1Q) and DQs excluding non-accruals were down ~2bps YoY to 3.89% (vs. -10bps YoY 
in 1Q). While 2Q losses continue to show a downward bias, a handful of factors have led 
to an investor focus on the path of retail auto losses through 2H26 as well as into 2027 
including 1) DQs beginning to flatten out on a YoY basis, 2) ALLY’s commentary around 
the potential for losses to be flat (to slightly up) YoY in 2H assuming a loss rate of ~1.90% 
(Exhibit 12) while the street is expecting a continued YoY improvement in losses (Exhibit 
13), 3) ALLY’s 2Q26 vintage data which showed the 2025 vintage underperforming the 
2024 vintage on DQs and losses (although in-line with company expectations) while an 
early read on 2026 pointed to credit trends in-line with or slightly worse than 2025’s 
vintage. Given this, we look to assess the path of losses for ALLY through 2H26 and into 
2027, where we see losses coming in at ~1.90% in 2026 (vs. consensus of 1.85%) and 
1.95% in 2027 (vs. consensus of 1.79%).  
DQ formation rates up on a YoY basis, with pressure largely coming from seasoning of 
2025 vintage 
 
In terms of DQs, as noted above ALLY has begun to see the YoY change in its DQs level 
off following the impact of tighter underwriting in the 2024 vintage as well as the 
burnoff of higher DQ vintages in 2022 and 2023 which is in-line with the trend in T12M 
DQ formation rates which troughed in 3Q25 and have since started to move higher 
(Exhibit 14). On a YoY basis, ALLY’s all-in DQs were down ~8bps YoY in 2Q, though we 
note that the T12M DQ formation rate turned positive on a YoY basis for the first time 
since 3Q23, which likely points to stable or higher DQs on a YoY basis in the coming 
quarters (Exhibit 15).

TFC: What could the potential playbook for its new CEO be? 
 
 
While there are a handful of different directions he can go, we believe the focus will 
likely be on: 1) reducing auto loan exposure, with a focus on Regional Acceptance 
Corp and prime auto, 2) accelerating investments in consumer deposit growth, 3) 
further investing in fee-based areas including TM, payments, and wealth, 4) 
broader investments that would bring TFC’s costs relative to AEA closer to peers, 5) 
the potential roll-out of a continuous improvement program to help offset gross 
cost growth and 6) and potential for a securities restructuring. While there are a lot 
of moving pieces, we estimate TFC should be able to deliver at least a ~14% ROTCE 
in 2027 (15% guidance) including the impact of all the potential strategic changes, 
which is ahead of the ~13% ROTCE that the market seems to be implying. So with 
the market pricing in downside to the ~15% target in 2027, we think committing to 
delivering at least ~14% and reiterating future targets would be well-received by 
investors.  
Following the announcement of the current TFC’s CEO retirement and appointment of 
Mike Lyons as an incoming CEO, investors have been focused on the types of strategic 
changes that could be made at TFC to enhance the LT earnings power of the business, 
though with the potential changes leading to lower revenues / higher costs in 2027.  We 
think (based on conversations) that investors are expecting the 2027 ROTCE target of 
~15% to be put on hold. While we expect the incoming CEO to focus on a handful of 
areas within the business including 1) a further reduction in auto loan exposure to 
generate a more balanced through the cycle risk-adjusted margin, 2) consumer deposit 
growth to regain market share as well as help pay down wholesale funding, 3) 
accelerating growth / investments in fee-based areas to improve ROA’s, 4) broader 
investment growth to bring TFC’s cost base relative to earning assets in-line with peers, 
and 5) a continuous improvement program to help offset some of the incremental 
investment growth.  
Below, we look to estimate the market implied ROTCE in 2027 (~13%) along with 
attempt to put some numbers around what the financial implication of the strategic 
changes could be. Within 1) auto, we think a reduction of RAC / indirect auto with 
proceeds deployed into repurchases would result in -$0.03 EPS headwind, 2) consumer 
deposit growth, an acceleration of investments would result in a -$0.06 EPS headwind, 
3) accelerating investment spend outside of consumer would result in a -$0.20 
headwind, 4) a continuous improvement program would be a $0.13 benefit. So all 
together, we think this implies at a minimum a ~14% ROTCE for TFC in 2027 which is 
above the ~13% the market is pricing in.

HBAN: Is there a path to $1.90-$1.93 of EPS in 2027? 
 
 
YES.  Although it likely would require slowing cost growth or releasing reserves. 
While we believe there is a path to $1.90+ of EPS, based on our expectations for loan 
growth, margin expansion and fee growth it will require some changes in other 
areas.  While we expect an exit run-rate of just above $1.60 in 4Q26, reaching $1.90 
will require close to 20% EPS growth from 4Q’s levels.  While our base case 
operating expectations gets us to a smidge above $1.80 of EPS, HBAN may choose to 
slow cost growth (which we believe adds $0.02) and release reserves (5bps adds 
$0.04).  Lastly, HBAN could also consider buying back more stock; however given its 
desire to remain above 9% on an adjusted basis combined with its strong loan 
growth, we believe the incremental buyback capacity would be marginal.  
Following the acquisition of CADE in 3Q25, HBAN laid out expectations for $2 of 
pro-forma EPS in 2027 (implying ~10% accretion), and in February provided an updated 
view on EPS for 2026 / 2027 where it revised its EPS expectations to $1.90 - $1.93 lower 
due to lower purchase accounting accretion (given finalization of rate marks) but 
increased its capital return expectations. Since then, HBAN has reported 2 quarters of 
mixed results (softer NII / NIM trends and higher fees) along with guidance revisions that 
pointed to slower NII growth trends (pointed to NII at the low-end of expectations of 
+39-43% in 1Q; NII at or a touch below the low-end of +39% in 2Q) while increasing its 
fee income expectations and reducing cost growth. Coming out of 2Q EPS, there has 
been an increased focus on 1) 2026 and 4Q NII expectations given the moving pieces 
within the guide point to NII growth closer to ~+36-37% YoY (vs. guidance of +39% or a 
touch below), 2) EPS expectations into 2027, including HBAN’s ability to reach its 
$1.90-$1.93 expectations or whether the 2027 guidance will need to be adjusted lower 
to reflect the slower NII growth trends.  
Below, we look to size the moving pieces of NII, fees, and costs through 2H and what it 
implies for 4Q exit rate EPS where we estimate ~$0.40 or a ~$1.61 annualized run-rate. 
With ~19% implied growth to reach $1.90+ vs. the exit rate annualized, we flow in loan 
growth (+5%), NIM expansion (+4bps) and higher fees (13% YoY vs. ~10% expected) 
which points to ~$1.83 of EPS in 2027.  So with EPS likely coming up short of the 2027 
EPS expectations, we think HBAN could either 1) look to modestly reduce its cost growth 
expectations in 2027 (a $0.02 contribution) as well as release reserves (~5bps release 
would provide $0.05 of EPS) to reach $1.90 of earnings, 2) reduce the guide to $1.80+, 
where we think a reset is somewhat expected given investors (based on conversations) 
are expecting EPS to come in below the $1.90+ and a clear path towards to $1.80 could 
support shares as it would imply a ~9.5x P/E multiple, a discount to peers.

KEY: Where do we see KEY’s 4Q26 exit NIM reaching and is 3.25% achievable in 2027? 
 
 
We see its NIM reaching 3.00% in 4Q26 and while we believe there is likely a path to 
a 3.25% NIM, given the competitive environment we believe something closer to 
3.20% is likely more achievable though it will depend on KEY’s ability to fund 
incremental loan growth at deposit rates closer to ~2-2.25%.  Any upside beyond 
that is likely to be driven by widening loan spreads, better fixed rate repricing, or 
lower-cost deposit growth. That said, we still think with a lower NIM KEY can reach 
NII expectations and importantly its 15+% ROTCE expectations.   
KEY’s 2Q26 NIM came in at 2.89%, up 2bps QoQ (but below consensus of 2.95%), as it 
benefited from core fixed rate repricing as well as headwinds from a 4bps move lower in 
overnight SOFR and the impact of higher NPAs. For 2H26, KEY adjusted its exit rate NIM 
expectations to 3-3.05% (from 3.05% prior) while it reiterated a 2027 exit rate NIM of 
3.25%. Following EPS, there has been an increased focus from investors (based on 
conversations) on KEY’s ability to reach its NIM expectations in 4Q26 and the pacing of 
progress towards a ~3.25% exit NIM in 4Q27. For 2H26, we think KEY can reach a 3.00% 
exit NIM as fixed rate repricing tailwinds (+5bps to NIM) as well as managing deposit 
costs should help outweigh the incremental pressure from a rate hike (balance sheet is 
neutral to slightly liability sensitive) though it points to NII that is in-line with the street 
(lower NIM and slightly larger balance sheet).  
To reach a 3.25% exit NIM, KEY had previously highlighted (at 3Q25 earnings) that of the 
~50bps of expansion expected, it expected 1/2 to come from fixed rate repricing 
tailwinds vs. the other 1/2 attributable to ‘business performance’, which we believe is 
largely due to funding new loan growth at an accretive net spread (loan growth priced at 
~6% funding with deposits priced at ~2% is accretive). With an incremental ~13bps of 
fixed rate repricing tailwinds in 2027, that should help KEY make progress towards its 
NIM target and while rate hikes are likely to be a slight near-term negative, if KEY can 
fund new loan growth with the low-cost deposits its expecting, it should be able to reach 
a ~3.20% NIM (vs. 3.19% street), and while we see exit NII in 4Q27 a touch below 
expectations ($1.416bn vs. cons of $1.428bn), we think it should allow KEY to reach its 
~15%+ ROTCE target even if the NIM expansion is slower.  
So while the stock has lagged a bit since EPS (-5.3% vs. -3.5% KRE; though its 
outperformed the last week) on concerns over the trajectory of the NIM, we think if KEY 
can demonstrate progress towards a ~3.00% exit and give the market confidence in the 
2027 path, we could see some of that underperformance begin to reverse.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
