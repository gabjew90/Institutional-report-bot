Japan at the line
The Nikkei has retraced to the trend line that has defined Japan’s bull market, just as the 100-day MA
gives way. A stronger yen, higher oil and rising US yields have created a toxic mix, while market
breadth remains narrow.
But this is not yet a full trend break. Medium- and long-term CTAs remain long, the key unwind levels
sit lower and the latest JPY move may have overshot. Japan is now at the point where a correction
either finds support, or becomes something much bigger.
Big in Japan
The Nikkei has retraced all the way to the major trend line in place since Japan’s bull market began.
The upside mojo is gone, but we are now approaching key support levels.
Note that the index has just dipped below the 100-day MA. The last time that happened, the break
lasted only a few sessions before the uptrend resumed.

The JPY connection
The massive moves in JPY have obviously weighed on the Nikkei. GS estimates that a ¥10 appreciation
in the yen reduces TOPIX EPS by 3.5%. Still, history offers plenty of examples of Japanese equities
rising despite a strengthening currency.

Japan’s FX sweet spot
JPM sees USD/JPY at 155–160 as the sweet spot for Japanese equities, but 150 is no major threat. A
move from 160 to 150 would shave around 5ppt from operating-profit growth, but strong TOPIX
earnings and improved dollar returns for overseas investors should provide a cushion.
The bigger warning level is above 160, where renewed yen weakness risks eroding wage gains through
higher inflation and becoming a Japan-specific equity headwind.

The oil problem
Japan is among the developed markets most exposed to higher oil, but JPY determines how much of
that damage reaches equities. A weaker yen cushions exporters, offsetting some of the pressure from
rising input costs.
Oil up alongside a stronger yen is the toxic combination: higher costs, a larger import bill and lower
translated earnings. That is the double hit currently weighing on the Nikkei.

The real risk
UBS sees an uncontrolled yen rally as the key downside risk for Japanese equities. A decisive break
below USD/JPY 150 could trigger a self-reinforcing appreciation cycle; combined with higher US yields,
that would hit both earnings expectations and valuation multiples.
That is not its base case. With almost two additional BoJ hikes already priced and the Fed expected to
remain higher for longer, UBS sees the yen rally as an overshoot and expects USD/JPY to return
towards 155–160.
