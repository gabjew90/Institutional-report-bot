Flows holding back 
Inflows to bonds slowed driven by funds, while flows to stocks and loans remained 
lackluster this past week ending Sep 9th.  
Inflows moderated for US HG bond funds and ETFs, with a +$4.74bn inflow down from a 
+$7.76bn inflow in the prior week. Since IG corporates account for about a third of the 
high grade universe AUM, that translates into ~$1.5bn demand for IG. That slowdown 
was primarily driven by HG Funds (to +$0.46bn from +$3.28bn), as previous week inflow 
was inflated by the typically large inflow during the first day of the month. Inflows were 
stable into HG ETFs (to +$4.28bn from +$4.47bn). Inflows slowed for both short-term 
HG (to +$2.93bn from +$3.19bn) and ex. short-term (to +$1.81bn from +$4.57bn). 
Loans > HY 
Stocks again reported small outflows this past week (to -$4.48bn from -$7.84bn), while 
flows turned flat for HY (to +$0.06bn from +$0.61bn). Inflows also slowed for loans (to 
+$0.17bn from +$0.37bn). On the flip side, inflows accelerated for global EM bonds (to 
+$0.34bn from +$0.15bn) and for munis (to +$0.40bn from +$0.21bn). Finally, money 
markets reported a -$5.97bn outflow this past week (the biggest outflow in 7 weeks), 
following a +$22.89bn inflow a week earlier.

inflow was +$0.46bn (vs +$3.28bn a week prior). Since 09/2025, both HG Funds and ETFs, and the 12-week 
moving average have dipped at the end of 2024 and April 2025. Flows have rebounded since then.

Appendix: defining high grade 
We define our high grade flows metric as a combination of “bond” and “corporate bond” 
fixed income funds and ETFs domiciled in the US (and investing in any currency, 
although the majority is invested in USD). We also exclude funds primarily focused on 
Government, HY, EM and MBS from the sample. The “bond” category refers to the broad 
high grade market, which includes Treasuries, mortgages and agencies in addition to 
corporate bonds. This category accounts for the majority of AUM and flows. On the 
other hand, the dedicated corporate bond funds and ETFs are much smaller in terms of 
assets under management. We also include the “total return” bond category in our 
tracking of high grade flows. Finally, note that “short-term” maturity refers to duration 
of 0 to 4 years. On average, IG corporate bonds account for about ~32% of the total 
AUM of our high grade bond fund / ETF universe.

Daily foreign demand tracker 
Trading overnight (prior to 8am) as well as with affiliates are proxies for foreign demand 
for USD IG corporate bonds. Negative values for “net dealer buy” denote net buying by 
foreign investors (i.e. US dealers taking down inventory in favor of foreign affiliates). 
Exhibit 19 and Exhibit 20 show net dealer-to-all volumes before 8am ET (biased toward 
Asian demand) while Exhibit 21 and Exhibit 22 show net dealer-to-affiliates volumes 
between 8am and noon (biased toward European demand). Exhibit 23 shows the 
relationship between foreign buying and change in treasury yields. A drop in yield 
typically leads to lower foreign demand, though not always.

Note: Negative values denote net client buying. Morning share of daily trading volume is correlated
