Key takeaways 
• PEG: EO1 comments should clarify the potential earnings and capital implications of 
New Jersey utility reform. 
• Data centers: Our Wyoming tour to provide insight on the visibility and infrastructure 
needs behind large-load growth. 
• WEC: Rate case hearing evidentiary hearings begin next week on proposed 2027-
2028 increases 
NJ EO1 comments provide the next read on PEG 
We expect the EO1 comments to narrow the range of potential reforms to New Jersey’s 
electric utility framework, a positive for PEG if the process preserves timely investment 
recovery. The state is considering performance-based regulation and other changes 
intended to improve affordability and customer outcomes. The key question is whether 
the resulting framework maintains a constructive earnings opportunity while holding 
utilities more accountable for bill impacts. Greater clarity would reduce uncertainty for 
PEG and help frame the regulatory backdrop surrounding PSE&G’s expected year-end 
rate case filing. 
BofA Wyoming field trip: data-center load opportunity 
We expect our September 16-17 tour in Cheyenne to provide a firmer view of 
Wyoming’s emerging data-center opportunity and its broader relevance across utilities. 
Visits to operating, under-construction, and planned sites will highlight the scale of 
prospective demand and the infrastructure required to serve it. Discussions with 
government, utility, and economic-development representatives will also test local 
support for further growth. The central investor question is which utilities can convert 
large-load interest into contracted demand, incremental investment, and earnings 
without shifting undue costs or development risk to existing customers. 
WEC’s ~$480m rate request moves to hearings 
WEC’s evidentiary hearings begin next week on proposed 2027-2028 increases totaling 
~$480m across electric, gas, and steam rates. The request includes ~$347m of electric 
increases and ~$132m of gas increases over the two-year period. A typical residential 
electric bill would rise by approximately $13 per month in 2027 and another $8-9 in 
2028. The filing supports system investment and resource adequacy, with new rates 
requested for January 2027 and January 2028.
