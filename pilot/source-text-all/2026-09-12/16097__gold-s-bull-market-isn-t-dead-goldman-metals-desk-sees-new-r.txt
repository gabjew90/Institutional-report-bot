Gold's Bull Market Isn't Dead: Goldman Metals Desk 
Sees New Record Highs Coming After "Elongated 
Pause" 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, SEP 10, 2026 - 12:15 PM 
Gold peaked in January, had a decent August, and is still sitting ~20% off the high. 
 
The obvious question that many are asking is: was that the top? 
In a word, Goldman thinks "no". 
Speaking from the London floor on 'The Markets'  YouTube channel, Goldman’s global head of 
metals trading, Tony Kim, isn’t buying the peak narrative. 
Clients have been hammering him on it for weeks. His answer is blunt: 
"From our perspective, this isn't the end of the bull market, it's an elongated pause." 
And later: 
"Ultimately the bull trend will resume and new highs are going to be in the future." 
The pause, he says, is two shocks hitting at once - and while it's easy to see their effect on price 
it is hard to separate them... 


[Page 2]
 
First: the Warsh nomination and confirmation as Fed chair. 
The market is still trying to map his reaction function inside a Trump administration that has not 
been shy about how it wants the Fed to behave. 
Second: the US-Iran conflict and the Hormuz disruption. 
Not just oil, energy, ag, metals, all triggering both inflation and policy risk - and most notably, 
that disruption has"disturbed" the recycling of reserve flows that used to leak into bullion. 
Additionally,  Kim notes that "a large amount" of positioning across the franchise has been 
slashed. 
The one flow that hasn’t disappeared is the one that really matters: central banks. 
"We mine about 3,500 metric tons of gold a year. Prior to Russia-Ukraine, central banks used to 
purchase around 400 to 500 tons a year. They're now purchasing something closer to 1,000, 
1,100 tons a year.  
What that means is the amount of gold remaining for all other purposes, jewelry, backing up the 
ETF, bars and physical investment, is much a smaller funnel.  
And so you don't need as much investment capital to drive prices materially higher." 
The debasement argument is the same one gold bulls have been running for years, just now 
with fiscal sustainability doing more of the work: 
"There's been a cheapening of fiat currency versus gold that's been a trend that we've been in 
for now several years." 
If fiscal fear - not just in the West, but Japan - becomes a driving factor, Kim says the usual 
inverse with yields can snap: 
"If fiscal sustainability becomes the real driver of whether people want to allocate into gold, and 
fiscal sustainability is a concern not just in the West, but in Japan, then you might actually start 
to see the correlations break down a little bit. 
So in other words, higher back-end bond yields because of concerns about the fiscal situation 
could lead to allocations going into gold." 
While Kim notes that day-to-day, gold still trades with real rates; but a longer-term trend basis, 
the break with the old real-rate playbook, in his telling, dates to 2022 - Russia-Ukraine and the 
confiscation of Russian central bank reserves. After that, EM official buying changed the regime 
around gold's ecosystem. 
Simply put, on a multi-year basis, the funnel got narrower and the official sector got hungrier. 


[Page 3]
 
 
Kim’s tell for when that psychology kicks in: official fingerprints on markets like USDJPY 
intervention and/or Treasury buying the long end to massage the curve. 
"I think any time you see official policy intervention, people tend to buy gold." 
Asia is the weak side of the pause. 
China retail, India physical, and official buying were all strong into year-end, but Hormuz and the 
energy shock changed the math. 
Reserves that would have been generated in the Gulf aren’t showing up the same way. 
Additionally, India has to defend the currency to keep energy flowing, therefore gold 
accumulation is not “top of mind,” and New Delhi has been trying to throttle domestic demand to 
protect the rupee. 
Kim’s view: you need a longer normalization of energy markets and Hormuz before that bid 
returns at prior scale. 
"Another reason it’s a pause, not a cycle top." 
Silver gets the usual warning label. Smaller is a much smaller market, he notes, adding that half 
the demand is industrial, which means the 20% that is investment demand is what actually 
clears the tape - at $50, $80, or $100, depending on whether India, China retail, and Western 
ETFs show up at the same time. 
When they did in January, you saw what a squeeze looks like.  


[Page 4]
 
 
Then 20–30% air pockets, with 'spot up, vol up' moves in the silver complex leaving the precious 
metal highly unstable. 
Silver, Kim says, is a higher beta outcome in a higher-gold market  if retail piles back into the 
theme. 
"For me, the fundamental trade still remains gold.  
That's where the institutions are deployed, that's what central banks are buying.  
And as I always say, central banks so far, to my knowledge, aren't actively accumulating silver, 
so you kind of have to bet on the retail reaction function." 
Finally, Chris Hussey asks "what's the trade?" 
"Look, we're still bullish gold." 
The level: 
"In terms of a level that we like 4,000 is a pretty solid floor.  
We see sovereign buying at those levels. We see institutional sponsorship at those levels.  
And so I think if you get a chance to scale in between now and the FOMC with some of the 
volatility around the data, closer to 4K, you want to scale into a long position there." 
The nearest-term catalyst is CPI (on Friday) into the September meeting. Kim is not just 
watching the print, he is focused on the market’s reaction function. 
Does the tape immediately price a September hike and a flatter curve, or does it admit it still 
doesn’t know how Warsh (and the White House) will respond? Gold will lean the way that 
interpretation leans. 
The bottom (bullish) line is that with speculative books washed out, the official sector still 
absorbing a third of annual mine supply, fiat still cheapening, and policy (cough Bessent cough)

hands still on the currency and the long end, $4,000 is where Goldman says the sovereigns and 
the institutions show up.
