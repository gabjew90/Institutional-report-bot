GS O'Connor - Lev Fin Weekend Reading Observations football is
here and so is trifurcation 13 Sep 2026
KC O'Connor  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, FICC & Equities
Sun 13 Sep 2026, 4:00pm ET
Following the long weekend, the S&P 500 fell 1.8% into Thursday’s close as investors assessed the impact
of stubbornly high yields and energy prices on risk assets. The 10y UST came close to topping 5% – a
level that has been surpassed just once since 2007 – and even then, only briefly, during a single trading
session almost three years ago16.
Friday’s August CPI figures came in slightly warmer than expected – the 66th consecutive month above
the Fed’s 2% target – lifting bets to ~87% that a 25bps rate hike was forthcoming at this week’s FOMC
meeting, a prognosis GIR now agrees with in their revised forecast (link)17.
Amidst this backdrop, credit spreads remained resilient – particularly impressive given borrowers tapped
the market at a near historic cadence, taking advantage of cash balances which had built over the end-of-
summer slowdown. IG recorded the fourth busiest primary week of the year at $73bn. $42.3bn of loans
launched across 30 deals with M&A and LBO-backed transactions comprising $10.8bn – the most active
week for such activity since the week ended March 20th and more than was seen in July and August…
combined15. Not to be outdone, HY was similarly active with $11.98bn printing across 10 credits and 13
tranches – the highest weekly tally since early June’s $13.28bn and just shy of the entirety of August15.
As the market (and football season) gets back into full swing, three distinct segments are separating within
Lev Fin: 1) the generics; 2) the AI universe; and 3) the lower-quality credits. Taking a look at all three,
below, shows just how different the respective components currently are, in spite of the tranquility in
spreads21.
THE GENERICS
Last week was not without its bumps from a total return perspective – on Thursday, the HY market
experienced its worst one-day selloff since March, as yields widened to their five-month highs26. Looking
under the hood tells a different story that is more reflective of the accommodative tone that currently
prevails:
1. Earnings have been good. With 2Q earnings largely in the books, leverage for the median HY issuer
remained well below the peaks seen in the 2015 commodity disruption and the 2020 pandemic, sitting
generally toward the lower end of the post-GFC range18. By sector, only communication services and
consumer staples saw EBITDA declines20 2. Spreads are tight. Despite last week’s move wider on yield,
HY spreads tightened 2bps WoW to 265bps, leaving the asset class in its 3rd percentile on a post-2007
lookback basis and well inside the long-run average of roughly 380-420bps. Digging in more shows 62%
of the index currently trades inside 200bps and 64% of double-Bs trade inside 150bps – levels more
consistent with IG-adjacent double-B paper than broader HY risk – reflecting the elevated double-B


[Page 2]
composition of the current index (~56%) and the outsized impact of the distressed tail, which skews the
mean significantly above the median (more on this to follow) 3. The capital markets window is open. Last
week’s impressive vintage of issuance told a succinct story: performing/generic risk is in demand. 12 of
the 13 bonds that priced commanded at least one double-B rating, and despite the asset class’s weekly loss,
every deal with visible secondary levels trading ~up – a substantial outperformance, particularly given that
overall new issue volume represented the second-highest post-Labor Day week on record, underscoring
how much demand there was even for valuation-sensitive credits22
THE AI UNIVERSE
The first point – by design – failed to mention the one topic 2026 has been defined by: AI issuance.
$38.9bn of HY AI paper has come so far in 2026, representing 17.7% of total annual issuance figure, and
yet, in such a robust week for primary volumes, none (in HY) came from the AI space.
Our HY AI datacenter custom basket (GSHYAIDC) now trades at a spread of 353bps – 88bps wide of not
only its tights from June 22nd, but also the broader HY market. Furthermore, when looking at all AI-
centric issuance that has come in 2025 and 2026, the respective OAS of that cohort is even wider at
381bps, showing that the growing universe of AI issuers is operating within its own playbook…

THE LOWER-QUALITY CREDITS
If AI has a separate playbook, lower-quality credits are playing a completely different game. Last week
saw more of the same, with several stressed instruments making new lows on limited sponsorship. This


[Page 3]
tested the market’s pain threshold and challenged investors to put incremental capital into already cheap
capital structures – rather than into the expanding AI ecosystem.
The par value of the Morningstar US HY Bond Index’s distressed subset shrank for the first time in nearly
two months, showing that the cohort of stressed vs. performing credits continued to narrow24. However,
the index’s OAS still widened, and triple-C yields touched new three-year highs, leaving the triple-C vs
single-B basis at its widest level since COVID23,.
GIR believes there are three primary reasons why the relationship widened:
1. Dispersion within the triple-C cohort remains elevated, reflecting greater issuer-level differentiation; 2.
Positive net rating migration (from the triple-C category into single-B) has created a mechanical effect on
index-level valuations, as the tighter-trading triple-C bonds exited the rating cohort; and 3. A persistent
'left tail' of issuers remains where weak balance sheets, sector-specific pressures, and limited refinancing
flexibility overlap
TAKEAWAY
Much like the first full week of football, last week offered an early – and at times humbling – reminder
that the standings can look very different by season's end. While one week does not a trend make, the
divergence across these three segments is worth watching closely. Many opportunities will likely arise
from lower-rated, currently unloved credits (reach out to compare notes with our FSG analysts) — but a
cheap segment of the market could also continue to get cheaper as investors determine where their
marginal dollar will be deployed for the remainder of the year.
That calculus will increasingly be shaped by AI. Up to $50bn of additional HY AI issuance could still
come in 2026 in an optimal environment – and as AI continues to comprise a larger share of the HY
universe, spreads within that cohort have begun to deviate meaningfully from the broader non-AI median.
The central question heading into year-end is whether these three distinct segments – the generics, the AI
universe, and the lower-quality credits – will continue to operate independently of one another, or whether
the median issuer will ultimately be forced to reprice in response
