EXECUTIVE SUMMARY - FIVE KEY TAKEAWAYS
The nature of the threat has changed, not just the scale
Global cybercrime cost estimated at $1.2tn to $10.5tn a year - the size of a G20 economy. Generative AI is the force multiplier: most 
phishing is now AI-generated, ~76% of malware is AI-polymorphic, and the first largely autonomous AI-run espionage campaign was 
documented in late 2025.
Cyber risk is now a key factor in geopolitics
WEF ranks geoeconomic confrontation as the top short-term global risk; NATO has clarified that significant cyberattacks may be 
treated as equivalent to an armed attack under Article 5. The JLR (£1.9bn), UK retail, and Poland grid attacks showed macro-scale 
transmission.
Regulation is moving from disclosure to liability
2026 is the enforcement year: DORA supervision live, NIS2 deadlines, CRA incident reporting from 11 Sep 2026, AI Act high-risk rules 
from Aug 2026; SEC 4-day reporting and Reg S-P bind US boards from June 2026.
Cybersecurity is the missing pillar in 'S' and 'G'
Hospital ransomware, grid intrusions and mass data theft are social harms with identifiable victims. Board oversight is now a 
proxy-advisor expectation, yet disclosure remains thin - a measurable, closable governance gap.
Investment implications: the SG Cybersecurity Index
SGIXCYSE (25 stocks) is up +36.2% ytd vs +16.2% for the Nasdaq 100; trading at 24.1x forward earnings (10% premium vs 20% on 
average) with a more resilient EPS growth profile to 2028.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM - FIVE CHANNELS
1. Infrastructure concentration
Compute concentrates in a handful of clouds whose customers now include governments 
themselves; the March 2026 European Commission cloud breach shows supply chains transmit risk 
upward. Idiosyncratic incidents become systemic - the DORA scenario.
2. Data gravity
Training and inference centralise unprecedented volumes of proprietary and personal data in new 
pipelines (vector databases, feature stores, fine-tuning sets), mostly outside legacy data-protection 
tooling.
3. The agentic action surface
Agents hold credentials, API keys and payment rights; compromising one compromises everything it 
can do, at machine speed. Prompt injection turns every document an agent reads into a potential 
instruction channel.
4. Shadow adoption
57% of employees use personal generative-AI accounts for work and a third upload sensitive 
material to unsanctioned tools - the fastest-growing data-egress channel is unmanaged by design.
5. The physical nexus
Data centres are energy infrastructure: grid interconnections, cooling and building-management OT* 
inherit the critical-infrastructure threat model, and regional concentration creates physical-cyber 
chokepoints.
Cyber exposure is a derivative of AI adoption, with positive convexity: each increment of adoption adds interconnection, 
autonomy and data centralisation simultaneously.
Source: SG Cross Asset Research / Sustainability; * OT: Operational Technology is the hardware and software systems used to monitor, control, and automate physical devices, processes, and infrastructure in the real world

Why the arms race stays asymmetric
Adoption lag
Attackers do not have to deal with procurement 
cycles, change boards or compliance reviews; 
defenders do.
Surface asymmetry
The defender must secure every asset, identity 
and supplier; the attacker needs one unguarded 
path - and AI searches for it continuously.
Verification 
asymmetry
Generating synthetic content is computationally 
cheap; verifying authenticity at scale is 
expensive and unsolved.
The gap between well-defended and poorly defended organizations is widening. Beyond AI, post-quantum 
cryptography migration ('harvest now, decrypt later') is already generating mandated spending, with migration deadlines 
clustering around 2030-35.

STEWARDSHIP TOOLKIT - 15 QUESTIONS FOR ANY ISSUER
These questions can be used for any portfolio company, not just technology names. They are sequenced from governance to operations 
to AI-specific controls. Evasive answers to questions 1-5 should be treated as findings in their own right.
Core governance (Q1-5)…
1.  Which board committee owns cyber and AI risk, and how often was 
it briefed in the last year?
2.  Who is the CISO, to whom do they report, and what share of IT 
budget goes to security?
3.  Is any element of executive remuneration linked to security or 
AI-governance performance?
4.  Has a board-level cyber crisis simulation been run in the past 24 
months - and what has changed as a result?
5.  Which incidents met internal materiality thresholds, and how were 
disclosure decisions documented?
…then operations & AI controls (Q6-15)
•
External assurance, detection/containment metrics, supplier 
security, 2026 regulatory readiness, CRA conformity costs
•
Shadow-AI governance; testing against prompt injection, 
poisoning and model extraction; sign-off before agents receive 
tool access
•
Authentication of high-risk instructions against deepfake and 
voice-cloning fraud; cyber-insurance exclusions and residual 
exposure
Four sector modules
A - Financials (DORA perimeter)   B - Healthcare & pharma
C - Utilities, energy & industrials (NIS2/CER)   D - Consumer, 
retail & platforms
Recommended question format: core Q1-5 plus one sector module 
per meeting - more is rarely feasible in practice.
Supplier-security programs* are an emerging engagement topic with the same trajectory as supplier audits we saw in apparel two decades ago.
Source: SG Cross Asset Research / Sustainability; *Supplier-security program: policies and controls a company uses to ensure its suppliers, contractors, software vendors, cloud providers, and other third parties maintain adequate cybersecurity standards

BOTTOM LINE - DIGITAL TRUST AS AN INVESTABLE SCARCITY
Cybersecurity has become a core determinant of corporate resilience
No longer a discretionary IT expense but a board-level priority, reinforced by regulation and directly linked to financial 
materiality and licence to operate.
AI raises the stakes on both sides of the equation
By lowering the cost of attack but also the cost of defence, it shifts competitive advantage towards companies that embed 
cyber-AI resilience into governance, controls and operating discipline - rather than treating it as an insurance policy.
Investment implication
We see the SG Cybersecurity Index as the most direct expression of this thesis: the rare case in which the financial thesis 
and the social-impact thesis are one and the same.
