South Korea Technology: Investor feedback: Positive view on memory 
with high interest level; Reiterate Buy on SEC (on CL) and SK Hynix
14 September 2026 | 5:41AM SGT
We met with investors in Toronto, Boston, New York, and San Francisco to discuss the 
South Korea Technology sector, and summarize their key feedback in this report. 
Most of the discussions centered around the memory space, where our sense was 
that investors in North America were generally more positive on the memory space 
compared to investors in Asia. From a stock picking perspective, we did not sense a 
clear preference between Samsung Electronics (SEC) and SK Hynix (Hynix), while felt 
that the overall interest level was very high on both names. We reiterate our Buy 
ratings on SEC (on CL) and Hynix. 
10 key North America investor feedback points 
1) Investors are generally more positive on the memory space compared to 
n
Asia: The North American investors we met were generally more positive on the 
memory space compared to investors in Asia. Most agreed that the industry 
fundamentals continue to be solid, and at the current valuation level there 
appears to be limited downside to the share prices of SEC and Hynix. At the same 
time, investors noted the main reason for not actively accumulating now was the 
lack of major near-term catalysts, as well as the concern over positioning for 
these names.  
2) Near-term pricing and earnings expectations have already come down: 
n
Investors’ expectations appear to have generally already come down for both 
near-term pricing and earnings for both companies, where most believed 3Q26 
DRAM and NAND ASP would rise around 20% qoq, and 3Q26 earnings for both 
SEC and Hynix could see downside due to the meaningful KRW appreciation 
against the USD during the quarter. 
3) Wide range view on HBM pricing, however expectations have come down 
n
as well: There was a wide range of investors’ views on HBM pricing, where bullish 
investors believe that it would have to rise significantly higher than 100% in order 
for HBM margin to reach a closer level to that of conventional DRAM, while more 
conservative investors were modeling closer to a ~50% yoy increase as they 
believed a more meaningful pricing increase would be difficult due to the rising 
customer cost burden. Our sense is that general expectations have come down 
compared to a few months ago, when there were expectations for as high as a 
~200% yoy increase. We continue to believe that HBM blended ASP for next year 
will rise around 100% yoy, with more potential upside for SEC as we believe the

4) View on LTAs still divided between the bears and bulls: This was a topic with

still divided views, where bears were skeptical around how binding the long-term 
agreements (LTAs) would be for the memory names over the longer run, while the

5) Memory despeccing and optimization likely due to supply constraints, 
n
however some near-term concerns: On memory despeccing and optimization, 
most investors we spoke to believed this is happening due to the supply shortage 
and not enough memory to go around every customer, not because any weakness in 
demand. At the same time, there was some concern that the lower memory content 
per box would not be able to be fully offset by the higher volume of chips, at least in 
the near term. 
6) Investors are already modeling meaningful supply impact from China 
n
memory, hence less concern over incremental supply: A lot of investors we spoke 
to were already modeling Chinese memory suppliers taking more than 10% market 
share by 2028, even for DRAM. In addition to the consensus view of a substantial 
memory supply tightness continuing over the medium term, there was less pushback 
vs. in the past that supply from China would lead to an oversupply at least during the 
next couple of years. Investors agreed that this would be the case especially for 
DRAM, where there is a relatively larger technology gap and limitations to bridge this 
gap without access to EUV tools. 
7) Stronger interest on SEC’s foundry business compared to both the past and in 
n
other regions: Higher number of questions on SEC’s foundry business relative to 
both the past and in other regions were asked during the meetings. Investors agreed 
that the company’s foundry business fundamentals has been recently seeing visible 
improvement, and asked questions related to profit breakeven timing, contribution 
coming from 4nm base die for HBM, and capex outlook for the business. While 
minimal profit contribution from the business over the next few years was the 
generally agreed upon view, there were divided views regarding the foundry 
business’ impact to the valuation of the company. 
8) Generally higher shareholder return expectations: Investors we met generally 
n

to help support limited share price downside for both names. However, given the 
high expectations prior to the shareholder return announcements, also received 
feedback that additional measures would be needed for this to act as a meaningful 
positive catalyst for both names. For SEC, many investors asked if meaningful 
buybacks were a possibility along with the dividends, which the company addressed

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
