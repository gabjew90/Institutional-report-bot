This note covers why the White House’s decision to permanently appoint FDA
drug and biologics center directors now is beneficial for pharma. We also provide
our updated views on Trump’s FDA commissioner nomination.
Top Line
HHS’ move to permanently appoint respective acting drug and biologics center heads,
Michael Davis and Karim Mikhail, to those positions is a win-win for industry. Both are
well-liked leaders in the “back to normal” effort at FDA, and permanently filling the
positions now would preclude the next commissioner from bringing in more roguish
leadership. We think the move is also an acknowledgement that Trump’s FDA
commissioner nominee, Heidi Overton, is unlikely to be confirmed until at least late
December.
HHS Secretary Kennedy yesterday evening announced that the current acting
directors of FDA's Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) and Center for
Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER), Michael Davis and Karim Mikhail, will
permanently fill those roles.
We see the move as a win-win for pharma. Both directors have been part of the
administration's "back to normal" efforts to stabilize FDA after former commissioner
Makary's turbulent year.
Filling the positions now with permanent directors who are known and, by all
accounts, well-liked will both remove some agency leadership uncertainty and
preclude the next commissioner from tapping more controversial figures for those
roles.

Both CDER and CBER have taken pains to stamp out much of Makary's and former
CBER Director Prasad's drug decisions and policy changes, including dropping vaccine
trial requirements and allowing several rejected drug applicants to try again. Mikhail
has lengthy pharma industry leadership experience, and Davis' tenure at FDA has
spanned multiple administrations, so we don't see red flags for either.
We think filling the positions now – in addition to appointing a new Deputy Commissioner for Technology and AI
and a tobacco center head – is likely an acknowledgement that FDA commissioner nominee Overton will not be
confirmed until at least very late in 2026, if not next year.
As we covered earlier, President Trump's curiously timed and controversial vaccine EO Overton was involved in
would provide Democrats a prime opportunity to highlight a politically unpopular topic heading into the midterms.
There's also the matter of exiting HELP Chair Cassidy (R-LA) questioning her fitness for the job.
If Republicans end up losing Senate control, we could see Republicans moving the Overton nomination in lame
duck to avoid having to move it through a Democratic Senate during an FDA user fee reauthorization year. If not,
we think it's more likely that Overton waits until next session when Cassidy is gone.
Trump nominated Medicare director and overall HHS fixer Chris Klomp to serve as Deputy Commissioner of HHS in
June. We think Klomp's nomination has a better chance of moving this year than Overton's, but the administration
doesn't appear to be in any rush.
