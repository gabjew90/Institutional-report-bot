◼ Yesterday, 10 September, saw significant bearish moves in rates as the ECB hike coincided with 
the Brent futures going to $107. The US Treasury’s expanded buyback programme failed to 
adequately support the long end of the curve. US Treasury 10- and 30-year yields rose by 12bp 
and 8bp respectively. German and Japanese government bonds sold off materially too. 
◼ Our rates strategists view is “neutral on rates and wait for more clarity before initiating 
positions”. Their base case, to which they attribute a 55% probability, is for a proactive Fed which 
hikes modestly, and this leads to a gradual decline in yields in the coming quarters. Their bear case 
– 30% probability – is of yields rising across the curve if the Fed remains on hold over the coming 
quarters. To quote them: “A combination of stronger growth, higher inflation, mounting concerns 
over fiscal deficits or a synchronous global bond sell-off could result in the 2yT yield rising to 4.65% 
and 10yT to 5.25% by Q1-2027.” This is a destabilising scenario which will require aggressive hiking. 
We strongly recommend reading Fixed Income Weekly: A rising tide for yields for more details. 
◼ The important takeaway for us is that our strategists think there is a 30% chance of the 
destabilising bear case playing out. Consequently, we highlight in this note systematic ways of 
positioning for this scenario that we had mentioned in our recent Quant Outlook. 
◼ Buying long-dated rates volatility was one such way for three reasons: (a) long-dated implied 
volatility (say 10Y x 20Y) prices only a small premium over realised volatility, (b) this volatility is 
reactive to factors that affect the long end of the curve, and (c) it is relatively cheap to carry. We 
suggested two strategies: (1) buying a long-dated delta-hedged straddle (ticker: SGBVV1ER), (2) the 
“triangle” trade which combines buying long-dated volatility with selling volatility at the short end, 
making the trade cheaper to carry at the expense of short gamma exposure (ticker: SGIXEVR4). 
◼ The factors we outlined in our Outlook remain in place. The chart on the left below shows that 
even though long-dated rates volatility has risen recently, it has room to go higher if macro 
uncertainty worsens and rates rise further. The chart on the right shows the term structure of ATMF 
implied volatility for the 20-year rate. It is inverted for long expiries, making long-dated volatility 
cheap to carry in case the stable base case envisioned by our rates strategy colleagues manifests.

◼ Investors looking to cheapen the long rates volatility position, but loath to run the short gamma 
position associated with the “triangle” trade, could consider using G10 + EM FX carry (ticker: 
SGIXF3CE) instead as a funding leg. The performance of long rates volatility and FX carry has 
historically exhibited low to moderate negative correlation (left chart below), and this is especially 
true in stable market environments. Furthermore, FX carry is currently attractive with an ex-ante 
carry of 12% p.a. The combination should likely work well in the stable base case scenario. 
◼ The question is: how would we expect FX carry to perform in the bear case scenario outlined 
above? This scenario involves higher inflation and rates. FX carry works on the difference between 
rates in various currencies. If rates rise across countries, the differences may not move significantly 
– FX carry may be hurt, but likely not materially enough to offset the potential positive performance 
of long rates volatility. The scenario that strongly hurts FX carry is one in which demand for funding 
currencies like the JPY and CHF rises suddenly and drastically, something more likely to happen in 
a strong, unexpected economic downturn. (EM carry currencies would be likely to suffer too in such 
a scenario.) The combination of the two strategies will likely lose in this tail scenario, which equity 
option-based strategies are better suited for as hedges. The tail scenario is almost the opposite of 
the bear case we have mentioned. 
◼ Rates trend following (ticker: SGMDIRTL) is another strategy well-positioned for the bear case 
scenario. We mentioned in our Outlook that rates trend following currently carries short bond 
positions – and so likely to gain as rates sell off (see chart bottom right).
