August CPI and PPI Imply a Firm August PCE 
 
August CPI and PPI data suggest that underlying inflation remained relatively firm. 
Stronger transportation and healthcare price pressures were somewhat offset by weaker 
portfolio-management services and softer software inflation. Our CPI/PPI mapping 
points to core PCE inflation of around 0.25% m/m, while super-core PCE likely remained 
broadly in line with July at 0.25%-0.30% m/m, centred around 0.27% m/m, indicating 
continued resilience in non-housing services inflation. Estimates are subject to greater-
than-usual uncertainty due to forthcoming methodological changes for portfolio-
management services and computer software, as well as the upcoming annual 
benchmark revision. 
 
August CPI: Firm Super-Core, Less Firm Core Goods 
Headline CPI for August increased in line with expectations at 0.4% m/m (SG 0.3%), up from 0.1% 
m/m in July, while the annual rate remained at 3.4% yr/yr. Core CPI increased 0.3% m/m, above 
expectations (consensus & SG: 0.2%), following a 0.2% increase in July, while the annual rate 
ended up at 2.4% yr/yr from 2.5%. Energy CPI came in at -2.1% m/m in August, following a -1.5% 
drop in July, while food CPI rose 0.1% m/m, broadly like the increase in the preceding month.

In general, CPI only maps partially into PCE, often with materially different weights in core PCE than

The CPI–PCE gap continues to be driven largely by differences in weights and source data. Core PCE 
places significantly more weight on healthcare services than core CPI, while housing services 
account for a much smaller share of core PCE than core CPI. In addition, some services categories 
rely more heavily on PPI data. Key August contributors to the CPI–PCE gap are: 
• 
Healthcare services: hospital and physician PPIs firmed in August, suggesting healthcare PCE 
inflation likely accelerated relative to July. 
• 
Transportation services: PPI Transportation Services rebounded to +2.1% m/m from -1.3% in 
July, pointing to firmer transportation-related PCE inflation than implied by CPI Transportation 
Services alone. 
• 
Financial services (portfolio management): PPI Portfolio Management declined 1.6% m/m in 
August after a 6.2% increase in July. However, the importance of this signal is diminished by 
BEA's forthcoming methodology change, which will move away from using the PPI as the 
primary price source. 
• 
Computer software: both the moderation in CPI Computer Software and weakness in the 
newly incorporated PPI components suggest less upward pressure on core PCE inflation from 
software prices than in July. 
• 
Housing services: CPI Rent of Primary Residence and CPI OER rose by roughly 0.2% m/m in 
August. Given the substantially lower housing-services weight in core PCE than in core CPI 
(roughly 18% vs. around 43%), housing exerts a negative weight effect on core PCE vs. core CPI.

Taken together, the August CPI and PPI data suggest that stronger transportation and healthcare 
inflation largely offset weaker portfolio-management prices and softer software inflation. While 
portfolio-management services remain an important uncertainty given BEA's methodological 
changes, August inflation data signal that non-housing services inflation likely remained broadly 
similar to the July pace rather than decelerating materially. Depending on the treatment of 
portfolio-management and software prices, core PCE inflation likely would have been around 
0.25% m/m, while super-core PCE inflation likely will have remained in a range of 0.25%-0.30% 
m/m, centered around the July pace of around 0.27% m/m. With energy prices rebounding in 
August, headline PCE inflation likely accelerated as well, probably around 0.35% m/m. 
The uncertainty around these estimates is larger than usual because the upcoming annual 
benchmark revision coincides with significant methodological changes to the treatment of 
portfolio-management services and computer software in PCE. As a result, the historical CPI/PPI-
to-PCE mapping may be less informative than normal and the eventual August PCE outcome could 
fall outside the ranges implied by past relationships.
