Credit Strategy Weekly 
Can yields give us a breather please? 
Sovereign bond sell-off pushes risk aversion higher almost everywhere… The war in the 
Middle East intensified this week, sending oil prices past $100 and driving up inflation 
expectations. The ECB hiked rates, and the markets priced even more rate increases to come. 
Sovereign bonds sold off quickly, and yields tested multi-year highs. Equities were not spared 
either, heading lower as risk aversion spread through the markets. Credit proved the exception. 
The various credit indices we follow barely registered any turmoil, with spreads remaining in 
very narrow ranges and some even inching a little tighter. Moreover, it wouldn’t be surprising 
if the higher credit yields start to attract even more cash into the asset class. We don’t see 
spreads tightening significantly from here, but neither do we expect a substantial sell-off this 
side of the New Year, and probably not before 2Q next year. 
The list of risks keeps getting longer. A back-of-the-envelope list of risks to the credit markets 
currently include possible AI disruption to various industries, as well as very heavy issuance 
from hyper scalers, potential worries about Private Credit (not many headlines lately), 
geopolitical risks and oil prices, higher inflation and, more recently, increasingly high yields. In 
our view, none of these issues can, on their own, do any significant or sustainable damage to 
the credit markets, as long as the economy continues to grow. But the more issues the markets 
have to deal with, the less room for error there is. 
Heavy new issue volumes, but nothing the market can’t take. The primary markets had a 
strong showing this week in both the euro and USD markets. The week started slowly given the 
US holiday, but by Tuesday volumes were rising and on Wednesday the euro market saw very 
strong issuance, led by the IG space. Overall, €27.37bn was priced, including several multi-
tranche deals. The bonds were taken down with ease, even though one deal was pulled – the 
first this year. We believe the markets can absorb more (around €35bn+ in weekly issuance), 
although we don’t think we’ll see volumes that high very often.

Macro view 
Oil prices, inflation and concerns over central bank rates 
trajectories deliver a blow to the markets: The war in the 
Middle East continues to deteriorate with more blows from each 
side, and oil prices surged above the $100 level. This is having an 
impact on inflation expectations and central banks seem ready to 
react. The ECB hiked rates by 25bp but was perhaps more hawkish 
than the market expected (our Economists believe we’ll see the 
next hike in December), and this gave a new push to the ongoing 
sovereign bond repricing. As a result, yields on both sides of the 
Atlantic continued to climb throughout the week, with many 
reaching multi-year highs. Next week will be the Fed’s turn, and 
this should be the main event of the week, although we’ll keep an 
eye on developments in the Middle East war and oil prices. The 
market is increasingly pricing a 25bp hike, although it is not a 
certainty yet.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
Cash 
A sea of stability amid the storm: The deterioration of the war in 
Iran pushed oil prices higher and sparked further worries across 
markets. Equities suffered and sovereign bonds fell further. But 
amid the turmoil the credit markets continued to post a 
remarkable performance, with credit spreads barely reacting to 
any of the negative developments. Additionally, we saw very high 
new issue levels, but these didn’t have a major impact on the 
secondary market either. Total returns dropped, unsurprisingly, 
and the euro and USD IG indices are now posting negative returns 
year-to-date. And still, excess returns across the board remained 
healthy as spreads stayed stuck in very narrow corridors. There is 
a growing list of issues that could impact the credit markets, but 
we don’t see a major spread repricing this side of the New Year.

Strategy Scorecard 
Regular readers will be familiar with our SG Credit Scorecard, a tool we have used for more than 
a decade to help us forecast the direction of credit spreads.  
For readers who are not familiar with the Scorecard, we construct it in three stages. First, we 
group indicators into various categories. Macroeconomic fundamentals, for example, 
comprises GDP and inflation numbers, but also indicators such as monetary policy and fiscal 
policy. Second, we compare our forecasts for these indicators over the next six to 12 months with 
the consensus view. This gives us an impact, which can range from a maximum positive of +3 to 
a maximum negative of -3. These are shown in the table as +++ and ---; a neutral reading is =. 
Finally, we assign a weight to the indicator. This ranges from 1 (relatively unimportant) to 3 
(relatively important), shown in the table as a number of boxes. The weights depend on our view 
of where we are in the credit cycle. The scorecard for each indicator is the impact multiplied by 
the weight, so it can range from +9 to -9. The average level of macroeconomic fundamentals is 
therefore the average of the two sub-indicators, GDP and inflation and policy; the average level 
of the whole scorecard is the average of the five indicators (macroeconomic fundamentals, 
corporate fundamentals, investor demand, supply, and valuations).

The week ahead 
Busy on multiple fronts: The war in the Middle East is grabbing the headlines again, but 
with few signs of any potential discussions soon. That said, our commodities experts believe 
there is the possibility of a narrow US-Iran agreement around the November midterm elections. 
In the meantime, oil prices are likely to remain high for a long while. Additionally, we’ll have 
the FOMC meeting next week, amid rising inflation pressures, and we suspect that the ongoing 
sovereign bond volatility will remain an issue for the markets. In general, there’s lots to keep 
the markets busy. 
The Fed meeting and the economic calendar: The main event of the week should be the 
FOMC meeting (barring any new shocks) and at the moment the markets are pricing in a 60% chance 
of a rate hike. There will also be some releases of data, including some hard data. However, unless 
the FOMC delivers a shock to the markets, we don’t expect a major impact on the credit markets, 
and any volatility is more likely to be concentrated in the sovereign bonds market.

Market review and outlook 
Market review 
The ECB, rising yields and the Middle East war take centre stage: In recent weeks, 
worries about inflation and central-bank tightening measures have accelerated the sovereign 
bond sell-off and pushed yields up to multi-year highs. This week saw a further weakening of the 
sovereign bond complex (see section below), while the ECB hiked rates by 25bp as widely 
expected. Moreover, the central bank gave a hawkish message, which sent sovereign bonds yields 
(eurozone and others) higher once more. 
Government bond yields keep rising 
 
Source: Bloomberg, SG Cross Asset Research/Credit 
In the meantime, the war in the Middle East continued without any progress towards a 
resolution. Oil prices headed higher, and while the moves have not been too aggressive, the 
upward trend took Brent contracts to above $100 per barrel, with WTI crossing the threshold a 
little later. Our commodities experts now see a possibility of the US and Iran reaching a narrow 
agreement around the November midterm elections with a more comprehensive solution by 
year-end.
