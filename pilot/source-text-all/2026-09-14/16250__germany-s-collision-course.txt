Something has to give
Germany is becoming a rather fascinating contradiction.
Merz's popularity is collapsing, the political center is under pressure and German gas prices are once
again becoming a serious problem. Yet underneath all that, the macro and market data are moving in
almost exactly the opposite direction: manufacturing momentum is leading, earnings expectations are
strong, valuations look attractive and money is coming back.
Politics, energy and markets are heading toward each other. Something has to give.
As unpopular as Heinrich Brüning
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's approval rating has collapsed.
For those rusty on their Weimar history: Brüning was Germany's Chancellor from 1930–32, as the
political center collapsed and the Nazis surged from the fringes to become Germany's largest party.
Not exactly a historical comparison any Chancellor wants.

A political apocalypse...
The next German state election is Sep. 20. There are doubts the CDU even clears the 5% threshold to
enter parliament — a potentially brutal blow to Merz. The CDU can change its leader. Changing the
underlying politics is harder.

Here comes the gas problem
Germany's old vulnerability is back. German gas prices are up 166% YTD in dollar terms while US gas
is down 24%, pushing the German/US price ratio from 2.8x to almost 10x.
And this isn't just an economic problem. With AfD pushing for a return to Russian energy, gas is
increasingly political too.
