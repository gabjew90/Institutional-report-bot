Taiwan Technology: Semiconductors: Day 1: Key takeaways from 
SEMICON Taiwan 2026: CPO in focus
4 September 2026 | 4:41PM CST
 
We attended SEMICON Taiwan 2026 and hosted a SEMICON Booth Tour during 3-4th 
September 2026. On 3rd Sep, we hosted 8 companies including Ebara (6361.T, Buy), 
Tokyo Seimitsu (7729.T, Sell), Shibaura (6590.T, not covered), Lasertec (6920.T, Buy), 
MPI (6223.TWO, Buy), WinWay (6515.TW, Buy), Hermes Testing Solutions (HTSI, 
7856.TWO, not covered) and All Ring (6187.TWO, Buy). 
Our key takeaways include: 1) AI-related advanced-packaging demand remains 
strong into 2027, with OSAT orders accelerating alongside foundry demand; 2) CPO 
equipment is moving closer to mass production in late 2026 with a broader ramp up 
in 2027, although the testing flow remains fluid and some suppliers increasingly 
expect Insertion 3 testing step to represent the larger near-term equipment 
opportunity as some Insertion 2 demand could shift from foundry to OSATs; 3) rising 
AI/HPC test complexity continues to drive higher test equipment and test interface 
content, including testers, prober, probe-card, socket and thermal-management 
content, etc.; and 4) FOPLP (fan out panel level packaging) and hybrid bonding are 
emerging as the next equipment opportunities. Below, we provide more detailed 
takeaways for the Taiwan Semiconductors we visited.  
MPI (6223.TWO; Buy) 
MPI showcased its new CPO prober solution - TS3500-IFE, a fully automated 
platform designed for the Insertion 2 stage of CPO testing. Management said 
development and customer engagement are progressing smoothly across multiple 
customers and are not limited to foundry demand, and is on track for initial volume 
shipments by end-2026. MPI views automation as a key differentiator vs. its key peer. 
As for CPO Insertion 3, MPI believes its solution is not a head-to-head compete with 
competitors (e.g. Ficontec etc.) as its uses probe cards whereas the others use a 
socket-based architecture. Management highlighted that a device can pass through 
multiple test and inspection steps at die-level testing and therefore does not view 
the two solutions as directly comparable.

production for AI/HPC devices featuring micro-bump interconnects. This probe card

WinWay (6515.TW; Buy) 
Capacity expansion supports rising AI test-interface content. WinWay currently has 
pogo-pin capacity of approximately 6mn units per month, with 40-50% produced 
in-house and the rest outsourced mainly from Japanese suppliers (and some Korean 
suppliers). The company targets annual micro-drilling capacity to double to 180-200mn 
holes in 2026 from less than 100mn in 2025 (equivalent to 36k-40k socket, based on 5k 
pins per socket).  
The company is also co-developing a liquid-cooling test socket with a customer, 
although the product is not yet ready for volume production. For Hypersocket adoption, 
management continue to expect it to account for around 5% of its revenue in 2027. In 
terms of new customers, the company sees potential new progress with a US IDM 
customer in 2027. 
Hermes Testing Solutions Inc. (HTSI; 7856 TT; not covered) 
Company background: Hermes Testing Solutions Inc. (HTSI) is a Taiwan-based semiconductor 
testing equipment and engineering-service provider. Its portfolio spans 1) probe cards and 
probe-card cleaning materials, 2) prober (TEL prober) installation and engineering services, 
thermal-control modules, and 3) equipment for CPO and advanced packaging, including 
optical alignment and reflow tools. The company operates as both a product supplier and a 
system-integration/OEM partner to global equipment vendors. 
HTSI sees its thermal module as one of its key growth drivers for 2026 and into 2027. Its 
thermal module is installed on probers to cool the probe card and reduce the risk of 
burned probe needles. The product has contributed more than 30% of revenue in 2026 
to date and is currently used mainly for US GPU customers’ devices. Management noted 
the attachment ratio of roughly 80-90% to prober shipment. A second-generation 
module, now under verification for CPU and TPU customers, could carry a 30-50% 
higher ASP. Management sees potential for at least around 30% penetration for the new 
module in 2027. 
Meanwhile, Management also expects the TEL prober installation volumes to accelerate 
into 2027. HTSI is currently the sole service provider for TEL prober installation (mainly 
for Taiwan). Management expects installation volumes to double in 2026 and more than 
double in 2027. Management noted that while it is still too early to comment on 2028, 
they see at least another 30-50% of growth into 2028. 
A lot of discussions also surrounded CPO. For Insertion 1, the company provides 
alignment equipment, and it is now waiting for its partner to finalize the software side 
ahead of an initial shipment to a customer in Singapore. It will also do alignment 
technique licensing to foundry partner.  
For Insertion 2, HTSI works with Ficontec as an OEM partner. While HTSI sees the 
possibility for foundry to bypass Insertion 2 when entering mass production, it sees

All Ring (6187.TWO; Buy) 
All Ring showcased a broadened CPO-related equipment portfolio across inspection, 
optical coupling and post-clean inspection. The company showcased three key CPO 
solutions: 1) IQC 3D metrology - automated optical inspection and metrology 
equipment, 2) CPO active alignment coupling system - optical-coupling equipment for 
both packaging and die level, and 3) CO2 cleaning AOI detection machine, which can 
address particle risk after coupling and during product transfer. 
Management believes the key differentiation for its CPO active alignment coupling 
system lies in its in-house six-axis module design. All Ring designs the full six-axis 
active-alignment system internally, unlike many peers that source standard modules 
from third parties. 
While remaining under qualification, the company addressed its positiveness towards its 
advanced underfill dispenser for the Chip-on-Wafer (CoW) side and Chip-on-Panel (CoP) 
side of the process, which could create more dollar content as currently All Ring’s 
equipment solutions are mostly for the WoS (wafer-on-substrate) side.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
