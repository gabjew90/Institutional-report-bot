On 9/3, the FHFA Director instructed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to approve all 
lenders to use VantageScore effective immediately and that FHFA is seriously 
considering replacing tri-merge credit reporting with bi-merge. The announcements 
accelerate changes across the mortgage credit ecosystem, though the implications 
differ for Buy-rated FICO and Neutral-rated EFX and TRU. For FICO, wider 
VantageScore availability increases competition, though broader access may not 
translate directly into displacement given FICO’s position across underwriting 
models, lender systems and secondary-market processes. FICO is also responding 
through its Mortgage Direct License Program, which offers upfront pricing 
comparable to VantageScore, and FICO 10T, which adds trended credit data and is 
currently available at no incremental fee with Classic FICO. Total score volumes could 
increase if lenders use multiple models to optimize borrower qualification and 
pricing, particularly for applicants near decision thresholds. EFX and TRU could 
participate in this growth through their joint ownership of VantageScore, though a 
move to bi-merge could offset the benefit by reducing the number of bureau reports 
purchased per application. We retain our Buy rating on FICO and Neutral ratings on 
EFX and TRU, as we believe FICO will retain the substantial majority of its pricing 
power, market position and earnings growth given its incumbency advantages, 
product initiatives and the potential for broader score shopping to expand the 
scoring TAM. 
VantageScore expansion broadens lender access and could increase total score 
pulls. On 9/3 after market close, the FHFA Director instructed Fannie Mae and 
Freddie Mac to approve all lenders to use VantageScore effective immediately, 
expanding availability beyond the initial 50 lenders delivering VantageScore-based 
loans. The GSEs currently allow lenders to deliver either Classic FICO or VantageScore 
4.0 on each loan, not both models. Lenders may still obtain both scores earlier in the 
origination process to evaluate borrower eligibility or pricing before delivering the 
selected model. This score-shopping behavior could expand the overall scoring TAM 
if lenders continue pulling FICO and add VantageScore to their workflows. 
Incremental score pulls may be less likely when a lender obtains VantageScore first 
and the borrower already qualifies for the lender’s most favorable terms, reducing 
the need to obtain another score. Adoption economics will therefore depend on the 
sequencing of score pulls, the percentage of borrowers near qualification or pricing 
thresholds and whether lenders standardize dual-score workflows.

Bi-merge poses an additional risk to bureau volumes. The FHFA Director separately 
stated that FHFA is seriously considering moving from tri-merge to bi-merge as part of 
an effort to reduce mortgage costs. A bi-merge framework would reduce the number of 
bureau files purchased for each mortgage application from three to two, creating a 
direct volume headwind for EFX, EXPN and TRU if implemented broadly. The impact 
would depend on whether lenders select the two bureaus, the GSEs prescribe a rotation 
mechanism or another allocation methodology is introduced. A lender-choice model 
could also intensify competition among the bureaus on price, data quality and service. 
The FHFA Director has not announced a final policy, implementation date or operating 
framework. We therefore view bi-merge as a meaningful policy risk, not yet a completed 
change. 
FICO’s incumbency and product initiatives could limit displacement. Expanded 
VantageScore availability introduces additional competition, though we believe FICO 
retains meaningful advantages from its longstanding position across lender workflows, 
underwriting policies and secondary-market infrastructure. Switching models requires 
lenders to validate performance, update systems, revise decision rules and educate 
stakeholders, creating tangible implementation costs and execution risk. FICO’s 
Mortgage Direct License Program also addresses affordability by allowing participating 
resellers to generate and deliver FICO Scores directly to lenders with upfront costs 
comparable to VantageScore. FICO 10T provides an additional competitive response 
through trended credit data and is currently available at no incremental fee with Classic 
FICO through FICO’s Free Access Program. Greater score shopping could expand the 
scoring TAM if lenders retain FICO within established workflows and add VantageScore 
for borrowers near qualification or pricing thresholds. 
The bureau read-through balances VantageScore upside against bi-merge risk. EFX, 
EXPN and TRU jointly own VantageScore, creating a potential revenue opportunity as all 
lenders gain access and VantageScore-based mortgage volumes increase. The bureaus 
could also benefit if lenders adopt dual-score workflows that generate incremental 
score activity without replacing existing FICO pulls. Bi-merge creates a separate and 
potentially larger offset by reducing the number of bureau reports purchased per 
mortgage application from three to two. The net financial effect will depend on 
VantageScore economics, the degree of incremental score usage and the allocation of 
bureau volumes under a bi-merge framework. Outcomes could diverge across EFX, EXPN 
and TRU if lenders select their preferred bureau combinations instead of operating 
under a rotation mechanism. The posts therefore introduce both a new monetization 
opportunity and a potential volume headwind for the bureaus.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
