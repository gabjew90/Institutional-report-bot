In terms of the highlights: 
●​ At first glance, August was a solid month. There was no late-summer wobble of the sort 
seen in recent years, and the economic data remained strong. The Eurozone composite 
flash PMI reached a nine-month high, while the US equivalent hit a four-year high. 
●​ That resilient macro backdrop, combined with a strong earnings season, helped equities 
deliver solid gains. The S&P 500 returned +2.7% over the month, with technology stocks 
leading the way as the Magnificent 7 rose +4.4%. 
●​ With risk assets performing well, the main story in August was on the rates side, where 
long-dated bond yields reached multi-year highs. On 18 August, the 30-year Treasury 
yield touched its highest level since 2007 at 5.31%, while Germany’s 30-year yield 
yesterday reached its highest level since 2011 at 3.81%. 
●​ Part of that rise reflected the risk-on environment. However, inflation concerns also 
resurfaced as the Strait of Hormuz remained closed, pushing commodities – particularly 
food prices – sharply higher. At the same time, fiscal concerns returned to the spotlight. 
●​ Even so, the US curve flattened over the month, with 2-year yields up +5bps (+11bps 
immediately after the Jackson Hole speech last Friday) while 30-year yields actually fell 
by -3bps. 
●​ That was partly due to the US Treasury Department’s announcement that it would 
increase buybacks of longer-dated Treasuries. The move prompted a temporary 
pullback in longer-dated US yields, but renewed concerns about financial repression 
also helped lift gold prices by +9.7% in August, while silver gained +15.6%.

●​ European bonds underperformed. Ten-year yields in France (+18bps), Italy (+13bps) and 
Germany (+12bps) rose more than those in the US (+2bps) and the UK (+1bp). 
Japanese government bonds were also weak, with 10-year yields up +15bps and 2-year 
yields up +23bps as speculation over further rate hikes intensified. 
●​ Food prices recorded some striking gains amid the continued closure of the Strait of 
Hormuz and this year’s El Niño weather pattern. Corn futures rose +16.8%, their biggest 
monthly increase in five years, while wheat gained +18.3%, the strongest rise in four 
years. Sugar climbed +21.5%, its largest monthly advance since 2018. 
●​ Finally, several assets were relatively stable after the sharp swings seen earlier this year. 
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose just +2.0% in August after four consecutive 
months of double-digit moves in either direction, while Brent crude oil gained only 
+0.4%, its smallest monthly move since 2024. 
 
With that, let's take a closer look at the August returns...  
Recent years have often seen a late-summer wobble in markets (especially in September) but 
this August was the exception, as robust data took risk assets to new heights. That meant the 
S&P 500 hit fresh records, along with other equity indices. However, it wasn’t all plain sailing for 
markets, as longer-dated bond yields hit multi-year highs in several countries. That included 
10yr bund yields, which ended the month at a post-2011 high of 3.32%, whilst the 10yr Treasury 
yield hit a 19-month high of 4.75%. In part, that was driven by the wider risk-on tone. But 
inflation concerns also played a role, particularly given the lack of progress on reopening the 
Strait of Hormuz. And as all that was happening, the US Treasury Department announced an 
increase in its buybacks for long-dated Treasuries. So that added to concerns about financial

Month in Review - The high-level macro overview 
There were several market developments in August, but the biggest was the rise in longer-dated 
yields around the world. For instance, the US 30yr Treasury yield hit a post-2007 high of 5.31% 
on August 17, and Germany’s 30yr yield hit a post-2011 high of 3.81% on August 31. Meanwhile 
in Japan, the 30yr yield hit 4.14% on August 18, the highest since that maturity was first issued 
in 1999. These moves had several causes. In part, they reflected optimism around global 
growth expectations, particularly as the economic data was robust through the month. Indeed, 
the flash composite PMI for the Eurozone hit a 9-month high of 52.1, and in the US it hit a 
4-year high of 56.0. Inflation concerns also helped to push yields higher still, whilst fiscal fears 
were also in focus again, so collectively that helped drive the move. 
Nevertheless, we did see a pullback in long-dated yields towards month-end. One reason was 
the US Treasury Department’s unexpected announcement on August 19 that they were 
increasing “by at least double”, the size of their buyback operations for longer-dated Treasuries. 
So that will take the maximum size from $2bn per operation to at least $4bn. The news took 
investors by surprise as well, because it was just two weeks earlier that the Treasury had 
released their tentative buyback schedule for the upcoming quarter as part of their regular 
refunding announcement. However, even as the announcement helped to lower yields, it also 
added to concerns about financial repression. So gold prices ended the month up +9.7%, whilst 
the dollar index weakened -0.5%. 
Alongside the buyback announcement, Fed Chair Warsh’s speech at Jackson Hole also led to a 
big flattening in the yield curve. He struck a more hawkish tone than expected, describing the 
Fed’s target of 2% inflation as “a firm, fixed target”. Moreover, he said that even though the 
summer inflation prints had been better than expected, “they do not tell me that underlying 
trends have meaningfully improved.” So investors ended the month still thinking that a 
September hike was likely on balance, with futures pricing in a 65% probability. 
Elsewhere, oil prices were broadly steady in August, with Brent crude (+0.4%) seeing its 
smallest monthly change this year. That said, there was a sharp initial decline at the start of the 
month, as hopes rose for fresh talks. That came as President Trump posted that he’d agreed to 
cancel an attack on Iran “subject to being able to rapidly make a DEAL.” He then said his latest 
offer of talks was a “last chance” for Iran. So that meant Brent crude closed beneath $80/bbl on 
August 4. However, no deal materialised, and on August 18, Trump posted that there were “no 
talks or conversations going on, or scheduled”. And then at the end of the month, the US and

ultimately ending the month at $90.49/bbl. Otherwise, there was also a sharp move higher in

Finally, it was generally a decent month for equities across the board, aided by resilient data and 
strong earnings. So the S&P 500 was up +2.7% in total return terms, which included another 
record high on August 13. Similarly in Europe, the STOXX 600 was up +0.5%, including a 
record high on August 11. On a sectoral basis, tech stocks did particularly well, and the 
Information Technology component of the S&P 500 was up +6.2%. 
Which assets saw the biggest gains in August? 
●​ Precious Metals: As concerns grew about both inflation and financial repression, that 
helped precious metals. Gold prices were up +9.7% to $4,437/oz, and silver was up 
+15.6% to $66.58/oz. 
●​ Agricultural goods: With the Strait of Hormuz still closed, and El Niño still ahead, food 
prices surged. That included a +18.3% jump for wheat, a +16.8% jump for corn, and a 
+21.5% jump for sugar. 
●​ Oil and gas: Oil prices moved slightly higher in August, after their sharp bounceback in 
July. So Brent crude was up +0.4% for the month to $90.49/bbl, and WTI rose +1.3% to 
$85.76/bbl. Meanwhile, European natural gas futures were up +18.2%, and US natural 
gas futures were up +6.8%.  
●​ Equities: Strong global data and earnings meant it was a decent month for equities. That 
included gains for the S&P 500 (+2.7%), the STOXX 600 (+0.5%) and the MSCI EM 
index (+3.4%). 
Which assets saw the biggest losses in August? 
●​ European sovereign bonds: With natural gas prices moving higher in Europe, inflation 
fears remained prominent, with yields moving higher. That included the 10yr bund yield, 
which ended the month up +12bps at a post-2011 high of 3.32%. 
●​ US Dollar: The dollar index weakened for a second month running in August, down 
-0.5%.
