US consumer credit trends incrementally improved in July with modest y/y declines 
in headline delinquencies across non‑mortgage categories, though underlying 
subprime performance remains soft. 60+ day bankcard delinquencies declined y/y to 
2.70% from 2.76%, personal loan delinquencies decreased to 3.18% from 3.25% and 
auto loan delinquencies fell to 1.46% from 1.51%, while remaining above long‑term 
averages in cards and autos. Beneath these aggregates, subprime and near-prime 
credit delinquencies drifted higher in cards and personal loans, while auto 
delinquencies were largely stable across both subprime and near-prime borrowers. 
This indicates improving credit conditions among prime consumers are driving 
headline improvements in overall credit delinquencies. Meanwhile, credit supply 
remains constructive with card originations up 5.1% y/y driven disproportionately by 
subprime growth and personal loan originations up 3.9%, mitigated by auto 
originations declining 5.8%. Against this backdrop, we continue to favor Buy‑rated 
Fair Isaac given our view that its Scores pricing power and market share leadership 
are durable and can drive sustained 20%+ annual EPS growth. We remain Neutral on 
Equifax and TransUnion due to macro sensitivity in volumes, subprime exposure and 
margin headwinds tied to evolving mortgage score economics.

Overview of US Consumer Credit Trends 
 
 
Delinquencies continuing to show signs of improvement. Delinquency rates, which 
serve as one of the most reliable indicators of consumer credit health, improved in July 
for non-mortgage categories. The 60+ day bankcard delinquency rate ticked lower y/y 
to 2.70% in July from 2.76%, though remained above the long-term average of 2.50% 
since 2009. Meanwhile, the 60+ day personal loan delinquency rate improved y/y to 
3.18% in July from 3.25%, and came below the long-term average of 3.76% since 2009. 
The 60+ day auto loan delinquency rate decreased y/y to 1.46% in July from 1.51%, but 
remains over one standard deviation above the long-term average of 1.01% since 2009. 
Declining delinquencies typically cause banks to loosen their underwriting standards, 
which benefits credit origination volumes. 
Mortgage delinquencies remain low. In contrast to card and auto delinquency rates, 
which are currently above their respective long-term averages, mortgage delinquencies 
remain benign and well below their historical average, reflecting banks’ tight mortgage 
underwriting standards over a decade after the Global Financial Crisis. The 90+ day 
mortgage delinquency rate increased y/y to 0.98% in July from 0.71%, but remained well 
below the long-term average of 2.48% since 2009. While low mortgage delinquencies 
point to a low likelihood of a wave of defaults, high mortgage rates, low housing 
inventory and elevated home prices continue to weigh on mortgage origination volumes. 
Consumer credit drivers generally mixed. Broader drivers of consumer credit health 
are generally mixed. The US unemployment rate of 4.1% in July ticked lower from 4.2% 
in June, and came below the long-term average of 5.7% since 1990. Household debt as a 
% of nominal GDP of 57.8% in 2Q remained below the 69.5% average since 2003, and 
debt service payments as a % of disposable income of 11.2% in 1Q compared favorably 
to the 12.4% average since 2005. That said, real wages declined 0.2% y/y in July, 
reflecting stalled purchasing power. Additionally, personal savings as a % of disposable 
income of 3.0% in July came below the long-term average of 5.7% since 1998.

US Mortgage and Housing Trends 
 
 
Mortgage account originations solid. Mortgage account originations increased 11.4% 
y/y in May (latest data available), decelerating from 16.0% in April, with full year growth 
estimated by the Mortgage Bankers Association to be 3.9% in 2026. The 30-year 
mortgage rate registered 6.7% as of 9/3, compared to a high of nearly 8% in early 
November 2023 and a low of 2.7% in early 2021. Home prices increased 1.7% y/y in July, 
indicating a resilient housing market despite high mortgage rates. Total mortgage debt 
outstanding increased 1.2% y/y in July to $12.8tn. The 90+ day mortgage delinquency 
rate increased y/y to 0.98% in July from 0.71%, but remained well below the long-term 
average of 2.48% since 2009.

US Credit Card Trends 
 
 
Card origination volumes up MSD y/y. Credit card account originations have recovered 
to positive y/y growth following a protracted period of decline between September 
2022 and June 2025. Card account originations inclusive of bankcards and private labels 
rose 5.1% y/y in May (latest data available), accelerating from flat growth in April, with 
overall prime originations up 2.3% and overall subprime originations up 16.2%. Total 
credit card balances outstanding increased 3.1% y/y in July to $1.2tn. The 60+ day 
delinquency rate for bankcards declined y/y to 2.70% in July from 2.76%, but remains 
above the long-term average of 2.50% since 2009. Examining lower-tier consumer 
performance, the 90+ day delinquency rate for subprime cards of 20.14% in July 
increased y/y from 19.36%, while the 90%+ day delinquency for near prime cards of 
1.41% in July increased y/y from 1.37%.

US Personal Loan Trends 
 
 
Personal loan originations up y/y. Personal loan account originations increased 3.9% 
y/y in May (latest data available), decelerating from 6.9% in April, with prime and 
subprime originations both up 3.9%. Account originations for personal loans had been in 
y/y decline in nearly all months between September 2022 and November 2024 as banks 
pulled back on risky unsecured lending, particularly in the subprime category, but have 
now registered sixteen consecutive months of positive y/y growth, with most of those 
months being double-digit growth. Total personal loans outstanding increased 3.5% y/y 
in July 2026 to $155bn. The 60+ day delinquency rate for personal loans of 3.18% in July 
decreased y/y from 3.25%, and comes below the long-term average of 3.76% since 
2009. Examining lower-tier consumer performance, the 60+ day delinquency rate for 
subprime personal loans of 13.70% in July increased y/y from 13.34%, while the 60+ day 
delinquency rate for near prime personal loans of 1.22% in July rose y/y from 1.16%.

US Auto Loan Trends 
 
 
Auto loan originations down y/y. Auto loan account originations declined 5.8% y/y in 
May (latest data available), worsening from a 3.0% decline in April, with prime 
originations down 4.6% and subprime originations down 11.8%. Account originations 
had declined consistently between March 2022 and November 2023, and fluctuated 
between growth and decline from 2024 through 2026. Auto loan account originations 
included a tariff-related pull-forward of auto purchases earlier in 2025, and originations 
have since moderated. Total auto loans outstanding increased 3.3% y/y in July to $1.7tn. 
The 60+ day delinquency rate for auto loans decreased y/y to 1.46% in July from 1.51%, 
but remains over one standard deviation above the long-term average of 1.01% since 
2009. Examining lower-tier consumer performance, the 60+ day delinquency rate for 
subprime autos of 8.90% in July decreased y/y from 9.00%, while the 60+ day 
delinquency rate for near prime autos of 0.59% in July increased y/y from 0.56%.

Investment Conclusions 
 
 
Against a backdrop of consumer credit delinquencies that remain above long-term 
averages, subdued consumer credit volumes and declining consumer confidence, we 
favor business models among the credit bureaus with the following characteristics: 1) 
pricing power, 2) monopoly or near-monopoly market structure, 3) recurring revenue 
streams, 4) track record of beat-and-raise results even against a volatile macro 
backdrop, 5) idiosyncratic catalysts and 6) superior margin flow-through. We believe 
Buy-rated FICO satisfies these criteria. 
Fair Isaac (FICO, Buy, $1,548 12M PT): Fair Isaac is continuing to generate robust Scores 
revenue growth lifted by strong mortgage score pricing increases. Mortgage pricing 
continues to drive outsized growth and margin expansion at FICO, with 97% y/y 
mortgage origination revenue growth in F3Q despite only low-single-digit mortgage 
volume growth and a subdued housing backdrop, following 127% growth in F2Q. 
Mortgage origination revenue now represents 62% of total Scores revenue, with 
mortgage volumes remaining in-line with industry trends and no volume share loss 
despite increasing VantageScore adoption. FICO continues to await final GSE approval of 
its Direct License Program and remains close to signing two large mortgage score 
resellers to DLP after already signing the top two resellers earlier this year. Within 
Software, Platform momentum continues to accelerate, with Platform ARR growth 
increasing to 62% y/y from 49% last quarter and Platform ARR surpassing non-Platform 
ARR for the first time, supported by new customer wins, expanded use cases and higher 
customer volumes. Strong mortgage pricing and favorable mix are driving significant 
margin expansion, with EBITDA margins increasing ~500 bps y/y to 63% in F3Q. We 
believe sustained mortgage pricing power, durable market share leadership in mortgage 
scores and a continued Software transformation will drive robust revenue growth, 
margin expansion and double-digit EPS growth, fueling valuation upside. 
Equifax (EFX, Neutral, $199 12M PT): Equifax is seeing solid execution across 
Workforce Solutions and USIS, including high-double-digit growth in Talent Solutions, 
strong Consumer Lending demand and ongoing mortgage share gains supported by 
TWN-related products in 2Q. The company also highlighted positive momentum in 
government, signing ~$300mn of annual contract value over the last four months, as well 
as early traction in VantageScore 4.0 adoption in the mortgage vertical among smaller 
lenders. That said, mortgage market conditions weakened further during the quarter 
due to higher rates, and EFX continues to expect industry mortgage originations to 
decline low-single-digits in 2026. Government revenue also declined ~4% y/y in 2Q due 
to a difficult comparison and contract timing, with recent new business wins to largely 
benefit 2027 revenue. Reported EBITDA margins are being pressured by the higher mix 
of zero-margin FICO royalty revenue, though ex-FICO EBITDA margins expanded 120 
bps y/y in 2Q, supported by operating leverage and accelerating AI-driven productivity 
gains as EFX doubled its 2026-2028 AI-driven cost savings target to $150mn. We believe 
EFX’s valuation fairly reflects a muted mortgage volume environment, subprime 
consumer weakness, margin headwinds from mortgage scores and EWS government 
revenues weighted toward 2027. 
TransUnion (TRU, Neutral, $89 12M PT): TransUnion is seeing stable consumer credit 
behavior, resilient lending activity and continued momentum across Financial Services, 
Emerging Verticals and International, leading the company to maintain its view that it

Valuation and Risks 
 
 
Fair Isaac (FICO): We are Buy-rated on FICO. Our 12-month price target of $1,548 is 
based on 24.0x our NTM + 1 YR EPS estimate of $64.51. Our target multiple comes at a 
premium to the Info Services peer group median of 17.6x to reflect FICO’s stronger 
growth and margin profile resulting from its pricing power in the Scores business. Key 
risks include an inability to sustain elevated pricing increases in the Scores business, 
competitive threats, a macro and credit market downturn, and a lack of traction with 
FICO’s decision management software transformation. 
Equifax (EFX): We are Neutral-rated on EFX. We raise our 12-month price target from 
$182 to $199 based on 17.5x (16.0x prior) our NTM + 1YR EPS estimate of $11.40 
(unchanged). We increase our target multiple to reflect peer group multiple 
appreciation. Our target multiple is in-line with the Info Services peer group median of 
17.6x in the context of EFX’s higher transaction revenue mix and mortgage market 
uncertainty, mitigated by its wide moat vs AI risks. Key risks to the downside for EFX 
shares include macro and interest rate risk, subprime consumer credit origination 
headwinds, and mortgage origination volume volatility. Key risks to the upside include a 
rebound in mortgage origination volumes, expansion in non-mortgage credit inquiries, 
rapid product introductions, further adoption of EWS offerings and healthy international 
expansion. 
TransUnion (TRU): We are Neutral-rated on TRU. We raise our 12-month price target 
from $82 to $89 based on 15.0x (13.75x prior) our NTM + 1YR EPS estimate of $5.94 
(unchanged). We increase our target multiple to reflect peer group multiple 
appreciation. Our target multiple comes at a discount to the Info Services peer group 
median of 17.6x to reflect TRU’s higher mix of macro-sensitive transaction revenues and 
exposure to the fintech and subprime categories, mitigated by its wide moat vs AI risks. 
Key risks to the downside include a macro and credit market downturn, mortgage 
origination volume declines, growth and margin dilution from M&A, FX headwinds and 
financial leverage. Key risks to the upside include a strengthening economic backdrop, 
healthy growth in international and emerging markets and traction in US emerging 
verticals.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
