Volkswagen (VOWG_p.DE) - Congratulations to VW.  Realism wins.
We need to congratulate not only VW management, but also the VW workers 
council, and even the State of Lower Saxony representatives. The VW Supervisory 
Board has unexpectedly unanimously approved management's Future Plan 2030. 
This is a brave plan and a realistic decision for all concerned. As we have 
highlighted, given VW's German plant competitiveness and lack of global revenue 
opportunities, VW simply had no other choice. The plan will allow VW to cut costs, 
to cut the number of models (and complexity), and to sharply cut investment 
spend by a further €6bn p.a. This decision should further allow VW to continue to 
move capital to its highest-return brands and models, without the need to 
maintain excess capacity utilisation. Whilst VW's LT EBIT targets remain 
ambitious, VW has once again proved its progress in recent years.

Yesterday, we hosted Evonik's COO and Head of Custom Solutions, Lauren 
Kjeldsen, for a London roadshow. The overall message was relatively balanced.

Strategy & Economics
Global Macro Strategy - Views and Trade Ideas - The Warsh Shadow Rate
Jackson Hole was more market-moving than expected. While Chair Warsh did not 
reveal his reaction function, he gave a good indication of the input variables into 
that function. It is noteworthy that most inputs are hot, while the indicators he 
downplayed are running softer. We construct a “Warsh shadow rate” from these 
inputs and find it very extended. This has clearly raised the risk for a September 
hike, in our view, even though it is not the base case from our economists. But the 
onus now relies on the August CPI to be benign to avoid a hike. We show how 
assets behave into a first hike. Given how binary the outcome is, we have cut some 
of our dovish Fed risks (EUR) but still have some positions that would benefit from 
the Fed on hold (gold, received October).

Quantitative Global Macro Strategy - September EMFX Factor Model Update
We update our EMFX factor model for September. Last month, our long/short 
model returned -0.12% net of transaction costs on an ex-ante risk budget of 1% 
p.a., while the equal-weighted EMFX benchmark gained 1.85% against the USD. 
KRW was the standout performer, supported by semiconductor-related equity 
inflows, while ZAR benefited from higher gold prices. COP and MXN also 
outperformed as the weaker dollar reinforced demand for carry. Factor 
performance was mixed: Economic Surprises, WPI and Quality contributed 
positively, while Momentum, Carry, Value PPP and Hiker detracted. Flows into 
Carry remain stretched across all three horizons, although one-month positioning 
has normalised slightly and cross-asset volatility remains below our trigger. The 
combined model turned long MXN, PHP and TWD, while moving short PEN and 
ZAR. Regionally, the portfolio is now long Asia against LatAm and CEEMEA, with 
the largest underweight in CEEMEA. Overall, the model remains marginally short 
EM and long USD.

Global Economics - Global Central Bank Monitor
Our global central bank monitor highlights that the balance between cutters and 
hikers has moved toward less-supportive policy. Since March, there have been 15 
rate hikes and only 7 cuts (across 27 major central banks) as inflationary pressures 
intensified due to higher oil prices. Accordingly, we have marked up our policy rate 
forecasts for 19 central banks since the onset of the conflict, while leaving 7 
unchanged and revising only Hungary lower. Notably, market pricing for a Fed hike 
at the September meeting rose from 35% to around 60% following Chairman 
Warsh’s remarks at Jackson Hole. In tandem, longer-term rates have moved up 
across many countries.
Nathan Sheets | Cole Langlois

Global Commodities - Uranium Outlook: We See Further Upside as the Market 
Tightens Ahead of WNA 
As market participants prepare for the WNA Symposium in London, where the Citi 
team will also be in attendance, uranium price action has remained relatively 
subdued, albeit with a modest upward bias. Term uranium prices continue to hold 
at record highs of $96/lb, while spot SWU prices have extended their rally to fresh 
highs of $215/SWU. The broader nuclear narrative remains increasingly 
constructive, reinforcing a favorable backdrop for long-term fuel demand. 
Meanwhile, supply growth continues to lag expectations and is likely to 
underdeliver again this year. Most importantly, the combination of tighter Russian 
import restrictions and declining waiver volumes is set to further constrain 
western enrichment capacity, forcing utilities and enrichers to continue 
overfeeding. In our view, rising inventories should not be mistaken for supply 
abundance. Rather, they reflect growing concern over future fuel availability and 
reinforce the case for sustained procurement demand across uranium and the 
broader fuel cycle. 
Arkady Gevorkyan | Maximilian J Layton | Anthony Yuen | Eric G Lee
