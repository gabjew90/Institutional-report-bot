GS McFerran - MEET bottleneck upstream oil 4 Sep 2026
Jack McFerran  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, FICC & Equities
Fri 4 Sep 2026, 4:06am ET
We have all seen what one of the biggest bottlenecks in history looks and feels like in DRAM — fewer
eyeballs are currently looking at oil & gas, yet. The last decade has seen E&P companies spend on
renewables, buybacks and payout cashflows, anything other than future reserves. Michele's Top 400 oil
projects almanac shows reserves on these projects have fallen a staggering 60% in the last 10 years. When
capex budgets for 2026 were set in late 2025, no one imagined this oil price playbook or these cashflows
— E&P 2027 budgeting sessions are going to look very different, especially when boardrooms reflect the
re-rate/de-rate of those with reserves vs those without (Total vs Shell, ENI vs Shell).
One small data point on how upstream capex has been utterly decimated: there were 60 seismic ships a
decade ago run by 8 firms; now only 16 owned by 3. The entire world's seismic data library is
consolidated to just three players. Michele Della Vigna is in London next week and will articulate how
tight this upstream bottleneck is — and how AI has transformed the accuracy and return on these dense
datasets. Sitting at the top of the world's oil cashflows with ~50% of seismic ships and ~50% of the
world's data library is tiny $3bn Norway name TGS (upgraded by a local broker yesterday), trading on
3.5x EBITDA. Michele argues Q1 will bring bumper Q4 for seismic plus new upstream capex budgets and
a focus on exploration vs production.
Favourite charts from the Top 400 projects: earnings-call mentions from the top 11 E&P companies of
“seismic, discovery, new acreage,” and the impact of AI on discovery rates. Longer reserve-life companies
have outperformed as the market rewarded resource depth.

Capex bottlenecks. What struck me was the size of the market cap AND market share at the bottleneck
(seismic data) in the oil & gas capex cycle. Consolidation means ~60% of the world seismic library sits at
TGS's $3bn mkt. Michele's work shows we are entering a structural upcycle in O&G capex — CEOs
under pressure to invest, market rewarding multiples for reserve life. SLB results transcript:
exploration/appraisal developing as a long-term trend for reserve replacement across deepwater and
frontier basins; Digital division margins ~35% on higher exploration data licenses.

Prior July note context (FYI bottlenecks TGS): TGS beat & raise; CEO: oil companies gradually more
