USD: Correcting lower ahead of today’s US CPI report 
The US dollar has continued to trade at weaker levels overnight after giving back all of 
its gains yesterday following the release of the stronger NFP report on Friday. The 
dollar index has fallen to a low overnight at 101.54 as it moves further below the high 
from Friday at 102.36. The US dollar has been undermined in part by the sharp ongoing 
adjustment lower for energy prices this month. The price of Brent has fallen back 
towards USD90/barrel for the first time since the end of August. For this month as a 
whole, the price of Brent has now fallen by around 23% which is the sharpest sell-off 
since between May and June. The worst performing G10 currency during that period 
was the Norwegian krone which has again underperformed this month. The sharp 
ongoing adjustment lower for energy prices has been encouraged by renewed investor 
optimism over progress towards a US-Iran deal following the US mid-term elections. 
Investor optimism over a deal was encouraged further overnight by comments from 
President Trump who told reporters that “Iran, militarily, is effectively over”. It followed 
a meeting earlier in the day between President Trump and Qatari Prime Minister

The move lower in energy prices is also helping to bring down government borrowing 
costs. The 10-year US Treasury yield has fallen by 50bps from last month’s high of 
5.36%. If there is further progress towards a US-Iran deal to end the conflict it would 
help to sustain lower energy prices and ease pressure on major central banks including 
the Fed to keep hiking rates. The main focus today will be the release of the latest US 
CPI report for October. It will be closely scrutinized by market participants and the Fed 
to assess if there is evidence of broadening inflation pressures beyond higher energy 
prices. Core inflation has surprised to the upside for two consecutive months putting 
pressure on the Fed to tighten policy, although the consensus forecast for today 
expects some relief with the monthly change in core CPI forecast to slow to0.2%M/M 
in October. The US rate market still expects the Fed to hike rates again as planned at 
the end of this year in December but has been scaling back expectations for further 
hikes in 2027. Market participants now only expect one additional hike in 2027. A 
December hike has been more fully priced back in after the stronger NFP report on 
Friday and the rebound in US AI-related/tech stocks overnight. The Nasdaq composite 
index rebounded by 2.7% and closed back above support from the 200-day moving 
average at around 25,107.  
 
EUR: Easing of external headwinds provides some relief  
The euro is staging a relief rally encouraged by lower energy prices, which if sustained 
would help ease headwinds for euro area economies. The recent unfavourable 
combination of higher energy prices and government borrowing costs particularly in 
France has reinforced euro selling pressure. The release of the latest IMM positioning 
report at the end of last week revealed leveraged funds are heavily short the euro. 
Short euro positions have increased sharply since mid-September and have hit their 
highest level since the start of the COVID shock in early 2020. It leaves the euro 
vulnerable to a short squeeze in the near-term if the investor concerns over the scale 
of the energy shock continue to fade.      
At the same time, the yield spread between 10-year French and German government 
bonds has narrowed alongside lower energy prices. The yield spread has narrowed to 
around 145bps down from a recent high of around 175bps in October. The recent 
narrowing has been encouraged by positive external developments rather than 
domestic fiscal policy developments in France. The price action highlights that further 
progress towards a US-Iran deal would help make it easier for French politicians to 
restore investor confidence. The reduction in French fiscal risk and rebound for AI-
related stocks yesterday contributed to the Swiss franc underperforming yesterday.
