Initiating at OUTPERFORM: Credible Microreactor and Fuel

The Wedbush View
We are initiating coverage of Nano Nuclear (NNE) at OUTPERFORM with a $18 price
target. NNE offers one of the most advanced U.S. microreactor platforms, and its
push toward vertical integration gives the Company control of its own destiny. Its
KRONOS micro modular reactor (MMR) is among the few Generation IV designs at
the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) Construction Permit Application (CPA)
stage. NNE's fuel supply, processing, and transportation assets de-risk deployment
by freeing the Company from a fragmented, capacity-constrained supply chain.
With ~$580m of cash and short-term investments, NNE has the balance sheet to
power data centers, military bases, and mines that cannot wait for the grid.
Economies of scale come from multiples, not size. KRONOS, a high-temperature
gas-cooled reactor (HTGR) built on five decades of operating precedent, uses
smaller, simpler components that allow standardized factory fabrication and draw
on a broader supplier base than larger small modular reactor (SMR) designs. Shared
site infrastructure, such as molten salt loops and power conversion, costs little
more for ten co-located units than for one, so economics improve with each added
unit. NNE acquired the design out of Ultra Safe Nuclear Corporation's bankruptcy
for ~$8.5m, and the Company estimates the design carries more than $120m of
prior development capital.
Speed to power and siting flexibility appeal to hyperscalers. NNE can manufacture
components offsite and assemble them onsite, so we believe ten stacked KRONOS
units can reach operations sooner than a single SMR of comparable output. Road-
transportable components, black start capability, and no expected emergency
planning zone beyond the reactor footprint allow KRONOS to run off-grid at the
point of load with few siting constraints.
A licensing lead over microreactor peers. The University of Illinois Urbana-
Champaign (UIUC) CPA, which the NRC accepted for review in May 2026, puts
NNE ahead of U.S. microreactor peers on licensing, and KRONOS became the
first microreactor to enter Phase 1 of the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission
(CNSC) licensing process. For future deployments, the NRC's proposed Part 57
microreactor framework could combine construction and operating licenses and
cut licensing timelines by 6-12 months.
Near-term catalysts and valuation. Catalysts include NRC finalization of the Part 57
microreactor rule (4Q26-1Q27), final engineering, procurement, and construction
management (EPCM) and tristructural isotropic (TRISO) fuel contracts (4Q26), the
UIUC construction permit (mid-2027), and an Alaska mining project award that
would make NNE an owner-operator under a power purchase agreement (PPA)
(4Q26). Our $18 price target embeds 3.4 GW of KRONOS capacity by 2040 and
limited value to the fuel businesses, but given heavy investor skepticism, we believe
the key to the stock is NNE advancing past milestones.

Company Overview 
NNE is an advanced technology enterprise seeking to become a commercially focused, diversified, 
and vertically integrated nuclear energy Company. The business is organized around five stated 
lines: 
1. Advanced stationary and portable nuclear microreactors 
2. Nuclear fuel supply chain 
3. Nuclear fuel transportation 
4. Nuclear space applications 
5. Nuclear consulting 
NNE’s fuel cycle initiatives extend well beyond the microreactor platform itself, spanning 
enrichment, planned fabrication, transportation, and now deconversion. The Company has entered 
fuel supply arrangements with Quadrant Nuclear Industries and LIS Technologies, acquired an 
operating fuel transportation business in STS alongside its development-stage subsidiary AFT, and 
holds a wholly owned fuel processing subsidiary, HALEU Energy Fuel Inc., which has not yet built a 
facility. On October 1, 2026, NNE and HALEU Energy Fuel announced a definitive agreement to 
acquire assets from Radnostix, Inc. for $9.5m in cash and $4.0m in NNE stock, including an NRC 
license and related technical materials for a previously planned depleted uranium hexafluoride 
extraction facility in Lea County, New Mexico that was never constructed. Closing is expected within 
approximately 90 to 120 days and remains subject to NRC consent, New Mexico approvals, and 
other conditions. Upon closing, NNE would own one of ten NRC-licensed fuel cycle facilities in the 
U.S., a regulatory foundation the Company believes can streamline future fuel cycle license 
amendments, although no final investment decision has been made. Founder, Chairman, and 
President Jay Yu is a serial nuclear technology entrepreneur with two decades of capital markets 
experience (see Management). Chief Executive Officer James Walker is a nuclear physicist who 
previously led construction of the Rolls-Royce Nuclear Chemical Plant and served as the UK Ministry 
of Defense’s subject-matter expert for nuclear material recovery capabilities.

Technology Overview 
KRONOS MMR 
KRONOS MMR is NNE's flagship design, which is a stationary, high-temperature gas-cooled reactor 
(HTGR) using TRISO fuel in a silicon carbide matrix within graphite blocks, cooled by helium and 
moderated by graphite, with a steel primary structure. The standard design characteristics include 
an enrichment level of 9.9% (optionally up to 19.9%), a rated output of approximately 15 MWe or 
45 MWth, an operating pressure of 6 MPa, a coolant temperature range of 300 to 660 °C, a fuel 
mass of approximately 950 kg, and a cycle length of 3 to 6.6 effective full-power years. The 
secondary loop uses a printed circuit heat exchanger transferring heat to a molten solar salt 
medium, driving a steam generator and turbine at a disclosed thermal efficiency of 35 to 36%. The 
Company describes the design as passively safe, load-following, capable of energy storage and 
black start, built from road-transportable components, and sited without an expected emergency 
planning zone beyond the reactor footprint. 
The KRONOS design was not developed in house; it was acquired out of Ultra Safe Nuclear 
Corporation's bankruptcy for ~$8.5m, closing in January 2025, and the Company states the design 
benefits from more than $120m of capital raised by its prior owner along with numerous issued, 
pending, or published patents. Engineering and construction services of the reactors are done with 
external partners. The design is intended to be as large a single reactor as possible while remaining 
substantially modular, allowing a standard unit to be configured to operate at or below 15 MWe 
for a single specialty application, multiple units can be distributed across sites, or many units can 
be connected to serve projects up to 1 GWe or more, which the Company positions as the basis for 
cost-effective scaling as customer demand ramps. 
KRONOS's HTGR architecture is not a novel reactor class; it builds on a lineage of helium-cooled, 
TRISO- or TRISO-precursor-fueled reactors operated in the United States, United Kingdom, 
Germany, Japan, and China since the 1960s. The Company characterizes this history as validating 
the core materials, coolant, and key operating parameters that KRONOS also employs, though no 
prior HTGR in this lineage matches KRONOS's specific combination of TRISO LEU+ fuel in a silicon 
carbide matrix at its stated 45 MWth rating.

A note on terminology used throughout this report: low-enriched uranium (LEU) refers to uranium 
enriched to below 20% U-235. In practice, the existing US commercial reactor fleet runs on 
conventional LEU enriched to roughly 3 to 5%. High-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU), by both 
NRC and DOE definition, is enriched above 5% and below 20% U-235, a formal regulatory threshold. 
KRONOS's disclosed enrichment level, 9.9% (optionally up to 19.9%), sits above that 5% line and 
therefore qualifies as HALEU by definition, notwithstanding the Company’s use of the term "LEU+" 
on recent earnings calls to describe it. The distinction that matters for supply risk is not LEU versus 
HALEU as two separate worlds, but where a given enrichment level falls relative to the 5% 
threshold, since the supply chains diverge meaningfully above that line. 
Mining, milling, and conversion to uranium hexafluoride (UF6) are identical regardless of a batch’s 
final enrichment target, meaning the front end of the fuel cycle is unaffected by whether NNE 
ultimately uses low-assay or higher-assay material. Enrichment is where the supply chains diverge. 
A cascade licensed for conventional sub-5% LEU is not automatically authorized above that level, 
since criticality safety requirements tighten and separate regulatory authorization is required. 
Conventional sub-5% capacity is large and mature, built over decades by Urenco, Orano, and 
historically Rosatom. Above-5% HALEU-capable capacity is comparatively minimal: domestically, 
it’s limited to Centrus’s demonstration cascade at Piketon, Ohio, which delivered on the order of 
900 kgs in its first two years, plus a small number of facilities still under construction or newly 
authorized, including Urenco’s 2025 authorization to enrich up to 10%, almost exactly NNE’s 
disclosed 9.9% base case. Licensing is similarly tiered by assay. The NRC distinguishes a lower 
Category III tier for facilities handling only conventional LEU from a more stringent Category II tier, 
the license TRISO-X received in February 2026, for facilities handling HALEU-assay material at scale. 
NNE’s near-term fuel plan therefore targets the newly opened, low end of the HALEU-capable 
supply chain rather than avoiding HALEU-side constraints altogether; its disclosed option to enrich 
up to 19.9% would push past Urenco’s 10% authorization into the same scarce, Centrus-dependent 
territory that constrains HALEU-dependent peers.

using TRISO-FCM fuel at less than 10% enrichment, graphite moderated, designed for

Regulatory Pathway and Project Status 
University of Illinois Prototype (KRONOS Flagship) 
NNE's first planned commercial prototype is sited on the campus of the University of Illinois 
Urbana-Champaign (UIUC). The CPA for this project was filed by UIUC itself, not by NNE directly; 
UIUC is the applicant for a Part 50 construction permit covering a Class 104c research reactor, with 
NNE participating as UIUC's industrial and technology partner under an April 2025 strategic 
collaboration agreement covering licensing, siting, public engagement, and research operations. 
 
www.wedbush.com

The Company believes KRONOS is the first commercially ready microreactor to reach the CPA stage 
of the NRC's formal licensing process. The CPA was submitted on March 31, 2026, and the NRC 
formally accepted it for review on May 18, 2026. 
Part 50 remains a two-step licensing process for this project: completion of the construction permit 
review, expected in 2027, is followed by initiation of construction activities, expected in the second 
half of 2027, and then a separate operating license application, fueling, and commissioning. The 
Company targets the project coming online in 2029 or 2030, while stating it is evaluating 
opportunities to expedite the timeline. The NRC's newly proposed Part 57 microreactor licensing 
framework, which is intended to more closely align construction and operating license processes 
for standardized, lower-risk microreactor technologies, was proposed in April 2026 with a 
comment period that closed in June 2026; it is not yet finalized and is not being used for the UIUC 
project.

Oak Brook, Illinois Facility 
In 2026, NNE acquired a 23,537-square-foot demonstration and office facility on 2.75 acres in Oak 
Brook, Illinois, expected to initially support up to 60 nuclear engineers, researchers, and support 
staff, and intended as a base for collaboration with UIUC on development and regulatory licensing. 
The Company's engineering team is developing a reduced-scale, non-nuclear KRONOS MMR 
engineering demonstration at the facility to support continued design refinement and technology 
validation. The Company cites Illinois's existing nuclear infrastructure, noting the state generated 
55% of its electricity from nuclear power in 2023 and accounted for 13% of the nation's total 
nuclear output, and notes the facility's proximity to the U.S.-Canada border as supportive of the 
Company's Canadian commercialization plans. 
10-Point Standardized Assessment Framework 
1. Technology Readiness Level 
KRONOS builds on a well-documented lineage of HTGR reactors operated since the 1960s, and 
TRISO fuel itself has an established test and operating history, but no KRONOS unit has yet been 
built or operated, and NNE acquired the design out of Ultra Safe Nuclear Corporation's bankruptcy. 
The Oak Brook non-nuclear engineering demonstration is a useful near-term proof point to watch. 
2. Regulatory Pathway 
NNE has achieved verifiable regulatory milestones: construction permit application (CPA) 
acceptance for KRONOS and first-mover status in Canada's Phase 1 CNSC process. However, the 
CPA is held by UIUC rather than NNE, an important structural distinction, and the newly proposed 
Part 57 framework that could streamline future microreactor licensing is not yet finalized. 
3. Fuel Supply Chain 
KRONOS's 9.9% base-case enrichment sits at the newly opened low end of the HALEU band (above 
5% by definition, notwithstanding the Company's "LEU+" terminology), where Urenco's 2025 
authorization to enrich up to 10% domestically provides a near-term commercial source, a 
meaningfully lower-risk position than peers requiring high-assay HALEU near 20%. The Company 
has disclosed active discussions with Urenco for enrichment and with multiple existing TRISO 
fabricators (B2XT, Standard Nuclear with Framatome, and TRISO-X) to secure near-term supply 
without redesign, though the disclosed option to enrich up to 19.9% would exceed Urenco's 
current authorization and require the same scarce, largely Centrus-dependent HALEU supply that 
constrains peers. 
Longer-term ambitions run through NNE's own HALEU Energy Fuel Inc. subsidiary (site tentatively 
identified, no facility under construction) and two further relationships: an arm's-length back-end 
recycling relationship with Quadrant Nuclear Industries, and a related-party front-end enrichment 
relationship with LIS Technologies (LIST; see Key Risks to Our Rating). None of the longer-term 
relationships has yet reached commercial scale. 
4. Thermal Efficiency and Levelized Cost of Energy 
The Company discloses a thermal efficiency of 35 to 36% and estimates that the first full-scale 
reactor at UIUC will cost $300m to $350m. This range is a conservative estimate and unit costs 
could decline sharply—to as little as $125m—for “nth of a kind” reactors. Specific to the UIUC 
project, NNE is currently evaluating investment tax credits in the 30 to 40% range as well as funding 
from the State of Illinois and UIUC that could also significantly reduce costs, although tax credits 
may not be applicable to this project if its primary function is research These figures are internally 
consistent with HTGR physics broadly but have not been independently verified at commercial 
scale, and KRONOS's small unit size means per-unit economics depend heavily on achieving the

multi-unit co-location scaling the Company describes. The Company could see a cost of power of 
$250–300/MWh for the first units, falling below $100/MWh for later units. 
5. Passive Safety 
TRISO fuel's layered containment and helium's chemically inert, non-activating properties are well-
established safety characteristics. Disclosed dose modeling for an 840 MWe illustrative plant shows 
a radiation footprint barely exceeding the nuclear site boundary under design-basis accident 
conditions, consistent with the Company's zero emergency planning zone positioning. We view this 
as a genuine strength of the underlying technology class rather than a marketing claim specific to 
NNE.

6. Constructability 
NNE has not yet built a commercial-scale reactor of any kind; the Oak Brook facility's non-nuclear 
demonstration unit is an early design validation step. NNE’s disclosed engineering and construction

modular, factory-fabricated deployment, but plans at project scale are in the early stages.

8. Management and Pedigree 
NNE has assembled a deep regulatory and technical bench for a company of its size and stage: two 
regulatory licensing leads with a combined roughly 70 years of NRC licensing experience, a CTO 
who led KRONOS and LOKI development at Ultra Safe Nuclear Corp, and a former Senate-confirmed 
DOE CFO leading government affairs (full bios in Management). Set against it is the related-party 
fuel relationship with LIS Technologies (see Key Risks to Our Rating), whose arm’s-length pricing 
and terms we cannot independently verify. 
9. Cash Runway and Dilution 
NNE is well capitalized in the near term, with ~$580m of combined cash and short-term 
investments as of June 30, 2026, a $900m shelf registration including a $400m at-the-market 
program, and minimal liabilities ($12m in total liabilities against $628m in total assets). Financing 
activity has been substantial, however: the Company raised $427m gross from common stock 
issuances in the nine months ended June 30, 2026, and basic weighted average shares outstanding 
grew from 36.66m to 51.26m over the same nine months versus the prior-year period, an increase 
of approximately 40%, reflecting meaningful dilution alongside the capital raising. 
10. Policy and Subsidy Sensitivity 
Nuclear-specific investment and production tax credits under Sections 48E and 45Y of the 2022 
Inflation Reduction Act hold a construction-start deadline at the end of 2033, putting them in a 
materially more durable position than wind or solar due to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act’s 2026 
construction-start cliff. These tax credits will generally flow to the owner-operator of the asset; NNE 
serves as the reactor technology provider. This is consistent with the arrangements NNE has with 
UIUC and Tillman, where the owners of the generating assets are positioned to claim the credits. 
We view this as a positive for NNE’s customers as durable tax credit economics incentivize NNE’s 
customers to commit capital to the deployment of KRONOS units, which will flow back to NNE as

Business Model and Commercialization Pathway 
NNE's model is asset-light by design across most of its business and asset-heavy in one place. 
Reactor manufacturing and engineering, procurement, and construction work is outsourced to 
partners. On the UIUC project, the university itself owns and operates the reactor while NNE 
finances the project and supplies technology and licensing support, meaning NNE does not carry 
the generation asset or operating liability on its own flagship project. The capital-intensive 
enrichment buildout at LIS Technologies, a disclosed $1.38b Oak Ridge, Tennessee facility, similarly 
sits off NNE's balance sheet; NNE's direct exposure is a $2m related-party equity investment made 
in August 2024, together with subcontractor status on the DOE's LEU Enrichment Acquisition 
Program, under which LIST is prime contractor. 
The one clear exception to this asset-light pattern is Secured Transportation Services (STS), acquired 
in May 2026 through NNE's Advanced Fuel Transportation Inc. (AFT) subsidiary. STS is a specialized, 
U.S.-based, globally operating nuclear logistics and transportation company founded in 2005, with 
more than two decades of experience executing complex nuclear materials projects and current 
approvals covering more than 90% of active NRC-approved spent fuel routes in the United States. 
STS reported audited fiscal year 2025 revenue of ~$7.1m and net income of ~$1.3m, and it is the 
source of NNE's first-ever recognized revenue in the quarter ended June 30, 2026. AFT separately 
 
www.wedbush.com

holds exclusive licensing rights to a patented high-capacity HALEU fuel transport cask technology 
(U.S. Patent No. 11,699,534 B2) developed by the U.S. government, three national laboratories, 
and funded by the DOE, and has signed an agreement with Gesellschaft fur Nuklear-Service mbH 
(GNS) to study an optimized HALEU transportation system based on that licensed cask design, 
covering multiple HALEU fuel types including uranium oxide, TRISO particles, uranium-zirconium 
hydride, uranium mononitride, and molten salt reactor fuel. 
On a per-MW basis NNE carries strong unit economics. Based on our estimates, NNE’s nth-of-a-
kind annualized gross profit is $68k per MW, modestly below X-Energy (XE) and well above 
Terrestrial Energy (IMSR), driven by high fuel revenues and high margins. Like XE, NNE also has a 
high proportion of lifetime revenues from high-margin TRISO fuel—a “razor / razor blade” model.

Fuel Procurement: Three Layers on Three Different Timelines 
NNE's actual fuel procurement plan is best understood as three layers operating on three different 
timelines and risk profiles, rather than a single vertically integrated pipeline. The first layer, and the 
one that actually fuels the near-term first-of-a-kind reactor, is straightforward commercial 
procurement targeting the low end of the HALEU-capable supply chain: the Company stated on its 
August 2026 Q3 earnings call that KRONOS's use of commercially available enrichment today is a 
deliberate design choice, and that NNE has begun discussions with Urenco, which received NRC 
authorization in 2025 to enrich up to 10% domestically, for mass-rollout enrichment needs at a 
level consistent with KRONOS's disclosed 9.9% base case. 
On the fabrication side, where enriched material is turned into TRISO particles and finished fuel, 
the Company said it is in discussions with multiple existing fabricators, specifically B2XT, Standard 
Nuclear (partnered with Framatome), and TRISO-X (X-Energy's fuel subsidiary), to negotiate long-
term supply arrangements, and stated that existing fabrication capacity is sufficient to support the 
 
www.wedbush.com
Page 12

 
 
first-of-a-kind reactor timeline without redesign, including a path to higher-assay HALEU later, up 
to the disclosed 19.9% option, if it becomes economical and more broadly available. 
The second layer is NNE's own wholly owned subsidiary, HALEU Energy Fuel Inc., through which 
the Company states, in coordination with the DOE, it is seeking to develop a domestic LEU and 
HALEU fuel supply chain to serve both its own reactors and the broader advanced reactor industry; 
NNE discloses it has tentatively identified a site and begun building the team to design these 
facilities, though no facility is under construction today. 
The third layer is the longer-horizon vertical integration ambition discussed above: Quadrant 
Nuclear Industries’ planned Vanguard facility at Idaho National Laboratory, a proposed commercial-
scale used nuclear fuel reprocessing plant designed to recover HALEU from existing domestic spent 
fuel rather than enrich fresh uranium, under a non-binding memorandum of understanding signed 
August 18, 2026; and LIS Technologies, which approaches the same eventual HALEU gap from the 
front end through laser-based enrichment of fresh uranium. The Company has framed this layer as 
a potential path to bring more of the fuel cycle in house or de-risk mass production through joint 
ventures over time, explicitly distinct from what fuels the Company's near-term reactors. NNE has 
also signed a memorandum of understanding with Dioxitek, an Argentine state-owned nuclear fuel 
cycle and uranium dioxide production Company, to evaluate uranium conversion capacity and 
supporting infrastructure in Argentina, a further, earlier-stage diversification within this same 
longer-horizon layer.

Incumbent Utility Fuel Procurement: Why Scale, Not Just Technology, 
Insulates the Established Players 
The largest existing nuclear operators are not similarly exposed to fuel supply risk, which is largely 
specific to pre-revenue developers rather than a market-wide problem. Southern Company's (SO) 
own Form 10-K filings state plainly, in the Business section under "Fuel Supply," that its nuclear-
operating subsidiaries Alabama Power and Georgia Power "have multiple contracts covering their 
nuclear fuel needs for uranium, conversion services, enrichment services, and fuel fabrication," 
adding that "the Company believes suppliers have sufficient nuclear fuel production capability to 
permit normal operation of the SO system's nuclear generating units." This language is essentially 
consistent across two decades of annual filings, with only the disclosed contract ladder length 
changing: remaining terms of up to 14 years in the fiscal 2020 10-K, up to 11 years in the fiscal 2023 
10-K, and up to 10 years in the most recent fiscal 2025 10-K (filed February 2026). The consistency 
of the disclosure, and the fact that the ladder length shifts modestly year to year rather than 
collapsing, indicates a durable practice of maintaining overlapping, staggered contracts across all 
four fuel cycle stages, natural uranium, conversion, enrichment, and fabrication, rather than a one-
time or opportunistic arrangement. 
Constellation Energy took a related but distinct approach in 2008 when it acquired Nufcor 
International, a uranium marketing and trading firm, through its commodities subsidiary, aiming to 
build in-house physical uranium trading and risk management capability; that holding did not 
persist, with Constellation sold Nufcor to Goldman Sachs roughly a year later, so it reflects a since-
abandoned experiment rather than Constellation's current fuel procurement model, which today 
rests on the same style of long-term, laddered contracting as Southern's. 
This incumbent playbook does not transfer to microreactor and SMR developers for three 
structural reasons. First, existing light-water reactors operated by Southern Company and 
Constellation run on conventional sub-5% LEU, the mature, abundant side of the enrichment divide 
described earlier in this report, while KRONOS and peer designs need HALEU-band material, the 
scarce, still-being-built side. Second, incumbents can trade on four or more decades of continuous 
purchasing volume and operating history to negotiate the kind of decade-long, laddered contracts 
Southern discloses; a pre-revenue developer with no operating reactor and no committed volume 
 
www.wedbush.com
Page 14

 
 
has little to offer a supplier in exchange for a comparable contract, which is why NNE is pursuing 
relationships and its own processing subsidiary. Third, regulated utility balance sheets, backed by 
ratepayer cost recovery, can underwrite multi-year and sometimes prepaid fuel commitments in a 
way a venture-stage developer's balance sheet cannot. Even the incumbents' security reflects 
superior contracting position and diversification within the same global mining, conversion, and 
enrichment base (Cameco, Kazatomprom, Urenco, Orano, and historically Rosatom), not 
technological or vertical independence from it; Southern and Constellation do not mine or enrich 
their own uranium any more than NNE does; they have simply been buying successfully, at scale, 
for decades.

Company is evaluating investment tax credits of 30% to 40%, funding from the State of Illinois and

Key Risks to Our Rating 
• 
Offtake conversion risk: The entire disclosed commercial pipeline, spanning BaRupOn, EHC 
Investment, DS Dansuk, Supermicro, and U.S. military discussions, is non-binding; slower-
than-expected conversion, or conversion on terms below current expectations, would 
directly affect our base case. 
• 
Licensing structure risk: The KRONOS CPA is held by the University of Illinois rather than NNE 
directly; a change in that relationship, or in the terms governing NNE's rights to the design, 
construction, and commercial operation of the reactor, could affect the replicability of this 
template for future commercial deployments. The Company has stated that it expects a 
future commercial license to be readily achievable after the UIUC reactor secures its 
operating license. This view is unverified as no commercial license under this structure has 
been granted in the past. 
• 
Execution and resourcing risk: NNE is simultaneously developing four reactor designs 
(KRONOS, LOKI, ZEUS, and ODIN) alongside fuel, transportation, and space subsidiaries with 
an 85-person team; NNE has not yet built or operated a commercial-scale reactor of any kind. 
• 
Related party governance risk: Chairman and President Jay Yu simultaneously serves as 
Executive Chairman and CEO of LIS Technologies, the fuel supply counterparty central to 
NNE's vertical integration narrative. While NNE's direct capital exposure is limited, the 
governance overlap warrants closer disclosure than an ordinary arm's-length supplier 
relationship. 
• 
Fuel supply and geopolitical risk: While KRONOS's ability to run on low-assay HALEU 
("LEU+") fuel available today from established suppliers is a near-term advantage, NNE's 
longer-term HALEU ambitions depend on its own not-yet-built HALEU Energy Fuel Inc. 
facility, LIS Technologies (unproven laser enrichment technology), and Quadrant Nuclear 
Industries (a proposed, not yet selected, reprocessing facility), all of which remain pre-
commercial. 
• 
Dilution risk: Basic weighted average shares outstanding grew ~40% (36.66m to 51.26m) in 
the nine months ended June 30, 2026 versus the prior-year period, alongside $427m of gross 
common stock issuance over the same nine months; continued reliance on the Company's 
at-the-market (ATM) program could further dilute existing shareholders. 
• 
Policy and regulatory framework risk: The NRC's proposed Part 57 microreactor framework, 
which could streamline future licensing, remains unfinalized, and NNE's DOE-linked funding 
threads (including its subcontractor role under LIS Technologies’ LEU Enrichment Acquisition

Valuation
Our $18 PT is based on a DCF of our project-by-project build (3.4 GW by 2040), using a 16.4% WACC and a 3.0x EV/revenue exit multiple
on FY40E revenue, with limited value for fuel.
Company Description
NNE is developing the KRONOS micro modular reactor, a ~15 MWe helium-cooled HTGR, plus the LOKI, ZEUS, and ODIN designs, and
is building a vertically integrated fuel business spanning enrichment, deconversion, and transport.
Risks to the Attainment of Our Price Target and Rating:
Offtake conversion risk: The entire disclosed commercial pipeline, spanning BaRupOn, EHC Investment, DS Dansuk, Supermicro, and
U.S. military discussions, is non-binding; slower-than-expected conversion, or conversion on terms below current expectations, would
directly affect our base case.
Licensing structure risk: The KRONOS CPA is held by the University of Illinois rather than NNE directly; a change in that relationship, or
in the terms governing NNE's rights to the design, construction, and commercial operation of the reactor, could affect the replicability
of this template for future commercial deployments. The Company has stated that it expects a future commercial license to be readily
achievable after the UIUC reactor secures its operating license. This view is unverified as no commercial license under this structure
has been granted in the past.
Execution and resourcing risk: NNE is simultaneously developing four reactor designs (KRONOS, LOKI, ZEUS, and ODIN) alongside fuel,
transportation, and space subsidiaries with an 85-person team; NNE has not yet built or operated a commercial-scale reactor of any
kind.
Related party governance risk: Chairman and President Jay Yu simultaneously serves as Executive Chairman and CEO of LIS
Technologies, the fuel supply counterparty central to NNE's vertical integration narrative. While NNE's direct capital exposure is limited,
the governance overlap warrants closer disclosure than an ordinary arm's-length supplier relationship.
Fuel supply and geopolitical risk: While KRONOS's ability to run on low-assay HALEU ("LEU+") fuel available today from established
suppliers is a near-term advantage, NNE's longer-term HALEU ambitions depend on its own not-yet-built HALEU Energy Fuel Inc. facility,
LIS Technologies (unproven laser enrichment technology), and Quadrant Nuclear Industries (a proposed, not yet selected, reprocessing
facility), all of which remain pre-commercial.
Dilution risk: Basic weighted average shares outstanding grew ~40% (36.66m to 51.26m) in the nine months ended June 30, 2026
versus the prior-year period, alongside $427m of gross common stock issuance over the same nine months; continued reliance on the
Company's at-the-market (ATM) program could further dilute existing shareholders.
Policy and regulatory framework risk: The NRC's proposed Part 57 microreactor framework, which could streamline future licensing,
remains unfinalized, and NNE's DOE-linked funding threads (including its subcontractor role under LIS Technologies’ LEU Enrichment
Acquisition Program contract) are subject to the same programmatic and appropriations risk as the broader DOE nuclear fuel agenda.
