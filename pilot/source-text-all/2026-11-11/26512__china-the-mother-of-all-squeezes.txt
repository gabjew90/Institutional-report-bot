China: The ingredients for a squeeze
China has added $5 trillion to GDP since Covid, yet its equities have underperformed global markets by
a staggering 110 percentage points. The disconnect between economic growth and shareholder
returns has been extraordinary. But Goldman sees 10% trend earnings growth and potential valuation
upside of 10–15%, while several key China indices are beginning to stabilize around important
technical levels. The damage remains severe, but the ingredients for a powerful squeeze are starting
to fall into place. Is the China bear finally running out of sellers?
GS
The growth trap
China's problem hasn't been economic growth, but converting that growth into shareholder returns.
Goldman points to subsidies, overcapacity and relentless competition that have forced companies to
chase volumes rather than profitability. The result has been one of the most extraordinary disconnects
between economic expansion and equity performance.

Goldman sees the upside
Despite the structural problems, Goldman remains constructive on China's medium-term earnings
outlook. The bank forecasts 10% trend EPS growth and normalized ROE of 12% over the next five
years. AI adoption, global expansion and anti-involution policies could drive a 10–15% valuation re-
rating. The market remains depressed, but Goldman sees a very different earnings story ahead.
China's AI opportunity
China's domestic AI ecosystem is gaining momentum. Nvidia's share of Chinese data-center AI
accelerator shipments fell to 34% in H1 2026, with Huawei closing the gap at 28%. TS Lombard argues
that US export restrictions are accelerating domestic chip substitution. For Chinese AI hardware
companies, this is becoming a powerful structural growth opportunity.

TS Lombard
The power advantage
China's AI advantage extends well beyond semiconductors. The country added roughly ten times as
much generating capacity as the US in 2025, while electricity costs at some Chinese data-center hubs
are around half the US industrial average. TS Lombard argues that cheap power could become an
increasingly important competitive advantage as AI compute scales. China may have a bigger role
in the next phase of the AI buildout than equity markets are currently pricing in.
TS Lombard
The $86 billion buyer
The national team has been stepping in aggressively. According to Hartnett, China has seen a record
$86 billion of national-team inflows over the past four weeks. That's a massive amount of buying power
entering a market where sentiment remains deeply depressed. The key question is whether this buying
can provide the foundation for a more sustainable recovery.
Hartnett

China's squeeze potential
The China bear has stabilized over the past week, with ChiNext now approaching its 21-day MA. A close
above would open the door to a potentially powerful squeeze. The damage has been so severe that
even a simple rebound toward the 50-day MA would represent a substantial move. That's how
depressed this market has become.

Fallen star
The STAR 50 remains below its downward-sloping trendline, but has reclaimed the 21-day MA. Another
few percent higher would put that trendline under serious pressure. A breakout would be an important
signal that China's innovation stocks are finally turning the corner.

The bottom line
China remains technically damaged, but the setup is changing. Goldman sees earnings upside,
domestic AI is gaining momentum and national-team buying has accelerated. With key indices
stabilizing, the squeeze potential is becoming increasingly interesting. A new bull market is far from
confirmed, but the risk of being aggressively short China is rising.
