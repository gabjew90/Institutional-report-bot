Introduction 
September has always had the feeling of a reset. Summer draws to a 
close, schools reopen, offices refill and attention turns to the final stretch 
of the year. The third quarter of 2026 began amid geopolitical tensions, 
higher energy prices, shifting rate expectations and fiscal concerns. Yet 
beneath resilient market headlines, the quarter was defined by sharp 
rotations, narrow leadership and increasingly differentiated outcomes 
across companies, sectors and strategies.
For hedge funds, those dynamics created clear winners and losers. 
Quantitative strategies were the standout performers, benefiting 
from elevated single-stock dispersion, low correlations and rapidly 
changing market relationships. Strength was broad-based across time 
horizons, asset classes and investment approaches; September was 
particularly strong as managers capitalized on opportunities created by 
the summer sell-off. By contrast, equity long/short faced a challenging 
quarter. Managers that had benefited from AI-linked hardware, memory 
and momentum earlier in the year were caught by sharp reversals, 
particularly among pan-Asian and U.S. generalist strategies. Activists 
provided an important counterpoint, particularly in Japan, where 
idiosyncratic corporate catalysts continued to generate opportunities.
The divergence was evident elsewhere. Multi-strategy outcomes 
varied with the underlying mix of businesses, as platforms with larger 
fundamental equity exposures faced greater headwinds than those with 
broader quantitative, macro and futures capabilities. Opportunistic/
macro was modestly positive overall, led by another strong quarter for 
reinsurance, while macro managers contended with repricing across 
rates, currencies and commodities. Public credit also faced a more 
difficult backdrop as yields rose and spreads widened late in the quarter, 
although resulting dislocations created more attractive entry points.
Higher interest rates have understandably put fixed income back on 
investors’ radars, with yields once again offering a meaningful source 
of return. Less appreciated is that higher rates can also strengthen 
the forward-looking opportunity for hedge funds. Structurally, higher 
cash rates can increase returns earned on short-sale proceeds and 
collateral without requiring managers to predict the future direction of 
rates or inflation. A higher cost of capital also places greater emphasis 
on fundamentals, from interest coverage and margins to balance sheet 
strength and financing needs, increasing differentiation between 
companies and creating a richer opportunity set for alpha generation.
Looking ahead to Q4 2026, relative value and opportunistic/macro remain 
our highest-conviction areas, as dispersion, changing market relationships 
and policy uncertainty continue to create opportunities. Within equities, 
we remain selective but see compelling idiosyncratic opportunities 
across activism, corporate change and longer-term themes. After a 
quarter of markedly different outcomes across hedge fund strategies, 
we believe manager selection, portfolio construction and the ability to 
adapt to changing market regimes will remain critical into year-end.

Relative value
Relative value strategies delivered a solid quarter, 
generating gains in each month despite significant volatility 
beneath equity market performance. Quantitative and 
systematic equity managers led returns, with September 
particularly strong as managers capitalized on opportunities 
created by the summer sell-off. Multi-strategy results were 
more dispersed: managers with larger fundamental equity 
businesses generally faced greater headwinds, while those 
with broader quantitative, macro and futures exposures 
proved more resilient.
Statistical arbitrage was clearly the best performer. Calm 
index-level performance masked significant single-stock 
moves, with equity dispersion reaching levels not seen 
since the dot-com boom. Market leadership also remained 
narrow: while the broader market gained approximately 
13% year-to-date through the end of Q3, the average stock 
rose less than 6% and nearly 40% of the market remains 
in negative territory for the period. This differentiation was 
further reflected in the proportion of S&P 500 constituents 
exhibiting negative beta to the index, which reached all-
time highs across both three-month and one-year periods. 
Together, narrow leadership and elevated stock-level 
dispersion created a particularly rich environment for 
alpha generation.
We continue to see differentiation between managers 
using a machine-learning approach and more traditional, 
hypothesis-led investment processes. The recent 
environment has favored managers able to identify evolving 
clusters, changing correlations and shifting regimes 
quickly. Machine-learning approaches have generally 
adapted faster as these relationships change, which is 
an increasingly valuable capability as thematic and factor 
cycles become shorter.
Our volatility arbitrage allocation extended its strong run in 
Q3. A sharp increase in realized and implied single-stock 
volatility in July was followed by an extraordinary contraction 
in August, creating a productive trading environment. 
Looking ahead, relatively inexpensive index volatility creates 
attractive asymmetry, with portfolios retaining the potential 
to benefit from a spike in correlations during a broader sell-
off or de-risking event.
Divergent performance across multi-strategy platforms 
reinforced the importance of diversification and strategy 
mix. Managers with larger fundamental equity businesses 
faced greater headwinds following the summer momentum 
unwind, although many have still delivered solid results 
year-to-date. More diversified platforms with meaningful 
quantitative, macro and futures allocations proved more 
resilient; Q3 reinforced the heterogeneity of the multi-
strategy universe and the importance of understanding 
where and how risk is deployed.
Quantitative investing remains one of the most attractive 
areas for manager selection across both emerging and 
established investment managers. AI-assisted coding, cloud 
storage and on-demand compute have lowered barriers for 
emerging talent, while also increasing the risk of crowding 
into similar tools, data and signals. At the same time, these 
technologies are expanding the breadth and complexity of 
problems that well-resourced quantitative organizations 
can tackle, while shortening research and implementation 
cycles. The opportunity is therefore not simply to back 
emerging managers or established incumbents, but to 
identify those with genuinely differentiated perspectives, 
proprietary data or repeatable edges, supported by 
disciplined research, portfolio construction and risk 
management. In an environment of elevated dispersion and 
rapidly changing market relationships, we believe these 
attributes will be increasingly important.

We continue to remain constructive on relative value 
strategies. Elevated return dispersion and rising factor 
volatility present both an opportunity and a risk. In this 
higher-risk environment, managers with better risk 
management should outperform; they can identify change 
early and capture the resulting dislocations, while crowded 
portfolios and weaker risk controls are more likely to be 
exposed. Outcomes are increasingly driven by manager 
quality rather than strategy labels. Our underwriting 
therefore favors differentiated strategy mixes, robust 
portfolio construction and dynamic risk management—the 
attributes required to survive a risk event and profit from its 
aftermath.
Spotlight on quantitative investing 
strategies (QIS)
QIS have evolved significantly from their roots in 
alternative risk premia. Industry assets have roughly 
doubled over the past five years, alongside a broader 
and increasingly sophisticated universe. The result 
is an expanding toolkit offering investors access to 
systematic, hedge-fund-like strategies through liquid, 
transparent and capital-efficient structures.
The opportunity set has been particularly attractive 
this year. Within our Multi-QIS portfolio, both carry 
and defensive exposures have contributed positively. 
Dispersion strategies have shown standout returns 
while event-driven, other low-correlation exposures 
and foreign exchange carry have also contributed 
to performance. Defensive rates-volatility strategies 
continue to provide diversification against periods of 
market stress.
Innovation in QIS continues across several areas, 
including rates volatility, equity dispersion and 
options-based strategies. Investors are also exploring 
QIS for portable alpha and tail-hedging solutions, 
where liquidity, customization and cash efficiency can 
be particularly valuable. As the universe expands, we 
believe active selection and portfolio construction 
will become increasingly important; they will enable 
investors to rotate exposures as market regimes 
evolve and tailor portfolios to specific return and risk 
objectives while retaining the liquidity, transparency 
and implementation efficiencies of QIS.
Opportunistic/macro
Opportunistic and macro strategies delivered modestly 
positive results during Q3, although outcomes varied 
significantly across sub-strategies and managers.
Reinsurance was the top performer across opportunistic 
and macro strategies, with seasonally elevated accruals 
leading to double-digit returns during the quarter. The El 
Niño weather pattern helped suppress storm activity in 
the Atlantic, leading to muted levels of insured loss levels 
during the quarter. While reinsurance remains an important 
diversifier across portfolios, we have continued to reduce 
our overall position size as the opportunity set reverts 
towards average levels.
Within discretionary macro, after several quarters of 
increasingly concentrated positioning around a relatively 
narrow set of consensus themes, predominantly in equities, 
managers meaningfully broadened their exposures in Q3. 
This resulted in a wider range of portfolios and greater 
dispersion in outcomes. Rates volatility picked up during 
the quarter after a relatively subdued period. Geopolitical 
conflict fed through to energy prices and inflation 
expectations, which in turn drove greater rate volatility. 
Moves in rates were broad-based across developed 
markets, including the U.S., U.K., and Europe. As a result, 
the opportunity was more about volatility rather than a 
one-sided move higher in rates, in addition to actual policy 
divergence between emerging markets that are already 
cutting interest rates and Western markets facing renewed 
hiking risk.
Manager outcomes were heavily influenced by positioning, 
with many entering September long developed market front-
end rates, which proved to be a detractor as rates repriced. 
Long gold and short U.S. dollar positions also weighed 
on returns during the month. Looking ahead, continued 
divergence in inflation, fiscal and monetary policy should 
create opportunities across markets, favoring managers 
able to capitalize on relative differences rather than rely on a 
single directional macro view.

Public credit
Credit performance was mixed in Q3 as rate-driven losses 
in municipal bond trading strategies were partially offset by 
idiosyncratic gains in distressed and structured credit.
Rising rates and a heavy issuance calendar combined to 
create a particularly difficult trading environment for munis 
during the quarter. The Bloomberg US Muni Bond Index fell 
4.4% in September, its worst monthly return since 2008.
September’s muni sell-off creates an attractive entry point 
for new capital to enter the space. Higher base rates and 
wider Municipal Market Data (MMD) spreads have increased 
forward-looking return potential, drawing in a wider array 
of both taxable and tax-exempt investors into the market. 
From a seasonal perspective, muni issuance tends to wane 
in late fall/early winter, creating a technical tailwind for asset 
owners heading into the end of the year. Our dedicated muni 
managers have increased risk materially in response to this 
attractive opportunity set.
From a geographical perspective, the opportunity set 
in Europe has continued to improve relative to the U.S. 
as we’ve seen an increasing volume of stressed credits 
and companies with immediate restructuring needs in the 
region.
The U.S. opportunity set has also improved at the margin. 
Both base rates and index-level spreads widened materially 
in September, creating higher forward returns for carry-
oriented exposures. Additionally, we’ve seen a continued 
divergence between CCC (and lower) credits relative to 
the broader high-yield universe in the U.S. We recently 
onboarded a global credit manager to take advantage of the 
improving opportunity set in both regions.
Looking ahead, dispersion remains a key theme. Index-level 
spreads still look tight compared to history, while lower-
quality credit continues to price in meaningfully more risk; 
this keeps the opportunity set open for managers willing 
and able to work down the capital structure and underwrite 
stressed situations. We continue to view convertible credit as 
an above-average opportunity, particularly where managers 
can combine primary market participation with active 
hedging and trading around volatility, though we remain 
mindful that performance can be episodic and highly path-
dependent.

Long/short equity
Q3 was a challenging quarter for equity long/short. Losses 
were concentrated among pan-Asian and U.S. generalists, 
many of whom had led performance for our platform in Q2. 
The same long exposure to AI-linked hardware and memory 
stocks which benefited funds in Q2 was among the largest 
detractors as momentum unraveled in Q3. Activist strategies 
were a relative bright spot with strong contributions coming 
from investors focused on both the U.S. and Japan.
Looking broadly across the equity long/short platform, 
the universe of AI-linked stocks remains a core theme in a 
number of portfolios, with managers broadly constructive on 
the medium-term opportunity set. That said, implementation 
has increasingly shifted toward select software names, 
where recent dislocations have created attractive entry 
points for into companies that have meaningfully adopted AI. 
Tech-enabled healthcare is another area where managers 
have begun to express views on AI beneficiaries while still 
avoiding crowded and overvalued segments of the market.
Asia-related strategies remained in focus in Q3, with Japan 
activism finishing the quarter as the top-performing long/
short strategy. While strong underlying markets provided a 
lift, manager alpha was meaningful during the quarter and 
largely driven by change-of-ownership catalysts, such as 
merger and acquisition (M&A) activity, competitive bidding 
and divestitures.
Japan still looks inexpensive and balance-sheet heavy, 
with non-core assets and under-optimized working capital 
offering a clear path for activists to create value. That said, 
after several years of strong performance, we’ve begun to 
modestly trim our Japan exposure as higher valuations and 
currency uncertainty are balancing out the opportunity 
set. We remain constructive overall and expect activists to 
continue driving alpha.
Korea appears somewhat attractive on the surface, but the 
setup is meaningfully different from Japan. While we see 
numerous similarities (cheap valuations, asset-rich balance 
sheets and policy initiatives), Korea’s market remains far 
more concentrated in the AI complex. That concentration 
can dominate index behavior, compress breadth and create 
more challenging outcomes for long/short investors. As a 
result, managers have been selective in adding Korea risk, 
often preferring to allocate the next marginal dollar to Japan, 
where the investable universe is broader and portfolio 
construction can be more diversified.
Biotech also remains a high-conviction focus, though rising 
rates have put pressure on valuations, pushing managers 
toward higher-quality names with clearer operating cash 
flow and less reliance on external financing. M&A remains 
a key swing factor as the ingredients for strategic deals 
remain well anchored; large-cap pharmaceutical companies 
are facing a steep patent cliff and increasing pressure to 
replenish revenue pipelines with biotech acquisitions.
Event-driven
Event-driven managers produced muted results in Q3 as 
their opportunity set moderated back to more normalized 
levels after a very strong start to the year. Both dollar values 
and volumes of M&A deals decreased materially vs. the prior 
quarter as higher market volatility and increased financing 
costs are leading to increased corporate uncertainty.
While the closing of the Paramount/Warner Bros transaction 
was a positive driver for our portfolios, it was also a reminder 
of the evolving complexity in this space. Deal risk is no 
longer contained to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) 
and Department of Justice (DOJ) reviews, shareholder 
votes, financing risk and other federal-level approvals. 
State attorneys general are showing a greater willingness 
to intervene, which can extend timelines but also create 
episodic spread volatility, providing an opportunity for 
managers which have a greater focus on trading. However, 
capacity constraints at the state level remain a meaningful 
limitation, which should keep intervention more targeted 
in nature.
The near-term opportunity looks more limited as we see 
both higher financing costs, as well as fewer and less 
controversial deals, limiting the potential to profit from 
transactions with wider spreads. That said, we remain 
constructive on the medium-term opportunity set. Pent-up 
strategic M&A demand following several years of heightened 
regulatory scrutiny under the previous U.S. administration 
should support a recovery in actionable deal flow.
