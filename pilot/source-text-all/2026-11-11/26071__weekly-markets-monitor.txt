•
Last week saw a shift in the US political landscape, weakness in technology stocks, a sharp drop in 
oil, and broadly resilient macro data. The Democratic midterms victory tempered expectations of 
further fiscal expansion while raising the prospect of tighter AI regulation. Meanwhile, US data 
remained strong, supported by strength in labour markets and business activity. In Europe, 
inflation edged higher and activity softened, while the BoE raised rates. Across Asia, activity 
moderated in China, wage growth strengthened in Japan and activity in India remained solid.
•
Global equity markets ended the week mixed, bond yields declined, crude oil prices fell sharply, 
while the US dollar strengthened.
•
The Fed might regret its inaction, and markets their downgrading of further hikes. Since the 
October meeting, inflation data have largely met expectations, yet PCE (the one that matters) 
continues to run well above the 2% target. Growth surprises have strengthened sharply, 
supported by stronger-than-expected payrolls (165k vs expected 70k), lower claims, stronger 
hourly earnings and consumer spending (C.O.T.W). Weaker equities may weigh on sentiment, but 
the data since that meeting should give the ‘pause’ crowd…some pause. With the policy outcome 
of the midterm elections still unclear, these developments could provide some near-term macro 
headwinds for gold. Softer expected US CPI tomorrow might allow for a breather.

All about Gold
3
Source: CFTC, Shanghai Futures Exchange, ETF Providers, Bloomberg, World Gold Council
•
Gold regained some ground. The LBMA Gold Price PM closed Friday at US$4,050/oz, up 3.5% w/w 
and back above the critical US$4,000/oz level, helping to narrow its y-t-d decline to 7.3%.
•
Surprise midterm election results partially fuelled gold's weekly rebound as investors repriced 
expectations on spending, AI regulation and foreign policy. Yields and the dollar fell and AI 
equities plunged, all historically boding well for gold. Positioning was mixed: gold ETFs saw 
outflows, led by Europe's first weekly loss since late June. But gold futures options' delta 
positioning turned positive for the first time since mid-September.     
•
Gold has held key technical support at US$3,858/oz, the 38.2% Fibonacci retracement of the 
entire 2015–2026 rise, and, with the market also seen at a 'typical' downside extreme (10% below 
its 200-day average) our bias remains to look for a floor here (p6 & appendix).
•
Despite the closely watched mid-term surprise, expectations for the Fed's policy path remain 
gold's key driver. For instance, last Friday's strong payrolls briefly lifted bets on a December hike 
and weighed on gold but they remain lower than Mid-October. The October US CPI (Tue) is 
therefore a key event this week for Fed expectations ahead of December’s meeting. Consensus 
points to slower core inflation growth (m/m as there is no data for a y/y comparison), which 
would raise the odds of a December ‘hold’ and likely support gold.  
•
Democrats‘ sweep of both chambers gave gold a near-term lift but initial reactions have faded. 
After Trump’s 2016 and 2024 wins, gold‘s early move was largely unwound as attention shifted 
to actual policy. Looking ahead, war funding may become more difficult; tighter AI regulation 
and higher volatility in the sector could continue; the dust is yet to settle, so expect wobbles.

Gold has tested and held what we see as key technical support from the 38.2% retracement of the entire 2015 – 2026 rise at 
US$3,858/oz and with the market also seen at a “typical” downside extreme (10% below its 200-day average) our bias remains to 
try and look for signs of a better floor here. Key risks to this scenario remain a broader “risk off” tone remaining in place, but 
probably most notably further USD strength, with the market seen critically placed at major resistance (see appendix).
Resistance is seen initially at the 38.2% retracement of the August/October decline at US$4,186/oz, above which can see what we 
view as more important resistance at the 55-day average, October high and mid-September low at US$4,226/oz – US$4,236/oz. 
We believe we need to see a close above here to add weight to the scenario a more important low has indeed been secured.
Should the “risk off” tone persist though and a sustained close below US$3,858/oz be seen this would suggest the immediate 
trend is likely to remain lower. Support would then be seen next at US$3,820/oz ahead of the 50% retracement of the 2022/2026 
rise at US$3,605/oz, with key price support seen at US$3,500/oz.

Recap of the week  
US
•
October’s jobs report beat expectations, with non-farm payrolls rising 165k and unemployment 
falling to 4.1% (from 4.2%), while Q3 productivity rose a strong 3.6% annualized, above the 
2.9% forecast.
•
Business activity remained strong in October, with ISM manufacturing PMI rising to 55.5 (from 
54.5), its 10th consecutive month of expansion, while services PMI eased to 54.5 (from 54.9) but 
remained in expansion for the 28th consecutive month.
•
Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the US midterms, reducing 
expectations of further fiscal expansion while raising concerns over greater AI regulatory 
scrutiny.
Europe
•
Eurozone inflation rose to 4.0% y/y in October (from 3.8%), double the ECB’s 2% target, largely 
driven by the surge in energy prices (by 21%)
•
The Bank of England raised rates by 25bp to 4%, its first hike in over three years, as inflation is 
expected to approach 5% by mid-2027.
•
Eurozone business activity moderated in October, with composite PMI falling to 51.7 (from 
53.1) and services to 51.4 (from 53), while manufacturing remained fairly resilient at 52.7 (52.9). 
Meanwhile, retail sales fell 0.7% m/m in September, well below expectations of a 0.1% decline.
Asia
•
China’s business activity weakened in October, with official manufacturing PMI falling to 49.3 
(from 50.1) and non-manufacturing PMI to 49.7 (from 50.2). The RatingDog/S&P Global surveys 
also pointed to softer activity, with manufacturing easing to 52.0 (from 52.1) and services to 
50.8 (from 51.6).
•
Japan’s wage growth strengthened in September, with nominal earnings up 4.6% y/y, the 
fastest pace since 1997, while real wages rose 2.2% y/y, marking the ninth consecutive month 
of growth. Meanwhile, business activity continued to expand in October, with services PMI at 
51.7 (from 51.3) and manufacturing at 53.3 (from 54.1) 
•
India’s business activity remained strong in October, with services PMI strengthening to 55.8 
(from 55.2) while manufacturing moderated slightly to 54.7 (from 55.1).

resistance from the top of its sideways range
12
The Bloomberg DXY (BBDXY) (which has a lower EUR weighting than the “classic” DXY) rose sharply at the end of last week as we saw a “risk off” tone take hold and this is seen to turn the 
spotlight to what we have continually flagged as important resistance from the top of the range at 1229/1231, with the key long-term 200-week average just above at 1233. A sustained hold 
above 1233 would be seen to mark the completion of a large base and change of trend higher for the USD with resistance seen next at 1242 ahead of the 50% retracement of the 2025/2026 
fall at 1249. If a base were to be established, we suspect this would likely act as a major headwind for gold prices. Below near-term support at 1218/17 is seen needed to ease the immediate 
pressure off this key resistance to maintain the broader sideways range with support then seen next at the 55-day average, currently seen placed at 1211.

Glossary of Technical Analysis terms
28

Technical Analysis Glossary
29
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council
Advance/Decline Line 
A popular type of Breadth Indicator (see below) which represents the cumulative number of individual stocks in a broader index that have risen during a session, 
 
against those in the index that have fallen.
Bar chart 
 
A bar chart shows the open, close, low and high of the price of an instrument over a specific time-period. A vertical bar shows the low to high move, with the open 
 
a small horizontal bar to the left of the vertical line and with the close a small horizontal bar to the right.
Bollinger Bands 
Shows bands that represent 2 standard deviations above and below a central move moving average, typically a 20 period average. The bands are expected to 
 
 
typically capture 95% of price action under normal conditions. 
Breath Indicators 
Breath indicators describe a range of indicators that aim to show the internal strength of a specific equity market index (see Advance/Decline line).
Candlestick chart 
A method of representing open/high/low/close data, originally from Japan. The candlestick (or candle) is formed of a rectangle which represents 
 
 
the open to close move, called the real body, with this shaded different colours depending on whether a higher or lower close was seen for the session. The low 
 
and high are shown as vertical lines above and below the real body/rectangle.
Continuation Pattern 
A pattern that indicates a consolidation phase which is a pause within the direction of the current prevailing trend.
Divergence 
 
When two separate measures behave differently. For example, when a new high or low in price is not confirmed/matched by a corresponding new high or low in
 
 
a momentum indicator, hence showing a divergence.
Double Top/Bottom 
A Double Top is a type of Reversal Pattern (see below) formed during an uptrend when two price highs occur at approximately the same level. Completion of the
 
 
pattern is signalled when the “neckline” to the pattern (see below) is broken. A Double Bottom is the exact opposite setup.
Fibonacci retracements 
Horizontal lines that can indicate where support and resistance can potentially be found when a market retraces following a trending move. The percentage 
 
 
value shown is how much of the prior trend the price has retraced. The Fibonacci retracement levels typically shown are 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6%.
Fibonacci projections 
Horizontal lines that can indicate where support and resistance can potentially be found in the direction of the current trend. The percentage values are applied 
 
to the prior trending move, projected off the low/high of the subsequent corrective counter-trend move. The Fibonacci projection levels typically shown are 50%, 
 
61.8%, 100%, 150% and 161.8%.

Technical Analysis Glossary
30
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council
Flag 
 
A Flag pattern in a classic continuation pattern, characterised by a sharp rise or fall (the flagpole) followed by a short-lived counter-trend move (the flag). They are
 
 
expected to be resolved in the direction of the prevailing trend.
Head & Shoulders Top/Bottom A Head & Shoulders price pattern is a classic trend reversal pattern that appears with three peaks, where the outside two are seen closer in height and the
 
 
middle peak is the highest. Completion of the pattern is signalled when the “neckline” to the pattern is broken (see below).
Measured Objective 
Most technical patterns, regardless of whether they are reversal or continuation patterns come with a “measured objective”, which is typically based on the size
 
 
or height of the pattern. The objective is a potential indication of where the price may move to after a pattern has been completed.
Momentum 
 
Momentum is the rate of acceleration or velocity of the underlying instrument/security. It is thus the speed at which the price of the security is changing.
MACD 
 
Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) is a trend-following indicator, often also used as a momentum indicator. It shows the relationship between two
 
 
exponential moving averages of a security’s price, know as the MACD line, with an exponential average then taken off this line (the Signal line).
Moving Average 
A classic statistical moving average of the underlying price data of the security to give a guide to the direction of the prevailing price trend. Different periodicities
 
 
are used to define short-, medium- and long-term trends. Also used to identify potential areas of support and resistance.
Moving Average Envelope 
Shows bands which represent the percentage distance from a selected moving average, which can be used to identify potential support and resistance.
Neckline 
 
A trendline which marks the point where a reversal pattern is confirmed, typically found by connecting the lows/highs of the pattern.
OnBalanceVolume 
A cumulative volume indicator constructed by comparing the amount of volume traded seen on positive sessions to those on negative sessions.
Overbought 
 
An overbought condition occurs when a price rally has extended too far to fast and is seen unlikely to extend further and a pause is likely to be seen.
Oversold 
 
An oversold condition occurs when a price decline has extended too far to fast and is seen unlikely to extend further and a pause is likely to be seen.
Pennant 
 
A Pennant pattern is a type of continuation price pattern, formed when there is a sharp rise or fall (the flagpole), followed by a short consolidation period within
 
 
converging trend lines, similar in shape to a small triangle (the pennant). They are expected to be resolved in the direction of the prevailing trend.
