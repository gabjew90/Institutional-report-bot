Biopharma: More 340B Scrubbing Likely To Come;
Policy Changes Unlikely to Have NT Impact
Our view: We had the chance to speak with an expert in 340B drug pricing on the practices being
implemented by manufacturers to address duplicate discounts and discuss the potential impacts of
proposed policies. Given the consistent growth of the 340B program and lack of transparency around
eligibility for Medicaid rebates, our expert believes companies will continue to invest in data solutions to
combat duplicate discounts, which we think will likely contribute to continued favorable GTN benefits.
That being said, these improvements are likely to be capped by the complexities and investment
required to dispute these claims, though could potentially be addressed through proposed policy
changes, which we believe will be important to watch moving forward especially as we see legislative
turnover. On the whole, we took away that (1) 3Q's seeming outsized benefits may actually be more
of a trend rather than a one-time blip, which could be favorable for sustainability of large biopharma
revenue trajectories; (2) On the flip side, increasing role of 340B means channel mix is becoming more
heavily discounted on the whole, particularly for oncology, immunology, and physician-administered
drugs, and (3) the HRSA pilot impact will be limited in 2027.
Please contact your RBC sales representative for a replay of this event.
We hosted an expert on 340B drug pricing, who previously held roles at Bristol Myers Squibb leading
340B strategy efforts, including duplicate discounts and forecasting, and now has founded a consulting
firm centered around providing strategic advisement on drug pricing policy to biopharmaceutical
companies, trade associations, and advisory consultancies.
340B - a channel where recall drugs are discounted similar to Medicaid - is likely to continue having
an increasing role: When considering the financial impact of 340B pricing for manufacturers, our KOL
noted the most recently released estimates from HRSA is $100B at the discounted price, though this
equates to $240B at WAC, outsizing the impacts of Medicaid and competing with Medicare. The relative
impact of the growing size of the program is dependent on the drug and the prescriber-base, making
it difficult to precisely quantify the impact of 340B, our expert estimates 30-40% volume for physician-
administered drugs, compared to 5% for more commonly used pharmacy drugs.
Though there has been greater focus on 340B scrubbing, or removal of duplicate discounts, 3Q
seems more indicative of its future evolution rather than a blip: The complexities around payment
and reimbursement, including lag times in which 340B status is not known at the time of billing,
have fostered a situation where drugs are frequently discounted through channels like Medicaid and
then rebated again, inappropriately, under 340B. Despite recent focus on scrubbing practices having
seemingly benefited large biopharmas disproportionately during 3Q26 earnings, our KOL notes that
manufacturers have always been trying to remove these duplicate discounts and that there is not any
one new trend that has suddenly increased this. She believes that with more and more different discount
types come into play (IRA, Medicare inflation rebates, discretionary rebates) and the growing impact,
this is just creating increasing incentive to address the issue. Our expert believes this trend is likely to
continue, given that there are no signs of slowing growth in the 340B program (20% growth y-o-y for
the past 6 years).

Identifying and recouping the duplicated discounts is still challenging for drugmakers, though
there still may be more room for companies, particularly larger, more well-resourced ones,
to benefit: Our expert notes large and ongoing investments by manufacturers to identify and
dispute duplicate discounts, with the larger players focusing on improving their data collection
policies likely recouping ~65-75% of these claims. Our sense from the expert was that there may
still be some room for further growth. However, this sounds very unlikely to get near 100% - our
KOL sees a likely ceiling in the absence of policy change, with additional complex barriers that
limits the ability to dispute claims even once identified, including provisions in PBM contracts
capping the disputes and the need for a third-party auditor when challenging covered entities.
Additionally, smaller manufacturers with less sophisticated resources are likely only recovering
~30%.
New legislative proposals are unlikely to have a major near-term impact: SECURE and SUSTAIN
- two policies being proposed by the House and Senate, respectively - though meant to replace
the HRSA Rebate Model set to begin Jan. 2027, our expert sees these programs as lacking in
their capacity to enforce data submissions by covered entities, and even if passed, unlikely to
have real impact for several years while manufacturers are still left to address 340B as they enter
MFP pricing.
Oncology and immunology have the highest 340B exposure. Our expert noted that different
therapeutic categories have different rates of 340B exposure on average, suggesting that
oncology and immunology tend to be most affected, followed by categories such as mental
health. On one hand, these companies may be subject to less favorable dynamics right now,
but as companies continue to invest in sourcing and scrubbing data, there could be more
room to improve G/N from current levels. Additionally, our KOL thought that CAR-T therapies
currently have relatively low 340B exposure, but this could become an increasing dynamic as
these treatments move more into the out-pt setting.
The HRSA pilot is expected to launch on January 1st, 2027, with 21 IRA-selected drugs from the
2026 and 2027 cohorts. The goal of the pilot is to streamline the rebate process. Manufacturers
will honor the 340B pricing as a rebate, and they will receive the required data upfront, rather
than after the fact, which is how it is done now. Manufacturers will still be running their own
data collection policies because this pilot does not help address duplicate discount exposure in
other channels. When asked, our KOL did not quantify the potential financial impact on the pilot,
noting that it was "too narrow" and that each drug in the pilot has a different channel mix. With
each company keeping their internal data collection infrastructure for the 340B program, our
KOL thinks the impact will be quite muted.
