Longevity weekly pulse #81: US midterm 
elections' impact on the health care sector
Transformational Innovation Opportunities (TRIO): Longevity
 
Authors: Eric Potoker, CIO Equity Strategist, US Health care, UBS Financial Services Inc. (UBS FS); Diana Na, Healthcare Sector Equity Analyst, UBS AG 
London Branch; Thomas Parmentier, CIO Equity Sector Strategist - Consumers, UBS Switzerland AG
• Democratic sweep brings greater longer-term 
policy uncertainty: The magnitude of Democratic gains 
could bring health care policy back into focus heading 
into the 2028 election, with some potential impact on 
investor sentiment.
• Subsector implications: Pharma and managed care 
may be relatively more sensitive to policy developments, 
while medtech and life sciences tools appear better 
positioned, with providers also potentially benefiting 
from coverage expansion.
• Near-term 
fundamentals 
largely 
unchanged:
Overall, we see limited near-term health care policy 
impact from the 2026 midterms, with gridlock likely 
prevailing through 2028.

Thoughts of the week
The overall outcome of the 2026 US midterm elections was 
broadly in line with market expectations, with Democrats 
gaining control over both the House of Representatives 
(House) and the Senate. The magnitude of the Democratic 
victory, however, exceeded expectations and was likely 
at the far end of the scenarios envisioned by investors 
before Election Day. While we continue to think that the 
Democratic congressional majorities will be insufficient to 
drive significant health care policy changes in 2027-28, 
we think the magnitude of the Democratic victory will 
eventually increase concerns about the impact of the 
2028 presidential election on health policy and health care 
company valuations.
Democrats have secured 54 Senate seats (52 Democrats 
plus two independents who caucus with Democrats). In 
particular, Democratic Senate victories in Texas, South 
Carolina, and Kansas are noteworthy: Democrats had not 
won a Senate election in any of those states for multiple 
decades until this week. Victories in these states raise the 
prospect of a major political shift toward Democrats that 
could continue into the 2028 election cycle, which would 
increase the probability of more significant health care 
policy changes after the 2028 election. Increased policy 
uncertainty would very likely be negative for health care 
investor sentiment and valuations.
As noted above, we doubt that significant changes in 
health care policy will occur in the next Congress. We 
think President Trump would likely veto any major health 
care legislation sponsored by congressional Democrats. 
Nonetheless, the size of the Democratic victory increases 
the likelihood of some modest expansion of government-
sponsored health insurance coverage either in the upcoming 
lame-duck session of Congress or in the next Congress. We 
see the potential for some Medicaid relief, which would be 
a modest positive for the health care sector overall.
Nonetheless, policy gridlock is the most likely outcome 
in the next Congress, and we think investors expected 
that outcome going into this election. To the extent that 
the magnitude of the Democratic victory increases investor 
concerns about health care policy surrounding the 2028 
election, we think investor sentiment could worsen. At 
a minimum, we think the direction of investor sentiment 
toward health care policy risks is negatively skewed, mainly

To the extent that health care policy fears increase, we 
think the pharmaceutical and managed care sectors are 
vulnerable. We think Democrats will likely raise the prospect 
of major health insurance reform, including Medicare for All. 
While we think the likelihood of major insurance reform is 
low, increased uncertainty could weigh on managed care 
stocks. We also think Democrats will focus on US drug 
pricing regulation, as they have in the past, which would 
weigh on sentiment toward pharmaceutical stocks.
Medical device and life sciences tools (LST) stocks have 
often been favored by investors during prior periods of 
health care policy uncertainty. We would expect a similar 
relative preference for these two subsectors if health care 
policy fears become more prominent over the next two 
years. We also note that health care providers, including 
hospital management companies, typically perform well 
when policymakers pursue expanded health insurance 
coverage. Any near-term Medicaid expansion, or reduction 
in upcoming cuts, would also be positive for near-term 
company outlooks, particularly for health care providers and 
medical device companies that sell to hospitals.
As noted above, we think gridlock on health policy will 
prevail through 2028, which would mean that Democratic 
congressional control will not have a significant impact on 
health care company growth outlooks over that period. In 
addition, the outcome of the 2028 election remains distant 
and uncertain and may not factor prominently in investors’ 
assessments of health care stocks in the near term. However, 
as the 2028 election approaches and potential candidates 
and policy platforms become clearer, we think health care 
valuations could become more vulnerable.

AIA Group
Risks include adverse government regulations across its key markets in Asia, a prolonged low interest rate environment, and disruption from non-
traditional businesses.
Abbott Laboratories
Risks to our thesis include a sustained slowing in ABT’s end markets and competitive pressures on ABT’s market share. We note the litigation 
related to ABT's infant nutritional products and the initial related negative impact on ABT's market value. While this litigation is ongoing, we 
believe the liability will ultimately be manageable for ABT. ABT faces similar ESG issues to other medical device companies, including product 
liability risk. We believe ABT maintains the systems and protocols to adequately manage ESG issues.
AbbVie
Risks to our thesis include market share losses in ABBV’s core franchises and drug pipeline failures. ABBV faces similar ESG issues to its 
pharmaceutical peers, most notably product liability risk and a societal focus on drug pricing and access. We believe ABBV has the systems and 
protocols in place to manage these and other ESG issues.
Alcon
Key risks include failure of pipeline products; new or tougher competition; legal threats including product liability cases; access, reimbursement 
and pricing challenges; a shifting political and regulatory environment; currency and interest rates. Alcon-specific risks include inability to trade 
patients up to multifocal intra-ocular lenses and further intangible asset impairments.
AstraZeneca
 Key risks facing biopharma companies include access, reimbursement and pricing challenges, new competition including generic drugs, legal 
threats including product liability cases, a shifting political and regulatory environment, failure of pipeline products, currency, and interest rates. 
AstraZeneca-specific risks include: 1) a slowdown in the Chinese business (13% of revenues) driven by government investigations; 2) greater 
impact of US Part D redesign and IRA vs. peers given its US oral oncology portfolio (Calquence, Lynparza, Tagrisso); 3) more onerous volume-
based procurement and related sales pressure; 4) new product launches particularly in oncology could also miss market expectations, driven by 
increased competition; 5) and greater-than-expected sales erosion following patent expirations.
Boston Scientific Corp.
 We think recent growth deceleration in certain key cardiology products has negatively affected sentiment, but that BSX's growth should 
stabilize over the next few quarters and its valuation should recover. From an ESG perspective, BSX faces similar issues to other medical device 
companies, including product liability and litigation risk. BSX has experienced material litigation liabilities in its recent past, which remains a key 
risk.
DBS Group Holdings
Key risks are a prolonged economic recession, higher-than-expected credit costs, a sharper-than-expected decline in interest rates, and asset 
quality deterioration.
Danaher Corporation
Risks to our thesis include potential budget pressure on governments and academic medical research spending, global trade tensions, and a 
potential decline in global pharma/biotech R&D spending. DHR faces similar ESG-related issues as other life sciences/tools companies, with 
modest product liability risks. We believe that DHR's emphasis on quality through its DBS culture enables the company to manage these issues.
DexCom Inc.
Risks to our thesis include reimbursement pressure and the eventual emergence of additional competition and/or alternative technologies. DXCM 
faces similar ESG issues to other medical device companies, notably product quality and access.
Eli Lilly & Co.
We think the main risks for LLY are pipeline failures. While we view gradual price erosion as in line with forecasts, more severe pricing pressure is 
a potential risk. LLY also faces various industry risks, including potential onerous regulatory/ legislative changes. LLY faces similar ESG risks to its 
biopharma peers, in particular potential product safety and access concerns.
Galderma Group AG
Key risks facing biopharma companies include access, reimbursement and pricing challenges, new competition including generic drugs, legal 
threats including product liability cases, a shifting political and regulatory environment, failure of pipeline products, currency, and interest rates. 
Galderma-specific risks include: 1) slower-than-expected launch of Nemluvio given US payer coverage hurdles from leading players in atopic 
dermatitis, and 2) an economic slowdown or out-of-pocket consumer pressures could also see a slowdown in the self-pay markets.
IQVIA Holdings Inc
The main risks to IQV are slowing R&D spending and onerous regulations. Key risks facing all healthcare companies include: failure of pipeline 
products; new competition; legal threats including product liability cases; access, reimbursement and pricing challenges; a shifting political and 
regulatory environment; and FX and interest rates. Slower pharma/biotech R&D spending and emerging competition in Asia are additional risks 
for IQVIA.
Innovent Biologics
Key risks include potentially worse-than-expected commercialization of key drugs.
Intuitive Surgical
Volatility in surgical procedure volumes could affect growth. In addition, hospital capital budgetary pressure could affect the short-term demand 
for ISRG's robots. Additionally, we note that surgical robots are a critical competitive differentiator for hospital systems, and we view any 
pressure on hospital capital budgets as temporary, likely resulting in negligible impact on ISRG's long-term growth.  ISRG is subject to the typical 
ESG issues facing medical technology companies, including potential product liability and litigation risk.
Johnson & Johnson
Risks to our thesis include pipeline failures, increasing policy risks, and larger-than-expected litigation liabilities, including from JNJ's ongoing talc 
litigation. JNJ's main ESG issue has been product litigation, specifically around its opioid and talc products. We acknowledge that JNJ has been 
more exposed to high-profile product liability cases than many of its peers in recent years. We believe these cases have informed JNJ's overall 
ESG approach in recent years and that JNJ is implementing systems to manage these and other ESG issues.
L'Oréal
Key risks: Unfavorable economic developments in key markets, weaker consumer confidence, rising competition, increased promotional intensity, 
unsuccessful innovation and M&A, changing beauty trends, and unfavorable FX effects.
Life Time Group
Risks to the thesis are fitness industry trends changing, fitness club competition, access to good quality real estate for growth, and a macro 
downturn.
Merck & Co.
Risks to our thesis include drug pipeline failures and potential onerous drug pricing legislation in the US. From an ESG perspective, MRK faces 
similar product liability and access risks faced by the entire pharmaceutical industry. We believe MRK maintains adequate systems and 
procedures to manage these and other ESG issues.
NN Group NV
Key risks include lower interest rates, large falls in equity markets, bonds or private markets, adverse claim experience in both the life and P&C 
divisions, adverse regulatory changes, and unit-linked litigations, adverse development in real estate, and increases in mortgage spreads, lower 
shareholder return due to lower capital ratios.

Risks include possible negative consumer spending trends (about 70% of revenues are from out-of-pocket spend), supply chain issues, 
competition from over-the-counter hearing aids or standard headphones, global trade restrictions, and currency losses due to its cost-overhang 
in CHF.
Stryker Corporation
Key risks include reimbursement challenges and competitive threats that could hurt its growth. In addition and similar to its peers, SYK faces 
potential litigation risk. Similar to its medical device peers, SYK faces potential product liability litigation risk. Beyond that, SYK faces ESG risks 
similar to other health care product companies. We believe SYK has the resources and processes to manage ESG dynamics.
Thermo Fisher Scientific
From an ESG perspective, we believe TMO faces similar issues to other LST companies, and lower product liability risk than medical device and 
biopharmaceutical companies, since most of TMO's products are used in research and not implanted into or ingested by humans. We believe 
TMO has the systems to effectively manage ESG issues.
UnitedHealth Group Inc.
As a leading health insurer, UNH faces significant regulatory oversight. In addition, UNH must manage the ongoing societal and media focus on 
affordable access to health care. Other risks to our view include higher medical costs; continued volatility in UNH's OptumHealth (OH) business, 
particularly its value-based care (VBC) assets; increased government regulation; and government reimbursement pressure.
Ventas Inc.
Risks include fluctuating rates, operating costs, rising new capacity, and a COVID resurgence.
Viking Holdings Ltd
Risks include a macro downturn, geopolitical/terrorism events, health concerns.
Wuxi Biologics
Key risks include geopolitical tensions leading to new international sanctions on the sector.
