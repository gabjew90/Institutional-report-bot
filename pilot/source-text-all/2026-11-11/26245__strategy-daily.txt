US CPI takes centre stage today, with headline and core expected at 0.3% and 0.2% MoM, respectively. 
That would be a decent moderation from September, particularly for headline as fuel prices stabilised, while core 
is expected to slow more broadly after last month’s strength. Tech-related goods, rents and the six-monthly health 
insurance reset are worth watching. With around 80% of a 25bp December hike priced, and another hike only 
priced further into H2-27, we still think the asymmetry sits with a stronger print. A 0.3%+ core number would likely 
put renewed pressure on the front end and bring 4.90% in 10Y quickly back into view, particularly with $42bn 10Y
UST supply later today. That comes after USTs struggled against yesterday’s sharp recovery in equities, 
with 2Y yields reaching 4.56%, around 11bp above Friday’s pre-NFP lows. The long end held in somewhat 
better as oil fell, leaving the curve flatter. The DXY continued falling despite the more hawkish rates repricing and 
is now moving towards 101.50. Brent has since extended the move towards $91.5pb, helped by Donald 
Trump signalling that further US strikes on Iran are unlikely for now and reports of potential progress on 
an agreement. But with no formal deal confirmed and disruption around Hormuz not fully resolved, we would be 
wary of chasing yesterday’s duration move purely on the back of cheaper oil. For today, CPI should take back 
control of the rates narrative. 
EUR rates were largely passengers to the oil move yesterday, with 10Y Bund yields briefly falling towards 
3.22% before reversing as equities strengthened. Lower oil and better risk sentiment also saw BTP-Bund 
tighten around 3bp and OAT-Bund around 2-3bp, exactly the risk-on relationship we would expect to persist if the 
Iran headlines continue to improve. EUR/USD has also extended its recovery and trades around 1.1240. For 
today, US CPI probably matters more for outright Bund direction than the European calendar (plenty of 
ECB speakers are scheduled). Lower energy prices remain supportive for duration, but with risk sentiment 
holding up and US front-end pricing becoming more hawkish since pre-NFP, we struggle to see a clean Bund rally 
unless CPI delivers some relief. On the curve, the recent flattening towards 20bp in 2s10s continues to make us 
more interested in steepeners, particularly against the much heavier German supply backdrop next year and our 
view that the ECB will end up delivering less tightening than what the market is currently expecting (+50bp into 
H2-27). Supply is also relevant today, with an €8-9bn EU 5Y and 20Y syndication alongside €3bn of 10Y DSLs. 
GBP rates were again showing their higher-beta nature yesterday, outperforming both USD and EUR rates
as global yields moved lower. The rally was particularly strong further out the curve, with 10Y Gilt yields falling 
to 5.08%, the lowest since late August, while 30Y yields briefly traded around 5.54%, almost 45bp below the 6% 
levels tested just a month ago. The 10Y swap rate also pushed below its 100-day moving average around 4.70%.
GBP/USD has extended the broader dollar-led rally and is now around 1.3460. We would still be a little cautious 
chasing the move from here. SONIA prices roughly a 50% chance of a December hike, with two hikes fully priced 
by April, leaving the front end sensitive to energy prices and incoming data. Lower oil clearly helps at the margin, 
but the speed of the recent Gilt rally means current levels already reflect a decent amount of good news. Today 
brings £1bn of 2056 gilt supply, alongside Breeden, Lombardelli and Pill, but US CPI should ultimately set 
the direction for global duration. 
Risk-on session in Asian equities, driven by the positive tone in US equities on Monday. In Japan, JGBs
bear-steepened following a weak 30Y JGB auction, mixed BoJ summary of opinions and weak household spending 
data. USD/JPY remained in a tight range around 158.
