Market comment 
Brent crude oil sank $4.60 to its lowest price since September, at $93.70/bbl on renewed hopes of 
world peace, as Trump announced over the weekend that it’s “highly unlikely” that we will order 
extensive strikes on Iran, assuming Iran signs a “very strong agreement.” The definition of what 
constitutes a “very strong agreement” may be flexible, depending on how the Trump 
Administration plans to define “winning” in the Middle East, after facing defeat in the US Midterm 
elections.  
But Hegseth is still coming under fire—not from the blue tsunami sweeping through DC in 
January, but rather from Republican Representative Thomas Massie, who has filed to impeach 
Hegseth. Massie wrote on X yesterday: “I’ve seen some of my colleagues say @SecWar Hegseth 
has been effective and they agree with the war, so they’ll vote not to impeach. That’s not the 
questions though. The question is whether he committed impeachable acts, and the answer is yes, 
eight times:” which, according to the articles, include starting the Iran war without congressional 
authorization, continuing the Iran war after Congress ordered US forces removed, extrajudicial 
killings, and more. 
A few Fed speakers crawled out of the woodwork yesterday to opine on the inflationary 
environment in the US, including alternate voter Barkin, from the Richmond Fed, and non-voter 
Goolsbee from Chicago. Rhetoric from both broadly aligned with the now more hawkish lilt across 
the FOMC, citing the persistence of core CPI inflation, especially in services, and the justification 
for future hikes. That said, the dot plot from the September SEP (which is noticeably missing a dot 
from Fed chair Warsh) projects one more Fed hike, followed by a hold throughout 2027, even as 
many FOMC board members project a second hike in 2027.  
After collapsing 4.4% in one day last week following the US Midterm elections, the S&P 500 is 
starting to show signs of recovery, having moved 3% higher, back to pre-November 4 levels. 
Despite the unfortunate headlines surrounding Jensen Huang, the Nasdaq also made a strong 
recovery, having moved 5% higher over the past two sessions, supported by the Philadelphia 
Semiconductor Index, which managed to work its way more than 11% higher. But the risks facing 
the US AI infrastructure buildout remain. 
The Democratic victory cements additional difficulties for the buildout, but many Republican 
lawmakers are also joining the opposition to data centres. A different midterm outcome would 
not have necessarily permitted the degree of investment that some AI bulls are hoping for, even if 
markets might have been more reluctant to price that in. Communities remain reluctant to 
welcome data centres without clear assurances of the benefits, and moratoriums continue to crop 
up, further complicating the US effort to win the AI war against China., But what does “winning” 
even mean? 
For the US and China, "winning" may look different. The US maintains a lead in frontier models 
and compute, while China benefits from a follower advantage; adapting successful approaches 
rather than funding all the experimentation needed to discover them means it may not need to 
match the US compute buildout to capture substantial benefits.

Day ahead 
The good fortune in equity markets translated little into US yields, which traded in a bear-
flattening fashion with the 2-year trailing 5bp higher on the day, and the 10-year up only around 
1.6bp as we head into October US CPI inflation today. Bloomberg-surveyed analysts are
