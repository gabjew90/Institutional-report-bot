2 November 2026 │ US 
Other New Materials & Processing 
 
Cameco reported 3Q26 revenue of CAD619mn, +1% YoY, attributable NP of 
CAD43mn, and adjusted EBITDA of CAD267mn, -14% YoY. Earnings were 
pressured by lower production and higher unit costs following scheduled 
maintenance at the Key Lake mill, alongside lower uranium sales volumes. As a 
leading integrated player in North America's uranium value chain, Cameco could 
benefit from robust global nuclear construction, the restructuring of Western 
nuclear fuel supply chains, and renewed demand for US domestic uranium supply. 
Progress on large-scale new nuclear projects in the US also creates medium- to 
long-term growth opportunities for Westinghouse's AP1000 business. We derive a 
target price of USD84 and maintain OVERWEIGHT. 
 
Core operations: Lower volumes & higher costs weigh on earnings 
By segment: 1) Uranium: 3Q26 revenue declined 1% YoY to CAD518mn, as lower 
sales volumes offset higher uranium prices. Sales volumes fell 8% YoY to 
5.6mn lb, while the average realized price rose 6% YoY to USD65.78/lb, 
equivalent to CAD91.76/lb, +8% YoY. Extended scheduled maintenance at the 
Key Lake mill and inflation pushed the average unit cost of sales up 21% YoY to 
CAD72.17/lb. Attributable uranium production fell 20% YoY to 3.5mn lb, including 
1.1mn lb from McArthur River/Key Lake, -50% YoY, mainly reflecting infrastructure 
upgrades and maintenance at the Key Lake mill. 2) Fuel services: 3Q26 revenue 
rose 4% YoY to CAD95mn. Sales volume increased 16% YoY to 2.2mn kgU, while 
the average realized price fell 13% YoY to CAD42.90/kgU, reflecting changes to 
the product mix. The average unit cost of sales fell 7% YoY to CAD38.57/kgU. 
 
Cameco narrowed its 2026 attributable uranium production guidance to 
19.5-20.5mn lb from 19.5-21.5mn lb and its sales volume guidance to 29.5-30.5mn 
lb from 29-32mn lb. This implies 4Q26 deliveries of 9.0-10.0mn lb (versus 5.6mn lb 
in 3Q26), pointing to concentrated deliveries in the fourth quarter. Reflecting lower 
production and inflation, the company raised its uranium unit cost of sales 
guidance to CAD67.0-70.0/lb (previously CAD63.0-67.5/lb). 
 
Westinghouse: Earnings rise YoY; AP1000 rollout accelerates 
Cameco's share of Westinghouse's adjusted EBITDA rose 15% YoY to 
CAD143mn in 3Q26, driven by higher nuclear fuel sales volume in its core 
business. The company maintained its 2026 guidance for its share of 
Westinghouse's adjusted EBITDA at USD370-430mn. Westinghouse's global 
pipeline of potential AP1000 projects remained at 91 units, totaling approximately 
105GW. The US Department of Energy loan framework and Department of 
Commerce cooperation framework each cover up to 10 units, while another five 
units have entered long-lead equipment procurement or the EPC stage. The 
company reiterated the long-term economics of AP1000 projects: near-term 
two-unit projects are expected to cost USD20-26bn in total, potentially falling to 
USD14-17bn once standardized builds are replicated at scale. Westinghouse can 
capture 40-45% of total project value, with a normalized adjusted EBITDA margin 
of approximately 20%. Once completed, each two-unit project can also generate 
approximately USD45-60mn in average annual revenue for Westinghouse from 
long-term fuel, maintenance, and operating services, while driving demand for 
Cameco's uranium, conversion, and future enrichment businesses.

Cameco (CCJ US) 
 
We believe the tight global uranium supply-demand dynamic and robust nuclear 
construction activity enhance the core uranium business's exposure to rising prices and 
long-term visibility for Westinghouse's new-build business. In uranium, resource depletion at 
existing mines, declining secondary supply, delayed start-ups of new mines, and a structural 
supply-demand deficit could drive prices higher. Recent small-volume contracts have 
inflation-adjusted price floors in the mid- to high-USD70s/lb range and ceilings reaching USD160/lb, 
with some fixed base prices already in triple digits. For Westinghouse, the US and South Korean 
governments announced a non-binding investment framework of up to USD120bn in September 
2026 to support the construction of up to eight large nuclear units in the US, including six 
Westinghouse AP1000s and two South Korean APR1400s. The two sides expect to arrange 
approximately USD10bn in advance payments by end-2026, including around USD7.5bn for 
AP1000 long-lead equipment procurement. The US Department of Energy previously proposed a 
conditional loan commitment of USD17.5bn to support advance procurement of long-lead 
equipment for up to 10 AP1000 units. This could enable supply-chain orders ahead of final 
investment decisions (FID), easing equipment lead-time constraints on large-scale nuclear 
construction. The AP1000 has a completed design and a commercial operating track record. 
Westinghouse expects to capture 40-45% of total project value, with an adjusted EBITDA margin of 
approximately 20% over the project lifecycle, enhancing its long-term earnings potential. Our 
valuation reflects expected 2027 earnings growth in the core uranium business and Westinghouse, 
as detailed below. 
 
1. 
Core uranium business: We value the business at USD15.2bn in equity value, based on 22x 
2027E PE. We forecast 2027 attributable NP of USD701mn, primarily based on assumptions 
for uranium and fuel services sales volumes, average realized prices, purchase costs, and 
exchange rates. According to Visible Alpha, peers including PDN, BOE, KAP, and CGN Mining 
trade at an average of 16.8x 2027E PE. We primarily benchmark against CGN Mining, 
reflecting comparability in size and liquidity. CGN Mining trades at 15.0x 2027E PE, while the 
New York Stock Exchange, where Cameco is listed, trades at an average PE of 19.50x for the 
same period, a premium to the Hang Seng Composite Index's 11.00x. Reflecting these market 
differences and Cameco's presence across the value chain, we apply a 45% valuation 
premium to its core uranium business, implying 22x 2027E PE. 
 
2. 
Westinghouse: We value the business at USD21.3bn in equity value, based on 45x 2027E 
EV/EBITDA. We forecast 2027 EBITDA of USD473mn, primarily referencing company 
guidance and EBITDA from the existing operating reactor fleet. Peers BWX Technologies, GE 
Vernova, and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries trade at an average of 17.3x 2027E EV/EBITDA. 
Westinghouse's global pipeline of 91 potential AP1000 units, US-South Korea cooperation, the 
US Department of Energy's USD17.5bn loan for long-lead equipment, and the Department of 
Commerce's USD80bn cooperation framework support growth in its new-build business. With 
the AP1000 also being the only mature US third-generation nuclear technology, Westinghouse 
offers stronger nuclear business growth potential. We therefore assign 45x 2027E EV/EBITDA. 
 
Our valuation implies a target equity value of USD36.5bn and a target price of USD84.

Cameco (CCJ US) 
Risks 
Weaker-than-expected nuclear power investment. Nuclear projects require substantial 
investment and have long construction cycles, with progress influenced by financing costs, 
approvals, and electricity demand. Slower-than-expected investment in new nuclear capacity and 
operating-life extensions for existing reactors could weigh on uranium fuel demand growth and 
Westinghouse's nuclear equipment order conversion, in turn affecting Cameco's earnings growth. 
 
Slower-than-expected capacity ramp-ups. Uranium mining and processing involve complex 
production processes and equipment maintenance. Slower-than-expected production recovery or 
capacity expansion at the company's mines and processing facilities, or disruptions from equipment 
failures, labor shortages, and rising operating costs, could affect uranium output and delivery 
schedules. 
 
Policy uncertainty. Nuclear industry development is highly sensitive to energy policy, nuclear 
safety regulation, and international trade policy. Changes to nuclear development plans in major 
countries, tighter project approval and safety requirements, or uranium fuel import/export 
restrictions and trade barriers could affect nuclear construction schedules, cross-border uranium 
fuel supply, and the company's global operations.
