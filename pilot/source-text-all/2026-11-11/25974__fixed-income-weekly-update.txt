What happened last week?
U.S. Treasuries extended their rally for a fifth consecutive week, with yields declining across the 
curve. The week began with modest gains as falling oil prices and weakness in stocks supported 
demand, before stronger economic data triggered a brief pullback. The rally gained momentum 
midweek as crude oil plunged amid hopes for a quicker resolution to the Iran conflict following 
the U.S. midterm elections, while a sharp selloff in AI-related equities fueled safe-haven buying. 
Economic data were mixed, with rising job openings and exceptionally low initial claims 
signaling labor-market resilience, while stronger productivity and declining unit labor costs 
eased inflation concerns. However, Friday's stronger-than-expected payroll report reinforced 
expectations for a December Fed rate hike, pressuring shorter maturities. Despite Friday's losses, 
the 2yr yield fell 7.6 bp to 4.51% and the 10yr declined 7.2 bp to 4.86% for the week.

Multifamily Delinquencies Surge: Multifamily
delinquencies surged 124 bp to 6.61% in October (Chart 1).
The increase was driven by nonperforming matured loans, 
which  jumped  133  bp  to  3.44%.  Other  delinquency 
categories  were  mixed  but  declined  slightly  overall, 
suggesting the deterioration is concentrated in unresolved 
loan maturities and refinancing challenges rather than a 
broad increase in newly missed payments. For lenders, rising 
maturity-related distress highlights persistent refinancing 
pressures and potential credit losses, even as early-stage 
payment delinquencies remain relatively contained.
