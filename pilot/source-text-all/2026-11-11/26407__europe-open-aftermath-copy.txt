Europe Open - Aftermath
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
US bonds on Tue slid after Fed Waller indicated chance of 50bp hike, with a hot CPI print and more Fedspeak (Logan,
Goolsbee, Paulson) indicated further tightening ahead. Goolsbee called the inflation report "borderline disturbing",
though Paulson still supported gradual tightening. While US bond markets are closed today, negative price action spilled
into Asia regional with JGBs -10bp. US equity future rally picked up with Nasdaq trading 1.5% higher, positive sentiment
across Asia too. FX price action modest but USD holds onto its gains. Oil prices modestly higher with trilateral talks with
US-Russia-Ukraine continuing for the second day. It's an extremely quiet day ahead with no catalysts scheduled. South
Korea 10d exports printed at a whopping 111% YoY, with KRW best performer overnight.

In Focus‌
Rates: US bond markets are closed but the move on Tuesday following hawkish Fedspeak and US CPI has spilled into
overnight bonds bonds. To recap, Waller set the slide after he put a 50bp hike on the table should core inflation print at 0.2%.
With core coming at an unrounded 0.296% MoM, Logan added further fuel saying it would make sense to pause after two
more increases. Goolsbee said that the Oct report was "borderline disturbing", and that the committee must continue to
address elevated inflation. Paulson added that gradual tightening seems appropriate for now, citing limited wage pressures
and inflation expectations consistent with the 2% Fed target. Dec OIS pricing now stands at +28.5bp, with PPI on Thursday.
Post latest moves, senior desk analyst David Bieber writes that USTs have moved tactically long with risks concentrated close
to current prices, but structurally still short. Bias remains to higher yield with next support at 106-00 / 107-00 in 10y TYs.
Equities: US stocks were unable to make up their mind trading extremely choppy post CPI, but ended up closing slightly
negative. However generally positive sentiment across Asia stocks with US equity futures jumping higher, Nasdaq up 1.5%
with strong CoreWeave revenue guidance helping. New Regime Model Update shows Equities move neutral with the US now
the largest underweight and duration stays overweight via JGBs and Gilts.Systematic strategies remain positive year-to-date
despite a consolidating month, as stable cross-asset carry and a modest uptick in value investing helped offset broad trend-
following losses.

FX: USD rally continues to hold - while we continued to see leveraged USD buying in flows, overall interbank volumes during
the Asia session were 21% lower than 30d averages. G10 realized vols outperformed the low implied event jump of 25bp but
apart from AUD implied vols that picked up fresh franchise demand as a risk aversion hedge, rest of G10 implied vols were
barely repriced higher overnight. We like owning AUD and NZD front end skews with 1m 25d RR at 1.25v for USD call. We
expect the Dec FOMC implied event jump premium to trade in demand at current pricing at around 50bp event jump. For
spot, our Asia trader remains short AUD, expressed against USD and JPY. 
Commodities: Trilateral US-Russia-Ukraine talks continue today. Ukraine regards the talks as constructive so far, according
to a person familiar with the discussions, asking not to be identified because the matter is sensitive (Bloomberg), but no
comments from Russia yet. Meanwhile, Iran says that an oil tanker caught fire in the east of Hormuz, once again highlighting
two-sided risks to the bearish oil case. We discussed further here. 
Elsewhere‌
KRW: 10d trade data saw exports surge towards 111.9% YoY with imports dropping to 45%. KRW was top performer
overnight making highs of 1333 against USD. Custody USD buying flows were muted as foreigners are no longer net sellers
in the KOSPI. Additionally, exporters routinely unloaded USD in the market amid subdued client flows on our end. Offshore
players are largely neutral today.
BRL was among worst performers on Tuesday, closing -1.26%. The move was partly attributed to more dovish BCB minutes,
but year-end trends also skew bearish. On average, BRL tends to underperform in Nov/Dec, with the dispersion of outcomes
in November and early Dec is high
