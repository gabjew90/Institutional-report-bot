Crowding Data
What is Crowding?
UBS comprehensive crowding factor measures the breadth of active investors 
positioning on the long and short side. We calculate the main Crowding signal by 
combining several proprietary datasets. Underlying components are z-scored and 
combined with static weights to produce a composite score within the range -30 
(most short crowded) to +30 (most long crowded). Crowding is available at a daily 
frequency covering over 10,000 global stocks with history back to 2017.
The UBS Quantitative Research team has created a composite crowding factor, 
based on a proprietary combination of multiple data sets. Crowding is too many of 
the same type of market participants holding the same or similar positions, in this 
case, a stock. Crowding can occur on the long and the short side of names.
A crowding score serves as a risk control indicator. A high crowding score in either 
direction could pose a risk as there is a high chance investor positioning starts to go 
in the opposite direction. We note the crowding scores have no imposed maximum 
or minimum values, but the range tends to be from "-30" to "+30" due to the 
scaling and combination of inputs. In other words, the higher the score on the 
positive side implies the stock is more long crowded. Conversely, the lower on the 
negative side implies the stock is more crowded on the short side. Please get in 
touch with ubs-quantanswers@ubs.com for more information.
For additional information, please refer to the following note: A Comprehensive 
Crowding Factor.

Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Our valuation methodology for the Alternative Energy space is based on a variety of metrics 
including P/BV, EV/EBITDA, P/E, and retained equity value per share. Our target multiples are 
derived from historical group averages with adjustments for expected growth rates, leverage, 
and earnings growth confidence. We identify the following risks for the sector: Demand for 
new renewable installations has historically been volatile around changes in state and federal 
policy, and we expect this volatility to continue. In addition, margins for product 
manufacturers can be impacted by swings in raw materials pricing and the overall level of 
energy prices.
EVgo: Our valuation reflects LS Power’s proposed take-private offer, which we believe is likely 
to be completed at the stated consideration. Downside risks include the proposal being 
modified or withdrawn or the transaction failing to close, as well as potential EV subsidy 
rollbacks, prolonged or worse-than-expected cash burn, potential conversion of Class B 
shares, and potentially significant cash payments under the Tax Receivable Agreement.
