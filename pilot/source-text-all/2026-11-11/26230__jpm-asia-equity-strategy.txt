Price action in recent months has become increasingly dominated by flows and 
positioning shifts with, additionally, a very negative skew in reactions to earnings/
newsflow (very “late cycle”). We believe this is largely due to the uncertainty 
around long-term fundamental outcomes in AI stocks (which is also driving, in 
inverse, the fortunes of non-AI stocks). This dynamic manifested itself in intense 
volatility last week following the US mid-term elections as AI stocks / 
Momentum factor / Large-caps / Tech sector saw an acceleration of the 
drawdown that was already in motion since early October. The market effectively 
read the election results as (1) lower geopolitical risk in relation to Iran, (2) greater 
fiscal discipline, and (3) constraints on data center buildout. Thus, despite the 
moderation in macro risks (rates, oil, dollar) and culmination of mid-term election 
uncertainty (which we expected would pave the path for a year-end rally), the latter 
interpretation drove AI stocks lower. AI stocks and Asian equity indices have now 
broken below key technical levels - which threatens some knock-on volatility as 
a variety of market participants re-adjust. So, even as the market looks oversold and 
positioning has lightened, and we remain thematically bullish on AI, it may be 
preferable for now to keep a diversified portfolio that is exposed both to a 
potential (sharp) recovery in AI stocks (Figure 21) and also exposed to 
uncorrelated emerging opportunities (Figure 22, especially those that benefit 
from the moderation of macro risks). 
•
Macro risks now moderating: We have been expressing tactical caution on 
Asian equities since early September due to growing macro risks then, with the 
understanding that the market setup would improve sometime in 4Q. We wrote 
then, “From here [mid-September] to late October, we continue to expect that 
risk appetite will be subdued as the Fed hiking cycle gets underway, long bond 
yields remain biased higher, JPY strength puts pressure on carry trades, oil 
prices remain elevated, and seasonal weakness bites into US mid-term 
elections. The Quality factor and Financials sector are the best hedge for this 
period.” From a macro risk perspective, it appears that all of these issues have 
been largely resolved. 
•

rolling monthly basis again - where it has tended to bottom over the past two 
years (Figure 2). Market-wide RSIs are down below 30 (Figure 3), with Tech 
sector RSIs in the early 20s. (2) Momentum factor in Asia has seen a second 
leg lower (Figure 4), bringing the correction in the Price Momentum factor,

All in all, at a regional level, our aggregate tracking of positioning in Asia has fallen 
to very low levels (<10th percentile), which typically tends to mark a bottom in markets 
(Figure 11). (6) In terms of AI stocks, the predominant positioning crowding at present

•
State of the AI trade: As we outlined last month, we continue to see the economics of 
AI working at every layer of the stack for now. JPM Tech analysts also argue for a 
recovery here, seeing no signs of cycle-end in inventories, shortening lead times or 
rapid capacity ramp-up. But, being the dominant draw for capital in the market, AI 
stocks have also had to contend with market concerns - monetization in 1Q, leverage in 
2Q, token pricing and memory supply in 3Q. Last month saw a sharp escalation in 
concerns about financing (particularly at neo-clouds). To be sure, there is a large 
funding need that will partly need to be supported by credit markets, and firms with 
weaker balance sheets and credit fundamentals could come under pressure. But we 
believe: (1) the economics are too good for capital holders to not participate (and in fact, 
rental rates have improved since October), (2) the funding needs on aggregate should 
slow from 2028 anyway as capex growth decelerates and revenues ramp up, and (3) the 
vast majority of participants in the datacenter buildout have strong balance sheets. As for 
US mid-term election impact, the focus is on how the incoming Democratic state-level 
leadership will change the rules for datacenter buildout. While the "Blue Sweep" could 
indeed limit upside to the capex curve, we note that either (1) tighter constraints on 
compute capacity will support greater pricing power and higher cash flows - and hence 
longevity of AI capex, or (2) there could be a number of workarounds for projects to get 
built (i.e., re-location, alternative on-site power generation strategies, sourcing different 
materials for environmental requirements). All in all, in line with our global strategists, 
we see this more as an intra-cycle reset and another deleveraging event in the AI 
supercycle and are more bullish vs what looks like cycle-end pricing in the market. For 
instance, SK Hynix on consensus estimates for 2026-28 (which are quite bankable) will 
generate free cash flows greater than its market cap. Any recovery in this space in the 
near-term will likely be led by higher-beta names that have seen larger drawdowns (Korea 
Tech, and Figure 21 outside of the mega-caps). 
•
Opportunities in the Non-AI space: While performance here continues to be driven 
more by the inverse of what is happening to AI stocks (Figure 7) than their individual 
merits, the easing of macro risks discussed above (lower bond yields, stabilizing 
terminal Fed rates, lower oil prices and stable/weaker dollar) are all positive for non-AI 
parts of Asia - which have really struggled over the past year. And a Democratic 
leadership in the US could also be positive for Healthcare and renewables stocks from a 
policy perspective. By market, investors have started to warm up to both China (fiscal 
stimulus, local AI story independent of US AI capex) and India (cyclical recovery, strong 
earnings, relief in oil import bill). In particular, across APAC, market-sectors that have 
shown low/negative correlation to APAC IT while still delivering positive returns (Figure 
19, 20) are – Financials (China, Thailand, Australia), Healthcare (Japan, Australia), 
Energy (China, Australia, Thailand), Staples (Korea, Japan, Australia), Telecom (HK, 
Taiwan) and Utilities (China A, HK, Australia). Single stocks that fit these criteria outside 
the mega-caps are in Figure 22.
