Gold: Post-election aids another defence of ~$4,000/oz, bullish 
bias intact but more fuel steel needed
Lighter positioning and a likely flip in CTAs set up a cleaner backdrop for gold and 
silver, but a range-break higher still likely requires more fundamental fuel
Given our bullish medium-term bias on gold, we like what we saw this week which is 
beginning to fortify our conviction. The post-election reversals in rates and energy, which 
were keeping gold under pressure in September and October, are encouraging.  As is the 
resilience gold showed as a safe haven over Thursday’s equity rout and pop higher in the VIX, 
drivers which have plagued gold at times so far this year. The fact that investor futures 
positioning had lightened significantly as CTAs flipped short over the last few weeks likely 
helped both of these in our view. 
That being said, gold still needs more fuel from here to solidify a rebound, including a 
more sustained softening in energy prices. Technically, gold still lacks clear evidence that 
a durable bottom is in place, which would come with a sustained break above the $4,189-
$4,240 short-term resistance zone (see Technicals section from Jason Hunter below).  From 
our more fundamental view, we still expect gold’s bullish window to begin to more materially 
open over the coming months as the JPM economic base case ultimately calls for a Fed under-
delivering on hikes (one more in Dec) vs current cumulative market expectations, opening 
greater sustained upside above $4,500/oz next year.  As we have seen over the swift drop in 
expected Fed hikes over the last 48 hours, continued bearish turmoil in equities over the 
coming weeks could further accelerate this rates market pivot, granted energy and inflation 
prints need to continue to cooperate. 
Copper: Chinese tightness acts as a life-boat amongst a towering 
Blue Wave
Overall, China’s acute market tightness is acting to slow copper’s descent for now even 
as copper’s darling status among investors has taken on more serious doubts. While we 
think AI demand is ultimately more important to copper’s “narrative” than it is to tonnes in 
our balance, narrative is important, particularly for a commodity where market dislocation 
has driven a significant premium into prices. Hence, copper will continue to take its top-down 
cues from AI and tech in the coming weeks. If these sectors continue to remain under pressure, 
copper will continue to trade with a bearish bias, though, we expect China’s shortage to 
remain a brake on this process for now. Unfavorable technicals remain the major near-term 
bearish risk at the moment, but it's also hard for us to see a material tumble in copper prices 
as long as China’s market remains this fundamentally tight and it looks to us like supply 
tightness could persist into year-end.
