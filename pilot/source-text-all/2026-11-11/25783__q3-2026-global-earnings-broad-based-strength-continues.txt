Q3 2026
Summary
The bar for Q3 earnings was very high following extremely high growth in Q2 and persistent upgrades to Q3 
consensus estimates. Against this high bar, earnings beats moderated across most regions, although they 
remained above historical averages in the US and Europe. Global earnings growth slowed from 38% yoy in Q2 to 
33% in Q3 but remained above 30%, supported by solid sales growth and record-high margins. Among sectors, 
Tech, Energy and Materials continued to lead earnings growth, while growth outside (12%) also remained robust 
at the top of its range. Across regions, earnings growth in the US held at an exceptionally strong 34%, with the 
median company growing 14% and growth excluding Tech, Energy and Materials remaining robust at 13% (Q3 
2026 Earnings Takes: Growth Holds In The 30s, Nov 02 2026). European growth accelerated to a four-year high of 
28%, narrowing the growth gap with the US to the smallest in 3½ years. Earnings growth in Japan (33%) and EM 
(39%) slowed sharply but remained extremely strong. Upgrades to consensus estimates, both Q4 2026 and 2027 
continued, led by EM (Korea and Taiwan particularly), followed by the US and Japan. The consensus expects 
extremely strong growth in all regions in 2026, before moderating in 2027 but remaining in the double digits in 
most regions.

Broad Based Strength Continues
The bar for Q3 earnings globally had risen substantially coming into the reporting season, as earnings growth in 
Q2 (38%) was arguably one of the highest ever and Q3 consensus estimates were steadily upgraded over the 
past year. Well into the earnings season:
Beats normalize outside Europe
Against the higher bar, earnings beats in Q3 normalized from extremely high levels in all regions apart from 
Europe where they edged higher. Aggregate beats remained above average in Europe and normalized to near 
average in the US and Japan, while EM missed.
Earnings growth in Q3 held in the 30s and continues to broaden
Global growth moderates but holds in the 30s. Global earnings growth declined in Q3 to still a very strong 33% in 
Q3, one of the highest rates on record. As in recent quarters, Tech, Energy and Materials drove the bulk of the 
growth, but even outside, growth (12%) remained at the top of its range. 7 of the 11 sectors reported growth in 
the double digits. Earnings growth was driven by solid sales growth and record high margins. Across regions:
▪
S&P 500 earnings growth sustains at extremely strong level (34%) and is broad based. Earnings growth for 
the S&P 500 remained extremely strong at 34% yoy in Q3 as in Q2, one of the highest ever except when 
coming out of recessions (Q3 2026 Earnings Takes: Growth Holds In The 30s, Nov 2 2026). Growth for the 
median company (14.4% in Q2 and in Q3) also remained very strong. Across sectors, earnings growth was 
again extremely high for Tech (54% in Q2 to 53% in Q3), albeit with divergences under the surface, and for 
Energy & Materials (108% to 118%). Growth excluding these was also a very robust 12.6%, one of the 
highest since the GFC. On the back of this continued strength, we see growth sustaining at a very high 32% 
in Q4 and raised our 2026 EPS forecast for the S&P 500 to $362 (30% yoy) and 2027 forecast to $430 (19%).
▪
European earnings growth accelerates to the highest in 4 years (28%). Earnings growth for the Stoxx 600 
accelerated sharply from 22% in Q2 to 28% in Q3, the highest in 4 years. While Energy drove much of the 
growth and acceleration, strength extended well beyond, with Tech, Industrials, Communication Services, 
Staples and Health Care all posting notable accelerations and tracking double-digit growth. At the other end, 
growth for Consumer Discretionary and Real Estate was extremely negative. Earnings growth in Europe has 
nearly caught up to that in the US, with the differential (-5.6pp) at its smallest in 3½ years. Growth in Europe is 
on par or ahead of that in the US in 6 sectors, up from just 2 in Q1 and 4 in Q2.

Broad Based Strength Continues
▪
Japanese earnings growth decelerates (33%). Earnings growth in Japan declined sharply from nearly 60% in 
Q2 to a still very strong 33% in Q3. Earnings growth in Japan has been driven by global growth and the yen, 
and the sharp slowing in Q3 coincided with a sideways move in the global manufacturing PMI and a rising 
yen. 
▪
EM earnings growth moderates (39%) but is the highest amongst regions. Earnings growth in EM declined 
from 49% in Q2 to 39% in Q3, on decelerations from extremely high rates across several sectors (Tech, 
Materials, Industrials, Financials and Health Care). Across regions, growth decelerated in Asia ex-China, driven 
by Korea (220%) and Taiwan (61%) where growth remained outsized, as well as in Latam and China, but it 
inched higher in EMEA. 
Upgrades to forward estimates continue
▪
Q4 2026 and 2027 consensus upgraded. The recent trend of upgrades to forward quarter (Q4) and 2027 
consensus estimates continued. Estimates for EM have risen substantially over the past year and further in 
the earnings season so far, driven by persistent upgrades to Korea and Taiwan, even as those for the rest of 
EM have gone largely sideways. The consensus for the US and Japan has also risen notably while that for 
Europe modestly. Almost all the increase to US estimates has come from Energy, while to Europe and EM 
from Tech and Health Care in addition to Energy. Consumer Discretionary, Staples and Materials have been 
downgraded in all three regions.
▪
Extremely strong growth globally in 2026 before moderating in 2027. The consensus is for 2026 earnings 
growth in the double digits in all major regions, led by Asia ex-China (137%) followed by the US, Japan and 
Latam (around 30%). Growth in EMEA, China and Europe is expected to trail albeit stay in an 18-23% range. 
Earnings growth is estimated to decelerate across regions in 2027 but stay very strong in the double digits in 
Asia (ex-China), Japan, the US, China and Europe, but drop significantly into the single digits in Latam and 
EMEA.

Raised S&P 500 EPS forecasts
▪We see earnings growth for the S&P 500 sustaining at a very high 32% again in Q4, 
while the consensus is only slightly lower at 29%
▪On the back of the continued strength in Tech, higher oil prices, and momentum in 
macro growth, we raised our 2026 EPS forecast for the S&P 500 modestly from $358

Macroeconomic fluctuations often account for most of the risks associated with exposures to instruments that promise to pay fixed or variable interest rates. For an investor who is long fixed-rate 
instruments (thus receiving these cash flows), increases in interest rates naturally lift the discount factors applied to the expected cash flows and thus cause a loss. The longer the maturity of a certain 
cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse 
macroeconomic shocks to receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets holding limits for different types of investors), 
changes in tax policies, currency convertibility (which may constrain currency conversion, repatriation of profits and/or liquidation of positions), and settlement issues related to local clearing houses 
are also important risk factors. The sensitivity of fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by indexing the contracted cash flows to inflation, to FX depreciation, or to 
specified interest rates – these are common in emerging markets.  The index fixings may – by construction – lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables they are intended to track. 
The choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating coupon rates (i.e., coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are 
exchanged for fixed coupons. Funding in a currency that differs from the currency in which coupons are denominated carries FX risk. Options on swaps (swaptions) the risks typical to options in
