November 10, 2026
Industrial Metals Weekly
Copper prices down with the market, but Chinese physical indicators
tighten
Our view: Following the democratic sweep in the US midterms, copper prices fell to ~$6.40/lb (from ~
$6.60/lb prior) on lower tariff odds and potentially weaker AI data centre demand, following broader
market sentiment. Copper equities mirrored the decline and valuations remain at historical discounts
relative to spot (see page 3). However, in China the physical market continued to tighten on weaker
production from Chile, with the Yangshan premium hitting a record $230/t, amidst Trafigura buying
large volumes of copper to boost sales into Asia with already low Chinese inventory. Shanghai copper
inventories fell to 33,848mt (-10,764kt w/w) on Friday as the physical market in China continues to
tighten. SMM reports eleven smelters underwent maintenance during the month, with an estimated
production impact of 103,200 tonnes as domestic copper cathode output fell 3.03% m/m to 1,086,700
tonnes. Meanwhile, earnings season comes to a close this Thursday with Hudbay reporting before
markets open. Q3 results have benefited from strong metal prices; however, weather and ramp-up
issues have resulted in downward guidance revisions across the board (see chart of the week below).
In this note we update our Lundin estimates post Q3 results (page 25).
• Base metals mixed, biased negative: Copper prices fell 3.0% to $6.33/lb, nickel rose 5.5% to $7.30/
lb and zinc fell 4.5% to $1.71/lb.
• Bulks mixed: Met coal prices declined 1.2% to $282.70/mt, while iron ore prices rose 2.9% to $93.30/
dmt.
• Published research: With this note we have updated our estimates on Lundin following Q3 results (no
change to PT/rating). Hudbay reports Thursday BMO which will mark the end of a generally challenging
Q3 for copper producers under coverage.
Chart of the Week: Widespread guidance cuts amidst a challenging Q3 so far
All five copper producers trimmed 2026 guidance (we'll see if Hudbay breaks the trend), with an
average production miss of 5.9% and guidance reductions averaging 3.8%. Extreme weather has been
the main culprit, impacting Lundin's Caserones, Capstone's Pinto Valley/Chilean ops, and Freeport's US
mines. Weather impacts also resulted in producers struggling with lower grades, deferring higher-grade
access to 2027. Ivanhoe's quarter was particularly challenging due to ramp-up delays at Kakula, and
while Capstone didn't cut guidance, actual production is expected on the lower end.
*Ivanhoe and Freeport pre-released production, excluded from Q3 production average. Freeport shown as sales, converted to kt Cu.

Valuation
We value Lundin by applying a 50% weight to 1.5x our NAV
and a 50% weight to 6.0x our 2027 EBITDA estimate (including
Vicuña NAV). The 1.5x and 6.0x EV/EBITDA multiples are at
premiums to peers reflecting the value embedded for Vicuña.
Our C$47.00 price target supports our Sector Perform rating.
Investment summary
Lundin provides exposure to copper, gold, and nickel across
a geographically diverse set of assets. Lundin should benefit
from strong base metals prices while higher gold prices
could provide an ongoing tailwind. Incremental expansions at
Candelaria, Chapada, and Caserones could drive 10% growth
within the next 3-5 years while development of the world-
class Vicuna district could provide transformational copper
and gold production growth post 2030.
Potential catalysts
• 2026: Final investment decision (FID) for the Vicuña district
• 2027: Ball mill commissioning at Chapada and Caserones
Angelica initial resource estimate
• 2029: Saúva first ore, driving a ~30% step-up in Chapada
copper production
• Q1 2027: Candelaria Franco-Nevada stream step-down from
68% to 40%, adding ~$56-90M in annual FCF
Risks to rating and price target
As with other mining companies, world economic growth,
commodity prices, and currency fluctuations could have
a material impact on the company’s financial position.
Execution and financing risk is notable with the development
of the Vicuña district in combination with BHP.
