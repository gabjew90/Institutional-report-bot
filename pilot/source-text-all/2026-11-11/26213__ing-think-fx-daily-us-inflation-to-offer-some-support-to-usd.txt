FX Daily: US inflation to offer some
support to USD today
The dollar has handed back most of last week’s gains on the back of a
rebound in risk sentiment, also helped by further declines in energy
prices. But we expect a slightly hotter-than-expected 0.3% MoM US core
CPI print today to offer the dollar some support. We see some
stabilisation in EUR/USD for the remainder of this week
USD: AI trade bounces back
The dollar has given back most of last week’s gains as global equities rebounded and oil prices
extended their decline at the start of this week. Despite the 4% drop in Nvidia stock, led by an
investigation into CEO Jensen Huang, the Nasdaq and S&P 500 are now less than 2% below
their 4 November closing levels. That sends a rather strong signal that last week was a bout of
jitters rather than the start of a bigger downturn.
A further improvement in risk sentiment should strip the dollar of the small residual gains from
the selloff. But the macro story may incidentally work in the dollar’s favour, leaving risks for
the greenback rather balanced, in our view, by the end of the week. We think today’s October
core CPI will come in slightly above consensus at 0.3% MoM, offering a bit more support to
front-end USD rates, which have also crawled back higher as sentiment has stabilised. We

EUR: Lower oil not enough for a rally on its own
We warned yesterday that EUR/USD was looking dangerously undervalued and called for an
upward correction. We saw that materialise quite rapidly yesterday, and our models now
show the pair has returned to the 1.5-1.7% undervaluation that prevailed before last
Thursday’s equity selloff. While still cheap, French risk continues to play a role and argues
against another fast tightening in the valuation gap.
At this stage, other market drivers need to turn in favour of EUR/USD to push the pair higher.
Plummeting oil prices on the back of renewed Middle East optimism may offer limited further
support on their own, with moves in short-term rate differentials likely to matter more. The risk
remains that a further decline in energy prices prompts a larger repricing in ECB rate
expectations relative to the Fed’s, especially as we expect today’s US CPI report to build a
stronger floor for front-end USD rates.
For now, we struggle to see the EUR/USD rally extending much further this week and instead
expect the pair to stabilise in the 1.120-1.1250 area. We retain a moderate positive bias into
year-end, largely due to positive year-end seasonality and a tentative view that some French
risk could temporarily leave the euro. Our year-end target remains 1.130.

NOK: Inflation endorses prolonged pause
Norway released October inflation data this morning. Headline CPI accelerated from 3.4% to
3.6%, marginally below the 3.7% consensus, while underlying inflation (CPI-ATE) slowed as
expected from 3.0% to 2.7%. The still-elevated headline print points to some caution in moving
away from Norges Bank's tightening bias, but CPI-ATE below 3.0% should prevent further rate
increases.
Norges Bank has been one of the most reactive central banks to inflationary risk, even before
the Iran conflict. With rates at 4.50%, policymakers are keeping the door open to more hikes if
needed, while sending a clear signal that a prolonged pause is the baseline scenario. We agree

CEE: Inflation is creeping up
Hungarian inflation rose from 1.6% to 2.0% in October, in line with NBH and market
expectations, with core inflation also rising slightly. Nevertheless, Hungarian inflation remains
the lowest in the CEE region and well below the NBH’s target. Inflation is expected to continue
rising in the coming months, peaking slightly below 4% in the middle of next year before
returning to the target. This should allow the NBH to continue with rate cuts in the second half
of next year. As is the case elsewhere, energy prices remain the primary unknown, with
Hungary remaining significantly exposed to this risk.
Later today, final Czech inflation figures for October will be released. The flash estimate last
week showed a decline from 2.5% to 2.4%. The focus today is on the breakdown and core
inflation, where we expect an uptick from 3.1% to 3.2%. Details of the CNB's new forecast will
not be released until Friday, but the central bank will share its expectations for October in a
commentary this afternoon.
Rates in the region saw another leg of a rally yesterday as the market priced out rate hikes
from the curve, following a decline in energy prices. FX saw only limited gains, despite a global
risk-on sentiment. Narrowing rate differentials and a persistently strong US dollar likely remain
headwinds; meanwhile, the rally in rates suggests a weaker outlook for regional FX once the
current stabilisation phase settles.
Frantisek Taborsky
