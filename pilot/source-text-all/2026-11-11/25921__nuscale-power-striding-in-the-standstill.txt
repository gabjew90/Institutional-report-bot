Striding in the standstill
Investment Recommendation
Yep, it has been a drawdown. In NuScale, in nuclear equities as a whole, and across
much of the landscape we cover. We see it, and we feel it. We understand a market
whose patience for great ambitions and modest near-term profits has grown, quite
simply, cold.
Into that fire, NuScale reported Thursday night. And to be plain, the disclosures were
measured, yielding few new certainties.
So the question stands - fair, sharp, and unavoidable: Why stay BUY rated?
Why remain anchored to a stock where, despite a momentous pact with the
Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) a year ago to build 6 gigawatts of power, a silence
of detail has lengthened into a shadow? Skepticism lingers around execution. It
lingers around an unusual financing structure between NuScale and Entra1. Above
all, it lingers around the missing proof: a binding power purchase agreement (PPA)
between Entra1 and the TVA. That signature is the gateway. Without it, the modules
remain promises on paper.
To be clear, we do not dismiss these anxieties. We share them. We want the answers.
We want the ink on the page. We ourselves ask: Why has it not happened yet?
But, conviction is not born of easy weather. We believe in a nuclear future - first,
last, and fundamentally. We believe that NuScale’s standard design approval from
the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) remains a singular, peerless (for now)
advantage in a crowded field.
We also watch them move forward, pressing ahead with component orders - such
as their recent agreement with Doosan Enerbility to forge the upper reactor coolant
systems. It is steel and intent; it is a sign of quiet confidence, at least to us. They are
striding in the standstill.
And let us be clear: a signed PPA with the TVA would be a monumental milestone. But
contracts on a desk do not build plants. The hard gravity of operational risk remains
for both NuScale and Entra1, contract or no contract.
We also recognize the current valuation: trading near cash - but to be fair, with a
quarterly burn. Even so, this represents to us a striking asymmetric entry point where
the upside potential could heavily outweigh the risk.
The suspense is palpable. But management’s aggressive march toward
commercialization is not merely understandable; it is essential. In the race for
advanced nuclear, the first cohort to cross the threshold will not simply succeed. They
may draw the line between tomorrow’s industrial titans and those who slip quietly into
the dark.
We maintain our BUY rating but are lowering our target from $15 to $11, which we
calculate via DCF using a ~15% WACC and a ~5% terminal growth rate. We continue
to de-risk our model by pushing out initial model deployments and tempering our
long-term assumptions given the continued uncertainty around the TVA contract.

The results
3Q26 results overview. NuScale reported 3Q26 results that mostly in line with our/
consensus estimates - detailed below.
• Revenue of ~$0.3M increased sequentially from ~$.75M in 2Q26 and fell from ~
$8.2M in 3Q25.
• Operating loss of ~($64.8M) - was essentially from ~($64M) in 2Q26 and
improved from ~($538.4M) in 3Q25 - coming in-line with our estimate of ~($64M).
• Net loss of ~($43.7M) - improved sequentially from ~($50.1M) in 2Q26 and
improved annually from ~($532.6M) — coming in above our estimate of ~($50.1M).
• NuScale finished the quarter with ~$1.9B of cash, cash equivalents, short-term and
long-term investments - essentially flat with ~$1.9B at the end of 2Q26.
• Below we list a summary of NuScale's 3Q26 financial results.

Quarterly commentary
Management's message, moving while waiting: CEO John Hopkins on the 3Q26
earnings call:
• "NuScale is moving from commercial agreements to delivery readiness. As Entra1
advances its customer engagements, we are advancing detailed engineering:
Locking in key suppliers, securing regulatory approvals that de-risk the pathforward,
and preparing to support the construction and operating license application process."
• "Our licensing team is already preparing to support the construction and operation
license application for the potential Tennessee Valley Authority project."
• And on TVA itself: "We are ready to go, as I commented earlier. We're continuing. It
is a process. It has been lengthy. But we are doing what we can and what we have
control over."
RoPower update. Outside of TVA, we continue to wait for material breakthroughs with
RoPower of Romania. Management update on the RoPower opportunity: "Turning to
Romania, with the new government in place, we remain engaged withour Romanian
partners. And as this process moves forward, our priority is ensuring that any
agreement appropriately allocates risk, delivers commercial value, and protects our
shareholders interests."
Management on exploring potential customers beyond TVA: "Our commercial
opportunities extend beyond TVA: We continue to engage utilities, hyperscalers and
major industrial customers seeking reliable carbon-free baseload power."
NuScale and Doosan advance manufacturing of the NuScale Power Module.
On October 29, NuScale Power announced an agreement with Doosan Enerbility, a
nuclear manufacturing and engineering company, to manufacture the upper reactor
coolant system for the NuScale Power Module. The companies have worked together
since 2019 to develop the manufacturing processes and supply chain needed to build
NuScale’s SMRs, with the new contract moving the partnership from preparation into
production of the first upper reactor coolant system.

• John Hopkins, President and CEO of NuScale Power, stated, “Doosan’s nuclear

schedules our customers require. The refinements made to the design through this

NuScale deploys nuclear-specific AI tools to accelerate reactor development.
On August 25, NuScale Power announced it is deploying nuclear-specific AI tools
from Nuclearn and NPX to help its engineers more quickly find and use technical,
engineering, and licensing information. An initial proof of concept showed the tools
could reduce the time required to find relevant information by as much as 80%, helping
NuScale streamline work as it advances its SMR toward deployment.
• José Reyes, CTO of NuScale Power, stated,“this partnership with Nuclearn and
NPX will equip our teams with the right purpose-built AI tools – grounded in our
extensive proprietary knowledge – to work more efficiently while maintaining the
highest safety and quality standards.”
• What are Nuclearn and NPX? Nuclearn provides AtomAssist, an AI platform built
specifically for nuclear professionals, while NPX provides nuclear project and AI
implementation expertise to help integrate the technology into nuclear workflows.
NuScale Power and MillenniTEK Advance Manufacturing Technology for
First-of-a-Kind Reactor Safety Component. On September 1, NuScale Power and
MillenniTEK Advance Manufacturing Technology for First-of-a-Kind Reactor Safety
Component announced the successful production of first-of-a-kind boron-oxide pellets
for NuScale’s passive emergency core cooling system. MillenniTEK is an advanced
nuclear materials manufacturer that converts powdered materials into specialized solid
components. The pellets are designed to dissolve into the reactor coolant during an
emergency and help keep the reactor core at safe reactivity levels without requiring
operator intervention, advancing NuScale’s manufacturing readiness for commercial
deployment.
• John Hopkins, NuScale President and CEO, stated,“By working with experienced
advanced nuclear manufacturers like MillenniTEK, we are progressing the
production-ready processes needed to support our first customer deployments.”
NuScale has negotiated over half of their 60+ supplier relationships. Previously,
management emphasized the importance and advantage of their quasi-established
supply chain on the 2Q26 call. CEO, John Hopkins, stated, "this shortens the path to
actual power generation not by months, but by years." The advantage stems from
having already negotiated supplier agreements with more than half of their 60+
suppliers. Many of whom, are long time partners.

(down from ~$519.2M in 2Q25) and ~$19.0M in other operating expenses (up/down
from ~$10.8M in 3Q25).
Balance sheet updates. NuScale finished the quarter with ~$1.9B in cash and cash
equivalents, short-term and long-term investments—essentially flat from ~$1.9B at the
end of 2Q26.

Past ENTRA1 and TVA developments
The "NuScale SMR development program:". In September 2025, Tennessse Valley
Authority (TVA) - in collaboration with ENTRA1 - announced a landmark 6 GW SMR
agreement, coined the "NuScale SMR Deployment Program." Pursuant to the
agreements, ENTRA1 will develop 6 energy plants - powered by NuScale Power Modules
- across TVA's 7-state serviceable area.
• Awaiting a binding PPA delay. While management previously guided for binding
PPA execution by the end of 2025, on the 4Q25 earnings call, management
announced ENTRA1 reached a "non-binding collaborative agreement with TVA."
While management is hopeful the partnership will result in NPM deployments, it is
yet not guaranteed. The company did, however, note "drafting of a PPA is underway
with robust engagement from legal teams and progress is being made on transaction
documentation and structure."
• According to management on the 2Q26 earnings call, "we're extremely encouraged
by our conversations that ENTRA1 and the TVA are having... The conversations we
understand are progressing well."
TVA in "fast-paced" negotiations to build SMR with ENTRA1 and GE Vernova.
On August 5th, the TVA announced they are close to negotiating a deal with GE
Vernova to build SMRs at an unfinished nuclear plant in Bellefonte, Alabama. The TVA
is also in negotiations with ENTRA1 Energy to construct SMRs at its unfinished nuclear
site in Hartsville, Tennessee. To fund these SMR developments, TVA is actively meeting
with Japanese banks, leveraging momentum from the $40 billion U.S.-Japan bilateral
trade agreement to secure advanced reactor project capital.
• CEO of the TVA, Mike Skaggs, when discussing the negotiations with GE Vernova
said, "I can't say it's going to be done, but I can tell you that the work that's going
on is fast. It's a hot pace. There's a ton of energy associated with it... These energy
contracts that we're working through are some of the biggest energy contracts ...
These are very complex negotiations that we're working through. But my feeling is
that the probability of this happening is pretty good."
More on site identification. In addition to the 4 identified, potential NPM deployment
sites, on the 1Q26 call, management stated "the prospective site for the first plant
deployment has been identified." While this progress is encouraging, we have been
unable to find TVA/ ENTRA1 project updates on the NRC Advanced Reactor Dashboard
- which tracks construction permits, early site permits, and combined license progress,
essential steps leading to reactor deployment. We await related NRC submissions as
material proof of plant deployment progress.

6GWs: enough to power 60+ new data centers. According to TVA, "this
collaboration could provide enough energy to power the equivalent of approximately
4.5 million homes or 60 new data centers—at a time when hyperscale data centers,
artificial intelligence (AI), semiconductor manufacturing, and other energy-intensive
technologies are driving unprecedented growth in electricity demand." ENTRA1 intends
to sell NPM-derived power to TVA under future PPAs - which could trigger the second
tranche of NuScale disbursements (more detail below).
• 6GW = 72 NPMs. Given NPM output of 77MW per reactor (see our previous note
detailing urate approval), we calculate up to 72 NPM deployments (i.e. 12, 77MW-
output NPMs per energy project, generating ~924MW of nuclear power per energy
project, or ~5.5GW in total). ENTRA1 developed Energy Projects will be disbursed
across TVA's 7-state serviceable area. Management expects the first ENTRA1 energy
plant to deliver power to TVA as early as 2030, "with additional plants phased in as
demand grows."
Contracted milestones trigger contribution disbursements. Associated with the
Entra1/ TVA expected to result in NPM deployments, NuScale has agreed to provide a
$35M - $55M contribution per NPM (6 GWs of power implies 72 NPMs; $3.24B at the
mid-point). NuScale's contribution will be dispersed in tranches triggered by contracted
commercial/ development milestone achievement - detailed below.
• According to management, "these payments reflect project costs that NuScale would
typically occur later in the process, such as development, project management and
other services. By accelerating these payments, we are helping Entra1 reach key
milestones more quickly, unlocking financing and speeding up construction."
• Management expects to recoup payments over several years. The company does not
anticipate making $3B of payments in the near-term, rather, management expects
project-related deployment orders to come in stages, recouping payments through
ongoing deployments.
Tranche 1 (achieved): ~15% disbursement following a non-binding third-
party energy project agreement. ~15% of NuScale's total anticipated contribution
will be distributed "upon the execution of a non-binding term sheet, memorandum of
understanding, letter of intent, or framework agreement with a Third Party... related to
the development of an Energy Project."
• $495M to ENTRA1 in 3Q25. Tranche 1 payments were distributed during 3Q25,
accounting for $495M of G&A expense. When asked how ENTRA1 will allocate the
$495M of payments on the 3Q25 earnings call, management stated: "I think the
short answer is that ENTRA1 is definitely into project development now. So, I don't
have a source of uses to share, but we know that ENTRA1 has pushed forward with
this term sheet. There are 6 projects to develop."
Tranche 2: ~35% disbursement following binding PPA/ off-take agreement
or NPM delivery. ~35% of NuScale's total anticipated contribution will be distributed
"upon execution of a binding power purchase agreement, energy off-take agreement or
documentation with a Third Party in connection with development of an Energy Project
or the deployment or delivery of one or more NPMs into a potential Energy Project."
Tranche 3: ~50% disbursement upon binding NPM purchase agreement. The
remaining ~50% of NuScale's total anticipated contribution will be distributed "upon
the execution of an original equipment manufacturing agreement with NuScale LLC for
NPMs or another form of binding documentation in connection with the purchase or
deployment of NPM."
