The great AI reset
Asia's AI trade has gone from crowded to crushed. Momentum has collapsed, tech positioning has
been slashed and Korean semiconductors have suffered brutal drawdowns. Yet JPM argues the
underlying AI cycle remains intact. With positioning close to capitulation levels and valuations
increasingly disconnected from earnings, the question is whether this is the end of the AI supercycle or
another massive buying opportunity.
Everybody out
JPM's aggregate Asian equity positioning indicator has fallen to the 8th percentile, a level that has
historically coincided with important market bottoms. Asian equities have suffered another roughly
15% monthly drawdown, while tech RSI readings have collapsed into the low 20s. Multi-strategy and
tech-focused hedge funds have substantially reduced exposure. The positioning reset is becoming
extreme.

The momentum massacre
Asia's price momentum factor has plunged 45% from its June highs, approaching the magnitude of the
historic reversals in 1998–99 and 2009. JPM suggests the growing leverage of multi-strategy and
quantitative investors may be amplifying these moves. This is no longer just a fundamental AI debate.
It is a positioning unwind feeding on itself.
KOSPI, the epicentre of the Asian AI trade, remains deeply depressed, trading well below its 200-day
MA. A violent oversold bounce could be in the making, but don't dismiss the death cross. The squeeze
potential is enormous, but the technical damage is real.

Too cheap, too broken?
SK Hynix has plunged almost 30% in a month, yet JPM's screen puts the stock at just 2.3x forward
earnings, with projected two-year EPS CAGR of 193.6%. Even more striking, JPM estimates cumulative
free cash flow over 2026–28 will exceed the company's current market cap. The market is pricing in an
earnings collapse, while JPM sees an exceptionally profitable memory cycle.
The chart remains ugly, with the stock deeply oversold and well below its 200-day MA. But that is
precisely the tension: fundamentals suggest extraordinary value, while price action still screams
liquidation. The next move could be explosive in either direction.

Valuations from another era
It isn't just SK Hynix. The valuation reset across Korean semiconductors has pushed multiples back
toward levels rarely seen in modern market history, according to Goldman. The market is increasingly
pricing a structural earnings downturn, rather than a temporary correction in the AI cycle. If earnings
hold up, the valuation disconnect could become difficult to ignore.

The earnings disconnect
Despite the violent price correction, Asian tech earnings revision breadth remains positive. JPM's
technology analysts see no evidence of the classic cycle-ending signals: inventory accumulation,
shortening lead times or aggressive capacity expansion. Prices are behaving as though fundamentals
have already deteriorated. The earnings data tell a different story.

JPM
Who buys the dip?
The next marginal buyer matters. JPM identifies corporates, long-only funds and pensions as potential
buyers in Korea, while Taiwan could see support from hedge funds and ETFs. Institutional AI exposure
has already been reduced, but retail and high-net-worth positioning remains elevated. The key
question is whether institutional buying can absorb any remaining retail liquidation. If it can, the next
squeeze could be considerably more powerful than investors expect.

The bottom line
The market is increasingly pricing an AI cycle collapse. JPM sees another violent deleveraging event
within an intact supercycle. That is a massive distinction.
Positioning is approaching capitulation, valuations have been crushed and earnings revisions remain
resilient. The technical damage is real, but if fundamentals hold, the setup for a powerful squeeze is
becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.
The question is no longer whether AI has been sold. It's whether it has been oversold.
