European Conference: What Companies
Said About The Macro Outlook
Matthew Cowley
11-Nov-2026, 08:17 ET (EST)
The overarching message from corporate presentations on day one of the UBS European Conference is one
of resilient but uneven economic growth, persistent inflationary pressures and increasing reliance on
productivity improvements to drive earnings. European activity appears to be stabilising rather than
accelerating, while AI, electrification and infrastructure investment are creating significant pockets of structural
growth.
Importantly, corporate confidence remains relatively strong despite subdued underlying demand, suggesting
earnings growth may increasingly outpace economic growth.
1. Inflation Remains Sticky, but Pricing Power Is Holding Up
Input cost pressures remain evident across sectors. Essity, Mondi, Siemens Healthineers and Magnum Ice
Cream highlighted continued inflation in energy, raw materials and distribution. However, management teams
generally remain confident in recovering costs through pricing and efficiency improvements.
Holcim expects a significantly positive price-cost spread in 2027, while IAG can now pass through
approximately 80% of fuel costs.
Macro implication: Inflation may prove slower to normalise than expected, but corporate margins could
remain resilient as businesses continue passing costs through to customers.
2. Europe Is Stabilising, Not Accelerating
Corporate commentary suggests a gradual, uneven European recovery.
Holcim sees improving German construction activity, ArcelorMittal expects European steel orders to recover
as destocking ends, and ABB and Schneider Electric highlighted early signs of industrial improvement.
However, Vodafone continues to struggle in Germany, Rightmove sees challenging UK property conditions,
and Primark reports softness across continental Europe.
Macro implication: Europe appears to be experiencing selective cyclical improvement rather than a
broad-based recovery, with infrastructure investment providing more support than consumer demand.
3. AI and Electrification Are Driving a Two-Speed Economy
Structural investment remains exceptionally strong despite subdued traditional industrial activity.
Schneider Electric highlighted 18–24 months of data centre demand visibility and potential capacity
shortages in 2028. ABB reported sustained electrification demand, while Vinci identified significant
opportunities in power transmission.
BHP and Antofagasta continue investing in copper capacity, reflecting expectations of sustained demand
from electrification.

Macro implication: AI, energy infrastructure and electrification are creating a powerful investment cycle,
supporting industrial activity while potentially sustaining demand for commodities, energy and capital
equipment.
4. Consumer Spending Remains Resilient but Selective
Consumer-facing companies presented a mixed picture.
Tesco continues gaining market share, with premium products performing strongly, while Primark reported
improving UK trading. However, Diageo expects North American weakness to persist into 2027, and
European retail conditions remain challenging.
Travel appears comparatively resilient, with IAG, Air France-KLM and Accor reporting encouraging business
travel or booking trends.
Macro implication: Consumption is holding up, but spending is increasingly selective, favouring value,
differentiated brands and travel over certain discretionary categories.
5. Banks Signal Credit Resilience and Productivity Gains
European banks delivered constructive messages.
NatWest expects sustained credit growth, while BNP Paribas sees improving profitability and limited impact
from French political uncertainty on corporate activity.
Santander expects its cost-income ratio to decline from 45% in 2025 to 36% by 2028, supported by AI,
restructuring and integration benefits.
Macro implication: Credit conditions appear relatively resilient, while AI-driven efficiency improvements could
increasingly support profitability without requiring stronger economic growth.
6. China Remains Weak, While Corporate Self-Help Dominates
China remains a challenging end-market for Diageo and Siemens Healthineers, although Chinese
competition and innovation are creating opportunities elsewhere, including automotive distribution and
pharmaceuticals.
Meanwhile, Stellantis, Vodafone, Santander and BNP Paribas emphasised cost savings, automation and
restructuring as key earnings drivers.
Macro implication: Companies are increasingly relying on productivity, pricing and capital discipline rather
than volume growth.
