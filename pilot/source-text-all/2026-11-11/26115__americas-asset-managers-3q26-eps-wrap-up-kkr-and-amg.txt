KKR (Buy): Consistently solid results, but balance sheet intensity remains an overhang for valuation 
 
 
Following 3Q26 results, we raise our 2026 ANI per share estimate to $8.40 from $8.02 
(mostly on strong 3Q), but lower our 2027/28 ANI per share estimates to $7.29/$8.74 
from $7.53/$8.90. The negative 2027/28 ANI/share revisions are largely driven by: (1) 
Lower Strategic Holding dividends from recent sales (~$140mn annually), resetting the 
baseline in 2027 partially offset by Strategic Holdings investments (KKR expects to 
recover ~50% of the shortfall by 2028); and (2) Lower PRE on the back of softer 4Q 
update for PRE and lower than expected NAC balances. Our FRE estimates remain 
largely unchanged driven by (a) Global Infra, Asia Infra and Helix in Real Assets; (b) Asia 
V and the Arctos/KKR Solutions build in PE (Keystone closed at ~$6bn; a GP-led 
secondaries strategy is expected to launch in 2027); (c) Private IG/ABF and third-party 
credit, where KKR expects a record year; (d) Continued K-Series growth in wealth. On an 
organic, ex-catch up fees basis, we see 2027E management fee growth of ~15%, vs. 
the ~20% the firm is currently running at (TTM as of 3Q26 on same basis). Taking a 
step back, KKR continues to derive significant value from its balance sheet 
(evidenced by recent gains) and its diversified business model is driving healthy 
growth in FRE and PRE (despite macro challenges and mixed results across the 
space). The stock’s multiple of 14X 2027 P/E (net of SBC) remains attractive, but the 
stock’s balance-sheet-heavy model remains a concern for investors.

Reiterate Buy 
 
 
Following AMG’s 3Q26 EPS results, we raise our 2026/2027/2028 EPS estimates to 
$39.11/$47.20/$56.31 from $37.52/$45.18/$53.81 prior on the back of stronger 3Q26 
core EBITDA, a higher recurring FRE run-rate into 4Q26, a larger 2026 buyback (now 
>$900mn vs. ~$600mn earlier guide), and higher 4Q performance fee contribution, 
partially offset by MTM headwinds so far in 4Q (blended down 2% through Nov 6). 
Notably, management now expects 2026 economics EPS growth of ~50% (up from 
~40% in 2Q), with alternatives contributing to ~70%. Although the tax-aware debate 
intensified this quarter, with SCHW capping new accounts and IRS/Treasury questioning 
parts of the ecosystem, AMG’s L/S tax aware strategies continued to put up robust 
growth (and did not see much of a pull forward in activity). We expect strength here to 
persist amid robust end-client demand and expanding custodian offerings. Bigger 
picture, we think the breadth of the flows at AMG remains strong (private markets seeing 
+21% LTM organic growth and non-tax-aware liquid alts +14%) and robust FRE mix 
supports the durability of the firm’s EBITDA growth. On the back of robust earnings 
growth, structurally improving EBITDA yield (EBITDA/AUM), and lower impact from 
market beta, management highlighted structurally improving cash flow profile - not 
currently captured in the stock’s valuation. Notably, management views that cumulative 
FCF over the next 5-7 years will approximate today’s market cap underscoring the 
meaningful disconnect between AMG’s FCF profile and its valuation. We see about 10% 
upside to Visible Alpha consensus EPS in 2027 and 2028. At ~8.2X 2027 P/E, we 
continue to see >30% upside to our price target. Notable items from the quarter include: 
Organic growth: AMG generated $20.7bn of net inflows (~9% annualized organic 
n
growth) in 3Q, with alternative affiliates driving net inflows of $31.5bn. Specifically, 
liquid alternatives contributed $21.9bn, driven by tax-aware and absolute return 
strategies, while private markets contributed $9.6bn — with fundraising 
broad-based across infrastructure/real assets, secondaries, and specialized 
strategies. Notably, liquid alternatives growth extended beyond tax-aware, with the 
rest of the business generating 14% LTM organic growth on strong institutional 
demand across multiple affiliates. Private markets generated ~20% LTM organic 
growth, while liquid alternatives grew nearly 40%. 
Looking ahead, management did not see evidence of future tax-aware flows 
o
being pulled forward into 3Q ahead of the SCHW changes and continues to 
view the strategy as in the broad adoption phase as product/channel 
availability expands. Further, management expects low turnover and 
continued contributions from existing clients given the strategies’ integration 
into longer-term financial plans. More broadly, AMG continues to see four 
separate secular drivers supporting alternatives growth across secondaries, 
infrastructure, absolute return, and tax-aware investing. 
Structurally, the EBITDA contribution from flows continues to run ~2x the 
o
asset-based organic growth rate given higher fee rates and margin expansion 
across the largest alternative affiliates. Notably, operating leverage here 
extends beyond AQR, with management highlighting Pantheon and other 
affiliates as contributors. Overall, we model organic growth of ~9% in 2026 
and ~5% in 2027/2028.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

and represent investment recommendations focused on the size of the total return potential and/or the likelihood of the realization of the return across 
their respective areas of coverage.  The addition or removal of stocks from such Conviction Lists are managed by the Investment Review Committee or 
other designated committee in each respective region and do not represent a change in the analysts’ investment rating for such stocks.    
Total return potential represents the upside or downside differential between the current share price and the price target, including all paid or 
anticipated dividends, expected during the time horizon associated with the price target. Price targets are required for all covered stocks. The total 
return potential, price target and associated time horizon are stated in each report adding or reiterating an Investment List membership.
