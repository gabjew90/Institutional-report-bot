◆ 3Q26 results came in well ahead of conservative guidance; 
CRWV made extremely positive comments on product pricing 
◆ No material change to our long-term non-GAAP EPS  
◆ Maintain Reduce and TP at USD39 on valuation  
3Q26 margins ahead of estimates: 3Q26 revenue came in at USD3,661m, 2.9% 
above consensus. Non-GAAP operating margin came in at 10.4% vs HSBCe of 
8.0%, consensus of 6.9% and guidance of 6.5%. We do not read much into the 
margin beat. The company has typically maintained long-term non-GAAP operating 
margin guidance of 25%. TTM non-GAAP operating margin has been depressed at 
about 5.5%. Prior to the TTM dip, non-GAAP operating margin averaged c16% and 
we have long expected margins to normalize at around the same level. 3Q26 
estimates were influenced downwards by overly conservative guidance. 
Bullish pricing comments need careful evaluation: CRWV said it is able to sell 
compute capacity at USD20-25m p.a. per MW while selling under four- to five-year 
contracts (short term: up to USD40m), well above USD12-13m per annum per MW 
we estimate the company has realized on its contracts historically. We believe the 
price comments indicate either excessive optimism or higher cost capacity per MW 
than in the past. CoreWeave’s asset turnover (annualized revenue/gross PP&E 
excluding under construction assets and excluding operating leased assets) came in 
at 30.5% in 3Q26 (2Q26: 28.6%, 2025: 30.0%, 2024: 34.8%). We think CoreWeave’s 
2026e exit ARR guidance of USD20-21bn, when read in conjunction with the 
company’s 4Q26 capex guidance, suggests exit asset turnover of 32.6%, which is not 
far from what has been realized historically. We have consistently modelled a 
medium-term asset turnover of c33% on life-weighted assets for CoreWeave. The 
evolution of CRWV’s asset turnover appears similar to the trends at Microsoft and 
Oracle, and does not indicate to us any runaway pricing. 
2027 EBITDA comments conservative or disappointing: CRWV expects 2027 
non-GAAP EBITDA/interest expense (ex the impact of prepayments) to more than 
double y-o-y. Consensus, as do we, expects the figure to rise to 2.4x-2.6x y-o-y in 
2027. More than double does not rule out a 2.5x increase, but the comments could 
be construed as either conservative or disappointing.  
Expensive valuation: CoreWeave is trading at a 2030e non-GAAP EV/EBIT (after 
SBC) of 11.7x, which is at a large premium to 6.6x for Oracle and a slight premium to 
the multiples of hyperscalers such as Amazon, Microsoft and Meta. Our DCF-based 
TP of USD39 is unchanged and implies 20% downside to the current price; therefore, 
we reiterate our Reduce rating.

Results ahead of guidance 
 
◆ 3Q26 revenue came in at USD3,661m (HSBCe USD3,663m, consensus USD3,557m). The 
slight beat vs consensus seems driven by higher-than-expected (and guided) capex.  
◆ Non-GAAP operating margin came in at 10.4% vs HSBCe of 8.0%, consensus of 6.9% and 
guidance of 6.5%. We do not read much into the margin beat. The company has typically 
maintained long-term non-GAAP operating margin guidance of 25%. TTM non-GAAP 
operating margin has been depressed at about 5.5%. Prior to the recent dip, the non-GAAP 
operating margin averaged c16% and we have long expected margins to normalize at 
around the same level. We believe that like ours, consensus 3Q26 estimates were 
influenced by overly conservative guidance.

Price comments need nuanced analysis 
CoreWeave said it is able to sell compute capacity at USD20-25m per annum per MW while 
selling under four- to five-year contracts. Prices are even more attractive at up to USD40m per 
annum per MW under short-term contracts. This is well above USD12-13m per annum per MW 
we estimate the company realized on its contracts historically.  
We believe the price comments may require deeper analysis and may indicate either excessive 
optimism or higher cost capacity per MW than has been realized in the past. 
CoreWeave’s asset turnover (annualized revenue/Gross PP&E excluding under construction 
assets and excluding operating leased assets) came in at 30.5% in 3Q26 (2Q26: 28.6%, 2025: 
30.0%, 2024: 34.8%). We estimate, CoreWeave’s 2026 exit ARR guidance of USD20-21bn 
(increased from USD18.5-19.5bn) when read in conjunction with the company’s 4Q26 capex 
guidance, suggests exit asset turnover of about 32.6%, which is not far from what has been 
realized historically. We have consistently modelled an asset turnover of about 33% on life 
weighted assets for CoreWeave.  
As asset turnover is not coming in very different from our estimates and historical ranges, we 
conclude that the pricing comments are either too optimistic, or the underlying capacity now 
requires substantially higher capex than historical capacity.

Customer prepayments are helpful for CoreWeave, in our opinion, as undrawn credit facilities 
declined to USD5bn in 3Q26 (USD10bn in 2Q26) and cash (including financial investments) 
dipped marginally to USD6,466m (2Q26: USD6,919m). We estimate, CoreWeave needs to 
raise USD33bn of fresh financing in 2027.  
Pivot to short-term contracts not clear 
According to our interactions with investors, one of the bullish thesis on neoclouds recently has 
been the pivot to short-term contracts. These contracts expose a company to higher re-leasing 
risk, but come with significantly higher prices. 
It is not clear to us that CoreWeave has made a material switch to more short-term contracts in 
its business mix. In 2Q26, 41% of total remaining performance obligations of USD103.7bn were 
to be satisfied over the subsequent 24 months. Total RPO rose to USD132.5bn in 3Q26, but the 
ratio that is scheduled to be realized over the next 24 months has fallen to 36%. This suggests 
a more long-term heavy order book.

interest cost assumptions for the company to reflect higher debt and customer financing. There 
is little net change to our long-term non-GAAP EPS estimates.

Expensive valuation 
Despite the recent sell-off, we consider CoreWeave shares expensive. CoreWeave is trading at 
2030e non-GAAP EV/EBIT (after SBC) of 11.7x, which is at a large premium to 6.6x for Oracle 
and slight premium to the multiples of hyperscalers such as Amazon, Microsoft and Meta, even 
though the latter appear to have a much more sustainable and diversified business profiles.  
 
We value CoreWeave using a DCF approach. We derive our TP of USD39 (unchanged) based 
on the following assumptions: a cost of equity of 11.5%, beta of 1.80 (HSBCe), risk-free rate of 
4.25%, market risk premium of 4.0% (both based on HSBC equity strategists’ assumptions), 
long-term pretax cost of debt of 10%, and WACC of 9.7%. Our target price implies 20.0% 
downside and we maintain our Reduce rating. CoreWeave is trading at 46x CY28e non-GAAP 
consensus PE. Our target price implies a CY28e non-GAAP PE of 48x, which is a c5% premium 
to its consensus based multiple. 
Upside risks 
Better-than-expected contract terms with customers, stronger-than-expected demand for GPU 
focused clouds, lower-than-expected competition, and better-than-expected macroeconomic 
and financial conditions.
