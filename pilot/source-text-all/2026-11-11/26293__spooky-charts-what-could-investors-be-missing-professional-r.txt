Beneath the buoyant surface of today’s equity markets lurk several haunting indicators. As bond
yields climb to multi-year peaks, the decade-long regime of cheap money and suppressed yields
has vanished. Yet broader equity markets, particularly in the US, continue to party like there are
no skeletons in the closet.
When we examine the anatomy of the current market environment, several eerie trends reveal
an uncomfortably high degree of investor complacency.
Twin Peaks of Equity Risk
The first chilling signal lies in the mirror image of US household equity allocations and market
valuation multiples.

US retail investors have piled into equities at proportions rarely witnessed in modern market
history. Rather than trimming risk as multiples approach dot-com levels, households have
doubled down. Ignoring the valuation monster at the door, in our view, may leave portfolios with
little liquidity buffer to absorb unexpected market shocks.
The Vanishing Cash Cushion
Equally ghostly is the disappearance of liquidity reserves across global markets.
Chart 2: Implied cash allocation by non-bank investors globally

Cash holdings among non-bank investors worldwide have withered to their lowest levels in
roughly three decades. Investors have thrown caution to the wind, leaving little cash cushion to
protect against unexpected drawdowns or to seize mispriced opportunities if market panic were
to strike.
Leverage at the Witching Hour
The scare factor intensifies when looking at institutional positioning. The world’s largest hedge
funds have increased their balance sheet leverage to elevated levels, running gross leverage
multiples well above historical norms.
Chart 3: Balance Sheet Leverage of World’s Largest Hedge Funds


Source: Federal Reserve Board Financial Stability Report, May 2026 (Figure 3.12). The information

Balance sheet leverage for the top 15 mega-funds has doubled in the past decade essentially
maxing out capacity. When institutional capital is geared heavily into crowded trades, even
modest market adjustments can contribute to rapid deleveraging.
The S&P 500’s Haunted Mirror
Today, the top 10 companies in the US represent roughly 40% of the entire index—all within
technology. To illustrate how narrow market leadership has become: if 485 companies in the
S&P 500 were excluded (including blue chip stalwarts like Johnson & Johnson and Walmart),
the remaining index heavyweights would keep the headline benchmark above its 2020 peak.

The AI CapEx Monster Keeps Growing
Forward 12-month capital expenditure projections among the leading hyperscalers (Alphabet,
Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, and Oracle) continue to swell, with estimates now exceeding
collectively. By comparison, these same tech giants deployed only a fraction of this capital five
years ago.

History offers a chilling reminder: whenever enormous sums of capital are poured into a single
thematic infrastructure buildout over a compressed timeframe—whether 19th-century railroads
or the dot-com telecom expansion—Has historically tended to be followed by over capacity,
fierce price deflation, and depressed shareholder returns.
Escaping the Valuation Nightmare
The combination of sky-high valuations in the US, vanishing cash buffers, and record leverage
etc. creates a fragile setup in our view for investors. We believe that it is unlikely that the
winners of the past decade will repeat their spell over the next ten years.
In our view, rather than sleepwalking into overvalued, crowded trades, investors may be better
placed seeking a diversified approach in portfolios to include a value approach to complement
their growth allocations. The Redwheel Global Intrinsic Value Team believe that portfolio
resilience comes from owning businesses with robust balance sheets and disciplined
valuations that trade at substantial discounts to their intrinsic worth.
