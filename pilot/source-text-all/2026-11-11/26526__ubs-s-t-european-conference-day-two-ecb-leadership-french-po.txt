European Conference Day Two: ECB
Leadership, French Politics, China-EU
Trade
Matthew Cowley
11-Nov-2026, 12:02 ET (EST)
Discussions on day two of the UBS European Conference highlighted mounting political and economic risks in
Europe, widening trade tensions with China, and continued US economic outperformance. Despite these
challenges, corporate sentiment remains resilient, with AI, electrification and infrastructure providing important
structural growth opportunities. To track coverage of the conference, follow this link.
Here are the top takeaways:
Knot's Path To The ECB Top Job Runs Through Paris – Former Dutch central bank chief Klaas Knot looks
like the most likely next president of the ECB despite having no French endorsement, panelists at the UBS
European Conference said. Any indication of support from Paris could sew it up for Knot. The incumbent,
Christine Lagarde, steps step down on Feb. 7, giving EU leaders time to pick a successor before a French
presidential election that polls show far-right candidate Marine Le Pen is favoured to win. The Netherlands has
nominated Knot, while Spain has put forward Pablo Hernández de Cos. Reuters has reported that France
would back Knot provided the post of ECB chief economist goes to a French candidate.
This comes as France is running out of money and time. The government will miss its deficit target, and
France's borrowing premium over Germany has widened to the most since 2012. Against this backdrop, the
ECB would ideally look to prop up France, panelists said. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)
Support On Issues Make France's Le Pen The Favourite – Marine Le Pen, leader of France’s National
Rally (RN) party, is well ahead of her rivals in next year’s presidential race on key topics, which underpins the
support LePen has in polling, according to panelists at the UBS European Conference. Le Pen is around 10pp
ahead of other candidates in polling for the second round. But Le Pen is most trusted on immigration, cost of
living and public services, and that can be more predictive for an election outcome. In the recent US midterms,
while polling underplayed Democratic support, the party was ahead on the key issues by margins close to the
ultimate result. The RN has typically faced a “cordon sanitaire” whereby other parties join forces against the
party in second round voting; this time, the cordon sanitaire seems to be against far left LFI candidate Jean
Luc Melenchon. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)
France's Le Pen Unlikely To Moderate Anti-EU Positions – France's Marine Le Pen is unlikely to moderate
her anti-EU views if elected president next year and is more likely to try to pull Italy’s Giorgia Meloni back into
the nationalist camp rather than follow into moderation, according to panelists at the UBS European
Conference. That means there could be clashes over the big policy items such as EU spending, Ukraine,
sanctions, defense and migration. Le Pen has the firm conviction France can win a contest with the EU and
doesn’t need EU funding in the same way that Italy did when Meloni came to power. For any moderation,
market pressures could be the most significant point of leverage. “All her instincts…work against the EU
institutions and against the EU rules in ways that would make it very difficult for her to temper in the short run,”
said one panelist. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)

Hard To Overstate Growth Of China-EU Trade Imbalance – It’s hard to overstate the rising imbalances in
China-EU trade, according to panelists at the UBS European Conference. Four years ago, China sent 2.75
containers to Europe for every container received from Europe; today that ratio stands at 6.2 containers for
every shipment received from Europe. The EU exports more to Switzerland than it does to China, and its
industrial powerbase is being hollowed out as a result, with job losses of 10,000 a month in Germany. China
recognises the problem: it’s looking at voluntary restrictions of exports and boosting imports. But it’s unclear
how effective these will be. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)
Why Don't Chinese Consume? – One of the big criticisms of China's economic policy is that it has been
unable to drive up domestic consumption, which could reduce some of the exports that are pressuring
countries around the world, according to panelists at the UBS European Conference. It’s going to be hard to
persuade Chinese people to consume more, as they are focused on working hard and saving for the future. In
particular, people are concerned about ensuring they have support for old age. Equally, policymakers are
caught in something of a trap: they look at strong export data and decent GDP growth and think everything is
OK. The trap is that exports are strong because domestic demand is weak. There’s no sense of urgency to
change things precisely because exports are strong. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)
China Likely To Bend On Europe Trade To Avoid Clash – China is likely to give Europe enough ground to
avoid a trade war before a politically important 2027, panelists at the UBS European Conference said. The
weak renminbi is hurting European exporters, though Europe's heavy regulation is also a drag. Chinese
subsidies are also a key source of the imbalance. Beijing credits its strength on innovation, but that strength
also rests on government support. Moreover, its overcapacity is rampant and chronic, producing growth
without profit. With weak domestic consumption, exporters such as BYD rely on profitable markets like Europe.
So, more compromises like the hybrids deal could follow, with China likely to concede just enough to appease
Europe. 2027 is a Communist Party congress year, so Beijing would likely want to avoid a trade war, and it still
needs Europe's affluent consumers. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)
What Companies Said About The Macro Outlook - The overarching message from corporate presentations
on day one of the UBS European Conference is one of resilient but uneven economic growth, persistent
inflationary pressures and increasing reliance on productivity improvements to drive earnings. European
activity appears to be stabilising rather than accelerating, while AI, electrification and infrastructure investment
are creating significant pockets of structural growth. Importantly, corporate confidence remains relatively
strong despite subdued underlying demand, suggesting earnings growth may increasingly outpace economic
growth. Full note here. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)
Democrats Want To Engage With Hyperscalers, Not Stop Them – The Democratic Party isn't anti-data
centre, but it is against the way in which tech companies have built their facilities without any regard or
consultation with local communities. A high-profile former Democratic Senator told the UBS European
Conference the tech companies are going to have to engage with local government and the public in the
future. In particular they will need to address how they are going to meet their energy consumption needs
without causing power shortages or rocketing residential pricing. The tech companies alongside national and
local government will also need to convince the public that US tech build out is critical in the fights against the
competitive threat from China. The run-up to the next presidential election is likely to see a very long roster of
Democratic candidates against a much shorter list from the Republican side vying for the candidacy. For the
Republicans, much depends on whether President Trump endorses JD Vance for the nomination, in which
case most other senior prospective candidates will step back; or Trump remains quiet and allows the
contenders to fight it out amongst themselves. The last time a Presidency remained in the hands of the same
party was in the 1988 election when Reagan handed to Bush senior. Given that and the mid-term results, the
Republicans have a lot of work to do. The panelists thought the Republican party will have to walk a fine line.
The mid-terms revealed that nationally the sentiment had shifted back to a more centrist or at least traditional
political bias in the party. But locally MAGA support could intensify as the movement doubles down on its
requirements and demands. That will be difficult for the party to manage at a national level. (Simon Penn,
Macro Sales, GBR)

US Dynamism Is Increasing the Growth Gap With Europe – Weak productivity and growth since the GFC
have become a broader developed-market problem, with the US the notable exception, according to speakers
at the UBS European Conference. One speaker described a Western European “malaise” of low growth and
low expectations, at a time when the US continues to pull away. The different responses to Covid were
highlighted as one potential factor. European governments used furlough and other support to protect firms
and employment, effectively freezing parts of their economies. Beyond stimulus checks, the US had no such
mechanism available and saw a much sharper initial increase in unemployment, but subsequently
experienced faster labour-market adjustment. One speaker argued this demonstrated the potential benefits of
greater economic dynamism, as workers moved towards better jobs and more productive companies while
weaker firms were allowed to fail. When asked about the divergence of fiscal conditions in developed and
emerging economies since the Covid pandemic, one speaker highlighted developed economies are now
increasingly having to compete for global capital, noting that government investor days, once more associated
with emerging markets, are becoming increasingly relevant for advanced economies too. (Wayne Gouro,
Macro Sales, GBR)
US President Trump Will Keep Pushing AI – President Trump will keep pushing AI as it’s likely to be one of
his main legacies, and there needs to be some federal regulation of the industry, according to panelists at the
UBS European Conference. On the other side, governors and mayors will push back on local regulations. Part
of the problem is that people conflate data centers with perceptions around the threats from AI, primarily lost
jobs. People are nervous because they don’t understand the issues. Data centers will have to undertake a
huge PR exercise to explain the benefits and frame this as a national security issue in the race against China.
Data centers will have explain how they are paying for their own utilities. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)
Opportunity For US Congress To Seek Bipartisan Compromises – A big message from the US midterm
elections was that voters want the two parties to work together in Congress, according to panelists at the UBS
European Conference. While both Democrats and Republicans have to be mindful of their base support, which
are loud, extreme and demanding, the broader voter base for the most part chose moderates. Given
Democrats have majorities in both houses, they will be proposing legislation, and Republicans will not simply
be able to vote against everything. The Democrats are likely to start with a bill to stop members of Congress
and the president from trading stocks; after that they will likely seek to restore cuts made to Medicaid. There
will be opportunities for Republicans to support both pieces of legislation. (Matthew Cowley, Macro Sales, GB)
Future Of OPEC Depends On Saudi Commitment – The departure of the UAE was a significant hit to
OPEC, but did not lead to the collapse of the organisation. The future of OPEC is largely dependent on Saudi
Arabia’s continued commitment, according to a panelist at the UBS European Conference. Saudi Arabia
continues to view the group as a useful tool for managing the oil market and is therefore unlikely to leave, the
speaker said. They added that is a possibility that the UAE could return to the group at some point in the
future. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales, GBR)
Governments, Markets Still Have Time To React To Demographic Change – According to a speaker at the
UBS European Conference, the debate around ageing populations is often framed too dramatically.
Demographic change is real, important and already under way, but it happens in slow motion, giving
governments and markets time to react. Historically, the issue has often been overstated in ways that risk
creating unnecessary angst.The speaker also highlighted misconceptions in the popular debate. One is that
increasing birth rates today will necessarily help pay for pensions in the future. Another is that ageing is
inherently negative for economic growth. Traditional dependency ratios can be too simplistic, they argued, and
should be complemented by measures that account for factors such as health and educational attainment.
(Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales, GBR)
Private Markets Eye AI-Driven Healthcare, Life Sciences – Private Markets Eye AI-Driven Healthcare, Life
Sciences - Life sciences and healthcare, powered by advances in AI, will be among the biggest opportunities
for private markets over the next five to 10 years, panelists at the UBS European Conference said. There is
fundamentally not enough capital going into the sector right now as a lot of it is being directed into AI and data
centres, but healthcare, would be an attractive opportunity in the medium term. Affordable housing, specially

Energy Markets Prove Resilient But Shocks And Demand Growth Vary By Region – The global energy
system has proved more resilient to recent shocks than expected, according to a speaker at the UBS
European Conference. Disruption around Hormuz had less impact as more oil was available at the outset and
Chinese demand weakened, while greater transparency around shipping has helped markets adjust more
efficiently. However, while Europe proved relatively resilient, parts of Asia faced greater pressure on
businesses and living standards as the system balanced through price. The speaker emphasised that energy
security also means different things across economies - in richer countries the focus is largely on affordability,
while reliability becomes increasingly important for middle-income countries and electricity rationing remains a
constraint in lower-income economies. Looking to 2040-50, energy demand in wealthier countries is expected
to remain broadly flat, with growth increasingly coming from emerging economies as rising incomes drive
higher consumption in the form of new demands for flights, cars and other goods. (Nana Antiedu, Macro
Sales, GBR)
Road To Oil Normalisation Will Be Bumpier Than Expected – When thinking about the path to
normalisation in oil markets, one should expect a much bumpier path than is currently priced into the forward
curve, a speaker said at the UBS European Conference. One of the first issues that needs to be resolved is
freight costs, followed by refining capacity. With many countries running refineries at high utilisation rates,
including the US at around 93% and others close to 100%, maintenance requirements could also increase
next year after facilities have been run hard. Another speaker added that even once the conflict is concluded, it
could take another year for oil markets to fully normalise. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales, GBR)

across upstream and transition businesses while bringing in external financing and reducing consolidated
investment requirements. Exploration remains a key strength, with over 1.2 bn barrels of oil equivalent (boe) of
resources discovered this year, supported by internally developed capabilities and increasing use of AI. Capex
is expected to remain around €7 bn per year, including €400-500 mn a year of exploration spending. In
Venezuela, Eni expects FID for Junín-5 by year-end or early next year, with the first phase targeting
200kboe/d within two years and potential to reach 1mboe/d in the early 2030s. (Sofia Martorana, Equity Sales,
