The Commodities Feed: Oil slides on
easing Middle East tensions
Energy markets remain under pressure as hopes for a de-escalation in
the Middle East reduce geopolitical risk premiums. Oil extended its losses
as Gulf supply flows continued to recover, while European gas also
moved lower
Energy - Oil extends losses
Oil prices extended their decline on Tuesday, with Brent falling below $92/bbl and WTI trading
around $86/bbl. The market has continued to unwind some of the geopolitical risk premium
built into prices in recent weeks as expectations grow that the conflict between the US and Iran
could move towards de-escalation.
Signs that regional oil flows are recovering have added to the downward pressure. Crude
exports from the Persian Gulf are estimated to have returned to around 80-85% of pre-war
levels, including pipeline volumes, while refined product shipments have recovered to roughly
35-40%. Reports that Iran may be softening its position on control of the Strait of Hormuz have
also reduced concerns over a prolonged disruption to one of the world’s most important oil
transit routes.

oil imports increased 13% month-on-month in October to around 42mt, or 9.9m b/d, helped by
a recovery in shipments from the Persian Gulf and stronger Iraqi volumes. However, imports
were still around 13% below year-ago levels, highlighting the lingering impact of the conflict
on supply.
Away from the Middle East, disruption to energy infrastructure remains a source of
uncertainty. Ukrainian attacks on Russian refining capacity have continued alongside Russian
strikes on Ukraine’s energy system, while a fire affecting Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline was
contained without a significant interruption to flows.
For now, the return of more Gulf barrels and the prospect of easing tensions are taking some
of the geopolitical premium out of crude. However, with regional negotiations still uncertain
and infrastructure risks persisting, we expect oil prices to remain sensitive to developments in
the Middle East.
Meanwhile, US natural gas moved sharply higher, with Henry Hub rising more than 8% to
above $3.2/MMBtu. Colder forecasts for the eastern US have lifted expectations for heating
demand and prompted short covering, while stronger LNG feedgas demand, including at
Freeport LNG, has provided additional support.
US inventories increased by 77Bcf last week, slightly less than expected, taking total stocks to
3.801Tcf, around 2.8% below the same point last year.
European gas moved in the opposite direction, with front-month TTF trading around €81/MWh.
Expectations for an easing of Middle East tensions have reduced concerns over energy flows
through the Strait of Hormuz, taking some of the geopolitical premium out of European gas
prices.
Metals - Gold slips as rate expectations remain a headwind
Gold edged lower in early trading on Tuesday, with spot prices hovering around $4,100/oz as
expectations for tighter US monetary policy continued to weigh on the market. The move
follows Monday’s rebound, when bullion benefited from a weaker dollar and a sharp fall in oil
prices.
The prospect of another Federal Reserve rate hike remains the key near-term headwind for
gold. Recent comments from Fed officials have maintained a relatively hawkish tone, while
resilient US economic data and lingering inflation concerns have strengthened expectations for
higher rates.
Meanwhile, lower oil prices have eased some of the inflation concerns that had recently
supported expectations for tighter monetary policy. Brent fell sharply on Monday as markets
assessed the possibility of reduced geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, although
uncertainty around the outlook remains elevated.

Central bank demand continues to provide an important source of support. China added to its
gold reserves again in October, extending the central bank’s buying streak to 12 consecutive
months. Continued purchases underline the broader trend of central banks increasing their
exposure to gold and should remain supportive for prices over the longer term.
We remain constructive on gold over the medium term, with central bank buying and broader
geopolitical uncertainty continuing to provide support. However, the prospect of higher US
rates and renewed dollar strength could keep gold under pressure in the near term.
