Benefit and cost discussions demonstrate the growing 
importance of AI economics as a signal to investors, though 
company adoption remains bifurcated  and  ROI assessment 
remains early.  Sectors furthest along in realizing AI benefits are 
also most focused on  cost and workforce efforts.
                      
                      
We leveraged an LLM to analyze approximately 18,000 earnings, conference, and 
investor day transcripts and track how management teams are communicating the 
quantifiable benefits of AI adoption to investors and AI's impact on employment. 
Company discussions suggest AI is moving from experimentation toward 
measurable financial value, even as scrutiny of costs increases. On a trailing 12 
month basis, 44% of S&P 500 companies cited at least one quantifiable benefit 
from AI adoption, up from 32% a year ago. The largest companies are the furthest 
along in measuring AI's impact: on a market cap weighted basis, 54% cite a 
quantifiable benefit, broadly in line with 53% a year ago. Our MS "Adopter" cohort is 
also ahead of the index average, with 49% of companies identified as Adopters by 
our analysts now citing quantifiable benefits, up from 40%. Productivity gain 
remains the most widely cited benefit, quantified by 32% of companies (up from 
25%), but financial impact (e.g., cost savings or revenue generation due to AI) 
has risen to 26% of companies from 16%. That 10pp increase is the largest of any 
benefit category and narrows the gap with productivity from 9pp to 6pp, a sign that 
AI discussions are moving beyond efficiency gains toward measurable effects on 
financial performance.

Companies are also increasingly discussing the costs of adoption. Over the past 
12 months, ~22% of S&P 500 companies cited an AI-related cost, up from ~8%, 
while ~19% cited both a cost and a quantifiable benefit, up from ~6%. Companies 
are paying closer attention to token, inference and API costs as AI usage scales 
across more workflows. These costs have become the dominant category cited, 
mentioned by ~51% of cost-citing companies, up from ~15%, and now exceed 
discussions of compute and infrastructure costs at ~26%. The trend suggests that 
lower unit costs are being accompanied by broader and more frequent model usage, 
keeping total AI spend in focus.
Workforce commentary is broadening as well: ~22% of S&P 500 companies cited 
a direct AI impact on labor, up from ~14% in the prior 12 months. The lower ~18% 
share on a market cap weighted basis (vs. ~27% a year ago) suggests this expansion 
is being driven primarily by mid- and smaller-cap companies. The primary ways 
companies are discussing AI's impact on labor are lower headcount growth, revenue-
headcount decoupling, and AI automation of workers.
Financials is furthest along the curve, both in terms of broadening and 
deepening of AI quantification. Over the past 12 months, 67% of Financials 
companies cited tangible AI-driven improvements, up from 42%, while 46% cited AI-
related costs, up from 14%. Nearly half of Financials companies also cited AI in 
workforce discussions. Importantly, Financials was also the standout on deepening, 
or increased intensity, of AI mentions: the average TTM number of AI mentions per 
Financials company grew 80% Y/Y, the highest of all sectors. Outside of Financials, 
Communication Services (61%) and Information Technology (51%) led other sectors 
on quantifying benefits.

the top 1%. Today, a key debate is if we're seeing enough end-demand for AI to 
support spending across the rest of the AI ecosystem. Ramp data shows that  the

What Are Companies Saying About AI?
One question we frequently receive from investors is: "are companies seeing tangible 
efficiency gains from AI adoption?"  Last fall, in collaboration with the AlphaWise team, we 
leveraged the GPT model to analyze earnings, conference and investor day  transcripts 
to track how management teams are communicating the quantifiable benefits of AI 
adoption to investors.  Since then we have added tracking of AI adoption costs  and AI's 
impact on labor. Today we update that analysis for earnings calls and conference 
transcripts for events that took place during  3Q and the beginning of 4Q (note, earnings 
calls taking place during 3Q were largely those covering 2Q results). We were strict with 
our prompting and only looked for instances where there was a clear link between AI and 
benefits, costs and the employment effects. 
Quantifiable Benefit Categories: (1) Financial Impact, (2) Productivity Gain, (3) Sales, 
Marketing, & Customer Growth, (4) Product Innovation & Development, (5) Governance, 
Risk, & Security, and (6) Other/Cross Functional Benefits.
Quantifiable Cost Categories: (1) Tokens (Inference/API Usage) (2) AI Software & Licenses 
(3) Compute & Infrastructure.
Labor Impact Categories: (1) AI Automation, (2) Layoffs to Fund AI Investment, (3) Lower 
Headcount Growth/Hiring (4) Not Replacing Attrition (5) Revenue/Headcount Growth 
Decoupling (6) Shift in Hiring Mix

How are AI Benefit and Cost Discussions Evolving? 
Benefit mentions are well above year-ago levels, but cost mentions are rising. Over the 
past 12 months, 44% of S&P 500 companies cited at least one measurable benefit from AI 
adoption, up from ~32% in the prior 12 months, and the share has risen steadily 
throughout the year. Among companies our analysts identify as MS AI Adopters, ~49% 
quantified a benefit, up from ~40%, though broadly flat since mid-year. On a market cap 
weighted basis the shares are higher still, at ~54% of S&P 500 market cap and ~55% for 
Adopters, though these are flat to lower versus a year prior (~53% and ~64%,

Talk of AI costs is growing even faster: ~22% of S&P 500 companies discussed 
quantifiable costs over the past 12 months, nearly triple the ~8% a year earlier, and 
among Adopters it was ~31%, up from ~17%. On a market cap weighted basis, ~42% of 
S&P 500 market cap cited AI costs (from ~33%). Analyst questions frequently trigger 
cost discussions; 40% of S&P 500 companies have received questions from analysts 
on AI adoption costs.  
Overlap between AI costs and benefits is growing. Over the past 12 months, ~19% of 
S&P 500 companies mentioned both an AI cost and a quantifiable AI benefit, roughly 
three times the ~6% a year earlier; among MS AI Adopters the share rose to ~24% from 
~13%. Most companies discussing AI costs also point to a measurable benefit: ~85% in the 
S&P 500 (from ~76%) and ~77% among Adopters (from ~74%). The reverse is lower but 
rising fast: ~43% of S&P 500 companies citing benefits also mentioned costs, up from 
~20%, and ~48% of Adopters, up from ~32%. Put differently, the majority of companies 
still describe AI benefits without discussing costs, though that is changing. Even within the 
overlap group, most companies discuss costs and benefits separately; only a handful 
compare the two directly or quantify net return on total AI spend.

 To better understand sector-level adoption, we analyzed the percentage of companies 
within each S&P 500 sector that referenced tangible AI-driven improvements over the 
past 12 months. Financials take the top spot, with 67% of companies quantifying an AI 
benefit, followed by Communication Services (61%) and Technology (51%). On a year-
over-year basis, Financials also saw the largest increase (from 42% to 67%), followed 
by Consumer Staples (28% to 43%), Health Care (23% to 38%) and Communication 
Services (47% to 61%, albeit from a small base). Technology (42% to 51%) and 
Industrials (35% to 44%) rose more modestly, and Materials was the only sector to decline 
(25% to 21%).
Importantly, Financials is also a standout when we look at a "deepening" of AI 
mentions (average number of mentions per company), with TTM mentions up 80% Y/
Y. More penetrated industries like Tech and Comms Services saw mentions per company 
roughly flat-to-up, while less penetrated industries like Energy, Industrials, Real Estate, 
Healthcare, Consumer Discretionary, and Materials saw a greater deepening of mentions 
off of a lower base.
Cost disclosure remains narrower but is broadening. Financials continue to lead, with 46% 
of the sector citing AI-related costs (up from 14%), ahead of Technology at 35% (from 
21%) and Industrials at 24% (from 6%). Communication Services (22%), Consumer 
Discretionary (16%), Real Estate (13%) and Health Care (13%) follow, while Consumer 
Staples, Energy, Materials and Utilities remain in single digits. Most companies citing costs 
also point to a return: 31 of 35 cost-citing Financials quantified a benefit, as did 21 of 25 in 
Technology and 15 of 19 in Industrials.

earlier. On a market cap weighted basis, however, the share has fallen, both for the S&P

being driven by mid- and smaller-cap companies rather than the largest names.
