USD: 2-year surges providing renewed momentum for the dollar 
We didn’t think we would have a near 20bp move in the 2-year yield yesterday and 
while we can quibble about the justification (we will a little) the move serves as an 
important reminder – fundamentals matter! The focus had very much shifted to the 
political fallout and potential AI consequences of the mid-term election results but a 
stronger than expected CPI print that followed hawkish comments from Fed Governor 
Waller were enough to prompt the biggest jump in the 2-year UST bond yield since it 
was matched in April 2024 (Liberation Day). The previous bigger move was back in 
October 2024. That was the day of a blowout NFP that prompted a rethink by the 
markets over its pricing of a 50bp cut at the November meeting. The Fed cut by a 
smaller 25bp at the FOMC meetings that followed.  
On this occasion the market rethink is to consider the prospect of a 50bp hike. This is 
only now being considered because of Waller’s comments prior to the CPI report rather 
than the CPI report itself. Waller’s comment that a stronger CPI print may warrant a

50bp hike has sparked a debate over whether Waller had knowledge of the CPI print. 
If he did, then there was intent in his comment to try and steer the market in the 
direction it has now gone. The OIS market is now partially pricing the risk of a 50bp 
hike on 9th December. Waller makes the point that the sticky inflation and energy risks 
warrant the reversal of the 75bps of cuts and hence why wait two meetings to do that. 
In regard to Waller’s knowledge, I guess in a way we may never know but taking the 
publicly available information on procedure, the Office of Management and Budget 
manages and issues statistical policy directives and states clearly that information on 
data releases are strictly limited to a small number of designated officials within the 
producing agency. So our assumption is that when Waller spoke (in London 4.5hrs 
prior to the CPI release) he did not know the data. Still, he’s certainly hawkish and 
along with Fed President Logan who also called for “more rapid rate increases” means 
there may well be FOMC members pushing for a more aggressive response in 
December.  
We remain sceptical of the need to hike by 50bps at this stage and suspect the majority 
will see the better risk-reward in going by 25bps. We still have another payrolls report 
(but no further CPI prints, although it’s the day after) before the FOMC meeting and a 
strong print plus a rebound in crude oil after the recent sharp drop would be one 
scenario of a possible more aggressive hike. It’s a scenario that might add to the 
reasons for President Trump to push for a deal with Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz 
and end the conflict.   
It’s still more a risk than a core view but it would alter the current balanced expectations 
in the markets across key central banks and shift rate hike expectations much more in 
favour of the dollar that would likely prompt a further lurch lower in EUR/USD and bring 
levels below the 1.1000-level into play.

JPY: JGBs sell off but individuals buying picks up notably 
The yen weakened sharply yesterday in response to the US inflation data and 
USD/JPY has returned closer to the 160-level, above which intervention becomes a 
much greater prospect. The JGB market has seen a heavy sell-off as well in response 
to the UST bond market move yesterday although it is noticeable that there has been

very little steepening of the curve. 2s10s is 2bps steeper but 2s30s is flat. There 
continue to be signs that confidence in the JGB market has improved, no doubt helped 
by the more active BoJ and the general consensus that within one or two hikes, the 
BoJ would be at the neutral policy rate. The move today followed a JGB auction 
yesterday that recorded a bid-to-cover of 3.36, slightly weaker than the 12mth average. 
Nonetheless, sentiment has certainly improved and there was further evidence of that 
today with the FT reporting of a strong pick-up in demand for JGBs amongst Japanese 
households. This could be a significant point in a potential gradual shift in flows 
reflecting the better returns available domestically. With inflation more elevated and 
now the BoJ formally confirming the achievement of the 2% inflation goal, households 
have to focus more on ensuring their savings are protected in real terms. Historically 
that required investing abroad, but now domestic assets offer a choice. Data in 
September revealed that 45% of households’ USD 16trn of household assets is parked 
in cash deposits (USD 7.1trn).  
The data confirming this pick-up in JGB buying by Japanese households comes from 
the MoF. The MoF has an entire portion of its website dedicated to JGBs for individuals 
and issuance data there shows a marked pick-up in issuance to individuals with the 
November total for 3,5 and 10-year totalling JPY 2,026bn, the largest single-month 
total. In August there was a new record of JPY 1,108bn with another record in 
September underlining the notable pick-up in recent months. This coincides with 
Finance Minister Katayama earlier this year calling on investors to invest more in 
domestic assets. There is also growing speculation that the government will soon make 
changes to incentivise JGB buying by including individual JGB holdings in tax-free 
NISA accounts and/or removing these holdings from inheritance tax.  
Early signs of a shift in flows perhaps, and the more examples of this we start to see 
the more compelling the case will become of a more sustained turnaround in the multi-
year trend of yen weakness. With intervention likely halting the prospects to the upside, 
and confidence in the BoJ improving, the risks certainly look more skewed to yen 
strength over the medium term.
