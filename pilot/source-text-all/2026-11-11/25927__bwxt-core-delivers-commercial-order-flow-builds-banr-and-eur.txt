BWXT: Core Delivers, Commercial Order Flow Builds;
BANR and Europe Add Optionality. Maintain BUY,
$192 PT
Key Message: We reiterate our Buy rating/$192PT on BWXT post-3Q26. BWXT's 3Q26
missed revenue estimates, but beat on EBITDA and EPS. Mgmt held its 2026 guidance
at the midpoint, with the low end of free cash flow guidance raised. The preliminary
2027 outlook calls for HSD-LDD growth in adjusted EBITDA and EPS, a slight step-
up from the investor day disclosure. The upcoming catalysts are U.S. commercial new-
build orders, expected by about 1H27, and more BANR wins after Janus and Prodigy.
European demand, led by Poland, is potential upside not in the outlook. The core
business continues to exhibit strong performance and the conversion of the order pipeline
seems to be a matter or when, not if.
BWXT reported total revenue of $953M (+10% YoY, +7% organic) missing cons. at
$970.5M.
• Government Operations revenue of $639.1M (+4% YoY) missed cons. at $658.1M.
• Commercial Operations revenue of $314.2M (+25% YoY, +15% organic) beat cons. at
$302.5M.
EBITDA margin of 18.9% (+145bps YoY) beat cons. at 17.4%
• Government Operations margin of 22.6% (+340bps YoY) beat cons. at 20.2%.
• Commercial Operations margin of 13.2% (-100bps YoY) missed cons. at 13.6%.
Adj. EPS, Adj. EBITDA, & FCF — Adj. EPS $1.26 (+26% YoY) vs. $1.18 Street, Adj.
EBITDA $179.7M (+19% YoY), FCF $150.5M (+59% YoY),
Guidance — BWXT reaffirmed 2026 revenue guidance at ~$3.8B, and narrowed the Adj.
EBITDA outlook to ~$667M (midpoint of previously disclosed range). Non-GAAP EPS range
was lowered to $4.72-$4.77, though the midpoint of $4.75 remained stable. Last, the FCF
range rose to $350M-$360M (from $340M-$360M). For 2027, mgmt's introductory guidance
is in line with its previously issued outlook at its investor day, revenue growth of HSD, HSD-
LDD EBITDA and adj EPS growth, and we note the adj EBITDA growth is higher than the
initial view provided at the investor day. FCF is guided to be flat to up slightly which is
generally in line with Street.
• Commercial nuclear awards expected between now and 1H27; BANR taking center
stage. Management still expects the first new U.S. reactor plant-build orders as soon as
year-end 2026 and no later than roughly 1H27. It called TVA's ten-reactor opportunity
at Clinch River and Bellefonte one of the closest in line, with AP1000 builds after that.
We think the timing risk is manageable. BWXT's 2030 framework ($5.5-6.0B revenue,
$1.1-1.2B adjusted EBITDA) only assumes modest success on commercial new builds,
so a slip of a quarter or two shouldn't pressure the long-term targets. The bigger change
this quarter is BANR, the company's advanced microreactor, which has moved from
concept to customer wins. It was selected for the U.S. Army's Janus program, which
plans more than 20 microreactors across five bases, starting with site selection at Fort
Campbell. It was also chosen for Prodigy Clean Energy's transportable plant in New
Brunswick, BWXT's first international microreactor win. Separately, a multi-year deal to
supply 1 metric ton of TRISO fuel to Antares adds a fuel business on the same production
line.
• Energy security in Europe puts nuclear demand in focus. With nuclear interest
escalating in Europe as noted by management, countries treat energy security as
a national priority. Poland stood out as a recent example. The CEO recently met
government and business leaders there and described strong demand for nuclear to
meet both energy security and decarbonization goals. He also described real openings
for BWXT to deploy microreactors and to join the SMR supply chain, and said demand

BWXT will only invest in Polish manufacturing capacity if firm orders and government

Higher earnings should lift operating cash flow, but management noted several items will
absorb much of that gain. The first is continued spending on working capital and CapEx to
support growth, with core spend expected to hover around 5–6% of revenue. The second
is a pension contribution headwind of about $10 million, which mgmt. called normal course
and tied mainly to the Kinetrix acquisition. The third is the timing of milestone payments
on newer special materials programs, especially high-purity depleted uranium (HPDU)
and the enrichment work. We note that mgmt. characterized this headwind as a modest
drag from investing for growth and some one-off items, with the core businesses still
converting earnings into operating cash well.

Valuation:
We arrive at our $192 PT by applying a 23x EV/EBITDA multiple to our 2028 EBITDA estimate. This compares to the company’s historical
average multiple of 19x and the current peer group average multiple of 13.7x. We believe the premium is warranted given the company’s
monopolistic position in the government nuclear market along with its positioning in the next-generation commercial and government
nuclear market supply chain.
Risks:
• U.S. Government revenue concentration and DOE/NNSA appropriations exposure
• Naval propulsion cadence and undefined AUKUS scope
• Fixed-price contract execution and EAC volatility
• Single-source supply chain and specialty materials exposure
• Skilled-labor and security-clearance constraints
• Nuclear safety liability and indemnity ceilings
• Canadian market concentration and acquisition integration
