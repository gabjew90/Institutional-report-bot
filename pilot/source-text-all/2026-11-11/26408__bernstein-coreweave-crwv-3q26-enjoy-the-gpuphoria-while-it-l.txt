CoreWeave (CRWV) 3Q26: Enjoy the GPUphoria while it lasts
CoreWeave delivered about the best quarter we could have asked for. They’re continuing
to mature as a company, executing better with every print, and managing the narrative
more competently than they ever have. Stats were good-to-great, funding concerns were
addressed, the strategic moves make sense, and, much as we were after 2Q earnings, we’re
left grappling with our Underperform.
We want to be believers, especially with the stock off 65% from its May high. If we were
sitting in Intrator’s chair, we’d make many of the same calls, and we congratulate the CRWV
team on the trajectory. But we just can’t get over our long-term fundamental concerns about
this business model. To make the case work, you have to believe:
1) Data center supply stays tight, keeping pricing for near-term capacity extremely
elevated. CRWV is “seasoning” their capacity, electing to not offer 2028 GWs today, but
rather to wait until they can lock in higher pricing (referencing $40M/MW). Hey, it worked for
SPCX (covered by Harned). But with an incremental 55GW+ announced between the major
cloud providers, neoclouds, and SPCX through 2028, we are extremely skeptical this holds.
2) Funding keeps getting easier. Prepayments help. They now run 15-50% of contract
value of every new deal, up from a 15-25% average at YE25. But the whole point of a multi-
year cloud deal is turning upfront capex into longer-dated opex...we’re not sure customers
keep paying upfront, especially once supply eases.
3) CoreWeave reaches true hyper-scale and flips its economics: self-funding,
investment-grade, and head-to-head with the big clouds. Today, Q3 interest of $846M was
more than 2x AOI, debt rose $11.8B in a quarter to $46.9B against $4.0B of equity, and
adjusted NI doesn’t turn positive until 1H28. Getting there takes either a wildly prolonged
supply-demand imbalance, or a pivot where software becomes a high-margin moat and/or
enterprise becomes defensible. We’ve written about why that’s unlikely, but could happen.
So what do we do with the stock? We maintain our Underperform and $54 PT over the
long term, but the near term is more nuanced. Over the next few months, we see a floor of
$45–54, with possible upside to $64–73 if AI sentiment turns. Execution keeps improving;
the fundamentals remain the overhang.

Investment Implications
We are Underperform on CRWV with a price target of $54. CRWV benefits from supply
constraint in the data center market, which undoubtedly continues to exist today; however, as
capacity eases, which we anticipate, this company will be among the first and hardest hit. We
value the company on a 22.5x EV/EBIT basis (previously 23.5x).

WHAT YOU HAVE TO BELIEVE (AND WHY WE DON’T)
SPOT PRICING STAYS WILDLY TIGHT
The bull case requires spot pricing to stay exceptionally tight, sustaining premium returns on short-term contracts and older-
generation GPU renewals even as industry supply expands. Investors must believe that compute demand will continue to
outpace new capacity and that older GPUs will remain economically competitive despite technological obsolescence. In this
scenario, recontracting fully paid-off infrastructure becomes a meaningful source of cash flow; if scarcity eases or newer
architectures displace older hardware faster than expected, that upside could prove less durable.
CRWV discussed two cohorts of GPUs rolling off contract in 2027 and 2028, respectively, with loose suggestions of the ARR
value. The implicit suggestion is pricing in the $20-25M/MW range, suggesting spot pricing for 3-5 year-old GPUs rivals long-
term contract pricing for new GPUs. We buy that this may be true today, but take a more conservative view in our model given
our perspective on supply.

In order to maintain the extremely tight supply and high pricing we have today, market-wide demand must also multiply many
times over. We believe this is extremely unlikely.

EVENTUALLY REACH REAL HYPERSCALE
The bull case also requires CoreWeave's scale to translate into hyperscale economics, with a larger installed base, broader
enterprise adoption, and growing internally generated cash flow driving durable operating leverage and reducing reliance
on external equity. The expansion in adjusted operating margin from approximately 1% in 1Q to 10% in 3Q provides early
evidence of that potential, but investors must believe these benefits can persist beyond today's unusually favorable pricing
environment. Ultimately, scarcity-driven profits must provide a bridge to a self-funding cloud platform, rather than a capital-
intensive infrastructure business whose growth is repeatedly offset by financing costs, dilution, and hardware replacement
needs. The challenge is that scaling the business also increases borrowing needs and interest expense, creating a funding
burden that established hyperscalers are better positioned to absorb through cash flows from their mature businesses.

THE PRINT ITSELF
CoreWeave's 3Q26 beat reinforced near-term growth visibility, but longer-term debates remains unresolved. Revenue
of $3.66B increased 168% YoY and approximately 42% sequentially, exceeding the prior $3.45B-$3.60B guidance range as
the company continued converting contracted capacity into revenue-generating deployments. Revenue backlog increased
to $132.5B, up approximately 138% YoY and 27% sequentially. Management indicated that more than 50% of backlog is
attached to contracts where customer delivery is expected to commence by year-end, and that qualified demand exceeds
available capacity through 2027 by roughly 10x. While we acknowledge that 3Q further validates CoreWeave's near-term
demand visibility, deployment execution, and ability to monetize scarce capacity, our longer-term concerns remain focused on
the durability of pricing, contract economics, and residual GPU values in a more challenging competitive environment.
Operating leverage accelerated ahead of plan, supported by stronger pricing and premium short-term contracts.
Adjusted EBITDA increased to $2.26B, up 169% YoY and 49% sequentially, with EBITDA margin expanding to 62% from
59% in 2Q. Most notably, adjusted operating income increased to $379M, or a 10% margin, from $128M and a 5% margin
in 2Q, exceeding the prior $200M-$260M guidance range and achieving double-digit margins one quarter ahead of plan.
Management attributed the improvement to deployment execution, stronger pricing, and a greater contribution from premium-
priced short-term contracts. Following approximately 25% price increases across SKUs in July, CoreWeave implemented
another approximately 10% increase later in the quarter, with pricing continuing to rise in 4Q. Management cited annual
revenue per MW of more than $40M for short-term opportunities versus $20M-$25M for new four- and five-year contracts.
These economics are constructive, although the contribution from short-duration pricing also makes the sustainability of current
margins more sensitive to the supply-demand environment as capacity rolls over.
Power deployment remained on track, with international expansion broadening the capacity pipeline. CoreWeave
ended 3Q with more than 1.7GW of active power, versus approximately 1.5GW in 2Q, bringing year-to-date additions to more
than 850MW and doubling its active footprint since year-end 2025. Management maintained its year-end active-power target
of more than 1.85GW. Contracted power reached 4.6GW, including more than 1.3GW outside the US across Canada, Europe,
and Asia. The company entered India and Indonesia during the quarter while expanding its presence in Denmark and Spain.
Management does not anticipate material risks to 2027 deliveries, citing fully permitted sites already under construction,
and noted that the overwhelming majority of its Texas power is either online or fully permitted and under construction. These
disclosures support near-term delivery visibility, but the scale of the remaining buildout continues to require coordination across
power, equipment, networking, cooling, and financing.

Managed inference is scaling rapidly, while GPU renewals offer meaningful upside dependent on future pricing and
the resilience of older-generation hardware. Booked managed-inference ARR has already exceeded $250M, reaching
the threshold previously targeted for year-end, with growth constrained primarily by allocated capacity. CoreWeave also
highlighted Forge as an enterprise entry point: 70% of new cloud customers added in 3Q were already Forge customers, 10%
of Fortune 500 companies are now customers, and financial services accounts for approximately 10% of backlog. Separately,
management provided more detail on the opportunity to recontract its installed fleet, with more than 130,000 GPUs coming
off contract in 2027 and approximately 170,000 in 2028. At current pricing, management estimates these renewals could
generate nearly $4B of annualized revenue from the 2027 cohort and nearly $10B when including the 2028 cohort, without
incremental deployment CapEx. These figures represent a recontracting opportunity outside reported backlog, rather than
committed calendar-year revenue or simply a pricing uplift over existing contracts. Management's disclosure that Ampere and
Hopper pricing increased 30%-50% sequentially supports the current value of older-generation capacity, but extrapolating
those economics through 2027-28 remains a key underwriting assumption.
Prepayments and secured lending strengthen the funding plan, but financing and dilution risks remain. CapEx totaled
$14.5B in 3Q, above the prior $11.5B-$13.5B guidance range, as certain deliveries were pulled forward from 4Q. CoreWeave
reported approximately $6.5B of cash, restricted cash, and marketable securities, nearly $5B of undrawn committed DDTL
capacity, and approximately $9B of expected customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months, including more
than $3B related to recontracting existing, fully paid-off infrastructure. Weighted-average debt cost declined to 7.9%, down
250 bps YoY, and management expects its next DDTL to finance more than 90% of associated CapEx at an implied yield of
7.0%-7.5% based on current SOFR. Management estimates that less than $5B of incremental parent-level capital will be
required through 2027 and stated that existing contracted cash flows should service and repay both asset-level and parent-
level debt, before renewals. These disclosures are constructive, but do not eliminate financing or dilution risk. In-quarter, the
company also reported approximately $4.2B of convertible-note financing and established an ATM program for up to 35M
shares. Customer prepayments improve upfront liquidity but should not be confused with incremental earnings or revenue
beyond the underlying contracts.
