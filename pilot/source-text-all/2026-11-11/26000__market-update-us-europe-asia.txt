U.S. Markets Recap 
US +0.31% | Nasdaq +1.09% | Dow -0.46%
Synopsis

US equities ended mostly higher on Friday, though off the best levels. Breadth was somewhat negative and the equal­weight S&P lagged the 
cap­weighted index by about 70bp. The AI trade supported the indices, with semis and semicaps, memory and neoclouds all largely stronger, 
and TSLA­US and NVDA­US were the notable Mag 7 gainers. Energy, airlines, biotech, steel, space, nuclear and quantum computing out­
performed, while media, road/rail, managed care, P&C insurers, restaurants, retail/apparel, homebuilders, staples and utilities lagged. AI 
and momentum names bounced after Thursday's sharp selloff on oversold conditions, multiple contraction and better sentiment around 
datacenter pushback, helped by positive comments from Texas governor­elect Hinojosa, a $25B buyback boost at LRCX­US and IREN­US 
results. Most names only pared Thursday's steep declines, however, with the SOX still down more than 12% for the week, and sentiment 
remains fragile. 


earnings rose 0.4% m/m versus 0.3% expected, reinforcing expectations for a December rate hike. Treasuries were mostly weaker with the

Macro

October payrolls beat sharply; December hike odds rise to about 80%
October nonfarm payrolls rose 165K vs +65K consensus, with 84K in upward revisions lifting the 3M average to +137K from +51K. Goldman 
raised its underlying hiring estimate to 124K from 51K, though analysts noted seasonal payback and technical factors contributed to the 
strength, with UBS attributing 68K of revisions to seasonal adjustments. Gains were led by professional/business services (+70K), health 
care (+42K), and transportation/warehousing (+25K), with AI­related demand potentially supporting hiring. Unemployment fell to 4.14% 
despite higher participation, while AHE rose 0.4% m/m, though annual wage growth remained somewhat subdued at 3.1%. Analysts noted 
the labor market is stable and not a major inflationary concern, with inflation data still viewed as the primary driver of Fed policy. UBS 
expects December/January 25 bp hikes, while Citi maintained its December hike forecast but sees a potential hold if the tech selloff contin­
ues. FedWatch December hike odds rose to ~80% following the report, up from 67% yesterday. 

NY Fed's Williams: bringing inflation to target is 'absolutely essential'
Speaking at an ECB conference in Frankfurt, NY Fed President Williams (voter) said inflation is 'absolutely too high' and it is essential to 
bring it back down to the 2% target in a timely manner and on a sustained basis (Reuters, Bloomberg). However, Williams also said inflation 
expectations remain well anchored and wage growth has been consistent with the 2% goal. He also said the October FOMC statement 
correctly captured the need to accumulate more data. There is likely to be some discussion about whether Williams' remarks are consistent 
with comments he made on 13­Oct about a lack of urgency, expectations for an additional hike before year­end, and no sign yet the Fed 
may need to hike in 2027 (speech, Bloomberg). The speech follows Cleveland Fed's Hammack (nonvoter) reiterating a hawkish stance and 
Barkin (nonvoter) advocating gradual tightening (Reuters). 

Trump says decision time on Iran as negotiators seek nuclear breakthrough
President Trump said the time has come for a decision on whether to make a deal or strike Iran with the aim of crippling its nuclear program. 
Axios reported Trump met with his national security advisers Thursday and is considering three options: new strikes, continuing the eco­
nomic pressure campaign, or negotiating a deal that includes significant concessions to Iran. Mediators are continuing efforts to forge 
consensus, with a draft proposal calling for confidence­building steps over two days. The first day would see the US end its naval blockade 
and partially release frozen Iranian assets, while Tehran agrees to invite IAEA inspectors to bombed nuclear sites. The second day would 
see Hormuz reopened and nuclear talks resume, focused on Iran's uranium stockpiles. NY Times sources said Iran is willing to re­engage 
in nuclear talks following weeks of refusal and is reviewing a set of proposals, and a Reuters source said Iran sent the US a nuclear proposal 
via Qatar if its seven demands are met. Crude is tracking its largest weekly decline since April following dovish signals from the White House 
and Iran. 

Democratic midterm gains weigh on AI and data center sentiment
Markets are grappling with the implications for AI following big Democratic gains in the midterm elections. Sentiment has deteriorated 
sharply amid concerns of a tougher political environment for cloud operators, particularly in states with a big data center presence like 
Texas, where incoming Governor Hinojosa has vowed to impose a moratorium on development. SpaceX and smaller cloud operators like 
CoreWeave, in debt­fueled expansion mode, are seen as more vulnerable to political risks, driving CDS to a record high (Bloomberg). 
Hyperscalers lost some ground, though Thursday's losses were mild relative to prior session gains. Positive takes note that scrutiny on the 
data center buildout may act as a handbrake on debt­funded overinvestment, with the group also seeing moat benefits from more stringent 
regulation of upstarts. The same dynamic is spun as negative for semis and memory producers if a slower pace of development impacts 
demand for chips and related equipment. 

Nasdaq in correction after record one-day loss of market value
The market recovered from Thursday's sharp drop, which saw the S&P and Nasdaq post their worst days since the April 2025 tariff shock. 
The Nasdaq slipped into correction territory, closing down nearly 12% from its 6­Oct record close, and saw its largest­ever decline in market 
cap at $2.49T. Big tech and the AI trade were at the epicenter, with the Mag 7 losing a collective ~$1T in value and the SOX logging its 
worst day since March 2020. BTIG observed that more than 80% of Thursday's NYSE volume was in declining stocks, the first 80%+ day in 
nearly a year. However, the S&P saw only nine more decliners than gainers and equal­weight RSP outperformed the cap­weighted index by 
146 bp (and topped it by 486 bp for the six sessions through Thursday). Nearly 63% of S&P constituents ended Thursday above their 50­
day moving averages (and 57.4% above their 200 dmas), a slip from Wednesday's post­election highs but still well above weaker levels from 
the end of September.

European Markets Recap 
Germany -1.9% | France -0.8% | UK -1%
Synopsis

European equities finished sharply lower on Friday, though off the worst levels. Travel & Leisure was the only sector in positive territory, 
with Technology and Real Estate also relative outperformers, while Insurance, Media and Retail were among the underperformers. Tech­
nology rebounded on selective dip­buying in oversold semiconductor names, and airlines rebounded within Travel & Leisure. Retail was the 
biggest decliner, led by apparel and diversified retailers, and Media, Personal & Household Goods, Insurance, Health Care and Food & 
Beverage also fell as investors rotated back into some cyclical plays. Some on the Street see European equities as an attractive diversification 
away from AI exposure, supported by strong Q3 profit momentum and European equity fund inflows of $13.25B, the largest weekly intake 
since 12­Aug (LSEG Lipper). 

German industrial production beat expectations after Thursday's jump in factory orders, Spanish industrial production was also firmer than 
expected, and the French trade deficit unexpectedly widened in September. Eurozone inflation accelerated to 4.0% in October and markets 
lean toward a December ECB hike. Sell­side takeaways were split on the next BoE move after Thursday's hawkish hike, with markets pricing 
more than a 50% chance of a December increase. UK 10­year gilt yields rose 3bp, while German Bund and French OAT yields were broadly 
unchanged. 

Outokumpu fell 15.7% after forecasting Q4 EBITDA near €10M versus €105M expected, Stabilus lost 12% after cutting its 2030 ambitions, 
and Man Group dropped 7.6% after a London High Court ruling largely favored Kuwait's public pension fund. Rating changes weighed on 
Kuehne & Nagel (­4%) and Rheinmetall (­3.4%), while IAG rose 2% and Pirelli 0.8% on a Q3 beat. 
Macro

ECB hawkish rhetoric builds as eurozone inflation reaches 4.0%
Hawkish ECB­speak picked up momentum this week, with high­profile policy officials like Nagel and Kocher increasingly signalling the 
need for further tightening as eurozone inflation accelerated to 4.0% in October, double the ECB's target. Markets lean toward a December 
hike, although the ECB wage tracker offered some reassurance that second­round effects remain contained. Commentators noted the key 
policy dilemma is whether the energy shock becomes entrenched or whether tighter financial conditions and weaker demand can contain 
inflation. The eurozone composite PMI slipped to a four­month low of 51.7 in October, while elevated energy costs continue to squeeze 
businesses and households, and low gas inventories and the risk of prolonged disruption around the Strait of Hormuz leave Europe vulner­
able heading into winter. 

BoE raises rates 25bp to 4%; economists split on the next move
The BoE delivered a hawkish 25bp rate hike to 4%, with policymakers signalling further tightening may be needed as inflation is forecast to 
reach ~5% in Q2'27, although timing will depend on energy prices. The language, macroeconomic forecasts and voting pattern were more 
hawkish than expected, and a majority of takeaways anticipate another rate increase, but whether the BoE hikes in December or waits until 
early 2027 will be determined by Middle East and energy price developments. Governor Bailey said the hike was necessary due to the extent 
of inflation in the pipeline, including expectations of a large increase in household energy bills in January. The vote was 8­1, with Mann 
calling for a 50 bps hike, while Dhingra and Taylor indicated a one­off move. Markets are pricing up to three rate increases over the next 
year, with an approximately 50% chance of a hike in December. 

German industrial production rises 0.7% in September, beating expectations
German industrial production rose 0.7% m/m in September, extending recovery for a second consecutive month after a large downward 
revision to August's gain (Handelsblatt). Economists had expected stagnation. The increase was driven mainly by a post­holiday 17.1% 
rebound in automotive output and an 8.0% rise in pharmaceuticals, following earlier weakness linked to changeovers and holidays. Con­
struction declined 2.6% and machinery fell 6.5%, leaving the broader industrial picture more mixed than the headline suggests. In a less 
volatile three­month comparison, production in Q3 was 0.4% lower than in Q2, and residual risks from elevated energy costs and the pro­
spect of further ECB rate hikes continue to weigh. Some manufacturers, including BMW (BMW­DE) and Audi (VOW.3­DE), recently cut 
guidance, while supplier Schaeffler (SHA0­DE) is closing sites and reducing headcount. 

EU leaders aim to appoint Lagarde's successor at the December summit
Reuters cited a senior Eurozone official who said on Wednesday that EU leaders aim to appoint ECB President Lagarde's successor at the 
mid­December summit, which does not leave a lot of time to complete formal steps. Eurozone governments have to put forward candidates 
by Friday and Eurozone finance ministers plan to confirm nominations and the process on Monday. The Dutch government nominated 
former Dutch central bank chief Knot on Wednesday (DutchNews.nl). Spain is expected to nominate BIS chief and former Spanish central 
bank governor de Cos, who has already received backing from Spanish PM Sanchez (eutoday). Germany's Nagel is reported to be keen on 
the role and there could still be surprise nominations. The official said that if formal steps take longer, the new ECB chief could be appointed 
in January. In comments on Wednesday, Knot said the ECB would have to stick with its hawkish stance and be wary of shocks (Bloomberg). 

UK-EU reset estimated to lift UK economy by about 17B pounds
The UK Treasury has revised up its estimate for the UK­EU reset, confirming it could provide a 0.4% long­term increase for the economy, 
worth approximately £17B. While the number is a relatively modest boost, there are thoughts that the real prize is the potential for much

Synopsis

Asian equities ended sharply lower on Friday, although benchmarks largely closed away from their lows. South Korea, Taiwan and Japan's 
two main boards each came close to a 10% decline in early trading before recovering, with the Kospi (­6.02%) and Nikkei (­5.96%) among 
the hardest hit. Greater China stocks were lower but off their troughs, declines were shallower in Australia, Singapore and Southeast Asia, 
and India's benchmarks again bucked the trend with gains. The Kospi, Taiex and Nikkei 225 all fell near 10% at one point, which triggered 
a trading halt in Seoul before authorities announced they would suspend some margin rules to stem the slide. The technology selloff deep­
ened on a poor handover from the US, and the won and Taiwan dollar came under pressure as overseas investors exited. 

Sovereign bond yields fell as investors sought safe havens, with JGB yields notably lower and CGBs briefly touching a record low. Minutes 
from the BOJ's September meeting showed members backed continuing rate hikes, with one arguing for a faster pace if signs of upward 
price deviation emerged. 

Defensive and value names outperformed, including Australia financials and utilities, Hong Kong oil & gas and bank names, and China 
financials, while Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix, TSMC, SoftBank and Kioxia led technology losses. Japan Tobacco fell 19.3% after four 
Russian manufacturing entities were placed under temporary administration, and Nintendo lost 7.9% on H1 revenue down 12% y/y. Sin­
gapore Post rose 6.9% on higher H1 adjusted net income. Qantas raised fares on higher fuel prices and Nippon Steel lifted its FY26 profit 
forecast by more than 50%. 
Macro

Asia equities plunge and bonds rally in tech selloff, but indexes end off lows
Asia stock markets ended lower Friday but with many stocks and benchmarks away from their lows. The MSCI Asia Pac ex Japan index fell 
around 3.7% from a 5.8% dip earlier. Technology stocks were worst off: Samsung Electronics (005930­KR) fell 6.4%, SK Hynix (000660­
KR) ­10.4%, TSMC (2330­TW) ­4.7%, SoftBank (9984­JP) ­10.8%, Kioxia (285A­JP) ­8.7% and Advantest (6857­JP) ­5.0% at the close. 
South Korea's Kospi dipped below 5K briefly before recovering after Seoul announced a suspension of margin maintenance rules to stem 
forced selloffs (Chosun), following an 8% decline at the open that triggered a trading halt. Taiwan's Taiex was down 4.5% and in a technical 
bear market, and the Nikkei 225 was down nearly 6%, also in a bear market. Traditional safe havens gold and yen saw some early buying, 
since largely reversed, and JGB yields were notably lower. Several defensive equity sectors were relative outperformers: Australia financials 
and utilities, China­listed utilities, and Hong Kong­listed banks and energy names. 

September BOJ minutes show support for faster rate hikes
Minutes from the BOJ's September meeting noted the decision to raise interest rates was underpinned by an assessment that economic 
activity and prices were tracking baseline projections, financial conditions remained accommodative with low real interest rates, and un­
derlying inflation had been fairly close to 2% recently. Members backed continuing rate hikes, with one arguing for the need to accelerate 
the pace if signs of upward deviation in prices were observed. One said it was appropriate to bring the rate closer to the approximate policy 
goal soon. Underlying inflation is approaching 2% with B2B pricing pressures spilling into prices of consumer goods and wage­price pass­
through continuing. Global AI­related demand and yen depreciation are also contributing to the rise in the price of durable goods. Members 
noted Japan's economic growth is being underpinned by government measures, accommodative financial conditions, AI­related demand, 
and a waning of the adverse effects of high oil prices. 

New Zealand election on 7-Nov looks too close to call
New Zealand holds its general election on 7­Nov with the center­right National­led coalition seeking reelection. Latest polls showed even 
support for the two major parties. The 1News poll had National up to 30% and Labour up to 29%, leaving both short of the 61­seat majority 
needed to form a government. The right bloc would be left on 58 seats based on the 1News survey and the left bloc at 56­60, positioning 
centrist Opportunity as the likely kingmaker. The campaign has largely been fought on cost­of­living and the economy, healthcare, crime, 
housing and infrastructure. Both parties are promising a return to surplus in 2028/29, though the fiscal trajectory may depend a lot on the 
influence of minor parties. From a monetary policy perspective, Labour has promised to restore the RBNZ's dual inflation­employment 
mandate after National removed the employment component in late 2023, which could have dovish implications given New Zealand's un­
employment rate is at an 11­year high.
