Reiterate Rating: UNDERPERFORM | PO: 3.50 EUR | Price: 3.83 EUR   
  
 
 
PO cut to €3.50, U/P: 2027 cash burn drives downside 
We reiterate our Underperform rating on Stellantis and cut our PO to €3.50 from €5.50, 
primarily reflecting a weaker 2027 cash-flow outlook. We lower our AOI forecasts by 
c.30%/50%/5% for 2026/27/28 (see Exhibit 19), while the PO cut is almost entirely 
driven by a c.€4bn lower industrial net cash position in 2027-28, equivalent to €2/share 
(see Exhibit 25). 2027 remains the key concern: we forecast AOI declining slightly to 
€2.2bn, or a 1.4% margin, well below consensus at €3.9bn/2.4%, as shipments fall c.3% 
y/y and material-cost inflation and competitive pressure absorb much of the expected 
VCP savings (€600m in Q4 26; around €2.0bn in 2027). Continued cash burn could 
intensify concerns around a credit rating downgrade and potential equity dilution, 
although we stress that a capital raise is not our base case. 
2027: Weak product cycle, falling volumes, cash burn 
Our Q4 26 estimates remain slightly below consensus, as we expect further shipment 
declines in North America, MEA and South America to limit the seasonal earnings 
recovery (see Exhibit 18). The weakness should extend into 2027: Stellantis has few 
major launches planned in North America and Europe (see Exhibit 23-Exhibit 24) with 
fleet age increasing, while rising Leapmotor sales will not offset declining volumes at its 
core brands. Intense competition, continued pricing pressure and a c.€2bn raw-material 
cost headwind leave little scope for earnings growth in either region. We therefore 
expect industrial cash flow to remain under significant pressure, forecasting a €2.7bn 
outflow in 2027 (vs Cons €-300m) and a miss versus Stellantis’ FCF breakeven target. 
2028 marks the turn, not the return to target margins 
We expect a more meaningful recovery from 2028 as Stellantis’ model offensive gains 
traction, supporting higher earnings and a return to positive industrial FCF. However, the 
weak 2027 base leaves our 2028 and 2030 AOI margin forecasts of 3.1% and 3.9% 
below consensus and well short of Stellantis’ 5% and 7% targets. The principal gap is 
revenue: we forecast sales reaching only c.€167bn by 2030, versus consensus near 
€180bn and Stellantis’ €190bn ambition, limiting capacity utilisation and fixed-cost 
absorption. Consensus estimates and company targets therefore require a further reset 
before investors can focus on the benefits of the 2028-29 product offensive.

Company Description 
Stellantis N.V. is world's 4th largest Auto OEM resulting 
from the merger of French OEM Groupe PSA and Italian-
American OEM Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, which merged in 
2021. Key brands include Peugeot, Fiat, Jeep, Citroen, Ram, 
Chrysler, Dodge and Opel/Vauxhall. The Company has a 
global footprint with North America and W.EU as key 
regions, and is a market leader in the European mass-
market segment. 
Investment Rationale 
We are below consensus for FY27/28, as we believe the 
market overestimates the magnitude of the earnings 
recovery in these outer years. A higher oil price 
environment could shift consumer focus towards fuel 
efficiency, potentially weighing on demand for highly 
profitable V8 engines in the US. In Europe, rising BEV 
penetration is likely to dilute margins, while Chinese OEMs 
increasingly capture share in the most price-sensitive 
segments.

 Short-term outlook: Below Cons for Q4 26  
Q3 results highlights: €.4.0bn cash burn, but outlook intact 
Adjusted EBIT of €256m was slightly ahead of Visible Alpha consensus of €162m, with a 
0.7% margin, but industrial FCF was deeply negative at €4.0bn. Industrial net cash fell to 
€5.4bn and, before the convertible proceeds and after adjusting for factoring, Stellantis 
is arguably already in a de facto industrial net-debt position (around €-5bn, BofAe), 
keeping capital-raise concerns alive. Total inventories rose 14% YoY to 1.4m units and 
remain too high in our opinion given that sales declined in various regions. On the 
positive side also trade payables declined, meaning that STLA had no negative working 
capital anymore in Q3. Regionally, North America remained at a marginal profit with a 
0.4% margin despite a high single digit y/y revenue decline, while Europe remained the 
clear problem at -2.2%, reflecting adverse BEV mix and persistent pricing pressure. 
South America (+10.5%) and Middle East & Africa (+14.6%) were remarkably resilient 
despite revenue declines amid intensifying Chinese competition.  
AOI bridge inconsistencies cloud visibility 
Stellantis’ reported Q3 AOI bridge differs materially from the bridge implied by 
subtracting H1 from 9M, although both arrive at the same total AOI improvement of 
€166m. At Group level, the largest deviations are in Vehicle Net Price, reported at +€23m 
versus an implied -€228m, a €251m difference; FX & Other, at +€68m versus +€226m, a 
€158m difference; Volume & Mix, at -€477m versus an implied -€327m, a €150m 
difference; and Industrial, at +€828m versus +€699m, a €129m difference. The regional 
pattern is not uniform: in North America, the bridge appears to shift items from Volume 
& Mix into pricing, while in Enlarged Europe the largest reallocation is between Industrial 
and FX & Other. The Q3 bridge in the presentation appears economically more plausible, 
particularly given lower Group shipments, the adverse geographic mix from weaker 
North American volumes, and strong European shipment growth concentrated in lower-
value vehicles. If the standalone Q3 bridge is correct, elements of the previously 
reported H1 bridge must have been reclassified. Stellantis has only started reporting 
quarterly figures this year and may still be refining its disclosure methodology, but these 
inconsistencies complicate forecasting and the interpretation of underlying earnings 
drivers.

Q3 Call: Positive Q4 cash guidance; 2027 outlook mixed 
FY26 guidance was reaffirmed, with net tariff costs reduced to c.€1.0bn from €1.0-
1.2bn. For Q4, management expects higher volumes (BofAe: -5.6% y/y shipments: see 
below), mainly in Europe, broadly stable pricing, mid-hundreds of millions of VCP savings 
and a large seasonal working-capital inflow, taking FY working capital from -€3.1bn after 
9M to around breakeven. This depends primarily on selling down company-owned 
inventory, while US dealer stock should fall to 320-350k units by year-end. The 2027 
bridge remains much less convincing to us: management reiterated positive industrial 
FCF but deferred detailed guidance to the FY results. VCP should enter 2027 at a run 
rate above €2.4bn, but these are gross savings before rising commodity and memory-
chip costs. Commodity inflation was c.1% of revenue in Q3 and is expected at a similar 
level in Q4, 26 before increasing in 2027 as contracts renew; chip pressure is also 
building but remains unquantified. Restructuring and Takata costs continue, European 
pricing remains a headwind, and the key Jeep Grand Cherokee, Wrangler and Gladiator 
launches (in our view, these are just facelifts), alongside the new Dodge Durango, only 
arrive in H2 2027.  
Our view on Q4: Weaker volumes, cost savings uncertainty 
October: Weak EU sales, but greater downside risk in the US 
October sales were weak in both core regions, Europe and the US: 
• 
US retails sales fell 9% y/y versus market growth of c.3% in October, with 
Ram’s 11% growth insufficient to offset Jeep declining 14% and the smaller brands 
falling almost 50%. Inventories fell by c.22k units, or 6%, sequentially, but remained 
8% above last year; weak sell-through pushed days’ supply to 99 days versus 59 
days for the market. Incentives remained elevated at c.$4,800 per vehicle, 37% 
above the industry average, and increased 9% y/y, slightly faster than the market.  
• 
In Europe, preliminary registration data shows that Stellantis including Leapmotor 
was roughly flat to down 2% y/y versus market growth of c.3% to 5% in October 26, 
implying further share losses. However, this masks a much weaker underlying 
performance, with legacy Stellantis brands likely down high single digits, partly 
offset by strong triple-digit growth at Leapmotor. Meanwhile, Chinese OEMs 
continue to fire on all cylinders ahead of potential import restrictions from 2027, 
adding further pressure on Stellantis’ European volumes and pricing. 
Below Q4 AOI consensus on weaker shipments and pricing 
We remain below consensus on Q4 2026, mainly due to weaker shipment assumptions 
(-5.6% y/y). While earnings in South America and MEA should remain resilient, we see 
pressure from destocking in North America and negative pricing in Enlarged Europe. 
With a further c.20k-unit inventory reduction in North America, we forecast regional 
shipments down c.15% y/y versus consensus at c.-7%. In Europe, continued expansion by 
Chinese OEMs ahead of potential import restrictions from December should sustain 
pressure on volumes and pricing. Stellantis is counting on c.€600m of VCP savings in Q4, 
up from c.€200m to €300m in Q3, potentially supplemented by further CO2 and 
warranty provision releases. However, these benefits may not fully offset weakness in 
the core regions. The guided c.€400m raw-material headwind, equivalent to c.1% of 
group revenues, also appears light given lagged supplier compensation, suggesting some 
costs could shift into Q1 2027 and further cloud the FY27 outlook. We agree that 
seasonal working-capital inflows should drive a Q4 FCF recovery, but it remains to be 
seen if that is as strong as expected by Stellantis if sales are worse than expected. We 
also struggle to see a positive earnings surprise versus consensus. Instead, continued 
volume and pricing pressure could trigger further FY27 estimate cuts ahead of the FY26 
results, likely due in late February 2027.

Difficult 2027, recovery starts in 2028 
2027 concerns drive our Underperform rating 
No revenue growth, tougher competition and inflation put us c40% below cons 
We believe Stellantis is taking the right strategic and operational measures, but the 
benefits are unlikely to become material before 2028/29, while weak market dynamics 
and a limited launch pipeline constrain organic self-help in 2027. We forecast group 
shipments declining 2.7% to 5.5mn units and revenues falling 0.8% to €158.0bn, both 
c4% below consensus. The earnings gap is considerably larger: our adjusted EBIT 
forecast of €2.2bn is c40% below consensus of €3.9bn, implying a margin of just 1.4% 
versus 2.4%. Around €2.0bn of VCP savings should be largely absorbed by raw-material 
inflation and moderately higher tariffs, while volume leverage and mix remain broadly 
neutral. This limited scope for an FY27E earnings recovery drives our Underperform 
rating. We show details of our forecast in Exhibit 18. 
Europe: Weak launches and Chinese pressure prolong losses 
Europe remains structurally challenging, with intensifying Chinese competition and 
limited new-product support. The Fiat Grizzly and localized Leapmotor production in 
Zaragoza (Spain), including the B05 and B10, should add volume, but not enough to 
offset further declines at Peugeot, Citroën and Opel. We provide more detail on 
Stellantis’ European launch pipeline in Exhibit 24. We forecast shipments falling 3.5% 
to 2.6mn units and revenues declining 3.0% to €59.6bn, around 4% and 2% below 
consensus, respectively. The division should remain loss-making, with adjusted EBIT of 
€-625mn versus consensus at c€-180mn. Chinese OEMs should continue gaining share, 
initially through PHEVs and potentially shifting back towards BEVs if PHEV tariffs rise. 
Leapmotor offers some protection, as competitive platforms and largely incremental 
BEV volumes should limit dilution despite tariffs. However, raw-material inflation, 
limited pricing power and greater use of fleet sales with buyback commitments increase 
margin pressure and residual-value risk.

2029: A stronger model cycle accelerates earnings and cash recovery  
The recovery should broaden in FY29E as Stellantis launches more vehicles on its new 
global and Leapmotor-derived platforms. In Europe, the Citroën 2CV and Fiat Pandina 
should lower the cost of competing in entry-level BEVs, complemented by new vans on 
the STLA Van platform. The European-built electric 2CV should be priced below €15,000 
and use the separate E-Car architecture, leveraging Leapmotor’s low-cost BEV expertise 
to target BEV-ICE cost parity and potentially profitable entry-level pricing. Enlarged 
Europe shipments should return to 1.5% growth, while further Leapmotor scaling and 
localization support platform savings. In North America, the new Ram 1500 family across 
ICE, PHEV and BEV powertrains, built on STLA Frame, should drive further volume and 
profitability gains. We forecast group AOI rising c40% to €6.8bn, with the margin 
reaching 4.1%, while FCF increases to €2.4bn and industrial net cash rebuilds to €7.5bn. 
By 2030: A credible recovery story, but not enough to meet Stellantis’ ambitions 
Our forecasts says that FY27E will be a transition year in which Stellantis may be 
overestimating both underlying markets and its ability to regain share without 
meaningful new-model support. The equity case becomes more compelling from 
FY28/29 as the product cycle improves, new platforms generate tangible cost savings 
and deeper localization strengthens the Leapmotor partnership. However, we still expect 
Stellantis to miss its 2030 targets. The combination of ambitious volume growth, 
broadly stable pricing and relatively benign material-cost assumptions looks unrealistic, 
in our view. We forecast 2030 revenues of c€167bn, around €23bn below the company’s 
€190bn ambition, and an AOI margin of 3.9% versus the 7% target. Our industrial FCF

Rising cash concerns, but no capital raise 
Underlying leverage is higher at a second glance 
We forecast c€2.7bn of FCF burn in 2027, versus Stellantis’ ambition to reach cash 
break-even, reducing industrial net cash from €7.6bn in 2026E to €4.9bn. However, the 
headline figure includes €5.0bn of hybrid bonds issued in March 2026. Investors should 
also consider €2.6bn of lease liabilities, c€4.0bn of pension and healthcare liabilities, and 
c€6bn of cash generated through factoring and reverse factoring (BofAe), of which 
c€5bn relates to receivables factoring. Adjusting for these items, we estimate that the 
reported €7.6bn industrial net cash position could be viewed as c€10bn of underlying 
industrial net debt. This remains manageable in the current benign macro environment, 
but would become more concerning in a downturn, particularly if lower factoring 
volumes accelerated cash erosion. 
Refinancing costs add another earnings headwind 
Stellantis faces annual industrial debt maturities of c€2-3bn between 2027 and 2029 
and it seven higher considering rising maturities for bonds linked to the new US financial 
services business. Stellantis yield curve has already moved higher in the last 12 months, 
reflecting both rising risk-free rates and a wider company-specific risk premium. We 
estimate that Stellantis currently pays yields of 5-6% on three- to seven-year debt, 
c2ppt more than a year ago. Refinancing at these levels should make the financial result 
increasingly negative, creating an additional slight drag on EPS.

Capital-raise fears could remain an equity overhang 
Continued cash burn may raise concerns that Stellantis will eventually require additional 
equity funding, but we stress that a capital raise is not our base case. Even if a 
downgrade to non-investment grade increased funding costs by 50-100bp, applying this 
to annual industrial debt maturities of c€2-3bn would add only c€10-30mn of interest 
expense per refinancing cohort, with the impact accumulating over time. This still 
appears manageable for Stellantis. We also see less risk for the US captive, as its 
predominantly ABS-based funding depends more on collateral quality and structural 
protection than on the parent rating, as discussed below. Nevertheless, with Stellantis’ 
market capitalization at only c€11.5bn, even a modest equity raise could result in 
significant dilution. The perceived risk may therefore keep investors on the sidelines 
until FCF turns positive and industrial liquidity stabilizes. 
US captive debt growth looks manageable though 
We are less concerned about the rising debt associated with Stellantis’ US captive-
finance build-up than about additional industrial debt. Stellantis is expanding customer 
loans and leases to support vehicle sales and retention, which requires the captive to 
fund a growing pool of receivables. Gross debt must therefore increase alongside the 
earning-asset portfolio. This is already visible across Financial Services, where financing 
receivables rose from €9.0bn in 2023 to €20.8bn by September 2026, while asset-
backed financing increased from €4.8bn to €19.9bn. Importantly, most of the growth is 
funded through asset-backed securities, with customer receivables serving as collateral 
and repayments servicing the debt. Senior ABS tranches can therefore retain strong 
ratings even if Stellantis falls below investment grade, although spreads and credit-
enhancement requirements would likely rise. We thus share Stellantis’ view that the US 
captive could continue operating and refinancing under a non-investment-grade parent 
rating, with the business remaining largely self-funded and requiring only limited 
additional equity.

Valuation: U/P; PO cut from €5.5 to €3.5 
Higher multiples, but weaker cash drives PO cut to €3.5 
Our SOTP remains based on average FY27E/FY28E estimates. The group EV/sales 
multiple declines to 0.14x from 0.16x and adjusted P/E to 3.9x from 4.1x, while EV/EBIT 
increases to 3.3x from 3.0x. The regional multiples reflect Stellantis’ geographic peer 
exposure, with higher US car maker multiples (i.e. Ford, GM) supporting North and South 
America, while lower EU mass market peer multiples (i.e. Renault, Volkswagen) weigh on 
Enlarged Europe. We also adopt a more generous P/E methodology by applying adjusted 
peer multiples to adjusted EPS, excluding c.€900m of negative after-tax special items in 
FY27E/FY28E. This better reflects normalized earnings and the expected recovery from 
FY29E. However, the revised valuation methodology is more than offset by lower 
earnings estimates and a weaker balance-sheet outlook. Our industrial net cash forecast 
falls c.€4bn to €5.2bn, reducing our valuation by c.€2/share. We therefore cut our PO to 
€3.50 from €5.50 and reiterate our Underperform rating. 
Exhibit 25: PO down from €5.5 to €3.5: Higher peer multiples and a shift to adjusted P/E better reflect normalized earnings and the expected 
improvement from FY28/29E. However, lower estimates and c€2/share reduction in forecast net cash drive our PO to €3.50 from €5.50.

Price objective basis & risk 
Stellantis NV (XVWGF / STLA) 
Our price objective of EUR3.50 (ADR USD3.92) is derived from a sum-of-the-parts 
valuation (SOTP) which we base on our average 2027/28 estimates. We value Stellantis 
on EV/Sales, EV/EBIT and adj. P/E peer multiples. We value the N. America/S. 
America/MEA segments at c30% discount to US peers. We value Enlarged Europe/APAC 
based on EU mass market peer multiples, and apply no discount, as such, our EV/Sales, 
EV/EBIT and adj. P/E multiples stand at 0.14x/3.3x/3.9x in our SOTP. We also consider 
cEUR5.2bn of industrial net cash, cEUR5bn of hybrid bonds, cEUR4bn of pension and 
healthcare liabilities, cEUR2.6bn of lease liabilities and cEUR1.6bn for 50% share of the 
Finco JVs. 
 
Upside risks: 1) a decline in raw material prices. 2) stronger-than-expected volume 
recovery as inflation moderates. 3) a stronger USD boosting earnings translation into 
EUR. 4) a lower oil price environment sustaining or even increasing demand for high-
margin V8 models. 5) potential disposals or partnerships for European plants with 
Chinese OEMs, which could improve capacity utilisation and unlock value. 
 
Downside risks: 1) Slower-than-expected earnings recovery. 2) US recovery too 
dependent on V8 mix and more exposed to higher fuel prices. 3) Chinese OEMs take 
more market share than expected in key regions. 4) STLA is not hedged on raw 
materials. 5) Cash flow: Negative working capital and due to factoring/reverse factoring, 
declining sales would also lead to a higher cash burn.
