Adam Wijaya  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Energy, Natural Resources & Utilities Sector Specialist, FICC & Equities
10 Nov 2026
OXY. ‘Race to the bottom’ on crude has been prevalent in conversations on the desk with erosion of risk
premium in the barrel and clear distinction between freight constraints and supply constraints. OXY is the
last Large Cap E&P to report EPS this season – so we have gone through some of the topical themes
within E&P already (cost inflation, capex potentially hotter, ground game narratives, continued efficiency
gains) – so there wasn’t really much left here with the print itself that folks were super focused on. The
quarter itself – don’t see much here that folks will pick at… EPS and FCF beat vs Street with oil
production ahead (and above midpoint of guidance). 4Q guidance lighter on the oil side – expect some
more color on the call here by region / asset where the implied softness is coming from. Interestingly the
company provided an updated on STRATOS timing, capex and long-term offtakes … I haven’t heard
STRATOS mentioned once in conversations over the last couple of months (probably given where oil
prices have trended) – so I don’t think there folks will have strong opinions on the actual offtakes – only
the associated spend. New $6 bn debt milestone established following achievement of $10 bn debt
milestone a positive – but bears will argue deleveraging is known and no real incremental update on the
prefs. 2027 capital program and early thoughts on volume guidance will be the focus today on the call.
Gas. “The commodity is tough, but maybe the equities aren’t”. Probably one of the better ways to describe
the specialist conversations on the desk recently – and likely a reflection of the current macro tape (oil px
continues to slide, gasoline seasonally weaker, TTF largely a call on weather + RUS/UKR, diesel tight but
known). Aside from some high-level questions on ‘how much gas demand is at risk if we don’t see a DC
buildout materialize in key states / regions’ – think folks are looking at the stock charts with most of the
dry gas names trading at the low end of the ranges and favorable seasonality – there’s some willingness to
lean in for now. We still think longer-term, there’s more interest in owning the volumes vs price – though
understand that (A) the trade is / was crowded, (B) the gas pipe stocks have generally been moving more
with the power trade /baskets and (C) there’s less of a call on valuation at this point.
Quick blurb on nuclear. ICYMI from the CEG EPS call yesterday … worth going back and taking a look:
‘So with that said, I have to say that it's been a bit of a head-scratcher for us, when people say that
customers are not interested in existing nuclear energy. As the deals we've announced demonstrate that's
just not accurate…. As Joe covered in his remarks, estimates for new combined cycle gas plants continue
to rise. In fact, the capital cost of our nuclear upgrades is not materially outside the range of current CCGT
estimates. And unlike new gas generation, the upgrades come with little to no incremental fuel or
operating maintenance costs. These are great projects for our customers, our owners, and frankly for
America.’
