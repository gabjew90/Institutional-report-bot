Exports already beat 2025 total as tech remains strong
China’s exports grew by 35.2% year-on-year in October, down slightly from 36.3% in
September, and coming in a little softer than expectations (market: 36.3%, ING: 40.7%).  
By product, auto exports slowed, falling to 37.6% YoY in October from 62.0% in September.
This slowdown has not yet reflected the EU import quota on hybrid vehicles, set to take effect
in December and potentially creating a headwind for auto exports in the months ahead.

Household appliance exports also slowed in October, down to 20.9% YoY from 31.7% in
September. The slowdown in these categories somewhat offset stronger export growth in tech
categories. Hi-tech exports (66.6%), semiconductors (137.1%), and automatic data processing
machines (114.7%) all saw export growth pick up in October. 
By destination, export growth moderated slightly in October, but key export destinations
continued to show strength. Exports to ASEAN (46.3%), the EU (24.3%), and the US (32.9%) all
cooled slightly but remained well above year-to-date growth rates. One key exception was
exports to South Korea, which surged to 92.4% YoY in October amid strong tech exports. 
Overall, exports are now up 22.7% YoY over the first ten months of the year. Exports of $3.78tn
so far this year have already eclipsed last year's total.

Tech, oil drove import outperformance in October
Import growth accelerated to 41.6% year-on-year in October, up from 31.0% in September
and above both market expectations and our forecast (market: 33.9%; ING: 37.3%). This
brought import growth in the first ten months of the year to 29.0% YoY, with total imports
reaching $2.74tn.
The main areas of import growth remained tied to technology products, suggesting that China
continues to spend heavily in the ongoing tech race. High-tech imports rose 72.1% YoY in
October, up from 63.9% in September, bringing year-to-date growth to 50.8%. Imports of
automatic data processing machines and parts surged 261.0% YoY, slower than the 296.5%
growth recorded in September. Semiconductor imports also accelerated to 78.3% YoY, up from
74.7% in the previous month. Despite crude oil import volumes falling 13.3% YoY in October,
higher prices pushed oil import values up 41.0%, the fastest pace since October 2022.
Trade surplus well on track to hit new high in 2026
The trade surplus fell sharply from $133.5bn to $104.5bn, coming in weaker than market
expectations (market: 125.8, ING: 131.2), thanks in large part to the stronger-than-expected
imports. 
October's trade surplus marked a 6-month low. Still, we remain on pace for China's surplus to
reach a new record high by year-end.
Strong external demand has been a major driver of China's economic growth in 2026, helping
offset soft domestic demand as consumption and investment disappoint. We may see trade
growth moderate next year, given the base effect. But AI-related trade is expected to remain
