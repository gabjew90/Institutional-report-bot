Repo market update: signs of life 
After several months of extremely benign funding conditions, repo markets have recently shown some signs of pressure. 
We (and others) were a bit surprised at the degree to which elevated SOFR prints into October month-end/CAD year-end spilled 
over into early November, although funding conditions have eased a bit in the back half of this week. The fact that pressure picked 
up after the statement date and has been apparent in both tri-party (i.e. TGCR) and bilateral rates suggests that this pressure is not 
a sign of broader balance sheet scarcity aside from the somewhat “artificial” mechanics around statement dates (repo dealers 
gradually building back triparty balances post-snapshot). However, it does serve as a reminder that even in a world with sponsored 
repo, eSLR easing, the Fed’s SRO backstop, RMPs, and GSIB surcharge changes, repo can still experience some stress.  
Bill supply has likely been a contributing factor. Markets have had to digest several heavy weeks of net bill issuance, which 
have come in even heavier than we had originally been forecasting. The $25bn CMB that Treasury unexpectedly issued back in 
late October will mature today, which will take some of the sting out of today’s settlement. All else equal, we do think conditions 
should continue to normalize from here, but it may not be as quick or as much of a straight line as some may like, as there’s still 
several more weeks of net positive bill supply on the horizon. Our forecasts have ~$120bn of additional net bill supply before 
paydowns begin in Dec.

Macro positioning could also be playing a role – albeit likely a minor one. In the past, we have noted a reduction in asset 
manager long duration positions as a stabilizing factor for repo. With yields rallying and asset manager longs increasing, the 
opposite could also be true. Asset managers often express duration positions via futures (see the recent TBAC discussion for more 
on that!), which increases basis opportunities for hedge funds. Hedge fund basis trading creates demand for repo balance sheet – 
hence, longer positioning by asset managers could be adding to the recent repo pressure. However, we think this is likely a 
marginal factor at best. Still, increased leverage demand out of the hedge fund community would be consistent with the rise in 
SOFR volumes (specifically the non-triparty portion of the sample).  
Somewhat relatedly, a duration extension by money funds could also theoretically increase pressure on repo if they were pulling 
cash out of overnight repo to buy bills (which have cheapened in the fall). But WAMs haven’t really been extending, so it doesn’t 
look like a rotation within the 2a7 community has added to the repo pressure.

Will the Fed ramp up RMPs again? This bout of repo pressure has prompted discussion of Fed RMPs restarting, but our view has 
been that Warsh will be more hesitant to pull that trigger than Powell was last year. That is partially due to his desire for a smaller 
balance sheet and (what we assume to be) a likely distaste for any new asset purchases – even more so with the balance sheet 
task force’s recommendations likely coming in Q1 2027. Also, we continue to argue that last year’s RMPs weren’t particularly 
necessary, and that they were a bit of an overreaction to repo pressure that largely would have resolved on its own as bill supply 
started to come down. This year, the Fed may have a bit more patience in waiting for December bill paydowns to relieve some repo 
pressure. All this being said, reserves are quite low (relative to total commercial bank assets), so there’s certainly a pro-RMP 
argument that could be made.

The recent pressure doesn’t seem to have caused any market concern about (US) year-end. Dec SOFR/FF basis has 
actually widened in the last few days (i.e., pricing less repo pressure) moving up to ~-2bp. This suggests markets aren't looking at 
this recent pressure as a preview of what's to come in December. For context, November would settle around ~-2.7bp if SOFR 
prints in line with IORB the rest of the month. Instead, we think markets may be looking at the recent stress as increasing the odds 
of the Fed restarting RMPs.  
However, we like being received December SOFR/FF basis at -2bp. This isn’t necessarily because we are calling for year-end 
funding problems. It’s more because we see decent asymmetry at current pricing and expect very little PnL volatility to hold this 
trade. To be fair, this may not seem to reconcile with the fact that we are forecasting a net bill paydown of ~$175bn in December. 
However, it’s worth noting that last year’s December contract settled at -6.25bp despite $176bn in bill paydowns and the rollout of 
RMPs. Additional balance sheet has been added to the system since then (largely via eSLR reforms that went into effect early this 
year) and repo conditions have been much more benign in the run-up to this year-end relative to YE2025. But as we noted in the 
opening paragraph, we think repo stress can still occur despite the array of factors easing dealer balance sheet constraints
