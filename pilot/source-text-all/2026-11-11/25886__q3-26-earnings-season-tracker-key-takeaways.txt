We are nearing the end of the Q3 reporting season, with 75% - 80% of 
companies having reported in both the US and in Europe. In the final edition 
of our Q3 tracker, we summarize the key takeaways. 
•
Earnings growth has been strong in both the US and Europe, tracking at +24% 
y/y and +28% y/y, respectively, supported by firmer activity momentum - see 
Fig 13.  Results are ahead of initial consensus estimates by 4%pts in the US and 
10%pts in Europe. The breadth of beats remains elevated, albeit down a notch 
vs last quarter, with the proportion of companies reporting better-than-
expected earnings still above historical medians - see Figs 4 & 6. S&P 500 Q3 
'26 blended EPS has been revised steadily higher - see Fig 3. Interestingly, 
despite an overhang from volatile oil prices, Q4 ‘26 and 2027 EPS projections 
have also been upgraded - see Figs 20–25, with the share of S&P 500 
companies raising guidance now at its highest since 2021 - see Fig 15.
•
At a sector level, Energy is the largest contributor to aggregate EPS growth in 
both the US and Europe. Ex-Energy, EPS growth stands at +17% y/y in the US 
and +13% y/y in Europe. Still, delivery is robust beyond Energy, with 6 of the 
remaining 10 sectors posting double-digit growth in both regions. Notably, 
based on the six Mag-7 stocks that have reported so far, S&P 500 ex-Mag-7 
EPS growth is beating Mag-7 - see Fig 1. Mag-7 earnings growth is behind 
the overall market for the first time since 2022 - see Fig 19.
•
Regionally, European earnings growth is coming in ahead of the US. This 
is the case even when looking at the median EPS growth, where both are 
tracking above initial consensus expectations, at +12% y/y for S&P500 and 
+13% y/y for Stoxx600 -  see Fig 2. The weaker Euro has also helped the 
relative sales beats in Europe vs the US – see Fig 14.
