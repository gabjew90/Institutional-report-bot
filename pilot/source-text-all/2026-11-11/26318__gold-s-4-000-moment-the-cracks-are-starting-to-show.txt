Gold's pressure cooker
Gold is increasingly trapped between the massive $4,000 level and the major downward-sloping
trendline. Today's candle is shaping up to be the biggest downside move since the latest squeeze
higher. Note that the bounce didn't even manage to test the 50-day MA. Gold's psychology has become
increasingly complex, with bulls and bears fighting for control. The compression is building, and the
eventual breakout could be violent.

The rates connection
Gold momentum remains poor, and the latest spike in yields is adding another layer of pressure.
Higher yields increase the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset, particularly when price
momentum is already deteriorating. Unless yields reverse, the path of least resistance for gold may
remain lower.

The dollar connection
The inverse relationship between gold and the DXY remains firmly intact. With the dollar strengthening
and gold struggling to regain momentum, the macro backdrop is becoming increasingly hostile.
Getting aggressively bullish on gold without a weaker dollar looks like a dangerous trade.

Cracks beneath the surface
Paying more, buying less
India's gold demand plunged 29% YoY in Q3, yet spending actually increased 3%, according to the
World Gold Council. Domestic prices were up 45%, forcing buyers to pay more for significantly less
gold. The divergence is striking: higher prices are supporting spending figures while underlying
physical demand is deteriorating. Not exactly the kind of demand backdrop gold bulls want to see.

The gold FOMO is fading
Indian consumers are becoming less convinced that gold prices will keep rising. The share expecting
higher prices over the next three months has fallen from 69% to 61%, according to the World Gold
Council's latest survey. Meanwhile, investment-led jewellery buying has slipped from 19% to 15%. The
bullish conviction hasn't disappeared, but the momentum psychology is starting to weaken. That
matters for an asset increasingly dependent on investors chasing higher prices.

The missing central bank buyer
50 tonnes → 4 tonnes → 0.3 tonnes. That's how much gold India's central bank purchased during the
first nine months of 2024, 2025 and 2026, respectively. According to the World Gold Council, the RBI
has effectively stopped accumulating gold, while gold's share of foreign reserves has fallen from 19%
in January to 14.5% at the end of Q3. One of the major official-sector buyers has gone from aggressive
accumulation to virtually zero purchases. Another potential headwind for gold.

The bottom line

Gold is struggling technically just as the macro backdrop turns increasingly hostile. Yields are rising,
the dollar remains firm, and physical demand in India is showing clear signs of strain. Bar and coin
demand plunged 40% YoY in Q3, while RBI gold accumulation has virtually disappeared.
The $4,000 level remains the key battleground. A decisive break below it could turn the current
compression into something considerably more painful for the bulls.
