Our view: We expect a supportive Q4/mini-CMD on Wednesday, with
a buyback program (potentially as large as €15-20bn) and new mid-
term targets helpful during wider AI uncertainty. This note also deep-
dives into the long-term trajectory for Gas Services. By analysing pricing
developments and installed base modelling, we forecast 2030E Gas Service
margins at 33% (vs cons: 25%). Even in our conservative base case (i.e.,
cycle turns immediately and aggressively), we forecast 2035E margins
stabilising at 23% - supporting an NPV that is ~75% of current group
valuation.
Key points:
Mini-CMD - buyback + continued mid-term momentum: The focus at Q4
will include 1) 2027E and mid-term targets, 2) the post-2030E outlook
and 3) capital allocation. We expect financial targets will broadly support
consensus, and should help soothe investors given ongoing cycle worries.
Similarly, a focus on Gas Services' aftermarket activity and Grid Tech's
non-datacenter post-2030E growth drivers should also help. The other key
highlight we expect is a €15-20bn buyback program, supported by strong
FCF generation and limited M&A opportunities.
Gas Services - 33% EBITA margin in 2030E: We forecast Gas Services
margins rising from 16% to 33% over 2026-2030E (2030E cons: 25%). The
key driver is turbine OE, with the existing backlog supporting 2026-30E
unit pricing growth of 99%. Combined with our cost-base analysis, we
forecast OE margins growing from ~12% to ~38% over 2026-30E. We see
aftermarket margins expanding from ~19% to ~27% over 2026-30E, given
the steady (but slow) accretion in LTSA margins. In particular, our installed
base/PoC accounting analysis suggests the highly attractive post-2025E
installed vintage will account for only ~17% of LTSA revenues in 2030E.
Post-2030E outlook structurally improved vs the past: We assume that
Gas Services' exceptional mid-term outlook eventually gives way to a
normalisation. In our base case assumption, we assume turbine prices peak
immediately beyond today's turbine commitments (i.e., 2032E revenues)
and fall to €300/kW by 2035E (~25% below 2011 levels), pushing group
margins down to 23%. While still ahead of Gas Services' 2000-26E margin
average of ~11%, our analysis shows this is well supported by contractually-
driven service margin accretion (LTSA contracts ~17 years long on average)
and a shifting sales mix.
Gas Services worth ~€76bn if cycle turns tomorrow (+€3bn for each
extra year): Discounting our conservative forecasts, we value Gas Services
at €76bn, equivalent to 75% of Siemens Energy's current valuation. In
our sensitivity analysis, we also see that each extra peak year drives an
additional €3bn of value, with each +€50/kW to terminal turbine ASP
driving an additional €7bn.

Q4– what to expect 
Mini-CMD – 3 key priorities 
We see the upcoming ‘mini-CMD’ (as part of the Q4 2026 results) as a positive catalyst for 
Siemens Energy. We expect 3 key areas will be in focus, and expect a positive conclusion across 
all three:  
2027E & mid-term targets: We expect 2027E and mid-term guidance to be broadly in line with 
consensus, with the exception of some possible upside in 2027E Gas Services margins and the 
mid-term growth outlook for Gas Services (GS) and Grid Tech (GT). Given wider AI uncertainty, 
we expect simply affirming consensus (which is already largely in order books) will be 
supportive for the stock. 
Post-2030 outlook: Given the perceived correlation to data-center activity (even if datacenters 
account for <20% of GS/GT activity), we expect a strong focus on Siemens Energy’s wider 
business longevity. For Grid Tech, we expect a discussion around growing electricity demand 
(including from non-datacenter sources such as HVAC, industrialisation and EVs) as 
underpinning transmission investment long-term. For Gas Services, we expect a strong focus 
on the long-term service revenue stream and a discussion of how maintenance activity on 
recently ordered turbines support strong high-margin growth over 2030-35E. 
Capital allocation framework: With higher earnings and prepayments and a largely similar 
capex/bolt-on strategy, we expect Siemens Energy will announce a multi-year buyback 
program of €15-20bn (though with the potential to do significantly more if it chooses). 
Thoughts on 2027 - and beyond 
We expect a strong Q4 to cap out 2026, with both ourselves and consensus positioned in the 
middle of the guided 14-16% organic growth range, but above the top-end of FY26’s guided 
margin corridor of 10-12%.

€15-20bn buyback announcement likely (but could be easily exceeded) 
Between M&A and share buybacks, we expect the focus to be on buybacks. We note that the 
existing €6bn buyback over 3 years, announced at the CMD, is now set to complete in ~1.5 
years. We expect a buyback announcement of €15-20bn, reflecting conservatism on the 
earnings guidance but also a LSD €bn set aside for M&A.  
Supporting this also are the limited opportunities for meaningful M&A in the key priority areas 
of Grid Tech and Gas Services. In Grid Tech, we expect small portfolio/technology additions 
(e.g., Camlin Group, a grid monitoring and asset analytics business) – but do not see a parallel 
to GE Vernova’s large-scale M&A given 1) an already largely complete high-voltage portfolio 
and 2) a lack of strategic desire to enter LV/MV market (given the potential for competition 
with Siemens AG) or software space – again limiting the possibility of great spend. 
For Gas Services, the already highly consolidated market means we expect acquisitions to 
continue to be small additions (e.g., service shop RWG).

Gas Services – 33% EBITA margin by 2030E 
Continuous margin ramp until 2030E, with back-end uplift 
While we expect management will put forward 2030E EBITA margin guidance for Gas Services 
that will encompass consensus’ 25% forecast, our analysis suggests the true margin delivery 
could be closer to 33% by then. Combined with the higher sales growth, we note our 2030E 
Gas Services EBITA estimate is 52% above consensus – and largely underpinned by the existing 
order book. 
Exhibit 5 - GS – Sales and EBITA margin (%) 
 
Source: Company reports, RBC Capital Markets estimates 
 
Our analysis takes a granular look at the developments by segment, focusing on OE, 
Aftermarket (LTSA) and Aftermarket (Transactional) developments over time. Summarising 
our findings for each: 
• 
OE: We estimate turbine revenue ASP (€/kW) will rise +99% over 2026-30E. Through our 
analyses of Gas Service’s cost base and industry commentary, we estimate ~70% of this is 
retained by ENR. Combined with leverage, we see EBITA margins rising from 12% in 2026E 
to 38% in 2030E, before declining thereafter to ~12% by 2035E. 
• 
AM (LTSA): We model out installed base expansion and changing average capacity factors. 
While we acknowledge new turbine sales will come in at a much higher profitability level 
(we estimate ~40%), slowing fleet retirements and PoC accounting means we expect 
margins will rise from ~15% to ~23% over 2026-30E (but reach 32% in 2035E). 
• 
AM (Transactional): We see this as the highest margin segment at present given the 
premium on maximising power generation. We expect continued near-term accretion, 
but are cautious given the ‘book-and-bill’ nature and forecast a levelling out from 2028 
onwards. 
 
OE margin uplift – pricier turbines (and more of them) 
While Siemens Energy have been highlighting a continued positive pricing environment for 
some time, we believe the scale of this has been under-appreciated – and not helped by the 
limited disclosures. Nonetheless, we forecast that average turbine pricing (on a revenue basis) 
will rise from €280/kW in 2026E to €558/kW by 2030E – representing a 99% increase.

What has ENR said on pricing? 
While Siemens Energy disclosures have been limited, there have been two important disclosed 
data-points: 
2025 backlog average pricing: FY25 backlog was noted as 23% new units by value, which given 
a firm backlog size of 41GW would imply ~EUR300/kW of average pricing. We estimate in-year 
order intake at closer to ~EUR350/kW (and note this would align with GEV’s commentary on 
y/y order intake pricing). 
Q3 2026: The order intake was stated as ~66% OE / ~33% AM. Given quarterly firm order intake 
of 15GW, we can derive an implied order price of EUR443/kW. We think this was a low-point 
in the year however and that FY 2026E order intake to be ~EUR510/kW (we estimate H1 order 
intake was ~EUR520/kW). 
Given order book developments (41GW firm in 2025, 72GW in 2026E) we expect 2026E order 
intake to largely provide for 2028-29E deliveries (excepting some contribution from high-
priced, quicker deliveries) with the continued pricing momentum meaning 2027E firm order 
intake will provide for 2030E-31E deliveries at higher prices. We note that GEV commentary 
has been much more forthcoming, and average turbine prices in 2026 so far have been ~130% 
higher than in 2024 (and ~50% higher than 2025).

Net pricing & operating leverage drives 38% OE EBITA margin by 2030E 
In modelling out the 2030E margin, we assume the following: 
• 
Volume leverage: Turbine delivery volumes will roughly double over 2025-30E (i.e., from 
~17GW to 30GW). We assume a relatively conservative operating leverage of 20% on this 
growth. 
• 
Pricing gain: External castings/forging components follow overall turbine pricing, with an 
assumed 3% p.a. growth in all other categories (e.g., labour, other components, D&A). In 
the later price-down environment, we assume castings/forgings pricing power also 
declines and that other cost-base inflation is broadly neutral. 
We note this ignores other possible sources of margin uplift, and in particular the 
manufacturing learning curve from turbine manufacture (particularly on the still fairly 
immature HL-turbine class).

Service – steadier (but slower) margin accretion 
Before diving into the specifics, it is useful to lay-out some context for the Service business. 
Services are at present the main profit driver for Gas Services and consists of two revenue 
streams:  
• 
Long-Term Service Agreements (LTSAs): Predictable maintenance contracts, with an 
average tenure in the portfolio of ~17 years. These are typically highly correlated with 
actual turbine utilisation. There is also a slight bias towards the larger turbine models (i.e.,

LTSA – what are the margin drivers? 
When thinking about LTSA margins development, we consider four key levers: 
Pricing on new contracts: Commentary from peer GEV has been quite clear that higher priced 
turbine sales are also leading to higher-priced service contracts – though the implication has 
been the scale of the price increase has been somewhat less, with a comparison being drawn 
to aerospace engine aftermarket margins (which we take as being ~40%). 
Pricing on renewed contracts: Siemens Energy commentary has been clear that this is not as 
strong as with new contracts, and we assume gains here are also more reliant on annual step-
up pricing impacts. We estimate these are capable of delivering 30% margins by 2030E. 
Productivity gains: Across the portfolio, growing service network density and learning curve 
benefits should drive higher margins – though this is somewhat harder to quantify, but has 
played an important role in the peer aerospace gas turbine market. 
Fleet retirement: Older contracts with less favourable terms (e.g., annual step-ups or inflation 
coverage) are less likely to retire given ENR’s young fleet (and the general need for power). 
We estimate these are on ~15% margins (based on GEV’s disclosures on AM/OE profitability 
splits). 
Relative utilisation of new installations: ENR has said new deliveries are expected to be 80% 
baseload/20% peakload, versus the installed base at 60%/40%. This drives an over-weighting 
to the higher-margin contracts (though is offset by the ~5 year-lag until the first service visit). 
New LTSAs only ~17% of revenues by 2030E (~33% by 2035E) 
Taking this all into account, we estimate the post-2025 fleet – on higher prices and margins – 
will only account for 17% of 2030E revenues, climbing to ~33% by 2035E. Critically, contracts 
still stuck on pre-2025 economics will fall from 100% to 50% by 2030E, and then 10% by 2035E. 
This supports LTSA margin expansion from 15% in 2025 to 23% by 2030E, and further onwards 
32% by 2035E.

Datacenter cancellation risks – present, but limited 
Following the US mid-term elections, there has been a heightened focus on potential 
datacenter cancellations. While we do not rule out some potential delays given some political 
uncertainty, we see the risk as mitigated for ENR by the following factors: 
Site multiplicity: Industry comments (including from peers such as GE Vernova and Legrand) 
have flagged that customers often have a number of back-up sites planned (in excess of the 
equipment they intend to secure), enabling them to re-locate facilities if there are issues with 
others.  
High down-payments:  Down-payments have significantly increased in quantum. GE Vernova 
for instance have flagged 20% down-payments for slot reservation agreements (and higher 
still yet for firm orders). This provides a substantial barrier against cancellations.  
Order book split: Datacenters are only 20-25% of direct Gas Services orders, with the majority 
of orders with fairly robust utilities/IPPs. Geographically, the order book is also fairly diverse- 
i.e., 27/27/27/20 between North America/Europe/Middle East/APAC. We would further note 
that neocloud exposure within datacenters should be below the industry average of ~20%, 
given their likely over-indexing to novel short-lead time power solutions (e.g., reciprocal 
engines, fuel-cells, etc).   
Datacenters will run: Much of Gas Services’ economic value from present day commitments 
is associated with the existing LTSAs, where there is long-lasting contractual protection of the 
revenue stream (~17 years on average). While this does require datacenters to run, it’s worth 
noting that the OpEx share of total cost of ownership (TCO) is typically <5%. Built datacenters 
continuing to run – even on a lower revenue projection – is hence the economically rational 
thing to be done (as was the case with the dot-com fibre infrastructure build out).

Sensitivity analysis – Varying the timing/magnitude of the drop 
There are a number of sensitivities in our estimates, of which the two most important are

Valuation
We value Siemens Energy using an SOTP approach. The key
moving parts include 1) Gas Services is valued via a two-stage
DCF with an explicit 10-year forecast and a terminal value that
assumes an 8.5% WACC and 2% terminal growth rate, and 2)
we value Grid Tech on 15x 2030E EBITA (discounted back to
2027E); this multiple is in line with peers' long-term average.
Our price target of €200 supports an Outperform rating.
Upside scenario
Our upside valuation of €240 is based on 13x 2030E EV/EBITA
on higher earnings (which can be reasonably underpinned
from 1) higher GT margins to reflect greater OE pricing &
service mix-shift vs prior cycles, 2) upside from GT margins
given supply-demand tightness, and 3) the achievement of
a more normalised 7% EBITA margin and higher growth.
This would value Siemens Energy roughly in line with GE
Vernova (using consensus forecasts), and also about at a slight
premium to the European electricals (again reflecting Siemens
Energy's higher growth trajectory and margin expansion).
Downside scenario
We see relatively limited downside risk to mid-term numbers,
though a rapid order collapse could negatively impact
sentiment. It also raises the risk of overcapacity in 2030 and
beyond. In such a scenario, we would expect Siemens Energy
to trade at a 20% discount to the sector on 2028E EV/EBITA,
implying a downside valuation of €115.
Investment summary
Our view: Siemens Energy is enjoying the benefits of a gas and
grid investment upcycle. We forecast 2027-30E 15% organic
sales p.a. and 35% EBITA p.a. We expect strong mid-term
performance as underpinned by the backlog, with even a
conservative outlook on cycle risk supporting an attractive
investment case.
• Gas & Grid focused story: Siemens Energy is a global leader
in power generation and electrical grid equipment. We
see the investment case as driven by the gas turbine and
transmission businesses (60% of revenues), where after
10 years of industry rationalisation there has been an
exogenous demand shock - strongly supporting growth and

Key fundamental questions
Our view
What is Gas Service's outlook on
growth and margins?
GS's growth and margin outlook are defined by the strong demand (both for OE
and aftermarket), pricing and backlog coverage. These underpin our 2027-30E 20%
organic CAGR and 2030E 33% EBITA margin forecast (2025A: 13.0%).
Why is the gas cycle different this
time?
While the industry has historically been very cyclical, differences in the Gas Services
business this time include a much higher share of Service revenues (66% vs 33% in
2012) and record order backlogs. Across the industry, we note greater diversification
of drivers than before, higher unit pricing & greater industry rationality - which limits
overcapacity risk. Capacity expansion also continues to be relatively constrained.
What is Grid Technologies' outlook
on growth and margins?
Our analysis shows GT's portfolio is set to over-benefit from shifting grid investment
priorities, with growth (2027-30E CAGR: +18.0%) and margin expansion well-
supported by a record backlog.
Why is the grid cycle different this
time?
Grid investment has generally trended upwards, though pockets of stalling growth
has at times led to overcapacity. Growth this time is well-supported by a record
multi-year backlog, modest capacity expansion (with 6 years of visibility enabling
appropriate planning) and a significant uplift in firm European grid operator
investment plans vs recent history. This helps limit airpocket risks.

Key sustainability questions
This section is intended to highlight key sustainability discussion points relevant to this company, as well as our views on the outlook. Both the
questions we highlight and our responses will evolve over time as the dialogue between management, analysts and investors continues to
advance. We welcome any feedback on the topics.
Our view
How does Siemens Energy help its
customers meet their sustainability
targets?
Siemens Energy's portfolio is well-suited to supporting the energy transition. In
Siemens Gamesa (SG), its wind turbine products help generate green electricity.
In Grid Technologies (GT), Siemens Energy's products assist with the transmission
grid build-out, further enabling wider societal electrification. In Gas Services (GS),
Siemens Energy's highly efficient gas turbines help provide consistent power
generation (balancing out renewable intermittency) at a lower carbon cost than
alternatives (emissions are ~50% of coal power plants). Within Transformation of
Industries (TI), products include green-hydrogen electrolysers which are helping
displace more traditional carbon-emitting fuels.
Does the company integrate
sustainability considerations into its
strategy?
Siemens Energy aims to reduce GHG emissions to be climate neutral in its own
operations by 2030, through a combination of offsets and a 55% reduction in GHG
emissions using specific levers such as renewable electricity, and reducing GT's SF6
emissions within GT - we note Siemens Energy already reached this 55% reduction in
FY2024. Siemens more broadly targets net zero products at some point (i.e. including
along the value chain), with an intermediate 28% target by 2030; this is currently
at 11%.
What is diversity like at board /
management level?
Women account for 33% of the Executive board and 40% of the Supervisory board.
Within the company, management target a 25% and 30% share of females in top
leadership positions by 2025/30 respectively; in 2024 this was 24%. Across the
broader company, women account for 17% of the workforce.
What policies / practices are in
place for the prevention of bribery,
corruption and anti-competitive
behaviour?
Siemens Energy take a zero tolerance approach to corruption and bribery. Taken
measures include customer due diligence, audits, employee training and the
company provides an anonymous whistleblower hotline.
Does the company measure
employee engagement?
Siemens conducts an annual employee engagement survey, with a response-rate of
81.5% (2023: 77%). Employee Net Promote Scores formed a part of the 2023 tranche
of variable compensation for the Executive Board.
