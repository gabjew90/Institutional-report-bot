Market Monitor 
Stocks rallied, as a drop in oil prices 
tempered inflation fears and helped the 
S&P 500 rebound from its biggest weekly 
drop since April 2025, and tech shares 
jumped even as AI heavyweight Nvidia 
slumped with controversy swirling around 
CEO Jensen Huang."The AI story is not 
going away if people are worried about 
that, the Democrats are not going to 
destroy AI. They can't. It's got a life of its 
own. They can put regulation around it, 
but they're certainly not stopping it," said 
Ken Polcari, partner and chief market 
strategist at SlateStone Wealth in Jupiter, 
Florida. Providing further support was a 
sharp drop in crude oil prices that helped 
dampen concerns about price pressures, 
as US President Donald Trump 
downplayed the likelihood of renewed 
strikes on Iran. "Oil prices for sure, if 
they're down 4%, that's certainly adding 
fuel to the fire," said Polcari. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 2.15% to 52,389.75, the S&P 500 gained 2.35% to 
7,549.94 and the Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.74% to 25,253.14.  
Treasury yields rose before October's highly anticipated consumer price inflation report on Tuesday, which may sway whether 
the Federal Reserve hikes rates at its December meeting. “I think CPI is still going to remain high enough for the Fed to 
continue to lean towards the December rate hike,” said Gennadiy Goldberg, head of US rates strategy at TD Securities. 
Benchmark 10-year notes were 4/32 lower to yield 4.8759%. Two-year notes were down 3/32, yielding 4.5656%. Thirty-year 
bonds fell 3/32, with a yield of 5.2656%.  Separately, The Treasury Department sold $58 billion in 3-year notes at a high yield 
of 4.627%. The bid-to-cover ratio was 2.53. 
The dollar weakened against major currencies including the euro and yen as easing concerns about an escalation in the Middle 
East conflict prompted investors to rotate back into risk assets following last week's selloff in artificial intelligence-linked stocks. 
"One of the main issues in shorts and trading around the US dollar right now is positioning," said Enrique Diaz-Alvarez, chief 
economist at Ebury. "The portion of the market that is actively trading and speculating is very long US dollar, which means it's 
difficult for the dollar to rally. We expect the euro to rebound over the medium term because the market is perhaps 
underestimating the strength of the recovery in the euro zone." The euro was up 0.70% at $1.1221. Against the Japanese yen, 
the dollar weakened 0.34% to 157.91 yen. Sterling rose 0.69% to $1.3441. The dollar index dipped 0.60% to 101.68. 
Oil prices slumped after US President Donald Trump downplayed the likelihood of renewed strikes on Iran, though attacks 
continued on ships near the Strait of Hormuz. "Crude futures are under selling pressure once again as oil volumes continue 
strong through the Strait of Hormuz", said Dennis Kissler, senior vice president of trading at BOK Financial. Brent crude 
futures were down 5.68% at $92.74 a barrel. US West Texas Intermediate futures fell 4.27 % to $86.95 per barrel. 
Gold prices climbed as the dollar eased and inflationary jitters took a back seat due to lower crude oil prices, while markets 
awaited US inflation data later this week. "We're seeing a lower US dollar index on this first day of the trading week," said Jim 
Wyckoff, a market analyst at American Gold Exchange. "We're seeing lower crude oil prices today that suggest receding 
inflationary fears — that's positive for the gold and silver markets, on ideas that lessening inflation fears will allow the central 
banks to lean easier on their monetary policies." Spot gold was up 1.79% at $4,138.39 per ounce. US gold futures for 
December delivery rose 1.86% to $4,150.70 an ounce.

TOP NEWS 
Nvidia slides as CEO Huang faces 
board scrutiny over affair with alleged 
spy 
Nvidia shares declined after an internal 
investigation into whether CEO Jensen 
Huang had been targeted by a foreign 
intelligence effort put his future at the AI 
chipmaker under scrutiny. The internal 
probe followed a media report that 
Huang had a close relationship with a 
Chinese businesswoman who allegedly 
passed information to Beijing. Huang told 
Nvidia's employees that he had been 
unfaithful to his wife, without disclosing 
details of the relationship. The 
investigation has put a spotlight on 63-
year-old Huang's future given Nvidia's 
status as the world's most valuable 
chipmaker and the CEO's role as the 
most prolific salesman of the AI boom. 
The leather-jacketed Huang enjoys rock-
star status worldwide, particularly in Asia, where he often mingles freely with fans, taking selfies and signing autographs for his 
devoted followers. Shares of the company closed 4.27% lower at $178.67.  
Boeing to cut 5% of commercial airplane division's workforce 
Boeing Commercial Airplanes plans to cut 5% of its workforce as it continues to struggle to increase jetliner production output, 
the division's chief Stephanie Pope told employees in an internal memo. "As shared in our third-quarter earnings, we need to 
reset the pace of our production rate ramps to provide our factories and supply chain partners additional time to achieve 
stability. Adjusting our production schedules and reducing aircraft output puts us in an overstaffed position," Pope said in the 
memo, which was reviewed by Reuters. Boeing CEO Kelly Ortberg told analysts last month the company would miss its 737 
production, and jet delivery targets this year. It now expects to hit a 737-production rate of 47 jets per month in early 2027, after 
previously projecting it would hit that pace by summer 2026. The reductions will happen through attrition and involuntary layoffs, 
with notices going out in December at the earliest. 
KKR profit hits record as asset sales, fees surge 
KKR reported record quarterly profit as the investment giant extended a strong run of asset sales and earned higher fees from 
managing client money. The company has ramped up investment exits this year and returned more capital to investors, even as 
higher interest rates have slowed asset sales across the industry. "We continue to monetize investments and return capital to 
our clients at record levels," co-CEOs Joseph Bae and Scott Nuttall said in a statement. The asset manager expects to return 
more than $40 billion to clients this year, more than 50% above its previous annual record. "More work to be done for KKR and 
the industry but considering the backdrop of 3Q that was at times slow, and at times volatile, investors should be mostly happy 
as KKR continues to scale across most asset classes and operating metrics," Evercore analyst Glenn Schorr said. 
Google unveils new AI chip variants as cloud demand surges 
Google introduced two new variations of its in-house tensor processing units, stepping up its challenge to Nvidia's dominance in 
the booming market for artificial intelligence chips. Google's TPUs have primarily been used internally for products such as 
Search, YouTube and Gemini, but the company has been making the chips available through its cloud business to attract 
enterprise AI customers. TPU sales made a meaningful contribution to results in the September quarter, according to Alphabet, 
with Google Cloud revenue more than doubling to $30.71 billion. Separately, a proposed Australian internet law could force 
Google to block under-18s from using its Gmail service, an Australia-based company policy head said, underscoring an industry 
warning that the draft regulation risks overreach. To read more, click here.

Skydance names Marvel veteran Latcham to lead DC Studios, replacing Gunn and Safran 
Skydance is replacing DC Studios chiefs James Gunn and Peter Safran with former Marvel Studios executive Jeremy Latcham 
as it overhauls its superhero business following the Warner Bros acquisition. Latcham will take over on December 1 and lead 
DC's film, television and animation slate, overseeing its creative pipeline, production, talent and strategy. He will report to Dana 
Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, co-chairs of Skydance Motion Picture Group, the company said. The appointment comes as DC 
looks to close the commercial gap with Marvel, which has enjoyed greater box-office success in the superhero movie business 
this year. Marvel's "Spider-Man: Brand New Day" has become this year's top-grossing film as it raked in more than $2.5 billion 
worldwide, compared with about $126 million for DC's "Supergirl", according to Box Office Mojo. Gunn and Safran have led DC 
Studios for four years and will continue to work as filmmakers and producers. 
Lilly's weight-loss pill improves sleep apnea in late-stage trial 
Eli Lilly said its oral weight-loss drug Foundayo significantly reduced breathing interruptions during sleep in obese adults with 
moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea, meeting the main goal of a late-stage study. The results build on Lilly's early lead 
in sleep apnea after its blockbuster injectable obesity medicine Zepbound became the first approved drug for the common sleep 
disorder, potentially offering a more convenient oral alternative if approved. In the 712-patient trial, Foundayo reduced breathing 
interruptions by up to 44% after 52 weeks in patients not using positive airway pressure therapy and by 27.5% in those using 
PAP therapy, Lilly said. Participants taking Foundayo also lost about 28 pounds on average over the study period, regardless of 
whether they used PAP therapy. 
US opens probe into Waymo vehicles over school bus safety violations 
The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration said it had opened a recall query into 2,895 vehicles operated by 
Alphabet's self-driving unit Waymo over continued traffic safety violations involving the vehicles' behavior when encountering 
school buses. The probe will determine whether a software fix Waymo rolled out late last year failed to stop its driverless cars 
from passing school buses with flashing red lights, as most of the violations happened after the company said the remedy was 
fully deployed. NHTSA said it was reviewing whether a software update implemented by Waymo under a December 2025 recall 
adequately addressed a defect that could cause Waymo vehicles to pass school buses displaying flashing red lights or 
extended stop arms after initially slowing or stopping. The auto safety regulator said most of the incidents occurred after 
November 2025, when Waymo said it had completed the software remedy for all affected vehicles. 
SpaceX plans $24 billion gas power plant, data center project in Texas 
SpaceX is planning a roughly $24 billion gas-fired power plant and super-compute facility in Starbase, Texas, according to a 
public hearing notice. The rockets-to-AI company's request comes as opposition to data centers grows, with voters rejecting 
them in 40 of 41 local ballot measures and Democrats, some of whom had campaigned on pausing new projects, taking control 
of the US Congress and the Texas governor's office in the midterm elections. The filing shows SpaceX, which bought Elon 
Musk's AI lab xAI earlier this year, has applied for a property tax abatement for the project. The Starbase City Commission will 
consider the abatement, plus a related economic development agreement, at a public meeting on December 9. The notice 
describes $24 billion as the estimated capital investment for the project's initial phases. It said the work may be done in multiple 
phases and could include future expansions. 
US FDA orders new eye risk warnings for Ozempic, Zepbound 
The US FDA informed patients and healthcare providers of a potential increased risk of a serious eye condition associated with 
the use of GLP-1 drugs for weight loss and diabetes. The US Food and Drug Administration said it is requiring GLP-1 drugs 
such as Eli Lilly's Mounjaro and Zepbound, and Novo Nordisk's Ozempic and Wegovy to add information to the "warnings and 
precautions" section of labeling about the potential increased risk of non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION). 
"FDA's drug safety communication applies to all medicines with GLP-1 RA activity and does not reference any findings specific 
to Lilly medicines. We are carefully evaluating the update and will respond to FDA promptly," said a Lilly spokesperson. The 
condition is a common cause of sudden, painless vision loss, and may lead to permanent impairment of sight in some cases. 
Disney, Adobe to co-develop custom AI models tailored for media giant 
Disney and Adobe have struck a multi-year deal to co-develop custom generative AI models for the media giant with the goal of 
scaling AI tools across its theme parks, studio and sports media businesses. The move will start with speeding up project 
development timelines at Disney's Experiences division, home to its global theme parks, resorts and cruise lines, and for 
concept exploration at StudioLAB, the innovation hub within Walt Disney Studios. Adobe will also work with Disney to use AI 
tools to boost engagement with sports fans at ESPN and bring more of ESPN's content archive to audiences. Through Adobe's 
Firefly Foundry, which lets enterprises build and deploy custom AI models, the two companies will work on gen-AI models 
trained on Disney's intellectual properties that will generate production-ready outputs. The models will be private and proprietary 
to Disney.

Insight and Analysis 
FOCUS-Banks, investors grow wary of AI debt, spooked by market selloff  
Banks and credit investors are upping their scrutiny of AI-related debt deals while showing reluctance to increase exposure to 
borrowers, jolted by the recent market selloff and changing sentiment toward the technology's growth trajectory. Some banks, 
which just a few weeks ago were lining up to fund AI deals, are now telling market participants they would be reluctant to finance 
additional deals even when they involve guarantees from the sector's marquee technology companies, according to several 
banking and credit sources. 
FOCUS-Trump administration's embrace of "perp" futures sets off risk alarms for Wall Street stalwarts 
The Trump administration's push to bring novel offshore perpetual futures to the US is raising alarms, according to interviews 
with four executives at incumbent exchanges and other key market participants who warned that the products will introduce 
serious new risks to the multitrillion-dollar derivatives market. Pioneered in non-US crypto markets, perpetual futures, or "perps," 
trade around the clock and do not expire, meaning investors can hold positions indefinitely. They are usually leveraged, 
sometimes by as much as 50 times, and are predominantly traded by retail or individual investors. 
AI power rush sends data centres hunting for used turbines and jet engines 
Utilities, developers and firms racing to power AI data centres are scouring the world for second-hand gas turbines, reviving 
mothballed machines and converting retired jet engines, as lead times for costly new equipment stretch into the next decade. 
Despite growing scepticism over the perils of artificial intelligence technology and a recent selloff in related stocks, demand for 
energy-hungry data centres is soaring, creating a critical bottleneck for essential power infrastructure and equipment, such as 
turbines.

Top News 
Barrick profit rises on higher gold prices 
Barrick Mining posted a rise in third-quarter profit, as higher gold prices outweighed the impact of a decline in production. Spot 
gold averaged $4,204.59 per ounce during the July-September period, up about 17.6% from a year earlier, driven by strong 
central-bank purchases and investor demand for bullion as a hedge against weakening currencies. Prospects for US interest-
rate cuts also lent support to gold, though prices later pulled back as expectations for monetary policy easing faded. Higher gold 
prices typically boost miners' revenue and margins. Barrick's quarterly average realized gold price rose 23% over the year 
earlier to $4,243 per ounce. Gold production, however, fell to 816,000 ounces in the third quarter, from 829,000 ounces a year 
earlier, largely due to extreme weather at the Veladero mine and delayed access to higher-grade ore at North Mara. The 
company said it expects to access the higher-grade material in the fourth quarter. The Canadian gold miner earned $1.32 billion, 
or 80 cents per share, for the three months ended September 30, compared with $1.30 billion, or 76 cents per share, a year 
earlier. 
Karniol-Tambour to leave Bridgewater to lead Canada's sovereign fund 
Karen Karniol-Tambour will leave Bridgewater Associates after two decades to become the inaugural CEO of the Canada 
Strong Fund, said Nir Bar Dea, chief of the US-based hedge fund manager, in a statement to Reuters. Canadian Prime Minister 
Mark Carney also announced her appointment to lead the sovereign wealth fund, bringing her Wall Street investment expertise 
to one of his key economic initiatives. Carney had launched the Canada Strong Fund in April with an initial endowment of C$25 
billion to invest in major Canadian projects and companies. Under Karniol-Tambour's leadership, the fund will be professionally 
managed, operating at arm's length from the government, with its own board of directors, according to a statement from 
Carney's office. Budget 2026 had outlined plans to establish the Canada Strong Fund as an independent Crown corporation. In 
the interim, it will operate as a wholly owned subsidiary of the Canada Development Investment Corporation — the 
government's financial and commercial adviser. Resource-rich countries, particularly those with large oil and gas reserves, often 
establish state-owned funds to manage excess revenue, investing the money in domestic and international markets and 
projects. The Canada Strong Fund will also offer retail investment products, giving Canadians access to investment 
opportunities traditionally available mainly to institutional investors.

have advanced 0.6%. They climbed 0.6% in
