What happened?
On 5 November, US technology stocks led a sharp global 
sell-off, reversing the initial post-midterm-election relief 
rally as investors reassessed the implications of Democratic 
gains, particularly at the state level, for AI infrastructure 
and data center development. The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% 
and the S&P 500 4.4%, while semiconductors were hit 
particularly hard, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index 
dropping 12.3%. Weakness subsequently spread to Asian 
and European markets. The declines appeared driven less 
by a deterioration in AI demand and more by concerns 
around the cost, timing, permitting, power requirements, 
and potential returns of the data center buildout. While 
greater regulatory scrutiny could delay or relocate projects 
and raise costs, we believe it is more likely to redistribute 
and extend the investment cycle than derail it. Underlying 
AI demand remains robust, and we continue to expect 
substantial AI capital spending in the coming years.
What investors should do?
Market sell-offs can create opportunities for investors to 
revisit their exposures. We continue to see upside for broad 
equity markets over the next six to 12 months, supported 
by an intact AI investment cycle, resilient earnings, and a

At the same time, elevated technology exposure and the 
prospect of continued market volatility reinforce the case 
for diversification. Our second screen therefore focuses 
on quality diversifiers with lower sensitivity to the AI 
investment cycle, identifying companies with strong balance 
sheets, resilient profitability and cash generation, and 
earnings drivers that are less dependent on AI-related capital 
spending. Please see the 2) "Quality diversifiers" list of

Together, the two screens offer complementary approaches 
to navigating the sell-off: Selectively adding exposure 
where the correction has created opportunities, while 
strengthening portfolios with quality companies whose 
earnings and return drivers are more differentiated from 
those currently driving AI-related volatility.
Risks to our thesis
The recent market volatility highlights the potential for 
abrupt shifts in investor sentiment and risk appetite. 
There is no guarantee that equity markets, sectors, or 
individual stocks have reached their lows, and further 
declines could result from weaker-than-expected economic 
growth, higher-for-longer interest rates, changes in inflation 
expectations, tighter financial conditions, geopolitical 
escalation, energy-price shocks, or other unforeseen events. 
Elevated valuations and investor positioning in parts of the 
technology and AI-related market may amplify price moves, 
while changes in regulation, data center investment, power 
availability, supply chains, or expectations for AI adoption 
and monetization could lead to further volatility and greater 
differentiation between companies and sectors.
The stocks identified by our screens remain exposed to 
company-, sector-, and market-specific risks. Recent share-
price weakness does not necessarily imply subsequent 
recovery, and positive earnings momentum can reverse 
if the earnings outlook deteriorates. Similarly, companies 
with strong profitability, cash generation, and historically 
low sensitivity to semiconductor performance may not 
provide the same diversification benefits in future periods. 
Historical correlations can change materially, particularly 
during periods of market stress, and low correlation does 
not imply protection against absolute losses. Company 
performance may also be affected by earnings execution, 
valuation changes, interest rates, currencies, commodity 
prices, regulation, competitive dynamics, and capital-
allocation decisions. As a result, performance across the 
selected stocks may vary significantly, recovery may be 
uneven or take time, and the quantitative criteria used 
in the screens should not be interpreted as guarantees 
of future performance or as substitutes for appropriate 
portfolio diversification.

2) "Quality diversifiers" list of stocks 
We favor complementing technology exposure with quality diversifiers that have strong fundamentals and lower sensitivity to the AI investment cycle, helping portfolios navigate periods of 
elevated AI-related volatility. 
 
Criteria: 
 
CIO Selected universe 
 
Market capitalization above USD 20bn 
 
Consensus earnings growth in both 2026 and 2027 
 
Return on equity (ROE) above 15% in both 2026 and 2027 
 
Resilient profitability, with the 10-year low in 12-month forward ROE remaining above 7% 
 
Free cash flow yields above 3%, with financials exempt given limited comparability of this metric 
 
Low sensitivity to global semiconductor performance, defined as a correlation below 0.2 based on weekly relative returns over the past five years
