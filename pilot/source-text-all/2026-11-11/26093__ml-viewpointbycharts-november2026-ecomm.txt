• In our view, the recent rise in long-term yields 
should be considered alongside a resilient 
nominal growth environment, rather than 
viewed solely as a reflection of mounting fiscal 
pressures.
• The U.S. economy grew at an annualized pace 
of 3.4% in Q3 2026, the strongest GDP growth 
in a year, and nominal growth continues to run 
above the 10-year Treasury yield, suggesting 
the recent move in rates is consistent with 
continued economic resilience and supporting 
the sustainability of rising government debt.
• As a result, we believe higher long-term yields 
may be more indicative of a stronger nominal 
growth regime than a fundamental loss of 
confidence in U.S. credit.
CIO Key Considerations
The combination of resilient growth and 
higher yields argues for focusing on the 
strength of the economic backdrop, 
rather than viewing rising rates as 
inherently negative for markets.

CIO Key Considerations
We view noise-driven Equity pullbacks as 
potential opportunities to buy or rebalance, 
supported by resilient growth, broadening 
earnings strength, rising productivity and 
robust business investment.
• We remain overweight Equities, with a 
preference for the U.S., as one of the world’s 
strongest and most innovative economies. We 
emphasize diversification across and within 
Equities, particularly as market rotations drive 
new pockets of leadership.
• Thematic opportunities continue to develop in 
biotechnology, life sciences, and robotics, 
alongside heavy investment flows into 
AI-enabled infrastructure, grid modernization, 
power generation, and aerospace and defense.
• We remain constructive on Fixed Income 
overall but are underweight to fund our Equity 
overweight. With 10-year U.S. Treasury 
nominal yields near 5% and real yields above 
2.5%, all-in yields are compelling. We favor 
moving out of cash and the front-end of the 
curve to extend duration toward strategic 
targets.

gains and operating efficiencies create 
additional room for companies to sustain

Fixed Income
• Hyperscalers have issued $272 billion (B) of 
debt in 2026*, up from $108B in 2025, 
reflecting their commitment to funding a 
significant, multiyear growth opportunity. 
• Despite record hyperscaler issuance, the 
resulting credit-spread widening has remained 
concentrated within AI-related issuers rather 
than spreading across the broader investment-
grade market. 
• Hyperscalers continued to benefit from low 
leverage, sizable cash balances, strong earnings 
growth and ample capacity to take on 
additional debt while maintaining strong credit 
profiles, suggesting recent underperformance is 
primarily technical and may ultimately create 
more attractive entry points. 
CIO Key Considerations
Credit-spread widening remains contained, 
while continued hyperscaler issuance and 
softer technicals warrant patience but could 
ultimately create a more attractive entry 
point.

Allocating to Alternatives allows qualified

generating revenue and scaling under 
private ownership before reaching public 
markets. In 2025, the average U.S. company 
waited 14 years to IPO, up from 8 years in 
2000, according to PitchBook. 
• Importantly, VC/GE exposure extends 
beyond immature startups, as a growing 
proportion of company development and 
value creation has been occurring within
