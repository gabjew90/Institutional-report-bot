Market recap
October payrolls rose 165,000, well above consensus near 65,000, while 
unemployment fell to 4.1% and prior months were revised higher. Several U.S. 
Federal Reserve officials called for a more restrictive stance, citing persistent 
inflation risks. Equities swung sharply, with a Thursday selloff tied to data center 
regulation fears that partly reversed on Friday. Treasuries rallied anyway, as 
investors sought safety after the election, pushing short-term Treasury prices higher.
Gains were broad, with convertibles the only major sector to decline as they 
absorbed most of the equity drawdown. Municipals led for a fourth consecutive 
week. Preferreds rebounded sharply after several weeks of underperformance, 
and agency mortgage-backed securities gained as the gap between their yields 
and comparable Treasury yields narrowed. High-yield bonds and leveraged 
loans showed continued divergence, with higher-quality issuers outperforming 
weaker borrowers. 
Outlook
Market pricing has shifted toward a December Fed rate hike following the strong 
payrolls report and hawkish Fed commentary. We continue to expect one more 
hike by year-end, with December as the likely window, followed by an extended 
hold through 2027. The path depends on the energy shock and its pass-through to 
core inflation.
Credit fundamentals remain sound. We continue to favor high-quality income 
and, within municipals, longer duration over Treasuries. We forecast the 10-year 
Treasury yield at 4.95% for year-end 2026 and 4.75% for year-end 2027, with the 
2-year at 4.70% this year and 4.50% next year. With the 10-year at 4.86% after four 
weeks of rally, we see modest room for yields to rise into December.

U.S. Treasuries 
Treasury yields fell for a fourth straight week as investors sought safety after the 
election. Short-term and intermediate yields dropped the most, while long-term 
yields moved less, so the overall shape of the yield curve barely changed. 
The 2-year yield fell 8 bps to 4.51%, 
the 5-year fell 8 bps to 4.65%, and the 
10-year fell 7 bps to 4.86%. The 20-
year and 30-year each dropped 4 bps to 
5.30% and 5.27%. 
Tax-exempt municipals
Municipals led all sectors for a fourth straight week, as the long duration profile 
continued to benefit from falling yields. The index yields 4.09%, and high-yield 
municipals yield 5.81%, levels that have historically drawn buyers. We view any 
temporary price dips as potential opportunities to add high-quality duration, 
and we prefer longer-duration municipals over Treasuries. 
The Bloomberg Municipal Index 
returned +0.92%.
Taxable municipals
Taxable municipals posted a solid gain as falling rates supported the sector’s 
long duration profile. Yields near 5.51% are attracting crossover investors. We 
see the current market as a good time to seek high yields at attractive prices.
The Bloomberg Municipal Taxable 
Index returned +0.47% with 
spreads at 56 bps. 
 
Investment grade corporates
Prices moved up and down but finished roughly where they started. Spreads 
widened early in the week, narrowed after the U.S. midterm election results and 
then eased back as volatility rose and investors pulled back from riskier assets. 
Credit held up well despite a heavy week of new bond sales, mostly from large 
technology and AI-linked companies. Investors are demanding slightly higher 
yields on new bonds, but those bonds have generally risen in price after issue. Fund 
inflows jumped sharply, well above the four-week average.
The Bloomberg U.S. Corporate 
Bond Index returned +0.41% with 
spreads at 91 bps.

U.S. high yield corporates
High-yield bonds had a generally positive week, starting firm and strengthening 
midweek before turning more cautious by Friday. Higher-rated bonds 
outperformed lower-rated ones. New bond sales reached $9.7 billion, with net fund 
inflows of $1.4 billion.
The Bloomberg High Yield 2% 
Issuer Capped Index returned 
+0.40% with spreads at 336 bps.
Preferred securities
Demand for Additional Tier 1 (AT1) market was strong despite some weakness 
early in the week. A dip on Wednesday caused by weaker economic data and a 
cheaply priced new issue lasted briefly before buyers returned. Thursday was 
another strong session for higher-risk and European periphery euro bonds, 
though some profit-taking appeared in shorter-dated bonds. Preferreds and 
hybrids then outperformed sharply while stocks fell more than 4%, supported 
by strong investor inflows and low dealer inventories.
The ICE Preferred Index returned 
+0.64% with spreads at 197 bps.
Senior loans
Loans firmed through Wednesday as demand from loan funds and investors 
buying back bets against the market pushed about 10% of loans above par, 
up from 5% the prior week. Prices fell Thursday as stock weakness spread to 
loans. New loan issuance was modest at $1.8 billion, and investors put $646 
million into funds.
The S&P Leveraged Loan 
Index returned +0.36% with 
spreads at 496 bps.
Securitized credit
Mortgage-backed securities recovered after an early-week decline, with 
spreads narrowing by Friday. The 30-year conforming mortgage rate fell 15 
basis points for the week and is now approaching 7%. Asset-backed securities 
softened midweek but held firm, supported by steady demand as consumer 
and job conditions remained healthy. Investors bought into a small price dip 
in top-rated collateralized loan obligations, and commercial mortgage-backed 
securities saw buying continue for a fourth week.
The Bloomberg MBS Index returned 
0.57%, with spreads at 30 basis 
points. The Bloomberg CMBS Index 
returned 0.38%, with spreads at 73 
basis points. The Bloomberg ABS 
Index returned 0.24%, with spreads 
at 52 basis points.

Global emerging markets
Hard-currency sovereign bonds were volatile and finished slightly lower, with 
no clear trend. Local-currency bonds were the bright spot, led by South Africa, 
Brazil and Colombia. Investor outflows declined substantially, though outflows 
from hard-currency funds accelerated. New bonds came mainly from Central 
and Eastern Europe, the Middle East and Africa, with orders nearly three times 
the amount offered, and most traded above their issue price.
The Bloomberg Global EM 
Index returned +0.31% with 
spreads at 196 bps.
