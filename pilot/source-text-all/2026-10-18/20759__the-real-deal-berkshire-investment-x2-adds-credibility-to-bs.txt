The Real Deal; Berkshire Investment (x2) Adds Credibility to BSP
Story
We reiterate our Outperform rating on BSP and see the Berkshire Hathaway investment,
totaling $930M (~4% ownership), providing enormous validation to the management
team and the broader BSP model. Our recent investor conversations have been
dominated by skeptics, worried about (1) BSP as a "consumer inertia" trade and (2) an
over-focus on the current perimeter assets as opposed to the durable and replicable
transformation engine that BSP has built. We believe both bear cases miss the forest for
the trees. The meaningful step-up in Berkshire investment, shortly after the IPO, provides
multiple boosts to the bull case including (1) credibility enhancer, (2) firepower for deals,
both with the added capital and as a partner going forward, and (3) the potential for further
equity investment that makes it difficult to bet against BSP, in our view. We reiterate our
$72 target and bull case of $95 per share.
Key Points
Today, Bending Spoons announced a new $330M investment from Berkshire
Hathaway, structured as a private placement of 10 million shares at $33.00 per
share. This follows Berkshire’s $600M investment at BSP’s July 2026 IPO, previously not
disclosed, and brings its total ownership stake to 4%.
We view the announcement positively. Most importantly, Berkshire's decision to add to
its position just a few months after the IPO and at these levels provides another vote
of confidence in the Bending Spoons model from one of the world's most respected
long-term investors. While the dollar amount is relatively modest in the context of BSP's
capital base, we believe the signaling value is meaningful given the company's limited
history as a public company.
We also view management's commentary around the raise as supportive of the M&A
story and reinforces our view that BSP continues to see a healthy opportunity set for
capital deployment.
From a balance sheet perspective, the transaction adds roughly $330M of incremental
capital on top of the $1.1B raised at IPO and the debt facilities secured around the
pending Airtable acquisition. While not transformative, the additional capital provides
incremental dry powder to pursue future opportunities without requiring additional
leverage. If anything, Berkshire's willingness to increase its ownership reinforces our
above-consensus view that BSP can continue to deploy capital at attractive returns and
compound value over time.
BSP a bad "consumer inertial" play: Investors worry the rise of AI agents like Muse or
Instinct (private) could lead to spikes in churn. BSP evaluates ~700 (2025) deals per year
and very specifically targets businesses that have long-tenured customers, where the
relationship is sticky with high exit costs, and where the aggregate cost is not so high that
there is a huge compulsion to churn even if prices rise. This is not the proverbial exercise
bike turned into a clothes hanger.

Price Target Calculation and Key Risks
Investment Thesis
We see Bending Spoons as a unique high quality M&A compounder that stands to put significant capital to work, at high returns, against
M&A auctions where the company has a significant edge with its operational playbook. We like the stock primarily for five reasons
including: (1) impressive track record with compelling returns, driven by both revenue growth and cost reductions, (2) scaling the capital
deployed with sustained efficiency while reducing the "Spooner" intensity, (3) diversified business expected to continue to diversify
with M&A, (4) non-correlated asset, betting less on any particular secular trend and more on operational execution, and (5) significant
addressable market of lagging businesses to acquire and optimize.
Base Case/PT
Our base case target price of $72 is based on ~17x FY28 Adj. EBITDA of ~$3.5B, discounted back by nearly 4 months and assumes
acquisition efficiency of 78%, i.e., future acquisitions obtain ~78% of efficiency compared to historical acquisitions. The ~17x multiple
is a premium to Roper Technologies (ROP, covered by Brett Linzey) at ~14x and Constellation Software (NC) at approximately 11x,
reflecting BSP's superior revenue / Adj OI growth CAGRs of ~70% / 79% (2025-28), estimated Adj EBITDA margins of ~55% by 2028,
but with execution risk.
Bull Case/PT
Our bull case price target of $95 could play out if BSP deploys approximately 115% of capital on M&A versus our base case at 83%
efficiency relative to historical, generating total FY28E Adj. EBITDA of ~$4B. At 19x, reflecting stronger demonstrated execution and
increased confidence in scalability, this implies ~130% upside to the current share price. The bull case is supported by a strong pipeline,
an AI transformation engine that is becoming more productive each year, and a leverage ratio well within covenant limits that provide
meaningful capacity to scale deployment.
Bear Case/PT
Our bear case price target of $25 would play out if BSP deploys less capital with lower transformation success, or approximately 75%
of our base case acquisition capital at 74% of efficiency relative to historical levels, generating total FY28E Adj. EBITDA of ~$2.8B. At
a lower 9x multiple, reflecting execution shortfall and reduced market confidence in the forward pipeline, this scenario implies ~39%
downside to the current share price.
Risks
Key risks to monitor: (1) scaling capital at forecasted volumes while maintaining deal discipline, (2) sustaining historical efficiency levels
as acquisition sizes grow, (3) execution risk on larger and more complex targets, (4) whether current perimeter assets hold or grow
earnings or face decay from competition or neglect, (5) AI disruption across portfolio products, and (6) preserving the management
team and culture of operational excellence. Skeptics point to the challenge of deploying increasing capital at attractive returns, AI risk
to existing assets, and uncertainty around baking in future M&A contribution into forward estimates.
