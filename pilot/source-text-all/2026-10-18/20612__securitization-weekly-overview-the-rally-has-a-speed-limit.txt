Securitization Weekly Overview 
The rally has a speed limit 
     
   
 
 
Hot inflation, lower yields: a tactical rally 
Treasuries rallied despite stronger inflation as equity weakness and short covering 
outweighed hawkish repricing. Duration led weekly returns, with 30-year Treasuries 
gaining 2.72%, 10-year Treasuries 1.18% and agency MBS 1.01%. The move supports 
last week’s tactical inflection call, but not a secular shift toward lower rates. With the 
10-year approaching a 5.0% technical objective and the Fed still expected to hike, 
participate in the rally but do not chase duration. 
Agency MBS finally has asymmetry 
The mortgage basis remained roughly unchanged even as Treasuries rallied. We moved 
to an overweight on October 9 because easing rates and volatility are more likely to 
tighten spreads than mortgages are to cheapen materially. October speeds should fall 
approximately 10% as mortgage rates rise and seasonal turnover slows. Agency MBS 
could perform without requiring a secular bond bull market 
Credit is widening for the right reasons 
Credit weakness remains primarily rates- and volatility-driven rather than evidence of 
broad fundamental deterioration. However, weaker demand for high yield and loans, 
together with pressure in AI-linked and CCC credit, supports remaining up in quality. 
Corporate spreads may recover first if volatility declines, while slower-moving 
securitized sectors could continue to cheapen relatively before following corporates 
tighter 
Supply is preserving securitized value 
Non-agency RMBS issuance reached approximately $4bn this week and $209bn YTD. 
Spreads widened again, leaving new-issue AAA non-QM attractive, although heavy 
supply should continue through the fourth quarter and into 2027. ABS spreads remain 
biased wider amid steady issuance, while conduit CMBS mezzanine spreads widened by 
as much as 50bp. Supply is delaying spread compression but preserving better entry 
points, in our view. 
Add carry, not beta 
Use volatility and new-issue concessions to add high-quality carry rather than 
indiscriminate credit risk. Favor agency MBS, new-issue AAA non-QM, CMO floaters, CRE 
CLO AAA and AAA SASB floaters. Maintain a defensive posture in CMBS and expect ABS 
spreads to remain rangebound with a wider bias. CLOs continue to provide dependable 
carry, but AAA relative value is less compelling than selected fixed-rate MBS, we think.

Securitization Weekly Overview | 16 October 2026 
 
 
 The rally has a speed limit 
Last week’s “buy the dips” thesis is intact, but this week clarifies the sequence. Hotter 
inflation preserved the case for an October Fed hike, yet Treasuries rallied as equity 
weakness and CTA short covering dominated. That combination is supportive for 
duration-sensitive securitized assets in the near term, while also warning against 
treating the rally as a lasting regime change. Rates strategy still prefers fading the rally 
once short covering matures, and technical analysis places the most important support 
for the 10-year yield near 5.02%-4.92%, with 4.81% possible. A break below 4.74% 
would challenge the near-term bearish-rates view.  
The macro backdrop remains “stronger, higher, steeper.” Strong nominal activity, sticky 
inflation, fiscal deficits and AI-related capital demand continue to pressure real yields 
and term premium. The practical implication is a two-stage trade: own the tactical 
Treasury rally while yields retrace, then emphasize spread sectors that have cheapened 
as volatility and supply created better entry points. The duration rally is the bridge to the 
spread opportunity, not evidence that the secular rate problem has disappeared. 
Inflation, the Fed and volatility 
September inflation surprised to the upside, lifting estimated core PCE to roughly 0.34% 
month over month and 3.1% year over year in the cross-sector summary. Rates 
nevertheless bull-flattened. The October FOMC payer was closed after CPI reached 
target, but the Fed is still expected to hike, and preferred rates positions remain short 
two-year Treasuries, a 2s30s flattener and long 30-year asset-swap spreads.  
MOVE near 98.5 is already in the historical 90-110 zone associated with weaker equity 
and credit performance. Tactical relief is possible, but a durable return to the summer 
volatility lows appears unlikely through first-half 2027. That argues for structures with 
carry, short duration or convexity support, rather than persistent short-volatility 
exposure. 
Credit: wider spreads, more discrimination 
Corporate credit weakness still appears primarily rates-driven rather than a fundamental 
downturn. Investment-grade investors increasingly regard wider spreads and higher 
yields as a buying opportunity, but flows show greater selectivity: IG inflows slowed to 
$4.3bn, while high yield, loans and EM debt saw weaker demand. The message is 
rotation toward quality rather than broad risk abandonment.  
AI-linked credit is the main emerging fault line. Declining profitability alone is unlikely to 
destabilize the market; leverage and the gap between returns on invested capital and 
funding costs are more important. High-yield AI spreads reached new wides, neocloud 
issuers remained under pressure and CCC-rated credits underperformed. This reinforces 
the preference for resilient balance sheets, less-cyclical sectors and adequate spread 
compensation. 
Agency MBS: overweight, but measured 
Despite stronger-than-expected inflation, Treasuries rallied week over week and 
mortgage performance held steady, leaving the basis roughly unchanged. We moved the 
basis to overweight on October 9. From current levels, easing rates and volatility are 
more likely to pull spreads tighter than mortgages are to cheapen materially toward fair 
value. The position is tactical and measured: valuations are not uniformly cheap and the 
longer-run rate outlook remains difficult, in our view.  
Prepayment technicals should remain supportive. BofA expects October speeds to 
decline approximately 10%, reflecting higher driving mortgage rates and normal 
seasonal slowing. In a rally, upper-belly 5% and 5.5% coupons should benefit. Discount 
pass-throughs and selected faster-paying pools offer attractive yield, spread and 
convexity, while CMO floaters remain a useful source of carry and price stability across 
rate paths.

Non-agency MBS: supply keeps value elevated 
Weekly issuance was approximately $4bn, bringing 2026 volume to $209bn. Spreads 
widened again, with non-QM AAA near 150bp, FCF Jumbo 2.0 near 160bp, prime 
treatment near 180bp and HELOC/CES near 140bp. The combination of wider spreads 
and yields above 6% keeps new-issue AAA non-QM among the most attractive 
securitized opportunities.  
The supply story is not finished. Even after record non-QM securitization this year, the 
full scale of 2026 originations points to new issuance records in the fourth quarter and 
in 2027. Elevated supply can keep spreads wide and produce repeated entry points over 
the next 12 months. The right stance is constructive but patient: add high-quality 
exposure as concessions develop rather than chasing a near-term tightening move. 
CMBS: defend against the rate regime 
Conduit mezzanine spreads widened by as much as 50bp this week. We recommend 
defensive positioning through continuing rate volatility by moving up in credit quality 
and down in duration. Elevated Treasury yields still constrain refinancing capacity, 
transaction volumes and property-price appreciation, particularly for weaker Class B and 
C properties lacking meaningful cash-flow growth.  
If current conditions persist, CMBS spreads could widen further. We therefore favor CRE 
CLO AAA and AAA SASB floaters, where carry is strong and senior credit protection 
provides insulation. Fixed-rate mezzanine exposure should require more compensation 
before becoming broadly attractive. 
ABS: robust supply, wider bias 
Thirteen ABS deals priced this week. The supplied sector summary reports YTD volume 
of $8.5bn and retains a $407bn full-year forecast; the YTD figure should be checked 
against the broader October digest before publication because the digest reports a 
much larger aggregate ABS total through mid-October. Directionally, the message is 
clear: steady issuance and broader market pressure have left spreads biased wider.  
We expect spreads to remain rangebound but with a wider bias. Credit metrics should 
soften somewhat, with better prospects for stabilization into the new year. Selectivity 
remains essential: prefer structures and sponsors offering adequate excess spread, 
established capital-markets access and stronger ratings histories broadly attractive. 
CLO: carry remains, behavior turns conservative 
CLO AAA spreads are near 122bp and BB spreads near 760bp. CLO ETF assets have 
surpassed $60bn, supported by $20bn of year-to-date inflows. The growing ETF channel 
reinforces demand, while elevated rates volatility gives managers opportunities to 
exchange loans priced at $85-90 for short-duration bonds.  
Manager behavior is becoming more conservative: new-issue portfolio margins, B-minus 
exposure and WARF are declining. That is supportive for credit quality, though AAA CLOs 
remain relatively rich versus selected fixed-rate MBS. Retain CLOs as a carry anchor, but 
direct incremental risk toward sectors with more upside from lower rates and volatility.  
Cross-sector conclusions 
• 
Rates: participate tactically in the rally toward a 10-year yield near 5.0%, but do 
not extrapolate it into a strategic duration call.  
• 
Agency MBS: maintain a cautious basis overweight; slower prepayments and 
lower volatility improve near-term asymmetry.  
• 
Non-agency MBS: favor new-issue AAA non-QM; record supply should preserve 
attractive entry points.  
• 
CMBS: stay defensive, emphasizing CRE CLO AAA and AAA SASB floaters over 
conduit mezzanine risk.

Issuance trends 
Securitized issuance remains on pace for another historically strong year despite higher 
interest rates, elevated volatility and wider credit spreads. Through October 16, 2026, 
gross securitized issuance totaled $2.07tn, up 14% YoY, while net issuance reached 
$439bn, up 18% YoY. Growth remains broadly distributed across most major sectors, 
indicating that borrower demand and investor appetite have remained resilient during 
the recent market turbulence.  
Housing and commercial real estate continue to lead issuance growth. Gross Agency 
MBS issuance reached $1.11tn, up 16% YoY, while Agency CMBS issuance of $148bn 
was up 21%. Non-agency RMBS remains one of the fastest-growing sectors, with 
issuance reaching $210bn, up 31% YoY. Private-label CMBS issuance increased 16% to 
$139bn.  
ABS issuance remains solid, with gross supply of $328bn, up 9% YoY, and net issuance 
of $50bn, up 18%. CLOs remain the only major securitized sector in which gross 
issuance trails last year: the $130bn issued YTD represents a 17% decline, while net 
supply is approximately 2% lower at $59bn. Recent spread widening and improving value 
may support demand, although issuance remains below last year’s pace.  
Outside securitized products, corporate issuance remains exceptionally robust. IG gross 
issuance reached $1.76tn, up 30% YoY, while net issuance surged 93% to $769bn. Total 
corporate gross supply increased 18% to $2.38tn, and net supply increased 51% to 
$968bn. By contrast, leveraged-loan gross issuance declined 10% and net issuance fell 
41%. Treasury gross issuance was essentially unchanged YoY at $3.41tn, while net 
Treasury supply declined 17% to $1.18tn

easing rates and vol more likely to pull spreads tighter than mortgages are to cheapen 
materially toward fair value. Prepayment speeds should remain muted, with BofA
