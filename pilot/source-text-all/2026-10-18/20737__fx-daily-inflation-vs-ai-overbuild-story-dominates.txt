FX Daily: Inflation vs. AI overbuild story
dominates
Financial markets have calmed a little after Coreweave stock bounced on
Thursday. But the debate between intensifying price pressures needing
tighter monetary policy and a tech correction requiring a softer policy
outlook continues. Expect more consolidation in FX markets today and
more interest in local stories, such as Indonesia's equity travails
FX markets remain caught between intensifying inflation and a re-pricing of AI risk
USD: What does the flatter yield curve mean?
The sell-off on Coreweave assets saw a modest reversal yesterday after Nvidia's CEO, Jensen
Huang, said that Coreweave's financial strength was improving and its cost of capital was
declining. Perhaps those comments should not be surprising from a major shareholder in
Coreweave; let's see how long this bounce in Coreweave assets lasts. The big takeaway this
week has been the recovery of the long-end of the US Treasury market, even as inflation data
continues to come in hot. Here it seems that investors are starting to find value in long-dated
Treasuries and are viewing those as a middling hedge against a correction in the AI trade.
Were the tech sell-off to gain momentum, that Treasury buying would move to the shorter
end of the curve.

While fears of an AI correction may be growing, inflation data continues to come in hot.
Yesterday it was US September PPI data blowing away consensus. And today, import prices
should come in near 8% year-on-year, the highest since summer 2022. Inflation data is
keeping short-dated interest rates supported and has delivered a near 20bp flattening in the
2-10 year Treasury curve this week.
What does that mean for FX? Typically, an environment where the Fed is applying the brakes
while growth prospects are being revised down is good for the dollar against activity and
emerging market currencies. It is only when short end rates break lower that the dollar is
ready to drop. And we are not there yet.
Barring another big leg down in AI stocks and risk assets in general, expect the dollar to
consolidate near recent highs. We would continue to expect some defensive outperformance
of the Swiss franc in this environment. DXY will probably struggle to get above the 102.40/50
area now, while support is 101.75/80.
Chris Turner
EUR: Inflation to be confirmed at 3.8% today
Today's eurozone data should see September CPI confirmed at 3.8% – well above the
European Central Bank's 2% target. Markets are still reeling from the hawkish set of inflation
forecasts delivered at the ECB September press conference, a key driver of the near 75bp of
further tightening expected from the ECB. Our team looks for just one more hike, however, and
developments in French bond markets will have a big say in whether the ECB needs to tighten
much more from there. 
EUR/USD has received a lift from this week's unease in US AI stocks and may be entering a
decent consolidative phase now. This could be worth a move all the way to 1.1340/50. But do
not expect EUR/USD to be the big outperformer on a further US equity correction. We think a
lower USD/CHF would take the lead there.
 Chris Turner
CEE: Wage embers and price sparks
The CEE week ends with mostly second-tier data. Hungary releases August wage figures this
morning, one of the central bank’s remaining domestic obstacles to achieving stable inflation.
We forecast wage growth of 7.1%, still well above CEE peers, while the agreed minimum wage
increase raises the risk of renewed wage pressures next year.
Later, the Czech Republic publishes September producer prices. Alongside agricultural prices,
these suggest inflationary pressures are broadening beyond fuel costs, posing an upside risk to
the CPI outlook, as discussed in our CEE report earlier this week. Turkey will also release

September inflation expectations, the final relevant data point before next week’s central bank
meeting. We expect the Central Bank of Turkey to resume its easing cycle, which was paused

CEE fixed income saw a strong rally yesterday, tracking core markets despite negative global

global narrative, and that higher energy prices will keep rate hikes priced in for longer. The
Hungarian curve will face a test next Monday or Tuesday with the release of the draft budget
for next year – an event drawing significant market attention, though expectations may be
overly optimistic. FX, on the other hand, remains more or less stable, and we continue to hold

IDR: MSCI downgrade warning sinks local assets
In a statement that will have been a difficult read for Indonesian authorities, MSCI said
overnight that it was launching a consultation to reclassify MSCI Indonesia indexes from
Emerging Market to Standalone Market status. Merely dropping Indonesia to Frontier Markets
would not properly recognise the depth of the market problems, says MSCI. These problems
are opaque shareholding structures and suspected coordinated trading behaviour, according
to MSCI. 
Local equity indices fell 10-11% on the announcement and an already weak rupiah gapped
above the 18000 threshold. Expect more FX intervention from Bank Indonesia to protect this
year's high at 18200 – but clearly pressure for a weaker rupiah is building. Please see ING's
Deepali Bhargava's latest comments on USD/IDR here.  
Indonesia's problems serve as a reminder that EMFX will increasingly be driven by local stories
over the coming quarters. How the AI trade fares will dominate Korea and Taiwan, Brazilian
assets could be re-rated further on a Bolsonaro presidency, and Hungary's euro ambitions will
have a major say on local asset markets. Please see our latest thinking on this topic in
October's edition of FX Talking.
Chris Turner
