Commentary: rates rally despite hot data, outlook positive 
Despite a shorter week, it truly is never a dull moment in markets these days.  The bond 
market rally that started last week continued, but the key event was mid-week’s inflation 
data releases with CPI and PPI both stronger than expected.  While markets sold off 
midweek, they largely reversed and rallied hard on Thursday with rates ending the week 
as much as 15bps lower.  BofA technical strategist, although still secular bears, they 
believe the near term rally has momentum to see 5.02%-4.92% yield support levels, or 
potentially deeper but not to fall below 4.74%. As such we hold our basis overweight.  
Light expected issuance was a theme at the MBA annual conference, where the tone 
from lenders was grim, but the light supply should be a positive technical for investors. 
2027 Issuance forecast: $1.12tr gross and $158bn net 
We project 2027 issuance at $1.12tr gross and $158bn net. Compared with our latest 
2026 forecast of $1.33tr gross and $206bn net, 2027 issuance is expected to decline 
meaningfully, with gross issuance down 16% YoY and net issuance down 23% YoY.  Our 
2027 forecasts are based on preliminary assumptions of a 5.25% 10-year UST yield, -
2.5% YoY Existing Home Sales (EHS), 0.5% YoY New Home Sales (NHS), and moderate 
(+1%) full year HPA. Weaker YoY net issuance is driven primarily by weaker home sales 
activity, a higher share of all-cash transactions in a persistently high-rate environment, 
and a smaller positive contribution from warehouse pipeline effects, plus an increase in 
Agency supply flowing to Non-Agency securitization. These headwinds are expected to 
be partially offset by a modest increase in cash-out refinance activity and lower GSE 
portfolio retention. 
RelVal: G2/FN cheap, with 5.5s and above preferred 
Ginnie/Fannie valuations have cheapened materially in recent sessions, pushing the 
G2/FN swap prices to their lowest levels since 2022, largely a function of weaker 
demand.  We think technicals could improve and see supportive fundamentals via flatter 
G2 Scurves.  Although the move is broad-based across the stack, we find particularly 
good RV In G2/FN 5.5 and 6s, which offer discount-dollar pricing, positive carry and 
convexity, and meaningful pickup in OAS and spreads vs comparable FNCL. Moreover we 
highlight that higher coupons 5.5 and above remain have no builder buydown collateral, a 
trend that should hold at these rate levels  as per recent rate lock activity. 
Lower payups + model error = value in lower loan bal 5.5s 
Despite refi risk still a very limited concern in the current rate environment, spec payups 
have come off to the point where they are worth a look. Conventional 5.5s low loan 
balance pools (up to $225k) are particularly attractive in our view.  Yield Book v97 
already shows an OAS advantage relative to TBA but a closer examination of model fit 
suggests that their convexity advantage is even stronger than the model assumes.  After 
accounting for this model bias, discount low loan balance pools appear even more 
compelling, with wider OAS pickup versus TBA and payups trading at a larger discount to 
theoretical value.

Updated 2026FY issuance: $1.33tr gross, $206 net 
Before delving into our 2027 supply outlook, we first revisit our forecast for 2026. So far 
through September, 2026 YTD has seen $1.05tn of gross issuance and $157bn of net 
issuance. Gross issuance is broadly in line with our full-year forecast of $1.32tn, while 
net issuance is tracking above our previously projected $164bn. 
The net issuance underestimate primarily reflects two factors. First, mortgage pipeline 
balances expanded following the recent increase in mortgage rates and resulting 
slowdown in prepayment speeds relative to year-end 2025. In effect, stronger 
refinancing activity in late 2025 shifted a portion of net issuance into 2026, whereas we

expect a much smaller carryover effect from year-end 2026 into 2027. This dynamic has 
contributed to stronger net issuance in 2026 than we had originally projected. Second, 
the reported share of all-cash home purchases has come in lower than anticipated 
despite generally elevated mortgage rates, supporting higher mortgage origination 
volumes. 
After adjusting for these factors, we revised FY 2026 issuance to $1.33 tr gross and 
$206bn net.  
Housing market: another tough year - expect -2.5% EHS, +0.5% NHS, and +1% 
HPA for FY2027  
With rates expected to remain elevated into next year, housing activity is likely to stay 
subdued, as the lock-in effect and persistent affordability challenges continue to 
constrain home sales. Correspondingly, we are expecting home sale driven net issuance 
components to see marginal YoY decline in 2027.  
Expect EHS to decline by 2.5% YoY  
When assessing the 2027 housing market outlook, we view 2023 as a reasonable point 
of comparison given the similarly weak levels of housing turnover and limited refinance 
activity. That said, we believe housing conditions in 2027 could prove even more 
challenging than those observed in 2023, as several demand-supportive factors have 
either diminished or reversed. Specifically, 
• 
In 2023, many borrowers still believed elevated mortgage rates were temporary, 
supporting a "buy now refinance later" mentality. While going into 2027, rates have 
remained high for nearly five years and the Fed has resumed tightening, reducing 
confidence in future refinancing opportunities. 
• 
Home price appreciation has moderated in recent years, while insurance, 
maintenance, and transaction costs have continued to rise. As a result, 
homeownership increasingly resembles a high-carry-cost, lower-return investment 
proposition. 
• 
The work-from-home trend has reversed, reducing one of the key tailwinds that 
supported housing demand during and after the pandemic. 
• 
Growing concerns around AI-driven labor market disruption could further weigh on 
consumer confidence and lead households to delay home purchase decisions.  
That said, there are still some improvements that could help contain some of the 
downside. The lock-in effect has eased modestly at the universe level (not the loan 
level), as the share of higher-rate loans originated in recent years has gradually 
increased. In addition, housing supply conditions have improved, and moreover, we have 
recently seen signs of localized recovery in some large housing markets, such as Florida.  
Taken together, these dynamics lead us to assume a 2.5%YoY decline in existing home 
sales in 2027.

New home sales expected almost flat +0.5% YoY 
We forecast new home sales to grow just 0.5% YoY in 2027. Builder incentives, 
particularly mortgage rate buydowns, should continue to support new home demand. 
However, declining builder margins may limit the scope for additional incentives, while 
improving resale supply and greater pricing flexibility among existing home sellers could 
create incremental competition. As a result, we expect minimal growth in new home 
sales next year.  Builder sentiment an outlooks have also been generally pessimistic 
given rate levels and high costs.

Cashout to hold stable to slightly increase 
With home prices expected to remain broadly steady, we expect cashout-driven net 
issuance to remain stable to modestly higher in 2027. Historically, cashout activity has 
demonstrated relatively low sensitivity to mortgage rates and has instead been more 
closely tied to the availability of home equity and borrowers' financing needs. 
We believe this gradual increase remains a reasonable expectation for several reasons. 
First, borrowers who purchased homes after 2022 have since built home equity and may 
increasingly view cashout refinancing as an attractive source of liquidity relative to other 
forms of borrowing. Second, borrowers who originated mortgages during the low-rate 
environment of 2020-2021 have now accumulated substantial equity, while their 
financing needs may naturally increase over time due to home improvements, education 
expenses, debt consolidation, etc. As a result, we expect cashout demand to remain 
relatively resilient despite elevated mortgage rates.  (For investors looking for the best 
exposure to cash outs in discounts, please see Agency MBS Weekly: What to do at these 
rate levels: cautiously overweight basis & fast paying discounts 09 October 2026) 
Put together we project cashout refinance to contribute $90bn of net issuance in 2027, 
marginally increasing from the est. $80bn in 2026.

Pipeline effect muted assuming higher rates in 2027 
The pipeline effect is another reason we project lower net issuance in 2027. During 
periods of strong refinancing activity, paydowns reduce net issuance immediately, while 
the resulting refinance loans return to the market as new issuance with a lag. 
Consequently, year-end refinance activity can shift issuance across calendar years. The 
sharp rally and elevated refinance speeds at the end of 2025 pushed a meaningful 
amount of issuance into January 2026, boosting FY2026 net issuance. In contrast, we 
expect refinance activity to remain muted through the end of both 2026 and 2027 given 
our elevated rate outlook, limiting the pipeline impact and reducing its contribution to 
annual net issuance for FY2027.

RelVal: G2/FN cheap, with 5.5s and above preferred 
Ginnie/Fannie valuations have cheapened materially in recent sessions, pushing the 
G2/FN swap prices to their lowest levels since 2022. The move is broad-based across 
the stack, with belly and premium coupon Ginnie swaps, particularly 5s and above, 
trading near the cheapest levels observed over the past two years for similar FN dollar 
prices. Relative value models also suggest Ginnies are attractively priced, offering 
meaningful pickup in both yield and spread versus comparable Fannie collateral.  
We understand the recent cheapening is mainly a result of lacking traditional 
sponsorship, with banks and overseas investors both very quiet given market volatility 
and rate uncertainty.   
While demand technicals may remain a near-term headwind, we think current G2/FN 
valuations are attractive given supportive fundamentals and the potential for technicals 
to improve over time. In particular we favor G2/FN 6s, which offer discount-dollar 
pricing, positive carry, positive convexity, and meaningful pickup in OAS and spreads 
versus comparable FNCL. Moreover we highlight that higher coupons 5.5 and above 
remain have no builder buydown collateral, a trend that should hold at these rate levels  
as per recent rate lock activity.

Improved fundamentals: the flattening G2 Scurves and discount pricing pools 
Fast VA streamline refinancing and weaker convexity were the key drivers of G2 
cheapness to conventionals from late 2025 into early 2026, but this pattern has 
noticeably changed.  
With rates remaining elevated, the Ginnie prepayment curve has flattened substantially. 
Ginnie speeds are also faster in the marginally out-of-the-money part of the S-curve, 
providing better extension protection, which is especially valued given discount pricing 
and high rates.  
Put together, at current rate levels we do not see Ginnie as having any disadvantage in 
its convexity profile compared to conventionals.

The recent sell off presents a paradigm: although many investors have been sidelined by 
recent realized rate volatility and uncertainty, yields are undeniably at attractive levels. 
We view the stabilization in rates over the past week and a favorable outlook from our 
technical analysts as constructive signs that current yield levels may start to look 
attractive to these investors. As such we have recently turned overweight MBS versus 
rates after being neutral and previously underweight.  (Global Rates Weekly: Duck and

No builder buydown collateral in coupon 5.5s and above

extra-slow turnover speeds affect the CTD profile and can therefore weigh on 
valuations.

has shifted in line with the broader move in mortgage rates, but the abnormally large

4%, 4.75-5% and 5.25-5.5% loan rate buckets highlighted with dotted shading in the 
exhibit. 
This then suggests builder buydown loans so far can reach at most 5s, while 5.5s and 
higher coupons should remain unaffected at these rate levels.

Lower payups + model error = value in lower loan bal 5.5s  
Conventional specs have cheapened meaningfully this year amid the rate selloff. As rates 
moved higher, the need for call protection diminished, while increased roll specialness in 
premium coupons also weighed on demand for specified pools. As a result, spec pay-ups 
have compressed substantially since the beginning of the year. 
Despite refi risk being a less pressing concern in the current rate environment, we 
believe specs remain worth considering for two reasons. First, they provide extension 
protection in a further selloff scenario. Second, they offer better convexity 
characteristics should rates reverse course.  While S-curves are currently flat, that trend 
is largely due to the high level of rates presently observed.
