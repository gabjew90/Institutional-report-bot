Brazil: Calm and volatile
While Asian tech gets crushed, Brazilian equities are holding their ground. The election trade is
heating up, the real is defying dollar strength, and falling rates could fuel another leg higher in
domestic stocks. But beneath the calm, volatility is exploding to levels not seen since Covid, while the
presidential race remains far from settled.
Can Brazil continue to defy the global selloff, or is the election about to deliver the next big volatility
shock?
Brazil: The unlikely EM safe haven
The IBOV has barely moved over the past week, while the Korean market has suffered an
extraordinary collapse. In a world where crowded Asian technology trades are being aggressively
unwound, Brazil is starting to look like a surprisingly attractive place to hide.

Vol do Brasil
EWZ implied volatility continues to explode, even as the ETF itself has barely moved. VXEWZ has
surged to its highest levels since the Covid shock, highlighting the extraordinary uncertainty being
priced into Brazilian equities ahead of the election.

The election squeeze
Brazilian equities have rallied sharply since the first round of the presidential election, with small caps
and rate-sensitive sectors leading the charge.
Goldman's historical comparison is interesting: following the 2018 election, the Bovespa gained
approximately 15% over six months, while small caps and rate-sensitive stocks advanced 30–40%.
The current rally in those domestic pockets is already around 15–20%, suggesting there could be
further upside if the election outcome translates into credible fiscal reform.

The real is sending a message
The Brazilian real has strengthened approximately 3.5% against the dollar since the October 4
election, despite a broader rise in the US dollar.
That is an impressive divergence. Markets are increasingly pricing in the possibility of fiscal reform
under a Flávio Bolsonaro administration, with his economic team proposing an adjustment equivalent
to roughly 1.5% of GDP.
But expectations are doing much of the heavy lifting. Actual implementation remains the crucial test.

The rate-cut trade
Brazil's central bank still has room to ease, even with inflation proving stubborn.
TS Lombard expects two additional 25bp cuts, in November and December, taking the Selic to 13.25%
by year-end. Real interest rates remain above 8%, compared with an estimated neutral rate of roughly
5%.
That leaves monetary policy extremely restrictive, even after the cuts already delivered.
For Brazilian equities, particularly domestic and rate-sensitive sectors, further easing could be a
significant tailwind.

The next leg of the Brazil trade?
EM funds already have relatively high aggregate exposure to Brazilian equities, but allocations to small
caps and rate-sensitive sectors remain comparatively low.
That creates an interesting asymmetry. The headline Brazil trade may already be popular, but the
domestic beneficiaries of falling rates could still have room to attract capital.

The election risk nobody should ignore
Flávio Bolsonaro remains the favourite ahead of the October 25 runoff, but the polls are far from
unanimous.
TS Lombard highlights a remarkable discrepancy between two pollsters that performed well in the first
round: Gerp puts Flávio ahead by 10 percentage points, while Palver gives him a lead of just one point.
Meanwhile, Lula's estimated probability of victory has rebounded from approximately 12% to 24%,
according to the Goldman commentary.
With Brazilian assets already rallying on expectations of fiscal reform, the market is increasingly
exposed to an unfavourable political surprise.
