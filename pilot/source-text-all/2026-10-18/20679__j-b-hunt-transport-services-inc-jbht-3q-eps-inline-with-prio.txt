The Bottom Line: Operating EPS of $1.82 was $0.02 above our forecast of $1.80 
and Visible Alpha consensus of $1.80 – and essentially inline with the company’s 
previously disclosed expectations for cost headwinds to impact sequential EBIT 
by 5%-10% (came in at -5%). As a reminder, JBHT called out higher costs related to 
fuel ($10M), claims ($10M) and driver pay ($25M). Actual cost headwinds came in at 
$50M, with group medical claims $16M of the $50M. 
Net, net the quarter came in largely as expected, with the outlook around peak and 
pricing pretty much lining up with what we thought JBHT would intimate – namely an 
expectation for a modest peak season, and intermodal pricing to pick up as new 
contract bid cycle kicks in - especially as the company implements a larger portion of 
new pricing as we move into 2027. 
We remain neutral on JBHT as we would like to get more clarity on 1) timing of cost 
amelioration and/or absorption, and 2) how much intermodal pricing (ex fuel) could 
actually recover as we move into 2027 so we become more convicted in our EPS and 
profit per load recovery. We still believe in the secular prospects for intermodal and 
would look to revisit the shares should we get a better sense for the above factors. 
For the time being, despite the positive secular factors that could cause us to have 
more positive stance on intermodal and JBHT, we remain positive on the more asset 
intensive TL and LTL sectors that stand to see relatively more offensive torque and 
profit recovery post the initial freight recession recovery.

Looking Ahead: Against the backdrop of truck supply exiting and improving rates, 
n
the question now arises that as volume enters into the broader market, if JBHT will 
be able to price that new demand above their cost to serve. Volume demand 
strength was reflected in strong volumes across intermodal, dedicated and ICS, 
however, the cost headwinds that occurred in 3Q26, management looks to rectify 
with greater pricing over the next bid season. The challenge is how quickly JBHT will 
be able to price above cost given their bid season cadence, as well as near-term 
startup cost headwinds that will likely weigh on Dedicated profitability, not to 
mention how transitory the $50M headwind in 3Q26 will be. What could make us 
more positive on JBHT moving forward is how quickly they are able to implement 
price increases to cover startup costs in Dedicated, as well as inflationary costs and 
catchup to TL rates in Intermodal.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
