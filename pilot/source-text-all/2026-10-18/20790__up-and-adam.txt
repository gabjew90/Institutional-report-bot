Echoing the CPI report a day earlier, September’s PPI came in much hotter than expected, rising 1.3% MoM (+6.3% YoY), its largest 
monthly increase since March 2022. Goods prices climbed 2.6%, the fastest pace since this category was introduced in 2009, driven by a

week’s inflation data points to persistent price pressures and strengthens the case for another Fed rate hike, likely in December.

Fed Chair Warsh did not give any signal on the Fed’s next move at the IMF/World Bank meetings in Bangkok last night. While he did not 
address this week’s hotter-than-expected inflation prints, he instead highlighted the surprising resilience of the US economy over the 
last five months. The key driver: a massive investment surge. With the Fed entering its communication blackout window tomorrow and 
the data calendar relatively quiet in the week ahead, the odds of a rate hike at the October 27-28 meeting now stand at just 16%.
After falling for seven straight days, the PHLX Semiconductor Index is down 12.4% from its October 6 high. The decline reflects investor 
concerns about the creditworthiness of certain cloud providers, which are key customers for chipmakers. Yet industry fundamentals 
remain strong, with consensus expecting semiconductor earnings to grow 66% in 2027. Meanwhile, AI adoption among businesses 
continues to reach new highs, according to fintech firm Ramp. Given this backdrop, we view the pullback as a buying opportunity.
