Global Daily 
Market comment 
Yesterday was CPI inflation day in the US, with headline CPI inflation printing in line with 
expectations at 0.6% m/m and 3.7% y/y, with about 60% of the monthly increase in prices 
stemming from energy inflation, as energy commodities registered their hottest m/m pace since 
March at 9.3%. Core CPI inflation, meanwhile, came in marginally hotter than expected at 0.3% 
m/m and 2.6% y/y, driven mostly by the shelter component.  
US Treasury yields spiked in response to the data and led to a repricing in the OIS curve, which 
was implying close to a 50% chance of a hike at the October meeting, up from just 23% the day 
prior. However, yields retraced throughout the day, and market-implied Fed expectations are back 
to around a 37% chance of a hike on October 28. But the slew of Fed speakers on the schedule 
yesterday did not hold back when it came to expressing their concerns about the US inflationary 
path, with Goolsbee saying that inflation has been “discouraging” and highlighting his concerns 
about overheating, with Barkin echoing his concerns. But Bowman, who has held her ground as 
the only remaining dove on the FOMC (at least, according to Bloomberg’s Fed Spectrometer), 
maintained that she doesn’t see an urgency to act on rates. 
Meanwhile, the S&P 500 is down more than 1%, fuelled by persistent Fed hiking expectations and 
the continued selloff in chipmakers, as seen when looking at the Philadelphia Exchange 
Semiconductor Index, which is down more than 12% from its recent highs and trending lower for 
the seventh consecutive day. Trump’s announcement via Truth Social that Japan would be 
investing USD 21 billion in the construction of “2 giant new Semiconductor Manufacturing 
Facilities in Malta, New York,” seemed to have little effect on the market’s woes. 
The Financial Times reports that Starlink speed restrictions have disrupted Ukrainian military 
operations, causing lapses in connectivity when drones are travelling at higher speeds. These 
restrictions are apparently new, and Ukrainian officials apparently received no warning. Zelenskyy 
said that he believes this was an intentional play by Musk and Trump, saying that he’s “sure [Musk 
and Trump] knew what they did,” and that “it’s painful when President Trump chooses one side, 
not our side.” This comes after several ballistic missile strikes on Kyiv that hit civilian infrastructure, 
including “26 facilities in Ukraine’s power system and four in the oil and gas sector,” leaving 
“600,000 people without electricity,” in addition to two Ukrainian deaths and 20 casualties. 
Things aren’t much more peaceful in the Middle East. After Houthi and Saudi forces sent warnings 
two days ago for civilians to evacuate energy infrastructure, parties followed through, with the 
Houthis hitting Aramco’s headquarters, but no oil production facilities. Despite tensions 
continuing to rise, Brent crude oil traded largely sideways yesterday at around $106/bbl. TTF 1-
month futures also continued trading higher up to almost €88/MWh. 
While the EU and China reached a preliminary trade agreement to limit the number of Chinese 
vehicle imports, recent reports are highlighting that the plan may have some holes, given the 
growing presence and investment of Chinese-owned auto-manufacturing plants on European soil. 
But Germany is seeking to encourage the EU to take a harder stance on China elsewhere too. 
Bloomberg reports that “Germany will seek EU safeguard measures to limit the import of goods 
from additional sectors, including chemical, mechanical engineering and potential 
pharmaceuticals…systemically important and core sectors of the European industrial model.”

Day ahead 
Today the sun rises with Australian labour data for September, with the unemployment rate 
printing at 4.6% and a contraction of almost 40 thousand jobs, erasing the job gains seen in July. 
The RBA will also be releasing its annual report later today. 
We will also see Eurozone industrial production data for August, which has registered depressing 
m/m growth since the onset of the war in Iran, and expectations for less-than-impressive y/y 
growth at -0.1%. The schedule for ECB speakers is much quieter today, with only Moulin and 
Nagel speaking in Bangkok. We will also hear from the SNB’s Schlegel. 
Quite a lot of data to get through in the UK, with the release of monthly GDP for August, 
industrial and manufacturing production, and trade balance updates. Industrial production in the 
UK is expected to have contracted by 0.1% m/m in August, but has been registering slow but 
steady improvement on a y/y basis, forecasted to measure 0.7% growth. Greene, Breeden, and 
Bailey are all scheduled to speak in Bangkok. 
Across the pond, we have manufacturing and wholesale (ex-petroleum) sales out of Canada, also 
for August, but both data prints have been, and are expected to continue to be, highly volatile. 
However, the trend in durable and nondurable goods sales in Canada suggests that the first half 
of 2026 marked some improvement in Canada’s manufacturing sector after three years of 
contraction, though July data marked a downturn for the first time in five months. BoC Deputy 
Governor Carolyn Rogers gets her opportunity at the microphone in Bangkok as well. 
South of the border, today is also a busy day for US economic data, including retail sales data for 
September, Empire Manufacturing and the Philly Fed Business Outlook for October, weekly initial 
and continuing jobless claims, and PPI data for September. Retail sales are unlikely to drive much 
market movement, especially in an inflationary environment, but many will be looking towards PPI 
as a leading indicator of other consumer inflation metrics. Headline PPI is expected to pick up 
from 0.4% m/m to 0.6% m/m, likely driven by higher energy prices, while core PPI is expected to 
pick up marginally from 0.2% to 0.3% m/m. An in-line print would likely confirm yesterday’s CPI 
inflation print, and any knee-jerk market moves are unlikely to have lasting market strength—
especially given the speed and conviction behind yesterday’s rates retracement after CPI—unless 
there is a significant upside surprise, as occurred in April of this year, when PPI came in at 1.1% 
m/m for the hottest print since March 2022. Bloomberg-surveyed analysts are forecasting Empire 
Manufacturing at 12.0, which would be conducive to the consistent upward trend in Empire 
Manufacturing we have seen since early 2024. Goolsbee will be speaking again today in a podcast 
interview, followed later in the day by Hammack and Warsh. 
Today also brings Brazilian retail sales data for August, expected to register 0.8% m/m compared 
with -0.8% m/m previously, and 1.5% y/y versus 1.2% y/y previously.
