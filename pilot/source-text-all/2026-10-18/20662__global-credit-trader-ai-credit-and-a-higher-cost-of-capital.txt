AI, Credit, and a Higher Cost of Capital 
 
 
Questions around the funding requirements of the AI capex buildout have become more 
pressing, leading to a correction across risk assets. In credit, the impact has been most 
pronounced in USD HY. USD HY AI-related spreads widened by 25bp between October 
13 and 14, reaching a new high of 360bp over Treasuries (Exhibit 1). By contrast, 
non-AI-related spreads were only 2bp wider and remain below their March peak. 
Within AI-related issuers, the widening has been concentrated in AI-related corporate 
bonds and data center project finance (Exhibit 2). While some softer AI-related 
sentiment can move datacenter bonds wider, especially if there is a reassessment 
around off-taker credit risk, we view AI-related corporate bonds, from either neoclouds 
or firms with significant frontier lab exposure, as being at the epicenter of the funding 
cost debate. Further removed AI-adjacent Energy and Utilities have been more resilient, 
in our view partly reflecting more diversified revenue streams and a relatively defensive 
earnings profile. 
 
The price action has been less severe in IG, reflecting generally stronger financial 
positions for the higher-rated cohort. USD IG AI-related spreads are only 3bp wider vs. 
1bp for non-AI-related issuers (Exhibit 3). Spreads have moved wider across both 
hardware and Hyperscalers, although funding concerns have driven a decompression 
bias across and within these cohorts (Exhibit 4).

Project finance issuance—a paradigm shift for credit markets 
As the cost of funding debate continues, we think this puts more of a spotlight on the 
project finance ecosystem that has been tapped to help fund this unprecedented, 
multi-year buildout. 
As shown in Exhibit 5, in the USD IG and HY bond markets, project finance issuance has 
already surpassed $106 billion, which is nearly three times the full-year 2025 level of 
$37 billion. 
For 2027, we are expecting $300 billion of global project finance issuance across the IG 
and HY markets to fund data center and compute investments. This is in addition to the 
$420 billion of direct issuance from the hyperscalers that we forecast for next year. 
Given the magnitude of the financing need, we view the risks to our $300 billion project 
finance projection as firmly skewed to the upside. While a higher cost of capital may 
shift the timeline somewhat, we see a high bar to derailing the investment.

At current valuations and given our expectation for heightened dispersion, we prefer moving 
up in quality within EUR HY. 
Similar to the US, we prefer HY bonds given the loan market's weighting toward software 
(and the potential negative sentiment overhang). The reaction function of the ECB has 
increased borrowing costs for floating rate issuers, given the central bank's focus on 
inflationary pressures.
EUR IG tranches offer carry pickup relative to USD IG CLO tranches. While the policy and 
macroeconomic picture is less supportive in Europe, subordination should protect tranches 
up in the capital structure from a continued idiosyncratic default picture. 
While we previously had a preference for IG vs. HY, we now view the case for an outright 
overweight in IG as less obvious based on valuations, rate sensitivity and supply related 
technicals. 
For floating rate investors, we prefer CLO mezzanine tranches vs. leveraged loans. 
Historical data points to low impairment rates for BB and BBB CLO tranches, and current 
valuations offer attractive carry and a cushion against moderate to elevated default 
outcomes.  Elsewhere, we are neutral between senior CLO tranches and USD IG; we would 
use spread widening to add exposure in CLOs. We are also neutral between CLO AAA and 
CMO floaters. 
While we previously had a slight preference for IG vs. HY, we now view the case for an 
outright overweight in IG as less obvious based on valuations, rate sensitivity and supply 
related technicals. 
The substantial software sector exposure in the loan market leaves us favoring the HY 
market, at least until we receive more clarity on the refinancing of the 2028 loan maturity 
walls. The lower-rated distribution of loans and the limited carry 'pick-up' vs. HY are also 
contributing factors. 
Agency MBS offers an attractive diversification benefit vs. a portfolio of IG corporate bonds.
We view the additional spread compensation of hybrids vs. BBBs as moderately attractive in 
the context of the tight spread environment. We also expect the hybrid spread premium to 
compress vs. BBBs, as the hybrid market grows further.
Regional
We prefer owning BBBs over the higher-rated segments of USD IG (As and AAs), as a way 
to capture additional spread. We also see scope for balance sheet deterioration at the 
highest end of the rating spectrum in IG. 
We recently shifted our view to favor BBBs over both AAs and As in the EUR market, owing 
to an improved macroeconomic backdrop which now favors a broad-based, down-in-quality 
stance within IG. 
We expect BBs will continue to encounter a challenging supply technical due to AI-related 
supply. As a result, we favor the B rated cohort. The CCC universe is highly idiosyncratic and 
requires careful credit selection, in our view.
