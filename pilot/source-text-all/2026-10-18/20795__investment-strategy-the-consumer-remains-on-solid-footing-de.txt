Will Micro Confirm Macro Strength? 3Q26 earnings season kicked off this week, and 
expectations are high. Despite the headwinds of higher oil prices and interest rates, 
economic growth has remained resilient, while the AI investment boom continues to gain 
momentum. As a result, the S&P 500 is expected to deliver 29% YoY earnings growth, 
marking a third consecutive quarter of 25%+ gains. Notably, 2026 earnings estimates 
have risen 7.1% since the start of the quarter, defying the usual pattern of downward 
revisions. While hotter-than-expected inflation and recent semiconductor weakness have 
injected some volatility into the market, the broader backdrop remains constructive. 
Still, with expectations elevated, this earnings season will be an important test of 
whether corporate results can justify the strength of the broader economy. As we head 
into a busy eight-week stretch, here are the five key questions we're looking to answer.
1. Will The Consumer Remain Resilient? | If the strong results from the mega-cap banks this past week are any indication, 3Q26 earnings 
season should provide further evidence of a resilient consumer. Credit card spending remained robust at Citigroup (+13% YoY), Wells Fargo 
(+12% YoY), and JPMorgan (+10% YoY), while Delta Air Lines reported healthy travel demand despite higher airfares driven by surging crude 
oil prices. The key question is whether consumers can maintain that momentum as higher prices continue to pressure household budgets. 
So far, real-time indicators, including restaurant bookings, hotel occupancy, and department store spending, suggest demand remains 
solid. If that trend holds, it could provide a tailwind for the lagging Consumer Discretionary sector.
2. Will Tech Earnings Stay On Top? | Tech earnings are expected to grow 64% YoY in 3Q26. While that's a modest slowdown from 73% 
growth in 2Q, it would still mark the 14th consecutive quarter of tech earnings outpacing the broader market, with the earnings premium 
(+36%) reaching its widest level since 2Q10. With tech margins 3x those of the rest of the S&P 500 and AI investment remaining on solid 
footing, tech earnings are expected to outgrow the S&P 500 each quarter through at least 2Q28. The key question is whether this earnings 
advantage can continue to support Tech’s leadership as earnings growth moderates. We think it can. Constructive earnings, increasing 
shareholder-friendly activity, and attractive valuations keep us overweight tech. 
3. Can AI Investment Remain Solid Moving Forward? | The slowdown in revenue growth at a major private AI company has raised questions 
about the staying power of the AI investment cycle. Given the scale of spending, those concerns are understandable. Still, the broader 
backdrop remains supportive. Hyperscalers remained capacity-constrained in 2Q26, and early 3Q26 supplier commentary points to the 
same trend, suggesting capex still has room to grow. Micron, for example, noted that demand for memory chips, a key AI input, continues 
to outpace supply. At the same time, AI adoption continues to broaden beyond tech. According to Ramp, business spending on AI reached 
a record high this week as 56% of businesses are now using AI, up from 44% a year ago. Adoption remains highest in the Information sector 
(~80% of firms), but other industries, such as Accommodation & Food (33%), Healthcare (43%), and Construction (43%) have increased AI 
usage by more than 10% over the past year. So, while volatility may remain elevated and headline-driven, the fundamentals still support 
strong hyperscaler spending and broader AI investment over the next 12 months. We expect that strength to show up in 3Q26 earnings and 
will be looking for clearer evidence that companies are translating AI investment into meaningful monetization.
4. Can Earnings Growth Broaden Amid Higher Rates And Input Costs? | Rising interest rates and higher energy prices were major themes 
throughout the third quarter. Early earnings reports have highlighted the impact, with both Pepsi and Delta raising prices to help protect 
margins. Even so, we expect earnings growth to broaden beyond Tech, supported by sales growth that is projected to remain above 10% 
for a third consecutive quarter amid healthy business and consumer spending. In fact, 9 of the 11 sectors are expected to deliver positive 
EPS growth, while S&P 500 earnings ex-Tech are projected to rise 17%, more than double the historical average.
5. Can 2027 Estimates Hold Up? | With the US economy remaining on solid footing, Technology's share of S&P 500 earnings is projected to 
rise to ~40% in 2027, up from 26% in 2026. At the same time, interest rates and energy prices are likely to ease in the months ahead, helping 
keep earnings estimates resilient. While the market will continue to lean heavily on Technology, improving earnings growth across the rest 
of the index should provide an additional source of support. And although the pace of upward earnings revisions is likely to slow, we expect 
the overall fundamental backdrop to remain constructive, supporting our forecast for $385 in S&P 500 EPS over the next 12 months 
(through September 2027). That earnings outlook remains the foundation for our 12-month S&P 500 target of 8,450.

OCTOBER 16, 2026
Economy
•
September headline CPI was up 0.6% MoM and 3.7% YoY. More concerning, core CPI rose 0.3% MoM (2.6% YoY), with elevated shelter 
and services components pointing to broadening price pressures and increasing the likelihood of another policy rate hike by year end.
•
Headline PPI surged a much stronger-than-expected 1.3% MoM in September, the fastest MoM pace since March 2022. While energy 
accounted for much of the increase, underlying price pressures were elevated, reinforcing inflation concerns following the CPI report.
•
September retail sales rose 0.8% MoM, although much of the increase reflected higher gasoline prices. Control group sales (a key input 
for GDP) was up just 0.1% MoM, suggesting that it declined on an inflation-adjusted basis as spending momentum is moderating.
•
Focus of the Week: With 30-year average mortgage rates reaching as high as 7.6% in September, Tuesday’s Housing Starts/Building 
Permits and Pending Home Sales reports are expected to show little-to-no recovery during the month. Meanwhile, Monday’s NAHB 
Housing Market Index (a gauge of builder sentiment) is expected to deteriorate further In October and reach COVID pandemic lows.
Equity
•
On the back of renewed concerns about future AI spending and hotter-than-expected inflation data, equity volatility returned this 
week. After falling more than 1% on Wednesday, the S&P 500 snapped its longest stretch in six years without a daily decline of at least 
1% (53 days). Beneath the surface, a meaningful rotation unfolded as investors gravitated toward more defensive areas of the market 
amid falling interest rates. While technology has lagged month-to-date, Consumer Staples (+7.7% MTD) and Health Care (+5.1%) have 
emerged as two of the strongest-performing sectors, while the Equal-Weight S&P 500 has also gained 3%. Although we continue to 
expect technology to lead over time, broader market participation is a healthy development and supportive of a more durable bull 
market. With the fundamental backdrop remaining solid, we maintain a constructive equity outlook.
•
After outperforming the S&P 500 by as much as 14% earlier this year, small caps have slipped into correction territory and now trail 
large caps by 150 bps on a YTD basis. Much of the earlier outperformance was driven by companies with no earnings. With interest rates 
moving higher, investors have refocused on fundamentals, putting the greatest pressure on non-earners. In fact, unprofitable Russell 
2000 companies are now down more than 20% from their highs, firmly in bear market territory, while profitable companies have fallen 
just 8%. Given stronger fundamentals and greater resilience to evolving macro risks, we continue to favor large caps over small caps.
•
Focus of the Week: Next week, 80 companies representing 16% of the S&P 500 market cap are set to report 3Q26 earnings highlighted 
by 20 Industrial firms—providing a real-time glimpse into the current operating environment across defense, machinery, and rails.
Fixed Income
•
Despite upside inflation surprises, Treasury yields fell across the curve this week. Safe-haven demand tied to concerns over AI spending 
and a semiconductor sell-off pushed the 10-year yield down 14 bps, on pace for its largest weekly decline since prior to the start of the 
US-Iran war, while reversing this month’s curve steepening. With the Bloomberg Treasury Index (+0.8% WTD) outperforming the S&P 
500 (-0.9%) on the week, Treasuries are showcasing their ability to provide diversification benefits during periods of uncertainty.
•
Those benefits haven’t come without greater volatility as daily ranges for the 10-year yield have notably widened since Labor Day. After 
Wednesday’s CPI release, the benchmark yield traded across a 15 bps range, briefly reaching 5.33% before risk-off sentiment drove 
yields lower. It was the second-widest daily range this year, trailing only late-September’s decisive break above 5%. Greater Fed and 
fiscal uncertainty, along with more frequent  AI jitters are likely to keep rate volatility elevated.
•
Focus of the Week: In the absence of major data and the Fed in a blackout period, stocks and oil are likely to set the tone for bonds.
Washington Policy
•
The political environment has shifted meaningfully ahead of the November 3 midterm elections, with recent polling positive for 
Democrats and a growing number of competitive congressional races. However, polls and prediction markets are imperfect tools as 
polling misses regularly occur, and recent misses have overcounted Democratic strength. As a result, we have updated our election 
forecasts and assign 50% odds to Democrats winning both the House and Senate, 40% odds to Democrats winning the House while 
Republicans retain the Senate, and 10% odds Republicans keep full control. Democrats, if they capture at least one chamber, gain 
leverage over must-pass legislation. In that scenario, we expect heightened regulatory risks for AI, data centers, tighter China-related 
tech restrictions, increased government shutdown risk, and potential headwinds for digital assets and tax reform.
Energy
•
The past month was full of cross-currents for Middle Eastern oil supply. Tanker companies are becoming more skilled at using ship-to-
ship transfers to export crude via the Strait of Hormuz, thereby reducing the risk of Iranian retaliation. On the other hand, an attack by 
Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthis temporarily shut down a major Saudi pipeline. Meanwhile, Iran continues to periodically strike ships in 
the Persian Gulf. The IEA’s latest data sheds light on the aggregate impact of all these dynamics. Production of OPEC’s Gulf members 
totaled 15.8 million barrels/day in September, which equates to 75% of the pre-war baseline—slightly surpassing July’s 74% to reach 
the highest level since the start of the US-Iran conflict. To clarify, this refers specifically to crude rather than refined product output.
•
Looking ahead, a firm supply recovery to pre-war levels hinges on a durable diplomatic settlement between the US and Iran. Until then, 
we see 80% to 85% as the maximum realistic run-rate. Once the political groundwork is in place, the next steps will be logistical: 
demining Hormuz, bringing shipping crews back to work, and resuming production at currently shut-in oilfields. All of this takes time, 
so the trajectory toward our WTI forecast of $65/barrel is unlikely to be smooth—some hurdles along the way may be inevitable.

EXISTING HOME SALES INDEX | The Existing Home Sales report is a monthly economic indicator released by the National Association of Realtors (NAR). It measures the total number of closed transactions

LEADING ECONOMIC INDEX | The Leading Economic Index (LEI) is a monthly composite statistic published by The Conference Board that predicts future shifts in the business cycle, typically looking six to 
nine months ahead. It combines 10 forward-looking economic components, such as stock prices, building permits, and manufacturing orders, to signal upcoming economic expansions or recessions before 
they become evident in the overall economy.
MICHIGAN CONSUMER SENTIMENT INDEX | The Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index (MCSI) is a monthly survey-based economic indicator measuring US consumer confidence regarding their personal 
finances, business conditions, and buying power. It is a leading indicator for predicting consumer spending, which drives the majority of the US economy.
