Situation Room: CPI tightens financial conditions 14 October 2026 
 
CPI tightens financial conditions 
September CPI was above expectations. The increase in prices was broad based, with 
inflation accelerating relative to August for both core goods and core services (Exhibit 
21). Our economists are now tracking September core PCE at +3.1% YoY and +0.32% 
MoM (Exhibit 17, see US Watch: September CPI inflation: Broad based CPI strengthens 
the case for an October hike). 
More volatility 
Higher inflation and a hawkish Fed are beginning to weigh on risk assets. The Treasury 
yield curve bear flattened immediately after the CPI data release (2yr +15bps, Exhibit 
18). However, following the selloff in stocks, let by the hyperscalers / chips, Treasuries 
re-traced most of the earlier move (2yr +1bps, Exhibit 19).  By the end of the day market 
pricing added just 3 to 4bps to the Fed pricing through mid-2027. IG spreads were 3bps 
wider for hyperscalers, Telecom and Media sectors (Exhibit 20). 
Moving in the right direction 
The elevated volatility related to Treasury yields and the Fed hiking cycle is clearly 
weighing on spreads. However, tighter financial conditions is how the volatility is 
ultimately resolved. The conditions are unlikely to tighten enough to impact IG credit 
fundamentals in a significant way, but more stable yields will be good for technicals. 
Hence, resolving the Fed uncertainties should ultimately benefit IG spreads (see IG 
Credit Strategist: Breaking good).

Situation Room: Weaker flows under the hood 13 October 2026 
 
Weaker flows under the hood 
Inflows to HG funds and ETFs improved in the latest week (ending on Oct 7th) to $10.2bn 
– the biggest weekly inflow in three months (see report: Situation Room: Time to 
rebond). However, that big inflow was driven by 1) ETFs, which tend to reflect more the 
activity by institutional investors and 2) the seasonally big inflow to funds (ex. ETFs) that 
occurs on the first calendar day of each month. Looking at funds only (more of a retail 
product) and adjusting for the big October 1st inflow shows that the average daily inflow 
so far in October has averaged -$257mn – the weakest since “Liberation Day” (Exhibit 
23). 
Weak environment for flows 
The Fed hiking cycle means 1) higher rates / lower bond prices and 2) more lackluster 
stock performance. Both are bad for HG fund flows (see Exhibit 7 and 8 here: IG Credit 
Strategist: More rate vol = wider spreads). Hence it is not surprising to see weaker retail 
inflows to HG funds so far in October. 
Seasonality and ETFs 
While inflows to funds are weak, ETF inflows accelerated to $1.8bn per day so far in 
October from $1.0bn daily average in September (Exhibit 24). Some of that could be 
dealers delivering bonds into ETFs to de-risk balance sheets that spiked in the second 
half of September (Exhibit 25). Finally, the inflow on the first day for funds has been on 
average as big as all the daily inflows for the rest of the month (Exhibit 26).

Situation Room: Buying more stocks, fewer bonds 15 October 2026 
 
Flows 
Buying more stocks, fewer bonds 
Flows moderated for HG this past week ending on October 14, and weakened for HY, 
loans and EM bonds. On the other hand, inflows to stocks jumped to the highest level in 
four weeks. 
Inflows to US HG bond funds and ETFs slowed to +$4.32bn this past week, down from a 
+$10.23bn inflow in the prior week. Since IG corporates account for about a third of the 
high grade bond universe AUM, that translates into ~$1.4bn demand for IG. Inflows 
moderated for HG ETFs (to +$6.31bn from +$8.98bn), while flows turned negative for 
HG Funds (to -$1.99bn from +$1.25bn). Inflows moderated for both short-term HG (to 
+$1.77bn from +$3.53bn) and ex. short-term (to +$2.55bn from +$6.70bn). 
Stocks are back 
Inflows accelerated for equities this past week (to +$43.91bn from +$2.76bn), printing 
the highest inflow since the week of September 16th. In contrast, flows turned negative 
for HY (to -$1.24bn from +$0.19bn) as well as global EM bonds (to -$0.67bn, the biggest 
outflow since April 2026, from +$0.16bn). The flows for loans were also weaker (to 
+$0.00bn from +$0.36bn). The only stronger bond flow this past week was for munis (to 
+$4.51bn, the biggest inflow since January 2022, from +$2.57bn).  Finally, money 
markets reported a -$53.90bn outflow this past week, following a +$97.76bn inflow a 
week earlier.

Appendix: defining high grade 
We define our high grade flows metric as a combination of “bond” and “corporate bond” 
fixed income funds and ETFs domiciled in the US (and investing in any currency, 
although the majority is invested in USD). We also exclude funds primarily focused on 
Government, HY, EM and MBS from the sample. The “bond” category refers to the broad 
high grade market, which includes Treasuries, mortgages and agencies in addition to 
corporate bonds. This category accounts for the majority of AUM and flows. On the 
other hand, the dedicated corporate bond funds and ETFs are much smaller in terms of 
assets under management. We also include the “total return” bond category in our 
tracking of high grade flows. Finally, note that “short-term” maturity refers to duration 
of 0 to 4 years. On average, IG corporate bonds account for about ~32% of the total 
AUM of our high grade bond fund / ETF universe.
