GS Curtin - Rates MarketStrats: Are higher yields
beginning to cause cracks? 16 Oct 2026
David Curtin · Goldman Sachs · Vice President, FICC & Equities
Fri 16 Oct 2026, 8:34am ET
David Curtin | Tushar Agarwal | Marie-Anais Francois
Have we seen the first cracks from the higher yield environment? As terminal rates and term premia have
shifted yield curves higher, we have seen stress emerge in some issuers with high levels of debt outstanding
and large projected future borrowing needs.
In sovereign space, OAT spreads have widened significantly, leading to position stopouts and a shift in
market betas – at least at the height of the stress - to the EUR sovereign crisis playbook. Schatz spreads
widened, sovereign credit curves flatted, banks underperformed and the Euro weakened.
In credit, we are seeing some stress points in the financing of the AI infrastructure buildout, particularly
among the neoclouds – see Coreweave for example (chart below). While the rise in the all-in cost of debt
funding for the hyperscalers at roughly 6-8% at 10y (in USD) may be manageable if projected returns are in
the teens or higher, for sub-investment grade borrowers the higher yield environment can bite more quickly.
We have been of the view that the combination of resilient nominal growth and heavy duration supply to the
market would continue to pressure yields higher and that the most powerful circuit breakers were fiscal
consolidation and a pullback in AI capex intentions. Measures to control the supply of DV01 through WAM
reduction*, buybacks or slowing of QT have had positive-but-limited impacts on term premium (see chart
below). The same goes for central banks shifting more hawkish in September – while making an effort to get
ahead of the curve should compress inflation risk premium, front-end forwards like SFR M7-M8 have only
flattened more recently, as the Nasdaq has fallen.
*the upcoming QRA may test this again if the US Treasury reduces long-end coupon supply
Therefore if either of the two more forceful drivers – fiscal looseness and capex funding – should change in
the eyes of investors, then rates can rally meaningfully. We are sceptical of fiscal consolidation – and
elections across Europe in 2027 are unlikely to change the picture there. On the AI front, compute remains
scarce and perhaps those players with larger balance sheets can help to alleviate pressure points in funding
along the supply chain – but we still think it's noteworthy that we are seeing some cracks emerge there. The
pressure is not coming from compressed expected returns on invested capital, but more around the cost and
scale of financing the build-out.
The big tail risk from a European standpoint remains energy, and Nat Gas in particular. Central banks have
broadly pushed back against the idea of another round of monetary tightening this month, guiding towards a
gradual pace. But Sam Dart & co point to the meaningful upside risk to TTF if Hormuz flows don’t improve
and the winter is 1std colder/less windy. That makes the risk-reward for curve steepeners out to 30y uncertain

in our view – at least in spot space - as energy and AI sentiment compete for influence (with dutch transition
flows in the background).

But we think the last month or so has revealed that markets will struggle to hold terminal rates at clearly
restrictive levels as it is causing cracks to appear in bond markets. That leaves us liking intermediate forward
rates like 2y2y, which can offer a degree of asymmetry if you believe that higher terminal rates will quickly
lead to weaker risk assets.
Energy upside scenario if energy does take another leg higher, central banks will likely get pulled into
tightening and second round effects will get priced as businesses struggle to absorb the costs in their
margins for another year (even with fiscal support, which don’t think would fully offset costs in Europe).
But we would not expect that terminal pricing to hold as the negative growth consequences are priced in
more quickly in the wake of the market stress over the last month. Z7-Z9 should flatten, with potential for
greens/blues to rally outright
AI worries scenario If the pace of AI capex is re-evaluated, yield curves shift lower as weaker nominal
growth is priced and supply term premium is reduced. Rates rally.
Status quo scenario If AI capex continues apace and the market holds term premium against the risk of
higher inflation outturns into 2027, forwards like 2y2y will struggle to rally meaningfully until we get
more confident about the top in inflation. But at 3.40% in EUR (or 3.20% in Estr), we are still priced
100bps higher than pre-Iran and only 20bps off the highs of Sep.
We are aware that September was a bruising month for many in the rates market, and in many cases
expressions like rec 2y2y caught up in the risk reduction wave. That gives us pause, but also signals that it is
less positioned - with risk appetite in general lower, we think it argues for having some risk here (either
outright or bounded in non-linear space).
We also like expressing long forwards in real yield space, to add some inflation protection against the risk of
energy prices rising
