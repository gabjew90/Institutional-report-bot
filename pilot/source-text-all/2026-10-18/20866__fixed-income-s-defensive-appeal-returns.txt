Recent market developments 
–––––––––– 
The sharp rally in US Treasuries overnight, alongside the weakness in technology 
equities, offers some interesting observations with potentially important implications 
for the fixed income outlook. 
The stronger-than-expected US CPI release initially triggered another Treasury selloff, 
with the 10Y yield briefly touching 5.33%. However, yields subsequently reversed 
sharply, with the 10Y falling towards 5.10% during Asian trading today, despite the 
inflation surprise. 
The reversal coincided with a significant selloff in US technology equities, particularly AI-
related companies. Concerns surrounding the sustainability of AI capital expenditure 
have intensified, especially among the more leveraged AI infrastructure providers 
(neoclouds), where rising financing costs are increasingly challenging business models. 
What is particularly interesting is the apparent shift in the underlying drivers of US 
Treasury performance. Since August, Treasury yields have risen substantially as markets 
repriced expectations for further Fed tightening in response to persistent inflation 
pressures. However, the recent price action suggests that concerns surrounding the 
growth outlook are becoming increasingly influential.

Observations from Asian credit markets this morning 
Credit spreads have generally widened as corporate bonds struggled to keep pace with 
the Treasury rally. However, the widening has been more pronounced among lower-
rated credits, while higher-quality credits have demonstrated greater resilience. 
Importantly, the spread widening in higher-quality credits has generally been smaller 
than the decline in underlying risk-free yields, resulting in modest price gains. In 
contrast, some lower-quality credits, particularly in the high-yield segment, have 
experienced marginal price pressures. 
On balance, we believe that lower Treasury yields should remain supportive of credit 
performance, provided the deterioration in growth expectations does not translate into 
a more significant widening of credit spreads. The combination of attractive carry 
income and potentially more supportive interest rates continues to underpin a relatively 
constructive outlook for credit, particularly in the higher-quality segments. 
Implications for fixed income investors 
• 
Renewed diversification benefits. The recent Treasury rally amid equity market 
weakness highlights the potential value of high-quality fixed income as a 
portfolio diversifier. This defensive characteristic has been less reliable during 
the inflation-driven selloff over recent months, but may become increasingly 
relevant if growth concerns intensify. 
• 
Attractive starting yields with potential capital upside. Following the significant 
increase in yields since August, bonds continue to offer attractive income 
opportunities. Investors are receiving relatively high starting yields while 
retaining the potential for capital appreciation should weaker growth prompt a 
reversal in Fed tightening expectations. 
• 
Resilience of higher-quality credits. The price action in Asian credit markets 
reinforces the merits of quality. Even with some credit spread widening, lower-
beta bonds have generally benefited from declining Treasury yields. This 
highlights the potential for investment-grade credits to deliver a combination of 
attractive carry and relatively resilient performance. 
• 
Higher income opportunities remain available. While lower-quality credits are 
more vulnerable to market volatility, higher-yielding segments continue to offer 
compelling absolute income opportunities for investors with greater risk 
tolerance. Importantly, selectivity remains essential as credit performance 
becomes increasingly differentiated.

Bottom line 
The recent market developments provide a timely opportunity to revisit the role of fixed 
income in client portfolios. 
With starting yields still attractive, fixed income investors can benefit from meaningful 
carry income while potentially enjoying renewed diversification benefits and capital 
upside should global growth risks intensify. 
There are different ways investors can capture these opportunities, from higher-quality 
Asian credit with relatively defensive characteristics, to higher-yielding Global Emerging 
Market opportunities and Singapore dollar/Renmimbi strategies that provide additional 
diversification. 
While inflation remains a risk, the evolving balance between inflation and growth 
concerns suggests that the investment case for fixed income is becoming increasingly 
compelling.
