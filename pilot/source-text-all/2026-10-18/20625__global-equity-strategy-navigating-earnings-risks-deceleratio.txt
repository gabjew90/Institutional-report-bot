Global Equity Strategy
Navigating Earnings Risks: Deceleration & Concentration
CITI'S TAKE 
Global EPS growth should remain strong next year, but markets will need to 
face down two key risks: deceleration and concentration. On deceleration, the 
pace of global EPSg is expected to fall by c20pp in 27E. A slowdown of this 
size would typically imply flat equity returns. However, we find that global 
stocks have risen c4% on average in “positive but decelerating" EPSg years 
akin to 27E, albeit with more Defensive market internals. On concentration, 
27E could be one of the narrowest EPSg years on record, with Tech 
responsible for 70% of global profit growth. We find little relationship 
between EPS concentration and broad market performance. Notably, sectors 
responsible for an outsized share of EPSg tend to outperform initially. The 
achievability of still-upbeat forecasts will remain in focus amid headwinds 
from geopolitics, rates, and AI sentiment. We remain comfortable with our 
call for earnings-driven upside for global equities into 27E.
Solid EPS Projected in 27E, But Two Key Risks — Global earnings are set to expand 
by an impressive +35% this year. Next year, consensus pencils in +16% EPSg. While 
growth should remain strong, two key risks emerge: (1) the pace of EPS growth is 
decelerating sharply (c20pp decline) and (2) EPS concentration is rising, with Tech 
set to contribute an even larger share of global profit growth in 27E than in 26E.   
EPS Deceleration: Lessons From History — Based on simple regressions, next year’s 
projected global EPS growth (+16%) would imply a c10% rise in global equities. However, 
the projected deceleration (c20pp) would more worryingly imply flat equity market 
returns. We therefore look at comparable “positive but decelerating" EPS growth years. 
Equities have risen by c4% on average (+10% median) in these environments (67% hit 
rate), with key downside examples (2000, 2011, 2018, 2022). In these “positive but 
decelerating" EPS growth years, returns are driven primarily by earnings. Valuation 
multiples compress on average, but multiple expansion remains possible.
EPS Deceleration: How to Position — When narrowing in on “positive but 
decelerating" EPS growth years (akin to 27E), typical performance patterns include: (1) 
Quality leadership, (2) Value over Growth, (3) Defensives over Cyclicals, (4) little 
differentiation between Large and Small, and (5) Health Care and Energy 
outperformance.  While there is a wide range of outcomes, this suggests slightly 
more defensive market internals in “positive but decelerating" EPS growth backdrops.
EPS Concentration: Lessons from History — On average, c40% of global EPS 
growth has come from the top contributing sector (over 30Y). Tech will deliver half of 
growth in 26E and 70% of growth in 27E (c80% in the US), which would make it the 
second most concentrated positive EPS growth year on record. Notably, we find little 
relationship between EPS concentration and overall market performance.
EPS Concentration: How to Position — Sectors responsible for an outsized share of 
annual EPS growth actually tend to outperform amid earnings concentration, while 
relative performance levels off in the subsequent year. While AI sentiment will remain 
volatile, this would suggest some comfort in holding Tech into next year.

Heading into next year, markets will need to face down two key earnings risks: 
deceleration and concentration. Not only is the pace of EPS growth slowing down 
by c20pp, but an even larger share of that growth will come from the Tech sector in 
27E than in 26E. How should equity investors navigate these risks? 
In this report, we lean on history to shed light on a unique fundamental setup 
heading into 27E. In the end, our findings on both deceleration and concentration 
leave us comfortable with our forecasts for continued earnings-driven upside for 
global equities into next year. However, risks remain elevated (via geopolitics, rates, 
questions around AI capex), and past experience would suggest an incremental tilt 
towards Quality and select Defensiveness in the months ahead.
Global EPS: Resilient in 27E, But Two Key 
Reasons for Concern
Global earnings are set to grow by a remarkable +35% this year, underpinning 
solid equity market performance (Figure 1). The AI buildout has been a key 
underlying driver of profit growth, propelling strong growth in Tech, EM, and the 
US. However, earnings strength has been notably broad-based, with all major 
regions and global sectors (ex-Real Estate) expected to see EPS expand in 26E.
While EPS growth should remain solid in 27E, we are clearly entering a 
deceleration phase. Consensus pencils in +16% EPS growth in 27E, with more 
comparable growth rates across regions. While this would still put global earnings 
growth well above trend, the projected deceleration (c20pp) would be one of the 
largest in our sample dating back to the mid-80s (see Figure 4).

“Positive But Decelerating” EPSg Years: Where Do Returns Come From?
Beyond index-level performance, we also look at the drivers of equity returns in 
“positive but decelerating” EPS growth environments. When breaking down 
constituent drivers (earnings growth, valuation change, and dividend yield), we 
find:
•
Earnings are the key driver of total returns. In “positive but decelerating 
EPS growth” years, EPS growth is the primary driver of equity market 
upside, delivering c10pp of returns (Figures 9-10). 
•
Multiple compression on average. In “positive but decelerating EPS 
growth” years, valuation multiples compress on average (Figures 9-10). 
While there are several “positive but decelerating” years that came with 
sizable multiple compression (2000, 2022), rerating is also possible (1997, 
2006) and actually occurs c30% of the time. This all suggests multiples 
are more likely to be a drag on equity returns next year, but multiple 
expansion would not be unprecedented.

Notably, a derating—like we’ve seen this year—does not necessarily mark the 
market peak. In 1994, earnings growth absorbed multiple compression, keeping 
returns positive, with further gains in 1995 despite slower earnings growth. 
Similarly, derating in 2004-05 accompanied double-digit returns, followed by 
further gains through 2007. In 2000 and 2008, by contrast, major compression 
coincided with the market decline rather than preceding it. These episodes suggest 
that de-rating can accompany continued gains or losses but is not a consistent 
signal on what lies ahead.
EPS Deceleration: How to Position
To answer this question, we take two approaches. We first assess average 
performance of various sectors in “positive but decelerating” growth years, as expected 
in 27E. We then narrow in on up-years and down-years for the MSCI AC World. This 
helps frame how performance could play out conditional on a broader market view. We 
expand our analysis to traditional factors (largely via MSCI indices), Cyclicals vs. 
Defensives, as well as global sectors and regions.

Key Risk #2: EPS Concentration
Beyond deceleration, EPS concentration remains very much in focus amid 
narrow equity market returns and sources of profit growth. While the AI buildout 
is unique in many ways, it’s worth asking just how concentrated profit growth really 
is, and how this has typically played out in terms of performance. Again, we lean on 
history for a guide before diving into potential implications for equity market 
positioning.

Earnings concentration is typically associated with sectoral outperformance, at 
least initially. In Figure 16, we show levels of sectoral concentration on the x-axis 
vs. that sector’s relative performance on the y-axis. The relationship is somewhat 
positive, suggesting that an out-earning sector will also outperform peers. 
In Figure 17, we then test persistence of these relative gains. To do so, we focus 
on relative performance of “dominant” sectors in years with above average (>40%) 
levels of EPS concentration. After high levels of sector-level concentration, the 
leading sector will tend to move mostly sideways on average in the first half of the 
following year, before eventually outperforming somewhat. This suggests less 
outperformance following a very concentrated earnings year, but not necessarily 
underperformance from there on out.

Views & Implications
History remains supportive of earnings-driven upside into 2027, in line with our 
view. "Positive but decelerating" EPS growth years have historically delivered c4% 
average returns (up 67% of the time), with EPS growth itself—not multiple 
expansion—the primary driver of returns (c10pp on average). This squares with our 
forecasts, which look for further upside into mid-27 driven by earnings rather than 
re-rating. For more, see: Global Equity Quarterly - Resilience or Complacence?
Quality and Defensives have been the preferred ways to play "positive but 
decelerating" EPS growth regimes historically. While we remain Neutral-to-
Underweight on Defensive sectors within our global equity allocation, Health Care 
(Overweight in US, Europe) would rank as our preference within the group, aligning 
well with historical relative performance in "positive but decelerating" EPS growth 
regimes.
Forecasts for highly concentrated EPS growth in 27E are historically unusual but 
could remain supportive of IT outperformance in the near-to-medium 
term. Today's earnings concentration in Tech has few historical parallels. However, 
we find that sectors responsible for outsized EPS growth contributions tend to 
outperform on a one-year horizon before more move in line with the market 
afterwards. Given a still attractive trade-off between EPS growth and traditional 
valuation multiples, we remain Overweight on the global Tech sector for now, while 
acknowledging that AI sentiment will remain volatile.
EPS revisions are constructive, but warrant watching. Consistent upward 
revisions to forecasted levels of EPS have been a rare occurrence historically, 
occurring only in the run-up to the GFC and the past few years. With markets still 
pricing in relatively optimistic EPS outcomes over the next 12m (i.e., single-digit pp 
upgrades vs. consensus), a shift away from consistent EPS upgrades would be key 
to watch. 
While many of our findings can be read constructively on balance, we find that 
underlying equity market exuberance is indeed climbing back towards levels 
only seen before major historical bear markets. As outlined in prior reports, our
