Q4 2026
Outlook
As we enter Q4, AI continues to drive 
economic growth, market leadership and 
political debate. While productivity gains 
from AI are widely anticipated, their scale 
and timing remain difficult to quantify. 
The near-term impact on labor appears 
broadly positive for now, with skilled 
engineers and IT developers in high 
demand as companies build infrastructure 
and integrate AI into their operations.
At the same time, the US–Iran standoff shows no clear sign 
of resolution, keeping pressure on energy markets and 
adding to inflation risks. Looking ahead, we believe AI is 
once again the largest source of both opportunity and risk. 
The security-led slowdown in frontier-model development 
could shift the focus towards adoption and inference, 
leaving the broader investment thesis intact (the recent 
Meta personal AI agent release is an example). However, 
the bar is exceptionally high: market valuations already 
embed trillions of dollars of prospective revenues over the 
next decade, leaving little room for disappointment.
Interest rates took center stage over last three months as 
curves backed up broadly across the board. While inflation 
remains above target mainly due to geopolitical conflicts, 
real rates have also climbed higher. Record capital 
expenditures combined with large government deficits 
require formidable amounts of funding, ultimately requiring 
a higher clearing price. While most macro managers failed 
to anticipate the transition to a higher-rate regime in 2026, 
we recognize that maintaining a structural short-duration 
position is increasingly difficult at current yield levels. This is 
especially the case in regions that have derived limited 
benefit from AI-driven growth and remain exposed to 
Middle East-related trade disruptions, particularly Europe 
and the UK. Nevertheless, with bond yields exceeding 
dividend yields and cyclically adjusted valuations at record 
level (post 1999-00), investors will inevitably grow more 
sensitive to any disappointing news concerning AI.

Credit / Income
In Credit, our corporate long / short exposure continues to 
rebalance towards more tactical long / short managers. 
While we do not expect a major widening in credit spreads, 
the recent decoupling of CCC points to increasing credit 
quality differentiation at times of record-level corporate 
issuance. While overall yields are improving, most spread 
products are trading at extremely low levels; therefore, we 
remain very selective in ABS and Other Income strategies.
Relative Value 
In Relative Value, we favor quantitative equity and merger 
arbitrage, especially on a liquidity-adjusted basis. 
Quantitative equity continues to face persistent crowding 
and factor-volatility headwinds, but we have observed 
managers progressively adapting. Higher cost of capital can 
further reduce cross-sectional correlation, providing a 
tailwind to the strategy. Merger and event arbitrage also 
remain attractive as healthy deal flow, supportive financing 
and differentiated regulatory risk sustain elevated spread 
dispersion. 
We are further reducing our allocations to fixed income 
relative value (FIRV). The recent increase in rates volatility has 
not produced wider spreads due to an abundance of balance 
sheet from banks and dealers. As such, we are mindful of 
rates RV specialists incrementally seeking return via more 
directional opportunities.

Equity Hedged
We observed Equity Hedged and Multi-strategy managers 
trimming exposures to AI infrastructure during the volatile 
summer months, and as a result, we feel less compelled to 
make incremental changes to our portfolios at this point. We 
prefer to have our AI exposure expressed in tightly managed 
market-neutral constructs or via funds with flexible net 
exposures (US- and Asia-focused). We continue to diversify 
our portfolios with allocations to generalists, and specialists 
in biotech, financials and Europe. 
Looking ahead, we expect AI applications to broaden along 
with the alpha opportunity set (e.g., one theme is consumer-
focused AI agents and their disruption to several 
e-commerce sectors). Our financials and biotech specialists 
have underperformed so far this year, despite relatively 
benign industry dynamics. Ironically, most of the negative 
alpha has come from the short side, driven by the very 
themes we have highlighted throughout the year: M&A 
activity and perceived AI beneficiaries. We expect long / 
short spreads across both sectors to improve as earnings 
season approaches, while continuing to provide valuable 
diversification away from core AI-driven exposures. Similarly, 
Europe should provide a diversification benefit as the market 
is much less exposed to AI growth and momentum relative 
to value and quality factors. Given more reasonable 
valuations, any good news on the geopolitical front could 
also offer meaningful upside in Europe.
Trading
In Trading, we maintain conviction in discretionary global 
macro strategies. Recent underperformance has typically 
been a function of back-footed positioning rather than lack 
of opportunities. As real rates become more attractive, 
correlation between equities and bonds could become 
negative again, and may facilitate changes to portfolio 
construction. Commodities offer one of the strongest 
opportunity sets, and we maintain our core allocations 
across metals, materials, power and natural gas. We are 
currently considering adding a tactical allocation to 
agriculture. Systematic macro (trend followers) have 
performed reasonably well this year, mainly thanks to long 
equities, short rates and long metals positions.
We maintain a neutral outlook but are increasing our target 
weight to systematic trading to reflect its growth within 
our multi-strategy managers’ allocations. Note that our 
systematic trading allocation has been relatively small with 
respect to our historical range and remains focused on 
differentiated approaches and / or alternative markets. We 
continue to expect systematic and discretionary macro to 
correlate positively over time and strive to maintain balance 
between the two.

S&P500 Index
The Standard and Poor's 500 Index (S&P500) is a 
capitalization-weighted index of 500 stocks. The index is 
designed to measure performance of the broad domestic 
economy through changes in the aggregate market value 
of 500 stocks representing all major industries.
S&P500 EW Index
The S&P 500 Equal Weight Index (EWI) is the equal-weight 
version of the widely-used S&P 500. The index includes the 
same constituents as the capitalization weighted S&P 500, 
but each company in the S&P 500 EWI is allocated a fixed 
weight - or 0.2% of the index total at each quarterly 
rebalance.
