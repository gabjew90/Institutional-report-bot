Industrial Production Jumped in 
September to Close to Record High
Headlines 
 
Industrial production rose 0.7% in September. Much of the gain came 
from hot weather, but underlying growth is solid. 
 
 
Strong tech demand is boosting the industrial sector. 
 
 
Mining output is up with high energy prices. 
 
 
The capacity utilization rate rose in September, but capacity constraints 
are not a major source of inflation. 
 
 
Industrial production is just below its all-time high in 2018. Although the 
September gain is likely to reverse in October, industrial production 
should hit a new record high in the months ahead.  
Details
Industrial production rose 0.7% in September from 
August, according to data from the Federal Reserve. 
This was the largest increase since April. However, 
much of the increase came from a 3.5% jump in 
utilities output due to hotter-than-usual weather. 
There was a 0.3% increase in manufacturing output, 
while mining production rose 0.2%. Industrial 
production rose 0.1% in August, revised up from no 
change.  
On a year-ago basis total industrial production was 
up 2.2%, including a 1.1% increase in manufacturing 
output. Utilities output was up 8.7%, with mining 
output up 2.8%.  
The capacity utilization rate rose to 76.9% in 
September from 76.4% in August. This is the highest 
utilization rate since 2023, although it is still well 
below its pre-pandemic level of around 78%. The 
manufacturing utilization rate rose to 75.9% in 
September from 75.8% in August. Both of those rates 
are well below the 80% rates that indicate capacity 
constraints 
are 
contributing 
to 
inflationary 
pressures. Strong demand for tech is driving some of 
the current inflation in the U.S., but much more is 
coming from tariffs and high energy prices.  
The industrial side of the U.S. economy has gradually 
improved since early 2025. After rebounding 
following the pandemic, industrial production 
moved gradually lower in 2023 and 2024. Solid 
economy growth, including strong demand for tech 
products with the AI buildout; some reshoring in the 
wake of tariffs; and higher energy production 
following the surge in oil prices with the U.S. conflict 
with Iran have all contributed to the rebound in the 
industrial sector. Overall industrial production in

highs in September. The weather drove much of the 
gain in utilities in September, but underlying growth 
is coming from strong demand, including from data

business equipment was up almost 7% in September

The large jump in utilities output in September was a 
weather-driven one-off, and is likely to reverse in 
October. But the trend is clear: the industrial side of 
the economy is growing thanks to strong tech 
demand, higher demand for electricity, and rising 
energy production due to the price surge this year. 
Industrial production should continue to gradually 
increase into 2027 as tech demand remains solid, 
but the surge in energy production in 2026 could 
reverse if a resolution to the conflict with Iran leads 
to price declines.
