EU China trade tensions add to 
case for portfolio protection
16 October 2026, 09:33 UTC, written by Thomas Lee Ruiz
Germany is pressing for wider European Union safeguards against surging Chinese imports as the bloc’s 
trade deficit with China reaches a record, according to Bloomberg. While broader restrictions could increase 
policy uncertainty and expose internationally oriented companies to retaliation, trade friction alone does not 
overturn our constructive equity outlook. Investors should remain invested while using capital preservation 
strategies and broad diversification to limit the impact of geopolitical and trade-related market setbacks.
Germany plans to press the European Union to consider quotas on Chinese imports in chemicals, mechanical 
engineering, and potentially pharmaceuticals, extending the bloc’s defensive trade measures beyond vehicles, according 
to reporting from Bloomberg. China’s goods trade surplus with the EU reached a record USD 34.8bn in September, 
almost 21% higher than a year earlier, as exports exceeded USD 60bn for the first time. The EU has already agreed 
to cap Chinese hybrid-vehicle imports at 350,000 units annually, with the measure potentially taking effect from 1 
December, and EU leaders are due to consider a broader response on 15 October.
The immediate concern is that wider restrictions could invite Chinese retaliation, increase costs, and complicate the 
outlook for European manufacturers with exposure to both markets. But the proposed measures are also evidence of a 
more structural policy response to trade imbalances, rather than by themselves a reason to retreat from risk assets.
Trade policy is becoming a more important source of company-level dispersion. The prospective safeguards span 
industries with complex supply chains and differing exposure to Chinese imports and demand. The economic effects 
will therefore depend on the scope, timing, and design of any measures, as well as China’s response. Companies with

Trade and geopolitical setbacks do not necessarily produce lasting market weakness. Historical analysis puts 
the median duration of an S&P 500 geopolitical drawdown at 16 days, while the index has been higher one year after 
the start of a geopolitical crisis roughly three-quarters of the time since 1941. Following the US “Liberation Day” tariff 
announcement in April 2025, the index fell 11.5% over five trading sessions but recovered those losses within a month. 
Resilient growth and robust earnings remain the more important medium-term market drivers, even though policy 
announcements may produce near-term volatility. Investors should therefore avoid allowing one potential escalation 
path to determine their overall allocation.
Capital preservation can reduce downside exposure without requiring investors to abandon return potential.
Investors seeking to retain equity exposure while limiting losses during a market setback can consider strategies tailored 
to their preferred preservation level, participation in potential gains, and investment horizon. These approaches may be 
particularly relevant for concentrated positions, low tolerance for drawdowns, or upcoming liquidity needs, although 
repayment remains subject to issuer creditworthiness. Broad commodities can add another source of resilience by 
providing exposure to real assets that may respond differently to inflation, supply disruption, or geopolitical stress. 
Together with diversified equity exposure, these measures can help portfolios withstand a wider range of trade and 
geopolitical outcomes.
So, we remain constructive on equities while emphasizing diversification, quality, and protection against periods of 
volatility. Rather than positioning for one forecast of EU-China relations, investors should diversify across sectors, 
regions, and return drivers. Capital preservation strategies can help limit drawdowns while retaining participation in 
potential gains, while broad commodity exposure can provide additional protection against supply shocks, geopolitical 
stress, and inflation spillovers.
