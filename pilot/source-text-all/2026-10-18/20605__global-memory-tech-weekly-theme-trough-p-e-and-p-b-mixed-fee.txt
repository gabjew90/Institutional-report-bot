Weekly theme: Trough P/E and P/B, mixed

Industry Overview 
   
 
 
Severe correction resembles cycle trough, but no downturn 
We believe memory-stock multiples bottomed after a deep correction lasting ~3.5 
months, led by SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics (both down 50%+ from their June 
peaks). Based on 2027-28E consensus EPS, Samsung and Hynix trade at just 2-3x P/E 
(30-50%+ below the 4-7x recorded in previous upcycles ahead of the 2001 internet-
bubble burst, 2008 global financial crisis, 2018 cloud boom, and 2022 COVID-19 
online/e-commerce expansion). P/B multiples have also fallen to near 1.0x 2027-28E 
BVPS, levels typically seen at cycle troughs (high chip inventories, 50%+ DRAM ASP cuts, 
large operating losses, and high borrowing costs, as in 2023 and 2019). Even so, we 
continue to expect supercycle-level ASPs, revenue, and margins not only in 4Q26 but 
also through 2027-28. We acknowledge low QoQ growth across the quarters of 2027, 
followed by a single-digit YoY EPS decline in 2028. However, absolute 2028 sales, OP, 
and EPS should remain near record highs and close to their 2027 peaks. We also expect 
“replacement demand” for AI memory chips used in data centers, including LPDDR5, 
SOCAMM, HBM, and eSSD, alongside more advanced GPUs/ASICs. Overall, the recent 
correction in memory stocks appears largely disconnected from memory fundamentals 
and reflects excessive concern over an AI-memory boom-bust cycle. 
Investor feedback broadly mixed 
We met Singapore-based investors this week. Feedback on the memory cycle was mixed: 
hedge fund investors were bearish, while most long-term investors remained bullish. Key 
concerns included neocloud companies' high borrowing costs; rising DRAM supply from 
China's 4F² process; lower memory content per GPU; slowing growth in AI capex or 
memory prices; and a potential earnings miss versus bullish consensus expectations. 
Nonetheless, our memory thesis generated constructive discussion even among bearish 
investors. We highlighted valuation multiples that remain too low even on softer 2028 
earnings; the limited impact of China's 4F² (see our recent report); substantial backlogs 
for AI start-ups and neocloud companies; memory chipmakers' high margins and ROIC 
even under softer ASP assumptions after 2026; and substantial shareholder returns. For 
example, dividends and buybacks could equal 10%+ of current market cap in 2027-28. 
We also see the potential for the global memory TAM to double from near-US$1.0tn in 
2026 to US$2.0tn+ in 2030, with OP potentially reaching US$1.0tn and the industry's 
market cap consequently reaching US$10tn, versus only a few trillion dollars currently. 
Hynix 4Q guidance likely bullish, incl. HBM4 and dividends 
Nanya Tech has already issued bullish 4Q guidance following upbeat 3Q results. We expect 
Hynix's 4Q guidance (28 Oct) to indicate upside to ASPs and margins, a strong ramp-up 
in 12-hi HBM4 production (and additional 8-hi output for new orders), and a special cash 
dividend despite the recent W40tn share buyback. We also see solid Oct memory-
industry data, incl. a monthly PC DRAM contract-price increase of 15%+ for DDR5 and 
even DDR4, stable spot prices despite rising US interest rates, and strong exports. Korea 
semis exports were solid, while China's memory imports also remained strong as of Aug.

Review of Hynix stock corrections over past 20+ years 
SK Hynix's share-price history over the past two decades highlights the memory 
industry's highly cyclical nature, with recoveries lasting longer and delivering 
substantially stronger returns than corrections. On average, the stock has gained 621% 
over ~25 months from trough to peak, versus an average 51% decline over ~15 
months during corrections. Every major downturn has ultimately been followed by a 
much stronger recovery. The largest rallies came after periods of severe industry stress, 
including the 1,314% surge from the 2003 trough to the 2006 peak and the 
1,696% rally from April 2025 to June 2026. SK Hynix has also experienced multiple 
30-60% share-price drawdowns during longer-term structural upcycles, suggesting that 
valuation compression and cyclical downturns have often created attractive entry points 
rather than signaled deterioration in the company's long-term earnings outlook.

Historically, SK Hynix troughs have featured deep share-price corrections, compressed 
valuations, and broad concerns over the sustainability of the memory cycle. The current 
correction closely resembles that pattern. The stock has fallen 57% from its peak, 
exceeding the historical average correction of 51%, and trades at just 2-3x 2027-
28E earnings, a valuation rarely seen outside cycle troughs. Unlike in previous 
downturns, however, the company enters this correction with exposure to the strongest 
secular demand driver in its history: AI infrastructure and HBM. Although the timing of 
the bottom remains uncertain, the historically deep drawdown, trough-like valuation, and 
intact long-term earnings drivers suggest that the current risk-reward profile 
increasingly resembles previous SK Hynix trough formations rather than the start of a 
structural downturn.
