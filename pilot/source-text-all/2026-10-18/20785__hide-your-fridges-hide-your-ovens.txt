Market comment 
Several weeks ago, it was announced that pioneer AI models had successfully proved several 
mathematical theorems which had remained unsolved for decades, which led to the somewhat 
humorous discussion on X that mathematicians weren’t expecting to be the first in the 
unemployment line, displaced by AI. There may be several kitchen appliances similarly caught off 
guard today. 
Indeed, a report came out that “small AI models may be months away from being capable enough 
to self-replicate on commercial internet-of-things devices, like smart ovens…” But if unmanned AI 
“worms” aren’t enough to send a chill through your refrigerator, cyber attacks are escalating to 
kitchen appliances as well. The Financial Times reports that a cyber attack impacted around 80 
French supermarkets on Sunday, with “attackers taking control of refrigeration systems and 
drastically raising their temperatures.” 
Food has always been at the focal point of national security. To quote Mao Zedong, “the rice 
bowls must be held firmly in our own hands.” But what happens when the temperature of the rice 
bowl can be controlled by foreign adversaries thousands of miles away (or by self-controlled AI 
worms)? 
Or what happens when your access to energy is controlled by a war taking place thousands of 
miles away? European natural gas 1-month TTF futures climbed to their highest level since 2023 
at 86.81 EUR/MWh, having risen more than €60 from their 2025 lows. The rally has pushed prices 
above their early-September highs as geopolitical uncertainty continues to cloud the outlook for 
flows through the Strait of Hormuz. As Rabobank’s energy strategist, Florence Schmit, notes, this 
matters all the more as Europe’s reliance on LNG grows ahead of the planned full phase-out of 
Russian gas from the start of 2027, increasing competition with Asian buyers and the JKM market. 
Middle Eastern LNG exports are likely to be lower in October than in September, even allowing for 
unreported or “dark” transits, while LNG arrivals into Europe have already weakened this month. 
Meanwhile, Saudi-Yemen military hostilities are only escalating further. The Houthis have 
requested Saudi civilians evacuate Aramco facilities, saying that these are legitimate targets, while 
the Saudis have encouraged Yemeni civilians to leave the Yemen Oil Company’s al-Sabah Central 
Station.  
The Trump Administration does not want Brent crude oil at $106/bbl going into the midterm 
elections, just a month away, and has said that he only plans to escalate offenses against Iran after 
the midterms are over, with what he hopes to be, a red wave. But, Iran knows when the midterms 
are too, and if they want to further complicate Trump’s plans, now is the perfect time to have the 
Houthis stir up trouble and make it increasingly difficult for the US to avoid intervening.

Day ahead 
Today starts the IIF Annual Summit hosted in Bangkok. 
Quite a few ECB speakers on the schedule today, including Elderson, Lagarde, Pereira, Escriva, 
Makhlouf, Schnabel, Kocher, and Simkus, many of whom will be speaking at the IIF in Bangkok. 
Quiet day for data today, with the exception of the US, which releases its September CPI inflation 
report. Headline CPI is expected to accelerate to 3.6% y/y and 0.6% m/m from 3.4% y/y and 0.4% 
m/m prior. Meanwhile, core CPI inflation is expected to cool to 2.5% y/y and 0.2% m/m from 
2.45% y/y and 0.29% m/m, with energy likely to account for the divergence between headline and 
core expectations. The Fed will also release the Beige Book.
