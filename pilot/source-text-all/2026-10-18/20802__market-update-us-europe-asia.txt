U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.33% | Nasdaq +0.08% | Dow +1.08%
Synopsis

US equities closed higher Thursday, ending near best levels. The equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP) outperformed the cap-weighted index by
~100 bp. The AI trade lagged again, with the SOX down seven straight sessions, though action was more mixed than Wednesday: semicaps
and HDDs were down, but there was some scattered recovery in select semis, memory and neoclouds, and big tech was mixed. Outper-
formers included healthcare, staples, banks, insurers, payments, exchanges, credit cards, software, transports, building products, A&D,
diversified/commodity chemicals, homebuilders, retail/apparel, restaurants, travel/leisure, entertainment and media, while energy,
E&Cs, tech hardware, cybersecurity, telecom and space (RKLB-US) lagged. Treasuries were firmer with some curve flattening, with yields
down 9-10 bp at the long end in a big safe-haven rally. The dollar index was down 0.5%, gold finished up 1.5%, silver was up 2.3% and
bitcoin futures were up 0.8%. Rotation was again the major theme, with staples and healthcare seeing big gains alongside continued
weakness in the AI trade, and consensus still looking for an October Fed hold despite a hotter core PPI reading. Today's round of super-
regional bank earnings was better received than the moneycenters', though the broad financial sector pared losses from earlier in the

expenses; and FMC-US made a big guidance cut amid broad pricing adjustments to boost competitiveness.
Macro

Flight to safety continues amid the latest wave of AI worries: Thursday saw continued weakness across the AI trade and a continuation of
Wednesday's defensive rotations. Anthropic on Wednesday released a $16.8B Q3 revenue estimate ahead of its November IPO plans,
though some analysts flagged possible q/q growth deceleration (Bloomberg). The developments follow a big focus on Wednesday on AI
debt dynamics, particularly neocloud CRWV-US, whose CEO said the market was acting "irrationally" in bidding down its debt (Bloomberg).
Sentiment around the AI space has deteriorated, renewing concerns about a broadening competitive landscape, open-source/open-
weight models, token costs, compute demand growth, IPO delays, the enterprise spending backdrop and ROI. In afternoon headlines,
CoreWeave credit risk hit a record high as investors soured on aggressive AI debt expansion (Bloomberg), and Nvidia's CEO said there
was no need to backstop CoreWeave, which is improving (Bloomberg).

Core PPI runs well ahead of consensus after the hotter core CPI: Headline September producer prices rose 1.3% m/m, topping the 0.5%
consensus and August's upwardly revised 0.5% (first printed at +0.4%), the largest monthly advance since March 2022, driven by an
expected rise in energy prices (gasoline prices up 20.3% m/m). Core PPI rose 0.7% m/m, well ahead of the 0.3% consensus, with the prior
month revised down to flat from a 0.2% rise. The core services increase was the largest since April amid higher prices for trade services,
truck transportation and food/alcohol wholesaling. The report follows a hotter-than-expected core CPI, which saw firming in both core
services and goods. September CPI takeaways were largely seen as hawkish: headline rose 0.6% m/m, driven largely by gasoline, while
core rose 0.3% against a 0.2% consensus and annual core accelerated to 2.6%. BofA maintained its October hike call, while Citi shifted
to a December hike from no further tightening this year. The Fed will not see the September PCE report until after the 28-Oct meeting.

Fed outlook: October hike odds fall back as Fedspeak leans dovish: Futures pricing after Wednesday's CPI briefly showed a 50-50 chance
of an October hike, but those bets retreated, and consensus is now looking for the next hike in December and ~70 bp of hiking through
year-end 2027. Treasuries rallied with yields down as much as 10 bp, supported by dovish-leaning Fedspeak from Vice Chair (Supervision)
Bowman and SF's Daly, who downplayed the urgency for an October rate hike. October odds fell back to ~40%, with Daly pointing to the
outsized role of gasoline in last month's inflation print (Reuters). Fed Chair Warsh was due to take part in a Q&A at the IMF's annual
meeting in Bangkok at 11:30pm Eastern, likely the last major Fedspeak before the pre-FOMC blackout begins Saturday; his comments
after September's hike leaned hawkish, with the chair calling inflation the panel's predominant focus. Strategists this week also spotted
reentry opportunities in the long end of the curve (Bloomberg), and Wall Street is bracing for further Treasury market activism as Bessent
prepares debt management updates (Bloomberg).

September retail sales beat, regional surveys diverge: September retail sales rose 0.8% m/m against a +0.3% consensus, following Au-
gust's revised 1.3% gain (was +1.2%), with sales ex-auto up 0.9% against +0.5% consensus. Control group sales, which feed into GDP,
rose 0.15% against a +0.3% consensus and August's revised 1.2% gain (was +1.4%). Gas stations jumped 6.4%, while furniture (+1.3%),
restaurants/bars (+1.1%), health/personal care (+0.7%) and electronics/appliances (+0.6%) also gained; ex-gasoline sales rose just
0.2%. Weakness was concentrated in miscellaneous retailers (-3.0%), building materials (-0.6%) and sporting goods (-0.5%). The October
Empire State Manufacturing Index jumped to +25.5 against a +10.3 consensus and September's +7.6, the highest since Dec-21, with
New Orders at +23.2 from +2.0 and Prices Paid easing to +59.5 from +63.1. The October Philly Fed Index missed at +20.9 against a
+28.4 consensus and September's +37.8, with Employment edging lower and Prices Received rising. Separately, US holiday sales growth
is expected to accelerate to 4.2% this year, helped by wealthy shoppers (Reuters).

Oil: geopolitical and supply risks stay elevated: Oil markets remained focused on elevated geopolitical and supply risks, alongside reports
the US is considering easing restrictions on Russia's "shadow fleet" as Washington looks to alleviate high energy prices (Oilprice.com).
IEA member countries agreed to complete the release of the remaining 100M barrels from the 400M barrel intervention program (Reu-
ters). Global tanker rates have surged, with freight now representing more than 40% of the price of US crude, versus ~7% in 2025 (Reu-
ters). Ukraine resumed attacks on Russian refineries, defying Trump, while risks around Hormuz and Saudi infrastructure remain elevated.
Reuters noted the prolonged Iran war increasingly resembles a structural supply shock rather than a temporary one, with traders pricing
elevated crude well into 2028.

Synopsis

European equities finished higher Thursday, near best levels, following a higher close on Wednesday. Defensives outperformed cyclicals,
with telecom and beverages the top gainers and tech and basic resources the biggest laggards. The tech selloff was again the big story,
with the sector leading the cyclical selloff as latest AI updates added to concern around growth durability and debt/ROI dynamics. Gov-
ernment bonds extended gains across the board: German Bund yields were down 7bps, French OAT yields fell near 10bps, Spanish Bono
and Italian BTP yields were both down near 9bps and UK Gilt yields declined 7.7bps. The strength tracked the US bond rally, with some
help from seemingly dovish ECB remarks, as Kazaks and Dolenc played down the need for immediate rate hikes. The dollar index was
down 0.4%, gold up 1.6%, silver up 2.6% and Bitcoin futures up 0.4%. In data, Eurozone industrial production fell for a third straight month
amid rising energy costs, France inflation rose to 3.4% in September, the highest since January 2024, and UK August GDP surprised to
the upside, while BoE Chief Economist Pill further downplayed the chances of a hiking cycle. Busy on the corporate front, AZN-US an-
nounced a collaboration with AI therapeutics company insitro, ERIC.B-SE Q3 sales beat consensus by ~7%, RI-FR Q1 sales declined less
than feared, EZJ-GB set a shareholder vote on the Apollo takeover, DNLM-GB Q1 sales rose 1.1% to £433M, beating consensus, and
DOCM-CH Q3 external revenue of CHF342.7M beat consensus. MNDI-GB posted softer Q3 EBITDA, mid-single-digit below H2 expecta-
tions, ANTO-GB Q3 copper output of 152kt missed consensus, NDA.SE-SE Q3 operating profit missed market expectations, and ENT-GB
reiterated full-year profit guidance at the lower end of its range.
Macro

Defensives lead while tech and basic resources lag: Telecom was the top performer, with Ericsson (ERIC.B-SE) surging after Q3 sales beat
consensus by around 7%, driven by 9% organic growth in Networks, with adjusted EBITA of SEK10.1B at a 17% margin and a better-than-
expected Q4 outlook. Telia (TELIA-SE) rose after Deutsche Bank upgraded it to buy, citing positive earnings momentum, cost-cutting
opportunities and scope for higher shareholder returns, and the sector's strength follows recent weakness on SpaceX's (SPCX) move into
US-wide mobile space. Food & Beverage was also stronger, with focus on Pernod Ricard (RI-FR) after Q1 fiscal 2027 sales declined less
than expected, as strength in India and other international markets helped offset weakness in the US and China; the company expects
FY2027-29 growth towards the lower end of its 3-6% medium-term target. Insurance, Personal & Household Goods, Health Care and
Media were also among the top gainers amid a flight out of cyclical and growth sectors into names deemed safer. Technology was the
biggest laggard on fears of persistent interest-rate pressure on capital-intensive AI build-outs. Basic Resources came under pressure as
weaker copper prices compounded negative company updates: Antofagasta (ANTO-GB) slid after YTD copper production fell 8.3% and
guidance missed consensus, prompting analysts to warn of 2027 EBITDA downgrades, and Mondi (MNDI-GB) slumped on softer Q3
EBITDA and price hikes to counter elevated energy and raw-material costs, while ArcelorMittal (MT-NL) received an upgrade to overweight
at JPMorgan on attractive valuation multiples and expected support from European Union steel safeguards.

European tech under pressure on AI financing, but demand signals intact: Pressure on the most AI-exposed names has built for days and
is now being felt more widely, with the prospect of a higher cost of capital for multi-year AI data-centre projects, concentrated in crowded
AI infrastructure trades. Demand signals remain intact: ASML-NL reiterated yesterday that it cannot fully meet 2027-28 customer demand
and is expanding capacity, but markets are prioritising valuation and financing questions. Saxo Bank noted an AI setback can drag indices
lower and curb spending without necessarily triggering a broad bear market, and that the key signal may be whether weakness stays
contained, allowing rotation into healthcare, select financials and industrials, or spreads into wider risk-off selling. UBS suggested the
correction is more a reset in expectations than a deterioration in the underlying AI investment cycle, arguing that AI monetisation trends
remain strong and that valuations and rates look manageable versus the 2022 correction.

Bonds reclaim safe-haven status as the ECB and BoE sound measured: Eurozone government bonds reclaimed safe-haven status as
mounting anxiety over a debt-fuelled AI spending binge triggered a broad exit from equities and corporate credit (Reuters). Germany's 10-
year Bund yield posted its lowest level since Jul, 10Y Gilt yields hit their lowest in weeks, and credit default swaps on several major AI-
exposed technology and infrastructure firms widened to record highs. French and Italian spreads were mixed to modestly tighter, with the
OAT-Bund gap near 135-137 bp versus an early-Oct peak of ~159 bp and the BTP-Bund spread at ~105-110 bp. ECB President Lagarde
said the solution to France's rising borrowing costs lies in domestic fiscal policy, not ECB intervention, echoing Bank of France Governor
Moulin. French 10-year yields eased from early-Oct highs near 4.9% (highest since 2002), and on equities, French fiscal and 2027 election
risks were widely flagged as an overhang, though Bank of America last week upgraded French stocks to overweight after ~20%
underperformance since spring 2024 left them at a 5% P/E discount, the widest since 2016. On the ECB, KBC said money markets assign
only ~11% probability to an Oct hike versus 75% last month, and Polymarket pricing for the 29-Oct decision shows 92% odds of no hike,
while December remains fully priced for a hike to 2.75%. Kazaks and Dolenc played down the need for immediate hikes, Belgium's Wunsch
and Lithuania's Šimkus indicated another hike is probably required, and BoE's Pill expressed skepticism about the degree of tightening
discounted by markets.

Growth data soften while energy carries Q3 earnings: UK GDP rose 0.1% in Aug, a third consecutive monthly expansion, defying
expectations of a small contraction after Jul's 0.4% gain, with manufacturing jumping 1.2% m/m. Lloyds said even flat output in Sep would
leave Q3 growth at 0.7% q/q, well ahead of the BoE's Jul projection of 0.1%, and the figures were seen strengthening the case for a Nov
Bank Rate increase, though softer details suggest the economy's earlier pace is moderating. Eurostat reported Eurozone industrial
production fell 0.5% m/m in Aug versus 0.0% expected, a third consecutive monthly decline after Jul was revised to -0.3%, and output
was down 1.1% y/y versus -0.1% expected. Non-durable consumer goods fell 2.7% m/m and intermediate goods 0.8%, while capital goods
gained 0.4%; ING noted the early-year bounce has faded and that industry is proving less immune to the Middle East conflict than hoped.

On earnings, energy companies are expected to lead European Q3 growth for a third straight quarter, with MSCI Europe Energy EPS growth

China safeguards and UK policy: EU leaders meet in Brussels with China's record trade surplus high on the agenda, as Germany presses

to be "scorecard positive" in 2029/30, with the government ruling out rises in income tax, VAT and employee National Insurance and

Synopsis

Asia equities fell almost everywhere Thursday, with technology-led benchmarks underperforming substantially. The Nikkei fell almost
5.0%, the Kospi lost 7.6% after another circuit breaker was called and the Taiex fell 3.4%; Shenzhen led Greater China benchmarks lower,
and there were significant declines on the Topix. Australia opened lower but rallied to the flatline, while India surged as its IT stocks rallied.
The US dollar was weaker but off its lows, the yen stronger, the NZD stronger on hawkish-leaning RBNZ comments, and the won and
Taiwan dollar notably weaker; JGB yields were also down. Precious metals rallied on the dollar weakness, base metals were lower and
cryptocurrencies were sharply down. Asia technology stocks fell from the bell as concerns over the AI build-out remained, exacerbated by
growing concerns over inflation and central bank responses. The Asia AI trade is now under severe medium-term pressure as debt loads
trigger balance sheet examination amid higher borrowing costs and expanding CDS spreads. Among decliners, Samsung Electronics

of the more bullish margin estimates. Elsewhere, BOJ board member Koeda said there was no need to be overly cautious with rate hikes
amid ongoing risks to inflation. Australia's unemployment rate rose to its highest in nearly four years with employment levels shrinking.
The IMF upgraded FY GDP growth forecasts for several nations linked to the AI supply chain, and separately warned China the yuan was
undervalued by double-digit percentage points, reflecting domestic economic imbalances. China's lending data improved, but on seasonal
issues and stimulus. The RBNZ chief economist said the bank's next interest rate decision was 'wide open', but the direction was 'most
likely up'. Oasis Capital increased its stake in Nidec (6594-JP) to 9.0% to become the company's single largest shareholder. MUFG (8306-
JP) said it is 'tentatively' re-engaging with Japan government bonds despite ongoing concerns over elevated yields. Jio Platforms is to
begin taking orders from next week in what will be India's largest ever IPO. TSMC (2330-TW) posted better-than-expected Q3 results with
revenue and profits both surging to record highs, and said it is considering expansion in the US beyond Arizona and possibly into Texas.

Bloomberg report.
Macro

TSMC Q3 profits beat expectations to brighten a dark day for AI-related stocks: TSMC (2330-TW) posted higher-than-expected Q3 results
after the close Thursday as the AI-related chip boom showed few signs of slowing. Revenue was NT$1.49T versus consensus of NT$1.46T,
operating income NT$876.5B versus consensus of NT$853.7B and EPS NT$29.43 versus consensus of NT$28.55. Net profit rose 68.7%
y/y to NT$763.1B, the sixth consecutive quarter of record profits, and operating margin also beat estimates. By product, 3nm chips saw
the largest share of revenue at 33%, with 5nm at 28% and 7nm at 10%. The company guided FY revenue 44% higher y/y at US$52.3-
54.3B versus previously just over 40%, with FY gross margin at 65-67%, and reiterated capex at US$60-64B, saying it is moving 'full
speed' to increase capacity to meet assessed demand. The chairman said the company will add an additional nm3 fab in Taiwan but will
take a 'sophisticated, disciplined' approach to capex planning, and is considering additional US capacity expansion beyond Arizona. The
company said adoption of agentic AI is fueling growth as the transition to AI assistance takes place, with the emergence of AI CPUs and
continuous demand for networking chips driving further demand (CNBC, Bloomberg). TSMC stock closed -4.3% lower in Taipei prior to the
announcement, and the ADR was trading below -1% in overnight US trading.

AI trade unwinds, with South Korea the epicenter: The AI trade is under heavy pressure, with the Kospi triggering another circuit breaker
and extending its wtd decline to more than 15%. A steeper Fed rate path was cited as the driver of the latest AI selloff, though pressure
has been building for days amid several bearish AI talking points. Ballooning tech sector debt loads are putting more scrutiny on balance

Japan rates: JGB yields fall as institutions plan a tentative return and BOJ's Koeda urges no over-caution: The JGB 10Y yield fell to a two-
month low amid the broader risk-off move, with Koeda's remarks ignored, and the JGB and Australian yield curves bull-flattened as
strength was considered a function of the equity risk-off move and renewed demand for haven assets. In a Bloomberg interview, MUFG
chief executive of global markets Atsushi Yamashita discussed plans to gradually rebuild JGB positions while carefully assessing interest
rate levels. He expects 10y yields to peak at ~3.2%, just above the three-decade high of 3.115% reached earlier this month, predicted the
next BOJ rate hike in December with follow-up moves at quarterly intervals to a terminal rate of around 2%, and ruled out repatriation,
while acknowledging the risk of further upside if the BOJ struggles to contain inflation or Prime Minister Takaichi fails to convince investors
of fiscal discipline. Reuters discussed expectations that some of Japan's largest asset managers will unveil plans to buy more domestic
bonds, with analysts suggesting consistent repatriation flows could put a floor under yen; Japanese life insurers manage ~$2.5T in assets,
about a quarter of which is held overseas, and there are doubts that buying would be funded via repatriation. In a speech, BOJ board
member Koeda estimated that neutral rates are considerably above 1%, roughly in line with the lower bound of the BOJ's aggregated
range, and one method that adds the 2% price stability target to potential growth (most recently reported at 0.8%) would place neutral
above 2%. She argued underlying inflation has been running at about 2% over the past year and appears to be anchoring at that level,
bolstered by steady wage growth, and said that given considerable upside inflation risk and continued accommodative financial conditions

IMF upgrades Asia growth forecasts and says the yuan is undervalued: The IMF said Thursday it expects Asia Pacific growth of 4.8% y/y

and its impact on energy prices, rising sovereign bond yields and El Niño-related disruptions. Separately, the IMF's external sector report
assessed the Chinese yuan as undervalued in real terms in a range of 17.3% to 25.3%, with a midpoint of 21.3%; Asia-Pacific Department
chief Krishna Srinivasan said the weakness reflects underlying domestic imbalances and that Beijing's recent mini stimulus efforts were
not sufficient (Bloomberg). The PBOC last week issued a rare and lengthy document defending its currency management, arguing the
IMF's research is not a judgment on the nominal rate or a justification to engineer yuan appreciation, though it acknowledged the recom-
mendation to rebalance the economy by boosting domestic demand. China is on track for a record trade surplus in 2026 that will top the
$1.2T recorded last year, and German Chancellor Merz and other EU leaders have urged Beijing to allow the yuan to appreciate.

20K increase, the largest monthly decline since Feb-2025, though the composition was stronger than the headline, with full-time employ-

the highest since Oct-2021, versus consensus for a fall to 4.6%. The market reaction was dovish, with the policy-sensitive 3Y rate down
to a one-month low, November odds of an RBA move falling to less than 20% and markets no longer fully pricing in another hike by May-
2027; Q3 CPI (28-Oct) is the next major input for the RBA. In China, new loans were CNY1.08T ($161.4B) in September, above consensus
of CNY975B, following CNY60B in August, with corporate loans growing to CNY1.29T from CNY260B and household loans stabilizing after
a CNY202.9B contraction in August, according to Reuters calculations. Outstanding loan growth edged down to a record-low 4.8% y/y,
total social financing was CNY3.55T versus consensus CNY3.27T and CNY1.66T in the prior month, with growth softening to 7.1% from
7.2%, and M2 growth slowed to 7.2% versus consensus of 7.4%. The data were seen as showing some effects from recent policy support,
though sequential growth was largely explained by seasonality as banks ramp up credit issuance toward quarter-end, and attention re-
mains subdued while markets await more meaningful stimulus.
