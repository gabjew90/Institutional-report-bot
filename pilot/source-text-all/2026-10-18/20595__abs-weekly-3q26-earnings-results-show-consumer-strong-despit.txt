Spreads unchanged to wider 
ABS spreads were unchanged or wider by up to 10bp week-over-week. The spread 
widening seen over the past few weeks has left all sectors trading above their 
respective 24-month minimums and closer to their respective 24-month average. 
Thirteen sectors are currently trading 20bp or more above their respective 24-month 
minimums. At the low end, retail auto lease ABS is trading just 8bp above its 24-month 
minimum, while data center ABS sits at the high end at 55bp above its 24-month 
minimum. We continue to believe spreads will be rangebound (+/- 5bp WoW) but still 
feel the risk is to the downside (wider spreads). This likely means most sectors will 
continue to trade closer to their respective 24-average than 24-month minimum. We still 
selectively prefer the higher yielding sectors (solar, data center, aircraft, fiber, 
franchise/whole business, and railcar ABS sectors). We continue to prefer ABS over HG 
corporates. Click links for ABS new issue volume and pricing by deal and ABS Spreads 
time series. 
Learnings from earnings 
Citigroup:  Management continues to expect a net credit loss (NCL) rate for U.S. credit 
card portfolio of 4.0-4.5% in 2026 versus 4.3% in 2025. The 30+ day DQ rate declined 
YoY and QoQ for the USCC portfolio but saw mixed trends for the PLCC portfolio. The 
NCL rate for both portfolios was down YoY and QoQ. JPMorgan Chase: Management 
noted that the consumer remains healthy and resilient, primarily because the labor 
market is strong. They expect full-year card net charge-off rate of ~3.2% versus 3.3% in 
2025. Credit metrics improved year-over-year for both the auto and credit card 
portfolios in 3Q26. US Bank: Overall credit metrics were mixed in 3Q26, with higher 
delinquency trends but lower net write-offs in the card portfolio. Management noted the 
higher deposit costs QoQ, partly reflecting the September Fed rate hike. Wells Fargo: 
Management noted U.S economic growth and consumer spending remain strong despite 
persistent inflation and interest rate headwinds. The 30+ DQ rate was flat YoY and 
higher QoQ, while NCL rate was down YoY and QoQ for the credit card portfolio. Trends 
for both rates were mixed for the auto portfolio. Originations for auto loans continue to 
grow YoY and QoQ. 
Flight tracker 
Within the US, TSA checkpoint travel numbers show YTD air traffic through the end of 
October 14 is down 0.7% compared to 2025. The management team at Delta noted 
demand remains strong, supported by consumers’ growing preference for experiences 
and travel. They also noted that recent corporate survey results indicate over 90 percent 
of respondents expect their corporate travel will increase or stay the same in 2027. 
Management expects total revenues to increase approximately 20% YoY in 4Q26. 
Data Show 
During August, CPI for Used vehicles declined YoY for the ninth consecutive month. 
Airfare fares saw another YoY increase exceeding 20%.

 Market View 
Volume expected to slow as market participants head to Miami 
This week, thirteen deals were priced for an aggregate principal balance of $8.5bn. This 
week’s deals included two subprime auto loan ABS, two data centers ABS (from the 
same issuer), two fiber ABS (from the same issuer) and one deal each in the retail auto 
lease, prime auto loan, private student loan, timeshare, franchise/ whole business, and 
small business loan ABS sectors. The new deal pipeline is expected to slow with only 5 
deals in the pipeline. 
So far this year, 463 deals have been priced for an aggregate principal balance of 
$327.5bn, up 5.5% versus last year’s comparable period. The Auto ABS sector (retail & 
commercial) continues to dominate new issue volume (51.1% of total volume) while the 
broadly inclusive Other ABS sector (all sectors except auto, card, student loan, and 
equipment) has seen the highest growth (up 23.2% YoY, or $21.4bn). Within the Other 
ABS sector, personal loan ABS (up $4.4bn), franchise / whole business ABS (up $3.7bn), 
aircraft ABS (up $3.4bn), triple net lease ABS (up $2.4bn), and data center ABS (up 
$2.1bn) sectors have seen the greatest growth in dollars. 
For 2026, we are forecasting new issue volume of $407.0bn. Although fiber ABS 
issuance has come in higher than expected ($11.7bn actual versus $10.0bn forecasted), 
data center ABS issuance is tracking below expectations ($13.9bn actual versus $20.0bn 
forecasted). The offsetting trends leave our overall digital infrastructure ABS forecast 
unchanged at $40bn, although risks are to the downside. If realized, our current new 
issue volume forecast would be a new record and would mark the fourth consecutive 
year volume has increased year-over-year.

Spreads unchanged to wider 
ABS spreads were unchanged or wider by up to 10bp week-over-week. Spreads widened 
5bp for solar ABS and 10bp for data center and fiber ABS while spreads widened 2-3bp 
for retail auto loan, credit card, student loan, equipment, floorplan, corporate auto fleet, 
and utility fee ABS. See Exhibit 9.  
We believe spreads continue to be impacted by several factors including heavy new 
issue volume, broader market volatility, continue concerns regarding inflation, and 
continued uncertainty regarding Fed policy and upcoming elections. Further, spreads for 
digital infrastructure ABS continue to be impacted by AI companies increasing leverage 
to fund capital expenditures (Capex), including substantial commitments for data center, 
and reduced free cash flows. 
The spread widening seen over the past few weeks has left all sectors trading above 
their respective 24-month minimums and closer to their respective 24-month average. 
Thirteen sectors are currently trading 20bp or more above their respective 24-month 
minimums. At the low end, retail auto lease ABS is trading just 8bp above its 24-month 
minimum, while data center ABS sits at the high end at 55bp above its 24-month 
minimum. 
We continue to believe spreads will be rangebound (+/- 5bp WoW) but still feel the risk is 
to the downside (wider spreads). This likely means most sectors will continue to trade 
closer to their respective 24-average than 24-month minimum. We still selectively prefer 
the higher yielding sectors (solar, data center, aircraft, fiber, franchise/whole business, 
and railcar ABS sectors). 
The ICE BofA 1+ Year AAA-A Fixed Rate ABS Index (RAB0) widened 4bp on the week, 
while the 1-5 Year AAA-A US Corporate Index (CV10) widened 1bp, resulting in a 3bp 
widening in the basis between the two indices. The basis now stands at 37bp, practically 
on top of its 24-month average of 37bp. The recent widening in the basis favors ABS 
over HG corporates (Exhibit 3). 
The current basis between AA-BBB rated ABS and AAA rated ABS (97bp) is slightly 
above the 24-month minimum (94bp) but well below its 24-month average (116bp) and 
24-month maximum (133bp) (Exhibit 4). As we noted over the past few weeks, AA-BBB 
rated ABS continues to offer incremental spread relative to AAA-rated ABS, though we 
find the segment more attractive when spreads are trading further above their 24-
month minimums. We believe the basis is more likely to widen than tighten with AA-BBB 
rated ABS underperforming AAA rated ABS leaving us to prefer AAA rated ABS.
