US NATURAL GAS - the medium term fundamental outlook remains bearish given expectations for
significant production growth. However, there is near term price risk given low salt storage, extreme short
positioning and consensus mild weather expectations.
Henry Hub futures have been supported by strong cash markets, particularly in Louisiana and the
Southeast, where salt storage continues to struggle to refill at a sufficient pace. Front-winter futures and
front-end vol have remained strong over the past week as production has failed to normalise. This is
primarily due to Appalachia production curtailments, which have averaged nearly 3 bcf/d month to date.
Regional prices remain below $1.50 and fall below $1 on weekends, providing little incentive for
producers to unwind shut-ins. The market saw a sharp sell off on Friday as TC Energy lifted pipeline force
majeure.
Front end has been rallying, but interest in downside option structures has continued, though largely in
limited-loss formats with tight put spreads and put flies active over the past few sessions. The market has
seen several large downside clips going through, which appear to offer reasonable risk/reward (especially
in JV27) if production returns soon and temperatures are as mild as the super El Niño narrative suggests.
The issue is that deploying this theme in large size now does not offer compelling risk/reward, when salt
inventories are so low and managed money positioning at the lowest level since March 2020. Call skew
screens rich vs. seasonal history and we therefore like to position for/protect against a potential relief rally
through call spreads.

Trade? Buy Dec26 $3.7/3.9 call spread costs indic $0.045/mmbtu. Ref = $3.4/mmbtu. Max loss limited to
premium spent.

FREIGHT - there was a notable retreat in freight since midweek peak (see detailed desk note). TD25 route
(dirty USGC-EU) came off by $8/bbl from the highs. The exact trigger for the move lower isn't clear but
main factors likely come down to:
• Reports around Washington potentially easing sanctions on the shadow fleet to increase available tanker
capacity, though in practice we think this would only provide marginal relief.
• Demand shifting away from long-haul trades to short-haul trades due to overextended long-haul rates
making arbs unworkable.
• More ships coming in as product tankers are switching into crude to capture the premium in dirty freight.
• More empty VLCCs heading from Asia to the Atlantic (potentially due to Iranian attacks pushing owners
out of the Gulf).
The WTI/Brent futures spread initially followed suit, rallying ~$4.5/bbl from the lows on Wednesday and
Thursday (Thursday’s being one of the largest one day moves in recent history). Lower freight and the
extended WTI/Brent rally softened the North Sea physical market significantly into the end of the week as
arbs reopened, seen in the Dated Brent MOC vs Brent futures spread.
GRAINS - another week of losses for grains, with both corn and soybean front contracts now trading near
two month lows and extending the move triggered by last Friday's WASDE and exacerbated by unwinds
of elevated speculative length. GS systematic positioning models show that over the past week corn has
moved from 1/2 max long to 1/3 max short currently and now targeting max short.
Fresh data this week further reinforced a looser balance story in both markets. (1) USDA reported soybean
harvest was ~46% complete, ahead of estimated pace (2) Chinese corn production is expected to reach a

record 306Mt and (3) Brazil soybean production is now projected to reach new record.
However, China purchasing spree of US soybeans gathered pace with the US reporting China booked 1.3
million tons this week. This signals China's commitment to fulfilling the 25mn tons annual purchase
target, although this feels broadly in line with market expectations. Meanwhile, El Nino risks remain in the
background and could still impact current crop development. Early signs of this are already emerging in
corn, where persistent rains across parts of the Midwest have started raising crop quality concerns and
delaying harvest.
Trade? Soybean vol is the cheapest in the grains complex and we like owning vol at these levels. It is
carrying well on a realised basis and at the lower end of the implied range for this time of year. Implied
vol has sold off 3v in the past two weeks and the risk/reward to owning implied at 15v seems reasonable.

IRON ORE - testing the lowest level since 2022. Market is focussed on whether prices will break down
toward $85/t, a level that would force downward revisions to miner production guidance and recalibrate
capesize fleet demand. However, GS trading desk sees an asymmetrical upside into Q4, targeting a
rebound toward $97–$100/t. Why?
Systematic short positioning across iron ore futures is near maximum capacity, creating significant risk of
a short covering rally.
Strong demand for Chinese steel exports. We see minimal probability of an onshore demand rebound with
domestic cold-rolled and plate demand softened further into 2H. Instead, global steel resilience and
regional supply shocks will clear the Chinese surplus with (1) firm Turkish scrap pricing and (2) drought-
related steel production disruptions in Indonesia, and power supply constraints in India.
Capesize dry bulk rates are nearing a seasonal bottom. Spot weakness is primarily driven by an elevated
capesize ballasters, with arrivals into Brazil and Guinea clustered around early November. The seasonal
cargo list expansion typically ramps up in mid-to-late November, the market faces a temporary 1- to 2-
week supply overhang. Freight rates at these lower levels ($29ish C3 spot fixing) have eased FOB margins
sufficiently to incentivize iron ore and bauxite miners to return to the chartering market.
Trade? Buy 1Q27 $100 call costs indic $1.1/mt. Ref = €91.8/mt. Max loss limited to premium spent.

GS COMMODITY RESEARCH SUMMARY
EU Nat Gas: Lower Hormuz Flows Raise Market-Implied Tight Winter Probability
Europe's window to build gas storage largely over. NW Europe is ending its natural gas storage injection
season in the next few days with inventories at approximately 62% full, in line with our expectations.
Whether that’s enough to go through winter still depends on (1) the amount of LNG exports from the
Persian Gulf in the coming months and (2) winter weather. While we maintain our 70 EUR/MWh year-end
TTF forecast, risks to our view remain skewed to the upside.
Global Gas: Asia Energy Tour: Takeaways on Global LNG: Tight in Near-Term, but Long-Term Outlook
Looser on Supply Additions and Soft Demand Trends
We continue our takeaways from our recent Asia Energy tour with a breakdown of views on global LNG
from our meetings with corporates, policymakers, and industry experts last month.
Overall, the trip supported our view that the global LNG S/D backdrop is tight in the near-term but could
weaken as supply growth outpaces demand growth later this decade.
Oil: Supply Adaptation Meets Global Restocking Demand
We maintain our base case that continued adaptation of Mideast supply will eventually reduce crude oil
prices, while potential renewed escalation still poses significant net upside risk.

We nudge up our Brent Dec26 price forecast to $90/bbl (vs. $85 prior) because ongoing downside risks to
supply likely slow the moderation in the timespreads risk premium—which we estimate at just over $20
today.
Our 2027 average Brent forecast of $80 is unchanged and balances downward price pressure from faster-
than-expected adaptation in Gulf supply against a slower moderation in the risk premium.
Oil: Diesel in Market and Policy Focus; High Freight Rates Widen Crude Differentials
Refined product exports retreated to 28% of their 2025 average (14DMA) from nearly 50% two weeks
ago.
Refined product tightness and an uncertain supply outlook keep our estimate of the global average retail
product price at $229/bbl, with diesel remaining a focus for both markets and policymakers.
Freight: Quantifying transportation costs by oil company. US and North Sea producers most advantaged,
Asian refiners the hardest hit
Freight rates have surged 10-50x across all tanker classes since year start, leading to a wide divergence in
realized price and margins for E&Ps and Refiners across the globe, and a return to regional trading in a
traditionally global market.
We introduce a route-by-route analysis with an evaluation of the relative advantages of oil producers and
refiners, based on their geographical location and their long-term tanker availability.
Agriculture: Black Sea Grains Rerouting: Only Partial and Risky
Wheat and corn prices have risen ~12% and ~15% since early July as attacks on Black Sea export
terminals and commercial vessels intensified.
Alternative export routes have kept grain moving, but only partially. Ukraine has rerouted more exports
through western rail links and the Danube corridor to Romania's Constanta port further south in the Black
Sea and further away from the areas most affected by the conflict.
But disruption risk may follow the rerouted flows. Recent attacks on commercial vessels near Romania
and Bulgaria suggest that the conflict may increasingly affect the southern Black Sea corridor through
which many rerouted exports now move.
Batteries: ESS helps, but we are not yet calling a cyclical bottom in batteries
Our battery supply-demand model (on an EV+ESS basis) points to a 2027E trough in ex-China demand-
to-supply ratio. We now include energy storage system (ESS) in our global battery supply-demand model,
after previously using an EV-only framework.
