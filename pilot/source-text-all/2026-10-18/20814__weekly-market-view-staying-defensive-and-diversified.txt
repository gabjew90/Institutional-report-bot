Editorial 
Staying defensive and diversified  
Strategy summary: Two weeks ago, we turned defensive 
towards equities, locking in semiconductor equity gains amid 
elevated bond volatility, narrowing market breadth and political 
uncertainty before the US mid-term elections. The near-term 
outlook reflected a less favourable risk-reward balance. Since 
we turned defensive, the Nasdaq 100 index has recorded its 
biggest weekly decline in three months as September’s hotter-
than-expected US inflation report briefly lifted bond yields. 
We have extended our defensive stance this week, 
downgrading US Communication Services equities to Neutral 
and upgrading US Healthcare to Overweight. We also closed 
our opportunistic bullish Communication Services idea and 
initiated a bullish idea on Healthcare, where earnings visibility 
and defensive characteristics appear more attractive. In fixed 
income, we see opportunities in US 10-year government bonds. 
We await clearer AI profit margins and spending guidance, 
broader earnings growth or lower bond and equity market 
volatility before turning positive on equities. Until then, 
diversification and disciplined entry levels remain important. 
Hot US inflation triggers equity pullback; not hot enough 
for an October Fed hike: Risk assets pulled back after 
September’s hotter-than-expected inflation data (headline 3.7% 
y/y; core 2.6% y/y). Higher gasoline and diesel prices were the 
main drivers, but passthrough appeared in airline fares, used 
cars and transportation services, showing pressures were not 
confined to energy. Almost half of inflation components are still 
rising above 3%, a measure recently cited by Fed Chair Warsh. 
Producer inflation also beat estimates. The reports revive the 
possibility of an October Fed rate hike, but we expect a divided 
Fed to weigh it against September’s soft jobs report and 
narrowly hold this month. A December hike appears more likely 
if inflation remains firm. Although Fed Chair Warsh did not 
comment on policy at an IMF speech this week, New York Fed 
chief Williams said he saw no need to hike in 2027. Our base 
case remains two further hikes by June 2027. 
Limited equity pullback so far. Equities fell sharply after the 
US inflation report, but the pullback is limited. The Nasdaq 100 
is 4.5% below its record high, while the S&P 500 remains close 
to all-time highs despite the latest decline. The S&P 500 
volatility index (VIX) has risen from below 15 to 18.2, still well 
under the 25–30 range typically associated with capitulation 
and forced selling. Meanwhile, US government bond volatility 
(MOVE) remains elevated after surging since September. Our 
stock-bond quant model turned more negative (-2) this week, 
signalling elevated equity volatility in the near term.  
When to turn more constructive on stocks? We would turn 
more constructive when capitulation signals align: the Nasdaq 
100 approaches a 10% drawdown, the VIX rises to 25–30 and 
our ‘Fear and Greed’ index enters “extreme fear”. Greater 
confidence would also require easing concerns about AI 
spending and technology firms’ exposure to neocloud revenue, 
lower bond volatility and our stock-bond model turning positive 
again. Until these conditions emerge, volatility may remain 
elevated, favouring a defensive equity stance. Political risk 
should ease after the US mid-term elections. We continue to 
seek selective opportunities following excessive corrections. 
US financials remain a preferred sector after Q3 earnings: 
US large banks’ earnings were mixed, but underlying trends 
remain encouraging. Healthy loan and deposit growth supports 
net interest income, while resilient consumer spending, wealth 
management activity and a solid M&A and refinancing pipeline 
strengthen the outlook. Credit risks appear manageable. We 
remain constructive and favour stronger, diversified franchises. 
US government bonds offer value as yields spike: The 
reversal in US government bond yields after their initial inflation-
driven spike suggests limited further upside. The 10-year yield 
briefly reached 5.32% before slumping as falling equities 
prompted short-covering. We expect a near-term 5.00–5.25% 
range and view moves above 5.25% as attractive entry points 
for our opportunistic 10-year government bond idea. Structural 
pressures, including term premium and increasing supply to 
fund fiscal deficits and AI investments, prevent us from turning 
outright bullish on longer maturities. We, therefore, continue to 
prefer 3–7-year maturities in broader bond allocations.  
—  Rajat Bhattacharya and Sundeep Gantori

Top client questions 
  How does the September 2026 US Consumer Price Index 
(CPI) report alter your view on the USD and bond yields?   
Our view: The hotter-than-expected CPI print raises the odds of 
an October Fed hike, though we still expect a hold followed by 
a rate hike in December. In the near term, we maintain our view 
that the US 10-year government bond yield will range between 
5.00% and 5.25%, while the USD will remain supported as 
markets reassess the likelihood of further Fed hikes. Additional 
gains would depend partly on markets pricing a faster or more 
prolonged tightening cycle, while clearer signs of an 
approaching policy rate peak could see the USD ease back. 
Rationale: September US consumer inflation came in above 
consensus across the board – headline CPI rose 3.7% y/y (vs. 3.6% 
estimate), and core CPI rose 2.6% y/y (vs. 2.5% estimate) – marking 
the fastest headline acceleration in five months. This shifts the 
balance of risks modestly hawkish. Unsurprisingly, the market-
implied probability of an October Fed hike rose over 50% overnight 
but closed the day at 32.7% – 9ppt higher than Tuesday’s close. 
We believe a split Fed will weigh this inflation report against the soft 
September jobs report and narrowly vote to keep rates on hold this 
month. Our base case remains for two more Fed hikes by June 
2027. A December rate hike now looks more like a done deal, 
especially as persistently higher oil prices keep headline and core 
inflation elevated for at least the next few months. Any re-escalation 
of the Middle East conflict after the US mid-term elections would 
exert pressure on the Fed, although we believe Republicans would 
show greater restraint in US foreign action if they suffer steep Senate 
losses, which looks increasingly likely. 
The intra-day price action in US government bonds was instructive. 
The US 10-year yield briefly spiked to 5.32% on the print before 
closing materially lower, as pre-existing bearish positioning was 
unwound and Fed speakers (Barkin, Goolsbee) stopped well short 
of signalling an imminent move. This dynamic reinforces our view 
that the technical selling pressure driving yields towards the upper 
end of our range is fading. Structural drivers – term premium, fiscal 
supply concerns and ‘higher for longer’ Fed repricing – remain intact 
and keep us from turning outright bullish on duration. However, our 
opportunistic bullish idea in US 10-year government bonds initiated 
at September-end remains, as we continue to view spikes in US 10-
year yields over 5.25% as a good risk-reward entry. From a longer-
term portfolio standpoint, we continue to favour the 3-7-year tenor. 
On FX, elevated US government bond yields should sustain the 
USD’s interest rate appeal. However, expected hikes alone will not 
drive further appreciation. Stronger data or firmer Fed guidance 
would provide fresh support, while evidence that tightening is 
nearing completion would temper the USD’s strength. 
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist 
— Ray Heung, Senior Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  What are your key takeaways from US bank earnings? 
Our view: Q3 2026 earnings results from major US banks have 
been mixed, but underlying trends remain encouraging. Healthy 
loan and deposit growth, resilient consumer spending and 
wealth management activity continue to support the sector. 
Credit risks remain manageable. We remain constructive on the 
US financial sector, favouring stronger, diversified franchises. 
Rationale: Healthy loan and deposit growth continue to support net 
interest income (NII), hinting that the US economy remains resilient. 
Capital markets revenue grew year-on-year (y/y), but moderated 
sequentially after a very strong Q2 2026, although performance 
varied across banks. Management commentary points to a healthy 
deal pipeline, supported by M&A activity and debt refinancing 
demand, although execution will depend on market conditions. 
Provisioning and net charge-off trends were mixed across banks, 
with no clear signs of broad deterioration in credit quality. Firmer 
US inflation raises the risk of further monetary tightening. While 
higher rates can support asset yields, they can also increase 
funding costs, pressure borrowers and weigh on securities’ 
valuations. We will continue to monitor lending demand, credit 
costs, NII trends, capital markets momentum and capital returns.  
—  Robin Xie, Senior Investment Strategist 
  Can Big Tech’s earnings guidance sustain confidence in 
the AI investment cycle? 
Our view: We expect Big Tech’s AI spending to remain elevated, 
supporting our constructive AI capex outlook. However, we 
await guidance on the pace of capex growth and the 
sustainability of profit margins. 
Rationale: Semiconductor leaders’ latest company guidance points 
to supply constraints extending through 2028, supporting continued 
capacity expansion for compute. However, while AI capex is still 
likely to rise, y/y growth is expected to moderate from c.100% in 
Q3 2026 to c.20% in Q3 2027. This normalisation in growth could 
raise concerns about the AI investment cycle’s durability.  
The Nasdaq-100 Index has rallied alongside accelerating AI capex. 
Slower capex growth is likely to moderate semiconductor revenue 
growth, while hyperscalers continue to incur depreciation costs on 
past investments. The slower growth is already partially reflected in 
the Nasdaq-100’s valuation de-rating this year. While we remain 
constructive on the AI investment theme, we prefer to stay 
prudent in the near term and await Big Tech’s guidance for greater 
visibility into spending plans in H2 2027 and beyond. 
—  Ryan Goh, Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  Would France face a credit rating downgrade by rating 
agencies this month? What might be the market implications? 
Our view: A rating downgrade is unlikely unless France’s 2027 
budget passes the National Assembly very soon with material 
changes to the proposal. We see French government bond 
yields remaining elevated relative to German Bund yields. 
Rationale: The French National Assembly began debating France’s 
2027 draft budget on 13 October 2026, with the first vote scheduled 
for 20 October. Near-term budget approval appears unlikely, as 
opposition parties have tabled motions to reject the proposal. The 
French Parliament has up to 70 days from the budget’s submission 
to complete the legislative process, although its final adoption and 
the resolution of political challenges could extend into early 2027.  
Last October, two of the three major rating agencies downgraded 
France by one notch to the high-single-A category with stable 
outlooks. The remaining agency rated France in the low-AA 
category with a negative outlook. As negative outlooks are typically 
resolved within 12-18 months, agencies have time to await fiscal 
clarity before acting. We believe downgrade risks are largely priced 
in after the recent rise in yields, but volatility is likely to persist amid 
fiscal uncertainty and the approaching presidential election. Banks 
and domestically-focused issuers would face the greatest near-term 
pressure. However, we would view wider spreads as an opportunity 
to add exposure, particularly to French banks’ subordinated 
debt, given their stronger balance sheets and the fact that France 
contributes less than 50% of major French banks’ overall revenue. 
—  Ray Heung, Senior Investment Strategist 
  Are you still positive on Japanese banks? 
Our view: We are positive on Japanese banks despite the recent 
market correction, as the structural drivers of improving 
profitability and shareholder returns remain intact. While 
political risk and shifting BoJ rate hike odds could sustain near-
term volatility, we see the recent pullback as an opportunity to 
build selective exposure to Japanese bank stocks. 
Rationale: Japan’s gradual monetary policy normalisation 
should support banks’ NII as floating-rate loans reprice and maturing 
assets are reinvested at higher yields. Healthy corporate lending 
demand, improving capital efficiency and continued share buybacks 
provide further support. While uncertainty over the pace of BoJ 
tightening could weigh on sentiment, we see gradual interest rate 
normalisation as supportive of medium-term bank profitability.  
Key risks include rising deposit costs, mark-to-market losses on 
bond portfolios and a disorderly increase in Japanese government 
bond yields. We continue to favour well-capitalised banks with 
strong domestic deposit franchises and improving returns on equity. 
—  Robin Xie, Senior Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  Could NZD/USD rebound from its one-year trough? If so, 
how far could it recover?  
Our view: New Zealand’s improving domestic conditions, 
persistent inflation and technical support provide a basis for 
NZD/USD’s initial recovery towards the 0.57 level, although 
elevated US bond yields could slow its progress. 
Rationale: In Q3, New Zealand’s seasonally-adjusted business 
confidence reading rose to a net 40% of firms expecting the economy 
to improve, up from net 14% in Q2. Hiring intentions improved, and 
filled jobs rose 0.2% m/m in August. These developments suggest 
domestic conditions remain supportive for the NZD.  
New Zealand’s above-target inflation keeps further RBNZ tightening 
in focus. The 22 October consumer inflation data release and the 
28 October RBNZ policy meeting are key catalysts: persistent price 
pressures and improving demand are expected to prompt firmer 
policy guidance and strengthen the NZD’s interest-rate appeal. 
NZD/USD’s position near the lower boundary of its downtrend 
channel suggests scope for a technical rebound. We see 0.57 as 
the first recovery hurdle; a sustained break above this level would 
strengthen the case for further gains. Firmer US consumer inflation 
remains a headwind, making a gradual recovery more plausible 
than a sharp reversal.  
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist 
—  Iris Yuen, Investment Strategist 
  Can the proposed Russia-Ukraine ‘energy ceasefire’ 
contain oil prices as Middle East risks rise? 
Our view: We expect escalating Middle East tensions to keep oil 
prices elevated. We maintain our three- and 12-month West 
Texas Intermediate (WTI) oil price forecasts at USD 100/bbl and 
USD 80/bbl, respectively, and continue to favour US Treasury 
Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) as a portfolio hedge 
against persistent inflation risks. 
Rationale: The proposed Russia-Ukraine ‘energy ceasefire’ still 
looks some way off. In our view, even if an agreement is reached, it 
would do little to ease the supply risks building in the Middle East. 
Gulf oil exports have recovered to above 80% of pre-conflict levels 
in recent weeks, helping moderate the pace of price increases. 
However, the recovery remains vulnerable to disruptions, particularly 
following the latest Houthi attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure. 
Diesel and jet fuel remain under the most pressure, with Gulf 
exports of these fuels in September at only around 60% of pre-
conflict levels. A sustained disruption to major Saudi production or 
export infrastructure remains the principal upside risk to oil prices. 
—  Anthony Naab, Investment Strategist
