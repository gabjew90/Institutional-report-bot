Thought of the day
US bond and equity markets have remained volatile over the past 24 
hours as investors weigh mixed signals on the outlook for AI alongside 
further evidence of persistent US inflation. TSMC offered a more cautious 
outlook, while Anthropic reported positive indications on revenue growth. 
Meanwhile, the producer price index in the US rose 1.3% in September, its 
largest monthly increase in four and a half years and well above the 0.5% 
consensus forecast. Although energy accounted for much of the rise, prices 
also increased across a broader range of goods and services, adding to 
concerns that inflation pressures remain in the pipeline. The data followed 
a higher-than-expected consumer price index in the US earlier in the week.
But US Treasury yields declined, with the 10-year yield trading at around 
5.09% and close to its lowest level in three weeks. Equity-market volatility 
increased risk aversion and supported demand for high-quality bonds. 
While further yield volatility cannot be ruled out amid elevated inflation, 
heavy debt supply, and persistent fiscal concerns, we continue to rate fixed 
income as Attractive.
Current market pricing of Federal Reserve tightening remains 
too hawkish. We now expect the Fed to raise interest rates by another 
50 basis points over the next 12 months, but this remains more dovish 
than the current market pricing of nearly 90 basis points more. Recent 
comments from several Fed officials support a more gradual tightening, 
with Vice Chair Philip Jefferson stating there is no immediate urgency to 
hike again, and New York Fed President John Williams saying he sees no 
need for further rate increases in 2027. San Francisco Fed President Mary 
Daly has also signaled little urgency in adjusting policy rates. This means 
that Treasury yields should decline from their current levels as markets scale 
back their expectations, leaving scope for capital gains in bonds.

Elevated starting yields offer an attractive income cushion. Elevated 
starting yields provide a stronger income base and have historically been a 
good indication of subsequent total returns. This income can also cushion 
portfolios against further increases in yields. Our analysis indicates that 
two-, five-, and 10-year US Treasury yields would need to rise by around 
257, 113, and 65 basis points, respectively, for capital losses to offset the 
income earned. We therefore believe investors can benefit from attractive 
portfolio income even if yields remain volatile.
Quality bonds offer diversification and stability in a portfolio.
The latest Treasury rally underscores how high-quality bonds can help 
stabilize portfolios by providing diversification when risk assets come 
under pressure. Our analysis of US stock-and-bond portfolios found that 
a conventional 60/40 portfolio had a maximum recovery period of 4.2 
years from bear market drawdowns since 1945, compared with 6.2 years 
for an all-equity portfolio. Medium- to long-duration quality bonds may 
also appreciate if tighter monetary policy slows growth or reduces longer-
term inflation expectations, helping to offset weakness elsewhere in a 
portfolio. This combination of income, potential price appreciation, and 
diversification can help smooth overall portfolio returns across different 
market environments.
So, fixed income should remain a key component in a well-diversified 
portfolio, and we see opportunities across regions and market segments. 
Investors should calibrate both credit risk and duration to their objective 
and investment horizons. More income-focused investors may focus on 
short-maturity bonds to reduce duration risk, while those who are willing 
to tolerate volatility may consider select tactical opportunities in medium- 
to long duration high-quality bonds. We remain cautious on the longest 
maturities given fiscal concerns and rising AI-related issuance.
Caught our attention
TSMC posts record earnings yet again. Taiwan Semiconductor 
Manufacturing Co. (TSMC) on Thursday reported a third-quarter net profit 
of TWD 763.1bn, up 68.7% from a year ago, and the sixth consecutive 
quarter of record earnings. The company added that revenue for the 
December quarter could rise as much as 61% year over year amid 
continued demand for AI chips. The results followed ASML’s earnings beat 
and an upbeat outlook, although chip stocks have been under pressure in 
recent days amid concerns over AI revenue growth.
Our view: Without commenting on single securities, the latest set of 
results reinforce our constructive view on AI fundamentals, as demand 
for compute and related infrastructure remains robust and cloud growth 
should accelerate further. That said, we see an increasing need for risk 
management amid the current macroeconomic environment. We thus favor 
diversified exposure across the AI value chain, with an emphasis on high-
quality semiconductor and hardware beneficiaries, mega-cap platforms, 
and defensive technology companies. Within Asia tech, we like companies 
with strong earnings visibility, including AI chip foundries, and the CPU 
supply chain.
Energy risks keep simmering. Ukraine struck a Russian refinery on 
Thursday after President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Kyiv was “done” 
supporting the ceasefire on energy targets announced by US President 
Donald Trump. The attack caused a fire at the Ryazan refinery, which 
accounts for nearly 5% of Russia’s refinery throughput. Risks to Persian 
Gulf energy infrastructure also remain elevated. Iran and its proxies

have carried out at least 135 drone and missile attacks on at least 84 
infrastructure sites since late February, according to a Reuters investigation. 
Targets included oil-routing systems at Fujairah, LNG facilities at Ras Laffan, 
and pumping stations on Saudi Arabia’s East-West Pipeline. Meanwhile, 
US crude inventories fell by 8.6 million barrels last week, far exceeding 
expectations for a 288,000-barrel decline, after a hurricane shut in as much 
as 71.5% of Gulf of Mexico oil output.
Our view: The breakdown of the proposed energy ceasefire adds to supply 
risks in an already-tight physical oil market. Iran’s demonstrated ability 
to damage critical equipment across Persian Gulf energy infrastructure 
compounds the threat from disrupted traffic through the Strait of Hormuz 
and the Red Sea. The sharp hurricane-related decline in US inventories 
illustrates how weather outages can amplify these geopolitical pressures 
when market buffers are limited. Emergency reserve releases may provide 
temporary relief, but they do not resolve the current imbalance and reduce 
the protection available against future disruptions. We recently raised our 
year-end Brent crude oil forecast to USD 100 a barrel. More broadly, tight 
supply and durable demand remain supportive of a commodity upcycle. 
We favor actively managed, diversified exposure across energy, precious 
metals, industrial metals, and agriculture as a potential source of returns 
and portfolio resilience.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
