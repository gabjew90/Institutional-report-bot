US banks’ 3Q results were strong, but messaging was unconvincing from the 
conference call with respect to the sustainability of the revenue dynamics 
related to AI super cycle growth as well as the impact from higher interest 
rates for longer. In addition, US banks’ strong B/ST growth is starting to be 
questioned, as we already discussed in our note (Global Investment Banks: Signs 
of late cycle IB balance sheet expansion: peeling the onion)with  focus on CIB 
B/ST expansion which we think should be valued at a lower PE multiple on 
earnings than capital-light CIB flow businesses. Hence we reaffirm our view, 
which we have also reflected through the year, as we have maintained a 
preference for European Banks over US as the valuation gap was too wide even 
after accounting for the superior franchise of US players in businesses such as IB. 
The weak price performance of US players, post what we see as a solid 
performance in 3Q, partly reflects a “normalization” of valuation multiples for 
what the market regards as peakish earnings with 2027E P/E de-rating of -9% YTD 
(see Table 2US Banks have de-rated YTD ) besides concerns around the impact of higher rates on activity levels 
and the wider economy. We think European Banks should be at higher valuations 
than US regionals (8.7x 2028E) rather than inline, which is the case today. 
What is next and what is the read-across for European Banks into 3Q? The 
operational performance in 3Q from US banks is a positive readacross on IB 
performance, especially in Equities, WM transactions and flows as well as Asset 
quality which remains benign although already assumed at normalized levels in 
2026-28E in our European Bank forecasts.  NII outlook from US banks for 2027 is 
positive and as discussed in, European Banks: Reconfirm OW, while the current 
macro environment may lead to bank managements baking in a degree of 
conservatism in their guidance, overall we continue to see potential for further 
small consensus EPS upgrades as Rates have moved higher. We expect valuation 
gaps to close further as European Banks are not operating at high PE multiples 
relative to some peers.  We also expect some outperformance in global businesses 
by Europeans such as DB vs. BofA in FICC, supporting valuation gap closures, and 
UBS in WM ex US, with UBS trading at significantly lower multiples. European 
banks are also operating with significant buffers to their CET1 ratios and with IB 
balance sheets generally constrained/capped, unlike in the US, we continue to 
expect payout ratio of c.75% p.a. to be well underpinned for 2026-28E. In addition, 
European Banks trade at YE26 P/TBV of 1.8x with a range of 0.9x-2.9x and hence 
buybacks remain an attractive instrument for most European Banks in our view. 
Finally, European Bank valuations of 8.7x P/E, 1.5x P/TBV for 18.6% RoTE in 
2028E implies 15% upside to an exit P/E of 10x, even assuming a higher CoE of 
10%. We maintain our OW on European Banks with a positive readacross for UBS 
into 3Q results on IB and WM. 
Our European Banks top picks portfolio is: UBS, DBK, ING, Natwest, STAN 
and Julius BAER.  We note US banks have highlighted several times their Asian 
franchise growth in 3Q as we witnessed in GS as well as MS - our portfolio is highly 
geared to Asia (especially HK WM growth) as we discussed in our 2H Outlook 
with this thematics reflected through STAN, UBS and BAER in our portfolio.

US IB performance has been solid: U.S. banks 3Q results have delivered very strong 
Equities Sales & Trading performance (+43% YoY, range +10% to +72%) with a more 
mixed picture in FICC (-4% YoY, range -17% to +4%) while IB fee performance (+8% 
YoY) has been solid as well. We also note that US bank management teams continue to 
broadly remain positive on the IB pipeline, but more importantly in the markets business 
which is the bigger driver of the Revenue wallet, geopolitical tensions continue to support 
activity levels which remain healthy and hence are revenue supportive, despite an 
expected seasonal slowdown in Q4, which is typically a “shorter”  quarter from an activity 
perspective and should not come as a surprise, especially after the stellar YTD 
performance with markets revenues +24% YoY for US players. We see this as a positive 
read-across for Equity geared players in Europe where we are forecasting YoY growth 
of 14% on average, with UBS most geared while in FICC the softer YoY progression is 
reflected in our forecasts with average decline of -5% YoY for European IBs in our 
coverage. The bigger question is around 2027 revenue outlook in Equities, where comps 
are going to be materially tougher post two stellar back to back years of 2025 and 2026 
so far. In our view, a lot depends on the ongoing AI refinancing and IPO pipeline, which 
now accounts for c.35% of 2026E ECM wallet and could be a driver. However, in our 
view, U.S. banks’ 3Q earnings calls overall did not give us a better understanding of the 
outlook for 2027at this point. 
•
NII Outlook remains healthy with WFC reiterating its +/- $50bn outlook for 2026 while 
Citi indicated 2026 NII ex-Markets up mid-to-high 6% range vs. up ~5-6% previously 
guided with 2Q results. JPMC increased its FY26 NII ex markets outlook to $97.2bn vs. 
$96.5bn provided with 2Q results. For FY27, WFC indicated the positive step-off point 
from 4Q 26 NII guidance of c.$13bn while JPMC indicated 2027 central case of NII 
excluding Markets of ~$107bn which implies c.10% YoY growth with JPMC indicating 
that the increase is driven by the impact of balance sheet growth and higher rates. BofA 
indicated >8% YoY growth in 2027 NII, while reiterating the 8% YoY growth expectation 
for 2026.
•
Credit outlook remains generally benign with no bank indicating a broad-based 
deterioration in asset quality, which is also reflected in the charge-offs and reserve build 
data which remain at relatively benign levels (Table 4US Banks: modest reserve build during the quarter, overal aset quality stil benign). Citi improved its 2026 net charge-
offs outlook (credit losses) for US Cards to ~4.0% (from 4.0-4.5%) while JPMC 
reiterated its 2026 Card Services NCO rate outlook of ~3.2%. We see this as a positive 
read-across for Barclays’ US card business.
•
U.S. banks continued to deploy balance sheet during 3Q as well, albeit at a slower 
pace, with combined Assets (GS, MS, Citi, JPMC, BofA and WFC) so far growing by 
c.$100bn or 1% during 3Q, bringing YTD growth to $1.7tn or 11%. As we have discussed 
previously (Global Investment Banks: Signs of late cycle IB balance sheet expansion: 
peeling the onion), we keep an eye on the significant growth in CIB division balance 
sheets of U.S. banks, partly driven by deregulation in the US and question if this will at 
one point start to eat into margins and/or lead to irrational behaviour by US players in 
particular.
•
CET1 ratios generally declined sequentially during the quarter (see Table 6CET1 ratios declined q/q, led by AOCI loses ), with 
money center banks also reflecting the impact from AOCI as bond yields moved higher. 
RWAs continued to grow as well with average 1% growth QoQ and 8% YoY while 
Group loan books grew 4% QoQ on average with the IBs GS and MS showing 6% growth 
QoQ. CIB loans increased +3% QoQ on average for the banks which provide disclosure. 
•
Capital returns during 3Q generally showed lower buybacks (see Table 7US Banks - Buybacks have started to moderate after strong growth, reflecting capital drawdown and deployment oportunities), which in 
our view reflects the balance sheet growth and CET1 drawdowns. Standardised 
CET1 ratio buffer to minimum requirements declined during the quarter and now ranges 
from 90bps (BofA) to 310bps (MS). Overall, as we have argued before, at current 
valuations, capital deployment in the business at attractive returns is of more value than 
share buybacks for US banks.
