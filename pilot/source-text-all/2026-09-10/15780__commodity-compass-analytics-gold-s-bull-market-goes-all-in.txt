Gold's Bull Market Goes All-In: ETFs, Futures and Options Align on 
 
◼ A synchronised bull market across every channel of demand. Gold has entered a 
new phase of its 2026 bull run, one defined less by speculative momentum and more 
by broad-based, structural conviction across every category of market participant. 
What began as a geopolitical shock, evolved over the following months into something 
far more durable: a synchronised build-up of physical, futures, and options exposure 
that now spans retail investors, professional money managers, and derivatives traders 
alike. The scale of this positioning is not incidental. August alone saw gold ETFs record 
their third-largest monthly inflow in tonnage terms since data collection began, money 
managers push net futures exposure to its second-highest level on record, and options 
markets reveal a strikingly asymmetric structure in which downside insurance is being 
priced close to spot while upside conviction stretches thousands of dollars beyond it. 
Taken together, these signals describe a market that is bullish on gold across all fronts, 
not because of a single catalyst, but because multiple independent channels of 
demand are reinforcing one another simultaneously. 
 
◼ ETFs flows. The physical ETF market illustrates how sentiment shifted from a reactive, 
geopolitics-driven spike into something more sustained. Gold's initial 2.6% gain at the 
end of February, triggered by fears over the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb 
Strait, proved short-lived as a stronger dollar and rising real yields drove a correction 
and 124 tonnes of cumulative ETF outflows between April and July. Yet rather than 
signalling fading interest, this pullback set the stage for a much larger wave of buying: 
August's 201-tonne net inflow ranks behind only February 2009 (the Obama stimulus 
package) and March 2020 (the global Covid lockdown), surpassing even the inflows 
seen after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine and the Fed's 2012 QE3 announcement. 
Since roughly 98% of gold ETFs are physically backed, with bullion transacted at spot 
to match daily flows, this scale of buying offers a direct, unambiguous read on retail 
and institutional appetite for physical gold exposure. 
 
◼ Futures positioning. Money managers moved in lockstep, with net futures length 
rising 21% over August, from 119k to 145k contracts, an 11-month high driven almost 
entirely by fresh buying rather than short covering. Notional exposure grew by around 
$15bn (31%) to a $65bn peak, the second-highest on record behind only January 2026's 
all-time-high price surge, and equivalent to roughly 77 tonnes of net gold added in 
futures-equivalent terms. A modest pullback in the final week, longs trimmed 6.3% 
with shorts largely unchanged, looks like early profit-taking rather than a reversal. 
 
◼ Options markets. Call open interest is concentrated at increasingly distant strikes, led 
by $5,000/oz (roughly 14.5% above the forward), while the most active put strike sits 
just 1.5% below it. This asymmetry, upside bets placed far out-of-the-money against 
downside protection bought close to spot, reflects a market that expects further gains 
but wants to insure against short-term corrections. Put demand is heavily front-loaded 
into the September expiry, while call buying is spread more evenly through February 
2027, consistent with a constructive medium-term outlook overlaid on tactical near-
term hedging.

Gold ETFs record the third-largest monthly increase in tonnage 
Since the start of the US-Israeli military operation against Iran and Tehran's subsequent 
response, investors have been forced to reassess the vulnerability of global trade and energy 
supply chains. The conflict renewed concerns over the Strait of Hormuz, a critical artery for global 
oil flows, while also raising the prospect of broader disruptions extending to the Bab el-Mandeb 
Strait. Fears that these chokepoints could remain impaired for longer than initially expected 
triggered a sharp increase in geopolitical risk premia across financial markets and prompted a 
renewed shift toward gold's traditional role as a safe-haven asset and store of value during 
periods of geopolitical stress, marking a departure from the more speculative and momentum-
driven dynamics that characterised much of the 2025 rally. 
The catalyst emerged at the end of February, when military operations commenced. Gold initially 
responded as expected to the surge in geopolitical uncertainty, gaining 2.6% as investors sought 
protection against escalating geopolitical and energy-related risks. Although the rally proved 
short-lived, with a stronger US dollar and rising real yields subsequently triggering a sharp 
correction, the episode marked a turning point in investor behaviour. Gold-backed ETFs recorded 
63 tonnes of net inflows in March as investors reacted to escalating tensions in the Middle East 
and growing concerns over critical maritime trade routes. 
This was followed by a period of profit-taking, with cumulative outflows of 124 tonnes between 
April and July, driven by a stronger US dollar, rising real yields, and expectations of de-escalation. 
Nevertheless, the correction ultimately attracted renewed buying interest. 
In August, gold ETFs registered a substantial 201 tonnes of net inflows, marking the third-largest 
monthly addition on record in tonnage terms after now famous world events: February 2009 and 
the stimulus package announced by the newly inaugurated Obama administration, and March 
2020 with the start of the lockdown for Covid globally. This month's inflow surpassed the strong 
inflows recorded in March 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine and in September 2012 
after the Federal Reserve's announcement of QE3.

Money managers ramp up gold exposure to near-record highs 
Money managers turned decisively more bullish on gold over August, building net length from 
119k contracts on 28 July to an 11-month high of 145k by 25 August, a 21% increase driven almost 
entirely by fresh buying rather than short covering. Gross longs climbed steadily and consistently, 
adding roughly 24k contracts (+18%) across the five weeks to reach 160k, the largest speculative 
monthly build since January 2025. On the short side, positioning stayed within a tight 13,000-
15,000 contract range through the month, close to the lowest levels seen over the past year 
according to our OBOS model. 
In notional exposure terms (contracts x price x contract size), money managers' net positioning 
reached the second-largest long exposure on record, behind only January 2026, when gold broke 
through $5,400/oz to an all-time high. This time, with prices roughly $1,000/oz lower, the scale of 
the dollar exposure is even more striking: it is no longer simply a price story. Positioning is now 
more aggressively bullish than it was even during the momentum-driven surge of January.

Professional asset managers’ notional exposure increasing by approximately $15 billion, or 31%, 
in August, while spot prices advanced 9% over the same period. Net position exposure reached a 
peak of $65bn in the final week of the month, the record is 68$bn. The underlying contract data 
paints an even more constructive picture than the notional figures alone suggest. Rather than 
reflecting a rushed, fear-of-missing-out reaction to rising prices, the build-up in positioning has 
been gradual and orderly. In tonnage terms, converting futures-equivalent positions, money 
managers added approximately 77 tonnes of net gold exposure over the month. 
Last week, however, marked a notable shift: longs were trimmed by roughly 10,100 contracts (-
6.3%) to 149,721, while shorts also eased slightly, leaving net length pulling back to 136,771, an 
8,000-contract retreat from the late-August peak. This suggests the first signs of profit-taking or 
reduced conviction after gold's strong run, rather than an outright bearish reversal, since shorts 
did not rise materially alongside the reduction in longs. 
Having examined how ETFs have accumulated physical exposure and how money managers have 
built up their futures positioning, we now turn to the options market to see whether this bullish 
conviction is being reinforced or tempered through derivatives.

What does the option market tell us?  
We use our options landscape framework to analyse positioning across the gold options chain 
and identify directional biases embedded in market expectations, comparing snapshots between 
17 August and 2 September. 
The largest increase in call open interest is concentrated around the forward price, particularly 
in the October and November expiries, indicating that investors are primarily adding exposure 
through near-the-money strikes. Put open interest is more dispersed, with significant positioning 
visible at strikes as low as $2,000/oz across the November and December maturities. This deep 
out-of-the-money put activity likely reflects protective hedging by long-underlying investors 
rather than outright bearish views, given the low premium cost of such far-dated, far-OTM 
insurance.

Isolating the most targeted strikes across all maturities sharpens the picture. On the call side, the

below the forward. This asymmetry is notable: investors are buying downside protection close to 
spot while concentrating upside bets much further out-of-the-money, a structure consistent with 
a fundamentally bullish gold market that is nonetheless paying up for near-term downside 
insurance.

Conclusion 
Across positioning, flow, and derivatives data, every signal we track continues to point in the 
same direction: the market is bullish on gold across all fronts. Options positioning confirms this, 
with call open interest accumulating at increasingly distant upside strikes even as near-term put 
buying reflects tactical hedging rather than a change in directional conviction. 
We are now awaiting August central bank data to confirm whether official-sector demand, a key 
structural pillar of this bull market, remains as robust as in prior months. Any acceleration (or 
slowdown) in central bank buying will be an important input in gauging the durability of the 
current trend. 
Looking ahead, a future paper will incorporate Tether's gold exposure into our analysis, allowing 
us to build a more complete picture of demand across both traditional and crypto-adjacent 
channels. Until then, the weight of evidence across positioning, flows, and options markets 
continues to support a constructive stance on gold.

interest. The height of each column represents the current construction ratio (CR) – the absolute change in aggregate open interest 
expressed as percentage of total volume traded during the week. A low CR could be a sign of heightened intraday trading activity. To

Commodity Outlook 
Stuck in stalemate 
Oil. Prices remain elevated in the short to medium term following the Hormuz shock, 
although in a June reopening scenario, prices ease back towards $80/bbl. Across our 
scenario analysis –ranging from a reopening in June through to one at year-end– 
prices span a wide band, from roughly $80/bbl to $180/bbl. In all cases, inventories 
take many months, if not years, to normalise, implying that price volatility and 
periodic spikes are likely to persist until stocks are rebuilt. 
Gold. Prices remain rangebound on muted ETF flows, and a pause in central bank 
purchases. Falling real yields are one of the strongest supports for gold and we 
believe that a re-opening of the Strait of Hormuz will be a catalyst for a resurgence 
in central bank buying. We forecast fold at $4,500/oz by the end of 2026 and 
$5,500/oz by the end of 2027. 
Copper. Prices should remain relatively capped in the near term, but we expect 
them to trend higher toward our 4Q26 target of $14,000/t as underlying 
fundamentals tighten. The key inflection point lies in China, where demand is set to 
re-accelerate as disruptions in the invoice system normalise, restoring financing 
flows and enabling inventory rebuilding alongside stronger physical consumption. 
At the same time, supply constraints look set to intensify.
