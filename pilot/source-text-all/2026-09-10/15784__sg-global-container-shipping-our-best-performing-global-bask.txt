SG Global Container Shipping: our best-performing global basket YTD 
 
In February 2024, following the Red Sea attacks, we launched the SG Global Container Shipping 
basket (SGGCSHIP) to hedge against the risk of a prolonged disruption to global trade (see Assessing 
the impact of the Red Sea attacks). Since then, the basket has surged 151%, more than twice the 64% 
gain recorded by the MSCI ACWI, despite having no exposure to AI, commodities or financials. We 
revisited the investment case in March 2026, after Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthis threatened to resume 
maritime strikes should the conflict involving Iran widen (link). A renewed rise in global container 
freight rates, combined with recent earnings outlook upgrades across the shipping industry, has driven 
a 50% gain year-to-date, making SG Global Container Shipping the best-performing basket in our 
global thematic universe. Notably, performance accelerated over the summer, with the basket rising 
33% since the end of June. The basket is now trading at a 12m forward P/E ratio of 14x versus 17x for 
the MSCI AC World. In this report, we review and rebalance the basket’s composition. 
Since early May, the WCI Composite Container Freight Benchmark has almost doubled, returning 
to levels not seen since the summer of 2024. While security risks in the Red Sea remain a key driver, the 
rebound in freight rates reflects a broader combination of factors, including resilient trade volumes, 
tariff-related front-loading, persistent port congestion and disciplined capacity management by 
shipping lines. Although some carriers have gradually resumed Suez Canal transits, geopolitical 
uncertainty and vessel rerouting continue to constrain effective supply, keeping freight markets 
significantly tighter than historical norms. 
We continue to view the basket as a hedge against risks of a more fragmented global trading 
environment. Whether the catalyst is Red Sea disruption, tariff-driven front-loading, supply-chain 
reconfiguration or renewed constraints at the Panama Canal, the result is the same: tighter shipping 
capacity and steeper freight rates. The main risk remains a sharp deterioration in global trade volumes 
(in the event of an economic slowdown, for example); but also, over the medium term, an increase in 
vessel deliveries which could normalise the freight markets and weigh on industry pricing power.

Composition 
Methodology. The basket includes companies with direct exposure to container shipping, either 
as liner operators or owners and lessors of container ships. We exclude tanker, dry-bulk and car-
carrier companies, as well as equipment manufacturers whose exposure is limited to producing 
physical containers. The basket is equally weighted at inception and on each rebalancing date. 
Rebalancing. At this rebalancing, Hapag-Lloyd and D/S Norden are removed as they no longer 
meet our liquidity requirements. Costamare and Global Ship Lease are added, as both companies 
now meet our market-capitalisation threshold. The total number of constituents remains 
unchanged at 16.
