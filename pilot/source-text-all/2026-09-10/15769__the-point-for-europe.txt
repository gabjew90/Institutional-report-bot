Western Europe Insurance - Reinsurers - Feedback from Monte Carlo
We met with 20 companies, executives and brokers over the course of two days. 
Nat Cat rates are clearly under pressure, and this is exacerbated by a benign 
hurricane season, with technical pricing likely to hit floors if projections of a 10-
15% decline at 1/1 hold. Our fear is they could be worse and discipline starts fade 
more quickly than expected. Brokers estimates could yet prove light, much like last 
year. Program structures were a key focus, with some reinvestment of savings. 
T&Cs appear mostly resilient but with inflation and frequency topical. Dominating 
our meetings, beyond further casualty deterioration, was new capital sources 
emerging that raise questions around the potential disintermediation of 
reinsurers. Separately, despite the focus on Property Cat rates, it is important to 
remember that the European insurers are very well diversified, with Hannover Re 
indicating low single-digit reductions expected for 2027 across its whole portfolio.
James A Shuck

Global Macro Strategy – Correlation Corner - #9: Looking for US-Iran hedges
Scanning across realised correlations over the past six months, we find that cross-
asset correlations have yet to normalise to pre US-Iran levels as the oil shock 
remains stickier than initially expected. In contrast, we see European equity/oil 
and equity/yield implied correlations have started to price a meaningful de-
escalation scenario. While we remain constructive on risk particularly in US 
equities as a bullish AI trade, we think current correlation pricing offers an 
attractive opportunity to add protection against a more prolonged Middle East 
conflict. Therefore, we think dual digitals combining lower European equities with 
either higher oil prices or higher yields look relatively cheap and could perform well 
if energy prices remain elevated and pressure subsequently persists across both 
equities and bonds.
Vinh Vo | Adam Pickett | Giammarco Miani | Dirk Willer | Alice Zheng

Metal Matters - China end-use demand showing signs of stabilisation, not yet 
recovery
In this note, we update our China Aluminium End-use Tracker (CAET) and assess 
aluminium demand through a broader China macro lens. Aluminium prices have 
traded broadly sideways around our 0-3mth price target of $3,300/t in recent 
weeks, supported by ongoing inventory drawdowns despite weak demand 
expectations and uncertainty around Middle East aluminium supply. With 
inventory levels already low, aluminium prices are becoming increasingly sensitive 
to any shift towards a more dovish Fed backdrop or lower real rates. China and 
global cyclical demand continue to struggle, although recent China macro 
indicators point to stabilisation rather than recovery. Our CAET remains weak (YTD 
-0.2% y/y, although July improved to +1.3% y/y), reflecting softness across 
construction and broader cyclical sectors. Decarbonisation demand has 
rebounded in recent months, helping offset continued weakness across traditional 
cyclical sectors rather than signalling a broad-based recovery in aluminium 
demand. 
Wenyu Yao | Shreyas Madabushi | Maximilian J Layton | Tom Mulqueen

Guide to Citi Research Fundamental Research Investment Ratings:
Citi Research stock recommendations include an investment rating and an optional risk rating to highlight high risk stocks.

expectations of expected total return ("ETR") and risk. ETR is the sum of the forecast price appreciation (or depreciation) plus 
the dividend yield for a stock within the next 12 months.  The target price is based on a 12 month time horizon. The Investment 
rating definitions are: Buy (1) ETR of 15% or more or 25% or more for High risk stocks; and Sell (3) for negative ETR. Any covered 
stock not assigned a Buy or a Sell is a Neutral (2). For stocks rated Neutral (2), if an analyst believes that there are insufficient
