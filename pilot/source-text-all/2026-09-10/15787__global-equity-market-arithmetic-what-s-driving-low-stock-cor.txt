Global Equity Market Arithmetic 
What’s driving low stock correlation in the US & Europe is not the same 
 
One of the more curious features of today’s highly concentrated equity markets is the 
exceptionally low level of stock correlations and the growing number of negatively correlated 
stock pairs. At first glance, this appears inconsistent with narrow market leadership. Yet it 
suggests that a handful of powerful themes are pulling stocks in opposite directions, 
suppressing correlation and index volatility, even as single-stock volatility remains elevated. 
Evidence that this is a thematic rather than a market structure phenomenon comes from the 
broad-based nature of the trend. Low correlations are evident across multiple regions  and 
across different market segments, such as intra-sector and intra-factor groupings. The most 
obvious drivers are AI and the ongoing impact of geopolitical tensions on the energy complex, 
with many of the most negatively correlated stock pairs aligned with these narratives. 
However, there are pronounced regional differences. The contrast between the US and Europe 
is particularly striking. In the US, AI, data centres and, to some extent, reshoring dominate the 
landscape. Some of the most negatively correlated pairs consist of Human Resource & 
Employment Services vs Semiconductor Materials & Equipment stocks! In Europe, by contrast, 
negatively correlated pairs are overwhelmingly concentrated around the oil and energy theme.
