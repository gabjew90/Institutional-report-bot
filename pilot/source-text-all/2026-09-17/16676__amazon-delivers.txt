powered products for residential, light commercial,
industrial and commercial end markets.

Amazon delivers
Investment Recommendation
"In late June, we officially signed our second hyperscale supply agreement, and we
are now in the final stages of negotiations with this customer on terms, conditions,
volumes, and timelines for 2027 and 2028 deliveries." - Aaron Jagdfeld, CEO - 2Q26
earnings call
And today was the official announcement.
Generac has locked in a landmark, multi-year supply agreement to power Amazon's
expanding data center infrastructure, unlocking up to $8B in total potential order
volume with initial deliveries projected at $2.4 billion across 2027 and 2028 (we
believe heavily anchored by firm backlog in those 2 years). Under this strategic
arrangement, Generac will supply custom backup power generators tailored
specifically to Amazon's demands, while issuing an Amazon subsidiary a warrant to
purchase up to 1,693,745 shares of common stock at an exercise price of $200.9266
per share.
Management’s Amazon announcement serves as a strong endorsement of Generac’s
ability to establish a competitive foothold in this nascent subsegment. To capture this
“generational” wave, management is sprinting at breakneck speed to triple capacity
by 2027.
Emerging pushback from skeptics is obvious: what if the AI infrastructure boom
falters? Having highlighted those exact risks for over 2 years in our work, we respect
the concern. Quite a bit.
However, we find it encouraging that the substantial portion of 2027 and 2028
deliveries are secured by what we believe is firm backlog.
In our view, Generac is taking a deliberate, long-term approach to hyperscaler
demand—balancing ambition with prudence. While many may rush blindly toward
the next bright shininess, Generac is building with intention—buying land rather
than leasing it, keeping its capital expenditures disciplined (albeit rising) in the
modest mid-single digits. Capacity is expanding fast at Generac, but we estimate
management has maintained flexibility.
The fundamentals driving the long-term residential segment growth are real. Generac
sees it in the living rooms of America: even in a quiet storm season, families are
calling (see IHC trends from 2Q26). The long-term truth is simple, in our opinion. The
weather isn't getting gentler, and the grid isn't getting stronger. That builds a very
real, very durable wind at this market's back.
Should severe weather return, Generac’s residential rebound could collide with an
accelerating data center surge—creating a formidable one-two punch. Watch out.
We reiterate our BUY rating and raise our price target to $375 from $275 based on
~22x (from ~20x) our 2028E non-GAAP EPS estimate of $17.05 (from $13.54). The
median 2028E EPS multiple in Generac’s comp group is currently ~19x. We feel that
this slight premium to the comps is justified based on Generac's growing backlog and
long-term growth potential.

Raising Target Price
The Amazon news. Generac entered into a long-term supply agreement to provide
backup power generators specifically tailored for Amazon’s data center infrastructure.
The arrangement is projected to drive substantial multi-billion-dollar order volumes
• Initial deliveries of backup generators are expected to total $2.4B in 2027 and 2028.
We believe a material portion of these 2027/2028 shipments represent firm backlog.
• Multiyear of up to a total of $8B.
Generac's data center backlog. As of Generac’s 2Q26 earnings call, the company’s
data center backlog stood at ~$1.6B, up from ~$700M as of 4/29/26, and up from >
$400M as of 2/11/26.
• 2026: Generac expects to recognize ~$450M in data center revenue for 2026.
• 2027: As of the 2Q26 call, is scheduled for deliveries in 2027. For reference,
according to Mr. Jagdfeld on the 2Q26 call, "$250M is going to ship in the second half
out of that $1.6B backlog".
• We expect, with the Amazon announcement, data center revenues to exceed
$2B in 2027.
• 2028: We expect revenue in the data center segment to exceed $3B.
• How much revenue per data center? We estimate that every 1 GW of data center
capacity represents a $600-$800M revenue opportunity for Generac. This projection
assumes a 1.2x generator redundancy requirement relative to total power capacity.
Under this framework, Generac would deploy four hundred 3.0 MW units, valued at
$1.5-$2.0M per unit.
How about capacity? Management expects to triple data center related revenue
capacity over the next 12 months to ~$4B. Some management snippets from lat
quarter's call:
• "Today, we're growing capacity. And obviously, we're talking about tripling our
capacity from the $1.25 billion to something closer to approaching $4 billion in the
next year, year-and-a-half."
• "As a result, we believe we now have a path over the next 12 months to triple our
production capacity from our original year-end 2026 target of $1.25 billion."
• "Those also require additional components. All of those areas we have second and
third sources identified, and we're in deep negotiations with those for supply starting
in 2027. So at this stage of the game, we feel like we have a roadmap both for our
own internal consumption, our capacity, that tripling of capacity internally, as well as
with our supply chain."
• "Our engine suppliers, they have indicated to us that capacity is not an issue."
Some details on the Amazon warrants:
• Warrant Issuance. As part of the transaction, Generac issued an Amazon
subsidiary a warrant to purchase up to 1,693,745 shares of Generac common stock
at an exercise price of $200.9266 per share.
• Vesting Structure.
• An initial tranche of 307,954 warrant shares vested immediately upon issuance.
• The remaining shares vest incrementally in staged tranches tied directly to
aggregate gross revenue/payments received by Generac from Amazon and its
affiliates for generator purchases, scaling up to the $8 billion threshold.
• Expiration. The warrant can be exercised through September 16, 2033, via cash or
cashless exercise, tying a significant equity stake for Amazon directly to its ongoing
purchasing volume with Generac.
