Americas Pipelines and MLPs: Permian Gas Processing Outlook: Plenty 
of Growth, Own TRGP for Further Share Gains and KNTK for M&A
10 September 2026 | 4:02PM EDT
We introduce our Permian Basin natural gas processing capacity model, 
supporting our view that: 1) Targa should continue to grow its already-leading 
market share, and 2) Kinetik looks increasingly attractive as a M&A target. In this 
note we: 1) discuss our view of significant Permian natural gas production growth, 
where we see a ~6% volume CAGR (wet gas) 2025-32; 2) outline required gas 
processing additions - with at least ~11.5 bcf/d needed and ~14 bcf/d likely to be 
added, with ~7 bcf/d already announced/under construction; 3) note market share 
across the various gathering and processing companies (G&Ps); and 4) break down 
our views of who will gain or lose share going forward. Overall, we expect midstream 
capex levels in the Permian will need to remain high, but we do not expect 
processing capacity to constrain overall supply growth; periods of tightness are more 
likely driven by gas pipeline capacity (2H27-early 2028, and again 1H29 before 
Solitude and DSW come online), gas compression (potentially YE27+), and water 
disposal (Northern Delaware later this decade). From a company perspective, we see 
Targa (TRGP, Buy) as most likely to gain further processing share in the basin on the 
back of continued organic wins (most notably their recent agreement with XOM), 
moving to ~27% by 2032 on GSe from an already-leading ~23% currently. Otherwise, 
we expect the balance of our coverage to largely maintain their relative shares as 
new large organic packages appear more limited, while smaller-scale privates and/or 
E&P operators are more likely to lose share over time as the value of downstream 
NGL integration becomes more critical.  Given their existing NGL capacity and large 
incumbent G&P footprints, we acknowledge room for Energy Transfer (ET, Neutral) 
and Enterprise (EPD, Neutral) to potentially outperform our forecasts, while others 
may need larger downstream footprints (MPLX) and/or have to accept lower margins 
to gain significant share (OKE). In this context, we expect M&A to continue (like OKE’s 
recently-proposed acquisition of Brazos Midland), with Kinetik (KNTK, Buy) in our 
coverage to be the most likely potential takeout target - see within.

This forecast is underpinned by our forecast of ~1% oil growth through 2032 
(2025-32 CAGR), with production plateauing closer towards the end of this decade. 
We acknowledge this could prove conservative as Exxon continues to execute on its

Therefore, to handle this wet gas production growth, we believe the basin needs to 
add at least ~11.5 bcf/d of processing capacity by 2032 - with levels closer to ~14 
bcf/d more likely to maintain current utilization rates. We estimate ~36.3 bcf/d of 
processing capacity in the basin today.  Given our basin growth forecast of +13-14 bcf/d 
from here, we estimate ~11.5 bcf/d of capacity is needed by 2032 to keep utilization 
below 90%. We estimate ~7.2 bcf/d of announced processing capacity currently under 
construction, and therefore at least ~4.3 bcf/d of incremental announcements will be 
required.  That said, given the wide geographic dispersion of capacity, the potential for 
changes in E&P drilling plans, preference from some E&Ps to underwrite redundant 
capacity, and high ongoing levels of competition across the G&Ps - we expect current 
utilization levels could be maintained (2Q26 at ~80%, which is -2% vs. the past 4 year

additions by 2032, meaning ~7.1 bcf/d of to-be-announced capacity on top of the ~7.2 
bcf/d already formally announced.

We do not expect processing capacity to constrain basin growth. Near term 
utilization should reach ~85% through YE2027 before a wave of new 2028 plant 
additions come online. As these new assets gradually fill, utilization is expected to 
re-stabilize around ~85% by 2032. The short-lead time on building (~1.5 years) and 
short-term ability to run high levels of utilization (>100%) leave us confident that 
processing shouldn’t be a constraint on the basin overall - particularly compared to 
other parts of the midstream value chain like long haul gas pipelines, gas compression, 
and produced water disposal. Last, we note capital requirements are meaningful but 
manageable. On our estimates ($1.1-1.5m per mcf of capacity), the ~14 bcf/d of new 
capacity could cost $15.4-21.0b over the period, or ~$2.2-3.0b annually.  
To-be-announced capacity is roughly half of this, as noted above, and we believe the 
group can handle the incremental spend; we note our relevant coverage (EPD, ET, KNTK, 
MPLX, OKE, TRGP) has ~$19b in FCF after growth capex on our 2027 estimates. 
We note sour gas needs could shift our view, requiring incremental capital and time 
for project completion. As the Permian matures, rising concentrations of “sour” gas 
(high H2S and CO2 levels) require midstream operators to treat the product for harmful 
and corrosive elements into a “sweet” or more pure gas stream. Standard processing 
plants cannot handle concentration of these acid gases in the gas stream without 
specialized equipment (amine treating units, integrated treating capabilities, AGI wells, 
etc.) We note EPD, TRGP and KNTK have more recently highlighted and continue to 
invest in their sour gas capabilities. This is evident by several recent transactions: MPLX’s 
acquisition of Northwind Midstream, which expands its Delaware Basin G&P business

Processing Market Share Outlook 
 
 
While top midstream players now have significant market share (TRGP highest at 
23%), we examine which companies could gain/lose share - as well as opportunities 
for further basin consolidation. Current market share is dominated by public pure-play 
midstream operators, representing more than 75% of total capacity. TRGP, EPD, and ET 
own the most capacity in the basin. Large refiners, E&Ps, and smaller private midstream 
companies make up the residual, ~15% of total market share as of 2Q26. Overall, the 
largest 8 operators own ~80% of basin capacity.  
 
Corporate and asset level M&A has shifted market share over time, though organic 
additions have helped all operators. Public share of capacity has significantly 
increased from 73% in 2019 to ~90% in 2Q26 - we have seen ET, TRGP, EPD, and OKE 
increase their share significantly through M&A (ET and WTG, EPD and Navitas, TRGP and 
Lucid, OKE and ENLC - among others). For TRGP specifically, post its transformative 
M&A in 2022 of Lucid Energy, the company has largely focused on gaining market share 
through organic growth. For OKE, the company first established its own Permian G&P 
footprint through its ENLC acquisitions and continues to focus on organic and inorganic 
opportunities to increase its Permian market share (as highlighted by its agreement to 
acquire Brazos - a private Midland G&P company). PSX and MPLX have largely 
maintained their respective share (particularly following PSX’s fold-in of DCP, which had 
been steadily losing share) while privates and E&Ps have lost share - highlighting a 
reduction in in-sourcing of midstream operations over time. We note KNTK has also lost 
modest share over time, despite M&A over the last two years given its smaller size and

Within our current model, we assume TRGP captures the majority of Permian basin 
gas growth. We estimate TRGP’s capacity additions represent ~39% of growth in the 
basin while ET, EPD, KNTK, and OKE’s capacity additions capture 15%/14%/8%/7% of

plants into 2027+ as of 2Q26 though we expect its processing capacity market share to 
be largely maintained by 2032. Elsewhere, we assume modest unannounced projects for 
OKE, EPD, MPLX, KNTK, and other publics outside our coverage. 
 
Tracking Capacity to Volumes 
 
 
Broad basin capacity gains do not automatically translate into proportional volume 
capture; realized market share depends on utilization efficiency and asset 
positioning. While our capacity framework tracks midstream operators’ Permian 
expansion and basin market share trends, capacity share additions alone do not 
guarantee full volume capture. Across the peer group, realized volume market share is 
based on plant utilization profiles and operating leverage. We project TRGP, OKE, and 
KNTK to grow their share of Permian inlet volumes faster than their capacity additions—
TRGP via superior plant fill rates, OKE by leaning into existing operating leverage, and 
KNTK as historical Waha-related producer curtailments ease in 2H26. In contrast, MPLX 
and EPD’s volume share should grow somewhat in line with its capacity expansion share 
growth, while ET’s volume share is expected to contract as steady system utilization lags

ET 
We are Neutral rated with a 12-month price target of $22 based on an unchanged

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
