Macy’s Inc. (M): 2Q26 First Take: Strong 2Q comp results and FY 
guidance raise; tariff refunds reinvested
10 September 2026 | 6:00AM MDT
M reported adj. F2Q26 EPS of $0.63, above GS/FactSet consensus at $0.31/$0.37, 
though reported figures included the benefit from tariff refunds net of reinvestment. 
Reported comps at +2.7% and retail sales of $4.866bn were above GS/consensus at 
+0.8%/+1.0% and $4.777bn/$4.805bn, respectively. Gross margin expanded 
~180bps Y/Y to 41.5% though included the tariff refund benefit. After adjusting for 
the tariff refund benefit, gross margin at 39.7% compared to GS/consensus at 
39.9%/39.6%. Adj. SG&A as % of total sales came in at 38.7%, well controlled vs. 
GS/consensus at 39.6%/39.1%.  
On the forward outlook, M raised FY26 EPS guidance to $2.15-$2.35 (vs. 
$2.00-$2.20 prior), with $0.05 of the increase due to tariff refund benefits net of 
reinvestment flowing through. This compares to GS/consensus at $2.19/$2.24. 
Comps (owned + licensed + marketplace) are now guided at 1.0% to 1.5% (vs. +0.5% 
to +1.2% prior), compared to GS/consensus at +1.1%, and net sales are now guided 
between $21.675bn to $21.825bn (vs. $21.5bn-$21.75bn prior), which compares to 
GS/consensus at $21.713bn/$21.716bn. For 3Q, M is guiding EPS at -$0.23 to -$0.19 
vs. GS/consensus at $0.02/-$0.06, though -$0.18 of the headwind is due to tariff 
refund reinvestments. Net sales are guided at $4.65bn-$4.70bn vs. GS/consensus at 
$4.684bn/$4.654bn, and comps are expected at -0.5% to +0.5% vs. GS/consensus 
at +0.3%/0.0%.  
Upon first glance, we are encouraged by the healthy comp delivery for Macy’s Inc 
and the underlying guidance raise for the total business. Bloomingdale’s comps at 
+11.3% sequentially accelerated despite tougher comparisons. That said, 
momentum modestly slowed at the Macy’s banner to +1.1% (vs. +1.6% in F1Q), as 
did trends at the Reimagine 200 stores (+1.9% vs. +2.4% F1Q). After adjusting for the 
tariff refund net of reinvestment, gross margin rate was largely in line with consensus, 
and SG&A was well-controlled. That said, we look for further detail on the 
application of reinvestment and the associated sales uplift from these initiatives, 
including management’s plan to cycle these refunds next year and the opportunity 
for net profit improvement on a multi-year basis on today’s conference call.  
Other topics in focus today include: (1) Drivers of the comp momentum by banner, 
including traffic vs. ticket, category performance, and the cadence of sales through 
the quarter; (2) Drivers and sustainability of Bloomingdale’s double-digit comp 
growth, including the contribution from market-share shifts; (3) Puts and takes to 
the gross margin delivery in the quarter including management’s view on gross profit

Valuation and Key Risks 
We are Neutral rated on M. Our 12-month price target of $21 is based on a 3.5x Q5-Q8 
EV/EBITDA and includes a $1 per share contribution value from M’s real estate. Upside 
risks include: (1) Faster-than-expected traction in ongoing initiatives and broader fleet 
optimization efforts; (2) A more resilient macro backdrop leading to 
stronger-than-expected discretionary spending; (3) A greater degree of insulation than 
expected from geopolitical and tariff risks. Downside risks include: (1) FCF Degradation 
caused by inventory imbalances or margin compression; (2) New strategic initiative 
underperformance on weaker execution; (3) A change in the regulatory landscape 
regarding late fees could harm credit contribution to profits.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
