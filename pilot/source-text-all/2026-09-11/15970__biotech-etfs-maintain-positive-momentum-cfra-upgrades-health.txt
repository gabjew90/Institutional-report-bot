Biotech ETFs Maintain Positive Momentum; CFRA 
Upgrades Health Care Outlook to Overweight 
Key Takeaways 
 
On August 25, CFRA raised its Health Care outlook 
from Marketweight to Overweight. 
 
Although Health Care is viewed as a defensive 
sector, the best-performing stocks in the sector have recently been small-cap biotech firms.  
 
Within biotech ETFs, stocks in the immunology and inflammation therapeutic category have had the 
highest median returns year-to-date (YTD). 
 
ETF flows confirm investor interest in biotech and pharma relative to other Health Care segments.  
Fundamental Context 
On August 25, CFRA raised its outlook for the Health Care sector from Marketweight to Overweight. 
Health Care has been the best-performing of the 11 S&P 500 sectors in the trailing three months through 
September 4, 2026. Within Health Care, the State Street SPDR S&P Biotech ETF (XBI 162 ***) has been the 
best performing of the key U.S.-listed Health Care segment ETFs, with a return of 27.4% in the trailing three 
months. It has outperformed U.S. ETFs representing the pharmaceuticals, health care providers, and medical 
devices industries (see Figure 1).

Analysis of Biotech ETF Returns by Therapeutic Area 
Based on median return by therapeutic category, XBI’s constituents focused on immunology and 
inflammation have been the best performers YTD in 2026. Table 1 is based on a classification of every XBI 
stock holding into a therapeutic category based on its product and business focus (large multi-business 
firms are classified into a diversified category). To avoid survivorship bias, we used XBI’s holdings at the 
beginning of 2026, with returns measured until the last date of trading for firms that were delisted (e.g., due 
to mergers) during the year.  
As of September 2, XBI’s holdings in the immunology and inflammation category had median and mean 
returns of 55.4% and 53.9% YTD, respectively. The YTD best performer in the therapeutic category was 
Oruka Therapeutics Inc. (ORKA 94 NR), which is developing treatments for plaque psoriasis and other skin 
conditions. ORKA could be a potential competitor for Skyrizi, sold by AbbVie Inc. (ABBV 249 ****). Other 
strong performers in the category include Spyre Therapeutics (SYRE 91 NR), an immunology-focused, 
clinical-stage firm up 165.7% this year, and Dianthus Therapeutics Inc. (DNTH 109 NR), which develops 
therapies for autoimmune diseases, up 157.8% YTD through September 2.  
The mean stock return in the infectious diseases category was also high at 56.0%, but with a caveat: it 
was skewed by Moderna Inc. (MRNA 140 **), which appreciated 411.4% YTD through September 2 due to the 
application of its mRNA technology to oncology, outside of its current infectious diseases core business. A 
significant driver was news of the landmark Phase 3 INTerpath-001 clinical trial results for a melanoma 
vaccine in partnership with Merck & Co. Inc. (MRK 148 ****). However, CFRA’s fundamental equity team 
believes MRNA is currently overvalued, and there is significant uncertainty around the regulatory and 
commercialization timelines for the melanoma vaccine. The median return for XBI’s holdings in the infectious 
diseases category was 23.4% YTD, much lower than the category’s mean.

Oncology and hematology stocks – the largest segment in XBI by portfolio weight at the start of the 
measurement period – had a wide dispersion of returns in 2026 YTD. Erasca Inc. (ERAS 16 NR), a clinical 
stage oncology firm, was one of the best-performing stock holdings in XBI YTD, up 340.1%. In contrast, 
Olema Pharmaceuticals Inc. (OLMA 11 NR) was down 56.7% YTD. This highlights how stock-specific risk can 
be very high in smaller-cap biotech segments, as several firms are still in the early clinical trial stage. The 
wide dispersion in the category is consistent with differing sentiments around each firm’s scientific 
approach, competitive landscape, and prospects for being acquired.

Small-Cap Biotech Stocks Led YTD in a Defensive Sector 
Although Health Care is traditionally viewed as a defensive sector, riskier small-cap stocks and ETFs 
have recently led the sector. The Invesco S&P SmallCap Health Care ETF (PSCH 54 **), which holds small-
cap stocks across multiple Health Care industries, was up 18.7% in the trailing three months and 23.4% YTD. 
It outperformed its large-cap counterpart, the State Street Health Care Select Sector SPDR ETF (XLV 167 
**), which was up 12.5% and 11.7% over the trailing three months and YTD, respectively.  
This small-cap outperformance has been more pronounced in the biotech sub-industry. As a result, ETFs 
that have skewed their exposure toward the biotech sub-industry and the small market cap range have 
tended to be the best YTD performers in Health Care. Figure 2 compares the broad-based XLV, the iShares 
US Pharmaceuticals ETF (IHE 103 *), and the biotech-focused XBI based on their exposures by market-cap 
range. As a subset of the S&P 500, XLV holds primarily large-cap stocks across multiple Health Care 
industries and returned 11.7% YTD. The more pharma-focused IHE also tends to skew toward large caps but 
still holds some small- and mid-cap stocks. In contrast, XBI has only 17.2% of its portfolio in large caps, with 
over half in small-cap stocks, and has been the best performer of the three this year as of September 4. 
However, investors in XBI have also taken on more risk. XBI has an annualized return volatility of 28.0%, 
significantly higher than 18.1% and 11.2% for IHE and XLV, respectively.

Biotech investors have gone deep into the very risky micro-cap range have not been rewarded. The 
median YTD return for micro-cap holdings in XBI was -32.8%, compared to a 30.4% median return for XBI’s 
constituents in the small-cap range. Small-cap firms represent relatively later-stage, de-risked assets 
compared to micro caps, and therefore, may be potentially attractive targets for acquisitive big pharma firms 
looking to replenish drug pipelines. They are also likely to have more cash runway compared to micro caps.

Looking Ahead 
CFRA expects biotech to continue to show strength while also expecting the Health Care trade to 
broaden to other industries in the sector. XBI currently has a high Power Rating of 94 (on a 1-100 scale) 
from Lowry Research, the technical division of CFRA. CFRA’s fundamental equity team also has a positive 
outlook for biotech, with an average market-cap-weighted STARS rating of 3.7 for biotech stocks under its 
coverage. This analyst-derived rating is based on earnings, other fundamentals, and valuation metrics. While 
the outlook for biotech is positive, CFRA’s team also has high STARS ratings for other Health Care sub-
industries, indicating that the Health Care trade could broaden. It has average market-cap-weighted ratings 
of 3.9 for both pharmaceuticals and managed health care and 3.7 for life sciences tools & services. While the 
average STARS rating for health care distributors is lower, at 3.3, CFRA has a positive trend outlook for that 
sub-industry as well.
