In this note, we take a deep dive into real-time residential REIT trends (including 
proprietary scraping data) heading into September conferences. Sunbelt demand is 
improving, with absorption near record levels and outpacing deliveries in 3Q26; 
however, the rent growth recovery remains gradual. CPT’s presentation ahead of 
conferences reflected a positive blends result (+1 to +2% for 3QE versus GSe +0.7%) 
while MAA’s came in below consensus expectations (0.6% QTD vs GSe of +0.9%). This 
tracks our scraping data of REITs’ listings websites, which showed that CPT’s rents 
held up better than MAA’s over the course of July and early August but have begun 
to recede in recent weeks (we believe reflected in CPT’s wide range of 3Q blends 
expectations). We expect continued seasonal deceleration in rents as we enter the 
weakest seasonal months (September - December). By contrast, ESS rents have held 
up better according to our scrapes, and West Coast fundamentals are improving 
across several realtime indicators: San Francisco/San Jose are seeing better 
migration trends, improving vacancy, stronger rent growth, and the lowest supply 
outlook across apartment REIT markets. Our deep dive into AI job postings trends 
shows that San Francisco, San Jose and Seattle (Bellevue & Redmond, in particular) 
are all seeing significant growth in AI job postings, which is driving accelerating rent 
growth across these key West Coast markets. We show that rent growth in San 
Francisco (City) may have finally peaked (though remains strongest in the nation). In 
single family rental, build-to-rent and shadow supply (for-sale converting to 
for-rent) headwinds are easing, evidenced in SFR listings finally declining YoY 
(particularly in Phoenix and Florida, where INVH has outsized exposures). In turn, 
market-weighted rent growth for INVH’s portfolio has caught up to AMH’s portfolio 
in recent months reflected in a slightly better rent update heading into the 
conference. The ZORDI (Zillow SFR Demand versus Supply index), a forward indicator 
of market rent growth, continues its positive inflection, strengthening our conviction 
in the trajectory for SFR rent growth over the coming months. See below for more 
details.

AI Hiring Data 
New AI job postings across major West Coast markets have accelerated sharply year over 
year. As of July 2026, postings were up approximately 230% in San Francisco, 204% in 
San Jose, and 100% in Seattle, reinforcing the West Coast’s leadership in technical AI 
hiring. New York has also emerged as a major AI employment hub, with postings 
reaching roughly 3.9 times the prior-year level. Sunbelt markets are experiencing similar 
momentum, although their absolute hiring volumes remain modest compared with 
coastal markets. Within the Seattle area, while Central and North Seattle have the 
highest absolute number of AI job postings, AI roles represent a higher proportion of 
overall postings in several Eastside submarkets, including Redmond and Bellevue.

Sunbelt absorption trends remain strong in 3Q 
 
 
3Q26 absorption remains strong across Sunbelt markets. Exhibit 14 shows 3Q 
absorption volumes since 2001. We note that the market is on track to absorb ~58k 
units in 3Q. This would represent the second-highest 3Q absorption since 2001. We 
highlight the following: 
Absorption finally outpaces supply in key markets.  Exhibit 15 shows Sunbelt 
n
markets’ absorption volumes and delivery volumes since 2019, and Exhibit 16 shows 
the difference between Sunbelt markets’ absorption and deliveries. 2Q saw 
absorption exceed deliveries by approximately 27K units, with this positive trend 
expected to continue in 3Q at roughly 18K units. 
Sunbelt absorption is expected to outpace deliveries for two consecutive quarters, 
n
supporting an improvement in vacancy rates. However, elevated concession usage 
continues to limit pricing power, keeping rent growth slightly negative to flat. 
3Q26 is also expected to mark a strong demand quarter for coastal markets, with 
n
absorption outpacing new deliveries across both the East and West Coasts (Exhibit 
17 and Exhibit 18), supporting tighter vacancies and stronger market fundamentals.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
