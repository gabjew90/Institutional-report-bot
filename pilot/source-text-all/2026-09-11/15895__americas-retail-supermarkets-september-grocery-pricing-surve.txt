Americas Retail: Supermarkets: September grocery pricing survey 
shows relatively stable prices sequentially; mixed trends across gaps
10 September 2026 | 3:10AM CDT
In this note, we discuss takeaways from our September grocery pricing survey. 
Overall, prices were relatively stable m/m. WMT continued to have the lowest prices, 
while we observed mixed trends across price gaps. We also evaluate HundredX 
insights on price and value across the food retail landscape. 
Takeaways from our grocery pricing survey 
 
 
We performed a brief study on September 3, 2026, surveying the prices of 38 SKUs 
in dairy, frozen goods, dry grocery, HPC, and produce categories. The retailers 
surveyed include Kroger, Albertsons, Walmart, Sprouts Farmers Market, Whole Foods 
(WFM), and Dollar General.  
WMT continued to have the lowest prices 
As highlighted in Exhibit 1, WMT had the lowest prices at -13.4% vs. the group 
average (narrowed from -14.3% last month), followed by DG at -6.1% (vs. -6.3% last 
month). WFM had the highest prices in the group at +9.1%, followed by SFM at 
+6.8%. KR had the highest SKU availability for the products surveyed at 38, followed 
by WMT at 37.

Looking by category (Exhibit 2), WMT had the lowest prices in dairy (-18.8%), frozen 
foods (-14.6%), dry grocery (-11.3%), and produce (-12.7%), while DG had the lowest 
prices in HPC (-10.5%). On the other hand, ACI had the highest prices in frozen foods 
(+20.8%) and HPC (+10.3%), WFM had the highest prices in dairy (+17.4%) and produce 
(+8.8%), and SFM had the highest prices in dry grocery (+23.8%). That said, we note 
limited SKU availability for SFM in dry grocery.  
 
Prices overall were relatively stable sequentially 
We compare how prices for our select SKUs have changed since our last survey in August 
2026. The overall average basket was relatively stable m/m with decreases in dry 
grocery and dairy, largely offset by increases in produce, frozen, and HPC. By retailer, we 
observed the most sequential decreases from KR, ACI, and WFM with 2 out of 5 
categories, and the most sequential increases from KR with 3 out of 5 categories. 
Looking at the tracked basket for each retailer, we observed the greatest m/m increase 
at SFM, followed by WMT, while ACI, KR, WFM, and DG decreased. 
 
Mixed trends across price gaps 
Considering how the price gap has evolved, we compare the price variance against the 
group average and learn that price gaps narrowed m/m for 3 out of 6 six retailers in our 
survey including ACI, DG, and WMT, while KR, SFM, and WFM widened. Compared to the 
group average, prices were relatively higher vs. last month for SFM, WFM, DG, and WMT, 
while KR and ACI were lower. 
Additionally, price gaps narrowed vs. a year ago for SFM, WFM, DG, and WMT, while KR 
and ACI widened. Compared to the group average, prices were relatively higher vs. last 
year for ACI, DG, and WMT, while KR, SFM, and WFM were lower.

HundredX insights on price and value across grocery 
 
 
Consumer survey data from HundredX revealed the strongest net purchasing intent for 
large regional grocers, clubs, hard discounters, and specialty grocers across our 
selection of food retailers, while dollar stores and conventional grocers tracked on the 
lower end. Looking to price and value as positive net drivers to each company’s NPS 
score (recognizing there are multiple factors evaluated beyond these two), we note a 
stronger correlation with net purchase intent (NPI) for value vs price, underscoring that 
value perception for consumers extends beyond simply price.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
